Serious Business

Apr 24 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
This is certainly a risky choice. Windy could have played one of his games, gone somewhere to help continue his change in image, but instead he chose this. And it certainly seems representative of the change he wants to make. You see, it’s generally all in the setting. It’s the originality and the attention grabbing nature of a promo, of an interview, that gets a wrestler over. Originality and interesting settings are what have made Windy Bridges successful, they are what have made him interesting and the talk of whatever promotion he has been in. And this setting is certainly attention-grabbing… ----------------------------------------------------- We are in the middle of a ring. Where this ring is, if anyone is there, who the ring belongs to… all questions that cannot be answered. It is an indistinct ring. Worn blue mat, no logo, dirty, dusty turnbuckles and loose black ropes. Right smack in the middle of the ring is a simple wooden stool. This is certainly not an MWE ring. It is lit by a bright light coming from above. In fact, the entire inside of the ring is completely lighted, completely visible. But beyond the mat, the four turnbuckles and the barrier that is the ring ropes… complete darkness. And if you haven’t gotten the metaphor yet, it’s the perfect picture of the struggle between good and even. Devine, blinding light. Vengeful, angry darkness. Jocking for position. Fighting for every inch of space in an endless war. Representing the eternal struggle that reigns over all of us. And this man in particular is facing the biggest personal struggle of his life. He appears out of the darkness. Sharply dressed, dark suit, as if he just came from a funeral (and he may as well have). The dark, almost black, pin stripped suit envelops a sharp blue shirt, and more importantly, the man within the suit. Windy Bridges takes a step through the ropes and enters the ring. Completely unphased by the change in brightness, Windy takes a step towards the middle of the ring, towards the wooden stool, and he takes a seat. Sitting as if about to take a high school picture, hand on his knee, looking straight ahead, Windy doesn’t smile. He doesn’t frown. His face is expressionless, much like this scene, and much like his composure in recent times. Gazing into the emptiness of his eyes, one is uncertain whether Windy has a lot on his mind or is simply focused on the task at hand. Nevertheless, after a moment of allowing us to take in this scene—or lack thereof, Windy finally gains an expression on his face. Windy smiles… ear to ear… almost a shit-eating grin. It does not seem natural, yet it is there, as he finally opens his mouth to address the people watching, and to address the many questions that are certainly flowing through his own head, yet alone the heads of his fans. “So… Sinc Mercier! I’ve heard a lot about you kiddo. You’re surely something from what I see. Former US Champion. Hell, just like me, you never lost your US title. Great feud with Mason Resoan, I hear you won feud of the year! Wow, you certainly have some nice things under your banner. I gotta say, very impressive indeed. Actually, incredibly impressive, for someone who’s made himself out to be a complete joke!” Windy’s smirk grows a little bit longer, a little wider. Maybe it’s the irony of Windy Bridges calling someone a joke. Maybe it’s the fact that if anyone knows when someone it’s a joke, it’s Windy Bridges, and therefore Windy knows that it is a fact, and it therefore amuses him. Or maybe it’s that he’s just starting to slightly lose his mind. “You see, I thought what I was going to say when I was going to come out here. When I set this little shindig up. I thought about treating you with respect. I thought about praising you and wishing you well in our match at Monday Night Mayhem. But to be perfectly honest, Sinclair, I don’t see how I could do any of that in this case. I have no respect for you. Why should I come out here and lie to the good fans of the MWE, and more importantly, to you? Why would I have any reason to do that? Now I may not know much about you. Our paths have only crossed on one occasion backstage. But I know enough to see right through your petty games… your little insults you stayed up until 2 in the morning to come up with… I know who you are, Sinclair.” Suddenly, Windy’s expression changes from pure shit-eating grin to pure seriousness. And incredible transformation. A transformation that has been brewing for weeks, months, maybe even years. This is not the same Windy Bridges people have come to know and love, and that cannot be emphasized enough. This is a man who has been through a lot, and is done with games and jokes. This is a man that’s ready for business. “It’s funny.” The irony of a man with a completely serious look on his face saying that what he’s talking about is funny. “Sinc Mercier is what Windy Bridges was a year ago.” The cockiness of a man referring to himself in the third person. “Sinc Mercier is EXACTLY what Windy Bridges used to be.” The cold-hearted truthfulness in a man’s voice. “You see Sinc, just in case you haven’t done your homework, and it doesn’t seem like you have, let me give you a little history lesson.” Could this get any more condescending? For a man that claims he was who he’s talking about, he certainly doesn’t have much sympathy. And he has certainly changed. “Two years ago, a man burst onto the wrestling scene, along with his partner, a childhood friend. And they were ever so popular. Eventually, they went their separate ways, but the one man remained prominent in the wrestling scene, and slowly became one of the most popular good guys in the history of the business. He was fun loving, he was care free, and most importantly, he was a joker. Although he enjoyed success, he did not care much for it. He enjoyed it when he won, he enjoyed it when he won championships and accolades, but what was most important to him was that the people that he was wrestling in front of were entertained. That at the end of his matches, he’d go back to the locker room and his peers would praise him for the effort he put in to be entertaining, win or lose.” Windy stops talking for a second. He looks out, into God-only-knows-what, since it’s nothing but darkness from one angle. Having turned his head slightly from the camera, he continues his story, almost as if he was visualizing what he was saying, reminiscing. “Then, slowly, but surely, things began to change. The losses began to pile up, even moreso than before. The frustration started to settle in. His peers stopped praising him and started making fun of him behind his back. ‘He’s nothing but a joke’. ‘He cares more about unrealistic, fantastic spots than winning and creating success for himself.’. Then the fans stopped caring. Then the injuries started racking up, and before you know it, this man actually became one… big… joke…” Windy hangs his head, almost shamefully. “And along with his reputation, and along with his success… went his pride… the glory he once felt to be able to entertain his fans and to compete in that ring… and more importantly… his health. He was constantly injured… constantly sick. And at any moment when he started to get his mojo back, he was tossed back down swiftly and mercilessly with another loss to another person who didn’t deserve to beat him. This man… was lost… and if you’re too stupid to realize… this man… was me!” Windy raises his head and snickers once again, that shit-eating grin that’s so difficult to become accustomed to. And it’s eerie, because this is not a moment a man would snicker or smile, saying all these negative things about himself. And even though his current facial expression may not make any sense to an onlooker, neither has his attitude or his actions in recent memory. “But so much as I was down, and just about out, something then happened to me, Sinc Mercier. I realized that I can no longer be satisfied with satisfying others. I realized that I was a human being, that needed gratification and could no longer take gratification from that of others. I realized… that I needed to look out for myself for once. And it hasn’t been that long, but if you having realized, I’m starting to get on a roll.” Once again, Windy’s smile widens. Probably from thinking of the pain and suffering he has caused since the last pay-per-view, which, again, is so odd, if you know the man behind the suit. “At Highway to Hell, I handily defeated the most highly touted debutant in a long time in MWE. And just two weeks ago at the last edition of Monday Night Mayhem, I not only handily beat the greatest female wrestler of all time and now MWE legend… but I retired her. She no longer wrestles! She hung up her boots! And it was after a match with me! That kind of stuff doesn’t just happen!” Windy begins to get rowdy. He takes his hand off his elevated knee, resting on the stool, which seems to not have moves since he first began talking, almost like his attitude and his composure. Windy readjusts himself and his tie, and now sits straights towards the camera, as he lifts his head and looks it dead on, almost as if he thought he was looking right at Sinc Mercier himself. “You see, Sinc Mercier, I don’t know if you haven’t been paying attention or if you just have a small brain, but if you didn’t know, this is the Windy Bridges you’re going to be getting in the ring on Monday night! The hype-killer. The career-killer! The edgy Windy Bridges! Not that washed up, washed down, thrown around, cereal-eating loser that so many people supposedly came to know and love! I am NOT a joke! And I am NOT a nugget! Nor a redneck!” Windy finally gets up from his stool. Now you gotta think he means business… one might even say… serious… business…? “I am no longer any of the things that people have come to know me for. I am a new man. I am a confident man! And I don’t know how much I have to keep saying it, but I am a man that will do anything and everything he needs to do to continue being successful! I’m through playing games! I’m through doing what I do for the fans. I’m through sacrificing my own satisfaction and even myself so that others go home feeling they paid their money’s worth.” Windy takes a step forward as he approaches the ring ropes. He walks forward, edging towards where the camera is. Step by step. Until he finally reaches the ring ropes and grabs them. “Sinc, I’m sure you’re sitting somewhere, eating some microwavable popcorn, wearing your pyjamas and scratching your balls, asking yourself… ‘Windy, why are you telling me all of this?’ Well, it’s very simple Sinclair, my boy. It’s a warning. Because what I see in you… is all the failure… all the misery… all the pain… all the tears that I endure for a long portion of my career before now. And it pains me to see it! It truly does! You see as much as I care for myself and I care about my success and my accomplishments to come, it truly does pain me to see someone go down the exact same path of ridiculousness that I did.” Windy shakes his head. Condescendingly. “It’s sad, really. But it’s not too late for you Sinclair! You still have time to get out of this downward spiral! And I’m extending my faithful hand to you, take my advice, and stop acting like a childish idiot, and start taking yourself seriously, and you might live to see the day that the words Sinc Mercier and respect can be spoken in the same sentence without any sarcasm. And trust me, you may not want to hear this, but some day, you’ll thank me. I’ve been through the exact same thing, and it isn’t any fun. So here’s to hoping you see the light, Sinc. I know you want to have fun, but Monday night, will be all about business.” Now, Windy looks as serious as ever. The fire in his eyes rages. His hands make the ring ropes tremble not only in front of him, but all around him. But yet, he remains generally calm. Collected… focused. “It’s going to be about the outcome. It’s going to be about the reward… the W under the winner’s record. It’s going to be about making a statement and making an impact, a sudden one. It’s going to be about everything that you don’t seem to comprehend in this business. And I hope you see that, or else there’s going to be hell to pay brother. Forget the strip clubs, the clothing stores, talking down to people from your past… forget all that crap and all those tactics. Sinc, this is me, Winston Montgomery Bridges, talking to you, Sinclair Mercier. And I’m being sincere.” And for once, me might actually look it. Whether he’s being serious or just a really good actor, however, remains to be scene. “I chose this setting for a reason. I don’t need any cheesy gimmicks to beat you. I don’t need any cheesy little outings to get into your head. All I need is my words, and myself. And come Monday night, all I will need to do my talking is what I do in this very ring. No gimmicks. No jokes. No games. Just Windy Bridges. And hopefully you will learn a thing or two… about respect… about pride… about the competitiveness in this business. I’m willing to give you a heads up Sinc, and I’m willing to help you out, but you have to cut the crap out. You’re a professional, start acting like one.” Once agan, Windy lowers his head. Gathering his thoughts. Displacing what he’s feeling and turning it into words. Something he seems to have become a master of in recent times. No longer is this a man of few words. No longer is this a man that speaks in, of all things, a thick southern accent. This is a learned man, a well-spoken man, and a man that clearly has a message. “And now, the olive branch has been extended. The kind, caring words have been spoken. The message sent. The question is… will it be received the way it was intended to? Will you take it for what it’s worth, and take it to heart and take it seriously? Or will you shrug it off and pull some more crap?” Windy raises his head once again, and his composure and expression has changed, once again. Two-faced, two-emotioned, call it what you will, but these theme to be the expressions of a man in an inner conflict with himself, regardless of what the man on the outside seems to portray. “If you try that kind of stuff with me… at this point in our respective careers… well… Sinc… then the olive branch will be retracted. The lesson cancelled. And then, it will no longer be at all about helping you, Sinc. It will no longer be a lesson in what can happen to you if you’re not careful. It will transform into whatever it takes for me to win. And if you have to learn the hard way, then so be it. I understand what it’s like, Sinc, but I will not sacrifice myself for you. Monday night, it will be all about business. And whether you like it or not, I am not there to have fun. I am not there to joke around. I am there to win, and to further my career. And if you get in my why, instead of out of it, where you belong, well, then, you have something coming that I cannot be responsible for.” Windy takes a final look at the camera. Eyes wide. Heart on his sleeve, or so it seems. “Monday night will be for the big boys. Monday night will be all about… Serious Business…” Calm, and collected, Windy doesn’t even flinch. He stares into the camera until he is certain his message is completely across, and it has to be. He has poured it all out. A sincere message, or so it seems. A plea, or so it seems. And whether it is received or not, doesn’t change what he is out to accomplish. As he said, Monday night, will be fun, for no one. Windy takes one last look out into the camera and finally lets go of the ropes. Instead, he goes through them and exits the ring and disappears in the darkness. The low buzzing noise of the light above the ring ceases, as the light goes out and the scene cuts to black. Pure darkness.
