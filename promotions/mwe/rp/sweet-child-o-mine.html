Sweet Child 'O Mine

Sep 3 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
“You know, Windy, I brought you into this because I thought you would help my campaign to run for Governor.” The voice is powerful and distinct. We’ve heard it before. But yet we do not see who the voice belongs do. Staring … rather listening… through a closed door, we can see a shadow moving through the glass panel. A large, intimidating figure, the voice is definitely familiar. “But instead, you’ve caused me nothing but trouble over the last month. First, you SOMEHOW let George Prax escape from the prison with the highest level of security in the country –“ The man’s voice is rudely interrupted by another very distinct voice, that we’ve heard plenty of times before. “Now I already told you that wasn’t my fault. It just… kinda… happened.” The voice is coarse and froggy. You can tell that it’s owner has been through a lot. It sounds almost humbled. “In any case he did it when our guard was down and when you were around. Which speaks for itself.” The muffled voices are coming from the other side of a door, surrounded by a plain, gray wall. No markings identify this building or area. “Hey man, I asked your guys to stay outside the room, not go for a smoke break. Nor did I tell the guards outside to hold their fire and let him climb over the fence. As a matter of fact—“ Just like his predecessor was so rudely interrupted by our horse-throated friend a few sentences earlier, the powerful man returns the favour. “In any case, it happened. And it didn’t make me look good the next day when I announced my intention to run for governor. And especially not when I announced you as my running mate.” “Well that was your choice, not mine. I’m glad to help you out, but don’t go blaming all your problems on me. I’ve got enough of those as it is. Alright, enough of this crap. We know exactly who “our horse-throated friend” is. It’s former MWE United States Champion Windy Bridges! A hand pops up from behind the camera, presumably belonging to the cameraman, as he knocks on the door and immediately opens it, not even bothering to wait for a response. “What the Hell do you think you’re doing?” The man is none other than Arnold Glynn. Warden of Oswald State Correctional Facility in Massachusetts, and prominent, up-and-coming member of the Republican Party. And as you may have already guessed, he is also the owner of the deep, powerful, intimidating voice. Glynn is wearing what he probably sleeps in, a business suit and tie. Like most politicians these days, his suit is very plain, dark blue in color with a plain red tie. A pin of the American Flag is on the top left corner of his jacket. The tall, powerful black man is pacing around the room, arms against his hips. “It’s OK, Arnold.” As we said, the other voice is definitely that of Windy Bridges. Windy is similarly dressed, although a tad flashier, all things considered. His suit is blue and striped, and tie is green with a unique pattern. Windy’s face has been shaven clean, and the scars he suffered at the hands of Ace Cannon nearly two months ago have healed nicely. A nice change from the seemingly bruised and battered Windy Bridges that we’ve gotten re-acquainted to in recent times. “I’m kinda contractually obligated to let them follow me around. For interview and promo purposes.” Glynn’s expression changes from one of surprise to one of pure annoyance, as he looks at the cameraman and Windy crookedly. “You know, that brings me to my second point. If it wasn’t enough that I had to explain to the press that my running mate DID NOT aid and abide one of the countries most prominent criminals, in a few minutes, WE have to go to a press conference and explain to them why there’s a video of that same person on MWE.com drinking and rambling about his matches at some bar in Pittsburgh!” Windy, sitting in a chair, looking much more relaxed than his counterpart, smiles and lets out a pretty loud laugh, to the annoyance (yes, he can get even more annoyed) of Arnold Glynn, who has taken a seat on a table across the room, rather awkwardly. “Arnold, let me ask you something. Did you not know what you were getting yourself into?” Glynn shifts uncomfortably in his convenient (for the situation) yet uncomfortable makeshift seat on top of his desk. “I’m a pro wrestler. I’m Canadian. And I’m a drinker and a loudmouth. Hell, I like to go around calling myself the Canadian Redneck. The only reason I’m even allowed to run in this election is because the person you’re complaining about – George Prax – made me get my American citizenship when we started REV~Pro. So go figure, Arnold.” “Windy, what are you getting at here?” “What I’m getting at, Arnold, is that when you brought me into this thing, you knew you were taking a risk. When you announced me as your potential Lieutenant Governor, you knew it was a long shot to try and save a floundering campaign.” Glynn grudgingly nods in agreement. “And look what’s happened? Despite my apparent controversy, you’re all over the press, getting loads of attention, and you’re leading the polls for the first time this election. It actually looks like you might win.” Almost like an angry child, Glynn half-pouts and crosses his arms. “People love what I do. They love Windy Bridges, they love it when I take a drink and turn into a redneck. They love it when I play music and rant about MWE stuff. They love everything about me. Now, I wasn’t so sure when you said you basically told me that you wanted to exploit that, but I like your platform and I like what you want to do for the state of Massachusetts. And hell, if it’s me and my antics that get you in that spot where you can make those decisions, then that has to be a good thing.” Windy stands up and smiles. Maybe wider than we’ve seen him smile in months. “Arnie, a couple ‘o beers and a rant ain’t gonna lose you an election. If you lose, this isn’t on me. I ain’t no Sarah Pailin.” Glynn smirks, almost chuckles, as he gets up and joins Windy in the middle of the room. “Windy, I really hope I don’t regret this. But alright. I trusted you enough to bring you in, even though my advisors were strictly against it. It’s only fair that I trust you and take your word. Now let’s go, we got a press conference.” Glynn and Windy shake hands as Windy extends his other arm to show the door to Glynn. Glynn exits and heads down the hall as Windy quietly approaches the camera. “Hey now, if you guys haven’t seen what happened at the bar the other night, check it out. It was a hoot!” Windy winks at the camera as he quickly steps up the pace to join Glynn halfway down the hall as the camera fades to black… The date at the bottom of the screen indicates that we are now 2 days in the past. Wednesday, September 1st, 2010, in Pittsburgh Pennsylvania. The scene is a familiar one. Well not exactly. But it’s a setting that isn’t exactly a stranger to many wrestlers and their promos. It’s a bar. Your run of the mill stuff. Average in size, with a fairly big parking lot on the side. Frankly, bigger than a place like this would probably ever need. Have you ever noticed that about these places? I mean, couldn’t they use that space for something useful, instead of parking spaces that people are never going to use? Anyway. The name of the bar is “The Lonely Moose”, a convenient name when you consider who’s promo we’re about to witness. Then again the name could have played a roll in the setting. The tall, cabin-slash-cottage-looking structure is fairly unique for the Eastern United States, and it might lead many to wonder if the owner of the establishment was maybe Canadian. The camera slowly pans into the scene and approaches the building. The work is somewhat shaky, as if the camera is being operated by an amateur. Are we watching MWE or TMZ? As we get closer to the structur0065, a car pulls up right to the front of the building. [img=http://farm2.static.flickr.com/1118/1336799676_2dc17710a7.jpg] The (very) dirty red truck has a Canadian flag painted on each door and the hood. Barely even looking at it, you can tell that it probably has more kilometres on it than Brian Michaels has notches in his belt. The scene doesn’t remain static for long, as the driver’s side door wildly swings open. Out of the car appears none other than Windy Bridges! But it’s a very different Windy Bridges than the one we just saw live, back in an office building in Boston, Massachusetts. This Windy Bridges is much more like the one your parents let you watch when you were growing up. This Windy Bridges is dressed in dirty ripped jeans. And not the ones you buy ripped, that’s stupid. These jeans are ripped because Windy actually does work in them, and because his parents taught him to never throw away something until you REALLY couldn’t use it anymore. He’s also wearing the newest MWE t-shirt, with the oh-so controversial “Throwback” logo: [img=http://i889.photobucket.com/albums/ac91/SinisterSuperman/logo11.png] To top it all off, Windy is wearing his patented cowboy hat, with his initials written out on the side. With a five o’clock shadow and a huge smile on his face, Windy takes in his surroundings as he notices the camera. “NEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEE-HAAAAAAWWWWW!” Windy lets out the loud exlcain, which catches the attention of a few non-suspecting passers-by on the busy boulevard in the background. Nonetheless, Windy, unphased, looks right into the camera, just like he did a few moments ago – or, really, 2 days into the future. “We’re here at the Lonely Moose in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania. I don’t know about you but it seemed like a great place to cut this week’s promo, don’t ya think?” Windy looks around and takes the situation in again. “Now I gotta admit. I never heard o’ this place before in my life. I got no idea why it’s named the Lonely Moose, or why I feel like I’m in the Old Country right now, but I gotta tell ya, it just felt right. And I’m likin’ this!” Windy steps away from the camera and makes his way into the bar as the camera fades out yet again. We fade right back in, right at the bar, where Windy is right back to work drinking beer. He looks back at the camera, with a somewhat surprised look on his face. “They’ve got Oh Canada Lager! Can you believe it!?” Windy clings his bottle with a stranger sitting next to him and down a large few gulps of the beer he manufactures back in Canada. In the background, we can hear the sound of amateur singing. Oh yes. This bar has Canadian beer AND Karaoke! Windy turns around, leaning against the bar as he takes another sip of his beer. The fairly attractive young blond on stage is wrapping up her (spotty) rendition of “Sweet Home Alabama”, as Windy, very familiar with that song, chuckles. He looks over to the random, generic man sitting on the barstool next to him. “Ya know I used to sing in a Lynyrd Skynyrd tribute band.” The man smiles at Windy, seemingly uninterested in what he has to say. But Windy, deep in thought at this point, continues without even a thought. “Yup. I was the lead guitarist and sand backup vocals. But that part of my life… well it’s long behind me. I don’t think I could go back to singing professionally now.” The man pretends to turn his attention elsewhere, but Windy will have nothing of it. “The lead singer of that bad – The Simple Men – well… he ain’t never gonna sing again. His voicebox, his larynx; completely shattered. And the guy who did it, wouldn’t even show up and face me like a man. Can you believe that?” Getting somewhat restless and uncomfortable, the man has almost entirely turned his back to Windy, as he looks on to the stage, now nearly completely removed from the situation, and takes another swig of his beer. “And then! While I was lucky enough to still put on a great match and finally get my Moment of Glory, I was so rudely interrupted by this little piece of trash punk kid, Casey Charisma. I mean, who walks around with a name like Charisma?” Having had enough, the man simply gets up and walks over to another part of the bar. But Windy, half dazed, continues on with his speech to no one. “Punks like that don’t even need to explain themselves to me. I know exactly why he attacked me, I know exactly why he came out to the ring at the end of my match with David Whitman and ruined any chance I had to salvage my night. He sees the old brass, the experienced guys, the guys who have paid their dues, and he decides to ruin their moment so he can have his own. So he can make a name for himself and he can take the spotlight for himself. Well lemme tell ya something, he’s gonna get his, he’s gonna get his soon. Once I take care of this other brat, J.P. Smith.” The bartender walks over as Windy finishes his sentence, and looks around confused. There’s no one around Windy. “Excuse me, sir, are you alright?” Windy comes out of his bubble and quickly realizes that the man he was half-talking too has now disappeared. “Ugh, ya. Hey, another beer?” Nice recovery. Windy smiles, cheeks red with embarrassment, as the bartender obliges and pops open another beer. Windy takes the final sip of his first bottle and anxiously moves on to the second. Almost like clockwork, the next karaoke singer finishes his rendition of “Tiny Dancer” by Elton John and an M.C. climbs onto the stage. “Great stuff Al, nice to see you hear again. Now folks, I don’t want you all to get TOO excited, but it seems like we have a big celebrity in the house tonight! Do you all want to see him come up here and sing a song!?” The crowd cheers in anticipation. “This man is a pro wrestling champion. He’s a big celebrity, a singer, a movie star, a businessman! And in two months, he’ll be running for Lieutenant Governor, and he’ll be at the MWE show right here in Pittsburgh this upcoming Monday… Windy Bridges! Come on now! Get up here!” Windy, sort of seeing this coming, blushes and acts like he doesn’t want to go up there. You now. Feigning humility. But the crowd’s cheers get louder, and Windy, finally, begrudgingly (not really), gets up from his barstool and head over to the stage. He takes the mic away from the M.C. and salutes the crowd. “Ya know… I haven’t sang a single note since my buddy Spud McKenzie was nearly killed… but ya know what? This is as good a time as any. And with the camera rollin’, might as well dedicate this song to him…. Ugh, whatever it might end up being.” A very familiar guitar riff begins playing, and Windy, as well as the crowd, quickly place it as “Sweet Child of Mine” by none other than Guns’n’Roses. “Oh ya, this definitely fits. And while we’re at it, might as well dedicate this one to my friend J.P. Smith, who I’ll be facing this coming Monday at the Mellon Arena!” The song finally get going. Windy has no problem with the lyrics, as he begins singing out to the crowd. She's got a smile that it seems to me Reminds me of childhood memories Where everything was as fresh as the bright blue skyyyyyyyyyyyyyyy Now and then when I see her face She takes me away to that special place And if I'd stare too long I'd probably break down and cryyyyyyyyyyyyyy WOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOW Sweet child o' mine WOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOW Sweet love of mine……… Soon thereafter, after a few more verses, the song ends and the crowd cheers, much like they cheer for Windy when he’s kicking ass and taking names. But obviously on a smaller, more intimate scale. The M.C. comes back onto the stage, but Windy tells him to hold off for a minute. “Ya know, I might as well take this opportunity to get a few things off my chest. Since SOME people around here can’t sit through a damn story like normal folk… I’m looking at you, bucko.” Windy does the whole Robart De Niro Meet the Parents fingers-to-the-eyes thing, pointing right at the guy who made him look like a fool right before his song. The indistinct man just shifts uncomfortably in his chair. “But anyway… as most of ya’ll know, I’m here in Pittsburgh on MWE business. And MWE business has decided that I’m supposed to face J.P. Smith on the next edition of Monday Night Mayhem!” The crowd boos at the first sound of Windy’s opponent. Not really out of hatred. Maybe more out of the awe that comes with having someone like Windy Bridges right in front of them. “Now if ya’ll aren’t really familiar with J.P. Smith, it’s real simple. Take a good look at me. Take a few years off my face, get rid ‘o some of the scars, and a lot of the wrinkles. Now add in about a foot of height and maybe fitty pounds, and ya got J.P. Smith.” Windy stands on his tippy toes to try and make himself look taller. He puffs up his cheeks and makes himself look big. Even imitating that silly little smile Smith walks around with on his face. “See JP, I see a lot of the younger me in you. Ya may be bigger, ya may be stronger, and from what I’ve heard, ya seem to have had one hell of a hard time growin’ up, but I definitely see it. Those bright eyes. That unbridled enthusiasm. That incredibly positive outlook on your career, on life, even though ya got nothing to back that kind of attitude up. It’s really remarkable dude. You’re the embodiment of what Windy Bridges woulda been if he had been blessed with better DNA.” Windy chuckles as he thinks of what he’s about to say next. He takes another swig of his beer. Holding that in one hand, and a microphone in the other, he’s slowly, but surely, beginning to slur his words. “It’s crazy. Ya even got it down to the mentor. Eric Derf is as much as a mini-Windy as you are! And I ain’t just sayin’ this cause the lot ‘o ya are Canadian. I mean it. And frankly, I gotta say that I’m flattered. Clearly I’ve left enough ‘o a mark on this industry that the imitators are bound to start showin’ up. ‘N speaking of Eric Derf, ya said somethin’ in your Crowning Glory promo that really struck a chord with me, J.P. Ya said that Derf had taught you that willpower and determination can exceed experience any time. That wrestling goes beyond the body, and into the mind.” Windy points to his head, beer still in hand, and chuckles. “Son, I’m sorry to say this, but ya need to gently remove your head out of Eric Derf’s ass and smell the roses.” The crowd has now gone solid. Uncertain of whether this is a promo or just a drunken rant, the people that are frequenting this bar have clearly gone from awe to discomfort. “Listen man, I really mean no disrespect by any of them, but it’s clear to me that you have a long way to go in this business. And someone’s gotta stop tellin’ you that you’re great, and they gotta start tellin’ you the truth. And the truth is that in this business, ya gotta step on the bottom rung first in order to climb the ladder. Ya gotta rev your engine before your car goes up the hill. Ya gotta walk an inch before you walk a mile. And brother, you feet haven’t even left the ground yet. Somehow, in the short time that I’ve even been aware of your existence, you’ve managed to score matches against guys Eric Derf, Leon Roberts, Supernova… and now… Windy MotherCanuckin’ Bridges.” Windy smiles and tips his hat at the mention of his own name. He takes another swig of his beer, and as if he doesn’t have a care in the world, he goes on with his speech. “Now, I know you got lucky in a couple ‘o times, but as far as I’m concerned J.P., ya haven’t proven anything to me. Ya haven’t proven anything to these people. You’re a rookie, you’re at the bottom ‘o the food chain, and frankly, you’re lucky to even be associated with anyone who has a name of some importance in this industry..” You gotta wonder which version of Windy Bridges is standing on this stage, making this speech. Winston Montgomery Bridges is a man of many faces, many masks, and a man who has been through a lot in this business. And right now, he’s showing that he may be as vulnerable as his opponent, as he takes the final swig of his beer and tosses it offstage. “It seems like in a very short amount ‘o time, you’ve gotten shot, after shot, after shot. Clearly, there are people that see somethin’ in you. And I respect that. I’ve been in a position where I’ve had to prove myself. Hell, all these years later and I still gotta prove myself to anyone and everyone or I ain’t gonna get an inch. Smith, you gotta Hell of a heart and a good head on your shoulders, and I totally understand how someone might see a little bit o’ themselves in ya, kinda like I do right now. But ya gotta realize that with great success in this business, comes great responsibility.” Windy raises an eyebrow and looks up at the sky, as a few select people in the audience that feel FULLY awkward let out a chuckle. “Nah, that ain’t right. What I meant to say is that is that you gotta realize that success comes with time. And in this industry, the way things work around here, ya gotta be patient. And Eric Derf is not the right person to teach you that. Just like you, Eric Derf is a great guy, a fellow Canadian! And he reminds me a lot ‘o me. But just like you, Eric Derf has a lot to learn about the wrestling industry, and he doesn’t know it. And brother, Imma do you a solid. Imma go out of my way, Imma forget about my own problems for one night. Imma forget about Ace Cannon and George Prax and Casey Charisma and Spud McKenzie, and Imma teach you a little bit about what this industry is all about.” Windy steps off the stage. Microphone still in hand, he starts to make his way out to the bar. “Unfortunately, the problem for you is that I currently have a lot of pent up frustration in me. In the short while I’ve been back here in Mayhem Wrestling Entertainment, I’ve been kicked, punched, tossed, battered ‘n bruised. I’ve seen my best and oldest friend brought to within an inch of his life, and I’ve lost all contact with the man who I considered a second father, all because he was too stubborn to serve his damn prison sentence. And son, the only opportunity I’ve had to release any of these frustrations were ruined by a little loudmouth piece of trash who thinks that I ain’t good enough for the spotlight that I’ve earned.” Windy reaches the bar, and lifts his finger up, signalling that he wants another beer. The bartender, as uncomfortable with this as the next guy, hesitates, but eventually obliges Windy, and pops open another Oh Canada Lager. “J.P., unfortunately for you, you’re gonna get some tough love at the next edition of MNM. And Imma give you one free lesson in humility, and teach ya all about payin’ dues.” As Windy grabs his beer off the counter, the camera pans out to show him, from head to toe. Smiling, with his arms out as if to show himself off, Windy does a little spin for the camera, and takes a drink from his fresh beer. And then, Windy’s tone takes a disturbing twist. “Ya see this J.P.? You could have all this some day. You could be as popular as me, as rich as me, as beloved as me. And most importantly, as decorated and revelled as me. When people think Windy Bridges, they think legend. They think franchise. They think championship material. And I ain’t sayin’ all this to sound big headed or cocky, but ya gotta admit that it’s true. Windy Bridges is an institution. But I didn’t get here by fluke. I didn’t just stumble upon all this fame and fortune. I got here with hard work. I got here by garnering experience, and earning respect. I got here by payin’ my dues, and not suckin’ up to someone who just happens to have a minimal amount of fame, like Eric Derf. And I’ve paid dearly to be in the spot that I’m in.” Windy begins to saunter back to the stage. Methodically, thinking clearer than what his behaviour might imply. “Now I don’t wanna slander your buddy Derf, I really don’t. But I gotta tell ya J.P., if ya wanna go anywhere in this business, you gotta stop looking to people like Eric Derf, and start looking at people like me. And I gotta ask you one hell of a serious question” Windy reaches the stage, and climbs right back on it. “Are you ready? Are you ready to make the type of sacrifices that I have? Are you ready to put yourself out there, to make yourself look like a fool once in a while, to embarrass yourself and to make the people around you feel awkward. They’re called sacrifices, Smith, and you ain’t gonna get nowhere without them. You want to be successful? You want advice, mentorship? Well ya ain’t gonna get it with the stuff that Eric Derf has been teaching you. Smith, this is the reality of our business. It makes you cynical, it makes you angry, it makes you have regrets and it makes you lose the people you care the most about.” Windy takes a seat on top of one of the amps on the stage and looks out towards the crowd. Now it’s certain. There isn’t one person out there that’s still comfortable with this awkward speech. “In this industry, you’re gonna get stomped on, you’re gonna get beaten up, down, and all ‘round. Hell, if you’re one ‘o the lucky ones like me, you might even get blown up once or twice. But as much as you give to this business, and as much as it takes away from you… eventually… eventually. It might just give just as much back, maybe even more.” Windy takes one last swig of this, his 3rd or 4th beer (who’s counting), as he looks back out towards the crowd and smiles. “So saddle up, and use whatever other metaphors and analogies you wish, cause on Monday night, Imma take you for a ride, and Imma show you what this business is truly like – and what a guy like Eric Derf has been keepin’ from you all along. And in the process, if any of these frustrations… any of this pent up anger that I’ve found myself re-acquainted with since I came back to this business creep up… well then brother… I’m really sorry.” Windy sits his now empty beer down on the stage, jumps off the amp and begins to make his way out of the bar. He looks out to the people who are staring back at him, and he remains unphased. Slowly, but surely, he makes his way out of the bar, as the scene fades to black. ------------ “So I just want to make things clear. Mr. Glynn and Mr. Bridges are going to make a short statement, but they aren’t going to take any questions. Mr. Glynn has a full itinerary tomorrow morning, and Mr. Bridges has to fly back out to Pittsburgh for the next MWE show.” The sea of local, state and national reports ahead of the sharply dressed woman in front of the podium is rabid. Speaking on top of one another, shouting questions, remarks, flashing their cameras and rustling their papers, any normal person would go mad. But this professional woman has clearly gotten used to the pressures or running a campaign. Seemingly ignoring all the commotion, she continues to speak. “Now without further adieu, please welcome YOUR republican candidates for the governor’s office of the great state of Massachusetts, Arnold Glynn and Windy Bridges! The questions and commotion gets even worse as Windy and Glynn appear from behind a curtain. Glynn steps up to the microphone, as Windy stands closely behind him. “Thank you, all. Now as Miss Roper just said, we will only be making a statement tonight. I just wanted to reassure the people that my stance on all the issues, from family, to the economy, to health care remain unchanged. We plan to improve this state in every sector, and do it in a tax-friendly way. We plan to increase government accountability, we plan to strengthen family values—“ He doesn’t get any further than that. One reporter has heard enough. “Ya, great family values you got there as your running partner.” Glynn sighs. “Now, I know that may have looked bad, but—“ Interrupted for the second time in a row, Windy steps up to the microphone. “It’s alright Arnold. He has a point. I was weak. I had a lot on my mind, I had been through a lot, and I just needed to vent. It happens to the best of us. Unfortunately for me, I had a camera on me in that bar in Pittsburgh, and as someone who’s running for office, I should have been more responsible. And that’s why effective immediately, I am going to remove—“ Windy can’t finish his thought either. A man appears on the stage, dressed all in black and wearing a ski mask. “Hey Windy, how’s about you vent this!” Windy turns his head, confused. And right then, right there, the man reaches into his belt, and pulls out a gun. A .45 handgun, there’s really no way to say how this man or his weapon got across security. But they did. And now we’re faced with this situation. Two men, standing in front of each other, cameras rolling and lights shining bright. The only thing in between the two… a gun… As quickly as the man appeared on stage, his gun goes off. As load as anything you’ve ever heard, the recoil is quick, sudden and strong. The bullet, almost in slow motion, disperses from the gun and begins its unstoppable trajectory towards the podium, and towards Windy. “NOOOOOOOOOOOO!” But just as quickly as the man appeared on stage, just as quickly as his gun discharged it’s bullet, another man steps in the way. Another main gets in between Windy Bridges and the masked man, and, of course, the gun and it’s bullet. And just as quickly, he’s on the ground. Holding his chest, eyes rolled into the back of his head. The crowd in front of them are speechless. The man, on the floor, managed to push Windy out of the way and into safety. But him, he wasn’t as lucky. Arnold Glynn, on the floor, near unconsciousness, is starting to be surrounded by a pool of his own blood. The masked man has already made his getaway, pursued by secret service agents. Over Glynn is the man the bullet was intended for. Nearly in shock, Windy Bridges can only hold his friend’s head as he bleeds, and as the crowd screams in shock and fear in front of them. “SOMEONE CALL AN AMBULANCE!” “STOP ROLLING THOSE CAMERAS!” “SOMEONE GET HELP!” Amidst all the confusion, the scene fades out into pure darkness.
