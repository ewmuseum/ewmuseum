Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The consortium's founding notice, posted 29 December 2007, describes the fed as formed “a few months ago” out of a London meeting that united five already-existing continental wrestling bodies under one banner — the North & Central America Pro-Wrestling Association, AustralAsia Action Pro-Wrestling (billed in the same breath as Triple A Pro-Wrestling), South America Wrestling, the All-European Wrestling Federation, and Africa Pro-Wrestling — convened, in its own account, by a Pay TV mogul chasing “a virtual monopoly in pay-per-view pro-wrestling.” Day-to-day business sat with a General Secretary figure and a Board of Directors drawn from the five member bodies' own heads, run out of a Yahoo Group mailing list.

It billed itself as a booked league: the operator alone decided outcomes, weighed by participation — angles, flashed roleplays, and match strategies filed ahead of a bout — and let a handler set their own level of involvement, from a “part-time” wrestler flashing once or twice a month up to a “full-time” one built for the world stage. Wrestler creation came bounded and original-characters-only: one wrestler or tag team per handler barring exceptions, no shorter than five feet nor taller than seven-foot-three, no lighter than 150 pounds nor heavier than 450, and no real wrestler's likeness — “if he starts acting and talking like Bret Hart he wont be accepted... never has, never will be.”

Each continental body ran its own weekly house show, a written account of a card drawing a few thousand fans at most and never featuring the promotion's own World Champion, while the whole consortium aired a monthly pay-per-view, “The Touring Main Event,” broadcast worldwide and moved city to city, alongside a weekly television program, “The Faces of the WWC.”

Two World Championships sat atop the card: a World Heavyweight title and a World Tag Team title, with each continental body also crowning its own regional champion — the AEWF's, for one, a European line defended only among European wrestlers. A champion owed a defense at least once every three months, against a challenger drawn from the top five world rankings (top three for a continental title) or designated outright by the Board of Directors; a champion sidelined past three months could be superseded by an interim titleholder, with the title declared vacant past six.

What survives is this one notice and the reply it drew the same day: a handler's application to rejoin, naming no card content beyond what the founding notice already gives. No later show, result, or announcement survives to say whether the five-continent structure it proposed ever ran a card.
