Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

This Ultimate Wrestling Federation announced itself as newly formed in recruitment posts of 27 May 1999, and a later report dates its own first card, “UWF Slam,” to 22 May 1999. Twin weekly broadcasts, “Slam” and “Fever,” carried the promotion through the rest of the year, building toward its own pay-per-view line.

A 5 June 1999 Slam broadcast opened a thirteen-man Royal Rumble to decide the last spot in a coming Ultimate Championship tournament, with Deathstalker outlasting the field until the final three came down to Cowboy Jack, Deathstalker, and Rockin' Rebel; Deathstalker won it by ducking a double clothesline and dumping both rivals over the top rope. That same card also crowned Archangel and “Big D” Devestation as rising threats: Archangel won a Boston Crab submission over Golden One after Golden One injured his own knee mid-match, and Devestation, the leader of a stable called the Dungeon of Devestation, knocked out a masked Unknown Wrestler for a ten-count win in a no-disqualification brawl.

Those threads converged three weeks later at UWF's own first pay-per-view, “Championship Mania” (27 June 1999), before 25,000 fans. Archangel fought through the bracket — beating Unknown Wrestler, then his own friend Unforgiven in the semifinal after a masked interloper named Chaple blindsided Unforgiven with a chair unseen by the referee — to meet Devestation, who reached the final after his semifinal opponent, Mighty Tak, turned on his own corner mid-match to join the Dungeon of Devestation. Archangel rolled Devestation up from behind to become the UWF's first-ever Ultimate Champion. The same card crowned Hoodlam the UWF's first Tag Team Champions over the Rhinestone Cowboys, with Devestation and Mighty Tak interfering at ringside to turn the match.

A report dated 28 July 1999 previews UWF's third pay-per-view, “Summer Scorcher,” with Archangel still carrying the Ultimate Championship a month and a half into his reign and defending it there in a Triple Threat match. By 18 November 1999, a UWF Live broadcast shows the roster reshaped again, with Mike “Showtime” Bradshaw holding the Intercontinental Championship and a guest, Great Numpty, appearing on the show. How the Ultimate Championship changed hands between those points, or how UWF's own year ended, isn't in the surviving record.
