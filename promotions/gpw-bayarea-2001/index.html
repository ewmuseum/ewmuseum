A Bay Area e-fed whose founding FAQ named two co-presidents, a full staff and two championships, but folded, by a reader's account, before it ever posted a card. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Global Pro-Wrestling posted its own founding FAQ on 17 July 2001, and it tells an origin story before it tells a single rule. Two wrestlers, the FAQ says, met in the early-to-mid 1990s and later stepped in to save a struggling independent, California Power Pro-Wrestling, from folding, buying a controlling stake with help from a conglomerate's sponsorship money. The new majority owner then renamed the promotion Global Pro-Wrestling over a partner's objections, and the FAQ says the choice “sparked a minor legal wrangle” with the owner of an earlier promotion that had carried the same initials in 1999 and 2000, settled out of court. A television deal and a purpose-built arena in the San Francisco Bay area followed, at least on paper.

Its own rules ran a standard e-wrestling ledger: one singles wrestler per handler, a minimum of one roleplay a week, and outcomes weighed on roleplaying quality first, angle involvement second and written match-strategies third. “GPW is about one thing,” the FAQ says, “having fun.” Its cast of on-air staff ran to a veteran referee, a boxing-announcer-turned-ring-announcer, two reporters fronting its secondary show, and a color commentator built around a running joke — an obsessive, foul-mouthed Vietnam veteran hired as a favor to the promotion's silent investors. Two championships were announced, a World title and a North American title, the World belt given “world” status, in the FAQ's own words, “simply for the hell of it.”

None of it was cast before the roster was ready. A recruiting call posted a week later put the roster at two wrestlers, looking to add six or seven more; four days after that, a further call put it at five, with four heels already signed against a single face and new heel applications held pending until the balance improved. No card, show or match of the promotion's own survives, and its own last surviving recruiting call still describes it as not yet open.

By November 2001, a reader who said he had joined the roster asked the newsgroup what had happened to it, having heard nothing since signing up. Another reader answered that he believed it had closed. Nobody speaking for the promotion denied it.
