Valentine and Curits P. Preston. The two men, who met in the early/mid-1990s, saved a small time independent promotion - California Power Pro-Wrestling - from folding. CPPW had given both MJV [along with younger brother McCloud], and Curtis their starts in the business, and they felt a certain loyalty to the promoter, their original wrestling trainer, the 49-year-old Casper Rickett. Upon hearing of CPPW's imminent closure after some eight years of semi- regular,independent action, Valentine and Preston decided to step in. The pair, good friends from their CPPW days, [along with smaller contributions from McCloud, and Rickett] managed to provide 51% of the capital needed to save CPPW. The other 49% was provided by the Dolphin group - a large conglomerate with which Mad had sponsorship dealings with during his time in National Extreme Wrestling. With a 26% controlling stake in the federation, Valentine - for some, bizarre reason - demanded that the federation's name be changed to Global Pro Wrestling. While this sparked a minor legal wrangle with Clayton Southstar, the owner of the GPW which Em Jiggy Vee had notoriously been an integral part of in late 1999 and early 2000, it was settled out of court by the Dolphin group. Through his father's, a California-based media executive, and his own connections in the televison business, Curtis P. Preston managed to negotiate a television deal for the new GPW. An old warehouse in the San Francisco Bay area was renovated to become the federation's very own, specially-built, one-and-a- half million dollar arena - The Globe Arena. So now, with everything set and a huge, marketing corporation behind it, GPW is ready to return, once more, to the screens of wrestling fans across the world. ---> 1.2 In Real Life (IRL) Global Pro-Wrestling is one of the many e-wrestling federations to be found on the internet. It is run by Portsmouth, England's two favourite sons [not to mention the top two guys on [name withheld]'s Christmas card list] - [name withheld] and [name withheld]. The main aim of GPW is to provide committed writers with an outlet for their talent, via the creation of their own, individual pro-wrestler, their own character, and all the things that follow. Above all - GPW is about one thing... having fun. ===> 2.0 Rules and Regulations <=== The most important part of any, half-decent e-wrestling federation are the laws which handlers must abide by. These ensure that the game remains as fun as possible for all involved. Please... be sure to have read these before submitting an application. Those blatantly ignoring/breaking the rules will be punished, in some way, shape, or form [possibly thumb removal, we dunno]: 1. GPW is a booked e-wrestling federation, meaning the matches are decided by the standard of your roleplaying, angle involvement and the quality of your match-strategies - in that order. [Ref: 3.0 Guidelines for success] 2. ach handler may control one singles wrestler only. GPW has no tag-team division [unless we receive significantly more tag-team applications than expected]. Singles wrestlers may be paired as tag-teams on a regular/semi- regular basis, in order to further storylines. Alliances between singles wrestlers are perfectly acceptable - preferable, even. 3. Your character must be original - no blatant copies of actual wrestlers will be accepted. 4. Each character must submit a minimum of one, and a maximum of three roleplays each week. You will be allowed an additional pre-match roleplay for the VELOCITY show, although this must be set in or around The Globe Arena. In-ring interviews will also be included, although this will be on a first-come, first-served basis. More than three or four in-rings on each show will not be allowed. 5. Flashes can include other handler's characters, but only with their permission/help. All flashes must be realistic [i.e. - no killing or maiming of any characters.] 6. Any challenges made to wrestlers via roleplays must be confirmed with the management beforehand - particularly where special stipulations are involved. 7. And, of course, the old chestnut - every wrestler will suffer a loss at some time during their GPW career. Whinging about losing matches in roleplays or out- of-character will not be tolerated. We suggest that anybody interested in simply winning matches and championships who isn't concerned with roleplaying and angle involvement should join another federation. ===> 3.0 Guidelines for success <=== In my opinion, these are very important in that they outline to you, the handler, what is required of you if your character is to have a successful stint in Global Pro-Wrestling. ---> 3.1 Roleplaying This is probably the most vital part of e-wrestling as a game, let alone GPW. Usually, the most talented roleplayers will be the ones at the top of this league. However, besides quality, consistency is also important. If we are aware that you are producing flashes of a lower standard to that which we know you are capable, if we feel you are not fulfilling your potential, then it is unlikely that your character will be one of the top stars in GPW. Another significant factor is frequency. You are expected to provide a minimum of one flash every week. Although it is important that you flash regularly, the emphasis in GPW is very much on quality over quantity - a single, excellent roleplay will always win over two or three inferior offerings. ---> 3.2 Character development Another very important requirement if your character is to be successful in GPW. If you show that, as a writer, you are able to develop your character, allow them to 'grow', through flashes, angles, etc., if you can show that you have the skills to roleplay your wrestler as something more than a basic, one-dimensional character, then you are certain to make waves in the league. ---> 3.3 Angle involvement If you see yourself as any kind of talented e-wrestler, then you will already be aware of the fact that angles are the single most important part of any half- decent e-fed. Naturally, handlers are encouraged to contact one another and collaborate on angles, to produce storylines which are entertaining not only to them, but to the rest of GPW as well. Angles are also an excellent way to build friendships and alliances - not only in the federation, but in real life as well. Whether you are winning or losing your actual matches in Global Pro-Wrestling, a good feud or angle is a surefire way of making sure your character gets noticed. ---> 3.4 Strategies Whilst they are often considered the worst part of e-wrestling, in this writer's opinion, match strategies are very important. They allow a handler the ability to control his character purely in a wrestling sense: it allows them to have a say in how that character acts when he steps between the ropes. Admittedly, my real point here is that strategies serve, mainly, to make match- writers lives a lot easier. It would be much appreciated if you submitted some sort of strategy for every match your wrestler is scheduled to wrestle in. These needn't be too long, although I feel that any worthwhile strategy must include the following; > Entrance - If you do not wish for your character to make the entrance which you have provided on the application form, then you will be required to provide an alternative in your strategy. Will he come out of the crowd? Will he be brandishing a weapon? Also - use this section to provide any short, pre-match promos. > Beginning the match - How will your wrestler start his bout? Will he be reluctant to begin? Will he attack his opponent before the bell? Will he shake his hand? > Offence - How will your wrestler look to attack his opponent? Which wrestling style [technical, power, brawling, etc.] will he look to employ? Which manoeuvres will he attempt? Will he look to focus on a particular body part? > Defence - How will your wrestler look to protect himself from his opponent's offence? What will he do if he gets into trouble? Will he try to counter his opponent's finisher? If so, how? > Other - How will your wrestler act during his match? Cocky? Nervous? Aggressive? Will he taunt his opponent? Will he play to the crowd? > Spots - These, generally, are for the match-writers benefit. They give you a chance to provide the writer with some ideas for what might happen in the match. Will your wrestler attempt a certain move? Have you planned any short segments for the match? Have you envisaged any particular finishes to the bout? > Post-match - What will your wrestler do after the match? If he wins, will he celebrate? If he loses, will he roll out of the ring, or stay slumped on the canvas, inconsolable? Will he attack his opponent after the match has finished? ---> 3.5 How to fail in GPW Having been told how to succeed in Global Pro-Wrestling, it's important that you also know how to fail in the league. Well, quite simply, one thing will guarantee an instant crash [and subsequent burn] in GPW... inactivity. If you don't roleplay for your match, then you will lose. It's pretty much as simple as that. If this starts happening on a regular basis... you'll be released, and possibly jobbed to hell and back before you go. Another thing that we don't look to kindly upon here at GPW is too much whinging. If you have a personal beef with another handler, that's your business, and theirs. However, just try and keep it to yourselves. We'll do our best to stay impartial and keep GPW free of politics, providing you don't let your egos get out of control to the point where we, and the other handlers, are getting jerked around. However, if you can't do that... there's a good chance you're gonna find yourself looking for a new fed. ===> 4.0 Characters <=== ---> 4.1 Referees Stevie Reubens A small, stumpy man, approaching fifty years old. Has short, greying hair and a moustache. Reubens is the main official in GPW - regarded as one of the best in the sport of wrestling. As a referee for over twenty years, in various feds, Reubens is difficult to outwit and is vastly aware all the tricks of the trade. Referee #2 Referee #3 ---> 4.2 Ring Announcer Frank Silverthorne Tall man, still handsome despite his obvious age. His white-grey hair is always impeccably groomed and he is never seen without his signature black tuxedo, worn immaculately. Silverthorne has made the transition to wrestling after working as a boxing announcer for thirty years. Popular with the fans. ---> 4.3 Reporters Nina Woo A young Asian-American [to quote Peter Novak] girl. Woo is slender, and beautiful - with long, black hair. She's not just a pretty face, either - it's rumoured that she's a UCLA graduate. She won't be caught giggling girlishly or swooning over the good-looking wrestlers - she knows how to ask the tough questions, and she's not afraid to do so. Usually seen wearing a black knee- length skirt and a white dress shirt. Nina is also the presenter of GPW's 'Millennium' programme. Everett Wilson An older man, again, and a compatriot of Casper Rickett from his CPPW days. Wilson, the former ring announcer, has now turned his hand to interviewing. He's very much part of the 'old school', constantly talking everything up and rarely getting to the point in under a minute. He's 50 years of age, with thick, grey hair and a strong jaw. He usually wears khakis and a GPW polo shirt. ---> 4.4 Announcers Lucas Squier Your classic, face 'mark' announcer. He used to work for CPPW as the sole announcer on their very few, video releases and occasional internet broadcasts. In his late twenties, early thirties, he's chronically uncool ["Why did we keep Squier on? Because's such a fruit, of course. It's hilarious!" - Em Jiggy Vee] but has a good knowledge of moves, and such. The perpetual bitch of Peter Novak. Squier is skinny, with short, black hair combed forward and a matching goatee. He usually wears jeans and some kind of GPW t-shirt while announcing. Peter Novak Color analyst. Novak is our take on how 'The Big Lebowski's Walter Sobcheck would act, were he an announcer. He claims that he's Jewish, although he's originally a Polish Catholic. He's also obsessed with Vietnam [where he was proud - and very active - member of the U.S. Army], bowling and his ex-wife Olivia. Novak is a passionate announcer, astonishingly short tempered [especially with Squier] and addicted to use of the 'eff word' to the point were censors have simply given up on him. He's not your typical, heel analyst, though - he despises heels due to his other obsession - law enforcement. The story is that Novak's ex-wife was actually the aunt of Valentine's agent, Kingsley Sweete. After his home security system business went down the toilet, he came to his former nephew looking for a job. He's always enjoyed the violence in pro-wrestling. Em Jiggy Vee and Preston were so amused by his foul-mouthed, in-your-face announcing style, they gave him the seat next to Squier [much to his bemusement]. He's a solidly built [_not_ fat!] guy in his forties, with a military-style crew cut and a squint. A few of Novak's signature catchphrase... PN: Squier, shut the fffffffuck up!! You're out of your element!! PN: I did not watch my buddies die, face-down in the mud, so this rich fuck could get away with that, Squier!! ---> 4.5 Other Madison J. Valentine The legendary. Say no more, really. Super-cool co-owner of GPW. Curtis P. Preston 25 year old Curtis P. Preston is tall and well built. Okay, he's not 7' and 400lbs but, for your average guy, he is bigger than most at 6'4, 240lbs. Curtis is permanently well-dressed and is very, _very_ rarely seen without a suit. His short, slightly spiky, blonde hair always looks well trimmed and neat - as you'd expect of any professional. However, Curtis isn't totally professional - like MJ, he as a slightly comic side. Casper Rickett Original promoter of California Power Pro Wrestling, also holder of a 5% stake in GPW. The 49 year-old has never been one for the spotlight, but still maintains a senior role in the federation. With Valentine and Preston brand new to the promoting game, he provides a wealth of valuable experience. Mendoza Milan Your typical 'rich fuck', as Peter Novak might say. The quintessential yuppie, Milan represents the Dolphin group in an on-screen capacity. He's hardly ever involved, but when he is, he's generally interfering where he shouldn't and spoiling the party. And the fans hate him for it. Milan has long, blond hair, slicked back into a pony tail, always wearing a Gucci suit and sunglasses. ===> 5.0 Shows <=== At the time of writing, GPW is planning to have one, live-action card, featuring around four or five, full matches, using the Action Block System [ABS]. The second card will be a flash show, featuring summary-style matches. The number of full matches may increase, depending on the availability, and reliability of match-writers. The booking committee will try to ensure that every wrestler has at least one match every week - although at times, it may not be possible. Also, I feel it is important to outline the fact that, in Global Pro-Wrestling, the emphasis will be on quality over punctuality. Of course, we will endeavour to have cards ready and in your inbox on time, however things may not always go according to plan. The use of numerous writers to produce a card is notoriously unreliable in e-wrestling, so it is far from inconceivable that things may not always turn out as we would all like. Also, we, like yourselves, have very busy offline schedules, so there will be occasions when we will simply not be able to produce the cards on time. However, be sure that we will try our hardest to post the cards punctually, but we are also certain that any of you would choose quality of writing over timeliness any day of the week. ---> 5.1 Velocity Coming to you LIVE from The Globe Arena in the San Francisco Bay area, VELOCITY is the premier show of GPW. Hosted by Lucas Squier and Peter Novak and featuring about four top-class wrestling matches every week, VELOCITY will play host to all the major superstars of the federation... ---> 5.2 Millennium MILLENNIUM is the place to see all the rising stars of the federation. Hosted by GPW's very own bombshell, Miss Nina Woo, this show will feature interviews, summaries of major dark matches, storyline recaps, et cetera. ===> 6. Formatting <=== We feel that most federations should have some kind of format for cards and roleplays if it is to look at all professional. Thus, the format for Global Pro-Wrestling is as follows... --> All action is written inside [square brackets.] --> All dialogue is written with the speaker's initials, and a comma. No speech marks are necessary. It should end up looking like... GPW: ... this. Please try to stick to this format as closely as possible. Although we will be fairly lenient with this, pieces which aren't properly formatted, particularly for VELOCITY, might be returned to you for correction. If you have any problems with this format, then e-mail us, and we'll discuss it. ===> 7.0 Championships <=== While I feel they shouldn't be the most important thing in an e-wrestling federation, titles certainly are important in sparking feuds and angles, as well as providing a benchmark to the rest of the league. The titles available in GPW are as follows... ---> 7.1 The GPW World Championship The top title in Global Pro-Wrestling. The holder of this title will be the number-one man in the federation - but only for as long as he holds it. Whilst the fed rarely ventures out of California, Preston and Valentine decided to give the belt 'world' status, simply 'for the hell of it'. ---> 7.2 The GPW North American Championship The second-most important title in the league, the NA title will bring prestige to the up-and-comers of GPW. Generally, this championship is a stepping stone to bigger and better things - usually the main event. ===> 8.0 Application form <=== PERSONAL INFORMATION Your name: Your E-mail address: ICQ number: AIM nick: IRC nick: Past E-wrestling experience: Can you match-write for GPW [Yes/No]?
