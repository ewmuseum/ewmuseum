TBirdSCIL — 11 March 2003 Please keep all RP for this match in this folder... PhantomZ — 11 March 2003 FADE IN… (A large man with a mane of long, dark hair pushes past the camera. Another man walks from behind the camera, into the camera’s view. They move down the aisle of an empty arena, towards the ring. TONY ROSS is still standing in front of the apron, collecting his things after his interview with Maelstrom. He looks up at the man walking in front of the camera…)
TONY ROSS: Hey Nemesis, what brings you out here? NEMESIS: Well Tony, I was just gonna get in a quick promo before heading out, and this was the only quiet place I could think of to cut it in.
TONY ROSS: Well um, would you mind sitting down for a few minutes? The bus shouldn’t be leaving for another half hour or so… (Picks up a chair and sets it down in front of the man)
NEMESIS: (sizing up the chair across from Tony, judging it suitable, and sits) Sure Tony, what’s on your mind?
TONY ROSS: (takes a chair of his own, and sits down) Well, it seems you’ve been put in the Gauntlet Match for the TV Title shot at this upcoming “Wired” show in Flint.
NEMESIS: That’s right Tony. TONY ROSS: It’s going to be you, Golem, Stephen Morgan, Cannonball Kidd, Steven James, and Jon Savage in that match to determine the #1 Contender for Poe’s TV title.
NEMESIS: That is, if Poe can keep his title through his match with Larry Tact. TONY ROSS: Very true. Now, you’ve faced two of the men in this match before in GLCW, with quite differing results in each.
NEMESIS: Yes, Stephen Morgan beat me not too long ago in an incredible match, and I took a hard fought victory over Cannonball a few weeks before then.
TONY ROSS: Both matches were very good, as have all your matches since debuting in the GLCW, starting with that barn-burner with Larry Tact at your debut.
NEMESIS: Like I always say Tony, I’m just looking for great matches to put on for those who buy the tickets.
TONY ROSS: Heh heh heh, Nemesis, so far so good. NEMESIS: Thanks Tony. You’ve seen a lot of stellar matches, I know, so I really appreciate it.
TONY ROSS: You’re welcome, but on to Flint, what’re your thoughts going into this match? NEMESIS: This match is one kind I’ve never been involved in. My first Gauntlet match. I’ve been in tournaments all over the world, but never anything so quick and successive as this. You beat one man, and instantly another comes in to try and beat you. No time to rest, no time to regroup. You have to train like you’re going to take on any one, or more, of those six other guys. Depending on the order, you could end up having to face them all, or only one, whether you win or lose. It’s all very exciting, and also gives you a lot to be concerned about. Preparing for a match against one competitor is difficult. Hours of watching tapes trying to learn a strategy, hours training trying to fine-tune your strengths and downplay weaknesses. All in order to take full advantage of a flaw you think you see in the other’s mode of execution. In Flint, looking through and analyzing all that tape is almost impossible, with five times as much watching and training to do, there just aren’t enough hours in the day.
TONY ROSS: We know you have experience with two of the competitors in this match, but what about Golem, Savage, and James? What do you know about them?
NEMESIS: I’m not gonna lie Tony. Right now, I don’t know much of anything about Steven James or Jon Savage. Come “Wired,” you can bet your ass that will no longer be the case. As for Golem, he and I certainly do have a bit of history together, spanning many federations. I’m not going to get into specifics, but I certainly admire what the guy has done for this business a whole helluva lot. He takes a lot of flak for his appearance, but the guy goes out there and ends careers almost daily. A good match between he and I is always welcome, and I’m sure this opportunity will not be squandered.
TONY ROSS: That’s right, if I remember… back in the FWF… you and Golem… NEMESIS: (interrupting) Tony, that was a long many years ago, and there’s no point bringing up the irrelevant past right now. Yeah, electrified steel cage matches were supposed to go down, but the FWF folded, and Golem and I never got the opportunity to dance. Bottom line is, NOW we do. NOW we get to see, just like with Minion and I, what WOULD have happened had these two forces been allowed to intermingle? What WAS to come out of a match between a legend like Golem, and a young up-and-comer like myself? Well Tony, I’ve been around a while, and I figure it’s about time I’ve shed my “up-and-coming” moniker. Rather, I say I cement my place in the GLCW, where the stairs only lead upwards. This match is how I plan to do that. After Minion, and after this match, it should be clear who has finally showed promise on fulfilling their potential.
TONY ROSS: With the caliber matches you’ve been having thus far, I don’t doubt it, Nemesis. You’ve been going off and on in the professional wrestling world for over three years, and the timing is definitely ripe for your career to take off.
NEMESIS: That’s exactly my plan. Wins don’t make the man, and neither do titles. They certainly help, but look at greats like Billy Matthews, Shawn Hart, Ricky Kabe, Stone Wolf… they’re all tremendous competitors, and their positions beside the legends of this business are solid. They all lost matches. Even our fearless champion, Maelstrom has lost matches, and cleanly too. Manson, Jobber, Anarky… they’ve all lost. It’s part of the business. It’s the spectacle they put on, how far they’re willing to exert themselves.
TONY ROSS: You mean, um, like laying it all on the line? NEMESIS: Sort of. Not risking life and limb on silly stunts in garbage “hardcore” matches. Not jumping out of rafters, through plate glass, onto your opponent. Unnecessary risks like that are wastes. I’m talking about putting your heart and soul right out there in front of thousands live and millions on television or Internet feed. No bull, no tricks, just blood and sweat. That’s what I try to do, give it all I’ve got, every time, so that it’s worthwhile. If I have a chance of landing hard, or taking a sick bump, and that’s it for me… That’s it, I’m done, out and done forever. I don’t want it to be in a match where the crowd is asleep. I’m not going out in a trail of glory compose of people getting up to use the restroom or grab a hot dog. I’m gonna go out there EVERY NIGHT and put on the BEST SHOW I KNOW HOW to put on… so that no matter when such an event happens, and God help me when it does, but NO MATTER when that might happen… it will be doing something that I LOVE, for people who CARE. I will not take my good fortune for granted. My abilities in the ring, my chance here in the GLCW, my health… none of it. I’m here to show my appreciation, and win or lose, titles or no titles, that’s what I do. And that’s what I’ll continue to do.
TONY ROSS: As is the norm Nemesis, strong words. You’ve certainly been on hot streak in terms of high-caliber matches, and you seem to believe you can keep it going…
NEMESIS: Tony, it’s not a question of belief. It’s just the very simple truth. TONY ROSS: Um, I see. Well, um, good luck in your match, Nemesis. I’m sure it will definitely be one to tune in to see.
NEMESIS: It certainly will, Tony. TONY ROSS: Thanks again for taking a moment here, and we’ll see you in Flint, or at GLCW.com.
NEMESIS: Sure thing, Tony, see ya there. (The man gets up and shakes Tony’s hand as the shot fades out.) FADE OUT.
