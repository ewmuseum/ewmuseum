GLCW — Roleplay

MAIN EVENT: Jean Rabesque vs. The Jobber

25 February 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
TBirdSCIL — 25 February 2003 Please keep all RP for this match in this folder... SteveA — 2 March 2003 (the camera comes in on the GLCW backdrop, with the normal scene all in tact, and in front stands Jean Rabesque, dressed in his all black gear, with his “SHUT UP AND WRESTLE” shirt fully in the forefront, he stands, a slight smile on his face, as he removes his sunglasses and speaks)
Rabesque: It seems as though the GLCW might be beginning to cave slowly. The day that I never dreamed would happen may soon actually be upon us. You see, it never mattered to anyone at the top what I did. I didn’t matter how many asses I kicked, and how many people I made tap. My ranking made no difference, nor did my record. No, I’ve been sitting idly by, watching as loser after loser gets title shot after title shot, while Jean Rabesque sits in the shadows. And then, when the day finally approaches that they think that I’m “worthy,” (chuckles) they decide to put me in some 4-way match in Green Bay. Well, the hell with that, the fans of the GLCW deserve better than that, and now it seems like they’ve finally come to their senses. No, like always, it’s not going to be easy and I’m going to have to do some work to make it happen, but hell, it’s an opportunity.
It seems as though Commissioner Krusher has finally decided to give Jean Rabesque a chance. Just the chance that I’ve always wanted. All I need is one. One chance, one on one, for the GLCW Great Lakes Championship! It’s one match away. That’s all. (chuckles) And to accomplish this, The Jobber awaits. Now, there’s a number of different approaches I could take with this. Now, I know, The Jobber’s sitting at home, probably at The Compound (smiles) waiting for me to fire the first salvo so that he can manipulate and pick apart everything I say. Yeah, I got that, and I know that’s his thing, but I really don’t anticipate he’s going to have much to go on. You see, The Jobber used to an immature man, and in many ways he still is, but in other ways, I have seen a slight maturity begin to develop. It’s in the way he wrestles, even in the way he speaks. Gone is the nonsensical ways that totally used to remind me of Mike Manson, and that has been replaced by a style that at times almost makes him seem intelligent. And for Jean Rabesque to say that about The Jobber, well, I think you all can read into how much of a statement that really is.
The Jobber’s tough, there really is no use denying it. The guy has fought some seriously tough battles, and he’s even won a few. Nobody holds the TV title for as long as he did without doing at least SOMETHING right. Now, as we stated earlier, there wasn’t exactly any title shot prepared for Jean Rabesque, but nonetheless he accomplished something. He’s definitely above average in his mat skill, and his ring smarts aren’t half bad. He really is good enough to beat most people. (Rabesque chuckles) Of course, most people doesn’t include Jean Rabesque. I still to this day remain the best damn thing going, and it’s going to take a hell of a lot more than above average talent to beat me, especially with what’s at stake.
Now a lot of people have been running their mouth continually trying to disprove the fact that I am the best thing going. Lots of people, friend and foe alike seem to have a problem with this statement. I tell them the same thing continuously. Step up and prove me otherwise, and you know what? NOT A DAMN ONE HAS. Now I’m sure Jobber is going to be the next to step up and make the claim. Fine, I dare you, hell, I CHALLENGE you to do what everyone has tried, and everyone has failed trying to do. Sure, I know you’ll take the game, they all do. And I’m sure you’ll be the same way, outside the ring.....and in.
The other thing that I forecast is Jobber giving me the rundown on how he dominated the GLCW, how he’s held a belt here and I haven’t, and about all his title defenses. Sure, it’ll sound impressive, until you do a little analysis. You know, winning midcard matches against Steven James while I’m headlining cards really isn’t too much to be proud of when you really look at things. Sure, he had Golem’s number, but other than that, what did Jobber really accomplish. Besides getting PUNKED by Jarod Poe of course. But, hey, that’s just me.
So, come on Jobber, you know this is the introduction. I hope I’ve given you a good deal of ammunition. Now come out firing. This is serious, and my crosshairs are locked.... on you. Sure, I know you’ll say you aren’t worried, and you have everything under control, but you might want to reanalyze your thoughts. Until next time, Jobber, enjoy. (smiles) No false gimmicks, no false hype, I am Jean Rabesque.
(fade out) ChrisHorowitz — 8 March 2003 (Cue up "The Bend Before the Break" by the River City Rebels) (Fade into the Jobber's Outer Whitelandia compound. Cut to the living room where the Jobber sits upon the couch, now without his Television title.)
The Jobber: I came to a realization that from time to time your achievements hold you back. For all of your success, for all of your conquests, you're stuck in one location you just can't get out of, because your success has just become a burden. In this case, letting yourself fall before your opponent is the only way to advance, and through failure you move on faster than you would have with another victory.
(The Jobber stands up, and moves towards the window.) The Jobber: I wasn't going anywhere with that Television titles, it was simply going to throw me into a never ending cycle of matches with the lower tier. That belt was a choke chain, and now I'm rid of it, and the champion of the bottom of the barrel is now someone fitting of that position in the barrel.
Now I must turn my attention to Jean Rabesque. Rabesque claims I just ignored him up until this point because I wanted to dissect his every word, and such. Frankly Rabesque, I'm ignoring everything you've said as it will likely be no different than those things you said two years ago, and the things you were saying to others 2 years before that.
Ironic he claims that I planned to trump up my accomplishments when at the same time he tries to pass off bit parts in title matches and main events as some evidence that he's a superstar. One thing Jean Rabesque has always wanted to prove is that he's a superstar, because he's never been one, always on the overhyped verge of becoming one only to lose a title he'd just later claim never left his possession. I'm surprised anyone even bothers to disprove that you are some sort of legend or amazing talent because you've never done anything in your career to even earn the honor of having a person rant about how those accomplishments were worthless.
The more you complain how your opponents build themselves up on false claims and immodesty, you see the same things in you Rabesque, except where you have to mock the accomplishments of a man in GLCW, you are correct your opponents can't mock your accomplishments in GLCW, and being the smart guy you are I'm sure you can add on your own punchline about how you've accomplished nothing. It's always the same tired spiel, you're the soon to be crowned king, and anyone who points out the flaws in your statements is obviously wrong, because your catch phrase talks about no false hype and such.
People, if it's in a catch phrase it's got to be true. And yes, I was "punked" by Jared Poe. Because dropping a lower tier championship to a man who you've proven on two occasions to be the better of is clearly evidence you are inferior to him. I won't go and play up one specific loss as evidence of your career being a failure, I'll just go by the total lack of respect you receive from everyone in this sport. I'd say that the perception of you as a whiney dry dinosaur who keeps clinging to glory he never had is a bit more damning than losing one bout to Jared Poe.
Because Rabesque, if I still had that Television title I wouldn't be in position to leap frog you and your place as a bit player in the world title scene, because no one in their right mind would give you a meaningful shot at a title, secondary or not. So cut your spiels about false hype, and the victimiziation complex you have, because it just adds onto your legend as wrestling's most worthless dull brat.
So Rabesque those games you played two years ago to leave yourself with an opponent in the ring other than me to recapture the belts you felt were yours and only yours will come back around in the ring. I haven't forgotten about having to see another man fall before you and cost me the belt I had pinned him for, just because you couldn't beat the truely deserving for that belt. Bitter? Of course, that's the only mindset in which revenge truely works.
(Fade out) SteveA — 10 March 2003 (the camera comes in on Tony Ross inside of the GLCW studios, he is dressed in a suit, as a GLCW backdrop is behind him as he sits)
TONY ROSS: “Welcome back ladies and gentlemen, and again today we are welcomed by a man that is by all means becoming a regular on this program. He has an absolutely HUGE match coming up in the main event at Riptide. He will be facing former GLCW Television Champion The Jobber to determine the Number One Contender for Maelstrom’s GLCW Great Lakes Champion Maelstrom! I’m of course speaking of Jean Rabesque, who joins us in studio today. Jean, welcome to the show, how are you?”
(the camera pans away to see Rabesque sitting next to Ross, dressed in his traditional all black gear, his “NO FALSE GIMMICKS” shirt the shirt of choice on this day)
JEAN RABESQUE: “Thanks Tony, always a pleasure to be here.” ROSS: “Ok, so let’s jump right in on this. Coming up at Riptide, everyone knows you have an encounter with The Jobber. What do you foresee being a challenge stepping into the ring with him?”
RABESQUE: “Well, Tony, The Jobber does have a wrestling ability few people can match around here. He’s also rather ring savvy and intelligent when he gets inside the ropes. In the GLCW, I’ve never had too much trouble manipulating my opponents. I mean, when you have guys like Kraven or Michael Manson, it really isn’t too hard to stay intellectually superior. But The Jobber provides a challenge in the fact that I know a lot of my tricks probably won’t work as well with him.”
ROSS: “The Jobber has gone on record in saying that he was BURDENED by the GLCW Television Championship. What are your thoughts about that statement, and do you feel it is the truth?”
RABESQUE (chuckles): “You know, the only people who make comments like that are ones that lose matches. If you were so far superior to that belt Jobber, then you wouldn’t have had any problems holding on to. But no, Jarod Poe beat you straight up, right in the middle of the ring. Cleanly, no interference, no nothing. You simply got beat Jobber. There was no burden, no burden at all.”
ROSS: “But Jobber now claims he can leapfrog you in the path for the Great Lakes Title now that this burden has been lifted from his shoulders.”
RABESQUE: “If my memory serves me correctly, The Jobber had a one on one match with Maelstrom at one of those non-televised shows. All he had to do was beat Maelstrom and he had a Great Lakes Title shot. It was irrelevant at the time that he was the TV champ. This is just another lame excuse The Jobber is using to try to mask this entire ordeal. The fact was that in that match he was being given yet another opportunity. And what happened, what happened Tony?”
ROSS (hesitating): “........Um.. Maelstrom won the match.” RABESQUE: “Yeah, that’s right. Not only did Jobber get beat, but Jobber QUIT! He submitted like the little punk he is. If he crumbled that easily to the Mortal Sin, what is he going to do when the Figure 4 is applied? Let’s go back to what I used to say to Sean Edmunds, before our I QUIT match at Massive Assault. Once a quitter..... always a quitter. I said the same thing to him, and he continued to affirm the fact that he had changed, that things were different. And at Massive Assault, I proved my point when I made him quit. Jobber, I know you’ll want to come out and tell me that everything with you is different, but you’ll tap. You’ll tap just like the rest.
“The fact is Tony, that The Jobber has now lost, not once, but TWICE in a row, cleanly....right in the middle of the ring. It seems to me that he’s losing his touch a bit. And now he thinks he’s just going to magically figure all of this out. Does he honestly think he’s going to end his losing streak against the world’s greatest wrestler?? (laughs) He’s got to be out of his mind once again!”
ROSS: “While we’re on the subject of the claim you make that you’re the best wrestler in the world, The Jobber refutes nearly every single one of the claims you make.”
RABESQUE: (chuckles) Yeah, he does. It doesn’t have to be true if it’s the catchphrase, but I still invite people to come in and show me what I say is wrong. Nobody can. Hell, I honestly believe it was almost the last century the last time someone actually was able to beat me cleanly in the middle of the ring. And what is it now? 2003? If you want to dispute it, fine Jobber, dispute in the ring, where you’ve had your ass beaten TWICE... in the last TWO shows you’ve been on. Sure, you were a champion, and those title defenses against Steven James sure captivated my attention, but I’ve simply been doing what I do best, and that is outwrestling everyone. You wonder why no one had bothered to prove me a fraud, yet you have to do it yourself. Is that saying anything to you Jobber? Is absolutely anything getting through there? All of tried, and all have failed. Every opponent I have says the same thing. They don’t believe that I really am any good, and then they get in the ring, and they find out the truth.
“While you mention the past, all I really have to say is that I’ve always done what I had to do. You can bitter about the past you all want, I was doing...... what I had to do. And it still does crack me up about how much MY old title used to mean to you Jobber. How used to carry it around from league to league, holding it was some kind of trophy. It was almost as if you were showing some kind of great pride from being in possession of a belt that I made famous.
“Face it Jobber, no matter what you do, no matter what you accomplish, you will ALWAYS be in my shadow. Just like you were back in the FWF, and just like you are now in the GLCW. I know it’s a pain, and I know it’s hard to accept. I just simply DON’T CARE!”
ROSS: “Do you have anything you would like to add before we sign off?” RABESQUE: “Sure, fans in Milwaukee, if you don’t have your tickets yet, run out as fast as you can to get them. Because very soon, right in the heart of your beautiful city, you will see a clinic you will never forget. You will the greatest wrestler on Earth, Jean Rabesque, Mr. NO FALSE GIMMICKS himself, show the world what wrestling is supposed to be. It’s not about the glitz, or the glamour, or where you live, or what you say. It’s simply about getting done in the middle of the ring. I’m on a mission, and this is the chance I’ve been waiting for. I will do EVERYTHING in my power to ensure that it does not slip away.”
(camera stays on Rabesque for a moment, and then slowly pans to just Ross) ROSS: “Well, thank you Jean Rabesque for joining us tonight. Until the next time, I’m Tony Ross, on behalf of all the GLCW, good night!”
(zoom out, fade out)
