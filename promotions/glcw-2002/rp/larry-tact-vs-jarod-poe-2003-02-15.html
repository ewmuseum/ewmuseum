Tact — 18 February 2003 [updated:LAST EDITED ON Feb-18-03 AT 05:22&nbsp;PM (EST)]Fade in: Light shines from behind the clouds, struggling to pierce the gray, but to no avail. As the sun is blocked, small, pure white snowflakes take their chance and escape from the clouds, descending through the sky. They softly fall, some landing on not so pure ground, with all the footprints in the dirty snow. Other frozen droplets fall onto the large snowbanks that blanket the ground -- and have been added to by snowplows -- in a soft, equally untainted white. As the camera takes this in, the unmistakable crunch of footfalls on snow can be heard. The camera pans over to a snow-covered sidewalk, and we spot Larry Tact walking out of a restuarant. His attire this evening consists of: a green sweater, the collar of a white shirt showing under it, with stonewashed blue jeans, a black ski hat, gloves and boots. He puts on a long leather coat as he walks, and it sways smoothly as Tact walks down a few steps in front of the small terrace in front of the entrance to the restaurant. He stops at the bottom of the steps, the cameraman approaching.
Tact: The dining room's still being set up for the meeting, so I have some time. Got that thing fixed yet?
CM: Yes sir, Mr. Tact. Tact: Good. A pause of silence ensues, as Tact just stands there. Then... Tact: Well..? Whenever you're ready....didn't you say you had that thing fixed? CM: Oh, yes sir. I've been filming for the last few minutes...I'm just waiting for you. Tact's eyes narrow a bit as he glares, but only briefly, as he then assumes a more formal appearance, giving his coat a tug on the collar.
Tact: It seems that wrestling has taken more of a dive than I'd previously thought, over the past few years. At first, when I got my 'break,' I thought there were plenty of solid ring talent around. The ones who could talk the talk, but not walk the walk..? They were not enough to cloud and disenchant the way wrestling should be orchestrated. I thought of wrestling much like I see this snow...
He reaches over to a pile of snow, and grabs a small handful, clasping it with both hands and shaping it.
Tact: ...pure, the core unharmed by the vile factor that is present in just about everything these days. It was still the art that it had been for years. You could shape your wrestling methods into many different ways...focusing on submissions, or suplexes...using short series of quick, consecutive maneuvers...or keeping things slower, and concentrating more on making each move as potent as possible. All crisp, all strategic, one move following another in an almost sequential order, each one setting up the next.
Tact now holds a well-rounded snowball in his gloved right hand, and holds it up to his eye level, looking at it thoughtfully.
Tact: Those desiring the pinnacle would work tirelessly, day after day, night after night, trying to mold themselves into the perfect embodiment of their technical skill. They wanted to be the 'best' wrestler. Admittedly it was, and still is, a fantastical goal. It's nothing more than fiction, to call yourself the "best wrestler in the world." However, that didn't stop people from aiming for that goal. Set your goals, your standards, high, right? That's what we did, and even if we were never expected to reach that goal, so what? If you set your standards high, and someone else sets their goal at something they achieve in a couple months, or even a year's, time, then that just means they'll stop working as hard. They might try to set another menial, small goal, but why bother doing that? So you can get a warm, fuzzy feeling each time you complete one of those smaller goals? To be dependant on such things is a weakness. But something happened from then to now. Do you know what it is that happened....well, do you?
CM: Um....people started complaining...? Tact: Complaining...complaining about what? People are always complaining. CM: I don't know! Complaining about wrestling, like this! Tact throws the snowball at the camera, and it hits the lens causing patches of white to block out parts of the picture.
Tact: You moron, of course not! This isn't complaining, it's informing. I'm merely showing the obvious trend of decline that has befallen wrestling over the past few years. It's all due to people getting lazy. People didn't want to bother putting in the effort for this job, when effort is a requirement! Clean off that snow, already, come on. Do I have to take care of the technical work outside the ring, too? Come on....
Tact pauses as the cameraman wipes the lens clean of the snow, except for some water that slowly slides down the lens every so often.
Tact: So things start to change. People trade-in technical skill for beating each other up with kicks and punches...the whole freakin' match!! People start using weapons, even moreso than they had been previously. Promotions want to be more 'entertaining,' and so they start hiring human equivalents to mexican jumping beans, having them fly off the top turnbuckles and throwing themselves out of the ring. All the while, technical wrestling is degraded, dubbed 'unentertaining,' slandered. Now, I have no problem with going to the top rope and pulling off a high-risk maneuver, where it's appropriate. But I'm talking every other move, at least, becomes some guy doing some 'death-defying' move. Is that supposed to be acceptable? Is that appropriate? I don't think so. But lots of owners and talent relations boobs thought so.
Tact takes up another handful of snow. Tact: So eventually wrestling turned from this... Tact drops the snow on the sidewalk, proceeds to stomp on it a few times, then bends down and picks up some of the slushy, brownish-gray product.
Tact: ...to this. (tosses down slush and tries to wipe it off his glove) Damnit all, it stuck, too...the crap stuck around. And it wasn't just the in-ring product, alone, that suffered. 'The asylum known as wrestling,' that's what it had become. The people who got brought in by promotions were just hideous, a disgrace. Very few had any talent. For the most part, the 'new generation' were unskilled, or minimally so, with a mouth that could ring off ten one-liners in thirty seconds. That seems to be how it is, to this very day, for the most part. Wrestling has been slowly corrupted. I saw the symptoms all too clearly, coming forth back then. I tried to promote the product that held this sport together for so long, doing my best to wrestle great matches. Heck, I even stopped doing top rope moves for a time, back in the former IWF. I won't start in on that place, though. My efforts, though, valiant as they were, could not possibly contend with the hordes of trash being pumped into the system. Vestiges of wrestling, as they had become, grew thin and far between. Places like the World Wrestling League, which had managed to avoid it for a while, eventually succumbed as well. And even I have my breaking point. That was it. So I left.
He shifts position, sitting down on the relatively snow-cleared steps of the diner, and rests his arms over his knees.
Tact: When the GLCW opened, I thought, "With that motto and style? Malec's living in the mid-late 90s. He's gonna get a reality check in a few months time." I was willing to give it some sort of chance. It'd stick around for a few months, then be infected, corrupted, and dumped into the gutter with the rest. Or be slaughtered and thrown into the mass that is the failed promotion graveyard. But, somehow -- miraculousy? -- it's become what we see today. It hasn't succumbed to 'sports-entertainment' via 'entertainment,' but instead via 'sports.' (momentarily feigns shock, throwing up his arms) It's a wrestling promotion that's about...wrestling! So I came back. At Riptide, I will start trying to purify the wrestling waters a little more, when I give Jared Justice just what he deserves: to be shown how pathetic his type is. Rejects are sad cases, but they should be pointed in the right direction, so perhaps they can reform and help out the cause of restoring wrestling. So I'm going to first show Jared how wrestling is supposed to be done in the ring. Then, I'll point him in the direction of the nearest mental facility. Then the choice is his. He can go exchange fire tricks with Manson, he can be useful and start wrestling like he probably knows how to...or he can just go away. But after Riptide, it's out of my hands. I can only do so much. However, there is also the upcoming house show in Wisconsin. The home of, quite possibly, the lamest symbol of fan appreciation in sports. The Cheese Head. There's no wonder why the New York Jets wiped the floor with the Packers. I mean, Favre has to look up into the stands and see ten thousand slices of cheese watching him! The foam and the fans! They're all cheesy doofs! So for Favre and the Pack to be looking at that half the season, it's gotta be demoralizing. Hopefully I won't have to see any such horrible things while I'm there. Because I'll have a hard enough time not vomitting at the sight of Jarod Poe's wrestling. He is another who is pro-garbage wrestling. He likes to stab people in the eye. He likes to do sick things, and I don't mean cool. I know the line on you, Poe. For Michael Manson to say you're the only one who'd want to be like him, it confirms two things for me. First, you are indeed a bit lacking in brain matter. You'll probably take it as a compliment, which is fine by me, so congratulations on that. Second off, you're pretty pathetic. Anyone who wishes to walk in, or anywhere near, the footsteps of Manson is a sad case. Sure he's accomplished a lot, but so have a lot of people. A lot of wrestlers, no less. But instead, you look to Manson. A man who was lucky; highest paid man in the company, so Malec had Golem go against him a lot early on. When you have a real wrestler like the Clawed One to contend with, it doesn't matter if you get beaten and humiliated by him for most of the match. He'll make a match look good if you don't have the skills, just as Golem did with Manson how many times? Several. Manson should consider it a gift from Malec, that he was able to get established through Golem. He's lucky that luck fell out on Golem at just the wrong time, with the pseudo-fall deal. Believe me, I'd love to say otherwise, but the fact is it was simply Golem in the wrong place at the wrong time.
Tact raises his gloved hands up to his mouth, and white breath can be seen being exhaled and inhaled and exhaled, as Tact warms himself up some. He then hangs his hands over his knees again.
Tact: Jarod Poe cannot look to successful pure wrestlers as models for him to surpass, though, for a pretty simple reason. He isn't quite the type. You like to use whatever you can to win. You don't have any sort of direction in your style, and probably not concerning goals, either. That works fine against a lot of people, in a lot of places, today. But there is still a small haven for the purists. There is still some white in the mulch of wrestling's corruption. I have the drive to continue striving to be better. I might not set my goal as, "to be the best wrestler in the world" anymore, but that is because I don't believe in that fiction as the pinnacle anymore. Instead, I make my pinnacle to go out and breath life into wrestling, by performing that fine art how it should be. Wrestling properly, and bringing justice to it, is much less selfish than striving, or proclaiming, to be, "the best wrestler in the world." It helps out the sport much more, and that's what I'm after. Because what will it mean to be the 'best wrestler,' if there's nobody around who wants to wrestle? It means nothing, then. I'm no savior, and I'm not vying for any particular prize like so many others. Perhaps Jarod and I have that in common, or so it would seem on the outside. But internally, the difference is clear. I am more focused than most, if not all, of the GLCW right now. Jarod Poe is a strangler, a man looking for a match, but not with any end purpose in mind. No direction. Another who can benefit from the education of wrestling like a true wrestler. That will give you the direction you need, and I'll demonstrate as much to you when we're in that ring, in the land of milk curd. Now if you'll excuse me, I've got a table reserved.
