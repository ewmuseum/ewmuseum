GLCW — Roleplay

"Gentleman" Jonathan Marx vs. Ryan Roberts

11 March 2003
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.

PaulNJ21 — 16 March 2003 (Jonathan Marx is standing in his tuxedo, posing for publicity photos to send to his fans, he notices the interview crew have fully setup his area to deliver his promo and he tells the photographer to take a break, he walks in front of the microphone and begins to speak)
Jonathan Marx: Good evening old school wrestling fans, for those who don't know me, I am "Gentlemen" Jonathan Marx, better known to you as your savior.
To know how little respect Ryan Roberts has for the sport of professional wrestling, all you have to do is look at him.
First off, he is so embarrassed by his physique, he wrestles in a t-shirt that any lower class bleacher bum could buy in any five in dime. I’m not afraid to take off my shirt. I’m in the gym three or four days a week to keep my natural physique and when I’m on the road, I make sure to stay at hotels with an adequate equipment in order to make sure that I’m in the best shape possible to go in a GLCW ring, while being able to provide a little eye candy for the beautiful women in the audience.
But if it was only his t-shirt, I may have been able to let that pass with the excuse that he just isn’t a very good dresser. No, it gets even worse. Ryan Roberts wrestlers in cut off jean shorts. He doesn’t wear regulation trunks that old school wrestlers use to wear, or even tights, which became more popular in the second half of the century. No, he wants to come into the ring and look like a pig, who could ever take a wrestler seriously who wears cut off jeans? That is the quickest way to find yourself in the opening matches... wait, you are already here, nevermind.
Now if we gaze down to Ryan Roberts feet, do we see regular wrestling boots? Sadly, no we don’t. We just see more disrespect for this sport. Ryan Roberts wears hiking boots into the ring. Apparently wrestling is too inconvenient for Ryan and he can’t even change shoes for his match.
Look at the way I carry myself, even when I am not at the arena, I’m always nicely dressed in a polo shirt and some Dockers khaki pants. You never know when a fan will approach you, our customers, for an autograph. What would he think if he saw you dressed as a common slob off the street? Why should fans go to pay to see someone who looks like everyone else that they see? But even if you only dressed down outside of the arena, I may have been able to forgive you. But you choose to disgrace this sport even more by wearing those clothes to the ring and wrestling in them. That I can not allow. I’m going to rip that t-shirt off your back and shove it down your throat. I’m going to teach you some respect for this bloody sport boy and I’m not going to show any mercy.
This league is in a lot of trouble because of people like Ryan Roberts, but I vow to save it even if I have to beat each and every wrestler in this company to do it. The old school revolution is coming for you.
::Marx walks off:: FTB LMccarver — 18 March 2003 FADE INA dark room. The Twisted Prodigy lays on a floor, looking up at the ceiling. In the distance you can hear a faucet dripping. Ryan never looks up at the camera and begins to speak.
ROBERTS: It seems that one Johnny Marx's has a issue with the way that I dress? Clothes do not make the man. I don't need to walk around and flash fancy clothes or fast cars. I'm quite happy with the simple things in life. Standing around in a monkey suit isn't my style. By looking at Marx, it's not really his strong suit either.
Ryan sits up and his long hair hangs in his eyes and the white tank top he wears has a couple of small holes in it.
ROBERTS: Do I look concerned with the fans? No. You see if they want to come and pay to get into the arena that's their choice, but I'm not going to conform to some set of standards for them. Fans are so fickle, they'll turn on you at the drop at the hat.
Ryan pushes his hair out of his face and glares into the camera ROBERTS: Johnny, If my clothing is your focus then I'd suggest you don't even show up for the match. Better yet, come to the match and bring a little notebook. I'll give you an up close and personal look at my hiking boots, my t-shirt and anything else you're bitching about. I'll make sure the fans realize that while I don't care for them that I'm the better man and that you are just full of hot air. I'll be waiting, Johnny.
Ryan stands up and walks away [b]FADE TO BLACK[/i] PaulNJ21 — 18 March 2003 [updated:LAST EDITED ON Mar-18-03 AT 07:16&nbsp;PM (EST)]::Jonathan Marx is browsing at Banana Republic for shirts, the sales girl is waiting on another customer and Marx begins talking to the camera::
Jonathan Marx: You say many things, which are correct Mr. Roberts, but sadly you come to the wrong conclusions.
When you say that clothes do not make a man, you are absolutely correct Mr. Roberts. Any slob can put on a good suit and pretend to be something special. But you are worse than any false idol because you refuse to even pretend, and by doing so, disrespect both the fans and your fellow workers, so tell me, how I can respect you as a wrestler? The clothes you wear may not reflect who you are, but they certainly reflect whom you are or whom you aspire to be inside. When you are dressed like a pig, how can I not treat you like anything else but a pig?
You are correct that fans can turn on you in the drop of hat. They can love you one day and boo you the next. But you must remember one thing, all of the fans out there pay our salaries. We have a responsibility as role models to all the little children out there to be the best that we can be, inside and outside of the ring. Some little boy out there will think that is proper to wear their hair like a little girl and dress in ratty clothes, is that the message that you want to send to all the young children out there who watch the GLCW? Don't you care about the children? By the way you dress, it looks like you barely care about yourself.
I won't bring a notebook, but I will bring a list of ways that you can improve your image so you can be worthy to compete in a GLCW with myself, and other fellow wrestlers. Once the match starts, I'll then give you a lesson on how to wrestle properly instead of doing silly stunts like the split legged moonsault and swanton bomb just to get the crowd to cheer. For someone who doesn't care about pleasing the fans, you certainly are quite the trained monkey.
Sales Girl: Sir, can I help you? Jonathan Marx: Mr. Roberts, I have to cut this short because this beautiful lady is finally free, but until we meet, goodbye and god bless old school wrestling.
FTB LMccarver — 20 March 2003 FADE IN: A local mall. The Twisted Prodigy enters the local Sears. Ryan is dressed in a long black trench coat, cutoff jean shorts, a black GLCW T-shirt and hiking boots. Ryan notices the camera and moves toward it.
: What do I want the kids to think, Marx? I really don't give a f<censored> what the kids think. You know what they should already know? They should know that we live in a world where they are free to express themselves anyway that they choose. If they don't want to go the prep route, then they don't have to. All you've done is sit around here and push your thoughts on the rest of the populous. Talk about hypocritical? Hello Kettle, Let me introduce you to the pot.
Ryan begins to move through the store and some people stop and stare, but it doesn't faze our Anti-Hero at all. Ryan continues with his words.
[b]Roberts:You talk about what I do wrong, yet I have to sit here and listen to your discriminating mouth. You think cause I dress differently than you that I'm somehow inferior? Maybe we should kill everyone with different beliefs? Or better yet, because someone's a different race, maybe we would shun them from society? Yet, I'm the ignorant one? Johnny, I don't care what you think of me. I don't care what anyone in the god damn store thinks of me. I am my own person and if I had to dress in the clothes that you wear. Well, (chuckles) I'd have to kick my own ass.
Ryan stops in the hardware section and begins to look at the cordless drills Roberts Enough about my attire, Johnny, Let's get to the match. You claim that you're going to give me a wrestling lesson? (smiles) Well then maybe I'll give you a lesson in pain. The snapping of anothers ankle always was a beautiful sound to my ear. Yeah, Johnny, You keep worrying about what I'm wearing and about how I carry myself outside of the ring. When it's said and done, You're still a bigot and I'm about to prove to you that I can not only stand toe to toe with you, but that I'm the better man. Good luck, Chump. You'll need it.
Ryan places the drill inside of his trench coat and walks off FTB
