Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Great Lakes Championship Wrestling opened on the fwrestling.com hub in June 2002. Its president posted the application for wrestlers on 11 June, a mission statement on 16 July and the rules on 18 July: old-school booking, a promotion based in the Great Lakes area, long and bitter feuds, tag team wrestling stressed, roleplay judged by quality, and short house cards between the television shows. Its first show, First Wave, was posted on 4 August 2002 from Cobo Hall in Detroit as the first round of a heavyweight title tournament.

Each card was announced in a lineup post and fought out in a folder for each match, where the members posted their wrestlers' promos; the president then wrote the broadcast, with Tony Ross and Rick Wiseman at the table, Matt Faley in the ring, Lady Veronica backstage and King Krusher as commissioner. Riptide in Madison on 26 August and Racine Riptide on 21 September carried the tournament to its semi-finals. Chi-Town Showdown I in Chicago on 10 October crowned The Jobber as the first television champion, Kraven and Flatliner of the Unholy Alliance as the first tag team champions and Michael Manson as the first Great Lakes Heavyweight Champion, after a final that its commissioner and its special referee counted differently. House shows followed in Gary on 18 October and Fort Wayne on 14 November, reported in a short recap form, and South Bend Riptide on 4 November.

Its board's threads from June 2002 to October 2003 carry the matches' roleplays, the broadcasts and their results, the applications, a roster and the staff posts. The Rockford Riptide broadcast of 30 November 2002 is told match by match, as are the Toledo house show of 10 December, Kalamazoo Riptide on 30 December, the Saginaw house show of 11 January 2003 and Massive Assault 2002 in Grand Rapids on 22 January, where Maelstrom took the heavyweight title from Michael Manson in a covered steel cage and Anarky first appeared. The Hammond house show of 13 February and Cleveland Riptide on 21 February follow, with Nemesis, Larry Tact, Jared Justice, Minion and Suicide among the newcomers; the Green Bay and Milwaukee cards are announced there. The Green Bay house show of 1 March 2003, Milwaukee Riptide on 28 March, Flint Wired on 5 April, Indianapolis Riptide on 28 April, the Duluth show of 18 May and Ringlords I on 9 June, the fed's first pay-per-view, are told match by match; the Dogs of War, Major Impact, the Krymson Dragons, Dakota Smith, M.W. Grossard, Jonathan Marx and Lincoln Jones are among the newcomers, and at Ringlords I Maelstrom, Jarod Poe, Jean Rabesque and Jared Justice beat the Unholy Alliance, Michael Manson and Anarky in WarGames. Columbus Riptide on 9 July 2003, Akron Wired on 19 July, Wisconsin Dells Riptide on 15 August, Joliet Wired on 27 August and half of Champaign Riptide, posted on 15 October after the fed had closed, are told match by match: Maelstrom kept the heavyweight title against Jean Rabesque, the Cannonball Kidd and Jonathan Marx, Stephen Morgan took the Television Title from Jarod Poe, the Unholy Alliance retired and handed the Tag Team Titles to King Krusher, and The Movement won them. On the night of 13 October 2003 the president announced that the fed was closing 'effective tonight' because he no longer had the time to run it; the members' farewells, and the characters' own goodbyes in roleplay, follow on the board.
