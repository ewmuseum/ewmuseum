Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Professional Wrestling League's own Handbook v1.0, posted 6 August 2001, describes a promotion already established rather than a new one — “a great-efed that has been around for a few months now and is rapidly gaining popularity” — booked and hand-written rather than run by simulator. It had just cleared roughly fifteen members who “did not take the PWL serious” and warned that the resulting openings would close to invited talent only once filled.

Its rules were plain and strict: roleplay at least once a week, no using another wrestler without permission, no complaining about a loss on pain of suspension, every deadline absolute, and swearing kept to a censored minimum. A Wednesday newsletter carried the roster, rankings, title contenders, champions, news, and rumors. Its cards ran Monday Mayhem as the flagship show and Thursday Terror for younger talent, the latter “temporarily shut down” at press time, with monthly pay-per-views announced after each one aired. Four championships ran the promotion: a World Title, a United States Title, a Hardcore Title for wrestlers who won it in hardcore matches, and a Tag Team Title.

The handbook also set out its own staged cast: a front office led by its president, billed as Rob Shyne, alongside its commissioner, David Mundof, backed by named referees, a ring announcer, interviewers, and commentators who worked its shows.

A separate document, dated 29 August 2001, stages a change at the top of the company as its own cold open: an incoming owner, J. Rose, tells the retired tag team of Eliminator and Kamikaze that they are “officially retired” as wrestlers and are her enforcers now, installs a wrestler called Mysterioso as the league's new commissioner with final say over matches, and sends everyone out to recruit, declaring “we've been in enough little piss-ant leagues. Run poorly. Always going on 'hiatus'.”

Two days after that, a recruitment post from the PWL's own president pointed readers to a new address, pwl.00go.com, and a newsletter dated 1 September 2001 reported about eight applications in the two days since, naming seven new signings — Tyronn “Dirty” Washington, Johnny Newman, The Sleepwalker, Mr. Hardkore, Fredd “Killa” Sapp, Justin Kashmere, and Yamchaken Phoenix — and promising a first Wednesday Warzone card the following week.

That newsletter is the last document the Professional Wrestling League's own record carries. Whether the promised Warzone card ever ran, how its handbook's own front office squared with the new ownership staged three weeks later, and how or when the league ended aren't in the surviving record.
