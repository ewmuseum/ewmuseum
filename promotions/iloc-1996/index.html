Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The Internet League of Champions announced itself on 27 May 1996: “I am starting a new league. It is going to be called the ILOC (Internet League of Champions).” It ran on a twice-weekly schedule, a Wednesday Turbo card and a Saturday Night Slamboree, plus a monthly pay-per-view, presided over on screen by the league's own commissioner and its president.

Its first pay-per-view, a ten-man battle royal broadcast from the New Corestates Center in Philadelphia, crowned its first-ever champion: Satar outlasted a field that included Dragon Lord, Mr. America, Robski, The Crow, Big Russ, Kamikaze Kid, The Hitman and the Refrigerator Rader to take the belt. A second title, the Internet League Championship, was decided three weeks later at the Saturday Night Slamboree in Boston, where King Tonka beat the Rock Drake Erich for it in a card promoted as clean after outside interference had marred a match the week before, when Outcast Rob Cole beat Satar with help at ringside.

By the league's own June Heat Stroke pay-per-view, from the Thunderdome in Miami, Outcast Rob Cole had taken the World Championship from Satar outright, and the card built an entire angle around Mr. America's return from a losing streak, his own promo tying it to a hospital visit to a sick child and a promise to climb back to a title shot “title or no-title.” The night's two title matches were both fought inside a steel cage: King Tonka defended the Internet League title against Dragon Lord and kept it, and Satar took the World Championship back from Outcast Rob Cole to close the show.

The last surviving card, a Saturday Night Slamboree broadcast again from Philadelphia on 6 July 1996, closed with both titles changing hands on the same night: Drake Erich beat King Tonka for the Internet League Championship, and Robski beat Satar for the World title with the help of a pair of brass knuckles, over the commentators' own protests that he “can't win fair.”

Nothing in the surviving record picks the story up from there. No further card, closure notice, or account of either title's next defense survives, and how or when the Internet League of Champions closed is not known from its own record.
