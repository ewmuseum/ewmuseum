Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

Wrestling Midwest billed itself the best in the Great Lakes area and named its top belt after them. It ran a Friday Night Riot most weeks from the end of February 2003, a pay-per-view most months, a phpBB board, a newsdesk that took reader comments, and two AIM chat rooms it called Ring #1 and Ring #2 — always open, sponsored only at the hours it posted.

What it kept most carefully was its own paperwork. The rankings table carries a win, a loss and a draw column beside every name, and a division letter beside that. The champions page tells six belts’ stories a paragraph apiece: who held each first, who held it longest, how many defences, and the exact day the reign running at the time began. One of those paragraphs renames a belt in the middle of itself — the first decision of a new Commissioner was to strip a man of the Hardcore Title and hand him the Cruiserweight Championship, all on a page headed Ace Superior Championship — and its own predecessor belt is carried here as its own title, chained forward by that rename.

Three pay-per-views survive whole: Ides of March, High Stakes and Blood Factory, sixty wrestlers and nine units between them once the roster page, the champions ledger and the news and column archive are read alongside the shows themselves. None of the eleven Friday Night Riots the surviving record otherwise dates does — confirmed against the fed's own result archive and newsdesk, not silently dropped. How it ended isn’t in the surviving record; the site stops in July 2003. But the fed did not: the RSPW-F Top 200 was still tagging ranked wrestlers to WMW a year and a half later, and two of those names sit on this promotion’s own rankings of 13 June 2003.

Storylines

The arcs below are recovered from a full read of all eighteen held documents, including all three surviving pay-per-view scripts.

Katy Hummer, The Court, and the fall of camp Johnson Written by AI, 2026

Katy Hummer opens Ides of March billed as President Brad Johnson's own Commissioner, standing in his office circle with Sub-Beast, Garvin and Matrix. In the main event she orders three unnamed security guards to block Sub-Beast's own corner while Dess steals the Great Lakes Championship, then reveals the guards, and herself, as a new stable — The Court. A staff column later fills in why: Johnson and Hummer fought a prior war for control of a defunct fed called Maverick, where Hummer first fielded a group called The Court against Johnson's own faction, and the two only rebuilt a working friendship before she signed a one-year WMW contract with, it turns out, the same agenda (ides-of-march-2003-03-28; columns). By High Stakes, Jordan Hammer and Adam King are wrestling under the Court's own banner and commentary describes a "strong bond" pulling Dess, Archangel and a newly arrived Volcano into Hummer's own orbit (high-stakes-2003-04-25). None of it survives Blood Factory: Volcano turns on Dess mid-match, revealed as a member of a rival stable, The Black Riders, underneath his own Court shirt (blood-factory-2003-05-23). By the fed's own 13 June champions page, Hummer is gone from the Commissioner's chair entirely — Donovan Merlot holds the office by 16 May — and no document in the held corpus records how or why the change happened (champions-2003-06-13).

The Great Lakes Championship's own war: Dess, Sub-Beast, and Matrix's betrayal Written by AI, 2026

Dess takes the Great Lakes Championship from Sub-Beast at Ides of March in the Court's own screwjob, then survives the rematch at High Stakes (No Disqualifications) when Matrix — Sub-Beast's own tag partner that same night — turns up out of the crowd and superkicks Sub-Beast instead of Dess for the pin, an act neither man explains on camera (high-stakes-2003-04-25). A month later at Blood Factory, Matrix challenges Dess himself for the belt in the fed's own signature weapons match, and the two put on what the broadcast frames as WMW's defining bout — both men in tears mid-match, the referee's own pre-match warning ("this is a wrestling match, not a butcher's shop") ignored completely. Dess wins clean, and offers a handshake Matrix appears to accept — then turns it into an ambush, declaring "a new era... PURE EVIL!!" as The DT runs in from the crowd to help him lay Dess out (blood-factory-2003-05-23). The title itself outlives the held record's own PPV coverage: the fed's own 13 June champions page shows it passing to Tami Weber at some undated point and back to Dess again on 13 June, on none of the eleven Friday Night Riots the surviving record otherwise only lists by date (champions-2003-06-13).

The Midwest Championship triangle and Umbrage's arrival Written by AI, 2026

Fury wins the Midwest Championship — the title's first ever change — from Lance Steele at Ides of March, only for Myst to run in afterward with a chair (Fury superkicks it into his face and retains anyway); Myst then beats Fury clean on Riot within the month to take the belt for himself (ides-of-march-2003-03-28; columns). The rematch at High Stakes is a three-way with Steele added back in, and it ends with a debut nobody predicted: as all three men lie out, the arena goes dark to Saliva's "Superstar" and Umbrage walks out, revives the downed referee, and helps Fury pin Myst — then immediately piledrives Fury and struts off with a smile, revealing himself as exactly the fourth threat to the division no one had accounted for (high-stakes-2003-04-25). Fury and Umbrage settle it one on one at Blood Factory in a bloody match that ends only when Fury drops Umbrage's own face and neck onto his knee for the pin, retaining the title (blood-factory-2003-05-23).

The Hardcore title's quiet vacancy and its own rename Written by AI, 2026

Matrix is already the Hardcore Champion when the surviving record opens, retaining it against Outlaw Biker at Ides of March. A month later he is billed for its own High Stakes defense — a 180-minute, multi-man Invitational — and never appears at all, leaving Jeff Hardkore to win the vacant match by pinning Johnny Blayze through a table (high-stakes-2003-04-25). Three weeks later, Commissioner Donovan Merlot renames the belt Ace Superior with Hardkore still champion; Blood Factory's own commentary has to explain the new name live on air, crediting Brad Johnson with wanting something "unique, different" for WMW (blood-factory-2003-05-23). The fed's own champions page treats the renamed belt as a title starting fresh rather than a continuation — see the Hardcore Championship's own page and the Ace Superior Championship's own page for how the two are chained here.
