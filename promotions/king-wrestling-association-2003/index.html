Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

The King Wrestling Association opened in early August 2003, staged as the work of two feuding half-brothers, Sean King and Aaron King, thrown together to run it by their late father's own will — “King Cobra” Rick King, a celebrated wrestler who died of cancer and left the promotion to both sons jointly. Its own handbook plays the pairing for its central tension: Sean, a quiet, self-made businessman, against Aaron, “more of a loose cannon who seems to always be in the mix, making things happen.”

KWA ran on its own point system, King Points, earned through roleplay quality, strategy, and wins and spent on gimmick matches, wrestler logos, and other promotional extras — a mechanism its own handbook frames as encouraging “competetion, creativity and cooperativeness.”

Its weekly show, Sunday Night SLAM!, aired Sunday nights, backed by a monthly pay-per-view; two championships, the KWA Heavyweight and KWA SLAM! Championships, ran the roster. Its broadcast staff included play-by-play and color man “Def Con” Connor Kain, a former thirteen-year NFL veteran with two Super Bowl rings; interviewer Nikki Green; ring announcer Tito Perez; and a father-and-son refereeing pair, Art and Chris Stokes.

No card, roster, or title result survives past the founding handbook. How the King brothers' own promotion actually played out isn't in the surviving record.
