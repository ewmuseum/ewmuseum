A federation of four self-titled co-presidents running two weekly broadcasts, Collision and Monday Night Distress, whose July 1998 cards carried a tag-team feud, a masked returnee's betrayal angle and a brooding newcomer's television debut. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

World Vintage Wrestling billed itself, in its own recruitment post of 26 June 1998, as “the innovators of tradition,” already running a roster and a two-show weekly schedule by the time that post went out. Four presidents ran it together, among them Rob Stryker, who also wrestled on its own cards, and an executive, Ian Walsh, who handled recruitment.

Its own Thursday broadcast, Collision, closed its 16 July 1998 edition with GlacierIce beating Brass Knuckles with a jumping kick he called Flying Chin Music, after Jack Vars had squashed “High Impact” Danny Wilson with an inverted piledriver, the Vars Drop, and Brutal Brian had pinned a trash-talking rival billed only as “The Psychotic One” with a double-arm DDT he called “Payback is hell.” The same card's main event, Rob Stryker against Scotty Tallion, ended in a double countout after both men brawled to the outside.

Its own Monday broadcast, Distress, ran four days later: GlacierIce beat Stryker with that same Flying Chin Music; “Stylish” Steve Milla won his own debut by disqualification after his manager Monica blinded his opponent and faked a ringside injury; a 310-pound, Roman-armored newcomer, Maximus II, beat Jack Vars with a claw-slam finisher called “The Fall of Jupiter”; and the Crack Connection took a chair-marred, disqualification-decided rematch over the Mischief Makers, who had beaten them the same way two weeks earlier.

The promotion's own last surviving document is that same 20 July 1998 broadcast. A rival newsgroup persona later claimed, in words the promotion never confirmed itself, to have “ruined the WVW and caused him to shut it down” that August; how, or whether, it actually ended isn't recorded in its own words.
