A roleplay federation whose top prize began as the Global Title in July 2003 and became the Universal Championship in September 2004, which ran its ReVolution show past its 250th edition and closed on a staged farewell tour at Colossus VIII in October 2012. Written by AI, 2026

Epitaph Written by AI, 2026

PRIME opened its cards in the summer of 2003, its own Title Histories page dating the 5 Star Title’s first champion, Mark Blaze, to 23 June and the Global Title’s first, Alex Caine, to 6 July — a roleplay federation, in its own Rules page’s words, “for created wrestlers,” where match winners were decided by the roleplays its competitors posted rather than by results alone, and a self-described “proud affiliate” of Primetime Central from early in its run.

Its top prize changed names once: the Global Title, ten reigns and eight champions deep, was declared vacant and its lineage carried forward as the Universal Championship at a King of Kings 2 on 26 September 2004, first held by Black Angel. Other belts ran alongside it for years at a time — the Intense Title, first Rock Startling’s and longest held by Vangelus Olsig at 361 days; the Tag Team Title, first won by Mark Blaze and Andy Magnum; and a title that changed its own name repeatedly (Xposure, roC-TV, PRIMEal Rage, PRIMEtime) before its record settled on calling it the Alias Title, merged into the 5 Star Title in a triple-threat “Champions Collide” match on 30 May 2007. The Universal Title’s own longest reign, 401 days, belonged to Jason Snow.

Its shows staged a real world tour beside its home arenas — Boston’s Fleet Center and the TD Banknorth Garden among them — reaching the Maracanã, Ellis Park in Johannesburg and the Palau Sant Jordi in Barcelona, Calcutta and Guangzhou in early 2007, and Paris, Turin, Casablanca and Beijing in 2011. On 4 November 2007 it shared the TD Banknorth Garden and a single pay-per-view broadcast with Global Championship Wrestling, each promotion running its own lineup under one roof for what its own announce team called the first time two e-wrestling promotions had ever done so.

Recurring characters carried its record across its run — the villain Boda and his rival Karina Wolfenden, Killean Sirrajin (billed as the Supreme Machine), Sun Tzu, its authority figures Lisa Tyler and Blaine Blair, and, in its final years, Lindsay Troy (the Queen of the Ring) and Tchu (the Inhuman Being) — and its tag division, once deep enough to run its own title picture for years, went suspended after Culture Shock 2010.

On ReVolution 244, 14 April 2011, PRIME broke its own broadcast to let a wrestler speak in his own voice, microphones on the announce desk cut, and remember Paul Scanlan: a writer who had held the ring as Paul Cain and Damien Kahn across half a dozen federations including PRIME itself, and who had died five days earlier. The tribute recalled him splitting a King of Kings 2006 match write with the man delivering it, and named the titles Paul Cain had held elsewhere in his career.

PRIME's own news called its last months a “farewell tour,” and its record ends at Colossus VIII, 14 October 2012: Lindsay Troy defeating Tchu in the company's final match, the two of them left alone in the ring afterward as the crowd chanted their names, and its own broadcast team closing with a plain thank-you to the audience rather than another storyline hook.
