Violence Jack Biography Nicknames VJ; 'The Bringer of the Black Gospel'; 'Father Shanahan'; 'The Pariah Saint'; 'The Druid of the Dark'; 'Boston's Bastard Son' Hometown Boston, Massachusetts Birthday August 8, 1968 Height 6' Weight 232 Fed Titles None Fed Awards None Biography Bruce Shanahan lived in a rough slum-region of Boston, Massachusetts. Knowing very little of his family, Bruce grew to respect the codes of brotherhood and loyalty and involved himself in a very tightknit network of friends that became his street allies. It was here that the seeds were planted that would someday spawn his personality trademark: hatred, rage and a volatile mental state. Violence, treachery, and loss were his most common bedfellows and he grew to especially detest those more fortunate in society. At age 20, Bruce collected what little savings he had from his dead-end factory job and used it to travel to Japan. He did this in order to make money and gain a reputation doing what he did best in life: fighting. He entered the notorious deathmatch wrestling circuit of Japan, slowly gaining renown for his uncanny threshold of pain and incredible stamina while waging war night-in and night-out in conditions that would cause the common 'extreme' American wrestler to have nightmares. It was sometime during this period that Bruce Shanahan garnered his now-recognized moniker of 'Violence Jack', in tribute to a Japanese anime character of the same name. As for exactly when or how this new nickname was christened upon the scarred native of the USA, the facts are hazy and primarily unknown. Having built a solid reputation around the Orient for his savagery and ruthless nature, Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan returned to the States and entered the American independent wrestling circuit. He spent a couple years toiling across the continent entertaining fans with his rage-filled speeches and in-ring fury. Jack entered several larger independent promotions full-time over a period of 5+ years and won a plethora of accolades nationwide. At about this time, Jack and his ICW tag team partner, Chris Havenstorm, were accepted into the WWA federation of Primetime Central as a prominent tag team. Both men also entered separately in the 2nd annual Global Title Tournament as virtual unknowns. V-Jack, an outspoken nobody predicted to fall early without making so much as a scratch in the competition, defeated a rising star of the NTICW company, followed by the soon-to-be Cruiserweight champion of Primetime Championship Wrestling, and then destroyed one of the preliminary tournament favorites the following round. WWA closed shortly thereafter, and when the promotion talent were collectively released as free agents, Jack was picked up and given a contract by Primetime Championship Wrestling. It was during this tournament that a new chapter was written in the life of Bruce Shanahan. He was haunted by a series of bleak, grisly revelations during his sleep. Finding conventional medication to be ineffective, Bruce sought out religious counsel by going to his old friend who had been called into the ministry in the Catholic Church. Father Randall Craven surmised that Bruce was under a psychic assault by a throng of malevolent demigods spawned from beyond the reaches of known space and fearfully spoken of in tomes of occult origin; beings officially denounced as myth by the Church, yet fought under a veil of secrecy unofficially. Few outside those claiming involvement believe this or any of the following to be true. These beings, coined the Great Old Ones, were alien entities said to hold great power to the point they were touched with demigod status. They reigned on dismal worlds across the cosmos, expansion and power limited by both their foes and a complacent disposition, with several said to be slumbering on Earth itself awaiting the day of their resurrection. Bruce joined an elite underground society/brotherhood called the Blackhelm Coalition, a secretive faction chartered with the intention of preventing the imprisoned entities from awakening and destroying mankind, and thwarting their deranged cults in the process. The connection Bruce had to these beasts was unknown, though one appeared obvious as their pursuing of his soul and mind eclipsed the possibility of his targeting being sheerly random. Their clash continued across Earth under the veil of conventional military operations and experiments and even terrorist-type attacks. Even throughout these ordeals, most claim the accounts of those involved to be lunacy; some professing to be involved actually have been committed. Somewhere along this line, Bruce went over the edge. Seeing the universal horrors of what lies beyond healthy human knowledge took a toll on the already-maddening man from Boston and he was committed to an asylum in upstate New York. When he was inexplicably released by doctors who had only days before claimed he was likely to never escape insanity, Bruce's demeanor had changed. What he had previously vowed to fight to his death, he now worshipped at the foot of obscene altars and read of in blasphemous books. Bruce started a vile cult for the reason that (as he said) 'was decreed on me by the Crawling Chaos himself, the Black Pharaoh, Nyarlathotep.' This cult was coined The Sect of Black Wisdom, based out of an old mansion in upstate Vermont. PCW, disturbed and caught off-guard by this extreme shift of attitude, released Jack from his contract with a nationally-televised stunt using a lookalike of the infamous star. Contacted almost immediately by Steven Caldera of Five Star Wrestling, Bruce agreed to come to the federation to help Caldera fulfill his greed-inspired wishes. He was also driven by the ulterior motive of using FSW as a forum to finally spread his dark new faith. FSW's existence was cut short by a series of political maneuverings across the industry, and as such left Jack without a mainstream avenue to spread his wild philosophies. But not long after FSW folded, PCW contacted their ex-employee with a new contract offer. Bruce, seeing an opportunity to prove the power of his beliefs to a worldwide audience, eagerly signed on yet again. Shanahan stormed back on the PCW scene with a renewed fury and vigor not seen since his independent days, making a bigger impact in a couple short weeks than his entire previous tenure. After a long and grueling war with Skylar ‘The End' Green (a struggle considered the feud of the year by many), he captured the PCW Continental title. All the while, he managed to elude agents of the Coalition (ex-allies turned enemies) and further the cause of his decadent gods. As luck would have it, history repeated itself with PCW as it had with FSW. PCW fell victim to the political panderings of various industry financial powers, and the proud and storied company was shut down. Shanahan completely disappeared for a period of nearly 5 months afterward, and only rumors exist as to his whereabouts during that period. Some claims state he was apprehended by Church or Coalition operatives and held for a period of time before escape, while others say he was temporarily institutionalized in a hospital for the criminally insane (escaping shortly thereafter or bribing his way out). Others say he simply went into a time of religious retreat, locked away with his followers worshipping their foul idols and preparing for a time of renewed vengeance on the society Shanahan so despised. VJ went on to enter Rush Pro Wrestling in North Carolina, the rival promotion of tSC. Though he had a strong run, contending in main events for the top title more than once, unfortunately RPW went on hiatus due to undisclosed matters. And in the aftermath of the 2004 King of the Indies tournament, where Shanahan defeated Karina Wolfenden, Coral Avalon, Jeff Garvin and then-tSC Champion Sonny Silver all in on evening to win the trophy, the Squared Circle immediately signed the infernal superstar. Things looked well in the Squared Circle for VJ, even garnering a shot at Sonny Silver and The Championship in one of his first shows, but alas tSC closed down after a period of management shifting. Things have continued this way for the past several years, with the cult mastermind inexplicably disappearing and reappearing across the globe to hatch his nefarious schemes. His strongest crusade yet was hatched amidst the GTT7 competition when Shanahan surged to the finals. Some claim Shanahan was only moments short of fulfilling the dark prophecies dictated centuries ago in his unholy books. The world was in veritable chaos, dark forces swirling across the dominion of man as he battled in the final bout against Garbage Bag Johnny and Desade. Desade triumphed, and with Shanahan's defeat, the shadows threatening to consume the world subsided. The dark priest's strength had been at its pinnacle and many say his failure was a combined result of Desade's heroic efforts and treachery in his own organization, a betrayal that resulted in three of his top lieutenants fracturing from the Sect of Black Wisdom to form their own cult. Again Violence Jack seemed to vanish from both the wrestling world and the public light. But again, out of seemingly nowhere, he has resurfaced. With the annual Jewel In the Crown tournament around the corner, the malefic Bruce Shanahan has opted to reemerge in PRIME and pick up where he left off. Accompanied by memories of his ultimate evil nearly being finally realized, and his release of a new grimoire ttiled 'The Black Testament"across the cult underground, he is ready for war. A new chapter is now written in the legacy of Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan as he arrives back in the territory of PRIME, his sights on the Jewel in the Crown and the Universal Title. Bruce still aims to spread his hideous gospel of 'the Old Ones' to all who will willingly listen, and crush all whom deny his words as truth, thereupon imparting his wisdom upon their broken bodies. A man who was the epicenter of a worldwide panic as GTT7 hit its climax, he's seemingly defied his age limitations and continues to fight as a man possessed, ceaselessly warring in the name of the most ghastly of divinities. Whether Bruce is truly insane or whether there is a degree of reality to his hideous beliefs is a topic still under scrutiny. And it's one Bruce claims 'shall be revealed when the stars are right and Great Cthulhu rises from his undersea temple to revel again.' Other Feds WWIWF; GWA; CIWF; ICW; WWA; PCW; FSW; RPW; tSC; ACW; Just Wrestling Other Fed Awards Countless independent chafederation titles/awards; PCW Extreme champion; PCW Continental champion; RPW/tSC King of the Independents 2004 Tournament champion; GTT6 tournament finalist/runner-up; GTT7 tournament finalist/runner-up; Just Wrestling Champion Wrestling Style Hybrid of brawling/street fighting with shades of technical knowledge. As to exactly where he trained is obscure but most of his uncouth, brawling technique is primarily self-taught. The rudimentary technical wrestling knowledge he possesses has been learned over many years by a handful of mentors (none lasting long). In a nutshell....VJ doesn't have the sort of insane depth to his in-ring approach as the industry's top technical masters, but he's seen it all and done it all. The heart and soul of Violence Jack is that's a fairly outright brawler, spending a great deal of the time just trading fisticuffs and landing brutal yet simple strikes. He has his share of technical knowledge but he's no Bret Hart or Dean Malenko. He beats his opponents into a pulp and mixes up his brawling methods with an array of devastating slams. His favorite target is the neck, setting up for his pulverizing R'lyeh Anthem head-drop (known to many as the Judgment Slam, Sugiura Olympic Slam or Painthriller among other names). The greatest strength of Violence Jack? Stamina. Not stamina as in he can go often for 80+ minutes, but he can take a lickin' and keep on tickin'. He's become renowned for what is deemed a borderline superhuman threshold of pain and ability to withstand unheard of damage and stay as tenacious as ever. He's been known to occasionally no-sell some of the more substantial head-drops in the wrestling business before coming right back with his own high-impact attack (highspot in his more epic matches usually), not unlike the top Japanese stars do at times. His approach is slow- to mid-paced and methodical. Standard heel tactics aplenty and, being a veteran who knows in-ring psychology like the back of his hand, he loves mind games. Jack will never be seen flying around the ring or taking insane risks that reap limited rewards, not at this stage of his career; he won't be tumbling off cages into stacked tables or experimenting with aerial techniques. He stalks his opponents, takes his shots to stay close, and relentlessly beats them into oblivion. Finisher Move R'lyeh Anthem Finisher Description Jack ducks behind his opponent, hoists them into what can be described as a crude Angle Slam set-up, and abruptly falls over and deposits the victim on their head/neck. This is his primary finisher and with one like this.....well, he doesn't really need any super finishers. Setup Description None Other Moves Common Moves Inverted atomic drop Sitout Tiger Bomb Scoop slam Various knee and elbow strikes Sleeper hold Brainbuster Vertical suplex Fireman's carry into gutbuster Double underhook flip into backbreaker Basic piledriver DDT Side Russian leg sweep 3/4 facelock Russian leg sweep (wrenches back on the neck as they fall) Quick-hitting Ace Crusher (typically he'll use the opportunity while his opponent is briefly dazed to seize them, quickly twist them around and land the Detonator) Signature Moves The Last Resort - Named after Jack's favorite bar back in Boston. V-Jack locks on a cobra clutch from behind and then launches the opponent over his head, dropping them on their head and neck in a brutal cobra clutch overhead suplex. Osaka Send-off - Jack gets behind his opponent and drops to a sitting position, using his full force and weight to spike the back of their head into the mat, floor, etc. Black-Jack - VJ lifts his opponent up as if for a body slam, then abruptly sits down and drops them on their head in a Michinoku Driver. V-Jack often adds a corkscrew to this move for effect. The Carlisle Legacy - Named after Jack's best friend who was murdered at the age of 19 on the Boston streets. Jack sits atop the top rope and executes a double-arm tornado DDT, bringing his opponent down at an unusual angle on their head and neck. Once a devastating 'super' finisher, now relegated to a semi-rare trademark maneuver after the recents addition to his repertoire. Detonator - Modified hangman's neckbreaker. Jack's pet hold for years and one he has mastered the execution of to the point he has created several variations, making it possible to land the maneuver from virtually any position. He has 'codenamed' these unique variations. ---Detonator Delta - VJ lifts his opponent into a torture rack. He then swings the opponent's body out behind his own, gripping the inverted 3/4 facelock position and dropping them in a falling Detonator. ---Detonator Charlie - VJ locks his opponent in the inverted 3/4 facelock for the Detonator. He then uses the side ropes as a springboard, springing off and spinning he and the victim around and lands in the Detonator. Resembles the Stratusfaction bulldog in its execution, performed as a hangman's neckbreaker rather than a bulldog. ---Detonator Gamma - VJ sits atop the top turnbuckle and locks his opponent up as if for a tornado DDT. Instead he somersaults forward, shifting the front facelock into the inverted 3/4 as he flips forward. He lands in a sitting position, maintaining the hold and landing the Detonator. ---Detonator Bravo - Jack hoists his opponent into a vertical suplex. As they fall backward, he maintains a grip around the neck and drops them in a version of a Detonator. ---Detonator Omega - VJ either hoists his opponent into position for a crucifix powerbomb, or sets them atop one shoulder in a Canadian backbreaker rack. He then either drops them from the crucifix or nudges them backward off his shoulder, either way dropping them into the neckbreaker position. Shattered Halo -- Corkscrew cradle tombstone piledriver. Qualifies as a finishing technique that has picked up many wins for Shanahan. Not as effective as the Anthem, but it does the trick nicely. Ode to Azathoth -- VJ hooks the victim in position for a fisherman suplex, then quickly spins to the side and snaps the victim down in what resembles a fisherman's swinging neckbreaker. Like the Shattered Halo, works well as a potential match-ender though not a near-guaranteed win like the Anthem. --The following techniques are classified not as as individual maneuvers but quick, relentless, transitional combinations that chain directly into one another for maximum efficiency. These are always used either as counters and escapes to snap momentum to VJ, or immediately following a close near-fall, fueled by adrenaline, in his favor to retain the edge and dish out as much hurt as swiftly as possible. The wily veteran has learned to harness reserves of strength to either turn the tide or cap near-fatal assaults, as needed. They have the potential to end things on their own (and sometimes may against weaker foes) but are mostly precursors to one of his 'big 3' standard match-enders. Always used late stage.-- (1) Like most of the man's brutal offense, this series of maneuvers targets the neck in anticipation of a R'lyeh Anthem finish. The string begins with a fisherman's capture before lifting the opponent up and dropping them straight down so their head/neck crash down over one of VJ's knees. As quickly as he can, he hoists them onto his shoulders in a fireman's carry, tosses/flips them airborne, and brings their neck down over his knee once more. Against lighter adversaries (Desade, Troy, other lightweights) he'll often keep his hands on them after the first move and sling them onto his shoulders in one motion for the second, as opposed to releasing them entirely before chaining into the next. He finishes by hitting the ropes and coming back with either an explosive diving lariat or a low Yakuza kick to the seated or kneeling victim. (2) His second combo surge. Inverted DDT (with Shanahan falling forward and not backward) into an inverted suplex ending (with VJ rolling backward with inverted facelock still maintained) and into a dragon clutch neck submission, seated on their back. While he rarely (and by rarely I mean he never yet has) attains a win with the submission part of this one, it cranks back on the neck and helps to wear the opponent down. (3) A barrage of Detonator variations. Typically the standard, Delta, and Bravo in one big surge of adrenaline. (4) Used straight out of a close near-fall in his favor, VJ will pull the opponent up, bend them forward and start with a flurry of Kawada-style short range kicks to the face. Then he gets a running start and blasts them with a running knee to the side of the head, usually staggering but not knocking them completely over. Next comes a fireman's carry dropped into a gutbuster. Then he lifts them into an inverted suplex, dropping them instead into a stunner. Holding onto the neck and not letting them completely fall, he uses the last of his adrenaline to pull them into a piggy-back position and finish the combo with a backpack stunner. Match-writers: Feel free to be creative and create more on the spot. Other Notes: As earlier mentioned, Violence Jack's threshold for pain borders on superhuman and his ability to 'take a hit' is unparalleled. A spot often used in his matches is for him to take a brutal attack, no-sell it, get right up, and deliver one of his own (that typically being one of his hard-hitting signatures or one of his headdrops). This is purely an adrenaline rush and VJ will typically collapse after delivering his 'retalitory' attack, leaving both competitors momentarily floored. Constantly taunts his foes in the ring, trying to frustrate them and get into their heads. Coupled with his 'talent' for getting beaten on and shrugging it off so easily, it often works. So when match-writing with VJ, toss in an insult or two in the vein of VJ. Fiery insults when things are heated and solemn, sadistic whispers when the pace is slowed. Good examples would be just some semi-cryptic verbal cut or the cruel type of thing you'd expect Marv in Sin City to say to someone he's about to kill or to someone screwing with him. I also get the visual of some Soul Calibur III quotes. Some examples (be creative and have fun with it): (Being beaten on) "Is that the best you can throw?" "Where's your heart, worm? I expected some semblance of pain out of this." "Cthulhu have mercy on your soul. Whoever trained you, I'd ask for a refund." "Were I you, you mangy dog, I'd hit the gym some more. I've had massages more uncomfortable than this." (While in control) "This is trifling. How can you even look yourself in the mirror, jackal?" "Stand and fight me! Where is your passion, child?!" "I'm not through yet. This is far too relaxing." "I grow weary of this game. May the abyss take you." "Send the Old Ones my regards. Farewell!" "The bigger you are, the harder you fall. No redemption for you today, vermin!" After panning around some bizarre undersea city, it zooms into the darkness of some vast open doorway and the words "VIOLENCE JACK" fly out in Olde English red lettering. From there, standard highlight video featuring he and his comrades. He typically enters these days in a dark robe trimmed with gold/silver and painted with an array of arcane glyphs and wild-looking symbols. No pyro or anything at this point. Standard entrance coupled with a hateful reaction from the audience. (Partial example) Static. And a muffled voice. "We do away with your kind....." A tangible chill almost seemed to envelop the arena; the feeling crept down enough spines at the ominous intro voice that many arena-wide shuddered. "Countdown to exterminate the human race......" Darkness itself was creeping into the coliseum. "I am war, I am pain. I am all you've ever slain. I am tears in your eyes I am grief, I am lies." (An old RPW example) The Rush ProVision roared to life, skimming over a computer generated underwater city. Dimmu Borgir's "Puritania" crackled over the loudspeakers, the crackling beginning of the song suited to the opening of the video montage as it halts on a towering cavern set above the rest of the aquatic necropolis. The video quickly zoomed in on the dark mouth of the cave as a demonic roar echoed forth over the song opening. 'VIOLENCE JACK' appeared in red olde English lettering on-screen as the thunderous blast beats of 'Puritania' began. Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan, greeted by a shower of boos and derogatory chants, ambled from the back garbed in his silver and gold-trimmed black robes toward the square circle. Many wished for the man to be reintroduced to the rubber walls of the insane asylum, and looking into the man's eyes reaffirmed the reason for that suspicion; insanity lurked in those unholy sockets, and it was to be the problem of Scott Matthews.
