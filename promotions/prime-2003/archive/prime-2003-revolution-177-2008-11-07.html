He charged forward toward El Spiffy! but was felled by a quick sweep from Tyler Rayne. Clutching his ribs, Rayne stalked towards El Spiffy!, who squared up for another heavy exchange. Dillion wasn't so easily vanquished, though, and he spun Rayne back around to insist that he be taken care of proper-like, lest he beat some ass of his own. Tyler obliged by kneeing Dame in the midsection and immediately dropping him with a DDT. He didn't stop there, though, and as his focus remained diverted, the mystery luchador sized him up for an attack. That was the plan until a wave of cheers rose up from the crowd and Chandler Tsonda headed out to the ringside area. It's important to clarify language here, because we're skipping over the minutes of dialogue between Nick and Richard that clarify the champ as being here for some reason other than to scout Tyler Rayne, his longtime frienemy and possible contender to the Universal Title pending the outcome of this match and (pending outcome again) any subsequent matches. They conclude that this isn't Tsonda's purpose because he doesn't do the tried-and-true "walk out on the ramp and make it painfully obvious that I am thinking and or observing" act. Instead, he comes out from behind the curtain with a purpose, and he makes a beeline for the ring - specifically for El Spiffy!. The luchador doesn't see Tsonda before the Viet Viper grabs him by the leg and drags him out of the ring like he just hit the left bumper on a Nintendo 64 game. As soon as he hits the mat outside and looks up at the Universal Champion, though, he begins backing away, notadequately prepared for this encounter, apparently. Tsonda stalks the luchador as he crawls, eyes afire and spouting something unintelligible but clearly nasty. There doesn't seem to be a response from the masked man aside from his continued attempts at escape. Those attemps seem to be proving fruitless until Tyler Rayne arrives on the scene. Leaning over the ropes (having kept Dillion at bay momentarily) Rayne says something to Chandler, who angrily snaps his attention from El Spiffy! to his sort-of ally. The words are still harsh, but not angry, as he tries to explain himself. It's hard to rationalize with a man in the throes of an adrenaline rush, though, and Rayne's equally harsh response draws Tsonda's attention further. The two men have a slight verbal dust-up that commands their full attention. In that moment, Dillion rushes forward with a knee to Rayne's back, and El Spiffy! kicks out Tsonda's knee and attempts an escape. Dillion follows up on the knee with a german suplex, rolling through and showing some semblance of technique, then showboating a little too much. Tyler immediately reverses once they're on their feet, and he gives Dame a German of his own. Then he rolls through and transitions to a t-bone position, following into a second suplex. Then he rolls through again and transitions to a front facelock for a fisherman's suplex. When he lifts, though, he immediately jumps and hooks the other leg as they both plunge to the mat. The small package driver with the fourth-wall-demolishing name leaves Dame Dillion planted in the center of the canvas, a victim of his own lack of formal training. Meanwhile, El Spiffy! races around ringside before hopping the barricade into the crowd. Chandler Tsonda catches up to him and grabs him by the back of the mask as he crosses into the mob. The two struggle with each other as Chandler tears at the back of the mask. El Spiffy! claws at his hands and swings his head around, trying to break the grip free. The laces on the mask give way and snap, and Tsonda's grip slips. Chandler looks up as the luchador disappears into the crowd, mask loose and flapping at the back. He makes special note of the shock of red hair underneath the lycra and looks into the camera as if to say "you saw that too, right?" "Stick 'Em Up" had already begun to play as Tyler Rayne was announced as the winner. Tsonda turned around to see his friend standing victorious, but neither of them looked particularly happy. Rayne kept a suspicious eye on his frienemy, none too pleased about the spotlight stolen from him on the first show after his title loss. Chandler locked eyes with Tyler for the second time in three show endings, and what troubled him at the moment was the thought that the growing discomfort between them might actually be the least of his worries.
