Civil Disobedience Biography Fed Titles PRIME Tag Team Champions: August 28, 2005 to September 28, 2005 (31 days) Fed Awards Weekly ratings: Rev 68, Rev 71, #5 Biography Early Years Considering that Seth and Trevor have been friends for over fifteen years, it is safe to say that Civil Disobedience is not a team that was born overnight. The two boys met in a local karate class at the age of ten and were quickly drawn together by their mutual love of professional wrestling. A casual conversation after class one day soon blossomed into a life-long comraderie. Thought by their parents to be merely a childish obsession, wrestling soon proved to be much more than that, as the two followed wrestling religiously throughout all of high school, speaking often of getting into the business after graduating. They were certainly athletic enough, both making it to state a number of times in their high school wrestling careers, in addition to being star members of the track team. Graduating from high school with honors, their families were shocked when they expressed no interest in attending college. For the two young men, there was never any doubt what they would do with their lives. Professional wrestling had taken a hold of them. Largely self-taught, they began their careers in a small independent promotion based in the suburbs of Chicago. Their natural athleticism quickly outshined the majority of has-beens and never-weres that shared the card with them, and it wasn't long before they improved to the point where they realized they were destined for bigger things. The two thus enrolled in Primetime Wrestling Academy in hopes of gaining entrance to what was then, to them, the nirvana of professional wrestling organizations, PCW. Subscribing the the philosophy that the cream will always rise to the top, they expected to be pushed through the academy in record time. Unfortunately, their high hopes were quickly revealed for what they were in actuality: mere delusions. In the PWA, they were forced to abandon their self-developed style in favor of a more high-spotty gimmick. They were now the high-flying, care-free, uber-sarcastic team of Civil Disobedience, wrestling's version of Bill and Ted, Wayne and Garth, Beavis and Butthead. They acquiesced to the demands, figuring that they had to cater to the demands of the industry and figuring that they would move along quicker if they worked with the system rather than fight against it. Even after making the changes, however, their time in the academy seemed to drag on. Under the tutelage of Boda, they languished, forced to wrestle developmental matches week after week against opponents barely able to string together a sentence. They may well have been forced to remain there for months, if opportunity had not come knocking in the form of the newly launched Old School Wrestling promotion, and they were only too eager to leave the academy behind. OSW Handpicked as members of the debuting organization's roster, they became mainstays near the top of the federation's tag division from the very beginning, launching an extended feud with the team of Heresian Bloodlust (Hessian and Azrael Cain) which would play itself out over the next six months in some of the federation's most memorable matches. Their popularity skyrocketed as they captured the tag belts on OSW's first PPV event, becoming the first team to hold the belts. It looked as if life couldn't get any better. Seemingly overnight they had gone from being nobodies in PWA to one of the premier tag teams in the wrestling industry. All their hard work seemed to have paid off in the fame, fortune, and success that they desired. Then, as the proverbial expression goes, the other shoe dropped when the results of a random drug test came back positive. Having injured his knee during a match in a fall from a ladder, Seth had been taking medication for the pain for some time. When the doctor stopped prescribing pain killers, Masters had looked to other sources to help him cope with the nightly agony. Unable to deal with the pain resultant from wrestling the high spot-oriented style that both the management and the fans thirsted for, Masters had developed a morphine addiction to enable him to keep going night after night. When these details came to light, the front office was less than sympathetic, threatening to cut him from the roster on the spot. It took a great deal of begging to reach a compromise. Civil Disobedience was busted down the card, dropping the belts to Heresian Bloodlust less than month after winning them, before being placed in a low card comedy feud with D-list celebrities Carrot Top and Gilbert Godfried that carried through the next PPV. Despite the attempt to bury the team, the OSW fans still flocked to the team. Polls showed that their merchandise was among the top movers in the federation. Seeing the marketability of the team and impressed by their willingness to endure their punishment, the front office decided to push them again, this time in a three way feud with Heresian Bloodlust and Da Westsidaz. The down time spent wrestling against the popularly dubbed Team Annoying had allowed Seth's knee to heal somewhat, so they were ready to rise to the occassion. The result of this was that the following month yielded some of the best matches of their career, most specifically the three way Weapons of Choice clash in which the feud culminated, which was considered a strong match of the year contender. Civil Disobedience reclaimed the tag gold soon thereafter, winning a tournament which was made after the Think Tank stripped Da Westsidaz of the belts. After beating the former champs cleanly in the first round they went on to defeat the teams of Howard Porter/Armour, NC-17 (Silence and Graphic Violence), and John Mammoth/PHD to claim the belts once more. The reign was another short lived one, as they dropped the belts in a four way ladder match to the team of the Throwbacks (Dave Gibson and Clay Vendetta). During this match, Seth again injured his knee, much worse than the first time. Seeing a number of specialists, he was told by all that the future of his wrestling career was bleak at best. An operation was needed, followed by no less than a six month rehabilitation period. It was decided that Hawke would wrestle singles during this time, and Seth agreed to put him over. In his last match before his sabbatical, he tapped out at a crucial point in a four-corner Ironman tag match for the belts, costing Civil Disobedience the match. Hawke turned on his partner after the match, and the next week joined the hated New School Wrestling faction vying for supremecy in the promotion. He established himself immediately as a number one contender for the Cruiserweight title, clashing repeatedly with the champion Kid Wonder over the course of the next month and coming close to taking the title from him on a number of occassions. It was at this point that Hawke parted ways with the company for the first time. Feeling that he deserved the Cruiserweight belt as a means to elevate his character, he protested when management allowed Wonder to keep the belt for his subsequent feud with Tyler Nelson. For one, Nelson wasn't even a cruiserweight, and secondly, the high profile competitors had a storied enough past together as things were; they hardly needed the belt to lend further heat to their rivalry. Little by little over the course of his tenure in OSW, Hawke had been becoming increasingly fed up with the way the company was being run and this was the straw that broke the camel's back. Vocally complaining of being kept underneath a glass ceiling did little to endear himself with the front office, and he soon found himself languishing in a matchless limbo. Within a matter of weeks he had left the company, which only too eagerly agreed to release him from his contract. Life touring the independent circuit wasn't all that it was cracked up to be, however. The combination of long nights on the road and shitty pay quickly began to wear on Hawke, and it wasn't long before he began to regret his decision to walk out on his lucrative OSW contract. He tried not to think about it, tried to put OSW out of his mind and concentrate on perfecting his form in the ring, but when he heard that the new tag champs, Ronin (Matt Register and Jeffrey Wayne) were cutting shoot promos trashing Civil D on OSW TV, his increasingly inflating ego just couldn't let it slide. He returned to the OSW offices, demanding to be let back into the federation to get a shot at the shit-talking new kids on the block. They laughed at his gall, telling him that he had already burned his bridges, but if he could get Masters to return they might consider it. Hawke was only too willing to oblige, persuading Seth into returning to the ring nearly a month before the scheduled end of his rehabilation for one last shot at the titles. The match took place at Ascension 2003, and Ronin was victorious in a close match. This was the last appearance made by Civil D on OSW programming. Seeing no real future for themselves in what had become over the past year and a half a stifling environment, the team sought to ply their trade elsewhere. RPW and Japan To be contuvred~! General In-ring Behavior Having functioned as a team for the better part of five years (and having been close friends for over fifteen), Civil Disobedience work as a cohesive unit when in the ring. By this stage in their careers, they know the ins and outs of tag team wrestling, and one has only to watch one of their matches to see how the years of working in the style have paid off. Neither man stays in the ring for long, instead making quick tags and utilizing double team moves whenever possible. They look to isolate one of their opponents and work them over to the point they can no longer offer any resistence. They also utilize many of the tricks in the traditional heel team rulebook. When isolating an opponent they will do anything they can to incense the fresh partner, such as hitting cheap shots to draw him/her into the fray. While the referee attempts to restrain the interceding partner they will then set upon the weakened competitor in the ring like a pack of wolves, pounding and choking them. Other tricks include: - the inactive partner performing a chokehold in the corner while the active one distracts the referee. - the inactive partner grabbing the free hand of the active one to lend leverage to an abdominal stretch. - kick, knee, or chair shot to the back as opponent runs the ropes. - inactive partner putting the foot of the active one on the ropes to escape a pinfall. - illegal submission breaks. Civil Disobedience's penchant for breaking the rules will sometimes backfire on them, as one may inadvertently hit his partner by mistake. They may also go to the well one to many times and be caught by the referee, leading to a disqualification (though woe be to any referee who keeps them from victory). Masters, with his injured knee, tends to stay away from any high risk maneuvers. Instead he focuses on a stiff technical ground-based style, honed during his tenure in Japanese rings. Hawke utilizes more high spots in his repertoire, though he has also focused on refining his technical abilities. Both wrestlers also have a solid foundation in Shotokan Karate, through which they know how to use leverage and momentum to their advantage in the ring. Any striking offense will take it's form in traditional martial arts strikes i.e. jabs, elbows, knees, stiff kicks. Hawke and Masters being only average sized, they are easily overpowered by larger opponents (6'8 and over). Unable to match them physically, they instead look to outsmart and outwrestle them. Typically, they will try to keep bigger competitors on the mat, horizontal, where they are unable to utilize their power and leverage. Leg work will be their primary focus in these situations. In no circumstance will they show any form of sportsmanship. Their goal, first and foremost, is to put themselves over at any cost, and they will look to injure, humiliate, and even end the careers of their opponents to further this cause. Gone is the showboating, sports entertainment style they embodied in OSW, replaced instead by a bitter, callous vendetta against the sport and any of its competitors that stand in their way. Entrance Music 'Cult of Personality' by Living Colour Ring Entrances No strobe lights. No pyrotechnics. Civil Disobedience will walk out of the back with minimal fanfare to the sound of 'Cult of Personality' playing over the PA system. They will usually take their time coming down to the ring, basking in the jeering of the crowd. They will usually be the first team to the ring and, once there, they will proceed to cut a shoot promo directed toward either their opponent or the crowd (or more likely both). The promo will finish with them either calling out their opponents or being interrupted by them.
