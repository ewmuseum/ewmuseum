In-ring Tactics Before he ever got serious about wrestling, Tyler Rayne was both a Marine (though that didn’t last too long at all) and a bouncer at a Japanese nightclub. He also spent ten years aimlessly wandering the Earth, making a quiet name for himself as one of the best in illegal, underground fighting. (This was before MMA became the sensation that it is today). Point? The dude knows how to fight. He’s not much for throwing punches in the ring, as he has quite the proficient background in both Muay Thai and Tae Kwon Do. He may not throw the hardest or fastest punch in the business, but he’s certainly got some of the best legs in the business (sorry, Lindz). While he may have, (and use), some seriously devastating kicks, what Rayne is really looking for is an opportunity to throw an elbow or knee to the head. (Knees to the body are always more than acceptable). He’ll throw a lot of forearms, hoping to follow through and connect with an elbow. When in tie-ups, he’ll be looking for a chance to land a well-placed knee to the ribs or under the chin, and maybe an elbow to the top or side of the head. He’s not much for rest holds, but if he does use one, he’ll usually try to sink in a front-face lock, which is great positioning for those knees and elbows he likes so much. Otherwise, a basic armbar or headlock to slow the pace is about the most you’ll ever get out of him. Tyler Rayne can take a beating. The guy’s tougher than leather, and harder than Hell to put down for the count. Of course, just because he can take a beating, doesn’t mean he likes to. Rayne always figures the best defense is a good offense, and he’s the kind of wrestler that likes to always be on the attack. He wrestles a fast-paced, high energy match from bell to bell. Once he’s in the ring, the guy is nothing less than full-throttle. And he’s got stamina to spare. He prefers to use a lot of springboard moves, whether they be kicks or DDTs or moonsaults. The more places he can jump from, the better. He uses a lot of high-flying and cruiserweight moves in his arsenal, which in conjunction with those springboards and repeated elbows/knees to the head, can do a damn good job of discombobulating an opponent. He likes to come up with random offense on the fly, priding himself as something of an innovator in the ring. His ultimate goal is to get the opponent disoriented. If you don’t know what attack is coming next, or where the attack is coming from, that makes it pretty hard to defend against. Though perhaps stronger than he looks, Rayne is far from a powerhouse. Nor is he an accomplished technician. His years in the ring (ten plus now) have taught him a thing or two about reversals, but he doesn’t have a collegiate wrestling background, and to be perfectly honest, he doesn’t care much for the slow pace and homo-erotic nature of mat wrestling. Fortunately, he’s a tough son of a bitch, so if he’s in a bind, it’s not too much to wait it out, look for the opening, and take advantage to go back on that offense. Rayne is, for better or worse, a man of the people. Despite his sometimes brash and even asinine behavior, he has developed quite the fan base. What started as something of a cult following during his years as MDK exploded into a full-blown mania after he removed the mask and revealed himself as the scarred-warrior Tyler Rayne. While Rayne first attempted to shun the following, the fans would not be denied, and over the years the Underground Pimp has grown to appreciate the support more than anything else in the business. Simply put, he knows that without the fans, Tyler Rayne would never have made it as far as he did. With that in mind, Rayne goes out there every night to put on a show. To give back to the people that supported him along the way. He never gives less than one hundred percent, regardless of his placement on the card or caliber of opponent. He sets a goal every single time he’s booked: have the best match on the card. That’s his way of giving back to the fans. Giving them everything he’s got, every time he steps through the ropes. And they, in turn, appreciate his efforts. So, it’s no surprise that Rayne can be a bit of a showman sometimes. While he’s usually pretty focused, he can be found playing to the crowd from time-to-time, particularly if there are any of those smokin’ hotties sitting in the front row. More often than not, he picks his spots well, making sure only to acknowledge the fans when his opponent is far from retaliation. But even the best laid plans go to shit sometimes. His love for the fans even has an influence over the way he wrestles a particular opponent. When wrestling faces, or anyone that the crowd will usually support along with him, he’ll play things very straight and by the books. No cheating. No dirty tactics. He’ll always offer the clean break and even go so far as to acknowledge an opponent’s successful offense with a short clap or polite nod. He doesn’t mind pulling the crowd into the match by getting some support for a well-liked opponent, as he’s ever-confident in his own support from the fans. On the opposite end of that, when wrestling a heel or someone the crowd absolutely cannot stand, there really is no cap on what Tyler won’t do in the ring. He’ll cheat. He’ll scratch and bite and poke eyes and break holds at the count of four. He’ll do everything that heel would do, and he’ll do it with a smile on his face. And the fans love every minute of it. People love to see a cheater get cheated on, and Rayne loves to do for the people. He’ll mock his opponent, slap him around when he’s in a headlock, maybe even give a dude a wet-willy, or the ever dreaded "ball tap." Whatever low-down, dirty, cheap tactic he can think of, he’ll use, as long as he’s using it on a guy who’s proven to have no problem using those types of tactics himself. And the fans will cheer him for every little scam and cheap shot along the way. They’re silly like that. Championships Tyler Rayne does not wear championship belts. Hell, to be honest, he doesn't even much care for championship belts. But as they are a necessary evil to the business, and he is damn good at what he does, it is inevitable that he'll garner a few during any worthwhile stay in a company. Point is, you'll never see Tyler Rayne "wearing" a belt. He won't come out to the ring with it "strapped around his waist." At best, he'll throw it over the shoulder. At worst he'll drag it behind him or forget to carry it to the ring at all. As it is, he likes PRIME, so you'll see him brandishing the belt around more often than not. Most times, again, it'll be hanging over his shoulder. But seriously, you should be happy he's carrying it at all. We've done much worse things to titles over the years. He's Hardcore For the vast majority of his career, especially under the masked guise of MDK, Tyler Rayne was always considered one of those "hardcore" guys. This was, of course, back in the years when hardcore wrestling was all the rage. Rayne spent many of his years wading through various Hardcore divisions, and in the few instances where he did pop up into the upper levels, was still plagued by a constant chain of gimmick matches. And to be perfectly honest, that's how he prefers it. The less rules and more danger, the better for Tyler Rayne. He loves his violence. He loves his mayhem. And he loves the gasp of the crowd when some punk ass cunt drip mother fucker gets busted with a flaming 2x4. Now, this isn't to say that Rayne will try to use weapons and whatnot when they're not allowed. In most all cases, our hero will follow with the rules as laid out before the match. But in any scenario where he is allowed to embrace that "hardcore" spirit...these are the matches in which Tyler Rayne shines best and, if possible, becomes even more dangerous and determined than usual.
