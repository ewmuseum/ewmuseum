Great American Nightmare 2008 ---- Don't Go To Sleep... From a black screen, we blast into heavy metal and heavy action. Rob Zombie's "Great American Nightmare" (wonder where we got this song idea from, right?) rocks your socks as you're treated to a montage of clips from Colossus V, Cataclysm and beyond. Delta Upsilon Iota, The Awakening and Union Jack all meet inside the ring and out, mouthing off to each other and itching for the leg up in an ever-changing tag team division. Troy Douglas and Jason Natas, two up-and-comers on the roster, find bad blood as well until Natas is suspended. The two men jockey for position to show everyone who the next PRIME superstar will be. Call me the American nightmare Call me the American dream Call me your soul corrupted Call me everything you need Team V.I.A.G.R.A. and the Princes of New England meet at Colossus V, then weeks later in the thick of the Princes' home region. The reactions are decidedly different, but the animosity between the teams remain the same. Cozen interjects herself in Dusk's life and career several times over. The Lost Soul pushes back against her, as well as her allies in the Dead Man's Hand. The dispute grows personal - far more personal than could ever be shown on camera. Cozen is fired after turning on PRIME at Cataclysm, but Dusk pleads for her to be allowed back for one final match. Call me the dark intruder Call me the haunted sea Call me your Monster Zero Call me anything you need A chance encounter turns Bryan Dawkins and Rhett Locke into bitter enemies. The dispute escalates quickly, and soon tumbles into their off-camera lives. When things get too intense, personal tragedy drives Dawkins to end animosities with an emotional and moving climax. The end pushes Dawkins towards the menacing Roulette match, while Kaiser Vashaun, the Intense Champion and only PRIME representative to win at Cataclysm, steps in to lay the belt down opposite Locke. Killean Sirrajin, Devin Shakur and Tony Gamble quickly step into spots in the match via their various interactions with Rayne. Bryan Dawkins is announced as an entrant to the surprise of many. Lindsay Troy is given a spot, but at Cataclysm, she's injured by Xavier Kannon and unable to compete. It’s a brutal challenge that requires nothing less than a hundred percent focus. If you lose track of any of your six competitors, you’re liable to join the unhappy ranks of the eliminated. And you don’t win. No, no, no. You don’t win. You survive. There’s no time to pal around or get too hung up on strategy or waste time on the moral high ground. Hyperbole aside, it is truly every man for himself (with apologies to Lindsay Troy, the only person on the planet who’s come out of a Roulette match with their win-loss record unscathed). So it makes sense that Chandler Tsonda is alone, stalking the hallways of the Verizon Center, Universal Title slung over his shoulder (perhaps in its last hours atop that particular perch?). Dawkins stays back and roots all of them on. Tyler tries to pull him up for another move, but Killean steps in, pushing Rayne aside and lifting Gamble into a gorilla press. He drops the Grin quickly and lets him bounce off the canvas, then turns…and gets shoved by Rayne. The underground Pimp doesn’t take too kindly to being one-upped, obviously, and the lingering animosity he’s had with Sirrajin begins to boil over. He kicks Gamble in the head and shoves Killean back. Not to be outdone, Killean stomps Gamble, then delivers a forearm shiver to Rayne. Tyler stumbles back, then charges forward, soccer-kicking Gamble in the head and then hitting Killean with a forearm of his own. Chandler steps back from the action and pulls Dawkins back as well. The two retreat to a corner and fake like sports commentators as they watch Tyler duke it out with Killean over the right to pin Tony Gamble. Killean picks up The Grin and tosses him at Rayne, who catches him, shifts to a standing Small Package position and drops back to drive him into the mat with von Doom’d! Not making the cover – thanks to the Hall-of-Famer looming over him – he stands up and drags Gamble along. He picks The Grin up himself and heaves him at Sirrajin. Killean catches, shifts him into position and drops him with the T-Bone, his modified suplex brainbuster! Twisting as he stands, he leaves Gamble in a heap in front of him. He doesn’t see Tyler Rayne hit the ropes, but he definitely feels Rayne’s knees hit his chest flush. The impact propels him backward, where only the bottom rope stops his momentum. Rayne bounces off of the knee-smash into a backflip, essentially a standing moonsault onto the downed Grin. The pinfall is academic at that point. Eliminated: Tony Gamble Gamble is rolled out and escorted to the exit door. A roar rises up from Killean Sirrajin as he rushes out from the ropes he’d tumbled into and delivers a high knee to the chin of Tyler Rayne. The forceful blow gets a reaction out of Chandler Tsonda, who rushes Sirrajin in defense of his friend. The high dropkick from Tsonda sends Killean tumbling back, and Rayne pops up. The numbers game continues as the two men double up on the Supreme Machine, who fights them off every step of the way. Meanwhile, Bryan Dawkins remains at a distance, still not mentally at the level he was before…you know. Still, he knows the stakes in the match, and he’s excited for his two somewhat-allies. Though he can’t quite fight with the fire he had just weeks earlier, he’s still caught up in what it means to be a PRIMEate. So enthralled was the Flyin’ Hawaiian at the action that he – like everyone else – didn’t hear the buzzer sound. And with the crowd already cheering for Tsonda’s actions, there was no opportunity for them to warn him that Devin Shakur had re-entered the ring. He didn’t even have the chance to turn before the Good Times, Painful Memories roundhouse kick connects with the back of his head. Without the opportunity to defend himself, the blow connects as hard as it could in a very soft spot. He crumples to the mat, and Shakur is already making the cover when Rayne and Tsonda notice what’s happening. Eliminated: Bryan Dawkins No sooner had the count been registered than Rayne descended up on Shakur, brutalizing him like his name was Xavier Kannon or Mayhem. Tsonda was on his way to turn in his own receipts, but Killean Sirrajin was there to stop him. Killean had been the passionate, fiery young buck before. He knew how easy it was to get carried away by emotions. It’s why he’d learned to take his life away from the cameras and the spotlights. His return over the summer had opened up new doors in his career, and the veteran presence people’d held him accountable for in the past was finally clicking. Now, with a comprehensive knowledge of his own mistakes while coming up in the game, he could pick off the youngsters when they repeated his follies. When Chandler Tsonda made the mistake of caring for another guy in the match, Killean was there to make the most of it. Shakur tries to get to his feet amidst the onslaught from Rayne, but doesn’t make it. As he puts the weight on one leg to lift the other, Rayne steps down hard on the inside of the shin, buckling him at the ankle. Devin instantly yelps – yes, yelps – as he tumbles back down to the mat. Instantly, he rolls out of the ring to the mats below. He doesn’t stop and check on the damage – he knows its bad. A striker on a bad leg is in bad shape for the ass-kicking contest to come. Reluctantly, he has to resort back to the escape door and the safety of the No Man’s Land area outside. Unfortunately he was still about 15 feet from the aisle, perpendicular and seemingly up Shit Creek. Rayne closes in on him and grabs the injured leg, but Shakur snaps to action like a rattlesnake, popping his hips up and swinging his good leg into a sharp up-kick that sends Tyler stumbling into the ring steps nearby. It didn’t swing momentum, but it bought him time. There was still the matter of escaping. As if on cue, the buzzer sounds and the “optional” exit slides open. Tsonda and Sirrajin are occupied, with the latter bodyslamming the former and lining up for a big splash. Rayne is shaking the cobwebs out against the steps and planning just how Shakur is going to pay for all this. Just like before, there’s only one man interested in using the door. Unable to just stand and walk out, Devin has to rely on the resourceful opportunism that earned him the Universal Title (and indirectly, a spot in this match) in the first place. He grabs onto the cage and lets it drag him along the safety mats outside the ring, taking him away from Rayne and closer to safety. One he’s lined up with the aisle, he releases the cage and lays there, praying for the door to come around before the Underground Pimp does. His wish is granted, in part – as Rayne stands and begins to approach, the door is just a few seconds ahead of him. It reaches the aisle and Devin stands up just enough to heave himself through, ignoring the taunts of ringside fans who start doing the “chi chi chi chi ha ha ha ha” Friday The 13th sound. Inside the ring, Killean pulls Tsonda up into a facelock. He sees Rayne and Shakur otherwise occupied and opts to take his time – the champ’s breakneck pace wouldn’t do him any favors, and he would be much better off if he slowed things down a bit. Devin crawls free of the door, dragging his body a couple extra feet. He looks back in time to see the door close and breathes a sigh of relief. In the moment, he was too busy trying to escape his tormentor that he didn’t actually pause to see Rayne locked on the other side of the cage. If he had, he would have been disappointed…among other adjectives. That’s because the Underground Pimp wasn’t stuck on the inside looking out. He was just a few paces beyond Shakur’s peripheral vision, reaching into the crowd for a folding chair. As Devin sprawls out to enjoy his few moments of respite, Rayne retrieves a weapon and holds it high to the delight of the fans. If the roar didn’t catch the Man in Black’s attention, the sound of metal smacking on the concrete aisle floor did. He jerks up to a seated position and almost immediately begins pulling his body back, dragging away from the 5-Star Champion like he was escaping a horror film character. A massive vertical suplex from the Hall of Famer leaves Tsonda laid out and clutching his back in the middle of the ring. He has little time for recovery before Killean pulls him back up and slings him into the ropes, catching him with a Mountain Bomb and immediately rolling over for the cover. It gets a two-count, but Tsonda kicks out with authority and Sirrajin is quick to move. He sees his opportunity with the other two competitors locked outside. Pulling Chandler up again, he executes another Irish whip and sends the champion across the ring. He makes a quick throat-cutting motion, signaling for his Supreme Justice finisher, then takes off in the opposite direction, getting momentum off the ropes for his clothesline. It never comes, because Chandler takes two bounding steps to get himself up to the top rope, then leaps backwards into a corkscrew body press! The Perfect 10 sends both men down to the canvas, splayed out, and attention turns back to the outside. Rayne smirks and his nostrils flare as he stalks his pray, shaking the chair just slightly in his hands to keep the juices flowing. The last hope for Shakur rests in Elvis Nixon. The plucky official steps in, patiently reminding Rayne that outside the cage was a no-man’s land, an attack-free zone where he and his ally-turned-nemesis would just have to coexist peacefully until they could reenter the battle. Tyler looks at Elvis with wild eyes, then raises the chair in his hand ever-so-slightly. He patiently reminds the referee who the fuck he is and what the fuck he can do, a response that works on more levels than Elvis has time to process in the moment. The problem was entangling at even the slightest touch. Disqualify Rayne and he overrules it. Call the match a no-contest and he restarts it. Order security down to help control the situation and he waves them off. The power Rayne had granted himself by virtue of his new contract was not often flexed, but it could be overwhelming when called upon. With Blaine, Lisa and the Board of Directors all watching this show from Chicago or other various corners of the globe, there was only one man in the building who could even match the Underground Pimp by letter of the law. And truthfully, Devin Shakur just wasn’t worth the trouble for C.P. Cantrell. Without the executive producer’s cockblock, Tyler Rayne could get away with murder – or something close to it. He raises the chair over his head and brings it down on the leg of the fleeing Shakur. The former champion howls, but can offer little else in the way of defense in his current state. Rayne sneers and raises the chair again. In the ring, Sirrajin slings Tsonda into the corner and drives a shoulder into his midsection. Before the Model Citizen can take a breath and replace the air escaping his lungs, a second shot flattens him further. He slumps against the buckle and Sirrajin backs off, preparing to ramp it up for his third shot. He retreats to the center of the ring, his back to the champion. Gathering his momentum, he turns and charges with a full head of steam, looking to crush Chandler against the buckles. At the last possible second, Tsonda slips out between the ropes, a la Shawn Michaels (whoever that is – blinders, everyone!). The Supreme Machine slams into the corner himself and stumbles back, dazed and confused (but capable of producing clean drug tests no matter what you say). On the apron, Tsonda vaults to the top rope and slings himself forward, flipping into a Blockbuster neckbreaker that has become a trademark of his during this recent run of success. Twenty assorted chairshots later, Devin Shakur is a broken heap of a man. Left for the wolves wolf, he had no chance on one wheel, and was forced to pay for his sins against PRIME and Tyler Rayne – mostly the latter. Never one to pass up an opportunity to add injury to injury, the Underground Pimp pulls the Commie Emo out to the center of the aisle. He places Shakur’s ankle in the chair in a very familiar position, and cheers and “ooh”s rise from the crowd. Clearing a section of the security barricade by waving off some fans, Rayne climbs up with nothing but bad intentions in mind. After a few seconds to milk the gravity of the situation, he lets gravity take its course, flipping backwards off the barricade and into an M. Bison double stomp onto the chair. Don’t even pretend like you heard Shakur’s ankle snap, ‘cause there ain’t no way nobody heard nothin’ over all those D.C. fans roaring in approval. Chandler Tsonda crawls to his feet with the help of the ropes. He didn’t see what just happened outside, but he saw enough to realize that it was happening OUTSIDE, and he needed to act quickly. Walking himself along those same ropes, he slowly climbs the turnbuckles, occasionally casting an eye back on the laid-out form of Killean Sirrajin. Tyler Rayne walks away from the screaming and mangled Shakur with the nonchalance of a serial killer. Officials swarm the former champion as medical care is summoned. Rayne walks to the cage and watches as the locked door begins to rotate toward him. He could probably order it open, storm the cage and continue his momentum. He could probably get a decent shot at winning the Universal Title in the match he didn’t intend to enter. He could probably bring the belt back to Brooks, or Troy, or whoever, and wrestle with the guilt, the Rayne Man’s Burden. But as the door passes by, he makes the clear distinction between using his authority to be a dick, and using it to make a dick move. A man’s got to have a code. At the top of the buckles, Tsonda stands tall. Normally he doesn’t think or pause or breathe, he just leaps into the Model Citizen and lets the magic happen. For whatever reason, though, he lets his eyes wander, and he sees Tyler Rayne standing at the foot of the cage, outside its walls, watching him. The frienemies-who-are-really-just-friends-and-too-manly-to-admit-it lock eyes and stare for the briefest of seconds. Then Tyler breaks his gaze to nod at something slightly behind the Model Citizen. Chandler looks immediately to see Killean Sirrajin standing to his full height. Calling an audible, the Viet Viper leaps from the turnbuckle into less of a moonsault and more of a roundoff. He grabs Killean’s head as he tumbles to the mat and spikes it into the canvas, completing a Super Tornado DDT that plants the Supreme Machine. He’s already to the adjacent turnbuckle and airborne before the fans had even stopped cheering, so there’s a momentary silence when all 20,000+ stop to catch their breath before screaming all over again. Nick: MODEL CITIZEN! ONE! TWO! THREE! Eliminated: Killean Sirrajin WINNER: CHANDLER TSONDA Coheed & Cambria pick up immediately under the swirling din of thousands of screaming fans. Chandler rolls off the cover and sits up, chest heaving, eyes still glazed a little from the heat of battle. He looks back at Rayne, whose tip had probably saved his title. The 5-Star Champion keeps his eyes locked on the man he affectionately calls “Tink”. Then, tilting his head back to raise his chin in an “I gotchu, dawg” acknowledgement, Rayne turns away from the cage and walks up the aisle. He sidesteps the crowd of trainers loading Devin Shakur onto a gurney and continues off into the sunset, ignoring the cheers of the crowd, letting them all focus on the winner…the champion. In the ring, Bernie Roberts hands Tsonda the Universal Title before checking on the stunned Sirrajin. The Model Citizen stands up and assesses his situation. He watches Rayne walk away, breathes deeply, then climbs the ropes again, holding his belt high for all the cheering fans to pay tribute. The first big test of his championship reign had been passed. But as Chandler Tsonda was quickly finding out, the satisfaction of being the champion was fleeting. The trouble with being at the top of the mountain is that you always had a view of what was on the horizon.
