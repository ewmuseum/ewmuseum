Brandon Youngblood Biography Nicknames The Only Diamond In The Sport, The Diamond In The Crown, The Diamond Hometown Winnipeg, Manitoba, Canada Birthday January 5th, 1979 Height 6'3 Weight 240 Fed Titles 5-Star Champion x3 [7/24/2005-Stripped] [4/12/2007-5/6/2007] [11/4/2007-Stripped] Fed Awards 2005 PRIME Turmoil Match Winner, 2009 Jewel In The Crown Champion Biography Youngblood has been on the major stage of professional wrestling since 2003, and over that time has established himself as one of the best wrestlers in the world over this time. Before starting as a professional wrestler, Youngblood was a multiple time Texas State Champion in amateur wrestling. He would move on to the University of Iowa, where he had mixed results in his two years on the team. He left in 1999, right after his sophomore year. He worked briefly in Action! Wrestling, debuting as a blue chip wrestling prospect. While his training was difficult, he became rather lax in the ring after signing a developmental contract worth over a million dollars in 2000. This was a product of Action! Owner Reed Young frivolously throwing money around, hoping to ensure he would have great young talent to funnel into whichever of his fleeting companies. When there was a mass exodus of talent from Action!, Youngblood would move onto to the Squared Circle, where he made his first real impact. At the time, Havard Avalon wanted tSC to resemble to roots of true professional wrestling; hard hitting and flashy action which could excite the fans. Jaded at the time, Youngblood was protective of opponents, doing his best to showcase a knowledge of professional wrestling without severely hurting his opponent. As time progressed, he went from being a cocky young kid who wore robes to a more aggressive performer who wore a singlet. Youngblood continued growing as a wrestler, having a breakout year in 2005. Becoming a critical success in 2005, he went from a solid in ring performer to perhaps the best in all of professional wrestling. Shaving his hair off, Brandon evolved into an in-your-face technical wrestler who used suplexes, grinding holds, submissions, and striking to break his opponents down. It was the year he won his first major promotional titles (the Jolt Triple Crown Championship), following it up later in the year by winning the first of his three PRIME 5-Star Championships. He had some of PRIME’s classic matches during this period, engaging in brutal wars with Karina Wolfenden, Nova, and Angelo Deville. This first PRIME run ended in a Universal Title match against Hoyt Williams in the main event of the 2005 King of Kings pay-per-view. In what could only be termed as a blood feud, Youngblood lost a hard fought contest with Williams after he was hit in the head with a chair by Duke Williams. Days later he was terminated by the company for problems related to drug abuse, as well as his conduct with the promotion. Following this devastating blow, he moved from PRIME to a few other organizations before returning to PTC. As a member of the GCW roster, Youngblood came in with high praise from Steven Caldera. While in GCW, Youngblood held a feud with the Dies Irae stable, being the first real non-Dies Irae member in the company to defeat megastar Lia Ambrosi as well as convincingly defeating Vivica J. Valentine twice during her record breaking United States Championship reign. During this time, PTC Global Champion Seymour Almasy and Youngblood engaged in a bitter conflict which would see one of PTCs benchmarks convincingly dismantled. Winning the United States Championship from Vivica, he would go on to finish second in the 2006 Dangerous Games to Lia. Following this, Youngblood defended the US Title against Jacob McKail and ended up ending his GCW career. He would move on, losing the United States Championship, but was rewarded with a title shot against the new GCW World Champion. Ambrosi/Youngblood II never took place, as Brandon demanded his release from the company as a result of creative differences with Caldera. Shortly thereafter, Youngblood surprised wrestling fans around the world by being the initial entrant in the 2007 Dual Halo match, where he finished joint eleventh with the Trashcan Man. Youngblood would make his bones, but Killean Sirrajin was there to put a stop to the Pariah’s diatribe. After winning a match on Revolution 123 against former AWC Transatlantic Champions Jack Murphy and Pierce Lavelle, Youngblood moved on and defeated Sun Tzu to claim his second 5-Star Championship. This reign would be short lived, as Killean Sirrajin beat Youngblood in a cage match, making him pass out to The Last Call. Youngblood would lose to Jonathan Winters, but then would follow it up by retiring Jumpin’ Jack Flash. After this, Youngblood would go on the injured list as well, electing to take preemptive surgery for bone spurs in his back. He would return weeks later, almost defeating Danny Ferguson. After defeating Tom Walczak and avenging an earlier loss to Ferguson with tag team partner Devin Shakur, Youngblood went after newly crowned 5-Star Champion Jonathan Winters. Winning their first match by count out, Youngblood would take Winters on at the 2007 Great American Nightmare in a two out of three falls match for the 5-Star Championship, winning the contest 2-1 to earn his record breaking third reign with the championship. Youngblood entered the 2007 Jewel In The Crown tournament, defeating Dusk in the first round. In the second round, he would take on proclaimed ‘PRIME Killer’ Digital Mortality and would defeat the highly touted combatant with a spinebuster. In the semi-finals at King of Kings, he faced off against Chandler Tsonda and Hoyt Williams, losing the contest after he was unable to make the save for Hoyt. Tsonda would go on to be crowned the 2007 Jewel In The Crown. Following this short span of time, Youngblood would battle the Codemaster to a draw, and would be stripped of the 5-Star Championship at Revolution 150 as a result of being unable to defend the title due to injury. Youngblood would make his last appearance in PRIME at the 2008 Dual Halo, being eliminated unceremoniously by Easton Hall. Youngblood would work for Seattle based Blood Alliance Championship Wrestling, working a rather infrequent schedule until he withdrew from the company due to legal issues. At the time, it was the consensus of the wrestling world that Brandon Youngblood went from up and comer to faded memory thanks to legal issues and drug addiction. But perhaps most crippling of all was Youngblood’s lack of passion for professional wrestling. He returns to PRIME in September of 2009 without a clear direction, though militant in making the impact in PRIME he never did. Wrestling Other Fed Titles Jolt Triple Crown Champion, ACW Scorpion Fighting Champion, GCW United States Champion, PTC Elite Champion Other Fed Awards 2005 The Mark Awards Wrestler of the Year, Ranked #4 in the PTC top 10 for July 2005, Ranked #1 in the PTC top 10 for August 2005, Ranked #1 in the PTC Wrestler Rankings for January 2009, Ranked #3 in the PTC Wrestler Rankings for February 2009 Wrestling Style Technical Finisher Move Half Nelson Suplex, Spinebuster Special Moves See Matchwriting Aids Other Moves Style Description Ever since making his major league debut in Action! in November of 2003, Brandon Youngblood has been known as one of the best technical wrestlers on the planet. While he started as more of a wrestling showman, his style has evolved into a very shoot influenced, in your face style. Where once he was overly protective of those he was in the ring with, now he could care less. Brandon takes great pride in his ability to deliver the goods inside the wrestling ring, as without this talent, he probably wouldn’t be anywhere near a professional wrestling ring. Youngblood falsely proclaimed himself to be a National Champion wrestler from the University of Iowa in 1999. Even so, he was still a highly sought amateur recruit from the state of Texas, eventually moving on to a collegiate career. He left after two years to begin his wrestling training, and learned in some of the toughest places in the world. He also has incorporated elements of shoot fighting into his arsenal. While he hasn’t necessarily wrestled with any consistency since 2007, he has been involved in some rather nefarious vale tudo fighting tournaments in South America in an effort to reclaim his manhood. While he isn’t a striker like Devin Shakur, he is perhaps the most legitimate specialist for ground and pound fighting PRIME has ever offered. He also is extremely skilled with submission wrestling. While there was a time Brandon would work more professional wrestling submissions into his arsenal, now he remains steadfast in his application of simple submissions which have legitimate roots. Youngblood's goal in matches is to break his opponent. Whether it be to break them physically or to break them mentally, Brandon is aggressively seeking ways to gain victory. Extremely strong and deceptively quick, Youngblood puts a premium as to make every single move count towards the goal of beating his opponent. Even a simple rest hold can sap an opponent of his stamina, as Youngblood is relentless on the attack. His style is very 'in your face'. Being a former amateur wrestler, he is extremely comfortable in close quarters and with his opponent in a tie up. In his younger days, Brandon was a somewhat lax performer, letting the art shine through. At twenty eight, Brandon has developed a knack for being one of the most brutal hitters in the game, with an almost endless supply of stamina off of which to work. Keeping in close and in control of his opponent, Brandon tries his best to get inside the head of his opponent and get them to break their concentration. In throwing something wild, or letting their emotions get the better of them, he believes he can capitalize on a mistake his opponent could potentially make. At the same time, Youngblood is just as prone to lapses in judgment. His uncompromising attitude can work to the benefit of his opponent. Youngblood is notorious for being easy to bust open. He can be coaxed into situations which might be advantageous to his opponent simply to get a crack at his opponent. And currently, Brandon’s in ring timing is off. His precision is not as sharp thanks to the layoff. He hopes his aggressiveness and his brutality will help carrying him early on. Mannerisms and Tone Part of ring psychology is the approach one has. While some heels are cowardly ducking challenges or trying to get away with master plans to one up their rival, Youngblood is different in that he has a reckless abandon for himself. He does not care if he hurts himself. Part of this comes from Texas wrestling roots, where men let their fists do their talking. Another part of it is that Brandon seeks to prove his legitimacy as a man, given the fact that it is now known he had relations with other men over the course of his life. Brandon is not afraid. He is a bully, after all, and has the arrogance to believe he can break anyone who steps in front of him. Youngblood wrestles methodically. Every motion is deliberate, and his actions are systematic. His amateur wrestling and vale tudo backgrounds have afforded him the liberty of working tight on people. Brandon wants to grind his opponents down to the point of breaking them, be it physically or mentally. He will try to isolate an opponent’s offense and cause them to expend energy and valuable oxygen. Even rest holds have a pulverizing quality. Whereas once, Brandon would taunt towards the crowd and jaw jack with fans, he is now focused solely on his opponent. He gives the crowd nothing but empty stares, and his expressions are stoic. He doesn’t taunt the fans at all now. Before a match begins, Youngblood may get face to face with an opponent. If an opponent is within his height range, he may extend his head towards his opponent, giving them the opportunity to the same and get face to face for a stare down. The only taunt he would do during the course of a match involves an opponent who is badly beaten right before the finish of the match. Spreading his legs wide, he will stand a few paces away from his opponent waiting for them to get up. As they struggle to their feet, he will punch the canvas continually, causing a loud thud to reverberate through the ring. His back will be arched upward, ready to explode through his opponent once they stand and stumble towards him for a spinebuster. Whether it is with both hands, or one hand after the other, is at the discretion of the match writer. Appearance and Attire I am putting this here because it is more important to give a match writer a physical representation of what the wrestler looks like. Brandon Youngblood is a Caucasian male who stands six feet three inches tall and weighs 240 pounds. While he isn’t shredded, he still appears physically imposing. I envision the man looking something body type wise like Wanderlei Silva. Youngblood’s trademark look is his shaved head. He has green eyes, and while he isn’t a heart throb, he features rugged good looks. Most of that has to do with plastic surgery; he has had scar tissue removed from his head, has had his nose straightened and narrowed, and has had his jawline shaved down. Brandon at one time was a pretty boy wrestler, and while some of that has eroded, he isn’t wearing the sins of wrestling all across his face. The other prominent feature of Brandon Youngblood is his tattoos. Brandon once only had a large scale tribal design around his right arm which essentially covered his entire upper arm and shoulder. He also had a cross tattooed to the bicep of his left arm. A picture of this is kinda shown here. Now, Youngblood has both his arms covered in sleeves. While there are plenty of intricately designed and meaningful tattoos now adorning his arms, the only one of color is the one on his left forearm; a Texas Bluebonnet with the name Cody written in cursive across a ribbon right underneath the plume clutched in the beak of a ghastly looking California Quail skeleton. In the future, I will write the placements of tattoos and also give the meanings, but I do not want to spoil roleplay material right here. Attire-wise, Brandon wears a glossy white singlet with black trim. There is a crimson block Y across Youngblood’s chest with a skull at the point where the Y joins. The skull looks like this. He wears black tape around both of his wrists, black knee braces on his knees, and a pair of black wrestling boots with black laces. Below is a breakdown of his moveset. Given his amount of knowledge, this is not all encompassing, and if you are a match writer are inspired to do something, by all means, do it. Or you can ask me. Key *=offensive staple **=trademark move ^=rare move, reserved for special situations ^^=kill move, inescapable/unkickoutable without rope breaks Strikes: Stiff knife-edge chops*, hammerfist chop**, hammerfists, clothesline, short arm clothesline, close quarters elbows*, brutal knee strikes*, muay thai clinch knees, elbows to the back, chop block, boots to a downed opponent, stiff kicks (especially to the back or face or whatever), knee drop, yakuza kick**, northern lariat, european uppercut*, back elbow, crossfaces, elbow drop, front chancery to the canvas with stiff knees**, headbutt, dirty boxing headbutt**, stomps to the head, dropkick As far as striking goes, Brandon is renowned for his knife edge chops. He throws them extremely hard, and has broken open the chest of many opponents’ with them. Bruising during a match to the chest is a very common occurrence. It's one of his bread and butter moves, and the one he will usually look to if there is distance between he and his opponent. If this is not enough, Youngblood can close his fist and throw a closed-fist hammerfist chop with the same lethal velocity. With the headbutts, Brandon will try to hit them from up close, usually with his hands clasped together behind his opponent's head so he can pull them forward. He will try and hit the person in the head with the crown of his skull, but more so from the left side. Youngblood tries to use his head as a weapon, especially against opponents who are larger than he is. The close-quarters elbows and european uppercut are more transitional offense, but a well placed elbow can down an opponent. Off a takedown on the canvas, Youngblood sometimes will try and elbow his opponent in the face or head, trying to transition to dominant positions to ground and pound his opponent. Since PRIME does not have rules dictating striking beyond closed fists, Brandon is very adapt at using his very powerful legs to knee his opponent. The most dangerous kneeing positions Youngblood can catch an opponent in are the muay thai clinch and the front chancery. While his muay thai skills are still very rudimentary, it is still difficult for people without a background in mixed martial arts to deal with it. The front chancery knees are infinitely more deadly; Brandon's amateur wrestling skill works for him, allowing him to stabilize his opponent while battering their head with knees from a slight elevation off the canvas. Brandon's kicking ability is not exactly anything to right home about unless he is dealing with a prone opponent. He has a crisp dropkick and drop yakuza kick, but other than that, he is more based on his ability to use space, velocity, brutality, and strength to his advantage. The goal is simply to nail the opponent as many ways as he can to erode their strength and will. Knocking someone loopy from a suplex makes it a lot easier to go for a submission. He knows a lot, but tries to stay in the realm of hurting the back, neck, and head of his opponent. His suplexes do not use a lot of crazy preparation, and are very straight forward. Holds/Submissions: Body scissors with rear naked choke, grinding side headlock*, reverse gut wrench, hammerlock*, armbar, americana, hammerlock w/ body scissors, grinding rear chin lock, wristlock, top wristlock, key lock**, guillotine choke, anaconda vice, kimura lock, omoplata^, rear naked choke, neck crank, north-south choke, knee bar^ As time has gone by, he has evolved from a guy with a passing notion of holding his opponent down just to get his breath back to a full out technically solid submission artist. He tries to catch an opponent with submissions much like an MMA fighter, and if he is able to clamp down the opponent needs to figure out a way to fight out of the submission attempt because he can fully utilize it. Other moves involved are staple close-quarters holds he utilizes. Takedowns: Running STO, STO, snapmare takedown, arm drag takedown, fireman's carry, waistlock takedown followed by a face lock on the mat, drop toe hold, go-behind takedown, side arm takedown, side headlock takedown, single leg takedown, single leg tackle takedown, double leg tackle takedown, headlock takeover, single leg trip, double leg trip, arm wringer takedown, ippon seoinage Being an amateur wrestler, as well as a submission wrestler, Youngblood is very comfortable with his opponent on the mat. He's got the capability to chain wrestle, and being able to bring his opponent down to work a submission, ground and pound, or simply try to gas his opponent makes him dangerous. It used to be that you knew Brandon would go for takedowns and you could easily defense that because of his lack of good striking. He now implements head fakes and fake outs, getting his opponent to think he is going for a takedown, then simply blasting his opponent with some form of strike. Breakers: Canadian back breaker rack, inverted face lock suplex back breaker drop, pendulum back breaker, pump handle back backbreaker, tilt-a-whirl back breaker, underhook back breaker drop, stomach breaker, swinging neck breaker*, shoulder breaker*, belly to back hammerlock suplex**, arm breaker, jawbreaker*, front suplex gut buster**, leaping over-the-ropes arm breaker, fireman's carry over the knee neck breaker, leg hook belly to back backbreaker^, muscle buster^, cradle neck breaker Breakers used to act as a flashy and devastating maneuver, but take a long time to set up. Youngblood sticks to the more simplistic breakers as a way to do damage without giving his opponent a chance to fight their way out of his move, thus wasting his energy in going for said move in the first place. Spinebuster For some, the spinebuster is nothing more than an impact move. Youngblood has made it into a lethal finisher. While an opponent can still kick out from it, Brandon has taken his time to perfect it. Youngblood catches an opponent around the waist, lifting them upward. What is different is that after lifting an opponent up, Brandon will use a hand to grab onto his opponent's collar bone. Throwing downward with his arm, he causes much of the impact to be absorbed by the back of the head and the neck. The idea is to drive the opponent into the canvas and have something which will realistically result in a pinfall. This is currently his primary finisher. 2.) The Gridlock (Katahajime) What once was Youngblood's main finisher. This has been changed as a result of rules against choking. Used in some forms of MMA and best known for being the submission of choice for Taz in ECW. Youngblood began use of this in the Squared Circle, and the impact was felt almost immediately. With Youngblood's wrestling style, everything he does is meant to build up to when he locks this hold on. It's put down many a man and woman in the sport of professional wrestling. Even more scary than the damage, however, is the inescapable nature of it. Only one man has ever broken clean of it (Dave Gibson), and he needed to use the ring posts to do it. Nobody has ever broken the hold clean by their own power (or at least without resorting to cheap tactics). It's either you get to the ropes, or you tap out. This submission realistically could still occur in a match, but he would have to let go by the five count. Youngblood is more apt to use the Gridlock if he is in a match where choking is legal. 3.) Gridlock Suplex (Katahajime Suplex) A nasty combo that surfaced in GCW. Youngblood locks the katahajimi from a stand, and then suplexes his opponent onto the top of their head. While the Gridlock is literally phased out of regular wrestling matches, this move can still be applied. This is one of those murderdeathkill finishing moves, so PLEASE do not have dudes or dudettes kicking out of this unless you contact me first.
