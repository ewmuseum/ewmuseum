Ol Jackson Rust -- probably you might say he doesn’t have long left in the business and you’d be right as rain. So take a chance, man. It isn’t as though he has a whole lot to lose these days, anyway. The man as a punk twenty-something could have done anything in the ring he pleased but no longer is that the case. The edge is gone. To replace it? Shit. Maximize results, even at risk to himself. All he has to rely on now is heart and where that might bring him who knows. Go ahead and do what you will with him. He was sound enough at one point to have done it, but to help you along -- Throughout the match entire: he runs a fairly fastpaced offense -- knife-edge chops, elbows, forearms, basic brawler-kicks of all sorts, quick sneaky rollup pins, basic suplexes (front, back, German (this latter to regain momentum)) and slams; he likes the corners and bandying off the ropes to, as mentioned, maximize the effect on some of these standard attacks. In the early stages: a long time ago Rust was a breaker-guru, so go nuts there -- if you want him to win by submission, the arm-and-shoulder is the place to break down; quick exchanges here, a lot of slick takedowns and a bit of brawling would do nicely. Armdrags and –holds. Whatever works. The controlling stage: everything amplified -- look to the top rope for action; DDTs of any sort, running pickup spinebusters (into the corner, if you’d like it); big suplexes, big slams; take it outside the ring for some brawling, et cetera. Nearing the end: keep on with his focus on the arm-and-shoulder region and drag on that a while; bigger suplexes and slams yet (he likes the fisherman’s suplex and the northern lights) -- looking for quick incapacitation, superkicks, roaring elbows; again, look to the skies for some results. Bear in mind if he messes up he messes up. Dude’s got some pain threshold, the former World champion of an organization that was little better than the hardcore circuit. Punishment is A-OK. The finishing moves: Abattoir Blues: a kimura lock, usually following a stiff shoulderbreaker -- escapable but respect its status as a finisher. Carrion Suite: a quick-falling vertical brainbuster -- up and down fully at a 90-degree angle in a split-second. The coup de grace: Levee Camp Moan: a top-rope guillotine legdrop -- his original finisher, used only in crucial, pivotal matches. There should be no kickout. Entrance Music Death Letter by the White Stripes.
