C.P. Cantrell Biography Staffer Name C.P. Cantrell Nicknames Chip, Ceeps, and a ton of other obnoxious ones given to him by his employees (mostly, Lindsay Troy) Hometown Wilmette, IL Birthday 12th February, 1983 Height 6'1 Weight 175 lbs Biography Chadwick Peter "C.P." Cantrell made his name in the television industry during the reality TV boom at the turn of millenia. Working his way up from PA to creative consultant, he became one of the most respected minds in the budding reality industry. He produced projects that turned into ratings powerhouses: Survival of the Fittest: Sixteen strangers are placed on a remote island with no food, no clean water and no shelter, and forced to live there for 60 days while also competing in a variety of athletic and aerobic events. Every two weeks, the two contestants who lost the least amount of weight were voted off the island. The show continued for five seasons, the final of which happened outside of C.P.'s watch, as he had moved to another network/project. During that final season - a hackneyed "celebrity" edition - the proceedings were marred by several unexplained rule changes, a shakeup that ultimately led to an incident only referred to as "The Don Vito Mutiny." The show was not renewed. Operation: Dancefloor: A touring van full of world-renowned (or at least world-traveled) competitive ballroom dancing instructors go on a coast-to-coast tour of the United States, stopping at small underprivileged rural communities along the way. At each stop, they set up a boot camp for the locals, preparing them to put on a dancing exhibition in front of the rest of the impoverished town. The project was intended to improve morale and economic situations in each stop. However, it faced some controversy in its third season when it was revealed that second-season destination Rexburg, Idaho had since fallen on even harder times as families struggled with the added costs of dance instruction and giant frilly dresses. C.P. is a shrewd businessman with a proven track record for improving ratings. Unfortunately, his savvy for television doesn't translate as well to his interactions with co-workers. The tenuous personal relationships at each stop on his whirlwind rise in the industry turned him into a sort of forced mercenary. He comes in, fixes your show, and moves on before there's any risk of digging in. Job Executive Producer Salary $775,000
