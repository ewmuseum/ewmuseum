Troy Douglas Biography Wrestler Name Troy Douglas Nicknames Megatron Hometown Greensboro, North Carolina Birthday June 7, 1975 Height 6’5 Weight 248 lbs Fed Titles PRIME Intense Champion (current) Fed Awards 8th Place, 2008 Dual Halo Match Biography Troy lost his mother due to complications following childbirth and was raised by his father, Jack, a history teacher and basketball coach. An excellent athlete through high school, Troy was All-State in both football and basketball, and earned a scholarship to Syracuse University, where he played four years of football and earned second-team All Big East his senior year. A fourth round draft pick of the Philadelphia Eagles in 1998, Troy had a moderately successful rookie season before tragedy struck. During the last game of his first year, he suffered brutal knee and spinal injuries after getting caught on the infamous Vet Stadium AstroTurf. His football career was over before he could truly get it off the ground. After kicking around the Delaware Valley for several months, rehabbing furiously even though he knew he'd never put on the helmet and pads again, Troy stumbled upon a wrestling career after a meeting with Jason Reed, a former star in America and Japan who had opened up a Philly-based training center. After months of training, Troy had finally gotten back into competitive shape and signed a short-term contract with EFW, a Philadelphia-area indy fed. He wrestled in indies across the country for two years before signing with GXW, but more injuries, this time a torn labrum in his right shoulder, prevented his career from gaining momentum. However, he returned, until in February 2003, while touring in Scotland, he received the worst phone call of his life. His fiancee, Lauren Bennett, and his father, Jack, both staying at Troy's home in Greensboro, were shot and killed attempting to stop the home from being robbed in the middle of the night. It crushed Troy, who nearly committed suicide that night. He took time off to grieve, but returned once again, capturing the Xtreme Title for a second time before GXW closed it's doors and Troy started the next phase of his career, signing major deals with both EPW and CSWA. He had success in both promotions, but in the summer of 2005 he seemingly walked away, never to return. Troy thought he no longer wanted to be the man that his life between the ropes turned him into. Being away so long had cost him the lives of the two people closest to him, and he wanted to return to a normal life. But, for a man like Troy Douglas, normal wasn't easy. Approaching his 30th birthday, he realized that in 6 years of wrestling, he'd never accomplished the one goal he'd set out for himself; winning a major world championship. In the year since his return to wrestling at A1E's 2006 Mercury Rising pay per view, Douglas has been on one of the biggest runs of his career, capping off his one year anniversary in A1E by capturing the Cyber Championship at Mercury Rising 2007, and, less than a week later, teaming with Dan Ryan to win the TEAM Lethal Lottery Championship. Troy's Other Feds A1E, EPW, TEAM, CSWA, GXW Other Fed Titles A1E World Heavyweight Champion (Current), PRIME Intense Champion, EPW Intercontinnental Champion, A1E Cyber Champion (2x), TEAM Lethal Lottery Champion (w/ Dan Ryan), GXW Xtreme Champion (2x) Wrestling Style Stiff, mat-based power wrestling with a lot of focus on his opponent’s head, neck and back. Finisher Move End of the Road, Scorpion Deathlock Finisher Description End of the Road: Second Rope Falling Underhook Piledriver Finisher Setup Move Corner Big Boot, Spinebuster Special Moves Lariat, Implant DDT, Series of leg kicks followed by an STO, Triangle Choke, Military Press dropped into a European uppercut, Butterfly Brainbuster, Standing Keylock into a Northern Lights Suplex, Shooting Star Press Other Moves Early Match Spots: Snap Suplex, Muay Thai knee strikes, Roundhouse kicks to the leg, Hiptoss followed by a dropkick to the back, European Uppercut, Spinning back kick to the gut followed by a running knee Mid-Match Spots: Fireman's Carry Gutbuster, Cobra Clutch, Release German Suplex, Half Nelson Suplex, Tope Con Hilo, Uranage Backbreaker. Late Match/Comeback Spots: Rolling Elbow, Spinning Gutwrench Powerbomb, Cradle Piledriver, Flying boot to the face, Running corner splash, Implant DDT, Military Press into a European Uppercut. SPECIAL SHOOTING STAR PRESS ADDENDUM: Troy has the Shooting Star Press in his arsenal, but considering his history of back injuries and the risk involved, he won't pull it out much. It's primarily for PPV title matches, feud enders and other match of such ballyhoo. Set-Up Moves: Sit-Out Gourdbuster, Spinebuster, Running Yakuza kick in the corner Primary Finisher: End of the Road - Sets the opponent up on the second rope, steps up to join them, underhooks the arms and falls away into a double underhook piledriver. He's been doing this move for eight years, and unless you've got a superhuman amount of willpower and an extremely strong neck, you are not getting up from this. Submission Finisher: Scorpion Deathlock - You know what this one is. Picked it up from his boyhood hero, CSWA legend Hornet, and he's learned to apply it as effectively as the best who've ever slapped this hold on. Alternate/Flash Finishers: Lariat - Throws it as hard as he possibly can, and it can come at absolutely any time. Might not finish everybody, but they'll still probably be knocked loopy by it. If he hits it at the right place at the right time, it's probably over. End of the Road '08: The newest weapon in his back pocket, it's a twisting double underhook brainbuster. He'll pull this out against opponents that he can't get onto the second rope, and it works a hell of a lot better out of nowhere. It's a little more easily countered than the original End of the Road, and it's minus the second rope impact, but this isn't exactly a lot of fun either. Strengths: 1. Creativity/Wrestling Knowledge - A true student of the mat and a wrestling historian, Troy is pretty well versed in almost every style of professional wrestling. He's comfortable working at any pace against almost any kind of opponent, and for a guy his size he can pull out some of the most imaginative moves and counters you've ever seen. 2. Deceptively Fast and Agile - For a guy who's 6'5", 260 lbs and has gone through a series of neck, back and knee surgeries, Troy can move surprisingly well. Coupled with fantastic cardio, this means Troy can either keep up with most smaller, faster opponents or outrun most men his size or larger. 3. Tough as nails - Maybe he's stubborn, maybe he's stupid, maybe it's just pride, but Troy epitomizes the "never say die" attitude. He's constantly firing back, always trying to beat the odds, and that makes him a damn tough opponent to put away quickly. Weaknesses: 1. Injury history - As noted above, he's got a well-maintained medical chart, so there are plenty of weak points for opponents to attack. The back still gives him the most trouble. 2. Flaky reputation - Troy's developed a reputation -- sometimes deserved -- as being inconsistent, unfocused and not dedicated enough. It hasn't really been a problem for him for a while, but he often tries to overcompensate by throwing himself headlong into wrestling, which can lead to getting himself in trouble. 3. Tough as nails - One of his greatest strengths is also one of his biggest weaknesses, as his bullheadedness can lead to him incurring worse physical damage than a guy with his medical history really should. OTHER NOTES: Troy has dedicated a lot of his free time over the past year getting a crash course in MMA and is incorporating more diversified striking and submission games into his matches. While nobody's going to confuse Troy's stand-up with the likes of Devin Shakur - and he still prefers pro wrestling-style attacks like the lariat, the Yakuza kick and the rolling elbow - his strikes, particularly his knees from the Thai clinch and hard kicks to the body, are go-to weapons for him. On the ground, he's not a wizard, but he is dangerous off his back, including a slick triangle choke that can put an end to any match. He's also become very adept at using his Brazilian Jiu-Jitsu base to transition out of dangerous positions with either submissions or sweeps.
