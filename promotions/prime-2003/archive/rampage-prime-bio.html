Rampage Biography Nicknames The Manbeast Hometown Pikeville, KY Birthday 04/02/84 Height 6'6 Weight 350 Fed Titles PRIME Tag titles with Vangellus Oxios for all of about twenty minutes at Tropical Turmoil '05 Fed Awards N/A Biography Scott Matthews grew up in what observers would call the All American home. His dad, a former drill sergeant and railyard supervisor, had a wife, a bright daughter and a linebacker son. But in that all, Scott despised his father through and through (think The Great Santini). Scott's drive to succeed on the football field was more out of the spite of his father's assertions. “You were third in the state in sacks? Son, you're capable of better.” Every accomplishment Scott had was never enough to satisfy the military patriarch and so Scott worked hard to take those sentiments and shove down his father's throat. One day, though, Scott's life changed. His father, misnotified of an incoming train, was caught on the tracks and the clamps of a set of railcars connected around his abdomen. The man stood there, his life ready to end when the cars came apart. So he said to his friend, “Tell Scott I'm proud of him. He's the man of the family now, he's got to take care of his mother and sister.” The clamps on the cars seperated and with nothing holding his organs in, he died on the spot. Scott had been on the football field at the time and was finishing up practice when the news came. It was almost more than his mind could take. His father was actually proud of him and tried to piss him off to get him to achieve. He was in charge of his mother and sister now, and was just fresh out of high school and in his first year at college. So Scott left and headed home to Pikeville, Kentucky, and took up work in a lumberyard to try and bring home enough money for the family to subsist on. While it gave Scott the ability to have them get by, Scott's determination to support his family wouldn't let him stop there. Made strong by football and even stronger by work in the lumberyard, Scott received quick training for the only other option he could think of. He'd always been entertained by two things: football and wrestling. He couldn't go to the NFL without finishing college, so wrestling was his only choice. Now, as a wrestler that sends everything home, Scott lives day to day out of his Jeep. He showers are the arenas, picks up bookings where he can get them and eats cheap. In his stint with RPW, he met and fell in love with Alyssa Sanders, a girl of wealth, but due to his hectic travel schedule and her job, he sees her only about once a week. Scott finally managed to land a big-fed contract, hoping to make enough money to make his family comfortable and finally live without the spectre of his father in his mind. Scott's major downfall, though, came from the night his dad died. His mind reeling, Scott had sat himself down in a bar and drank himself blind. The mixture of chemicals in his brain and the alcohol triggered a rage complex that is with him to this day. When things get to be too much, Scott can be found pounding down cheap liquor. This habit has almost gotten him killed on two occasions, but given it's the only way he knows to deal... he's not about to quit. Other Feds RPW Other Fed Awards RPW Tag Team Champion (Invincibleness~! with Invincible Gabe) Wrestling Style Simple power wrestling (aka green as grass) Finisher Move RAGE Finisher Description Massive Gore Finisher Setup Move Seeing Red Setup Description basic version: Scott will put the man over his shoulder as if for a powerslam, then drop to a seated position, effectively using his shoulder to execute a chestbuster. Other variations can be from a gorilla press, to counter crossbody blocks and so on. It's one of the few moves Scott is very proficient with. Other Moves Entrance The heavy bass and mournful harmonica of “When the Levee Breaks” by Led Zeppelin hits the speakers, harkening the arrival of the Manbeast. Scott Mathews, aka Rampage, steps out on the ramp to a moderate crowd reaction. He looks side to side as a burst of white smoke explodes behind him, ruffling his unkempt mane. With heavy steps, Rampage makes his way down the aisle, his massive hands clenching and unclenching, his eyes burning with dangerous intensity. Style of Wrestling -If you were to ask Scott what his style of wrestling is, he'd laugh in your face. Scott's a mix of basically practical brawling and elementary power and lots of football. Advantages -Strong, strong strong. Scott's experience as a linebacker means that he'll move whoever is in front of him and his time in the lumberyard means he can lift about whatever he put his mind to. -Scott's strikes hurt. When he hits you with his fist, it's got his whole body behind him, and his forearms have the power of his big arms and shoulders which are fast and pretty powerful. But Scott's most powerful attacks are his lariats. He'll take you off your feet with a running lariat or lay you flat with a standing. -Scott has mastery of one move: the Seeing Red. As long as he can get you face down over his shoulder, he can execute it on you. That move hurts quite a bit, but it's meant mainly as a setup for the RAGE. So while he can do it, it's not the sort of thing that will put you out. Detriments -Scott's big and strong and S-L-O-W. He's lost matches to people like Tessa Windsor and K-Wolf because he couldn't keep up with them. If he could land a lucky shot and stop them for a moment, then he could start hammering or throwing them. But if you keep the pace high, you're going to keep out of his grasp. -Scott is not a top rope kind of guy. He'll never go there, he never even considers the second rope. He's a feet on the ground type. -Scott isn't a submission guy. He'll slap a bearhug on you if it's required like in a submission match, but it never really gets anymore in-depth than that. Special Notes -He's had very little training when it comes to wrestling, so when it comes down to doing moves... well, he basically grabs you and throws you (ie: BIG hiptosses), throws you down (ie: bodyslams), hits you really hard (ie: stiff forearms or right hands) or knocks you down (ie: tackles or lariats). -While Scott is green as grass in the ring, he knows how to go toe-toe with someone. He was an all-state linebacker, which is why his RAGE is so devastating. He knows how centers of gravity work and knows how to fight. It's just in the wrestling ring, he has no grasp of technical ability. -When it comes to striking, Scott will either pummel you with big fists in heavy haymakers or for faster attacks, he'll just pound you with nasty forearm shots. -Scott throws lariats and clotheslines like Stan Hansen: hard and really hard. -For execution of standing lariat, grab the hair (prevents moving away) with one arm and rear back. Turn to the side to give as much swing for the momentum of the strike. Bring the arm forward and downward so that it mows through the victim, hitting hard at the top of the chest to compress the top of the rib cage and force some of the air out of the lungs at the same time. The opponent is pained by the damage to the thin muscular wall, and the whiplash of being quickly driven to the mat by a tree trunk batting them across the body. -For execution of the running lariat, the performance is much the same, only with the added force of 350 moving pounds of muscle behind it, this will knock the opponent clear out. Scott's strike is so powerful, putting so much of his weight into it that he himself falls forward off of his feet to throw his whole being into the maneuver. -Scott has so far learned how to throw, really, just one style of suplex. In the manner of the belly-to-belly, Scott will drop low and hook his arms around the opponent's waist, getting his center of gravity beneath the other man's. He'll then twist and stand, making his suplex into basically a really strong throw that happens to resemble some kind of suplex. -If Scott gets hit in the head once, he gets pissed. Twice, he gets more so. If he gets hit in the head three or more times, you've got his rage complex triggered. See Simple Yays and Nays at the bottom. Anytime Moves -Scott throws people well, so he's got high elevation on stuff like hiptosses and back bodydrops. Granted, he's not well trained, but he's strong enough to make even those elementary moves more painful. -Scott strikes with big fists and bigger forearms. He doesn't throw fast offense, but he takes his time to make sure his strikes hit and hurt. Plus, headbutts really don't faze him. Throw a headbutt at him, he'll throw one back, harder. Momentum Shifters -Rampage's big lariats are enough to change the momentum of a match all by themselves. -If Scott can trap someone in a corner, he can take control of a match. He'll use shoulder thrusts, forearms, headbutts and maybe even a lariat against the turnbuckles. -Sideslams are something Scott can get away with. They aren't complicated, they aren't fancy. They involve grabbing someone and then dropping them. Late Match -Okay, here's where you see the most out of Scott you'll ever see in the technical area. First, he can do a mean spinebuster. He won't rotate like Arn Anderson, instead he'll just catch you, lift you and plant you. Remember how Farooq did spinebusters? Yup, like that. -Scott's lariats are damn strong, as has been said at least three times in this friggin' bio. If he hits you with two or more, than can end a match. -Scott doesn't use submissions because he doesn't know any, save one. If the RAGE and Seeing Red are applied (more probably the Seeing Red), and does NOT get the pinfall, Scott will slap on a bearhug with his massive arms. With the air already forced out of the opponent's body, well, a bearhug is only going to make things worse. Simple yays and nays -Scott doing pins is usually hooking the far leg. He'll hook it by the calf and pull it high. -If Scott gets hit in the head multiple times, his rage complex will kick in and he will FLIP OUT. You will get MURDERED if you trigger this and he gets his hands on you. He'll hammer on you, he'll powerbomb you and hold on to go into a big swing, that sort of stuff. That's how people lose: they trigger his complex and then don't GET AWAY. Entrance Music 'When the Levee Breaks' by Led Zeppelin
