The HIT Biography Nicknames The Deville's Disciple, The Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid (Forever to be forgotten) Hometown Salt Lake City, Utah Birthday 8/20/81 Height 5'10' Weight 224 Fed Titles None Fed Awards None Other Feds IWO, IML, PURE, UWF, PCW, RPW Other Fed Awards IWO North American Title, IWO Extreme Title, IML United States Title, PURE Jr. Heavyweight Title, UWF THC Champion (4x), PCW TV Champion (2x), PCW Cruiserweight Champion, Co-Winner of the 2000 IWO Mayhem Match, Winner of the UWF Hardcore Prom Wrestling Style Calculating Technical Submissionist Finisher Move Justinian's Code Finisher Description Half-nelson chicken wing submission with body scissors Finisher Setup Move Hallmark Setup Description Throwing Cradle Piledriver; stolen blatantly from Ellie~!'s husband Xavier Kannon to annoy her. Other Moves The HIT’s Fighting Style: Hardcore Calculus: Hardcore Calculus is the HIT’s name for his new style. The second half of the name comes from the new complexity that Trapezoid has injected into his strategy. Having come a long way from his days as only a spot machine, Trapezoid will now chain wrestle. Many of his new signatures reflect this, as he will chain together high impact maneuvers in search of a victory. HIT has also changed his moveset around so as to be better able to combat any type of adversary. Combine this with his hardcore and suicidal nature, and you have a very dangerous man. Suplexes Suplexes make up a large part of Justin's offensive against opponents he can lift for them. Trigonometric Series The Trigonometric Series is the HIT’s name for three chained suplexes. He has twelve different suplexes he can select from for his chain, one for each possible operation in trigonometry. Sine: German Suplex Cosine: Tiger Suplex Tangent: Tiger Suplex ‘85 Arcsine: Belly to Belly Suplex Arccosine: Double Arm Belly to Belly Suplex Arctangent: Reverse Tiger Suplex ‘85 (Only finishes a series; very rarely used.) Secant: Vertical Suplex Cosecant: Northern Lights Suplex Cotangent: Millennium Suplex (Only finishes a series) Arcsecant: Inverted Facelock Suplex Arccosecant: Belly to Back Stomach Flop Suplex (Only finishes a series) Arccotangent: Armtrap Necklock Suplex (Only finishes a series; rarely used) Of these, the HIT has various favorite combinations, but he can combine these in any fashion during a match if he sees fit. Sine, Cosine, Tangent: The combination that inspired him to create the Trigonmetric Series, and one of his oldest moves. German-Tiger-Tiger ‘85, with the last suplex bridged. Arcsecant, Arcsecant, Cosine: The two inverted suplexes are rolled through backwards, so HIT ends up behind his opponent. After the second, it is a simple matter of releasing the head and hooking the arms for the Cosine. Arcsine, Arcosine, Cosecant: As in the prior combo, the Arcsine and Arccosine are rolled through backwards, not to the side. After Arccosine, Trapezoid simply throws one of the held arms over his head to prepare to deliver Cosecant. Sine, Sine, Arccosecant: The two Germans roll through normally into the belly to back stomach flop suplex, which acts like a third German. One of HIT’s favorites for mid match, as it does not bridge. Geometric Trapplexes: Geometric Trapplexes are singular suplexes, not chained. This is because they are often specifically designed by the HIT to effect back or neck, and are all released (“no bridge, all impact”) Triangle Trapplex: Another of the HIT’s first creations, a simple reverse full nelson suplex that drops the opponent on his head and neck, so named because the nelson resembles a triangle. Angular Trapplex: Trapezoid cinches his opponent in a belly to belly, and raises them in the air, then falls back at an angle, snapping at the last second once the opponent’s head is about to hit. This throws the opponent’s weight in the opposite direction of his head. Bad things result. This is one of HIT’s most dangerous moves, and he reserves it for only when absolutely necessary. Circular Trapplex: So named because the opponent turns a circle in mid air. HIT lifts him or her for a simple gut wrench, but applies sufficient torque on release so his foe lands on his or her stomach. The Parallel Bases Group The Parallel Bases Group is a series of pump handle based moves that the HIT has used throughout his career, primarily as finishers and set up moves.. This tradition can still be seen today with Parallel Bases IV. Parallel Bases Alpha: The first of the moves to bear the name Parallel Bases, his IWO Minor League finisher. A simple top rope pump handle power slam. Parallel Bases: The move that HIT adopted upon entrance to the IWO. A Pumphandle Michinoku Driver, a move that has seen use in Japan in the form of Magnum TOKYO, who calls his version the Egoist Driver. Parallel Bases II: A Pumphandle into an Emerald Frosion. Trapezoid had been using this move for several years before CIMA of Toruymon took it into the Japanese mainstream as the Egoist Schweiza. Parallel Bases III: The third installment of Parallel Bases begins in a pump handle setup, but is swung into a pump handled piledriver. Parallel Bases III-Beta: A secondary version of PBIII, it ironically has seen more use than the original. Innovated during a PCW Television Title match when HIT could not swing Venneman Pendragon for the regular PBIII, Trapezoid simply pulls his opponent up as if to administer regular Parallel Bases, but drops to his knees in a Tombstone. Parallel Bases SIGMA: One of the HIT’s ultimate weapons. As denoted by the name, it is a sum of prior Parallel bases. HIT will execute Parallel Bases, Parallel Bases II, and Parallel Bases III or III-B in succession. Needless to say, this will 99.99999999999 times out of 100 be the end of a match, and it is only used in the most dire of circumstances. Other High Impact Moves: The HIT retains an impressive number of high impact tactics that DON’T involve pump handles or suplexes. They are listed here. Logarithmic Function: Trapezoid doubles an opponent over, and crosses his arms. He lifts up into a power bomb, but steps to the side, as he drops the opponent over his shoulder with a crossarm Thunderfire power bomb. Long Division: HIT lifts his opponent up in suplex position, and slingshots him off the top rope. This gives HIT the momentum to lift him overhead, and pivot into a Stunner on the way down. Geometry 101: HIT scoops the opponent on his shoulders, and leaps in the air, spinning 180 degrees and planting his foe with a DVD. Trigonometry 201: The same as Geometry 101, except the HIT gets a running start with the opponent on his shoulders. Statistics 301: The most dangerous of the math class series of Death Valley Drivers. HIT scoops the opponent into the fireman’s carry, and ascends to some height, and executes a forward flip into the DVD. This move can only be done in ladder or hardcore matches, where HIT can leap from a height higher than the top rope. Algebra I: A standing somersault senton with a full twist. Algebra II: A cartwheel into a moonsault senton. Linear Algebra: A standing shooting star leg drop. Numberline: HIT runs at the opponent, spins around, and clubs him with a forearm clothesline. Quadratic Equation: HIT double-underhooks both of his opponent’s arms, and lifts him vertical for a butterfly brain buster. Indefinite Integral: The Trapezoid takes one of the foe’s arms, and pulls it back and down, holding it by the wrist. His other hand goes around the opponent’s neck and grabs his other hand. From here, HIT steps forward and drops, completing a Side Effect like move. He can either release, or hold this as a submission. Aerial Manuevers: The HIT’s suicidal nature is readily evident in his aerial tactics, consisting of planchas, topes, and your traditional off the top rope moves as well. 10/18: One of the HIT’s few non mathematically named moves, the name refers to his second tenure in PCW, which he began on 10/18 against the Front Office. The setup is similar to that of the 619, as HIT drop toe holds his opponent across the middle rope. From here, Trapezoid ascends to the top rope, and comes off with a somersault leg drop, driving the foe’s throat into the second rope. Trapezoidal Translation: A top rope Inverse Shooting Star Press. In other words, HIT faces the crowd, and flips forward 270 degrees, landing back first on the opponent. Trapezoidal Translation II: The more powerful sequel, a corkscrew Shooting Star Press. One-Half Base One Plus Base Two Times Height: The area of a Trapezoid, and the HIT’s top rope flying elbow drop. Four-Thirds Pi Times Radius Cubed: HIT’s former finisher in PCW. Trapezoid ascends to the top rope, and leaps off in a Swanton, rotating through into a frog splash. The Minus Sign: The Trapezoid’s tope suicida, performed with a perfectly rigid body. Eccentricity: Somersault Plancha into a hurricanrana. Submission Manuevers: The HIT does not have a wide variety of submissions, but the two he possesses are favorites of his. Extraneous Solution: A half-nelson combined with a chickenwing, often taken to the ground so HIT can lock in a body scissors. The Word Problem. Trapezoid crosses the opponent’s legs, as if for a Regal Stretch, and proceeds to lock in a Goku-Raku Stretch (Straitjacket Camel Clutch), using the legs for leverage and pulling back with everything he has.
