Shawn Stewart Biography Nicknames The Panther; King of the Cage Hometown Lexington, Kentucky Birthday 03/25/76 => 30 Height 6'3' Weight 240 Biography Shawn Stewart: master of unarmed combat, fighter extraordinary and intelligent man. But who is he? Let's start waaaaaaaaay early on. When Shawn was ten, his father, an "efficiency consultant", as he was hired to be, moved the family to South Korea to work with Hyundai. While there, Shawn became engrossed in Tae Kwon Do as a means not only to fit in with other children, but as a sport for himself. Three years later, his father got a job back in their home state of Kentucky and the family moved back. Shawn's hobby came with them and he continued to practice all the way to college. Once there, he decided to branch out. The arts of boxing, of Muay Thai, brazilian jujitsu and greco-roman wrestling called out to him and he immersed himself in them. His college years were spent technically studying police administration, which lead to him becoming a SWAT member and unarmed combat instructor. He became known throughout the federal law enforcement agencies as one of the top instructors when he did something no one expected: he resigned his commission and purchased a gym. His reason was simple: to get into the realm of professional fighting was to hone and perfect his craft to the point of, hopefully, optimum efficiency. Fights that ranged from the untelevised small events escalated into larger, higher profile bouts and training with some of the big names of the sport: the Gracies, Ken and Frank Shamrock, Chuck Liddell and Kevin Randleman. The result was a sharp, analytical mind that studied fighting from every aspect available to him. You had a man that knew how fighting worked on the streets and against professionals, a man that knew pressure points, weak spots, joint manipulation, chokes and so on. Armed with this knowledge, Shawn Stewart joined Old School Fighting, which came to be his first big exposure, yet unfortunately in a realm few cared about. After OSF closed, Shawn took the OSF world title for himself and filled out his work dates with OSW until the close of the institution. Finally, after months of intensive training, running seminars for members of the national law enforcement agencies and getting married to his longtime girlfriend and former ballerina, Nicole, he returned to fWo. Shawn Stewart came not as a wrestler to a wrestling organization, but as a fighter come to prove dominance. If only they were willing to go along with that. Even though he remained as lethal as ever, he remained solidly midcard without so much as a first-look in the booking. Or perhaps it was because he was as lethal as ever. fWo had it established heirarchy; something that Stewart didn't care for one bit. He wasn't looking to rock the boat, rather to lead a one-man mutiny and captain it. And anyone in his way, woe be unto them. They encouraged Nick Brandish to show Stewart 'his place' in the organization. Brandish, who was quite the dangerous man in those parts. That man, so used to having his own way, made the mistake of bringing Stewart's wife into the feud. After a 53-hit Kobukson, Brandish nearly died. fWo couldn't deal with someone so dangerous, someone they couldn't control. He was promptly given his release. No one had heard of him, as far as the wrestling world was concerned. At least, not until January 4th, 2006. Shawn Stewart returned to the ring, along with Skylar Green, to serve as temporary replacements to Teh Greatness. Together, thanks in no small part to the ruthless efficiency of the King of the Cage, they defeated Baltasar, Chet Worth, Vangelus Olsig, and Ozric Mortimer. It is unclear how long their tenure in PRIME would have been, but he made the most of every opportunity.After serving very distinctly on the roster, Shawn and Green were cut by Tyler Nelson. Shawn's aspirations of reaching the top of the mountain are still unfulfilled and he finally returned to PRIME's ranks to pull that brass ring. Other Feds OSF/W/E, RPW, tSC, fWo Other Fed Awards OSF title (awarded after closing), RPW MidSouth Title, fWo Hardcore Championship Wrestling Style Personal blend of fighting styles including boxing, TaeKwonDo, Muay Thai, Brazilian JuJitsu and Krav Maga Finisher Move See Moveset Other Moves Unique Traits: Focused, intelligent, fierce, determined, loyal, analytical Advantages: hard (stiff) striker, capable mat wrestler, strong submissions, developed understanding of human physiology, videotape study Detriments: tunnel vision, very intense __________________________________________________________ Common Moves: -Any manner of boxing punch -All types of elbow strikes that don't come off the top rope -Kicks: front snap, roundhouse (snap or Muay Thai), front pushing kick, sidekick (executing with snap, NOT a superkick), ax kicks to the back/head, spinning heel kicks (rarely), spinning sidekicks -standing/lunging/rising headbutts -stiff finger eye gouges -palm strikes (NO CHOPS) -knee strikes (again, nothing off the top rope) -armbar -armbreaker -cross armbreaker -kneebar -leg grapevine -top wristlock -arm triangle choke -rear naked choke -front facelock -front chancery -leg scissor choke -forearm choke -hammerlock Signature Moves: Signature Moves, not finishers Intentionally Sloppy Suplexes One of Shawn's trademarks are his intentionally sloppy suplexes that he throws folks in. Some specific ones that you'll see more commonly are as follows. Trueno: The thunder of this head and arm suplex that throws his opponent overhead and drops him on his skull with a thunderous slam that gave this move it's name. Relampago: The backdrop suplex that you saw Terry Funk screw up by dropping a guy on his neck Shawn does intentionally. The speed with which Shawn performs this toss gave it the name 'lightning' to his brazilian training partners. Garras da Pantera: Shawn's only plain wrestling suplex, this T-Bone suplex is meant to drive the man face or skull first into the mat. Dor Jogando: Another product of Shawn's work in Brazilian jujitsu, this belly to back suplex is, like all the rest, actually a rather sloppy headdrop. Arkhangelsk: what can be known as a sambo throw/slam, Shawn pitches the man over head to land on his shoulders and neck. Shawn used this move most prominently against a competitor from Siberia. Brechen Des Bodens: Shawn hits this move as a sweep that drives your head into the mat, but to others it's known as the STO. Shawn adopted the German phrase that an opponent asked him if he was 'breaking ground' with his head. Finishers Shawn does not like the idea of boxing himself in with only one particular way to come out victorious. In fact, he would prefer to win in an unexpected manner. However, knowing himself, he knows there are a few options that he favors which have definite potential for ending a match. KtHO -A move that can truly come from nowhere is what Shawn wanted to bring into his repertoire. With this move, he did just that. All it takes is his opponent to be bent over. Shawn will pull his right arm back and fold it, as if holding a marble in the crook of his arm. With all the might he can pull, he rotates his body, swinging over his body in a high arc that cracks the elbow down across the back of the opponent's head, flattening them out and quite possibly causing a concussion like the move's first victim, an up and coming fighter that had gotten in over his head. Note on this move: If you take a look at it, it's a move used to break multiple concrete blocks being applied to the back of someone's head. There's no reason for anybody to be getting up from it. Click here to see the ending position. Lion's Triangle -Shawn reverted a bit more back to his MMA roots after his tenure in OSW. This particular hold is one of as much brutality as he could combine into a simple, fast combination. After an initial tackle or knockdown move, Shawn will take the mount position, or basically sit atop the opponent's abdomen. From there, a series of strikes popularized by training in the Lion's Den gyms follows: a series of punches and elbows that strike to the head and temples followed by a quick strike or two towards the ribcage and kidneys. With the opponent on his or her back, the likelihood of someone with his experience being bucked off is next to none. From there, the repeated attacks on the lower portion of the body open up the progression, wherein once an arm drops to where the lower body punches are landing, Shawn will first strike with an elbow to the unprotected side of the head and then follow that arm, wrapping a leg around the neck of the opponent and then hooking his body, throwing the other leg around to complete the leg triangle choke. From there, it is a simple matter of time before the opponent either taps or passes out. The name is a combination itself, as an homage to where he originated the combination and the final stage of the combo. Click here to see triangle choke. Brazilian Garrotte -Named after an ancient weapon, this hold of Brazilian Jujitsu is incredibly simple and incredibly painful. Shawn will usually begin by apply a sleeper (or rear naked choke, same thing) to an opponent and using leg scissors on the body. He'll then drag the person to the ground and roll until the opponent is face down. Shawn will then, still holding the sleeper, shift his legs to a camel clutch-style position and pull back, arching the back and putting the weight of the choke and the back against the windpipe. Click here to see a variant of the hold. Mordida da Pantera -Stewart spent quite some time in Brazil working with the mat grapplers there and thus, was given many new names for some of his holds, such as this one. Shawn will hook his opponent's head with his right arm, similar to a DDT position. With his free arm, he will reach under the opponent's right arm and lock his hands together, then lift, forcing the right forearm across the opponent's throat. Leg scissors are sometimes used in order to create more pull against the neck. Commonly known as the guillotine choke. Click here to see guillotine choke. Garganta da Ruptura -Shawn uses few suplexes, but he does recognize their damaging ability. The Garganta da Ruptura, a name taken from a Brazilian that witnessed Shawn practice this move, is his version of his most damaging suplex, a german suplex variant that he performs by first grabbing the opponent in a reverse bearhug and then snapping his body to forcefully throw his opponent headfirst on the mat. By changing his initial hold application, Shawn is able to minimize the ability of a competitor to fight how the move will land or even flip out of it. With the grip around the ribcage, there is no way to shift the body enough to escape this signature headdrop. Broken Resolve -Perhaps one of his cruelest submissions, Shawn begins with a standard armbar, but instead loops his arms around the opponent's in a figure four motion into a keylock. The leg scissors provide not only leverage, but pressure on the shoulder which the keylock attempts to separate the elbow. Shawn has also performed a hanging version of the move in extreme circumstances, much like a hanging figure four. Click here to see keylock without leg scissors. Larme de Cheville -Dubbed after a winning a bout against a french savate specialist, this move is a simple favorite. While many prefer the anklelock for quick application, Shawn chooses to use the anklelock due to its ease of application. Shawn's ability to reach an anklelock from several, often mind-boggling, positions gives him the ability to slow press for a submission or quickly twist his arms for an easy break of the joint. Shawn's preferred method of application has the opponent facedown with the leg being held clasped in a leg scissor while Shawn twists at the joint. Other variations include dropping and sweeping to grab the leg or creating a tackle that puts the man over his back and into his clutches. Click here to see anklelock in preferred application. Kobukson (very rarely used) -This is the move that got Shawn released from fWo after his attack on Nick Brandish. Named after the first ironclad warship, this move demonstrates devastating potential. With opponent lying facedown on the mat, Shawn drops his elbows on the opponent's shoulders and fires a series of knees into the opponent's skull. Shawn favors this as his primary finisher because he can very easily apply it to an opponent of any size.
