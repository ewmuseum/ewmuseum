Hunter Sabuani Biography Wrestler Name Hunter Sabuani Nicknames Peerless, PHS, Sublime, The Raja of Roguishness, The Sovereign of the Sport, etc., etc., ad infinitum. Hometown Las Vegas, NV Birthday 12/10/71 Height 6' Weight 237 lbs. Fed Titles Infinite Gauntlet. Fed Awards None. Biography His name is Hunter and he wrestles. Stuff has happened in his life. Other Feds National Wrestling Council (Georgia Championship Wrestling, Liberty City Wrestling, NWC: Pacific), Diamond Wrestling Syndicate Other Fed Titles NWC World Heavyweight Champion (x2), NWC World Tag Team Champion (final champion), GCW Georgia State Champion, GCW Southern Heritage Champion. Other Fed Awards 1998 Most Beloved Figure, 1999 Heel of the Year. Manager Madison Wrestling Style Slightly past his prime high flyer who is changing his style to be more of a brawler/high spot artist Finisher Move The Perfect Ending Finisher Description Springboard or Second-Rope Death Valley Driver Finisher Setup Move Game, Set, Match Setup Description Rapid-fire double knee --> Monkeyflip --> standing shooting star press Special Moves The Subapult (springboard front-flip legdrop) Other Moves Basic Style: Hunter Sabuani has no right to be doing what he's doing anymore. At the age of 37, he's beaten himself up to the point that he should be slowing down and he shouldn't be jumping off of things on a regular basis. But he still does it, and he still manages to be goddamn good at it. Sure, he's a hair slower than he was seven years ago when he last won a major wrestling federation championship. Put him in the ring with some kind of ring general and he's in trouble. Put him in the ring with some wet behind the ears kid who can run at Mach 9, and he'll have a problem keeping up. But stick PHS in the ring with your typical wrestler, and he'll still be the fast one, the one who's lighter on his feet, and the one who's bouncing around the ring. The fact that he can't do what he used to be able to do bugs only sometime, but it's more than made up for by the fact that he's almost literally seen every kind of wrestling style that there is, and he will force you to play his game, not the other way around. That means lots of brawling, lots of wild action, and a ton of time spent on the floor. It used to be said by people that if you could force Hunter Sabuani to stay in the ring, you had a better than average chance of beating him, but he made it so difficult because he surrounded himself with people whose entire job was to distract you. Hunter's not an extreme wrestler; he's not someone who makes their fortune based on their ability to hit you with a weapon. He's someone who excels at the utilization of his surroundings, though, and if that means he'll get a chance to use the ring bell, a chair, the stairs, the security barrier, some chick's purse, a ladder, and a brick... that's okay by him. Standard Movelist: Chop block Corkscrew plancha Discus clothesline Dragonscrew legwhip Flying forearm High knee (often running, usually to the side of the head) Leg lariat Playing possum Reverse mule kick Seated senton Standing dropkick Signature Moves: 1.) The Sub-a-pult: A springboard front-flip legdrop. Has been in the Sabuani arsenal since the beginning. 2.) Game, Set, Match: Irish whip to the corner. Fly in, double knee or flying splash into the chest, monkey flip out, double knees to the face. Bang-bang-bang fast. 3.) Wicked Justice: A perverse tribute to his now ex-wife, Miss Madison. It's a dreadfully brutal hurricanrana -- Sabuani doesn't let go until the last second, so sometimes, it's almost more akin to a flying piledriver. 4.) Facialized: Sabuani hooks standing reverse Indian deathlock with a surfboard and then lifts his free leg up, placing it on the back of the head of the opponent. He releases the surfboard and stomps the leg down to drive the opponent's head face first into the mat. Can be done multiple times.
