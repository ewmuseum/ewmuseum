Seek And Destroy Biography Fed Titles None Fed Awards None Biography Fame. Money. Power. Respect. Tragedy. Heartbreak. These words flash across the screen as the nifty VH1 music plays in the background. Several images of rock stars jamming, playing some skins, and singing go by in opaque colors, and finally the title screen comes to pass: VH1 Behind the Music: Seek and Destroy Narrator: In the world of rock and roll, many great bands have come to pass. Led Zeppelin, ZZ Top, Aerosmith, AC/DC, The Rolling Stones, and The Who, just to name a few. None of these men and none of these bands however could have walked in the shoes of the two men in what is called one of the loudest acts in rock and roll history. Their early success was shown at many clubs and finally when they signed a record contract at a young age. Life wasn’t always easy for the rockers though, and many journeys into and out of assorted professions, assorted bands, and with assorted band members led to a turbulent past. Preaching good times, loud music, and pretty women, this hard rocking duo carved a path in the world of music all their own. Money, women, respect, and fame would all follow them wherever they went, but as the saying goes, that would all come crashing down. It all began in 1973; Londonderry Mass. Jason Bass was born in the small St. Hubbins Hospital at 2:43 a.m. The doctors first noted that born unto the mother of the future rock star was not only an abnormally large baby boy, but he also had abnormally large mane of thick hair. Thus was “Jammin” Jason Bass brought into this world. Many people that worked that day had made reference to the fact that they had never seen such a thunderous head of hair. Jason was a strong child and even at a very young age, he was exposed to the wondrous sound of his father playing in a band. At the age of 5 he began the spiritual journey that would lead him to the path he strides today. He began to listen to rock and roll on a daily basis, even sitting in his room after school with 45’s of Credence Clearwater Revival, Foghat, and of course the great Led Zeppelin and John Paul Jones, oft considered one of the great bassists of all time. He began the great gig of his life with an old beat up electric bass his father had laying in the garage. Learning all he could from the instructions of his dad, he began to focus a lot more on his guitar than anything else, and it was very obvious at times that he lived in a world that most other children did not. By the age of seven, The Hairy Beast of Londonderry was the biggest kid on the playground, but his soft spoken words often made him less intimidating and more misunderstood. That caused the gentle giant of a seven year old to retreat farther back into his studies of the sonic arts. It was around this time that the future yin to Jason’s yang was being born. They said that in the First Self Righteous Church in Hollywood California, lightning struck the building the day that Eric Evans was born. The very moment he was delivered a massive bolt hit the top of the building and shorted out the main power. The building, running only on generators to sustain life support, was dark and ominous, but the young man born into the hands of Dr. Nunzio Strombolli had a bright and vibrant future as “The Electric One”. Life in Los Angeles was never an easy one, and especially not in Hollywood. In the 80’s, pornographic material, drugs, and excess abounded. It hit close to him with the young mother of Evans. Penniless and heartbroken, she did the only thing she could do for the sake of her child. She sent him for adoption. This act which Eric could not even remember left him heartbroken years later. Luckily for him, the young child of 2 years old was adopted by a benevolent old rocker by the name of Steven Sinclair, who was a retired roadie. Steven lived far away from Hollywood, but he knew of its power and what a man could do if they could master the beast. He raised Eric Evans in a small house in a small town in the great state of New York, becoming the master to the learner. Sinclair took care of Evans, and in the time he had began to teach him to play the guitar. Not just that though. No rock star could ever be complete with only one tool at their disposal. The electric guitar would become the sword to Eric’s knight, but his presence…well that was the most important aspect of all. He had to learn to walk the walk, talk the talk, strut the strut, and play the game. Playing the guitar itself actually came naturally to him for some reason. Most people who are truly gifted in the art of the six string can attest to the fact that it just comes naturally. Eric was a young man of 10 years old and already his life seemed to be pre-destined to become a great star. He could play scales, he could hammer on the frets, and he could pick. Life was a whirlwind for the young man. It was around this time that 17 year old Jason Bass began a journey that would eventually lead him to become part of the band now known as Seek and Destroy. The time was 1990, and grunge had not quite hit the scene yet. Hair metal was waning, and the new wave of metal was coming up fast. Metallica was not yet the undisputed king of the heavy metal world, as The Black Album had not been released. However, Jason’s favorite plucker, Cliff Burton, had been tragically killed in a bus crash several years before. Vowing to walk in the footsteps of his idol, he made a trek to the clubs of New York. There he would meet many a great artist who was on the underground scene. Most notably he had met some of the members of Pantera once. Seeing their high energy raw rock and roll brought out the animal in him, and Jam knew he had to be a star. The problem with the story at this point is that Eric and Jason still had yet to meet. Eric was ten years old, and a prodigy, but he still did not hang in the same circles as The Bad Mutha Plucka. No, Eric was still in grade school, and causing problems left and right. The teachers could never get through to him, as he believed it was his divine right to be the center of attention at all times. More often than not he would spend all night staging imaginary shows in his basement for imaginary fans. Several years passed, and now Eric had become a man. 18 years old and he could play any instrument with strings. Mandolin, violin, cello, guitar, bass, it didn’t matter. He was a veritable master in his field. Jason had become so attuned to his bass guitar that he was a stand in at world famous rock club CBGB’s. He saw many an act come through, and while he was so mile mannered that he rarely asked them to jam or even sign an autograph, he still followed his dreams. Fate, as it would seem, handed him the keys to its Ferrari one summer night in 1998. The show was a local band, and they had to duck out because of a spontaneously combusting drummer incident. The fans were restless, and the owners were unsure of what to do. Suddenly, like a bolt of lightning itself, a clear loud voice could be heard from the back of the club. There stood a young man, maybe 18. He had an old Fender Stratocaster in a gig bag strapped to his back, and long black hair with red streaks in it. Jason locked his eyes on the young man and it was like an instant connection had been established. The Lightning to his Thunder had arrived. Walking to the stage with a cock sure attitude the young man brushed past the security with few words, and then hopped up. He drags the electric guitar from the bag and pulled out a California cable to hook to the amp. The fans, unsure what to make of the upstart performer began to clamor. When that young man stepped to the microphone however, history began writing itself. Eric: What I need is a band. I don’t care how good you all are, and I don’t care if you can sing. I just need you to be able to keep up with me, because this is one lonely rocker in a club that is in desperate need of a sonic journey. A man stood up and volunteered his services on the kit. Another stepped forward to play some keys, but they were still missing their heart and soul. They were still missing the bass line. Jam stood up, his full 6’9” frame towering up over the stage. He looked Eric in the eyes and brushed back some of his thunderous hair. With a hand, he stepped up onto the stage and strapped on his faithful Ibanez bass. Eric: You guys know any Metallica? Jason smiled and nodded. The rest of the band nodded a bit, and Eric brushed his hair back, pulled a pick from his pocket, and turned the dials up on his guitar. Eric: This one’s called “Seek and Destroy”. Narrator: “Up next, the rise - Jerry: So Eric goes back on the stage, buck naked, and looks at the screaming crowd. Nearly inseparable from the first show they played; it wasn’t long before the dynamic duo had become the talk of New York. They were a high profile, high energy act and often times they would outperform the highlighted band of the evening. Their old road manager, Jerry Burnside tells of one particular show in 2000. Jerry: We were at, oh I think it was Kansas City, and out comes Seek and Destroy. The fans were going nuts when they tore the stage up. Eric would point out the pretty ladies in the crowd and the roadies would all take them back for a brief interview – well when I say interview, they had to have a rack that was perfect. He shifts in his seat temporarily. Jerry: So they play their set right, and toward the end, Eric is doing his normal routine, wooing the ladies. Jason is pretty much rocking along, when suddenly Eric jumps into the crowd, right into the thick of a sorority chapter. They begin tearing his clothes off, and he barely escapes with his…well you know. He laughs. Jerry: So Eric goes back on the stage, buck naked, and looks at the screaming crowd. The women are swooning and the police on hand for crowd control make their way to the stage to arrest him for indecent exposure. The manager sits back and puts his hands up in the air. Jerry: Eric yells at them “If someone’s gotta go to jail for rock and roll, it might as well be me!!” and he proceeds to jump into the crowd again and head for the door, streaking buck naked down the road of New York. Old Jerry laughs a bit then continues on, finalizing the story. Jerry: So the next act is scheduled to come out, The Red Hot Chili Peppers I think…and they are all playing in their famous sock routine. The crowd jeered the snot out of them for not having the balls…well literally…to take it all off! But that wasn’t the only incident. In all count, Eric had been arrested on charges of misdemeanor due to a show going wild over 13 times in their first year. It almost seemed like “Electric” Eric Evans was spiraling out of control. Jason Bass, on the other hand, was forced to watch his friend’s crazy antics from the sideline, as he kept the bass line the entire time. It was not within Jason’s nature to become overly rambunctious like Eric, even if he was the larger of the two men. Jason: Eric liked to get into trouble. There I said it. He was always the one guy in the crowd that you could point a finger at and say “that guy, he’s lightning in a bottle”. I lost a lot of money bailing him out of jail. It was trouble that eventually led to Eric’s hiatus from the band, forced by everyone that cared about him. Jason retells the events that transpired on the night of September 23rd, 2001. Jason: The power for the show goes out. Eric, trying to be intrepid goes backstage and grabs an acoustic guitar. I mean, I knew he was a jokester, but what he did man, I don’t know if even he knew what he was doing. Jason grabs a drink and shifts nervously in his chair. Jason: He starts playing some song in the dark, in the middle of a rock and roll club, and before long we realize he is playing some god awful country song. Probably the Dixie Chicks or something. Thing is, he doesn’t even like country; he was just doing it to be an asshole because the power was out and he wanted to be the life of the party. Bass shakes his head. Jason: Someone throws a bottle at the stage, and its pretty dark in there. It hits Eric, and busts his cheek open, and man he just loses it. Into the crowd, guitar swinging, and the guy who threw the bottle gets smacked over the head like Honky Tonk Man or something. Three of his friends get up and start a fight, so I jumped in. We all eventually got pulled back from each other, and the courts had to get involved. Jam almost cracks a smile. Jason: That’ll be the last time those pussies in Limp Bizkit will fuck with us…oh shit, can I say fuck on cable? The fight would prove costly to the band, as Eric was forced to attend anger management classes, and missed a lot of practice. Eventually he began drifting away from the band, and found himself a girlfriend in one Kimberly Stone. Jason: I never liked her, let’s get this straight. I’m not like gay or anything, but man, she wasn’t right for him at all. She kept him away from the band, sometimes late into the night. The relationships began to strain after a few months of Eric showing up late or not at all. He had grown into a prima donna placement in the band. Pretty soon, even Jason couldn’t handle it anymore. His long time best friend and band mate had gone from stable to barely there. What was worse, he began to slip up in his guitar skills. One particular show in Canada, Eric decided to upstage the entire group and began playing a solo in the middle of one of their hits “Smells Like Sex In Here”. The result was the band standing up and walking out of the show, leaving Eric high and dry. He tried to play on; imagining himself in his basement, listening to his foster father telling him how to play the crowd, but it was over. The band had left him. This movie is not yet rated. End commercial break. Eric Evans had no direction anymore. He had no band, he had no great show to play, and he had no money to pay for his rent. Worse yet, his girlfriend Kimberly left him for the lead singer of Warrant once his money and fame ran out. Jason retired his bass professionally, and moved back to his home in Londonderry. The remaining band members split up and moved back home, with Seek and Destroy nothing but a stain on the parchment of Rock and Roll history. The year was 2003 and Eric Evans was no longer “Electric”, he was merely the artist who used to be in the most rocking band this side of the continent. He thought about cutting his hair and getting a real job, but the rocker just couldn’t bring himself to it. He tried to go to other clubs and perform solo, but it was never the same. Eric talks about this period in his life for the first time on television. Eric: Basically, I fucked up. I don’t know what was happening or where I made the mistake, but somewhere along the lines I lost the vision of what it took to be a great rocker. He looks down and continues. Eric: I lost whatever it was that was inside me and I didn’t think it would come back. I mean, look at the situation: Jason was gone, the band broke up, no money, my girl left me for a guy who looks like a walking leotard, and I couldn’t even get respect at my old haunts. It was viscous. Evans was alone in the world. Fate, though would step in once again to lend him a helping hand. Eric: So I’m sitting alone in this club playing a small crowd, and who should walk in the door but Jerry Burnside. He waits for the set to get over and buys me a drink. We talked for a while about old times, and then says something I’ll never forget. Jerry: I said “Listen dude, Jam has been sitting in his basement for 3 months now. He can’t play solo like you can. You guys need to get back together, forget that old shit, and do it right this time.” Eric smiles and takes a sip of water. Eric: Most people aren’t supposed to get a second chance at something like this. I was handed the gift of renewal though, and through the newfound faith in god above me, I decided to give it a try. Evans did go to Jason’s house, and talked to The Hairy Beast of Londonderry. At the time there was a show coming up and they conversed about the past. They knew that pretty soon, Metallica itself would be coming to town, and if they could get the band back together, they might have a shot at playing a gig with the boys. So Jason and Eric parted on good terms, each to go find their road band members. Jason was more successful than Eric when he convinced “Bangin” Buddy Samson to come back. Evans however, was hard pressed to convince his old friend Jeremy Hunglo, who they simply called “Keys” to rejoin. It took several weeks, but eventually they all agreed to meet in one room for the first time in over a year. Jason: The initial spark wasn’t really gone, but it was hard to find. We had some rough sessions before we were ready to play again, let me tell you. But it all came together, and the night was also coming closer. Pretty soon, Metallica would be in town, and they absolutely had to be on that show. Jerry did his diligence and got them booked, but they had to play under the stipulation that no matter what, they would not upstage the main act. Agreeing to the stipulation, they made way to the show that would determine their entire future as a band. Jason, Eric, “Keys” and Buddy all hung nervously behind the curtain as the opening act of three bands began their show. Soon enough, Lars and James Hetfield themselves came to talk to the young men. Eric: So James comes to talk to me about the business and how this was a big chance for us. Like I needed to be reminded. Meanwhile Lars is saying something to Buddy about some timing issues with one of our songs. I didn’t know it yet, but man it was going to be a long night. Seek and Destroy went on stage before a sold out crowd and began the opener of their hit “Event Horizontal Mambo”, and it happened. Jason looked at Eric, and Eric looked to Keys, Keys looked to Buddy and they knew what had to happen. They had to go to eleven. Much like Spinal Tap had done in the past, they needed that extra push off the cliff, and Eric gave the roadies THE signal. The crowd was stunned as suddenly the auditory output from the speakers was tripled, and the arena went ballistic as Seek and Destroy claimed title to the loudest rock and roll show in history. The Decibel meter used for most shows broke when Eric sang an amplified high G note. The show actually could never be recorded as a record because of this fact. Backstage, Lars Ulrich was taking a drink of water from a glass, and it shattered in his hand, cutting him. With no drummer, Metallica was forced to cancel the show and the only thing that could keep the unruly crowd from rioting was for Seek and Destroy to take the stage once again. The boys played their hearts out, and put on the greatest show of their careers. Backstage however, it was no great show. Eric: Lars comes to me, and tells me that this was bullshit, yada yada, and they go to court over it. I didn’t realize that they could sue someone for upstaging them, but apparently that was part of the deal. So what was once the most promising act in rock and roll history was now relegated to be embroiled in legal problems. The boys who had come from such a humble beginning now faced the largest monster of all, corporate rock. Subsequently, not only did Metallica blame them for the show that had gone awry, but the lackluster sales of their newly released album St. Anger, coupled with its poor reviews by the fans lead the old guard to only step up their attack even more. In late 2004, after a long and drawn out court case, Metallica won arbitration to the rights of Seek and Destroys American contract, and bought out the merchandising rights. Now, the boys were forced to travel…a different path. Jerry: I got a call from a promoter in Japan. Seek and Destroys last big hit “The One Eyed Monster” was a huge hit over there. And since our agreement didn’t say anything about oversees gigs, we packed our bags and headed for the orient. It was there, playing a sold out show with Sonata Arctica that the boys of Seek and Destroy met up with an enterprising young wrestling promoter with the last name Pham, and they were offered a lucrative contract in the world of professional wrestling. Feigning interest at first, they let it go to the back burner until the orient tour was over. One day, though when they were about to return home to America with no contract, they had a heart to heart with each other. Jason: Well basically we figured out a loophole in the system. We can’t play a gig in an American rock show, because of legality, but we CAN play one if we are under contract from the Pham family, since they have connections in the Far East business. So it was that the circle was now complete. With “Keys” and “Bangin” Buddy Samson at their side, “Jammin” Jason Bass and “Electric” Eric Evans signed a tag team contract to PRIME. Metallica could not touch them in this new agreement, as they were playing the songs they love in arenas full of wrestling fans, not rock and roll clubs. The story does not end for Jason and Eric, but only continues to grow larger. For now however, they are just happy to rock. Eric: It’s a great feeling, being able to play here again. I…I can’t describe it. Jason: If you asked me last year if I thought we could be playing in the states to crowds this big, I would have told you, no way man. Bro, lemme tell you a secret though, these shows are way bigger than anything we played as rock stars. Eric: Turn on the lights, and put on the tights…it’s time for a rock show baby!
