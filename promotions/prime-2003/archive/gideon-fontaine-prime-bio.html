Gideon Fontaine Biography Nicknames 'The Osaka Assassin' Gideon Fontaine or Gideon 'The G-Man' Fontaine Hometown L.A. California and Osaka, Japan Birthday January 12, 1973 Height 6 ft 9 Weight 271 Biography The Story of Gideon Fontaine Gideon is the youngest son of Alan Fontaine. Alan has been grooming his oldest son Bruce to eventually take over the family fortune. Alan had no interest in young Gideon and sent him to Japan to be educated and tutored. This would benefit Gideon in the long run but age 5 would be the start of the worst years of his life. He was also enrolled in the martial arts at the age of 5 learning Zangi-Shinginkin-Ryo (Swordmanship favored by the Samurai) and Muei Thai under the master Hiroshiro Tanimoto. Gideon became very distant and withdrawn. He was at a foreign land, struggling to learn the language, studies were tough, he had no friends, and the other students hated him. He was sparring with students that had over 2 years on him and were able to understand what master Tanimoto was saying (Excerpt #1). He devoted himself to his studies in the martial arts. At age 11 he was entered into advanced training. This training usually happens after 10 years not 6 (Excerpt #2). The advanced training was a mix of other forms of martial arts which are commonly faced such as Kick Boxing, Boxing, Jiu Jitsu, Judo and even Shoot Wrestling. He was also taught Shotokan Karate to add to his impressive skill in Muei Thai and Zanji-Shinjinkin-Ryo. He completed the Japanese equivalent of grade 9 at the age of 12. He was given 2 years to himself and trained nearly non stop in a very strict training regiment to gain muscle mass and more speed. After the 2 years Gideon was enrolled in Matsuhiro High school. He was confident and had a very good grasp of the language and now spoke it as fluently as any native. At age 14 he was at the same age as the other students and joined several sports teams. He was a legend in basketball. He could sink a 3 pointer from half court. His foul shot percentage was a unbelievable 97.674%. His 3 point percentage was at 89.36% and many were from half court. He was good at baseball as well leading the league in hits and homeruns. This was the time he earned his nickname the G-Man (Excerpt #3). He would keep the nickname through his adult life. When he graduated at age 17 he returned back to America and his Sensei came with him to L.A. He trained as strictly as ever and took a wide variety of classes at L.A. University. He took up Law and Psychology. He was required to learn Business related skills as well focusing on Computer Operation + Programming as well. He had nearly no free time to pursue hockey, basketball and baseball. After he completed the required 3 years of programs he now had the necessary free time to go for his own goals. He was able to join the sports teams now. He was popular with the ladies before and now he was a sports star and had gotten a little bit of fame. This would not be without a price though. A rival Team confronted Gideon outside a local bar and threatened him. Gideon replied to the 6 of them. 'I don't want any trouble. I have completed 14 years of training in Zangi-Shinjnkin-Ryo and Muei Thai KickBoxing I have been trained in Shotokan Karate from age 11. Any confrontation could result in serious injury'. Gideon was right (Excerpt #4). He was taken to jail but was freed in only 7 hours by his father. He was forbidden to 'go clubbing' or he would be disowned. This didn't bother him much but he still needed the money to pay for his education so he obeyed. The next 2 years were long and Gideon was isolated from any contact outside of the University. He took an interest in University football and hockey and followed both. He now had a degree in corporate and contract Law. He was 20 when he went to Vegas and became a Kick Boxer to earn his own way. He was told that if he did he would be cut off from the family fortune. The shouting was so intense by Alan that he suffered a fatal heart attack. He was not allowed to go to the funeral and was even given legal notice by the family that he was not to go within 100 ft of the funeral (Excerpt #5). Gideon didn't go to the funeral despite Tanimoto's urgings. Tanimoto helped Gideon through this emotional ordeal. Tanimoto disapproved of the KickBoxing career but Gideon explained that it would give him a chance to test himself against World class opponents. Since the matches are televised he could see himself in action and improve his style from an observer's point of view. His first 3 matches ended at 54 seconds, 33 seconds, and 1 minute 27 seconds all in the first round (Excerpt #6). Tanimoto was a 'trainer' for Gideon and was disappointed by his showboating for the fans. He considered this disrespectful and mocked the seriousness that unarmed combat is supposed to have. In his rookie year he had a record of 9-0 with 9 KOs. He won the WKBA championship on August 12 after only 2 years as a fighter. He defended the belt remaining undefeated for 6 years. With a record of 39-0 with 36 KOs he was to defend the title against Alexi Stradvich 'The Russian Tank' a man who was termed unbeatable. No one has even hurt the man in over 17 years of fighting. The controversial end was a biased decision, which cost Gideon his title (Excerpt #7). Gideon was bitter and depressed. He had a lot of money from the fights invested in stocks and local businesses. He now had a substantial amount of money to work with. He was now 23 and had no direction to go. Tanimoto decided that Gideon was ready to go to Thailand to compete in the Pit Fights and the secret Kumate. He used the name Frank Dux as an alias, which worked until he met Neil Whittaker (Fracas). Neil is a fan of Kickboxing and recognized Gideon easily despite the short hair and the new hair color. The 2 talked for hours about their reasons for being for being there. Gideon used the Kumate as a test and Neil was there for competition. Gideon talked about the upcoming Kumate and how it would be for his Sensei Tanimoto. Neil said he was in it for the challenge. Neil was eliminated in the quarterfinals. He cheered on Gideon who won the tournament and the ceremonial Katana (Excerpt #8). The Katana is a sacred weapon to the Japanese it is a symbol of honor and is generally reserved for the Samurai caste. Gideon was the first Gaijin (non-Japanese person) to win the Kumate in over 200 years. He offered the sword to his sensei Tanimoto but he would not accept it. 'You have proven your skill and earned it. To accept the sword would insult the effort and skill you used to win the sword. You are the only non-Japanese person who deserves the honor of the Katana sword. I will get a Wakizashi (Samurai short sword) to match your sword and you will be a true Samurai with the Daisho (The paired weapons of Katana+Wakizashi this is reserved only for the Samurai caste) to prove it.' Gideon bowed overcome by emotion at his master's words. Gideon continued to compete for the 2 year period. his record was 94-0 81KOs. He became friends with Neil and left when he did for the States undefeated. They have always kept in touch since then and talk regularly. Gideon was now 25. He was home for less then a year when his brother was killed in a skiing accident leaving him the heir of the family fortune. He had the family lawyer's help to manage his family's estate. Things were about to change he was offered the role of Tyler Stone in Showdown in Tokyo. This would start his movie career. In this movie he was the leader of a western mob trying to take over Tokyo. This was a great role for Gideon he could speak fluent Japanese and had the 'look' of a Mafia hitman (Excerpt #9). This is when he started wearing the Black suit and tie look regularly. The movie generated huge box office sales in both Japan and North America. He was to star in several other action roles and get recognition as the top action draw in years. People considered him a fake martial artist which made him and Tanimoto very annoyed by the press. Gideon could do his own stunts and dub his own dialogue in Japanese or English. He starred in Sudden Impact, The Dragon, Blood Sport IV, and Lone Wolf. These never took off despite his efforts. They were poorly made movies designed to use Gideon as a draw to sell tickets. It caused him to lose credibility and marketing draw. Tanimoto sensing that Gideon was growing soft and losing his edge decided to push him towards getting his WKBA belt back. Gideon didn't want to go back until Neil started pushing the idea too (Excerpt #10). After several years in Hollywood and a long layoff Gideon was 27 and started his KickBoxing career again. He signed himself to fight 2 fights in one night against different opponents (Excerpt #11). The competors were high quality but Gideon beat them in under a minute each. This showed that he was back and sharper then ever. This also should have set up the obvious rematch between him and the current champ Stradvich. Gideon was being attacked by the press and couldn't get the fan support to force the rematch to be signed. Gideon fought more matches and ended his career with a record of 43-1 39KOs. He was now 28 and not doing much but managing his family holdings and a little bouncing in New York for fun. That is when Neil called him and asked him to manage his career in UJW. That is when the G-Man was started his wrestling career. 3 days after his 29th birthday he debuted in UJW against Kevin Nash the WCW and WWF Tag and World champ. Gideon beat him in record time and a star was born. He has won 11 straight 1 on 1 matches and all but 2 were by submission victory. At the time he left he was the Web champ and had a very impressive singles record with only one blemish. The blemish was the 4-man elimination match for the HC title. Beast booked himself that night to face the injured Trash, Badd Ass, and Gideon. All 3 of the opponents were much less then 100% and fell to the fresh Beast and the Death submission hold. If Badd Ass and Gideon hadn't been 3 days from their grueling death match and Trash wasn't recovering from injuries things could have been different. Wrestling fans still speculate on what may have been. The good thing was that his friend Neil would later get the belt from Beast and become a hardcore legend. During this time Neil and Gideon were part of opposing stables. Gideon was part of a stable called the OutKasts. The name of the Alliance came from the fact that they were an 'Alliance' of the 2 greatest stables in the UJW at the time. Before leaving Gideon was asked to job the Web title to a promising young star Mad Dog. Since his contract expired Gideon wasn't required to fight. Gideon agreed under the following stipulations that it would be non-title. Gideon argued that Methos could still have a Web title match but it wouldn't be for the same belt he had. Neither Methos or Gideon was satisfied with the final decision but it was something that they could agree on. Gideon's plans in the LWA are unknown but it has become public that he wants the LWA Web title to match the one he brought over from UJW. This is what Gideon thought of a few of the guys in the UJW. LWA was problems from the beginning. Beast now had a group that followed his every word called the Empire. Despite all of the interference and biased decisions he and the Ex-MH dominated their matches against the Empire. He had a match against the Empire's golden boy Fear that was lost due to Executioner and Beast attacking Gideon even breaking his finisher the Painkiller. Executioner was pinned by Gideon but had the ref restart the match after threatening the ref. Gideon won the tag belts from the Troops of Doom after Trash gave the belts to Gideon and his partner at the time Fallen Angel. Trash was a member of the Troops of Doom who had been betrayed by his fellow T.O.D. members Executioner and Fear and got revenge by using the 3 man tag rules against them (excerpt #12). Fallen Angel betrayed Gideon and other Ex-MH members Neil and Trash. This led to The Alliance taking on his former partner Fallen Angel and a partner of his choice. The Alliance got the tag belts that night giving Gideon his second tag reign without losing the belts. Gideon finally got the Web title after several swerves against him against 'Bad' Barry Horowitz and the former champ Silver King. He currently is the LWA web champ. Wrestling Style Gideon is a Martial artist. He has trained since the age of 5. His style is based on Martial arts and Submission fighting. Over the years he has expanded his style which he calls 'The way of the Malevolent Fist'. This style combines Muei Thai and Karate with a comprehensive knowledge of several grappling styles taking moves from judo, jiu-jitsu, sambo, aikido, and pro wrestling styles. Neil Whittaker (Fracas) has sharpened his skills in grappling quite a bit. Gideon while comfortable and fully capable of taking a guy's head off with a strike prefers the submission victory to a KO. Finisher Move The Painkiller (Wakigatame) Armbar Finisher Description Gideon is a master of the Painkiller armbar. He executes it differently then the Fujiwara version. He focuses on the elbow working to hyper extend the arm at the elbow seriously injuring it ripping tendons and pulling it out of socket. When it is done by Gideon he immobilizes the bicep in between his arm and side bringing it up to his armpit. With the bicep held in place Gideon then holds the wrist with one hand to prevent the arm from turning to ease the pressure. With a hold of the wrist and his free arm he pulls up on the forearm and wrist of the trapped arm putting as much pressure as possible to the elbow of that trapped arm. The arm triangle choke (or simply arm triangle) is a generic term describing chokeholds in which the opponent is strangled in between his or her own shoulder and the performers arm. This is as opposed to the regular triangle choke, which denotes a chokehold using the legs, albeit with a similar mechanism of strangulation against the opponent's own shoulder. An arm triangle choke where the performer is on the side of the opponent and presses a forearm into opposite side of the neck of the opponent is called a side choke, such as from the kata-gatame hold. Standard Wrestling Style: Gideon’s style involve a lot of studying and preparation. When possible he likes to prepare for his matches ahead of time with Neil. They analyze the opponent to see if they telegraph any of their moves and exactly how they are telegraphed. Next they study each move for flaws and come up with counter moves for each one. They practice the counters for his opponent's favorite moves learning how they can be anticipated and stopped cold. They also watch the most previous matches and look for any injuries. With this knowledge Gideon is prepared for the match and has a plan in mind. Gideon then plays cat and mouse with his opponent sizing him up for opportunities the tapes didn't pick up on. Next Gideon softens up the opponent with striking moves and countering moves testing his strategy. Next comes the wear down moves to slow down the opponent and to set up a submission victory on the weakest point he notices. If Gideon fails to find a weak spot he sets up the Painkiller Armbar. Gideon prefers to win by submission rather then using his striking skills to KO the opponent. Gideon can be downright sadistic to merciless when wearing down a weak point. Since Gideon the UWL he has been using power moves in his style which adds a bit of an edge and unpredictability. When his opponent is trying to dodge a strike or avoid a lock up and gets grabbed by the neck and chokeslammed they don't know what Gideon is going to try next and has to be even more cautious. Gideon is not a power wrestler by nature so he only tosses in the odd power move to throw the opponent off their game. Gideon has 4 wrestling styles which he uses regularly. 1: Shoot wrestling: Gideon shoots in and goes after a limb and tries to weaken it. Prefers the Painkiller Armbar and the Katahajime (Tazmission) but he will go after the knee, ankle, or neck. Gideon likes getting in many submissions and prefers the submission victory to any other type of wins. A very wide variety of submission skills including those from the martial arts as well. 1: The most important thing for this style is for Gideon to use his speed. While Gideon has not got an amateur wrestling background he is familiar with Judo, Aikido, and Shoot Wrestling takedowns as part of his Martial Arts training. Neil Fracas is perhaps one of the better wrestling technicians today. He has been drilling Gideon over the years with counters for his shoots and how those counters are countered. When he takes a person down he goes straight to a submission. Favored submissions are the ankle lock, kneebar, keylock elbow lock, heel hook, and chokes. While going for a submission he will hit the target area with forearms and knees to tense it up. These submissions are not put on to get the submission so much as to wear the opponent down though he will take a submission early out of practicality. 2: Counter style: This is totally devastating against a big man. He counters moves and strikes and weakens them for a submission. If someone goes for a shin kick Gideon would legdrag them or use a dragon screw legwhip. If a clothesline is attempted then a single armbreaker or Painkiller armbar might be applied. This is mainly to demoralize because not only are you countering them you are weakening them and put the fear of submissions in their mind. 2: This style is very self-explanatory. Person tries a move or a strike and is put into a submission or taken down. When an opponent leaves their arm open Gideon will go after it with the Painkiller. The slower the move the easier it is to employ this style. Gideon loves to demoralize opponents by stopping their offense dead in its tracks. 3: Power/Technical style: This is more of a cockiness thing then a regular style. If he figures he can toss in the odd power move he does. While it isn’t part of his style he does possess a very sudden chokeslam. His Overhead Belly to Belly, German Suplex, and Powerbomb are very fast and vicious as well. 3: This isn’t necessarily limited to pure power. He generally uses this style as an add on to his other styles. How heavily this part of his style is used depends a lot on his mood. Gideon may decide to do a lot of suplexes and moves like the STO and Flatliner. There have been a few times where he used an opponent’s own style against him just to say I’m not only better then you I can do your moves better then you can as well. 4: Street Fight style: Generally not used but has been when he has been particularly annoyed with someone. He will delivers Muei Thai style knees to do damage to the body to make things harder for them to breathe and Muei Thai elbows to bust someone open. Then he will either continue the punishment to a point where the referee steps in or just flat out lay the person out with a kick or punch. 4: This is perhaps the most brutal of Gideon’s styles. Gideon’s striking has been honed over nearly 30 years. Gideon’s elbow strikes are not generally planing on knocking out opponents. If Gideon throws elbows he wants to bust the opponent up. Knee lifts and shin kicks to the ribs are designed to demoralize the opponent by making it hard for them to breathe. Shin kicks are to weaken the opponent’s legs so they can’t move and eventually not even be able to stand. The ones to really worry about are the punches and kicks.
