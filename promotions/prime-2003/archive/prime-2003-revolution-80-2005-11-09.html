ReVolution 80 ---- The Next Big Thing The tropical theme might no longer apply, but turmoil is still in the air. A rippling undercurrent of unrest still rumbles through the foundations of PRIME and without adding unnecessary drama, things haven't been going according to plan as of late. Angelo Deville might have been earmarked as main player and a future Universal Champion, but he'd called time on his tenure with the company following the disappointment of his second ever loss in PRIME, coupled with other tensions that involved the fall out from his brief relationship with the Negasonic Lupine, Karina Wolfenden. Then there was Nova, arguably the biggest fan favorite currently on the payroll. From the early days of battling with old adversaries like Big Poppa, to challenging for the top prize in business, Nova had created a nice niche from himself at the top of the promotion... that was until Tropical Turmoil. Injury put paid to that, leaving the former Advantage Wrestling legend on the sidelines until the end of the year at least. Then there was Ellis Easton, a fast rising (if unappreciative) performer who was also out of the picture at present as well as the former PCW alumni of Boda, Trashcan Man and Jon Kano who all called time on their careers. Just like last year (despite different circumstances) the roster was undergoing a period of severe change and somewhere, there was a dropped ball to be picked up and ran with. Last year the likes of Black Angel, Tony Zatarok and Munson took that ball in their arms and ran with it, whilst twelve months on, that task is down to a new batch of superstars or not so new as the case might be. Those fans who hold a memory duration past twelve months might well remember a certain Hoyt Williams making it to the Jewel In The Crown Final. His (along with Joey Troy's) defeat to Munson might have delayed his ascension to the summit, but now YOUR Personal Jesus reigned supreme as the Universal Champion. Usually the rollercoaster metaphor was only applied to ReVolution and not the company itself, although that terminology would probably be an exaggeration of the current situation. This was an annual blip that management had hoped they would avoid this year, but regardless of the actions taken, it's deja vu all over again. Somewhat reduced TV ratings, waning house show attendances and lower turnover were a cause for concern, yet not a critical one. No one ever said the road to success didn't contain the occasional pothole. When the proverbial chips are down, you naturally consider your options. Perhaps indulge in a little self-reflection? Taun Pham and his father had done just that in the past month, that included exploring one particularly controversial option. Negotiations began in secret, even avoiding the keen eye of the internet dirt sheets as possibility of PRIME merging with Core Wrestling became more of a reality. Following the events of a pre-Colossus II edition of ReVolution, the PRIMEates should be more or less familiar with the head man of cW. Who could forget that chaotic night in Madison Square Garden where Matt Lanier and his posse of Core superstars took advantage of the "invitation" to the PTC Global Title match and sparked a brawl of pier six proportions. Whilst the full scale war escalated to a point where members from nearly all the PTC promotions were trading blows, backstage Lanier and co made their getaway, but not before the owners of two of the biggest promotions in the land laid eyes on one another. Taun Pham and Matt Lanier haven't met face to face since, however that minor detail could well be addressed tonight in Seattle. It seemed the proprosal from Pham and his father appealed to the Core supremo. Much like PRIME, Core was enduring it's own lean period and the concept of not only doubling his capital, but eliminating a key member of the competition was too good not to at least explore. Representitives from both companies had performed the initial legwork, but now it was time for the men in power to broker the deal once both parties had terms that were mutually acceptable. Should Lanier feel he holds the strongest bargaining posture and thus view PRIME as an entity to be consumed rather than united with, the potential merger would be dead in the water. On the other side of the table, Pham and his father want to preserve a semblance of their own promotions identity and any sacrifices made on their part, should be equaled by Core's representatives. Their view was a completely new promotion, a super promotion if you will combining talents like Jason Hughes, Munson, Nick Worrell and Fred Fusion with Hoyt Williams, Killean Sirrajin, Brandon Youngblood and Vangelus Olsig. Until this night such a unification of promotions remained nothing more than an idea or perhaps a smark's wet dream. In less than two hours time, it could become reality.
