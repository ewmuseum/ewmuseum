Little Trouble In Big PRIME Written By Sebastian Touching down on the tarmac outside of the Pepsi Center, Taun Pham exhaled in order to see his breath hang in the air, before escaping towards the atmosphere. This bitter night in Denver seems to be far less welcoming than last weeks forray into the warmer Calafornian climate and in comparison is the polar opposite of the PRIME product at this current point in time. In other words, PRIME is HOT! Ratings are up, revenue is up, general interest is up, the companies reputation is through the roof and the early buyrates suggest that Colossus II will break the record previously set by last years event making it the biggest grossing PPV the promotion has held. Indeed even the smarks are buzzing at the prospect of seeing Brandon Youngblood take on Nova, Killean Sirrajin defending his title against Hoyt Williams or last weeks hastily arranged Angelo Deville versus Karina Wolfenden, not to mention all the other pieces that were falling into place like the newly bolstered Tag Team division. Having contracted talent working other shows wasn't exactly hurting PRIME's profile either, exposing their talent to new audiences through mediums such as ACW's recent End Game event, which was dominated by one Seymour Almassy. Almassy was one name that was yet to be lured to PRIME, although in truth the "big name" tag was becoming a tiresome one. With a roster containing talent such as GTT3 Winner Angelo Deville, aW legend Nova, Jolt Triple Crown Winner Brandon Youngblood, PCW Hall Of Fame inductee Jon Kano, former fWo Hardcore Champion Aimz and many others, such a label was inevitable. It didn't bother Hin See one jot, he'd got to a point where he could collect marquee names for fun. His son on the otherhand viewed the label as more of a slur, an implification that PRIME merely hosted talent rather than created it. Apparently the likes of Ignatius Lisieux, Emilio Rage, Ian English and numerous others don't count for whatever reason. Regardless of how many promising talents plucked from the indies or rough diamonds from rival promotions are nurtured or how many "legends" received a new lease of life in the company, the label would probably stick. It didn't help that it was frequently reapplied by various disgruntled superstars who either failed to score a developmental deal or an ex-worker whose big fish in little pond status didn't cut the proverbial "it" once arriving or failing that, some pro-wrestling hack who views "big" and "success" as failings and would instead offer unreserved praise to a promotion who pushed a lockeroom of human faeces into the ring night after night that connected with the "real" fans as opposed to some form of corporate wrestling machine whos larger fanbase ovbiously originate from some virtual realm. Despite the inaccuracy of such comments, it was still irking to find some attention starved individual filling their web blog with poisoned statements when if such determination and focus were to be channeled into their in-ring work, they might actually be something noteworthy on their own merits. Still it could be worse, they could have to put up with the same amount of crap that the fWo are forced to endure. PRIME wasn't at that level yet, the regardless of what quality you attributed to the current fWo product, they were still the market leader, the top brand. Sheer longetivity would back that up, but at least PRIME was going in the right direction now. In a past riddled with front office conflict, skullduggery, money problems, comas, fake shootings and red headed english rogues, PRIME was slowly starting to find the key to fWo's (and other similar promotions) success... stability. Add to that a considerable fanbase created over two years, not to mention a reputation for devising the most innovative gimmicks in the business, plus the excessive funds from a successful Japanese technology firm, several marquee names and a unique timeslot on a network primarily known for adult programming. It all leaves you with a highly marketable, runaway sports entertainment juggernaut. Taking all that into consideration, you'd have thought that Taun Pham might be able to muster a smile as he arrived. Instead the expression on his face is one of dejection. Slamming the limo door shut, the co-owner makes his way towards the arena entrance with his laptop aka his new best friend, tucked underneath his right arm. Last week it seemed he had found a breakthrough after his conversation with Tony Rolo's wife after Allison had disclosed something about a "hospital operation" that Pham had not been previously aware of. The details of which, could have provided him with a big piece to slot neatly into one of the gaping holes in an elaborate puzzle that the co-owner had created. Of course this being PRIME, things were just not that simple. It appeared that large portions of Tony Rolo's medical record were missing. Instead of an answer, Pham had found little more than another question. To make matters more confusing, he had found that with a little extra research, several superstars medical files seemed incomplete, including the currently sidelined Emilio Rage as well as Ignatius Lisieux and other longer serving superstars. Either PRIME has some of the most incompetent medical logging procedures around or something else was amiss. As you'd expect, Pham favored the latter explanation. Pushing through a set of double doors, the co-owner makes his way through the backstage area where some of the aforementioned superstars are shown roaming the area as production staff scuttle backwards and forwards with handfuls of final show notes and running times. It's a typical Wednesday night as showtime approaches. Blaine Blair: I'm sorry, but I can't understand silent frustrations... maybe if you could get a piece of paper and write them down in something that isn't Spanish, then I... Cimmerian threw up his arms a gesture of frustration and submission, turning away from Blaine after failing to properly convey his feelings about having his dark match cut because Dragon Silver and mischievously switched around the match assignments and thus booked himself into the rather non-lucrative encounter with Scott Woods in the place of the masked Mexican mute. The intern sighed, scrawling something on his clipboard, before glancing upwards to see Pham standing a few feet away. Blaine Blair: Oh, hey Mr Pham er... how are you? The conversation between the two was still awkward in the wake of what happened back at Overkill. Taun Pham: I'm good thanks Blaine... you? Blaine Blair: Yep... I'm good too... well other than rushing around here and trying to tie up the usual loose ends. Cue some forced laughter. Taun Pham: Look Blaine, I'm sorry the other week if I was a bit "off" with you... it's just that I need to work things out my way and that involves not worrying Pops about it. I appreciate that you're concerned for my well-being, I just need you to trust me on this. Taking a few seconds to compile an answer in his head, Blaine looks at the far wall for a few seconds, then back at the co-owner with a sincere smile beginning to form on his face. It seemed wise for both men to bury their current issues, particularly since Pham might well have to utilise Blaine in order to resolve the issue of missing medical records. Blaine Blair: Thank you, Mr Pham. I... I... Taun Pham: How about we drop all this awkward pretense and move on... The co-owner outstretches his hand, the one not still wrapped following the burns sustained in the Eve/coffee incident several weeks ago in New York, extending it to Blaine. Blaine Blair: Agreed. The pair shake hands, exchanging warm smiles until a loud "OOOOOOOOH!" is heard in the background, the sort of noise that would normally be attributed to an episode of the Jerry Springer Show. Relinquishing their hands, the co-owner and his intern pivot so see some of the ringcrew sitting on a crates in a circular fashion, downing beers with their eyes transfixed on a small portable TV monitor. Moving in, Pham instantly recognises the face on the screen. Matt Lanier. That's right, Matt Lanier, the creator of Core Wrestling. For many of the PRIME viewers who don't know, Core screens its weekly show (PRIDE) on the same night as ReVolution and judging by the fact that you're seeing this, you're not watching Pride, unlike the rather elite (elite being code for lazy and out of shape) backstage crew who Pham and Blaine have just stumbled upon. Pham remembers Lanier well, he met him face to face on the edition of ReVolution that tee'd off the road to Colossus in Madison Square Garden. That of course was about two minutes or so before Tony Rolo returned to the arena and started laying waste to those around him, including Spearbustering Hin See's hapless son. "OOOOOOOOOOOOOHHH!" Another "Oh no he didn't!" noise rose from the gathered rabble. Pride was currently screening a prerecorded version of a "shoot" interview conducted by Lanier himself. It captured Pham's attention, even if the subject matter for the most part wasn't that relevant to him. Obviously he'd missed the start, Lanier was currently halfway through commenting on Tim Shipley, a superstar who Hin See turned down for a part time contract, also at the MSG show. It continues with a few remarks about his former promotion Fluorescent Light-tube Wrestling... or something close to that and the AWC. The only thing that came to mind when the AWC's name is brought up, was an incident earlier in the week where some draft artwork for PRIME's post Colossus PPV was leaked onto the internet and some opportunist decided to use it as a reference point when commenting on the companies upcoming tour. "Those hoping for any 'Tropical Turmoil' gimmick matches are, therefore, out of luck." It was either a lack of professionalism and basic respect or "Tropical Turmoil style gimmick" was a new and long-winded way of typing "decent". There wasn't too much else of note, Core's PTC Global Title disrespecting antics (screened LIVE on ReVolution) had pretty much put the final nail in the coffin of their PTC relationship, they were now part of some other circuit... the one that sounds like they're more involved in producing softcore porn DVD's than dealing with wrestling promotions. Throw in a minor reference to PRIME, including a small comment of personal preference against some of the latest signings, but a general mark of approval and that was about your lot. It wasn't so much of a shoot promo as opposed to shooting fish in a barrel. Stepping forward, Pham switched the monitor off, which came as a surprise to the backstage grunts who hadn't realised that he'd joined the watching group. At least it spared them the risk that former PRIME, redneck, crazed, janitor Munson might appear on the screen next and also effectively terminated about 12% of Pride's overall viewing audience for the night. Taun Pham: Show's over gentleman, I believe there's some work to be done as... "SLAM!" Eve slams the door behind and marches out into the expanse of the backstage area, carrying her belongings and looking in the foulest of moods. The body langauge was a sure fire sign that something was wrong and the path of the Intense Division head suggested she was leaving the arena in a hurry. Blanking the onlookers, she she storms past Pham and Blaine and barges her way past the English Luchadore as she makes her exit. Sighing, Pham turns back to Blaine. Taun Pham: I guess it's going to be another one of those nights. Interview With A Legend Written By Jon Kano Nick: Earlier this week, our resident chief interviewer and broadcast journalist, Matt Mills, caught up with Jon Kano at his Las Vegas home, shortly after Mr. Kano returned from Florida where he has been attending to his former friend turned enemy turned friend Boda, whom is of course recovering from injuries. Richard: I still don't get their relationship, Kano and Boda hate each other, now they are all made up? Did I miss a meeting? Nick: It is a strange situation, no doubt, but lets just watch the footage from earlier in the week. Earlier this week... The interview is taking place in Kano's lavish living room. In the center of the room is a glass topped coffee table with golden trim. Matt Mills, in his finest grey K-mart suit is seated in a black leather L-Z Boy chair across the table from Jon Kano, in a "Kano ROCKS~!" t-shirt complete with baggy blue jeans and Doc Martin boots, and Kano's wife Kristin, in a tight pink t-shirt with blue jeans, and leaning on her husband's shoulder. Matt: Jon, first off, thanks for inviting me into your home. I have to ask the burning question right off the bat, what the hell is going on between Boda and yourself right now? Kano just looks at Matt and smiles. Kano: Matt, what ever do you mean? I guess a lot of people don't understand this whole situation because it has changed so much so quickly. Let me try to explain this best I can. Chris and I go back six years or so. We were two of the first in PCW. We were tag team partners and we traveled together. You get extremely close to people that you travel with. We began to hang out more and more. Hell we took vacations together. Kristin and I would go to his and Stacey's house and vice versa. Chris and I were best friends. Now lets fast forward to the last incarnation of PCW. Matt: The part where you screwed over Boda? Kano: Let me explain that. With that last incarnation, it was on Boda and myself to carry PCW until we could establish the new stars. We had to carry the company, plain and simple. That isn't being cocky, it is the truth. The best way to do that is the old best friends, better enemies story. We hated to do it, but we had to. It was a business decision. Unfortunately, Chris and I couldn't carry PCW any longer and we walked away from it. I was done. I left everything I had left in PCW, honestly I was exhausted. I was perfectly content to sit my legendary ass right here and run Kano Enterprises. I was happy. Kano pauses to put his arm around his wife and kiss the top of her head. Kristin: So was I. No more of having Jon on the road all the time. We were like a normal family. We were expecting. It seemed like everything was perfectly fine and right, finally. Kano: And to be quite honest, I didn't watch more than maybe an hour or two of wrestling every couple of weeks. I still talked to Chris about once a week, but I was very cautious, knowing that the phone call was coming where Chris needed me. But lets skip over that crap and go to three weeks ago. Matt: When Boda was hit by the truck? Kano: No when I stubbed my toe rolling out of bed, yeah when he was hit by the truck. When it happened, I had mixed emotions at first. I was scared, hell I was inches away from getting hit, but thankfully I was safe. Then I looked down and saw his lifeless body and I thought he was dead. And death itself doesn't bother me, I have seen people die before when I was in the Army. But this wasn't some nameless stranger, it was Chris Sloboda, a man whom I hate and love all in the same breath. There is a fine line between love and hate, and I found it impossible to hate a man whom could be laying dead at my feet. I wasn't seeing Chris when I looked down, I was seeing his daughters. Matt: You went to the hospital with Boda that night, what happened? Kano: Chris was in surgery most of the night and then into intensive care. The whole time he was in surgery, I was on the phone to Chris' ex-wife Stacey, whom I chartered a jet for her and the two girls to come to the hospital. I then was on the phone to Kristin the rest of the night. Stacey woke me up in the morning as I had fallen asleep in the waiting room. I was set to walk then. I didn't really want to see Chris, because well, our history and such, I felt it would be very uncomfortable. Stacey grabbed me by the arm and forced me into Chris' room. We talked at great length about a lot of things and now we are slowly rebuilding a tolerable relationship. Matt: What did you and Boda talk about? Kano: That is between Boda and myself. Matt: Shifting gears a little bit, on ReVolution this week you will face Brandon Youngblood. Any thoughts? Kano takes his arm from around his wife and leans forward. Kano: Look at me. I am supposed to be a washed up legend at age 29. Youngblood is one of the hottest young superstars in wrestling today. What a matchup this could be. Young lion taking out the old lion. My final curtain call. Bullshit. I believe I have lost my gift. Whatever I had in years past that made me that damn good that gave me the right to be called a legend is gone. But I still got something to offer, I do believe. Matt: What do you mean? Most people would retire at this point. Kano: What the fuck am I supposed to do? This is what I know, so to me, this is my life. I am too young to retire for good. I am professional wrestler, damnit. I may not have what it takes anymore to be super-competitive, but I can still entertain. I hope. I think. I pray. Now get out of my house, I gotta get ready to catch a flight for ReVolution.
