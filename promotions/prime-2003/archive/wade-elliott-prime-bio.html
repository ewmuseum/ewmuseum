Wade Elliott Biography Nicknames The Bad Dog, The Blue Collar Brawler, The 'Bama Bruiser, The Southern Sparkplug, The Country Boy, Drifter (by Lindsay Troy), Country (by Tyler Rayne), The Son of a Bitch. Special Moves Big Boot, Spear followed by mounted punches, Mudhole Stomps, Southern Hospitality, The South Shall Rise Again. Other Moves Physical Appearance -Wade is the definition of "rugged." He is by no means a monster, but years of labor, life on the road, and bad company have turned his body in a six foot, four inch, two-hundred and fifty-sven pound piece of iron. Where most wrestlers sport chiseled six packs, Wade keeps a steel plate. There is nothing "smooth" about Wade. Hands like sandpaper, arms covered with scars. He keeps short brown hair and a bushy brown goatee. His eyes are of a sharp blue. On his left pectoral muscle wave two crossed Confederate flags Ring Attire -Wade will step onto the entrance ramp shirtless with a pair of old, faded, worn out, somewhat baggy jeans and a pair of just as dirty and worn out brown, steel-toed work boots. Personality -Wade returns a changed man. Still the Bad Dog, for sure, but he no longer feels like the world left him behind. -Oddly enough, he'll probably be saying "fuckin'," and "cunt," a lot less. -Still vicious, and still a brawler's brawler, Wade will display a much more controlled persona in PRIME's final run. -He's finally, truly learned the meaning of respect. Wade in the Ring -Wrestling Style: Brawler. A brawler's brawler. Any kind of brawling. EVERY kind of brawling. A basic one-on-one match will result in lots and lots and LOTS of fists, boots, and roughneck brawling tactics. In tag matches, Wade's a stubborn bastard. He'll try to take the opposing team on himself, and doesn't like to tag his partner in very often. For Wade, it's a fight in that ring, not a wrestling match. Writing for Wade in the Ring Here's a few pointers if you end up writing a match including The Bad Dog. -A future nickname for Wade might be "Captain No-Sell." An example might be Wade taking a chair shot square to the head and at the most blinking at the opponent before dropping them. So, go ahead and have him no-sell big, hurty moves when the time is right. -ALSO, where a lot of characters have these match-ending finishers or some such, Wade's gimmick is the ability to kick out of them. These big finishers can and will damage him considerably, of course, but feel free to have him kick out of em' once or twice. -Have him connect all of his signature moves during the course of the match, (see Signature Spots below.) -Feel free to use stairs, security walls, chairs anything and everything at Wade's disposal. There are no rules for Wade, even when there are rules. -Please don't write him going top rope. Period, sans the Super-plex. -If the match involves an opponent Wade's having a gripe with at the moment, he's not going to much care about anything or anyone else. Example being a tag or cluster-fuck type match. Send him after the jackass he's feuding with, and yeah, it will work against him. -No smiling. -In the case of Wade losing a match, please don't write him bitching, moaning or complaining. -Returning to the subject of finishers; I'm not one of those guys who is under the impression that their character's finisher is absolutely un-kickoutable. I don't have a problem if an opponent kicks out of The Rebel Yell as the match dictates, especially in a main event scenario. Strengths -Unmatched toughness and resiliency. Wade is, quite simply, the toughest son of a bitch in the sport. There has been no one with the ability to shrug off blows like the 'Bama Bruiser. It will take everything the opponent has to bring Elliott down for good. You can have this big bad finisher that NOBODY kicks out of and yadda yadda yadda, that's cute and all, but Wade's your exception. Expect to use that big scary move more than once. Prepare for a third. (Now, check it out. This isn't to say that NOBODY can pin Ol' Wade, that's why we have loss records. You're just not going to put him down for very long. The opponent can hit that big bad finisher and pin Wade for the 1 2 3, but you can bet your ass he'll be standing up more than a little pissed a couple seconds after.) -Wade's pretty damn good when weapons come into play. -Wade is very aware of his surroundings, and will use his environment to his benefit. Security walls, stairs, chairs, tables, etc. Guy's got a P.h.D. in barroom brawling. -While Wade is easily subject to tunnel vision, he can multi-task in the right scenario, and is more than comfortable taking on multiple opponents by himself. -A new found sense of personal control. Weaknesses --Adaptability. Wade can brawl and brawl only, and finds it difficult to adapt to wrestling styles that counter his own. -Undisciplined. His brawling is incredibly strong. Everything else is almost non-existent, and he has no drive to train himself in other disciplines. -Zero aerial ability. -Wade's not fast. He can execute maneuvers quickly, but he can't run around the ring like a cruiser-weight. He lumbers around, and gets visibly winded faster than most (heavy smoker). -Haphazard in the ring, with no set game plan. In-Ring Tendencies -A fight's a fight, and there's no such thing as a fair one. -He doesn't wear title belts around his waist or over his shoulder. He always holds them in his right hand, dangling close to the floor. -His weapon of choice is a sledgehammer. Common Spots -All manner of punches, stomps, elbows, knees, head-butts, etc. Clotheslines, DDTs, snapmares, suplexes, basic wrestling. Power Spots -Piledrivers, powerbombs on mid to small opponents, Death Valley Driver, breakers. Aerial Spots -None. Submission Spots -Camel Clutch, Boston Crab, Fishook. He doesn't use submission often, or at all, really, but if and when he does, he likes the simple ones. Hardcore Spots -Use of sledgehammer, chair shots, either of his finishers through tables, trashcans, chains, whatever. Wade is very hardcore dominant, and he'll use any thing in any way possible. Signature Spots -Mudhole Stomps (ala Steve Austin). -Spear followed by mounted punches. -Big Boot to the chest. -Southern Hospitality - Clothesline from Hell. -The South Shall Rise Again - Picture this: Wade's getting the crap beat out of him for a spell, really hammered on, and the opponent leaves him lying on the mat, and takes a second to play the crowd. At this time, Wade will suddenly sit up, rub his head, and much to the opponent's dismay, will stand up and get back to work as if nothing happened. Finishers -The Rebel Yell - Wade will quickly heave the opponent up and over his right shoulder, draping them down his back while holding onto the back of their knees, then swing them violently to the mat. He can hit this from a variety of scenarios. Stare-downs, on the move, while the opponent is moving at him, etc. It's pretty nasty, creates a big boom on the mat, and gets a good "OOHHH!" from the crowd. -Super-Plex - This is more of a hardcore finisher. Wade will use a thundering super-plex through tables, onto chairs, etc. Entrance Music 'Still Unbroken,' by Lynyrd Skynyrd Song Link http://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XCy7IaHNqIs Ring Entrances The lights dim a touch, an ominous hum rising through the arena. The PRIME*View slow fading from a black screen to the outline of a man. As the screen brightens it reveals the man more clearly, clad in the gray uniform of the Rebel Army. And then that guitar hits. "Still Unbroken," by Lynyrd Skynyrd. The soldier grabs hold of a tattered Confederate Flag, breaking into a sprint. He charges up a war-torn hill as the wicked riff from the greatest band to ever play make the PA beg for mercy. The soldier reaches the top of the hill, planting the flag into the ground as the guitar dies off, giving way to the rugged words of Johnny Van Zant while that tattered flag waves on the screen. "Broken bones, broken hearts, stripped down and torn apart. A little bit of rust, I'm still runnin'. Countin' miles, countin' tears, twistin' roads, shiftin' gears. Year after year, it's all or nothin'!!!" And then, that guitar again with the chorus. So loud. So. God. Damn. Loud. Shutting out the arena's fans completely. The PRIME*View ignites to life once again, showcasing a different piece to a puzzle long-gone with each hammering of those strings. The cap of a worn out, steel-toed boot. "But I'm not home!" Roughed up leather belt. "I'm not lost!" Taped fingers clenching into a fist. "Still holdin' on to what I got! Ain't much left, Lord there's so much that's been stolen!" Crossed Confederate flag tattoo, waving proudly on a left pectoral. "Guess I've lost everything I've had!" Left half of a steely blue gaze, eyebrow furled. "But I'm not dead, at least not yet!" Still alone, still alive, still unbroken! I'm still alone, still alive, I'm still unbroken!!!!!" And as that guitar throttles the eardrums, out walks "The Bad Dog," Wade Elliott, letting that Confederate flag tattoo fly proud on his shirtless chest, clad in the usual worn blue jeans and roughed-up steel-toed work boots. A red, white and blue light-show forces the crowd to throw on sunglasses they may have brought with them, burning retinas as they pulse with the guitar. The 'Bama Bruiser continues his march down the ramp as the music slows once more. He walks by the roaring fans, looking for high-fives and pats on the back. He reaches the bottom, starting up the steps as the chorus brings the music back to that brain-rattling noise level. He lumbers to the far turnbuckle, climbing to the second rope, throwing that arm in the air. "I'm still aloooone! Still aliiiive! Still unbrokeeeeennnn!!" He pauses a moment, gaze drawing across the crowd, before hopping down and taking place in his corner, music trailing off and the lights returning to normal.
