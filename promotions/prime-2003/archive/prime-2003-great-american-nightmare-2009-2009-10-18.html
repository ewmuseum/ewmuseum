Great American Nightmare 2009 ---- Performance Evaluation The Great American Nightmare arc will go down as one of the more agonizing in the data logs of Lisa Tyler. Tyler Nelson glorified intentions to take over the company and sought to bring BLT down at every turn, forming an almost unstoppable army of superstars; the men and women slated to be in cushy positions when he acquired power. Even though Lisa considered his followers to be a giant group of fiends, the talent amassed between them was undeniable. She felt the heat from all sides and those who supported her cause ended up getting flattened like pancakes. Tyler Nelson knew he was pushing the right buttons and could be on the verge of seizing control. Lisa had dug herself into a very deep hole with almost no way out. Simply put, her champions don't support her, her roster doesn't support her, and revenue is on a sharp decline. The feelings of doubt only intensify when her head rises from the desk and takes in the surroundings. Her office is smaller than usual, a consequence of endurance. While Great American Nightmare might be a complete sellout, and movie theaters in the surrounding area are featuring the Pay-Per-View for twenty bucks a pop, the Heart O Coliseum only holds 9000 people. In its heyday, PRIME sold out 30,000 to 40,000 seat venues within two minutes. Merchandise couldn't stay on the stands for five seconds before being pinched away by cashiers, who were flinging the shirts and caps into a wild crowd while having money thrown at them like a feature stripper in the club. Now Lisa is a shell of her former self, concealed in the office behind a padlock door and massive one man security team, Dametreyus Fuqueiawytas. She figures it won't be long before even the most loyal dog in the pack wants a bigger bone. Dam stands guard at the door, refusing to let anybody in without knowing the proper password. The man walking down the hall doesn't need a password, and hasn't for the past few years ever since ascending the PRIME ranks. The wave of suits falling in behind him suggests a man of great power, or really awesome bifocals. If one saw him walking down the street, comparisons of a much younger Larry King would hound his ears. It's a comparison he frowns upon, and he is grateful that he doesn't walk on streets or take the bus. Lisa has no idea of his impending arrival because she wasn't informed.
