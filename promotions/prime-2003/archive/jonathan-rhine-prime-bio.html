Jonathan Rhine Biography Wrestler Name Jonathan Rhine Nicknames The New Life Hometown New Orleans, LA Birthday 7/7/85 Height 6'2 Weight 238 lbs Biography Gimmick/Attitude: Jonathan Rhine is the epitome of the fresh face. Twenty-three years young and ready for the world he's only seen before, the very talented young wrestler wants to make the fans crazy and see the bad guys fall. After all, that's how it's always happened on TV, right? Biography: The condensed version is Rhine grew up very typically until middle school, when a rash of violent crimes around his neighborhood made his father nervous, causing him to enroll Rhine in both martial arts classes and the wrestling team. Rhine carried both into high school, and when the academic scholarship he was given to Rice University was taken away after one year due to legal issues back home, Rhine returned home with one desire: to make wrestling his career. He worked as a bag boy in a local grocery store for a few months (where he met his current girlfriend, Katie Malick) until he could earn enough to enlist in the Gray Wrestling Academy, a New Orleans-based academy run by former National Wrestling Council wrestler Foster Nackedy. Nackedy immediately took a liking to Rhine, seeing in him Foster's talent and determination. Foster took him under his wing, teaching him not only technique and discipline but how to live balancing life and wrestling. After two years in his school and wrestling for Foster's school-run promotion, winning the top belt and holding it for his final year there, Rhine graduated and sent out his bio to a few big federations. FUSE bit, and Rhine's life has changed forever. After a year on top of FUSE and the resulting fed SCCW, Rhine won the FUSE title, and then the SCCW Universal Title. He is the current SCCW Universal Champion and appeared on PRIME television to ruin Xavier Kannon's celebration ceremony on Rev 162. Kannon is...how shall we say...less than pleased about it. Headlock to Judo flip 48. Judo Toss to lateral press (pin) Rhine's Benchmark Moves The following is a list of moves that Rhine will use in every match. Just like many faces have a set of trademarks that they will use every time and the fans will respond to, Rhine does most if not all of these things every time he's in the ring (I have listed counters for those who want to add some spice to the matches): 1.) Striking Combo: Rhine's strikes are simply three left hand punches followed by a quick turnaround-elbow to the face, sending the opponent to the mat. Rhine usually follows this by pacing around the ring, getting momentum and getting the crowd pumped up. Ambrose will refer to this as simply three left hands and then...'oh! sends him down with the elbow!' or something similarly generic. Countering this maneuver: To counter this, the opponent will take the first three punches and then duck under the elbow and take control. Of course, the opponent can also duck and be hit by the elbow as Rhine turns around. 2.) Judo Throws and Holds: There are a few moves in his movelist (31, 46, 47, 48) that he will do to slow down a match, like in 46 where he will toss the opponent over and hold onto the arm, turning it into a submission. Ambrose will point out that Rhine has an extensive Judo background and that these tactics wear down the opponent and give into Rhine's tempo for the match. Countering this maneuver: There is no true counter to this, although the opponent can elbow out of 31 and reverse the judo tosses typically. 3.) Corkscrew Dropkick: This is Rhine's secondary move, and sometimes can finish off a match. The technique simply calls for Rhine to climb up the ropes and flip backwards, twisting in mid-air to plant his feet into the opponent's chest. However, a good way that Rhine uses this is when the opponent is dominating him and sends him into the corner. Rhine will jump onto the turnbuckle and quickly backflip into the maneuver, changing the tide of the match and going for the pinfall. Countering this maneuver: Opponents can send a dropkick to Rhine's chest before he can reach them, or they can simply move out of the way. 4.) New Life Moonsault: Rhine usually does this in the beginning of the match, when he's first building momentum. Rhine will perform an overhead belly to belly on the opponent, and when they are down he will raise his arms, getting cheers and turning his back to his opponent. Then, Rhine flips backwards in a moonsault, driving his knee across the opponent's throat. Rhine will go for the cover after hitting this move. Countering this maneuver: Moving. (Duh) 5.) Desperation Cross-Arm Powerbomb: Rhine will be beaten badly, with the opponent having full momentum. Then, they will throw Rhine into the rope and he will come back, duck under an attack, and plant a boot in the opponent's midsection. Then he will grab them, put their head between his legs, pull their arms across their body under their stomach, and lift using their arms. It is a quick snap powerbomb, and Rhine will either fall over and try to collect his wits or he will roll on top for the cover. Countering this maneuver: Rhine will sometimes not have the strength to hit the move and will drop the opponent on him. 6.) Breathtaker: This usually happens early in the match, if the opponent is gaining momentum. The opponent will be attacking him, or something, and Rhine will stop the attack with an elbow or something to the face, causing the opponent to back away, his back to Rhine. Rhine will leap up to the turnbuckle and jump without posing or pausing, flipping in midair then landing right next to his opponent, bringing his head down in a Bulldog as he does so. Scott Ambrose will mark out big time for this move. Countering this maneuver: The opponent can move, and either Rhine will fall on his ass, or Rhine will complete the flip, landing on his feet.
