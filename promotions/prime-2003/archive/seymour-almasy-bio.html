Seymour Almasy Biography Wrestler Name Seymour Almasy Nicknames The Dynast-King (preferred), The Final Fantasy (shunned), The Galbadian Destroyer, Almasy WEAPON Hometown Billed as hailing from Orphan's Cradle. Actually is currently Las Vegas, NV. Birthday July 3, 1981 Height 5'7 Weight 177 lbs Fed Titles Reigning PRIME Intense Champion Fed Awards None yet. Biography 2009 saw Seymour Almasy’s final appearances in both Primetime Central and one of its member feds, Sin City Championship Wrestling. For all of 2010, the return thought inevitable never happened. In fact, 2010 was the first year since 2003 in which Almasy never made an appearance on national television in a wrestling capacity. Beyond only that, the improbable happened. A man virtually stalked by wrestling media for his entire career fell totally and completely off the radar screen. It was enough for many pundits to wonder if Seymour Almasy had actually, finally, willingly retired. 2011, however, sees the return of the Final Fantasy to in ring competition. As usual, there are questions swirling around Almasy: is, for example, this latest return borne not out of a desire for championship glory, but out of financial necessity? Those who know Seymour well claim his finances have always been in order, but far more intelligent men than he have lost fortunes in the past several years. Others speculate that Almasy may have a project on the horizon he wishes to plug. He has been rumored to have been working on his autobiography for years, as well as putting together a wrestling training academy bearing his name. The publicity PRIME could offer him for either exploit could well be more than enough to lure Almasy to the one company he publicly promised to never work for. Or maybe it’s simpler than that. Maybe, after a year off, the wrestling bug bit once more, and with SCCW gone and GCW never friendly, perhaps the only place for Almasy to go was in fact Number One By Definition. Making his return to PRIME on PPV in an interview with Angelica Brooks, Almasy soon find himself embroiled in the fires of the company's top competition, defeating Tom Walczak and Violence Jack before falling to the self-professed best wrestler in the world, Alexandra Pierce. During this run of time, Seymour also became notable for his assault of Jacob McKail. Seemingly done for no reason, Almasy explained that the incident that had caused McKail to be blackballed from the sport hit close to home, as a former independent wrestler who had risen to prominence in that capacity. Vowing to make his "dream job" of PRIME a safer place, Seymour promised to eliminate McKail. The two men traded sneak attacks until Almasy got both a restraining order and Devin Shakur edicts preventing McKail from approaching him. Almasy's early success (and sucking up to the boss) earned him a shot at Lindsay Troy's Intense title. Copious interference from the Codemaster and new heavy Thanatos resulted in Almasy lifting the belt from the Queen of the Ring. Other Feds ACW, UWF, Global Wrestling (a combination of wrestling and ultimate fighting), SCCW, FUSE, tSC ([name withheld]'s 2005-on verse), lots of others. Other Fed Titles PTC Global Champion (3x), PTC Tag Team Champion (with his ex-wife Laura), UWF World Champion, ACW World Champion (2x), ACW Television and Scorpion Fighting Champion (at the same time for much of the reign) Other Fed Awards Winner of the PTC Cruiserweight Tournament and Primetime Invitational Tournament, PTC Co-Wrestler of the Year 2004 (w/ Saij), UWF Hall of Famer, unofficial PTC Hall of Famer, second man ever to hold the UWF Grand Slam (all titles in the company) Wrestling Style Show-offy all arounder. Modern Almasy is less concerned with shooting star presses and 450 splashes than he is in beating you and showing the world how good he is while he does it. He mixes in strikes, grappling moves, aerial attacks, and a smattering of a submission game, all with the unique charisma that has made him loved or hated since his arrival in the business. Other Moves WRESTLING STYLE: Seymour Almasy brings to PRIME a more well-rounded and less risk oriented style than he is generally known for in the past. Fewer chances taken leaves fewer opportunities for an opponent to capitalize. He makes up for his lack of eye-popping flips and twists by being a deceptively hard striker, especially for his size, as well as having moves for practically any situation that one can think up. Versatility is the new name of Seymour’s games. RING PSYCHOLOGY: Almasy will generally work over either the ribs (for the Paradigm Shift) or the head/neck (for the Birth by Sleep) of an opponent. If he’s facing a bigger opponent, he will generally start by trying to take out a leg, and then switch to the desired body part once the leg is weakened. Most of what Seymour does is high impact, so with a weakened opponent, he will try to chain together bigger moves to leave an opponent dazed and susceptible to either finisher. Seymour will, at all costs, try to prevent contests from going out to the floor, where he will almost always be at a distinct disadvantage. He is not an adept cheater primarily due to a lack of practice, but he will use ropes to choke opponents, and the occasional low blow if he can slip it in undetected. When on the defensive, Almasy will go for his arsenal of rollups and cradles to try and fluster an opponent. If he’s lucky, he can steal a win, but even if this isn’t the case, constant pinning predicaments pressure an opponent and lead to mistakes. Almasy can, will, and does tap out when trapped in a submission hold with no way out. He is no man’s martyr and considers it better to live and fight another day. SIGNATURE MOVES: Seymour’s signature moves can be divided into two categories: named, and non-named. Generally, he will only use a handful of named signatures in any given match, anywhere from 2-3 so as to keep opponents off balanced and guessing. His non-named signatures you can expect to see a bit more often, as they are reliable weapons in his arsenal that he likes to go for when the time is right. NAMED: - Dissidia: Cross armed front-flip neckbreaker. Executed with a running start for momentum, or a la a Tornado DDT while seated on the top rope. - Jecht Shot Mark III: Springboard gamengiri, from the second rope back inside the ring. (debuted 2/1/2011 v. Tom Walczak) - Shining Summoner: Step-up, Shining Wizard style, into a short, head-spike huracanrana. Seymour will use it sometimes as a setup for the Paradigm Shift. (debuted v. Violence Jack) - Horns of Valmar: Inverted bridging front facelock. Almasy will throw knees to the head to weaken an opponent for it. Primarily a weardown hold, but he can get the submission with it on occasion. Also his go to move in matches requiring an opponent submit. (debuted v. Violence Jack) - Megido: Springboard Swanton bomb. - Dirge of Cerberus: Roll of the Dice, Last Rites, you know the move. - FIRAGA~!: Time-honored flash paper enabled FIREBALL. Used only rarely when he truly despises an opponent. - The Burning Crusade: Falcon Arrow. Also has a headdrop variant called "Wrath of the Lich King," and a final, spinning headdrop variant serving as an alternate finisher called "the Cataclysm." All of these, obviously, are for use on smaller opponents. - Time Compression: Straitjacket piledriver/straitjacket Pedigree. He uses the latter on opponents he can’t lift for the piledriver. - Blue Magic: After being hit earlier in a contest by an opponent’s signature or finishing maneuver, Seymour will utilize that same move. Occasionally played for comedy (imagine, for example, Almasy trying to hit Crucified and Saved on Hoyt Williams.) NON-NAMED: - Rolling koppou kick (to an opponent in the corner, or tied up in the ropes) - Springboard elbowdrop (Almasy likes to sail 2/3rds of the way across the ring with it) - High-jumping double stomp to the abdomen, from a standing start. - Abdominal stretch, accentuated with punches with the free hand to the ribs. Can also be turned into an abdominal stretch cradle pinning combination. - Spinning backfist, aiming for the opponent’s nose. - Top rope front dropkick, thrown to an opponent’s knees. - Crucifix-style Lungblower. - Inverted Death Valley Driver into the corner. Done to drive the foe’s ribs into the turnbuckles. - Northern Lights suplex, followed by Almasy flipping back to a vertical base, hitting the ropes, and catching the recovering opponent with a low lariat. - Bow and arrow lock around the ringpost, used to torque the back and ribs. Always held to the count of four before the break. - turnbuckle walk-up moonsault bodyblock. Think the Whisper in the Wind setup. Almasy never covers after this, opting instead to pose and bask in the jeers of the crowd. - Triple jump huracanrana to an opponent seated on the top rope. Again, he likes to pose after this move. - Slingshot spear, from the arena floor back into the ring. Used to counter an opponent who comes after him when he’s on the floor. - Release Dragon suplex (a favorite against lighter opponents) - Beautiful, picture perfect standing dropkick on a running opponent (followed with AJ Styles-esque posing) SETUP MOVE(S): The Euphoria Factor: Straight, lightning fast superkick to the jaw. One of the most important weapons in Almasy’s arsenal, as it stuns an opponent and allows for any of his finishers to be gone for. The kick might knock an opponent down, allowing Almasy to measure them for the Paradigm Shift, or stagger an opponent enough for Seymour to lift them for the Birth by Sleep. ALTERNATE FINISHERS: For matches when Almasy doesn’t want to use a conventional finisher, or needs to pull something else out of his arsenal due to specific needs of a contest (ie, stipulations and what have you.) Some of these are moves already in his moveset, others are things he’ll pull out when the situation calls for it. - Horns of Valmar: As noted earlier, this is his primary submission, and in a submission or "I Quit" match, it’s Almasy’s best chance of victory. In such situations, he will absolutely knee the head to set it up, and sometimes return to knees to the head from the move, only to go back into it. - Wrath of the Lich King/The Cataclysm: Either of Almasy’s headspiking Falcon Arrows can be alternate finishers against fellow cruiserweights. - The Euphoria Factor: As an HBK-style counter to a flying opponent, this can finish. If he hits it so spectacularly, his desire for flashy wins will inspire him to cover rather than go for another finisher. - Wrist-Clutch Inside Cradle: Seymour’s favored pinning combination. Used best when Seymour needs to steal a victory when outmatched against an opponent. - 450 Splash: Almasy rarely takes huge chances anymore, but if he needs to go up top to finish off an opponent, this is probably what he will go for. Consider it a rare move. FINISHERS: As is his usual wont, Seymour has three finishers. Two of them are meant to be used interchangeably when the situation suits itself, and the final is a last-ditch, when all else fails move. Paradigm Shift: Standing moonsault double knee drop to the chest of an opponent. Can be used on practically anyone, and is most effective following the Euphoria Factor, particularly on larger, tougher foes. Seymour can also execute this off of the top rope in particularly big match situations. Consider this at about a 75% success rate if hit. It’s a painful move, but the particularly tough can survive it. That tends to be when Almasy goes for multiples. A second Paradigm Shift in a contest is 95% likely endgame. A third, or one off the top rope at any point that connects, is death, period. Birth by Sleep: The Go 2 Sleep, named according to Seymour’s usual bent. Almasy likes going for the BBS against smaller opponents, as it’s an emphatic way to end a contest. Still, it’s easily counterable. Seymour generally has to beat up an opponent considerably more to use it, but if he hits it, the match is more likely over than a usage of the Paradigm Shift. You’re not kicking out of this 9 times out of ten. The Chrono Cross: A cross-legged half-Boston crab. This has been one of Seymour’s most potent finishers for years, earning him huge victories outside of Primetime Central. The psychology behind the move is unique, as Almasy never really sets the submission up over the course of a match. He applies the Cross when his opponent is exhausted, ideally having taken the Paradigm Shift and/or Birth by Sleep but being too stubborn to just die. Almasy uses it to wear down the foe and force a tapout by sheer attrition. It is not unheard of for opponents to be in the hold upwards of a minute and a half to two minutes before submitting. The Chrono Cross has been escaped once, ever, by an opponent reaching the ropes. 99.9% a kill without fail move. UBER-FINISHERS: The following are moves Seymour probably won’t ever use in PRIME. If he does, they might be used once. One of them might get pulled out of mothballs, maybe. They are all ridiculously dangerous, all of them have a special significance to Seymour, and they’re all moves that are tough enough for him to execute that he doesn’t want to unless he’s got a REALLY good reason. They are listed here, in order of most likely to be used to least likely. Shockwave Pulsar: Fireman’s Carry to leg-cradle DDT. At one point in Seymour’s regular finishing rotation, the move has gained considerable cachet for its use as the move that countered Lia Ambrosi’s Levin Bolt Phoenix Splash and gave Seymour his third and final PTC Global Championship. While not much more difficult to use than the Birth by Sleep, he prefers to use it as a very rare weapon, especially once the BBS is established and opponents learn to look for it. If Almasy ever has a chance at the PRIME Universal Championship, and he’s facing a smaller opponent, the Shockwave Pulsar will be in his quiver. Glory to Airygylph!: 630 Senton. Easily Almasy’s most breathtaking aerial move, and, to be honest, one Almasy’s not sure he can use anymore. Only ever used twice, one time it brought him ACW’s World Championship, and the second time it missed and cost him that same title. If Seymour’s going for the Universal Title, or he decides that he wants to end his career in, say, the Dual Halo with a spectacular highspot, this move’s in play. One-Hit Kill: Head-spike Styles Clash. You know how AJ occasionally drops someone on their head with the Clash? Yeah, Almasy does that, but on purpose. At one point this was actually his primary death finisher, used to win him both his first and second PTC Global Championships, and also used, twice, to punish Jamie Shock for severely injuring Seymour’s ex-wife in the UWF. This is a hatred move. This is not something he’ll bust out any more to just win a match or a title. This is for Rich Rollins, if that SOB was still alive, and anyone else who rises to Rollins’ scum tier over time in Almasy’s mind. Ring Entrances Coming Soon!
