ReVolution 85 ---- The New Dawn And An End Of An Era Taun Pham cuts a lonely path through the corridors of the Nassau Coliseum, his head sunken. Despite passing a few members of the backstage crew, no words are exchanged. People had been doing their best not to talk to the young co-owner since the Great American Nightmare. What exactly do you say to a man who had to watch his father perish in front of his very eyes, his body consumed in the fireball that destroyed their limousine. The english language might consist of a multitude of words, but no combination of them could bring any peace to the soul of the late Hin See's son. Christmas had passed by and the new year arrived, not that any of it really mattered now. Pham's had tightly gripped the handle of a black leather briefcase, which contained several documents and files relating to his father. These had all been opened frequently in the past few days, unlike his presents still resting underneath an artificial tree back in Kobe. As tragic as the scenario sounds, there was no tragic element to the events that saw out 2005. Hin See was taken before his time and given a couple of minutes, Pham would have joined him. This was no tragedy. It was cold-blooded murder. Several months ago, Toshiaki Motoki would appear the prime suspect for such an act. Hin See's former business partner however, was a changed man and whether you found such a transformation hard to swallow, his alibi was water tight. There was CCTV footage of Motoki for almost every minute that he was not in Pham's company, he never left the arena. Perhaps his alibi was too water tight. Any suspicions that he'd somehow commited the perfect crime or arranged an accomplice to perform the deed were also being put to bed as the authorities completed checks on mobile phone records, computer data, financial transations. One by one, they were all coming back clean and Motoki's innocence was all but confirmed. In Pham's mind however, Motoki was always innocent. Obviously he had no way of knowing if his trust was a notion planted by the NetBIOs chip imbedded in his cranium or a genuine feeling, but there seemed no way the man who saved his own life would be responsible for taking that of his father. If Motoki was indeed innocent though, that left the list of potential suspects looking rather threadbare. What made the whole series of events more unnerving, was that the detonation of the vehicle he occupied, wasn't the only attempt made that night to rob Hin See of his life. With the investigation that followed, a bottle of Bourbon sitting on the Asian Cowboys desk was found to contain a lethal substance following a hunch. The fact that Hin See had left a bottle of his favorite tipple open, yet not fully consumed was highly out of character. Someone had attempted to poison him and it was still unclear as to whether they succeeded or not. The police had a number of scenarios in mind, including the possibility that Hin See might have already been dead before the explosion and simply transported to the limo, perhaps to even bait Pham into the vehicle. Right now however, the police investigation was lodged somewhere near the back of Pham's mind. There was nothing he could do now in regards to it. In regards to PRIME however, the show had to go on. That's what his father would surely have wanted even if Pham in all honesty would rather not be present in Uniondale tonight. His father had to be honoured, no matter how painful the experience would be for him or how public his grieving process might become, he had to oblidge. He passes through the curtain. Every single eye in the arena was on him from the fans in the back row, to the superstars and staff assembled on the stage for the occasion. The mighty PRIME-A-Tron screen beamed an image of Hin See with the a simple line of plain white text illustrating the duration in terms of years spent on this planet. The solitary dates really didn't do anything to summarise the importance of his fathers life. There were no fireworks to introduce ReVolution this week, simply a silent respect and several solemn faces. A majority of the crowd appeared to be emotionally moved to some extent, although Hin See hardly ranked as high in the popularity stakes as some of PRIME's more legendary babyfaces, his passing still brought sadness from the many who could relate to Pham's loss. The ten bell salute follows, the traditional way to mark the transition from life to death in this industry. As each note of steel reverberates around the hushed arena, Pham's expression remains constant as if his facial muscles had been coated in cement prior to walking out. His eyes never deviated from what lay directly in front of him. He didn't turn to exchange glances with the personnel around him. He simply stood there until the final ring had sounded. The crowd applauded, the staff likewise, but Pham didn't reciprocate. Instead he simply turned and disappeared behind the curtain from wence he came as if he'd completed the only duty in life he needed to accomplish. ---- Taking Liberties The office of Hin See, recently deceased owner of PRIME, seems eerily cold this night.
