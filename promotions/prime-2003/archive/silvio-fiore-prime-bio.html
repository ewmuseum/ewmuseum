Silvio Fiore Biography Nicknames The Cutting Edge, Quicksilver, Ray Hometown Hays, Kansas Birthday August 27, 1988 Height 5'9" Weight 164 lb. Fed Titles ZERO PRIME titles won Fed Awards Overall record of 3-5 Biography THE CAREER Oh well. That last venture didn't work out. I was looking forward to getting back into the American wrestling scene after that venture down to Mexico. True Talent Wrestling was a Midwest regional promotion and looked to pride itself on pure wrestling. They'd just started catering to states that you usually don't hear anything from: New Mexico, Arizona, the Dakotas... states in which they might run a wrestling show once in a while but don't spring to mind when you consider "great states for wrestling action." Alas, for reasons of bankruptcy, TTW closed. Its sister federation (of sorts) was Outlaw Pro Wrestling out of Raleigh-Durham, North Carolina, but they live up to their name: the wrestlers there are people who, for reasons of being too violent or backstage cancers, can't get work elsewhere. I didn't think myself so hard-up for bookings that I'd start out at what is acknowledge throughout the sport's circle to be the bottom of the barrel. Actually, after looking around for some different digs, and putting ou applications where I could, I did get some calls back. A lot of people seem to like the fact that I work for cheap. Not that I wouldn't accept more, but I'm still young and nationally-untested; I can't expect to get much more. Anyway, I got some calls back, and did the negotiations. In the end, I signed with just one company: PRIME. A few months ago, I wouldn't have given it a second look, maybe even a first glance. While I'm not stranger to being in a corrupting environment (we all know the backstage and onstage insanity that took place around me during my run in Death Row Wrestling Federation), but it would have been too much for me to accept to wrestle on a program that aired on the Playboy Channel. I'm quite forgiving of many things, but being program-flanked by pornography just didn't sit right with me. Now, however, PRIME has made a step down to cable broadcast, with the FX network. Even if they try to push the limits, it's not outright jumping over the line everyday. I can live with that. The federation itself has a collection of different wrestlers, big and small, of myriad motivations. The matches are consistently good, and that's what I care about. There's still much that I need to learn. Styles to face, challenges to meet. I've only been at this for so long and I'll admit that even though I've wanted to do pure wrestling, I kept getting wrangled into those hardcore matches. I got through it well because of staying true to my desire and training in standard hybrid wrestling, and I won't discount that I'm probably tougher for having to deal with bigger people hitting me with weapons. Still, I want career longevity, and want to improve my wrestling, and being surrounded by the athletes that I've seen in PRIME I can make that goal happen. That's why I wanted to go into TTW. Not much for the actual contract, but they ran an independent "Best of Cruiserweights" type tournament called the Sky High Cup. The federation itself was all about pure wrestling. One of the training philosophies that people like Eichiro Honda and Daniel Loesscher taught me back at Spartan Regimen Facility in Kansas City was that the best training that I could possibly get would be to wrestle people better than me. I look at PRIME and I see a lot of people better than me. Suffice to say, I'll be learning a lot there. For now, PRIME has not officially given out any information about me. So those of you reading this are all my existing fans, rather than curious passersthrough from my new place of employment. THE READERS It's come to my attention that even more of you, faithful readers (thank you very much for sticking with me these past 19 months now), that you're linking or verbally redirecting many people to this website. I can't tell you how wonderful it is to know that I have a dedicated base of fans who essentially pimp me out on whatever forums that they visit. Anybody can get that, though; what we've developed, though, is different. I may do some nice moves in the ring now and again, but it's more than that. From the beginning, I've endeavored to give a frank, honest, outsider's/newcomer's view of the sport and spectale of professional wrestling, and so far I've done my best to stick to that. I've told you what I liked and what I thought could use a little improvement. Of course, through it all there have been those I've worked with who expressed opinions to the effect of "You're just some kid, what right do you have to criticize." But as you've seen, I never felt personally discouraged because at the level at which I wrestled, most roster space had been filled by 20-somethings with less than 2 years experience; hardly the crowd to talk about being quiet paying one's dues. On top of that, I've been more than willing to serve as your voice within the wrestling world, expressing your salutations and displeasures on your behalf. That's what we have, isn't it? I'm respectful, but I don't take myself (or others) too seriously. I'm not awfully complicated, I see things as I do and say what's on my mind. I do what feels good and like to share that with others. Incredible how a simple kid from the middle of nowhere in Kansas could become so loved. Thank you all for your support. Well then, that seemed more like a concluding paragraph. But, that was directed more toward the people who've read me throughout my short career. To those new readers I mentioned earlier, now it's your turn. Periodically, I like to reintroduce myself. It never takes too long; besides, I find that handling it this way rather than forcing people to troll through my blog archives makes it easier for new guests to pick up. So, to you new reader, welcome to my little website. The index page holds the most current blog, and you'll see the archives below for whatever I've had to say in the past. The music you're hearing right now is "Simple Man" by Lynard Skynard. I like it, and it fits in pretty well as one of those 'soundtrack to my life' things. You'll also see short interviews I conducted with some of the people I worked with during my brief trip to Japan and my longer stay in Mexico. Finally, clips of moves I've done and matches I've wrestled, just so you can get an idea of what I can do, (since I am a wrestler after all :-P). I don't really keep biographical information on the site, just my wrestling specs. I know that there's already a great level of abstraction in dealing with people over the internet, letting 0's and 1's represent the intricacies of speech and complexities of human personality, but even at that, I just feel that there's something completely impersonal about posting up my life story as though I can't be bothered to give a short explanation of myself to people I've just met. "Hey, who are you?" "Pfft. Check the bio page, buddy." THE LIFE So hello, my name is Silvio Fiore. I'm 17, took the G.E.D. test to get out of school at 16. Have you ever heard of Gillian Flats? Probably not, unless you're one of the 200 people who live there. It's is northeast of Hays, Kansas; a little town that I call home. The word "town", I suppose, is used loosely. Each house is separated by about 3 miles of fields and dirt roads. On the plus side, you get a lot of practice learning how to drive earlier than you do other places. My parents are Corrado and Maria Fiore. My great-grandparents came to this country as a young couple shortly before the immigration quotas in the late 1910s. Through descendancy, they led down to my dad Corrado. Maria Billings is a local woman he fell in love with. They had me and now you know the rest of the story. I can't say that I'm a longtime fan of wrestling. Really I only started watching it about 4 years ago. At that it was local feds in syndication, and old AWA shows. From there, I got more interested in it, checking it out online through the library (when I could get there) and doing what I could to learn about the wrestling scene on the whole. Regions aren't dead, but travel is easier than it used to be. Wrestling gets around, wrestlers get around; as long as someone's willing to front the travel expenses, anyone can make the rounds. I was getting my G.E.D. anyway, since my high school had no early graduation programs available. Dual enrollment or school-to-work were hardly well-established. I just took my books and left, pretty much. But I've always been someone who wanted to get out and see the world. Or at least the rest of the country outside of Gillian Flats. So looking at my options, the easiest thing to do seemed to be wrestling. Perhaps more clarification is necessary. I'm not saying that wrestling is easy, just that it wasn't a big deal for me. I've always been athletic, but hey, you don't have much to lounge around about in GF. And while wrestling does give one its fair share of bumps and bruises, I never felt it. CIPA-- Congenital Insensitivity to Pain with Anhidrosis. I couldn't give you a full scientific explanation, but the long and short of it is that I have a rare genetic condition which keeps me from feeling pain. That doesn't mean that I have no sense of touch; tactile sensory information comes in just fine for the most part. I don't feel pain, and my body doesn't respond to temperature changes in the environment by sweating when it's hot or shivering when it's cold. This is a boon to me, but for many people it causes a great deal of health problems. Inflammation of joints, injuries, infections, and whatnot are all possible. I take a routine physical before and after every match to make sure things are okay. While I do take steroids, they are mild muscle enhancers that I use to repair potential tears in the fibers that I cannot feel from day to day. In order to shift my weight in the night, I have a periodic alarm; every 2 hours, it goes off and I switch positions slightly. And in most cases it is a bit of a drag; I've got to be extra careful in day-to-day life that I don't bite off my tongue or cut myself... I wouldn't feel the pain of it, and that wouldn't be good for need of immediate medical treatment. Still, CIPA has allowed me to wrestle when otherwise I might not. I've no idea how painful it really is to take a bump; I could be doing something else, probably would be doing something else, if the pain got to me. We do what we can with what God gave us. I could run down my accomplishments in DRWF, PHW, and ELL, I don't think that matters. I'm starting anew, on the big stage. It's time to introduce myself to a whole new, bigger crowd. Welcome aboard the Fiore-Train before it becomes a bandwagon. I promise that we'll keep chugging along. Don't forget that the "Appearances" tab currently leads to a blank page. Once PRIME gives me my working schedule, I can tell you guys where I'll be. In the meantime, anyone who's in or near Charlotte, North Carolina can see me after my debut. I'll be at the arena all day, so come early and get tickets. We'll hang out. :-) Other Feds DRWF Cutting Edge Title, PHW Hardcore Title Other Fed Awards DRWF Top Ten, Projekt Xero Top Ten, PHW Top Five Wrestling Style Doing whatever comes to mind Finisher Move Whatever he pins someone with. Finisher Description Odds are, it won't be a submission (that's not his forte); rather, some sort of aerial attack for larger opponents. Those under 210, he might be able to use a grappling-based finisher on them. He knows that PRIME's wrestlers are some of the best, so whatever he does he'll do his best to pull out the big guns. Finisher Setup Move n/a Setup Description None listed yet Special Moves None listed yet Other Moves -- In all things, Silvio is unafraid of confronting opponents. He cannot feel pain, so getting a punch to the face or a powerslam doesn't really concern him. Fiore has routinely sat right up after slams, powerbombs, clotheslines, etc. If the move doesn't disorient him or deprive him of oxygen, it's almost completely useless. The boy goes in hard, goes in smart, to make it a competitive match; the opponent is required to keep Fiore's rare genetic abnormality in mind if they hope to stand a good chance of winning the bout. -- HOW TO BEAT SILVIO FIORE. Step one; attack the head. Step two; don't stop, don't you fuggin' stop. Step three; knock some of the air out of him if you can. Step four; he's 160 pounds... get him down good and even if he isn't knocked out there's a chance he just won't have the strength to kick out of a pinfall. -- A handshake will be offered before the match. If it's refused then, he'll just get right into the match. It's a simple matter of courtesy; if the opponent doesn't want to do it, he doesn't take it personally. -- Fiore begins a match different ways. He may charge in with a striking flurry, go hold-for-hold mat wrestling, or hang back and try to counter whatever his opponent does. Sometimes, it's a random choice; in other situations, a calculated decision. -- For the most part, Silvio wrestles against everyone the same way. Even if an opponent is his size, he'll wrestle them just the same as a much bigger opponent. This comes from Fiore's lack of wrestling experience; he doesn't know a whole lot of techniques yet. As such, he doesn't change his moves to what his opponent does; instead, he tries to use his repertoire in such a way that it will be effective against anyone. -- Fiore can most simply be classified as a high-flyer, but that doesn't give him a lot of credit for his true specialty. Silvio is a special kind of combatant, especially for as young as he is. He earned two nicknames on the indy circuit, those being Quicksilver and The Cutting Edge. -- The former moniker comes from the speed he possesses in thinking of and launching an attack. His mind and body are uncannily coordinated, and with even a split-second of opportunity he can do something to attempt to capitalize. -- The latter nickname speaks to his full talent, what will help him succeed in wrestling even more than his fast moves. Silvio is preternaturally-gifted at analyzing the movements of his opponents in action. He seems to catch on quicker when actually wrestling the person he's trying to read. As Fiore goes through a match with someone, he can quickly gain an understanding of the opponent's rhythm of movement, attack strategy, general repertoire (through physical ability), and match philosophy/psychology. Silvio has been observed in one of his only matches in Japan to face a much tougher Muay Thai expert; while this man's unfamiliar style and strength dominated Silvio for much of the match, by the end of it Fiore had so fully understood the man's abilities that Silvio could block and counter nearly everything thrown at him. -- To the above analytical process, it should be noted that it still takes a bit of time for Silvio to fully devise counter movements and strategies to his opponent. The aforementioned Muay Thai fighter felt the need to drag out the match, which led to his defeat. Fiore is far from unbeatable, especially if someone get get him pinned in such a way that his low body weight will prevent him from escaping. -- Silvio Fiore possesses the standard high-flying repertoire (as well as some more flashy techniques), but he tailors use of it to his opponents. We've seen this with the direct attack on Kenjiro Ito, style of counter-wrestling observed against Vangelus Olsig, and Fiore's brief but intelligent method of slipping underneath and through Titan St. James's attack range to hit him from outside of his line of sight while remaining close. -- Aside from flips and kicks that make Fiore look like a more respectable and technically-capable Jack Evans, Silvio also uses overhand chest chops and forearm strikes against opponents of a suitable height. Over 6'5", he won't even bother. -- Fiore can also make use of some lifting attacks. His limit is around 250ish pounds. There aren't many options for power moves available to someone his size. If he can find an opponent 190 or under, more doors open to him, and he can use moves like Suplexes, Slams, and Powerbombs (though he still keeps from relying on these moves so that he can surprise people with them). Over 200, he's restricted to a Death Valley Driver, Monkey Flip, Sideslam, or a Ippon Seoinage (One Arm Shoulder Throw). Above 270 there is to be absolutely no lifting whatsoever. -- Silvio's match philosophy (I'll try anything once) and his inability to feel pain mean that he's not in the habit of tapping out to submissions. He'll feel that he's being touched, but the pain doesn't register to him. Wrestlers are best to pin him or attempt sleeper holds. If put into a submission, the quick-witted young man can often slip out by unorthodox escapes. Entrance Music "Gravity" by Audiovent Ring Entrances Silvio Fiore doesn't have anything complicated for an entrance. The normal "young babyface" entrance will work just fine for him.
