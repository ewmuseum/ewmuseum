Entrance No strobe lights. No pyrotechnics. Masters will typically walk out from the back accompanied by his partner, Trevor Hawke, to the sound of 'Cult of Personality' playing over the PA system. They will usually take their time coming down to the ring, basking in the jeering of the crowd. They will usually be the first to the ring and, once there, they will proceed to cut a shoot promo directed toward either their opponent or the crowd (or more likely both). The promo will finish with them either calling out their opponents or being interrupted by them. General In-ring Behavior Masters started his career as a spot machine to complement his partner Trevor Hawke as a high flying duo, but a knee injury resulting in several operations over the years forced him to adapt his mat game. The result of this was the evolution of what is today a ruthless ring technician. Masters' ring pacing is slow and methodical, but what it lacks in intensity it makes up in viciousness. If the opponent shows any weak point, he is sure to zone in on it and exploit it. No longer are random flashy moves performed in sequence. Instead, each is thought out, each has a purpose, building toward the finish of the match. If the opponent appears to have no obvious weak points, Masters will typically spend the majority of the match working over his opponent's neck in preparation for his fisherman buster finisher, the Primo Plex. The neck-work also aids in softening the opponent up for his partner's finishing hold, the Suburban Crab. As mentioned above, Masters has had a series of operations performed on his right knee, and thus favors it in the ring. Because of this he will perform no high-flying maneuvers, having purposefully adapted his style to exclude them. He will attempt to guard his knee throughout the course of the match, expecting his opponent to target it, but it still remains his most vulnerable body part. If particularly incensed, Masters style will incorporate more of an emphasis on shoot-fighting. Double leg takedowns, greco-roman suplexes, and other grappling will be used to get an opponent into a position where he can unload on them with mounted punches. Masters, being only average sized, is easily overpowered by larger opponents (6'8 and over). Unable to match them physically, he instead looks to outsmart and outwrestle them. Typically, he will try to keep bigger competitors on the mat, horizontal, where they are unable to utilize their power and leverage. Leg work will be his primary focus in these situations. Masters is not a sporting competitor. In fact, he is quite the opposite. No handshakes, no signs of respect, no mercy. He's not in this sport for the thrill of competition, and if he gets any thrill it's that of getting himself over at the expense of others. If he taunts his opponent, it'll be more likely to take the form of profanity laced insults as he kicks their head in than any more showy display. More likely, though, he'll just seek to dismantle and hurt his opponent as efficiently and brutally as possible. In singles matches, Trevor Hawke will interfere to an obnoxious extent.
