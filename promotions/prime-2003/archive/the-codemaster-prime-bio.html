The Codemaster Biography Nicknames The Breaker of Kingdom Hearts, the Twilight Prince, the Blackest Brother in the Hyrule Kingdom, the Tethe'allan Rattlesnake, the Hyrulian Hellraiser Hometown Salt Lake City, Utah Birthday July 15th, 1975 (32 years old) Height 5'10 Weight 207 Fed Titles PRIME World Tag Team Championship (w/ Coral Avalon, 1x) Fed Awards None. Biography The youngest son of a wrestler from the western territories named Curtis "the Hammer" Brown. Allen Brown never considered himself destined to be a wrestler. He hated being the son of a wrestler and hated being expected to follow in his father's footsteps. While Allen excelled at sports, he found his father beginning to give up hope that his youngest son would follow in his footsteps as well. As a result, he began to drift away from his father's influence, and eventually found an escape... video games. Starting from the Atari and working his way up, Allen found himself immersed in the medium of video games so much that he earned enough money to open up his own video game store. Life went well for Allen until the day that his older brothers, Robert and Jordan, came to his video game store. Allen, who never got along with the twins, found himself goaded into being a professional wrestler on a bet that Allen couldn't be more successful than either of his brothers. Allen did two things that further distanced himself from his father. The first was that he left his store in the hands of his employees and left Salt Lake City to go to Phoenix, Arizona. There, he trained under the direction of another wrestler named Joey Malone (who would later become a two-time World champion before retiring due to family). The second was that Allen refused to join the independent promotion that his father was co-running, and instead opted to join the promotion that was run by Kyle Morris (Joey Malone's own trainer) instead. Allen proved notable enough to attract the attention of the fans Wrestling organization in 2003. There, based on the suggestion of Samuel Smooth, Allen became known as "the Codemaster"... a wrestler who claimed to be a master of all things video game. At first, Allen hated the new gimmick, but became accustomed to it and even began to admit that it was the closest thing to who he really was. He made many friends, such as Mega Job and Coral Avalon, but also made many enemies like Lindsay Troy (a former friend), Sonny Silver, and Keith Scott Zimmerman. Still others, he had uneasy allies such as Craig Miles (a former enemy). He also made an enemy of the fWo itself by disagreeing with the Disney-run faction and soon formed his own faction to combat against it: the Blue Rogues. Now in PRIME, the Codemaster intends to make his name heard around the world. Other Moves OVERALL STYLE: The Codemaster is a gifted performer who is known for having a lot of style to make up for his lack of substance. Although this version of the Codemaster is an all-arounder, he primarilly uses complex but impressive moves mixed with some high flying to defeat opponents. A charismatic showoff, the Codemaster often plays to the crowd and doesn't consider the consequences of high risk maneuvers. Although he has a little more than a dozen signature moves, the Codemaster usually seeks victory by any means necessary, which includes the use of weapons (particularly the Megaton Hammer). However, despite the Codemaster's prior history with hardcore enviroments, he is not suited for that thing in this incarnation and is more at home in traditional wrestling matches. The two biggest weaknesses of the Codemaster are his size (although big by cruiserweight standards, he's still small by heavyweight standards) and his reliance on moves of complexity. Many of the moves he uses can really only be used on other cruiserweights, which severely limits many of his options when faced against significantly larger opponents. As a result, he has to use more flying techniques and risk getting hammered out of one of them. The Codemaster does cheat, but he no longer relies on cheating to win matches, instead using his wide variety of maneuvers to put down his opponents. MOVESET: Anytime: Open hand chops, kicks (of some martial arts flair, usually side kicks to the thigh, but he can do a roundhouse kick too), Shoryuken (Jumping uppercut), Triforce Clothesline (discus clothesline), Lambda Burst (Side Effect, normally a counter move), Double Damage (Backstabber, usually a counter move) Early: Uses about anything here. He could start with some brawling, or he could start by getting a quick cheapshot, or he could start with some technical stuff. Use your imagination. He likes to use open-hand chops, if that helps. Middle: German suplex, Dragon Whip kick, Samoan Drop, rolling fireman's carry slam, somersault swinging neckbreaker, springboard dropkick, head-and-arm backbreaker, STO backbreaker, fireman's carry gutbuster, fireman's carry swing into the ringpost, fireman's carry over-the-knee neckbreaker, superkick, double arm DDT, step-up enzuigiri, single leg powerbomb, fisherman's neckbreaker, 1-Up Stomps (Repeated double stomps) Late: Wrist clutch fisherman's buster, crossarmed brainbuster, half nelson suplex followed by a dragon suplex, wheelbarrow powerbomb into the corner (sometimes used to set up the Horns of Valmar), cross-arm Iconoclasm, tornado reverse DDT, shining enzuigiri, electric chair driver, Death Mountain Driver, Element of Treachery (Yokosuka Cutter), Wings of Valmar (Double Underhook Facebreaker), Heart of Valmar (Full Nelson Russian Legsweep), Horns of Valmar (Running knee strike to the face of a cornered opponent), Claws of Valmar (Iron Claw STO) FINISHERS: Although you could really establish anything as a finisher through finishing with the move at least once, these four moves are the big ones he'll look to end matches with. All but one of them have deliberate setup moves. - Will of Valmar (Pumphandle Half Nelson Driver) (ground primary) Primary Setup: Wings of Valmar Secondary Setups: Claws of Valmar, Heart of Valmar The Will of Valmar is the Codemaster's primary finisher. Commonly set up after stunning an opponent with the Wings of Valmar, the Will of Valmar can flatten opponents by dumping them directly on their heads. Very few will have the ability to kick out after being hit with this move and we might consider them to be cyborgs after such a thing happens, so it's usually a deathstroke if it hits. The move does have one flaw: It requires Codemaster pick up his opponent. Although he is rather strong for a man of his size, he shouldn't be throwing this move at very man heavyweights. It also can't really come out of nowhere in the strictest sense, it has a deliberate setup sequence... usually requiring the Codemaster to hit the Wings of Valmar before applying the pumphandle and half nelson needed for the Will of Valmar. - Magnum Fang (Diamond Cutter) (ground secondary) Primary Setup: None, comes out of nowhere. This is the original Codemaster finisher, a simple Diamond Cutter. It has the fortune of being the move Codemaster can use to finish much larger opponents. However, in the realm of the cruiserweights, the Magnum Fang has seen a decrease in use compared to the Will of Valmar since it is more of a deathstroke, whereas the Magnum Fang is more of a surprise move on opponents that can easily stun them for the three count. Because of its use as a stun tactic, the Magnum Fang is likely the least effective of the Codemaster's four finishers. - Tower of Druaga (Elevated Diamond Cutter) (elevated primary) Primary Setup: Horns of Valmar Strictly speaking, the Tower of Druaga is a variation of the Magnum Fang. It involves the opponent crotched in the turnbuckles, then the Codemaster grabs the head and suspends the opponent with their feet on the top rope and their head in the Codemaster's grip. Then the Codemaster drops down for the cutter. This is much more effective than the proper Magnum Fang, as it commonly goes into a headdrop situation. The Tower of Druaga has one flaw and that's the fact that it requires a specific setup, which is to mount the opponent on the top rope. - The Lonely Rolling Star Press (450 Splash) (aerial primary) Primary Setup: Death Mountain Driver The Codemaster's aerial finisher, the Lonely Rolling Star Press has been with the Codemaster for three years. It is a showy 450 splash, usually with some theatrics before he executes the move. The good part about the move is that he can achieve great elevation due to the relative simplicity of the move and his leg strength, often jumping high before landing hard. He can also hit the move quite a distance from the turnbuckles, though usually only to about midway across the ring. As an aerial move, though, it's all or nothing. If he hits it, he's probably going to take the win. If he misses, the opponent can easily capitalize on the mistake.
