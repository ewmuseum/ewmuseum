Danny Ferguson — PRIME biography

Danny Ferguson Biography Nicknames 'Superstar,' 'Irish' Hometown Hollywood, CA Birthday 5/10/78 Height billed as 6'1 Weight billed as 230 Fed Titles Tag Team Champions (2x - 1. w/ The Illustrious Face-Eater and, occasionally, Chandler Tsonda; 2. w/ Lindsay Troy); 5-Star Champion (with The Illustrious Face-Eater and Chandler Tsonda); Intense Champion (sorta) Fed Awards General Manager For a Day that one time. Biography Danny was the second (known) man to don the mask of El Spiffy!, "purchasing" the gimmick, Tom Brandi-style, from former Old School Wrestling competitor Matt Register. Most of El Spiffy!’s "glory days" during the Rush Mason (think WO/ew24-7) era of RUSH Wrestling came with Ferguson under the hood. Being a former child star (of the hit 80s sitcom Four Is A Crowd), he was desperate for a return to fame, and was trying to use wrestling as a launching pad to Hollywood. The mystique of El Spiffy! is a different story altogether, but let’s just say Danny wasn’t quite the one in control, and found himself unable to break away, even at Spiffy!’s zenith. RUSH folded, and he found himself considerably downgraded, working indy shows in New Jersey among some other washouts. Later on, when he was completely strapped and at wit’s end with wrestling, he was able to "free" himself from El Spiffy! and go his own way. No one cared enough about Danny to keep track of him during this in-between period, but the next time anyone remembered seeing him, he was considerably larger in size and starting to make waves in Hollywood. Not one person remembers how his foot got jammed in that door, but he began popping up in supporting roles for a couple high-grossing films, and soon had enough buzz around him to get his own starring vehicle. That movie, Hard Money, became a startling surprise, and suddenly Danny Ferguson, former hooded worker and child star, was an "It" guy in Tinseltown. Capitalizing on his flash-in-the-pan fame, and trying to cultivate a reputation as a tough guy to court action film scripts, Danny returned to wrestling, and he brought a little bit of Hollywood with him. He quickly found success as both a singles and tag wrestler, making it to the semifinals of the 2005 Jewel In The Crown tournament before his unwillingness to strike a woman ("It's a PR nightmare!") led to a disappointing loss to Aimz. It is widely theorized that he would have gone on to win the tournament and rule prime with a bejeweled fist, had he not been screwed by the bracketmakers.* During his stay in PRIME, Danny was cast as the title role in More Than Just Hardcore: The Terry Funk Story, a new biopic directed by Michael Mann. During this period, under Mann's meticulous guidance and emphasis on character development, he took on a number of traits of the Funker, and sought to duplicate his in-ring style with limited success. He did, however, pick up quite a bit of the Funker's ruthlessness and bloodlust. This did not work in his favor, however, as Colossus III was the beginning of the end. Unable to control his hatred for Kyle Lamen, a well-liked but cancer-stricken former PRIME competitor, he dragged out their confrontation, alienating his allies and friends and destroying himself emotionally when Lamen was able to pin him and end their feud once and for all. Then, just an hour or so later in the show, Danny's frequent tag partner, The Illustrious Face-Eater, exploded in the ring. Although Danny took Facey's Intense Title in an attempt to carry the torch for his blazing ex-ally, it would be discovered soon thereafter that Danny was somehow responsible for the combustion. At ReVolution 107, Danny Ferguson was apprehended and sent to prison for his illegal exploding of the Illustrious Face-Eater. He insists that he was set up, but since there wasn't even concrete evidence to prove that he did it, there's no way to refute said evidence. It was a bit of a catch-22, really. Except not at all, since there's clearly an end that no one acknowledged. That's Hollywood for you... Danny returned early in 2007 at the request of then-general manager Chet Worth. With the faction known as Fuck You running rampant in PRIME, Worth was desperate to find someone who could add firepower to his fledgling group of PRIMEates defending the company's honor. Alongside Hall of Famers Tchu, Killean Sirrajin and Lindsay Troy, Ferguson battled with the forces of Fuck You for several months. Team PRIME eventually emerged victorious when FU crumbled at its foundation. Along the way, Ferguson and Troy were named Tag Team Champions when Worth un-retired the belts. They went on to have a lengthy reign and form an unexpected alliance and friendship. After FU crumbled, Ferguson had his revenge on Nova, whom he held partially responsible for his incarceration in late 2006. At Colossus IV, Ferguson defeated Nova in what was then the Risen Star's final PRIME match, effectively driving him from the company. The landmark win was one in a line of many for Ferguson, who went through nearly all of 2007 without suffering a singles loss. He reformed the A-List with Chandler Tsonda and the Illustrious Face-Eater, but the group didn't have the same cohesiveness it once did. During this stretch, Ferguson carried the group to a win in the prestigious TEAM Dupree Cup tournament. Despite the numerous wins, Ferguson was never given a one-on-one shot at the Universal Title, which was held (for most of this time) by his odd-couple ally Lindsay Troy. Ferguson repeatedly refused title shots against his friend and former partner, frequently sidestepping his own glory to let the spotlight focus on the Queen of the Ring. Danny's run of success would end with his first and only singles loss in 2007. During a match with Devin Shakur at Great American Nightmare/Devil's Night, he was knocked unconscious by a knee smash from Shakur and suffered a severe concussion and broken nose. While injured, he toyed with the idea of retirement, even announcing his intention to not return on the PRIME website. During a promotional appearance at the first ReVolution of 2008 however, Ferguson was goaded into one last match with then-Universal Champion Sonny Silver. He lost that match to Silver via dirty tactics, and immediately signed a new contract in an attempt to right the wrongs. Ferguson would go on to lose his rematch under triple threat rules, as longtime friend Lindsay Troy won the Universal Title instead. With his third straight loss, Danny reprioritized his life and focused on ascending to the top of PRIME again. After coming second in the 2007 Dual Halo match, he was determined to go all the way in 2008. Several weeks of training paid off, as he set a new distance record in the 2008 match, entering first and lasting well over an hour. However, during an encounter with Chandler Tsonda, Ferguson was hit with a double-knee strike from Tsonda-ally Tyler Rayne and knocked from the second level of the Dual Halo down to the ring, a plunge of 25-30 feet. The fall gave him his second severe concussion in under six months, as he lost consciousness for nearly 30 minutes. His Halo distance record was broken later on in the match by Wade Elliott. Ferguson once again faded away from PRIME, driven away by injuries, a star whose brightest moments may just have been behind him. * - By widely, I mean within the Ferguson camp. Team Ferguson Bios Reggie Delray: (Wrestling Manager) The name is pronounced phoenetically, like Reggie Del Ray, as in Jimmy Del Ray, former Heavenly Body. Reggie is a renowned manager in the sport, most notably for tag teams. He was recently hired to Team Ferguson to specialize in handling of Danny's wrestling affairs. He will always second Ferguson on the way to the ring. He will always wear a pair of plaid (red- or blue-based) pants and a powder-blue sportscoat. He will often be carrying a nine-iron golf club, his trademark. He's worked extensively in the south, but he is Midwestern by descent, and as close to white trash as a wrestling legend can be. Allan North: (Agent) The twin brother of adult film star Peter North, Allan's attempts at Hollywood agency have been a struggle. Danny is one of his most high-profile clients, and it's a case he has only because he was the only one to take Danny in back in the days before Hard Money hit it big. A better agent (a title that nearly everyone in Hollywood could lay claim to) would not have allowed this foray into wrestling. Peter will often accompany Danny backstage, but never out to the ring. The mediocre goatee is the only thing separating him from his brother. Vicki Siguchi: (Manager) Vicki has the kind of ebullient personality that is necessary to offset Danny's naturally cynical one. Her Masters degree in film wasn't getting her anywhere in LA until one day, working as a production assistant on the set of a commercial that Danny was appearing in, she used her extensive knowledge of 'Four Is A Crowd' trivia to impress him. He offered her the spot as a manager shortly thereafter. Phyllis Stein: (Publicist) A leading PR person in the Los Angeles area. Danny pays the big money for her services, and she rarely makes appearances because she's in high demand. However, she can make any move he makes look like a good idea, including this PRIME experiment. She's the problem-solver, and is very intense about what she does. Jim 'Polar' Pibb: (Bodyguard) Former NFL Player for the average 2-3 year career. Pibb didn't have the agility for pro ball, but he definitely had the strength. That let him to a bouncing gig in Hollywood, which made him a part-time actor and ruffian, which led him to some films of questionable content, which led him to Allan North. Danny hired him on Allan's suggestion. He's a family man with no family, and thus stays loyal to his employer, despite any abuse he takes. With Dametreyus, he will be with Danny practically at all times. Dametreyus Fuqueiawytas: (Bodyguard) Dametreyus is the more dynamic of the two, but his penchant for violence keeps him out of the regular spotlight, and regular work. After 'accidentally' mortally injuring a contestant on the popular game show Colisseum 2000, he was essentially blackballed as an entertainer, and turned to bodyguard work to make ends meet. He still gets recognized on the street by his character name from C2K, Obsidian. Like Jim, he will always accompany Danny. --> Other Feds RUSH (as El Spiffy!) Setup Description What he said. Other Moves Character Notes - Danny is the last of a dying breed - the action movie star who actually looks like an action movie star. He comes from the Arnold, Jean Claude, Sylvester mode. Right now, his only natural competition is Vin Diesel, but since every hack with a pretty face can get a gig kicking ass on film these days, the muscles and the pedigree don't carry the same weight in Hollywood, and he's not really a natural the whole "acting" part. - Here's the thing about the bodyguards - I know your dude or dudette is a tough character and can kick the asses of any 25 normal men. The guards are NOT background monkeys. They are both lineman-proportioned walls of flesh that are paid to protect their guy. They only way to get at Danny is to somehow work around them. If they are around, you are NOT going to go through them. You can beat them in a straight wrestling match, hopscotch, tennis, Halo and maybe even Scattergories, but you WILL NOT beat them in a fight. They earn money by hitting quick and hitting hard. I can't stress this enough. - As a specialist in the kinds of movies that win big at the box office but not the award circuit, Danny is prone to cheesy acting. If he's given the punchline to a scene, he will most likely deliver something that might make you roll your eyes a little. In other words, he's perfect for professional wrestling. He won't say something stupid to embarass himself (ie, like David Caruso on CSI: Miami) but he will, on occasion, drop something with a little too much self-importance - think of the one-liners in any enjoyable action flick. They're funny, but they don't always translate to real life, right? Well, that's how Danny is. Appearance: Stout. Slightly smaller than what he's billed at, but movie-star jacked. And I'm not talking the pretty boy shit that's going on nowadays. I'm talking Van Dam, Schwarzenegger, Bosworth, Stallone jacked. He doesn't just run on the elliptical, he picks up a barbell every once in a while. And maybe he and Barry Bonds have more in common than a California zip code. Wrestling Style: At his base, Danny is a mediocre worker, relying on heavy brawling and rest holds. He'd get booed out of the VFW if he worked one of these 'super indy' shows you see in the real world. However, had he been born 10-20 years earlier, he'd have been the king of Tennesee. He specializes in the 'Memphis lazy stuff,' as Lance Storm puts it. But now, things have changed. He has spent several months adapting the styles of Mr. Terry Funk. What caught on most was his intensity when it comes down to knock-down, drag-out shit-kicker kind of matches. However, a surprising addition to his arsenal was the inclusion of methodical mat work, a Funk staple during his NWA champ days. It isn't there to stay, but he shows flashes of brilliance. Against someone who takes to the mat with the greatest of ease, he'd be outmatched, but he would still hang in with the dude a lot more than you'd expect. In short he's gradually becoming well-rounded, but he is still no wrestling god. Strengths: - Uh, he's kinda strong, pun intended. He can push around a guy twice his size. Not that they wouldn't push back, but Danny is unnaturally strong. Probably because he didn't get that strength through natural means. - Big muscles from big needles means small testes and small impact from low blows. - He doesn't use a lot of complicated stuff, and doesn't always leave room for a reversal. Kick to face trumps spinning double underhook face breaker any day of the week. - When he's on offense, he can be relentless. Like any good heel, he can pause to taunt, but he often will string attacks together to maximize damage. This is something that developed from his Funker training. - He has a posse that stretches anywhere from three to five people (bios listed in the History - all but Phyllis Stein are potential seconds). None of them will assault another wrestler, save for Reggie Delray, who will get in the business of anyone possible. Weaknesses: - His temper has a nasty habit of clouding his judgment. Revenge and showing off his mettle are more important to him than making the smart tactical move. - He's always thinking business, which means he'll worry about his acting career first. He is a wrestler second, so going after the face is a big-time no-no. He will protect the Hollywood good looks at any cost. - He is a heel, and therefore must occassionally pause to remind others that they are the suck and he is the rule. - He cannot take to the air. He will never jump from a turnbuckle unless he has someone locked in a superplex or other power move. Aerial attacks don't work when you have no vertical leap. The Box Office Bomb: The Bomb is a move to be hit from a variety of situations, like any good finisher. The straight up, typical, squash-match performance is Ferguson premeditating the attack as his opponent is dazed, then picking them up for a scoop slam and, as they are at the zenith (full-blown legs-over-head), he drops to his knees for the tombstone. Against lighter wrestlers, if he has the control, he'll make it a one-armed scoop or he'll walk around a little before the actual drop. He'll also try to whip it out in unconventional situations (ie, tilt-a-whirl, catching a high-risk move, etc.) Bigger guys mean less flash on the Bomb, but it's still effective. As we've said, Danny's unnaturally strong, so he can make the lift, he just doesn't have the control for a full slam. Hence, the drop is better off for him, since all he has to do is get them up, and their weight will take care of the impact. Think Andre the Giant taking Hulk's bodyslam at WMIII, except on a smaller scale. There's a lot of strain, and the actual tombstone would be more like a drop than against lighter wrestlers. He would also look for it more in a surprise situation with this kind of opponent. For all situations, it's essentially a knee-drop Michinouku Driver. The faster he hits it, the more the impact is on the shoulders and not the 'head.' This is where the Bomb name comes from. Although he can hit it from multiple situations (as long as he gets that upside-down scoop positioning, it counts), he won't just bust it out. Heel psychology dictates less surprises. Hence, think of it more like a Pedigree in the way he hits it. Pausing, taunting, really letting it sink in. You do not kick out of this move. He does not bust it out unless he is ready to finish it all, and there is nothing the victim can do once Danny hits it clean. Only a run-in can save you. It's also important to know that he will never hit it ON to something (chair, cement, etc) unless he really wants to hurt you, and it will hurt you. If you get Bombed one week, plan on your character being sore the next. If you get Bombed on a chair or some other amplifier, don't plan on having him stand under his own power or wrestling for the next two shows. 'Trademark' Moves: Five Seconds of Fame: Acid Drop turnbuckle-run into a Tornado DDT. The FSF is Danny's fanciest move. It's a holdover from his days under the hood. Now, it's not as flashy...more like bounding to the top turnbuckle and spinning down with such force that it looks like an Evenflow DDT. Fergoplex: soccer kick to the nuts. Accompanied by commentators and everyone else screaming 'FERGOPLEX! FERGOPLEX! FERGOPLEX!' Spinning Toe Hold: Funk holdover. Duh. Entrance Music 'Soul Survivor' by Young Jeezy ft. Akon. You fucking love it. Ring Entrances No full write-up, but guidelines: - Lots of green and gold/yellow lighting - Beaming follow spotlight from entryway to the ring - strobes facing the entry, flickering like paparazzi flashbulbs - MNM-esque red carpet, if possible - Danny wears a green boxing-style robe over his gear. Hood usually down, but occasionally for a big match he'll wear it up. - Bodyguards come out first and flank the entryway while Danny emerges. Anyone else accompanying him to the ring then follows. The primary image of a Danny Ferguson entrance is a wide shot of the stage, surrounded by gold and green lights, with a bodyguard on either side of the entryway and a lone, powerful spotlight casting a silhouette of the Ferginator as he emerges, with the occasional "flashbulb" strobe giving us a flickering glimpse of color.
