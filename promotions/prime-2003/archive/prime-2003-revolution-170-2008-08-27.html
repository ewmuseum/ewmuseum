ReVolution 170 ---- And Here We... Go. Backstage, there is an elevator, and there are questions. How many times has ReVolution come on the air? How many times has it opened with a series of superstars that aren't under contract to PRIME? The van outside the Mellon Arena is a flat gray, the color of damp stone. The wheelchair strapped to the lift on the side is a black, mechanized monstrosity. The woman in the white pantsuit that's perched in the chair is reviled. "BOOOOOOOOOOOOO!" Her name is Alexandra Pierce -- though most people simply call her "Desade" -- and that sound will just keep getting louder as the night progresses, mark our words. Pierce is not a large woman -- in that contraption, she looks even smaller than her five-foot-eight, one hundred and forty pounds. But there's something almost... regal about the woman they call the Spider in the Web. Maybe it's the pristine, perfectly pleated suit, or the thoughtful set to alabaster features that seem positively ignorant of the bandage affixed to her forehead, the last remaining sign of her battle (some would say "beating") at Sin City's most recent extravaganza. Maybe it's the legion of very bad people at her beck and call. Looming behind the Director is a stick-thin man with dark, slicked back hair and perpetually frowning, craggy features, leaning heavily on a slate-gray stone club of a cane. His name is Mr. Hawke; you can call him "Mr. Hawke." Slamming the door to the van is a misshapen man-mountain with an ugly-looking (and probably smelling) cigar jammed into the corner of his sneer. His name is Mr. Ashe, and he is everything his longtime partner is not: large, foul-mouthed, not appreciably smart, and possessing two functional legs. The larger of the pair that used to be called Black Ops has a voice like sandpaper with an accent that's utterly unpleasant. Mr. Ashe: So this's fuckin' PRIME? Color me un-the fuck-impressed. He also says "fuck" a lot. The girl with the neon purple dreadlocks who clambers out of the van as Mr. Hawke raises the lift isn't big, either, her upper lip curled around the stud in it. Desade is cold, vicious, and more than a touch paranoid. When one's boss is paranoid, this means several things for her employees; for the DMH, it means never traveling with everyone in the same vehicle. Some of them arrive in nondescript gray vans with motorized lifts for the handicapped. Others prefer the kind of flashy entrance that leaves no doubt trouble's coming. Take the matte black Hummer and its huge rims that jerks to a halt near the van. The passenger door is open almost before the thing stops moving, another slight redhead (this one in dove gray, hair bound into a tight braid) slipping out to step to the Director's side; these people call her "Malice," and her relationship with Pierce is a good deal closer than the rest of these folks'.
