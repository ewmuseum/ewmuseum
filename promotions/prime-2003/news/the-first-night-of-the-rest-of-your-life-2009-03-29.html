The First Night Of The Rest Of Your Life

29 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
In early action, David Noble made his PRIME debut against Hank Cobb. D.U.I. was fresh off losing their tag team belts, and Cobb was ready to let out some aggression. Noble, however, had other ideas, eager to start a winning streak, while capturing hearts and minds in his new stomping grounds. The second in-ring offering featured Nine squaring off against the returning Troy Douglas. Owner of a perfect 1-0 record, Nine wanted to keep to his winning ways, although someone was Maliciously toying with him throughout the night. Would it be enough to take the judo star off his game or would the former Intense Champion come back with a bang? Shifting to tag action, the submain event was highlighted by the homecoming of Bryan Dawkins. In front of a raucous crowd, Dawkins teamed with Chandler Tsonda to take on Jason Natas and Hessian. The match was marked by a special entrance for Hawaii's favored son, tension between the very unbromantic team of Natas and Hessian, and some doubts about whether the former Universal Champion isn't biting off more than he can chew. In the end, old and new rivalries burned fiercely. Champion against champion. That was the treat for Honolulu fans in the main event. One could argue that High Flyer is a legitimate Universal Title contender, and while this match wasn't for the coveted strap, it was for mucho bragging rights. Having swatted away contenders like Dawkins and Jay Phoenix, Flyer wanted to put one more impressive win on his resume. Meanwhile, Snow, who'd done his fair share of talking the talk on the night, wanted to prove, once more, that he ALWAYS backs it up by walking the walk. Ending in somewhat controversial fashion, the match lived up to its billing.
