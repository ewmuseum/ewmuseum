The Destruction of Tchu - After Revolution 115.

17 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Remember Aladdin? Specifically, remember when he pretends he’s royalty and the genie hooks him up with that ridiculously bigass parade? Picture that sort of procession heading down the ramp to the happy tune of Sympathy for the Devil. This way it shaves off a few paragraphs, so maybe – perhaps, possibly, doubtfully – those in denial about having nothing worth talking about can bore you with something other than "Wow, Deville’s in-rings are loooooooooong. Real long. Ooooooh. Pwned!" Behind the scimitar-waving jihadis, the elephants . . . Richard: Damnit, Stewart, can’t you just give tickets to your family like everyone else? . . . the spaceship, and the fully-armoured honour guard, an army of slaves (black, white, yellow, whatever – we’re equal-opportunity slavers) appears, struggling under the weight of two great litters. On the first, sprawled out like Cleopatra, is the evil enchantress, Eva Van Lorne. On the second stands Caesar, the Divine Angelo, The Deville himself. Nick: Uh . . . He remembers losing at King of Kings, right? He does. But he also remembers telepathically telling Nick to shut up, not to mention having him hauled off by goons that one time he resisted. Nick suddenly remembers that too. Richard: Hehe. A fugly degenerate in the front row waving a sign that reads "Deville choked on KoK!" is incinerated by lightning, miraculously causing all other such signs to disappear. Now standing in the ring side-by-side (I told you: I’m saving words here; no screwing around tonight), Angelo and Eva survey the moaning masses with all the respect they’re due. The biting smirk washes off The Deville’s face as his eyes find the row of turbans in his menagerie. Darkening, he turns his scowl from evil to EVL. Angelo Deville: The fuck are terrorists doing out here? Eva Van Lorne: >.> Angelo Deville: I can’t associate with moon-c*nt-worshipping sand-n*ggers. Eva Van Lorne: <.< Striding over to that side of the ring, shaking his head in disgust, Deville leans on the top rope and loudly clears his throat. Angelo Deville: Hey Muhammad-spawn . . . He points to the back. Angelo Deville: Go face your "holy" meteorite and smack your heads on the ground somewhere else. I don’t have the time or the patience to explain freedom and reason to you, but the fact that we embrace it here means that until you filthy Ummah realize your Koran is the epileptic meanderings of a marauding child-rapist, not divine scripture – until you abandon your taqqiya and embrace life instead of death – you aren’t fucking welcome. The only thing you can spread with your swords is your ass-cheeks; your faith is a joke, and it won’t take here, so give up the shit. Trembling, the jihadis turn as one to engage in reasoned interfaith dialogue. Jihadis: Allahu Akbar! Then, also as one (they’re Borg-ish that way), they depress their CAIR-sponsored detonators and explode, martyring themselves in exchange for seventy-two men who could never get laid this side of the eternal curtain. Their intent was to take out the arena, but . . . Angelo Deville: Inbreeding idiots. This is e-wrestling. I can no-sell it here. With a wink for the Pope, Deville swaggers back to the centre of the ring, facing Eva with a grin. Angelo Deville: Now why did I come out here again? Van Lorne returns the mischievous look, dripping with sarcasm. Eva Van Lorne: I believe you were getting right down to business. Angelo Deville: Ahhhhh, right. Finally The Deville produces a microphone, which he raises to his lips only long enough to briefly engage the crowd, blue orbs twinkling. Angelo Deville: Watch closely. Spreading his arms wide, he slowly turns his head to the right, holding the stretch for a moment. Then he turns all the way to the left, once more holding it. Eva rolls her eyes as he frowns approval and nods. Then he pivots at the waist, once again twisting in exaggerated fashion, seemingly oblivious to the boos. All the way to one side, all the way to the other, followed by another nod and a chuckle. A finger stabs the air to beg another moment of indulgence. The Deville straightens, regulating his breathing, and hits a picture perfect backflip. Sneering at the fans, he addresses them through the microphone once more. Angelo Deville: Amazing, isn’t it? Spry as the day I conceived you. As Deville goes to speak again, most likely to waste more time before finally getting to the point, Eva steps forward, hands firmly planted on her hips as she whispers into his ear. In response he grins and narrows his eyes, motioning her to be patient. Then . . . Uh-oh. He’s starting to pace. Better get comfortable. Angelo Deville: I know. I know. As the lovely Eva has just reminded me, you’re all already intimately aware with my unmatched athletic prowess, with my superhuman capacity for convalescence. She points out that you don’t need any demonstrations, that you don’t care about that . . . what you want is for me to address Tony Gamble, and how he defeated me to become the Jewel in the Crown. At last the crowd has reason to cheer, drowning the ring with it, crashing down over him in waves. The Deville smiles. Angelo Deville: You want to see me as a broken man, solemnly paying homage to the new number one contender to Nova’s title. The cheers peak at number one contender before faltering at Nova’s name. His smile spreads. Angelo Deville: Most importantly, my dear enchantress astutely points out that you’re too stupid to grasp the subtlety, too ignorant to appreciate that that’s exactly what I’ve been doing. She thinks I should just spell it out for you, so there can be no confusion. Grudgingly, I agree. Welcome back to booing. Angelo Deville: Tony Gamble defeated me, right here in this ring. And in so doing he joined the ranks of Alan Kriegman, of Exceptional Dave Hurst . . . In defeating me, he died. To conquer me he had to break himself, to become a cripple, and so, as always, I’m the one left standing before you. As always, I win, even when I lose. Nick: Oh for! We all read the reports of Gamble’s injuries, but I don’t think it’s anywhere near that serious! Angelo Deville: Number one contender by default doesn’t have the same ring as King of Kings, but hey, I’ll take it. And so will you, quite simply because you have no choice. Like it or not, King of Kings will go down as a total victory – not only did Nova bring home the Universal strap, but we’ve protected it – King of Kings will go down as a grand "Fuck You" moment, and Culture Shock promises the same. Deville holds up a hand as if to stem the flow of hatred, or maybe just to wave hello. Angelo Deville: But! But . . . Contrary to popular belief, I’m not devoid of honour. When you see me battling for the Universal Title against my best friend, you’re going to know I deserve to be there. "How?" you ask? It’s simple, really. Suddenly halting, Deville slowly lowers his gaze to the canvas, pointing to a spot marked well in his mind. Angelo Deville: I’m going to remind a man of a commitment he made . . . I’m going to summon a sneeze and blow you all away. With a grin he raises his eyes to the back, staring defiantly at the former Universal Champion he knows is watching. Angelo Deville: Surely you remember the challenge you made, eh Tchu? Anywhere, any time, right? Well, now that you don’t have any gold to preoccupy you, I’m calling your bluff. Quit stencilling your neck and get down here. Now. Tonight, right here in this ring, you’re getting dismantled. Tonight, I’m uniting you with the fallen Mr. Gamble. The explosion is for the prospect of the clash, not the manner of introduction, but it’s thunderous just the same. Backing up from the ropes as if to clear space, The Deville waits, patient as a coiled spring. The sudden eruption of music from the loudspeakers expectantly raises the decibel level even more, though not in the expected way. Better Than You by Metallica Face scrunching with confusion, Deville whips his head back to look at Eva, who’s a mirror image. The surprise quickly morphs into mirthful disbelief as he turns back to the empty stage, knowing it’s a joke. He’s just about to begin a golf clap when a figure appears. ## I look at you, then you me Hungry and thirsty are we Holding the lion's share Holding the key Holding me back 'cause I'm striving to be ## With the 5 Star title wrapped around his waist, Tony Gamble marches proudly down the small portion of ramp, this time, the crowd cheering for him after he became the first man to hand Angelo Deville a submission loss. Up above his head on the Wal*tron, footage from Revolution 94 when Gamble locked The Illustrious Face Eater into his 'Smile For Me' submission and won the Internet Title plays. ## Better than you Better than you Better than you Better than you ## Tony takes his time walking up the ring steps, staring into the ring for a few seconds with his left hand on the top rope, before ducking between the top and middle rope to step into the ring. The Wal*tron now shows footage from Revolution 106, where Gamble slams Kenjiro Ito face first into the mat with his 'Stop Laughing At Me' signature move. ## Lock horns, I push and I strive Some how I feel more alive Bury the need for it Bury the seed Bury me deep when there's no will to be ## Gamble doesn't take his eyes off of Deville, and likewise, Deville does the same. ## Better than you Better than you Better than you Better than you ## And then, something else happens, unexpectedly... I Fucking Hate You by Godsmack Grudgingly, Deville and Gamble break their staredown, backing out of one another’s reach and glaring toward the back. Nick: And here comes Tchu! Gamble and Deville may have forgotten the challenge was laid at his feet, but he sure didn’t! He’s not wasting time with ceremony, either. By the time Nick orgasms, he’s already halfway down the ramp, and his long strides eat up the remainder in a hurry. Deville backs to the far side of the ring as The Inhuman Being dives in, popping up a bare few feet from Gamble. A respectful nod from Gamble is not returned; Tchu simply stares him down, then turns his focus to Deville. There’s nothing he needs to say, nothing he wants to say. Tonight he’s bent only on violence, and in The Deville he’s found his perfect outlet. Snarling, he strides toward his prey, rage clouding all else out of his singularly focussed mind. He doesn’t see the look of horror dawn on Nick’s face. The sudden adamant booing doesn’t register. The rapid footfalls of Gamble charging up behind him fall on deaf ears. The vicious forearm shot Gamble delivers, however, lands square across the back of his neck, and he feels it numbly as he goes down, as he does the deluge of boots the duo deliver. Richard’s laughter drowns out Nick’s sputtering as two members of the honour guard unmask and slide into the ring, revealed as Jason Snow and The Renegade. Eagerly they join the stomping. While Nova is off, oblivious, drinking and partying and celebrating as is his due, his comrades lay waste to his friend, heaving him up only to spike him back down with a four-man powerbomb, and again only to push him around between them, clubbing him down with elbows and knees and fists. The blood that begins trickling from his mouth and nose, then from his eye and the crown of his head, only seems to feed their fenzy. Eva ducks out of the ring, but before Nick can even think to claim it’s out of disgust or some sense of humanity, she grabs a chair and hauls back in, handing it to The Deville. Snow gathers Tchu up, literally needing to support him on his feet. The steel bends a full 90 degrees over the top of Tchu’s head, trying to crack it open and force it down into his abdomen at the same time. Deville points once more to the spot Tchu challenged him from so many months ago, discarding the dented chair there as if that sends some sort of message. A few feet away Tchu struggles in a daze, a pool of blood rapidly forming around him. There are screams of terror, pleas for a cease to the assault, sobs from children in the first ten rows… a little too close to the violence. But all the sounds, despite their impressive volume, fall on deaf ears. They fall on the deaf ears of Angelo Deville… too busy standing over his fallen victim, basking in the glory of wrecking PRIME’s wrecking ball. They fall on the deaf ears of Matt Ward… too busy trying to stay conscious, fight through the pain, and rise to his feet. Lifting his gaze from The Inhuman Being, Deville mutters a few words to his fellow stable mates, then gives a wave of his hand. With a nod of their heads, they slowly start to exit the ring, stepping through and sliding under the ropes, making their way back up the entrance ramp. And for the first time ever, a wrestling ring finds itself filled with the presence of Deville and Tchu. Richard: Why the hell did he wave off FU?! Nick: Perhaps there’s just a bit of mercy in the man’s soul. Richard: God… I hope not! For a moment, it appears as if Nick may actually be right. It appears as if Angelo Deville has called off the dogs and decided to leave his victim breathing. But few things are rarely as they first seem. It’s a thought that crosses Tchu’s mind as he stares up at the legend he called out following Colossus III. And, just a moment before being ripped to his feet by two handfuls of hair, he realizes that mercy is the furthest thing from Deville’s mind. A heavy right fist lands, then ricochets off the eyebrow of The Inhuman Being. The sound is disturbing, soft, yet powerful… somehow managing to echo into the ears of all the fans, trumping their screams, rendering them silent. Another punch follows. Then a third, and a fourth. The impact of bone on bone quickly takes its toll. Tchu’s vision blurs, then goes red, then blackens. Sounds begin to fade behind a sharp ringing, then everything starts to mute itself. His body goes limp, but he doesn’t fall, thanks to the hand that grips his scalp, holding him in place for each and every blow. Nick. STOP THIS! He screams, though he doesn’t have too. The fans are dead quiet. Tchu is barely conscious, and The Deville doesn’t care. And he doesn’t stop. Fist after fist connects with Tchu’s skull. It could be said that The Inhuman Being sports the infamous crimson mask, but it’d be most accurate to simply say that blood gushes uncontrollably from the horrific gashes making a roadmap of his head. Now, with each punch , the color red splashes through the air, splattering the mat. Like an abstract artist working his craft, The Deville continues the brutal act, painting the canvas with color. Nick: For the love of God! Someone, somehow, someway… STOP THIS! Richard: I…it…. You’re right. This is too much, I think. The barrage of punches slowly trails off, the sound of cracking bone still chilling the spine of all those within earshot. With a pause The Devil’s Don thrusts his neck forward, headbutting The Inhuman Being, the blood of his victim smearing his own smirking face. The act isn’t necessary… Tchu having blacked out somewhere around the seventh or eighth punch, but the act seems to punctuate the scene… a most memorable first ‘face to face’ encounter between the two iconic superstars. With a disinterested movement, The Deville releases his grip on Tchu and lets the broken man fall limp to the mat. Casually, he crosses the ring and steps between the ropes, hopping to the floor outside. For the briefest of moments he turns back to gaze at his work… but just as quickly turns away, satisfied with watching the image in larger than life form on the Wal*tron as he disappears to the back. Nick: I…I can’t believe what just happened here. Richard: The blood is just… everywhere. Nick: I… unbelievable. Unthinkable. In the middle of the ring, surrounded by thirty thousand eyes that watch a man with shocked and saddened eyes, The Inhuman Being doesn’t move, marinating in a pool of his own blood. The bright red hue that flows across the ring begins to dull as it fades to black and the PRIME copyright and logo appear in the corner of the screen. And the scene of a man’s career bleeding a slow death is just another segue to commercials.
