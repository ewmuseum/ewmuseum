Post-Show Parking Lot Brawl Set for ReVolution!

24 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Needless to say, it was a surprise when I received a phone call in the early morning hours on the day preceding ReVolution from a man identifying himself as Michael Sloan. "I’ve had enough of this no contact bullshit," he said while I tried to keep up with my notepad. "If Snow wants a piece of me, it doesn’t have to be in a ring. It doesn’t have to be backstage. It doesn’t even have to be in the fucking building. Where there’s a will, there’s a way." "Can you clarify that for me?" I asked, feeling nervous even though the man was likely hundreds of miles away. "The terms of Blair’s no contact order say that Snow and I can’t lay a hand on each other inside an arena while Revolution’s taking place," came the sinister voice on the other end. "Fine. If I can’t get Snow in the building, I’ll get him in the parking lot. So this is my challenge to him, and if he’s not a pussy, he’ll accept it. This week when ReVolution is over, he and I meet outside and we settle this. I’ll be waiting there for him." The line went dead after that, and I spent the next several hours trying to hunt down a phone number for Jason Snow. As far as the press knows, he lives somewhere in the north east, but we know very little else. Between dawn and noon, I tried my hand at coaxing three different PRIME office secretaries into releasing a phone number, but each time they told me that they were under strict orders to keep Snow’s personal information confidential. So finally, I gave up the fight and made a final request. "Can I get you to get a message to him then?" "A message? I... I suppose I could do that." I gave her the details of Michael Sloan’s challenge and left my name and number. From there, it was a waiting game, and not one that I was particularly fond of. Late that afternoon, when I had resigned myself to the fact that Snow probably thought little of the message, if he got it all, my phone rang. When I recognized the voice as Snow’s, I immediately prepared myself for a barrage of insults about my "inferiority" and possibly a few accusations of homosexuality. Instead, Snow’s voice was grave, and after identifying me, he said only two more words. "It’s on."
