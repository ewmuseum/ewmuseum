Why Was Steven Caldera at Revolution?

16 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"But, Mister Caldera needs to understand one thing, and one thing alone: In the event something happens to Rich Rollins at his hand, and it results in lost time for Rollins, then I will not hesitate to sue him, his company and his entire roster of talent. He's more than welcome to discuss. But, the second things get physical he's going to find himself at the mercy of a Chet Worth kick to the face." When asked about the possibility of PRIME hosting a Rich Rollins vs. Steven Caldera match, Worth was less than committal. "Caldera would have to sign a PRIME contract for that to happen. And even for one night, what kind of message does that send to his GCW talent? That he wants to get his hands on Rollins so badly, that he'll do whatever it takes to make sure he does? That he'll sell them down the river to be a PRIME employee, even for one night. I think Mister Caldera is smarter than that. Even if he did hire Brandon Youngblood. "The bottom line is simple: If Mister Caldera comes on Revolution again, he's going to have to sign a contract to be a PRIME Employee. I cannot in good faith allow him to go out to the ring and take a chance of him being seriously injured. His knees and back aren't what they once were, despite him being rather young. If Rollins were to tear his knee apart, or hurt his back in some way, I would be liable, and I can't have that on my hands. I'm in business to make money, not support another company while their owner heals." What about Seymour Almasy? "That was a shock, actually. I had no idea he was coming. Unlike Almasy, Caldera did it the right way. He told me he was coming, and I allowed him time. Almasy just showed up when he felt like it. Maybe if this were 2005, Almasy could still do whatever it is he wants to do. Fact is, his name means nothing anymore, and I could care less about him. As far as I'm concerned, he can simply turn around and walk away." And the budding rivalry with AWC? "I'm friendly with David Harber. We've talked in the past about talent sharing, and even doing a show together for charity. I think Mister Harber is smart enough to realize that the comments made by certain wrestlers are just ways of blowing off steam, getting themselves over in the eyes of the PRIMEates. I like AWC. I really do." What about Pleasure and Pain, the rumored team signing with another company? "We've had no discussions with them. They called, but, we weren't interested in the slightest. Tag wrestling is dead. If GCW and AWC can keep it up, good on them. We want nothing to do with more teams that won't show up when they're supposed to.
