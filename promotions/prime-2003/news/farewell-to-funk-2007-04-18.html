Farewell to Funk

18 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Basically, Asa has a fractured right tibia and some heavy ligament damage in his left ankle," said Evans. "Now, for your average, twenty-five year old wrestler these are perfectly treatable and somewhat minor injuries. Fountain, despite his strong physical condition, is not your average, twenty-five year old wrestler. These injuries are much more serious at the age of forty, and I'm afraid Mr. Fountain will be out of competition for at least three months." However, that wasn't the worst of the news. "I'll be honest," continued Evans, "I'm going to recommend that Asa retire. He's been told before that injury in this sport can be much more devastating at his age, and he's already paying the price. Professional wrestling is not meant for his age bracket, and unless he wants to find himself in here with a heart attack, that means retirement." I was able to speak with the Ayatollah of Funk' n' Soula as he lay in a hospital bed, a caste on his right ankle and a brace on his left, and was surprised to find his opinion was rather similar. "My age went n' caught up with me, brotha," said Asa. "Shakur fucked up my feet real good, but it wouldn't've happened if Garbage Bag Johnny hadn't jumped in n' sat my head through a god-damn table!" Asa remained calm, however. Almost accepting. "I don't know, man. I think my first mistake was getting' that Intense Title in the first place. All that hardcore shit ain't my thang, n' it sure ain't healthy for this agin' body. Where'd it get me? The god-damn hospital, that's where!" Asa went on to comment on his newly discovered child. "I got a kid now, cat daddy. Never thought that was gon' happen. But, I gotta be a man 'bout it n' raise 'im, an' I ain't sure if I can do that bein' part of PRIME. When it comes down to it, I'm too damn old for this business. I think it's time for me to stop beatin' up my body and start bein' a father." Disheartening words to hear from the fan favorite. Asa left me with this. "Ya know, I had a helluva run. I came back, proved I still had what Ed used to call "It," and got to do my funky thang for the fans again. But, at this point in my life, I think it proved to be just a lil' too much. Time to take that step into fatherhood. That'll be my biggest challenge yet." On behalf of PRIME, this is Thomas C. Robinson wishing Asa the best of luck in life. PRIME will be a much less colorful place without him, and the funk and soul he brought to the ring will surely be missed.
