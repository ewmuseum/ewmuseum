The Broken Being

21 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Those words were said with an almost defeated smile, and in fact it seems, the illustrious career of Matt Ward may have come to an untimely end. The beating Ward suffered at ReV 115 left him in bad shape. It was clear from the moment the attack began that the once self-proclaimed "Anti-PRIME" wasn’t going to walk away with just a few cuts and bruises. FU, more specifically… Angelo Deville… delivered the kind of assault that left many children in attendance in tears, and left their parents’ jaws dropped in a state of shock. Since that night, Ward has remained silent, doing his best to steer clear of the spotlight completely. But, away from the cameras and journalists, a long road to recovery is left to be traveled, and slowly but surely, The Inhuman Being has started to take the first steps of what will surely be many more. "Matt suffered a severe concussion." Dr. Allen Greene gave his diagnosis. "This is the sort of injury that cannot be taken lightly. A lot of people hear the word ‘concussion’ and think a quick recovery since there are no broken bones or torn ligaments… but the truth is, concussions are extremely serious and can have long term affects that can cripple a man’s career." In fact, it is the third documented concussion Ward has suffered since joining PRIME in September of 2005. Adding to that are concussions diagnosed earlier in The Inhuman Being’s career, plus any that may have gone undiagnosed. . "When you’re talking about that many concussions" stated Greene, "it’s time to start thinking about retirement." But its not just the concussion that has Ward sidelined. Several days after the assault, Ward underwent an operation on his right knee to remove torn cartilage among other things, and the rode to recovery has just begun in that regards. "It was some simple surgery that he’d been putting off for the past year or so." Greene added. "He’s started the rehab, and that could take anywhere from three or four weeks to eight or nine. Either way, I’ve advised Matt that, in my personal opinion, retirement is the best option; however, if he’s determined to make a return to the ring, and early April comeback would certainly be possible." Ward, it seems, has made up his mind. "I think it’s time to call it quits. About four years ago, I left the world of pro wrestling and promised my wife I had retired. A year and a half ago, I joined PRIME and have been failing to live up to my word every day since. I’m 30 years old, ancient by this business’s standards. I’ve got a baby girl who’s learning to walk and talk and doesn’t need her father stuck in a wheelchair the rest of his life." ‘The Inhuman Being’ is officially still under contract with PRIME, with several months remaining, but any lost wages don’t seem to be swaying him. "I have no idea what the money issue would be with an early retirement… you’d have to ask my agent, Marc, that question." Marc Avit couldn’t be reached for comment, but whatever hurdles there might be in the technicalities of an early retirement, Ward seems ready to jump them. "I figure it’s about time I start making good on that promise I made to my wife."
