Nothing Is Sacred In Sactown

2 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
But PRIME isn't one of those top-heavy feds that feeds you scraps for appetizers, neither. No, there was plenty of slobberknockerin' action throughout the night, starting with a tag showdown, waged on the singles side of things. Vejumin Rippa, one half of Wicked Ways, offered to put his (see: Ruby's) money where his mouth was, stepping into the squared circle with longtime veteran Duke Williams. The Hossmaster General and The Shark got the crowd moving with the night's opening contest. The penultimate match, on a night where PRIME put quality above quantity for its in-ring action, featured the peculiar stipulation of making Hessian the special guest referee. Now, here's the worst kept secret in the world: Hessian doesn't like Fusenshoff. So if you really want things to get violent, what would a responsible steward of PRIME (see: Tyler Rayne) do? Obviously, he'd thrown Fusenshoff in said match. He'd also pair him with Jay Phoenix against the Union to test not only the GTT standout, but also see whether the new tag tandem is worth their salt. The Murder Show was completely unbiased and things went off without a hitch...right? And sure he had the unpleasant task of facing his two best friends in the main event, but Tyler Rayne still had administrative work to do. Kaiser Vashaun, for one, is officially sick of the Rayne reign, but someone else got to enjoy being handed a Universal Title shot for next week at ReVolution 185 (see: Angelo Dev-wait a second, go read the damn show, you lazy jerk!). Plus, Jay Phoenix finds his true calling, Devin Shakur decides to pursue peaceful means (see: no), and Hunter Sabuani reminds PRIMEates why they have a tendency to hatehateHATE the folks from down the road in Vegas.
