Shake Ups for ReVolution 247

16 June 2011
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
It started out as a joke, then developed into a nasty rumor, and now for the final show of the long and excruciating Culture Shock 2011 arc, it has become a reality. Christian Daniels is going to be in charge of the administrative duties for the program. Last week, Devin Shakur was caught up in meetings with the Board of Directors, although still found time to interact with Hessian and Alexandra Pierce amongst others. This week, he's not going to have that luxury so he has designated his right hand man to do his bidding in the office. Coming out of a BoJangles, Daniels and Shakur were more than happy to talk about the decision (because Shakur forgot he parked on the other side of the building and he paid someone 20 bucks to go find his car). "Last week was one of the more tiring weeks that I've had." Shakur remarked. "I was in and out of the executive suite all night and didn't get all of the dealings done that I would have liked to. So, in an effort to ensure everything runs smoothly, I've delegated the duties to CD, who has had sufficient on the job training for quite some time so I think he'll be more than capable of handling the dealings." Daniels gave his own interpretation of what he felt the job required, "Yee just sit behin'a desk'n pretend'a do paperwork 'til some'ne walk'n." Shakur added, "Don't forget we're having the big screen shipped and we've got Hulu Plus on there." Daniels grinned when hearing that. "I's been on SNL kick lately's tha'be good." "I'll have to put in a call about the Web Only feature though, because well we're not having that if we're going to be stuck there for hours upon hours." Shakur concluded. The camera man managed to get off a question about Lance Marshall and whether or not Daniels would give him fair and unbiased treatment given his newfound role for the program. His reply was quaint, "Nah"
