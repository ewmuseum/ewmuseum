Colossus Spotlight: Devin Shakur

3 September 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Matt Mills filed a report after taking a trip to Shakur’s suburban palace in Raleigh, North Carolina days before Colossus VII. Shakur is a man who is all about a bottom line of making money for PRIME, but won’t spare a few bucks for a feature he created to send a limousine out to Raleigh Durham International. I had to get a taxi. It’s not as bad as trying to flag one at LaGuardia, but people in this state hold a bizarre loyalty to an otherwise universally hated individual. I arrived at his home and was astounded at its sheer size and elaborateness. He lives like a king and doesn’t have a problem making that a well known fact to anybody who drives by. An iron gate encompasses his home and guard dogs are always roaming. I sped walk past them to ensure I wouldn’t become lunch and knocked on his door, leaning against a column while I waited. Christian Daniels, not wearing traditional biker garb, but rather a pair of jeans and AC/DC T-Shirt, let me in. He pointed me three floors up to a master office I could barely make out from my vantage point. Not wanting to draw his ire, I took off my shoes before walking on a carpet worth more than what I have made during my entire stint with PRIME. Upon reaching a spiraling staircase, I pondered whether I should take those stairs or a nearby glass elevator. I asked Daniels and he shrugged his shoulders, more captivated by an uncensored version of Daniel Tosh: Completely Serious. He laughed hysterically at a joke about fabreezing homeless people while I trudged up stair after stair until I reached floor number four. After staring at a few paintings on various walls, I hung a left and came upon Shakur’s master office with the door already nudged open. I tapped his door lightly and was taken aback by former 5*Star and Intense Champion Sun Tzu screaming Chinese at me and holding her signature pistols in my face. She lives her gimmick to its fullest. Shakur laughed at my misfortune and called his longtime girlfriend off before pointing to a beanbag chair and asking me to sit. It was the most comfortable experience I had all night. Shakur was playing poker at Full Tilt Poker under his screen name OMGKeanuReeves. He chuckled in delight after beating Patrik Antonius out of a six figure pot which he got lucky on when rivering an ace to make Broadway. He told Patrik he had to handle an interview and swung around to face me. "Let’s get this over with because I have to make more money, lots more money." Checking my notes, I asked, "Why and how did you get involved with PRIME?" Shakur responded, "I got mixed up with the wrong crowd while at the height of my business days, a bunch of idiots trying to get at Gamble. They wanted me to do their dirty work, but that fell through. I got a chance to hone my fighting abilities and beat people up. It intrigued me so I kept myself around. I gained a few friends, bagged Sunny, won some Championships, and the fame from that kept me around even longer. Then I just wanted to beat the shit out of Tyler Rayne and everybody associated with him. I devoted a year to that and it was one of the most productive of my career." "So what has PRIME meant to you?" I asked, trying not to sound robotic. "I’m an example of someone who can come in from the bottom with minimal fanfare and become the biggest asshole in the company. I’ll go down as a true villain, the biggest villain in PRIME’s history. Nobody will be able to come close. No group will come close to causing the same amount of destruction I have. Plain and simple, I’m the most infamous entity PRIME has ever produced: Beyond Lindsay Troy, Matt Ward, Killean Sirrajin, Jason Snow, and whoever else you want to name. I’ve surpassed everybody and gone into a class of my own because I’m at the top and running the show." I blurted back without really thinking, "Wouldn’t Tyler Rayne also hold such a prestige?" "Little cockbag has his own prestige, but not on my level. He’s got secondary prestige. Big whoop, he won a Dual Halo. I stopped putting faith in how that got someone over when Dusk finished top five. 5*Star prestige doesn’t garner you much other than a bunch of What If’s. He got put down by me on two different occasions, so until he can come back and beat me, I’ll always be a tier above." A quick glance down at my notes gave me my next question, "Give some thoughts on Colossus: the stage, spectacle, and moments of its past." Shakur chuckled, and took a sip from his can of Pepsi, "You need to be more off the cuff. Reporting isn’t really your forte is it?" "I ask questions. I’m an interviewer. You know that." "Mix it up a little. You had a bit going there when mentioning Rayne earlier. I’m not in a bad mood, and we’re not on camera, so I’m not trying to make an example of you." "Fair enough," I said, and put forth a question from previous responses, "So you really are so obnoxious that you believe you’ll be the most remembered name in PRIME history?" A tip of his Pepsi can and a smile gave me his approval, "There you go. Yes, I’m really that obnoxious, because I’ve got a list of people I’ve put down that goes longer than anybody else out there. Nobody else in PRIME has created such a longstanding impact without letting up. Every show I’m doing something. Every Pay-Per-View I’m making an impact. All the babyfaces might not be intimidated by me, but they know never to try and go after me. I’ll put them down like all those in the past." "So how do you explain the situation with Matt Ward?" "I’m proud of that kid for stepping up and believing in himself. He can’t allow himself to be controlled by his past. He’s got to blaze a path for the future, and I think he’s on his way to doing that. I’m confident in his ability to knock Troy at Colossus, handle the Elite tournament, and become our franchise player." "And what about Castor V. Strife, you’ve handpicked him for success?" "Strife is someone who brings a demographic we haven’t had since Ferguson. He’s underground Hollywood to a tee, and I’m glad we can bring someone like that in and feature them as heavily as we have. I think he can handle the top of the mountain and deserves to be there." "What happens if Olsig comes out on top? Surely you didn’t anticipate Olsig getting this far as the Universal Champion." "No, I didn’t. Olsig winning the Dual Halo was a shocker in itself, beating Strife at UltraViolence, and retaining against Chainz were also pretty stunning. I knew he was capable of handling himself in the Intense division, but not much above that. If he manages to get past Strife, I’ve got someone waiting in the wings to give him a massive run for his money." "Who would that be?" Shakur shook his head, "I can’t reveal that in an interview intended for the Internet. It wouldn’t reach the broad audience a ReVolution would. I’ll answer all questions on that front after Colossus." A quick shoulder shrug preceded my next question, "How do you exactly go about being a front office guy? What is your business model?" "I want to torture all those who have stood in my way in the past, but I always want to make money at their expense. I could force issues, but wrestlers always seem to have problems with one another, so issues kind of flesh themselves out. If I wanted to, I could book a Rayne/Troy feud and sell it to the board as a money making success. I don’t think anybody would object, but they seem to find themselves in other quagmires and probably will for quite some time. We’ve turned things around since UltraViolence, and Colossus stands to be our biggest moneymaker of the year in all aspects. Over the year, we’ll have been making a healthy profit since two of our three arcs have been booms." "Mind if we get back to Colossus?" "Why not, we’ve got all the other shit out of the way. What about the Colossus stage? I’m not a wrestling connoisseur, but Colossus is the Super Bowl, World Series, and so on for PRIME. It’s the crème de la crème for our company. Because of moments in the past such as Rolo, Sirrajin, Ward, and Troy, we’ve got a great amount of prestige. We’ve had classic matches like Ferguson/Nova, Youngblood/Nova, Sirrajin/Ward, and so on. Legends are made at Colossus, and careers can come to an end. As a company, PRIME has showcased every big name in wrestling at Colossus." "And you specifically?" "I have a memory from each Colossus that will notoriously go down in history. IV gave me the chance to expose Dusk, Lavelle, and Winters as the frauds they are. Colossus V gave me access to an exclusive club of people who have headlined the program. Yeah, Tsonda ended up beating me, but he caught me on the downswing. I got my revenge so I’m fine to say he got me. I managed to pull it into my own home state. Not many people can say that. VI showed Bryan Dawkins as nothing more than an upstart who couldn’t hang with the big boys. I gave him a big league injury and he never recovered. All his mentoring from Rayne and Tsonda couldn’t save him when he was locked in a cage. VII will cause my greatest rival ever to go down in flames." "Which leads me into my final question, about Tony Gamble," I asked, "You guys have been up and down the road for almost your entire career in PRIME. The match between you two was rumored for both Colossus V and VI, but never materialized. You finally get what you want, but is this really what you want?" Shakur took a moment and pondered his response, slamming down his laptop to avoid any incoming Instant Messages or notices. "To see Gamble and Shakur on a big stage is what people have been craving ever since we forged an alliance all the way back in the mid 100s of ReVolution. I know Gamble is one of the most accredited individuals when he steps in the ring. He’s made people like Deville and Snow tap. Most people can’t attach that to a resume. Most people can’t say they’ve taken Nova to his absolute limit on two different occasions and defeated the likes of Wade Elliott, Chandler Tsonda, and the Illustrious Face Eater. He’s got a Hall of Fame career, but none of those people hold the weight I do." Shakur leaned forward in his chair, hand rested comfortably under chin while he finished his thought. "I’ve got my special stipulation, a steel cage with no door. If he really wants to continue this nice guy bullshit act he’s got going on, which everybody knows is a fucking crock, then he’ll get eaten alive in a matter of minutes. I’ll drag it out to prove a point, but he’ll be unable to hang with me. I’m a fighter by trade. I don’t do a lot of actual wrestling. I’m not looking to get him down to the mat and exchange submissions. I’m looking to hurt him. I’m looking to fight him. If he decides to show his true self then he’ll have a bit of a chance. I’m not going to submit. He can contort me in ways I’m not supposed to go, but I’ll never tap out to Tony Gamble. I’m not going to lower myself to that level. He better bring every trick from his years in the business if he wants to come out with a chance for a future in PRIME. I’m going in there looking to cripple him like I’ve crippled the rest, and I like my chances. One way or the other, I’ll come out ahead at Colossus. Bank on it." I got an indication Shakur didn’t want to continue and showed myself out after a handshake. I glanced at his laptop and saw him immediately back at poker. I wanted to ask if I could get a piece, or share in a hand, but I didn’t figure he would let me lose his money.
