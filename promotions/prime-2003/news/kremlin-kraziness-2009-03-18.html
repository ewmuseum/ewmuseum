Kremlin Kraziness

18 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
But before Night Two could begin, there was much to do. Unexpectedly, Tyler Rayne and Devin Shakur had a fight. Not a match, a fight. Yes, there was technically a winner and loser, but there wasn't a referee to be seen, nor were there any rules. And at the end of the showdown, there was no debate about who had gained the upper hand. Then, in tag team action, the Union took on Wicked Ways. Ruby's boys had made themselves quite visible in recent weeks, doing the dirty work of Devin Shakur and irritating Lisa Tyler. The Union, though, would have none of it and challenged the Wicked duo to a match. Who would find themselves with a leg up in the tag division? If there exists such a thing as total domination in the wrestling world, it's embodied by Hessian. The Murder Show, newly-crowned Intense Champion, has swatted aside all-comers since his arrival in PRIME. At Culture Shock, Fusenshoff dared step in front of the monster, offering up himself as a legitimate contender to Von Kelsig's belt. Next up was a match that almost never happened. Indeed, sacrificing the last spot in the Dual Halo to make sure he got his match, Jay Phoenix had taken the moral high ground, all just to make sure he could get his hands on Hunter Sabuani. This match, though, only served to enumerate the ways in which the Raja of Roguishness is a real bastard. Team V.I.A.G.R.A. sought to make history as the night continued. No team in PRIME has ever held the Tag Team Titles more than once, and the reunited trio of High Flyer, Tony Davis, and Mary-Lynn Mayweather was going for a record-breaking third run with the straps. But standing in their way was Delta Upsilon Iota, current champions. Duke, Colby, and Hank had a little something to say about putting their own stamp on the PRIME record books. The night's submain event was born at ReVolution 180. The then-inactive Lindsay Troy encountered a surprisingly brash Kaiser Vashaun, who told him that he would use Troy as a springboard to superstardom. Now, it was time for Kaiser to put up or shut up. Without his Intense Title to define him, the Next in Line would have to live up to that name and do something rarely seen in PRIME: beat Lindsay Troy in a big-time match. And then...there was one. Jason Snow. Chandler Tsonda. Dozens of superlatives were thrown around for this match, but it boiled down to one thing. The two best in the business, one-on-one, for all the marbles. Snow pranced around with Tsonda's Universal Title, making the claim that it was as good as his anyway. Here was his chance to prove it, with the big money wrestler in PRIME across the ring from him. These two legends didn't fail to deliver an immediate classic. While that would've been a superb PPV in its own right, it was on to Night Two, where Bryan Dawkins was ready to end High Flyer's dominant 5-Star Title reign. As an appetizer for the Halo, these two sacrificed their bodies (Flyer for the second time in two nights) to put a definitive answer to the question of who deserved the belt more. The big Kahuna. The primetime match for those who love primetime matches. Little needs to be said about the Dual Halo and what it means. This year featured over forty participants, some insane spots, surprise guests, and some serious history-making. At the end of it all, just like we promised, one man or woman would be handed the literal keys to the kingdom. Check out Night One, then take a quick breather, before plunging into Night Two to see what happened at PRIME's inaugural PPV of 2009!
