Shakur Goes Over the Edge; New Contenders for Championships Named

27 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Colossus 230 didn’t fail to disappoint in terms of federation shaking movements, and is almost certainly the beginning of the end for one of the greats in the industry. The night started off with no opening segment, instead a package of Colossus highlights followed by a rundown of upcoming matches kicked off the 230th installment of PRIME’s quasi-weekly house of fun. Patterson Loch introduced himself to all the referees on staff along with his opposition for later in the week, The Ego from New England, Nitz Donnelly. Needless to say, the man all about standards and practices probably should have practiced some better standard conversation etiquette because he left Donnelly and the zebra patrol scratching their heads. Before Donnelly and Loch could hash out their awkwardness in the squared circle, Maine was greeted by an appearance from Devin Shakur in one of the most peculiar segments, at least in terms of crowd reaction. The Boss in Black is known for generating more heat than the sun in some areas, but on this night he was able to walk the aisle in silence, a good thing since he wasn’t feeling spectacular about having to face a public after submitting for the first time in his illustrious career. All inquiries about the crowd’s allegiance were answered with a boisterous "You tapped out" chant once Shakur started talking. The chant became so visceral, Shakur ended up leaving. Individual fans were able to take their own pot shots, but a line directed at Shakur’s longtime girlfriend, turned Shakur around and may have caused him to lose whatever marbles that were left. He blasted fans in the front row, hopped the barricade, targeted some teenage girls, a small child, his father, and then gave more general statements before saying PRIME would never come back to Portland, Maine again. When someone mentions the right way to do wrestling, now there is a symbol for a new generation to observe, Patterson Loch. He went out of his way to try and perform in his contest the proper way with Donnelly, but the ire of Donnelly resulted in The Ego pulling out a move from his High Class Ass heel days and pulling the tights. He got the victory, leaving Loch in absolute shock and on the verge of a seizure. Patterson Loch, 0-1 in his PRIME career, and another momentum building win for The Ego. The explosion of anger that was Devin Shakur continued to roll over into the backstage, as the Boss in Black was kicking everything nailed down and launching everything not nailed. He knocked an employee down and beat an intern to a bloody pulp for mentioning the word tap. Shakur also made a decision which he seemed to believe would infuriate the crowd, by signing over a piece of paper to Lisa Tyler. Shakur then attacked a defenseless television when Mike Goldberg advertised tap out. The start of three consecutive in-ring promos kicked off with The Big Bad Boda Daddy and his brother in arms, David Slobada. David introduced his brother before both entered the ring and gloated about Boda’s Colossus victory. Boda also brought out what appears to be his new catchphrase of, "I’m 5*Star and I’m Better than You." He also vowed to take on anybody for his strap. Speaking of championship matches, our next guest booked two pretty highly strung ones. Emerging from the backstage area dressed to the nines in purple was Lisa Tyler. This wasn’t the same Lisa Tyler who took abuse from Michael Sloan during her days as the handler of PRIME, or when she was Shakur’s secretary. This was the Lisa Tyler who emerged from out of nowhere on 185 to take over PRIME and did so with a vengeance. She announced The Boss in Black relinquished 49 percent of his Board of Directors powers over to her, thus giving her a healthy amount of authority once again. She made two huge matches and set a location for ReVolution 234, the Fargodome in Fargo, North Dakota. Boda would compete in a triple threat match against Vangelus Olsig and Nitz Donnelly. She also claimed Lindsay Troy would have an opponent, but at the moment she didn’t provide a name. The blockbuster match she booked was one for Castor Strife. His first Universal Championship defense will come against none other than fellow Shakur henchmen, Hessian. Matthew Ward decided to grace PRIME with his presence, and the usually snarky Ward provided some legit commentary. He admitted to submitting, and said pardon the lack of caring if it was the first time in his ten year career of submitting. He seemed all but ready to issue an apology after going through a brief history of Troy, himself, and fellow Hall of Famer Killean Sirrajin, but was interrupted by a stagehand who summoned him to the back. Vangelus Olsig and The First had a bout next, stemming from Colossus VII’s Main Event. The First had a steel chair and almost cost Olsig the Championship. The match started out in Olsig’s favor, until on the outside, First was able to wangle away and focus on Olsig’s knee. For the duration of the match, Olsig’s knee was targeted. It took one mistake from First, and good ring awareness by Olsig, for The Prince of Delusion to sneak away with a pinfall victory. Seconds after that match, fans got to catch a glimpse of Tony Gamble walking down the hall and whistling a jolly tune. Well, he was whistling until he came upon the two biggest cats in PRIME, Christian Daniels and Hessian. It seemed, with Shakur appearing from a side room, Gamble had walked into a setup. However, there to save the day and provide some of her classic pop culture was Lindsay Troy. These six were all set to brawl and start their match without Rayne and Strife, but Dam stepped in and said Lisa Tyler needed to see Troy in her office immediately. The segment concluded with a CHEEAH CHEEZE bitch slap from Troy to Shakur and Dan Ryan wiping cheese on Daniels shirt. They swore death by impalement to both. Next, Matt Mills was able to gather Hessian from that fiasco straight into an interview about his thoughts regarding Castor Strife and the Heavyweight Championship. Needless to say, Hessian was none too pleased about his past, having only now just received his first heavyweight title shot in ten years. He also made a claim he didn’t care who faced him, because he never lost a match as a number one contender in his career and he didn’t intend to start anytime soon. The Main Event saw a chaotic 8 person match eventually break down after a bit of civility. Although with the folks involved, nobody expected a traditional tag match. Brawls took place inside the ring and outside the ring with no one gaining a clear cut advantage. The two highlights of the night will be Dan Ryan leaping over the top rope to splash Daniels, Shakur, and Strife, and then Hessian trumping Ryan to splash all seven people on the floor. The end saw Shakur almost submit to a double submission from Troy and Rayne, Ryan clothesline Hessian and Daniels after they made the save, and then Shakur getting caught with a von Doom’d for the pinfall victory.
