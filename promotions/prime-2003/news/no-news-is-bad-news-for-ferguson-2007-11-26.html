No News Is Bad News For Ferguson?

26 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
After Ferguson was knocked out by Devin Shakur at the Great American Nightmare/Devil's Night supershow, he underwent what doctors termed as "emergency cosmetic surgery" to fix a broken nose - the product of a particularly vicious knee from Shakur. The operation, intended to eliminate any lingering trace of the break (known in plastic surgery circles as Owen Wilson Cleft), was announced as successful immediately after the show. However, that seems to be the last that anyone - PRIME officials included - has heard from the Superstar. "Danny declined the help of our physicians and chose to take his medical care under his own supervision - and his own checkbook," said Blaine Blair when pressed on the issue. "We haven't heard from him, but we also haven't made tremendous effort to contact him. "The typical medical suspension for injuries like his ranges from 45-60 days, pending a doctor's approval. When we reach that window, I'm confident Danny and I will be in touch." Ferguson's latest contract, a two-year extension signed after Colossus III in 2006, ends in August of next year. The company did not express any concern over Ferguson's current state of obscurity, given the large amount of time remaining on the agreement. The loss at GAN was the Superstar's first in a singles match this year. He had previously won five straight, as well as five more in the non-PRIME-affiliated Dupree Cup tournament.
