Post-Culture Shock Personnel Report

21 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
To the surprise of no one, Troy Douglas and Jason Natas are back with PRIME. While neither man's original contact was due to expire until the end of the fiscal year, both inked new longer-term deals designed to spread out their salaries over a longer period of time, giving both men and PRIME more financial stability in a financially unstable time. King Blueberry and Jon Rhine, both of whom have competed in the PRIME's squard circle, but in small doses, were given lucrative short-term deals. While Tyler refused to discuss the length of either deal, a source who wished to remain anonymous from the Legal Department said the contracts were "short and sweet, with the ability to be renewed at will for the indefinite future." No one seems to know what the long-term future holds for either man, but signs are very good that we might see both men competing at Colossus. David Noble, recently of fWo, also inked a standard PRIME contract, and Tyler was said to be pleased at the cooperation between industry giants PRIME and fWo. As the winner of the Dual Halo, Garbage Bag Johnny will certainly be getting a boat load of attention in the upcoming weeks, as many questions swirl about just what GBJ might do with his Golden Ticket. One question that won't be swirling, though, is his contract situation. Johnny has signed on the dotted line, making him an employee of PRIME. However, the same Legal Department source said, "I've never seen so many weird stipulations in a contract," adding that Johnny "got a lot of leeway for a guy negotiating his first contract." Although the source wouldn't clarify, there seem to be some anomales regarding the contract, which may lead to questions, after all. The memo from the Talent Relations Department also added that more signings would be sent to the Legal Department this week, with several familiar faces expected to show up on the road to UltraViolence. On the slightly more negative side, Lisa Tyler issued lengthy injury report, detailing the various aches and pains of what seemed like the entire roster: Good news was that the Universal Title showdown at Culture Shock was everything fans hoped it'd be. While fan favorite Chandler Tsonda dropped his title, the match was generating huge hype on message boards and blogs, with fans praising both competitors for delivering a match that lived up to the massive hype. Bad news was that neither man walked out unscathed. Jason Snow was diagnosed with a mild contusion on his right shoulder, but is said to have already resumed training for this week's ReVolution. Chandler Tsonda, on the other hand, has repeatedly refused surgery on his bad back in the past, and reports of a slipped disc emerged after Culture Shock. Apparently, the Model Citizen hasn't been cleared yet for Rev 188, but he's on the card as of now. The memo said nothing of a "game-time decision," but that appears to be the case for Tsonda. If the former Universal Champion can't go, sources say that Lindsay Troy may replace him. The Board of Directors, wary of Ms. Troy's leg holding up to the pressure of a big-time match AND the Dual Halo, was thrilled to see the Queen of the Ring come out of Culture Shock with nothing more than standard bumps and bruises. After putting on a classic of their own as a preamble to the Dual Halo, High Flyer and Bryan Dawkins both ignored doctors' advice and competed in the vaunted main event. Both men were treated for dehydration and exhaustion, with Dawkins requiring extra care for a re-opened cut on his forehead and whiplash, courtesy of Kaiser Vashaun's finishing manuever. Flyer, who's schedule to wrestle in ReV 188's main event, was slightly loopy after the Halo, continually asking passersby if they were interested in a used tricycle. Dawkins, a proud Hawaiian native, has vowed to compete for a hometown crowd at ReV 188, and Mary-Lynn Mayweather was quoted insisting that "Flyer will wrestle at ReVolution, continuing to resist all logic and common sense." Other injuries sustained were Colby Korver's strained left rotator cuff, Hank Cobb's bruised clavicle, and Hessian's myriad lacerations. None of the medical staff could confirm Tyler Rayne's injuries, as the Underground Pimp refused treatment from Dr. Fihlguud's team, despite having been carried out of the Halo by EMT's. Rayne was seen limping out of the arena at night's end with the help of the aforementioned Troy. Wade Elliott, in what seems like perpetual competition with Rayne, also refused medical attention. Devin Shakur had to be escorted from the building in a wheelchair into his limousine, forgoing an ambulance and hospital stay. Reports from the PRIME medical staff claim that Shakur was unable to stand after the Dual Halo. Christian Daniels stated that The Man in Black was suffering from "stingers" and would be fine in a couple weeks. Both of his hands were also heavily taped. Prometheus' new "made" superstar will be kept off ReVolution 188 as a precautionary measure, but is expected to attend the show. More seriously, Tony Gamble suffered a devastating neck injury, which led to his early elimination from the Halo match. Initial MRI's were inconclusive, and the former 5-Star and Intense Champion was being flown to a specialist this week. But the personnel report took a grim tone, wishing Gamble luck in his recovery and admitting that it would be "at least two months" before The Grin would be in a PRIME ring again. As a last word, Lisa Tyler's office suggested that more would be done to alert the public about signings, hirings, and injuries in the future.
