ReVolution 225 Recap: Independence Day Extravaganza

5 July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Vangelus Olsig opened the program... Well, not entirely. He was in a portion of the opening segment, but Chives the Agent bickered back and forth with Matt Mills until Olsig emerged from the locker room with a flamboyant new image and demanded a threat, a challenge, to his reign as Universal Champion. The Prince of Delusion also set his sights on Garbage Bag Johnny and the 5 Star Championship, seeking to complete the Grand Slam of PRIME Championships. The opening contest saw The First and Steve Starr making their 2010 ReVolution debuts against one another. A contest which featured a plethora of high spots, vicious knee strikes from The First, and classic acrobatic wrestling from Steve Starr. In the end, Starr couldn't connect on a Starry Nights and left the door open for The First to land a Cut The Thread for his first pinfall victory. After the contest, Devin Shakur appeared on the PRIME*View and gave a run down of giant announcements, setting up the next two shows and Colossus Pay-Per-View. He booked a contenders match featuring Tyler Rayne and Castor V. Strife, with the winner being in line for a Universal Championship match at Colossus VII. The Boss in Black also responded to Vangelus Olsig's taunt and gave him a legitimate threat in the form of Chainz. The Monster from Hell's Kitchen will get his first opportunity to vie for Universal Championship gold at ReVolution 227. Shakur also placed Boda as the top contender for Garbage Bag Johnny's 5 Star Championship at Colossus, but also tacked on a defense for The Dirtiest Dude in PRIME at 227. Shakur would not reveal the name of GBJ's opponent, vowing to tell all at the end of ReVolution 226. Skylar Montgomery cut a scathing promo against JUST Wrestling and PRIME, claiming himself to be against PRIME and also saying he would lose his upcoming match against Adam Garcia. To open the bout, Montgomery tried throwing chairs in the ring, but his tactic was thwarted by senior referee Bernie Roberts. Once the contest got underway, Montgomery and Garcia battled back and forth, but Garcia ended up dominating the contest. Skylar refused to submit, claiming he was too avant-garde for a submission loss, so Garcia opted to pull another finisher from his play book (Heat Check) and pin The Imaginary Man. Michael Sloan, better known to wrestling fans as Chainz, got a triple dose of airtime leading up to and including a match. First, Devin Shakur and Christian Daniels warned The Monster about his place in the company. Despite receiving the privilege of becoming a contender to the Universal throne, Shakur said Sloan was replaceable and a part of the machine. Immediately afterward, Sloan and wife Tracy had a sweet reunion in Sloan's locker room. To top the evening off, Sloan then had to encounter Tony 'The Grin' Gamble. In a contest that saw The Grin battle valiantly, it was The Monster who managed to squeeze out a victory after Gamble's resilience and speed ran out. Tchu has had a rough go in PRIME ever since becoming the Intense Champion, thanks in large part to a 24/7 rule implemented by Devin Shakur. His night didn't get any better when an opponent for 226 showed up at his locker room door and warned him of the beating to come. According to guidelines directly from Shakur, this opponent could pick and choose when he wanted to fight Tchu on 226. The opponent was none other than Christian Daniels, Shakur's personal enforcer. Lindsay Troy didn't have the anticipated return at UltraViolence she would have liked and ReVolution 225 will be another night stashed in her rear view mirror sooner rather than later. She went out and did battle with Castor V. Strife in a back and forth contest, almost pulling out a victory with her Crowning Glory. The God of Snuff, however, denied Troy the victory and caught her with The Director's Cut for a pinfall victory. Once the match concluded, she had a conversation with Tyler Rayne and a more heated conversation with Tchu. The world of Lindsay Troy will no doubt have some strange twists and turns in the upcoming weeks if 225 was any indication. In a wild Main Event, Tyler Rayne squared off against Garbage Bag Johnny. Both men gave almost everything they had and took each other to the limit. The amount of times each man was dropped on his head got lost in the shuffle of the match, but neither man will be right in the noggin for a few weeks. It took almost every move in his arsenal, but Rayne pulled out a nail biting victory over the 5 Star Champion and showcased himself to be a legitimate threat to anybody who crosses his path. Most fans thought the show would close on such a hellacious Main Event, but when the lights went out and Peter Frampton's version of "Black Hole Sun" crept over the speakers, those fans knew one more revolution of the roller coaster was about to take place. Devin Shakur and Christian Daniels emerged. Shakur sarcastically thanked Rayne and Johnny for their performance before announcing the first official match for Colossus VII, himself against Tony Gamble. In the shocker of the evening, Shakur also decided to address the growing number of enemies he's made ever since becoming the head honcho in PRIME by adding onto his personal security. A man's body came flying through the curtain, and the body belonged to none other than Tony Gamble, Shakur's top enemy. The Grin was beaten severely and wasn't moving. Who would, or rather, who could Shakur align himself with to do such damage? Hessian. The Murder Show stalked down the aisle with Gamble over his shoulder and dumped the former 5 Star Champion at The Boss in Black's feet. To close the show, Hessian, Shakur and Christian Daniels saluted themselves in victory over Gamble, leaving the audience to wonder how Shakur managed to pull off having another massive bodyguard, one believed to be an enemy, at his side.
