The Annual PRIME Fan Lawsuit

15 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
"Obviously, we are sorry about what happened to that young woman," Tyler said, in an e-mail interview granted to this site. "But for her to file a lawsuit over it is ridiculous. If you’re in the front row, it’s obvious that you have to pay more attention to your surroundings. From nearby fan accounts, she was doing her hair and chatting on her cell phone when she was struck. We’re confident that her frivolous lawsuit will be thrown out." Not quite so confident was former PRIME legal counsel, Mr. Jason Andrews. "She’s got a case, as much as I hate to say it," Andrews told us. "She took a punch straight in the face from a 6’2", nearly 220 pound man. Almost all of the other cases of fans being injured we’ve seen involve being knocked over by a fight in the crowd, or maybe getting kicked in the leg when a wrestler flies over the barricade. Aragonne got knocked clean out by a right hand that broke her cheekbone, and if that gets before a jury, the jury’s going to eat her story up with a spoon." As fans know, this is not PRIME’s first brush with bad publicity. An incident that occurred during a Chet Worth match (PRIME policy still requires this incident to not be discussed in detail on PRIME programming or media outlets) resulted in a series of drastic measures taken by FX to ensure that a repeat did not occur. Only recently have these measures been rolled back. Andrews went on to advise PRIME to settle out of court, if at all possible. Devin Shakur could not be reached for comment.
