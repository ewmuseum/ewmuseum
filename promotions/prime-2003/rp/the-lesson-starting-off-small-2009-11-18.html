The Lesson: Starting Off Small

18 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/18/2009 Shifting uncomfortably in the front passenger seat, the 2002 Silver Toyota Camry not made for someone of his height, Geiger adjusted the heat setting downward, adding in some air-conditioning. Camal, without looking up from his notebook and newspaper, shifted the heat back to its original setting. “Hey, I'm burning up in here,” Geiger complained. Camal simply noted, “Then take off your jacket. Some of us aren't descended from countries where cold is a climate possibility.” So he did. Geiger's family was primarily from Austria, Holland, and Norway. His Van Herpe patrilineal heritage put him squarely in the land of legalized marijuana but his mother was directly raised in Oslo. The fifty degree temperatures, made worse by the overcast skies and 5 mph breeze, didn't bother Geiger one bit, but Camal was covered head to toe in heavy gray sweats to keep as warm as he could. For whatever occasional annoyance that Robbie would present, he wasn't anywhere near as bad as Geiger Herpe. Camal just didn't like the new recruit. Though being 37, the man had never really matured. A life of utter stagnation after dropping out of high school at the age of 14, taking a series of “gigs” under the table, living the life of an unskilled migrant worker just to live on a steady diet of dollar sandwiches at fast food sandwiches. And Camal would've been happy to leave him to it had it not been for Fallon. Geiger had nothing to do. He didn't have a phone that could do more than make calls and take a few low-res pictures. And his job here was to just wait for everything to finish up. While Fallon was inside the Workforce Partnership center working on her resume postings on Careerbuilder, Monster.com, and most importantly Craigslist, Camal was reading statistics from the past years' races, running numbers on his own paper. This left Geiger with an eager, and idle mind. And that led to the cardinal sin. “How much you think we’ll ma--” Geiger never completed that sentence, receiving a hard back fist strike directly to his mouth. Camal, deadly serious, commanded simply, “Out.” Geiger gave a look, that bewildered expression that indicated that he was surprised not only that he'd been hit but that Camal would even think about doing so and have it be so immediate and determined an action. That hesitation only angered Camal further. “GET THE FUCK OUT!” Geiger was the kind of man who responded to yelling. His parents bred that into him, though not entirely by intent. Taking his brown leather jacket off of the back of his seat, Geiger pushed the car door open and swung his legs out onto the street. Standing up to his full height of six foot eight, he was glad to get his legs stretched. Sitting in that car was killing his knees. But Camal wasn't done. “Spin around.” “What the hell are you--” *Click* The sound of the hammer on Camal's SIG Sauer P226 ST pulling back. Geiger was quiet. He may have been 270 pounds but he wasn't bulletproof. It was real, Geiger had no doubt. Camal was a con but he didn't lie within the circle. “Spin,” Camal repeated. “Three times.” The sight of a tall European-American wearing an aviator's jacket, khaki shorts and sandals, twirling around in place in the middle of a sidewalk, would strike one as odd if anybody had seen it. But on this block, most of the office parks had been abandoned due to the high rents not agreeing with the recessed economy. When he was done, Geiger stopped in place to find the gun still pointed at him. “Spit,” Camal then commanded. No arguments this time from Geiger. “Not on the car, dumbass.” Satisfied, Camal put away the gun, resetting the safety and putting the gun into the front of his pants. “I hope you boys are playing nicely.” Fallon opened the rear driver-side door, slinging her black polyester Sumdex Impulse tote-brief into the car. Fallon Herpe, Geiger's younger half-sister by 15 years, was the real catch. Very intelligent, very hard-working, just enamored enough with the idea of making a quick buck without being so disdainful about the economic system to be unwilling to use it to her benefit. Fallon would be a great catch for any team of con artists by herself but sadly, she was a package deal. Without Geiger being included, Fallon wasn't interested. Camal had to adjust by coming up with very minor tasks for Geiger to take on, chores of little to no significance that weren't designed to trick Fallon but merely to keep Geiger in the dark and out of the way for a small cut of things. If anything, Geiger was little more than some deployable muscle in a pinch, but he didn't know much about fighting and around Kansas City he was a small fry so Camal didn't tell this to Geiger so that the man didn't get an undeservedly big head to complement the usual stupidity. “Is Robbie done yet?” she asked, letting down her brown hair from its bun. Camal shook his head. “Haven't heard final report from him. He texted half an hour ago; used the cooked IDs to open four bank accounts so far but he's got two more to do.” She saw the gun, but then they were only half-siblings, she the bastard daughter of a one-night stand with a Dominican stripper. Out of familial obligation Fallon wanted Geiger in on things to get paid, but his mistakes were his own to answer for. “I put out every desperate plea for work that I could think of. I should be getting some hits back on those in a few hours, as early as it is. The turnaround on those is what, a week? Gei, get in the car.” Geiger never really talked back to Fallon. He recognized her as the smarter of the two. She was the leader; he only trusted Camal to a point because Fallon did. Camal capped his blue Bic and folded the loose newspaper sheets into the spiral notepad. Placing everything underneath his seat, the Mayan turned the key to start the engine fully. “You'll be getting offers for the next two weeks. You know how they'll look: poor spelling and grammar. We're not interested in the ones that ask you to send any money to get started on a business opportunity.” “Of course,” Fallon rolled her eyes. “And turn down all those Saudi princes and Nigerian lawyers and U.S. Soldiers who somehow need my help to get millions of dollars out of their country. I was pretty sure the idea was to get richer, not poorer.” Every now and then, Camal would have to catch himself. Being alone too long with Geiger would make him have to carefully explain everything to everybody, which wasn't a great way to build morale among smarter accomplices. “Keep a log on all the offers you get for transferring money orders outside of the country.” Camal pulled off, checking around just to be sure that nobody else was on the street. He could never be too careful. Everything could impact a score, whether it was part of the job or not. “When they get back to you, give them all the information from your false ID and the mailbox that Robbie's setting up for you at Delivery Express. We'll collect the checks together, I have more numbers to run.” He had a hand in a little of everything around the city and the world, and kept his eyes out for everything. This was just the latest job. Not everything was a one or two day affair. Sometimes advance work had to be done.
