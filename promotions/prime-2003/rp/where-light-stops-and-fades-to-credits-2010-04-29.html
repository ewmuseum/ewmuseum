Where Light Stops, and Fades to Credits

29 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 29, 2010 The sun rose again in the San Fernando Valley. Seems a fact I’ve been reminding myself a lot of lately, having lived in the suburbs of Los Angeles for over two months now. There are hills and rays of light that spill over them, like the opening scene of some cheap film. Pills, ghettos, coffee shops, tanning salons, talent agencies, $437 sunglasses, and resumes; God are there resumes. Everyone believes the dream can come true until it doesn’t- and that’s when sunrise becomes a relief. That’s when you’re glad it ever came back. While one neighborhood awoke to daylight, another hid in plain sight, nestled away in a glittery coffin. Hollywood and its bastard son, Castor V. Strife (my new employer, as it were), only rendezvoused at night. Otherwise you made an appointment like everyone else. Lucky for me, I had a reserved seat at the front of Castor’s waiting room line; one of the many perks to being a biographer to the stars. “I want you to visualize horizons dreamt but not touched. Felt, but not penetrated. Glimpsed, but not seen. And then I want you to reach inside your head, pluck the thing out, put it in a Ziploc bag, and throw it in the ocean,” said Castor to a particularly bright-eyed female prospect sitting at the other side of his desk. Ziploc bag, did he say? Why so brand loyal, I wonder, before I remember not to wonder about things like that. There genius switch has no ‘off button’, they say. Well, neither does the eccentric switch. “Jamie, are you listening to me? THROW IT IN. Let the undertow do with it as thou wilt,” HUH? “and someday, whether near or distant, if that bag with the thing in it should wash up on shore, you’ll know then it was yours to have.” She look confused the girl did, but making sense out of anything at Castor Strife Productions is something…something that begs for a clichéd Alice in Wonderland analogy. And frankly, I just don’t have the cojones to make one. “Ohhh I get it! This is one of those like, trick questions where like, if I tell you the answer I’m supposed to give, then I don’t get the job, right?” For a second she had me wondering, myself. “OK well then I’m definitely NOT going to throw those bags into the ocean ‘cause that’s a $5,000 fine from California Parks and Rec!” “Shame yourself,” Castor said abruptly. “What?” “Shame yourself,” he repeated deadpan, no lifting of the eyebrows. For his faults, Castor was at least convincing. “Callbacks are on Thursday, my secretary will notify your agency of the time. Shame yourself.” “Why do you keep saying to shame myself? I…umm…” she looked at me as if I had the answer. I shrugged and nodded. “…I don’t get…exactly…what you want me to do…exactly,” she said with nervous laughter. “SHAME YOURSELF!” yelled the filmmaker. The room stood quiet except for the faint voice of the secretary behind the office door asking what was going on. Castor leapt out of his seat and an inch away from the girl’s face; her color-drained, visibly shaken face. “SHAME YOURSELF! SHAME YOURSELF! SHAME YOURSELF! SHAME YOURSELF! SHAME YOURSELF!” he said in rapid-fire verse, each syllable ringing with volcanic signature. As this monotonous diatribe ended with dry spit shooting from the final ‘F’, from his teeth to her brow, the girl’s eyes stayed locked on the empty chair straight ahead. Immediately, tears began to stream down her face. An uncomfortable three second silent passed between them before the girl crossed her arms one over the other to pull off her shirt. Just then, Castor let out a roaring laugh that soon became raspy cackling. “Are you seeing this, Spades? SHE THINKS…SHE THINKS” he began, interrupting with laughter, “SHE THINKS I WANT HER SHIRT OFF!” Walking back to his chair, he turned his attention back to the girl. “Do you honestly believe I wanted to see your tits? As if ten-THOUSAND other women who look just like you haven’t walked in here and did the same thing?” She was now crying, uncontrollably, but mustering enough mettle to speak a sentence or two. What was once a raunchy looking rodeo queen had been reduced to dripping mascara and spit-drool collecting around the mouth. She looked like one of those prom-night whores after throwing a tantrum about the limo driver being late. “I don’t know what you want me to DO!” She was at a loss, but Castor simply closed his eyes, inhaled strong, exhaled loud. “When Castor Strife tells you to shame yourself , he doesn’t mean take your shirt off…like you did for every other talent scout, producer, director, and studio exec from here to county. He wants you to make it special. And the way you can make it special for me, is to lose three pounds.” It’s times like these where instead of overanalyzing the situation at hand, I visit my happy place. And for me, that happy place is three o’clock yesterday afternoon in front of Arby’s, eating one of three delicious roast beef sandwiches, overhearing two men discussing the merits of the Robert De Niro/Cuba Gooding epic, “Men of Honor.” “IF A NIGGA WANNA SCUBA-DIVE, LET A NIGGA SCUBA DIVE!” It was the kind of day you don’t get twice in a week, and- “I can lose three pounds. Whatever you need,” she said wiping her tears, almost relieved at the request. Castor explained further… “Here. I want you to lose three pounds here, in my office. You will do this…as my personal biographer and I watch you.” “Here?” There was no response, but an expectant stare and the pressure of authority. Castor blinked twice, and at that the young girl closed her middle to her forefinger. In the span of a minute or two, she went from aspiring starlet to just another marionette hanging from the fingers of Castor V. Strife. The fingers slid down the throat, and then came the gagging. Err-uh. Err-uh. HACK. Err-uh. Err-uh. GARGLE. Out spilled a liquid stream of bile, latte, lactated chunks, brown stuff (or was that the bile?), and more latte. She looked up at Castor, but he was unflinching. His silence was a demand for “More! More!” And so she obliged. Err-uh. Err-uh. HACK. Err-uh. Err-uh. GARGLE. “Enough, enough,” he said, putting up a hand. “Come back Thursday, I might have something for you. “ “…” ”You can leave.” So it was written, so it shall be cleaned up by Castor’s secretary. Literally, that’s what it said on the notepad he had me pass to Davina at the front desk. “Mess on floor. Please clean immediately. –C” Later in the day, my new sponsor and I walked the rectangular shaped garden in back of Castor Strife Productions. We discussed topics ranging from Italian cinema, to his early days in the wrestling business, until we came to his recent loss in the main event of UltraViolence. The topic seems to unsettle him every time it’s broached. Castor was fairly open most of the time, but when it came to the machinations that brought him to PRIME, he was silent, always silent. This time was different. “Not long ago, maybe at the turn of the new year, I received an envelope with a letter inside. It was a request, not unlike the many similar requests I’m sent on a year to year basis. This letter, I cast it aside and forgot about it. Until the next one came. And the next. One by one, sometimes with money, sometimes without. The requests…they became stranger and stranger. And I became more intrigued.” He was holding back, I knew, but for good reason. Suddenly we found ourselves back to the young girl from earlier. One more who shamed themselves in the hope of being a star; one more in the presence of Castor Strife. He was like the Indian slumlord from that movie. You know, the one who steals and blinds the children who beg for him to increase their earning potential. Castor Strife: Lord of Hollywood’s beggar children. Scarred by him so that no one else would take them. Psychologically enslaved and taught to love their master. Tobeys, every single one of them. Oh, he’ll give them new names, he will. They’ll never forget who made them. Don’t read this with pity; read it with fear. Today, it was the young girl with a dream and a resume. Yesterday, it was someone else. Soon it could be Lindsay Troy, Tyler Rayne, Olsig, anybody. “I don’t need to be a veteran of PRIME to know every person has a weakness. Where there is strength, I will create weakness. And I will…write this down Spades…I WILL exploit it.” His hand crept over my arm; he wanted me to look him in the eyes as this next sentence was spoken. “What separates them from the girl in my office is nothing. William Spades, they’re all the girl in my office.”
