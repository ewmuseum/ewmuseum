Date: Here and there “A hero is someone who has given his or her life to something bigger than oneself.” -Joseph Campbell February 1st, 2008 Outside Battalion Hall, Seattle, WA 11:38 PM The night brought forth a wintry tundra to the streets of downtown Seattle. Puget Sound Wrestling’s latest show was long since complete. Once the fans left the comfort of Battalion Hall, they scattered like mice looking to bunker down into the nearest shelter, hoping to stay as warm as humanly possible. They had places to go, sleep to catch up on and loved ones to return home to. One such individual had no place to go, so he found himself wandering those streets for lack of anything better to do. Soren Knox took one fleeting glance over his shoulder to the Hall and laughed before marching down the long, listless city block. The last three hours saw him thrash around some Indy kid while donning the mask of Captain Justice; his best character yet, the boisterous defender of justice. Far from his early days as the inadequately-named Terra Ryzur and Dr. Strangle-love. He attributed the former to lack of research on a character and the latter to copious amounts of Nyquil from the cold he fought that evening. The amount of money mattered not and the fans even less; Hell, he just got a promotion earlier that week at Puget Sound Pipe to Warehouse Foreman. Money wasn’t a problem. This was a gig on weekends; a gig he loved. Hell, to play such a fun and obscure character, he’d do it in an empty arena for free if that’s what it called for. It was almost his lifeline away from the commonplace 9-5 world he’d been clawing away in for so long. That was all he needed. But, alas, as the old cliché goes, all good things blahblahblah. And as such, he found himself once more singlehanded on a Saturday night. He’d taken this same walk down Pike every last Saturday in the last year and had this exact trip down to a science. He’d walk three blocks down the road, catch the Metro #3, ride it seven or so blocks down and head home to his dank one-bedroom apartment, then cuddle up in his King-size bed, ready to start the day anew. Midnight assaults, muggings and any combination thereof were of no concern. A man that was pushing the two-eighty mark on the scale and towering over the average man was in no danger of having his wallet lifted. The routine continued like clockwork as Soren reached the end of the last block and found himself at the bus stop. Taking a seat, he stretched his throbbing knee (worked over by the same really green kid) and his eyes scanned the two individuals to his right: some gangly-looking rocker kids that always frequented this particular corner. One of the kids made his way over to Soren and held out a hand. “Got a light?” He inquired. “Not a smoker.” Shrugging, the kid turned his iPod on full-blast and cranked whatever stupid Dragonforce song came up on random mode first. Soren went back to his business of staring straight ahead at the languid section of buildings across from him. He’d counted all the busted windows many times in one particular building and wondered what lazy-ass super was under their employ. One that needed a good punch in the face, he thought. Pfft. Who was he kidding? He wasn’t some kind of hero. He liked his life the way he was. He was young, healthy, and still had about fifty, sixty years tops to live out the way he was going. No sense mucking all that up. Acquiescing the notion of breaking routine, Soren whipped out his copy of The Treasure Box and buried his nose within the text. He still had about twenty minutes till the bus got the-- “HEY! WHAT THE FUCK!” Snapping out of his trance, all that registered to Soren was the tread of tires burning as a black Ford Taurus sped off into the darkness. A brick had whizzed by the heads of one of the rockers, smacking with a dull thud against the sign behind them. One of the two kids shook a fist futilely in the mysterious vehicle’s direction, cursing a storm so people on the rooftops could hear him. “YOU FUCKING COWARD! GET BACK HERE!” Shaking his head, the kid turned to his friend and muttered. “Dude, you all right?” “Yeah.” “Where’s a fuckin’ cop around when you need one?” “Pssh. Probably out gettin’ sucked off by a bitch or somethin’.” “Figures. Twats should be out doing some good where it’s needed.” The two merely exchanged glances and took a seat on the bench to settle their nerves. Soren merely shot a glance at the flickering halogen bulb hovering over them and sighed to himself before returning to his book. Yup. Just another boring Saturday night. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ February 10th Silver Lining Academy “Hey, Mr. Knox. Have a seat, would you?” “You know I hate it when you call me that, Sonny. Just call me Soren.” “Hey, anything for one of PSW’s biggest wrestlers. Let’s discuss business, son.” “Sure. So… what’s this about? You usually don’t call any meetings like this impromptu. When do the other guys get here?” “They aren’t coming. I closed down the gym so we’d have some privacy. What I’m about to ask of you, sir, goes far beyond what I normally ask of my pupils.” “What do you mean? What business?” “Well, Mr. Knox… er, Soren… I have a proposal for you. One I truly think you could benefit greatly from.” “I’m listening.” “How would you like to be a hero?” “Pardon?” “A hero, my friend. I’m not talking one in front of fifty people on weekends… how would you like to be one on a much grander scale?” “Heh.” “You think this is funny?” “I’m sorry. Just... something about the way you said that. You make it sound like I just say yes, then I report for duty, sir! “At ease, Captain Justice. Thank you for coming to my aid on such short notice.” “It’s both an honor and a pleasure to be called to arms by such a respected individual such as yourself, Mr. Silver, Chairman of PRIME.” “Gotta say, kid. I admire your moxie. It reminds me of a young… well, me. The young days when I was but a young the-eliminator-danny-williams of worlds. Back when PRIME was just a bunch of smaller territories, then I got rich, bought out the joint and paved way for PRIME Attitude! Ah, the Attitude era… Raisin’ hell, drinking beers, throwing the finger during federal court hearings involving steroid use… where was I? Right. Captain Justice, thank you for meeting with me this evening.” “Aye! To what do I owe the honor of this meeting?” “Good sir, I’m not going to waste time blowing smoke in your ass. I’m going to give it to you straight. The world… the very world I created… it’s found itself in a state of turmoil.” “Seriously?” “Like a burial from Jean Levesque, my patriotic compatriot. Times have changed. The people no longer hold our sport in high regard as it once was. They’re all tuning into other shit like Mixed Martial Arts or watching some roid monkeys hit balls over the fence. Many of our more bankable stars have come and gone like farts in the wind, taking my money with them. Some have retired, some died, others went MIA and I think one of them got into some sort of freak accident involving an electrical cord and steroids, going crazy as is my understanding from my local media outlets. I’ve had to resort to hiring… *shudders* Floyd “Money” Mayweather to keep my audience tuning in. “ “…” “We paid him Twenty Ms to appear at Culture Shock to take on Tom Walczak in a boxing match, but one of them is a black hole of charisma and can’t even do an interview to save his life… and the other one is just a stupid boxer that keeps nagging me for more money.” “OH, MY STARS, GARTERS, AND FLAGS!” “That’s putting it mildly. What do I see now when I tune in? It’s just… it’s God-awful. It’s sickening. In my absence, the Powers-That-Be-A-Thorn-In-My-Ass have driven a perfectly capable champion in Sonny Silver out of this business and now it’s in the hands of… HER. The evil beast that broke my arm and put me on the shelf for six months! Evil has run rampant. There’s swearing, debauchery, lewd acts of drunkenness, gratuitous violence… AND I’M NOT EVEN THERE TO BE PART OF ANY OF IT! The project has been contaminated, sir and I am at my wits end trying to figure out what to do!” “I can’t believe it, Mr. Silver. It sounds like PRIME has fallen into some sort of dystopia from which there may be no hope of revival.” “That was my original thought. And I…I have to be honest, sir, I thought this was a suicidal undertaking for anybody else, but for you, Soren, you have it in you to accept this proposal and do great things with it.” “Wha…? This has gotta be some kind of joke.” “I joke a lot, yes, but this particular instance, what you are looking at is indeed, a finely-crafted contract for PRIME.” “I don’t be-” “Oh, believe it, Soren. Out of all the pupils that I’ve trained, the countless bodies that have stepped foot on my door begging to help me aid them on their personal quests for infamy, it’s your case that captures my attention most.” “Come again?” “My other pupils… they’re in it mostly to get famous, bang chicks, and show little to no discipline and any regard for anybody but themselves, but you… you ask for nothing, just show up on the weekends, help out other students, and your gentrification over the last two and a half years has been nothing short of amazing. Tim and Rex have given you great tutelage and can back up my claims. They’ve given me glowing reviews of your progress when I’ve stepped away.” “I… wow… I’m not sure what to say.” “I know you know what to say. After a meeting with some of the new owners, sir, I’m to return in a managerial duty and I’d like to represent you.” “But what about PRIME? It’s rather weird you asking this of me. Last I heard, and no disrespect to you, Sonny, but I heard you walked out on your contract due to creative differences with them. I heard you weren’t on good standing with them.” “No need for apologies, you have a right to know… it’s true. There were creative differences. I wanted the belt, they didn’t want me with it. All I did was win a title and suddenly, people started spreading unfounded rumors about me. That I left my family for this business; that I was out having sex with hookers and all this other bullshit. Management wasn’t too keen on somebody like their unfounded misconceptions of me representing their company, so they kicked me to the curb first chance they got.” “Damn.” “I know. But new owners means new ballgame and that means we get to show the world that we are something to be reckoned with. I agreed to the new managerial contract so I can heal up some injuries to my arms and back for a while, but in the meantime, I want you to shine, Soren. I come to you because I believe you have exactly what it takes to take PRIME and exorcize the evils that have cluttered up what was once hallowed Sports Entertainment ground!” “Just tell me what I have to do, sir.” “That’s the thing. There’s one addendum that I have to throw in here to before we officially get you into PRIME. I’m still recuperating from injuries to my arm and back and it could be a while before I can return to active duty, but before I do, I want you to take a look at this.” “Take a look at what?” “Inside that manila folder is your official PRIME contract, allowing you to join should you accept my proposal. I’ve heard about your body of work and I’m quite impressed, sir. You’ve gone from small-time place to small-time place cleaning up evil and straightening out all the bad apples and other garbage that sullies the sport for good men like us. But now, Captain, I’m calling you to duty in a much bigger environment.” “Sir, I am honored!” “Glad to hear it. There’s millions of people out there we need to pull back into the fold and I believe you embody the qualities that the masses look up to. Truth. Justice. Some other stuff about American something or other. Eight hours of sleep, no drugs, and eating the right animals, vegetables and minerals. You can be a hero. You can be a star! You’ve read it over, Soren. What do you say?” “…” “Well? You can truly be a hero looked up to now, no longer having to confine yourself to normalcy. You’re unique, yes, but your eyes I’ve seen many times before. You want to be somebody and make a difference. What are you waiting for, son? Stardom is calling your name.” “Heroism is calling, Captain Justice. Will you accept the charges?” “Sir, allow me to shake the hand of my new client!” “Sir, allow me to shake the hand of my new manager!” “Attaboy, Soren! Just put your future autograph on the dotted line and we will show the world the name of Soren KnoxCaptain Justice! “Great! And with a wave of this pen, justice shall prevail and exile those that dare exist in the name of villainy. So my next question…When do I start?” “How’s March 10th sound for you?” “Sonny, I do believe Captain Justice has a date with destiny!” “Excellent. We’ll do great things, You and I.” “Your first gig will start soon, son. Hop to it.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ MISSION STATEMENT You see this cage, son? This structure represents everything that’s wrong with this business. One of the world’s most perilous constructs stands before you and in mere moments, it will be all in your hands. Athletes from all walks of life from the skinny and the small to the fat and the tall have found themselves trapped within its iron clutches like lab rats in an experiment viewed by the blood-thirsty masses. The people that reduced my legacy to nothing more than smoldering ashes didn’t just stop with me. They created it to risk the very lives that people like you and I live; honest, hard-working men that worked themselves to the bone for a business that remains ungrateful to them. The war you are about to wage is one on par with a brainchild that only Peter Jackson could fathom. It’s a battle of supremacy; a nesting ground of impure, conflicted and battle-tested creatures that will no doubt destroy you if you find yourself unprepared for the job. However, should you live up to your name and strike one against the tyranny of PRIME, its very power will become your own. No longer will we have to take part in a world where good men are tossed to the wayside in favor of evil chosen children; freaks that deserve not to be uttered in the same breath as people like us. No longer will evil reign supreme. Justice can prevail! The world of PRIME will be our oyster and we can shape it however we choose with that Golden Ticket. We can eradicate the evil that exists within its halls and we can rebuild it... stronger than ever. In our image. You, sir, are not conflicted. You aren’t bogged down by impurities like any of these guppies you’ll be swimming with. Your convictions are your own and they are pure. You have but one vision in front of you and that is to win this for the side of good. With your iron resolve as your mightiest weapon, never falter and you can complete your mission. All you have to do, son, is be the last man standing and our operation will be complete. Can you do that? Can you stand up for justice and win one for the good guys? It’s all in your hands, Captain Justice. Do us proud.
