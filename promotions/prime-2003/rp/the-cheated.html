Date: Mid-December ~December 14th~ He looked down at his hands, and the crusty blood that dried beneath his fingernails caused the repugnant taste of vomit to fill his mouth. Behind him, he heard the door creak, and from his seat on the front steps, he turned to see his brother stomping out onto the porch. “He dead?” “Nah. Woozy as hell though.” Scott closed the door behind him. “Sorry about your wall.” “Nothin’ that can’t be fixed.” “Let me know how much it costs ya. I’ll foot the bill.” “Damn right, you will.” Scotty crossed the porch, leaning forward on the banister. “What the hell was all about?” “Are you kiddin’ me?” “I mean, I know the guy’s an asshole, but we were this close to shaking to closin’ the deal, and then you go and lose it. You realize what the hell you just did? One simple shake of the hand and we’d have had a simple little plan that would have made this whole mess go away.” “It’s your mess, Scotty.” “Yeah, I get it. I fucked up. But all you had to do was shake the guys hand and say “deal”, and at least we’d all be able to go to sleep at night without having to worry about waking up with a limb missing.” “Guy’s a chump. He’s not gonna hurt anybody. I work with some nasty sonsofbitches… the kind of guys you really gotta keep an eye out for. This guy’s a nobody, just likes to puff his chest out.” Matt pushed himself off the porch steps. “And I’m not throwing the JitC, or any other match just because this guy thinks he can draw a beard on with a pencil, put on a JC Penny suit and threaten people.” “What the hell do you even need another Jewel in the Crown for?” “A guy needs to know everything he did wrong, he at least did so something could go right.” “Thanks Socrates.” “I gotta go. Need to get this blood off my hands.” Pulling up the hood on his sweathshirt, Matt crossed the front lawn, heading for his car. Ohio December’s were cold and grey, like a corpse, but on this particular day, the wind was worse than anything. It stung the flesh, especially on his hand, amplifying the throbbing . He hoped he hadn’t fractured it on the guy’s face, but the swelling seemed to indicate it was as broken as the wall by Scotty’s television. “Never woulda been a problem if you’d just said ‘yes’, and not flipped your lid.” “You and I are a lot alike, Scotty. Toss someone in the sewer, and chances are they come out lookin’ ugly. But when you’re the one that shoved them in the shit, whaddaya expect?” ~13~ ~The Day Before~ A man can find himself worrying about little things. Strange things. Is that a receding hairline? That’s the last roll of toilet paper. Is it enough to last till the next trip to the store? But such concerns are only given thought to help the mind avoid issues worth the worry. With a yawn that nearly dislodged his jaw like a snake, Matt pushed himself out of bed and threw on a pair of sweats. The clock said it was only six o’clock, but the smell of bacon was already drifting through the house, rolling along the walls. Stumbling out of the bedroom, he placed a hand on the railing, and cautiously guided himself down the stairs in a partially unconscious state. His nose led him to the kitchen, and as Matt rounded a corner, he was forced to shield his eyes from the sudden burst of light. “You’re up pretty early.” “Woke up and couldn’t fall back to sleep.” Mary Ward poked at some bacon in the frying pan with a fork, jumping out of the way as grease popped in the air. “Whatcha makin’ for breakfast?” “Bacon n’ eggs.” “Sounds good.” “Yeah.” “Think the smell woke me up.” “Sorry.” “It’s ok.” Matthew walked up behind his wife and kissed her on the top of the head. “Smells great.” He crossed the kitchen and opened a cabinet, pulling down a couple glasses. “Milk, please.” “You got it.” Heading for the fridge, Matthew grabbed a nearly empty gallon of milk. “Hey, you ok?” “Why?” “You been awfully focused on those bacon and eggs. I can’t get more than two words from ya.” Mary began to flip one of the strips of bacon, then stopped, placing the fork on top of the stove. When she turned to face her husband, her eyes were already watering. “Matthew, I need to tell you something.” “Ok.” “A few years ago, while you were out on one of your longer road trips…” She stopped, turned to the stove and flipped a switch, shutting off the burner. “…I cheated on you, Matthew.” Matt’s head dropped. ~December 14th~</CENTER There was no pause, no second thought to his explosion. Perhaps it was a career made where the simplest hesitation resulted in failure. Maybe it was something else. But nothing was there to save Leon Gibbs smug face from sixty seconds it would never forget. With one hooking right, Matt’s fist connected with the bridge of the nose, breaking it easily. By the time he withdrew his hand, blood was spilling from Leon’s nostrils, catching on his bottom lip, further staining his yellow teeth. “Shit!” Leon went to clutch at his face, but before he could feel the mangled mess of his nose on the tips of his fingers, he found a large palm swallowing his vision. With his fingernails digging into the flesh of Gibbs’ face, Matt shoved the bookie, driving his skull backward till it wouldn’t go any further. “Jesus Christ… my fucking wall!” Scott’s words were came out in choked, high pitch whine, as his dry wall caved and cracked beneath the back of Leon’s cranium. “Pieces of shit like you don’t scare me! You’re nothin’ but a busted-up, wannabe pro athlete, who couldn’t make it to the big leagues. And now yer nothin’ but a crooked little bookie who talks too fuckin’ much!” Matt could feel his fingers, clinching ever tighter, struggling to hold their grip as they slipped in the blood they smeared across Gibbs’ face. He bent his elbow as he palmed Leon’s skull, closing the distance between them, and Gibbs’ rotten breath beat against Matt’s hand in short, panicked bursts. “Let ‘im go, bud.” “Forget it, Scotty. That’s the problem with guys like this. Everybody just let’s ‘em go.” “So what are ya gonna do? Kill him?” “It’s an available option.” Matt continued to stare down Gibbs, catching the occasional glimpse of Leon’s fear through his fingers. “Y’know somethin’, superstar… I don’t think you’re gonna step foot near my house. I don’t think you got it in ya. And remember one thing, Leon, just cuz I’m on the road a lot… doesn’t mean I don’t know what’s goin on. Those road trips always end. I always come home. And if I come home and find out you were even in my neighborhood, we’ll just do this all over again, and I’ll go ahead and kill you next time.” Finally releasing his grip, Matt took a deep breath, enough of a pause to survey the damage he’d caused. Scott’s wall looked bad. Gibb’s face was worse. Leon slumped to the ground, his suit jacket bunching up behind his neck. “Jesus, man.” “I’m goin’ home, Scotty” Matt practically spit as he turned and headed for door. The second he was outside, he noticed he was shaking, and Matt made his way for the porch steps. Forcing long, slow breathes, Matt calmed his nerves, and found it surprisingly easy… as if he hadn’t gotten that worked up in the first place. He looked down at his hands, and the crusty blood that dried beneath his fingernails caused the repugnant taste of vomit to fill his mouth. Behind him, he heard the door creak, and from his seat on the front steps, he turned to see his brother stomping out onto the porch. “He dead?” “Nah. Woozy as hell though.” Scott closed the door behind him. ~13~ ~The Day Before~ “I’m sorry, sweetie. I can’t…” “It’s ok.” “What?” “It’s ok.” Matt twisted the cap back on the milk and returned it to the fridge. “I kinda knew something was up.” “That’s it? That’s all you’re gonna say? Aren’t you angry.” “Was it… ya know… a long-term thing?” “Just one time.” “Who was it?” “I… dunno. I didn’t want to know his name. Some guy I met at Dunaway’s. Just was so lonely, and made a stupid mistake. Not a day has gone by sense that I haven’t regretted it, and when you said you were going back to wrestling, it just gutted me, and…” “Then I’ll quit.” “What?” Mary took a step towards her husband, then stopped. “You shouldn’t quit because of me. It was my mistake, Matthew. Don’t try to make my life easier. Be upset. Be furious. Scream at me.” “Mary, I just can’t do it.” “Why not?” “Because you may have cheated, but I pushed you to do it. I cheated you, too. Right out of a good marriage. I’m always gone, and you deserve not to be alone.” “Not by going behind your back. I had no right to do that.” Now it was Matt who walked towards his wife, only he didn’t stop. When he reached her, he wrapped his arms around her waist, and kissed her on the head in the same easy manner he’d used to say good morning. “It was just that once.” “Yes.” “Ok.” He kissed her again. “I love you.” “I love you too, Matthew. The last of the grease finished popping as Mary squeezed her husband, pulling him close. “It’s probably a good thing you didn’t ever get the guys name.” “Why’s that?” “Cuz if I knew who it was, I’d hunt the guy down and kill ‘im.” ~December 14th~ “Hey, bro” Matt pulled down the hood of his sweatshirt as he stepped out of the howling Ohio wind. His brother stood to the side, allowing him entry to the house, and before Matt could even get both feet indoors, he saw Leon push himself off of Scotty’s couch and hold out his arms. “So there he is! Face to face, the legend himself.” Leon stepped forward, like he was expecting some warm embrace. He didn’t get one. “You’ve made quite a career for yourself.” “Likewise.” Matt didn’t take a seat, opting to just stand in the middle of the living room. Scotty closed the front door and slapped his hands together. “Either of ya want somethin’ eat? Drink?” “I just wanna straighten this out and get the hell home.” “Don’t let me stand in your way, bro. You guys know most of the story, I just think we need to talk about the way to square away this debt.” “So what’s the deal?” Matt folded his arms and chewed on his bottom lip. “I’ve bailed your brother out a couple times, Matt. I’m sure you know, he’s got a nasty habit of gambling with money that he doesn’t have. So, Scott owes me a fair chunk of change. I was willing to bail him out the first time, but when I’ve got to do it time and time again… then I can’t just be his old pal from high school, I’ve got think about my business. Guys gotta put food on the plate, right?” “Where do I come into this?” “I told Leon that I think we can help him get his money back with one simple trick.” Scott injected himself back into the conversation. “And that’s where we need you.” Matt looked at his brother in confusion, then as he turned his gaze to Gibbs, his brain began to solve the equation. “Let me guess…” “It’s pretty simple, Matt. I place a large sum of cash on your opponent in a match where you’re the favorite. Given your track record, I would assume I’ll have plenty of opportunities… like, say… next weekend at King of Kings. With my money riding on the underdog, you take the dive. I get my money back, Scotty’s debt is erased, and we all go our separate ways. All you gotta do is lose.” “Just one match?” “Just one. That’s all it’ll take.” The ticking of a miniature grandfather clock took over the discussion, dominating the uncomfortable silence. Matthew stared at Leon, then at his brother. Scott looked anxious… hopeful. “One time. That’s it?” “You got it. A simple shake of the hands, and we’ll have a deal. Take one dive for me, and all’s forgotten.” Leon extended his right hand and smiled, ready to make the agreement official.”You’re making the right choice, Matt. This is easy. Beats the hell outta sayin’ no. Then I gotta pay a visit to your home while you’re out traveling, sneak under the sheets with that wife of yo…” There was no pause, no second thought to his explosion. Perhaps it was a career made where the simplest hesitation resulted in failure. Maybe it was something else. But nothing was there to save Leon Gibbs smug face from sixty seconds it would never forget.
