Date: several different ones in 2008 Of all the awful, horrible sounds in the whole, wide world why did it have to be that particular one? Her tongue caught the salty tears that started to trail down her face, just as they had the first time she'd heard it. Fear grasped her heart so tight it imitated Mike Collins. Her breath could not escape her lungs, nor could the scream that tickled at her lips, trying desperately to escape. bang There it was again. That sound. Carrot quietly removed the comforter that shrouded her small frame and slid out of bed. She fell to all fours, the sound of her knees hitting the carpeted floor was loud enough to cause her to freeze... ...and wait for the briefestofmoments... No footsteps filled the hallway. She was safe for now, both hidden and comforted in the darkness of her bedroom. In a slithering fashion, she moved to take cover. After maneuvering herself underneath her bed, that noise and all of the emotions that it brought with it welled up inside of her heart and broke it in half. Twice in her life that sound had taken from her something dear. Twice in her life that sound had destroyed her. Her cries began to grow loud and she fought an incredible extent to keep them choked, throaty whispers. bang, bang, bang Carrot gasped for just a taste of air, panic gushing from her eyes and mouth along with the tears and bawling outbursts she simply couldn't control anymore. Nine was with her mother and father now, she knew. She'd be right behind him, she reasoned. With clinched teeth that only the hardest cries escaped through, she made up her mind as of what to do. In this samurai movie she watched with Nine and Carlos, a man wanted to leave the world peacefully and with his favorite things. Conveniently she'd stored all of her favorite belongings right underneath the bed, unbeknownst at the time it would become her hiding place. She seized Barry, a stuffed, blue rabbit Nine had won from a crane game in Wal-Mart as well as a crayon colored picture of the two standing beneath a purple sun. Carrot hugged them as though they were him. Though she didn't hear much about God and Satan and Heaven and Hell and the other things at school kids knew, she had heard what Nine had told her. "Nothing ever truly dies, I don't think," He'd told her, explaining why running over a squirrel just a moment ago in the car was nothing to cry over. "What do you mean?" "Well... when something passes away, their body turns into energy. And... it turns into something else. In this way, everything continues." She didn't understand what it meant then and didn't quite get the whole concept even in what she knew would be her final moments. But if it meant what she thought it did, she decided she'd come back as a dolphin. The pattering of feet filled the hallway and then her ears. She wasn't ready. Her breaths grew rapid and louder in an uncontrollable fit of anxiety with each passing moment the steps drew closer. Her door creaked open. She covered her mouth and held her breath. Mike Collins, the sickest bastard Porter had encountered yet in his first year on the force, was in handcuffs and ready to be placed in the car and sent to where his kind belonged. It took a while to catch him. Like all vermin, those of Collins' ilk required extermination to truly get rid of. However, that type of thinking is frowned upon when out in the field. These days, as the vets told him, you shoot a man, you'd better know he's guilty and he'd better have shot first. Well, Officer Charles Porter knew Mike Collins was guity but he didn't shoot first. Porter, however, knew the kind of justice that Mike would reap the benefits of in County. It brought a smile to the Rookie's face. "Won't be no children for you to defile where you're headed, buddy," Porter grinned. He led him to the vehicle. What an arrest this would be! Charles would make the papers again this week, for sure. Something else for his proud mother to hang up on the refrigerator and annoy all of her friends with. "I aint headed nowhere," Collins spat and struggled to no avail. He had managed to avoid the police for months, having finally been caught while taking a risk. Truth be told, he wanted his niece back. Carrot had become an obsession, a full-blown addiction and an outlet for Mike's perverted fantasies. "You in fuckin' denial, boy? 'cause you aint got much choice." So Mike had reached a man he'd heard of from a friend of a friend, who was said to be real dangerous. While on the phone with Edward Christmas, he reached a conclusion that this guy didn't fuck around. A real business sort of guy, who'd do anything if the price was right. "Fuck you, Pig." Mike's eyes lit up. An unlikely savior. "Fuck me? Yeah, that's ric--" Before Officer Porter could finish his sentence he had fallen to the asphault below him. A pale, gaunt figure with a head of red hair dropped the cement block he'd bashed the Officer in the head with. "Holy fucking shit! You just fucked hi-- ohmygod is he dead? Is he fuckin' dead!? Ha haaaa. Christmas? Edward Christmas?" "Yeah," Edward replied, scooping up the Officer's body beneath his shoulders and dragging him to the back of the Crown Victorian. "You didn't tell me your place was hot." "Well, it was a risk..." "Anymore 'risks', and our discussions are off. You don't want that, because I'm the only guy that's able to get you what you want." Mike challenged his business partner as he unlocked the trunk using Porter's keys, "Confident, aint you?" Edward Christmas smiled. If only this little bitch even knew what he was capable of. ********* "Carrot?" It took her a moment to recognize the voice. When she did, the river of tears streaming down her face dried up like the drought had done the rest of the state of Georgia. "Is it safe?" She asked quietly in a whimper. "Yeah... You're safe." She let go of Barry and her colored picture of them beneath the purple sun. In the same instant she scurried from beneath the bed. She met his warm, brown eyes with her own emerald orbs. It was definately Nine. A smile blazed a trail across her face as Carrot ran and jumped into his arms. "What's going on?" It was a story he could not quite tell the way he wanted to. Honesty was his ally, but in this case so was deceit. You simply don't tell your eight year old daughter (he dropped the term adopted about a month into their relationship) about the stacks and stacks of heroin lining the mysterious safe in the backroom which was otherwise empty. A few details were left omitted. Important details to include to anyone except Carrot, his prescious girl. "A man broke in and tried to rob us. I've got him tied up now though. I'm so sorry Carrot... I'm going to take you to Carlos'. I already talked to him and you'll only be there while the police sort things out." Carrot always had a hard time with Nine leaving her alone anywhere. Her psychiatrists believe this is due to deeply rooted abandonment issues. Whatever it was, she seemed okay with it this time. "Okay! I love you Nine... I really, really do," Carrot sighed and hugged him tighter. Nine grinned, scratching the beard that'd somehow grown out in full since shaving the day before. "Well, I really, really love you too. Pack your bookbag with of some of your clothes while I pack you some snacks." ********* How long had it been? Eight, maybe nine hours? Either way, Christmas was starving and thirsty and sweaty and anxious and tired all rolled up into one big, giant ball of shit. Tied, ducktaped and maybe even glued to a chair, he realized he was completely helpless. He'd failed. Like a stupid, fucking amateur he'd failed. He had the house mapped out in his mind. Edward even REMEMBERED the knife that Nine had picked up and eventually used to stab his stomach with. It was a shame that such a simple mistake may have cost Edward his life. Sure, it was compounded by the fact he missed Nine's forehead with three gun shots. "I'm such a fucking flop," Edward said, feeling sorry for himself. "Nah. That's not the first word I think of, anyway." Nine stepped through the open door to that dusky backroom. A flip of the lightswitch revealed himself completely to his prisoner. Christmas whistled sarcastically, "Look at the pretty lady stepping through the doorway." Nine smiled. If only this little bitch even knew what he was capable of. "I've got some questions," the Pythagorean offered. Edward answered quickly, "Sorry. I'm no rat. You can break off all my fingers and toes and I still won't say shit." Nine was quiet. Real quiet to the now loud mouthed Christmas, who expected a retort almost immediately. He walked in a slow pace behind Edward, opening a toolbox. Edward knew it was a toolbox. He knew where everything was. "Interesting choice of words," Nine chuckled. "'Break off,' I mean." He walked back into Christmas' view, a hammer held tight in his hand. Christmas was like a dog, lashing out in his fear. "I know you wouldn't do anything like that. You're too much of a pussy." WHAM Hammer, meet the back of Edward's left hand. "AAAAAAAH GOD DAMNYOUMOTHERFUCKER!" "Maybe," Nine laughed. "Juuuust maybe, I'm not as quick a study as I seemed to be yesterday morning. And maybe, just maybe, you'll make it out of here with your hands still workable. But that depends on a lot of variables. Such as your cooperation." It was a speech made through the gasps and moans and pleas that spewed forth from Christmas' mouth. However, Edward suddenly seemed in the mood to cooperate. "I like your behavior right now," Nine noted. "So, where is Mike Collins?" "...Fuck you." "Wrong answer." WHAM Hammer, reintroduce yourself to the back of Edward's left hand. The scream that rushed from Ed's open mouth was both ungodly and unnatural. A primal scream that belonged to a man in true pain. "You're being really loud right now," Nine replied. "Though, all of my neighbors work. So the only ears you're hurting are mine. "Now, where is Mike Collins?" Edward closed his eyes and braced himself, "...FUCK YOU!" WHAM Christmas screamed in such a fashion that it even made Nine question his actions... ...but only for a second. WHAM WHAM WHAM WHAM A word that described Edward Christmas' left hand after that moment was: obliterated. "I want you to know," Nine said loud enough to be audible over Edward's screams. "I gave you every opportunity to not have your hand destroyed. I'll be back here in a couple of hours. I'd have brought you some water, but you're an asshole." Ed's vision was blurred and hazy as he watched Nine leave. "You're going to die in here you fucking bastard," Nine offered before closing the door and locking it behind him. Overwhelmingly light-headed, Edward feinted just moments after deciding how he'd escape from both this chair and this room. ********* Omar Hernandez ruled over his congregation with an iron fist. However, the iron was shiny and painted pretty colors. He killed with kindness and dictated with a smile. Omar practically lived inside of his church, which was esentially a warehouse on the east side of Homerville. This was a decision that puzzled many of the church, but none would question. When Nine had come to him first so many years ago, he was a sixteen year old boy named Owen Farhat. Now a man, with a child who nearly everyone associated with the Brothers and Sisters of Pythagoras absolutely adored, Nine had come to Omar once more for help. Though a meeting through the phone was unusual and normally unacceptable for most of the congregation, Omar had always held a special place in his heart for Owen. Or Nine. Whatever he liked to call himself. "Thank you for listening, Blood Brother." "Please," Omar hissed through his line of the phone. "You are one of the few who may call me by my name. So do so." Nine recalled how he preferred discussing things with Omar over the phone. Such a serious guy. Always trying to make a point. Nine continued, "Omar, then. There is a man, he tried to rob us." "Us as in you and Carrot?" Nine hesitated for a brief moment, "As in Carrot, myself, and the Brothers and Sisters." Omar showed a glimpse of vulnerability, gulping. "Explain." "He said he came for the safe and he came for my daughter. Carrot, I'm positive he was being paid for... the safe he was just being greedy, I think. He tried to kill me and he's... uhm, well, he's tied up now." There was anger in Omar's voice, and the echo of the warehouse could be heard through the phone when he lifted his voice. "This piece of trash tried to steal all of our bricks, then? That's what you're saying, Brother?" "Yes," Nine began. "But the thing is, he mascaraded as a police officer. Even stole a police car that's still parked outside here and I'm sure they'll find it any moment." "Owen," Omar began. "You are my second in command and arguably the strongest mathematical mind in the entire church. You are an asset I cannot afford to lose, and a brother I love dearly. This mess will get cleaned up." "So you'll send someone then?" Omar chuckled. It was a sinister sounding satisfaction, "I'll send several and come along." "Good!" Nine offered, trying to make a glass half full out of a glass almost empty. "You can finally see the house and all of that. Though, I think I'm going to move." Omar coughed, "Sure. Sure. See the house. We'll be there in less than twenty minutes." Nine's smile could be heard even at the other end of the receiver, "Thank you Brother." "Oh," Omar recalled. "And Owen?" "Err, it's Nine." Omar didn't bother correcting himself. He'd make a point to later, "I'm going to kill him myself." That was Omar, though. Always trying to make a point.
