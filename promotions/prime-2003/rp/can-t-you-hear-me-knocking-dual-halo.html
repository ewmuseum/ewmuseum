Can't You Hear Me Knocking? (Dual Halo)

Date: From then to now February 22, 2007. Melbourne, Australia. Following ReVolution 120, not televised. Angelo Deville should have been a chef with the way the Australians were eating out of the palm of his hand. The Devil's Don coolly glided across the ring, letting his fingertips graze along the top rope as he fed the crowd their helping of dessert for the night. After feasting on a buffet of appetizers for most of the night, the main course was a successful Universal Title defense by Nova, which the crowd did not particularly care for. But Angelo didn't want their night to be filled with too much disappointment. Hence why he ventured down to the ring to give those left something special to take home with them. "That picture, you see, was from a time forgotten. At the time of receipt, I was at a loss for remembrance. Then, it hit me. "What Lindsay Troy wants me to remember isn't the two of us meeting when we were young children. She wants me to remember something far more important. "And, well, if her lips weren't the sweetest thing I had ever tasted..." >=) ~**~**~**~**~**~ Spring, 1986. Tampa. "But Daddyyyyyyyyyyyyyyyyy, *sniffle* I wanna go tooooooooooooooo..." The pink bedsheets found themselves being pounded by tiny fists, an early sign that a temper tantrum was on the horizon. Sitting upright in her four-poster bed, Alaina Troy spoke in a high pitched whine, unruly blonde hair askew from rolling around restlessly in her sleep. Her heart-shaped face was cast down toward the comforter and tears streamed down her cheeks, a combination of sadness and pain from a nasty ear infection that had tormented her for the better part of her school vacation. After another over-exaggerated sniffle, she looked up at her father with quivering lip and fresh tears welling up in her eyes. Marco Strozzi slowly eased his six-six frame onto the edge of the bed, careful not to sit on his daughter's slender legs. Built like a linebacker, with deep brown hair and crystal blue eyes, the patriarch of the Strozzi/Troy family looked down at his youngest with a softened expression. "Sweetheart," he prefaced, wiping Alaina's tears from her face with his thumb. "The doctor doesn't want you traveling when you're sick. Going up in a plane may make your ears hurt even more than they already do." "But I've been wanting to go foreverrrrrrr." "I know, sweetie, I know. But you don't want to make things worse, do you?" Another sniffle, followed by Marco handing her a tissue from the small nightstand. Alaina studied the Kleenex in her hand, thinking it over slowly and trying to stall before answering her father. "Well?" She lifted the Kleenex to her nose and wiped. "No," came the reply, indignant and stubborn. Marco pushed some strands of hair behind her ears, smiling in an effort to try and ease her disappointment. As the baby of the family, Alaina was always coddled just a little bit more, especially since her mother, Cecelia, died while giving birth to her. But Marco hated seeing either of his daughters upset. Being the head of a major communications firm meant frequent business trips, long hours and extended time way from the loves of his life. When he was home, however, his daughters were his only priority and he spent as much time being a father as he possibly could. At the behest of an old friend, Marco had planned on taking Alaina and Lindsay on their first international trip. Sure, it was only to Canada, but it was an exciting experience for the girls nonetheless. Now that Alaina was sick and couldn't go, the resentment of life not being fair was starting to become apparent to the six-year-old. "I'll tell you what, Lanni," he said, lifting her chin up. "Aunt Elise and Uncle Brent have said that they'd take care of you while we're gone. If you're good, I'll bring you back a present. Something really special." "Like a pony?" Suddenly, the tears had evaporated and Alaina's eyes lit up at the thought. A pony would really make her feel all better and forget about her sister getting to go on the trip and not her. "Well, maybe not a pony, peanut. But something nice. OK?" Alaina nodded, then shrugged. "I still want to go." "There'll be other times. Maybe we can invite them down here this summer when you kids are out of school." "Yeah, but they might melt from the heat!" the sing-songy voice of nine-year-old Lindsay Troy remarked as she flounced onto her sister's bed, brown curls springing off her shoulders and falling over the top of her head to hide her face. Alaina giggled as Lindsay rose to her knees and struggled to get her hair out of her eyes, but quickly stopped when her older sister shot her a look. "I'm going to go put the things in the car while you two say goodbye." Marco leaned over to give Alaina a hug, and she jumped into his arms, clinging tight to his neck. "I love you, Lanni. We'll be back before you know it." "I love you too, Daddy." ~**~**~**~**~**~ In the backseat of the Towncar, Lindsay looked out the tinted window at the landscape rolling by, wriggling with excitement every now and again. She didn't know what to expect from this Canada place; her friends had said they heard stories of gigantic moose attacking towns and carrying kids away in their antlers, but so far she hadn't seen any moose or any kids outside of the ones in the airport. In fact, she hadn't really seen much of anything at all outside of cars and buildings in the city and now freshly-budding trees and grass on the outskirts. Lindsay gave her father a sidelong glance out of the corner of her eye and saw that she wasn't the only one having a hard time keeping still. If he wasn't smoothing his pants, Marco was tugging at the bottom of his Armani suit jacket or aimlessly rubbing his hands together. These curious habits made Lindsay forget about what was going on outside and, instead, made her stare in wonder at her normally composed and collected father fidgeting about like she was moments before. Marco must have picked up on the subtle shift of atmosphere, because he looked down to find his daughter looking right at him. "You're squirming, Dad." A hearty laugh as a result of Lindsay's bluntness escaped his mouth. He unfastened his seatbelt and moved closer to Lindsay, putting his arm around her. Still belted in, she cozied up in the crook of his arm and smiled. "Is something wrong?" "No, nothing's wrong Lin." "Then why are you so antsy?" He smiled and smoothed her hair with his hand. "I haven't seen Ferruccio in awhile, is all. We were quite close once, but things changed over time." "What changed?" "Well, sometimes when people get older, they move off in different directions. Sometimes they mean to and other times they don't. We didn't mean to not remain close, but some things happened that made it necessary." "Like what?" Marco sighed, wondering what gene of his or Cecelia's made this little girl so inquisitive. He knew that the intricate details of what happened would go right over Lindsay's head, because she was too young to really understand. So, he tried to put it in terms that would make sense to her. "Your grandfather and Mister Deville are in the same line of business and don't really like each other too much." "Grandpa Nico knows Mister Deville?" A nod confirmed this revelation. "He does. And he told me that he didn't want me and Mister Deville to be friends. Do you know how it would feel if I told you that you couldn't be friends with Allison or Melissa anymore?" "I'd be angry. And I wouldn't come out of my room or do any of my chores ever again." "Well, I was angry too. But I didn't want to directly disobey your grandfather because I knew that would hurt him. So Mister Deville and I stopped talking as much. But because your Grandpa Nico told me we couldn't be friends, I told Grandpa that I wouldn't be seeing him and Grandma as much as I used to." "Is that why we haven't gone to Italy to see him and Grandma Alessa?" "That's right, sweetie." Lindsay pondered this development for a moment, scrunching her eyebrows together as she looked at her father's knees. She always thought that she and Alaina were too young to travel all the way across an ocean to visit relatives in the country where her father grew up, or that her grandparents were too old and too set in their ways to want to fly to Florida to see them, but to find out that the real reason was because her father and her grandfather had a fight wasn't something she had ever considered. "Well, I think that people should be friends with whoever they want to be friends with as long as everyone's nice and gets along OK." "That's what I think too, Linny," he replied and kissed the top of her head. "We're about to reach the gate, Mister Strozzi," the driver replied, looking into the rear-view mirror at the pair in the back then reaching for the car phone in the front seat. "I'll let Mister Deville know we're about five minutes away." "Thank you Lucas." The driver turned his attention back to the road and Marco turned his attention back to his daughter. "I think you'll like Mister and Misses Deville, Lindsay. And they've got a son who's a year younger than you. From what Ferruccio told me, he's about as mischievous as you are." ~**~**~**~**~**~ "Marco!" After being admitted through the gate and into the front door, Marco and Lindsay were instructed by a pencil-thin butler to wait in the hall and to, please, admire the artwork. The jovial cry that echoed off the walls of the foyer preceded the man who uttered it, Ferruccio Deville, who was walking toward them with arms outstretched and wearing a tailor-made Valentino suit. Strozzi grinned and started to close the distance between, hand-in-hand with his daughter. Lindsay had to half-run in order to keep pace with her father's long strides and was silently thankful when the men stopped and embraced in a hug. Marco kept hold of her hand, while Ferrucico embraced him with both arms. They started to talk quickly in Italian, parts of which Lindsay was able to understand and other parts were spoken so fast her brain couldn't process them quickly enough. Finally, they came up for air and resumed conversation in English. "Ferruccio, I'd like you to meet my eldest daughter, Lindsay." "My dear, if you aren't the exact likeness of your father..." Deville held out his hand, which Lindsay took with a small, shy smile. "That's what everyone says." "And they'd be correct. But I thought you had two daughters, Marco. Did you lose one on the way through customs?" "Hah, you joker!" Strozzi clapped him on the shoulder. "Alaina is sick and couldn't make the trip. She's with my sister-in-law and her husband for the next couple of days." "Such a shame. I was looking forward to meeting the entire family. Perhaps some other time, then." His warm beam was infectious and Lindsay felt herself becoming a bit more at-ease in his company. She looked up at Marco, and he squeezed her hand. "And what about you, Ferruccio? Where is your family?" "Ah yes. My wife is out doing errands and should return in a few hours time. As for my son..." he looked behind him to find a young boy walking toward the trio, arms swinging freely by his side. His hair was the color of sun-kissed wheat, and he had a grin on his face a mile wide. "Ah, here he is now." Deville's offspring stopped next to his father, who put his hand on his shoulder. "This is Angelo. Angelo, this is Mister Strozzi and his daughter Lindsay." Angelo offered his hand first to Marco, who shook it firmly, and then to Lindsay, who attempted to shake it. Instead, Angelo stepped forward, lifted her hand to his mouth and kissed it lightly. The fathers laughed as Lindsay turned bright red and yanked her hand away, not knowing what to do or how to respond. "Florida boys aren't taught manners, Marco? Your daughter looks about as red as a tomato." Ferruccio laughed and touched the top of his son's head. "Dad! My hair!" Angelo smoothed his golden locks down and smirked up at his father. "Most Florida boys are too busy trying to keep up with her at recess than to show any cordiality, Ferruccio. I think they're a bit intimidated myself. Right Linny?" Lindsay responded by burying her face in her father's pant leg to hide her flaming face. "Not my son," Deville said proudly. "I've got a camera set up in the other room. I want to capture this moment before time gets away from us again. Then, we'll let them get better acquainted while we catch up." ~**~**~**~**~**~ "This is the playroom..." Angelo informed with a wave of his hand as Lindsay walked through the threshold of the open door. Looking around, she noticed a lot of toys and cars and other things that boys at the time were crazy about. Before arriving at this juncture, the chip-off-the-old-Deville thought it polite to give his guest a tour of the mansion, hitting all the high points and showing Lindsay the expansive backyard, complete with in-ground pool. After the hand-kissing incident, Lindsay didn't say much of anything at all, but Angelo tried to make her feel a little more comfortable by engaging in small talk. Under the careful eye of the various staff members that Ferruccio kept employed, the two children started to get to know each other a little bit better once Lindsay became more at-ease. Angelo seemed refined for an eight-year-old, although there was something about him that made her think his behavior was just part of the show. Nevertheless, she thought it best to give him a little slack. "This is neat," she replied, taking in her surroundings without appearing to gawk. "Your dad calls you Linny?" She smiled and turned around to look at him. "Yeah, he calls us by a bunch of different names. Does your dad have a nickname for you?" "No." Angelo picked up a remote-controlled Corvette that was in the middle of the entrance and placed it against the wall, out of the way. "My mom sometimes calls me Angie, but that's a girl's name. I don't really like it." "I could come up with a better nickname for you, if you want." Lindsay put her hand to her chin and started to think. Angie did sound like a nickname for a girl and if she was named Angelo, she didn't think she'd like it either. "That's OK. You don't..." "What about Devvy?" There was a pause as Angelo mulled it over, staring at Lindsay as he did so. She pulled at an errant thread that was hanging by her black and white striped shirt, not doing well at hiding the discomfort that him looking at her brought. "If you don't like it," she said, breaking the silence, "you don't have to use it." "It's better than Angie," Angelo concluded, then walked toward her, taking her by the arm and smiling that infectious smile that he learned from his father. "My dad got me this really neat doctor's kit. A real one from a real doctor! Do you want to see it?" "Okay!" He jogged across the room, pulling her along with him, to a small table and chairs that was set up in the corner. Lindsay sat down while Angelo pulled the kit off a nearby shelf. She looked over to the door and saw a maid watching them from the hall, checking to make sure they weren't getting into any trouble. Lindsay waved at the woman, who smiled and disappeared from view. "Got it!" Angelo proclaimed and set the leather holder on the table, pulling the tie that held it together apart. Lindsay looked and saw a package of wooden mouth sticks, a rubber hammer, a stethoscope and a small flashlight. "Don't you have a white coat or something?" He frowned. "I spilled juice on it the other day. Mom's trying to get the stain out." "Oh." "But I don't really need it anyway. I'll examine you first and then you can examine me." "Do I have to call you Doctor." "Yes. Doctor Devvy, at your service!" He bowed deeply and she laughed as his hair fell forward. Angelo straightened up, put his hair back into place and then took a stick from the package. "Okay. So Lindsay, what's wrong with you today?" "Well Doctor," she said, straightening up and put a look of mock-anguish on her face. "My sister has an ear infection and I think I may be getting it too." "So your ear hurts?" "Yes." She nodded, and pouted. "And my throat." "You may be getting a cold too. But I'll check and see what I can do. Say 'ahhhh.'" "AHHHHHH!" Angelo put the stick on her tongue and used the flashlight to look inside her mouth. He feigned a quizzical look as he shined the light around. He then removed the tongue depressor and threw it in a trash can. "Hmm...okay. Your throat is fine but I'll check your ear now. Which one?" "My right one." Angelo shined the light into her ear and 'hmmm'ed again. Lindsay kicked her legs, letting her shoes graze the carpet. "Is it really bad doctor?" "Oh yes, very serious." "What should I do?" "Well," Deville straightened up and stood in font of her. He leaned forward to explain the graveness of the situation. "Your ear is really red and gross." "Ewwwww!" "But luckily, I have medicine that will cure you almost instantly!" "Really? I'll be all better?" "Mmhmm." "Where is it?" Lindsay looked over to the table, then back to Angelo. "Right here," he replied, lightly pressing his lips against hers. ~**~**~**~**~**~ November 22, 2006. Tampa. Early evening. "What the hell am I doing?" Elbow and arm making a ninety-degree angle, forehead resting in the palm of her hand, the thirty-year-old Lindsay Troy sat behind an ancient mahogany desk in her father's old office. Since his death, the room was now used only as a private sanctuary for Troy's personal reflection and thought. The rules of the house, even after all these years, stated that when the office was in use, the occupant was not to be disturbed. With Melton left behind in Houston to enjoy Thanksgiving without her, Lindsay knew that the opportunity for distraction was lowered. Her travel bag was packed and sitting by the door, ready to be taken on yet another cross-country journey. However, this trip was one that Troy hadn't planned on, especially not at the expense of the person who requested her help. Killean seemed to mean well, and sounded truly genuine when he appealed to her over the phone six hours earlier, even though she had been less than cordial to him since arriving in PRIME. Lindsay never liked to pre-judge people, but given the nature of the wrestling business and where Killean was from, it was entirely possible that the Supreme Machine was aware of her past transgressions against certain Winnipeg-area wrestlers and she thought it better at the time to keep herself guarded and standoffish. Now, the landscape of PRIME was starting to change. As Fuck You!'s numbers grew, the opposition remained small. Troy knew that, sooner or later, she was going to get involved somehow. Even if she didn't necessarily want to and had absolutely no reason to. "Always getting involved with a stupid crusade, Lindz. Damnit, "she grumbled out loud to absolutely no one. "Why the hell can't I just stay away and not care?" You'd think I'd have learned my lesson by now. She kept the thought to herself, knowing that letting it escape out of her mouth would only reaffirm that she was just as stubborn now as she ever was. Instead, she lifted her head from her hand, made a fist and forcefully brought it down on top of the desk. The sound of the BANG! bounced off the walls and, from the inside of the desk, a crack was heard. Lindsay looked down and found that the small drawer underneath the lip of the desk had jarred itself open from the force of her pounding. For the first time since Marco had been alive, the drawer was unlocked. She had never thought about having it picked open; there was nothing in any of the other drawers and moving the desk around never gave any indication that there was anything inside. Everything of great importance was more easily accessible and no keys to the desk had ever been found, which lead the remaining members of the Troy/DiPetrillo family to believe that the only set had been with Marco and his body was never recovered from the site of the plane wreck that claimed his life. Her hands slid cautiously over the top of the desk, the smooth wood offering her nerves no comfort, until they reached the underside of the drawer. Slowly, she slid the compartment open and found what looked like a wrapped box taped securely to the velvet lining. Lindsay quickly tore the box away, only it wasn't a box at all. "A tape?" Troy's eyebrow quirked skyward as she unwrapped the note that surrounded the discovery. The handwriting was wholly unmemorable, but the message written on the stationary was not. Frank-- Nicolo was sent this anonymously, but nothing can be done now. There's no sense rekindling the strife and the grief it caused, he says. Put this in a safe place for the girls, in case they ever seek the truth when they get older. Marco trusted you, and loved you like a brother. He would have wanted this. -Stefano Swiveling her body around to look out the window, Lindsay let her gaze fall on the front security station where Frank Macera, longtime head of the home security team, kept watch over the property. "Frank knew about this and never told me? How did he get in the desk?" The question hung idly in the air as she glanced back down at the giant mystery hidden inside a tiny tape. "And what's on this tape...?" ~**~**~**~**~**~ Present. Inside my haunted head. It's taken me some time to get to this point, Devvy. After that day, and for a time growing up, I had forgotten all about you. Traumatic experiences, like the death of my father, have a way of erasing some of the past. Even now, there are things about my father that I struggle to remember, but have a hard time doing so. I've clung to as much as I can, ferreting away the memories as soon as I can recollect them, so they don't escape me again. When the memory of you resurfaced, I began to wonder what happened to the boy with the golden hair that I met all those years ago. I remember telling Alaina about you when I returned back to Tampa, and she was even more disappointed that she didn't make the trip. I think the look on my face when you first kissed my hand and then gave me my first real kiss would have been worth risking punctured ear drums due to cabin pressure on the airplane. I remembered you and then forgot about you all over again. I lived my life as a hollow shell for a time, and when you're ten years old that's no way to live at all. My uncle gave me a purpose, and the training I received from him saved me from spiraling even further away from the people who were still here and who loved me. I learned discipline. I regained focus. I started living again. I started a career from the fighting skills I learned from him and from others and, pretty soon, the business became my life. I never forgot my family, and they still are and always will be the single most important thing to me. But I was haunted by something I couldn't quite understand and couldn't quite put my finger on. Over time, I became haunted by other things...things I can never forget but try to bury anyway. The death of my best friend and fiance six years ago. Japan, five years ago, where the second shell I regressed into nearly encompassed me completely, until I was saved by the man I served divorce papers to on a company's biggest pay-per-view of the year over a month ago. And now, this tape... You should know this tape will be the end of you, and not even a structure such as the Halo will be able to contain what I plan to levy. A win means the world at my fingertips, but I just want them wrapped around your neck, silencing your ego completely when oxygen can no longer reach your brain. I have taken control. And I will take everything away. I'm about to open a door that's been bolted shut for twenty-two years. Can't you hear me knocking?
