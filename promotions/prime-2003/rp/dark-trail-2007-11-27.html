Dark Trail

27 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/27/07 “Who said family ain't family no more, nigga?” -Ghostface Killah November 8th, 2007 CE 10:12 P.M. Mamas, don’t let your sons grow up to be mobsters. They’ll end up like I was, sitting in Jimmy’s Harborside, still in Boston and lamentably so. Here I was, the rich and famous Chandler Tsonda reduced to playing the role of pawn. For these crooked Mafioso fuckers, no less. Jimmy’s is preposterously great. Everyone’s got on their nicest duds, taking a brinksmanship approach to looking good. Upscale and posh, it attracts criminals of all sorts: Polish union leaders, Irish gangsters, and Congressmen. The drinks are strong and the women are lethally beautiful. It shouldn’t surprise you that I was here for these reluctant mob dealings; it’s a philistine’s paradise. The setup was a date. They told me all I had to do was chew the fat for a couple hours with some bimbo and wait for someone to approach me. It would be “an enemy,” someone looking to make waves by hurting the Lucchese operation in Boston proper. The story I was told had this enemy proposing a sum of money to me in order for the secrets of Calvino’s crew. You see, the past two days, the entire “family” had been out rumor mongering, propagating a myth. The myth? That Ettore Calvino’s health was failing and that I was the next in line. The story was airtight. I was ideally placed, with my wealth and celebrity, to become the next John Gotti. The wops would have their media darling czar to lead the troops again. And those were the wheels that I thought were in motion. I should have expected the diabolical intentions, but for once in my life, I had forgotten to see my father for what he was. M’lady was blabbering on about her uncle, “Worcester’s Worst” Junior Izzo. Her eyes were green and shallow, fluttering around her place setting nervously as her mouth kept running. In another time and place, maybe I would have taken a shot…but I was well past the recommended dose of mob ties. Being polite was all I could muster, anxiety-ridden as I was about the whole ordeal. But she was beautiful, stealing glances from happily married businessman in that wailing green dress. “You ever off anybody?” “What?” It was a verbal flinch, a move to stall while I thought of some appropriately insightful and cynical response. “Killed anyone.” “Yes,” I responded with a killer’s coldness. Wearing this liar’s crown, though, I couldn’t meet her eyes for the moment. I wondered, quietly, if these people had some inbred intuition that showed them whether a man could kill or not. “If I told you, I’d have to kill you too,” I said, turning the charm up to lukewarm. She smiled, all lips−no soul. Calvino had instructed me to remember everything and observe every detail. There would be some incongruity, something out of place. “You’ll feel it, your instincts will tell you, “ he had assured. But I felt nothing wrong or right. I felt like a man watching a floodplain fill up, serenely confident in the last moment before the surge. “Will we be having any desserts tonight?” The waiter’s voice disrupted an increasingly convoluted inner monologue. “Coffee perhaps?” My date didn’t look to me for any sort of approval; she was drinking on Calvino’s bill, like it or not. As she ordered a wildly expensive and as wildly mispronounced dish, I looked for something that wasn’t there. “My boss wants to talk to you.” Only when he spoke directly to me did I notice how young the waiter was. “Come again?” “We know who you are.” “So does everyone with a TV. Who is your boss and why would I waste my time with him?” I wanted desperately not to know who this boss was; the survival instinct tries to reject morbid curiosity such as this. “Don’t be ridiculous. He’s not doing this in public.” “So I should collude with on your turf?” On some unknown cue (probably the increasing volume in my voice), a figure appeared from over my shoulder. I recognized him as one of Calvino’s foot soldiers, the same one who had opened the door when I first arrived at his house. “Is there a problem, Mr. Tsonda?” The waiter looked around sheepishly, trying to regain his confidence in the face of this armbreaker. “I dunno, ask this goomba.” The two met eyes and then the big goodfella turned back towards me. “Mr. Tsonda,” he whispered, producing a small handgun underneath his coat, visible only to those at the table, “I think you should go outside.” I froze, looking across the table. My date had a sudden streak of indifference, staring at the ground. Whether she was in on this con didn’t matter; if she wasn’t, she wanted no part of helping me extricate myself. With no choice but the follow orders, I walked outside, trying not to let the fear manifest itself physically. The workers in the kitchen didn’t even bother looking away, but certainly didn’t seem surprised. You can compromise with your self to look the other way so easily. I stepped outside and saw a well-dressed man. Probably not the big boss, I thought, but some sort of capo. I waited for Calvino’s traitorous crony to give me space, but it didn’t happen. He stayed within arm’s length, that gun probably still pointed at the small of my back. “This is quite the strong-arming for a negotiation.” The man in the suit laughed, his breath spilling out rapidly in feathers of milky smoke. Things stopped. Swallowed by the dark. / / / / Later That Night Exact Time Unknown I didn’t have to wonder for very long why my head felt like a detonated minefield. My fingers found the goose egg on the back of my skull. They could not, however, tell me where I was or illuminate whatever dark bowels I was in. Remembering the last seconds that I could, I knew that calling out for help was useless. At this point, fear took up a serious residence in my gut. This wasn’t some wrestling gimmick; I was in the den of thieves and worse. A door opened in front of me and the light slipped in somberly around a silhouette. After my eyes overcame the shock of light, I recognized the man in the suit. He gestured inside within the doorframe and two more figures followed. The overhead light came on, forcing me to close my lids tightly. “Is this the part where you tell me you’ve been expecting me and cackle wildly?” The two flunkies ignored me, while the well-dressed mobster kept his eye and gun on me. The room was your basic bathroom with grimy white tile that hadn’t been cleaned in months. A shower curtain hung half-attached to the halo rods around the bathtub. The absence of windows explained why the room had been so dark. Noting that the setting did not bode well for me, I tried to engage my captors. “If you wanted the information, why didn’t you just pay for it like civilized human beings?” Sarcasm was the only tactic that came to mind, and the only thing that would mask my swelling fear. But no answer came, just the continued chuckles of the devil in the suit. “Do you think we took you because of information?” His wild-eyed smile was perverse as he took in my reaction. “Ahh…so you don’t have a clue what you’ve gotten into, eh prince?” Do not show fear. Do not seem uncertain. You cannot become disposable to these people. These were the guidelines I had to live by now, grasping at straws for just why I was here and what in the unicycle-riding fuck was going on. “I know who you people are. You’re some scum who want to get at Calvino’s crew because you’re insignificant. Gotta do something ambitious to get on the map.” The traitor, who I now clearly recognized as one of the two other men in the room, took a swing and knocked my kneeling form to the ground. The man in the suit waved him off. “Don’t waste your time on this chink.” He turned back towards me and of all the things running through my mind, I was puzzled by what I desired most: I wanted to know this man’s name. Just like the feckless hero who makes a last stand by engaging the super villain in conversation. Blood was slipping out of the wound where my forehead had just bounced off the tile floor; pain was no object in the face of my forthcoming trial. “What’s your name?” I heard the bathtub filling up. “Not important, kid. This ain’t no movie.” And with the speed the first assault had taken place, I took another blow to the back of the head and was immediately plunged headfirst into the bathtub. I struggled, but the two hired hands were not rookies. They had the angle and leverage against me and every thrash of my body only submerged my neck and then the top of my shoulders. I knew I couldn’t open my mouth to scream, but I couldn’t let myself die without a yell, a shout, a stand. Water rushed into my lungs more quickly than I thought, burning savagely. Limp appendages now seemed to fall away, drifting away from the rest of my body. Everything was gossamer, light enough to float off. There was death, no longer bad or good. I still seemed to be somewhere real, though. I could hear a giant clamor through water-clogged ears. Were these the throes of death? From the fog, I emerged, coughing maniacally. Ettore Calvino was kneeling down almost directly on top of me, his hands still on my chest. Still coughing, I turned my head to both sides to see that I was lying outside the bathtub. “Least I could do,” he mumbled quietly. He wasn’t smiling as I pulled myself up into a sitting position. There were many more people in the room, now. The devil and his two thugs were kneeling, guns pointed at the back of their heads. Calvino’s cavalry had made it. “What happened?” “These people,” Calvino sighed, “are not enemies of mine, but employees.” He snarled as he looked over at the three of them. “Chandler, you weren’t bait for a rival gang, but for traitors in my own crew. They thought if they killed you, then...I’m…” No apology, just a final truth. So we could both sleep better. “Wha…you goddamn coward.” I could see the shame on his face and he turned back towards his own turncoats, unable to look me in the eyes. It all made sense: long ago, he had chosen a side in life. He couldn’t now pretend to value my life more than the sanctity of a sanitary operation. “Johnny, you make my heart sick.” He was talking to the suit, whose smugness had disappeared. As Calvino turned away, two of his men grabbed Johnny by the arms and led him towards the tub for a final baptism. Johnny said nothing, but started to sob quietly when one of the men ripped the crucifix from off his neck. I tried to get to my feet, but Calvino stared daggers through me. “Watch.” He seemed proud to be killing the assailants. “These men tried to kill me.” “None of them was supposed to be my father.” One of Calvino’s nameless wannabe mobsters moved towards me. “Hey,” he yelled, “who the fuck are you? Your pops is a fucking king! You should be so lucky, you closet chink queer.” I didn’t look at him, though. I just stared at Calvino, who stayed mum, those sad lifeless eyes back on his face. It was time to go. Probably forever. “I don’t need to watch this. I’m not a fucking canary, but don’t mistake that for giving a shit about any of this.” I waved my hand around the room, encompassing all the crime, all these greasy wops, and everything that my father had chosen over Vietnam and my mother. Calvino followed me as I made my way towards the door. He didn’t stop me physically, but I knew there was some lingering words that he had left, some final admission that I would reject. “Chandler…” Two loud cracks ended the lives of the two traitors in the tenuous silence between us. “You never meant for this to happen. There’s some grand reason why all this isn’t your fault, why you wish it had never happened. Yeah, save it.” For the first time in my life, I looked at him. Really looked at him, at his eyes and everything else. It was cathartic and awkward and neither of us said anything for a couple slowly moving seconds. “I am an old man. And I don’t…have a lot left in this world.” His voice moved slowly through thick sighs. “It’s too late for me to get out from this life. And that’s the God’s…honest truth. I want to tell you that…so many things.” The tear peeking out from the corner of his eye could not stay my hand. “So many things,” I repeated slowly with a voice of rock. “So many fucking things.” My voice was harsher than I expected it. I slipped past him and felt his palm come down on my shoulder, a last gesture of so many things. I didn’t turn around, but let his hand rest there for a half-second. Maybe he could die with that concrete memory or maybe it would wash down with those other memories in bathtubs and dim corners of the world. I stepped outside into the same night I had abandoned hours ago. The Boston sky was busy with satellites and stars. Across seas and space, the sun was setting in Vietnam. Ghosts of an old soldier and a young doe were stepping out for their first promenade under the moonlight.
