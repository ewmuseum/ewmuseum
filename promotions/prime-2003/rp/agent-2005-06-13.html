Agent

Date: 6/13/05 When you want to be a star, it often comes down to this… it’s not WHAT you know, it’s WHO you know. That’s why I pay a nice chunk of what I earn to Maury Morris, talent agent extraordinaire. If you ask me, it’s the ultimate job. Getting paid millions simply to know beautiful people and introduce them to other beautiful people. That’s pretty much what an agent does. And it’s all mover and shakers, baby. Yeah, the actors get their names on the marquees, but the agents don’t have to kiss up to the unwashed masses like they do. That would be a perfect world for me, wrapped around in all things fabulous like a baby in its mother’s womb. And not having to walk within arm’s reach assholes in John Deere hats like I have to in PRIME. I swear if it wasn’t for the fact that being a star wasn’t so DAMN dependent on the public, I’d KILL every one of those fuckers. As I enter the waiting room of Maury’s office, the old bitch he calls his receptionist snaps at me to stop. I have NO idea where he found this hag. Maybe some Eastern European country exports them. All I know is that she looks like she should be hitting kindergartners with a ruler for coloring outside the lines, not speaking to incarnations of star power like myself. “And joost vhere do you tink you are goink, Meester Easton?” she asks me in an accent that would be called “foreign” in every damn part of the world. “Uh… I got an appointment. With Maury, ma’am.” I don’t call her “ma’am” out of respect, it’s just that I can’t pronounce her name without hacking up a wad of phlegm. NOT sexy. “Your appointment vas for ten in der mornink, Meester Easton.” “Uh… yeah?” “Eet ees now past von een der afternoon, Meester Easton.” “Oh. Yeah. Traffic. You know.” “You do know you must vait until you are already a star before you are allowed to keep everyone vaitink on you, Meester Easton. And you… are merely semi-famous at best.” It takes EVERY… OUNCE… of willpower I have not to take a couple of the pens the old bitch has stuck in her brittle gray hair and stab her in those judgmental eyes of hers. “Uh… yeah. So is, like Maury… in, you know?” The hag sighs deeply, like I had just asked her to donate one of her saggy, wrinkled tits to science. “Yes, Meester Easton. Go on een.” Maury is behind his big oak desk, gabbing on the phone. Each word makes his jowls quiver. “Listen, you schmuck! I’ve got my biggest client here, so I can’t waste no time! Do we got a deal or not? Oy vey! You’re busting my balls! Fine… fine… but I’m giving you the shirt off my back!” Maury slams down the phone and looks up at me, his eyes glimmering even behind those Coke bottle glasses. “And there’s my boychick now!” Maury stands up and holds his arms out for a hug. I oblige him, feeling his cellulite and polyester press against me. “I’m so happy to see you, I’m getting verklempt!” Maury Morris has his old Brooklyn Jew act nailed down so well, you wouldn’t now he was raised Methodist in Ohio. That accent is as fake as the “conversation” he was having when I walked in. It’s a little something he does to make his clients feel important. Sure, he’s not sincere in slightest, but in this town it’s a close as you get. “Sit, sit…” I park my ass on the vinyl coach. It lets out a brief sound that bears an incredible resemblance to the noise Maury makes when he’s had to much dairy. I just hope he cleaned the damn thing off after nailing the last Lindsey Lohan wannabe on it. Hard to tell with the amount of Old Spice Maury wears. “Boychick, have I got some great stuff for you!” Maury hands me a folder lableled ‘Fruit of the Loom.’ Inside are sketches of a magazine ad campaign for their briefs. “Uh… I don’t think I can, like… do this.” Maury looks shocked. “You can’t do it? Why not?” “Well… these are like, tighty-whities. And I’m a jockey style kind of guy, bro. It would be, like… unethical.” Actually, I just get tired of knowing millions of queens out there would be getting half-chubs looking at my package. I’m just glad most of them are too afraid of accidental poon to turn on the Playboy Channel when I’m on ReVolution. But I don’t tell Maury this because it’s not just his religion that flip-flops. “All right, all right. You know, you’re too big for undie ads anyways. But listen to this. You remember ‘O,’ that modernized version of ‘Othello.’ Well, there’s this studio that wants to make a whole series of urban Shakespeare flicks. Like instead of ‘Mucho Ado About Nothing,’ it’ll be ‘Ain’t Got Nothin’ To Do Wit Me.’ And instead of ‘As You Like It,’ how about ‘How Ya Like Me Now?’ Ain’t that great, boychick?” “I don’t know, Maury…” “What’s not to know? It’s young, it’s hip, it’s fresh!” “Well, it sounds like those would appeal to a, uhhh… BLACK audience. I want to appeal to a more… classy demographic.” “I understand you there, boychick. You want classy, I’ll give you classy I saved the best for last.” Maury gives me the rundown for a new commercial. I can’t believe how perfect it is for me. Maury can see how pleased I am. And when I’m happy, he’s happy. Because that’s how he gets paid. “Is it solid gold or what?” “Solid, Maury.” “I thought we could cross-promote it with that little wrestling show you’re on.” “I don’t know, Maury. PRIME has a pretty… low-bow demographic. Hell, they got me wrestling a guy name Darilek, for Christ’s sake. Yeah, it gets me SOME exposure, but as soon as something better rolls along, I’m gone. Even if it’s a UPN sitcom. I don’t want my image tarnished forever by this little gig.” “Don’t worry, boychick! This is gonna be the start of something big! Just watch!” “All right, Maury. I gotta jet, bro. Facial. You know.” After another hug from Maury and a glare from the old bitch, I escape Maury’s office. I gotta hail ass to make my facial appointment. Which means, of course, that I hit every red light. Typical. The problem with driving a classic like I do is that it doesn’t have AC and Manhattan in June can be hell on Earth. Fuck LA, New York’s got enough smog of its own. And to make things worse, I see this FUCKING BUM headed towards me with a grubby paper towel and a nearly empty bottle of Windex. Oh… shit… no… “Get away from my car. Get away from my car. I don’t want my windshield cleaned. I don’t want my windshield cleaned.” I know this asshole can hear me, but he keeps heading towards me. Some old piece of shit wearing a nasty, filthy army jacket. He probably wasn’t even a Boy Scout. I scream at him to stop, but he still sprays my windshield with his pathetic bottle and starts wiping it with that grubby towel. I see streaks of grime appearing on my formerly PERFECTLY CLEAN glass. I soooo can’t deal with this right now. I get out and shove the old bastard. “Are you fucking DEAF? Are you fucking RETARDED? I said no and I meant NO!” I snag the bottle from him and throw it down the street. I don’t see it land. Maybe it hit someone. I don’t fucking care. I backhand the piece of shit. “Do you understand me now? DO YOU?” “Man… I’m a vet…” I tag him right in the middle of his filthy face. He lands against my hood, clutching his face. He takes his hand away and there’s blood pouring out of his nose. And then… then… HE PUT HIS HAND ON MY CAR! THIS SHITSTAIN PUT HIS FILTHY DISEASED BLOOD ON MY CAR! “YOU FUCKING SON OF A BITCH!!!” I grab his booldy stained arm by the wrist and drag him by my door. He’s too dazed to struggler at all. I open the door and lay his arm on the frame. I see his pathetic eyes fill with fear when he sees me grab the door. The fucker howls like a stray dog when I slam the door on his arm. The door bounces back, so I slam it again. This time there’s a pop along with the howling. I let the bastard take his arm out. It’s hanging limp and useless, probably like this fucker’s cock. All around, people are staring at me like I’m some kind of monster. Like they haven’t dreamt of doing the same damn thing. “What the fuck are you all looking at?” I give the piece of shit a kick in the ass and he falls to the pavement. I look up and notice the light's green. As I get back in my car, I figure I can just make my facial appointment. I hope I don't have to reschedule.
