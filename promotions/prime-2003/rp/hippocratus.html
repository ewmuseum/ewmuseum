Date: April 16-17 I'm in a hospital bed, again. It's become a bit to common a resting place for me over the years, and no matter the place, no matter the time, hospitals all, by and large, all the same. Flat, white, dull and impersonal. Which, when you think about it, is kind of odd. The need for sterility aside, to have places dedicated to healing feel like nothing more than an overdressed morgue has always struck me as a bit bizarre. Still, when I've got doctors poking around my forehead to stitch me up and when my right shoulder feels like at any moment it's going to detach itself from my body, I'm not one to complain about ambience. It's just, that as I sit here, yet again, while being probed and prodded like an alien abductee, my mind tends to wander. Around me, I here the familiar hospital chatter. Phrases like "additional testing" and "hold him overnight for observation" flit around on the air, telling me what I already know. That rental car pickup was going to have to wait another day. As I lay there, the hustle and bustle of a metropolitan hospital at night carrying on around me, I try and think of anything but the image that flashes into my mind every few seconds. That piece of jagged plywood digging into my skull, making the blood spurt out of my head like Daniel Plainview just struck California oil. Not exactly a pleasant memory. Finally, I feel the stabbing pain in my shoulder begin to dull. With all the anesthesia I've undergone in my life, pain medication tends to take a little longer to take effect. Now that it has, though, I know that only one thing can come next. At long last, I sleep. ***** Nobody knew his name, and he liked it like that. His clients new him only as Casper, and there was a reason he'd picked that as his nom de guerre. He was a ghost, alright, but not a friendly one like the moniker implied. This ghost thrived on going places where others wouldn't dare, which was why his services were in high demand. This job, he'd only been on a few days, but it already had led him to the Mile High City. First, to an arena, where he sat, a face blending in with twenty thousand other faces. Now, to an empty hospital room, where he sat directly across from the same man he'd watched as part of the throng, still unseen. Most wouldn't be able to get this close. He was. A police scanner and a few well-placed hundred dollar bills could get him as much access as he wanted. Casper leaned forward in his chair, looking intently at his sleeping target. He didn't know why his employers wanted this man, this Troy Douglas taken care of, and frankly, he didn't care. A job was a job, money was money, and for that, Troy Douglas was soon going to be dead. Not tonight, though. Tonight was a scouting mission, reconnaissance before the final strike. Know thine enemy, his handlers had always taught him. Besides, he wasn't so beholden to cliche as to kill a sleeping man in his hospital bed. There were better ways for this to be done. So, as Troy Douglas lay motionless in his bed, Casper watched, gradually pulling himself ever closer to the bedside until he could finally see the muscles twitch in Troy's face as he breathed. "You don't know what you're in for, pal," he said. He leaned back, smiling to himself, content in his surveillance. His relaxation didn't last long. His normally keen senses let down, he didn't notice the pitter-patter of a nurses' feet in the hallway until the fluorescent lights flashed to life. Momentarily started, he nearly staggered backwards out of his chair, but quickly returned to the state of readiness borne from countless years of training. "Excuse me, what are you doing here?" "Just visiting an old friend," he said, snapping up the reply out of thin air. "Well," the nurse said. "Visiting hours are over. I'm afraid I'm going to have to ask you to leave, sir." Wordlessly he obliged, plucking his leather jacket off the back of the chair and slinging it over he shoulder. If the nurse saw the butt of a semi-automatic pistol barely peeking out of the left pocket, she never mentioned it. ***** "Mr. Douglas, it's time to wake up." As I was pulled from blissful sleep, I ached all over. Nothing more or less than the familiar morning soreness, but unpleasant nonetheless. I stretched and yawned as best I could to work out the kinks, but if history was any indication, those would remain until I was a good four ounces into my second cup of coffee. The nurses' face hovered above me, a pretty redhead no older than 25, probably handling one of her very first shifts by the uncertain look about her. "The doctor's said you can go whenever you're ready." A hot shower was probably in order, but after that, I was gone. On the road again. But, just before I sat up, something crossed my unconscious mind. A figure and a voice, perched right next to my bed as I slept. "Excuse me, were you here last night?" "All night." "Did anyone come in here?" "Other than myself and Dr. Purcell? No." I thanked her, and looked to the pile of clothes on the chair next to the bed. Guess it was nothing to worry about. --To be continued--
