Metallic, mechanical clunks barely broke through the still night as fleeting steps that disguised Angelo's bulk. Though the open window, a leg extended, feeling around the floor, while barely disturbing the light curtain. A seconds, sneaker-clad foot touches down, just out of the moon's enhancing glow. Like a limbo dancer, Kari's body contorted, seemingly uncurling her inside the room. 'Don't move,' went Deville's gravely warning, crackling through the night. As his eyes adjusted to the light, and he stared down the barrel, all that was in his sights was the painting on the far wall. 'Boo!' Angelo nearly jumped out of his skin in a rare, blink-and-you-miss-it moment, before eyes roll as the voice is recognised. 'Look, if yo-' 'Shut up.' Before he could even turn fully around, Karina strode towards him, her body - a force of nature - backing Angelo up as he lowered his weapon. 'And why can't you just come through the do-' Deville went to ask, backtracking, before the K-Wolf pushed a finger across his lips, then gestured for him to 'shhhh'. 'Hate to say it,' she casually quipped as her body met Angelo's, who was happy to ride along on the current. 'Your hallway smells. Musky.' The sound of the metal muffled by carpet was lost to both of them as Karina leant in, replacing the finger across Deville's lips with her own. Further pecks prevented Angelo firing off any hesitant questions, before she swept his legs out, stealing his balance away to leave the Crimson Angel thumping down onto the mattress. VI 'Here'll be fine,' Kari told the driver as she leant forward through the front seats. 'I'll walk the rest.' Far from the stereotypical cab driver, caring only for his fare and tip, the driver had actually made the journey as close to a pleasure as the K-Wolf could experience in such a confined space. A middle-eastern man, flirting with his fifties, and sporting a face to match, his voice rose and fell like a vast, spiking mountain range. 'You're sure?' he enquired with gentlemanly concern in his voice. 'Is late, it is out.' Desperate to delay seeing Angelo again, she'd engrossed herself in conversation with the man, and all but held a PhD in the life of Ismail Daud. A bond was forged as soon as 'Summer In The City' rumbled from the cab's aging speakers, and they both burst into snappy song. For once, someone was further off key than herself. A few polite words of conversation turned into a temporary bond of friendship, always bound to be severed when money was paid, and the door slammed shut. With Kari feigning hunger, they took a tour of the local cab driver's haunts, while his constant updates on the meter were met with, 'yeah, I'll cover it.' Chapattis and chickpea stew gave the car a fragrance of cumin, as well as adding a new, tucked-away place to the K-Wolf's 'so need to eat there again' list. He'd talked about how his daughter was growing up too fast in the accelerated western world, speaking only as a concerned father, without any undertones. She'd told him that despite most perceptions, the youth of today had their head screwed on. Even in the short time he'd known her, the self-belittling and honest take on the world that his passenger offered told him he could trust her. 'Take 10 off the meter,' he then offered, throwing in a discount for the company he'd enjoyed, breaking up the monotony of the night. 'Have a good life.' 'And you,' she warmly smiled back, wishing all encounters could be as fleeting as satisfying. The K-Wolf flashed a wave as the car pulled away, tooting its horn in parting. The warmth of spice still bathing her taste buds took the nip off the night air as Kari strolled the last few blocks towards Deville's. She could never quite put into the right words - meaning, ones that made sense to normal people - why she could never just stop outside. Sneaker soles scuffed the pavement as her legs leisurely swung into step, arms dangling loosely above them. With a weight shed from her shoulders, she'd discarded the rigid posture adopted to bare it. Normally, she'd breeze over to Angelo's. He'd become a gut reaction, an instinct... even during their tiffs. But even though he might well have hated her at the time, and admittedly had good reason, she needed to see him. Even if it was to awkwardly tell him everything but what she'd found out the day before, Kari had to see him. He could have been the father of her child, and her immature acts had all but pushed him away. She had some growing up to do. Maybe they both did. For two personalities that always seemed to clash rather than meld, they'd done too little to change. Both being as guilty as the other. Karina didn't know exactly what she'd expected hopping down over the wall opposite Angelo's home. Maybe her fairytale side, tucked deep down in the comfy confines of her psyche, wanted to see him there waiting, arms open and apologies gushing forth. The sun nudging the moon from its spotlight, while hills displace the surrounding houses to come alive with the sound of music. 'Greetings,' he quipped, eyebrows arching. She couldn't break her stare away from him... Angelo Deville, as he really was, as she knew he was, rather than who she'd wanted him to be. Dreams of settling down to a life of suburban domesticity abandoned her, and the realisation that the negative result was truly for the best. As the door clunked shut, so did her eyelids, as the K-Wolf turned and walked away, leaving her dream family behind.
