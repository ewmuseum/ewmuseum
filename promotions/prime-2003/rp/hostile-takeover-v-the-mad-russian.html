Hostile Takeover V: The Mad Russian

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Back Then “Ya know who fuckin’ I am?” The greasy-skinned, fat man questioned. The combination of muddled words and a deeply embedded thick Russian accent made him almost incomprehensible to Jonathan Winters; not that he was paying much attention. “Tha fuckin’ devil reincarnate, that’s who.” Winters turned to Aeryn, and then glared at Kuschev speculatively. The stench of the mad Russian’s cheap cigars made it difficult for either of them to breath, never mind laugh at his poor excuse for a toupee that was cocked to one side at a completely ridiculous angle. Combined with his nineteen-eighties purple and grey sports jacket, his rather tight Levi jeans and completely unnecessary thick rimmed sunglasses, it was easy to see just why Kuschev had adopted the nickname ‘the Mad Russian’. Of course, it was the numerous gold rings, bracelets and chains that really gave his look a certain unparalleled uniqueness; at least this side of the A-Team years at any rate. “The devil?” Aeryn reiterated, raising her eyebrows. “Yes, yes,” Kuschev abruptly confirmed. “Me tha devil. Kuschev fuck, maim or kill anyone who get in his way.” Winters shook his head, noting that the Mad Russian had begun to refer to himself in the third person and glanced around the establishment with detached interest. Kuschev’s primary base of operation was a slimy strip joint in the very heart of Brooklyn, with it’s interior illuminated only by cheap neon lighting hanging above the bar and the main stage, which proved a limited field of vision. Not the most ideal situation when dealing with the likes of Kuschev. The Mad Russian had come a very long way in the world of organised crime, in a very short time and that made Jonathan Winters’ employer, Cornelius Stakiri, suspicious. With the recent insertion into his territory by a relatively unknown, but clearly powerful, force Stakiri had every right to be cautious of the man. However, within two minutes of coming into contact with the Mad Russian, Winters quickly came to realise they were wasting their time; Kuschev was just like the others. Someone with very big pockets had been financing a lot of incompetent Don Corleone wannabe’s, keeping them in sufficient automatic weapons and muscle. Stakiri had uncovered another dozen similar upstarts over the past few months and had swept them aside accordingly, but Winters got the feeling whomever was pulling the strings behind this whole thing wanted it that way; it would soften Stakiri enough for them to swoop in for the kill somewhere down the line, like the vultures everybody already knew them to be. The Mad Russian would probably be swept aside come the afternoon and then it would be a matter of waiting until the next outfit emerged for the cycle to begin anew. Winters was getting sick of it and he wasn’t the only one. “So,” the Mad Russian began, forcing Winters to tear himself away from the particularly leggy brunette who’d taken to the main stage. Aeryn glared at him angrily and crossed her arms to confirm her mood. “What can Kuschev do for you?” “Nothing,” Winters told him, allowing a sideward glance to Aeryn. She’d probably be upset with him for days, but that was the least of his worries now. He just wanted to get the hell out of there before he suffocated in cigar smoke. “We’re leaving.” Kuschev’s eyes widened with rage and his right hand shot outward, with a speed unbecoming of a man his size, clamping down around Winters’ wrist firmly. He dragged Winters closer, into the blue-red glow of the neon light hanging above the bar. “Kuschev is displeased with--” The Mad Russian paused in his tracks, examining the black-purple scorch marks etched into the side of Winters’ face. “Dear god...what has happened to your face?” Winters winced, as the memories of Ember’s fireball scorching him flickered into his conscious mind. He forced them out the best he could and simply answered, “I was burnt.” The Mad Russian eyed him up warily and then glared at Aeryn with fire in his eyes. “You have come to Kuschev for a reason. You must know what Kuschev sells and more importantly what Kuschev is capable of.” He paused. Winters suspected that the Mad Russian was in awe at his own ego as much he and Aeryn were. “And you dare snub Kuschev in front of his comrades?” He rocked back with outrage. “Are you mad?” Approximately five of Kuschev’s trusted men had attempted to encroach upon their position in a something of a stealthy fashion about five minutes ago and only now, once they were in place, had Kuschev decided to show his hand. Trouble was, both Winters and Aeryn had noticed their advance long before the soldiers had entered into their field of vision. Everything was perfectly under control. Kuschev hung a wide smile upon his face. Apparently, he was under the impression his less than subtle display of power had intimidated them. “So, what will you buy from Kuschev, eh? Women? Drugs? Guns?” His eyes darted from side to side, pointedly and he leant closer. “Children?” Aeryn rolled her eyes. “Oh, beautiful!” Winters shook his head and turned to leave, Aeryn following in suit. However, Kuschev’s men gathered together, providing a makeshift blockade and effectively stopping them in their tracks. The Mad Russian laid a firm hand upon Winters shoulders and whispered into his ear, “Now, tell Kuschev why you two really are here.” Winters shot Aeryn with a cautionary glance, prompting her to raise her wrist to her mouth. “Now would be good, Miguel.” Miguel was Stakiri’s man on the streets and he and his crew were supposed to be backing them up. When Aeryn called for help, it should’ve happened instantly. Miguel and his men were supposed to be in place and by now, the doors should’ve been busted open and bullets should’ve been unleashed. Something was wrong. Winters raised a speculative eyebrow and Kuschev’s men watched on confused, but the Mad Russian simply chuckled. “You think Kuschev wasn’t prepared for this?” He questioned, his grip around Winters’ shoulder tightening like a damned vice. “You think Kuschev just a dumb crook?” Aeryn shot Winters an anxious look. “You think--?” Winters nodded. “We’ve been set-up.” “They must’ve bought Miguel off,” Aeryn added. Kuschev chuckled, as his men tightened the net around them and Winters spwned an uncharacteristic expression of anger. “Kuschev, you claim to be the devil, but I know you’re not.” “Is that so?” The Mad Russian questioned, the smug emanating from his face now able to take down a brontosaurus at two hundred paces. “You see, I’ve seen the face of the devil,” Winters told him, wrenching his shoulder away from the Mad Russian’s mighty clasp. “And he certainly doesn’t wear a toupee.” Winters reacted quickly, pulling his newly freed arm backward and propelling it right into Kuschev’s face, breaking his nose instantly and sending him sprawling over his bar, his cigar flying off in one direction, his toupee in another. Meanwhile, Aeryn had already flung herself at two of his men, knocking them to the ground with minimal effort and creating a clear path to the exit. As Winters darted for the door, he could see Kuschev’s men fish around inside their jackets, desperately trying to lay their hands on their weapons. This left the majority of them wide open and as Winters scooted past them, he threw random punches sending a few sprawling into their nearest comrades and providing a sufficient distraction to facilitate their escape. They were through the door and onto the streets before they even heard the gunfire and by the time Kuschev’s men had come out into the open, they were already gone.
