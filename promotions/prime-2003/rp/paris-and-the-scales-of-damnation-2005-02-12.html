Paris and the Scales of Damnation

12 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-12-05 For the first time that night, before I went to sleep, I thought about whether or not God exists. I have no idea why, but I had never really put much thought into religion. I was never for or against it, I just figured that if there was an afterlife, I’d find out about it when it was time. I never bought into any theory that granted man admission into an eternal paradise, nor did I fear a place of eternal torture. To me, such places seemed too fantastic; too implausible to consider. They seemed like endings of simple stories meant to ease the mind. As much as man frets extinction, he fails to realize the threat of an everlasting existence in his own conscious form. Life was one of those mysteriously wonderful, and sometimes painfully tedious things, that I never questioned. I was here, and that’s all that mattered to me. Mankind had always been too reliant on explanation. The line between science and religion has quaked into a terrestrial chasm, and as the two plates collide at the root of thought, the split in the ground grows larger, and that which stands on either edge gets sucked into the widening gap. Science and religion are both snakes, wrapped in a circle where each one is trying to swallow the other by the tail. Minds float around on opposite sides of the circle, and squeeze around the neck that connects soul to skin, choking it of its pulse. Each entity works contractively against one another to simultaneously disprove each others’ claims. We must stop to ask ourselves what exactly is it that keeps either side viciously tugging against the other with a rope of conviction held unshakably by steady hands. Man has always struggled with his purpose in life. Generations of philosophers pondered life’s meaning and whether any meaning was more than myth. Perhaps there is no meaning. Perhaps there is no anything. Reality is an illusion? Every thought now is an echo; a reflection of the energy of collective human wisdom that flows intermittently in and out of us as we live and as we die. Somewhere, I imagine a collectively conscious mind that connects us all as we drink together from this stream of consciousness until our bellies swell and our bodies sink beneath the surface; our last flashing embers bubbling to the surface with a soft, inverted patter; one borne from underwater rather than from the sky. My own thought right now is just a reverberation that bounces around the seemingly hollow skull of the universe. However, it only seems hollow to the individual mind, the lonely captive of a bony prison. We are all just receptors, helplessly waving microscopic flagella at ricocheting bullets of this sustaining energy that flash by at invisible speeds. Only randomly are we bombarded with ideas that, by chance, collide into us and then absorb and fan out in mushroom bursts deep into the underground encasing of our thread-thin roots and beneath The skull is the mind’s mask, and the spine a fettering chain affixing our souls to the surface of the amorphous core of life we rise from in fluttering bubbles that ripple like sine waves through the calm of balance. Each upswing of momentum is reflected by a twisted trough through a hall of mirrors. The core breathes this way. A gentle turbulence lulls the brain to stasis. We ripple and refract in and out, bobbing our heads above the surface in spite of the thick currents sweeping us into the undertow; in rhythm with the perpetuating exhalations of our soil. As the tiny pieces woven together in this existential fabric brush against each other, the energy expelled through friction rises in waves and clouds up to the edges of our universal skull. The universe swells and roars at its own expansion. The clouds impressing upwards upon the skull’s dome move too slowly to crack the dome open, for that would destroy all within. A man who has become accustomed to pain learns to appreciate its purpose. As it subsides, he ceases to feel and ceases to know if he’s still alive. The gravity on Earth allows us to live in that the pressure pushing on our bodies from the outside is equal to the pressure within that pushes in counteraction. The force outside of the universe that pushes against the slowly expanding skull is the possible realm of God. In simpler days with simpler explanations, God was able to keep a balance, but as the rippling grows faster and more frequent along the surface of man’s collective consciousness; a force not too unlike God’s own, God’s ability to push back weakens. Through reaction or predestination, our consciousness draws its strength to grow from the sweaty beads emptying from each tired burst against us from the other side. We absorb this sweat with a desert thirst, and like salt to our spongy tongues, each drop only makes us thirst more. In essence, God commits a slow, sacrificial suicide (our unique gift in his image) as a last ditch attempt to save his creation. Though we spin off through space without direction, we do not end up crushed beneath our own weight. This God, however, can be in no way be responsible for our creation, only our maintenance. In fact, this is what leads me to believe that man created God in his own image rather than the popular yet inverse tale. Mankind, in vanity, would idealize this conception of God. This mirror image of God, reflected through the mirror that encircles everything and makes space infinite was once our most perfect creation. Regardless of faith, we worship who we see as the most triumphant of storybook heroes. Our Gods are Gods of suffering, of martyrdom. There’s an almost strange necrophilia vested in the living. The dead have a sacredness about them; one that we envy and simultaneously hope and fear to achieve. In time, these stories lose meaning. In time, we achieve more, obscuring these visions of grandeur regarding the soul of man and his connection to God. We build towers past the heavens and play our own Gods; high on the power to create, enslave, and kill more indiscriminately than any otherworldly overseer ever could. As the idea of self-worship becomes less abstract and increasingly literal, the magnificent stories of Gods end, almost ironically, in a final self-sacrifice and a foreboding fade into blankness. Pressure; in this case it is the force that slowly breaks God. It is the force that man struggles against as he tries to no avail to escape the same death he scripted for the God who once humbled him in awe. It’s a pressure that I once knew all too well. It’s a pressure that I struggled against so people would perceive me as a God. It’s a pressure that I feel no longer and one I surprisingly do not miss. I thought about the Dual Halo match, the biggest match of the year in PRIME. Never before have I been involved in a match where the stakes were so great that the winner was awarded whatever he or she desired. I’ve been in matches where I’ve stopped just short of killing myself for a piece of gold. I’ve been in matches where I’ve expelled every last ounce of whatever I could find inside of me to prove my dominance. In short, I’ve been a fool. In every case, I look back and realize none of it was worth it. What’s worse is that most of the time, after leaking every drop of fluid from every pore in my body, realizing that I’ve fallen just short; short of expectations, short of the win, short of beating back the pressure I felt going into every match. And that’s the beauty of the Dual Halo. Nobody expects me to win. I don’t expect to win. I remember failing in the face of possibility. I remember being physically exhausted and emotionally drained. But now, nobody expects me to win, and nobody is putting a bullseye on my chest. If I lose at Culture Shock, then I lose at Culture Shock: No broken body, no broken heart. And then I thought more about God in a different sense. I thought about God as the creator and the gap between nothing and everything. Assuming that molecular theory is correct, everything is made up of smaller and smaller particles which in turn are made up of smaller and smaller particles. With the overwhelming sensual proof that everything actually does exist, the concept of nothing seems ridiculously unfamiliar. There must be some link between the presence of nothing and the faintest blip of matter. It is this link, however, that creates us all; that creates everything. It is this small particle that composes every being, every molecule, every atom, every quark that is our creator. Or perhaps it is nothing at all. Perhaps when you strip everything down to the barest core, everything is composed of nothing. Maybe God is the elusive space between matter. Maybe the Nihilists were right. There is a difference between the worshiping nothing and not worshiping. Our creators are the very things we stray from the farthest. Our Gods are simpler than us and yet too complex for us to comprehend or even imagine in such a state. Of all the things and creatures known to man, nothing is the most unfeasible concept we could imagine. As I try, the surface of the mind stops rippling. The waves and clouds of energy emerging from our mental soil slow to a gruesome halt. The last puff of smoke fades and the skeleton shrivels. Nothing exists in masses surrounding us; in endless waving fields beyond our deepest reaches and in incalculable rifts and folds all around us. The vast immensity of nothingness is exponentially overpowering. It is perhaps the matter of existence, and we are simply nothing to it but nothing itself. Nothing, a merciless, neutral God crushes the skull with an automatic claw. This is the kind of God I need to believe in. My knees are killing me from bowing before false idols and golden icons. I cannot begin to count how many nights I’ve wasted and how much breath I’ve given to prayer. Laying down on a bench, breathing in and out with each repetition. I never counted up. I always counted down, each contraction of muscle burning me one step closer to being the best. I’d push myself to run faster, and each time my feet would bounce off of pavement, I’d imagine that I was a step closer to perfection. Every push up, every protein shake, every reel of tape used for study, every hold I learned, every tub full of ice... Everything all for something, when it should have all been for nothing. Light pried its way through my eyelids, and once it had jammed open a point of entry, I felt a brightness rush through my sinuses and into the front of my brain. It’s a burning feeling, vaguely similar to chill wisps of winter air upon breathing it suddenly through the nostrils. And I had felt like I had been breathed. The brightness surged around me as my body felt bathed in a pool of light. I buoyantly rose, my body bobbing up and down in rhythm with being drawn into the light. My eyes felt burnt, and upon opening them, I was newborn blind. Blotches of white fire ringed by a prism of color spread like cigarette burns through paper thin skin across my field of vision. Clarity poked holes in the center of each blotch, and the fuzz began to drift towards the edges of my tunnel of vision. I was caught in a morning daze, almost instinctively searching for some button to swat to kill the buzzing in my head. The edges that lie between consciousness and sleep always house the most echoic of environments. These are the times when the faintest of noises are amplified by the serene calm. In these moments, the rest of the world disappears as you transition in and out of one reality and into the next. Upon waking, the mind must readjust to recreate the structures and balances into which it has integrated itself. In death, the parts of the brain that control this phenomenon are abruptly destroyed or slowly deteriorated. I’m not so sure I’m alive right now. The ground beneath me is just a blur of color, and the air around me is transparent. I can still see the traces of light blue off in the distance. I’m floating without moving. The ground moves beneath me, the air around me. This is the life of a cloud; a life of simplicity and beauty. I watch over the green field beneath me as the grass burns into a slow brown, and then into nothing at all. I pass over mountains, jagged gray shards of rock that glow purple from the sun. They stand tall and peaceful, behemoths of nature guarding over running crystal streams and secret valleys, isolated pieces of the planet oblivious to all beyond the pointed plateaus that cradle them. The stoic protectors of these untouched shades of utopia roll on beneath me as the planet revolves beneath me and the skies whir past overhead. Mountains give way to a golden tan string of beach, tying the immense wonder of earth with the deep, vast loneliness of oceans which sway back and forth in cool patterns. The string of beach, blessed and kissed by the sun; each warm grain of sand banding together to keep from being washed away; it all drifts behind me. I pray for it to stay, to keep me safe from the dead waters below. I’m trapped in a room with no walls and no exits. The blue borders of reality keep me in like a bubble. When the environment becomes uniform, there is no movement. I begin to long for the overlooked pleasure in how the environment slightly shifts and constantly rearranges its pieces with each crane of the neck or each inquisitive step. We move like pieces on a breathing puzzle that refits edges and snaps in and out of place to keep us from losing ourselves. I’m lost now, and I’m beginning to panic. A scream tunnels its way down my spine and up my throat, hectically bouncing off of the walls of my trachea. It wells up inside of me in the vacuum tube-like chute, pacing in darkness until my mouth opens. Release. Everything rushes out at once in a mixture of noise, vomit, and pain. In a cold sweat, I look around. I’m surrounded, not lost. I must have been floating inside-out. All the imagery escapes. Walls close in on me within my full reach in all directions. The unsettling quiet hushes and is replaced with the screeching, tribal sounds of lunacy. The walls begin to pound and shake. Go away. Please, God, go away. FLIGHT ATTENDANT: “Sir, are you alright? You’ve been in there for a while, and there are other people waiting to use the bathroom.” Her voice is muffled as the door provides an almost adequate sound barrier. Unfortunately, for the diarrhetic passenger, the inch or so of plastic separating the bathroom from the cabin only muffles the gaseous expulsions, boosting the bass of each low end emission, making movements sound far more ominous like tympanic requiems to the damned. I gain my bearings, noticing the plastic seated commode right in front of me as I’m on my knees. BIG POPPA: “What the...” I mumble to myself, victim of a spontaneous hangover of sorts while temporarily putting the stewardess’ question in the back of my mind. I look down to my hands, and I’m holding a vial with a few pills in it. Other pills are scattered on the floor, and I have a handful of them in my free hand. I drop the pills back in the vial and brush around the bathroom floor to pick up as many of the little white tablets as I can scrape into my hands. These tablets are Oxycodone, a painkiller I had prescribed to me after my last stint in wrestling. Unfortunately, thanks to fame and a very giving doctor, I had developed quite an addiction to these painkillers. I was only supposed to take the pills for about a month back in August. My lower back had been killing me, but after a month, it had started to feel better. I figured I should see it through and take some more Oxycodone just to make sure my back healed completely. Unfortunately, things started to get worse. My pain started increasing again, and as it did, I started taking the painkillers more and more. My second prescription ran out much faster than the first, and when I went back to talk to my doctor, he told me that the increase in pain was coming not from my lower back, but from my abuse of the Oxycodone. He urged me to get off of the medicine, but I couldn’t bear it. The pain was getting worse and worse. The more Oxycodone I took, the more my back would ache, but it never ached nearly as much as withdrawal did. Late last year, I tried several times to just stop taking the medicine completely. I’d last an agonizing twenty-four hours or so before I’d just pour some of the pills down my throat. This is when the hallucinations started to happen. Random images and sounds would surface from time to time, but the grandest hallucination of all was the euphoria I felt when I was on Oxycodone. I’d be in another world; one where none of the logistical bullshit existed, and I was able to simply appreciate everything as it was. There was no past to haunt me and no future to build. All of the stagnant intricacies of the present, bad and good, were simply there as they were, unable to affect whatever was supposed to happen next. This was my last vial of Oxycodine. My doctor cut me off. I knew he’d been trying to cut me off for months, but I’d beg and plead until he caved. He called me last week and left a message on my machine. He told me that he could no longer see me as his patient. It was too much of a moral dilemma. He wished me luck, but I deleted the message furiously before he could finish. I needed these pills, and ironically, they’re scattered all over the floor of a telephone booth sized room with a toilet and a sink. The irony is almost laughable because as a side effect of these painkillers, I haven’t been able to shit in days. I never thought I’d trade anything for the satisfaction of my daily logsplitter. FLIGHT ATTENDANT: “Sir? Are you...” I cut her off, trying to buy myself sometimes. BIG POPPA: “Yeah, I’m fine. Go on ahead. I’ll just be a second.” I gather what I can of my remaining pills and tuck them away, sealing the vial and hiding it in the inside pocket of my jacket. Aside from my doctor, nobody knows about my addiction. I’d like to keep it that way. Perhaps it would explain my eccentricities, my insecurities, and my general confusion when wandering about, but perhaps there are certain things that needn’t be explained. I stand up, flush the toilet once for good measure (even though my digestive system has sealed my sphincter to such a tight level that Pat Buchanan would marvel in awe at the inability for anything to slip the border), and brush myself off before opening the door...scratch that...before reproducing the vial, popping some pills in my mouth, and washing them down with some sink water I’ve cupped in my hands. I secure the tiny bottle from view again before exiting the bathroom. As soon as the lights in the cabin hit my, my vision blurs and my head starts to spin. As I start to regain control of my equilibrium, I scan the narrow path ahead of me in search of my seat. The cabin seems a lot longer than I remember it. Everybody seems to be staring at me, more so when I claw the back of their seats trying to trudge through what can narrowly be defined as an aisle. Worse yet, I haven’t the slightest idea how the person sitting next to me looked. I find the first open aisle seat that I can and rush over to it. The man sitting there doesn’t seem to acknowledge me. He’s busy combing through a thick paperback by Michael Crichton or someone of the like. I move to sit down and he eyes me with only his right eye, his left still seemingly glued to the page. When he peripherally confirms that I am, in fact, trying to sit down, he barks at me in a tight, surprised manner. DOUCHEBAG: “Hey! What do you think you’re doing?” I straighten back into a standing position as non-threateningly as I can. As I look at him, he’s still reading his book. I can’t tell if I imagined the last five seconds or not. He’s combing through that paperback, alright, and he looks like he hasn’t been disturbed. Still, I keep moving forward. Suddenly, a man with an empty seat next to him stands up and motions for me to move inward. I shuffle into the aisle seat, keeping my eyes on the floor to avoid tripping over his carry-on luggage, the most average, black duffel bag I’ve ever seen. I utter out a stuttering thank you before slouching into my seat and staring out the window. The plane passes through clouds, poking in and out like a bullet through a marshmallow. The blue hue has returned to the sky. The world looks so calm and stable below, when really, at the surface, cities are teeming with movement. Even in barren areas nothing is really still. Altitude creates quite an illusion, distance ignorance. Still, I’m further away from everything, until the voice next to me brings me back. PASSENGER: “It’s been a long time, Poppa.” I recognize the voice. It’s a voice I never hoped to hear again. It’s the voice of a man I walked out on when I was younger; when I thought I owned the world. It was a voice I last heard when I was on top of the world, and now I feared that the past was coming back to laugh at me; to rub his rightness in my face. But he didn’t. PASSENGER: “I know we’ve had a falling out of sorts, but I have forgiven you. Let’s let the past be the past. I know you’ve matured enough to start making better decisions. I know that life has been teaching you many tough lessons. I’m not here to teach you another one. I’m just here to offer you a new chance at life, and a way to rebuild yourself into the man you once were with the knowledge you have now.” BIG POPPA: “And how do you propose we go about doing that?” PASSENGER: “The same way we did it in the first place.” I force out a tired laugh. The devil is a charming figure. He seduced woman before man ever could. He wears many disguises and goes by many names, but I’ve only seen him in human form. I’ve only known him under one name. PASSENGER: “Remember, God is the one that punishes, not me.” BIG POPPA: “Heh. You’re no angel yourself.” PASSENGER: “I used to be.” BIG POPPA: “So you really think there’s something left in me?” PASSENGER: “There’s something left in all of us. I’ve risen the dead before. You’re not as far gone as you thought. You beat Peter Vetra, didn’t you?” BIG POPPA: “Yeah, but that was Peter Vetra.” PASSENGER: “It’s still a start. Now win this Dual Halo. All you need are some dual horns.” BIG POPPA: “Yeah, sure.” PASSENGER: “Once you stop acting like a joke, people will stop treating you like one. You shouldn’t be finding anything about your life funny right now. Maybe other people do, but that’s because it’s always amusing to watch someone else’s failure. The harder you try, the louder the laughter when you fall.” I continue staring out the window, and after a while, I drift from the conversation. Part of me knows he’s right, and the rest of me is reluctant to listen. Was my success a result of my nature or was my nature a result of my success. And what about my demise? What does it matter? If there is fate, I don’t believe that everything is predetermined. We’d all be more like dominos, each waiting to be knocked over by the domino before us into the domino after. The few billion or so years of chain reaction have flown by at a breakneck speed. As we topple, time accelerates. And if it’s free will, that’s even more chaotic. There are almost seven billion separate wills, each clashing against one another in a glorious clusterfuck. No progress can be made. Noone ever falls in an orderly fashion, in rows and columns that glide intricately across space. We all just lean together in one giant flesh orgy, dirty skin grinding against dirtier skin until skin rubs to bone. Free will. It’s the sound of thirty competitors caged together like animals. And like animals, we will cannibalistically hunt our pray, packed tightly in the same glorious clusterfuck. Twenty nine competitors have their own separate agenda, and twenty nine competitors will grind skin against skin until skin breaks and blood sheds. Twenty nine competitors have their minds set on a boundless prize. And then there’s me. If I win, I don’t know what I’m going to ask for. I haven’t put much thought into it because I haven’t really put any thought into the possibility of winning. My dirty skin is already ground off, shed like a naked snake, slowly crawling in pain upon muscles drenched in its own poison. My skin is already worn, and I’m just bones now. Fortunately, I hear there are a lot of good plastic surgeons in California.
