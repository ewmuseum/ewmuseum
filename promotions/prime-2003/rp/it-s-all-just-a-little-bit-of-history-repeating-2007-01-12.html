It's All Just A Little Bit Of History Repeating

12 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 12, 2007 I've got this ... headache. A repetitious pounding at the front of my cranium, inches above my eye sockets, that never completely ceases. I'm not the kind of person who knows how to fully de-stress, since ninety percent of the time I'm running on all cylinders, and the simple act of taking medication or lying down in a dark room does nothing to ease the pressure. I shirk at the idea of taking pills to begin with. But right now, a jackhammer has taken up residence in my brain and the chisel keeps penetrating the recesses of my mind. I get no peace. I never do, and I never have. Some days, the pain is dulled, and it leaves only a faint reminder of its presence. Other days, like today, the tension is heightened. I'm not sure if it's the cabin pressure in first class, where I try to recline the best I can in a leather seat that still only offers leg room sufficient enough for people of "normal height", or if it's the reason I'm currently waiting for this jet-engined beast to finish its descent into the Atlanta airport. I can't string three thoughts together without the hammering throb interrupting the train, reminding me to not forget it's there. I'm never alone, even when I am. Besides my reluctance to take doctor recommended relief in capsule form, I also cringe at the idea of hospital stays. I've had my fair share of check-ins, the majority of which I've been ordered to go at the behest of a company owner, but there's also been a handful where my unconscious state couldn't offer any protest. I hate the latter reason the most, since my say in the matter was rendered null at the hands of someone else. I don't like being told what to do, but I hate not being able to voice an opinion even more. The plane's touch down to earth is smooth, and the taxi to the terminal quick. As those around me prepare to deplane, the jackhammer kicks in to a higher speed. With a shake of my head, I stand up to grab my carry-on bags and make my way off this wind wagon. It's only later, when I'm waiting for my luggage to appear on a conveyor belt, when I begin to wonder... Is this really a headache? Or is it just memories squeezing against my brain, desperately trying to get out... January 12, 2007: Emory University Hospital. One day removed from ReVolution 115. In the fading light of another non-daylight savings time day, Mary Ward kept watch at her husband's bedside, chair pulled close to the bed-rail and pink-tipped fingers gently caressed the top of Matt's hand. Just enough warmth to let the Inhuman Being know she was there and enough pressure to reassure that she wasn't about to leave anytime soon. Her husband was a mess. His head half-mummified with gauze and bandages, he laid propped up in bed, groggy and in pain but coherent now. His voice was soft, the tone of a battle-weary man whose strength had been depleted at the hands of an army whose numbers had been underestimated, regardless of how few there were to begin with. "Do you need anything, Matt? Is the pain coming back? I can get the nurse..." He shook his head slightly. He just wanted to reassure her that he'd be alright. It's just part of the job, a minor setback...nothing to worry about. But after all these years...after all the wars, the battles, all the talk and the actions to back the words up, Matt wasn't sure just how minor this really was. Sure, he'd heard the diagnosis somewhere in the haze of trying to recollect what happened to him. But after the painful separation from his wife, the long months of not speaking or seeing one another, this dismantling felt like he had drawn a card that unwillingly moved him back twenty steps from all the progress he had made and all the obstacles he had overcome. But Mary never said a word about any of that. Gentle Mary just wanted Matt to get better, to go home to his daughter who she couldn't bear to take with her. She knew no daughter should see her father like this, and explaining how he ended up this way would be too painful a task. So the couple sat in silence, Matt moving his thumb over his wife's wrist, Mary inching the chair closer to lay her head next to her husband's, until a knock on the door broke the stillness. "Mister and Missus Ward," the nurse addressed, sticking her head through the threshold of the door. "I know the doctor said that there was to be limited visitors, but there's someone here who wants to see you." Mary lifted her head and looked at Matt, who had a better vantage point to see who the nurse was talking about. He was slightly smiling. "It's alright." The nurse nodded and disappeared from view. Replacing her, with hands clasped in front of her and a leather jacket folded over her arm, was Lindsay Troy. She moved further into the room to allow the door to quietly shut behind her and gazed at Matt in silence. Her face remained stony for a moment, before her eyes fluttered shut. "What'd he do to you..." It wasn't quite a question and it wasn't quite a statement, but it was delivered with a shake of her head and a hushed growl. Lindsay re-opened her eyes and found Mary on her feet, moving toward her. "You must be Lindsay," she said, stopping in front of her. When Troy affirmed this, Mary offered her hand in introduction, which Lindsay took. "I wish we could have met under more pleasant circumstances, Mary," Troy said before ending the handshake. "Chet had been talking about a company picnic for us and our families, but the idea got shot down faster than an enemy of Jack Bauer's." Despite the circumstances, Troy's wit was still sharp. Mary smiled and Matt tried to laugh, but could only manage a half-hearted chuckle. "I'm glad you came, Lindz." "I would have been here sooner, but it's snow bunny season down in Florida. Flights are filling up." Lindsay moved across the room, pulling a chair along with her, and sat on the opposite side of Tchu. Mary returned to her seat as well. "I'm surprised you don't have a private plane. You're quite a big shot." "Look at you, Matt, finding the strength to rib me when you're lying here like this," she said, placing her jacket across her lap and letting her purse slink to the floor. "Deville didn't take your humor from you, I see." "That he didn't," came the groggy reply. "Have you heard from Killean?" "Not since before I went to the airport. My phone's been off, needed to charge, and I can't use it in here. I'm sure he knows, and he'll come." Mary nodded, her hand finding her way back to her husband's. Lindsay glanced around the room, looking at nothing in particular but trying to avoid the machinery that was hooked up to Matt's body. She looked uncomfortable, but was trying to hide it. "How bad is it, Matt?" she finally, reluctantly, asked. "It's not good, but not terrible, if you know what I mean." She nodded. "He lost a lot of blood," Mary said, taking over, "and was unconscious for a while. Deville cracked his skull and there was some swelling, but that's gone down. Are you alright?" While Mary was speaking, Troy was absentmindedly rubbing her head. "Huh? Oh. It's nothing. Just a headache." "Mary, I think I'll take a glass of water, come to think of it. And it's almost time for another dosage," Matt whispered, and squeezed her hand. Mary bobbed her head in assent and left the room. For the second time, Troy shook her head, and Matt slowly turned his head to look directly at her. "Not bad, but not terrible?" Troy repeated, quirking an eyebrow. "That wasn't very convincing." "I don't want to trouble Mary even more than she already is," he replied. "She looks like she's holding it together pretty well, but she's a wreck, Lindz. No one should have had to see what happened." "I know..." she trailed off for a moment. "More than you think I do, I know." What she didn't want, or what she couldn't bring herself, to say was that right now she saw Alaina in that bed instead of him, a victim of a blindsided attack three years ago from a force much greater than herself. Lindsay blamed herself then, when she could do nothing to stop it, and she partly blamed herself now, for not being in the Georgia Dome the night before. Even at the expense of herself, Tchu's beating may not have been as brutal if there was another to fend off the assault. When she wouldn't say any more about the matter, Tchu pressed forward. "I think you know that I'm going to be out for awhile because of this. And with Killean taking care of his own problems, that doesn't leave a very united front against Deville and the Gang." "No, it doesn't. Chet can't wrestle and Blaine isn't very useful in any sort of capacity beyond administrative duties." "That just leaves you, Lindz." "It does. And y'know something, Matt?" "Yes?" "I kinda like it that way."
