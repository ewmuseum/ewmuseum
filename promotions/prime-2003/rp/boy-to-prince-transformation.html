Boy to Prince (Transformation)

Date: Past Life is generally considered when you live your life under a microscope of scrutiny or constant surveillance. There are some who thrive under the bright lights and who embrace their ‘Hollywood’ status; others crumble beneath the inordinate pressure. Crossroads: Darwin Jersey City, New Jersey; Thirteen Years Ago A family of five had just sat down at their dining room table in a modest three bedroom home in Jersey City, the scummiest section in the Garden State. The heavyset father sat at the head with his wife across from him, his two daughters to his right, and his eldest, a boy, to his left. Mealtime was quiet aside from the occasional clank of silverware or knife tearing through steak. The mother watched her children with much vigilance; her eyes and mind always analyzing. “Giancarlo, how was school today?” she asked, her worried expression reaching out to her ten year-old. The boy’s gaze remained fixed on the full plate ahead of him with the occasional blink showing life indeed existed. He was a small boy, his black hair complimented his charming features and olive tone perfectly. An uneasy silence over the table, and the mother’s focus turned to her husband, who was keenly aware of his role. He cleared his throat as his eyes drifted toward his son. “Your mother asked how your day was, son. So, how was it?” His voice was harsh like a growl even when he aimed to be gentle. Again, the boy did not respond. He jabbed his fork into his potatoes, but opted build rather than eat. His sisters whispered to one another, which caught the attention of their father. “You two have somethin’ to share?” His question sounded more like a demand, and his demands went unanswered. “Answer me! So help me God, Monalise, Catarina, if yous two don’t answer me!” His temper flared quickly, even his wife snapped upward in her seat. It was he who now had the attention, both nervous girls scrambling for words. It was Monalise who replied, “The other kids at school pick on him.” Satisfied with their cooperation, their dad nodded his head. He turned back toward his son, whose trance had never broken. “G, what the hell ah these kids pickin’ on you about?” With the cat out of the bag, Giancarlo slowly looked up at his father, first from the corner of his eye and then by rotating his neck. “They call me a runt ‘cause I’m the smallest kid in our class and they call me fairy ‘cause I don’t play sports like the rest of ‘em.” The little man looked down at the wood floor, unwilling to risk seeing the look of disappointment across his father’s face. There was none, however. Only a question. “And what do you say to ‘em?” “I don’t say anything. They’re bigger than I am,” he started, eyes gravitating from the floor to his pop, “and I don’t want to beat up at school. I’ll get laughed at and picked on even worse.” The fury that had shown earlier was gone, but now it resided in the children’s mother. Concern had turned into heartbreak, and heartbreak quickly produced anger. She tossed her napkin onto her plate and waved a pedicured index finger at her husband. “I say we call those little snots’ parents, Lou. I’ll go down to the school tomorrow and straighten out all this bullshit,” she threatened, worked up to the point of swearing in front of her three darlings. Lou chuckled at his wife’s fury. Hell hath no fury like a woman scorned. It was a major turn-on, this hidden wrath beneath her soft cover. If Lou had really thought about it, he would have remembered two-thirds of his children being produced due to that wild, passionate side. That thought was merely temporary, promptly replaced by the situation at hand. “You ain’t going down to the school and we ain’t talkin’ to any of them kid’s parents,” Lou sternly informed his wife. She piped down momentarily before disregarding her better judgment to prod her husband. “And why the hell not? He can hardly eat, Lou. You just want to keep sending him back into the same hellhole?" Her husband’s glare spoke volumes, albeit silently. She sat back in her chair as Lou addressed his son. “Your mother is not gonna stick up for you, Giancarlo. It’s your battle and she’s not fighting them for you no more. Yous got two options. You can either stand up for yourself like a man, or yous can forever be the gutless runt that these kids seem to think you are.” Giancarlo inhaled deeply, slowly releasing the air. “What would you do, pop?” The oldest of bonds, a son asking his father advice. Lou slid his meaty hand onto his kin’s frail arm. “You’re a Coppano, Giancarlo. Whatever decision you make will surely be the smart choice, that I do believe. Our lives are changing, G, and they’re changing quickly. I realize you’re young, too young to know about what your old man does for a living, but you could have all the opportunities in the world.” The young boy nodded along. He was a smart child, always had been. “I know what you do, pop,” the boy admitted, his eyes searching for his father’s reaction. He heard kids at school talking and noticed how nice local shop owners were to him. To him, his dad was just that – his dad. However, to New Jersey, his father was Lou “Kitten” Coppano. Lou grinned. His son was an intelligent young man. Good, he thought, because you need intelligence to rule a kingdom. “Then you know, Giancarlo, that someday … Jersey could be yours.” Manifest Destiny Princeton, New Jersey; Six Years Ago Lou “Kitten” Coppano, a Cuban cigar hanging from his mouth, leaned back into his leather armchair. As usual, his Bengal cat rested on his lap inside his study. Around the room were the most highly respected of men, many whom had known Lou since he was a kid. Most also smoked cigars and nearly ever man held a drink in their gold decorated fingers. The dozen or so men joked, laughed, and hollered. “Gentleman,” Lou started, his voice ceasing the merriment and celebrations, “as you all know, Phillip Gamanetti has retired to a shallow grave somewhere in Jersey.” Chuckles filled the room, and Lou couldn’t restrain his own grin. “Gamanetti was the last of the old family Dons and he was the final obstacle standing in the Coppano Family’s path. With him gone, Jersey belongs to the Coppano Family and that means changes. Good changes, too. Wealth, freedom, and a certain sense of pride,” Coppano announced, his speech receiving unanimous head nodding from his audience. Lou puffed on his cigar and deposited the smoke away from him. “For now, go out and enjoy yourselves, fellas,” Coppano encouraged, another grin forming. Only the men made no attempt to clear out and celebrate. Instead, they formed a line, much like schoolchildren lining up at the fountain, and each man waited for his opportunity to kiss, on bended knee, Lou “Kitten” Coppano’s hand. Nick Diamonte. Giovanni Diamonte. Jackie Rossi. Francesco “Frankie” Ferrari. Tommy Ricci. Dino “Joe” Marino. One by one they filtered through, each man performing the Mafioso ritual and then exiting the room. There was one man who did not stand in line, who did not kiss Lou’s hand. He stepped out from the shadowy corner of the study, the light shone on his chiseled facial features. He was clearly Giancarlo Coppano, the grownup version of a charming boy. Giancarlo, eyes locked on his father, moved over toward the new Don of New Jersey. “Congratulations.” Lou smiled at his son before looking to his right, where another individual who had remained in his seat throughout the proceedings sat. The man, Keegan O’Doherty, slid his chair closer to Lou. Keegan was half-Irish and half-Italian, typically a mutt like him had no business being a consigliere. Keegan was the exception; he’d proven his value to Lou ten fold, having saved Lou from certain death long ago. “Giancarlo, I want to talk to you,” Lou started as Keegan settled down beside him. “First, are you sure that you aren’t going to college?” A sigh escaped Giancarlo’s lips. Before he could answer, his father said, “I know you’ve told us that you want to follow in my footsteps. That you want to live this lifestyle … but, I remember a boy who wanted more once. So, I ask you again, are you sure?” Giancarlo nodded. “I chose to live this life a long time ago, father.” His father, grinning like a Cheshire cat, was pleased to hear that. “Very well, Keegan and I have talked this over, and we’re going to make you earn your stripes. You won’t be running any errands and shit, but you’re gonna have to get your hands dirty, capeesh?” “It’d be my pleasure,” was the confident reply. Lou smiled again at his son’s confidence, a trait he was not always blessed with. “You’ll run a three man crew, but you’ll be checking in with Nicky and Giovanni. What they say fucking goes, understand?” Another nod. “Good, we’re all real proud of you.” Lou took Giancarlo’s head in his hands and planted a kiss on his forehead. Keegan extended a hand which Giancarlo met halfway. He had officially stepped into the role that he’d been playing for years. Giancarlo turned and headed away from the two highest ranking members of the Coppano Family. “And Giancarlo, Jersey is still yours for the taking,” his father started, halting his exit, “only this time, you’re man enough to do it.” With those parting words, Giancarlo Coppano left the study with the whole world seemingly ripe for the picking. Prince Untouchable New York City, New York; Six Months Ago “Agents are all fuckin’ weasels, sports and government alike,” spat Jimmy Riscitelli. Jimmy was an old wrestling pro under the name the Italian Stallion. Even as his body aged, his slick black hair remained in pristine condition. “Don’t I fuckin’ know. Just think about the cash I’m missing out on, and sure, maybe I don’t need more money, but it’s the principle of the matter.” The other half of the conversation came from Giancarlo Coppano, who happened to be most notorious and talented student. Giancarlo and Jimmy were holed up in Jimmy’s office at the back of his wrestling school. Jimmy paced in front of Giancarlo, who casually sat with his legs propped on Riscitelli’s oak desk. Closer to the door, two of G’s crew sat guard, but mostly just listened to the heated topic of conversation. Long story short, Giancarlo was one of the hottest up-and-coming wrestlers on the market. Many thought FWO, NFW, or even ACW would have lined up to have the Jersey Prince. However, there had been surprisingly little activity on the wrestling front. Something was amiss. “You’ll get signed, Carlo.” The statement promptly snared a glare from the Jersey Prince. “I mean … Giancarlo,” Jimmy quickly corrected his mistake. The teacher-student relationship only held so much weight and calling Giancarlo anything else but that was much too heavy. Giancarlo smirked at his trainer. Old bastard. “Oh, I’ll get signed, but these assholes are going to regret making me wait,” he threatened with a toothy snarl. Jimmy nodded in agreement before resting a hand on Giancarlo’s shoulder. “You been inside the ring at all? Wanna roll wit’ one of da pups here?” As Jimmy grinned as wildly as one man could, Giancarlo quietly contemplated the notion. “Sure, why not,” he replied. Jimmy brought his hands in and rubbed them together. The foursome – G, Jimmy, and the two muscle heads – stepped out of the office and, led by Riscitelli, headed toward the ring. Upon arrival, Giancarlo and Jimmy slipped inside while the other two remained on the floor. “Alright boys, gather ‘round here,” Jimmy called out, capturing the attention of a large contingency of those inside the gym. As Jimmy stepped forward toward the ropes to address the growing crowd, Giancarlo’s attention landed toward his personal guard, who unleashed a whistle and held his boss’ phone into the air. Giancarlo caught the phone in mid-air and brought it to his ear. “You can’t be serious ... well, who the hell told you this?” Giancarlo spoke quietly, but at a furious pace. “I’ll stay right the fuck here an’ if anybody shows their face, I’ll bury ‘em. I’m fucking untouchable.” He flicked the phone shut, wiped the scowl from his lips, and moved up next to Jimmy, who took his phone and placed it in his breast pocket. Untouchable? He seemed to think so. However, there was a stocky Irishmen standing in the crowd who shared a much different outlook. He’d show, that inside the ring, Giancarlo Coppano wasn’t untouchable. For starters.
