Back where I belong? (ReV 93 RP)

29 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29/04/06 I don’t know why I returned to this. I’m sitting here, glass in my hand, writing this and I still can’t think of what drove me to come back. Granted, it’s been worth it – while a clusterfuck like the Halo can’t really be counted for much in terms of individual performance, a solid win in the first one-on-one contest (against Killean freakin’ Sirrajin, no less) surely proves that my presence is warranted.Still I don’t feel 100% comfortable here, but when I think back to my last run I’m not certain I ever did. We’re all misfits here, of one form or another, and I certainly don’t feel edged out or ignored... maybe it’s just my background; I’ve never felt completely comfortable in a group, hell, even just being close to one person is a challenge for me – popularity’s never, ever been my game. Thanks High School – you fucked me up for good.Nonetheless, I try and at least be social – try and work in a group, disjointed as it may be. It works, sort of, but the loner instinct still persists. I think I end up oscillating between the two – the previous Adam/Ignatius relationship stands testament to this; sometimes I’m deadweight tied to someone’s leg, dragging them down with me. Other times I desperately need a hand to stop getting stuck in a rut by myself and I vehemently refuse it.Sporadic as my successful work is, I still keep at it – week in, week out. I’ve never turned up with nothing in my hands, never not put up a fight; even if the ‘Eternal Underdog’ tag was more than warranted that week. I can’t complain about it though: if a loss is expected in every match it only makes an occasional win sweeter. I have no idea what the first loss after a ten-win streak must be like, but being the upset on the card with a win over Lance Jackson or Killean Sirrajin when not even I seriously thought I could take it... it’s wonderful.No offence to either, but it’s only after the match that acknowledgements from them come my way – basically to cover their own ass for the surprise upset. I don’t blame them, you can’t really – I won’t say I’d do the same in their position, because I never have been and never will be in it. Even if I do end up winning 99% of the time, I know (or at least I hope) I’d still remember my humility from now when that 1% happens; I’d still extend a hand to my opponent before and after the contest, even if they choose not to accept the offer, I want it to be there.The more I write here the less structure I see, but I can’t rearrange it without losing whatever thread is running through all this – so the apology for that is here. Hopefully you can follow, and if not them you should be able to pick little somethings up from sections here and there.Back to where I started though, to try and regain some semblance of order, I don’t know why I’m back. I knew before my return, and I’m seeing it so much now that I’m actively here, that it isn’t the same place I left – the whole ethos is totally different, there’re only a few names and faces I actually recognise and even then... it’s sometimes only the name and face, I don’t recognise the person underneath at all. That aches in really odd way. I’m trying to keep looking at the positive side of it; I get to work with people I’ve never worked with before. Nothing like not knowing anything about an opponent or their previous record to keep you on your toes.It’s not even like the competition bothers me, saying I expect to lose every match is going too far, but I’m not exactly hugely surprised if I have to rack up another notch in the ‘L column’. Maybe it is the competition though, and not the result; I have no idea of my long-term W/L/D record, but I do recall the best clashes more than the ‘best’ wins.Maybe I’m just not established back in the fold yet, because I distinctly remember the first time feeling properly ‘in’ was after picking up the Jackson win first-time round, that was when my work was first properly noticed. Hopefully that’ll happen again with the Sirrajin shock; remind everyone that I’m here and get back into the swing of being ‘one of the guys’.Hold up and let me clarify that, though – I don’t feel entirely comfortable here, but I certainly don’t feel perfectly out of place or in place. There’s a big difference between being ‘one of the guys’ and feeling like you really belong here. But that’s all down to me, in my head, and not a reflection on the atmosphere in this place – no-one’s expecting everyone to love everyone else, there’re always differences, and the fact that competition is involved doesn’t exactly make things roll any smoother. But nothing’s perfect, and even if everything was it’d be way boring.Back on track, though; I love being here, and I love what I do, but it doesn’t mean I feel that what I do is worthwhile or that the work I put in is good enough, hence my (to use a cliché) downward spiral leading to my exodus last time. The time out did me well, in some respects, but I still felt stifled – like I wasn’t doing something I should have been. The longing to come back was faint, but it was always there, even just after I left. I just didn’t have the energy or means to do what I needed to at the time, seems like I do now, but it’s still too early to know for definite.It’s definitely weird being the prodigal son, especially after the jokingly-predicted ‘slaughter’ of the ‘fatted calf’ upon my return turned out to actually happen. What I want to know is what happened to the prodigal son after the feast. ‘He was dead but now he is alive!’ – that’s me, I was dead here for a long time and suddenly I’m alive again and welcomed back with open arms and a grand re-entrance... but now what? Handily skimmed over in the big book, onto another story with a moral behind it. Typical.I think it’s just being able to have a voice out there, saying what you want said and having that voice be heard, digested and appreciated. That’s what matters and that’s why we’re here. You may get waylaid picking up titles to put beside your name or brag about to whoever’ll listen, but what more does a title do than give your plinth a bit more height so the words of your story are given a bit more stature?Maybe it’s just being able to have my words heard and my story told that drives me to being here. Well... the story that I choose to present. It’s not like there’s a chance, a drive or a reason to know the life and history of everyone here – but the snippets given are enough to keep interest going until the next instalment. I think the key word here is ‘story’ – it’s what we decide to tell of ourselves, or the character we portray around here, that gets told; not our actual lives. It could be a true to the letter account of your life, but in the little bit that’s seen it leaves a lot of the rest to imagination. How many people have had a good book spoiled after seeing someone else’s vision of it in a movie? Everyone here has a different mental image filling in the blanks of everyone else’s story between what we’re actually told. Following all these other stories while weaving mine through it for everyone else to enjoy/criticise/praise... maybe that’s why I’m here. A bit-part in someone else’s story that’s followed by a million others; surely being noticed by one single person following it is better than 24/7 coverage of my own self-told story that no-one cares about?This isn't my story. And with that Adam sips the very last of his vodka, hits Ctrl+A then Del and stands up. After a stretch to ease his back a little he flicks the screen off, picks up his empty glass and walks off. Note: I think the comments on the OOC about your character being an extension of yourself prompted this, to an extent. I’m not really certain where it came from, or why I decided to write it and submit it, but here it is. I’m deliberately leaving it vague as to whether it’s ‘Adam’ or myself writing, because I honestly don’t know for a lot of it.
