The Shelf-life of The Human Ideal

17 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/17/05 The Shelf-life of Metaphysics The tinkling glasses, idle conversation, and white noise filled the room like humidity. Damien Cruz shook hands until his was sore, smiled politely until his face cramped, and forgot names like he were filling an overrunning cup. But he had to make the rounds. He had agreed to do an interview for a magazine he never read, and showing face at a company function would give the readership an excuse to buy the next copy. So he performed his duty without complaining, but it was not easy. He never lied to himself. He never pretended that his profession was still a noble venture for the noble warrior. Like medicine, art, and journalism, his lifestyle was fed by the same artery that nourished AOL and The MGM Grand. Without a face to market, a story to sell, and a slogan to live by, he was just another nobody beating into submission another nobody for a free voucher at the hotel buffet. Damien Cruz was realistic, but that didn't mean he liked this part of his job. Even as he sipped $75 a flute champagne, and picked on $80 a dish caviar, he wished he were in a dark gym, preparing for a match that would be twice as hard as the one he lost in his debut. So, when the opportunity presented itself, he took it. The organizer of the affair took to the stage, and all eyes turned toward him. And as they did, Damien Cruz slipped out, and into the hallway. The Latin Assassin, feeling very much like Mr. Cruz, popped an aspirin with a sip of champagne, and headed toward the nearest exit. It was a fire exit, actually, and lead to a rooftop balcony. Somehow, the logic oof that set-up evaded him, but the fresh air was more than enough to let the thought go. Damien Cruz looked down on the city below him, and the altitude was dizzying. Of course, a media empire would be perched on the highest tower that man could build. The Tower of Babel metaphor was too easy, so he let it go the way of the burning rooftop escape plan. ' You too?' Damien turned to the voice, and saw the woman sitting in a corner, sipping on a glass of wine, and trying to keep the dress she was wearing from riding any higher up her thigh. Smiling, Damien walked to where she sat, on the steel steps of the fire escape. It would have been cliche if the woman was enchanting, mysterious, and as philosophically bored with the media circus as Damien. But she was plain-looking, almost sixty, and assuming that Damien wanted to escape the party to smoke a joint like she had. ' No, I just wanted some fresh air. I figured it would be a good time to do so. Mr. Henderson was just about to go into his thirty minute fundraising speech, and I already sent in my dues.' The woman, aided by the THC, didn't know what that meant, but found it very amusing. So she laughed, hard. ' My name is Damien Cruz. I am working with one of the writers for a piece on my life in the independent wrestling circuit.' That one had her in stitches, and Damien couldn't help but chuckle along with her. She may have been half-baked and wholly-fried, but she was fun to watch. He offered her a hand, and she shook it, heartily, and the meeting was official. After a period of silence, in which he enjoyed, she continued the conversation. This time, she was less jovial. ' I've been coming to these events for almost thirty years. I remember when they were held at the local community center because our office was too small to fit the entire staff at the same time. It was back then when this magazine was supposed to live up to the ideals of changing minds and breaking down barriers. Of course, that was before the Reagan Era. All of a sudden, we were talking about profit margins, and reader turnover.' She smiled, but Damien saw what she was driving at. ' Things change, Mr. Cortez, and we must change with them, eh?' Maybe this was just as cliche as he first thought. He nodded, and let her vent. ' I'll tell you something, when you set your mind on something, and I don't care what the fucking bumper stickers say, when you set your mind to something, you're bound to be let down. I set out to report the next Watergate scandal. I dreamed of carrying on the traditions that had broken down barriers in the Sixties, and Seventies. And instead, my stories were buried under gossip reports and stories about crack houses as soon as the magazine realized that sensationalism sold more copies.' She shook her head, and stared out over the city. Damien stared with her, feeling the parallels form themselves. ' I began to feel the pressures of the pocketbook around the time this place started serving thousand dollar dinners to investors, and paying the staff per diem. And I hoped to ignore it, thinking that I was being naive and idealistic. So when I was getting more attention when I wrote about the president's colonoscopy than I did about his apathy towards the AIDS epidemic, I knew that I had crossed the line from tolerant into compliant. I won't say that I sold-out, but I won't disagree if you did, David.' He smiled, and decided that correcting her would be pointless. ' I'm sorry to here that...um...Missus...or...I'm sorry...' ' Beth. Call me Beth.' ' Beth. Yes, well, I'm sorry to hear that you must look back on your career that way. I understand, though. Now I'm not sure if you know anything about me, and I doubt that you care, but I started out as a small-time wrestler in an incredibly poor company back in Mexico. The only reason why we had a gym is because the US government built it to get first dibs at the oil they thought we had. But when OPEC offered it cheaper, we were left with a gym, a half-built school, and a promise of American jobs that never reached us. But anyway, that is where I started. Wrestling for a group of my friends and neighbors, and pulling in some tourist dollars. So I'd be a hypocrite when I complain about commercialism in wrestling. If I tried to tell you that I did it for the love of the sport, I'd be lying. But I'd say that when I was 15, with nothing else in my life to look forward to, it was easier to forget the times I was told to take an extra high fall to keep the tourists happy.' Beth, with a surprising clarity, watched Damien speak. So he continued. ' So as I brought in the audiences, I was given more freedom. And mistaking freedom for...you know, freedom, I left the company and signed with another company across the world, in Japan. Now, technically, I had the love and support of my old company, but that was only because I left them with a marketing icon. The Los Locos Lucha Libre could therefor be known as the Birthplace of Damien Cruz. And it lasted long enough to make the owners retirement a comfortable one. As far as the other talent, they ended up working for American corporations who realized that oil was cheaper in Iraq, but Mexican wages were right under their noses.' Damien, finding his flow, continued. ' So in Asia, I whored myself out to any company that would get closer to the best promoters in the country. And while I was chasing the dream, which I had indoctrinated myself as being personal growth, I was making headlines, drawing crowds, and giving the Neon Temple a loyal following. By that time, I had forgotten the yen, and started to immerse myself in the culture of wrestling. I idolized the men who seemed to care the least about who was watching, only focusing on the fact that they were being watched. They put on bloodbaths in the name of a distant honor that they carried with them from the antiquity of Bushido, and the Warrior's Code. Man, I swallowed it up. I lived the lifestyle of a man who had been put on earth to evolve the Human race in battle. Simple living, simple thoughts, single-minded determination.' The woman, sipping her drink, nodded, prompted Damien to continue. ' And then, when I had fully convinced myself that this was the life I wanted to live for the rest of the time I had here on earth, I spanned the globe and found the American audience. I held in my heart this contempt for an industry that had marketed what I held to be sacred. I resented the ways my personal philosophies were being sold to Hollywood as fodder. I truly hated the business aspect of the wrestling industry. But, like I had years before, I used that resentment to fuel my actions and kill the two birds with the single stone. It worked, and I assumed it worked too well. You know, I get really angry when I think of the ideals I butchered just to sleep at night.' Damien looked at Beth, who was staring at him, her eyes fixed on his face. She smiled, and spoke gently. ' That is the biggest load of horseshit I've ever seen spoken with a straight face.' Damien thought she was joking, and she expanded her premise. ' My heroes were Bernstein and Woodward, your heroes were The Iron Sheik and George The fucking Animal Steel. What you do is simple. You are a product, a piece of merchandising. And no matter how much you convinced yourself that you were anything more, you knew all along that you weren't. So you can tell me this heartbreaking story of how you sold your soul to the Corporate Devil, but it's basically just a story of the opiate wearing off. Dammit, Son, are you so dense? Your job is simple. You are no different from the bearded lady or the three-inch pianist.' A slight tug of defensiveness made Damien lose his humor, but she hardly noticed. ' Now whatever you have to tell yourself to get through the night is all that you need to worry about. So whatever existentialist dilemma you're having, is overdone. Now if you were blowing sailors down at the docks for smack, I'd suggest you think about your deficits, but you're gaining fame, wealth, and prestige for doing what some kid in Jersey is doing for bragging rights. Now, unless I'm misunderstanding you, you're bitching because you can no longer justify some baronial philosophy in the face of shameless fame-suckling. Get over it, kid, because that is the way of the world. If that bothers you, if you can no longer make peace with that, change the world or change yourself.' Beth's words struck Damien in the gut. Any and all pithy retort he had hoped would come became tar on his palette. She spoke it, between puffs of thick blue smoke, and never lost the smile. The Last Son of Lucha libre remembered that he had a few hours between his second match, and realized that not being in the gym because he had to do a press junket was not the grand design of a vast Military-Industrial Complex. He was at that stupid gathering because he knew that increased visibility meant increased public interest which lead to increased ratings for PRIME. And what was good for PRIME was good for him. It was hard to have lofty moral standings when his appearance was bookended by Tour Girls Down Under and Naughty Home Video with Jesse Jane. ' Maybe you're right, Beth. Maybe I have lied myself into my own angst. So what, in your infinite insight, is the solution.' ' Lie some more. Lie and hide, and pretend until the questions stop coming. Step into the ring and give the public what they paid their kids' college tuition to see. If they want blood, give them blood; if they want fire and barbed-wire, give them thumb tacks and bullwhips too, for good measure. Ram down their throats the product. There is no room for subtly or nuance in this culture. Don't stop trying. If there was such a thing as too much, we wouldn't have ten years of reality shows about fucked up people doing fucked up things on camera to escape the numbness of being fucked up and invisible. Whoever you step into that ring with this week, make them earn the spotlight. This isn't 12th Century Japan, and your not battling to maintain the Shogunate. You're working for ratings, sponsors, and the overall contentment of being overexposed. So embrace it. And when you feel the bile in the back of your throat stinging, that's your precious naivete trying to make you remember the things you once held dear. Swallow it. Push it back into the depths where it belongs, and smile wide for the cameras, Honey.' Damien watched a jolly old lady turn into a seething, jagged woman who has seen enough dead heroes to fill a stadium. And what he thought would repulse him drew him in. He understood. Not what she was saying being true, but what was true in what she was saying. No. He didn't agree with her advice, but knew good advice when he was given it. Even though she eventually fell silent, and he excused himself with a handshake and a thank you, she stayed with him. As the plane landed, and the stadium sat on the horizon, he took the last of the gems from the mud, and understood just what he was left to do.
