A Slight Problem

20 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20/2/07 ‘Is this thing on?’ Derek Lanson hurried back from the VCR recorder that was humming along silently and sat down beside his partner Lucy Pinder. They looked at each other briefly, inhaling sharply between their teeth giving off that so very irritating noise that only your grandparents can truly appreciate. ‘Ready?’ The question seemed almost posed with dread and Lucy shrugged her shoulders, ignoring her partners attempt to build the moment. It was only a stupid tape from some concerned employer. She didn’t get this whole fuss anyway, it was only a dumb wrestler. A dumb, steroid pumping, pill-popping freak that would probably die before she had kids. She sighed at the notion, absent-mindedly patting her stomach. ‘Someday platito…someday…’ ‘I’m sorry what?’ Lucy jolted to her senses. She hoped that Derek hadn’t heard her pet name for…well she’d rather not think about that. Quickly, she turned to him and put her hand on his shoulder. ‘Nothing. Let’s just get this tape out of the way shall we?’ The audible gulp from Derek let Lucy know that it had worked. Ah men, so predictable. Without another word, Derek flicked on the screen to where they could see the beginning of the tape. With a simple click the tape started. *** PRIME 2007 The title emblazoned itself across the screen before the sound hurtled to life. ‘Well folks we’re coming to you live from sunny, sunny Melbourne! And today is a special day as it’s Valentine’s Day, but not only that it’s the arrival of PRIME and its wrestlers to put on another action packed show for its fans!’ The excitable interviewer from Australia twanged away in front of the camera, jabbering and flailing his arms. It’s notable that his voice is never quiet even when he’s throwing the microphone away from himself. He continues to ramble on about useless facts about Valentine’s Day but really Derek and Lucy aren’t listening. Largely because they’ve turned the sound down. ‘Do you reckon we can fast forward this bit?’ ‘I’m not sure, let me check when the incident they’re referring to happens.’ Derek flicks through an effortlessly organised ring binder and finds his laminated sheet for the interesting facts about this particular tape. Thumbing his way down (and idly pushing his spectacles back up his nose with his other thumb) he finds the pertinent information and looks back at Lucy. ‘Yeah we can flick forward about two minutes and thirty-two seconds Lucy.’ ‘You know, I love it when you’re in your perfectionist mood Derek.’ They both sniggered to themselves in the dimly lit room, the pig like snorts filling the silence. For a moment the two nerds locked eyes before they turned back to the screen, slaves to their own particular compulsions. The haphazard, jagged lines of the fast forward filled the screen and our intrepid reporter now appears like he’s something out of the Animaniacs. Body parts elastically extending themselves and pointing to different supposedly interesting sights and sounds. Finally he settles (if you can call it that) and turns away from the camera to look on at something in the background. ‘Ah here it is.’ As the sound blooms back into the room we can hear that people are gathering round to watch an exhibition match between one of the local heroes, the Dingo Warrior. Clad in fetching lycra fluorescent pants and tassles on his shoulders, he’s a sad mix of ice dancer and eighties rockstar. The kids seem to love him though as he’s shaking the ropes and doing some sort of tribal ritual in the centre of the ring. In contrast though we see that his opponent is in no mood for this blatant chicanery. Standing across from him stands one of the PRIME wrestlers, Jack Murphy, an Irishman who’s been around the block a couple of times over the years. Currently he’s not getting along with the fans too well, and to be honest he’s not doing much to endear himself. Largely because he just hocked up a whole load of phlegm and spat it square between some kid’s eyes. The tape stops. ‘Did you see that?’ ‘Yeah I did Derek, so what?’ Derek shook his head incredulously at the throwaway remark from Lucy. She seemed unfazed from this shenanigans from a grown man. Before he answers her, the thumb comes up to the nose again. ‘So what? The man just spat in a kid’s face! This alone should get him fined by his employers.’ ‘Well, according to your notes that’s not what his employers want us to look at.’ ‘How DARE you take my notes! Give them back!’ Frantically Derek snatches the ring binder back from Lucy who’d been nonchalantly flicking the pages and turning a couple carelessly. Lucy knew full well that Derek couldn’t stand uneven edges. That’s why she did it; by getting him flustered she’d be able to maybe get his mind off his OCD for a change. ‘Let’s just keep watching alright?’ ‘Ok, but no more touching my binder!’ The tape returned to normal. The reaction to the spit is pretty raucous as there’s an awful lot of drunk men in the audience. Murphy sneers wholeheartedly, happy with the reaction and clicks his neck. The Dingo Warrior is right up in his face and it takes the referee to pull him back so that they can get the match started. Right from the bell things get interesting as the Dingo Warrior has already gassed himself into oblivion. The antics have totally shaken him up and his attempts to deal some damage to Murphy are feeble and pathetic at best. He easily takes control of the pathetic local hero and begins to bring the pain. All seems normal for the first few minutes and both Derek and Lucy seem confused. Then the blood begins to flow from the Dingo Warrior’s head. Then it pumps. Then it seems to be spurting. Both nerds lean closer to the screen, intrigued and abhorred by the sight at once. The most interesting moment of it all is the look on Murphy’s face. He’s relishing the moment. The crowd is embittered by the experience. Kids are crying, men are churning in their seats and you can tell that things are looking ominous. Some of the security are fingering their batons nervously and it all looks like things are going to go down the tubes. Of course, Murphy doesn’t seem to be trying to halt the emotions. The third elbow right on the head which finally, mercifully knocks the Dingo Warrior unconscious is the breaking point. Slowly, some of the crowd amble out of their seats and begin to rush the ring. ‘Hey what are you doing?’ ‘I don’t know, I just reckoned that slow motion would hype the tension of the moment.’ ‘Good call Derek.’ Pleased with his tangible addition, Derek inches closer to the screen, juking and jiving along with the men. The two nerds reel and convulse as elbows and closed fists impact against their faces, guts and teeth. Several times we see teeth flying in different directions to their owners. ‘Maybe this is what they were getting at?’ ‘Apparently not Lucy. It’s after this that gets the crowd going.’ ‘Really?’ Derek looked at her gravely. ‘Really.’ The crowd continues to roar but no more assailants try to bring their own retribution for their fallen hero. The referee is long gone and the security are still standing nervously, unsure how to act. Murphy now moves and stands directly on top of the Dingo Warrior. He begins to talk to the crowd, loudly but just out of the pitch of the camera. Whatever is said it really gets the crowd going again and you can see some of the men beginning to stir. The speech is short but the actions following are what we finally get to see as the problem. Murphy sets up the Dingo Warrior’s jaw by the edge of the apron. With a big boot looming, the tape abruptly halts before we get to see what happens. The two nerds look to each other, jaws gaping wide open. ‘So…’ ‘So…’ ‘I think we got the gist there.’ ‘Yep.’ ‘Yep.’ Lucy was the first one to broach the subject. ‘What do we tell them?’ ‘I don’t know Lucy, I don’t know.’ *** ‘Murphy!’ The dull prison cell flickered into a bit more light as one of the guards came to the door. I stood, knowing that PRIME had finally either decided to get me out in time for the show, or was going to leave me to rot. ‘Yeah?’ ‘You got two hours to get to the arena.’ A wave of sadness came over me as I was dealt the reprieve. As the door opened I had to ask the question. ‘What happened to that chump?’ ‘You mean the Dingo Warrior? He’s going to need reconstructive surgery but he’ll survive. You’re lucky Murphy because he decided to not press charges.’ ‘Why?’ ‘I guess someone needs you wrestling.’ I didn’t understand, the opening of the gym had opened the possibility of retirement but the contract I’d signed with PRIME apparently was pretty airtight. Unless of course I did something so stupid that they’d have to get rid of me. Today was merely that, a way out. But it hadn’t worked. Someone wanted me wrestling which was becoming less and less important to me with the prospect of passing it on to the next generation. So who the hell wanted that? It was something I would ponder for some time, but for now I had to get to the arena. *** Back in the dim lit room Derek and Lucy pulled the tape back out of the recorder. A phone began to ring in one of their bags. Lucy pulled out her cell phone and answered it. ‘Hello?’ There was a long pause before Lucy gave the feelings of her and Derek to whomever it was on the phone. Another pause happened before Lucy hung up and put the phone away. ‘Well? What did he say Lucy?’ ‘Burn the tape.’ ‘What? I thought…’ ‘We thought wrong Derek. Apparently they wanted to make sure that this tape was what had occurred and now that we’ve witnessed it, it has to be erased. Permanently.’ ‘What about us?’ ‘We have to keep our mouths shut. Or else.’
