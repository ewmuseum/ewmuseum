We Bathe in Starshine

22 August 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/22/10 Four months is a hell of a thing. May-June-July-August, one big yesterday and you can write it down, crumple it up, stick it in a bottle and sail it over to whom it may concern. Yesterday, I was an investigative journo-phile, imbedded as it were, sifting through layers of a man I wish I never met but whom fate dictated I should know on an intimate level. Before that, I was a scene writer in Orlando. Today? Today I have 38 missed calls (mostly from home), text messages wondering if I’m alright, how my finances are holding up, if I’m ever coming back, etc. To those, I say: "I’m unavailable at the moment." This is Los Angeles, where we’re all unavailable at the moment. Today began a little while ago (four A.M. is a hell of a thing) and it’s a new day, I decided. First thing’s first though: sleep. So I knock on the door across the hall. "Spaaaaaades" he says, eyes half closed as he nods his head back. I don’t know who he is, except that I’ve seen him around and he’s got lots and lots of RX. "Hey man, you got any oxys? I can’t sleep." Oxycontin, generically known as Oxycodone, is a time released single ingredient opioid prescribed for the relief of moderate to severe pain. Approved by the FDA in 1995, Oxycontin fast became the best-selling pain relief narcotic in the U.S. I like Oxycontin. "Oh yeah, I got those. Come on in man. Como Esta!" I think he meant to say "Mi casa e su casa" but that’s alright, I just wanted my pills. "Sorry to bother you. Castor gave me two bottles and I’m out." We stepped over the sleeping bodies strewn across the floor and the edge of the futon, making it to the dresser. He pulled it open and his hands began to shuffle. "Oh noooo it’s totally cool. How many you need? I got twenties only." "Yeah that’s the same as I had. Two should be good." He popped a couple of pink tablets into his palm and dropped them into mine. I went to my wallet but he immediately put up his hands, raised his eyebrows and closed his eyes. "Dude…dude…" This…isn’t important. What is, is that 20 minutes later I was fast asleep. I dreamt of rented out Bentleys pulling up to the Roosevelt to greet TMZ flashbulbs; traffic on Sunset; sommeliers in restaurants off Mulholland and the incessant angry chirping of a blue-tooth tirade. For a second I think they’re yelling at me, but soon I know better. Hollywood is a face you’ll never know, and a conversation you’ll never understand. … From where I was standing, I could see a bare ass in front of a couch with two legs draped over hands. The male was thrusting into his female co-star, while she sucked off his buddy kneeling next to her. Of all the sets Castor had personally designed, this was the least elaborate. It was a living room setting, and both men were wearing construction boots, reflective vests, and yellow hard hats. But this was no highway project… "MMMM yes! Harder! HARDER! I SAID HARDER!" Christ, how hard does she want it? Well, he sped up anyway while she jerked the other dude off. "Is that hard enough baby?" he asked, so sure of himself. "No, HARDER! It’s NEVER enough!" "CUT!" yelled the director, owner of the studio, and the man who’d be vying for a Universal Title in another week. Castor had been on edge for some time now. "Do I have to hold your fucking hand? This is a pathetic effort, and I’m embarrassed FOR YOU. Look at them, Kendra. They are municipal road workers on lunch break whom you’ve lured to your apartment. Taunt them, let them know they’re beneath you. Don’t just yell ‘HARDER!’ Get income specific. Make it a class struggle. Remember, daddy’s little spoiled angel shouldn’t be bringing roughneck blue collared men back home. Remind them their place!" Castor was vigorous in his approach. "K, got it." The starlet knew her place "Alright then. In three, two, one…ACTION!" They continued their penile assaulted on her extremities. Eh, there’s no fancy way to describe it. They were double-teaming the shit out of her, like two corn skewers trapping a juicy cob. She couldn’t have been older than 20, but man…what a pro. "Mmmmm, that’s right, fuck me you minimum wage motherfucker! PAY your taxes!" Castor looked on, satisfied but undeniably detached. Then again, there wasn’t much artistic beauty to behold on set. "Give her the jackrabbit, dude. The jackrabbit!" yelled the guy whose dick she was sucking. The performing male began to spasm from his nether region, which is indeed referred to as the jackrabbit. "Yes! Mmmmm yes! Push it! Drill me where I’m filthy!" "LIKE A MAD BADGER I WILL!" one of them yelled before switching holes. Suddenly, Castor jumped out of his seat. He took his notes, tossed them into the air, and abruptly left the set. Personally, I couldn’t tell you what it’s like to be standing there with your dick in your hand while the director has a tantrum off set, but Jake could. Out of all Castor’s sycophants, Jake was the most devoted, and whatever he was getting paid to wait for his cinematic master while dressed like a construction worker almost assuredly wasn’t enough. In fact, I’m half sure none of them are making more than an intern’s salary, which is to say their bi-weekly paychecks are in the triple digits. Shit, my paycheck is only triple digits, and nobody works longer hours than I do. This is a fact I’d be reminded of shortly when the text came through my phone: "Basement bar, 10 mins. –Castor" Teary eyed and rubbing his temples, Jake caught a glimpse of me packing my things and preparing to leave, wanted to know where I was going. "Bossman needs a drink and a song. I’m taking notes, as usual." "He likes you, Spades. Tell him I’m sorry," the young man said. Carrier pigeon of pornstar apologies, trapped in the Hollywood grind armed with a pen to write my way out. I guess dreams really do come true. … By profession I am a journalist, not a biographer, though I’m learning the more I go that it’s virtually an identical trade. A strong commonality is that biographers are paid to listen as much as they are to write. For the subject, the entire process is cathartic when not pure indulgence, two luxuries that form the many-headed hydra of Castor V. Strife Productions. "So when you die, who gets all this stuff?" I said amongst bar scenery including mannequins dressed in 1930’s fashion aesthetic, stuffed baboons, antique furniture and Dutch cuckoo clocks. The layout of the room was more than a bit confusing, as if Feng Shui or interior planning were antithesis to the lounge that time forgot, and a portal was simply conjured wherefrom eclectic belongings were vomited forth. Flashing a smile as he drank from a rocks glass, the tortured artist (as he describes himself) appreciated my humor. "Nobody. They are going to bury me like Ramses III, like a Pharaoh, encased beneath a tomb surrounded by all my things," said Castor. "Oh yeah? Won’t the U.S. Government estate tax have something to say about that?" "Congress let it expire this year. Believe me, in the shape I’m in, Spades, such things are of great concern to me." "What, you got cancer or something?" He took a sip of his drink and waved me off. "No, I’m talking finance. We’re living in the End Times of good taste, and the world is in need of a final production. THIS," he motioned his hand around the room, "is that production. But it requires money, constant investment, and as I said...these are the End Times. A good investor is hard to find." "What about Devin Shakur?" He looked down, smiled, picked up his drink to finish, and casually slid the empty glass a few inches down the bar. "We’ll see about that. I have the upper hand at Colossus, make no mistake. But a foe is most dangerous when he makes the choice you think he doesn’t have. I’ve made a career out of giving up the high ground, if only to avail myself the element of surprise. Though I don’t believe Olsig to be so cunning." "Profits hard to come by these days? I mean…you have a loyal user-base." I could see the conversation was doing it’s best to wipe the glow of starshine off the director’s face, and his frustration was readable. "Profit? You think I profit from what we do in that studio? Even with the low brow entertainment, I’m still only making enough to break even. My Avant Garde and Film Noir offerings have set me back considerably, even during good times, and production costs are increasing all the time. Sponsorship is what keeps me above water. Sponsorship Spades, as it were in better centuries when an artist was appreciated for his work." "And the Pharaoh’s tomb? What’s that cost to decorate?" Castor paused to stare at me, a clear indication of offense. Ever the journalist, it’s second nature for me to call out intellectual dishonesty. After a few seconds, he answered. "Gifts," he said. "What about the Third Reich memorabilia? You told me what they cost and everything-" he interrupted me quickly. "And the money was a gift I used to purchase them. What’s the difference?" Castor stood up and walked around the bar and stopped before the stairwell. "I’m not paying you to investigate me, I’M PAYING YOU TO LEARN! And to watch! To listen, and to write. And as long as you’re paid on time, then you write what I tell you to write!" Furious, he picked up his a rocks glass from the bar and flung it against the wall where it shattered into pieces around the room. I ducked for cover. "And you WILL NOT ask about my finances any longer!" What does a stuffed baboon cost these days, anyhow? I suppose that’s a topic for another day. Up the stairs went my financial sponsor, my boss, and supplier of the little pills I use to, as the song says, outrace the speed of pain. … Dinner was a self-excursion, but a group desert was spontaneous. There I was, surrounded by the lot of them, beautiful in their indignity, but maybe it takes an eye like mine to see it. Maybe it’s because I was becoming one of them that I was able to. The table featured a spread of grapes, ice cream, fruit, honey, chocolate dipping sauce and bottles of cheap wine. The conversation, usually more dormant, was loud and continuous. Castor sat across from me, which was out of character for him. With the guest of honor present (in his own home, a guest of honor, if you can believe it), all were eager to impress. Jennifer, a strawberry blonde and also North Hollywood trash, spoke loudest. "Spades, weren’t you telling me today you were considering going back to Florida?" she asked- no, interrogated. Did I tell her that? Shit, I can’t remember, but if I didn’t she’s a goddamn mind reader. "It’s a possibility, sure." Castor was paying particular attention. "You didn’t make it sound like just a possibility the other day. So what’s in Florida?" This bitch was starting to get on my nerves. "Besides home? Friends? Family? Work? Nothing." That triggered some laughter. "You don’t have friends here? Or work?" "In this town? Does anybody?" More laughter. I was on a roll. "That’s true. He’s right about that," said Castor, and now Jen was red-faced. "OK yeah, but you guys know what I mean. I feel like, at least, HERE, I have a home. And I have work. Not only that, but I think this town is the reason I enjoy being away because it’s not home, with all the limitations. This town is limitless. I love it!" Jake chimed in as well. "I love it too. I came here with a dream, and thanks to you guys and especially you Castor, it’s come true. I’m surrounded by stars, and I’m working film. What could be better?" And another one, her name I forget. "It’s just the lie they tell us, our generation, since the time we were born. Go to college, get a degree, make more money than your parents. Except, MY parents are still working, full benefits, no college education. Brother too. I’m supposed to be the one with unlimited income potential, yet I’m out of work. The baby boomers wanna work until they’re 80, and where does that leave us? Seriously." "Yeah, and when you get the office job, believe me, it’s still like living with your parents," said Jake. "I did the whole office thing, and they’re always talking down to you like a child. It’s like no matter where we go, no matter what we do, we never grow up." "We weren’t allowed to grow up," she responded. Castor looked across at me, unable to contain an impish grin. He knew what they were getting at, and he liked it. He fed on it. Hollywood runs on it, like fuel. For one dream to come true, ten others need to be crushed. "It’s all about determination. If you want it bad enough, and you’ve got the talent, the right people will eventually see you," said the guy down the table from me, two seats. "And you believe that bullshit?" said Jake. "Work hard and succeed? Yeah I do believe it. That’s why I’m here." "It’s different here. Out there, out in the ‘REAL WORLD’ it’s not like they told you it was. You’re determined? OK great, send your resume to our office through the website and we’ll file it with the rest of them, and if you’re LUCKY we’ll call you back." The tension at the table was mounting, causing one girl to leave. "Yeah, that’s the idea. They call the ones who meet the qualifications, what’s wrong with that?" "How about the fact that EVERYBODY has a fucking degree now?" said Jennifer. "Do they? " said Castor, more taunting than quizzically. "Oh yeah, definitely. Everybody’s going to college now, ‘cause everybody’s parents told them to. We all have a piece of paper that says we’re a genius, and the lucky ones are getting paid much less than what they’re worth in a workplace that shuns individuality." "I’m sorry, but I disagree" said the guy sitting two seats down. "Good, disagree. But that’s the way things are. You’re only 20, you have no idea. Trust me when I say that’s the way it is." "And you’re 23, who the fuck cares? You’re a nihilist at 23, and I’m an optimist at 20. Will I be this bitter in three years? Hooray!" "Hooray!" Jen said sarcastically. "Yeah, fucking hooray," he said back. "Dude, grow the fuck up," Jake said in her defense. "Nah I’m leaving. Have fun guys. I’m here to act, not to make friends," and he exited the table. "Why not both at the same time?" said a female voice, to laughter. Jen turned to me, and almost implored as she spoke. "When you’re in this town, and pretty much anywhere in LA, trust me, you’re way ahead of everybody else." I bet. "Castor’s giving us a big opportunity here." "That’s true, I am," he said smiling. Castor, ever smug, was confident in his brood’s slavishness. They adored him, they followed him, they took abuse and abused one another all in his name. To them, he was elegance personified, and opportunity afforded. In this house, Castor Strife was more than a director; as far as they’re concerned, he’s the Pied Piper of the Peter Pan Generation. They could never grow up, these children. As long as he had use for them, they would always be young. And the look he gave me told me I was no different. I was one of them; one of his.
