A Beauty to Behold

31 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 31/10/2006 The rain beat down heavily upon the streets below and the rooftops above, with each droplet dancing against each surface in quick succession, putting on a performance that some would consider a beauty to behold. Jonathan Winters did not subscribe to that point of view and such poetry was lost on him. Especially when he hadn’t even noticed it was raining. Stood upon the building top, propped up against the cold metal leg of a brightly lit advertising board, he glared into the window in his immediate eye line of the building opposite, observing the scene before him and committing every detail to memory. He allowed a shiver, but neglected to follow up the involuntary action with a subsequent thought. He had neither the time nor the inclination. Up there, the night air had a cutting bitterness to it already, long before the heavens opened, but now the temperature had dropped another few degrees and Winters found it increasingly difficult to ignore. Neither the weather nor his comfort were paramount issues to him at present -- his mind was focused on something else entirely. Revenge. A word and a desire so foreign to Winters, the combined meaning was something he was still desperately trying to come to terms with. A strange notion to accept, he was willing to admit, but a concern he needed to deal with nonetheless. Why have I never encountered a desire for revenge before? It was an important question, but he already had the answer long before the subject even arose. Strange as it may seem, he didn’t care enough about anything in this world to have the issue raised before now. Before Aeryn. Before she was shot. The wrestling industry? Yes. It was important to him, but it wasn’t nearly important to pursue such a destructive desire. Winters knew entering the business, precisely how cutthroat it was and how to remain in control at all times. Defeated? He wasn’t good enough. He needed to be better. Attacked backstage? He wasn’t aware enough. He needed to be more aware of his surroundings. Stolen title? He wasn’t alert he enough. He needed to be alert at all times. Tricked? He wasn’t smart enough. He needed think things through more. What begets revenge? Anger. Winters had been angry before, but not nearly angry enough to tantalise his untapped thirst for revenge. When Tony Gamble stole the PRIME Internet title from him, did Winters go searching for the title to get it back? Yes. Could this be categorised as wanting revenge, wanting to see justice done and the belt back in his hands? Possibly. But no action was taken and it was left to the appropriate grudge match later on in the month. The intent was there, but the necessary acts of accompanying violence were avoided. Winters refused to count it. He wasn’t nearly angry enough back then as he was now. If he truly wanted to take his revenge that day, then he would’ve done and nothing could’ve stopped him. Just like nothing will stop him today. Revenge is a dish best served cold. Such an elegant phrase has been dubbed by the media to describe such acts of revenge, fictional or otherwise. It suggests that emotional detachment is often best used to reap such sweet vengeance and Jonathan Winters is the very embodiment of emotional detachment. Through the window, in the building across the way from him, he could see the pack of bodyguards and soldiers thin out some and go about their various tasks. The target was there, ready and waiting. He’d shot Winters’ girl, Aeryn, in the shoulder and he was going to pay for that. Winters pushed himself up from the metal leg of the advertising board and muttered to himself, “I’m going to enjoy this.” [^[^[^[^[^[^[^] Consciousness came and Michael opened his eyes. “Wha-where?” His moved side to side rapidly, desperately trying to grasp his current surroundings. But he couldn’t see a thing. Apart from the small bulb that loomed ominously overhead that only illuminated the immediate area, the room was pitch black. He struggled in the chair, but he couldn’t pry himself free. He felt the metal dig into his wrists and legs. He was chained to his seat. “What the fuck...?” “Some say the pursuit of revenge is a misguided way of exacting the concept of justice,” the voice suddenly whispered. It was soft -- a murmur at best and Michael couldn’t tell where it was coming from. “Who the hell are you?” Michael asked, frantically trying to remember just how in the hell he got there. “Where am I?” Whoever was behind the voice ignored him. “The same people suggest that such a quest will do better to hinder, rather than help.” Jonathan Winters stepped into the light. “But that’s not a theory I subscribe to.” Michael’s eyes widened and his face contorted in horror as the memories of the past couple of hours came flooding back to him. “You...! Don’t hurt me. I didn‘t mean to hurt...” “Three hours ago, you shot a woman in the shoulder,” Winters interrupted. “Now she’s receiving treatment in the hospital for the wound.” “But I didn’t mean to...” “You didn’t mean to shoot her?” Winters asked. “Don’t lie to me.” Michael remained silent, allowing the memories to come flooding back to him. The incident with the woman from Stakiri’s camp poking her damn nose around in his affairs. He had to shoot her, she’d seen too much. Then, an hour later his bodyguards falling to the floor all around him, knocked unconscious in quick succession. His retaliatory gunfire hit nothing but thin air, as the individual stalked closer and finally swooped in for the kill and now he was here at the mercy of his captors. His bowels emptied themselves, accordingly. “I’m going to be suitably blunt with you, Michael.” Winters fished inside his jacket pocket and retrieved a mobile phone, holding it up pointedly, for Michael to see. “One of my colleagues will be ringing me from the hospital within the next few seconds in order to update me on your victims condition. Your fate will be decided on the basis of that call and it will be an eye for an eye.” “What do mean?” Micheal questioned, too scared to think straight. “I mean, if Aeryn dies I will kill you. If she loses an arm, I will hack off yours,” Winters stopped. “You get the idea.” “B-bu-but you can’t!” Michael screamed. “Sure, I can,” Winters replied. “I have money!” Michael tried to bribe. “So do I.” Came the reply. Michael remained silent. There would be no reasoning with his captor, he could see it in his deranged eyes. He closed his eyes and hoped for the best. The cell phone rang. Winters hit the receive button. Michael watched on from his shackled position, trying to judge his captor’s facial expression. Winters smiled and Michael’s heart skipped a beat. “Good news,” Winters told him. “She’s stable and she’s going to be fine.” Winters glared off into the dark, at either side of him. “Boys...” Then two built guys emerged from the darkness at either side of Michael. “Come on guys,” he told them, “get me out of here...” The two of them didn’t even attempt to release him. Instead, they reached inside of their leather jackets and produced silenced pistols. They jammed the barrel’s into each of Michael’s temples. His eyes widened and panic gripped him. “But you said...” “I said I wouldn’t kill or maim you.” Winters nodded at the two others. “They don’t work for me. They work for Mr. Stakiri and he has other ideas for you it seems.” With that Winters left the room. He didn’t care enough about it, to hang around and watch what happened next.
