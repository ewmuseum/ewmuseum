Dying on a Mountain

24 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 24, 2009 Jack Conway stared down the mountain, happy for the cold, against his face, as well as the fact that he was wearing good gear to keep the rest of himself warm. The skis under his feet, as well as the poles in his hand, were rented - there weren't exactly many chances to go skiing when you traveled to a lot of warm locations - but the feel of them was familiar. Unlike the idiotic things that Luis had tried to have him do before - skateboarding, for example - this was one athletic activity with which he was familiar. It was the only annual vacation with his family which had always been guaranteed. Sometimes, there would be two vacations if his dad did especially well in his job - a Disney park, maybe a trip to New England, and one time there was a great week in Mexico - but skiing in the Colorado or Washington Rockies was always promised. There was not yet a year in which he had failed to visit at least one mountain and enjoy the slopes. This time, however, was exceedingly different. If nothing else, he had never been to this particular resort. The slopes were unfamiliar, the routes unknown, and the moguls shaped in a way that he could not predict. Then, of course, there was Luis. Currently, he was trying (quite unsuccessfully) to ride a snowboard. He was treating it like a surf board, and that was exactly how NOT to use it. As Jack looked to his right, Jack saw a red and black blur blast by him, shouting as it did so. "coooOOOOONWAAAAYYYYY, I'M GOING TO KILLLllll youuuuu...!" Jack was amazed that Gallegos had managed to get up such speed when he had spent the entire morning face planting, landing on his butt, or, in one memorable moment, catching an edge in such a way that it caused him to roll end over end for about three rotations. As if the thought had an impact on the quantum reality of Gallegos' current plight, Luis failed in attempting to turn his face up the mountain to slow down, and wound up spinning vertically before falling face first and sliding about five feet. Jack sighed and pushed off on his poles, heading down to where Luis was trying to get on to his back. The snowboard was proving a bit ungainly (he had chosen a board bigger than he was because the only other ones available were kids' boards and womens' boards, and as Luis put it, "I'm not a kid or a chick, dude,"), but after a bit of a struggle, Gallegos was once again looking skyward. What greeted Luis' eyes was Jack's inverted face, staring down. "Still think you don't need lessons?" "I've got a helmet." "You're not the one that's going to get injured, the way you're going." "Dude, the mountain is empty." "Still, do you see why I didn't want to do those street rides?" Gallegos shook his head and said, "Bro, could you sit down? It's kinda' weird, looking at you like this." "Sure, I guess." Conway went to Gallegos' side and carefully sat, his legs twisted so his skis were on the left side. "I still can't believe your dad was in to this stuff. I mean, with all that rhetoric you know, isn't this a bit... aristocratic?" "Skiing isn't the most accessible of activities, I'll admit, but my dad was more in to the Winter Olympics than the Summer Olympics. Go figure he'd get us all to learn how to do this." "One more thing - I'm used to riding a board. What am I doing wrong?" "Simple. When you surf, what you're riding is behind you. When you snowboard, what you're riding is in front of you. Change your steering accordingly, and you'll be okay." Luis sighed. "Do you think we're ever going to find something besides wrestling that we can both actually enjoy?" "Magazines?" "Sports Illustrated." "Newsweek." "Maybe books?" "Charles Dickens." "Tom Clancy." "Hold up - you like those governmental style thrillers?" "Sure, Jack. It's not like I have to follow the stereotypical beach bum. I mean, sure, I've got the hair cut, but that's just because it works with my face. And the clothes are just a fashion." "And the books are insanely long in paper back." "They're insanely long anyway, but at least he fleshes out his characters." The two shrugged. "Speaking of characters - do you think Fusenhoff is going to be sober enough to be a reliable partner?" "Luis, if you can't trust a co-worker, who can you trust?" "Hey, two of our so-called 'Co-workers' tried to beat the hell out of us." "Point taken. But we'll give them theirs. Want to try riding again?" "What other choice do I have?" Jack shrugged as both men got to their feet, jumped so their respective snow riding gear was pointed down hill, and continued to ride. They may have a conflict coming soon, but for now, all of their worries are forgotten in the joy of speed and white powder.
