Prime

5 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/5/05 Let's be completely honest for a second here. I'm not a hero. I never said I was. See…a hero…there is indisputable proof that what they are doing is just. What I am doing…what I am becoming…I am not a hero. But I am also not a villain. I don't have evil intentions. What I do I feel is right. I took Nova's 5-Star Championship belt. I've played mental chess with him since we crossed paths. But to consider me the indisputable bad guy? Nova wanted the cuffs, and it was a slap in Melissa's face. She isn't a wrestler by any stretch of the imagination, but she is an athlete who does not need sympathy from anyone. Nobody told Amy Campbell or Karina Wolfenden that they would willingly handcuff their hands behind their back when they squared off. He's a pothead. A man who gets off on drug culture. On his weak wit. Hell, every person I've come across in PRIME talks like they graduated from Harvard or Yale. I've got Deville hamming it up about whatever miscellaneous bullshit he wants to say. I got Nova acting as if somehow my pursuit of him is unjust. Everyone talks about how I beat a beat down and broken Karina. She's still competing. I mean, she was in the same ring as Kodiak Vic Creed, Alias and Craig Miles at All-Star Wrestling's End Game. From what I've heard, she just had a contest with this kid named Andy Sharp. And she was jumping around and kicking faces in and everyone cheered and blah. Blah. Blah. Envy. That's what it is. Nova isn't in her league. Deville will lose to her. Was she beat up when we squared off? Maybe. I didn't challenge her with her 'knee condition' in mind. We went damn near a half-hour and simply put I outlasted her. She was tired at the end of the match, made a mistake, and I capitalized. Did she put a hurt on me? You're damn right she did. But in the end, it wasn't her knee that made her lose. It was endurance. Will. Her knee wasn't in the Gridlock. The back and neck I worked over for an entire match are the area's targeted by that move. I take pride in that victory as I saw into the Lupine I hunted for so long. I saw I was truly wrong. I saw not someone oblivious, but someone so free of the simple trappings of emotional chivalry that her actions were not the vile things I thought they were. Do I detest what she has done? Yes. But anytime I look at a bottle of alcohol, I get a front and center view of my own past. I detested myself. I do not hate her. I respect her. And dare say, I love her. And that is why she is my rival. She is not sports entertainment. She has flash, but her essence deserves it. She is not a gimmick. She isn't some fake ass Mafioso cut out. She isn't a stoner with an Associates and a bad case of the munchies. She's the Lupine. A true wolf. A big bad fernis wolf just like Randalls. They are ageless, and they will wander through the desert wasteland of life unabated for eons when they pass from this mortal coil. She is Valhalla bound. So the next time I get that thrown in my face, I'll just absorb it. It's jealousy. It's knowing that if the chips fall as they should, that you will not lord anything over me. I have submitted. And I have been pinned. But the war hasn't started. It hasn't even begun. And when it does, Brandon Youngblood isn't going to go hot and cold from week to week. He isn't going to even put up with people spinning their masturbatory speak made to entertain an audience. He's simply going to hold the Universal Title high above his head. You see, you two have helped me more than you could ever realize. I wanted to just turn around and curl up into a ball because of this sick feeling in my gut. This sharp pain like a knife stabbed through my stomach. It was almost crippling, this disgust I felt. But I got over it. And I'm the better man for it. What does not kill you makes you stronger. You'll both regret not killing me when we meet in combat. Jon Kano is simply going to be taking your place this week. *** The high elevation played a large factor. His usual stamina had completely failed him as he rolled to his stomach, trying to collect his breath. However, every deep breath he took brought in nothing. He desperately needed oxygen to stave off the onslaught from Jon Kano, being that his usual grinding mat style of wrestling never came to fruition. It was less than a minute into the contest and every blow landed on him felt like a meteor crashing into a planet without an atmospheric barrier to lessen the blow. He could taste iron in his mouth as he flopped on the mat like a fish out of water. The Kanospear had surely broken his ribs, and he was suffering from internal bleeding. He tried to collect himself, tried taking in that one desperate soothing inhale of oxygen. But it never came. Brandon could feel his entire body tense up wildly. He felt constricted. Every nerve ending was on fire, shooting signals of unadulterated pain throughout his body. And he began to pick himself up. He looked around, trying his best to do a double take. He wanted to make sure that he was not knocked into another plane of existence. Everything he saw had a glaze to it, a sort of window-like appearance that heightened his sense of fear. Blood began to flow from his mouth to his lips and his chin. Their faces were twisted, snarling. They had red orbs for eyes and jagged teeth. Their skin was snow white, with an reptilian texture to their skin. They were barely even representative of this plane of reality. And then the feet began to stomp in a harrowing manner, like a war drum being pounded just before an old Indian tribe would run their streams red with the blood of those who opposed. And their chant grew louder and louder. 'YOU'RE A FRAUD!' 'YOU'RE A FRAUD!' 'YOU'RE A FRAUD!' His entire body shook wildly as he looked around, trying to find Jon Kano. He could not see him, it was almost as if he simply vanished into thin air. He tried to find him, tried to listen for him but any noise was impeded by the fact that the crowd was deafeningly loud. And then out of nowhere, he felt sick. He was lifted high and tight, landing on his head on the mat. He could feel something within him snap. His neck. His neck was broken. His entire body went numb. And then, he heard the mat be slapped three times. And then, everything slid into focus. Standing above him was Jon Kano, his hand raised in victory. And then he heard the beat of The Watcher by Dr. Dre. He was shell shocked. But he could not move. He could not react. Kano had just beaten him and taken away his career. He could hear the crowd now, laughing wildly. 'YOU'RE A FRAUD!' 'YOU'RE A FRAUD!' 'YOU'RE A FRAUD!' He looked up, the big Pepsi ball above him beginning to turn. They were laughing. His career was over. And he could not even move. And then, the Pepsi ball fell, and his world became black. And then, he snapped back into reality. He looked around, looking out the window of the plane as it coasted ever closer to Denver. He tried to shake it off, but the nightmare he just had played upon all of his fears. Not necessarily about Kano, but in general. He'd seen all the work put in to defeat Karina blow up in his face with back to back loses against two of his most vocal antagonists. Angelo Deville and Nova. The evening sky had turned an effervescent apricot hue. It was just short of breathtaking. But as he drew ever closer to his destination, he could only think about what a loss in mere hours would mean. He would become irrelevant. He'd be destroyed. He'd never be able to recover and capture the Universal Championship he so desperately coveted. The altitude changes would be interesting. Big men usually can not move, and from what Brandon understood about Kano was he was the same. He had trouble dealing with leaner, more aggressive opponents. It would be difficult to ground him, but he could do so. It was not an impossibility like it was when he faced Deacon for the fWo World Heavyweight Championship. That match, Youngblood had faced the antithesis of Jon Kano. Deacon was a pillar of perfection, a man who could wrestle with anyone be they big or small. He was a legendary figure who did not use his background as a crutch to remain important in the ever changing world of professional wrestling. Kano might have been good in his day, but Brandon understood that his day had come and gone long ago. He was like Willie Mays in a Mets uniform, struggling to field fly balls that in his prime for the Giants that he would have snagged from behind his back. He was like Michael Jordan with the Wizards, completely unable to deal with the prowess of more skilled and hungry athletes like Kobe Bryant and Tracy McGrady, still think he could lead the team to victory by taking a majority of the shots even with talented younger players like Richard Hamilton or Jerry Stackhouse around him. He was like Deion Sanders, a former All-World cornerback and kick returner that now struggles mightily against the best the NFL has to offer, acting as a gimmick player for a defense that was strong even without him. PRIME was like the Ravens defense. The organization had the Karina Wolfenden's, the Angelo Deville's, the Killean Sirrajin's that were recognized as elite. The Ravens have players like Ray Lewis and Chris McAllister that were elite, and then guys like Terrell Suggs who were on the cusp. Those would be the Nova's, the Easton's. But Jon Kano was like Deion. He did not even deserve to lace up the shoes of the wrestlers in PRIME now. He had no business in a wrestling ring. It was outlasting your shelf life, what Kano was doing. He was a shell of his former self, trying desperately to find that spark that would bring him back into the hearts of wrestling fans. But the fact of the matter was that the spark had disappeared. If Kano would reclaim his place, it would be after basically rebuilding himself. Jon Kano was only twenty-nine years of age and had already seen his prime. Maybe that was the true fear. Angelo Deville was still a great wrestler, but at the age of twenty-seven and with the miles put on his body in his days, maybe he had seen his prime. Or maybe he hadn't. Ric Flair was said to be done going into 1989, and he had perhaps the greatest years of his career from 1989 on. With Angelo, there was still a sense that he was far from finished. He had not been exposed. Kano looked lost out there at times. Brandon was just a shade younger than both of them, and had not really been wrestling as long as either of them. He did not take professional wrestling super seriously until he got his big break, at times just going out and giving a half hearted effort. And looking back, Brandon could only shake his head. All people reach a peak and then slide. When it happened to Brandon, he worried that those ill spent years drinking and half assing would catch up to him. He worried that at any time, his neck might snap, a knee might get blown out, and his career might be over. He'd just started to truly come into his own as a wrestler, and he was just turning into an elite talent. But would his growth end soon? Would it end five years from now, after reaching a pinnacle? The ghosts of the past, the thoughts of the drinking and the depression, they plagued his fears. Jon Kano was an example. He hoped he'd reach the top and bow out gracefully, not cling to relevance in a world he could not compete in. Brandon had done the amateur thing. He'd won the 1999 National Championship in amateur wrestling (let's suspend belief for one moment here, the writer understands that Tim Hartung in reality won the title, but this is a fantasy world and WE. CAN. BEND. HISTORY.), having worked his butt of for more than eight years as an amateur from high school to the college ranks. He'd never reached a prime there, and he was worried that he would reach it in the professional ranks and lose it just as quickly. He looked out the window, trying to ease his somewhat troubled mind. He finally got over it, rationalizing that as long as he gave everything he had, he would be alright. The purity of the sport, as much as he would yell and scream that it was to not be sports entertainment, was not true. It was about fulfilling potential. Nova did drugs, stunting him from reaching his peak level. Angelo Deville...it was his own ego. If he could simply get over himself, that would be the difference between Universal Championship contender and calling broken down retired men to face off against. Karina, Brandon saw within her in that battle that she was not holding herself back. And that is why he now actually respected her. Jon Kano, he was far from his top. He was shorting himself by smoking, and god knows what else. As the plane made its way towards Denver, Brandon looked over the Rocky Mountains. A tower of rock, arduous to climb. It was like the mountain that was PRIME. Brandon needed to focus. He had Kano, and no one else tonight in the ring. And while it was not necessarily a huge match up for Brandon, the thought was that even the small steps count. One can take for granted those small steps and die. And Brandon... Brandon wasn't going to let Kano have that satisfaction.
