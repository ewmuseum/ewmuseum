Ghost Story. (Jewel in the Crown Round One vs. Joshua Kosidlo)

20 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 20th, 2007 "Hey, wake up." The tired, drunken visage of Wade Elliott rose from his rugged, folded arms. His brown drifter’s hat hung from his head at an obscure angle while his piercing blue eyes made a strong attempt to come into focus. A skinny, balding bartender was shaking Wade’s broad shoulder. "Mmmmgffhuh?" groaned Wade, planting his hands on the bar to push himself into an upright position. "It’s closin’ time, bub," said the bartender, clearing bottles and glasses from the mahogany table. Wade blinked, wavering on his stool. "Real fuckin’ courteous’ve ya," he slurred, adjusting his hat. "Any bar over’n ‘Merica woulda kicked my ass out *hic* hours ago. "Toronto’s got a pretty good reputation for kindness." Wade looked around, he was the only patron left. Employees of "The Maple Leaf" were sweeping up cans and wiping up spills. "Whadda I owe ya?" asked Wade. The bartender pointed to a bill on the table in front of the Bad Dog. Wade mouthed an "oh," and tipped his hat. He squinted at the tiny piece of paper, still confused with the Canadian exchange rate. He finally succumbed and pulled out his wallet, grabbing a handful of Canadian bills and slapping them on the table. "Oh, that looks like a bit much, sir…" Wade waved off the bartender, who smiled and nodded. "S’alright," said Wade, standing to a pair of shaky legs. "They pay me pretty good." "Thanks," said the bartender. "Go get some sleep." The Drifter tipped his hat once more before turning away, staggering out the door and into the cold Toronto street. The bartender gathered Elliott’s plentiful pay, which caused his brown eyes to widen. "For your sake, you’d better hope they pay as well as you say." * * * * * * * "REAL FINE FUCKIN’ ‘STABLISHMENT YA’LL GOT ‘ERE!" The Southern Sparkplug roared into the Toronto night, gripping his hip flask tightly. He occasionally bounced off a building as he made a nice zig-zag pattern down a sidewalk. "Pffft," he spat. "I don’t care WHAT they say ‘bout ya’ll canucks! Fuckin’ fine people if yer askin’ me!" Wade stumbled into the road, which caused a passing car to swerve into the opposite lane, blaring its horn at the drunken ‘Bama Bruiser. "’XCEPT FER YOU!" Wade lifted a middle finger into the air as he staggered back onto the sidewalk. His pace slowed, leaving one big steel-toed boot-print in the snow after another, his head hanging low, the stench of bourbon spewing through his bushy brown goatee. "Don’t make no fuckin’ sense," he mumbled, unscrewing his flask’s cap. "It’s they’re fuckin’ fault! Puttin’ on all kindsa pressure n’ shit that I don’t fuckin’ need!" Lucky for Wade, the road led to a park area, with plenty of wonderful benches to sit on. He veered toward them, thudding through the thin layer of snow that covered the park. "I show up jus’ to make some fuckin’ money s’all, and all’ve a sudden they’re screaming ‘he’s the Big Dog! He’s the next big thing! He’s gon’ be KING someday!" Wade spun on a heel, which nearly sent him down to the ground. "WELL I AIN’T FUCKIN’ KING!" he bellowed into the city. "I AIN’T THE FUCKIN’ ‘BIG DOG!’ I’M THE BAD DOG! N’ THE BAD DOG AIN’T NO FUCKIN’ KING!" Wade spat at nothing in particular, and turned back toward the park, a terrible scowl strewn across his face. "Shit, the Bad Dog cain’t even keep the fuckin’ Intense belt. The Bad Dog gits beat by some four foot fuckin’ midget! Out-fuckin’-smarted! Kings don’t git out-smarted by fuckin’ midgets! He approached a bench and flopped onto its cast iron seat, oblivious to the snow under his ass. He threw back another mouthful of bourbon, dragging his forearm across his mouth with a grimace. "Fuckin’ Jewel in the Crown. Big fuckin’ cluster fuck s’all it is. Buncha cunts lookin’ fer a crown. There ain’t no fuckin’ crown! Whadda they want from me? Win a couple rounds jus’ t’git put on my fuckin’ back by some piece’a shit?" Wade’s head rolled to the side at an awkward angle, resting on his shoulder while the bourbon in his flask spilled to the snow. "Fuck em’…" "Wake the fuck up." Wade jolted into consciousness, eyes wide as he looked left and right. He nearly leaped from his seat to see an older man sitting next to him. The man sported a wide, round brim, western style hat, and his stoic face yielded a long handlebar mustache that hung past his chin. He was clad in leather cowboy boots and an elaborate vest, surrounded by a heavy cloak. A red sash sat around his waist, and long, curly locks of gray hair hung down to his shoulders. Elliott took a moment to calm down as the stranger locked eyes with him. "Where in the hell’d you come from?" "What’s the fuckin’ matter with you?" the man scolded with a rough, gravely voice, swiftly changing the subject. "Huh? Ain’t nothi…" "Just fuckin’ lookit ya." "Lissin’ here," started Wade. "I ain’t needin’ some cunt slappin’ my wrist ‘bout my drinkin’ problem…" "I ain’t talkin’ ‘bout yer drinkin’, Wade," the man interrupted. "I’m talkin’ ‘bout yer god damn blubberin’ like a fuckin’ baby!" Wade squinted. "How’d’ya know my fuckin’ name?" he asked. "I know every fuckin’ thing ‘bout you, Elliott." Wade cocked his head, confused. "Who the fuck are you?" The man chuckled, showing the first emotion not linked to anger in their conversation. He extended a hand. "Bill Hickock," he said, gripping Wade’s hand and giving it a shake. Wade squinted again. "As in Wild Bill Hickock?" "That’s what they call me." "Ain’t you dead?" "As a fuckin’ doornail." Wade shifted away, rubbing his temples. "Sir," said Wade. "I’m far too drunk to lissin’ to some fella tell me he’s a fuckin’ ghost." "What’s the matter, Wade? Don’t believe in ghost stories?" "Nope, can’t say I fuckin’ do." "Best start believin’." Wade sat up again, taking a deep breath. "Alright, say you are Wild Bill Hickock, an you are a fuckin’ ghost come to talk to me. If that’s all true, then what in the fuckin’ hell d’ya want with me?" "I’m here to set ya straight, boy," said Hickock, his stern tone resurfacing. "I’m here to tell ya to stop actin’ like a fuckin’ cunt an’ get a grip on yerself." The Drifter shook his head. "I told ya, I’m fine. Just need to do some drinkin’ s’all…" "Do you remember yer life before you started wrestlin’? Bill interrupted. "Pretty god damn shitty if ya ask me." "You don’t know shit about my fuckin’ life," snapped Wade, baring his teeth like the dog he was. "Yes I fuckin’ do," shot Bill. "I know more ‘bout yer life than you do." Elliott didn’t reply. They locked eyes, Wade’s keen blue orbs matching the light blue marbles of Hickock’s. "You got a real god damn lucky break, Wade. PRIME handed you a contract that’ll make you comfortable fer the rest of yer life. But what’s more fuckin’ important is what ya’ve done while ya been there. You’ve taken the damn company by storm, beat down a lot’ve cunts, made a big name fer yerself." "Right, an’ lost my belt to some gum-flappin’, five foot-nothin’ piece-a shit," Wade interjected. "Ya let ‘im git the best’ve ya, Wade." "I ain’t the top dog, Bill…" "But you could be!" More silence, tension tight like a fishing line pulled by a blue whale. "Why me?" asked Wade, breaking the silence. "What makes Wade Elliott so fuckin’ special to git a fuckin’ pep talk from Wild Bill Hickcock?" "There’s a whole lot more to this ghost story than I’m to tell ya right now, Wade," was Bill’s reply. The Blue Collar Brawler looked around. It was still dark, and the park was empty. "I ain’t goin’ anywhere," said Wade, ready to listen. "You’ve got a job to do," said Bill. "You’ve got a throne to claim." Wade could not respond. Speechless. Locked onto Wild Bill’s sharp blue eyes. Hickcock leaned in closer to Wade. "Yer dog’s waitin’ in yer truck," he whispered. * * * * * * * Wade jolted. Bill was gone. He whipped his head in every direction, but there was no sign of the legendary law man. He calmed down, eyes squinted in confusion. "Was I dreamin’…?" Something snapped in his head. "Shit, Angus." Wade jumped to his feet, snatched up his empty flask from the snow and ran off into the night, in what he hoped was the direction of the bar he left his truck at. "My truck best be sittin’ where I left it," Wade huffed, taking long strides down the sidewalk. "If anythin’ happens to Angus, Lindsay’ll kill me fer sure."
