Blueprint

31 January 2007

Date: 31/1/07 The flickering of the dull television screen could be heard from outside the room, at least that’s what Jessica had told me before. She mentioned often that whenever she could hear the crackling of the sepia-toned videos she wouldn’t even bother to stop by. She knew I wouldn’t be awake enough to give her a conversation. She knew that I wouldn’t be lively enough to even give her a smile. More than this though, she knew that she didn’t want to watch her father’s matches, not this soon anyway. It had been well over nine months since Dan ‘The Firebrand’ O’Grady had passed away suddenly, leaving a world in which he truly had given his all. Leaving his only daughter to his trusted pupil probably had seemed appropriate for him as no matter how much he tried to hide it, it was obvious that he hoped someday we would get together and pass on the wrestling name; to start a family that he would have been happy to be a patriarch of. Perhaps it could still happen but it would be some time before it would come to pass. First of all, I had to establish the name which was easier said than done. When many people think of the great wrestling families, they’ll remind you of the Von Erichs, the Harts, the Funks. Others still would remark on the McMahon family, but probably not in the same vein as the others. Because the others were special in their production of talent and sheer force of will. So many of them have had lasting impacts on the industry whether it be through the Harts and their successful way of changing the mindsets of generations of wrestlers, generations that are still impacting, or whether it be the Von Erichs who are a hushed reminder of the excess and the problems that can be associated with the industry. One cannot pass by a veteran of the business in conversation and the Erichs not being brought up. It is a shame, but one that would be held for a long time to come. It was these kinds of names that inspired and enervated so many people into their chosen professions, seeing how successful one could be if they put their mind to it. Many flocked to the Dungeon to test their mettle and see if they would be able to make it. Few did, but the few were always something special. So what had Firebrand’s name got that could be built upon? What could be the standing stone that people would flock to and instantly identify with. With this in mind I had turned to the room. It’s called the room in this house because it was the one place where neither Jessica or myself could go. It was the last bastion of peace and quite that Firebrand had had in his own house. He had been the kind of wrestler that generally couldn’t get away from other people because he was likeable. People honestly liked him as a person. So, now being able to retire and relax, this room became so important. It was the place he could go to to empty his thoughts and forget about the world. For some time we didn’t dare go into the room after his passing. It just didn’t feel right to walk over the threshold and cross into forbidden territory. It took a couple of months for me to do it, but eventually I plucked up the courage. When the door cracked open from my tentative push there was a wave of revelation. The room had turned out to be his own shrine to everything wrestling. Wall to wall there were pictures and snippets of newspaper clippings, famous title wins that go across the globe from Flair taking the WWF title at the Royal Rumble right to Misawa getting the torch passed to him in Japan. It covered so many different aspects of the wrestling culture that it was simply amazing for me to see. I hadn’t even gotten to the tape library either. Dan was always a student of the game and his collection of old matches was simply phenomenal. Whilst it is easier these days to find copies of matches from Japan, back then it was a lot harder. The fact that he could potentially pull out a match from the seventies in All-Japan Pro-Wrestling was immense. It was incredible to go through the tape library. Ever since I had walked in there I had consumed myself with immersing in the culture of the time period that I most felt affinity for now. Despite being more active and involved in the late eighties and nineties right up till now, the late seventies had been the learning period, the time with which I had formed my mindset, for better or worse. This evening was no different from my ritual to the previous month or two. I sat down right around six o’clock in the evening, picked out a tape and watched it right through, taking in all the action and waiting for something to spark the imagination. Most of the tapes were generally labelled by Dan and so I literally had no idea what I was going to get beyond a year. This one was around the mid 70’s. The crackling hiss of old footage sparked to life and I inched closer to the screen to get a better look. It wasn’t the best quality by a long mile but it was good enough to have an idea of what was going on. No commentary but I had gotten used to it by now. As I stared into the screen the two wrestlers were being announced. The two wrestlers were very familiar and my mouth hung slack just a little. Conscious of the match I searched for a single person in the crowd: me. *** March 1975, east coast of America Awash in a backstage of men who weren’t strictly taller than me but were superior in terms of wisdom of experience I treaded carefully. I looked for Dan but he was busy preparing and wouldn’t be in the mood for disturbance. So I stood and waited, patiently in the corner trying not to knock into anyone. Of course with my impressive agility (in other words; none) I crashed right into someone who was behind me. He was roughly the same size as me, but bulkier and far more imposing than my own 220lb weight. The bulk made him look like a monster, someone that you couldn’t cross. As I gazed into his face though you could see a gentleness that totally set him apart from other hulking, brooding men in the locker room. Also, he was Japanese. He smiled at me and bowed quickly, a gesture that seemed very honourable. Without thinking I just repeated his action and nodded to him, our silent conversation ending as quickly as it had started. He continued on quietly and left me to my thoughts. A couple of minutes later, Dan finally was ready to come and over and talk to me. He seemed excited for the evening, a change from the usual humdrum of the carnival style events. He said it was because he didn’t get paid enough for them, I knew it was because there was simply no competition for him usually. There was just a simple buzz about him that let everyone know he was in full mood for fighting. ‘Jack, I’ve something to tell you.’ ‘Sure Dan, what is it?’ His arm snaked over my shoulder and brought me closer to him, almost like a huddle for American football, a game I still couldn’t understand. He brought his face right up against mine so that he could address me directly. ‘Tonight, I want you to be front row so that you can watch this match you hear? It’s important.’ ‘Why?’ ‘Because tonight I’m going to teach you a lesson.’ I groaned inwardly. Dan as a taskmaster was relentless, but he usually laid off on nights when he had to work. This one came as a surprise. ‘Another one? And now?’ ‘It’ll all make sense to you when you watch the match son. It’s a very important lesson.’ ‘About what?’ ‘Tradition boy. Tradition.’ *** Present Day The words reverberated around the room, seeping through them as if they were just spoken again. Maybe for a moment I could hear his voice clearly, emanating from right behind me. I turned but nothing. The memory had worked instantly though as I rediscovered a lesson taught so long ago, but so valuable even to this day. Wrestling is a business built on tradition. Whatever you do in this business, there is always a precedent for what is going to happen. It is tradition to work over the crowds with gimmicks and roles so that the audience can better ingratiate themselves into the whole ordeal. It is tradition that there’s going to be hazing and goading as new people enter into the industry, to test them and see if they’re gonna make it. Above all, there is tradition with the respect and honour you should give your opponent, irrespective of where they’re from and what they do. Wrestling is a tradition and one that hasn’t been followed properly for well over ten years by now. It’s typically considered an entertainment freak show nowadays. It had lost its way, because back then it was all about tradition. On that tape was a match that probably few would have known was so special at the time other than those within the industry. You can tell that by the sheer amount of wrestlers who were in the crowd, watching intently. The give and take of the match perhaps is a little slow and isn’t up to the usual standard by these two but the emotion and excellence of technique is there in all its glory. It didn’t matter that the two people in the ring were of different nationalities (Dan fought the Japanese man I bumped into), because they were able to connect on a more instinctual level. That was the way wrestling was meant to be; not the way it is now. Thank you Dan, you’ve reminded me why I’m in this business. I rushed out of the room and Jessica was in the hallway, looking a little confused as I frantically ran up to her. ‘I finally figured it out. Finally…I know what I have to do.’ ‘What? What are you talking about?’ ‘I have a message to bring to the people Jess and it starts in PRIME. Right now though I’ve got to start work on opening the gym.’ ‘But I thought you were giving up on that?’ ‘I’ve no time! I’ll talk to you soon!’ I raced out of the house and into the darkness of the night. Thank you Dan…I won’t ever forget what you’ve done for me. And now I’m going to pay you back, the only way I know how. Wrestling.
