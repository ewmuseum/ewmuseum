To have to lose ~><>…<><~ The newspaper crinkled dejectedly as Deville refolded it along the creases and slapped it atop the pile to his right, simultaneously reaching down the other side of his leather lounging chair to scoop up the next edition. Among his many leisurely hobbies, reading the world’s news ranked fairly low on the list, between doing logic puzzles and playing the piano, but it was still an undertaking he engaged daily. Glossing over the centre photograph on the cover, he scanned the headlines and sub-headlines, scouring for something to catch his eye. With media from all over the world delivered to his doorstep fresh from the presses, The Deville never lacked for material to read, and with the different spins imposed by various governing agencies he was treated to a prismatic view of otherwise one-dimensional and colourless stories. The stories themselves weren’t always what interested Deville. Most of the time he was looking for something deeper, the unspoken messages hidden within. A lot of them had them, some placed, some unintentional, but he liked to believe he never missed them, and delighted in figuring them out. Sometimes more could be divined from what was said than what wasn’t. Of course, sometimes the stories were just what they appeared, sensationalist trash meant to entertain the unwashed, rife with some contrived point to inspire sympathy or angst. Usually that’s all the front page was. Finding this one no different, Deville felt an uneasiness stirring as his eyes drifted across the room, to the shovel in the corner, the shovel he’d carried around with him for days as a constant reminder and test of will. He still didn’t feel quite right with the absence of the four pounds strapped against his chest. Since his mid-teens he’d carried a gun at almost all times, with the rare exception coming when he was forced to travel commercially or he donned his tights to hit the ring. Even in his home he was perpetually strapped, knowing that danger struck when it was least expected, knowing that his myriad levels of security would never prove impenetrable. It wasn’t a feeling of nakedness, but more of withdrawal, an unceasing desperation and sense of dread. He had so many enemies. Too many people wanted to kill him, would revel in his demise. Too many people ached to make a headline out of his name, out of rubbing him out. Did the guns stop that, or perpetrate it? He shifted uncomfortably, snapping the paper open. Either way, he was done with that chapter of his life. Resisting the urge to scratch his arm, to provide temporary relief from the nettles that had grown in his blood, refusing to test the dexterity of his trigger finger, he glared at the shovel. He didn’t need the guns. The shovel was a reminder of that, a testament to his evolution. It made him feel good to have it so near and not be obligated to use it, to dig up what he’d left buried. He wasn’t a slave to them, they were simply a tool he had used to survive. He didn’t need them. That sort of need was for the weak and confused. Deville quirked an eyebrow as the dulcimer of his front door banged its deep melody, announcing a visitor. His first instinct was to reach for his gun, earning the shovel another glare before his hand could move. He should have been informed of any arrival by the guards stationed at the outer gate. While it disturbed him that he hadn’t, it also mollified him, as whoever was rapping was obviously someone his sentinels knew he trusted. That raised his hackles all over again. The list of people he trusted was shorter than that of those who trusted him. From that list, he could count the number who would drop by without advance warning on his middle finger. Setting the paper on his knee, he entwined his hands and waited, brooding. He didn’t need to hear the commotion to know it was Karina, nor the adamant protests of his butler delcaring that “Mr. Deville doesn’t wish to be disturbed. Especially by you.” He winced as he heard a vase shatter, then shook his head musingly. Any doubt it was Karina had been firmly put to rest. More protests from his butler were lost in his wonderment at her presence, at her re-emergence now after weeks of silence. Something else clattered but didn’t break, then he heard her hurrying up the stairs, extolling her mantra with a feeling that belied the words. “I don’t care.” That was Karina in a nutshell, The Deville told himself bitterly, standing up to turn the chair. He scolded himself immediately for the thought, knowing it false; it wasn’t that she didn’t care, it was that she had trouble distinguishing what she cared about from one moment to the next, and why. He felt childish turning the chair, like an infant covering its own eyes to hide, but screw her. She could come in and find his back turned, be forced to swallow her pride and march around to face him if she wanted to talk. She’d put him through enough of a hell to suffer that minor inconvenience without complaint. He shifted. She was in the room. He knew it by the sudden flaring in his stomach, by the clenching of his throat, the heat in his cheeks and the tightening of his brow. Whatever game she was up to now, he didn’t care to know about it. He wanted her like nothing else, but he’d come to terms with her individual solidarity, the fact that she could love only herself. To her he was simply an instrument of amusement, a challenge to overcome. She looked at him as she did a virgin mountaintop, a thrilling journey to traverse and leave behind, scarred by her passing. She enjoyed it, even liked it, but love never entered the picture. “What is it now, Karina?” he demanded tiredly, putting a cutting edge to her name instead of caressing it as he yearned to do. “Just turn around,” she pleaded, impossibly sounding meek. “Please.” Why? Breaking my heart isn’t as sporting when you can’t see it stain my face? He’d never heard her sound so sorrowful, so lost and forlorn, but he couldn’t chase the acidity from his mind, and it leaked onto his tongue. “Why?” he barked gruffly. “It’s hardly like saying things to my face ever makes a difference with you.” He wished she’d leave, but his piqued ears picked up no trace of movement, just a sharp inhalation of breath. Squinting into the window for her reflection, he found only the muggy outdoors, and cursed himself for calling up a demure image in his mind. Demure? Demure?! Karina was allergic to being demure. “Please,” she repeated, nearly begging. Angelo licked his lips and bit his lip, certain his ears were playing tricks on him. She sounded on the verge of tears. “No, really,” she quickly recovered, lightening the subject to put herself at ease, “that funny little guy you have around the place didn’t let me wipe my feet, so this isn’t doing your carpet much good.” He chuckled. Damn him, he chuckled. She’d broken through, shattered his defences, as he’d always known she would. Bracing for the inevitable rejection he rose stately to his feet, forcing a growl into his voice for “Fine,” before turning to face her. She was closer than he’d expected, a bare few feet away, so near he could smell her. Not perfume, not shampoo, but her, rich and vibrant. Her eyes were wide and focussed, steeled and determined. She dropped down to a knee. It was like a punch to the gut. He stretched as if to hold her up, reaching to make her stop. “What? No. Kari--” “Marry me.” He knew she was going to say it as soon as she started to bend, but actually hearing it . . . Fireworks erupted in his chest, a symphony of joyous explosion. In his wildest dreams he’d hoped it, planned it, wished that it could be. His breathing quickened, his hands started to shake. He reached out to take her hands, to lift her and kiss her, mouth opening in a smile to agree. And then their eyes locked, his searching and hopeful, hers doe-like and desperate. Fireworks became a hammer, mercilessly crushing. It’s always in the eyes, swirling beneath the surface, where the real story lies. He took her hands as she licked her lips, lifting her to her feet, then pushed her back by the shoulders, feeling his spirit die inside him as confusion marred her unparalleled defenceless beauty. Though bewilderment clouded it, what he’d seen still persevered, certainty grounded in ambiguity. She wasn’t doing it because she loved him, or because she wanted to be wed; it was some strange twist of need, something she felt obligated to do. As always, it wasn’t about him, or them, but her, about some duplicitous emptiness inside her, some ill-begotten sense of duty. He wanted to say yes, or to ask why and set them free, but his duty outweighed hers, grounded in altruistic liberty. With his heart peeling like an onion, he shook his head and answered the only way he could. “No.” ~><>…<><~ Integrity. Honesty. Forthrightness. Individually, those things will take people a long way. Combined they’ll deliver anyone to their dreams and desires, moving them smoothly to the top of the mountain with nary a hitch along the way. Lacking them, you’ll be lucky if you fall before you reach the halfway point. If they’re missing, you better have something strong to take their place, to bullshit your way to the shallow peaks that interrupt the depressing valleys of your life. Cunning will work. So will opportunism. And Youngblood, Campbell, you two have those things in spades. You invented an entire life for yourself, Youngblood, and you’re almost cunning enough to pull off the façade. Were it not for pesky details like Tim Hartung beating a guy not named Brandon Youngblood for the NCAA Championship, were it not for the wonders of modern technology cataloguing your contradictory rants from week to week, you might actually have a case for believability. I won’t pretend you’re devoid of skill, or that you never wrestled as an amateur, but the accolades you claim are as honest and tangible as your victory over Cross. Peel just one layer of the deceit away and you’re left with the pathetic truth. So you wrestled as an amateur through high school, maybe even won yourself a plastic medal or two. Big deal. So did I. So did half the roster. So did a bunch of burnout kids who never had the audacity to rob Hartung of his success, content with the reality of their life as it stands, not as they wish it was. This isn’t amateur hour anymore, Youngblood, and the people you’re facing off with aren’t undergoing voice changes or worrying about the shit their parents are gonna give them for draining the gas out of the car. This is PRIME, a professional environment built around the best in the universe. It’s nothing like slapping on a half-nelson and rolling chubby kids for points; it’s the real deal. No punches pulled, no quarter given, no three minute rounds. It’s about the better wrestler, the more rounded fighter, period. It’s about having the skill and the integrity to be a champion. A real champion. Go ahead and claim that that’s you until you’re blue in the face. It won’t faze me in the slightest and it certainly won’t fool anyone. Like NCAA history, Youngblood, the tapes don’t lie, and anyone suffering from Alzheimer’s who cares to check it out can witness you fall to me not once, not twice, but three times. They can watch me break the Gridlock with a low-blow, then lock you into The Soprano for the tap-out. No amount of cunning linguistics can change that fact, and despite your most heartfelt efforts, no amount of bullshit can bury it. I know you’re hoping to steal another win this week, but I’m afraid I can’t accommodate you like Cross did. As much as I’d like to put you over for the sheer sake of putting you over, to give you a shine you can’t show up to give yourself, as you’ve already inarguably discovered, I don’t quit. This week, cunning or not, you’re out of luck, and the opportunity for thievery simply won’t manifest itself. You’re not as bad as he is, Campbell, and really, you’re a paper-depth shy of being beneath my notice. With that in mind, I’ll keep this short and sweet. You fluked a win while I was in the vicinity of the ring. Not over me directly, but in a match I was involved in. As you’d remember if you weren’t loopy and strung out when it happened, I had Dragon Silver ready to tap when Baltasar broke through the cage and broke the hold, dragging me out of the ring to brawl. While I was roughing him up, teaching him a lesson for his insolence, you snuck back in, planted Tony Rolo, and stole the victory. Bravo. I have a golf clap here for you somewhere. This week, I’m sad to report, you won’t have the same opportunity. There’s no weak link to give me the loss, there’s no irate Mexican ready to thwart my plans, and better still, there’s not a no-disqualification proviso to gift you legal interference against me. Flying under the radar might have worked briefly in OSW and the fWo, and it might have won you a clusterfuck here in PRIME, but this week is about earning the victory, about making your opponent lose, not about celebrating the meekly mediocre. Your cunning and opportunism may have bought you a spot in the Tropical Turmoil match, but it isn’t going to pin Nova’s shoulders to the mat, and it sure as hell isn’t going to put me down for the count. Opportunism and cunning have brought you both to a peak. You’re both coming off victories, and hell, I can respect that. I mean . . . Aimz, you beat a metrosexual pretty-boy from Hollywood with less pro-wrestling experience than Youngblood has NCAA. And Youngblood, you beat a coming-out-of-retirement never-was by the sole virtue of his renewed retirement. Can I do the golf clap twice during the same speech? This week, as if Nova or I wasn’t bad enough, you two have to face the both of us . . . together. The man who abhors druggies and non-technical wrestlers teamed with the incurable addict who doesn’t even know enough wrestling holds to settle on a finisher. Does it bother you, Aimz, that the man you’re looking to tag is the same man who uses you as a feature in his rants about how the business went wrong? Does it bother you, Brandon, that the woman you have to tag is the scourge of the sport, the pretender that’s ruining the legacy of legends like yourself and Benoit? What am I saying? Of course not. I’m sure you two will magically become friends by match-time. Between your lies, Youngblood, and your promiscuity, Campbell, I know you’ll come together and make a mockery of reality. Hell, I bet Youngblood will even artlessly retract all of his words and let you know how he’s always respected your abilities, Aimz. It’s the definition of cunning opportunism. And it’s about to take its ritualistic fall.
