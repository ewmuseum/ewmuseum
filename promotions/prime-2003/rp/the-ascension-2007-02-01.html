The Ascension

1 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 1st, 2007 This is Vance King of Channel 4 News, on location in Washington, with a special report. Only moments ago, the Secretary of State declared a state of emergency in the U.S., citing the worldwide epidemic that has sprouted chaos and riots across the globe. One panicked official stated that, quote, "it's as if the very laws of science have been turned upside and nature itself has declared war on mankind", unqoute. Hysteria, madness and violence have plunged the world into complete pandemonium and, here in the United States, National Guard units have been deployed to suppress the looting and riots in every major city. No one is yet sure how or why this happened. All that is known is that around approxiately midnight a large portion of the world's population was spontaneously and almost collectively gripped in a state of violent mania that has continued nearly nine hours now. Psychologists have said it appears to be some form of hypnotically induced psychosis, though what could have caused such a phenomenon is anyone's guess at this time. Agencies across the globe are already looking into it, and while a small tremor was reported last evening in the Pacific Ocean, few believe such a thing could be the actual source of any kind of neurotic epidemic. Most of those affected are said to be normal, healthy people, though exceptionally violent surges are being reported in asylums and similar institutions the world over... * * * * January 17, 2007 Rourke burst through the doors of Weirsburg asylum, his face drenched and hair matted by the icy rain. A cold sweat has also broken out above his brow and now trickled down above his eye. He took a step back, his eyes darting back and forth between open cell doors. Some of the inmates were still inside, many huddled in a fetal position and refusing to move, obviously terrified of something. Others wandered the halls, glassy eyed and catatonic as they mumbled in some strange, alien tongue that Rourke had never heard before. The doctor made a beeline for Nester's cell, finding it empty. No Devin and no demon. He neither knew nor cared about the location of the latter and secretly prayed it had slipped back into what blackened dimension it had slithered out of; Nester was a different story. He'd find him, but first he needed to get back to the office. He hoped Leonard was still alright. Alarms rang deafeningly all across the compound, shadows drowned out by wildly spinning red halos in every corridor. Most of those he ran across staggered about in a stupor, all muttering the same guttural gibberish as they stumbled about and completely unresponsive to anything Doctor Rourke did. Dead eyes stared back into his own as he tried to break them from their catatonia, and with a heavy heart Rourke knew it was all futile. He approached the office in a cold sweat, eyes darting frantically for any sign of Leonard Bulwark. The sight beyond the threshold did nothing to alleviate his concerns over his friend's health. Papers were strewn about the room, crumpled and half-shredded, while a jagged hole was punched through the computer monitor. The swirling lights of the corridor alarm illuminated the office contents in a blood-red hue as he walked foward, fearing the worst. He had seen the stupefied and dazzled of the asylum population walking the halls like zombies, impotent with a glazed look to their eyes, but feared there were others loose in the Weirsburg Institution. Others not so weak or unwilling to hurt. The violent were as likely to be free as their pacifist counterparts. He stepped into the room, raising a hand to rest on the back of his chair. An axe nearly sheared the chair in two before his hand came to fall on the backrest. Barely holding back a shriek, Rourke recoiled and looked to the source of the attack, ready to run in the case his assailant wanted to push the issue. Leonard Bulward, shaking like a leaf in the wind from his refuge beneath the desk, sighed as if a great weight had been lifted from his shoulders. It was only minutes ago that the shivering doctor had been offering Daniel assurances of safety. Nothing would happen when the clock tolled midnight he said, life would continue on as they'd known it. The delusions of a madman were nothing to fret over, regardless of the strange things that had been happening at Weirsburg Institution For the Criminally Insane. Now it was only minutes past the hour and the asylum had plunged into chaos, the rational Bulwark cowering under a desk and gripped by stark terror. Chaos foretold by Bruce Shanahan. One of the many manifestations of his dark god's release from its undersea prison. Rourke had no inclination to fight it anymore: Hell had opened on Earth, and its denizens stalked the halls of this house for the insane. Denial would only prove futile now. Bulwark stammered as he gazed at his coworker with the same glassy eyes as those in his care. "What....what's happening, Dan? I saw....oh my god....!" His breathing became heavy and fast, laborious for a man his size. Rourke didn't know what his friend had seen but he was near hysterical, causing the former to fear for him going into cardiac arrest. He could only guess at what wickedness had impelled him to this state. Likely a glimpse into the demonic, not unlike Rourke had experienced before he fled the compound earlier. Bulwark collapsed backward, head resting against the wall. It was obvious he could scarcely control himself; whatever he had seen had terrified him beyond all measure. "Goddammit, Dan. I'm sorry. He...." The other man paused again, unable to find the words he desperately feared to say. It was like looking into a mirror for Rourke, a mirror that reflected his image from twenty minutes ago. The nightmare he feared had swallowed the logic he clung like a suckling baby to, and no prayer would save them now. "He was right all along. Something terrible is alive in this place. The world might be in danger, I don't know." Rourke's hand gently patted his friend's shoulder, an empty reassurance. But it was all he could offer as the reality of what he needed to do dawned on him. "Stay here, Leonard," he softly requested. "I have an appointment I need to make." Rourke drifted from the room as Leonard Bulwark did as he was asked, moreso out of fear of entering those accursed corridors again than any obedience. The staff chief nearly broek into a run as he traversed the halls, crossing into the next ward and making his way down the long passage in the direction of the room he aimed for. The room that housed the warped prophet, the demon. He only hoped the unholy bastard hadn't been released with the others. No one else could answer his questions. Not a sound, not a movement in the hallway. He waited for a shadow to lurch in one of the open doorways, or a horned fiend to spring from the darkness between the emergency lights. He nearly expected to hear chains rattling or to take in the symphony of Hell's tortured damned as they cried out from the abyss. Whether Shanahan had truly brought a demonic presence to this place or whether he was simply an oracle of such infernal things was something that Daniel Rourke dwelled on in spite of himself. That wasn't an issue now as he came forth seeking truth, seeking a glimpse into the future of the world. A dim emergency light meagerly illuminated the confines of Shanahan's cell. The one he searched for hadn't left along with so many of the others. His back was to Rourke as the doctor crept silently toward the threshold, pangs of dread wrenching at his gut. Shanahan's intellect was a thing that commanded respect, but his diabolical mind was one that bid only a fearful reverence. "I see that this bleak reality has finally been made apparent to you, doctor," said Shanahan from the middle of his cell. "He has risen, and darkness descended onto the world. There was no stopping it, no impeding its progress. A force of nature can never be halted by mere man." The doctor lowered himself to eye level with the other. He became faintly aware of others in the room, shrouded in the darkness. Others that had come to hear the prophet's macabre dogma and given themselves over to him. Like him, they'd seen everything they'd held true proven to be illusion, excessively optimistic filth as preached by charlatans. Demons existed, and they walked about in the very building that he called his office. "Is...is it evil?" he said with a quiet awe in his tone. "The things I've seen tonight..." The cult mastermind shook his head in the negative. "What you refer to as evil, we know only as truth. Good and evil are products of an ancient belief designed to control. I pity you, Daniel. You've been indoctrinated to believe in the ways of the world, to swallow half-truths fed to you by a society that embraces lies for the sake of comfort. And its that ignorance that will condem them all. But if you want spared...I can help. The promised land is yours if you are willing to see what lies beyond the veil." Rourke spoke nary a word, completely focused on what the other was saying and afraid to interrupt. He found himself enraptured by the words of the man known as the Bringer of the Black Gospel. He had seen what lurked beyond the light and feared falling prey to it. His only sanctuary lay in the promises of Bruce 'Violence Jack' Shanahan. "The truth lies before us, open like a book. However, its riddles are incalculable, and its language long forgotten by humanity. I can translate for you, Daniel Rourke. I merely ask one thing in return for my assistance. One minor favor in return for leading you to salvation." Rourke nodded feverishly, his eyes welling up in a mix of relief and sorrow. A pact with the devil seemed the only escape. "Make your request and I will grant it." Bruce Shanahan smiled, a thin and malign expression. "Two men can legally release me from this asylum. Marcus Stryker, and yourself. Sign the documents and release me from this damnable prison. With that done, Marcus and his rabid Blackhelm dogs have no legal channels to bring me back here. I'll be completely free again." It was a small price to pay. Rourke nodded his accession, feeling the warmth flow down his cheeks as relief flooded him. He prayed, to whatever gods were now listening, that it was more than just a brief respite. Bruce Shanahan stood, a giant amongst men silhouetted against the light. "Then go. Make it be. Tonight....we are both freed from the chains that bind us." * * * Marion Sterling, sultry in her green attire, cocked her head and set her eyes on the clock. "It's nearly time." Horace Tully moved his fingers about in the gloves he commonly adorned when bell-time arrived, the stretch of rubber audible as he flexed his knuckles and loosened his joints. Ethan Knight, dwelling in solitude in the dark-cloaked corner of the room, uttered some final words of an abominable prayer before the exited the locker room. The 118th installment of PRIME Revolution was about to begin and already the backstage echoed with the roar of the crowd. It was sold out, or so the box office reported to Chet Worth. There was a chance the numbers were inflated, as was common in the industry. "Where's Johnny?" Tully asked contemptibly. He'd openly showed little love for the newest to join their fold, referring to him often as a mindless degenerate. Of course, he did so when the other was out of earshot. He had no desire to ruin Fath Shanahan's plans by attacking their acquisition from the late AWC. Sterling shrugged. "About. Father Shanahan seems content with letting him roam as he pleases. As long as his loyalty remains, all is well." "He better keep a short leash on that pup," quipped The Adamant. "His follies could ruin everything. It's bad enough that PTC prevented us from laying waste to Snow and Ember. We don't need that deviant to bring us further misfortune." A shape stirred in the doorway. Tully and Sterling looked up, Knight pausing in his blackened words of grace as the other snickered. "I wouldn't worry about Garbage Bag Johnny. We keep him fed, we give him shelter. And we promise the man things that he would betray his own mother to hold on. He's our lapdog." Tully and Sterling merely nodded agreement and went back to their preparations. His crusaders had been mostly silent in the wake of what had befallen them at the airport over a week ago, Sterling herself informing him that Brothers Knight and Tully felt responsible for their failure. They felt they had been too weak and feeble to prevent it. Unfortunately for Shanahan, he realized the blame rested on his own near-sightedness. He'd taken into account the movements of Blackhelm and their other enemies, but not the agents of the tournament sponsorship. He simply hadn't counted on the betrayal of the self-professed neutral party. He'd thought they were too buried in their own avarice to offer a concern as to what the Sect's reign over PTC would mean. "Before me," he said firmly, wringing his hands as the plans of the night repeated over and again in his mind. His three subordinates came to kneel before him. They seemed worried, haggard. But their master felt only pride in what they had accomplished. "We had been completely discounted in PRIME, and in this world in general," he iterated, lifting one of their many unholy tomes and gazing at the letters decorating its leathery spine. "But now, after all that has happened, they again fear us. You may consider what happened in GTT6 to be a failure, but in truth, it's as great victory as we've ever attained. Primetime Central, PRIME, and the entire world saw our power, and acted to stop us. They had to. It took the might and efforts of many together to hinder us, but that's all they have done. Hinder our march to domination. Awakened, there isn't an entity in PRIME that can stand before us and triumph. Not while we act as the fist of the Old Ones." Smiles all-around. Twisted and demented were the zealots of Bruce Shanahan's hellish brainchild, his Sect of Black Wisdom. "This will be our greatest hour. Tonight will see Primetime Central's newest champion toppled in his first bout since those sniveling, pencil-pushing cowards offered him safeguard from our wrath. Snow and Rollins will be crushed and our enemies will come to fall. Let this be the era of the Sect of Black Wisdom!" Violence Jack looked down on Horace Tully and saw the gleam in his eye. The man who would one day become his successor was ready to ascend to the top of the mountain, and prove why PTC was right to keep them from the tournament final. Tonight, the crumbling power known as Fuck You would be extinguished at the hands of The Covenant.
