The Way Of Things (Second Chance In This Life)

11 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/11/07 It's an unseasonably hot night in Los Angeles for this time of year, the intense glare of the sun making it hard to focus. It's another date and another show, not that much unlike the old days, save the fact that I ain't on the bill. I had a hand in it though. They wanted to do a mixture card, something where they bring in a legend to make him feel useful again. They get him out there, let him sign some autographs, and hope the public hasn't completely forgotten about him. Three forth's through a pack of Marlboro's already. It's one of those kind of migraine headache nights, and those kinds of nights involve lots of smoking. I still remember the days when I said I'd never suck on these things. I decided earlier in the week to approach the booker about some idea. He has some hot young talent he wants to get out there, and I am figuring that we have a legend, why not do a spot in there? Get all of them in the ring, past and future? Let him take a few swings, get some cheers. Hell, I think it is owed to him for all the hard work he's done in the business. The bookerman went along with it, but said I might want to watch out. Said these two guys have a bit of an ego problem. Tag team or something. Oh well. I understand where they are coming from. He took the idea on to them apparently, and they reluctantly agreed. I was here solely for my own talent. They pay pretty decent, but outside that? Not that great. I'm sitting front row this show, with the booker to my side. Annoying little shit. Smells like sausage and talks in wheezes. Just another night with another shithead booker in a place that barely houses four hundred. But I guess that's the way of things. *** He straightened his tie, grimly looking into the mirror. His skin was beaten and worn, his forehead scarred with the kiss of the past. He was filled with aches from a path his body could never forgive. The white dress shirt hung loosely from his skin, belying the skeletal structure his once hulking frame had become. Every movement was a chore. This was the simplicity of the duplicitous edge from the life he had led; a God in his youth, he was now a shriveled husk which vaguely resembled a human in his twilight years. He closed his hands, the arthritis causing a stabbing pain to shoot through his forearms. He winced as his once mighty hands struggled even to make a fist. Opening his hand, he reached into the pocket of his slacks, pulling out a paper pack of cigarettes. If it wasn't his liver that was going to bury him, it was his lungs. Caked full of tar from the years of chain smoking, their poor state caused him to have violent coughing fits, usually causing him to wince and push into those around him. They couldn't help but check on his quaking form. His lived in a dump. One of the many ghetto apartment complexes in the metro Los Angeles area, it had the trappings of white asbestos tiles that began chipping away over the years. Bars were set over the windowsills. He could not see out his own windows clearly; it was a wonder if he would even want to? A glance would only act as a harrowing reminder of what he had lost. Putting the filter on his lips, he struggled with his small lighter. His fingers didn't respond like they used to, which made this all the more embarrassing when he had a nicotine fit in public. His grip shook, causing the small flame to sway from side to side in jagged motions. The smoke rose from the end, and he sucked in as much as he could, blowing the carcinogens out of his flared nostrils after a few seconds. Looking over, he saw the display case with all his accomplishments that had left him in such a debilitated state; championship trophies and ornate gold belts. Photographs from his highest highs. Critical awards from industry insiders awarded to him throughout the course of his career. The display was less than half full, the faint imprint in red velvet showing that something which was once placed there with the most meticulous care. It was hard for him to part with those past accolades. The things he had fought so hard for that had been said to be priceless apparently did have a price at pawn shops. But the lights had to be kept on somehow, and he was not old enough for social security. He had no pension plan. His wealth had been wasted on trivial items and divorces. The smoked bellowed from his dry, cracked lips. It swirled around and touched the stained drywall ceiling. The previous tenants had been party animals, or so he heard. The more likely reason for the poor quality of his living conditions probably had more to do with his landlord simply not caring. Just mere days before, the railing in the fire escape fell to the ground when one of the neighbor kids applied pressure to it. It had rotted away, but the landlord blamed the kid's horseplay for it. The piece of railing still laid out there. He wasn't at the point of eating canned dog food, but he was in constant pain. All his money literally went into buying medicine for his heart and liver. He supplemented his income with small independent bookings, where he was brought in to sign a few autographs and shake hands. It was funny, seeing how in the past, he was one of the most hated individuals to ever grace the squared circle. Now? People acted chipper towards him, forgetting the teeth this broken mutt once had. He struggled to stand. He masked his pain because he couldn't let the fans know how much entertaining them hurt. He was a former wrestling legend. A titan amongst men. All he could do in his final years was pick up scraps left to him under the table. *** "He's gonna get in the ring and give them young kids what's for? You sure about this Brandon? You saw how much trouble the guy had just movin' his fuckin' legs. You expect him to pick one of these guys and drive him into the canvas?" Bookers come and go, their names fading out of my mind. I just knew which ones paid the best. Those were the one's I sent the BloodWorks guys to. Bigger cut. This guy smelled like he'd been through a meat grinder five days back, but he was one of California's best when it comes to paying. "Give the guy a damn chance. He probably doesn't get much action now a days," I flatly state, my mind wandering. "I ain't askin' him to lift him up and spin him around and shit. I'm saying a few chops, those guys bail, and we all go home happy. Easy as pie. He gets some glory and those fuckin' ego cases learn a lesson in humility." I did not even know them personally. They were getting some play, so I figured that they would be perfect for this sort of thing. Be a sort of fun little aside to the evening. Give a wrestling legend something to be happy with and also have them try and put themselves over as heels. But the guy was in rough shape, and honestly I was hoping I didn't completely fuck this up. The worst possible scenario would be for this to backfires and the old man gets booed. With how fickle fans are these days, you can never be completely certain. The veteran came out to the ring with theme music, getting a round of cheers from the audience. He limps up the ring steps, bowing his head to show the fans respect. But I remember this old salty dog. Him taking a bow and getting cheers is a far cry from the old-- "I'm honored to be here. I always thought that wrestling was the best thing in the world. And darn it...I still do. To see the new generation take over is truly something awe inspiring to watch, " he struggles through rasps, his arms shaking as he struggles to maintain his composure. "I'm glad that there are still those out there that respect the contributions people like me made to this industry--" And that's when Motley Crue's 'Too Young To Fall In Love' blares on the speaker system, causing the upstarts to come on out with a saunter. *** "No way! No fuckin' way I go out there and fuckin' take shit like that from some old motherfuckin' geezer who shits in diapers...y'know?" his questioned rhetorically as he flipped his dirty blond ponytail around like a scorpion's stinger. The fluorescent light bulb hummed up above him as he massaged his temples with his forefingers His dress was form fitting, showing off his pretty boy looks and washboard abs that masked his lacking personality. Vanity was not his greatest sin; his would be hubris. It was a wrestling house, where those coming up in the profession would stay. Usually it would be a haven for people coming in to tour, with a group making a living there and paying the rent. It was a better option than sleeping in the confines of a compact car. For them, it provided a chance to bleed some peers dry for beer money. The place was pretty bare bones, with milk crates and bean bag chairs acting as vital furniture set pieces, the real money going into entertainment centers and video game systems. And to sugar bowls. There was always a sugar bowl in the place. Theirs was in the shape of a cow, and it rested in the center of a painted over table that was used for any number of assorted things. A thin layer of saran wrap with frayed edges protruded from the neck of the cow. Beside it was a package of straws that remained half cracked open. It was probably the spot that he and his Asian tag team partner spent the most time, with loud rock music playing as a backdrop. "Come on man...let's just do our job. We're in hot enough water as is with people always challenging us. Motherfuckers jealous man--" He was quickly cut off by his larger friend. "Their fuckin' jealous. SO WHAT?! We're the motherfuckin' kings of this shit! This industry is gonna be made off of us. And I'm gonna rub it in their faces. They think I'm puttin' over some old man? Fuck 'em." There was a moment of silence between the two. The Asian seemed to be very twitchy, while his American counterpart was sweating bullets. "You need to realize somethin'," he started, waving his finger around as if it meant something. "The only way to impress people is by puttin' your foot down. And if you don't, people will walk all over you." The American bred arrogance drenched the air around them, making the world around them heavy with ignorance. "I respect nobody but myself. Respect is earned. I don't act like I am supposed to bow to someone unless they deserve it. And fuck, this guy, I ain't even heard of him. He's like some punk. I ain't going to dump my fuckin' stock down, especially when we both know that the headhunters are out lookin' to make us rich." The young Asian man couldn't take it anymore. Grasping the razor from a box cutter, he reached towards the bowl and peeling back the saran wrap. Using the scoop inside, he tossed some white powder down on the table. He started whisking the powder on the table, cutting it with the razor and pushing it into a long line. Burying his face into the table violently and snorting, his friend could only ponder if his tag team partner had broken his nose with the ferocious dive. He snorted the line quickly, shooting his head back and grabbing at his head before falling to the hard wood floor. "This is just the beginning man. We're gonna be kings man. You pulled me from the depths, now let me return the favor. Follow my lead man. We'll be the fuckin' talk of the industry. And ain't nobody gonna fucking come in and try to pull that kinda shit on us ever again. You got that? They disrespectin' us with this bullshit." Blood starting to flow down the young Asian's nose. His vision blurred and his voice shaky, he took his friends words to heart. He wanted to make it, not be stuck in the shithole of living on borrowed dimes to get his highs. "The fuck you suggest," he asked before a sharp pain caused him to lose his train of thought. "Ah shit!" He grabbed his nose. "Fuck, man." His American friend laughed heartily, the cocaine running heavy in his own veins. "I say fuck him, man. Break from the script. Teach the fucker a lesson." His Asian counterpart nodded his head. "Fuck man, yeah alright man. I got to get a smoke. Want one?" He shook his head. "Hell no. I'd never smoke one of them fuckin' cancer sticks." *** They stepped through the ropes, wearing cocky grins on their faces. He stood there, looking at them as they made their way to the ring. Their size caused him to grow fearful, his knees knocking together as he tried to remain calm. I was having second thoughts the second I saw the way those guys came out to the ring, thinking that they seemed overeager. From what I had heard about them, these two were aloof personalities that had grown into full blown egotists. "You see here old man? Standing before you is the future of professional wrestling. There ain't no legacy sittin' here. There ain't room for you and your ilk. So, old man, hit the bricks." The call for him to leave got the usual round of boos from those in attendance. But I was more fixated on the guy talking. He spoke with the same reckless abandon I once had, his rage filled words masking a hunger to prove himself no matter what the cost. The old man tried to bring the microphone up to his lips, but it was smacked out of his hands. In a flash, the old wrestling legend is lifted into the air and planted in the center of the ring with a spinebuster that shakes the foundation of the ring. I jumped the guardrail. They scatter It's too late. I looked down at the veteran as he writhed in pain. I couldn't look away. Looking down, I could have sworn I saw my reflection peering back at me.
