Date: I don't like dates ONE - 'I think you worried for me then...' If you were Amy Campbell right now, you'd be a very confused young lady. Well, if it was you... I guess we'll leave out the 'young lady' part. But don't make me check. So, if you're Amy Campbell, you're confused. That's been established. You're conflicted in every sense of the word. Woe is you. Really! So, you want to talk about drugs? Let's talk about drugs. Let's talk about lines upon lines, cut with a credit card that's not about to buy you another pair of jeans to rip and tear until they look appropriately grunge or punk or whatever it is they market you as. Tell a story of sex, drugs and the occasionally heavy rock 'n roll soundtrack. Put a song to the next line. Insert lyrical interjection so they'll think you're a starving artist. Remember, cocaine's always in fashion when you're a celebrity that hardly makes the B-list. You're cool, you're crazy and you're the face of women in an industry where... well - where women are wives and girlfriends and sluts to the guy with the next biggest muscles. Do you want to be Amy Campbell, circa 2004? Didn't think so. Her brain doesn't work the same way your average chick's would. She's got the constant nagging of crossing a very thin, white line in the back of her head constantly - and the battle to stay to one side of that powdered barrier never ends. Her entire body shows the signs of a hard life; The scars, the tattoos, the ever so slight presence of flesh on her bag of bones. All of it would tell someone to stay far, FAR away. She'd be trouble for anything that wanted to live past tomorrow. 2005 saved her. Where she'd left fWo and subsequently destroyed a marriage that her heart WAS once in, the 22 year old wandered aimlessly down halls that were papered with THC, MDMA...anything to get you out of the head you had. She faded away to barely a hundred pounds, and slept on the couch of her best friend's apartment while regailing in the tales of fights gone by. World title shots and Hardcore reigns. At the beginning of 2005, one thing was very clear: If you were Amy Campbell, you would either become Aimz again, or you'd go home and help your mother pick out your coffin. If you were Amy Campbell, you might have given up. ''I wouldn't let that shit kill me. It's been sitting on my shoulder forever, but when I got the call from PRIME...'' Her head dipped down and sucked on the straw that lead to the coke - Cola, that is - again, wetting a throat parched by one of the longest speeches she'd given since... well... ever. ''...Are you even listening?'' Now, if you were Dave Gibson, you'd be half asleep because you've heard this story ten times over. When he nodded sleepily and obediently from across the table, she smiled. ''Good. Now, remember when you met me?'' Another nod. ''Cokecokecokecoke. That's all I could think about. I mean, even a month and a half ago, when my sister had to move in with me because of her divorce - she caught me on the coffee table with three lines, one already so far into my nose that it was probably sitting just behind my right eye. The five days of rehab were forced on me, because I couldn't have her telling our parents again - it'd break their hearts - but it was probably the best and sweetest thing anybody's ever done for me, even if she was screaming at me until the glass doors of the detox muffled the sound out. I mean, you remember what I was like... I couldn't look out the window without a white shadow getting in my way. I made baby steps and had weeks where I wouldn't touch anything - but the next big match, the next fix I needed... it was always there. I thought I performed better when I had that. I tricked myself. It's intense! You and I are about to do some teamworky trust workshop bullshit when, three weeks ago, I couldn't even trust myself! Crazy, huh?'' Dave seemed to be stirred slightly from his nap by her foot swiftly beating into his shin under the table. It wasn't even deliberate; The little redhead was riled up, buzzing with an excited energy. ''But now I can beat Karina fucking Wolfenden cleanly! I can win shit consistantly! This is all amazing, isn't it?'' She was picking at the knees of her jeans. The girl plainly needed to expell some of that mountain of energy that sat as a chip on her shoulder. Dave raised an eyebrow when he noticed the empty mugs in front of her. While she'd been talking, the tiny redhead had consumed her weight in caffiene. ''That, uhh...'' he mumbled, ''Had enough coffee, Ames?'' Amy grinned, bouncing up and down in her seat gently as she glanced down to the brown rings at the bottom of each cup, ''Whaa? Oh, the coffee? Nah, I hate coffee. It was capucc--wait, haven't you been paying attention? You look like you need a nap.'' ''Compared to you right now? Anything would need to rest.'' Coffee, it would seem, acted like a drug for the suddenly ditzy young lady. She leapt up from the table with a quick exclaimation of ''We're late!'' and snared Gibson by the wrist, tossing down a 20 dollar bill for whatever waitress cleaned up her tiny castle of white cups and dribbled drinks. The geniuses behind PRIME had sold their team on going to a teamwork and relations building workshop that day and, although Gibson and the little Campbell were far from late, they sure were moving. In an unfamilliar rental car, Amy was scaring the Old School right out of Dave Gibson - weaving and winding in amongst busy lunch hour traffic at rather terrifying speeds that he wasn't quite sure she was at all aware of. At least - if they made it in one or two pieces, of course - the workshop would expend a little bit of her energy. After that, he'd take her back upstairs and send her to her room to pass out sleepily. David Gibson has no idea that his waif-like best friend was about to make him live a nightmare. ----------------TWO -'...the subtler ways that I'd give in...'The Prior Night ''Stop biting your nails. C'mere... what's your problem?'' Some things, no measure of communication would fix. Anxiety was one of those things. Still, a fiesty redhead fell easily into the arms of a Hercules. ''It's not a problem as much as it's a fucked up situation,'' she'd say, ''How do I look right to these people? PTC. They remember me as the junkie from OSW. This isn't PRIME, or its fans and even they don't know me.'' They were laying there, in bed. In bed and somehow not at all sexually related in that moment. As opposed to the unit they'd been weeks before - a relationship consisting entirely of hotel rooms and sneaky little moments - they were awkward friends now. No, Dave Gibson was miles away from this room. Killean Sirrajin and Amy Campbell, entirely able to talk and discuss the hardships of life without the necessity of a penis entering the equation. As opposed to seducing, he was comforting her. ''How would you know that?'' was the question initially posed by a man entirely unaware of the answer she had already loaded into her verbal sniper. ''Because I know how to use a computer. I go to their forums, and everybody's got their opinions on everything. And you think I could just sign up and clear the air? Forget about it, because there's already this dumb little bitch who's practically stolen my identity. I'm pretty sure she's stalking me, actually; Likes the same bands, has the same opinions, NEVER knows when to shut the hell up, always has an excuse or an opinon on everything...'' ''I think I've seen h--'' ''Of course you have! Everybody has, because if she's not acting like an asshole, she's whoring out pictures. Helloooooooo self-obsession! Dumb bitch would probably claim I stole her life to use as a marketing tool if I ever replied to the shit they say about me, so I've gotta prove it without adding an opinion to that online shithouse. Seriously, girls like that should just stick to writing whiney little pop songs and calling people fat to win their anorexic asses arguments.'' She shuffled around in his grip, unrelenting in her agitation that found itself aimed at basically anything that dared stand within a foot of her aura. Killean, she knew, was slightly beyond grasping the subtler points she'd make within her jilted tongues, but he could understand her frustration easily enough. It was the most apparent point in the entire situation - She'd been detached from the organization she was fighting inside of for so long, that who she was in the modern day would seem like a ghost of the girl they knew. How would that be recieved? How could that be recieved, when your audience was a group of plugged-in individuals, all with their own opinions and far from sheepish trains of thought on the wrestling world and how it operated? Simply put, she was about to run into a realm of public opinion where she needed more than ever to rely on those around her. And relying on others was not one of Amy's strong points. In a team, she always worked as the loose cannon - the one you couldn't count on to know how to hold a teammate up in a snag... the one who'd plow down legions of bastard wrestlers without any thought of a Boston Crab or the concept of tagging. She was efficient, effective... but very, very unreliable when operating within a team was part of the game. Before either knew it, they'd gone a minute without speaking. She'd slid back into his chest, nuzzling as far into the chisled muscle as she could without causing either of them any pressure. His head laid against the headboard, the arm that wasn't around Amy laying idle over the edge of the mattress. It was the perfect moment... lazily lounging about on a humid afternoon. Perfect, if it weren't for the turmoil both knew was stirring within an overly anxious Aimz. ----------------THREE - '... But I know you liked the show.' It was a convention room on the ground floor, labelled by a little whiteboard with a ridiculously perky message scrawled onto it. No-name Oreos on the table across the room. No-name chips. Hin See, cheap bastard that he was, had obviously avoided having their comfort in mind. That was fine, though... Amy didn't like chocolate, and Dave was dieting. ''HELLO, and welcome to our team building workshop!'' Dave looked at the little man shouting at him. It was Carrot Top as a cheerleader! ''Will Landon here! I'm going to be your team builder today. You ready to have some fun?'' ''I didn't know the workshop was cancelled...'' Gibson mumbled. ''Hey, a sense of humor! I love that! So, where's everyone else?'' Both of their heads turned toward the door, where a raincloud wearing Vans sneakers was making her entrance. ''Hi there!'' Will called out to her. ''Oh. HELL. No.'' She turned around, in absolute disbelief that PRIME had dared to put such a man - creature - within strangling range of her, especially in a goofy little t-shirt like he was wearing. ''Come on, let's build some spirit! There's punch!'' Dave had to glide across the room and snag the tattooed redhead by the arm as she began her trek toward Mr. Landon with balled fists, ''Amy, no...'' ''What? He said there was punch!'' ''Not punching,'' Gibson murmered to her, ''Come on, we can pull through this crapfest.'' When he let go of her, Aimz thought of a thousand better places she could be. She began to display bounding enthusiasm... towards running at the nearest exit. ''AMY!'' Dave called out while Landon raised a perky eyebrow. It was useless - she was gone, and Dave was the only PRIME team member left in that room... at that useless team-building workshop. His team? Somewhere else. Gone from him. He'd been abandoned, just like the old days. ----------------FOUR -'the ice is thin...'minutes later ''God, there're a million things I should be doing right now. Hearing some jerkoff tell me how to work with a team isn't one of them... y'know?'' Killean Sirrajin had found himself employed as a statue to speak at. Not to, 'at'. She wasn't speaking to him in hope or expectation of response, he was simply filling a quota of company that said she was crazy if she was talking to herself. If she was talking to a person acting as an inanimate object, she was fine. Perfectly sane. Nice and stable. ''I need to find a place to practice. Somewhere the company doesn't send me. A little dive that I can jump around in... if this damned city has one. I can't just lay around here, in this hotel full of executives and lackeys. It's making me insane, isn't it? I'm a nervous wreck about this fight. My team is a joke, isn't it?'' ''Amy, you need t--'' ''It's all going to shit, Kill. This is the downfall. I can't be the face of PRIME in a fight when I don't even know if I'll be able to stand in the ring for it! I'm gonna go insane bef---'' ''You're gonna go insane if you don't shut up and listen to yourself for a second.'' That was... curt. Not what she'd expected at all, and it took her a moment to digest. How could someone speak to her with balls like that? ''I should beat your f--'' Killean spoke plainly, ''You won't. I said what I said because you've gotta snap out of whatever buzz it is you've got going on. You're gonna drive yourself up the wall. Worse yet, you'll drive ME up the wall. Now settle down.'' People didn't speak to her like that. They didn't. They weren't right like that... it just didn't happen. And now, it had. He'd figured her out for once, and acted almost like her mother. Someone had recognized her poor form and put a stop to it. He'd curbed her anxiety, at least for the time being, and was entirely unapologetic about it. It was a blessing in disguise, but would only serve to push her further away. ''I...'' she was meek, ''...No. I've got to go do what works. I'll go train alone. I'll find a place.'' He grabbed for her arm, only to be turned away from and by the time she was at the door, he had nothing to say... ...But she did. ''If Dave comes around, tell him that this is best for the team. You seem convincing enough tonight.'' She was snarky, halfway angry and less than motivated. The only thing Amy knew going through that door was the fact that she'd eventually have to gather back around her peers, and the thought was dreadful... but she'd committed to this. She was confident enough in the past weeks to think this to be possible, why not now? It was simply a matter of training, and taming the temper that staved off the rest of her teammates. She had everything and nothing to prove, depending on who you asked... Maybe - just maybe - it was time to get serious about this 'Primetime' thing. Don't let the place down, Amy. You're not the OSW junkie anymore. Hopefully.
