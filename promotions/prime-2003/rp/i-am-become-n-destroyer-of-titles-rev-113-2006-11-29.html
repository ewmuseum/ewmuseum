I am become N, destroyer of titles. (Rev 113)

29 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29th November Monologues are wonderful, aren’t they? A little epiphany here, a selfishly-shared soliloquy there. I’m sure at this point, someone’s had one about me. They can probably be broken down into two categories. The first of which would be attributed to the inexperienced and the kind of person I despise in this little game - those who allow their confidence to consume them and their greed to overthrow their careers. They will pass me off as another person to stomp down on their way up to the top, most likely seeing this entire sport as a large salary to a life of luxury. It is those people who are most likely to be the first ones lacking a decent set of moves and will find themselves looking for a steel chair when the situation goes south. And then there are the wiser. Not the wise, merely wiser than the latter. Those are the ones who will be wondering exactly who I am and why I demand this return to the finer days when "extreme" was not an issue and "no holds barred" would actually see a wrestling hold used in such a match. What reason could I possibly have, after all? I’m certainly far too young, if I do say so myself, to be a part of the good old days when all one needed for promotion was a catchy, self-glorifying nickname. Or at least, I’d like to believe so. But then, who would I be? What kind of person would mask his identity from the world and repeat his ideas of how wrestling should be conducted? Why is he so adamant about the arrogance of today’s grapplers? Maybe it’s the scariest explanation of all that ends up as the truth. That this bleached bone-white mask is merely the bone of my being and there is no face beneath. Maybe I’m not a person, but an immortal spirit - the personification of this very sport itself, the essence of wrestling, given meat and muscle so it can express the anger it feels to how it’s been mocked. Laughed at. Abused. Mistreated. Taken for granted. Maybe for every 22 year old who decided to put on a wifebeater and blare Nu-Metal to overdone fireworks has built up to breaking point… and this is where I pour my anxiety, into this shape, so I may express myself. And if this proves to be the truth, then the wrestling world has much to fear. But I too feel fear. However, it isn’t the idea of a man towering 2 feet above me, or weighing twice as much as me that I fear. It is the notion that eventually, wrestling will lose the meaning it so proudly carries. There won’t be anyone who can perform a submission hold any longer, they’re simply thinking of how many firework blasts there will be during their entrance. They’ll forget the importance of a strong headlock and be looking for a flaming chair, or calling out entire gangs to ringside to the point that every match has a set of lumberjacks. Live shows will no longer be held, unless the audience are set behind protective barriers because of thrown weapons and the concept of a match ending in the ring being completely alien. The referees will need to sit in a box above the ring, no longer able to see a pinfall with his own eyes but cowering away inside a veritable tank laden with enough cameras and monitors to make him believe he is a creation of George Orwell. But I notice that there is a catalyst for all this. Winning is one thing. It can be resolved in rematches, or avenged by a spineless backstage attack. But the thing that brings out the worst in people, the devil that people sell their souls for is not a shade of crimson, but glitters as gold. In every professional and amateur wrestling organisation, there lies a championship belt, usually more numerous as time goes on and the need is felt to illustrate a variety of champions of different levels. But I see no point to all these temptations… there is only one prize needed in such a sport. With a new belt made just to appease the growing number of greedy gluttons on the roster, I thought it was time rectify this situation. Only one man or woman needs to represent this company as their champion, leaving everyone else clutching onto theirs so they can pretend to feel worthy. No more. I now see what needs to be done, what needs to be fixed. Every belt, every title that has no importance needs to go. Beginning with Sun Tzu’s Intense Title, I will proceed to capture and retire these little glistening belts of bait, so people can focus once more on keeping their pride from a three count instead of a disqualification to retain. Revolution 113 will mark the genesis of a new chapter, whereupon dignity and the art of wrestling are restored to their former glory and people will be forced to appreciate their careers. No more false divisions. No more segregation by weight differences. No more separation by regional divides. Just wrestling, the way it was intended. And those who fail to appreciate the spirit of wrestling shall find that their matches shall end by their own admission of the lack of will continue. The words "I quit" will be permanent, for the undeserving shall not meet their defeat through a stack of tables, but through submission. And that is how this era of ingrates and incompetents shall end. Not with a bang… but with a whimper.
