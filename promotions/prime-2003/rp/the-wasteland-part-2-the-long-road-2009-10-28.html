The Wasteland: Part 2 - The Long Road

28 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 28, 2009 Let's Recap Imagine you're a 25 year old kid. An adult on paper, but a kid in every sense of the word. You've been wrestling in this little shit promotion based out of Santa Fe because it's the only gig in town, and you're good, and you know you're good, and even the fans know you're good, fickle as they are. But you and the boss don't see eye to eye, the old man is jealous or racist or whatever, and he's not cutting you any breaks. You're doing the job for has-beens and skinny heels hoping to eventually get ahead, but knowing in your heart that each time you lay down is another step in the wrong direction. Imagine you're this kid and you're not particularly satisfied with your life. You've got a day job teaching remedial English to the children of immigrants and borderline retarded white kids at a local high school. You're not qualified for this job in any way, but nobody else wanted to do it, and you have bills to pay. And even though you hate this job, you're worried it's going to get pulled out from under you. There's talk in the papers about the state making big cuts to the education budget, and you're the low man on the totem pole, here just like everywhere else in your life. You're going nowhere fast, and you're haunted by the vague memories of the confidence and dreams you used to have. So you smoke a lot of pot to forget about your worries. Imagine one day you wake up to find your father has returned from out of nowhere. You haven't seen the man in sixteen years, ever since he walked out on you and your moms when you were just a little shit. You want to lash out at the man but his presence is strangely disarming. He's harsh and hard and direct and tears your life apart with his words that he has no right to speak, this deadbeat, this nonentity, but he's only echoing your own thoughts, the ones pushed back to the furthest corner of your mind, and you know he's right, you're a fuck-up. He's hard, but there's a softness to him, too, a sadness behind his eyes that maybe you're just imagining, but you hope not. He surprises you by accepting part of the blame for your broken life, offers you a boon, to help you get your life together, back on the right track. And you know this doesn't even begin to cut it, but you say nothing, going along with it. Imagine you train with him every day for three months, getting ready for your big chance, a match with Jason Snow in the GTT that isn't even guaranteed. Your boss could pick anyone to represent his federation in the tournament, why would he pick you, who he hates. And even if he did, you're not ready and you know it. You've got the fundamentals but months of smoking pot have slowed your reflexes, dulled your killer instincts. But he hones and tempers you, your father, like a blacksmith forging a sword, and with beat-down after beat-down you can feel yourself hardening, becoming more and more like him. You ready yourself in the face of the impossible, Kierkegaard's knight of faith in action. Imagine that you're finally seeing results, you're back at the top of your game. On your federation's weekly cards it's becoming clear that just by standing next to them you outclass the aging veterans, the fat slobs, the talentless, masochistic kids. So you go to the boss, tell him you've been working hard, ask him for the chance to face Jason Snow, to prove yourself, but the fat fuck, he doesn't even give you the time of day. You were never on his radar, just some greasy wetback trash clinging to the bottom of his roster. He gives you the boot. Your father commiserates, but three days later and he's gone too. And then imagine that the very next week your boss is found strangled to death in his trailer. That's when the bottom really drops out. A lot of people had seen that argument you'd had, on your last day in the federation, and you'd be a prime suspect if you didn't have a solid alibi, having been out that night drowning your sorrows with the few friends you had on the roster. As it is you were still dragged down to the police station to sit through two stressful days of questioning, white detectives stinking of cigarettes in your face the whole time, giving you dirty looks. You're cleared of suspicion eventually, though, while meanwhile everyone forgets about the GTT, more concerned about the future of the promotion itself, finding bookings elsewhere. Or even if they do think about it, they assume they were just passed over, didn't make the cut. Now imagine it's one month later and you're across the ring from Jason Snow. Everyone in the arena expects a squash, and the only thing keeping these people from wandering off to the toilet or concession area is the desire to tell their friends they saw Snow hit his finisher on some green kid. Nobody cheers for you when you come to the ring, they're laughing at you if anything, or else feeling sorry for you. But thirty minutes later, nobody is laughing. They're cheering, screaming their asses off. For half an hour you take Jason Snow to war, scoring reversal after reversal, surprising the champion with near fall after near fall. Gasps of disbelief reverberate throughout the arena. You turn a match no one wanted to see into a potential match of the year candidate. And even though you're semi-conscious when Snow hits you with the Snake Eyes and covers you for the pin, you imagine you hear him utter a sigh of relief at the sound of the bell. Everyone in the back pats you on the back, shakes your hand, tells you what a great job you did, and you smile back at them, saying thanks, but you don't feel great. You feel let down. Drained. Desperate. Can you imagine that? After all that shit, your best not being good enough? Ok then, let's move on. "Long is the way, and hard, that out of hell leads up to the light." - John Milton. The Aftermath October 24th Diego Foster is sitting on his futon in his boxers, watching Saturday morning cartoons. Every now and then he laughs quietly to himself. A family-size bag of cool ranch Doritos is sitting next to him. A faint cloud of smoke hangs in the air. As soon as he got home from his trip to Washington for the GTT the previous Saturday, he'd immediately found himself besieged by well-wishers. The internet was by now buzzing with talk of the match, some illegal footage had made its way onto Youtube, people were digging up clips of his matches in DFW. In short, Diego found himself elevated to the rank of minor celebrity in the wrestling world. Checking his messages, he saw that his answering machine had maxed out sometime Friday afternoon. He skipped through the messages, listening only long enough to hear the name before moving on to the next. None of them were worth listening to. He just wanted to zone out, to be alone with his thoughts. After answering five calls in his first hour home, it was a small leap to the decision to unplug his phone. He didn't limit himself to his land-line. He turned off his cell. He had no interest in checking his emails. He even called off work at the high school for the week, claiming the flu. It wasn't the smartest thing to do, he knew, knowing his position there was shaky as things were, but he just couldn't stomach the thought of going back to the same old bullshit routine. The $2000 check he received for his first round loss made the decision easier, though. After buying an ounce of mid-grade weed from his usual guy, Diego made a quick shopping run and proceeded to lock himself in his apartment, planning not to emerge for a week, if ever. He'd spent the time doing absolutely nothing besides smoke pot, watch TV, and eat trans-fat heavy snack food. Anytime his thoughts turned to wrestling, or the GTT, or Jason Snow he decided the time had come to light up another bowl, and so he passed his days in a mindless funk. One week isn't quite enough to undo four months of hard work and training, but his midsection is already looking less cut than it did the night he tangled with Snow. The sound of the doorbell ringing causes Diego's head to turn away from the TV, but only for a moment. He ignores it the second time. It can't be his landlord, he thinks, he'd used a chunk of the GTT check to pay his rent two months in advance, and anyone else could fuck off for all he cared. He's due for a haircut, he thinks, his semi-layered hair having grown unkempt. Could use a shave as well. Not today, though. Maybe next month. He wishes that annoying knocking at the door would stop. "Seriously D, you better open this fucking door," he can hear faintly in between the clubbing salvos. Must be Wally, he thinks to himself, judging from the voice's snobby nasal pitch. He considers what would happen if he didn't answer the door, laid down and took a nap instead, but if he knew his DFW roster mate, he wasn't about to give up anytime soon. No way he could get sleep with that racket going on. With a sigh Diego gets up from the futon, throws on a dirty white t-shirt he picks up off the floor and walks over to the door. As the door swings inward, he can see in an instant that Wally Simmonds is not happy, his jaw set in irritation. He seems overdressed in comparison, clad in a blue polo shirt and chinos, and though he looks up at the taller Diego, his expression communicates that he's clearly looking down on him. "Who do you think you are, ignoring me at the door like that? You think I've got time to waste standing around all day with my thumb up my ass?" "Uh...but I did answer the door," Diego says, looking confused. "Took you long enough," Wally snaps. He looks over Diego's shoulder into the apartment. "So are you going to invite me in?" Diego swings his head backward, signaling his on-again, off-again, kind-of-a-friend into the apartment. Wally walks past him, sitting down on one end of the futon and pulling a can of Coke from the cooler on the floor without waiting for the offer. He does shit like that all the time. "Hey Wally, I was sitting around the other day and I thought of a new idea for a show." Diego says, sitting down an opening up a Coke of his own. He makes sure to leave one hand free, in order to accompany his story with exaggerated flourishes. "See, it's about this guy, and he's always driving around getting stoned. And then one day he's driving along this country road and he passes this sign that says 'Beartopia', and the next thing he knows he's looking at this big anthropomorphic bear in overalls and a straw hat standing outside, waving at him, saying 'Howdy'. And out from behind the bear's legs comes this little baby bear, also anthropomorphic, who's all like 'Hey Pa, we got some company?' And it's fuckin' Beartopia, man, the happiest, most peaceful place on Earth, no worries in sight. Except the thing is, the guy can only see the bears when he's high!" "D, what the fuck are you talking about?" Wally asks, slightly pissed, as confused as anybody would be after hearing that. He sniffs the air, understanding appearing on his face as he looks down at the bowl on the table. "You're stoned off your ass again. Didn't you tell me you were quitting a month ago." "Well, I...." "Never mind, that's not what I'm here to talk about," he says, closing his eyes. He briefly massages his forehead before reopening his eyes with a mischievous smile. "You little schemer. That GTT match? I have no idea how you pulled it off, but that was nicely done. Should have been me, of course, but hey, whatever, you had the chance, you jumped on it, good for you. But I've gotta know, how'd you get a hold of that contract? I know there's no way McKenzie signed off on that. So what's the deal?" Diego's mouth turns up. He's hesitant to speak, already anticipating Wally's reaction. He takes a sip of his Coke, then places the can back down on the table. "Well, it must have been a week after the news dropped about McKenzie. It was after the cops were done talking to me, for sure. It was really weird, though. The thing was just sitting in my mailbox one day. The confirmation from the GTT board, that is. The thing had already been mailed out with the fat bastard's signature. Before he died, if you want to believe the date on the thing." "Oh come on, I don't believe that," Wally says, shaking his head. "Who the fuck besides you would send in a signed contract to the GTT with your name on it. You're not exactly Mr. Popularity, you know." Diego can only shrug in response, his expression more bemused than Wally's. "Come on, you can tell me," Wally says, smiling, nodding, eyebrow raised in complicity. "What, you broke into the old man's office and pulled the thing out of his drawer, right? Forged his name, sent it in? I won't tell anyone. Hell, I can respect that, doing what you can to get ahead in the business. It's like everything I've been telling you is finally starting to get through to you." "I wouldn't know where to even begin looking for the thing, or what I was even looking for. Believe me, it really just came in the mail." "D, you can lie to the GTT board, and lie to the fans, you can even lie to the police. But don't lie to me. It's disrespectful. You know I've been nothing but straight with you since we met." "But it is the truth!" Diego says, getting upset now that Wally is still shaking his head. "I don't believe it, either, but that's what happened." Wally crosses his arms, eyes narrowing in distrust. "Something's fishy about all this, D. I don't like it one bit. Not one bit at all. If you think you can hide what really happened from me, you're wrong. I'm gonna find out." He looks at the door briefly, then down at the bowl of marijuana on the table, before finally returning to eye contact with Diego. "I'm going to leave in a minute, but let me get a hit of that bowl first." Diego holds Wally's gaze for a three count before signaling his consent with a wave of his hand. Wally, still looking unhappy, lifts the bowl to his mouth, lights it, and takes a deep hit. "Don't think this makes us cool," he says, his voice tiny near the end as he finishes with a cough, smoke spilling out into the air. Another Night on the Town Arms pumping, sweat pouring down his face, down his hairy back, his exposed paunch gleaming in the moonlight, a shirtless Jimbo is sprinting down the street, running as if his life depended on it, a grotesquely obese antelope with a lion on his tail. Panic on his face, he doesn't have a clue where he is or how he got here. Some subdivision in the suburbs of the city by the looks of things, yeah, but he's not about to slow down to catch the name. The sound of his pursuers has long since faded, but that doesn't mean he's safe. He's got to keep going, keep going, he knows. This isn't the first time he's ended his night on the run. The night started off innocent enough. Wally had given him a call earlier, asked him to come out to the bar. Seemed he had stopped by D's place earlier in the day, had a mouthful to tell him about the guy. Jimbo didn't particularly like to sit around and listen to Wally bitch all night, but he'd had no social life to speak of since his lifetime ban from the comedy club was handed down, which left Wally as his only alternative to a night at home watching network TV. And at least he didn't feel as bad drinking when other people were around. He'd arrived at the bar to find Wally already talking with "Major" Mike Valentine, their DFW roster mate. He was a bit relieved that someone was there to share the burden of conversation, as a night of talking with Wally could sometimes have an enervating effect on a guy. And Mike was good people, Jimbo thought, even if Wally did talk shit about him being a deadweight has-been behind his back. Yeah, he wasn't the greatest wrestler, but really none of them were, Jimbo least of all. And he was a lot more down to earth than Wally, that was for sure, even if the guy was a tad pessimistic. "So what are you two crazy bastards talking about?" Jimbo asked, placing an extra pitcher of beer on the table as he took a seat. "It seems like Wally has a secret admirer," Mike said, turning to Jimbo with a rare smirk on his face. "Oh fuck you," Wally said, not appreciating the attempt to make him look bad at all, even if he was the one who brought up the topic originally in his conversation with Mike. "I was just telling him about the time I caught Benny boy trying to take my picture, that seven foot faggot." "Oh, that," Jimbo said, nodding his head. "Yeah, that was pretty fucked, wasn't it." "The worst part was his excuse!" Wally said, as if he were still in disbelief, despite having rehashed this story with half the DFW roster. "That I looked so cool that he just wanted to take my picture to show to the guys at his work. Either the guy's trying to suck my dick or the guy's trying to suck my dick." "I actually feel sorry for the guy," Mike said, smirk dissolved into an unamused half smile. "I work with autistic guys that have a better feel for interpersonal communication than Ben. There must be something about professional wrestling that just pulls in the socially incompetent." Mike turned to Jimbo, raising an eyebrow ironically. Jim felt a jolt of panic, for a moment unable to tell if the veiled barb was aimed at Wally or himself. By the time Wally got around to bringing up the topic of Diego, Jim's head was already feeling hazy. It was near the end of the third pitcher or maybe the beginning of the fourth, as Wally droned on about Diego's twisted plans and Mike rolled his eyes sarcastically, somewhere around this time he began to notice a familiar creeping sensation crawling back from out of the dark alleys of his mind to sit with him for a while, keep him company. He wasn't feeling funny at all, he thought, and though he knew there's no more deplorable specimen than the weeping clown, that old Paggliacci story, that's how he felt. As the alcohol worked its fingers into his brain with its deep, numbing massage, Jimbo felt the heaviness of depression settle his chest, the accumulated weight of all his failures in life and love, wrestling and comedy, pressing down on him, squeezing the joy from his heart. The two men across from him were engaged in an increasingly heated argument regarding the future of DFW and though he had his own personal stake in the company, Jimbo couldn't make himself feel interested at all. It was all rubbish, how could they not see it, not smell it, that noxious, rotting odor, that soul stink, stinking all the worse because of the nothingness in which it is suspended. It surrounded their lives, it was their lives. He felt like crying, but the thought of what was sure to be Wally's unsympathetic, belittling response was something he was not prepared to deal with at the moment. So he pressed it down inside himself, where it began to fester and grow into something ugly, nasty, and brutish. What is anger but concentrated sadness, formed with pressure over time, diamondized grief? Though he had no actual recollection of the incident itself, he was able to piece it together from secondhand stories in the days to come. As the night wore on and they traveled from bar to bar, drinks piling up, Mike had noted the change in Jimbo's demeanor. He had become progressively quieter, drawing back almost entirely from the conversation. Seeing the coals smoldering in his eyes, Mike suggested it was time to call it night. Wally, oblivious, called him a pussy, but reluctantly agreed. So the three of them went outside to hail a taxi, Jimbo three steps behind the other two, lost in thought. Wally and Mike climbed into the back seat of taxi, leaving Jimbo to sit up front with the driver, a middle-aged Mexican by the sound of his accent. The taxi had already been heading for two minutes in the direction of Wally's place, their first destination, when Jim started to flip his lid. "There's no seatbelt up here. Why the fuck isn't there a seatbelt up here?!" It was at this point that Wally, having been present during previous incidents involving a drunken Jimbo, and knowing that they rarely end pleasantly, finally began to worry. All the snark disappeared from his voice as he attempted to talk Jimbo back into a calmer, more rational frame of mind. "Jimbo, I've ridden with you over a dozen times and I've never seen you once wear a seatbelt. Why does it matter now." "No. No! That's not the point, Wally. "Who are you calling a c**t, you fat mother...." the driver snarled, turning to the side to give him a dirty look. Things only escalated from there, with the two exchanging a slew of insults over the protests of the two men in the backseat. It finally reached the point where Jimbo, seeing the driver reach in his direction, grabbed the man by the collar and delivered a headbutt to his nose. There was a unnatural cracking sound upon impact, as the driver slumped backward, blood flowing down his mouth and chin. To make matters worse, Jimbo had neglected to take into account that the car was in motion, and three seconds later the car swerved off the road onto the sidewalk, hitting a lamppost with enough force to bend it over backward. "Fuck! Oh shit!" Jimbo was awoken by his friends shaking him. He pushed away from the deployed airbag, suddenly realizing the shit he had gotten himself into. The driver was still alive, but looking barely conscious slumped in his seat, eyes fluttering weakly. Kicking the door twice, fiddling with the handle frantically he finally swung the door open. Taking one last look at the inside of the taxi, he saw what the driver had been reaching for. There had been a seatbelt after all, trapped inside the fold where the back meets the seat. There was no time for apologies, though. Jimbo knew if the police took him in for this, it could very well cost him his job. Kindergartens aren't exactly known to employ teachers with charges of felony battery, among other things. Despite the calls of his friends to follow them, he immediately began booking it in the opposite direction, desperate to get as far away from the scene as possible. Two hours later, he was still running. He had ripped his shirt badly when jumping over a fence, then later discarded it, thinking it too conspicuous. Later, he had the bad luck to be caught cutting through the backyard of tall angry looking man who had mistaken the drunken, shirtless Jimbo for a creeping pedophile in his bushes. After a kilometer or so the man had finally stopped chasing him, and now all he wants to do is stop, catch his breath, a fat man is not designed to run like this, but the sound of police sirens faint in the distance only serve to spur him on further, farther, faster. Don't worry about Jimbo, though. We all have our bad days. He'll be feeling funny again tomorrow.
