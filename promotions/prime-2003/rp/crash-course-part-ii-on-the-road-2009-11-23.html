Crash Course: Part II - On The Road

23 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23-11-09 With every mile of blacktop put behind him, he could feel part of his soul draining away onto the road. The GTT was just a memory now, punctuated not by his hand being held aloft in the final but with Tony Gamble claiming victory over him for a second time in just the second round. Although he was set to power through the Jewel in the Crown after decimating Troy Douglas in his first round match, that Grin hounded him every time he thought about work. He imagined this must be how Douglas felt after thrice coming up short against him in contest. Von Kelsig never conceived that he would ever hit a barrier he couldn't break through in this business, never mind one that was about as physically imposing as some of the children who attended PRIME broadcasts. When he came crashing back onto television nearly a year ago at King of Kings he was ready to rain hell down on everyone, and he did. In singles competition only Snow, Tsonda and Rhine were capable of besting him. Three legends in their own right. Then? Along came Gamble. Von Kelsig should have taken the night off. Anyone in their right mind would have opted for a leave of bereavement to mourn the loss of a loved one. Gloria Carmichael had been his other half; his partner and lover, for three years. In fact it would have been three years to the day that they had first met if he could only see past the disgust to remember such detail. She broke his heart, and in turn he went into ReVolution 204 ready to break bodies, if only to take his mind off the callous bitch. Contemplating the affair would have driven him stir crazy, but sitting at home in the very house where she was slain would have killed him. Suffice to say, squaring off against Gamble provided no respite from the torment, and if anything proved more of a hindrance which eventually cost him the match. The worst part? That single loss legitimized Gamble's return to form, when once he was a running joke shared by the whole company he was now a bonafide threat to any and all in PRIME. Still, that second loss at the GTT was the reason Von Kelsig was driving from Tennessee to Texas with his face tripping. The rental truck was a far cry from his once fierce F-150, which by now had probably been melted down and recycled into some inane green product if it wasn't festering in a junkyard somewhere. Each year Von Kelsig traded his current model in for the new edition of the pick-up, and Ford were more than happy to accommodate him and his reputation. He had arranged to pick up his new 2010 model in Knoxville after ReVolution 213. With his bank account he could have been racking up air miles between venues or being chauffeur driven to each ReVolution, but there was something romantic about trekking over the highways and by-ways to get to the next show. That romance fizzled out the moment he laid eyes on the biscuit tin he was enduring now. The 1966 Chevy C10 was held together by rust if nothing else. A gaudy red paint job poorly masked the beaten bodywork, and the exhaust spluttered every time he pressed the accelerator, as if pleading with him to just pull over and let it die in peace. He didn't even notice the faults at first, too consumed with worry and doubts about making the trip between shows to even question the single cab. Signing off on it he squeezed into the driver seat and took off, aiming for his destination with tunnel vision. While his kit bag and cases slid around in the bed under cover of a flimsy tarp cover, a smaller man-bag filled with the essentials sat next to him on the passenger seat. Among his wallet and cell and papers were three folded pages stapled together at the top. While he should have been heading straight to Knoxville, the paper in his bag demanded he travel back to Waco. It was a summons from the local civil court to appear as the plaintiff against the defendant Derek Hamilton, the driver of the car involved in the collision that would have killed Von Kelsig weren't he buckled up within the confines of the tank-like F-150. It had been a month since the accident. The aftermath of the Cell match at the Great American Nightmare was gruelling enough to cope with, but coupled with the accident Von Kelsig had a lot of healing to do while still actively competing. His nose was crooked, but the pain from the break was gone and he wouldn't have to suffer that infernal D-Mask any more. Even his beard had grown back to within an inch of its once mane-like glory. The cuts and bruises of the accident had since healed, joining the likes of chairshot scars and thumbtack holes on the great canvas of war that was his skin. With his strenuous exercise regime he had powered through the neck strain to heal the whiplash as best he could, though it still nagged every so often whenever he turned his head suddenly. All that remained was to replace the totalled pick-up and see the ignorant prick that caused the whole mess answer for his crime. And it was a crime. Before he picked up the rental truck, Von Kelsig had contemplated simply flying out to Texas and back to Tennessee. The crash had jiggered something in his head, and in his travels to the GTT matches and ReVolution 210 and 211 he found himself much more aware of other road users. He was fixed on every overtaking car and every merging lane. Even roundabouts and busy junctions put him on edge, his heart pounding in his chest and his palms sweating at the thought of something happening. The only problem was that this was his life. He wasn't commuting a short distance every so often, he practically lived on the road. He had given serious thought to giving up the macadam for the clouds, as expensive and time consuming as that would be. After all, it was a statistical fact that air travel was safer than that of the road. In doing so however he would be sacrificing his freedom. Sure air travel was quicker, but it had its downsides. First class was personal, but a year in the air equalled two on the road in terms of cost, and Von Kelsig certainly wasn't willing to throw his hard-earned cash into sitting for hours and waiting for the onslaught of deep vein thrombosis with no room for exercise or pit stops. Besides, with promotional appearances and such it was easier to drive between venues. Hunched over the wheel even with the seat set all the way back he was crammed into the cab like a sardine in a tin. Sighing, he fiddled with the A/C as a burst of hot air washed over him and quickly turned to freezing cold air. Twisting the knob the cab continued to chill, and with a grunt he turned off the air con and shuddered as the freezing November winds turned the pick-up into an ice box. He caught sight of a shimmering silver object out of the corner of his eye, but before he could process it the BMW had already cut in front of him. The manoeuvre was nothing out of the ordinary, behind and ahead most other vehicles were doing the same but the suddenness of it caused a snap of the synapse that racked him with fear. "ASSHOLE!" he roared, slamming a large paw onto the horn. The Chevy croaked a feeble honk, and through the rear window Von Kelsig could see the BMW driver's head bobbing as he chuckled at the giant in the old pick-up. Gripping the steering wheel he ground his teeth and tugged until the rubber squeaked and creaked. A whimper escaped his throat as he realized his foot was shaking over the accelerator, and biting his lip he fought the nervous shivering to push on ahead. "Can't quit now..." he muttered to himself, unable to stop or go back. Only forward. ---- The courthouse was a polished Beaux-Arts style monument to justice, forged of granite and marble and concrete and decorated with statues of eagles around the base of the magnificent dome in the centre of the building. Atop the lantern above the dome looking over the land stood a large statue of Themis complete with scales and the hilt of a sword in her grasp. Six columns towered over the entrance, and the words 'McLennan County' were proudly inscribed above the doors in capital letters. Parking the pick-up across the street Von Kelsig unfolded like a blossoming flower from the small cab. Regarding the courthouse he planted his feet onto the tarmac and stretched his arms into the air, listening as bones cracked and cartilage popped throughout his body. Reaching back inside he retrieved his bag from the passenger seat and ran his arm through the strap, shouldering it as he locked the doors and checked the tarp was secure. Pressing the wrinkles from his black shirt he shuddered as a cold breeze blew through the street and jogged across the road, taking the great stairs two at a time up to the entrance of the court. As he walked up to the front door he noticed three people arguing quietly to themselves. One was a man in his forties with a military air about him, barking at a pretty blonde girl while a woman in a plain blue dress held her hands clasped at her lips, staring worriedly at the girl. Thinking nothing of it he disappeared into the courthouse. The inside of the building smelled old and musty, though it looked as fresh and clean as the day it was built. Polished mahogany lined the walls and the varnished floor reflected the sunlight peeking in through high windows, giving it an ethereal glow. A harsh, moustached face peered out at him from behind a plexiglass window in the wall on his left, one of only a few people milling around inside the hallway. Approaching the window Von Kelsig produced his summons as the receptionist scratched at his crew cut, trying to figure out why a mean looking giant covered in scars wasn't being accompanied by any officers. Handing over the summons, Von Kelsig waited as the receptionist looked over it. "Mr. Von Kelsig." he said with a tone of relief, "Have you been to court before?" "Can't say that I have." replied the giant. "Ok," the clerk continued, "if you make your way to Waiting Room Two an attendant will be with you shortly to discuss the matters of your case, which is due to take place at eleven-thirty." "Waiting room? I thought I was going straight in..." Von Kelsig muttered, checking the clock behind the clerk to see that the case would be starting in fifteen minutes. "Well sir, as I'm sure you can understand most cases take a lot of deliberation before finding a verdict. I can't put a definite time on when your case will be starting but as I said the attendant can fill you in on all the ins and outs of what's going to happen. Waiting Room Two is just down the hall to your left there." Stamping the summons, the receptionist handed it back to Von Kelsig. Tottering off down the hall he ignored the weary gaze of a passing judge as he rounded the corner into a smaller corridor lined with doors, above which hung placards noting each waiting room and its number. Approaching the second room he pushed open the heavy door and sighed. The waiting room was platitudinous at best, like an old Victorian classroom. High walls of stale white were segmented by large windows reaching from floor to ceiling that looked out onto the maple and sycamore trees planted around the sides of the building. Rows upon rows of benches ran down the middle of the room, and piles of old magazines were sporadically placed on random seats throughout the room. An old cork noticeboard covered in pamphlets and posters hung by the door, and Von Kelsig felt that any of the spirit lost beneath the treads of the Chevy along the way wouldn't be revitalised within this place. Taking a seat next to the door opposite the noticeboard he immediately opened his bag and retrieved his cell phone, thumbing through to the text messages and opening a new blank text. Hi how are you? Im back in Waco for court. TB if yu wanna meet up x Scanning his phonebook he selected Beth Marquis from the list and sent the text away with a soft smile. As he slid the cell into his jeans pocket the door creaked open and a tuft of fine white hair peeked through, looking around the empty room until a little face bespectacled with half-moon lenses peered around the door and smiled at him. "Hello there." she croaked, adjusting her glasses and taking in the great mass of man sitting before her, who just nodded politely in retort. She took two careful steps into the room and closed the door over with both palms pressed flat against the wood before ambling over and taking a seat next to him. She wore a white blouse with a little rose pin stuck to the lapel, and a black skirt that swished even with her slow movements. A dainty little thing that was either not intimidated by the giant or too polite to show it. "My name is Gladys, I'm employed by the court to assist anyone that has any questions or concerns regarding their case. Have you been to court before?" "No, ma'am." "Is there anything you'd like to ask or would you like to go over the process of what happens?" "Um...I guess so. I don't really know what's expected of me." Von Kelsig replied coyly. "Alright," Gladys replied, producing a series of cards that he didn't notice her bring in as she scooched closer to him and licked her lips. "When time comes a representative of the court will call you to Courtroom Two. You mustn't take any belongings with you but you can leave them at reception and pick them up later. Phones included." She showed him the first picture card, which depicted a number of seats in the middle of a room adjacent to a witness dock, while the judge's bench sat at the rear of the room. "You'll sit there in the dock, while witnesses and members of the public will be seated in the middle of the room there. The judge will-..." vvvvvvvt... vvvvvvvt... He felt the cell phone vibrating in his pocket as a text message came through. While Gladys focused on the different picture cards, pointing out various settings and procedures, Von Kelsig snuck his phone out and flipped it open to read the message. Hey hun! Day off but over at mom n pops. Wat u in court for? Call me. B x Grunting, he read over the message a few more times savouring the kiss and the hun comment and wondering if she composed all her texts like that. Slipping the phone into his pocket he turned his attention back to Gladys, who was just finishing up with the last picture card which was the same courtroom as the first but at a different angle. "...and in the event the defendant pleads guilty your presence won't be required and you'll be free to go. I understand the wait can be frustrating but unfortunately that's just the way of the courts. If you get restless there's a canteen outside at the bottom end of the corridor, and you can go outside for a smoke but you must stay within the grounds of the court in case you need to be called." "No problem, I don't smoke. Thank you for your help ma'am." he growled. She smiled again and struggled to pull herself up out of her seat. Von Kelsig held a hand at her back and helped her up by the arm, to which she thanked him and patted him on the shoulder. Before she could reach out for the door it opened up in front of her and the young blonde girl Von Kelsig saw earlier stomped in, straight over to one of the benches in the middle of the room and plopping down into a seat. Turning back to Von Kelsig, the little old attendant made a face as though to question offering her help, but plodded over regardless and sat down with the girl, talking to her in hushed tones. Von Kelsig studied the girl, who every so often glanced up at him with her big beautiful eyes as Gladys asked her the same questions he had been asked. She shook her head and smiled, and with another pat on the shoulder Gladys got back up, though the girl didn't assist her in any way. Pointing out the door she mumbled something else to the girl before taking her leave, shooting a smile at Von Kelsig as she left the two of them alone in the waiting room. It was the girl who broke the silence first. Cautiously rising up out of her seat she crept over to the giant and took a seat at the bench in front of him. He flicked his gaze from the windows to her and back again as she cleared her throat. "Hi, are you, um...here for the car crash case?" she asked nervously. "Yeah. I was the lucky duck in the truck that got hit." Von Kelsig replied dryly, trying to express his ire without intimidating the poor girl who already looked scared enough in his presence. "Oh. I was in the car with my boyfriend when he hit you. I haven't spoken to him much since." "Yeah I'm not too happy with him myself," he grumbled, "I take it you're OK?" "Well..." the girl replied, pulling up her jean leg to show a dressing around her ankle, "I twisted my ankle pretty bad when I tried to brace myself but apart from that I'm fine. My boyfriend's car had strong harnesses so I was just disoriented more than anything. How are you?" "Whiplash and cuts and bruises. If I'd been in anything smaller than a truck I'd have been dead for sure." "I'm so glad you're alright." she replied with an honest smile, extending her hand, "My name's Christy." "Angelus." he returned with a handshake. Her skin was incredibly soft and he felt it knead between his thick fingers. From the look in her eyes she must have been contemplating his too, before she shook her long golden locks from her face and sorted her blue halter top underneath her jacket; white with a caramel fur-lined hood. "Cold out, isn't it?" he continued, "You here yourself?" "Well, yeah. My Mom and Dad dropped me off, I was outside with them when you came in." she replied, remembering his massive frame sauntering past her while her father made it very clear where he stood on her boyfriend and what he expected of her in court. "They might be in the room when I go in, my Dad pretty much lost it when he found out so I've been grounded for ever and banned from contact with Derek. That's my boyfriend." she continued, her eyes rolling a little in her head begrudgingly at the thought of being under the thumb. "How old are you?" Von Kelsig asked, probing her looks again, "If you don't mind me asking." "Nineteen." "Ah." he remarked, easing back into his chair and letting his arms hang by his side. It took him by surprise when she suddenly started delving into thoughts that were obviously bothering her, given the sideways glance she adopted as she spoke and avoiding eye contact with him. "Yeah it sucks living at home. My Dad is ex-military and a real hard ass and my Mom is just a quiet wifey-type, so whenever I'm in trouble she never sticks up for me. But they both give me hassle about school and whatever, and if it's not school then it's my boyfriend, and if it's not him it's who my friends are or what I'm doing. Makes me wish I could just get away from home and start fresh somewhere. It wouldn't be so bad if it wasn't constant abuse but God..." She trailed off and sighed, staring at the floor while Von Kelsig studied her eyes. She flicked her gaze up to meet his and he felt shame at ogling her like that. Instead of looking away however he simply smiled knowingly and ran his fingers through his beard. "Yeah it's tough I can imagine. But hey, that's what parents are for, right?" he offered. "I don't know about that." she scoffed, "I mean it's different nowadays. I'll bet you didn't get that when you were nineteen?" Von Kelsig shifted in his seat and made a low unsure humming noise. He didn't think she noticed but when he looked up at her again he caught her looking up from his seat after observing the shift. "By the time I was nineteen I was long gone from home," he began, "I had things pretty bad myself and I haven't spoken to my folks in decades." The word decades warranted a drawn breath from Christy, who hadn't even imagined that kind of time frame before. The idea of freedom had clouded her judgement of her parents, but as the word rolled off Von Kelsig's tongue she wondered just how bad things would have to be not to get in touch with them every so often, and out of politeness tried to change the subject. "Wow. What do you do for a living?" she asked, unaware of just how important his answer would be. "I'm a professional wrestler." he replied, catching the surprised look in her eye, "I know I know, you had me pegged for Wall Street or the catwalk." he chuckled. She giggled, and he felt his heart penetrated by the innocence of that wonderful noise. "I thought you might be a wrestler or a fighter or something." she remarked, "You look...tough." "Yeah, that's the thing about looks..." Von Kelsig muttered. "So," Christy continued, unaware of his mumbling, "that's pretty interesting. Are you one of those TV millionaire wrestlers then?" In retort Von Kelsig reached into his bag and produced a black XXXL t-shirt. Holding it by the shoulders he let it unfurl to reveal a glossy print of himself, poised with barbed wire wrapped around both hands and blood running down his arms while slobber flew from his roaring maw. Grinning at Christy's whoop of awe he turned it around to reveal the words "The Murder Show" written in a messy blood-style script. "You could say that." "Wow," she replied, a response Von Kelsig was becoming used to, "that's amazing." "It's not all glamorous." he said in the style of a warning as he bunched up the shirt and tucked it away into his bag again. "I guess so. I can tell that by the scars." she uttered, ogling the many imperfections spread across his exposed arms. "Oh no. They're more like mementos than anything. I'm talking about the long hours on the road and the boredom and the loneliness. It's only excitement and glitz for a couple hours every Wednesday. Everything else is hard work and perseverance." he explained. "But if you're making lots of money being on TV isn't it just a case of going to the gym then flying out from your mansion for the show and stuff?" she asked, expressing that innocence again. "Oh God no!" Von Kelsig laughed heartily, "Sure a few guys do that, but it's not all peachy for everyone. You've got personal appearances between venues, segments to film and so on. I'm on the road so much I just sold my house in Tennessee. I don't even have a fixed address, all my mail goes through the company's headquarters. There's a hell of a lot of work to do behind the scenes, and unless you're in a tag team or know someone pretty well on the road you travel by yourself. You were talking about leaving home, well I can tell you that for me was one of the toughest choices I ever made...and I haven't stopped paying for it ever since." "So why not just fly first class everywhere then? At least you'd have people around you to talk to." Christy probed, trying to justify her earlier remarks as well as question his choices. "Well it's swings and roundabouts. Funnily enough I was thinking about this earlier before I came here. I travel by car because most of the time it's more efficient than flying...that said the little stunt your boyfriend pulled made me question all that. First time in over ten years on the road I've ever been in a major accident, and it wasn't even my fault. Boy's got a lot to answer for..." Von Kelsig growled. "I'm sorry." Christy replied sympathetically, then after a momentary pause said, "I don't even know if he's going to plead guilty or not." "If he knows what's good for him he will." Von Kelsig chuckled, though his eyes were serious, "He'd be safer behind bars than facing me with a 'not guilty' plea." Christy muttered something under her breath and sighed. They sat in silence for what felt like an eternity while various witnesses and plaintiffs for other cases wandered into the room and waited for their names to be called. ---- By the time Gladys poked her head back through the door over two hours later the occupants of the room looked like zombies. Many perked up at seeing the little old attendant show face again, after taking just three people away in the last hour they were desperate to have their names called. "Ahem..." she inadvertently teased, licking her lips and staring over everyone, "Mister Lennon if you'd like to make your way to Court Three now..." From the back of the room a sigh of relief rose up with a tall, stocky gentlemen in a bowler hat. Wiping the sweat from his brow he passed by Gladys as she hung onto the door and studied the disappointment in the faces of the others. "Oh...Christina Wilson and Angelus Von Kelsig?" she asked, leaning around to where the giant was half asleep in his seat while Christy texted away on her cell. The both gazed lazily at her, expecting nothing but more hours to add to the wait. "The defendant has pled guilty. You're free to go now, thank you." "Gotta be fucking kidding me..." Von Kelsig whispered to himself. Christy regarded him as she stuffed her phone into her purse and fixed her hair before getting up with the giant. Together they left the waiting room and trudged through the courthouse to the front entrance. Reaching into the sky Von Kelsig cracked his back while Christy yawned into her hand. "Derek made the right choice," he declared, "wonder what he'll get..." "I was texting him before he went in." Christy replied nonchalantly, "He thinks he'll get a ban and a fine. No jail time." "A ban will do him good. What about you? Are you gonna claim any compensation or anything?" Christy scuffed her feet against the polished floor. "I don't want to but my Dad says he's going to sue Derek into the ground so I guess it doesn't matter what I do." she sighed. "What would you do?" Von Kelsig asked. "I don't know..." Christy replied, sucking air in through her teeth, "I think I still want to be with him but there's no way we'll be allowed with my Dad breathing down my neck all the time, especially now. Do wrestler's need cheerleaders?" she asked bluntly. He laughed loud enough for the receptionist to crane his neck and investigate the noise. Pushing the door open for her he kept the smile on his face. "You don't want any part of the lifestyle I have," he said, "it's tough enough for men, but the women? Well, you don't want to know how tough it is for the women. Look you might not think it but you need your parents and they need you. Stick it out and when the time comes you can spread your wings and find your own way in life..." "Ugh," Christy sneered as she spied her parents waiting for her at the foot of the stairs, "I don't know how much more I can put up with." "Well?" Von Kelsig could hear her Dad bark from the street while her mother looked on, and immediately he understood the ire she felt towards her parents. "Derek pled guilty, Dad." she called out as her little feet touched down on the sidewalk. "Took his goddamn time," he replied, turning his attention to the giant by her side, "Who's this?" Von Kelsig held back a sneer as Christy's father eyeballed him. Thrusting out a massive paw he introduced himself. "Name's Angelus Von Kelsig, friend." he said, "I was in the other car your daughter's boyfriend hit." "Bob Wilson." Christy's old man replied, taking Von Kelsig's hand and trying to match the firm shake. There were only a few years between them but the giant felt that Bob looked far older than his years, which was funny coming from a piece of meat like him who was held together by scar tissue. "Christy go wait in the car." he sneered at her, "Sir I'm extremely sorry this had to happen to you. No doubt if my daughter hadn't been there that stupid bastard wouldn't have had the cojones to put his, her and your lives in danger." Christy's big blue eyes welled up, sparkling like diamonds in the afternoon sun. She ran off across the street to the family W220 station wagon, tears streaming down her face. Her mother leaned as though to follow her but her husband placed an arm across her back and pulled her close. "My wife and I were devastated when we heard the news. If there's anything we can do for you, anything at all, you just let us know." Bob said. "No worries, everyone's alive and well." Von Kelsig replied, "I'll claim back my losses from the guy and be on my merry way." "If there's anything left to claim once I get done with the prick," Bob spat, "I put up with that asshole for long enough, but no more!" He could see the fire in the eyes of Bob Wilson, and understood why Christy hated them so much. Her mother was like a doe caught in the headlights, saying nothing and simply hanging off her husband's side like a marionette, while he was a raging mouthpiece. His glare slipped past Von Kelsig to the doors of the court, and they flashed with a different kind of anger. "That little motherfucker..." he growled, glancing back at Von Kelsig, "excuse me sir." He pushed past the giant, his wife tagging along as he stormed up the stairs. Standing at the front doors Derek Hamilton gasped as his girlfriend's dad raced towards him. Von Kelsig never hung around to watch happened next, instead turning and taking off across the street. Fiddling about for his keys he jumped into the cab of the old Chevy and started the ignition, letting the engine idle for a moment as he pondered on Christy and her family. "Tough break kid." He muttered to himself before pulling out onto the road and heading off back towards Knoxville for ReVolution 213. ---- Around four hours after leaving Waco, the needle on the fuel gauge threatened to dip below empty. It was another several minutes before Von Kelsig saw a sign for gas at a place just outside of Benton, Arkansas. Letting a great yawn fill the cab, the giant continued on towards the gas station and let the truck splutter to a halt beside the pumps. As the sun began to set in the west, draping a veil of darkness over the surrounding fields and woodland Von Kelsig stretched his way out of the cab and ambled around to the passenger side. As he twisted off the fuel cap his gaze was drawn to a flap of the tarpaulin cover which had wrinkled back from the rim, the knotting loosened around the area. Glancing at the clerk inside the station, Von Kelsig let his hand fall away from the nozzle hanging beside him and ran it along the seam of the tarpaulin. A skittering noise from beneath it made him pause and withdraw his hand at the thought of some kind of animal gurrying its way inside the bed. Poised over the opening, Von Kelsig held it down and worked out the knots along the side of the bed, ready to throw it open and let whatever was inside scarper away without bringing harm to itself or him. When the last knot was untied, Von Kelsig grabbed the tarpaulin with both hands and simultaneously threw the cover up and stepped to the side. "I'm sorry!" Von Kelsig gasped, frozen in place with the tarpaulin held high as he stared into the bed at what lay before him. There, nestled in amongst his bags like a little dormouse with her hair thrown across her face and her jacket draped over her like a blanket was Christina Wilson. "What...the hell?!" he gaped at the girl. "Please don't tell anyone, I don't want to go home! Please!" she begged, scurrying to her knees and clasping her hands around the rim of the bed, "Please just let me go with you, just take me far enough away that they can't find me, please??" Von Kelsig stared in disbelief at the distraught girl, and glanced up at the clerk who threw his arms up as if to gesture a problem. Shaking his head, Von Kelsig held up a firm hand to assure the clerk as he mulled over the situation. Sighing, he turned his back on her and reached for the nozzle to fill up the tank. "Get your things and get out of here..." he began, to which she choked and began welling up, "...go sit in the passenger seat and wait for me. Jesus Christ girl, what were you thinking?" Thinking she was being kicked to the curb, Christy whooped with glee as Von Kelsig offered the passenger seat to her. Throwing her arms around his girth she gave him a quick hug and leapt out of the bed, grabbing her things and jumping into the cab while the giant let the tank fill to capacity and replaced the nozzle. Scratching his head he made his way over to pay for the gas, wondering all the while how he was going to explain this one when the shit inevitably hit the fan...
