Bad Guys

undated

Date: Mid April “Sometimes, I feel like I’ve committed this awful crime.” “A jury of your peers found you innocent of the death of Anth…” “No, not that.” Kaiser leaned back in the couch. “I feel like I’ve treated so many of my family members and friends like such garbage. I’ve dropped the ball with people who love and care about me. That’s the crime. Why am I such a bad guy?” “It’s easier.” “What?” “It’s easier.” Dr. Ward repeated. “Maybe not in the world of fairytales, where the good guy always triumphs, but in real life… it can be easier being the bad guy. It’s much more convenient to deal with things and go places when you’re not held down by certain codes of ethics. Or, in your case, certain emotions.” “You sure know how to make a guy feel better about himself.” The doctor chuckled. “Did you do what I asked you to do last time you were in?” “Yeah.” “Ok. Let’s have a look.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Jesus... Twelve or fifteen feet might not sound like much, especially when compared to say… plummeting over the hill of a 200ft tall rollercoaster, or skydiving out of a plane 10,000 feet above ground, but I’m not jumping out of perfectly good airplanes, and I’m not at an amusement park. I’m near the top of a ten foot ladder. And from my vantage point, that’s about ten feet further off the ground than my ass should ever be. Stupid fucking fans and their right to vote. I’d like to find the guy that came up with that idea and let him know just how difficult his decision has made my life. Oh shit. I probably should have spent a little less time cursing everyone involved in this process, and a little more time reaching up and grabbing gold, because by the time I’ve decided I want to kill the man who invented the ladder match, I’m barreling towards the ground. The landing… I don’t remember… just the sound of 18,000 voices cheering as Flyer pushes over the ladder, and then, Bernie Roberts ugly mug looking down over me yelling “Kaiser! Kaiser!” I’ve woken up to worse sights. My vision is a little blurry, a combination of the blood pooling in my eyes and having just regained consciousness from a 15 foot fall. I reach to my left and grab the middle rope, using it to help pull me up to a seated position, and almost immediately, I feel like I’m gonna puke. In a fit, my stomach does flips, twisting around itself like a Slinky in the hands of reckless child. I’m dizzy, nauseous, can’t see straight… I don’t have to be a doctor to diagnose a concussion. That’s not going to make climbing a ladder any easier. Of course, that’s assuming I haven’t already lost. Panicking, I turn my head towards the center of the ring. The sudden motion doesn’t do much to help my dizziness. Flyer is struggling to get the ladder properly positioned, twenty minutes of battle showing its affects. If I want to win, I better drag my ass up and stop that Mexican jumping bean from climbing the ladder. I don’t find myself moving. Do I want to win? Is it worth it? Right now, I’m sitting against the ropes, my forehead is split open, I feel like I’m going to lose my lunch courtesy of career concussion number two… and at the end of the day, all this for a hunk of metal on leather? Dr. Ward sometimes speaks of the wrestling business like it’s a street gang… once you’re in, you can’t really get out. It just locks you there, keeps expecting you to give to it, until finally, its ruined your life. Once you can offer it no more, it dumps you. I don’t particularly want to get to that point. Dr. Ward didn’t… his knees are shot, but he’s got a pretty good life. Still, what I hear in the stories he tells, what I think I might have seen in the eyes of Lindsay Troy while we squared off at Culture Shock… it can be unsettling. At this point, ‘The Snowman’ is halfway up the ladder. It’s now or never. Do I need that title? No, of course not. And five or ten years down the road, I may not even want it. But right now… … I crave it. With the aide of the ropes, I pull myself to my feet and stagger towards the ladder in the middle or the ring. One hand finds a mid-level rung of the ladder, the other locks on to Flyer’s ankle, causing him to kick his leg, trying to shake me loose. Hold still, prick. I make my way up a couple rungs and quickly slip my head under Flyer’s arm, wrapping my own limbs around his waist. Falling backwards, I deliver what I suppose could be called a super belly-to-back suplex, but the truth is, there’s nothing super about it. It’s slow, it’s sloppy, and it hurts… probably me as much as him. Stupid. Why didn’t I just tip the fucking ladder over like he did? I can hear the fans booing, I suppose because they hate me. And while I lay there, looking up at the rafters, it occurs to me that if the fans could see me the way I am around Ellie, they would probably like me. At the least, they wouldn’t boo me just for competing. The strange thought dissipates, leaving a ringing of the ears in its place. With a groan, equal parts agony, exhaustion, and annoyance, I push myself to my feet. It’s just a second or so later when Flyer does the same and before I even realize what’s going on, we’re both throwing punches. Three… Four… Five…. After his knuckles have collided with my face about a half dozen times in a row, the thought crosses my mind that I’m not getting the best of this exchange. Reaching out, I grab him by the wrist and whip him across the ring. Hard. My weight and strength advantage send Flyer hitting the ropes with so much velocity that he spills outside the ring, crashing to the mats. It’s only now that I notice the ladder has toppled over during are scuffle. With an anguished sigh, I reach down and stand the ladder upright, trying my best to center it in the perfect spot. A few deep breaths fill my lungs with oxygen, as I take a moment to try and clear as much of the dizziness from my brain as possible. Once I feel some semblance of a clear head, I start to climb the ladder. It’s a slow process, ascending to the top… something that’s never set too well with me. Dr. Ward says I’m impatient, but I’m not so sure about that. I’d argue that a lot of things are moving faster than I’d like. Maybe he just means professionally. Steadying myself as I make it to the top of the ladder, I slowly reach up towards the 5 Star Championship. The fans have been booing the entire time I’ve been climbing, and as slow as my ass is moving… that’s been awhile. My fingers grasp the leather strap of the title. I can feel it send new life through my body, tasting gold for the second time in my PRIME tenure. It seems just as sweet as the first. Spinning the belt around, I grab an end and pull, several of the buttons unfastening. And that’s when it happens. I see him flying at me, spring-boarding off the top rope, soaring across the ring, landing with great balance on the opposite side of the ladder. There, staring face to face with me is High Flyer. The crowd goes nuts at his sudden burst of energy, and the last thing that goes through my head, prior to his fist, is to think… now I know where his name comes from. THUD My torso smacking on the canvas makes an unpleasant sound that Bernie Roberts is sure to hear in his nightmares for the next week. It’s just a few seconds later, through the continued ringing in my ears and roars of the fans, that the bell sounds and I hear the opening chords of “Crazy Train” fill the Pepsi Center. Shit. Vince Howard raises his mic and announces the result, as if the fans can’t see it for themselves. “Ladies and Gentlemen… your winner of the match… Hiiiigggghhhh Ffflllyyyyeeerrrr!” It sounds as lousy as it feels. The music keeps playing. The fans keep cheering. Flyer and I keep gasping for air, lying on the canvas, feeling like a freeway accident. This is bullshit. I’ve been robbed. I had set up the ladder. I had positioned it under the title. I had even unfastened half the buttons on the god damn strap. And then, like a worthless thief, Flyer had just jumped in and reaped the rewards of all my hard work. He’d robbed me of my dignity when he dumped that cheese on my head, and now, he’d flat out robbed me of my moment. As Bernie Roberts helps Flyer to his feet, raising the champ’s arm in victory, much to the crowd’s delight, I roll under the bottom rope and head towards the back. I have no desire to sit there and watch the celebration of a guy who’s just stolen the opportunity of another man… I already feel like I’m going to throw up. Halfway along the ramp, Flyer’s image on the PRIME*View catches my attention. Life a fifty foot mug shot. Fuckin’ Criminal. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ “You lost?” Dr. Ward looked up from the sheet of paper as soon as he’d finished. “Yeah. Is that bad?” “Admittedly, when I gave you this writing assignment, and told you to envision a career moment… I hadn’t suspected that you’d write yourself losing a major match.” “Oh.” “I’m simply a little surprised.” “It was just a story. Besides…” Kaiser reached across the coffee table and plucked the pages from the doctor’s hands. “fairytales always have a happy ending. Everyone knows real life isn’t that way.”
