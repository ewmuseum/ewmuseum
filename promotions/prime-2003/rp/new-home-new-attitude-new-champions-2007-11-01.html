New Home; New Attitude; New Champions?

1 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/1/07 RUN: A CONTINUATION As Told by Pops Nackedy I’ve never been much of a philosopher, so maybe these words won’t come out right. In addition, I’ve been running for about five days straight, so my brain’s a little off. Either way, I’m going to try my hand at a theory and see how it goes. You can preach about karma all you want, but it simply doesn’t exist. However, bad things will end up happening to bad people. There’s a very logical reason for it, and I’ll use myself as proof. I haven’t been what you could call a model parent. In fact, the statement wouldn’t lose any accuracy if you replaced “parent” with “manager,” “husband,” or the ever-reliable “human being.” So now, if you knew exactly what is happening to me, you’d probably say it was karma and wrap it up in a neat little bow. Maybe try to use it in a short story as some sort of moral: if you don’t take care of your loved ones, you’ll get in big trouble. But you’re not going deep enough. Here’s what I believe. Let’s go over my qualities, and let’s not mince words. In a situation like I’ve found myself in, there’s no room for anything but the truth, and all decoration to mask it just seems extraneous. I’m self-centered, I’m easily distracted…and I have a terrible addiction. One that I have never been able to control, one that has dwelled under the surface like a monster in a child’s closet, waiting for that one time of weakness to spring out and take over completely. It’s a bad analogy, but like I said, I’m tired and I’m going through withdrawals. These are my flaws, my problems. And the reason it isn’t karma is that these problems directly or indirectly led me to this exact position. My self-centered nature caused me to alienate my children. I found them after years of not being there for them, and I had a chance to make up for lost time. But I was more concerned with feeding off of their fame to line my pockets. My distractable nature was a factor in that even when my sons told me that my actions were going to get me fired, I was able to avoid facing the problem by not thinking about it. It’s not that the warnings weren’t there; I just chose not to see them. Again, that’s all my fault. And as far as the addiction…well, where do you think the money I stole from my sons went? So yeah, I’m no hero. I’m a terrible guy, and I did terrible things. Now terrible things are happening to me. Karma? No. This is simple cause and effect. I am the one who has these flaws. I am the one who did these things. I am the one paying for it. I am the one running for my life. You want to call karma it? Fuck karma. This is redemption. And it’s killing me. ACTUALLY TRAINING As Told by The Spectator Ian Nackedy was having some trouble getting used to the changes his life had recently gone through. For example, he was having trouble concentrating on his upcoming match against Olsig and St. Romani because he was worried about his current financial situation and about his current father situation. He was having trouble living under his older brother’s roof, constantly having to refrain from dropping the f-bomb, lest a tiny five-year-old repeat it for the rest of the week. Most of all, he was having trouble keeping his spirits up after another heart-wrenching loss. But having a gym in the back of the house? That’s something he had no trouble getting used to. “I feel pretty good, Gil,” Ian said, standing up erect with a dumbbell over his shoulders. “I’m lifting more than usual, I feel physically ready to challenge for the belts…what could go wrong?” Gil shrugged. “You mean besides the fact that someone is hacking into our banking accounts and our father is mysteriously missing?” Ian stopped, dropping the dumbbell behind him, and stared at his foster brother incredulously. “Yes. Besides that, you fucking tool.” Gildenstern smiled. “Nothing at all.” Ian nodded. “When’s the last time you saw Pops?” “I think it was when he showed up at our NWC:South Hall of Fame induction, looking for some money. I told him to stop bugging me and leave the arena.” “Family love,” chuckled Ian. There was a brief pause, and The Random Renegade sighed loudly. “Is this something we’re honestly prepared for? We’re not super heroes. Not this week anyway. We’re not hitmen, or movie stars, or daily news reporters, or government agents. We’re wrestlers. We’re not equipped for this sort of thing.” Gildenstern shrugged again. “Maybe that’s why we’re not winning matches.” “Either way,” continued Ian, “I feel good about one aspect of life. Sure, the world might be shitting on us more than a port-a-let seat during Mardi Gras, but I feel optimistic about this match. Don’t ask me why. Maybe it’s some sort of divine feeling. Things might just be looking up. In fact…” Ian trailed off, going into the house. Gildenstern walked after him, shouting “Where are you going?” “Need to find a pencil,” answered Ian calmly. AN OPEN LETTER TO VANGELUS OLSIG As Told by Ian Nackedy Sorry for the mocking video last Rev. You probably don’t know much about me or my brother, and that might be a good thing. We’d like to get an advantage, you know? The element of surprise and whatnot. But I won’t do that, because I’m starting to think all of this random silliness is for naught. So here it is. My name is Ian Nackedy, Vangelus. I am a reasonably good high flying wrestler. I do a bunch of crazy spots that make crowd members shout OH EM GEE. My tag team partner Gildenstern is a stronger guy than he looks, and he’s always pumped up for wrestling, despite his silly demeanor. That’s what you have to expect from us in the ring. It doesn’t sound menacing, I’m sure. And really, it’s not supposed to. Because I’ve frankly given up on playing any sort of game anymore. What you’d usually have to expect from us is weird insults, funny jokes, and gimmicks. Tons and tons of gimmicks. Did you know that Gil and I once came to the ring dressed as nuns? We won that match too. We’re not what you would call the consummate professionals, and you’ll probably take offense to that fact. From what I’ve seen you’re an intense guy who likes being intimidating. Is it true you threatened to murder an entire city? I read that in the gossip pages. You also haunted an operahouse once, I think. Anyway, that’s what you’d usually expect. But we’re not going to do gimmicks this week. And honestly, I’m not sure if we’ll keep doing it. It all boils down to one important reason. We left our trunk full of costumes in Indianapolis. But more importantly than that, life is going too fast for us to continue pretending like we’re the only things that matter. While we prance around in the ring, making pop culture references and wearing sparkly clothing, people are going missing. People are stealing money. People are sucking enormous amounts of cock. You should totally stop doing that, by the way. It makes your breath smell salty. Really, though. I saw your video (obviously, because we sufficiently mocked it thirty minutes later). And you said that the new reign of Vangelus Olsig continued that night. That coming into Great American Nightmare, you were going to come back better than ever, and eventually win the Universal Title. And it made for good footage, sure. A lot of people probably went through two buckets of popcorn. But there’s a warning I want to give to you, if only because I feel it’s fair to let you know in advance. This is a new team you’re facing. A team that isn’t concentrating on dressing up and getting laughs. We’re trying to win now. At least I am. All the ambition you’re claiming to have in that video? It doesn’t match the ambition in my little finger. Life is crumbling around me right now, and this is all I have. And if you think that your ladder to the top is going to go smoothly, you must be looking over this night. Because I’ve been goofy before. Gil and I have been a lot of things. We’ve been superheroes. We’ve been detectives. We’ve been lawyers. But we won’t be any of those things when we face. We’ll be ready. We’ll be prepared. We’ll be champions.
