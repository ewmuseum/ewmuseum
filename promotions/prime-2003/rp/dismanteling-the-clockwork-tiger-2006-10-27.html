Dismanteling the Clockwork Tiger

27 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/27/06 Dismantling the Clockwork Tiger Part I: Equilibrium Sitting down on the porch of his newly-purchased Cape Cod home, Damien looked out across the small body of water on which sat his home. There was a small fishing boat tied to the dock, one that he had become quite found of. The temptation to take it out again was lessened by reports of a coming storm. So, instead, he sipped his coffee on the porch, and enjoyed the chilliness that had rode in on the storm clouds. The sky became gray as he watched, and thick veins of white scattered across the ocean as the water became increasingly choppy. The wind was picking up rapidly, and Damien was curious just how hard it would hit. Damien Cruz could hear the pattering of rain on the overhang above his head, and he set his head back, thinking about the simplicity of where he now sat, and the chaos that had been a staple of his existence since he was 16 years old. For the past week and a half he indulged himself in the reading he promised he’d catch up on, for meditation, and mid-noon naps, and living room love-making sessions with his fiancée. Since winning the Intense Title, and taking time off to recuperate from the challenge of earning it, he had allowed himself the luxury of living like a civilian, and letting things like the price of crab meat become the central motivation in his life. He had left behind the cookie-cutter hotel rooms, the press conferences with people who didn’t even know who he was, the schizophrenic airport schedules, and the fine art of bleeding and bloodying his co-workers in a public forum. In a few hours, he would wake up and get dressed in the early morning darkness to do it all again. With a deep sign, Damien knew that he wanted to be back, and he knew exactly why, but there was still that part of him that wondered if it would be so terrible to just call in sick for a few months. His eyes snapped open at the thought, and he felt uneasy. No, he wasn’t at that place yet. Damien Cruz was still, despite the deep tissue massages, the deep sea fishing trips, the homemade dinners, and the hot showers pleasantly interrupted by Sylvia, not ready to walk away. And with a new challenger salivating to topple him from his position in PRIME, he could never walk away from that. So in that moment, staring out at the storm that was painting sky, land, and sea a violent gray and white haze, he stood up, and ended his time away. Damien Cruz was ready to return to what he had so eagerly tried to leave behind. Part II: The Bargain. His fiancée was awake before he was, assuming she had slept at all. She sat curled in one of his robes on the overstuffed chair next to the bedroom window, looking out into the blue landscape outside, sipping a cup of Earl Gray. Damien felt a tug of guilt as he remembered the many mornings that he left her asleep to wake up to an empty bed. He didn’t know if she was making a conscious effort to see him off, or perhaps a last-ditch effort to convince him to stay. “Baby, did you get any sleep last night?” Sylvia took a sip of her tea, and nodded, “Not as much as I wanted to, but enough.” “Are you okay?” She rose and walked over to the bed, reassuring him. “Yeah, I’m fine. I just heard what the storm was doing outside, and I got a little jittery.” Damien pulled her onto his lap, and asked her why she didn’t wake him up. She kissed him and admitted that she would have felt silly. She had spent the last seven years traveling the New York City subway at all hours of the night so a stupid tree falling had no business keeping her up. She rested her head on his shoulder and began to examine his hands as if it were the first time she’d seen them, and not the hundredth. She traced the scars of his knuckles. She massaged the joints that bulged where the bones healed badly. She pressed her hand against his, noting how his hands dwarfed her own. Sylvia made a ritual of these moments, and Damien sometimes wondered if she was somehow committing him to memory, piece-wise, in case something went wrong. As morbid as the thought was, he could not completely dismiss it as paranoid. It was in a hospital ER that they had first met, after all. Damien was carried in, screaming, cursing, and delirious with pain as his right knee ballooned up in a throbbing, red, blood-filled mess of bone fragments and torn veins. She, covering the night shift so that her friend could go to a bridal shower, was there to greet him. The history between them was long and complicated. It involved broken promises, repaired promises, eerie coincidences, selfish indulgences, selfless lies, and a dramatic late night reunion that resulted in an annulment from her husband and Sylvia deciding to go back to school to become a doctor. That is why she was patient while he continued to do what he felt he needed to, and that is why she could not join him. She was freshman again, and Damien a rising star. “I’m going to get into the shower, want to join me?” Sylvia shook her head, “You have no time for that. You have a flight to catch and I have a breakfast to cook for you. So go.” Damien was disappointed, but it felt refreshing to see that she had truly accepted who he was and why he did what he did. It wasn’t the same feeling that he was having an affair that she knew about but chose to ignore for the sake of keeping up appearances. It wasn’t that sense of abandonment as she looked through Bridal magazines and he he searched the closet for his pads. He felt, for the first time, that she took it as seriously as he did. As ironic as it seemed, she had become his cheerleader in his progress, and not just the long-suffering housewife. Damien pulled her into the bed, and slipped his hand under the robe, and she laughed loudly, then squirmed away, pretending to be serious when she announced herself off limits until he came back home in a few weeks. He ignored her, and pulled her back into the bed, before she deftly swatted him away. After calling a truce, she pounced on him, and took the easy 3-count from the PRIME Intense Title Champion. Of course, the fact that she was grinding her bare body against his the whole time made the pin less disgraceful. They kissed passionately, but she held to her promise, and he was left with a cold shower as a consolation. When the taxi pulled up, Sylvia sat with Damien on the front steps like it was his first day of school. Damien kissed her one more time, and then walked to the cab, his duffle weighing him down like an overstuffed book bag. On the ride to the airport, he soaked in the sight of his new hometown, and began to miss it for the first time. Part III: Stepping Into The Role. Night had fallen hard on that unnamable city where Damien was just hours away from his first Title Defense. He held his breath, and exhaled slowly, feeling the electricity crackle in the minute spaces between his cells. He felt the wholeness return to him, and could not help but celebrate his return to the PRIME ring. It had been an absence that was very necessary, but far too long. A lot had changed, but a lot had not. He was the Champion; he was the Gate-keeper. He was the one who would need to suppress the insurgents, and he felt giddy with the responsibility of the task. Damien had arrived at the venue hours early. He had arrived before the ring was even constructed. Damien Cruz sat in a basement storage room of the arena, listening to whatever aggressive, toneless, growling music he could load onto his iPod as he warmed up. He preferred to get his focus in that place as opposed to in some locker room with the others, or in a dressing room that reminded him of a bad sitcom set. Damien had been born and bred in dark, moist, cluttered storage rooms and back stage areas; so it was a matter of tradition. He would go up and do his corporate duties eventually, but he wanted that time to himself before he did. A smile creased his face as he realized how close it was to Showtime. The Latin Assassin was anticipating a bloodbath, and the potential was intoxicating. A voice echoed from the hallway, and Damien could swear he heard his name being called. He walked to the stereo, and turned it off, then listened again. He heard it again. He stepped out into the main clearing and saw his cousin, the magazine writer, tip-toeing through the tangle of pipes, and supplies, and old cables. He had a large messenger bag on his back, and was trying hard to remain upright. “Chris, what are you doing?” Chris Martinez was a staff writer for a magazine called Shooter’s Press. It had miserable circulation and often wrote way above the heads of it’s supposed target audience. The three-issue series on Utopian Themes in Professional Wrestling was hailed as a literary triumph by everyone but the actual readership. But for what it was worth, the writing was top-notch. Chris stepped over the last pile of bingo hall tables and handed Damien a folder full of papers.“Damien, I’m going to have to ask you to trust me on this. But you cannot fight that woman.” Damien chuckled, and began to thumb through the papers curiously. Most of them were bad copies of bad copies, and many of them had more black-out than writing. “I’ve been doing some reading on Sun Tzu, and I don’t like what I’m finding.” “What is it that you’re finding exactly?” “A lot of stuff I don’t like.” Damien laughed, and lead his cousin to the vending area at the end of the hall. There, he bought himself a water and placed a cup of coffee in front of the chubby reporter. “Okay, so start from the beginning.” “Fine, he goes; When I saw that PRIME had signed Sun Tzu, I did a little Google search on any of her previous work.” “Previous work?”, Damien asked, kind of knowing better. “Fine, I was hoping she had done some modeling as a means of breaking into the business. You know, you’d be surprised what ends up on the internet.” “Okay, so when you didn’t find the nude photos you were looking for, what then?” Chris ran a hand through his thinning hair, trying to compose himself. “So what I did find was a lot of links to government agencies. I thought it was strange, but kind of assumed that The Art of War, War, Armies, would all be cross linked and screw up the search parameters. So but I started to realize that they were referring to a person, not simply the book’s author. Damien, there is a dossier on this woman at the Dept. of Homeland Security.” Still skeptical Damien explained that his name ended up on a watch list after he did a speech at a Social convention a few years back. In this climate, it was not uncommon. “No, she is listed specifically. And here’s the weird part. Sometimes she is, sometimes she isn’t ,and sometimes she disappears hours after she is listed.” “What the hell are you talking about? And exactly why are you looking at the DHS watch list?” “Okay, when I first did the search, her name was there. That was on October 9th. Then, when I went back to the list to show my editor, her name was gone. But then I did a back log, and saw that her name had appeared on some lists and not on others. Damien, someone is either adding her name where it doesn’t belong or taking it away from where it does.” “Okay, so she is a Chinese Nationalist who raises a few red flags…pardon the pun.” Chris seemed to be fueled and encouraged by the suggestion. “That’s what I thought. So I did some reading and found other Chinese Nationalists that raise red flags in the US, kinda knowing that we’ve gone batshit crazy lately. And there are about a thousand that keep popping up. Then I looked on INTERPOL, and The British Data Base, and the list gets trimmed down to about twenty. Now, among those, most are drug dealers, or slave traders, or gun runners, or suspected torture generals. Here, look at this…” Chris slid a page over to Damien with a list of these individuals. “See, hers is the only one with a classified status. So that means she is very dirty, or they can’t pin anything on her.” “That can be a completely different Sun Tzu. I mean, as far as fake names go, hers is easily a common one.” “You read my mind, Damien. So I did more research. And while I’ll concede the Chinese Health Statistics is neither the most accurate, nor accessible, I found something very interesting. Seems that is no record of her birth, her education, her citizenship, or her existence. The first time she appears anywhere is in an article about her participation in an acrobatics program celebrating the Chinese New Year ten years ago. And then, she disappears.” Damien was entertained by his cousin’s enthusiasm, but was growing a little impatient to get back to the real world issue of his match. “Then she’s under an assumed name, maybe she was adopted, maybe she was sold to the gypsies and traveled a lot. There are a hundred reasons why she, and someone who shares her very common and very phony name, are hard to track and accused of unspecific crimes.” Chris smirked, “Even though you are mocking me, you have struck upon some very interesting points. For one, she has changed her name. According to the FBI’s records, which became openly available after Abu Graib scandal, she is listed under almost seventy five aliases. However, a very smart person realized that most of those aliases are just random names taken from the role sheet at a Chinese sweat shop in San Francisco. Apparently, someone uploaded a virus to the FBI database that masked the Chinese Names by randomly slapping on names from other FBI files, sometimes using the same name numerous times across numerous suspects. So now it’s almost impossible to pick the fake names from the…well, real fake names.” “That just proves my point.” “No, it proves MY point. Because the only name that appears nowhere else is Mei. No last name, just quotation marks. So guess what I did…” “You searched that name on INTERPOL?” Chris nodded emphatically. “And that name was associated with a theft of a Chinese sword from this British tycoon in Hong Kong. And guess what else I found. A picture of that man’s son and his date at an event the night of the theft. Now she is much younger, but I’d know that face anywhere.” “Brilliant work, so why didn’t the police put two and two together and arrest her?” Chris shrugged, “That, I don’t know.” Damien rubbed his temples, “This really is fascinating, but I bet half the guys on this roster have criminal records, shady pasts, and some smudges on their PRIME applications. Exactly what are you trying to convince me of?” “This.” Chris handed Damien another piece of paper. This one was an article written by an underground Chinese newspaper accusing the Chinese Government of using enhanced drugs and training regiments and creating an army of super soldiers. They claimed that these soldiers were being actively dispersed as a covert faction of the Chinese military, and were responsible for acts ranging from industrial espionage to large-scale terrorist attacks. The text was clearly a bad translation, and the quality of the text was sketchy, but the accusation was being made. And central to that accusation was the testimony of an unknown female who claimed that she and her sister were a part of that program until the drugs drove her insane and she was sent to an asylum where she was rescued during an uprising by The Western Rebellion, who had published the article. Damien laughed loudest at that one, and finally got the joke. “You know, I was with you until that last bit. Really, way to commit to a premise, Chris. Now let’s go get something to eat before the fans start filing in.” Chris was shocked. He thrust another handful of papers at Damien, and begged him to listen. When Damien realized that Chris was serious, he pleaded with the man to think about what he was saying. “Are you listening to yourself? You believe that the Chinese military has assigned one of the their hyper-enhanced super soldiers to infiltrate PRIME, and thus incite a global act of war upon the American people by toppling the standing Intense Title Holder? No, wait, better yet, they are going to subvert the culture of American pro wrestling to further their National and Global influence on the World Stage. Is that what you’re saying? They have the single largest military in the World, they have alliances with many of our powerful enemies, and they stand to overtake the American economy by decade’s end, and they have decided that the best use of their super secret, and highly-effective covert army is to assign her to this company? Let’s forget the fact that they are the rightful heirs to the Russian Empire’s place as the only Nation able to challenge our own. Let’s put aside the fact that they have recently put into place treaties with many oil-rich nations in order to gain access to petroleum that would cost us three times what they are paying. Let us ignore the fact that China, and its interests are holding a 325 billion dollar debt over our heads. China, as of today, is on its way to world domination, and yet they are sending this Human Killing Machine to PRIME to drive the nail in the coffin. You know, Chris, I love you, but you need to stop buying that lab-grown shit that you’ve been smoking.” Chris held up the paper again, but then let them drop. “I thought it made so much sense.” “It does, as long as you don’t have any critical thinking skills or sense of rational common sense. It makes for a great story, though. And the dots you connected are near genius.” Chris shrugged, “Yeah. It took me almost a month and a lot of favors I’m not sure were worth it now that I think about it. But anyway, I still think she’s a bad ass.” Damien shrugged, “Listen, I am going to face this woman like I’ve faced all of my opponents. And even if she isn’t this crazy Communist Assassin, I think she’s worth taking seriously. And I plan to. I have a lot to fight for and the tools to do it. I have seen her tear through some pretty good fighters, and I’ll be damned if I am next on her list. So trust me when I say that I’m not taking her lightly. In fact, I would be more than happy to see just how far she can push me. It’s been a very long time since I felt this alive, and I’d imagine the competition she’s bringing to the table is a large part of that. Dude, it’s like a fucking drug. So if you think I should be ready, rest assured that I am. I swear to God I’m prepared.” Chris hugged his cousin, and the two decided that lunch could wait. Damien decided that some more time to gather his thoughts would do him well, but the distraction was a nice way to clear his heads. Chinese Super Soldier sent to usurp the American Government via professional wrestling success…that was a good one.
