Don't Shoot the Messenger

21 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/21/08 (Note: This will be edited in the near future to reflect the gigantic chunk missing out of the middle of the roleplay due to poor time management on my part. I did not want to completely no show, so this is posted. --Sean) He wasn’t supposed to be here. David Walter Smith was not a posterboy for the sport of professional wrestling. He was not one of the faces of PRIME, not by a long shot. He was no Lindsay Troy. No Cozen—did she even HAVE a face to be a face of PRIME? No Devin Shakur. No Tyler Rayne. No Killean Sirrajin. That fact, Logic knew, had to be eating at PRIME. Sirrajin had failed two drug tests. Their Hall of Famer, the Supreme Machine, one-time face of the company, was a confirmed user. With the heat from the feds on, they had to do something. Events like the one that Logic had been dispatched to were meant to help clean up the company’s image. But with suspicion flying, there was only one man that the company could guarantee wouldn’t be a liability, namely the one man to pass the drug test to begin with: David Walter Smith. Logic. And so it was David, not Troy, not Cozen, not Shakur, not Rayne, and not Sirrajin, who stood in front of an auditorium of high school students, to tell them about the evils of steroids and HGH use. By virtue of being clean, it had become his cause to champion. This, in turn, meant he had to face a lot of tough questions from people. Questions about his colleagues. Questions about who he thought used, and who he thought didn’t. Questions that he wasn’t qualified to answer, no matter how intelligent he was. ************ Telling a bunch of kids that their hero is a cheat is not something I take pride in, but it is something that had to be said. Rafael Palmeiro and Roger Clemens both took the same tactic that Killean Sirrajin has: blind denial in the face of all fact. And what we have here in PRIME is absolute, pure fact: two positive tests for HGH. How can he have the audacity to expect us to believe his lies in the face of evidence? Irrefutable, logical evidence. We are a forgiving people, this nation of Americans. All he would have to do is admit it. Admit using it. Apologize for it. Tell people not to follow his path, and he would be on the road to forgiveness. But no. His arrogance is overwhelming. He expects his celebrity status to save him, his fans to believe the accusations false simply because he is Killean Sirrajin. Truly, I know not if Sirrajin has used his entire career. Perhaps his Universal title reign and his Hall of Fame status are built on lies. But even if not, I can say one thing for certain. His 2008 run with this company is inflated by HGH. What makes a man with Sirrajin’s credentials turn to performance enhancement? One word. Fear. Fear that he can’t cut it anymore. Fear that younger, fresher, and more capable talents will defeat him. Talents like myself. At UltraViolence, it is no disqualification between myself and the PRIME Choice. At least now I understand why he uses that name. He is as pumped full of steroids as the beef most people eat on a daily basis. In the end, Sirrajin will find out a simple fact. No amount of HGH or enhanced performance will make up for the fact that I am simply smarter than he could ever hope to be. I am a natural athlete. I am the sort of man that the high schoolers of America should look up to. They will not, of course, but I will be the shining beacon for the enlightened few who so choose a proper role model. It’s not as personal as you might think, though, Killean. It’s simply logic.
