They’ll put you in the nut ward. Because here in America, he thought, everybody fights to become top dog, and then to stay top dog. And maybe, just maybe, that is why the underdogs that get to be top dogs and there is nothing left for them to fight for, wither up and die or else get fat and wheeze and die. Lee Prewitt, From Here To Eternity The First Star: Raging Against the MachineAdvantage Wrestling Advertising Office, Houston, TX, September 17, 2002, 11:37 AM “Larry? You wanted to see us?” Nova peeked his head in the door, his eyes falling on a man standing behind a desk, middle-aged, balding, wearing black slacks, a white button-up with the sleeves rolled back, and red suspenders. “Yeah, I do. Come in here.” Nova entered the room, followed by Big Poppa. Larry waved his hand at the two chairs in front of his desk. “Why don’t you boys take a seat, huh?” They did, albeit cautiously. Larry wasn’t known for inviting random wrestlers into his office to congratulate them on being generally exemplary employees. Nova brushed his hair out of his face, back behind his ears over his bandanna. He pulled a cigarette out of his pack. “You mind if I…?” “What?” Larry said, looking up distractedly, as though Nova’s question had interrupted a particularly important train of thought, “oh. Oh, yeah…whatever. Listen, guys…we need to talk.” “What about, Lar?” Big Poppa asked, cracking his neck casually from side to side, “is this an official employer/employee meeting?” “I guess you could say,” Larry started, but Nova interrupted him. “Where’s Razor?” he asked, lowering his cigarette beneath eyesight level of the desk to ash it sneakily on the floor. “Razor Blade isn’t here,” Larry replied with a sigh, “he doesn’t actually know about our meeting here. If he did, he’d want to come, and I think there are some things I need to tell you guys without him here.” Nova and Big Poppa looked at each other, a mixture on both their faces of curiosity and uneasiness. “We’re all ears,” Big Poppa replied, and Nova nodded. “Look,” Larry said, his face hardening, “I’m just gonna be straight with you guys. The bullshit has got to stop.” Nova cocked an eyebrow, his eyes narrowing. “The…bullshit?” “We don’t exactly follow you, Lars,” Big Poppa seconded. “The hell you don’t,” Larry said thickly, “this comedy shit. All the weird crap you’ve been pulling, in the promos, on the shows…it needs to stop.” Seeing the confused looks still present on their faces, Larry’s face grew several shades redder. “Don’t play dumb with me…here. Fine. You want an example? Here. The end of Insomnia 4…Nova’s station wagon gets blown up, right? Great heat, if properly directed. What do you do? What do you fucking do? You attach a sail to its remnants and ride it down the interstate! What did you call it?” Larry began rifling through papers, but Nova saved him the trouble. “The Sail-Wagon.” “The Sail-Wagon!” Larry echoed, irritation growing in his voice, “donning pirate costumes and smoking weed and shit? What the hell was that, guys?” Nova was trying unsuccessfully to hide a smile and Big Poppa’s shoulders visibly shook with silent laughter, but Larry wasn’t having it. “Oh, sure…laugh it up, fellas. But as I’m the only one in the room with a degree in marketing, let me tell you guys something. That shit isn’t gonna help you in the long run. It isn’t gonna help Advantage Wrestling in the long run. The clowns get their fifteen minutes, but you think people will still be laughing a few months from now? Yeah, maybe…but they’ll be laughing at you instead of with you, because you’ll both be the fucking jokes of the company!” The smile had faded from Nova’s face by now, and he stood up from his chair. “Now hold on…we don’t need some advertising prick telling us what we can and cannot…” “You obviously do!” Larry interjected angrily, “you guys need to get with some of the promotional writers and work your gimmicks out, because I think you’re a mess, and I’m willing to bet I’m not alone there!” Nova’s mouth hung open in shock, threatening to suck any wayward insects into its maw. “Gimmicks? Get…get with writers to…WE DON’T NEED ANY FUCKING WRITERS!” “Word,” Big Poppa offered, still seated in his chair. Larry trembled with rage, his bald head growing more and more crimson by the moment. “That’s your problem. You guys think you have free reign to do whatever the hell you want, and maybe you do, but it’s all thanks to that man!” Larry thrust a finger back at a portrait hanging on the wall behind his desk, with Razor Blade standing around a circle of other high-level employees and investors of Advantage Wrestling. “You two fucking misfits are damn lucky there’s someone as benevolent as Razor Blade who’s willing to hire you! Don’t think I don’t know the score, boys. You’re both tight in with Razor from way back, and he’s protecting you, letting you run wild on his show…but know this: while you two muck up the airwaves, he’s the one backstage at board meetings getting his ass chewed on by the network because of your goofy asses!” “Maybe he doesn’t mind taking a little heat for something he believes in,” Big Poppa offered. “Maybe he doesn’t want to tell his buddies to stop acting like dickheads and fouling up his show,” Larry countered viciously. He quickly pulled open a desk drawer and ripped out an 8 x 10 photograph, slamming it down on the table in front of Nova. Nova snatched it up, staring at the visage of a tall, lean black man, muscular arms folded over each other as he glared menacingly into the camera. “So?” “So,” Larry said, putting an annoyance emphasis on the word, “that’s Rock fucking Startling.” “I know who the fuck it is,” Nova growled. “Well, take a good look hot-shot, and then pass it over to your friend,” Larry continued, “because that’s the market right now, gentlemen. That’s what the fans want, and that’s what the companies want to give them. We’re working in a Big Pecs, Big Tits, Big Heels business, in case you hadn’t noticed, and right now, the bad-ass reigns supreme, not fucking dick jokes.” At this, Larry directed an icy glare towards Big Poppa, who returned it with a grin, though his eyes weren’t visible behind sunglasses. Nova, on the other hand, was more than happy to play the Glaring Game. Another lit cigarette dangled from his mouth. “Are you trying to tell me there’s no market for us? US? The North-American Champion and the World Champion?” As if on cue, Big Poppa hoisted the World Title up and gave it a hearty slap across the front. Larry rolled his eyes. “As it stands? No, I honesty don’t think there is. The way you boys are working now, this comic-booky, slap-sticky bullshit? It ain’t gonna last, and neither are those straps. But it could. Why don’t you boys take a month off? Shit, take two months off. Whatever. You can come back all pissed-off, like the fans turned their backs on you or something.” Nova stared at him incredulously. “That…that’s the oldest, most trite play in the book!” “And it still sells like fucking hot cakes!” Larry fired back. “The fans eat that shit up, so you might as well start cooking while they’re still hungry for it, because right now, you two are the appetizers! The fun sideshow attractions before the fans move on to the main course!” “Stop with the culinary analogies!” Nova shouted, stepping away from his chair, “come on, man. Let’s bounce. I’ve heard enough.” Big Poppa nodded and got up, pushing his chair back to follow his friend. Nova stepped forward and pointed a finger at the 8 x 10. “Fuck that, and fuck you.” With that, he stormed out of the room, Big Poppa following him. Larry held up the photo, shouting after him. “I’m only trying to help you! There’s no fucking market for you, and there isn’t going to be! No fucking market, do you hear me?!” We heard him, alright. In fact, it wasn’t the first time we’d been bombarded with this message. No one will ever take us seriously. No one will remember us. There’s no market for guys like us. We rejected the idea wholesale. We basically had to. For our sanity’s sake, we had to believe that there were people out there who didn’t want the archetype heel-badasses. We had to believe there were people out there who saw what we were trying to do…inject a little life into the testosterone-fueled repetitiveness we saw in the business. “I’m a former XCW Heavyweight Champion and I’m the meanest sonuvabitch around!” “Oh yeah? I’m a former CCA Heavyweight Champion and I’m the meanest sonuvabitch around!” “Why I oughta…” There had to people out there who saw through the weed, the dick and fart jokes, the slapstick, the wonderful, liberating idiocy, to see that we were as willing to fight for what we believed in, as willing to shed blood in the ring, as willing to compete, as willing to excel… …we just wanted to have fun while we did it, and be our own mother-fuckin’ people while we did it. There had to be people out there who saw all that in us. There had to be a market for those people… …and by God, we were going to find it. The Second Star: DisillusionmentAW Presents Redemption, Locker Room, Post-Show, September 30, 2002, 10:45 PM “Ah! Ah!” Big Poppa groaned as Sapphire leaned over him, dabbing a cut on his forehead with an antibacterial swab. She looked down at him bemusedly. “Will you hold still?” “You’re not scrubbing mildew off the shower wall!” Big Poppa scolded, “try to show a little tenderness…” Across the room, Nova lay out across a bench, staring up at the ceiling. The area surrounding his left eye was already turning a deep purplish hue, and blood crusted his right cheek around his nostril and mouth. He stared up into the fluorescent overhead lights until his eyes shifted out of focus, the glare spreading out from the source and encapsulating his vision. He shook his head and blinked, and all returned to normal. “Fucking mother of God!” Across the room, Big Poppa had an arm out against Sapphire’s chest, pushing her away. “You stay away from me! What are you using, a Brillo pad?!” “Quit being a baby,” Sapphire said, trying to wrangle her way in to finish cleaning off Poppa’s cuts. Nova swung his legs around and spun around, sitting upright on the bench. He picked up the cigarette pack that had lain on the floor beneath the bench and pulled one out, lighting it and blowing out the foul first drag of paper smoke. “What happened out there?” Big Poppa and Sapphire froze in their battle, heads craning over to look at Nova. Seeing their bewilderment, Nova expounded. “How did we arrive top dogs and wind up alone in the locker room licking our wounds?” Sapphire moved away from Big Poppa, sparing him one last scowl that said I think you’re clean enough before walking over to Nova and sitting down next to him. She turned his face to look at him and began dabbing his cheek before speaking in low, calming tones. “Tonight, you lost. Big Poppa lost. You can’t beat yourself up over this, Nova.” She began wiping under his lip. “It happens to everyone…even you. It hurts worse because of who beat you, but it doesn’t make them any better than you, or any better than Poppa. It’s just what happened tonight.” She stood up and backed away from as Big Poppa collected his duffel bag behind them. “There…you look half-presentable, anyway.” Following Poppa, she walked several paces toward the door before turning around, an eyebrow raised. “What?” Nova asked, expelling smoke from his nose. “Oh, no,” Sapphire said with a groan, “We’re not letting you sit here and sulk this over. You’re coming with us, you’re rolling a joint, and we’re catching a late-night showing of Barbershop.” “Saph, I don’t want to see a fucking mo-” Nova began to protest, but she cut him off. “Quit being a bitch and get your bag,” she demanded, the smile on her face gone now. “Barbershop it is,” Nova conceded, grabbing up his gear, unable to fully conceal a grin that was threatening to mutiny against the domineering scowl still fighting for control of his face. Sapphire threw an arm over his shoulder as he reached his friends, the other clasping Poppa’s hand, and the trio walked out of the locker room towards the parking garage. Nova flicked his cigarette at a garbage can on the way, and smiled. Friends like these, huh? I was never the same after that night. I lost part of my innocence that night, a part of me that still believed that, when it counted, the good guys would triumph over evil…save the day and all that good, gooey stuff. We were the “good guys”…and we got our asses kicked in New York, by two scumbags who represented everything we were trying to rebel against. Poppa lost the World Title, and I lost the North-American Title. The Brotherhood of Evil Mutants defeated the X-Men. Bluto pounded Popeye and made off with Olive Oyl. Doomsday killed Superman… …only “Superman,” in this case, metaphorically speaking, was just my naiveté, still frailly held in place after my hiatus from the world of professional wrestling. No, after that night, I knew that fairy tale endings were just that… …fairy tales. Stuff meant for books. If Don McLean were present that night, he might wax philosophical on his lyric about “the day the music died.” I didn’t think the music was dead. I just realized that from then on out, I was going to have to listen to it with my eyes open. The Third Star: Possibility and RealityTriad Wrestling Presents Genesis, Madison Square Garden, NYC, January 5, 2003 Nova took a deep breath, running a hand through his hair as he compulsively ashed the end of his cigarette. Big Poppa paced in front of him, clad in an FBW t-shirt, his hands clasped together anxiously. “This is for all the marbles, man. This is FBW versus XwC. This is Supernova vs. Rock Startling for the Triad Universal Tit-” “Jesus, will you take it down a notch?” Nova snapped irritably, “you’re freaking me out.” Big Poppa stopped pacing and grinned goofily at his friend. “You’re right. You’re right, by God. It’s just…man, we’ve waited so long for this. You’ve waited so long for this…and I know you can do it.” Nova was genuinely touched, though he tried to conceal it for fear it would embarrass his friend. Big Poppa, as a friend and as an employer (the owner of First Blood Wrestling), had put so much faith in him, so much work into making him into a bonafide star. Poppa had chosen Nova first overall in the Triad Wrestling draft between his FBW and two other federations. He had booked Nova in almost every main event in FBW’s short history. He had, for all extents and purposes, given Nova the proverbial ball. Nova, for his part, had hauled ass with it…and it had brought him here, to Madison Square Garden, to the Main Event of Triad Wrestling’s first inter-fed Pay-Per-View, against “Tha 1 and Only” Rock Startling for the TW Universal Title. It had brought him here, standing in front of his best friend, his best friend who looked more emotional now than Nova had ever seen him before, emotional because of everything he had invested in Nova and how much he believed in his investment…how much he believed in his friend. Yep, it was all Nova could do not to hug the son of a bitch…but somehow, he restrained himself. They stared at each other, all at once comrades, as well as an employer and his star performer, a stare that wasn’t at all uncomfortable in its lengthiness. Both looked towards the curtain as the squealing guitar signaled the beginning of “Blue Dwarves and Red Giants.” The roar of the crowd rattles the backstage setup. “They’re callin’ for ya,” Big Poppa said with a proud grin. Nova nodded, suddenly very sober, and stepped up onto the first step leading to the entrance ramp. Big Poppa stuck his fist out. “Bring it home.” “You know it,” Nova replied with a wink, bringing out his own arm and knocking knuckles. SMASH!!! “This could be it!” “Nova! Get up!” “YOUR WINNER, AND THE NEW TRIAD LEGENDARY CHAMPION…ROOOOCK STAAAARTLING!!!!” “Rock is bringing the Triad Title home to XwC!” “Damn good showing from Nova!” “Yeah, but in the end, he was exactly what I always said about him…a loser!” … “I’m telling you, man, fuck ‘em. Fuck XwC. Their shows are boring as shit, anyway. This isn’t the end of the world for us like those pricks on the other side are making it out to be.” “You…you did everything you could, man. We were all behind you. Startling…that guy’s a nutcase, and an asshole. And when he wakes up tomorrow, he’ll still be an asshole…and you can get another title shot.” Nova had been half-tuning Big Poppa’s words out of his mind. Poppa had been going on since they first got back to the locker room, like an FBW cheerleader, trying to raise Nova’s spirits about the loss. Nova had to admire his friend’s stoic attitude. He knew Poppa was pissed as hell about the way things had gone down, but right now, he was defiantly putting on airs of confidence, of the comeback that would rise, like the mythical phoenix, from the ashes of this dark day in FBW’s history. “Those fuckin’ guys,” Big Poppa continued, his arms crossed angrily, “those fuckin’ XwC guys probably think they have us all figured out now. All pegged. Like hell, I say…” Nova wished he could share in his friend’s retaliatory excitement…but all he felt was numb…and in need of solitude. “Leave me alone, man.” Big Poppa continued, in all likelihood wholly unaware of his friend’s request. “That’s what I’m saying, man, chin up! One lousy match! One lousy match…” “POPPA!” Nova yelled suddenly, surprised at the volume of his own voice, “LEAVE ME THE FUCK ALONE RIGHT NOW, MAN!” Big Poppa stopped mid-sentence, his mouth hanging open for a second before he clamped it shut. He quickly hid the frown on his face, but his eyes betrayed how hurt he obviously was that Nova was telling him to go away, during his emotional epiphany about the future of his company. But he also understood, and so he nodded. “Alright, man. Alright. I’ll…um…I’ll talk to you soon.” The locker room door closed loudly behind him. Instantly Nova regretted telling him to leave. With a sigh, he lit another cigarette and rested his forearms on his knees. “Goddammit. Fuck.” He said it to no one, and at the same time, he said it to everyone. He looked down, and a Coke can standing upright on the concrete floor caught his attention. He peered down at it, curiously. It was a Triad Wrestling promotional can, and it had “Supernova vs. Rock Startling” written in silver lettering around the side of it, with posed photos of both men. Nova kicked the can across the room with a sudden loathing that he could not control. It clattered noisily. He sat, hunched over on the bench, clenching and unclenching his fists rapidly. “Oh, man…” “…oh, man.” Big Poppa understood, in a way, but he didn’t really understand. That was a bad night, one of the worst of my career. I wanted the Legendary Title so bad I got cramps in my stomach just thinking about it. I had never been a true World Champion before in my career (I had won the “Universal Title” in my first professional fed, but that place was a piece of shit, and it closed down a week later). Moreover, the Legendary Title was more than a World Title. It signified lordship over three feds, not just one. Seventy-plus men looked up to that belt as the benchmark for how success would be measured in Triad Wrestling, and I wanted to be the guy they all looked up to. Yeah, the belt was really important to me…but it wasn’t about the missed opportunity to get the belt. Not really. Nor was it, honestly, about losing to Rock Startling. Now, anyone who remembers Triad Wrestling will tell you, Rock and I hated each other. It was amazing, really, how in such a short time, we developed such an utter disdain for one another. It was as though we were born to clash. It seemed like every night leading up to the match, we released promos just berating the living hell out of one another. We were like fire and water. But again, it wasn’t even really about losing to Rock Startling, though some from that era might disagree. My beef was with losing to the Rock Startlings, dig? Not the man himself, necessarily, but all the men like him, and what they stood for…all the cocky, self-absorbed, stereotypical, machismo-soaked ‘asshole for the sake of being an asshole’ sons of bastards that had made my life a living hell up to that point. It was about a front office schmuck slamming down an 8 x 10 photograph in front of me like an ultimatum, saying “that’s the industry standard! That’s success! There’s no room for you if you don’t want to go this route!” It was about having the opportunity to grab that photograph and shove it back in that mother-fucker’s face and scream “I DID IT! I DID IT! You see the guy in that photo you love so much? You see him?! Well, I went to the big one, and I kicked that guy’s ass, and to quote Franky Sinatra, I did it myyyyyy waaaayyy, you son of a bitch! How do you like that?!” It was about having the opportunity to send out a shockwave in the circle of talent I ran with…to have every Rock Startling out there who ever got in my face take a step back. It was about forcing them to admit my accomplishment, to admit my legitimacy in their world. It was about having the opportunity for all of that…and fucking it up. From the possibility of everything… …to the reality of nothing. No, Big Poppa didn’t understand, not really. I didn’t think then, and I’m still not sure now, that anybody really understood what the outcome of that match meant to me. How could they? The Fourth Star: LegitimacyAdvantage Wrestling Presents Sole Survivor, Houston, TX, May 25, 2003 They were already waiting for him when he got backstage. Whizzz! Nova ducked a cork that flew overhead as Big Poppa sprayed a bottle of champagne into the air. It was a class act on his part, and Nova knew it. Big Poppa had been out there, had been a part of the match. He had wanted the AW World Title, too, and even though Nova was his best friend, it took some kind of stomach to suck it up and not only congratulate, but celebrate. The headlines were already creeping out across the web: “NOVA OVERCOMES BRONX, POPPA, STARTLING, TO BECOME CHAMP IN HOUSTON!” AW employees filled the hallway, clapping loudly as a champagne rain sprayed down on the new World Champion. Deciding to spring for the cheap pop, Nova hoisted the title into the air, and the clapping turned to uproarious cheers. “Make way! Make way, here! Don’t you people have work to do? What do I pay you for?” The crowd parted respectably as the boss made his way towards Nova. Razor Blade stepped out in front of him, extending a hand. “Attaboy, kid.” Nova shook it vigorously before yanking the man he always referred to affectionately as “a fat scarred-up bastard” into a hug. He knew at least part of the reason why Razor was all smiles tonight, and it wasn’t the PPV’s buy-rates. Back when Razor was a serious competitor, he had been sixty-six and zero before Rock Startling gave him his first loss. Razor never squared the defeat in his mind, and of course, Rock didn’t make it easy for him. The look on his face now gave away the victory he had experienced tonight vicariously through Nova. The marks would have a field day with it the next day, but that night, it seemed a revelation to Nova of some significance that he had won the World Title in Houston, the site of the first Advantage Wrestling show back when the company was stationed more out of Texas. He had really come full-circle from when Razor dragged him out of the depths of possibly permanent retirement to join the first Advantage Wrestling almost a year prior. Jesus, a lot of things had changed since then. A fifth of Ikon Vodka was passed to Nova in the shuffling crowd of celebrators. He stared down at the quadruple-distilled wonder bottle and grinned as a champagne-soaked Big Poppa clapped him on the shoulder and motioned to follow him down the hall. It was time to get violently drunk and call his wife, in that order. It was time to celebrate with the people who had always put their trust in him. It was time for a lot of things. I didn’t understand at first why Big Poppa was so enthusiastic to celebrate my victory. I appreciated it, sure…but I didn’t understand it. It was later that night, after drinking enough to make Jesus cry, that it dawned on me. It dawned on me that labeling this my celebratory party was awfully selfish… …because this wasn’t just my victory. It was mine, and Big Poppa’s, and Razor’s, and Advantage Wrestling’s, all for different reasons, and yet in a way, all for the same reasons. It was my victory because I had still never held a World Title, not one truly coveted by the rest of my peers. It was my victory because I beat the man who had beaten me, and I erased any doubt that that match could have just as easily gone the other way. It was my victory because I had done what I once thought impossible by becoming the top guy in a fed where I started off on the bottom rung. It was Big Poppa’s victory because he was finally vindicated, finally justified in his faith in me. It was like he could look around and point to me and yell, “See?! Wasn’t I right all along?!” It was Razor’s victory not just because Startling had gone down…that was the icing on the cake. It was Razor’s victory because, in his eyes, he had built me up from a snot-nosed kid to a top-notch competitor. Hell, in some weird way, it was probably like seeing his son graduate high school or something. It was also Razor’s victory because it was Advantage Wrestling’s victory. The PPV was a huge success, and Razor’s little venture of a wrestling company was on top of the world. He couldn’t be prouder. But most importantly, what made the victory so great for myself and Big Poppa was the same thing that had made the loss to Startling so hard to handle…the triumph of one ideology over another. We had beaten them. We had won, by God. The war was far from over (I knew it would never be over), but in the Battle of AW Presents Sole Survivor, the Rock Startlings had lost. And we had become what we always wanted to be… …the heros. Justified. Credible. Legitimated.
