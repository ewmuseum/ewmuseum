What should have been a victorious homecoming

14 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/14/07 When Titan limped out from the airplane ride home, he took careful look around. It was a flight that landed at four in the morning, meant to prevent any kind of contact with the normal crowds of an airport terminal. He took a tired breath and limped over to the baggage claim, his head heavy with exhaustion and weariness. It throbbed from the impact of Tchu's shots, and his body ached from repeated falls and the impact of crashing through the ring. “Not that breaking the ring was something I wanted to do again,” he muttered to himself, hoisting his bag up onto his shoulder. “I was so worried when I saw that,” came the voice from behind him. Titan turned and saw the slim little redhead looking at him, a tremulous smile on her face. Stephanie smiled at him, and stepped forward, wrapping her arms around his waist. She was almost comically small next to him, but an issue that never seemed to bother anyone that was the two of them. He smiled down at her. “What are you doing here?” he asked, the words gravelly in his voice. “I saw the show,” she replied, leaning her head against him. “I came out here to make sure you were okay when you got back.” “It's awfully late for that,” he replied, starting for the door. “Never to late,” she responded, falling into step beside him. By falling into step, three of hers for every one of his. “Not to make sure you're alright.” He couldn't help but smile. “How did you get here?” “I took a cab. I know you keep your car here and drive yourself home.” “Bad habit, I think,” Titan said, instantly regretting it. “Is that another one of those?” she asked, looking up. Her blue eyes flashed. “Another one of those ideas that someone is going to find you and come after you?” “It'd be easier than ever,” he said. “I'm rather high profile now, and I'm just worried about you.” “What do you mean?” He ducked his head as the automatic doors whisked open. “Really, if I go down, it's a bad thing, but it's just me. I don't want anything to happen to you.” “So,” she said, her voice taking on a rather sharp tone, “what makes your life worth less than mine?” He stopped and looked down at her. “What?” “Your death would apparently mean less than mine. Why? You're important to me, and it'd be horrible to lose you.” “It's because it'd be harder for me to lose you,” he said quietly. Quietly for him, anyway. They stopped, looking at each other. Her eyes shone with unshed tears and his hard face softened. Gently, he wrapped his arms around her, holding her close while she clutched at him with all the strength in her little arms. He sighed and let her go, and smiled. “I suppose we know where we stand with each other then.” “I guess we do,” she said, smiling and wiping at her eyes. Over her shoulder, Titan spotted his Escalade and reached into his bag for the door remote. He pushed the infuriatingly-small button to unlock the doors... And the black Escalade exploded. Titan's eyes hardened and he took reflexive action, grabbing Stephanie and pushing her to the ground before shielding her body with his. The shockwave washed over them, tugging at Titan's clothes and ruffling his hair. The wave of heat hit just a split second after, causing him to immediately break out in a cold sweat. He immediately dropped his hand to his hip, reaching for the sidearm he couldn't carry anymore. His senses were all tingling, except for his hearing, which rang loudly. He pushed himself to his feet and scanned the nearby area with a measured glance. There was no movement in the parking structure, though the few people in the terminal started bustling. He turned to Stephanie, who looked back at him with wide eyes. He reached a hand to her, and his massive paw enclosed her small, delicate hand and helped her up. “What... what happened?” she asked, her voice trembling. “Someone wanting me dead,” he said, probably too loudly because of the ringing in his ears. He looked at her, dropping to a knee and running hands over her, checking for any wounds. She clutched his head to her shoulder, for once on that level. He murmured reassurances and pattted her back and got to his feet. “Who?” she asked. He turned his head and tapped an ear. “Who?” she said, slowly. “This isn't from the Middle East. There are plenty of people for them to deal with over there. This stinks of personal.” Titan felt the cell phone in his hip pocket vibrate. He reached in, delicately pulling out the Motorola and flipping it open. It had only a text message, from an anonymous number, reading one thing: “Poison Tree member loose and responsible.”
