Realities

14 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/14/05 Airports are incredibly busy places. This is, perhaps, one of the most god awfully obvious facts of modern existence. We’re in the section of New Orleans International Airport that handles domestic flights, a section of the airport that would normally be jammed wall to wall with people coming, going and just generally being. But the one thing we notice about the people here now is the sheer absence of them. Those long lines you might have heard about on the evening news? Gone. Ever since one of the most devastating natural disasters to ever hit the United States, Hurricane Katrina hit, not only is the population of the city of New Orleans minimal, but the amount of travelers in the airport is few. Among the folks who’ve decided to come back to the place he has always called home, Mister Tyler Lopez and his fiancé, Passion. They’re dressed, as most non-business passengers tend to do when traveling by air, rather casually. Tyler is standing slightly off to the side of the check-in line, wearing a black polo shirt, a black pair of jeans and a pair of sneakers in black. His hands are in his pockets and he just stands, waiting patiently. Over at the head of the check-in line is Passion, taking care of the final arrangements for the flight, three very large pieces of luggage sitting beside her on the scale. She’s wearing a red T-shirt with a logo of the ladies who played Charlie’s Angels in the film (Lucy, Drew and Cameron) done up in a deliberately overly-retro 70’s style, a pair of blue jeans which cling to her like a second skin and a pair of simple white sneakers. Her hair hangs loosely about her, the highlights catching glints of light from off of the fluorescent lighting. Passion and the ticket agent handle their business and the ticket agent hands over to Passion a small stack of papers, smiling as she does so. As Passion walks casually away, another ticket agent loads their luggage onto the conveyor belt. Passion hands over the tickets to her fiancé. Lopez: All settled? Passion: Uh huh. Round trip, first class to Columbus. Lopez does a minor double take at this. Lopez: We booked business. Passion: They bumped us up. For free. They’re that desperate. The lady at the counter told me there are only 15 people on this flight. Lopez: Geez… Passion: I’m glad we came as early as we did. Getting the car searched, the bags searched, I mean if we would have waited until the last minute, we would have missed the flight. You would think that they would slack off on these things since the hurricane, but I guess not. Lopez glances quickly at his watch. Lopez: Well, we have about an hour before our flight. What do you wanna do? Passion: Can we hit the newsstands? I wanna get something to read for the flight. Lopez: Your choice of cover stories about this hurricane. Passion: Or a story about the shows Michael Jackson did to donate money to the Red Cross. Lopez looks at his soon to be wife as though he can’t quite believe that. Passion: Oh, I’m sure they’ll make it the cover story next week. Lopez: That’s right. Y’know, it’s good to see people coming together like this. Passion: Well, sometimes it takes the worst to bring out the best. Listen, I’m gonna pick up some magazines. Why don’t I meet you at the Starbucks in…oh, fifteen? Lopez: See you then. Cut. Lopez and Passion are now seated around a formica-topped table located in front of an airport Starbucks concession. Again, an area of the airport that would normally be jammed with people is almost deserted. Passion is seated at the table with a plate of chocolate cake in front of her, plastic fork on the side of the plate. Lopez is right next to her, a steaming cup of coffee lying on the table in front of him. Passion: I wanna start this one off. And I wanna direct this to every single wrestler out there who’s been wasting their valuable time accusing thinking that the tragedy the natural disaster, Hurricane Katrina would take Tyler Lopez out of PRIME. I have one thing to say to all y’all: Passion carefully enunciates every word in the next sentence. Passion: Fuck…all…you…bitches. Fuck each and every last one of y’all. My, aren’t we the little potty mouth? Passion: Let me explain something to the truly stupid among you… a whole lot of folks involved in PRIME happen to live around the United States. For many of you, Katrina was only a fear. My husband and I were lived it. It wasn’t a fear, we lived the reality. Passion pauses, getting a grip on her rising emotions. Passion: What happened is not some minor, inconsequential thing. This is not like stubbing your toe or injuring yourself playing flag football or any of the inconsequential little things that make up the highpoints of what you pathetic little fuckers call your lives. This is catastrophic, life changing, history marking stuff. This is a death toll that is climbing with every passing day. This is an entire city that was put under water. Round-the-clock coverage on every major news outlet for a week, cover stories on every magazine out there, the lives of every single person in the country changed forever…you cannot “exploit” this. This is life, and this happened in my backyard. This is me spending hours on the phone assuring my family that I’m ok. This is my husband not knowing for hours whether he’d lost one of his brothers. This is people roaming the streets desperate to find their loved ones, kids slowly dealing with the realization that Mommy and Daddy aren’t coming home… Passion trails off in mid-sentence. Passion: Fuck it. I’m tired, these idiots are far too stupid to be reasoned with and I am in desperate need of some chocolate. With that, she begins to tuck into the slice of cake in front of her. Lopez: Some may question the wisdom of what I am doing. That with all that’s happened, how can I even think of wrestling on a card? The best that I can think of as a response is this: I really don’t have any other choice. Lopez: For me, this is not entertainment. It is my livelihood; it is how I put bread on the table. For me, the choice is non-existent. I must go on with my life, go on with matters such Revolution because I have to. I have no other choice. Like people across the nation right now, we find ourselves forced to deal with realities. Lopez: Realities that I’d gather half of you don’t tend to come across all that often in that self-constructed ivory tower that you call home. Yeah, I know, how can I engage in something as petty as smack talk right now…all part of the job, I guess. For me, I don’t have the patience to deal in hypothesizing this week. I’ve had to deal in realities. And as far as you and I go…well, the realities have been quite clear. Over the tannoy comes the following: AMERICAN AIRLINES FLIGHT 46 TO COLUMBUS WILL BEGIN BOARDING IN TEN MINUTES. WE ADVISE ALL PASSENGERS TO HEAD TO GATE E6 TO BEGIN BOARDING AT THIS TIME. Lopez: That’s us, babe. We better go. Lopez and Passion arise, Passion grabbing her magazines and Tyler grabbing his cup of coffee. Both walk off into the further reaches of the airport, on their way to catch a flight to Ohio. As they head off into the distance, the picture slowly starts to fade away until, eventually, there is nothing left and we reach the end.
