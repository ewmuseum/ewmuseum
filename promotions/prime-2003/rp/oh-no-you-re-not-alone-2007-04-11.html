Oh No, You're Not Alone

11 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/11/07 “Damn.” Sun Tzu cursed herself as she as soon as she opened her eyes and her the sounds of running water. Lao had managed to wake up, get out of bed, and start the shower all without waking her. Her time in the States had dulled her reflexes. If General Chang was here, he would’ve torn her a new asshole. Still, Sun Tzu chuckled as she wondered just how the general’s stony expression would change if he saw two of his prize pupils in the afterglow of passion… especially ones of the same gender. “Hmmm...” muttered Sun Tzu to herself. “The old war horse would probably just think we’re like the Sacred Bands of ancient Greece… united in bed and battle.” Sliding out of bed, Sun Tzu stretched her limbs, grimacing at the sounds of the joints popping. It reminded her of an arthritic old woman. Even if wrestling wasn’t lethal combat, the grind of the road along with her years of no holds barred hand-to-hand training hadn’t done wonders for some of her parts. She recalled what Confucius said about the likes them not having to worry about cancer. Chances are old age could be added to that list as well. The soft sounds over humming barely audible over the water tickled Sun Tzu’s ears and brought her out of her grim precognitions. One could only live in the present and right at that moment there was a perfectly lovely young women in the shower that made the most endearing squeals when the inside of her knee was tickled. Sun Tzu walked into the bathroom and threw the curtain opened. Lao Tzu did not appear the least bit startled. “And just what do you think you’re doing?” she asked with mock indignation. “Being a better communist than you,” replied Sun Tzu as she stepped into the shower. “Wasting the people’s utilities by hogging the shower like this… you’d think you were the one stuck living amongst the decadent Americans instead of me.” The heat of the water felt good on the sore muscles of Sun Tzu. She looked down at her body, long and slim. Not like Lao’s shorter physique with its larger curves. She looked like a real woman while Sun was sill the lanky and awkward girl that got her first kiss behind the mess hall. Still, Lao’s body stirred other feelings besides envy from Sun Tzu. She slowly leaned towards that wet and pretty face. “And now what do you think you’re doing?” whispered Lao huskily. “Having a drink before the war,” answered Sun Tzu softly just before their mouths touched underneath the streams of water. The mood was very different two hours later as the women sat in a state-provided hatchback across the street form a warehouse. This was time for work, the work of soldiers. Still, the connection was there, one that predated that night in the mountain caves years ago. It was the bond of comrades, part of a well-oiled machine. When they worked together they clicked, just like the sound of a safety turning off. “There are two entrances, one on either side,” Lao stated. “I’ll take the back. In all there will be half a dozen men with Confucius. Ordinance will be nothing out of the ordinary.” “And you base this intelligence on what?” Lao Tzu rolled her eyes at the question. “Please. Some of haven’t had our investigative skills atrophied from sitting around hotel rooms and dining on room service while waiting to go on the telly.” “Don’t hate me because I’m beautiful.” “Very profound. What philosopher said that?” “The existentionalist LeBrock. Now let’s get this over with.” Both women exited the car. Lao Tzu looked across the hood at Sun Tzu. “Remember… he’s one of us.” “Yes. But we are two.” Lao Tzu darted across the street and down an alley. Sun Tzu gave her a minute before crossing herself. She casually walked up to the scruffy-looking young man leaning against the wall next to the door. She smiled sweetly at him. “Excuse me… could you help me with some directions?” As the young man straightened up, Sun Tzu fired two shots from a silenced 9mm, one in the throat and the other in the chest. “Can you tell me the fastest way to Hell?” Sun Tzu caught his body as it slumped forward. She held it in front of her as she opened the door. Two men were in the small office inside. Taken by aback by the ghastly appearance of their dead crony, they fell easily to a pair of shots to each of their skulls. Sun Tzu let the body drop and stepped cautiously through the office. She exited out another door and into the storage area of the warehouse. Looking across the dark interior, she spotted Lao Tzu amongst the shadows. Sun Tzu held up three fingers, a gesture that was returned. Good, that meant all six were taken care of. That left only Confucius… somewhere. Suddenly, what sounded like a pack of angry dogs barking tore through the expanse. Lao Tzu’s chest exploded into blossoms of deep crimson. Sun Tzu didn’t watch her fall, she was searching for the source. There was a catwalk just above her running the length of the interior. With a low growl, Sun Tzu dashed forward and fired the direction of the shots. She was rewarded by the sound of a heavy automatic weapon striking the concrete. It was followed by footsteps on metal, so Sun Tzu rushed to a nearby ladder. Reaching the catwalk, she looked down at the grinning face at the other end. Confucius. His hair had been bleached a platinum blond and his clothes looked like they belong in a nightclub instead of a dingy warehouse. But the nonchalant expression was the same. In one fluid movement, he leaped up to a ladder hanging down a few inches above the catwalk from the ceiling, grabbing a rung with one hand while the other threw a knife right at Sun Tzu. She dodged easily, even in the cramped area, leaning against the metal rail of the catwalk. That’s when every fiber in Sun Tzu’s body was set on fire. She recognized the pain… electricity, a hell of a lot of it. Her muscles convulsed, but there was noting she could do to escape the torture coursing through her body. Confucius released the switch installed into the rung of the ladder. He dropped to the catwalk nimbly while Sun Tzu fell into a heap like a ragdoll. Even though she couldn’t move, Sun Tzu could hear his approach, then she could see him when he entered her line of sight. His expression showed a touch of regret as he held up a knife. “You know, it would be poetic if I gave you your first kiss as well as your last. But since you’d try to rip my throat out with your teeth, pragmatism wins out over poetry, girly.” He leaned towards her, the steel blade glimmering in the darkness. Letting out a scream spawned by pain in both body and soul. Sun Tzu wrapped her legs around his neck. Drawing on the rage she felt seeing the life of her closest comrade driven out of her, she twisted until she heard a sickening pop. She released him and Confucius dropped, his face totally calm, just like always. As Sun Tzu lay on the catwalk with the corpse that was once a fellow soldier, she heard the sounds of sirens. Police. As she struggled to rise, Sun Tzu cursed their timing as well as well her own limbs still weak from the shock. There would be no way for her to escape from the doors below, a task made even more impossible if she tried to collect Lao’s body. With no small regret, she stepped over the remains of Confucius and made her way to the ladder. She climbed up it and through the trapdoor to the roof just as she heard the footsteps of policemen. The daylight felt like a savior from the dark crypt the warehouse had become. Ignoring the cramping she felt, Sun Tzu sprinted across the roof and leaped across the gap to the top of the building next door. It wasn’t her most graceful landing, but she didn’t have time for self-criticism as she darted across and jumped to yet another building. Sun Tzu climbed down the fire escape and dropped to the ground. She wearily walked over to a dumpster and practically collapsed while sitting down. Leaning against the metal, Sun Tzu listened to the sounds of the police and their machines for a bit before reaching into her jacket and taking out a battered brass cigarette case. Inside was a single cigarette in a plastic bag. She removed it and stared at the white stick in her hand with eyes that held just a hint of moisture. Then she made a fist, crushing perhaps the only gift she had received when she was a child. Sun Tzu opened her hand and the paper and dried leaves fall to the ground. Then she rested her head against her knees and stayed that way for a very long time. Miles away, in a non-descript government building, a phone rings on the large oak desk of Minister Shen, head of Propaganda. “Hello…” … “Ah, yes. Everything went as planned, I assume?” … “Just how did you convince her of your unfortunate demise?” … “Of course, magicians never reveal their secrets. I know I never do.” … “Well, that would have been simpler if that had been our objective. But our goal was to sever any emotional ties our little media darling has to the homeland. This way we killed two birds with one stone. A very efficient operation.” … “Please… he already wants to kill me. This would be just another reason added to his list. As long as he doesn’t act on his impulses, I’ll be fit as a fiddle. And if there is anything that old dog is not, it’s impulsive. Now I must go. I don’t know how long I can keep this line secure. Have a lovely day.” Hanging, Shen leaned back in his fine leather chair. He turned a looked out his window at a flock of birds flying over the Beijing skyline.
