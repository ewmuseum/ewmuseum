Paying my Respects

14 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 14, 2005 Part I – I Lied Moving around my loft in Winnipeg, it felt empty. It felt cold without the comfort of her being there. Until I heard the news today, I didn’t understand why I couldn’t get a hold of her. She said that she would come here yesterday, but I never heard anything from her. Every attempt to call her went without conclusion. But then I got the phone call from PRIME management. It cut right to the chase. Being notified that Amy Campbell was no longer with PRIME was a shock to say the least. But the real shock was the phone call I finally received this morning. PRIME had dismissed her. There was nothing she or I could do to change that. But the real shock came when she said she was cutting all ties with everyone involved in PRIME. I had to wonder if I was included in that and it turned out I was. I was hurt, surprised and a little angry that it was just supposed to end like that. I didn’t know what to say so I said the first thing that came to my mind. “If it has to be that way, then I supposed I could accept that. I lied. I didn’t want to accept it and damnit, I wasn’t going to. She knew how I felt about her but that was all thrown away. I wasn’t going to agree with what she said. Words couldn’t describe what I was feeling at that moment. I felt crushed, like I had my heart ripped out and someone did the Mexican hat dance on it. All I knew was that I needed to get off the phone and I needed to have a drink. I felt abandoned and alone. Part II – I cheated I had met up with some old friends from San Diego on the weekend. They all came to see my place in Winnipeg. I knew about this quite a few weeks back already. This was supposed to be the weekend that Amy was supposed to meet my friends but of course, that never came to be. Vegas had taken the early flight in on Saturday and later that afternoon, Billy G and Richardo showed up. We had sat down and shared some stories about the past. They had come to watch me wrestle once in the Underground and soon they were planning on coming to see me in PRIME. But today was not about wrestling. It was about drinking, swearing and playing poker. We had ordered three pizzas and the guys brought something like 4 cases of beer. It was the weekend to get hammered and probably lose a couple hundred bucks just having fun with my friends. The cards were dealt for what seemed like the one hundredth time. A can of beer was popped open for seventh time on my end and the game was only a couple hours old. I had lost a total of fifty dollars but I had a plan to get it back. A few hands later, my plan was not going well at all. I had lost an extra seventy-five dollars in about thirty minutes. I had to crack open another beer and after taking a few sips, the alcohol started to take effect. I finally had the ultimate idea to get my money back but it would take a lot to hide it from my friends. I was dealing the next hand and it was a good thing that I knew how to shuffle cards. But it was made easier by the fact that everyone around the table was feeling a little tipsy. I dealt the cards and sure enough, I had four jacks in my hand with a queen high. I knew I was well on my way and I had made my largest bet of the night. I couldn’t keep a straight face though but that actually turned out to work to my advantage. The guys thought I was bluffing and I accented that with a very large bet. “I’ll go fifty.” Vegas made the opening bet. “Call.” Richardo was next. “I’m out.” Billy G obviously had to have a poor hand. “I’ll take your fifty… and raise you a hundred-fifty.” I could see their jaws drop. “The fucker is bluffing. He hasn’t had a good hand all night.” Vega laid down the chips and had the nuts to raise it another fifty bones. “I think your right.” Richardo was next to call the bet, which was now at two hundred fifty big ones. I just had to smile. It wasn’t often that I was able to out bet these guys because my hands always sucked. “Bluffing huh? Well I’ll see your bet, and raise you another hundred.” They were surprised but figured beyond the shadow of any doubt that I was lying to them. “Fuck off; you don’t have anything that good. I call.” Vegas was quick to even the boat, quickly followed by Richardo. Billy G just downed another beer instead of paying attention. But the win was certain. “Four jacks, boys you just made a huge mistake.” Vegas just slammed his hand on the table, causing the chips to bounce in place. Richardo just put his cards face down, looking disappointed in my one good hand for the night. I had won a total of seven hundred bucks in one hand. These boys really played for keeps, but I’ll be damned if I was going tell them that I cheated and dealt myself a great hand. Everyone had too much to drink to bring any notice to it anyways. After about seven more beers and a couple dozen hands of poker, we had called it a night. I was up three hundred bucks and I was happy. Although Vegas downed a few more beers than I did, I was done for the night. Part III – I Stole I had walked into the arena, confident and full of energy. When I left my dressing room, I made my way to the ring. I had passed the locker rooms of many superstars and I had imagined what my life and career would be like had it not been for the influence of every single person in the locker room, past and present. Walking down to the ring, I was following in the foot steps of many legends. I was The Supreme Machine. I was one of the most respected names in wrestling today. I had won many matches and I had lost a few too. But the biggest most important thing was that I entertained the people. Everything I had done in the ring was for the fans. When I walk through that curtain into the main arena and I hear everyone chanting my name, I knew I had made it. But I also knew that whoever chanted my name, they were being entertained. The cheers and the applause of the crowd gave me an energetic high that I couldn’t experience anywhere else. Whether I had a title around my waist or not, they chanted for me the same way. I felt respected by everyone in the building. I didn’t always think I deserved the respect, but I got it either way. That's what helped me succeed as well. Sure I had lied and I had cheated sometimes. But the fact was I stole as well. I stole… their hearts. (RIP Eddie. You will be missed in the wrestling world. Your fans, friends and family all love you. You were an inspiration to everyone. Thanks for the memories.) Eddie Guerrero 1967 – 200538 years young, it was too soon…
