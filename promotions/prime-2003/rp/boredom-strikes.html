Boredom Strikes

2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-9-07 So this week Tony leaves me alone in the brand new apartment. It's our third move in as many months. He decided that he couldn't live in the old place anymore, he didn't think I was safe enough. Now he squanders his cash on a large two bedroom East Village joint that looks out on the park. I miss our old place in a way. But I can't go back there after what happened. I can't really go anywhere these days. The other night Tony and I had dinner at this Italian restaurant; it's called Cabela's. Nothing important happened at the meal, but I am still smelling the repercussions. I'll explain. After we finish our meal, the waiter drops the check down with a handful of these little chocolates. The kind that are half milk chocolate and half radioactive wintergreen. Tony isn't looking at that moment, so I have my chance to reach out and snag a few of those wonderful mints. I pop one in my mouth and the other two I drop into my front hip pocket. We check out a movie before heading home. The point is that time elapses. I wake up to find a note on the TV in Tony's less than legible handwriting. It says: I had an early flight and didn't want to wake you. I'll call as soon as I get in. -Tony I sigh and move to the kitchen where I catch a cool breeze from the window that doesn't shut right. My odor is lifted on this, carried into my nostrils, and it occurs to me that I haven't washed this shirt, or anything, in days. I hate doing laundry. We'll have to get someone to start doing it for us. I think we can afford it. I pull on some sweatpants and a rancid gray hoodie. I comb last night's tangles out of my hair with my fingers before catching a glimpse of myself in the mirror. My eyes are sort of red, but nothing a little bit of make-up wouldn't take care of if I end up getting noticed. I take three Excedrin and brush my teeth. Sunglasses and hood, hair in shambles on the underneath, and I'm out in the streets with a duffel bag filled with dirty clothes. I can't catch a taxi and the wind isn't so bad, the sun shining, so I walk the eight blocks to the laundromat. The bag filled with my clothes is heavy, and I have to keep passing it between my shoulders every time it starts to hurt. My pockets jingle with quarters and there's this punk kid with green hair, leather, and chains that keeps circling me like a dog looking for a treat. I throw all my nonconformist, expensive, retro looking clothing into two washers and collapse into a folding chair in one corner of the laundromat. I can't look at these folding chairs the same way anymore. Green hair and leather lingers around out front for a little while, occasionally staring at me through the wide front window. Eventually he migrates and I feel comfortable enough to take my hood down. The place is filled with immigrants and NYU flakes. None of them recognize me. Before long this mousy woman is walking in. She hovers over me while I pull my overly heated apparel out of the dryer. I'm humming something that sounds like summer, and when I turn around she smiles like she knows me. My hands are filled with a pair of jeans so I can't pull my hood up without looking impolite. "I'm sorry, but could you excuse me?" I ask, exerting all my girlish charm. She steps politely aside to let me get at the duffel bag. She's carrying a basket full of boring blouses and studious skirts. She sets that down on the table next to my bag and follows me back to the dryer for the rest of my stuff. "Can I help you?" I ask, as I continue unloading. "I'm Barbara Masterson," she tells me so that I know what to write on the scrap of paper she's producing from her purse. The scrap is actually a card that says Barbara does something with her life. I turn it over and scribble six cursive letters on the back. Barbara sneers at me. "I don't want your autograph." She cocks her head to the side as I stand there with my things hanging out of the dryer. "I'm a journalist," she concludes. "So good for you," I say and grab the sleeve of a white shirt that's somehow turned damp and brown. "What the hell?" "What is that?" Barbara is craning her neck and not leaving me alone. She's looking at my private things. This is that kind of invasion. "What the fuck," this time it's louder and I'm ignoring Barbara the journalist. There is something all over this shirt. Something brown and gooey. "It looks like-" "Those fucking chocolates. Son of a bitch," I rage against the dryer. My entire wardrobe slathered in the melted remains of Cabela's finest. "Time to start over," Barbara smiles. "I don't have any more quarters," I say with my face buried in the crotch of my best khaki capris. It's definitely chocolate. "That's not what I meant, but here." Barbara hands me two dollars worth of change. I take it and stuff it stiffly into my pocket. I don't need the charity of some wannabe Dr. Phil with tits. "I'm doing an article on Tony. It's going to be run in a few major 'zines, or so I'm told. I have a very interesting start." 'Zines. Who says that? "Do you have a cigarette?" I ask her. She goes back to fishing around in her purse. I transport my freshly dried clothes to the washer from whence they came. She hands me a cigarette and a book of matches. I light up. "I don't think you can smoke that in here," she calmly asserts her position as part of the establishment. "They're not serving food," I spout. "Besides, I'll just say that I'm famous." Barbara smiles at me knowingly, and I start blowing my smoke through a dryer sheet. "Anyway, Ms. Rayne." She grins at the use of my name and I notice she's tucked her card with my signature on it into her pocket. "Tony and I had some trouble during our first meeting, but I'd very much like to talk with him again." "If he didn't want to talk to you then, I doubt he'll want to talk to you now." "That's fine, I meant another time. I'd like to talk to you too, actually. Ask you a few questions, go over some material I've collected, you know." She waves a hand, but I don't really know. I nod and she smiles. "Are you busy later today?" I glance at my laundry. "What time?" "How about Four Thirtyish?" I stare down at my watch. That's three hours. I nod absently as she hands me another card with her office address on it. We exchange polite good-byes before she exits the laundromat without ever washing her clothes. Or maybe she was here and I hadn't noticed the plump journalist with the Naired face. Either way. I finish my first second load of laundry, cram my stuff in the duffel bag, and walk outside. The wind has picked up. Green hair is lingering outside, wrapped in his leather jacket. Four other colors of the rainbow have joined him to spread cheer. One of them, Red, is wearing shorts and a Misfits T-shirt. It's like twenty degrees out and he's trembling while burning little holes in me. "So your boyfriend don't like emo, huh," Blue confronts me. "I'm sorry?" I dumbly rejoin. "You know what the hell I said." Oh great, not this shit again. "Is there a problem here?" I turn toward a voice that has crept up behind me and find a large man in a dark blue three piece suit, dark gold framed sunglasses shield his eyes from the sun while a toothpick dangles loosely between his lips. "This ain't none of your business, man." "I assure you that anything that has to do with Ms. Rayne has something to do with me," How the hell did he know my name, and who the hell is he... They? "My associates and I have specific instructions not to let anything happen to her, so if it's change to start a new life you were hoping for." I watch as he nods over his shoulder to the three other gentleman wearing practically the same clothing walk up behind him. "I'm sure we can find you some over here in the alley." "Maybe next time," Blue says before waving for his friends to join him in back stepping. The gentleman then turns his attention down at me. "Your father asked us to discretely keep an eye on you." "My father?" He hadn't spoken to me since the day I told him I planned on making a life with Tony. I never had a doubt that he cared about me, but I never expected this. I guess the attack worried him more than I expected. Nevertheless, I had to get home before Tony called. Not to mention, I had plans to meet with that reporter. "Well, thanks," I say before pulling the duffel bag over my shoulder and pulling the hoodie over my head. It's going to be a long walk home.
