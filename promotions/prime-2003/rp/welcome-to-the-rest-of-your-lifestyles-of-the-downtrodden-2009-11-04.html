Welcome to the rest of your lifestyles of the downtrodden

4 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 4, 2009 |October 25th, 2009| "Dearest... the fucking fuck?" Carly knew I was just being facetious. "I'm sure the locals will love you, too." Verona, Missouri is a quiet rural town, with just a little over 700 people cashing checks in town. Much of the land is undeveloped tracts of hilly fields of water-starved grass, so it looks almost like if you watched the 2008 version of Rambo in that final scene where he's taking that unnecessarily long walk down the road to his dad's old estate (come on, Stallone, it's like $29 to rent a minivan for the day -- I'm supposed to believe Rambo can't afford that?). Passing the occasional old house, mixed in with a few neighborhoods that look like they were put up in the mid-to-late 80s, I couldn't help but wonder if I should have checked Carly's work a little more when I let her pick out a place for us to live. It's true, I wanted someplace in the midwest, decent sized house and without having to deal with a lot of taxes. Missouri fit the bill for that. But I suppose I was expecting a place where... I don't know, people lived? I swore, as I passed a ranch-style home with dead grass, a really big pine tree leaning precariously toward the house, and a rust-colored (no wait, that's just rust) 1989 Dodge Dakota sitting on bricks in the front with an implausibly fat Judah Friedlander impersonator trying his best to wriggle underneath with an oil pan in hand, I swore that if the answer to What is there to do around town? was Wal-Mart that I was going to burn down the home we just purchased and to take the rest of Lawrence County with it. With a yawn I asked, "How long until we get to our hovel?" My mind couldn't overcome the compulsion to stretch out my legs; at least our car's interior could comfortably accomodate somebody my height. "Long enough to fit in another power nap." She knew me -- particularly my love of sleep -- so well. I am to sleep what sharks are to lamprey... I don't resent fight it, I understand that it's better to just let it come and go as it pleases. And I NEEDED the sleep, believe me. Flying out of Puerto Rico was an ordeal and a half. Traffic out of San Juan couldn't have been any worse. The government is all but bankrupt -- Puerto Rico's just an Austrian governor away from being California -- so they had to close some services down on rotating days. Nothing essential, just the post office. Schools. Police. Understandably disgruntled workers marched in the streets, joined in their struggles by tourists who were there to buy jewelry and didn't realize that shit would be going down and they had to join in the flowing direction of the strike in order to get anywhere on foot. The tourists were catered to by the outlet and factory store owners, who kept their shops open because a strike is a strike but damned if that's gonna stop people from buying seven t-shirts and a box of 2003 Romeo e Julietas for forty dollars. I'd passed out on the way to the airport, sitting and waiting for my plane at the airport, and once I got onto the plane at the airport, respectively. For ego's sake I wish I could say that my final match obligation in San Juan (Johnny Raindance vs. Brick Thomspon) was an easy one but that kid wore me the hell out. Yeah, Thomspon -- that's no mistake on my part. The guy won Global Federation of Wrestling's International Junior Heavyweight title in his debut five months back, but the guy who wrote the announcement cards made a typo and it just stuck since then. It proves that old adage "when 12,000 people in Bayamón hear something you can't make them unhear it." I suggested he shorten his ringname to just "Brick" but I suppose somebody else has claim to that name -- either way, that kid's gonna be something big in the next few years. And then that match was on top of a 2 week tour with me wrestling every other day. We hit Aguada, Caguas, Bayamón, Ponce, Cabo Rojo, Arecibo, and then the last weekend shows in San Juan that had back to back packed stadiums on opposite sides of town each day (wrestling's big in Puerto Rico, but we're still not stopping the other sports from happening). So with my overall dedication to the fine art of sleeping like Garfield, especially when I seriously needed it because I was sore all over, I really wasn't paying attention to the area. There was probably a quaint charm about things in that kind of "I could see myself raising kids here if not for the town policy against dancing" way, but I had to get my rest. ---- |October 26th| Phone rings. I pick it up. I can hear Robbie grin as he says, "Yo, C! I got the Herpes here." I am tired. Sore. Out of shape. Training is killing me. 2 AM. He should not call me this early. No patience for his antics. "It is pronounced Herps. Call me when you sell out. No sooner." That was the arrangement. I provided the materials, marked the safest selling locations. Fallon and Geiger Herpe package the goods. Robbie sells after that. My cut is get 30% plus materials reimbursement. Has to be. Robbie is young, dumb, greedy. Would steal off the top if he got a larger share. He knows I'm serious. "Yeah, I'll holla at ya later." I toss the phone down. Literally, on the floor. An older, bulkier Nokia. It handles bumps well. Not like me at all. ---- |October 27th| "Hey. Wake up. Get fooded." Carly had a habit of making verbs out of words that shouldn't ever be verbs. But she was right -- it'd been two whole days and some change since we got to the house on Lawrence 1167 in Verona and for the most part I'd just been sleeping in. Eventually my body was going to need something to digest just to keep my energy up in the event I decided to roll my lazy ass out of bed and do something by the end of the calendar year. "What's in the kitchen?" I droopily asked her. I didn't even open my eyes yet, on the chance that it was morning and sun was coming through the blinds. The sun and I aren't really on the best of terms. It bothers me in the morning, makes me sweat during the day, and constantly bombards me with harmful radiation for no good reason. If it were a person I'd have kicked its ass by now. Carly's answer clued me to the time of day. She wouldn't suggest a roast beef sandwich to start the day, or a bowl of Cheerios at 6 in the evening. "There's fruit in the fridge. I picked up some kind of vacuum seal bags that's supposed to keep it fresh longer. And you can fix yourself an omelette." Early morning. My eternal nemesis. We meet again. "At least try to get up and get showered before heat of the night." That was code, so I'll explain. My working theory is that in at least one town in every time zone in the world, reruns of In the Heat of the Night come on at 1 pm. So Carly saying that tells me it's sometime around noonish, maybe a little earlier than that, but at least it's late morning and I've managed to sleep in for a consecutive 1,229 days in a row. After fathering the strength and courage to open my eyes, I saw Carly sitting up in the bed beside me. She had her laptop on and her fingers danced over the keyboard with such silent speed. She was always the whiz at that stuff, making me feel like an old man with the way I hardly ever use a computer for more than sending some brief e-mails. "Is that for you or me?" I didn't bother to look at the screen. Carly's not much of a "computers are for entertainment" type of person. To be online she had to be doing something involving work. Carly said, "I'm seending feelers out everybody in the area, let them know you're in town now. I did a little advance research, mostly word of mouth, just to see who was around." She was coordinating my attempted bookings, sending out my name in the area. Professional wrestling, at all points in one's career, is all about blanketing the region with your resume to let promoters know you're available. Running more than a show a month is a great financial challenge for many places in the region, so talking to one wasn't enough. She continued, "WMW wasn't interested; didn't say why. I figure it's financials. They're only making a fraction of the money they used to. Most guys they hire anymore have to come in with the understanding that they're working for an employer with a shoestring budget paying them in peanut butter sandwiches. But it's probably for the best they passed on you so you didn't waste your time." Being better with the money, not only in spending it and saving it but also in properly valuing things, I took Carly's word that it wasn't the best place to work. There was some bit of interesting news she gave me. "I did manage to get you a free agent booking into a tournament for PRIME. I name-dropped your Krav Maga instructor, that seemed to be enough for them." I have no idea what I'd do without Carly Enson. She lived in Puerto Rico, worked at a Marriott resort in San Juan. I kept a long-term relationship with her, half of the time it being long-distance when I worked Canada, but she never stopped being fun or sweet or anything that a beat-up kid like me needed whenever I came into town. But she was a serious business woman. She got her degree for Hospitality Management in the U.S. and always saw herself doing that. I don't know how I convinced her that it would be a good idea for us to move to the United States when hospitality there is little more than a meaningless buzzword outside of the most expensive of hotels that only see maybe three guests a month. But she sank herself into the project of moving us from Puerto Rico to the mainland. I gave her $250 thousand cash (okay, on debit but you get what I mean) and told her that I trusted her to make it happen. Lucio thought I was nuts. You sent a woman you're not married to with quarter of a million dollars to another country and you seriously believe you'll ever see her again? I have to apologize to Q. Walker, THIS is the dumbest thing I've ever heard. So now you know why I had to wrestle my old mentor in Caguas before I left Puerto Rico. But my trust was well placed. Carly got us a sweet deal on a home in foreclosure trouble -- four bedrooms and 3 bathrooms for $100k to own it outright on the short sale. I sold the old '99 Saturn in Puerto Rico, and she upgraded us to a Lexus LS 430. It had a bigger interior than the Lincoln Town Car and more luxury features, and she figured it would look better with the locals to not be driving around in a Nissan Maxima or something overtly Japanese. Your average joe off the street doesn't realize that Lexus is owned by Toyota, so we were a little better that way. The most important part was that the house was entirely furnished by the time I got there. None of that IKEA crap, but since I'm a stickler on easy-to-move furniture she went with Broyhill. So I could collapse right into bed as soon as I got there. At least as soon as I went around and did the usual routine of looking impressed with everything and congratulating Carly on how fantastic a girlfriend she is. And she was. It's just that I get worn out sometimes. But then she does things like sit on top of the sheets wearing one of my old brown Valparaiso shirt, covering her pelvis but showing just enough of her legs to excite me without even really trying. And lean over and kiss her thighs, which gets me a reminder that "I've already washed up today, hun." To which I remind her, "We'll have to christen the new sheets at some point." She with a sly smile she noted, "You think I haven't done that already? I did beat you here by two months." I never accept defeat, rather I always thought of it as a change in tactics. So, rather than press the matter, I sat up beside my curly-haired West Virginian princess and stretched out my arms over her head. "Fine then. To the shower it is." Of course, as I slid out of bed and set my feet on the carpet, I let her know that she was coming with me. I knew that the shower was still virgin territory; if she's going to take care of herself she likes to be lying down for it. For that, she didn't argue. Glasses set down by the nightstand, those legs carried a 5'10" former NCAA swim champion toward me. She kept in shape over the years, and while she wasn't more than a wide A cup and more muscular than soft in her curves, and she had a plain face by typical expectations of what you'd see out of wrestlers and the women they fall for, she was irresistable in that subtle sort of beauty she had. She didn't try, she didn't want to try, and that ended up being the sexiest thing about her. Rest assured we christened the hell out of that shower that day. ---- |October 28th| St. Louis is full of idiots. Easy marks. Robbie should be made in minutes. But he sells it all. The con is so simple. Even calling it a con is overstating. === 1) Rip tracks from an obscure but skilled rapper from South Africa. People put anything on MySpace. 2) Burn the tracks to one CD. No more than that. This is the control. 3) Play the CD around St. Louis. Stay out of the major trouble neighborhoods. 4) Make up a false name. Yung Tek, we called our avatar. It is remarkable, really. The black community claims to have seen Stomp The Yard. Yet, show them an image Columbus Short. They have no idea we stole a promotional picture for the CD cover image.</list> === Robbie makes himself out to be part of Yung Tek's street team. Already, this adds a kind of legitimacy. Yung Tek, as an underground artist, has a street team. He must be good. Yung Tek is from Kansas City. He wants to expand into St. Louis. So he is putting out his new album to the streets. He calls it Blank Disc. It is his best album ever. St. Louis is the murder capital of the United States. I tend to avoid the city, for this reason. Yet, if the marks here are this brainless... I see now. I see that St. Louis takes pride in being the most dangerous city in the U.S. That seems to be the only thing St. Louis excels at. My cut -- estimated gross profit $1700. Tax free. In less than four days. I love this country. Life is not a game. Life is serious business. People... people are a game. You play them and you get played by them. Everything is in the aggregate. You have to fool most of the people most of the time. That is how you make it in America. That is what success means. -- C
