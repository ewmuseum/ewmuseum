On This Day We Become Legendary

13 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/13/07 “…like a fart in the wind.” At first, I don’t hear her as I’m lost in my own world, trying to prepare myself for the Roulette in the middle of an airport. I can feel the pressure mounting on my shoulders. Some would say it’s pointless, a waste of my time preparing because there’s no chance of me winning this match. Yet, I continue to push. I feel her eyes on my skin and I try to ignore it, but it’s no use as her gaze continues to distract me. “What did you say?” I ask her and Katie just sighs, annoyed at how I’ve been distant the past few days, trapped and locked away in my own mind trying to find the answer that eludes me every time I get close to it. It’s aggravating. “I said it’s like a fart in the wind, your losing streak that is; it’ll eventually pass on,” she says once again and I can only nod my head. I don’t know what she expects from me; to just ignore it? I can’t, not any longer. I’ve tried to quit PRIME, even attempted to get removed from the match, and nothing has worked up to this point. Now there’s no choice but to face my demons head on, and try to defeat them this time around. The only thing though is that I’m afraid of losing, again. “Yeah, I hope so,” I tell her, dismissing it, and I can feel her rolling her eyes at me. My eyes stare straight ahead yet there’s nothing there, not to me anyways. I continue to visualize the match in my head and wonder if I honestly have what it takes to even place higher than last place. Then my concentration is broken again, this time by a teenager no older then seventeen judging from the acne on his face, walks up to me with a smile on his face. “Hey man, you’re Dusk, right?!” he asks me and I nod my head, my eyes never meeting his. “Can I have your autograph by any chance?” he asks and I finally take a look at him. “Sure,” I tell him and I take the pad of paper from him and sign away. “What’s your name?” I ask him and he tells me his name and I make out a personal message to him. As I hand it back to him, he smiles at me, and I just smile back. It’s people like him that make it tougher to step into the ring because I’m just frightened of letting them down. Yet, it’s also because of him that I do step into that ring; kind of ironic to be honest. They make it worth living through the pain and heartache of defeat. “Can I get a picture as well? My friends will never believe that I got to meet you!” he says rather happily and I stand up. Across from me I can see his mother, slightly overweight, but with a large smile on her face. I can see the pride in her eyes and it melts away the ice surrounding my heart. I wrap my arm around the kid and smile for the camera. The flash blinds me momentarily, but I manage to shake her hand before saying bye to the kid as he walks away. “Good luck at DDT. I think you’re going to do well, but I think Troy is going to retain the title,” he says and his words pierce me much worse than they should. I sit back down in the chair and try to push his words away. This isn’t the first time I’ve had somebody say that to me. Sometimes it’ll be Lavelle that they’re favoring or even Shakur; Snow and Silver are popular choices as well. Doubt had set in across the country when it came to me though. Nobody knew what to make of me anymore and I couldn’t blame them. In a poll on the PRIME website fans could vote on the winner of the Roulette match, and I had the least amount of votes. I can only pretend for so long that it doesn’t affect me. Truthfully, it’s tearing me apart. “Well, that was sweet,” Katie says and I just ignore her. “Let me ask you something,” she begins to say to me, but she stops when I don’t turn towards her. Silence fills the air and I just know she’s waiting for me to at least acknowledge her. My red eyes turn towards her and she just smiles back, trying to get me to wake up from my stupor. “Sorry, just in my own world,” I tell her. “I know. Lots of pressure, but do you ever wonder if you’re wasting your time?” she asks me and at first I don’t quite understand her question. “What do you mean?” I ask her. “I mean, do you think you’re wasting your time wrestling?” she asks and the question is so pointed and out of left field that it takes me by surprise. “I—don’t quite understand what you’re trying to say,” I tell her and she sighs. “Okay, just listen to me. You’re becoming more successful by the minute in areas outside of wrestling. Sure, wrestling is what started it all, but you can’t stay a wrestler forever. You’ve got a restaurant and a night club opening out in Los Angeles, you just signed another contract for something that I consider to be rather huge, and you’ve got your own television show. Maybe you’ve accomplished everything that you can in wrestling and now you’re just wasting your time instead of focusing your efforts elsewhere,” she tells me and I just take the words in as I sit there. I ask myself if she’s right. The truth is that I can’t wrestle forever, but has it passed me by? Am I wasting my time? I keep asking myself that question over the next few minutes and each time, the word no flashes before my very eyes. For years, I’ve dreamt of these moments. I’ve been waiting on this my whole life and the dreams keep waking me up at night. Yet, I can’t help but to think that she’s got a rather valid point. Maybe it’s time to say goodbye. “You could be right,” I finally tell her, a victory in her eyes as I never admit that she’s ever right or at least she tells me so. I sit back in the chair and just ponder her words. “I think you’re taking a big gamble with this. First off, you don’t have the time to continue wrestling as you travel the world to search out the answers that keep you up at night. You’ve got your legal issues and now you’ve got businesses that need your full attention. I know that you don’t want to--,” and she then lowers her voice, “die as much as you think you do. You can keep fighting these charges, keep delaying them, and continue to live your life. Yet, if you keep splitting your time and your attention up like you are then you’ll be watching it all crumble before your very eyes,” she tells me and as much as I hate that fact, I know that she’s right. Still, I can’t walk away from the sport, not on the skid that I’m going on, and not without accomplishing the goals that I’ve set out before me. I continue to sit there, the minutes passing by me as I wait for them to call our flight. Then, a little girl walks up to me and taps me on the knee. I look down at her and wonder what she’s doing, that she can’t possibly know who I am. However, by the look in her eyes she knows exactly who I am. The look in her eyes are soul wrenching as I can see the sorrow there. “Mr. Dusk?” she asks, making sure that it’s me and that I’m paying attention to her. I nod my head and a tear rolls out of her eyes. I look over at Katie who’s looking at the girl as well. “My daddy, he passed away a few months ago, but you were his favorite wrestler. My mommy, she doesn’t like me to watch wrestling, but I tell her that when I watch you it’s as if my daddy is still there with me.” I just sit there and can’t find the words to say anything. The fact that this girl looks at me as the only chance to be with her daddy tears at my heart and I can only nod my head. “I hope you win tomorrow. I know you can,” she says and I try to say something comforting, but no words are good enough for this situation. “What’s your name?” I ask her and she looks away, a little scared at my voice I’m assuming. She then looks back at me and wipes away the tears in her eyes. “My name is Rose. My daddy’s name was Brady,” she says and I realize who I’m talking to. I pat her on her shoulder and she just walks away without muttering another word. I look over at Katie before grabbing my bag and stand up. “That’s not--,” she asks me, but she can’t find the words to finish her statement. I know what she’s trying to say though and I nod my head. I look over at Katie who is stunned by all of this, but not as much as I am. Brady had been the name of a man that said I ruined his life. It couldn’t be a coincidence. Not with my life. “That’s why this I don’t think this is a waste of time, Katie. Because I have people who count on me to go out there every night so they can be happy, so they know that they’re not taking the gamble or wasting their time trying to achieve their dreams for nothing. I might be taking a gamble, but it’s one that I’m going to keep on taking,” I tell her before I begin to walk away from her, needing some time alone. I wonder what it means to find your dreams. Deep down, I pray that I’m not wasting my time. I then turn back towards her and I nod my head. “Eventually, it’ll pass,” I tell her. It’ll pass like a fart in the wind.
