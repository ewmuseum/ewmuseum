Master of the Universe: Part I - The Epiphany

22 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 22-11-10 Von Kelsig's ears rang with a high-pitched whine from the roar of the ten thousand strong crowd. Spots danced in front of his pulsing eyes from the constant barrage of flashbulbs firing at him from every direction. He ached all over thanks to the fight Castor V. Strife had put up, and he was dog tired. None of that mattered a damn, not now. Not as he fought the strain in his muscles to raise the strap overhead did he care that he'd paid such a price to hear so many chanting his name. For ten years he had put his body through hell and every bump, bruise and scar had been for this moment. He thought the feeling would last forever, but no sooner had he walked through the curtain to Vince Howard's announcement than Nick and Richard were signing off and he was trudging right back through it. With the title clutched tightly in hand the electric miasma of noise and light gave way to the cold dim solitude of the backstage area, and suddenly he found himself staring at his reflection in the mirror. There used to be a twinkle about his blue eyes, used to be fewer lines around them too. His skin was smoother and he was much more handsome, probably still would be if he didn't have to hide all those scars beneath that beard. His hairline had receded at least an inch, and he remembered a time when the Phantom Booker had convinced him to grow dreadlocks and don a skull mask for the incarnation of Hessian that would run riot in Old School Wrestling. The world melted away and he looked back at himself through the years, going all the way back to the Primetime Wrestling Academy. Ten years. All those titles, matches and feuds, all that time felt like it had passed as quickly as he snapped out of his daydream and greeted the stubborn old giant in the glass with a grunt. Then he drew his gaze to the title next to him and felt a fluttering in his gut. He smiled lovingly at the shimmering gold plate and the intricate design engraved especially for champions like him to admire, and everyone else to envy. The belt had an infantile allure about it, and something about the contours and even the thickness of it excited him almost as much as the list of of men who had held it before, a list now topped by his own name. He was a definitive part of PRIME's history now. Whether he held the title for just over a fortnight or over a year his name was up there among the greats of the sport that had held the same title that was in his possession right now. Even if his body failed him and he was no longer a part of the industry people would be able to look back on the records and the archives and know who Hessian was and that he was Universal Champion. He had cemented his place in history, and the feeling of accomplishment was greater than that of both his PTC title wins combined. Ten years. A decade of blood, sweat and tears that had finally paid off. Most wrestlers couldn't even fathom a career as long as that never mind winning a heavyweight belt after all that time and still giving their all for the fans. Von Kelsig had been a late bloomer, breaking into the business at the age of thirty, and his hardiness had helped him break boundaries where a more youthful counterpart would have been hindered by their inexperience. He was a lot bulkier back then, and at his heaviest hit the scales at four-hundred-twenty-five pounds, all muscle. Here he was pushing forty and showing it, leaner than he had ever been and composed of more gristle than muscle. Christ, was this how Ivan felt when those grey hairs started showing in his promo shots? The giant lifted the title and held it up. Enamoured by the gold he pulled the strap to his face and breathed in the scent of clean leather, and sighed satisfactorily as he exhaled. Hillary had Everest, Armstrong the moon, and now Von Kelsig had the Universe. Ten years to achieve this moment of glory. It felt like the culmination of a life's work, coming to the end of the road and being rewarded with this prize, and it had all been worth it. It couldn't have been timed any better, not now that his best years were firmly behind him. He couldn't recall many names other than that of the Russian Bear that had kept the engine rolling this many years into their life. A shower would feel good right about now, followed by a couple of days rest before he got back out there and started living it up as the new face of PRIME. The Universal Champion. No matter how many times he repeated it to himself it never sank in, even after the energy that resonated throughout the arena had fizzled away. He began to undress, putting the thoughts out of his mind for a moment as he hopped on one foot to kick off his wrestling tights. Pulling the sleeveless hessian shirt over his head he flicked his gaze over to the title and smiled again. The Universal Champion. With only boxer shorts left on he folded up his attire and placed it back in his kit bag before retrieving accoutrements for his shower and finally removing his shorts. It was then that he looked again at the mirror, at his aged body in the flesh, and fully realized just how far he had come. He had to hunch slightly to alleviate the dull pain in his back, and the posture reminded him of an old man, something he'd never imagined himself as. But he felt old. Then that thought popped back into his head and the shiny gold before him glimmered as if to confirm it. The Universal Champion. It all rolled into a conclusion right there and then. Ten years of his life dedicated to pain and suffering and now he had the biggest prize in the industry....but what now? Nearly forty years old, practically a shadow of his former self, a broken down and tired version of the man he used to be. How many men his age were still throwing themselves around the ring and keeping pace with the younger guys? How much longer could he keep it up before his body said enough was enough and simply gave up on him? There was one question though that sent a shiver down his spine and made him turn away from the mirror and the title in fear. Where do I go from here? The butterflies and pride and excitement drained away like the colour from his face. His stomach twisted and the pain all over his body seemed to amplify as visions of the future flashed in his mind. He had made it to the top of the mountain, but it had taken nearly an entire career to do it, and now his mortality shamed him from his own reflection. Suddenly he wasn't thinking about the Jason Snows and Lindsay Troys any more; those who held the prestige and either continued on a path of glory or got out while the going was good. His mind turned to the Bodas, the Hoyt Williamses, the Novas. Great talents who had held the same belt he had and then delved into obscurity and mediocrity when they lost it. What would happen when his shoulders were pinned for three seconds and someone else took the title from him? Where would he go from there? What else is there left to achieve in this business when you've made it to the top and then been pushed right back off, or worse...fallen off yourself. Those ten years suddenly felt like an eternity. He began reminiscing on all those times he came close to glory and fell short of the mark. All the tournaments he had competed in and been eliminated from, the title opportunities he had in the past that he couldn't quite pull a win out of. The Tony Gamble atrocity. The nightmare of Chainz. His legacy came grumbling to a halt and he felt his heart palpitating at the thought of what it would mean to him when the next loss took from him his prized Universal Title. The clock on the wall said he had only been champion for the best part of twenty minutes. It hadn't even taken half an hour for him to realize that his days were numbered now. His hands started to shake and his mouth went dry at the sight of the title this time. Looking at the soap and sponge in his grip he tossed them aside, narrowing his eyes and thinking that maybe he didn't want to take a shower now. He couldn't bring himself to wash away the sweat and the smell that had come from winning that belt. It took ten years to get here. Ten fucking years. What if he lost the belt on his first defence like Castor had? The only difference was Castor's career seemed to be peppered with heavyweight title reigns, whereas he had worked his ass off for a decade for just this one. What if it took another ten years to do it again? He couldn't go on forever, certainly not at the pace of his return in 2009. If he lost the Universal Title where would he go from there? Feuds were becoming too personal and wrestlers were becoming increasingly aware of his mental disposition. He had almost quit back in the summer after what Michael Sloan had put him through. Shortly after Colossus when he was named number one contender a rumour had surfaced that if he lost he would retire. A completely unfounded lie, yet it was engrained in his mind now. He couldn't continue without the belt. Sure he could compete for the Intense again, or try his hand at the 5-Star division, but nothing would come close to the feeling of being Universal Champion. This was the reason he joined PWA back in 2001. This is why he was loyal to every company he ever wrestled for, this is why he stayed with NTICW, PCW, OSW, RPW and now PRIME; all for a chance to win the ultimate prize. The Universal Title was undoubtedly the highlight of his career, but it would undoubtedly he his last. There was no guarantee he would get another shot, and there was no certainty he could win it again if he did. He was the number one superstar in PRIME now, the champion. Even if he wanted to his body wouldn't let him go through it all again for another shot. It had to be now. This was his moment and it had to last forever. He stood there in all his mortal nakedness and suddenly the Universal Title didn't feel like a prize any more. It felt like an epitaph.
