"Roots" Part Two: The Walter Effect

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: GAN Prologue Cornelius Stakiri: Jonathan Winters employer, big money industrial pioneer and domineering underworld mob boss and he’s at war. The Enemy: A rival business organisation, known as OmniTech, whom attack both Stakiri’s legal and less than legal enterprises aggressively and without remorse. The First Casualty: Aeryn Sun, Jonathan Winters long-time girlfriend and co-worker in Cornelius Stakiri’s elite inner circle of confidants. She was sent undercover to infiltrate the enemy, but failed. They caught her, broke her and re-programmed her mind using a combination of powerful drugs and hypnotherapy. They sent her back to Stakiri as a veritable time bomb wired to explode. The Result: Explode she did, but was eventually contained. Crisis averted, the only way to prevent Aeryn from harming herself (or anyone else for that matter) and to destroy the deeply implanted programming was to wipe her mind entirely, including all the inside information pertaining to the enemy she‘d acquired during her capture and infiltration. The Future: Now the war has reached a critical juncture and Stakiri needs said information regarding the enemy desperately. The good news is, Aeryn’s memories are beginning to return to her and Winters has been assigned to help nurture her once lost memories back into fruition. Walter: Winters assigned partner whom has also been drafted by Stakiri to accompany them; a wild card and world class idiot, it is yet to be seen precisely how he will be beneficial to the endeavour or why Stakiri assigned him at all. Jonathan Winters Somewhere in Kansas, 2 Years Ago… Bloodied and battered, the man with no name, and more worryingly no memories, propped himself up against the cold and filth ridden prison cell wall, curling himself into a ball in an utterly pointless attempt to combat the arctic night breeze the local sheriff had deemed appropriate to inflict upon him. He wasn’t angry, at least, not anymore. Thirty minutes of the local sheriff and his boys beating the holy hell out of him had pretty much seen to that. Now, his mind was plagued with but one thing; why. Why were they keeping him here? Why were they doing this? Why him? Why fear an outsider? Why fear somebody they didn’t recognise just because he couldn’t tell them his name or where he came from? Why did the world have to work this way…? “What’s up boy?” The voice boomed, shaking him out of his self-imposed reverie. “Beginning to regret messin’ up old MacGregor’s cornfields?” He could only see the rather sizeable silhouette of the man standing just beyond the bars of his cage, but he recognised the voice well enough to know that it was the sheriff, and the man with no name just damned well knew the bastard was still wearing that same damned cocky grin as before. His anger returned. “Ain’t no shame in that,” the sheriff continued. “I run a tight ship up here. Don’t take no shit from no outsiders.” Backwards logic unworthy of the twentieth century worldview, but who was he to judge? He’d woken up in some damned cornfield but an hour before without a clue as to he is or, more importantly, who he was. For all he knew, he could’ve once shared this yokel’s antiquated views on the world, hell, he could even have been worse if such a thing was possible. “So whatta ya say, boy?” The sheriff asked, stepping into the light. The man with no name was right, he was wearing that same damned cocky grin. “Ya learnt yer lesson?” The man with no name silently shot him an angered expression. “I learnt a lesson alright.” The sheriff shook his head and grinned a vicious and toothy grin. He fished inside his thick wool-lined police jacket and extract a set of large keys in a metallic jangle of movement. Carefully, he located the correct key and promptly unlocked the cell door, standing to one side and motioning the man with no name to leave. From the corner of his cell he stood and, reluctantly, approached the exit. “Well come on!” the sheriff prompted. “I ain’t got all day!” Never taking his eyes off the sheriff for one second, the man with no name took his leave, but it wouldn’t be the last time the two of them meet. Cornelius Stakiri Stakiri Towers, New York, Yesterday… Although Stakiri’s exterior was the perfectly serene, his interior was certainly not and what’s more, Mikey knew it. Making an arch with his fingers and leaning back in his chair, Stakiri allowed a smirk to crawl right across his face. It was only then that Mikey knew his life was in serious danger. He’d been working for Stakiri for many years now and although he knew all the signs leading to danger, he’d never actually fallen victim to them before. He gulped and felt his stomach lurch, filling him with an unreasonable queasiness. “So,” Stakiri finally began, his voice as calm as his exterior, “what happened?” Mikey couldn’t look his employer in the eye. “Th-the-they took the South Bronx safe house, sir.” Stakiri frowned. “They ‘took’ it? That implies you and your men tried to stop them. You did try to stop them, didn’t you?” Mikey nodded. “Yes, sir.” “Then, what happened?” Stakiri questioned, his eyes burning a hole into the side of Mikey’s head. “The enemy took us by surprise, sir,” Mikey explained, “and there were too many of them for us to handle. The five of us were lucky to escape…” Stakiri raised an eyebrow. “Lucky, eh?” Mikey nodded, though he was no longer sure certain of his answer. “Y-ye-yes sir.” “I see,” Stakiri replied, “and just how did they take you by surprise?” “We didn’t see them coming, sir,” Mikey explained, “and by the time we knew just what in the hell was happening, it was too late.” “You didn’t see them coming?” Stakiri asked. Mikey nodded again. “Yes, sir.” “But a moment ago,” Stakiri said, “you told me there were too many to handle? If that was the case, then just how the hell did you not see them coming?” “It’s hard to explain, sir--” “Well try,” Stakiri demanded. “The men guarding the entrance were taken off guard and then they stormed the place, sir,” Mikey told him. “But it’s hard to go into any great detail, sir. It happened so fast, I--” Stakiri leant forward, his elbows resting on his desk and his face became dark and terrifying. “Let me guess; you busy stepping over the bodies and getting your ass to safety!” Mikey shook his head frantically. “No, sir, I--” Stakiri held up his hand for silence as the standardised ringtone of his mobile sounded. He checked the display carefully and then shot Mikey and angered look. “You’re just lucky I gotta take this. Get the hell out of here and don’t think this is the end of it. You’ve still got a lot of explaining to do.” Mikey nodded and silently rose to leave. It would do him no good to plead his case now. The phone call was obviously important, too important to risk the likes of him overhearing it, and any delay in answering it would only serve to anger him further. As he left the office, he wondered whether he’d even make it back to his apartment alive. Aeryn Sun Pheonix, Arizona, Yesterday… It’d been one hell of a trip - hell being the operative word. But what did Aeryn expect? Walter was with them and where there was Walter, trouble was never too far behind. As soon as they landed in Pheonix, and after preventing Winters from strangling the moron with his bare hands, she called Stakiri immediately. “Aeryn?” Stakiri answered. “Is everything okay?” “If that‘s your idea of humour, Stakiri, then I‘m not laughing.” “Humour?” Stakiri asked. “What the hell are you talking about?” “Walter.” “Ah,” Stakiri began, but didn’t get to finish. “What the hell did you send him along with is for?” Aeryn asked. “He’s been a pain in the ass all journey and he nearly got us all fucking arrested three times already! Not to mention Winters threatening to quite literally kick him off the plan once he tried to grope the air hostess mid-flight! The cabin crew had to restrain him from cranking open the emergency exit door!” Stakiri sighed. “That fucking idiot…” “Yeah, that fucking idiot. He thought it was highly amusing to suddenly burst into the embarkation lounge panting wildly out of breath and screaming ‘The Arabs are coming! The Arabs are coming!’ at the top of his voice.” “Oh Jesus,” Stakiri muttered. “Oh wait, there’s more. Plenty more, I assure you,” Aeryn told him. “Then he started a fight with the customs clerk when he asked to ‘check his bags’. For some reason he was under the impression that this was some kind of homosexual euphemism and promptly put the gentleman straight that just because he has a great ass, he didn‘t need to be harassed in streets by the likes of him.” “And was he gay?” “No. He wasn’t even camp, but Walter is under the distinct impression that every male who works in the airport is gay and, in his words, ‘not just those obvious faggart flight attendants’. Said he watched a documentary on it one time.” “How’s Winters handling this?” “You really need to ask?” Aeryn responded. “He’s gone scouting out the local gyms. Said he wants to get in a good workout before heading out to Boston for Great American Nightmare, but I think he just wants to punch something. Problem is, if he gets to Walter first there’s be high likelihood of him not stopping at just one punch.” Aeryn paused for a second. “Just why the hell did you make us bring Walter anyhow? You must’ve known he’d be like this!’ Stakiri sighed. “You needed the help, believe me.” Aeryn frowned. “Help? This idiot’s only made things worse…” “Nevertheless, he’s with you now so you’re going to have cope with him,” Stakiri told her. “Call me when you get an update.” He hung up, leaving Aeryn to glare at her phone in disbelief. She didn’t like this one bit. Stakiri knew Walter and exactly what he was like, but he only ever did things for a specific reason. This was no mistake She placed the phone back in her pocket and went off to hail a cab, pushing the unnerving thought to the back of her head. Perhaps it’d all blow over in time… To Be Continued...
