Date: UltraViolence 2009 It was late when it finally happened. The king-size bed in Room 314 of the Minneapolis Marriott was proving to be a frustrating opponent as I went through my nightly ritual of tossing and turning, trying to find the one spot where it didn't feel like flaming knives were stabbing into every nerve ending in my back. As per usual, I was losing. The battle with mattresses, pillows and blankets was a familiar one, ever since the injury 10 years ago. Recently, however, it's gotten worse than usual. For years, I'd writhe in bed for an hour or so, contorting my body into whatever position it decided would be comfortable for that particular night in that particular bed. I've coped with it for a long time, and eventually, it became routine. The last few weeks, though, it'd gotten worse. Much worse. By the time my eyes would finally let themselves stay shut, I'd be so violently entangled in linen and down that a professional excavating crew might be needed to extract me come dawn. Funny thing was, this wasn't the pain anymore. That stabbing sensation in my back was the mildest it had been since nearly tearing the spine off my skeleton on the Astroturf at Veterans Stadium. This wasn't my body telling me not to sleep. It was my mind. I'd been trying everything. Showers, reading, watching TV, New Age music, Ambien, even warm milk. Nothing but exhaustion so deep it threatened to cause volcanic eruptions inside my head could get me to sleep these days. Not that I'd quit trying. This particular night was the worst of them all. A delayed flight had gotten me into the Twin Cities far later than I'd hoped, and three nerve-racking hours of fidgeting had earned me less than ten minutes of real sleep as the sun was already threatening to grace the Land of 1,000 Lakes with its early-morning illumination. The first peek of sunlight through the tiny gap between the window blinds was all I needed to give up my quest for sleep. I stretched, trying to work out the dozens of kinks that are part and parcel with three hours of restless insomnia. I reached around in the pre-dawn light for the remote control, flicking on the TV and letting the early-morning edition of SportsCenter provide the soundtrack as I tried to gain my bearings after rolling around in the dark most of the night. I ignored the latest news on Manny Ramirez's apology to the people he already apologized to and turned towards the bathroom, stumbling as my right foot caught the heel of the discarded Nike I'd kicked off the night before. My bare feet scratching against the carpet, I flipped on the light in the bathroom and let my feet rest on the chilly stone floor. Trying to shake the haze out of my eyes, I turned on the faucet, cupped some cold water into my hands, leaned down and splashed it onto my face. I let the water bead down my forehead for a moment before I looked up, my head finally clearing. When I looked into the mirror, I saw him. Standing there, plain as day, the vision that had eluded me for weeks now. A dark-haired man, so nondescript it was utterly remarkable, standing right behind me in black slacks and a black shirt, his face expressionless save for a quick flick upwards of his eyes to acknowledge my presence. Out of fear, I froze for a moment, then wheeled around. Nothing. Nothing was there. I waved my hands around, pawing at the empty air like I was trying to catch a smoky trail. I stood there, on the floor of a Minneapolis hotel bathroom, when I heard a voice from nowhere in particular. "Back here, pal." His voice was even, measured, with one of those indistinguishable Middle American accents that was so perfect it could only have been learned and rehearsed over decades of work. I looked into the mirror again, and there he was. An apparition, no more or less. A figment of my imagination, no more than that. The man in my dreams, the man in the mirror was just something my mind had conjured up. Still, I had questions. "Who the hell are you, and why do I keep thinking that you're trying to kill me?" He smiled wryly and raised an eyebrow, like he was surprised at the question. "Hey, I only showed up because you wanted me to. Everything I've done is under your orders." And now comes the part where I completely lost all grip on reality and started talking to the man in my head. Good times. "I didn't asked to be followed around by a creepy little bastard in black." "Sure did, pal. Well, it's not like you're going to remember it or anything, but trust me, it's all in there." I reached down into the sink and splashed myself again, thinking maybe I could wash my descent into madness away with cold water. No dice. I looked up again and there he stood, encapsulated within the mirror, taunting me with his vacant eyes. "And why would I want to think somebody was out to kill me?" "Not my job to know, boss. All I do is what I'm told." "Bullshit, you're not even real." "Yet, here we are, talking. Geez, no wonder you've gone completely off your nut." Now I was pissed, and I didn't quite know at what. I was pissed at this thing, whatever it was, for tormenting me, and I was even angrier at myself for creating it. "But why?" I asked. "Why now? I'm happy now, I'm working regularly, the pain's the lowest it's been in years! Why the hell do YOU come along NOW?" He smirked. "Now that ... that's the real question, isn't it?" All I could do was stare blankly into his imaginary eyes. "See you later, boss." And just like Keyser Soze, he was gone. ... ... "That's it?" "That's it." Troy postured up on the leather couch, angling himself towards Dr. Marc Bergen, who peered at him over tilted bifocals. The psychiatrist looked down for a moment, twirling his pen aimlessly in his right hand as he gave the notes on his yellow legal pad a cursory glance. "So, Troy, why do you think you're seeing this, this man?" "Isn't that what I'm paying you to find out?" Bergen only smiled. It's a question he faced a lot in this profession. "Well, this is going to sound straight out of Jerry Maguire, but I'm going to need you to help me help you. I can't understand what's going on in that head of yours until you come to terms with it." Troy groaned inwardly. He'd been through enough post-traumatic counseling in his life to know when he was in for the long haul. This Bergen guy was obviously good, but that didn't mean a hell of a lot in Troy's book right about now. "You know what, Doc, we can keep going through this whole thing until I'm blue in the face and your bank account is nice and fat, so why don't we just cut to the chase." "Then answer the question, Troy." Douglas leaned back, his hair scraping against the polished leather of the sofa. "I ... I don't know." "Sure you do." "I really don't." "You sure about that? It seems to me that he had a pretty good idea, which means that answers somewhere in there." Troy blinked, searching his mind for some kind of explanation. Frustrated, he threw out the first thing that came to mind. "Maybe ... maybe I don't think I'm supposed to be happy. I've gone through so much crap in my life with Dad, Lauren, the injuries. Could it be I've gotten to the point in my life where I'm finally at a good spot, and something inside me doesn't want that to happen?" Bergen nodded. "That's good, Troy. Times up for now, but I'm going to give you this prescription. Should help with the insomnia." He scribbled something on a blue pad and shoved it into Troy's hand as Troy got up to leave the office. "Thanks, Doc. Same time next week?" "You're already booked." ***** Six hours later, he was back in Minneapolis, back in the same hotel bathroom, looking down at the same sink as he filled a plastic cup with water. He took the two tiny pills in his hand, popped the into his mouth, and chased them down with a quick gulp of water. He kept his head down for a moment, allowing himself to swallow. Then he looked up, and there he was. Right in the mirror, same black shirt, same black pants, same vacant expression. "Miss me, boss?"
