Who DOESN'T like to boogie dance?

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/31/2005-4005 Everyone has a skeleton in the closet- well, not an actual skeleton because that would be kind of creepy, and all of their clothes would start to smell like rotten bones, but that’s beside the point. For example, many of you may not know it, but Nova’s closet has a box of sex toys in it: whips, handcuffs, gag balls, anal beads, you name it! John Wayne Gacy had a bunch of boys’ corpses sitting in his crawl space. Even Abraham Lincoln, a man revered by all, had his secrets. Everyone knows he was a theater aficionado, but little know that the play he saw the night he was murdered was Cats...and it was the ninth time he’d seen it. Anyway, you’ve all read the Telltale Heart by Edgar Allen Poe, right? I know Dani Furher has! Well, sometimes those skeletons in the closet can start to talk, and when they do, they tell skeleton jokes! SKELETON ONE: “What did the skeleton say when he was riding his motorcycle?” SKELETON TWO: “What?” SKELETON ONE: “I was BONE to be wild!” I’m sorry. Can I start over? As I was saying, sometimes those skeletons in the closet can start to talk, and you can start to hear them. Like the beating of a dead heart, I heard the voices of my past echoing in my brain. I enter my closet, following a faint murmur I picked up while trying to sleep. The murmur followed a nearly indistinguishable crescendo. The bones inside of the coffin were rattling, fingers scratching at the inside of the lid. I pulled the lid off and stared at the contents of the box in front of me with a bewildered look on my face. I haven’t seen these in years. I pull the pants out of the box and unfold them in front of me, pinching the corners of the waistband as I stand up in awe and back out of my closet into the slightly less dim reaches of my bedroom. These are the assless pants, the demon I thought I could escape when I locked them away, never to be seen again...in an old shoe box. My mind is emotionally scrambled. My head is in such a state of disarray right now that I haven’t been able to sleep on a normal schedule in a long time. All these dangerous factors mixed together give way to such late night hallucination. I can tell what exists inside and outside of reality, but I cannot stop myself from looking out of the window of my own sanity. What the...I think Ozric Mortimer is trying to take over my flash. Get out of here! Alright, back to business. The assless nature of the pants is disrupted as a faceless mouth, snarling teeth and all forms in its place. It smiles at me, mockingly. These were the same pants that caused my demise. I had gone crazy with power and confidence. I thought that I could do whatever I wanted. I thought I was the best. And then the pants... Behind my very back, no pun intended, they turned me into a joke. As soon as I’d turn around, people would start laughing. I thought they were laughing at my ass, but they were laughing at a much larger ass. They were laughing at K-Wolf’s ass. Wait, they were laughing at me. I was the ass. Well, damn you, the pants! Damn you! BIG POPPA: “DAMN YOU!” The flick of a light switch clicks, and I turn my head to the door as my eyes slowly readjust to the light. I stand, holding a mouthless, normal pair of pants...well, normal except for the assless part. My grandpa stares at me wearing a blue and white striped pair of pajamas with matching nightcap. I stare back at him with a dumbfounded expression painted on my face. I hold the pants up in front of me with my arms and fingers frozen in confusion. I look back at the pants. Then I look at my grandpa. My grandpa is still looking at me. He looks at the pants. He looks back at me. I look at the pants. The pants look at my grandpa. I look back at my grandpa. He is still looking at me. GRANDPOPPA POPPA: “What the hell are you doing, Big Asshole?” BIG POPPA: “I...uh...” GRANDPOPPA POPPA: “You ain’t no fruit, are you? I’ve always had my suspicions.” I feel like now’s not the time for words. I just keep my mouth shut and hopefully, the verbal berating that I’m about to endure passes over me rather quickly. The old man shakes his head a few times. GRANDPOPPA POPPA: “It’s no wonder you lost to Tyler Lopez. You’re a sissy boy. And you’re going to lose again to Peter Vetra! Ha! Peter Vetra is the weenie of wrestling, and he’s still going to kick your ass. You ever go to the movies and see the preview with the dancing hot dog?” BIG POPPA: “Yeah, I like those previews.” GRANDPOPPA POPPA: “Good! Now imagine getting pinned by the dancing hot dog. That’s what’s going to happen to you on ReVolution!” For a few seconds, I wonder how my grandpa pronounced ReVolution with the ‘V’ capitalized. Then I relish the idea of being pinned by the dancing hot dog (no pun intended again), and it leaves a sour taste in my mouth (I’m on a roll!). GRANDPOPPA POPPA: “If you want to have a chance against him, you’ll need more training. But I won’t train you until you get rid of those pants! Now that’s your first mission. Get rid of the pants first thing tomorrow morning.” My grandfather starts to leave as he turns the lights back off, but I interrupt him as he is almost out of view. BIG POPPA: “Hey, grandpa? Can you tuck me in?” Awkward pause. GRANDPOPPA POPPA: “No.” The next day... Grandpoppa Poppa bursts through my door at about 5:32 according to my alarm clock. The old guy is brimming with vigor as he hits me with a sock filled with quarters to wake me up. I rub my eyes a little bit, wondering what the hell is going on before turning to see the old man beating away at me. BIG POPPA: “Stop it. Stop hitting me.” GRANDPOPPA POPPA: “The day’s half over, and those pants are still sitting on your floor. Get cracking!” BIG POPPA: “Technically, the day isn’t even a quarter over.” That smart aleck comment just bought me a smack in the mouth with the change encrusted sock. The thing that bothers me most about this smack to the mouth with this sock isn’t so much the fact that a mass of quarters is smacking me in the jaw, but that every Tuesday night, my grandpa brings the same sock full of different quarters to the local peep show. As the sock empties itself of quarters, my grandpa fills it with...OH GOD! I’m going to vomit. GRANDPOPPA POPPA: “Get up!” I follow the instruction of my grandfather and gingerly get out of my bed. He points to the pants, and as I sorely bend over to pick the pants up, my grandpa sends me a loving boot to the ass. I stumble forward a few steps before picking up the pants with all the grace and coordination of FDR’s zombie. GRANDPOPPA POPPA: “Now I want these pants gone on the double!” I exit my room and make my way downstairs. I know these pants must be rid of forever, not because my grandpa said so, but because they represent everything about myself that slid into the pathetic, washed up, mechanical being I am. Maybe getting rid of the pants won’t scrape the rust off of me and it definitely won’t put any championships around my waist, but if I need to restore my dignity I’m going to have to start at the bottom. As I make my way into my garage, I picture each third count I’ve ever had counted on me since donning the trousers with no backside. Each one is more and more painful as my opponents become less and less challenging. The one with Tyler Lopez hurts the most. Fans may come to my defense- “Yeah, Poppa, but he cheated!” So what? I showed no control over that match. There’s no sign that I would have won if he hadn’t cheated. Foul play or not, my shoulders were the ones pinned to that mat. There used to be a time when cheating didn’t matter because regardless, I could always kick out. When I wasn’t dominating opponents, I was getting up from anything and everything thrown at me. No move could keep me down. No weapon could knock me off my feet long enough to end the match. I find a shovel hanging up on a nail and grab it in my free hand. My work boots are placed on the mat in front of the door to the driveway so I slide my feet into them, and with a small degree of difficulty, manage to jam them on without using my hands. I open the garage door by pressing a button next to the doorway, and the dull light of morning spreads into the garage. I step out of the shadowed area over the contrast line and leave my garage, trudging into my front yard. After dropping my pants to the side for a second, I pick a spot and jam the shovelhead into the dirt. One stomp from my work boots embeds the shovel deeper into the soil before I pull a chunk of dirt away. I dig continuously like this until I’ve created a nice hole in my front yard. With the shovel, I drag the wretched pants into the hole and gaze upon them one last time. It’s like in a slasher film when the main character finally kills the sociopath mutant who has been pursuing him, and he thinks the guy is dead, but against anything physically possible, the sociopath mutant gets back up and scares the shit out of the main character before chasing him through a cornfield into an abandoned grain factory with all sorts of dangerous looking machinery covered in cobwebs in the dark. Except it’s almost nothing like that at all, especially since the pants don’t do anything...cause, well, they’re pants. I begin patching the hole with the free dirt, and before long, the pants are gone from sight. As I pat the lump of soil down with the shovel in the attempt to make it more even, my job is complete. I let the shovel drop, and rub my hands together while exhaling a satisfied breath of air...or more accurately, carbon dioxide. But it would’ve sounded awkward if I said that I was exhaling a satisfied breath of carbon dioxide. BIG POPPA: “I just hope that nobody ever digs those cursed things up again.” The next day...two thousand years in the future! A man who looks exactly like me but is obviously not Big Poppa emerges from a titanium sleep capsule in his robot house. He is wearing a tight silver jumpsuit and a space age plastic helmet. I can’t believe it took over ten thousand years for mankind to realize that skin tight jumpsuits are the most comfortable and efficient clothing ever invented. FUTURE POPPA: “Boy, I’m starving. I could go for a nice plate of nutritious dirt.” Also, in the future, food has been replaced with nutrient capsules just like they predicted we would be eating now back in 1960. Things with any flavor whatsoever, like dirt and battery acid, are considered delicacies. This is because I need an easy way to segue to the poor climax that waits ahead. Future Poppa walks down the stairs past Grandpoppa Poppa who is somehow still alive without experiencing any further aging. He’s sitting on a pressurized flow of air while downloading the newspaper on a touchscreen tablet. GRANDPOPPA POPPA: “God damn jumbles! Who needs them? If they took out the jumbles, they could fit another bra advertisement in the newspaper!” Future Poppa begins to harvest some good eating from a lawn torn apart and spotted with holes from previous meals when suddenly, he realize that his breakfast is contaminated with something. FUTURE POPPA: “Gross! There’s something in my dirt!” Future Poppa unearths the assless pants from the soil and holds them before him, awakening an ancient family curse. Then he looks down into the dirt and realizes that there are bones all over the place. So as it turns out, the pants weren’t the reason for my demise at all. Instead, I was just living on an ancient Indian burial ground. Huh.
