Date: October The heat in his room was such that the telephone receiver was like a wet bar of soap in his clammy paw, even with the window open. Though gentle wisps of cool night air kissed his skin through the mosquito net they weren't enough to quell five poorly-insulted floors worth of body heat, and San Diego's pleasant Autumn twilight provided his perspiring body with no comfort. His wait ended the way it always did; ten rings, no answer, straight to voicemail. “Hi, this is Sylvia; I'm not here, obviously, so leave one and I'll get back to you.” He'd heard the sentence so many times he could recite it in the perfect pitch and tone, at just the right pace, but he'd never hear back from her, no matter how often he called. “Uhhh, hey Sylv,” he began in the same nervy quiver his voice fell into every time that tone sounded. It never got easier, speaking to her. Never. “I haven't called in a couple of days, just thought I'd tell you how things are going for me just now.” Pausing to wipe a layer of perspiration from his brow, he soon pushed the phone against his ear with his elbow, no longer able to hold it in his sweaty hands. “I got a wrestling gig today. Pretty big thing for me, had to beat this guy the boss is really high on. Well, I say 'beat'. Didn't exactly out-wrestle him – heck, he's almost thirty years younger than me. Got to a point, after about ten minutes, where I thought I was gonna keel over in there.” The man chuckled laughter that she'd never hear and pulled his sweat sodden vest away from his beefy torso. Soon his demeanour returned to normal, and a fractured smile faded in a room lit only by a muted black and white television as quickly as it had appeared. “Had to do something I'm not proud of, I'm not gonna lie. I-... maybe I went a little bit too far this time, but damnit, I had to do it. I had to do something for me, something for our little boy. When this contract comes through, even if I just last one week in the tournament, it'll be enough cash to keep me afloat for months. Might even have a little left over to do something real nice for you guys.” He paused, struggling to keep his breath as anxiety and the muggy heat overcame him. “Feel real bad about the other guy; think he's hurt pretty bad, but without this... well, I don't know what might've happened to me. Maybe you'll read all kinds of nasty things about it on the internet, but I know you'll understand. You always do.” A lump began to bulge and swell in his throat as he clutched the phone's cord nervously. He wasn't having a conversation with her, but he new Sylvia was there, and even if she wasn't she'd hear the message eventually. Two years had past since the split, but communicating with his ex-wife only seemed to get more and more troublesome as the days passed by. “How are you guys, anyway?” he said, rising and slowly shuffling towards the old futon he spent his nights on. “I miss you guys; you and Alejandro. Maybe we could do something soon? I dunno, a picnic or something. Just a one-off, like a family. It'd be nice.” He plopped his bulgy frame down on the futon and sighed. “Been over a year since I saw him, and I know things are never gonna be the same between you and me again, but he's my son. I--” he stammered, struggling for words. “I'd like to see him again.” Even in the darkened light he could just about make out the family photos that lined the table on the otherside of the studio apartment's one room. Usually when he gazed it them it made him smile, but not tonight. Inside he closed his eyes. Took a deep breath in and let it out. So cathartic. The first thing he saw when he opened his eyes was the red and white wrestling mask, hanging from the peg by the window. “I'll talk to you later, Sylvia. To Carry the Load * * * * * SHOCK IN 'DIEGO AS TURNBUCKLE COLLAPSE HOSPITALISES RANDALL; DE LA RED ADVANCES by Louis Picker, 10.09.09 Crowds in San Diego, California were left shocked yesterday evening as a horrific turnbuckle accident rendered much-fancied Mission Valley Wrestling grappler Jay Randall unable to continue his PRIME Jewel in the Crown qualifying match against an ageing Roque de la Red. Having dominated for long portions of the match, the twenty-one year old “Vagabond” took to the top rope, looking to finish de la Red off with his trademark Shooting Star Press. However, no sooner had Randall placed his full weight on the turnbuckle than it gave way beneath him. Amateur footage (available here) show clearly what happens next; the MVW Champion falls awkwardly, his knee becoming twisted in the loosened ropes, causing him to slingshot face-first into the mat. Randall remains in a stable state in a local hospital, but speculation suggests that it may take four months before he's able to compete again after suffering a concussion and a knee ligament tear. The match was called-off after a referee's check, and de la Red was quickly announced the winner. This means, of course, that MVW's entrant to PRIME's Jewel in the Crown will not be it's shining star Randall, but a man who's best days came twenty years ago. Roque de la Red has been dogged by demons since his forced exit from Mexico's CMLL promotion in 1989, and his career has never quite recovered. As alcoholism and rampant drug abuse took over his life, de la Red took a self-imposed hiatus from the sport, only to emerge on the US indy scene four years and two rehab stints later. His career has never been the same, though. Seemingly incapable of controlling his vices for too long, a man who was once one of the brightest stars in the continent has become notorious for a spate of no-shows, lacklustre (and sometimes intoxicated) performances, and a shoddy work ethic. His decline has been likened to that of Jake “The Snake” Roberts, and really, it's not hard to see why. Make no mistake, his participation in JitC is an absolute disaster for both MVW and PRIME. In Randall they had a star tailor-made to take such a challenge and run with it. He's still very much learning his trade in the business, but he's accomplished more in his four years as a professional than most indy workers do in twenty. He's got the look, the charisma, and the flashy in-ring style to make himself a star; all he needed was the exposure, and now it seems very much like that has been snatched away from him. De la Red, on the other hand, is a man going nowhere. His role was simple; he'd put up a solid fight against Randall and help put the kid over, but would ultimately fall short and allow the young grappler to move on and realise his dreams. Now, this trainwreck of an individual is going to be competing for the world's biggest wrestling promotion, in front of a global audience of millions. Put in short, this wasn't supposed to happen. We'll bring you more on this story as it develops, but for now, it looks very much like Lisa Tyler' and her crew are going to have to put up with a forty-nine year old waster and not the youthful blue chipper they were expecting. * * * * * “Take me through what happened, one more time.” Bobby Bosch leaned forward in the cheap plastic hair, removing his navy-blue Chargers cap to scratch the itching bald patch beneath. He watched as the long-haired man in the bed before him closed his eyes and exhaled through his nose. “Do I really gotta say it again?” the bed-ridden man, tired and fed-up, responded in a gruff tone that belied his bright, youthful complexion. “Just trying to build up a mental image here. Somethin' ain't adding up.” The younger man opened his eyes, gently tilting his head to the side to shift a troublesome strand of brown hair from his vision. “Well, it's pretty fuckin' simple. I climb up to the top. Steady myself, turn around. Go full stretch and then next thing I know there's a click, and poof. S'all black after that.” “You don't remember falling?” “What'd I just say?” Bosch nodded silently, raising a hand to his fat chin. This was the first time, in two decades' worth of promoting wrestling in Southern California, that one of his rings had fallen to pieces with such disastrous consequences. There'd been the odd hiccup every now and then; ropes coming loose, canvas getting torn, ring boards breaking, but nothing that had ever incapacitated one of his competitors. Nothing that had ever incapacitated his champion, his great white hope. Jay wasn't looking so good. His knee was mangled; not broken, but bad enough to have to be raised at all times, and his green eyes were still a little bit glazed as the concussion's hangover lingered. This was a man that should've been on the cusp of something monumental. He wouldn't have been the biggest name in the JitC field, but he was one of the best young wrestlers Bobby had ever seen. Boy had that unknown x-factor that'd surely see him passed the first few rounds, whereas Roque de la Red would be lucky to even make it to the first match. The Mexican was a human car crash, and it pained Bosch to have to throw him into JitC as MVW's representative, but he couldn't exactly send Randall in his current state. “That fuckin' cunt, man,” Randall muttered in his over-starched, white hospital bed. “I tell ya, next time I see his fat face, he's gettin' fix knuckles straight between the eyes. I swear I'll cu--” “Whoa, cool it,” Bobby said, trying to calm his young champion down. “It was an acc--” “Accident my ass.” Bobby's wide, full moon face twisted with confusion. He leaned forward. “C'mon, you know how seriously we take our performers' safety. We must've went over the usual ring checks four or five times last night.” “Shit, I ain't sayin' it was no slight on your behalf. Had to be that Mexican fuck, I'm tellin' yah. What other reason would he've been sniffin' 'round the turnbuckles durin' the interval?” Jay paused, flashing Bobby a mask of scorn. “I saw him messin' with that shit, an' he sure as hell ain't no ring technician.” “Wait a second. You're suggesting that Roque had something to do with this?” Jay groaned. “Don't tell me it doesn't make sense. He knew he was booked to lose, an' let's be honest, guy ain't exactly had much goin' for him these past twenty years. JitC, a chance to see his name in lights again, an' a big fat paycheque. Not exactly a massive stretch of the imagination.” “I know that guy has his problems, Jay – but really, this?” Bosch's brow raised as he quizzed his champion. “I don't know. He's 'bout as reliable as a chocolate coffee pot, but it takes a twisted fuck t'do somethin' like this. Not sure he's that type.” Pushing his skull down into a pillow that was too lumpy for his liking, Jay grit his teeth and tried to suppress a sigh. Barbs of white hot pain shot up from his knee and he yearned for more painkillers, but he wouldn't call the nurse until he'd said his piece to Bobby. “Else can I say, man? He did it, I'm sure.” It was an option that Bosch hadn't even considered, yet not one that was completely impossible. He'd seen first-hand the way de la Red lived in that shitty studio, and it certainly wouldn't have surprised Bobby if the has-been's desperation had grown to such a level that he'd deliberately derail the career of a younger worker for his own benefit. “Think you saw him messin' with the turnbuckle durin' the intermission, huh?” Jay nodded. “Yeah. Dude was out there doin' his usual thing, signin' a couple autographs, then he hops up an' starts messing with the 'buckles while he's workin' the crowd.” “If you saw this, why did you try an' climb it?” “Dunno. Was 'in the zone' I guess, wasn't really thinkin' about it.” Bobby sat back in his chair and looked up at the stained roofing tiles. He sighed, letting off some steam as he reclined in the curtained-off section of ward thirty-seven, pondering. Never before had he heard of anything so vindictive around MVW, but Roque wasn't exactly the most trustworthy personality in the world. He couldn't rule it out. “You can't let this fuck get away with this, man,” Randall piped. “Gotta take that JitC slot off him, he don't deserve it.” “Shit, Jay. I gotta send someone, you have any idea how badly Tyler'll fuck me over if I burn her?” “Give it to someone else then, fuck if I care. Just get that cheatin' prick out.” Bobby shook his head. “I can't do that. It's all signed and sealed; fax came through this morning. I break this and I've got PRIME's legal team breathin' down my neck.” “What the fu--?!” The rage in Jay Randall's voice was almost tangible, but it was something Bobby Bosch had to interrupted. Raising to his 5'6”, the indy promoter looked at his most promising of prospects. “I'm sorry Jay, my hands are tied.” Randall parted his lips to utter a “but,” but Bosch had already disappeared through the curtains before the air ever had a chance to escape his lungs. Closing his eyes, he clenched a fist and bashed it into his stiff mattress. * * * * * The afternoon sun beat down on him as his keyring MP3 player pumped an old Roy Orbison song into his ears. Dressed from head-to-toe in his Wal-Mart uniform, Roque took great care in manoeuvring his convoy of shopping carts towards their bay by the main building, a routine he'd repeat a thousand times more all for the sake of a $7.25 p/h pay packet. A sense of satisfaction washed over him as he slid the six carts into a row of twelve others. He smiled; a small victory, but that's all you get when you work retail. Shuffling the carts closer together, Roque thought he heard a muffled voice over the top of his music. He pulled both earphones out and turned around, squinting in the mid-day sun to see one of his significantly younger colleagues standing before him. “How's it going, man?” his co-worker asked. He couldn't have stood at more than 5'8” and he was built with the body of a broomhandle and the complexion of Casper, which didn't do much to make the thin “moustache” on his upper lip look anything less than horrendous. “Oh, hey Kevin,” Roque responded, reaching into his pocket and killing the music – the last thing he wanted was to run out of tunes before his ten hours of monotony were through. “Didn't see you there.” Kevin nodded. Roque had never seen a man who needed with the love of a good woman more than Kevin did, but he was a good kid for the most part. Little nerdy at times, yeah, and his knew things about Roque's wrestling career that even Red couldn't remember, but he was all right. “You look pretty pleased with yourself today. Guess it's nothing to do with this place?” Roque smiled. “Heh, think I might've got myself a job.” “This is your job.” “No, a wrestling job.” The twenty-something wrestling fan's face lit up. “Oh, for real? Something decent? A tour, contract?” “Can't say anything just yet, but yeah. Could be the biggest thing I've done in a couple decades.” “So you'll be outta this shithole like a shut, huh?” “Dunno. Might end up being just one show, depends if I can make it past the firs--” Roque wanted to elaborate, but found himself interrupted by the bolshy familiarity of Bobby Bosch's deep baritone. “Yo, Roque,” he said, strictly business as he came marching across the parking lot in his all-black outfit. Kevin saw this as his cue to leave. Having attended more than his fair share of indy shows over the past few years, he was quite well-acquainted with who Bobby Bosch was, but the tone of the MVW head honcho's voice was one that suggested he wasn't there for pleasantries. As Kevin scampered back into the deepest recesses of the store, surprise came over Roque. “Hey boss. What'chu doing here?” Bobby stopped himself a few feet from Roque and took a couple of minutes to eye-up the Mexican's ill-fitting uniform. “So this is what you do on the side, huh? Good God, man.” “Yeah, well. Pays the rent.” Roque shrugged. The last thing he'd expected was for his promoter to show up at his other place of work, and it felt awkward to be standing before Bosch in such modest attire, but there was little he could do about it now. “You don't usually come see me at work. What's up?” “Last night.” “Oh. Terrible shame,” Roque said awkwardly, his face sagging with regret. “So harsh on Jay; I ain't above admittin' he had me there; I guess fortune just wasn't with him. Really feel for the guy. How's he doin'?” “Knee's fucked, dude probably ain't gonna be walkin' on his own for a while. Four months he's gonna be out, too,” Bobby said. There was suspicion in his voice, and Roque didn't like it. “Shit. Well, uhhh, I guess every cloud has a silver lining and all that,” Roque retorted, mustering the slightest of smiles. “I'm not gonna let you down in JitC, boss.” Bobby didn't respond. Instead he furrowed his brow, tilted his head back a little. Eventually Roque gave in. “Alright, what is it?” “Y'know, that kid has everythin' going for him, Roque,” Bobby began. “He's gonna be a star, and he ain't gon' have much longer to wait neither. He's improvin' faster than anyone I've ever seen, and he's got a face that was just made for magazine covers. He was gonna enter JitC and make a name for himself; this was it, his big chance, an' now he can't go 'cause a fuckin' turnbuckle collapsed on him.” “Ye--” “An' now I gotta send you instead, and let's be honest, Roque, you ain't no spring chicken no more. Hell, you were only in that damn match t'make the kid look good on his road to PRIME. I mean, you ain't got nothin' left. You can't move like you used to, and lord knows you're at least twenty pounds overweight. An' with your personal record, you ain't 'xactly the most marketable guy in the world either.” Roque felt himself begin to sweat, and it wasn't from the sun either. Twitching, he slid a hand into his pocket and felt his heart pound harder beneath his ribcage. Bobby stared coldly at Roque's chubby face, at his thick caterpillar eyebrows and his round button nose, and suddenly Jay Randall's theory seemed quite conceivable. “Hell of a thing to happen, huh Roque?” “Yeah,” Roque replied unconvincingly. “Hell of a coincidence.” Sheepishly, de la Red's gaze turned to the crack concrete beneath his ragged Adidas trainers. Desperately he tried to muster some kind of response, but it was pointless. The decision he'd made last night was now staring him dead in the eye, and with nowhere to run, he disappeared inside himself. “Hell of a coincidence.” Bobby shook his head, disappointed. “I knew it. I fucking knew it.” Roque said nothing. “You're one disgusting fuck, you know that? You got any idea how far this could set the kick back? Fuck, Roque.” His face growing read, Bobby shook his head. Roque looked up shamefully as Bobby stepped forward, wagging a finger at his leathery skin. “Well, shithead, you're in luck. Fortunately for you the contracts came through this morning, so you're in Jewel in the Crown, I can't pull you from that. But let me tell ya, you come anywhere near an MVW show again an' I'll personally kick your ass right out the buildin', ya hear? There's cheatin', there's lyin', and then there's this – the downright despicable.” A pause lingered between the two. Bosch wasn't willing to wait longer than a couple of seconds for a response. “Nothing to say for yourself? Fuckin' typical,” he spat venomously. Slowly he began to back away from the ageing grappler, shaking his head. “You'd best be hopin' this JitC shit works out for you, 'cause you're fuckin' finished in this town. Finished.” * * * * * Groggy and dishevelled, his dishonour exposed, Roque trudged into the squalid one-bed studio that was his life and kicked off his trainers. Picking up his mask on the way, he walks towards the telephone and dials Sylvia's number.
