The Compelling Argument of Zeros

May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May, 2007 Early May. “Hey! Easy with them boxes!” Wade Elliott, releasing a large cardboard box en route to God knows where, grumbled to himself as he loaded a UPS truck with the packaged goods of theBirmingham Tractor Supply Factory, his current place of employment. “Yeah, keep mutterin' to yerself, boy! Any of this shit's busted an' it's yer ass an' yer paycheck!” Wade lifted a lack-luster middle finger to the UPS man's turned back as he closed up the truck. He leaned against the hard cement wall, wiping his brow with a dirty forearm. Much to his hidden delight, the loud buzzer indicating the 2:15 break rang throughout the factory, and Wade lumbered himself down the length of the Birmingham Tractor Supply Factory and toward the break room. The refrigerator in the tiny room presented a small paper bag, which Wade gripped in his hand before taking a seat at a table. The bag gave Wade a shiny green apple and a bottle of water, which he promptly chomped into and sucked dry. “Another truck just swung in,” said the voice of Wade's fellow, but not likable, co-worker Evan. “Go load 'er up.” “I'm on break, buddy,” replied Wade, thick eyebrows narrowed with annoyance as he took a big bite of apple. More factory workers piled into the tiny room, digging into the fridge for their afternoon snacks. “And I said to load the god-damn truck, Elliott,” said Evan, tilting his head, as if Wade's words of defiance were out of the ordinary. Evan took a seat across from Wade, staring him down. Evan was a large guy. Fat, but not in the beach-ball sense. Rugged? Maybe. Plenty of tattoos to at least feign rugged-ness, and the red bandanna wrapped around his head presented the tough biker look, although Evan's four-door sedan would say otherwise. “And I'm tellin' ya to kiss my ass,” answered Wade, staring Evan down, chewing pieces of apple. “Who do you think you are, Elliott?” asked Evan, attempting to look imposing by shifting his shoulders forward. “Some low-down, piece of shit factory worker in Birmingham, Alabama, Evan...just like you. And since you ain't my boss, I suggest you shut yer fat fuckin' mouth and let me have my fifteen-fuckin'-minute break before I stomp yer jaw into more pieces than you'd generally like, how's that sound?” Wade chewed his apple. Evan stared him down from across the table. “You piece of horse-shit...” growled Evan, as he shoved the table forward into Wade's gut. Wade paused, eyes locked on the douche-bag across from him. He set down what was left of his apple, swallowed the pieces chewed up in his mouth, and flipped the table over, bowling the fat-fucker across from him. Wade quickly leaped over the table, mounting Evan with rough fists clenched around his neck. “You ain't chose the best day to give me grief, you fuckin' shit!” roared Wade. “I work ten hour days, doin' shit labor, an' I don't need fucks like you runnin' their fat fuckin' mouths at the time I get a god damn break! You fuckin' understand me you fat fuckin' waste?!” Of course, Evan couldn't answer, as the life was being choked out of him by Wade's well muscled arms. It was at this point that a group of Wade's co-workers wrenched him off of Evan's neck, leaving the fat bastard to choke and cough on the floor. “Kiss my hairy-god-damn ass!” Continued Wade. “I'll break yer god-damn neck!” * * * * * * * “We can't keep you, Wade.” Wade sat in front of a large desk, arms folded, eyes narrowed, staring past the man in front of him telling him his time at the Birmingham Tractor Supply Factory was over. “This is the third fight you've been in this month, and we just had to put Evan in the hospital for throat injury. Sorry, but the third strike means you're out,” continued the man behind the desk. An older fellow, with a well trimmed beard, wearing a flannel collared shirt. “What about some god-damn compensation?” asked Wade, beginning to unbutton his blue jump-suit. “If yer gonna just fire me I'd better get somethin' to keep me on my fuckin' feet for a li'l while.” “Pffft, this ain't some desk job, Elliott,” replied the man. “Sorry, but that's it. You'll get your last check in the mail.” “Suck my swingin' cock.” “Get out.” * * * * * * * Wade's red, rusty, old '94 Ford Ranger rumbled into a parking lot behind an old apartment complex in Birmingham. He brought the rig to a stop and cut the engine, exhaling heavily and leaning his head against the back window. He took off his drifter's cap and ran a hand over his dirty face, dragging his fingers through his thick brown goatee. His eyes fell over the rear view mirror, catching his own blue orbs in the reflection. With another heavy sigh he hopped out of the truck and ascended a rickety old staircase toward his apartment, one heavy steel-toed boot thumping after another. As he rounded a corner to his apartment he noticed a small white piece of paper tacked onto his door. With furled brow he stepped up to the note, gritting his teeth as he read the scrawled penmanship. Wade, consider this an eviction notice. You've got til' Friday to move the hell out. -Buck Wade grabbed up the note and tore it into pieces. Thank God he gave him til' Friday. Being Wednesday that should be plenty of time to find a new home. Near explosion, Wade threw open the door and barged into his apartment, slamming the door damn near off its hinges. He flopped onto an old couch, trying to contain his anger. He looked around at the disheveled apartment. Busted window here, cracked walls there. Lucky for Wade he didn't own much. A sack of clothes, some footwear, his truck and a few bottles of bourbon. It wasn't his stuff that was the problem. It was finding a place to put it. A day or two to find a new place. Thanks, Buck. Wade threw his burly arm over the couch and drove his knuckles into the off-white wall, leaving four roundish holes, all with little spider-webs darting this way and that. He took a good nap. * * * * * * * “Good set. Take a breather.” Wade sat up from the bench-press, sweat dripping down his arms. He breathed heavy, grabbing a towel before heaving himself to his feet. His spotter, Rick, a small little guy of 5'5” with shaggy hair and a clean-shaven face took his spot on the bench. Wade stood above and behind, catching his breath. “Sorry to hear 'bout yer job, Wade,” said Rick, gripping the bar and lifting it over his torso. “Buncha cock-suckers anyhow,” said Wade, irritatedly indifferent. “Rough luck on the apartment, too,” continued Rick, beginning to pump up and down. “Yeah.” “You know I'd bring ya on into my place fer a li'l while, Wade, if the wife didn't hate yer guts.” “Offense intended, yer wife's a fuckin' whore, Rick.” “Yeahp, that's what they tell me.” Rick set the bar back after twelve repetitions, hopping up and grabbing a towel of his own. Wade grabbed two extra ten pound weights, setting one on each end. He took his spot and began pumping the iron once more. “Whaddya plan on doin' now?” asked Rick, wiping sweat from his brow. “*Huff!* Fucked if I know. Ain't like I've never lived outta my truck before. *Huff!* I'll find work somewheres. *Huff!*” “Yeah, s'pose so...why don'tcha ever go try n' do that wresslin' thang again? You always loved that wrestlin'.” “Grah, tried that bag years ago.” Wade set the bar back with a heave, standing to his feet. “Didn't like my style. They can kiss my ass fer all I care.” “That was back then, though, Wade,” replied Rick, taking the weights off. “Shit, it was ten years ago ya tried out, whadn't it? Wrestlin's changed since then, Wade. Been watchin' that PRIME lately, got all sortsa characters on it!” Wade stood as spotter while Rick took position once more. “Yeah, well, wrestlin' world told me 'no' once. I say fuck em'.” “Cain't hurt to try again,” said Rick, gripping the bar. “Whaddya got to lose?” “Well, already lost my job n' my apartment, so that'd leave me with, well...nothin'.” “'xactly! Maybe yer streak of bad luck is a sign!” “If God's sendin' me a sign he's got a real ass-hole way of goin' 'bout it.” Rick lifted the bar about halfway before his vein started popping out of his head. He puffed his cheeks, and after a few moments Wade grabbed the bar and swiftly put it in its holder. Rick caught his breath. “Whoo! Okay, that's enough.” “C'mon, let's git outta here.” Rick snagged his towel and the two ambled through the rather passive gym. “Tom said somethin' 'bout a talent scout comin' through here a little bit back,” said Rick. “We should swing by n' see what it was all about. Cain't hurt, can it?” Wade agreed with a grumble. Tom was the proprietor of this fine establishment, a well-muscled guy covered in tattoos with a gray goatee that damn near reached his belt. He was probably a Hell's Angel in the day. Wade and Rick walked up to the desk where Tom was folding towels. “All done, boys?” asked Tom, flopping a pile of towels into a large hamper. “Yessir!” replied Rick. Tom reached under his counter to retrieve Wade and Rick's member cards. “Hey Tom,” continued Rick. “What was that thing 'bout some talent scout runnin' through?” “Oh, that fella,” said Tom, after thinking about it for a moment. “Some city slicker workin' fer some wrestlin' organization. Was lookin' fer talent to join some tournament, told me to send anyone worth sendin' his way. Hold on, I got the flyer here somewheres...” Tom rifled through a stack of papers and soon retrieved a sheet, handing it to Wade. Wade reluctantly took the flyer. Chris Williams Memorial Cup A Tournament of Champions to Honor a Fallen Comrade of the Wrestling Community Applications are Open Wade handed the paper back to Tom, raising his eyebrows in some sort of interest. “I dunno...” “Oh, come on, Wade!” Piped up Rick, smacking Wade in the shoulder. “You'd be real perfect for it!” “What makes me more deservin' than anyone else frequents this place?” asked Wade. Tom and Rick looked around the gym, eyeing the overweight Alabamians who would no doubt replace the calories they just burned with Budweisers at the bar later that night. The two then turned to Wade, obviously in extremely good condition, intimidating even. “Wade,” said Tom. “I don't think ya got nothin' to worry 'bout.” * * * * * * * “Let me just say, I am thrilled we found you, Wade.” Kenneth Coffrin, talent scout, pulled some papers from a black briefcase and set them on glass coffee table in front of Wade. Wade sat across in a chair, hunched over with obvious interest in his eyes. “So yer sayin' not only am I gettin' in on this tournament here, but this PRIME place is interested?” “Oh, they're not just interested,” said Coffrin, slicking back his black hair. “They want you, Wade.” “That a fact.” “It is.” “So if PRIME wants me, why should I join this Christian Williamson, whatever the hell his name is tournament?” “Cash prize of a million bucks.” “I'm in.” “Excellent.” Coffrin pulled out piece of paper and slid it over to Wade, who promptly signed with a sloppy scribble. Wade lifted his pen, then began chewing it, thoughtful. “If I win this tournament,” said Wade, coming out of deep consideration. “Why would I sign the PRIME contract? I could get mighty comfortable with a million bucks.” “This is why.” Coffrin slid another piece of paper across the table. Wade took a peek, and Coffrin grinned when Wade's eyes popped out of his head. “Those zeros make a helluva good argument,” said Wade, eyes still transfixed on the paper. “They sure do.”
