Can You Run Away?

24 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 24, 2007 CUE: "Thieves in the Night" by Mos Def & Talib Kweli ---- Easton's feet drummed against the ground as he hustled through the narrow alleyway. Brick walls were thrown up on his left and right, the buildings looming over him, so tall that they seemed to come together somewhere miles in the sky. His hair flowed behind him as if a fierce wind blew in his face, but there was no air against his face. His eyebrows knitted together and he dipped his head lower, legs pumping harder. The robed silhouette before him seemed to move effortlessly, always faster than he was, ever elusive. He could hear the way that its footsteps splashed into puddles and knew it was there, knew it was in front of him. If only he could reach it, go faster. He sucked in air and grunted, trying to go faster. He didn't know how hard he could continue to push his body. His muscles were melting with every step. The exit of the alley was before him and Easton dreaded it. Once out of this straight track, the shadow could move anywhere, lose itself. Easton growled, leaning forward, forcing himself faster. He wasn't going to catch him. He had to catch him. Easton's eyes narrowed, focusing only on the shadow. Three steps from salvation. Easton's fists balled. Two steps. Easton's arms worked back and forth as if climbing, as if his arms could possibly help him go faster. One. Easton's lips drew backwards, his scowl like a feral wolf's. He wasn't going to catch him. He couldn't afford not to— A gunshot cut the air as the shadow ran into salvation and still on, not looking backwards for a second. Easton skidded to a halt, the momentum swinging his hands slowly upwards. Just as they were suitably spread, his left arm a bit higher than his right, a body fell from some height. His knees buckled just a bit and he looked down to see who it was. The grotesque visage of Rita Sammael stared back at him, the bullet wound dripping blood and bathing her face. Easton looked at her, bewildered, before dropping her to the ground and stepping over her. He couldn't stop. Already, he had to be so far behind. Easton tore into a run once again, pulling out of the alley and starting into the open space. There was a crowd on the sidewalk and Easton pushed his way into it. The people flowed away from his touch, not speaking, not shouting, not resisting at all. Easton barely registered it. His head began to throb, three heavy beats that rattled his brain. Easton shook it off and continued running, shoving people aside. He could catch him. He just had to keep going. Again, three pounding beats into his brain. Easton's knees turned to water ballons, unable to support his weight. He stumbled forward, bowling people over. "EASTON!" Easton blinked, pressing his palm to the bridge of his nose. BOOM, BOOM, BOOM again, crushing his brain inward. "EASTON!" Easton toppled to the ground, skinning his arm. He had to catch him, dammit! He couldn't let him go. If he didn't catch him, no one else would. He had to do it. Easton's head snapped backwards, smacking against the sidewalk. CUE: "Gun Music" by Talib Kweli ---- Easton's eyes were opened and he looked at the door, his expression something akin to irritation. "EASTON!" BOOM BOOM BOOM Easton rose from his crosslegged position and moved towards the door, flipping up the peephole cover, but he already knew who it was. His hand was already working, unlocking the door, and he stepped away to pull it open. Detective Stan Hepton pushed his way into the apartment, trenchcoat and all. "Sometimes," Easton said drolly, closing the door, "I like to be alone." Stan turned around and pulled out the single chair, sitting down. "Since when did I start looking even remotely like a guy who gave a fuck?" Hepton said bitterly. Easton nodded and moved to the center of the room, looking down at the detective. "So, what is it?" Easton asked. "We haven't gotten further on this case," the detective said. Easton frowned at the detective's singlemindedness, but Stan Hepton didn't seem to notice. "But more importantly, you haven't gotten any further on this case. You wanna tell me why that is?" Hepton's eyes bored into the Canadian, but Easton just snorted faintly and turned on his heel, walking towards the window. "I'm not a fucking psychic," Easton said. "I can't tell where he's gonna be by magic." "So tell me," Stan growled, twisting around in his seat. "Why I shouldn't just deport your ass and yank you out of this PRIME contract?" "Because I'll find him," Easton said, turning around so he could look Stan Hepton in the eye. "I didn't say I wouldn't find him." "So tell me how." Easton sighed. "Don't," Easton warned, walking away from the window. Easton knew in his gut he would find his mentor. He had to find him; this wasn't a responsibility he could run away from even if he wanted to. The problem was that Stan Hepton wouldn't believe his explanation in the slightest. The fact that Hepton seemed to be satisfied with results more than method made it unnecessary to go into it. "Listen, you've got me up already, so I'm gonna pack." "This world tour thing…" Stan said, rubbing his chin. "I don't like it. Hear me?" Easton stopped before heading into his bedroom. "Yes, I've got it." "You keep in touch with the PRIME reps over there, 'cause if I hear you've dropped off their radar for a second, there'll be hell to pay." "Whatever, Hepton." Easton made to enter his bedroom, but stopped to say something else: "I don't want to see you out here when I come back." "Shut your suckhole, Hall," Stan snapped. "You remember who owns you." Easton rolled his eyes and stepped into his room. He knew exactly what Hepton was afraid of. The detective thought Easton might just sink out of sight on this tour, escape the law, and leave the bald detective empty-handed. What he didn't understand was how impossible that would be for Easton. At least Stan had taken the liberty of leaving by the time Easton had filled his dufflebag. CUE: "Peddlers of Doom" by Mood ---- Easton lunged low, holding the position with closed eyes. He wasn't used to South Africa and he didn't like being a stranger, so he had been staying in his room, training his body, trying to keep himself limber, at his peak. With his eyes closed, he remembered dreams and conversations two weeks gone, but he couldn't help thinking about what was to come. Since he had come into PRIME, he had been saddled with a responsibility of coming to the ring week in and week out without slipping, showing his ability. Yet, he couldn't get away from controversy. He was like a magnet for people who couldn't reach his level, who wanted to slight him out of the ring, away from honor and rules. When Easton brought these people into his realm, into the ring, he had the upper hand. He knew he did. This blindsiding business was a little more than annoying. Still, that couldn't steer him from what he had to do. After one match, he was in the undermain against someone who had reached the highest levels of the company, and all he had to do was crank her in Life Functions Terminated. All he had to do was come out on top, and then? Then he would have come into his own in a way he couldn't do in GTT6. He exhaled slowly, changing his direction, focusing his body now over his other leg. It was easy to say, but he had spent two years around the body of talent that made up High Impact Wrestling before reaching the top, and even then, he had only been there a month. All he had to do was steer himself, grab hold of his rudder and master his craft, but he always seemed to falter. Now, though, he seemed to be gaining some sort of control over the waters, especially after that victory over Bastille. His control was always fleeting, though, as if he was trying to hold the wheel with flippers rather than hands with thumbs. The obnoxious ringing of a phone broke his concentration. He glared back at the white plastic thing; it was the hotel phone. He considered not answering it for a second, but as soon as that second had passed, he straightened and made his way over to it. He let his hand hover over it, waiting for it to ring again before lifting it off of the holster. "Hello?" "Mr. Hall?" The voice lacked the thick Afrikaans accent of the hotel staff. "Yeah?" "Hey. Ah, we've received a message for you from our stateside office. From a… Kelly Hill?" Easton's eyebrow lifted. Kelly? "Do you know her?" "Yes." "Shall I play the message, then?" "Yeah, go ahead." "Just a second." Easton waited, his mind twisting the situation over and over, trying to figure it out. Why would the man have allowed Kelly to make a call, especially to the person who was currently trying to hunt the both of them down? Was this just to taunt him? "Easton," came Kelly's frightened voice suddenly. Easton pressed the phone harder against his ear. "Easton, I think I'm in Los Angeles, but I don't know. This is a pay phone – PRIME is the only place I could think to call. Easton, I can't stay anywhere more than a night or he'll find me. I need you, Easton. Please. Find me." The message ended and Easton was left staring straight ahead, bewildered. "Mr. Hall?" "Y-yeah." "Alright, Mr. Hall. Goodbye." Easton let out a heavy sigh and hung up the phone. Like holding onto a steering wheel with greasy flippers in the A Perfect Storm. Easton knew he had to call Stan Hepton; he couldn't wait until this tour was over. What he didn't know was if he would be able to regain whatever control he thought he'd had in time for ReVolution.
