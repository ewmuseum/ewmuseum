Recreational

10 June 2008

Date: 6/10/08 “Many of us spend our whole lives running from feeling with the mistaken belief that you cannot bear the pain. But you have already borne the pain. What you have not done is feel all you are beyond the pain.” -Saint Bartholomew June 7th, 2008 12:17 P.M. My head was killing me. A diet of cigarettes, water, and Tylox will do that to you. Lucky me, those were the only categories of the food pyramid that I’d filled up since UltraViolence. Even the clinching, fateful moonsault off the top, was fading out of my memory. In contrast with the stark, unflinching clarity of my loss at Culture Shock, the match with Gamble barely seemed real. I was thinking about this, and other things, as I lowered myself into the ice bath. The first instinct is always to recoil in horrified cold shock, but this wasn’t exactly my first trip through those saloon doors. I’d been rehabbing with ice baths since forever. The ice screamed against my skin, but it was better to feel that extreme than the alternative: the piercing, cautionary pinch against my spine. It was a gift, an early warning sign against everything I was doing. And since I was throwing that away, for another shot at the Universal Title and its foreverness, I had refined my other coping mechanisms. “Jeri! Could you grab my BlackBerry off the dresser?” Her footsteps traced faint sounds, as she rounded the corner into the bathroom. Jeri gave me a reproachful look. “Uh…could you please grab my Blackberry?” The transition from cleaning lady to cleaning lady and live-in nurse had brought out the etiquette Nazi in her. I let out a low groan as numbness set in. Maybe I was running away from pain, but everyone does it. That’s why it’s fucking pain. At least the ice baths had lessened the need for round-the-clock Tylox dosage. But what would happen when I returned to PRIME action? Jeri reemerged, handing me my phone with a very unladylike grunt. In hiring Jeri to clean my apartment, I had broken with tradition: she was neither good-looking nor pleasant. “Jeri,” I called from the bathtub, “do you know why I like you?” She breathed loudly in the other room, ignoring me. “It’s not just because of your beauty. Or your brains. Or the aggressive voice mails you leave when I forget to leave a check.” There was something calming about how candid I could be with this disgruntle woman. “It’s how damn good you are at your job, ya know?” “I get all the appreciation I need from your checkbook, Chandler.” I had promised her a generous bonus for helping me out while I recovered from the PPV. She reminded me. Like every fucking second. “I used to always hire these bimbos to clean,” I confessed. “And to be honest, I wasn’t paying them for their cleaning skills, ya know? Left me with a messy apartment and the constant fear that I had crabs.” I picked up the BlackBerry and scrolled through my contacts. “But I’m sure you’d know all about the fast life, right Jer? You’ve probably seduced a client here and there, huh?” “Stop trying to flirt with me,” came the voice from the bedroom. I imagined Jeri twenty years younger, but it still wasn’t a very pretty picture. Her pudgy fingers and hog-like jowls were part of the character I knew. “It’s weird.” “Jeri, darling, I’m not flirting. This is playful employee-employer banter.” Neither of us spoke for a second, as I found the name I was looking for: Gil Lavenstein, my lawyer. “I think they have a name for that. It’s called sexual harassment.” I left Jeri to her cleaning. I didn’t need anything for the moment; I was happy as a pig in shit since I couldn’t feel my back. Clicking down on the green call button, I only had to wait for one ring before Gil picked up on the other end. “Gil Lavenstein.” Back in the day, Gil had been the only lawyer I knew. So when I had gotten into wrestling, I asked him to watch my back, which he agreed to do on the basis of being a good Samaritan. And the basis of me paying him assloads. Now, he was a big enough deal that he answered the phone just by saying his name. “Gilbert,” I whispered, “I’m not wearing anything.” “Good afternoon, Chandler.” He said this in about the same, unenthused voice one would use to explain the physical symptoms of I.B.S. “Oh come on, Gil. There’s not a chance your other clients call you from the naked comfort of their ice baths.” I liked to think that I had helped Gil rise up a bit in the world, from the top one percent to the top half percent. Either way, the guy had far more on his plate now than ever before. It showed in his voice, once robust and sprawling. Now, he forced his words out in a half-growl. “That they don’t. Then again, most of my clients are suspended from their place of employment.” I clicked my tongue against my throat. “Pardon?” “The whole suspension thing with PRIME.” I blinked several times and yelped when my right arm dropped into the icy water. Gil was out of touch with my life, and clearly with PRIME. What could he know that I didn’t? “I’m not sure who told you that, but they’re full of shit.” “Nope, yeah, here it is. It’s right on PRIMEates.com.” I could have followed along on the BlackBerry, but I was waiting for Gil to realize an error, to come to his senses. “A source, who spoke on the condition of anonymity,” he read, “said that Chandler Tsonda has been suspended indefinitely for conduct detrimental to the company.” It was like waking up from a night of blackout drinking: the bedroom smells like perfume and there aren’t any condoms in the trash, but what the fuck happened? “Surely, PRIME wouldn’t dignify those rumors with-” “Looks like they already did,” he shot back. “It’s plastered all over the front page. Heh, they even did the ‘Ts’ thing with the word ‘suspended.’” Gil chuckled, then recognized my silence as the calm before the maelstrom of anger. “Chandler, I’m sure it’s nothing.” “I’ll call you back.” I didn’t wait to hear his response. I wanted to check in, see how some investments were panning out. Now, I was staring a suspension in the face. Surely, PRIMEates had gotten it wrong; it was the same source that had “reported” Angelo Deville’s return for this year’s Dual Halo, among other half-assed “anonymous tip” stories. Still, I saw the difference between unsubstantiated rumor and something that PRIME puts on its official “this company is sold on Wall Street” website. It didn’t help that I was numb and helplessly immobile. I forged a fist as hard as diamonds, swallowing loudly. It was the most fight I could put up; I couldn’t even stand up without the help of a middle-aged cleaning lady. “Chandler, I’m about to take off.” Jeri poked her head back into the tile bathroom, finding me with my eyes closed, rubbing my temples. “I gotta pick up my kid from baseball.” “Wait, wait, wait.” I needed to slow things down. “Do me a favor before you go? There’s an orange pill bottle next to my bed. Will you bring it to me?” Jeri didn’t think twice about grabbing the Tylox bottle and tossing it my way. “Get out of that thing once in a while. I don’t wanna show up here tomorrow to find you frozen and pruney.” I flicked at the bottle’s top, forgetting to say goodbye as Jeri slipped out of the apartment. I wasn’t alone; the trembling doubts had slipped right into the tub with me. If I hadn’t popped a couple Tylox, those doubts might have rotted me from the inside out. It wasn’t ideal, but it was beautiful. Time oozed by, without pain or complex thought. I was stupefied.
