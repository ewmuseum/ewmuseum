Lights Out

25 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/25/2007 CRASH! I stand there and look at the table, flipped over, and all of the items once on top it now strewn all over the place. My breath is shallow as my red eyes just stare at the scene before me and I find out that I don’t care. Slowly, I take a step back, and turn around only to see a glass cabinet standing before me. Within seconds, my fist slams through the glass not even feeling the pain that’s normally associated with it. The noise is short, but loud enough to make people wonder what’s going on. My mind is in a completely different world as I barely hear the door opening to the room. Yet, as the door clicks from the closing of it, I know someone is in the room, and I already know who it is. I know that she’s standing there, looking at the destruction I’ve caused and she’s asking herself what’s wrong with me. My hand shakes from the adrenaline as I pull my hand away from the shards of glass from the cabinet. Several pieces fall out of the case and onto the carpeted floor as my hand is removed from the now shattered cabinet. My eyes lock upon my knuckles as it’s bleeding steadily. Not surprisingly, I don’t feel any pain though. “What’s wrong with you?” Katie asks me, her tone rough and angry, but I ignore her question. Instead, I turn towards the bathroom and walk in, immediately grabbing a towel off the rack. My opposite hand turns on the water faucet and I quickly put my hand under the warm water. In the back of my mind, I know that my hand is stinging from the open wound, but I never feel it. To be honest, it’s an odd feeling to be aware of something that’s not happening when it should be. As I watch the water wipe away the blood I can feel her eyes upon my back. Somehow, I continue to ignore them as I make sure I don’t catch their gaze. One look from her and everything would be gone. I don’t need logic or common sense. I need pain, emotions, and raw aggression. The blood continues to seep out from the broken skin and I tell myself that it’s a loss cause. With the towel, I wrap my hand inside of it and apply pressure so that it can stop bleeding. Then, I turn off the faucet and look at myself in the mirror. In the reflection, I can see her standing there with a disdainful look on her face. “You can’t ignore me forever,” she tells me and I turn towards the exit, and brush by her. I know that I can be cold and this is one of those times. Yet, even in those situations she never gives up. It’s what makes me know that eventually I’ll come out of my funk. She has a unique way of fixing things up. Right now though, I don’t need her to fix anything. Instead, I need for her to just let me feel the way that I’m feeling because the way I was feeling before hasn’t been working for months now. Tonight capped off another loss for me; my fifth loss in a row for PRIME and six out of my last seven. To be honest, I hadn’t won a match in quite some time, my last victory had come back in May against Devin Shakur and that’s when I won the Intense Title. Since joining PRIME I had only won five matches, and two of those had been tag team matches. I didn’t belong there any longer and I knew it. For weeks I had been trying to ignore that truth, but in the reality of the world, I didn’t deserve to be there. In a few weeks time I would be competing for the Universal Championship, and I didn’t merit that title shot. I’m not the one to take a title shot when I haven’t earned it and this is one of those times. I know what I have to do. I know what needs to be done. “Sure I can,” I tell her coldly and she looks at me perplexed. She walks over to me and smacks me upside the head. “Stop being dumb,” she replies and I know she’s right. “I’m not being dumb. I lost again tonight, Katie,” I tell her calmly, the anger though still residing in my chest. “So? You’re not going to be able to win every single time,” she tells me and I shake my head at her. She doesn’t understand it, doesn’t understand why it kills me so much. I can’t continue to lose and not at the rate I’m going. How can I continue to look at myself in the mirror if I continue to lose? How can I consider myself a wrestler if I can’t defeat those that I enter the ring with? The truth of that matter is that I’ve lost it. I came out red hot in GLOBAL and now, I’d lost it. The level of competition between the two is completely different and I’m just now realizing it. “Yeah, but apparently I’m going to lose every single time. In eight months, I’ve won five times. Five times,” I tell her, emphasizing the final two words. I get up, not able to take this anymore. She grabs my arm though, hoping to reason with me. “They see something in you obviously or else they wouldn’t continue to give you these chances to compete. You’re going to get a shot at the top championship in PRIME and that has to mean something. They’re not just going to hand that to you, trust me. You’re in a rut, and soon you will find your place. Trust me, I know you will,” she tells me and I immediately rip my arm from her grasp. She just doesn’t understand and the words coming from her mouth isn’t helping the situation at all. Instead, it’s just making me more and more angry by the second. She looks at me, sadness in her eyes, as she hates to see me like this. This time though, there’s no fixing this. I look back at her, fire in my eyes, and yet she doesn’t show any fear. It’s one of the things that I like about her. “You’re not listening,” I begin to tell her. She opens her mouth to reply, but I shake my head. “No, you’re not going to be able to fix things this time. I can’t do this anymore. I thought I could and that’s why I came back. When I got fired from GLOBAL I thought I could just pick it up and continue to move forward, but I can’t. I just can’t. They book me in this match and I’m not ready for it. I haven’t earned it. You tell me why I deserve a shot at Lindsay Troy for the Universal Title. Can you tell me what I’ve done to deserve an honor as that?” I asks her and I know she doesn’t have an answer for that one. I pause for a brief second before finishing what I have to say. “Exactly. Nothing.” “Then what’re you going to do, Craig?” she asks me and I look at her. From the minute the referee’s hand slammed down onto the mat for the three count three hours ago I knew what I had to do. I couldn’t be part of this match, not when I didn’t have any faith in myself to get the job done. I’ve never walked into a match before with the thought that I couldn’t win it. However, for the first time I knew I couldn’t win this match, and it scares me. Competing against the likes of Troy, Silver, Ferguson, and Silver didn’t induce confidence in me. Instead, I already knew the outcome. I reach into my back pocket and grab several hundreds before handing them to Katie. She looks at the bills with a foreign look on her face. “Give that to the hotel staff for the damage. I’ve got to go. I’ve got to get out of here,” I tell her and for the first time I see defeat in her face. My feet drag me to the door and I grab the handle. My insides feel heavier then ever before. “And then what?” she asks me, and I can’t bear to look at her. Defeat stares me in the face and behind me is Katie. I’m in a no win situation. Instead, I look at the ground and close my eyes. “Then, I go to Blaine Blair, Lisa Tyler, and the PRIME executives, and tell them I resign,” I tell her, and I walk out, ending my career forever. End
