(Rev113) One in four

29 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 29 November 2006 The trip back from Mexico City had been the epitome of uneventful. As Ember sat back in his air-conditioned Cherokee jeep, one hand lazily holding onto the steering wheel while the other rested, comfortably, on the cushioned armrest that was angled out from the moulded seat that did its best to envelop him forever in its embrace, he let out a long sigh. Ever since leaving the old mask maker back in the City, two and a half thousand dollars … U.S. of course … lighter but one new mask, one cherished mask heavier, Ember had been plagued by a feeling of disquiet. It wasn’t something that he could actually pinpoint or put his finger one but, nonetheless, it bothered him. As the miles between him and the City had increased, their passage marked by nothing more than a blur of scenery out of the side window, an ever increasing milometre gauge and an ever decreasing gas level, so to did his unease. The passage of time wasn’t easy to track when nothing seemed to change. After thinking about it for a few seconds Ember realised that he hadn’t seen another vehicle in at least two hours and the last glimpse of another living creature had been at least twice as long. He knew himself well enough to know that something was bothering him; the only problem was that he couldn’t get his mind to focus in on what it was. It couldn’t be the tournament, he told himself silently. Primetime’s GTT was something special, even to him – though that admission was never made out loud – and his upcoming match with Steve Watson was definitely hanging over him. At the end of the day, though, he wasn’t too worried about it. Watson may have been good enough – or lucky enough Ember joked to himself with a smile – to get as far as the bracket finals but it was pretty obvious that his luck would be running out. There is no chance that a part-time indy wrestler Ember told himself, whether because he believed it or to make sure that he would believe it, is going to take my moment of glory away from me! Out of all the opponents that Ember had faced in the tournament Watson annoyed him the most; annoyed, not worried. One reason was that simple fact that he tried to walk the line between so-called ‘real’ life and wrestling. An accountant by day and a wrestler by night. Fly by night Ember mocked to himself. His whole life was centred around one ting and one thing only. Wrestling. To him there was nothing else that mattered. Of course, like anyone else in the industry, he had a life outside of wrestling but the one thing that Ember never forgot was that it was outside of wrestling; first and foremost came the job, the career … the life. Everything that he did was so that he could be the best at what he did. There was, of course, another reason that Ember felt a strong distaste for Watson; he, too, wore a mask. Ember’s knuckles whitened even more than usual as his grip tightened on the wheel in response to his train of thought. Watson wore a mask to hide himself, to lead a double life. For Ember the mask wasn’t a disguise, it wasn’t a way to hide himself; he was the mask. The mask was him. Taking a deep breath Ember calmed himself. He knew that, in just a few short days, he would get his shot at Watson. At that moment, in that battle, he would be able to kill two birds with one stone, so to speak. He would, of course, make his way to claiming the bracket championship – taking him one step closer … just two more to go … to his ultimate goal. Winning the tournament itself and proving, finally, that he really was better than his brother. His eyes tightened beneath the ever-present material of the mask that covered the majority of his face. Just the mere thought of his brother was still enough to evoke a reaction in Ember that he had little or no control over. That would soon end though, he knew, when he managed to eclipse his achievements and take his rightful place – one step higher than he had ever managed! The thought that his familial past could be bothering was discounted as fast as it came. While it was true that he was always thinking about it, he knew exactly just what that ‘itch’ felt like. This wasn’t it; this was different. His mind wandered to the next logical step; his first ‘official’ match with PRIME. He knew that there was a nervous edge to the prospect of facing three men he knew nothing about but, also, this wasn’t exactly something new to him. During his tenure in the WWA he had faced ‘new’ men on an almost nightly basis in his first year alone; going through them all, eventually, to take his rightful place as their ‘World’ champion. He laughed to himself at that thought. At the time he had thought that he was on top of the World. Still relatively new in the industry to be told that he was a ‘World’ champion was exciting – it wasn’t until he spread his wings, so to speak, and ventured out into the wider World of the PTC that he realised the truth of the matter. While he may have been, admittedly, the best that the WWA had to offer that didn’t mean that he was definitely the best that the World had to offer. Probably Ember thought with a smirk, and more than likely the best, but not guaranteed. Facing off, during inter-promotional events, against wrestlers from all over the World Ember had had his eyes opened to the possibilities – and opportunities – around him. He knew that he was at a crossroads in his life; to stay with the WWA where he had already run out of challenges but was a big fish in a small and fetid pool … or do the unexpected. Give it all up and start to swim up stream. The decision was easy; if he was going to fail – and that wasn’t exactly likely – then he would rather do it by pushing himself to the limits. Better to burn out in a blaze of glory after all, he knew, than to fade away. …and so he had taken the offered spot in the Jewel in the Crown tournament against his ‘dear old friend’ Jonathon Winters and made his ‘splash’ in the pool known as PRIME. Many people would assume that Ember was disappointed to not have beaten Winters in that match just as many people would assume that he had wasted a golden opportunity to get a shot at one of the major titles … if not the major title … in the industry. Then again they were probably the same people who assumed that they had been born because their parents loved them and not simply because they were too stupid to know what a condom was for. The game, as Ember knew, was a long one. While it was true that he could have beaten Winters and possibly even – though even the walking/talking ego-trip that was Ember knew it wasn’t a guarantee at all – go on to win the whole tournament he did exactly what he set out to do. He sent a message. Winters may have been the recipient of it – with burned in scars that would no doubt fade but never quite go away complete … at least not where it mattered most, on the inside – but it was something that Ember knew would have gone out to everyone in PRIME. They say that there is no smoke without fire, well Ember made very sure that everyone realised that the fire had been lit. He was here. He was in PRIME. The fact that he was scheduled to face three other men in one night – Malachi, Diego and the enigmatic ‘N’ – was proof that someone in PRIME had ‘got’ the message; the fact that he was in a match to decide the number one contender to the Intense Title was proof that someone in PRIME had understood the message as well. The fact that in Ember’s first official match he was being given the … His eyes reacted slightly after his mind and body did. The wheel was being sharply turned, the jeep going into a wide spin, before he registered exactly what was happening. By then, of course, it was a little too late. The unidentifiable animal that he had run over was dead before he saw it. Pulling the wheel back to the other side, pumping the brake at the same time, Ember managed to control the spin and pull the car to a stop by the side of the road. His breath exploded out of him and he did a small double take as he realised that he must have been holding it for the last few seconds. Undoing his seatbelt he glanced over his shoulder, intending to see if he could tell what he had hit and then paused as he saw his long jacket … discarded on the back seat as it had been too hot and humid to wear it … laying half on the floor with the contents of its inside pocket peeking out. As Ember realised just what it was, that it was the PRIME contract that he was meant to have signed and handed in to Chet Worth nearly a week previously, he finally figured out just what had been bugging him; just what he had forgotten to do. He had been so caught up with the idiot of a security guard; he had been surprised by the appearance of a certain wrestler in the tournament; he had been preoccupied with Winters himself … and he hadn’t done the one thing that had to do to ensure that his first match with PRIME – on a one night contract – wasn’t also his last. Malachi, the ‘self-professed’ God. Diego, the ‘self-professed’ King of Kings. ‘N’, the ‘self-professed’ terrorist. All three of them, when Ember had fisrt heard he was facing them, pegged as idiots who would be easy to bypass on his route to the Intense Title. All three of them, as far as Ember was concerned, nothing to worry about at all. All of them were expecting to go into the fight and face three other people; all of them were wanting the exact same thing, undoubtedly, as Ember himself … to win the match and got a shot at the she-devil who held the Intense Title, ... and now, all of them, were possibly going to have a much easier time of it – a much better chance. One in three was definitely easier than one in four, Ember knew as he stared, open-mouthed, at the contract that should have been with the legal team at PRIME HQ a week ago … especially when that missing ‘one’ in the equation was him. How could he have been so stupid? He had fixated so much on the GTT, on Winters, on everything other than the most simple of tasks that he had screwed up. Completely. How was he going to take part in a match with a federation that he didn’t even – officially – work for? How on Earth was he going to make his mark within PRIME when he couldn’t even expect to just walk into the ring and beat the other guys. Surely someone as professional as Worth, head of a multi-million dollar business like PRIME, wouldn’t overlook something like this.
