Flashbacks

12 May 2006

Date: 5/12/06 What does a four hundred pound wrestler do to train? Whatever he wants to, as the old joke might go. But in reality, Titan never has been much of a gym rat. His time in the service saw him far from a gym most of the time, and the idea of barbells, machines and so on never really appealed to him. Instead, he trains using his own body. Push-ups, sit-ups and so on are more of his repertoire. And so, it was on about his two hundred and thirty-odd push-up that his cell phone rang. He held his position, looking at the small device. He could just barely see the outside screen... he immediately dropped his knee to the floor and reached for the small Samsung. “Hello, Mom,” he said as he flipped the device open. “How are you and Dad doing?” The big man sat down on the edge of the hotel bed, which sagged under his weight. He pressed the phone to his ear and smiled as his mother's voice came through. “We're doing quite well, son,” she purposefully didn't mention his name. For some reason, Dad had been oddly fixated on the biblical hunter when he was born, so when he was growing up, that's what the called him: Hunter. “I understand that you're doing well yourself.” “Yeah, mom, I'm doing okay. Things are going pretty well.” “From what I saw on the television, you're doing very well, Hunter.” Her voice was warm and he could almost see the smile on her face. They'd lived a long time in Israel, and after he left the IDF, they moved as well. The whole family had dual citizenship, and so when he moved, so did they. But he ended up for a time in Quantico, Virginia and they ended up in Savannah, Georgia. “I saw that you went all the way to the finals.” “Yeah, Mom, I am.” He paused a moment to wipe the sweat from his face. “I really can't believe management's letting me go through this by myself.” “I have to admit, it does seem unfair to me,” his mother responded. “You're going in there alone against at least two other men at a time.” Titan marveled to himself how much the warm, humid Savannah air had seemed to vitalize her and his father. “Well, I'm doing pretty well, so maybe it is fair,” he said with a chuckle. “Well, that's fine, then,” she responded quickly. “I just want to tell you, your father and I are happy that you're doing this. You're having fun and get to go all over the country. How are you holding up, though? Traveling that much has got to be stressful.” “Not really,” he responded. “I learned how to get some sleep pretty easily.” “You have a big day tomorrow, don't you?” she asked brightly. “Yeah, I do, mom,” he said. “Listen, I'll call you all when the whole thing's over, okay?” “Love you, Hunter, goodnight.” And like that, the line was dead. Titan leaned back and sighed. His parents were great, but they were wrong. Traveling all over the world might sound glamorous, but for a big man like him, it was torture. This hotel room had him sleeping with his knees bent at the end of the bed, he had to get first-class seats on an airplane, and rental cars? He shuddered at the thought of some PRIME road agents reserving a Corolla for him. Already, he had been getting fan mail, which prompted him to reach into his duffel that sat on the floor for the first bundle he'd gotten. It was just the same over and over again. “I wish I was as big as you.” “I wish I was as strong and tough as you are.” Just that, coming from little boys all across the country. Not one of them realized how exactly hard it was to be as big and strong as he was. Every piece of furniture in his apartment was specially-made to hold his weight and almost all his clothing had to be tailor made. Now and again, he found a letter from some self-professed wrestling historian that berated him for a lack of something called 'workrate' and how he 'hadn't payed his dues.' Those letters he threw out immediately. His dues had been paid a long, long time ago. That's when it hit him. His heart began to race, and in his mind, he didn't see his hotel room anymore, but instead, a bombed street. All around him were the wounded and screaming. He'd been detached to respond to an emergency call, so Profile 21 and he had set out and ran the three blocks from where he'd been doing psychological training to the scene. Upon arrival, Titan stopped to survey the devastation. It was a low-power bomb, but it had been packed tight with tiny bits of shrapnel, so almost everyone on the street was hurt in some way. As he took a step to the first person near him, he noticed 21 step sideways and seemingly disappear. Titan took a moment to look around and saw the man nowhere. As he knelt, he almost vomited right then and there. A man had gotten hit in the leg with a piece of shrapnel about as long as an average person's finger. He was sitting on the ground, tears running down his face, but it wasn't the pain that had him crying. At least, not mostly. Next to him, on the ground, was a five year old boy, with what looked like the same kind of shrapnel firmly embedded in his forehead. Titan looked at him and his heart sank. There was nothing he could do. A little child killed in a senseless act of violence. And for all his strength, there was nothing he could do. A shout brought his head up and he looked to see Profile 21 with a man on his hands and knees, standing over his back with a knife to his throat. The victim was dressed in a polo shirt and khaki pants and next to him, on the ground, lay a man dressed exactly the same way but very much less alive. 21's knife glistened in the Israeli sun with already-shed blood. The man on the ground showed no fear on his face, and only muttered in Arabic. Titan recognized a few words from what 21 had insisted he study: “I will go to Allah.” Titan took long, reaching strides across the blast zone and shoved 21 off the man. Profile 21 didn't stagger, but instead took some long, smooth steps back before stooping to wipe his blade on the other man's shirt. He sheathed it and looked up. As did the victim, who looked at Titan with an open sneer. “He says you robbed him of paradise,” 21 said coldly. “You should have let me finish him. He's part of the splinter group that's been doing these shrapnel bombings.” “Then you can't...” Titan started. The man grabbed his leg and shouted at him. With a dangerous gleam in his eyes, Titan swung his fist, his knuckles making contact with the man's temple and dropping him unconscious to the ground. “Then you can't track who did this and this man can't give more information.” 21 smiled at him. “This is why I picked you. You have principles, but you're also practical.” The sound of sirens rounding the corner echoed in the big man's head as he shook out of his reverie. He blinked, looking around at his hotel room. He was on the floor, on all fours, his breath coming in ragged gasps and his body sweating wildly. He sat down and pulled an unsteady hand across his forehead. He had to keep it together. He had a match to win. He had to keep it together.
