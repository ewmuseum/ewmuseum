What the New Life Means

22 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/22/08 As you sit in the back corner of a bar your friends picked out, you start to down your beer and really think about the events that have happened in the past few weeks. Perhaps one of your greatest strengths and also one of your biggest weaknesses is your ability to react and move without thinking. It is how you’ve pulled so many victories out of seemingly nowhere, and it’s why even with the incident you haven’t completely lost your step in the ring. However, in times like this it really becomes a weakness: you can’t really remember thinking anything during the past few weeks. All you’ve been doing is acting, and so when you think of your actions, especially the ones that took place on Revolution last week, you aren’t exactly happy with what you remember. “WHERE ARE YOU KANNON!” “THEY WON’T BE THERE AT COLOSSUS!” “I’m taking your name. I’m taking your aura. I’m taking your myth. I’m taking your legend. I’m taking everything you ever were.” Your voice doesn’t sound like you remember, and those words aren’t making you too proud either. When you were asked by FUSE management if you had any sort of nickname 15 months ago when you joined (was it only 15 months? It seems to you like it’s been years), you thought about it for only a moment before you answered, “The New Life.” The talent scout looked at you for a few seconds before asking, “What the fuck does that mean?” And you never explained it, but over the course of your career you let your actions speak for you. You showed that the New Life of wrestling was fighting for what was right, ignoring the glamour of the wealthy life, working hard and standing in the face of tyranny. This, you planned well beforehand, was what New Life was all about. Slamming Xavier Kannon into a car several times and beating him within an inch of his life in an unprovoked (relatively) backstage attack was certainly not the New Life. The more you think (and the more you drink), the more you realize that what you did last week was not only not your usual way, but also might be turning you into something you would never want to be. And when your friends arrive, they don’t make it any easier. ---- “I don’t like it,” says Shweta Kallemullah, relative newcomer to the party by way of being the girlfriend of Foster Nackedy, the man who trains Jonathan Rhine. She is sitting next to Foster with her arm wrapped lazily around his shoulders, sighing. “I mean, you’re a beacon of morals and suddenly after a few bad breaks you nearly kill a man? That’s not showing a strong sense of character.” Foster looks at Shweta oddly. “Baby?” “Yeah?” “He lost a fucking eye. Of course he wants to get back at the man who hurt him. He played dirty, so why not play along?” “Because,” said Katie Malick, Rhine’s girlfriend and owner of the heel that was used to strike her boyfriend, “there is a line between right and wrong that for some reason is easier to see in a wrestling ring. When you perform the tactics that the wrong ones do, even if it is well deserved, it doesn’t make you any better.” Jonathan Rhine grimaces and finishes his beer, then sets it aside with a growing community of empty bottles. “I’m not trying to do anything wrong. I was just frustrated. I’m fine now, guys. I don’t need an intervention.” “Oh yes you do!” Shweta said, nearly screamed; she took the table by surprise as they all stared at the newcomer, forcing her to blush. “Sorry, Jon, but it just makes me nuts. You seem like the sweetest guy in the world and I don’t want you being turned evil by the business, like some other people.” Shweta looks to her left at Foster, who suddenly feels the rest of the group follow her gaze. “Oh, so I’m evil now?” Katie tilts her head to the side inquisitively. “When did you do something like this, Foster?” Foster sighs. “Jon never told you? Fine. About five or six years ago a guy named Anton Dufresne choked my baby nearly to death. He was six months old at the time.” Jonathan merely stares at Katie covers her mouth. “Oh my God, that’s so horrible.” “Not as horrible as what happened next. I was so hellbent on revenge and making sure that no one would hurt my boy again, I paralyzed Anton. Slammed him in the spine with a lead pipe. Repeatedly. I made sure that motherfucker would never walk again. And he hasn’t, I heard that he died recently, still in his wheelchair.” Shweta slaps Foster. “And you’re encouraging Jon to keep going further into this spiral? You want him to become like you?” Foster doesn’t answer, merely turns to Jonathan. “Listen, Jon. I know it sounds bad, and honestly there may be a little piece of me that is upset about what I did to Anton. But to tell the truth, I’d probably do it again if I had a second chance. Or a third. It was the right thing to do to make peace with my demons, and the fucker deserved it. Just like this one does. And you’re not done yet.” Katie shakes her head. “What exactly are you telling Jonathan to do?” “I’m telling him to take XK out. To make sure that at Colossus, that not only will you be the last man standing, but that Kannon will never stand again. Do whatever you can to show him that just because you get cheered and because you are on the face of little kids’ T-Shirts doesn’t mean you can’t get revenge.” There is a long silence as the two women at the table stare at Jonathan Rhine’s trainer, hoping that they are hearing things, or maybe that he’s just drunk. But there are no bottles in front of him. Suddenly, Shweta starts to tear up, shaking her head. “I can’t believe you’d say that. You actually advocate revenge, violence, even death? It’s disgusting. I can’t…I just have to…” And without finishing her sentence, the Indian woman leaves the table and ambles out of the bar, nearly bumping into a person playing pool near the door. Katie watches the door of the bar, then turns to Jonathan. “Jon, please…just finish it the way you always do. Be The New Life. Don’t be a monster.” Jonathan nods absently, looking to Foster, who shrugs. “Do whatever you want, but a simple victory over Xavier Kannon won’t get your eye back.” “Neither will a murder,” growls Katie. “Yeah, but it’ll sure make him feel better.” Jonathan sighs and calls for another beer. It’s time to stop thinking. ---- About thirty minutes later as you left the bar, you got a frantic call from Shweta saying that she and Foster broke up. It was quick and decidedly mutual, and it made the night even worse for you. You barely know Shweta, but you liked what you saw: she was polite, smart, nice, and seemed perfect for Foster. But after tonight, it seemed that she couldn’t deal with him anymore, because she couldn’t think of him as anything other than a good person. Those bad years for him, and the fact that he isn’t apologetic about them, drove her away. And you’re convinced that the same thing would happen with you and Katie were you to follow Foster’s advice. You did what you did to Kannon, and you can’t take it back. It was well deserved, and it made you feel good for a moment, but you can’t keep going that way. There is a proper way to do it, and that is to take it to the finish, give it all you have, but don’t press the advantage. Don’t hurt him more than enough to win the match. Don’t make good on your threat. Because you want to make sure Katie doesn’t find you as disgusting as Shweta finds Foster. But most importantly, because that is the way you operate. That is the right way to do things. That is the New Life.
