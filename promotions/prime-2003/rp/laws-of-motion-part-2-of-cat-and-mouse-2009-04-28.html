Laws of Motion (Part 2 of \"Cat and Mouse\")

28 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/28 8,000 feet... As the Boeing 757 made its climb across the inky Southern sky, Troy Douglas coaxed himself through his standard in-flight ritual. Ever since he was a kid, the subtle pressure differences accustomed with air travel had given him headaches, but two Advil a few minutes after takeoff had always seemed to do the trick. His head already throbbing as the jet continued to ascend, Troy wasn\'t quite so sure that the headache was going to go away this time. He\'d felt the relentless pounding against his forehead since leaving the hospital in Denver, and no amount of aspirin or ibuprofen had put a stop to it. Even sleep was little relief. The few fruitless hours of rest he was able to get, at least. And with his pain beating out a disturbing samba in his head, Troy figured that sleep wasn\'t going to come in the flight to the Midwest, either. He contented himself to stare at the rapidly shrinking nighttime landscape out the window, hoping that the medicine kicked in. Unfortunately, the guy in seat 22E had other ideas. While Troy battled the satanic pounding inside his head, this balding, middle-aged guy with bagel crumbs littered over his oxford cloth shirt had apparently main-lined extra strength Ambien and was knocked out in a coma so deep that they could be passengers on Oceanic 815 and this guy wouldn\'t know about the crash until he got sucked through one of the jet engines. Normally, this wouldn\'t be a problem, but their were two little thorns sticking in Troy\'s side. First, the snoring. Long, loud, drawn out snores ripping through the air like a clogged, aggravated vacuum cleaner. They\'d been taking a direct route from this guy\'s nose and mouth to Troy\'s ears for the better part of 20 minutes, providing the melody to carry the rhythm of his migraine. Then, there was the actual pain in his side. That being his next-door neighbor\'s right elbow digging deeper and deeper into Troy\'s ribs. He\'d tried on several occasions to extricate himself from the situation, but the attempts had gained nothing but the knowledge that his new friend\'s right arm was either a lead weight or some kind of bizarre guided missile. Either way, this wasn\'t shaping up to be the best flight of Troy Douglas\' life. Thankfully, God or the flight attendant intervened, and Bagel Shirt was awoken by the crash of the drink cart against his left elbow. Suddenly jerked out of sleep, Troy\'s formerly snoring companion gathered up the complimentary pretzels being passed his way. Pulled from his hypnotic staring out the tiny window, Troy turned to the aisle. \"Sir, can I get you anything to drink?\" \"Not now, thanks.\" The 20-something brunette passed a cellophane packet of pretzels his way nonetheless, and Troy grabbed them, never intending them to reach his mouth. Bagel Shirt had other ideas. \"Uh, you gonna eat those?\" Troy wasn\'t listening anymore. At long last, the Advil had kicked in and he was already well on his way to finding sleep. ***** 23,000 feet... Three rows behind seat 22F, crammed in an uncomfortable coach seat between a woman who could most generously be described as plus-sized and a man who made Larry the Cable Guy look like the peak of human sophistication, a ghost waited. He didn\'t move, he didn\'t speak, he just watched. Watched, and stewed in frustration. On any other day, the job would\'ve been done. The man in seat 22F would have gotten up to use the bathroom or grab something from his overhead compartment, and a quick pour of a certain chemical into his Coca Cola would finish the job. They\'d find the target in Kansas City without a breath left in his body, dead of an apparent brain aneurysm, and the man known only as Casper would be off to transfer yet another six figures into his numbered Swiss account. No such luck this time around. For some reason, the damn flight was overbooked, making movement around the 757 all but impossible. There was no trip to the bathroom, his carry-on bag was tucked neatly at his feet and he\'d foregone any type of refreshment before drifting off to sleep. Troy Douglas was becoming an infuriating subject. But, he was nothing if not a patient man. With the money waiting to be put into his personal coffers, he could most certainly wait until a better opportunity presented itself. One most certainly would. His target was a public figure, and as such, there were plenty of places available to find him and extricate him from his life. So he would wait, and watch. Of course, in seat 22F, a man was stirring, his headache having returned in the middle of what had been a peaceful sleep. As the fog began to clear from his brain and his eyes adjusted to the flat light of the cabin, Troy Douglas had the feeling that somebody\'s eyes were trained on him. He leaned forward, looking over his left shoulder. There was little to be suspicious of, and only one empty seat. Three rows behind him in the middle row, right between a larger woman and a man in a ratty, sleeveless flannel with a Confederate flag tattoo and a glorious mullet. Troy relaxed, trying to ease back into sleep as Flight 323 soared towards Kansas City, and his next PRIME show. Must be the headache, he thought. He didn\'t know if he was lying to himself, or not.
