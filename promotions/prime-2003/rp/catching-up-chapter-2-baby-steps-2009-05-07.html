Catching Up - Chapter 2 - Baby Steps

7 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 7, 2009 THURSDAY MAY 7, 2009 “Yeah bruh, I need to see a public defendant.” Bryan leaned against the marble countertop across from the receptionist in the brightly-lit office of the Hilo County Courthouse Public Defendant’s office. The place was as clean as a whistle, with pearl white walls, mint-condition marble tiling, and rows of chairs, lined up perfectly straight. The small reception area was the image of perfection. Dawkins, however, was clearly not. Donned in a tee and pair of shorts that appeared to be straight from the dumpster, Bryan was obviously not back to his old self. The hair was still frizzy and approaching small afro length, and his beard was almost to the state in which Hank Williams Jr. would have been proud of it. Also, gone were the signature orange and aqua specs normally seen on the Flyin’ Hawaiian, replaced with mundane black ones, probably purchased at the nearest dollar store. “And your name is?” The receptionist’s voice was painfully familiar to Bryan, but he couldn’t quite put his finger on it at that moment. She was dressed in a white blouse with light and dark pink flowers scattered all over it, and some tan dress slacks. Her gaze was strong and firm, as she repeated her question. “Sir, your name?” Snapping out of a temporary trance, Bryan quickly responded. “Uh yeah, Bryan Dawkins. Got a call a couple of days ago from some cranky bitch about not havin’ a lawyer for my custody hearing over my son. So I’m here to get a free one.” The receptionist glared toward Bryan. She knew him, and knew him quite well actually. “Oh, it’s you.” A questioning glance was thrown her way. “Me? What’cha mean, ‘it’s me’?” Politely, the receptionist clasped her hands together and smiled. “And which receptionist did you talk to the other day, Mr. Dawkins?” “Uh, think her name was Maria? Mary? Marissa? I dunno, somethin’ like that.” Cracking a sly smile, the receptionist continued. “Was it Marie?” Bryan’s eyes lit up as she said the name. His memory had been slightly jogged. “Yeah! That was it. Real cranky bitch. Dunno what her prob’ was. I didn’t do anything to her; I just let her know I wasn’t thinkin’ that I was gonna go to the trial, ‘cuz let’s face it, the odds of me comin’ out victorious in this is pretty slim, don’tcha think?” The receptionist simply sat and smiled throughout Bryan’s monologue. “I mean, all I’m tryin’ to do is let these people know that I can take care of my kid, and they go and file some sorta lawsuit against me. I don’t get it, really. Then, I end up getting a call from this bitch, who pretty much threatens to get my ass thrown in jail if I don’t get a lawyer or whatever. Well I’m not gonna get a lawyer. Know why?” More silence from the receptionist. “Cuz that’s money I don’t have, babe. Haven’t been to work in weeks, they stopped givin’ me paychecks, and honestly I dunno if they’d even care if I came back. So really, everything’s a lose-lose situation, dear. So I figured I’ll come get a free lawyer who probably gives as much of a shit about this whole situation as I do anymore. He knows we won’t win, I know we won’t win. So whatever, right?” Patiently waiting for Dawkins to finish, the receptionist continued to flash those pearly whites of hers. After a moment of silence, she decided to break it up. “Yeah, I’m that ‘cranky bitch’ you’re referring to. Marie’s the name.” “Oh. My bad.” With a stern look, Marie reached into a file cabinet close by and pulled out a manila envelope with Bryan’s name written in all caps on the tab. Dawkins, meanwhile, had slumped over in embarrassment onto the marble counter and was avoiding eye contact at all costs. After glancing at the contents for a moment, she peered up at Dawkins. “If you take a seat, Mr. Dawkins, I’ll let Mr. Kaneua know you’re here.” And with that, Marie exited her receptionist station and made a b-line for a door out of sight from Bryan, who took a seat in the waiting room. “Well, that was great.” * * * Harper Kaneua was one of five public defendants employed by the Hilo County Courthouse, and if you were to rank those 5 employees, Harper would most definitely be at the bottom of the list. Barely finishing law school with passing grades, he got lucky and was offered a spot in one of the lesser known law firms in his hometown of Los Angeles as an assistant to the higher up lawyers of the firm. Almost a year after he was hired by the firm, he got his big break in a small case involving a drunk driving incident involving some teens and an older man. The case would have been an open-and-shut case for most lawyers. Sadly, Harper isn’t one of those lawyers. He froze up during the proceedings and subsequently nearly lost the case before his supervisor took him off of the case. Following the embarrassment of being booted from such an easy case, Harper moved to Hawaii in hopes of finding a better life on the island. He applied for the Public Defender spot nearly five years ago, and has been losing cases left and right for clients who simply knew they could not win, or just didn’t care about the status of their case. Bryan Dawkins knew he couldn’t win his case. Or at least, he thought that, which brought him to Harper’s office. “Bryan Dawkins, wrestling superstar. Never thought I’d see you sitting in my office.” Dawkins peered around the office in disgust. Legal books were piled everywhere, a plastic fern was the only sign of outside life inside the cramped little office, and the chair Bryan was sitting in was losing most of its stuffing due to a massive gash in the side of the seat cushion. Bryan didn’t want to know what caused the gash, nor did he care. He wanted this to be short and to the point. “Alright bruh, let’s skip the pleasantries and get down to business. I don’t really wanna be here, and to be perfectly honest with ya, I think this is a complete waste of both of our time.” “But—“ “But nothin’, bruh. I know what you’re all about. You get paid whether you win or lose, so this shit don’t really make a difference to you. And that’s fine with me, because I know this is an open and shut case in the opposition’s favor, bruh. There’s no way we can fight this, so there’s no use sittin’ here tryin’ to come up with some kinda gameplan to ambush the competition, ‘cuz that just ain’t gonna work.” “This isn’t—“ “BRUH. You ain’t changin’ my mind. The case is over in my mind, and you’ll honestly be lucky if I show up for the trial. It all really depends on how I’m feelin’ that day. Ya know, Harper?” Kicking his feet up onto the grungy desk of Harper’s and leaning back in the rickety old chair, it seemed like Bryan was done talking. “So if I’m hearing you correctly, Mr. Dawkins, you’re essentially giving up?” “That’s what I’m sayin’, bruh. You deaf?” “Can’t say I am. What I can say is that while you might not get a clean win, we could still steal some terms here.” Snapping upright again, Bryan peered at Harper, who was slicking back his greased up hair and pulling out a 99 cent cigar from his desk. “Steal some terms? As in…?” “As in getting to see Nicholas on weekends…” Bryan’s face almost lit up. “…and holidays.” A hint of a smile appeared on the Hawaiian’s face. “Possibly more. Depends on how much we can squeeze out of this.” “Bruh, I dunno. I mean I’m not home much on weekends.” “Who said you had to see him at your house?” “Well that’s how it usually works, right?” Lighting up the cheap cigar, Harper leaned over the desk toward Dawkins. “You’re a working father, Bryan. They can’t take that away from you. You need your livelihood to take care of little Nicholas. Without that, he’ll grow up poor. Nobody wants that. We’ve got enough poor people in this world already. We don’t need Bryan Dawkins and his son to be the next two.” “Maybe you’re right, bruh. So what’s the plan?” Leaning back in his oversized desk chair, Harper exhaled a massive puff of rancid smoke into the air of his cramped office. “Well, this is how it’s gonna work…”
