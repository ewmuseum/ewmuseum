Road to Recovery Chapter Two: Taking the Edge off

13 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13/11/2010 Lebanon Hospital Center, The Bronx, New York 24th June 2010 Jacob McKail climbed out of Archie Steiner’s overly opulent silver Mercedes and instantly became enthralled with the Victorian monstrosity that was to serve as his place of forced residence for the next six months. Archie was a highflying lawyer from uptown who usually charged three hundred bucks an hour and had decided to take up McKail’s case. Archie claimed to have loved wrestling since he was a kid and was dumb enough to list McKail as one of his heroes. He’d offered to represent him for free and McKail was in no position to decline the offer. Why he’d insisted on driving McKail to the rehab facility himself thought was another matter entirely. “Jesus!” Archie exclaimed, slamming the driver’s side door shut and making his way to the rear of his car to fetch McKail’s stuff. “Looks more like an insane asylum than a rehabilitation center!” McKail grunted in agreement, as Archie approached with the small trolley suitcase that contained all the meagre possessions he had to his name and all of the memories he’d fought so desperately over the years to forget, but was too afraid to let go of. The alcohol held all of his painful memories at bay; it numbed him to the point where he was able to force his past out of his head for a time. His late wife’s death, his failed wrestling career and every other goddamn awful thing he’d ever seen or done in his time were locked up tight in a bourbon fuelled vault he hoped would never be reopened again. This rehabilitation bullshit was going to blow the vault door off its goddamn hinges and let everything spill right on out. The prospect truly terrified him. “You okay?” Archie asked. “You look a little pale.” “No,” McKail told him. “Spent all day puking your guts out, huh?” Archie continued, with an inappropriate chuckle. “Hear recovery gets pretty rough, but I reckon you’re gonna do just fine.” McKail ignored him and cast his eyes out onto the horizon. The sun was setting, cutting a purple black laceration into the sky, the wound spurting out the red orange glow of the sun behind the silhouette of the clouds. I could make a run for it, he contemplated. I could hit the road like back in the day. No one would ever catch me, hell, I don’t think anyone would much care. The smell of cherry blossom assaulted his senses, resurrecting something within him he thought was long dead: hope. This is the right thing to do, he told himself. This is something you need to do. Without another thought, he lowered his head and set off on a determined towards the daunting entrance of the rehab facility. “Bye then,” Archie called after him. “And good luck.” McKail ignored him. Luck had no part to play in six months of sheer torture; this was going was going to be a biblical test of endurance and he wasn’t so sure he’d even want to survive at the end of it. *** Clancy’s Diner The Bronx, New York 13th November 2010 Cynthia nodded a hello as Jacob McKail entered the diner and desperately tried to hold back a chuckle. Her hands littered with plates and cups, she used her elbow to nudge her friend and point him out. She laughed at him too. It was understandable. McKail frequented Clancy’s on a regular basis and back in the day, in a drunken state he’d hit on more than one of the waitresses. But that wasn’t why they were laughing. They dealt with drunks in this place all the time, it was a twenty-four hours diner after all, trouble was McKail had successfully managed to secure a date with Cynthia. She was a woman of loose morals and easy virtue, so it didn’t take McKail long to get her in the sack. And that’s where things went downhill. He didn’t live up to her expectations and she got all upset about it. She thought the problem was with her and so decided to lash out, telling her friends far and wide about the whole affair. Of course, it the problem didn’t lie with her, it was the booze but that wasn’t any kind of excuse she’d buy into, not that he tried all that hard to sell it to her. She was one drunken easy lay in many and the averages dictated it would happen at least once. McKail found a booth as far away from Cynthia’s waitressing jurisdiction as he possibly could and eased himself into the cherry coloured leather seats, groaning in agony all the while. It was funny, in all the time away from the business, he’d forgotten all about the little tweaks, niggles and bruises that regularly besieged him after every match. He didn’t miss it all that much and today he was paying the price for it. He didn’t remember it being this bad though. But then it was never this bad for him at all. The booze numbed him, it took the edge off. His first match was again Lindsay Troy though. A woman. This week on ReV he’d been booked to fight Big Bear. Big-fucking-Bear! God only knew what state he wasn’t going to be in after that match. McKail winced just thinking about it. “Cup of coffee, hun?” The question shook McKail from his somewhat pessimistic thoughts and he nodded a yes at the waitress. It wasn’t Cynthia. The waitress (her name take said her name was Andi) placed a cup on the table and poured the coffee. It didn’t smell all that fresh to McKail, but he didn’t really give a fuck about that right about now. He was more concerned about his pain and what the hell he was even doing in the diner in the first place. Elle, his ex-girlfriend, had called him up late last night and asked him to meet her here. She said she was just ‘passing through’ and she wanted to ‘catch up’. Naturally, McKail was suspicious. Who the hell passed through the Bronx? “There ya go, hon,” Andi told him. “That should pick you right up.” She couldn’t help herself from giggling and in the distance he could hear further giggles. Fuckin’ children, he silently cursed. Thing of it was, he was no damned better. McKail turned to see Cynthia standing behind the counter with another waitress laughing uncontrollably. McKail raised his cup to them and smiled. “Sorry, hon,” Andi apologised. “Just had to do it. Was too funny. But don’t worry. I hear it happens to a lot of folk.” “I didn’t want to say this, because I’m a nice guy,” McKail lied. “But the reason I couldn’t get it up was because your friend’s cunt is broken.” Andi’s face twisted in horror and the laughing from behind the counter stopped. He let the horror of his words sink in a while before smiling. “Nice coffee.” McKail wasn’t a drunk anymore, but that didn’t mean he wasn’t a prick. *** Lebanon Hospital Center, The Bronx, New York 24th June 2010 As soon as McKail and the rest of the new arrivals were herded into the small area the staff had previously informed them was the ‘Introduction Room’, he slumped into the closest chair he could find and tried not to hurl over himself the best he could. The ‘Introduction Room’ was small, cramped laden with bolted down hardened plastic chairs. Just a couple of minutes into the inevitably torturous affair, McKail was already squirming in his seat uncomfortably. If this room was anything like the rest of the place, McKail reckoned he would’ve been better back in the holding cell they had him stashed in after the trial. The alleged hospital seemed filth-ridden at its very core and thick white paint peeled and bubbled off the walls at various points. Hell, even the bars on the windows looked like they were rusted. As McKail was absorbing his new surroundings, the other new arrivals shuffled past him. Only then did he bother to observe his fellow ‘patients’, to see exactly what kind of folk he’d be spending the next six months with. The majority of them were broken men who looked like they were hanging onto life by the thinnest of threads. Judging by the tattoos and the joviality, the rest looked like convicts eager to work the rest of the debt to society by doing so called ‘easy time’ in a rehabilitation center. “Listen up, ladies!” The voice rumbled, as the door flung open and a portly fellow standing at around six foot five marched pointedly to the front of the room. He had everyone’s attention immediately; the talking and the laughter stopped in a heartbeat and all eyes faced front. The man’s arms were behind his back and he forced his chest and gut outwards as he cast his eyes over the rag-tag like he was some severely out of shape drill instructor. “If you’ve come here expecting an easy ride, think again,” he barked, pacing back and forth at the front of the room, establishing eye contact with whoever would take him on. “This isn’t a holiday home, you will not be coddled. My name is Buzz Copeland and I am going to be harsh, I am going to be strict. You will loathe me, you will hate me and I couldn’t give a flying fuck about it either way. You will follow my rules or you will be punished. Is that understood?” A few murmurs spread through the drill instructors audience, but evidently Buzz wasn’t all that happy with the response. “I said, is that understood!?” He screamed. The others mumbled a ‘yes’, some of more terrified members of the audience even replied with ‘yes, sir’, but McKail could only bite his lip. God only knew what the punishment was for laughing loudly and openly in the man’s face. However, he soon found out. Someone at the end of McKail’s row burst out into laughter and the drill instructors face turned bright crimson with anger. Buzz nodded in the direction of the door in which he entered and two muscle-bound staff members wearing all white stormed into the room and headed directly for the perpetrator of the laughter. They grabbed the man by each arm, lifted him out of his seat and dragged him towards the door. The man was laughing no more; he was glaring wide-eyed at the two staff members in quick succession a little shocked by the transpiring events. “Wh-wh-where are you taking me?” Buzz ignored him and continued to address the crowd. “Insolence will not be tolerated in any degree. You will do as I say and if you don’t, you’ll be paying our ‘physical therapy’ facility a visit, like Mr...” He thumbed through a pad of paper on a small wooden desk at the front of the room until he finally found what he was looking for, “...Edwards here.” If the other folk around him hadn’t realised that Buzz’s fanciful labelling of the ‘physical therapy’ facility stood for something entirely more sinister, they did when Edwards screams echoed along the hallways of the building. The room filled with tension and fear almost instantly, undoubtedly the effect Buzz was striving for. Buzz closed his eyes at the screams and in synch thuds of a sturdy substance against bone and smiled. The sick fuck was actually getting off on it. “Is that understood?” Buzz asked, once the agonising screams had finally stopped. But he didn’t wait for any kind of response. Instead, he bounded out of the room leaving most of his audience shocked and terrified in equal measures. One of the clearly broken men sitting in front of McKail threw up at the very prospect of what he’d got himself into and it took McKail all of his concentration not to follow in suit, his withdrawal alcohol withdraw still getting the best of him, all the while hankering for a bottle of bourbon to take the edge off. *** Clancy’s Diner, The Bronx, New York 13th November 2010 The smell of lilacs filled his nostrils and enflamed his senses, bringing back nostalgic memories from not all that long ago. Back when times were good and he had the makings of a life set out before him. It was Elle. There was no question in his mind about that, but her scent only lingered. He dragged his attention reluctantly away from his coffee and saw her standing at the end of his table, with tears in her eyes. “I missed you, Jacob,” she spluttered. McKail could only blink. “But you only saw me last week.” “I know,” she replied. “That’s how I knew.” McKail scrunched his face up, somewhat perplexed. “Knew what?” She smiled. “That I’m still in love with you.” Oh Jesus! He silently exclaimed, wishing he had a tankard of bourbon to drop in his coffee. Just to take the edge off, naturally. He stood and took her into his arms. What else could he do? “Jacob,” she began, after a while. “Why are the waitresses looking at us funny?” “Jealousy, most likely,” he replied.
