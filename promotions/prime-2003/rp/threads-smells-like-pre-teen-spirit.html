Date: ReV 187 (w/Wicked Ways vs. Fusenhoff & The Union) I: A Mulatto The woman who stood in front of the class wasn't tall or particularly imposing, in a soft blue sweatshirt and a pair of what could only be called "mom-jeans". She adjusted her eyeglasses (likely a nervous habit) as she cleared her throat. "Children, please... if you'll take your seats?" All around her, Mrs. O'Reilly's second-grade class raced back to their desks, and among them were Dakota and Summer Sinclair. Little Summer (so small her family called her "Lil") sat front-row center, and, if she adjusted the position of her own glasses, it was out of a sense of mimicking her elders. Her sister, the taller, more outgoing of the pair by far, dumped herself into the back row. Normally, Dakota (we'll call her "Dee" since she prefers it) might have been right next to her sister – they were considered inseparable by their teachers, even given that Lil was a better student than Dee – but today was a special day. "Do you have a Froot Roll-Up?" the kid next to her asked. "No," Dee said. "Momma didn't pack any today. I think my sister ate them all." There's one thing about this particular kid that stood out. "Damn." He was thirty-nine years old. "Dad!" Dee hissed. "You can't say those kinda words in school!" "Children, children..." Mrs. O'Reilly called. "I know you're all excited about Show and Tell here, but we need to get started." The room quieted down somewhat, two-dozen sets of tiny eyes focused forward. If he'd noticed, Sabuani might have been impressed with the teacher's ability to hold even the most recalcitrant of children (read: his daughter). But Dakota Lee Sabuani-Sinclair didn't get her... we'll call it "unfocused" nature from her mother. "Now, I know a lot of you brought in some excellent items to show the class, but in the interest of not keeping her father, I think we'll start with Dee Sinclair. Dee?" "Dee Sabuani," Hunter muttered as his daughter pranced – there is no other word for it – up to the front of the room. She clearly got her strutting skills from her father as well. On this day (and perhaps only on this day), Dakota wore a dress to school, deep burgundy with a pleated skirt and her good shoes. Her honeyed hair was pinned up (a process she'd made her mother spend a full thirty minutes doing that morning – if not for their father's notorious lack of adherence to speed laws, they would have been late for school that morning). The young girl pulled a stack of 3" x 5" index cards from the hip pocket of her dress, clearing her throat softly. "Uhm, hey," she said with a short, shy smile. "My sister said yesterday that she thinks I don't like show & tell. She liked it a lot – remember when she brought her froggy?" Several assenting nods came from her classmates. "But I have alwaysalwaysALWAYS wanted to do this." Dee stepped back, gesturing broadly with the hand that didn't have a deathgrip on her cards. Her voice deepened (as much as a seven-year-old's can deepen). "Now coming to the front of the classroom... from Las Vegas, Nevada... the two-time heavyweight champion of the world... MY DADDY! Peerless! HUNTER! SABUANI!!!" Both of Dee's tiny index fingers swung in an arc to point at her father at the back of the room. Her blue-gray eyes locked on her sister, brows wiggling significantly. After a brief moment, her face red from embarrassment, Summer drew a small CD player onto her desk and pressed "Play." Though the sound quality wasn't what it might have been in an arena, it was definitely Weezer's "The Greatest Man That Ever Lived (Variations on a Shaker Hymn)" that accompanied the Raja on his circuitous route to the front of the room. Sabuani slapped hands with his daughters' classmates (which just turned Summer redder) before lifting his hand for Dee's own high-five. If Mrs. O'Reilly didn't look pleased at this show, he... well, he completely ignored her. "Yo," Hunter said by way of greeting. When no one responded immediately, he repeated himself. "Yo!" "Yo!" came the response from the class – or most of it, at least. "For those who don't know, aside from being Dee and Lil's kick... uh, kick-butt dad, I am also a professional wrestler by trade. The girls' momma and I currently work for PRIME Entertainment, and you guys can catch us every Wednesday night on FX Network – though it's probably way past your bedtime. Maybe you can just tell your folks. Remember: FX – There is No Box. Whatever that means." He cleared his throat gently, rubbing at the back of his neck. "Anyhow, I have lots of great stories, and – if she's not a total stick – Teach there might let me tell some of them." He tossed his too-wide grin at the teacher. "But I figure, I figure it'd be better to open the floor to questions. So let me see those hands." A mop-headed husky kid's hand was the first one to jab into the air. "Isn't wrestling fake?" he asked. Hunter chuckled, scratching his cheek. "Why is that always the first question? Look, kid, I've been doing this for close to fifteen years. I tore my ACL in 2000 when the roof of a cage collapsed under my feet. I suffered my... fifth, maybe sixth concussion when a big, ugly son of a... gun dropped me from the top rope..." A long whistle. "Down to the steps below. I've broken ribs, I've chipped teeth, and I've spilled whole pints of blood on that mat. If your folks or your older sibling scoffs, 'Oh, wrestling is sooooo fake? You tell them to come to a PRIME arena – we'll be in, like, Alaska or whatever this week. They can ask for me, and we'll see if they're still singin' the same tune. Next question." A thin, coffee-skinned boy with a head full of curls eased up out of his seat. Beside her father, Dee's expression darkened. "I just have one question," the boy began. "Shoot with me," Hunter said with a beckoning smile. "How come you're such an asshole?" "Corey James Evans!" Mrs. O'Reilly exclaimed, half-rising out of her seat. Dee shot the kid a look of daggers. Hunter silenced both women with an upraised palm, favoring his accuser with a smile that approached (but didn't quite reach) gentility. "Now, now. Corey, was it?" The young boy nodded. "Corey, what makes you think I'm a..." Hunter glanced at O'Reilly, who was officially fuming. "A that word?" "My brother watches ReVolution all the time and he's all, 'Is that your friend Dee's dad?' And said yes. He's all, 'Gosh, that guy is a jerk.'" "Did you ask him why?" "He... he said you'd said some pretty mean things, and he showed me this video where you pushed over a ladder with some guy on it. He looked hurt pretty bad." "Listen, there, Junior..." Sabuani circled around the podium. "First, that word you called me? It's not nice and it hurts me. It hurts me deep. When I was a kid, the teacher woulda rapped me on the knuckles – hard. You are lucky you've got such a swell Teach who'll just bark your name a bit when you insult a guest." As the Saint of the Squared Circle approached the boy, Mrs. O'Reilly rose from her seat again to intervene. "Mr. Sabuani..." she warned. His gesture at the woman was a little more flippant than the first one. "Second," he continued. "You oughta tell your big brother that he shouldn't tell tales out of context. It's rude. Every 'mean' thing I've said has been wholly, one hundred percent deserved. And that guy I pushed off a ladder? I kind of hope he's hurt pretty bad, because he threw a bucket of paint at my little girl's momma, and he spilled hot coffee on me. And that's rude, too." Hunter loomed over the kid, grinning devilishly down. "So you may wanna watch that footage and ask yourself what happens to people who are rude to me." Before this could be another case of OMG PHS HITZ KIDZ!, Linda O'Reilly slipped up out of her chair and interposed herself between Sabuani and the child. "That is just about enough, Mr. Sabuani. I let you come here today as a favor to your daughter, but I will not allow you to threaten my students, so I am going to have to stop this now. We have quite a few presentations to get to today." "But Mrs. O'Reilly, it was Corey who started it..." Dee whined. "That may be," the teacher said. "But your dad is an adult." "It's okay, Dee-girl." Hunter turned his daughter to face him. "I'll see you before your mom and I go to Alaska, okay?" He tousled Dakota's hair, raising his hand to the quieter of the two girls. He stepped back, glancing to the teacher. "Take care of my girls," he said, slipping through the door and out into the hallway. "Now," Mrs. O'Reilly said as the door swung shut. "I understand that Geri's brought her hamster today..." ---- II: An Albino (An Interlude) "It's all there," Cecilia Sicarii said disdainfully, turning away from the man on the other side of the wooden booth, her hazel eyes lifting to the lazily spinning ceiling fans in the small bar. The man picked at the knot on the Crown Royal bag in front of him. "Did you have to tie it so bloody tight?" The Little Dynamo's left hand clasped around a stein of Guinness. "Hadda make sure that it didn't open and spill all over the place. It'd be friggin' embarrassing if I sucked one up with my vacuum cleaner. Besides, can't you just... I dunno, hold it in one hand and tell how much is in there based on weight?" The man looked up, eyes shadowed by the brim of his hat. "Why the fuck do you think I could do that?" Rat shrugged as she lowered her glass down. "Saw it in a movie once." Finally, the man worked the golden cord apart. He dipped a hand pocked with liver spots into its contents, which tinkled softly as he touched them. "Seems like they're all here." Sicarii's mug hit the table with a dull thud. "So where is it?" Two dull snaps unlatched on the bench beside him and a beaten manila file folder slapped down onto the table between them. To his credit, his hand only shook a little. "It's all here," he said, dropping the bag into the attaché at his side. "But you didn't count them or any of that shit." "Don't need to," the man gritted. "Weighs right." Yellowed teeth gleamed in his smile. Sicarii flipped open the folder, pawing through the paperwork within distractedly. "This seems awful light. He won't be happy." The man hunched his shoulders and doubled over with his wet, hacking cough. "That's all there is," he gasped. "And I went through hell herself to get it, so he'll have to make due." "You don't... y'know, look so good," Rat remarked off-handedly, more in the interest of conversation than concern. "I will be fine. Take it and go." Sicarii nodded, lifting the steel mug to her lips and tipping it back, back, back, back... and then down onto the pocked wooden tabletop. "Don't have to tell me twice, Doc." She slid up out of the table, tossing a few crumpled dollar bills onto the table. "Take care of yourself." The pale-skinned man huddled further into the shadows, providing no answer. ---- III: A Mosquito Nurse Wendy Markham's tiny little office was always so damn organized; everything had its place and everything stayed in that place, until or unless the nurse (who was almost the very definition of a little old lady) needed it. Which made the blood staining the vinyl-covered examination table stand out all the more, little dots of red on crinkled white paper. The bleeder was gone by then, forehead bandaged and the child sent home with worried parents. But Mrs. Markham hadn't had the time to clean – at least not yet. Instead, the kindly (if disturbingly quiet) school nurse held a small girl's hand between her own, gently dabbing at scuffed knuckles. The girl's sister shifted from foot to foot nearby, desperately trying not to pay attention to the increasingly heated conversation their mother and teacher were having across the room. "Dude, I can't believe that just happened," Dee Sabuani said, her blue-gray eyes wide. "You just punched Corey!" Lil Sinclair endured the nurse's ministrations serenely, using her free hand to straighten the wide, red-framed glasses she wore -- which had gone askew during the altercation. She didn't say anything (really, when you're sisters with Dakota, you don't need to carry), but her smile was dangerously calm. "I mean, Corey's a real jerkbag, but... you punched him! And then—" "I know," the younger (by fifty-three minutes) of the girls said. She seemed strangely unaffected by what she'd done. Not so her sister, who nervously flicked her eyes to Mrs. O'Reilly and their mother. "She looks mad," Dee whispered. Mrs. Markham finally spoke, her voice soft and soothing like a glass of warm milk. "It will be okay, girls. Mrs. O'Reilly just has a—" "Don't give me that!" It was Madison on the other side of the room, discretion abandoned as her frustration grew. "A particular way of doing things," the nurse finished, as if the interruption hadn't occurred. "I assure you, Ms. Sinclair. Everything I do is in the best interest of all of my students." O'Reilly folded her thin arms over her chest. "Tell me, how is pushing for my daughter to be suspended in her best interests? She was defending Dakota." "I realize something like this is hard for a mother, but I have to weigh the needs of all my students. Based on the damage Summer caused, it's simply not safe to have your little girl in my class." Madi blinked, looking briefly to the twins (Dee had found a lollipop, and Lil was flexing her fingers) as her voice dropped to an urgent hiss. "So you're telling me Summer isn't being punished because she came to Dee's aid, but because my ex-husband made sure his little girls could defend themselves?" "Your 'little girl' punched one of her classmates in the stomach and then dropped him headfirst into a tile floor. The fact that she was protecting Dakota is the only reason we're not talking about expulsion. I think one of your husband's professional wrestling maneuvers goes above and beyond the word 'protection,' Ms. Sinclair. But it will be the hearing that decides what happens to Summer, not I." "Mrs. O'Reilly, please..." Madison's smile was rueful. "If the girls were a little rambunctious today, it was just because their father was here with them this morning." It was suddenly clear O'Reilly had been waiting for the Babe to open that door. "I saw that. Given that he threatened the same boy Lil injured, I am halfway tempted to label it as a premeditated assault, which would mean the Allendale Columbia School would be well within their rights to expel both of them." "Mrs. O'Reilly – Linda." Madi tried to pour on the same charm she'd used so often in wrestling – and out of it. "This school has meant so much to the girls, and my ex-husband and I have occupations that take us out of New York quite often. We're actually going to Moscow next month, and we would love to bring you—" O'Reilly frowned. "Perhaps you should be around for your daughters' youth, Ms. Sinclair. They might learn more if you were home than Summer appears to have learned from what she's seen of you two on her television set." When her eyes bulged, jaw dropping, Madison Sinclair was nothing but a parent pushed to the edge. It was like a larger version of the look Summer had spared Corey Evans before the DDT that started this mess. But Madison didn't lose her cool, however much she may have wanted to. She didn't strike the teacher, and there was no wrestling move used in real life. Her smile bloomed (the one most people in the wrestling industry knew was fake), and she extended her hand to the woman. "I'm sorry," she said. "I don't want to hurt the twins' education or in any way stand in their way. I'll just have to plead my case to the principal." The two women shook briefly – little more than an up-and-down pump of their joined fists. Madison stepped forward when she released O'Reilly's hand, bumping into the teacher as she went back to her girls. The Babe would remain fairly convinced that O'Reilly initiated the contact as the teacher went back into the school, but Madi didn't care much. She flipped open a silver and black cellphone, snatching a discarded hunk of newspaper as she scanned the phone's address book. She smiled as she settled on a number, free hand slipping down to the iPhone that was always holstered at her waist. A few flicks of her thumb later, her call connected. "Hi, it's me," she said. "No, I'm fine. Just tired of dealing with some parasites. Look, I need you to reverse directory me a couple numbers...." Ten minutes later, after the twins and their mother headed home, Linda O'Reilly would come into the nurse's office and find her phone (which she'd thought was in her pocket) lying on the floor beside the bench she'd argued with the insufferable woman next to. Mrs. Markham wouldn't say how it got there, but O'Reilly would tell her friends later that the old woman was giving her the eye. * * * Make Hunter Sabuani mad at you and he'll probably invent some new curse words, make a series of dire proclamations, or maybe – if you're extremely unlucky – push you off a ladder through a table while you were about to win your most important match in several years. Inevitably, however, something shiny will come along and he'll wander off – there are only a few ways to anger the Raja to the point that he'll never even forgive you. It's in his nature – some people would call him a little "flighty". He'd like to tell those people, "Fuck you." But if you make Madison Sinclair mad at you... then you might as well call it quits now, because she's never going to stop coming for you. She's vindictive, mean, and not a little conniving (three reasons why Hunter might not love her, but sure likes her a lot more). Now imagine your name was Linda O'Reilly, and you'd just threatened the education of her children, the only two people she cares about. Yeah, your future isn't looking too rosy. She is the one of few people (in PRIME or out of it) who looks at a situation and asks, "WWDD?"
