A Matter of Faith

28 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-28-06 There was an old fellow of LymeWho lived with three wives at one time.When asked, 'Why the third?'He replied, 'One’s absurd,And bigamy, sir, is a crime.' -Edward Lear ~~~~Pangea Compound, upstate Illinois… It was a quiet place full of quiet people, a friendly and familiar haven to every child who grew up within its well-kept perimeter. When there were visitors (which was rare; The Prophet didn’t appreciate pagans and their Godless ilk snooping around the affairs of his congregation), many people forgot where they were and simply marveled at its beauty, its simplicity. An old-world and decidedly rustic charm settled on the rolling hills that were only now starting to turn yellow from the cold, surrounded by five hundred acres of purebred Illinois farmland. It was five hundred acres a full three thousand practicing polygamists called home. They called it Pangea. “Jennifer!” To them, it was a paradise to rival the Garden of Eden. “Jen, honey? Wake up sweetheart, it’s time for supper.” A place where they could be proud of their Principal, free to glorify the love of the Lord and the bountiful gift He saw fit to grant them. “I’m serious Jen, nap time is over. Moira and I cooked a duck. It’s getting cold.” A place where even the Light will never eliminate some shadows. “Stop acting like we cut your pigtails yesterday, Jennifer. Wake up, this isn’t funny.” Some secrets. “Jennifer…?” A scream. ~~~~A PRIME Press Conference… A beyond-capacity crowd tried to pack ten rows of folding chairs in the otherwise spacious press pit, forcing most of the wide-eyed journalists in attendance to rub elbows with the competition. Flashbulbs blinked a steady rhythm. Murmur became overwhelming roar became murmur again, silenced every few minutes as a face appeared from behind the simple black stage curtain. Each time a sound technician revealed himself to toss a signal to his partners at the control board, the roar rose up and consumed the ant farm once again. When Rich Rollins and Angelo Deville finally broke through the black screen, the roar reached a decibel too painful for the triple-checked sound board to take. A feedback screech filled the room and was killed. Taking their respective places behind the monumental press desk set up center stage, Deville and Rollins exchanged inaudible dialogue as they sat and stared at the crowd, their voices lost in the shuffle. An infallible grin donned each face, smeared from ear to ear in cocksure confidence. A nameless MC made sure their microphones were live and raised his hand to quell the chatter. “Ladies and gentlemen, we’re going to do this as civil as possible,” he said. “One question at a time, please.” Let the fun begin. A stodgy pencil-pusher in the third row rose to his feet, letting his hand drop once it was acknowledged. “Mr. Rollins,” he began, whipping a blank notebook out like a chest protector, “what exactly brought you to PRIME?” Shifting assuredly, The Renegade tossed Deville a smug wink before facing the crowd. “Free food and frequent flier miles go a long way in this business, bucko. If Worth offered everyone the same deal he did me, Kyle Lamen would have been diggin’ his way back up for a comeback last Thursday.” Uncomfortable laughter filled the room. Folding his arms across his chest, Rollins nodded slightly and smiled. “Okay, second question.” The same reporter cleared his throat, throwing a glance at his notes. “Angelo Deville, what prompted you to form an alliance with Rollins? It seems highly unusual for someone to throw his name in the hat with a man who has defe-” “I’d watch the way I worded that, if I were you.” Placing both palms on the smooth surface of the table, Angelo Deville shot the man a sharp glare. It dulled into condescension far too easily. “To put it quite simply, or in other words, any way in which you nescient nitwits can hope to comprehend, the reasoning is both obvious and unshakable. I don’t trust this man sitting next to me any more than I can carry him wrapped up in that sweat-stained bandanna of his, but it isn’t a matter of trust. This is a matter of faith, gentlemen.” The Deville preened, obviously quite proud at the sounds of confusion that swept the room. Rollins took his cue and leaned into his own microphone. “Both Angelo and I have come to an agreement, so to speak. Think about it, folks; if either one of us had to choose one man he’d avoid if at all possible, one guy who’d be more trouble to take down than to simply ignore, who would it be? No, seriously, who the fuck do you think?” Jerking a thumb toward the man at his side, The Renegade snorted. “If you can’t beat ‘em, join ‘em. Ain’t that right, Angie?” A millisecond of surprise slipped through Deville’s cool exterior, disappearing with renewed assurance in those oh-so-devilish baby blues. A few scattered chuckles wafted up the stage, courtesy of a room full of journalistic dignitaries. Angelo Deville leaned back with a sneer, calm as a dormant volcano. “Now there’s a concept our opponents at Great American Nightmare would do well to remember. Too bad for them we’re not accepting grizzled old strays who’ve long passed their glory days.” Satisfied, Deville shared a knowing smirk with Rollins as a dozen new hands shot up from an ocean of faces.Trying to remain invisible pressed against the farthest wall she could find, Lori Goodwin allowed herself an inward smile as she watched the verbal tap dance. Hugging her purse close to her middle, she caught Rollins’ eye for the briefest of glances and received a quick wink. Her cheeks flushed. She knew she had other places to be, but none of them seemed half as important as the here and now, watching her brother build up to one of the biggest matches of his entire career. Family was so important, especially in these Godless times. ~~~~The Prophet’s residence, Pangea Compound… “I… I… she… oh Lord, what’s wrong with her?!” Huddled protectively over the convulsing body of Jennifer Holly while trying not to hyperventilate, the sister-wives Elizabeth and Moira had never seen someone seizure so violently. The sixteen-year-old Jennifer’s left leg jerked randomly and her eyes fluttered open, already rolled back to the whites. Moira began to sob as she held the girl’s wrists down on the couch, trying to keep Jennifer horizontal despite her spasmodic throes. “Where is Ben?” Moira moaned. She glanced up at the digital grandfather clock that loomed near the parlor entryway, expecting to see her husband’s knowing face. “We need help, Liz! Oh God, is she dying? What’s happening to her?” “I don’t know!” snapped Elizabeth. “I tried to reach him on his cell, but he wouldn’t answer!” “Is Markus on his way?” Eyes glistening, Moira had to bite back a shriek as Jennifer’s right leg nearly kicked her. “Is anybody on the way? What are we going to-” “Where is she?” Benjamin Holly burst through his front door, charged across the glossy wood floor of the spacious entryway and skid to halt once he saw the trembling trio. “Oh Lord…” Elizabeth tried to explain something, but she had no idea what to say. It didn’t matter; Ben was already by their side, relinquishing Moira of her nursemaid duties. Thumbing open swollen eyelids that had suddenly clamped shut, Ben began to soothe in a way that came from years of practicing politics. “Jen, sweetheart? Can you hear me baby?” No response. “Liz, have you called Dr. Stewart?” “No, I-” “Well start calling the fucking doctor! How long has she been like this?” Now crying, Elizabeth tried not to blur her words. “I d-don’t know, I just-” “Useless woman!” he seethed. Whipping out his cell phone, Benjamin Holly tossed his suit jacket aside and rolled up his sleeves as he waited for the good doctor to answer. “Come on, you overpriced jackass, answer the damn phone!” Moira sat stupefied, watching as her beloved sister-wife continued to die. Releasing a ragged breath, Ben swapped the phone to his other ear. “Yes, Harold? Oh Jesus Harold, it’s Jen. She’s having some sort of seizure… yes, yes just like James Uldaman, but she can’t…” He trailed off as a mortified expression did laps across his face. “Her tongue? No, she isn’t choking, at least not yet.” Moira jumped as Ben snapped at her impatiently. “Moira, go outside and grab a stick. A stick, not a twig. We need to keep her from swallowing her own tongue.” The shock wouldn’t leave Moira’s face. Tears ran a trail over her chin. “Now!” Ben screamed. She jumped again, this time out the front door. She nearly knocked the first-wife over in her flight to find a dry stick. Dropping her purse, Lori Goodwin-Holly’s brow furrowed when she noticed her fellow sister-wife’s flustered face. “What’s the matter, Moira?” Pushing Lori away without saying a word, Moira ran outside and fell to her knees in the grass. Confused, Lori entered the house and walked into the parlor. “Benjamin, what’s going on?” When she noticed her husband holding the youngest of the four against the couch, she gasped. “Good heavens, what happened?” Running to her husband’s side, Lori took hold of Jennifer’s jittering ankles before they could strike her Prophet. Standing in the corner, Elizabeth continued to cry. It happened before Lori had the chance to get a good grip. Snapping his first-wife’s head around, Benjamin Holly slapped Lori as hard as he could. She fell back with a muffled cry, bringing a palm to her stinging cheek. Ben snapped his phone shut and turned, towering over the confused woman like a monolith of pure fury. “I know what you’ve been up to,” he said, his suddenly quiet demeanor made all the more terrifying under the circumstances. “I know you’ve been seeing that convicted felon, that blasphemous wrestler who wears the red scarf-” “Ben, no! You don’t understa-” “And we’ll talk about it later.” Scooping his jacket off the floor, Benjamin Holly refused to look his wife in the eye. “I have to go pick up Dr. Stewart. His car died again. Of all times to neglect a tune-up…” To his second-wife Elizabeth, Holly said, “Make sure Moira finds a proper stick. Keep Jen as comfortable as possible until I return.” And he was gone. Shaking enough to rival her youngest sister-wife’s seizure, Lori Holly sucked in a raspy weep. “He’s my brother,” she whispered, but the only ears to hear her confession were too busy swallowing a tongue. ~Fin~
