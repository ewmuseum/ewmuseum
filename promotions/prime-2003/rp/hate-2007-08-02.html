Hate

2 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/08/2007 Chapter I: Into The Darkness We Shall Go… The alley was a bad idea and Aeryn knew it. Whoever the hell was chasing had been shepherding here in this direction all along and she was too dumb to see it. Before it would’ve been a different matter entirely. Before she would’ve been smart enough to avoid such an easy ruse, before she would’ve had the courage to turn and fight her aggressor, before he would’ve been the one running from her. But times change and, by all accounts, ever since the enemy disassembled her mind for she still couldn’t hope to understand, she’d become an entirely different woman. Aeryn still hadn’t decided whether was entirely a bad thing. She couldn’t turn back now, he’d cut off her entrance and now slowly stalked forward, laughing just loud enough for her to hear it. She persevered along the alleyway, hoping and praying there was some kind of possible escape. The alley was so dark she couldn’t see more than a foot in front of her and almost ran into the brick wall of the adjacent building when in finally approached. Quickly, she made sure there were no concealed doors or perhaps partings between the buildings, but a few frantic seconds of searching, there was nothing. “Oh god, oh god, oh god, oh god…” she muttered to herself, turning to face her aggressor. He stalked closer, slower still and Aeryn could see his sadistic grin from yards away. “P-p-p-please don’t do this!” She begged him. “You don’t know what you’re getting yourself into!” The man rocked his head back and his laughter grew louder. The entrance of the alleyway was too far away now, nobody would dare venture inward towards them -- nobody would come and save her. “I don’t know what I’m getting into?” His rough New York twang was heavy on the ears and painted a ell enough picture for her to realise he was a career criminal. He’d done this before. Many times. “My boyfriend is-is connected,” Aeryn told him. “Y-yo-you’re in deep shit now!” The man laughed loudly again and lunged forward, grabbing her by her hair. “Bitch, your boyfriend ain’t here! It’s just me an’ you, baby!” Her face contorted in agony, Aeryn spat in his face. “Fuck you!” Anger quickly filled the aggressor’s face, as he maintained his grip upon Aeryn’s hair with one hand and wiped away the saliva with the other. Her attacker then raised his hand in the air and made sure she saw, which ultimately proved to be a mistake as Aeryn reacted quickly, bringing her knee up between his legs with force. The man doubled over in agony and muttered a long string of obscenities under his breath. Aeryn took her chance and attempted escape, but did not realise the attacker’s grip on her hair still held strong and she was dragged back screaming as the man continued to recuperate. “No!” She screamed as loudly as she could, despair filling her eyes with tears. “Help me! Somebody help me!!!!!!” Chapter II: A Journey Towards Hate… Hate is a strong word to use, but that does not prevent people from over utilising it without the appropriate degree of emotion to back it up. Often they know they don’t mean what they’re saying, they’re blowing an angry moment in their lives completely out of all proportion. Does this make it right? Of course not, but it certainly doesn’t make it hate. The road to hate is a long and laborious one, with many twist and turns along the way thrown in for good measure. Don’t believe me? Require proof to substantiate such a grandiose claims? Good. Imagine this. Imagine three other men wanting your spot on the PRIME roster. Imagine these men making your life a living hell for the better part of three months on the basis of this. Imagine being restricted to your locker room because what so laughably passes for PRIME management these days can’t trust you to walk three metres along the corridor without getting into some kind of conflict. Would you be angry enough to hate everyone involved in this fiasco? Would you hate the regime that allowed it to get this far? Now, consider being crammed in a wrestling ring with your enemies. Consider teaming you with someone you don’t even know, a possible future rival that you know you can’t trust. Consider losing the match through no fault of your own, relying on other people to have the backbone to kick out just one more time. Consider having that loss then promptly rubbed in your face. Is your anger evolving yet? Then come back to your apartment to find an answer message informing you that your girlfriend has been mugged in an alleyway. Then rush to the hospital for the doctor to tell you that she’ll pull through, but it was touch and go for a while. Then discover the attacker got away without being seen and it took an hour for someone passing by to find your girlfriends battered and beaten body. Whilst it’s an accurate statement that Jonathan Winters is a hateful man, it cannot be said that it isn’t for good reason. At least, not today. Chapter III: Every Reaction… “She’s going to be okay,” the doctor told him. Refusing to rip his eyes away from Aeryn’s battered and bruised body, Winters allowed a sigh of relief. Standing just outside of her room, Winters positioned himself by the window so that he could keep her within his line of sight the entire time. “Based on what she’s been through in the last couple of hours, she’s doing remarkably well,” the doctor continued. “We’ll just need to keep her in for a couple of days for observation.” Winters grunted a response under his breath and the doctor took it as sign to leave. He wanted to be in there with her, sat by her side and holding her hand gently, but he thought better of it. Like the doctor said, she needed her rest and, if truth be told, the sight of her in such a weakened and helpless state was even enough to make his heart break. “Winters!” The voice called. Winters did not react. “I got here as soon as I could! Is she okay?” It was Cornelius Stakiri -- industrialist, crime lord, warlord and Winters’ employer. Stakiri drew closer and landed a reassuring hand upon Winters shoulder. Winters looked at it then at him and, seeing the cold look in his eye, Stakiri quickly removed it. “So?” He asked, anxious. “Is she going to be ok--” In a blink of the eye, Winters hand wrapped itself around Stakiri’s throat, cutting him off mid-sentence and only then did he take his eyes off Aeryn. “Tell me, Stakiri!” Winters screamed. “What--ugh--the hell--ugh--are you talking about?” Stakiri responded, eyes wide with a powerful combination of shock and fear. “Tell me you had nothing to do with this, Stakiri!” Winters yelled. “I know you want what’s locked away inside her head, but tell me this isn’t how you’re trying to get it!” “I--ugh--know you’re--ugh--angry, Winters,” Stakiri told him, “but--ugh--you can’t really believe--ugh--I had something to do with this!” “No,” Winters admitted, releasing his grip entirely. Stakiri adjusted his shirt collar accordingly. “But I needed you to believe I would’ve ended you, if you had.” Stakiri nodded. “Yes. I got that message loud and clear.” “Good,” Winters told him. Stakiri turned and glared at Aeryn through the glass. “I take it she’ll live.” Winters echoed his gaze. “Indeed. She was stabbed in the abdomen, but it was sloppy. They got to her just time.” Stakiri raised a speculative eyebrow. “What’re you thinking?” “This was no ordinary mugging,” Winters replied. “It has something to do with your little underworld war.” Stakiri shook his head. “Doesn’t make sense. If the enemy wanted her dead, I have no doubt in my mind that she’d be dead.” “Perhaps,” Winters said, “but I don’t believe in coincidences.” Stakiri developed a thoughtful expression. “Maybe it was never their intent to kill Aeryn.” Winters glared at him. “To what end?” “To send an invitation,” Stakiri glared him in the eye. “They wanted you back in the game. Despite their best efforts you re-aligned yourself with me, that much is true, but your main concern was Aeryn and not the war. Seems they want you involved in some way.” It was a territory war and nothing more. Two groups of highly organised and well-funded thugs battling it out for bragging rights to New York City and beyond. To Winters, it seemed like a complete waste of time, money and lives and until today, none of it concerned him. They’d used Aeryn before. Both sides had. Firstly, Stakiri had sent her over as an undercover operative and then, when discovered, the enemy brainwashed her and sent her right back again. But now, they’d taken it too far and what’s more they knew it. Winters nodded. “They were successful.” Chapter IV: Perseverance + Violence = Answers… Four thugs, five pimps and an homeless guy who was unfortunate enough to look at him in the wrong later, Winters stumbled across what he believed to be his only vaguely plausible lead thus far and even that was hard to come by. “Fuck you, mang!” The scumbag yelled, desperately trying to ignore the fact that his own knife was lodged deep into his shoulder blade and his nose was unmistakably broken. “I won’t tell you anything, mutherfucka!” “Yeah,” Winters told him, rolling his eyes and twisting the knife against his victims shoulder blade sickeningly. “Hence the torture.” The rest of the moron’s gang lay scattered deep in the alleyway surrounding him and he half considered taking the questioning to one of them, if this buffoon dared to continued to defy him, but then decided against it. After all, it would actually mean waking them and who needed the hassle. Instead, he pounded the knife deeper into the bone. “Arrrrrrrrghhhhh!” The thug screamed in agony. Winters released the pressure on the blade and the moron allowed a heavy sigh to escape, yet managed to splutter, “Muther--*cough*--fucka…” Winters smiled, sadistically. “Care to rethink your position, or do I nail you to the fucking wall?” “N-no,” his victim pleaded, his eyes welling up with tears of intense pain and anger. “D-don’t.” “You know what I need to know,” Winters told him. “One name. The man who was working these alleyway’s last night. Scored big on a brunette and then cut her.” The thug shook his head. “I-I-can’t! They‘ll kill me!!!” Winters eyes widened. They? “What the fuck do you think I’m going to do you, dipshit?” His victim shook his head. “N-no! I-I-can’t, I--” “At least if you cooperate with me,” Winters began, taking a step backwards. “There’s a chance you run and hide from them…” The thug contemplated the offer and then finally nodded in agreement. “O-okay, o-okay…his name is Alphonse.” “And where can I find this Alphonse?” Winters questioned. “T-t-the Bay City Bar,” the thug nodded in a general northerly direction. “J-just a few blocks away from here…” Winters smiled and extracted his mobile phone from the pocket of his jeans. He dialled the number and Stakiri picked up almost immediately. “What did you find out?” Winters’ employer questioned. He always was direct and to the point. “It’s them alright,” Winters responded, strolling away from his victims. “My informant danced around the subject, but someone had put the fear of god into him.” “Sounds about right,” Stakiri agreed. “What’s next?” “Got a location,” Winters told him, exiting the alleyway and crossing over to the other side of the road. “On route now.” “You realise that it’s probably a trap of some kind…” Stakiri advised. “I hope so,” Winters told him. “I could do with letting off a little steam.” He hit the disconnect button and shoved the phone back into his pocket before Stakiri could even attempt to talk him out of it. At this point, he couldn’t be reasoned with. Chapter V: An Offer He Can‘t Refuse But Can Ignore… When Winters arrived at the Bay City Bar, his anger almost blinded him from the vibe of desolation and despair the place was mercilessly secreting and it almost made him hesitate when he shunted the heavy-set wooden door open. The placed stunk of old tobacco and stale alcohol, but Winters found it mildly appealing for reasons that thus far eluded him. It was dark inside, lit by its dim main lights and illuminated in various placed by the colourful neon brand advertisements that were strategically located all around the place. The bar was emptied, with smashed glass and pool cues scattering the décor painting a rather distinct picture of violence and destruction. Winters considered it rather strange that no bodies lay with the other debris, but decided it pointless dwelling on the subject much. He wasn’t here to solve a damn mystery. “About time you showed up,” the deep-set, gravely voice began. “I’ve been waiting.” At the other end of the room, a man with long white dreadlocks, crammed rather clumsily into a crisp white suite, with a sky-blue shirt and a plain white tie, emerged from the bathroom, with a rather a limp African-American gentleman in tow. Winters remained silent, but eyed up the quasi-albino’s victim carefully and nodded at him speculatively. “Alphonse, I presume?” The man with the dreadlocks nodded. “He’s dead and I killed him. The man who my superiors hired to take out your girlfriend.” He produced a wide shark-like grin. “Doesn’t it just make you furious that I got to him before you? Doesn’t it just make your blood boil that I took your revenge from you so casually? Doesn’t it make you hate everything and everyone in this big wide world?” Winters shook his head. “No.” The dreadlocked man was visibly shocked. He either underestimated or overestimated Winters resolved, in either case Winters didn’t really give a fuck. He was through playing games and he had far better places to be. Winters grabbed one last look at Alphonse’s lifelessly body, cracked a grin and left. Chapter VI: The Hateful Man…? This crisis over and a brief lull in his supposedly hate-filled life, most would expect Winters to go right back to his apartment and start making the appropriate preparations for Colossus. After all, the match was the culmination of three months of pain, violence, but mostly hate. That should be enough for him, right? That would fulfil the stereotypical archetype most pictured Winters to be, because he’s that cold, he’s that angry, he’s that ignorant… Forget what you know, because Winters already had. He headed back to the hospital. His wrestling career could wait.
