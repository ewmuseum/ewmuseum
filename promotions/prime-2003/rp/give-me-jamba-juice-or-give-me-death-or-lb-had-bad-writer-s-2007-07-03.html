Give Me Jamba Juice Or Give Me Death! (Or: LB Had Bad Writer's Block)

3 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 3, 2007 Miscues happen. So do missteps. They're an inevitable part of this business, no matter how long you've been in it. Oversleep an hour, miss your plane because of security lines. Show up late to a promotional appearance, make the company look bad by keeping people waiting. Save a friend-turned-quasi-enemy from a potentially career-ending move onto a sledgehammer and multiple chair shots a week later, alienate a former-tag partner-turned-friend who has a big time score to settle with the other person. For all the good Lindsay Troy has done lately on personal and professional levels, she can't seem to stop having a hand in the bad things too. While the small satisfaction she took in breaking Sonny Silver's hand at Overkill has sated her thus far, the inevitability of payback is never far from her mind, regardless of the extent of her erstwhile tag partner's injury. Now with her friendship with Danny on the rocks and the fence between her and Nova not yet mended, the fastballs Life's throwing her keep coming at her in head-hunting fashion. When the stresses of life are too much to bear, there's one way Troy knows how to ease them away, pending availability. Jamba Juice. ~**~**~**~**~ "You need a vacation." Lindsay cocked her head to the side and scrunched her eyebrows down. Outside the smoothie establishment on the corner of 16th and Camelback in downtown Phoenix, people walked by either fanning themselves (tourists) or showing no indication of the 110*+ heat bothering them. Across the table from her, half-hidden by the shadow of the large umbrella overhead, is long-time friend and former independent wrestler Tiffany Crenshaw. While Crenshaw's ring name of Tiffany Valentine had the tendency to give unfamiliar coworkers and fans the connotation of bubble-gum and ditzyness, the raven-haired, violet-eyed, six-foot-one woman would sooner dislodge teeth with a roundhouse thrust kick than flip her hair and give a cutesy giggle. "I need a vacation...coming from the woman who took over the dojo after Brent had the stroke and has been running non-stop ever since, I find that to be slightly hypocritical," Troy remarked with a smirk as she took a sip from her drink. "I'm allowed to be hypocritical every once in awhile," Tiffany returned the smirk. "Y'know, your uncle still wants to run things. If it wasn't Elise insisting on him taking it easy still, he would be." "He'll always want to run it. He and Elise couldn't have kids so the dogs and the school are his babies. Who's running it while you're out here?" "Colin." "And who's watching Colin so he doesn't hit on the women in the beginner classes?" "Joz." "There's a relief." "A bigger one than you know!" Tiffany adjusted in her seat and swung her legs over to an adjacent chair. "So while Joz is keeping 'I have Seniority' Colin in check, I get to come out here and do some scouting for the Impact Nationals tournament next month." "That's always a big to-do. Kids getting excited?" "The younger ones are. The advanced kids haven't fed off their eagerness yet...they know the ropes too well by now to get overly excited over a month in advance." Troy nodded. When she had nothing else to add, Tiffany jumped right into another topic. "You don't come around much anymore. The kids were asking about you before I left. I think they miss you a bit." "Hmmm..." Lindsay trailed off. "Truth is, I don't like going home much anymore. Nothing there to go home to." "Besides Brent, Elise, me, the kids, our other friends..." "You know what I mean, Tiff." She leaned forward in her chair, placing her drink on the table and resting her head in her hands. "I used to stay home longer when Joey was still here, fly out later and come back sooner. But with the house empty again, I just..." Again, she trailed off. "I hate to say it, but it's an awfully big house to be lonely in." Taking her feet off the chair and leaning forward toward Troy, Tiffany made deliberate eye contact. "There's no shame in feeling that way, Lindz." "But there is. I was married, couldn't make it work for the life of me, and now I'm in a state of flux. I'm thirty years old and inbetween separation and divorce. Melton won't sign the papers and I guarantee you that I'll turn thirty-one next month and will be in the same position I'm in now. It's just easier to not go home and be reminded of all this." "You tried with him. We all know you did. But you know Joey better than all of us. If you couldn't make it work with him then it's not fair to you to keep running into a wall and getting hurt." "I've boxed myself in now," a sigh escapes from between her lips. "I have the ineludible promise of Sonny coming back to avenge his hand. I'm on rocky ground with Caesar. I'm on even rockier ground with Danny, who won't return my phone calls and part of me can't blame him. All this, and then to put a lid on this cage I've got a midget and a new kid this week. Both of them would just love to get one over on the Universal Champ and they know that I've got all these other professional situations just gnawing away at me. Plus with Gamble, I've got a feeling he's got an even bigger point to prove than Wade does, given his failure to procure this gold where I succeeded and the crumbling of FU." The now-visible weight that had been pressing between Troy's shoulder-blades seemed to pull away with this admittance. Tiffany smiled, lifting her sunglasses away from her eyes in a gesture of complete certainty and frankness. "Well, let me tell you something, Lin. I've never, in eight-and-a-half years of knowing you, seen you go into any match, any tournament, any competition unfocused and unprepared. That's something you just don't know how to do. You have this uncanny ability to let your problems melt away right before you step over the performance threshold, yet I know you needed to get this out before you do that this week. I know this because I know you and you keep shit bottled up until the exploding point is reached and everything comes rushing out at once. The thing with Caesar will work itself out in time. The thing with Danny will more than likely work itself out once he remembers just how few friends he's got in that company." "And the thing with Sonny?" "Fuck Sonny. From what I've seen, you had to carry him ninety-eight percent of the time anyway." "You---..." "I know I'm right. You don't have to say it." Tiffany grinned. "I keep a closer eye on you and your travels than you think I do given the limited free time I have. Wade's focused and Gamble's hungry, but they don't have the staying power. You've always had that, despite the knocks and setbacks and interruptions." "I should probably go see a shrink for all this baggage," Troy laughed and relaxed back in her chair. "Maybe, but I've found friends to be much more therapeutic, Lin," Tiffany raised her cup in a salute, "especially when there's Jamba Juice involved."
