Nature vs Nurture: Episode 1 "Breaking Point"

22 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/22/2010 I know I wasn’t a perfect father, but I tried my best at it. I gave her everything she ever asked for, made sure she did her homework, did her chores, and ate everything on the plate, including the green beans she hated so much, before she went to watch TV or eat dessert. I went from changing her diapers to lending her money so she could go to the theatre with her school friends. And I did it all on my own. I’m not making any excuses for myself, or trying to pat myself on the back. All we had was each other, and I was never worried about our future. It was because I knew that no matter what happened I would always be there to take care of my little girl. Then it happened. I still vividly remember the way she looked sitting on the edge of the hospital bed. Her skin was so pale, and her eyes were void of all the cheer they once had. The detective was standing by her side asking her God-awful questions about the incident, while a female doctor did some swabbing on her injuries. And while they were trying to get answers from her, I was trying to get answers from God. What could she possibly have done to deserve something like this? Or was it I who was the one being punished? Was I just another Job, a loyal follower subjected to unnecessary cruelty just to test my faith? I couldn’t bear to stay in the room any longer, left, and stood outside. I started to cry. The doctor outside tried to reassure me, tried to tell me that he knew how hard it was to watch my daughter in there like that. He said that she needed me, needed me to tell her it was going to be all right. I knew he was right, but how was I going to tell my daughter everything was going to be all right now. I had no idea what to say. I began to think back to the last time I had felt this helpless. It was the day my late wife, Abigail, and I went to see the fertility doctor. I remember that day because of the smell of lilacs in the doctor’s office. Everything that day seemed so perfect, too perfect. We had been trying for over a year to have a baby and were not succeeding. We tried all the home remedies we had heard, but none worked. Finally, we needed real answers. We got one. Abigail wasn’t able to have any children. The doctor rambled on about something to do with her uterus. I don’t remember what it was. I didn’t care. All I could think about that day was how it nearly killed my wife; all I could think about was how helpless I felt. I didn’t think I would ever feel that hopeless again. Fate, it seems, has a funny way of making us eat our words. Nature Versus Nurture: Episode 1 "Breaking Point" June 2010 The spring semester was over and Angela was looking forward to spending the summer with boyfriend, Harvey. They had been dating since the start of the fall semester and the relationship was getting serious. Angela realized this and finally felt comfortable enough to tell her father about Harvey. Harvey was as good a person as any man would tolerate dating their daughter. He was a pretty good student. In fact, he was first in his class. He worked part time in a pharmacy after classes as a stock clerk. Like Angela, he was an only child and he lived with only one parent. He and his mother had been living alone ever since his dad packed his bags and went on a trip. He never came back. There was no accident, or shooting on the six o’clock news. He just never came back. If anyone knew what it was like to grow up with only parent, Angela knew. She knew how Harvey felt and it made sense that the two would find each other. Harvey made every effort not to look up from his steak, meticulously cutting the meat as if he were performing some kind of religious sacrifice. Out of the corner of his eye, he could see Angela’s father, Wolfgang, giving him a cold stare. It seemed to Harvey that everything he did was judged. “Dad,” Angela muttered under breath. Her dad was always this overwhelming whenever she had guys visiting. Those guys were usually just friends. She knew it was different this time, different because Harvey was not just a friend. “Stock boy is it?” Wolfgang asked. Harvey looked up from the steak he had cut into a dozen pieces. “Yeah.” “What kind of a future is that?” Wolfgang asked, his voice stern. “Dad, please.” “It ain’t no kind of future, sir. I’m just doing it until I finish college. It pays the bills.” “You know what else pays the bills?” Wolfgang asked, taking a sip of his beer. “A real job. A real blue collar, get dirt under your finger nails kind of job.” “That’s what I aim to do, sir.” Harvey replied, his left hand was visibly shaking and he quickly ducked it under the table. “Harvey’s in the medical program at school,” Angela quickly interrupted. “First in his class, too.” If that was meant to impress her father, it failed. “So you’re going to be looking at people’s bodies and telling them what’s wrong with them?” “Hopefully,” Harvey said, quickly taking a sip of his soda to avoid answering any more questions. “So is there anything wrong with my daughter?” “Dad!” Angela cried out. “What the hell are you trying to do?” “Your friend wants to become a doctor; I’m merely helping him out.” Wolfgang replied. He turned to Harvey, who was making every effort to avoid eye contact. “Pass me the A1 sauce, would you.” “Yes, sir.” Harvey said, and reached for the bottle. He handed the bottle over to Wolfgang’s outstretched hand. But instead of grabbing the bottle, Wolfgang grasped Harvey’s hand as well. He held both firmly. Angela’s heart was beginning to race. “Uh, sir.” Harvey said. “Try to take the bottle away, son” “Dad, please stop this,” Angela said. “It’s all right,” Wolfgang said. “Harvey knows I’m just having some fun with him. Go ahead, take the bottle away.” Harvey looked to Angela for answers. After an awkward moment he made an effort to free his hand from the grasp. It wasn’t much of an effort and he knew that Wolfgang saw it as well. And he knew at that moment that he just made things worse. So, he pulled back with all strength to free his hand. But the muscles of a medical student who works part time as a stock clerk in a pharmacy store were no match for the muscles of a professional wrestler with a twenty year career. Wolfgang finally let go and Harvey nearly threw the bottle behind him from the left over momentum. “How can I expect you to take care of my daughter, when you can’t even take a bottle away from an old man like me?” Harvey didn’t say anything. He felt like he should have defended himself, said something. But it was Angela instead who came to his rescue. She stood up from her chair and walked over to Harvey. “We gotta go,” she told him. “Where are you going?” Wolfgang asked. Angela ignored him. She was so furious with the way her father had been behaving; she knew that if she said anything it was most likely going to be something she would later regret. So, she did the next best thing: kept silent. Harvey excused himself from the table and walked Angela to the front door. Angela whispered something into Harvey’s ear, but they were too far away for Wolfgang to understand what she was saying. “Angela, where are you going all of a sudden?” Wolfgang asked. “I’m just going out dad,” Angela replied, standing at the doorway. “Unlike you, I know how to treat my guests.” Wolfgang started to say something, but Angela had already left the house and Harvey was outside having forgotten his coat. Harvey opened the door to his Civic for her. He took a deep breath and looked back at the house before getting in the car. # Harvey lived only ten minutes away in Elmhurst, Queens. They did not talk much during the drive over, with the exception of Angela apologizing for her father. Harvey said he understood and they both knew he was telling the truth. Like Angela, he was an only child with one parent. His mother was just as protective and intimidating as Wolfgang was when he first introduced Angela to her. They arrived at the house at eight that night. The late summer sun had finally begun to set in the sky, and the night was well on its way. The lights were off in the house, which was a curious thing to Harvey. He never knew his mother to go out without calling him first. “I think it went pretty well,” Harvey said in a voice that was meant to suggest he was trying to make light of the situation. “He’s just being a jerk.” “You mean protective?” Harvey suggested as they walked to the front door of his house. “Oh, so now you’re taking his side?” Harvey laughed a bit. “No, I’m just trying to make everyone happy here.” Angela smiled. It was something that she always loved about Harvey. He was selfless to the extreme. He was always thinking of other people first before himself. However, she sometimes wished that Harvey would be a little selfish. “But he’s right about one thing,” Harvey said, digging the keys out of his jeans pocket. “What’s that?” “How can I take care of you when I’m not that strong?” Angela scoffed. “You know I don’t care about that shit. I’m not the kind of girl that wants to date a steroid freak. Besides, we both know that when it really matters you’ll look out for me.” “That’s right,” Harvey agreed. “I’ll take care of you when it really matters.” Harvey tried to turn the key, but couldn’t. He tried again before realizing that the lock was already opened. “That’s weird,” he thought out loud. “The door’s open.” They stepped inside the house. Harvey locked the door behind them. Even though it was still relatively bright outside because of the clear sky and the street lights, the inside of the house was very dark because all the shades had been drawn. The light switch to the living room was just beyond the chair, which was a chore to walk around with in the darkness. “I got the light,” Harvey said and turned on the light. As soon as he turned on the light, Angela screamed. But her screams were quickly muffled by a gloved hand covering her mouth. Harvey started for Angela before a thick python of an arm grasped around his neck. Harvey felt the cold sting of a revolver pressed against the back of his head. “Shut up,” the masked intruder repeated over and over to him. “I swear I will blow your shit if you try anything funny.” “Just take whatever you want,” Harvey replied in a soft voice. “The fuck you think you is nigga,” the masked intruder replied. “Shut the fuck up.” He looked at his accomplice who was holding onto Angela and muffling her screaming. “Take that bitch down stairs with the other bitch.” That other bitch. Harvey’s mother! “Where’s my mom?” “Didn’t I tell you to shut the fuck up?” He slammed the handle of the revolver against Harvey’s head. Harvey dropped to the floor, blood flowing from the gash on the back of his head. The pain was a stinging sensation in his head. Before Harvey could react, he felt the impact of a size 14 boot kicking him in the ribs. “Get the fuck up, bitch” the man ordered him. “Go downstairs and if you try any shit, I swear to God I’m gonna fuckin’ pull this trigger on those two bitches down stairs.” Harvey felt incredibly angry and incredibly afraid at the same time. He never once thought that this would ever happen to him. When he got down to the basement, things got much worse. Angela was crying, her sobbing being censored by a gloved hand. Harvey was relieved to see that she had not been physically harmed. Then he saw what she was crying about. In the middle of the basement on a bare mattress was his mother. She was laying there naked with bruises all over her body. There were a few blood stains on the mattress. “Mom!” He cried out, and tried to run towards her. His assailant was very strong, however, and yanked him back. He forced Harvey down to his knees, and kept a firm choke hold on him. He wasn’t trying to suffocate Harvey, just making sure that Harvey would remain sedated, weak. On the other side of the room, opposite where his mother lay, were three more masked assailants. But unlike the two who had grabbed Harvey and Angela upstairs, these three were not wearing any pants. Harvey could not look away from his mother. He felt his fists clenching. One of the three masked men on the other side saw this and laughed. “Skinny bitch thinks he’s Jet Li or some shit,” he laughed. The two others joined him. “How the fuck this faggot get a girl like that.” “She’s probably his sister, dawg.” The man who held a gun to his head spoke up. Harvey guessed this guy was the leader of the group. “You think you gonna do something, bitch?” “No,” Harvey stammered. “Dat’s right you ain’t,” he said. “You missed a damn good show, dawg.” “Best pussy I had in a while.” “You know what?” The leader thought out loud. “We owe this son here a good show. You guys think you can go again?” The three of them nodded. Everyone in the room knew what was going to happen next. Harvey was never much of a catholic, but in that imoment he instantly became the most devote catholic he ever knew. He prayed silently for a miracle. There was none. The man who was restraining Angela lifted her up and walked her over to the mattress. Angela tried desperately to free herself from his grip. The three other men rolled Harvey’s mat off the mattress like she was a heap of trash. They pinned Angela to the mattress, holding her arms and feet while ripping her clothes off. “Don’t do this please,” Harvey sobbed. “Oh it’s already been done,” the leader replied. “My boys are ready to go, I can’t ask ‘em to stop halfway, son. Just the enjoy the show.” Every time Harvey tried to look away from Angela being raped repeatedly by her four assailants, the leader hit him in the head with the handle of his revolver. He kept Harvey’s head aimed straight at the scene, making sure he saw every moment of it. Angela was no longer screaming or crying. She was no longer even trying to fend her attackers off. She just lay there, having accepted her fate. # “What the hell happened, detective?” Wolfgang demanded to know. He was furious and his eyes were red and swollen. Detective McKenzie was a twenty year veteran who had literally seen everything there was to see in this job. But no matter how many times you walk into a scene like that, it still gets to you. McKenzie realized a long time ago that it’s not about getting used to it; it’s about looking past it. “We’re not entirely sure,” Detective McKenzie said. “The woman and the young lady aren’t ready to be questioned. They’re still being treated by the doctors. But according to the young man, the door was already opened when they got home. He said he thought it was weird that the lights were off and the door was open but at the time, he didn’t think anything was wrong.” Wolfgang looked like he wanted to kill someone. “That fucking idiot. How the fuck can he not know something is wrong when he comes home and finds his door unlocked?” “Sir,” Detective McKenzie said. “There was no way anyone could have known. We checked the locks and didn’t find any signs of forced entry. It’s possible the mother let them in thinking they were someone else. But we know for sure until we’re able to speak to her.” At that moment, Harvey came out of the room with the doctor and two nurses. Harvey had his head bandaged and looked weak and pale, a sure confirmation that Wolfgang was right about him. He was too weak and too fragile to take care of his daughter. “This is your damn fault,” Wolfgang said. “If she wasn’t with you, none of this would have happened.” “I’m sorry.” “Sorry?” Wolfgang said. “What the hell you think being sorry is going to do?” Wolfgang had never felt so angry in his life. He wanted someone to pay for what happened to his daughter and that someone was going to Harvey. Wolfgang punched him in the face and before anyone could react, Wolfgang was on top of him landing blow after blow. Detective McKenzie ordered his police officers to restrain Wolfgang. But he did not make an arrest. He apologized to Harvey and told him that he was done questioning with him and that he could leave if he wanted to. Harvey declined, saying he wanted to make sure Angela was all right. Another doctor came out from the room where both Angela and Harvey’s mom were being treated. “How is she?” Wolfgang asked the doctor. “Are you her husband?” “No I’m her father.” “The other lady isn’t-“ “I don’t know who she is,” Wolfgang explained. “I just want to know how my little girl is doing.” The doctor sighed. “Well, she’s doing all right considering what she went through. She suffered some internal damage, a few bruises, and some heavy anal tearing. We’ve taking blood tests and pregnancy tests. We will let you know the results when we have them.” “So she’s going to be all right?” “Physically, yes” the doctor answered. “As far as her mind goes, well I think it’s best if you talk to her yourself.” The doctor opened the door and let Wolfgang inside the room. “Is she able to answer any questions, doctor?” Detective McKenzie asked. “Yes, but I really think you should wait another day. She is very traumatized by the whole incident. I don’t think it’s a good idea.” “As inhumane as it sounds, doctor, I really need to get these answers from her while it’s still fresh in her memory.” The doctor nodded and let the detective inside the room. “What about my mother?” Harvey asked. “Is she going to be all right?” “I’m sorry.” “What does that mean?” Harvey asked, his voice breaking up. “If she had been taken here sooner…” “What does that have to do with anything? She’s here right now, isn’t she? You got all these doctors working can’t any of you figure this out?” “The injuries she sustained during the rapes are treatable. But it seems that during the incident she also sustained some heavy internal bleeding. She’s lost a lot of blood.” “What are you saying?” Harvey knew the answer already. “She’s on bypass right now. We can keep her on it for a few days longer, but until then there’s nothing more we can do.” “God,” Harvey wept. “I’m sorry.” Two Days Later Wolfgang stood on the podium wearing his Sentinel mask. Today he wasn’t Wolfgang Moriarty; he was The Sentinel. He took a deep breathe and waited for the reporters and fans to quiet down a bit. In his mind, he imagined what questions they would ask him. It was he who arranged this press conference in the first place. He had some pretty big news to announce. There were many rumors afloat as to what exactly the big news was. The Sentinel knew that they would probably ask about him avoiding GTT7 and if he had any plans for any future events or for signing with any of the PTC federations. The Sentinel was going to announce his future. “I ordered this press conference because I’ve made a decision that quite frankly is the easiest decision I’ve ever made in my life.” Dozens of cameras flashed in the room as people began to whisper amongst themselves. “I know there are those of you who have been wondering about my absence from the latest GTT and from the wrestling business in general. I don’t have any real reasons to give you. I’m not hurt, or hiding any kind of illness that prevents me from doing this. I don’t have any reasons to explain my absences, but I do have an announcement for the future.” The Sentinel took a deep breath. “Two days ago something happened that made me realized that I was putting my attention on the wrong thing: Wrestling. As of this moment, I announce my retirement from wrestling.” The room immediately became abuzz with reporters all asking questions simultaneously. “I am a family man and I need to be where I am needed most: at home.” With that, the Sentinel put down the microphone and walked off the podium. He wasn’t going to answer any questions. As far as he was concerned, he was done with the wrestling business. October 2010 As the months passed, both Harvey and Angela had difficulty resuming their normal lifestyles. Angela never spoke to anyone – not even her dad or Harvey – only acknowledging people with a nod or a headshake. She was very defensive about being touched, even by Harvey, and would sometimes go into a screaming fit. It did not take long for her relationship with Harvey to start breaking apart. However, it had more to do with Harvey’s inability to move on. Harvey felt guilty. In his guilt, he became very moody and apprehensive. He was no longer the selfless, caring medical student that Angela knew. He had become somewhat of a loner. He dropped out of school and quit his part time job at the pharmacy. Angela sensed Harvey distancing himself away from others and thought that all he needed was some time alone. If she knew what Harvey was really experiencing, she would have interfered sooner. Harvey came to the realization that the only thing that matters is survival and only the strongest survive. Harvey started going to the gym on a regular basis to achieve this goal. At first, it was nothing more than trying to vent his anger and finding a way to cope or to make amends. Eventually, it turned into something more intense. Harvey wasn’t just satisfied with getting stronger, and building muscle, and gaining more weight. He needed to feel the same thing those masked men must have felt that day: he wanted to feel in control. So he would often get in fights just for the sole purpose of feeling the thrill of overpowering another man. Eventually, Harvey heard rumors that the store manager of the 7 Eleven on Woodside, Queens ran an underground fighting organization. Anyone who thought they were anyone went there and did nothing but fight. It took only a week for Harvey to earn the manager’s trust. Harvey thought that have a consistent pool of men to fight would help satisfy him, help quench his thirst for violence. He was wrong. If anything, it had an adverse affect on him, and Harvey was become an addict. He resorted back to getting into fights with random strangers. It was never about winning or losing with those fights. That is until the police got involved. Detective McKenzie stood outside the bars looking at Harvey inside the holding cell. Harvey looked like a completely different person than the last time he saw him in that hospital hallway. His nose seemed wider and set a bit higher, the result of repeated breaks and surgery to instill a metal plate. The area around his eyes was darker than the rest of his face because of constant bruising. He was no longer the skinny kid in the hallway. “Your bail’s been posted,” McKenzie said, opening the door. Harvey stood up without saying a word and walked outside the holding cell. Outside, McKenzie grabbed him by the arm. McKenzie said, “I know it’s tough for you, but you have to let us handle the situation.” Harvey looked him dead in the eye. “No, you have no idea how it is.” “If you keep going down this road, it’s only going to end in a bad way.” “I’ve already hit rock bottom, detective. There ain’t no where to go but up.” Harvey walked to the front under the escort of McKenzie and saw that the person who had posted his bail was Angela. On any other day (especially before June), he would have told her how beautiful she looked in her blue jeans. Now she was nothing more than a reminder of his inadequacy to protect her. “What happened?” she asked, even though she knew the answer. “Nothing. Just got into a fight is all.” Angela shook her head. “It’s not going to change a thing you know.” “What?” “The stuff you are doing to your body, quitting school and your job, getting into fight with strangers. None of this is going to change anything.” “If I do nothing, nothing changes. If I do something, then something changes. I didn’t realize that before. I thought I could just sleepwalk through life. But that ain’t how life works.” “And what’s going to happen next?” Harvey didn’t respond. He walked out of the precinct leaving Angela to wonder just how much he had truly changed. Early November 2010 The Sentinel would enjoy retirement more if it were not for his daughter’s psychological health or the constant badgering of wrestling organizations begging him to a return. Even the Prime Time Central approached him about a return to the Infinite Gauntlet. The Sentinel was retired and he was staying that way. The truth is he had been thinking of retiring for a while now, but like any true competitor, the urge for a new challenge was always too much. Wolfgang, as the Sentinel is named, was tossing his dirty laundry, including the Sentinel outfit, into the basket when Angela walked into the bedroom. "You doing all right, today?" He asked her. Angela gave a weak smile and nodded. "What's wrong?" "Nothing's wrong," Angela said, sitting on the bed. "It's just that I'm worried about Harvey." Wolfgang sighed. When he saw that Harvey was not coming around the house lately, he assumed the best - that they had broken up. Her concern now was an obvious indicator that it was not the case. "I thought you two broke him, hadn't been seeing him here lately." "That's the problem," Angela explained. "He's been out fighting, and doing God-knows-what to his body. He's even bigger than you now. He's just been so obsessed with fighting, I thought maybe you could talk to him with you being a wrestler and all." "Former wrestler," Wolfgang corrected her. "I don't think that's a good idea. Asking me to talk to him about violence and fighting is like asking a cocaine addict to talk to a heroine addict about getting better." "Don't you have any words of wisdoms, or tales of despair to scare him off?" Wolfgang put his hand gently on her shoulder. "People who are no longer concerned with their own safety, like Harvey seems to be, no longer care for any consequences. My advice is to stay away from him." "I can't do that," Angela said, walking towards the door. She stopped and looked back. "He's coming over tonight. I hope you change your mind." Wolfgang wasn't going to change his mind and both he and Angela knew that. Wolfgang picked up the laundry basket and headed down to the basement. # Harvey paced back and forth inside Angela's room. The door was deliberately kept open on the request of her father. Angela sat on the edge of the bed and looked at Harvey. "I don't know how much longer I can do this," Harvey said, finally. Angela nodded. "That's good. At least your acknowledging it." Harvey stopped and looked over at her. His body still twitched and Angela saw this. "You don't get it. I mean I can't go on without fighting. You know what happened after you bailed me from jail?" "What happened?" Harvey though quickly about how best to explain to Angela that he was involved in an underground fighting ring. "The gym I was going to told me I couldn't be there anymore. They said it was because I was becoming too dangerous to myself and to everyone else. Can you believe that shit?" "I...well..." "And I said fuck you, I'll go somewhere else. And you know what happened next?" "What?" "There is no fucking other place. It's like they got some damn network and they're posting my picture on their fucking facebook page, or tweeting my name out there telling them 'hey don't sign this sonofabitch'." "Maybe it's for the best." Harvey let out a small laugh. It was not a kind gentle laugh, but the kind that instills fear. "I don't expect you to understand. Anyway, I even went to that place the Prime Time Central. You heard of 'em?" "No," Angela lied. "Who are they?" "Ah, well they're this big fucking organization that oversees a few wrestling organizations or some shit. Anyways, one of them is called PRIME, and they're supposed to be having this open invitational tournament at the end of the month. So I take my ass down to their headquarters, fill out the application, and the quack head doctor tells me I'm fucking unfit to wrestle there. Says my head ain't right, that I look like a damn crack addict." "Now do you see what I've been telling you? What everyone has been telling you?" Harvey shook his head and gave a sinister smile. "The doctor says I could try again if I could prove I'm mentally fit for it. So I figure all I gotta do is convince the doctor I'm great, just pretend to be someone else." "Oh God, you are fucking hopeless." Angela said. "I wanted you to come here because I thought maybe I could talk some sense into you." "And I thought I came prepared with condoms and shit." Angela was stunned by the remark. "Get the fuck out. Now." "You still got my coat? I want it now." "I don't know. It's probably in the basement in one of the laundry baskets. Check there." One Week Before The Jewel in the Crown Opening Round Match "I must say it is a pleasure having you compete this year, Mr. Sentinel." Doctor Troy said. "It's a pleasure to be here," The Sentinel replied, slouching on the chair. Doctor Troy was the last obstacle for any would be entrant to the JITC, the so called psychology doctor. He said, "The field is very competitive this year. PRIME is absolutely stoked to be having such high profile, and skilled, competitors fighting this year. The reigning GTT champion, Desade, will be competing. I'm sure you know more about her than I do." The Sentinel laughed. "Oh, don't give me too much credit. I'm just coming off retirement you know. Yeah, I've heard of Desade. I'm really looking forward to all of this." Doctor Troy finished writing off the last of his recommendations and observations onto the form and handed them to the Sentinel. "Well, everything is absolutely perfect here. If you would just sign at the bottom, you'll be good to go next week when the opening round starts." The Sentinel took the clipboard and borrowed a pen. While he signed 'The Sentinel', the doctor asked him why he decided to come out of retirement. "The thirst. It just became too much." Two Days Before Jewel in the Crown Opening Match The Sentinel stood in front of the bathroom mirror, looking into his reflection intensely. "The thirst," he said. Harvey pulled off his mask. "It just became too much."
