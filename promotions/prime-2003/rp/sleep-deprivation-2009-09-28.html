Sleep Deprivation

28 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/28/09 11:13 p.m. A book signing, two local radio interviews and a quick dinner at the hotel restaurant. All in all, a relatively light day on the road for Troy Douglas. Of course, his dragging feet and slumped shoulders as he made his way out of the elevator and into the seventh floor hallway at the Doubletree told another story entirely. Easy schedule aside, Troy was at the end of his rope. He was nearing the end of yet another marathon PRIME tour, and with the book release and the requisite press and public appearances taking up almost any free moment not spent in a plane or navigating the Midwest's never-ending web of highways, his eyelids were exerting greater gravitational pull with each passing moment. So, his Nikes barely came up off the plush carpet as he made the quick journey to room 711 and delved into his right pocket in search of his key card. It took a good 15 seconds of fruitless poking around before he realized that the key was, in fact, already in his left hand. Welcome to Troy Douglas' night. Finally, after all the fumbling and absentminded follies, Troy swiped the card through the slot and pulled the doorknob. Only to have the door remain resolutely shut. Another swipe, same result. One more, no change. There's an old saying that the definition of insanity is doing the same thing over and over again while expecting a different result. As Troy Douglas furiously slid his card through the slot time after time, hoping to see the plush, king-sized bed on the other side of the door but instead getting nothing but a blinking red light on the card reader and a stubbornly locked door, his head shot could've been thrown up next to that definition in Webster's next unabridged edition. He was nearly resigned to a night of turning the carpet into his mattress and the door into his pillow before, at long last, he looked up. This was room 713. A palm to the face and a quick shuffle to his left later, Troy was finally in his room and staring at his quarry: a freshly-made bed with four squashy pillows adorned by the requisite mint. The candy was quickly discarded as Troy, shoes, jeans and all, whipped his 6'5", 260 pound frame down onto the bed, not even bothering to pull a blanket over him. Well, at least he'd get to bed early. ***** 1:21 a.m. Unfortunately, eight hours in the sack was little more than a pipe dream. Although, Troy would be happy for any kind of dream right now. Because for all his compounding fatigue, his eyes flatly refused to stay shut for more than a couple of seconds. It's not like he wasn't used to insomnia. He was, likely far more so than most people. To be truthful, he'd lost more sleep in more beds in more hotels criss-crossing the planet than he cared to think about. He'd lost sleep for every different reason. Pain. Fear. Anxiety. Even hallucinations. You name it, it had probably cost Troy Douglas more than a few hours of shut-eye in the past decade. This wasn't any of those things. This wasn't a recurring problem or an old burr popping up to aggravate him once again. This was his brain fighting against his body for no particular reason, and if it weren't for some exceptional self control, his left shoe would probably have been embedded into the flat screen television 15 minutes ago. It was the worst of all worlds. He couldn't sleep, but he was far too tired to do little more than reach over to the nightstand, grab his iPhone and dial the first listed name. He was hardly shocked when Samantha Wade didn't pick up. Long distance relationship aside, he knew she wasn't the kind of night owl who waited by their phone well after two in the morning. Still, he mustered up whatever words he could. Hell, it was something to fill this void of endless insomnia with something other than another infomercial starring the late Billy Mays. "Hey, Sam," he started, his voice crackling as he barely managed to creak the words through his tired vocal chords. "I know it's like 2:30 back there, so you've probably been asleep for a couple hours right now, but, uh, I'm obviously not, and if I didn't do something, I was going to start seeing what heavy objects I could smash against my head to remedy the situation. I sent you a plane ticket for Waco, so ... guess I'll see you there. I'm gonna try and go to sleep again, okay? Love you. Bye." His heart a little lighter, Troy put the phone back next to the alarm clock and flopped his head back down onto his pillow. Still nothing. He was fighting a losing battle. He picked up the remote and clicked the TV back to life. Billy was selling OxiClean again. ***** 2:39 a.m. "With my Big City Slider Station..." ARGH Charlie Brown couldn't have said it better. ***** 3:43 a.m. At his wit's end, Troy finally hauled his wilting carcass out of bed and trundled over to his suitcase. Sleep didn't look like an option tonight, so he figured he might as well do something with his time. He'd picked up The Lost Symbol at the airport bookstore when he landed in Oklahoma City, and flipping through Dan Brown's latest piece of formulaic fiction seemed like a perfect way to kill some early morning hours. If he was lucky, it would knock him out straightaway. He haphazardly dug through what had been to that point some carefully folded shirts and pants to find the hardcover brick of a book, but stopped when his hand brushed across the other erstwhile item left in his luggage; a small velvet box that, prior to his digging around, had been encased in a larger, paper-filled box for protection. He didn't pull out that box just yet. It too, he'd see next in Waco, as would Samantha Wade. Still, he smiled as he fingered the box holding the ornate diamond ring, until his hand blindly bumped into the spine of the book. Too tired for any more whimsical fantasizing, he grabbed the book and stumbled back too bed. He'd need a strong cup of coffee in the morning, probably several orders of magnitude more than that just to get through the day. As the clock ticked past quarter to four, Troy Douglas didn't care. That was several hours from now, and he had plenty of time to go before sunrise came to Oklahoma City. He flipped back the cover, and started to read.
