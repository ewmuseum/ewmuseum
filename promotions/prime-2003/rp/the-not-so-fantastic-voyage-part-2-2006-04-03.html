The Not-So-Fantastic Voyage (Part 2)

3 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 3rd, 2006 Pre-ReVolution 90… A vintage - and overly glitchy -1970s shot of a plane taking off to Peter Gabriel’s “Big Time” is shown, before cutting to what appears to be a poorly cut-out cardboard airplane in front of a brick wall half-painted with blue sky and white puffy clouds; a paintbrush sits in a can of blue paint at the edge of the screen. All this is done, of course, to disguise the fact that Clyde spent all their earnings on sending ten hookers at a time to James’ hotel room every hour on the hour the previous night, just for the sake of being a dick, and now they have to travel to Japan by partially-deflated inner tube. The arm of a techie comes into view, pulling the paint can out of the shot, while ropes hooked onto the top of the plane raise it up and down. James and Clyde’s voices sound slightly distorted by static and poor recording quality as they come from somewhere behind the camera, presumably having been taped before the duo set off on their voyage to Japan. Guatemala, if the water turned rough. James’ voice is heard first, the assumed fear and surprise completely drained from it. Farwell: Darn it, Clyde. It is pretty turbulent up here. Clyde’s obviously reading from a script. Walkins: Not nearly as…tur…t-tri… The sound of pages flipping can be heard. Walkins: And, that’s the last time I ate chicken. More pages quickly turning, followed by James stammering along as he tries to follow Clyde’s lines. Farwell: That’s still not as bad as the time we were chickens! Complete silence engulfs the set, while the ropes lift the airplane a few more times. Nothing. Several seconds pass, then faked laughter from both men cuts through the silence. Farwell: Ha-ha-ha. That was real funny. Walkins: Hehehe…chickens… Clyde and James force laughter for a few seconds longer, before Clyde’s laughter turns real, and James coughs to a nervous stop. Nearly a week later, resulting in them no-showing ReVolution and subsequently getting fired from PRIME, but luckily remembering to use Clyde’s portable time machine that he made ‘the house’ fashion for him, and appearing in Japan at the exact moment their flight should have originally landed… Walkins: Wow, what a fun flight. Farwell: Yes…flight… James narrows his eyes and looks from side to side, while Clyde decides to reminisce about their travels. Walkins: What great times we had out on the sea. Farwell: I thought we were sticking to the plane story? Walkins: Yep, good old waters sure treated us well… Cut to Clyde and James sitting on a crude raft, laughing and splashing as they float along the ocean waters, the sun shining brightly behind them. In the next scene, James slaps Clyde in the head for leaving their supplies back on the beach, where a group of horny teenagers later defile themselves amidst an orgy of Evian and grilled cheese sandwiches with the crust cut off. As the sun reaches its midday peak, Clyde tries to stir some fun back into the trip, splashing a grumpy James in the face with salt water; James clutches his left eye, the contact lens in it forever ruined. A little later on, James sets to work on carving a spear from some broken tree branches that drifted by, staring a hole into Clyde the entire time. The spear finished, and the sun now ready to set, Clyde and James wearily lean back against the edges of the raft, both weakened by thirst and hunger. Suddenly, out of nowhere, a hungry shark with a six-pack of Coca Cola caught on each fin snatches Clyde from behind, trying to pull him down into the water as the Universal Champion kicks and screams in an attempt to get away. James picks up the spear, and, depth perception skewed by the loss of sight in one eye, misses Clyde and hits the shark right in the face. Darkness overtakes the waters, and Clyde and James both chew on a piece of the shark as its tail still flips in the air, spear still through its head, the brothers suspiciously eyeing each other from opposite ends of the raft. Back to The Forsaken. Walkins: I’m feeling lucky tonight, James. No matter who the Six-Hundred-And-Fifty-Eight Dollar Man throws at me, I’ll be ready for them! And, you’ll easily win that Playgirl Fantasy Camp thing. I mean, who else is there? Damien Cruz? Don’t make me laugh. Clyde and James both share a laugh, but James stops sooner than Clyde, realizing that a) he lost to Cruz before, and b) the only time anyone has any faith in him at all is when he’s the lesser of all evils to win. Plus, the whole Playgirl thing. The more important half of The Forsaken goes from laughter to song, his positive demeanor never faltering as they near the gates of Japan. Walkins: Ain’t nothin’ gonna break my stride. Nobody’s gonna slow me down. Japan is blocked from the outside by a giant concrete wall that stretches as far as the eye can see to the south, and about nine or ten feet to the north, where it abruptly ends. No gate, no barbwire fencing, nothing. Just a free ride to Japanese soil. But, The Forsaken have never been apt to taking free rides, unless they were with strangers that offered them candy, and so they naturally head to the lone doorway found along the gate’s path. It’s cold, and it’s steel, but it still looks like freedom to two men who just battled sea monsters and pirates aboard their raft of sure doom. Just as they reach the door, a diminutive Asian man appears from within a small guard’s booth to the side, an oversized grin on his face. Standing in front of Clyde and James with his arms and legs bent almost exactly like Mr. Mime from Pokémon, the man hops back and forth on each foot, waving his arms in the air while singing a happy tune. Asian: Ha-ha-ah-ha-ha-ha-ha-ha, ha-ha-ah-ha-ha! Clyde claps his opens palms together like a little kid, giggling in his schoolgirl way. Walkins: Hehehe! Now, do it again for James! Asian: Solly. Onry one gleeting pew gloup pew day. Slightly dejected, but with a smile still on his face, Clyde shrugs his shoulders and keeps walking along, the Asian man letting him pass without problem. James, on the other hand, is stopped by a tiny hand on his chest, while the other points to a sign that reads: You Must Be This Happy To Enter Japan. And, there’s an arrow pointing to the happy little Asian. James huffs and rolls his eyes as Clyde disappears into the wilds of Japan, while he’s stuck there with an Asian that won’t let him pass. The corners of his mouth twitch a little, then he goes back to default angry James mode. Asian: Happiew. No change. Asian: Happiew! Still nothing. Asian: HAPPIEW!!! Not a thing, and James even seems to look angrier, if that’s somehow possible. The Asian stares at him with a wary glance, then gives in, stepping to the side in order to let him pass. Asian: Srightry bettew. The Asian man still smiles widely, happy as ever, as James passes through the door. Calling to him as the somewhat intelligent half of The Forsaken tries to find Clyde before he gets himself stabbed, the Asian man locks the door again after his fortune is complete. Asian: Ret youw smire be youw umbwella. As the door clicks shut, and James is out of earshot, the little Asian turns and heads back to the booth, mumbling to himself as he picks up the morning paper. Asian: Cocksuckew. At the same time, back in China… Once again, Shoufang Li kneels before his computer, while a woman about his age - presumably his wife - stands behind the counter of the little curio store. On the monitor, Chinese characters appear in an e-mail, and Shoufang whispers the words to himself in his native tongue, roughly translating to this: “Your great uncle has died suddenly. You, Shoufang Li, are the last remaining member of the Bloodline, and the new Head Master. It is your sworn duty to carry on the legacy that your ancestors created, and to pass it onto another when your time is at an end. I hope this letter finds you well, Head Master Shoufang, for you are the last of your kind.” A grin slowly spreads across his lips, as the bell attached to the front door of his shop rings in the background.
