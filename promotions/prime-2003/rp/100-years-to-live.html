Date: Various Backstage at UltraViolence 2008... One month past his eight-year anniversary in professional wrestling, Troy Douglas had finally hit his stride. He'd been broken, beaten and pushed down time and time again, but in the middle of one of America's most hallowed sports venues, in front of 50 thousand in attendance and millions watching live on pay per view, he did something he'd never done before. He had his first shot at a title, and he took it. And on this, what should have been one of the greatest days of his life, he had nobody to share the moment with. It sounds silly, it sounds cliched, but as much as he realized the gravity of what he'd done, there was still far too much of an empty feeling accompanying it. Should I be, maybe, I dunno, happier? After his brief celebration in the ring following his victory over Dusk, that was the only thought that kept racing through Troy's mind. That this should mean more. For the life of him, he didn't know why he felt so damn unfulfilled. He didn't know why, after eight years, he couldn't just let himself be happy with what he'd done. But, here he was, sitting in his temporary locker room in the outdoor staging area PRIME had set up at Wrigley Field, staring at the Intense Title and not quite knowing what to make of it. At the very least, the feeling of vindication he always thought would be there... Well, it just wasn't. Did I really earn this? Do I really deserve this? Where the hell do I go from here? As the questions raced through his mind at a speed most internet providers would envy, Troy barely moved a muscle. He just sat there, still in his gear, half-watching Logic and Killean Sirrajin on a monitor, half in a blind stupor. If you would've walked by at that exact moment, you probably would have been compelled to step in front of him, maybe wave your hand in front of his face, snap your fingers, do something to jolt him back to reality. And then, something finally did. Like had happened at so many seminal moments in his life, the phone just rang out of the blue. He waited a few moments, seemingly confused as to where this strange ringing was coming from, before he reached down into his bag and pulled out the phone, which had an all-too familiar set of initials on the display screen. B.C. He flipped the phone open and hesitated for a moment, not sure of what to say. "Hello, anybody there? You listening, mute boy?" Ben Castor, even over the phone, never lacked for an acerbic comment. "I'm here." "Good, needed to make sure you weren't passed out drunk in the streets of Chicago already." "Nah, that's more YOUR style. Remember?" "No, I was drunk at the time." "Point taken. That all you're calling for? There's kinda 50,000 loud people behind me, so if this is important, you might want to shout." "Nothing important," came Ben's voice from through the phone. "Just wanted to congratulate you on winning the shiny leather thing from the other large, oiled up men. Wow, when you think about it, that really sounds gay, doesn't it?" "Just a little." "So, what kind of celebration do you have planned? Gonna paint the town red, toss pennies off the top of Sears Tower and see if they impale passing stangers, that sort of stuff?" Troy still didn't know why, but celebration was still the last thing on his mind. "No plans. I'm not even really sure what I'm doing right now." "Oh, not this again." "What again?" "Your 'I feel guilty and alone' complex. It springs up every five to 10 months. Stop being so fucking weepy and just live your own damn life for once." "Geez, thanks for being so gentle with that." "You need it, moron. You work so goddamn hard at something people told you that you needed to stop doing years ago, and when you finally accomplish something, you can't even be happy. Worry about tomorrow when it comes. Let tonight be tonight, damn the fucking consequences." "I don't even know what that means." "It means you're so damn focused on the end of everything that you never sit back and enjoy the ride. Put it on cruise control for one night, and let the overbearing pressure of it all crush you some other time." Sometimes, it helped to have a conscience that would call you long-distance. They talked for a while, and when Troy hung up, he grabbed a spare t-shirt out of his bag, didn't even bother to take change out of the rest of his wrestling gear, left his locker room and walked straight out of the building. If C.P. Cantrell fined him for it, he didn't really give a damn. Tonight was his night, and he'd make due with whatever happened after when it actually happened. The road ahead might be bumpy, but at least for tonight, there was smooth sailing. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ July 7, 2008 New Orleans, Louisiana... "You're going to owe me big time for this, you know." Anthony Deschain wasn't quite expecting a visitor at 2:30 in the morning. Especially this visitor. Especially this visitor, soaked to the bone with water. Especially since it wasn't, you know, raining. "Fell into the pool," was all the explanation Anthony got from Troy Douglas at the start. The two went back to Troy's wrestling school days, when the 5-foot-7 redheaded Cajun was Jason Reed's only other pupil. A former All-State high school wrestler and a top level gymnast for much of his life, Anthony entered Reed's tutelage with considerably more talent and far more natural ring intuition than Troy. However, he lacked both the dedication to the business and the iron-headed stubbornness of his classmate, so while he could pull off a flawless Phoenix Splash two months in, he never really cared. The two had their first professional match together -- an absolutely wretched 10 minute draw on some godawful indy show in a ratty rec center near Scranton -- and after tagging together for a few shows around Pennsylvania and New Jersey, Anthony had enough and went back to New Orleans to join the family business. Eight years later, Troy was still plugging away -- though now on somewhat grander stages -- while Deschain headed one of Louisiana's most successful contracting firms. When the hurricane hit, he was one of the first people back to the city, spearheading the rebuilding effort no matter the cost. Turns out, some of his old partner's bullheadedness had rubbed off on him. Just took a while to manifest itself, is all. The pair had remained semi-close friends, but when Troy showed up at the door well past midnight and drenched with water, it represented the first face-to-face meeting between the two in almost three years. Exactly one week before Katrina, in fact, though that little tidbit really isn't relevant to our story. Their conversation, however, is. They'd been sitting in Tony's living room for a while, exchanging the kind of awkward small talk that was perfectly natural when a man inexplicably showed up at your door soaking wet at 2:30 in the morning. You know, bullshit. After the conversation had cycled several times back to the exact same point, Troy finally came forward with the reason he turned up at such a strange time, in such a strange way. "I was, uh, coming out from this bar a little after midnight, and my trusty sense of direction failed me." "Trusty sense of direction my ass. I'm still not convinced you could find the broad side of a barn if it was standing in front of you naked, dancing and singing 'look at me, look at me, I'm the broad side of a barn!'" "First, that was the worst joke in the long, sad history of bad jokes. Second, I have a FANTASTIC sense of direction when I know where the hell I am. Third, to continue the fucking story, what I thought was the door to a stairway in my hotel, uh, kinda wasn't." "What?" "Yep. Stumbled right into the deep end of the pool. Fried my cell phone, soaked all my cash, and I dropped my room key down the drain, so I can't get into the room for another three hours until someone with authorization to make a key card gets in to work." "Alrighty then." For awhile, the conversation didn't get all that much more exciting. A few old inside jokes, a few snappy comebacks, a good deal of reminiscing and chuckling. And then, Anthony asked the question that Troy seemed to run into every time he caught up with an old friend. "Why the hell are you still doing this?" Although he'd learned to deal with the question, hearing it from the man he broke into the business with caught Troy a little bit off-guard. "Excuse me?" "I'm not indicting you or anything, I just thought you'd be tired of all the bullshit by now. You always had more of a tolerance for it than I did, but it has to have caught up to you by now." His standard answer to the question had become so rehearsed that he almost started talking before thinking at all. But, something stopped him, and Troy decided to deviate from the norm for someone who wasn't accusing him of living out some stupid fantasy. "There are a lot of times when I wondered that too, Tony. I've bounced around a lot, I've been criticized and scrutinized and blocked by every form of bullshit you could find, and for a while I was pretty sure the only reason I still did it was because I was addicted to it. Somewhere along the line though -- and this was recently -- something changed." "What?" Not wanting to get into the details of an anesthesia-induced hallucination, he decided to go with the short version. But, as he did, he subconsciously reached down to touch the scar just above his recently repaired right knee. "I, uh, got a little perspective. Realized that I don't know how much time I've got left, and I might as well do what I want with that time. And, well, this is what I want to do, stupid as that sounds." "That's cliched crap, you know that." "Doesn't make it any less true."
