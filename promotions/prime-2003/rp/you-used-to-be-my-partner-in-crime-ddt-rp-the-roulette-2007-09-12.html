You Used To Be My Partner In Crime (DDT RP, the Roulette)

12 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 12, 2007 "Alright now...can you tell me who did this do you?" It's the same line...always the same line as I peer through the two way mirror that's shrouded in darkness to the illuminated room beyond, squinting my eyes and trying so hard to remember the events that landed me in this predicament. This mess. This nightmare. I remember the Roulette, cinching Gamble up in a chancery after an eternity of war...I remember my spaghetti arms lifting his dead weight, semi-conscious body up...I remember knowing that my intent was to create a head-shaped hole in the canvas via a brainbuster, one that would leave only one of us smiling, scar or not... I remember the searing pain emanating from the back of my skull, shooting to every nerve ending my body possessed. I remember the blackness. And then I remember coming-to in this room with the two way mirror, I remember the fuzziness and the attempt to focus, and this disembodied voice asking me to tell him who did this to me. "Did what?" I asked the very first time, clutching my head and trying to massage the pain away. "Took your title from you," was its reply. I groaned, struggling to survey the scene in front of me. They were lined up on the other side, everyone, from Gamble to Titan, all holding a Universal Title and all smirking like the fat cat who ate the canary. I blinked, trying to process this tidbit through my unrelenting headache. "I lost?" My inquiry was a whisper of disbelief. "You don't remember?" "No..." And then the voice persisted, urging me to give a name. But I didn't know. I couldn't see, not then and barely even now. So I guessed. I've been guessing for six nights now, each night the same scenario, with the same voice asking me the same question. Each night I gamble with my choices, some logical guesses (Sonny was my first) and others shots in the dark (Dusk was my most recent.) Each night, my guess is wrong. And even as I whittle the names down in this recurring nightmare, I know that this is a game that I just can't win. I'm not supposed to, because even if I guess right I'll have still lost. For six nights I've woken up in a cold sweat, unable to breathe and knowing that I'll never get back to sleep. The Universal Title is packed away in my bag in the corner of the room, safe for now, but I can't shake this haunting premonition of everything that DDT seemingly holds in store for me. I'll go about my day, knowing exactly what the night will bring. My foggy dreams will start off in another place with different people, but the rapid shift always happens and I find myself in this room looking at fewer and fewer people each time. Tonight, I'm down to two men. And it kills me to have to make a choice... ~**~**~**~**~ Wednesday, September 12, 2007. Casa di Troy: Northeast. 4:00PM "Of all the things I expected you to start off a conversation with, 'I think Isis is depressed' was not one of them, Tif." "Lin, it's bad. She mopes around my place all day long. She eats, but not enough, drinks when I practically force it on her. I really think she misses you and Ciro." Lindsay sighed as she shut her car door and engaged the alarm, then walked out of the garage with bags in hand and Bluetooth in her ear. The whir of the garage door shutting temporarily drowned out Tiffany Crenshaw's voice, but as the Queen of the Ring walked around her house to the front door her voice became clear once again. "I know you're not there a lot but I know you've got Frank's stepdaughter taking care of Ciro when you're on the road. Isis is miserable and it's killing me to keep her here," Tiffany said in the persuasive tone that wrangled many a promising student into Brent's dojo instead of one of its competitors. "You know I'd love to take her back, Tiff. I miss her. But if Chelsea can't handle both dogs then I don't want to force her. I'll call her later on when she gets out of her last grad class." "Alright, call me as soon as you know. And hey, I hear you signed the papers to get your own workout space?" "Just got back from the meeting," Lindsay smiled as she walked up her front steps and fumbled for her keys. "No room in the basement here so I snagged a building not too far from the house." "Nice." "Yeah, just got to fill it now. Listen, I'm about to walk in the door. You'll be hearing from me later." "You got it." The call went dead and Lindsay took the Bluetooth out of her ear, put her keys in the locks and entered the house. Quick fingers disengaged the alarm after shutting the door and she walked further in, dropping the Target bags onto the couch. "Ciro!" Troy called, puzzled that the dog didn't come running right up to her as she came inside. "Ciro?" Nothing. Lindsay walked halfway up the stairs and called again, to no discernible response. She knew he wasn't in the basement, and his leash was hanging by the door. "Ciro?" she said with a bit more urgency, quickly walking into the kitchen and not even bothering to consider the possibility of something rather awful going on and herself defenseless and guard-dogless. But there he was, laying on the floor with his stubby nub of a tail wagging, head looking off toward the door that lead to the deck. She didn't notice right away what he was looking at; instead, Lindsay bent down to scratch behind his ears. "There you are, silly mutt. Why didn't you come when I called?" The dog quickly rose to all fours, stepped closer to the door and whimpered. Troy looked up then, and her heart stopped. "Heya, Curls," his silky-smooth baritone floated through the air as he uttered his pet name for her. The sunlight poured through the door, outlining his hazy figure and bringing the sheen out in his jet black hair; hair that she hadn't run her fingers through or ruffled in nearly six years. She could feel her throat close and she struggled to both breathe and stop the waterfall that was building up in her eyes. Her voice was lost and she could feel her entire body start to quiver uncontrollably. When the air came back into her lungs, Lindsay finally found the words that had escaped her, but she could only utter one, softer than a summer breeze: "Jaime..." ~**~**~**~**~ Thisisn'thappeningthisisn'thappeningthisisn'thappening... "It is, Curls," Jaime Thomaselli, or the spirit of, said after a moment, his translucent figure moving very slowly toward her; just a couple of steps. "And I know what you're thinking right now." "You're gone, Jai....you're gone...." She was trying very hard not to blubber and flub over her words while steadying herself against a chair. "I know. That's how I know what you're thinking right now. It's funny how these things work..." Lindsay said nothing, in shock over both this vision and the fact that he...it...was making a joke at her expense. "Are you...is this some Ghost shit?" she accused, not caring that she scolded the apparated spirit of her dead best friend/former fiance and not realizing right away that she had somehow found her old self in this crazy scene. "You're making me play Demi? Or is this one of those things where we talk, then I suddenly wake up and wonder if it's all a dream?" "You know I wouldn't make you live those tacky cliches out. And you also know that you're very much awake right now." "Am I?" Lindsay decided to test one cliche out by pinching herself, hard. "OW! FUCK!" "See?" he smiled with fuzzy lips, and she nodded in response. Really, she wanted to run...run out of this house that she bought to get away from her demons and guilt, but her feet were glued to the hardwood floor and Ciro was now standing behind her, not letting her budge. "I don't have much time, Curls," he stated, voice remorseful. "This visit is something that's been long overdue, but the timing hadn't been right." "Timing? Jai...I'm still having trouble grasping what's in front of me...you, in front of me, and I'm talking to you...." "Don't talk, Lin. Just listen," he moved closer, still keeping fair distance between them. "You're amazing, you know that?" "What?" "Shh. You've managed to surpass every obstacle put in front of you, steamroll right over them and not look back. You've dealt with more then most will deal with in a lifetime, personally...professionally...and you've done so with your head held high. You constantly push yourself to be a little better all the time and nearly everyone secretly admires you for it, whether they want to admit it or not. But despite all this...despite everything...there's one thing you harbor in your heart that you refuse to let go of, one thing you let blacken and taint all the good that you've done for yourself and for others. "You've got to let me go, Lin. You need to stop blaming yourself for that accident and you need to really live. You haven't in almost six years, not that anyone who wasn't close to you would ever know. You put me on a pedestal I shouldn't be on and your happiness suffers because of it. Alaina sees it. Dan sees it. Your mother and father see it..." Niagara Falls ran down her cheeks, but she didn't care. "What happened wasn't your fault." "It was. I should have quit that company when they gave me that out. But I stubbornly stayed...and then they...that spot..." "You stayed because it was the right thing to do. Because you knew you had that obligation. Now, your obligation is to set me free...and set yourself free from me. Please." "It's not that easy, letting go of you," she murmured helplessly, using the back of her sleeve to wipe her tear-stained face. "I know. But you know you need to. And you know we'll see each other again." He moved beside her then. Lindsay felt his fingers along her cheek and she let out a ragged sigh. "I love you, Curls," he softly said, "Always will." In spite of herself, and the cliche, she replied, "Ditto." He moved past her to the darker living room, but Jaime's voice lingered for another moment. "Lin..." She turned and watched him begin to vanish. "Yeah." "Gamble and Snow..." he started in that way in which he always used to talk strategy when they were partners in crime, "they're hungry. Very hungry...moreso than you give them credit for. Don't let them feast on your gold." And then, he was gone... ~**~**~**~**~ "Alright now...can you tell me who did this do you?" My eyes are sharp, fiery lasers piercing through double-sided glass at Jason and Tony, their smirks setting off a snarl from my lips. I focus, eyes narrowing dangerously with the realization that the nightmare could end tonight if I just choose wisely. Wisely.... Wisely... Something behind Snow's mane of blond catches my eye and I move forward toward the glass to get a better look. Something...I didn't see before. Something...brown... "Me," I whisper. Then, louder, "ME!" Gamble and Snow whip their heads around. I see myself stepping out from behind Jason, his muscular body hiding my much leaner one, but my pulled-back hair unable to be hidden any longer. I watch my reflection walk toward the mirror, Universal Title strapped around my waist, and see my infectious smirk curl upwards. "Come on," I hear myself say. "You didn't think you'd go out like that...did you?"
