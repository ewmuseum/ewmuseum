Snow Day

6 December 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12-6-09 It was snowing. Austin Rogers nearly leaped out of bed when his mother told him that school had been cancelled for the day because of it, and he felt like writing a thank you note to Travis Herzog for making the prediction the night before. A smile the size of Texas spread from cheek to cheek as he tossed the covers to the side and hopped off his bed. It didn’t take long for him to hop from foot to foot over to his dresser and slip on some socks, because cold and tile floors do not go well together. He opened the bottom drawer and pulled out a pair of thermal underwear, quick to toss them over his shoulder and head toward the bathroom with a quickened pace. The outline for the start of an unplanned three day weekend began to form in his mind. He knew that the day would definitely start with spending a little time outside in the rare winter wonderland – miniature snowmen and snowball fights were a must. After he had his fill, or the snow melted, he would spend the rest of the day notching a couple of kills on Modern Warfare 2 before assassinating Italian Renaissance men on Assassin’s Creed 2. He hurried back into his room in his thermal underwear and a white tee shirt with John Paul II Saints in blue on the front – he played basketball for the school. He grabbed a pair of jeans that were balled up on the side of his bed and slipped them on. This day was going to be so much fun. ---- Dominic Carter kicked at the snow as he took another hit off the joint he and his crime partner Jarrell Jackson were passing back and forth. It was cold out, and the breath that escaped their lungs turned into frost as soon as it passed their lips. Dominic passed the spliff to Jarrell, then watched as he blew the smoke out into the air slowly. Jarrell brought it to his lips just as a black Lincoln Navigator pulled up to a stop in front of them. “There’s Sylk,” Jarrell motioned toward the SUV with a nod of his head. “Come on,” Dominic made his way over as the window started to roll down. The man they called Sylk was seated on the passenger side, a pair of dark sunglasses and a Indiana Hoosiers baseball cap left only the lower portion of his face exposed. He flashed a gold and diamond encrusted grille when he smiled at the pair, his name spelled out across the front in said diamonds. “What’s up, niggas?” Sylk leaned forward in the seat and poked his head out the window a bit. “Chilling out,” Dominic said with a chuckle. His hands were tucked in the pockets of his oversized Indiana University jacket, and the IU beanie only further showed a commonality between all of the men present. It was the logo of choice for the Ultimate Idols. “So what’s the word?” Jarrell passed the toke to Sylk, who took a hit before he passed it to the driver, who went by the name Kray Z on the streets. “Got a little business to take care of,” Sylk smiled, he possibly had more ice in his mouth than there was on the ground. “You nigga’s down or what?” “Shit, you know it,” Jarrell had his red Hoosiers cap tilted to the left with the brim flat out. He was the youngest of the group, but always the first to throw a punch if shit went down. “Hop in the back then, mother fuckers.” The two boys did as Sylk said, the man made more money in a week than their parents made in two years combined. They admired the man, wanted to become him. He just liked someone else doing his dirty work. ---- Tyrell Dawkins sits behind his desk flipping through a few pages of a file in his hand. As good as things had become in PRIME, he still had a business to run. Taxes would be due at the beginning of the new year, and there was no better time to get started on going through the books early. Especially on a day where the city is covered in a few inches of snow. “Hey, Mr. Dawkins, what’s up?” Tyrell turns to find a teenage boy standing in the doorway with a smile that could light up a room. “Nothing much, Jason,” Tyrell replied. “What are you doing here, shouldn’t you be out playing in the snow like the rest of the city?” “Nah,” Jason answered. “I figured I’d come in and get a workout. I have that fight coming up in a few weeks, and I want to be ready.” “Yeah, well, it never hurts to be ready.” “That’s what I was telling my brother, but he said I was stupid if I thought boxing was going to get my outta the ghetto.” “Don’t let him get to you, Jason,” Tyrell closed the folder and set it aside. “There are lots of people that have fought their way out of poverty, and don’t let anyone tell you otherwise.” “But he’s my brother, Mr. Dawkins.” “Sometimes it is the people that we love that hold us down the most.” “I guess you’re right.” “Speaking of your brother,” Tyrell folds his arms across his chest. “How is Jarrell doing these days, he hasn’t been by the gym in a while.” “He’s been spending a lot of time with Roach lately.” “Roach?” Tyrell’s eyebrows arch a bit. “Dominic Carter, they call him Roach.” “I couldn’t imagine why.” Truth be told, Tyrell knew exactly why. He found Dominic smoking marijuana in the alley behind the gym one night, and it was no secret that he hung around with a small time drug dealer the police couldn’t pin anything on. If Jarrell was hanging around with Dominic, then he was surely hanging around with the rest of the Ultimate Idols. “So I’m gonna go hit the heavy bag for a few rounds.” “Knock yourself out,” Tyrell motions toward him, giving him free reign of the gym. His mind, however, was on his brother - Jarrell. Hopefully he wasn’t doing anything stupid. ---- “GO! GO! GO!” The Lincoln skidded around the corner of the residential neighborhood, the snow causing it to slide sideways and nearly spin out. The sound of another set of tires screeching against the frozen over asphalt is heard not too long after. “They’re right behind us, Sylk!” “Don’t you think I know that, nigga!“ Sylk turned back from the front seat, then ducked his head when the sound of the back glass shattering echoed throughout the sport utility vehicle. “Faster, bit-” Sylk turned back to find Kray Z slumped over the steering wheel. The luxury SUV skidded out of control and jumped a curb, but only rolled so far before smashing against a red Toyota Corolla. Jarrell smashed his head against the back of the seat, while the airbags kept Sylk and Kray Z from crashing through the windows. Of course, with Kray Z it didn’t really matter since he was already dead. “Let’s go, Jay,” Sylk pushed the door open and stumbled out of the front seat. Jarrell could feel his head spinning, the impact jarring him more than he realized. He pushed open the door and fell out, the frozen slush sticking to his face as he pushed himself up off the ground. He could hear the tires of the car that was chasing them screech to a stop. “Come on!” He felt Sylk grab him by the arm, trying to help him to his feet. “Shit!” Sylk shoved him back down, and started firing the nine millimeter Glock he pulled out from under the seat when the deal he had set up went bad. ---- The sound of gunfire filled his ears, but Austin kept his calm and fired a few shots off of the AK-47 he equipped himself with because of the red dot laser sight. “Quit camping, pussy!” he heard his friend Randy McCall yell in his ear. Randy went by the call sign ‘Kr4T05247’ on the Playstation Network. “You’re just pissed that I have a better kill ratio than you,” he yelled back into the blue tooth headset he had plugged into his ear. The bass on the surround sound system in his room was turned all the way up, as he leaned back against the foot of his bed and stared up at the thirty six inch LCD television his parents bought him last Christmas. “Whatever, cheater.” He heard Ryan in his ear again. “Hey, you wanna go to Mickey D’s and grab some lunch in a bit?” “Yeah,” Austin replied as he opened fire on someone that walked by the door of the office he was camped out in. “I could eat me some nuggets and fries.” “Alright, stop by my house on the way.” “I will.” Oh, boys and their toys. ---- Houston - People in the Kingwood community of Houston are outraged over a gunfight between two rival gangs that turned deadly Friday afternoon. Four people are dead and two were rushed to the hospital with severe gunshot wounds. One of the fatalities was a young man named Austin Rogers, a resident of the Kingwood community that was caught in the crossfire. Police say that eyewitnesses place two more suspects at the scene, but managed to flee in a late model four door sedan before authorities arrived. Tyrell Dawkins shook his head as he placed the paper on his desk. Jason Jackson was seated across from him with streaks of tears on his cheeks. “How bad is he?” Tyrell asked the young man that inhaled deeply with each sob. “He got hit in the shoulder,” Jason said with his head down. “Doctors say he’s gonna be fine.” “Well that’s good, right?” Jason shook his head. “They want to try him for that white kid’s murder.” “What do you think I could do, Jay?” “I don’t know, Mr. Dawkins,” Jason looked up and had a fresh set of tears in his eyes. “It’s just that… Jarrell says he didn’t do it. That he didn’t even have a gun.” “Well then he needs to tell that to his attorney.” “They’re not gonna care,” Jason said. “Them white folks want someone to pay for that boy’s death and Jarrell’s the only nigger alive to pin it on.” “What about the other kid…” Tyrell waved his hand almost dismissively. “The other one that was hurt.” “He died overnight.” Tyrell wasn’t sure what he could do, doubted he could do anything at all, but he couldn’t say that. “I’ll see what I can do.” “Thanks, Mr. Dawkins.” Jason had a slight smile on his face, and Tyrell couldn’t help but know it wouldn’t be there for long.
