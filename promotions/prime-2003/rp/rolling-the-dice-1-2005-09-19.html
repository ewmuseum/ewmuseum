Rolling the Dice (1)

19 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Sept. 19, 2005 Popular culture paints the picture that addiction can be overcome. It happens all the time on movies and TV shows; a character gets hooked on booze or drugs, decides to kick, goes through rehab and seemingly is fine for the remainder of his days in the collective consciousness of mass media. They make it seem like people get over alcoholism like the flu; as if a rehab stint can cure a cocaine habit like a flu shot. You know it’s bullshit. You know it because every day, when you wake up, you say the same thing. “Not gonna drink today.” You know it because thoughts of doing a swanton bomb off the wagon float through your mind on a regular basis. Every time you see a beer commercial. Every time you see some rapper proudly displaying a refrigerator full of Krystal on MTV Cribs. Every time you walk through a bar. Every time you smell booze on someone’s breath. Every time you wake up sore the night after a match. And every time you suffer a tough loss. What do you do? What do you do when it’s been two years and 23 days since your last drink, you’ve just seen a chance to return to the bigtime slip through your fingers and the thirst is so strong? What do you do when it’s not just strong… but OVERWHELMING, a constant burn like someone’s scouring the inside of your throat with sandpaper? Quite simply, you taunt that thirst. Then, drown it in nacho cheese. It’s been nearly two weeks since you lost to Brandon Youngblood, and you’re indulging in one last bit of “coping” before your first-round match in the Jewel In The Crown Tournament. You sit at one of the corner tables at The Graduate, a sports bar within a stone’s throw of Ericsson Stadium and watch as the Panthers slug it out with the Patriots. That’s right, a bar. Your AA sponsor (assuming you had one) would be pitching a fit right now. Tempting the old demons. Considering all the beer and bourbon that passed through your gullet in bars just like this, it’s the equivalent of dangling naked 8-year-old boys in front of a thrice-convicted child molester who swears he’s “much better now.” But you don’t give in. “Anything else I can get for ya, hun?” The waitress chews her gum with enthusiasm as she stands before you, hip cocked provocatively. She’s wearing an authentic Jake Delhomme jersey; the Daisey Dukes, meanwhile, are most definitely NOT NFL regulation. Not that you’re complaining, of course. “Maybe at the end of the quarter,” you say, refocusing on the nearest TV screen. The Panthers are driving against the Patriots. The current score? Carolina 20, New England 10. Stephen Davis already has two short-yardage touchdowns, which in your opinion can barely compare to the half slab of ribs and ultimate nachos you consumed during the first half—good thing Madden’s not doing the game, he’d call that a “light snack.” The radio announcers getting piped in over the TV feed are wondering how the “genius” Bill Bellichick is going to counter Carolina. They make it sound like a foregone conclusion. “Nothing to drink? Maybe something a little harder?” “Nope, Coke is fine.” So fine, in fact, that you’re already on your fifth. Maybe the genius will call a timeout so you can go let the tide roll out. It takes a couple of plays before you realize the waitress is still standing there. “Ya know, you look kinda familiar…” You repress a sigh. You thought you’d done a good job this time. UNC hat pulled low, ratty T-shirt, cargo shorts, flip flops. You didn’t look any different than one of those seventh-year seniors you see scuffling around the Charlotte campus from time to time. “I, uh, was in here last week.” Carolina Panthers play-by-play man Mick Mixon: Gibson going for the subliminal suggestion. He’s banking on the fact this woman no doubt sees hundreds of customers such as himself every week… Carolina Panthers color commentator Eugene Robinson: There’s a good chance she’ll bite on the fake here. After all, she IS blonde. “Yeah, that must be it.” Mick: What a break for the veteran! “So, you a Delhomme fan?” Mick: See, another GREAT misdirection by Gibson. He’s trying to guide her down an alternate topic of conservation. Eugene: Yeah, he doesn’t want any recognition to sink in. Who knows how this waitress made the initial connection. Perhaps a boyfriend who watches PRIME… Mick: I thought hot waitresses never have boyfriends, they’re always too busy. Eugene: Sure they don’t, Mick. SUUUURE they don’t. Mick: Don’t you have a undercover cop to go solicit? Eugene: YOU BASTARD! “No,” she says, “Just what I’m wearing today. Last week I was in Julius Peppers’ jersey.” “Maybe that’s why he played like shit.” Mick: Waiting for the recognition… and… NO GOOD! Eugene: She knows nothing about football, Mick! Mick: Meanwhile Gibson has been eyeing the competition… and, wait… yes! Looks as if the second unit is gonna start warming up on the sidelines! *crowd roars* “So… um… I’ve got some more tables. You want anything right now?” “Naaa, I’ve tempted fate enough. But maybe I’ll buy YOU a drink—after your shift?” Eugene: Ooh, risky play call by Gibson… Mick: We’ll have to see how it works! “Maybe!” Eugene: That’s just too close to call, Mick! Mick: We’ll have to wait on the replay! Seven hours, seventeen minutes and five seconds later… “Enjoying your daiquiri?” “Mmhmm!” she almost squeaks in delight as she finishes the sip. The drink can’t be that good. Maybe she’s just glad her shift is over. You’re in a different bar now, not a big screen TV in sight. The waitress—Angie—doesn’t seem to mind. The surreptitious foot massage you’re giving can’t hurt. It’s the way to many a lady’s heart—especially a waitress. “You’ve been here all day…” “Yeah, I’m a football fan.” “And you were hoping for the chance I’d let you get some.” “That too.” “Seriously, though, who hangs out in a sports bar all day? And why?” “This is how I take risks.” She sits up and shoots a questioning look at you. “This… is risky?” “With my lifestyle, sure.” “I don’t quite follow.” “Ever try wrestling with a belly full of ribs and nachos?” Feet slide out of your lap. “That’s the LAMEST pick-up line I’ve ever heard…” “It’s not a line, I really am a wrestler.” “Mmhm, well I’m afraid you’ll be facing the bald-headed champ again tonight.” Oh, goody. Cause that went SO well last time. * * * * * “So did you?” “Did I what?” “You know…” The sound of her hand hitting her cheek with a series of quick, light slaps echoes through the connection thanks to the magic of cellular technology. “That’s none of your business…” “You did?!? EWWWWWW!” Amy Campbell’s cackling brings a smile to your face regardless. “You OK?” “Blargh… I guess.” “C’mon, Dave, what’s wrong? You can tell me…” “All right. Two weeks ago, I had my chance. My shot to get back on top of the game. I had Brandon Youngblood hurt and bleeding like… well, me. He was BEATEN, Amy. I knew it, he knew it and everyone in that arena knew it. His fucking wife wanted to end the match for Chrissake! And all I did was fuck it up… “How can I teach kids how to wrestle when I can’t win a match that was handed to me? And now I’ve got to face Walczak, AFTER him and Porkchop beat me at the pay-per-view. I feel like I’m thise close to some career-killing losing streak and I’m not sure why and I’m wondering if maybe I should just pack it in now before it gets any worse.” A sigh from the other end of the line. “Dave, you’ve taught me a lot about how to wrestle in the past few months. But I would never have blown that match with Brandon.” “Know why?” “Cause you’d have distracted him with your sumptuous breasts then pinned him?” “Smartass. It’s because I take risks to win, Dave.” “I take risks! I went to the air twice against Brandon and STILL got beat.” “Uh huh. And then what did you do when you got the upper hand?” “OK, maybe I got a little too passive.” “A little? Abdominal stretches don’t win titles, Dave! Not in this century!” “OK, OK, maybe so, but you can’t say I don’t take risks. I started my own wrestling school… and I used to drive drunk. A LOT.” “There’s a point of pride. Seriously, how can you expect to risk it all inside the ring and go balls-out, when you won’t live your life that way?” “Who says I don’t?” “Eating wings and nachos doesn’t qualify as living dangerously, Dave.” “Well maybe it should! Have you seen the saturated fat content in that processed cheese?” Another sigh. “Just think about it, Dave. Ever since you came back, you’ve been scared. It’s obvious. Scared about breaking your neck again…” What redheads don’t know won’t hurt them. “And scared about LIVING. So terrified about falling off the wagon or back into your old bad habits that you’ve just retreated from the world. I just think it’s time to start getting back out there again.” OK, maybe that one had a point. “Yeah. Yeah, I guess.” “Just think about it, Dave. Life’s too short, ya know?” “I guess. Listen, I better go. I’ve got a beginner’s class tomorrow bright and early.” “OK. See you at the show.” You hang up and walk to the bed, shedding clothes as you do. About two years ago you were staggering through life in an alcoholic haze, working crazy hours behind the scenes in OSW while performing in the ring. And now what? The booze was gone, the power was gone, and so, apparently, was your life. You roll back out of bed and slide a DVD into the TV in your bedroom—one of the new accoutrements to make your apartment slightly less shitty than it was before. The tape of a four-team TLC match plays. The night you and Clay Vendetta won tag team gold in OSW. You watch as Ronin tap an artery with some Singapore Cane shots, and Dave Gibson flies to and fro with reckless abandon. You watch as Gibson hooks Riptah in a Silencer—while the big man was climbing a ladder—and observe as the late Matt Register sends you both backwards through a table from an electric chair. It’s the old you. The one who’d do anything to succeed. The one who went balls out. It’s time to get reacquainted. It’s time to start taking more risks. It’s time to start rolling the dice.
