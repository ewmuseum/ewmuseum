Date: Today The wintery skies made for the perfect backdrop for the perfect evening. The sun was just setting in the west, dancing crimsons and oranges through the cloud bottoms as the light filtered through the water in the sky. Hey, is that a butterfly? Odd. Chet Worth and Peter Maxwell drove down the roads of Pennsylvania for the second time in as many weeks. Last time, the roads and scenery were covered by a thin blanket of snow, glistening in the moonlight like millions of diamonds fallen from the skies. This time, the snow was gone, leaving the winter scorched earth behind, naked trees, barely green grass and dismal images of lifeless void. I really should have bought more toothpaste. Damnit. Silence hung heavy in the car. We join the two just seconds after an argument over who was the better basketball player: Ron Harper or Danny Ferry. Everyone who knows anything about basketball knows the answer is Ron Harper, which is the side Worth took. But, in some bizarre, random, drunken logic, Maxwell thought Ferry was the more accomplished NBA player, thanks to becoming an NBA executive. Anyone seen my phone? I think I left it over there. As usual... Is that it? Its not? Well, where the hell is my phone? ...the two didn't see eye to eye on anything. ...the two didn't see eye to eye on anything. ...the two couldn't see eye to eye on anything. ...the two woldn't see eye to eye on anything. Anything. Anything. Everything. Nothing. Something. Somehow. Some way. One way. One day. One day, back in 1986, an eleven-year old Chet Worth rode his bike down Theola street. Back in those days, BMX bikes were all the rage, and Chet Worth had the best Huffy BMX money could buy. It was a Stu Thompson designed bike, a BMX rider no one really heard of, but, thought his bike was pretty cool. Chet knew all the tricks. He could bunny hop, and ride no handed. He could wheelie from his house to Old Man Witherspoon's place, an estimated four thousand miles, by his standards (about a block). He would have been able to spin the front forks, had his stupid mom not insisted that he keep the front reflector on. For hours at a time, Chet would be on that bike, trying to learn all the cool tricks. By the time he mastered riding on one side of the bike, standing on the pegs, it was all over. Some kid named Tony Hawk had started to make it big, and the guys that were riding bikes the week before on some dirt mounds at the construction site, were all hopping on their banana boards, and ollieing. He did what any other kid would have done, and parked the bike, and begged his mom and dad for a skateboard. They laughed and told him he'd hurt himself, and that he could stick with his bike, a death sentence for any hopes of this overweight, bespectacled kid had of becoming cool. No, he was to be tried and convicted in a mock court of his peers the next time he showed up on the corner on a bike, instead of a skateboard. Months later, at the end of the summer, Chet Worth finally mowed enough lawns to afford a skateboard. And he bought one about ten minutes after getting the last ten dollars into his hand. He practically sprinted to Jim's Bike Shop five blocks away. I know I could have taken my bike. Shut up. He looked carefully, and picked out a Powell Peralta board with Slimeball wheels, and really tight trucks. Chet walked out, put the board on the ground, and started to skate down the shopping center sidewalk. Did you find my phone yet? Once again, Chet had been saved from death by laughing squad. He'd appealed his sentence by saying he was going to work for his board, so that his parents would help pay for it, an impressive and noble feat to say the least. Most of his friends had stolen their boards from the rich kids, who didn't so much as bat an eyelash before replacing them. Chet felt empowered. It was almost as if he were gliding on air. The board and the tools cost him three hundred and ten dollars, all told, only one hundred of that being money from his parents. He rode that board home, and then down to the corner, where he expected to find his buddies skating in the parking lot. Oh. Found my phone. It was in my pocket. Oops. There was no one there. Chet skated around a little bit, waiting for someone on the block to notice him, but no one came. He skated down to his friend Rich's house, hoping to show off the board. Anyone for some ice cream? I could use some. You know what's good? Mint Chocolate chip. I love that shit. Man, a cone of that is like crack. Seriously. Especially when it's actual chips, and not chocolate peices. Sometimes, I collect the chips, and eat them at the end, instead of eating it with the ice cream. I don't know why. It's just kind of neat. What was I saying? Chet knocked on the door, and Rich's mom answered. A delightful and plump woman, Mrs. Williams was known through the neighborhood for her cookies and her loose integrity with the soda. Some parents only bought a two liter, and expected you and your friends to make it last the entire day. The Williams family didn't see anything wrong with guzzling the carbonated swill morning, noon and night, making the Williams house a popular gathering spot. 'Hi, Chet. Rich is in the basement. Want a Pepsi?' 'Yes, please. Thank you, Mrs. Williams.' Such a polite boy, Chet was. Man, I would fuck the shit out of Jennifer Anniston. He walked down the stairs to the basement, and saw Rich, Tony, Billy and Chris, his buddies from the block staring at the television in the basement, two of them with a white rectangle in their hands. 'Hey guys, I got my...' 'Shut it, Worth,' Billy said. 'We're busy.' Chet looked at the TV as a pixelated form jumped into the air, crashed his head into pixelated blocks, and jumped on pixelated creatures. They watched as each one moved left toward the side of the screen, the background moving along, bringing new challenges with it. And once again, Chet's skateboard was obsolete. Instantly. The boys had just discovered Nintento, and the next three weeks were spent in Rich's basement, playing Super Mario Brothers, and shooting innocent ducks out of the sky. Duck is a funny word. His skateboard, cool just ten minutes prior was never ridden again. It was useless. It was a relic. It was worthless. Chet was worthless. Chet was Chet Worth. Chet Worth. Chet Worth was always behind the times, and never up to date on what was cool. Chet Worth is and will always be a throwback to a time when things were different, even if it was just ten minutes ago. He's never used the internet, but, was thinking about buying a computer the other day. He heard through a few friends that there were people talking smack about him over the internet, and he wanted to tell them what's what. But, he didn't. And he probably wouldn't. The internet's just a fad. There were people there he didn't see eye to eye with. There were people there he didn't see eye to eye with. There were people there he couldn't see eye to eye with. There were people there he wound't see eye to eye with. People who couldn't agree on anything. People who wouldn't agree on anything. People who shouldn't agree on anything. People like Chet Worth and Peter Maxwell, who pulled into their hotel parking lot in Hershey, PA, still veiled in their silence. So, who wants some ice cream? Finally, Worth broke the silence. 'So. Lamen, Tsonda and I this week, huh?' 'What? Yeah. That's the idea, I guess.' 'Who made those teams?' 'Random draw I guess.' 'Oh. Well, it's a good draw. I mean, I can prove myself against a former Universal Champion.' 'Yeah. I guess you could. But, you won't.' 'What's that supposed to mean?' 'Dunno. You had Lamen beat last week, and couldn't put him away. What makes you think you could do it this week?' 'I ... have no idea.' 'Exactly. Just keep losing, Chet. It's what you're good at. I'm going to the bar. Please don't follow me. I'm planning on playing some pool, and hustling for a few dollars. I'd hate to lose since you have that effect on people.' Asshole.
