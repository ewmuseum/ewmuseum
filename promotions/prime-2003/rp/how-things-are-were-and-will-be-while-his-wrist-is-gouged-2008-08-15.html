How Things Are, Were and Will Be While His Wrist is Gouged

15 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 15, 2008 His home looked broken, though he was never (well, to be fair, rarely) there. He had been up for three days on some substance or another. Not that he was upset about it all. He was never upset about making a shitty decision. If he were honest with you, he'd say he's been making shitty decisions his entire life. And if you were honest with him, you'd agree. The problem these days is that no one was honest with Rhett anymore. Not even himself. The T.V. was on and projecting the only light in the entire house. This was the case most of the time. Locke couldn't find what he was looking for and for the first time in the few days he'd been home he turned the light in the living room on. Cockroaches scattered across the floor seeking to escape to whatever dark nook they could find. Laura, his married but drug-hooked lover, laid naked on the couch. He was certain she wouldn't make a peep. She never did unless she needed a fix, be it cocaine or a penis. Sometimes, Rhett felt sorry for her husband. But in the same second he would dismiss that notion. Sure, he was probably awake with the kids at home having to go to work in just a few hours. But that wasn't Rhett's problem. The poor bastard's name was Steve. Sure as shit, Steve was probably sweating by the phone, anxious to hear word that everything's okay and she'd be home in just a minute. Unlucky fellow. In years to come, his drunken speech to every lady he'd meet in a bar would turn into a spiel like, "You think you KNOW someone else well enough to marry them, and then you find out you don't know them at all." Steve loved Laura. Laura loved drugs. That's where Rhett came in. Truth be told, Rhett loved her too. She had a way of forcing you into it unwillingly. It was the way she carried herself. It was her sultry southern accent... Her playful nature. Though deep down he knew the relationship would never work he spent all the time he could around her. He'd wasted a few hours before staring at her, wondering if she knew that Rhett would kill a man for her. He wondered if she felt that strongly for him. Rhett doubted it. He figured in ten years when he stumbled into some crack den and saw her for the first time in a long time, she'd be the prettiest crack whore in the whole house. He was pessimistic, surely-- but in a world where people like Steve stay up all night taking care of kids that may or may not belong to him waiting on his wife who's too fucked up to be awake and come home... why bother with optimism? He found what he was looking for. It was tiny, black cardboard box of 500 individually wrapped box-cutter razor blades. There was something about the initial touch of the razor that sent a chill up Rhett's spine. The anxiety before the release was what made it so wonderful and he couldn't help but shiver as he walked to the kitchen. He had taken a single wrapped up blade out of the box of 500, though there were perhaps twenty five or so left. They had all served a purpose. Either to cut cocaine or something else. He glared through the rust at his reflection in his kitchen sink. Inside him from the time he was a child he felt what could only be described as an extreme self-hatred. A loathing so dark and twisted that it tended to overpower who he really was. There was a Rhett Locke inside there somewhere, who was alive, wide-eyed and embracing. A Rhett Locke whose favorite things were the sun, bright colors and picking flowers. He had taken medication after medication after medi-fucking-cation to help with the repugnance he held for himself. But he found self-medication was way more effective, at least temporarily. That's why cocaine was so prominant in his life. That's why he placed the blade to his wrist and slid it down three times. This was not a suicide. He knew where not to cut from experience and he always made sure to avoid this place on his skin. This was a release from the bitterness he had for himself. Whatever the feeling he couldn't handle was began drooling down the side of his arm like water in a creek into the sink. He smiled. His pigment shortaged skin now covered in a crimson mud. He dipped his finger in it and smeared it around on his flesh. Some people would suggest he needed serious psychological evaluation, siting he might be manic depressed-- or worse. But he had been there. Locked in there, even. For years. What he was doing for himself, right then, was the best therapy he had ever had. The wrestling fans in OSW touted his bravery. His "never give up" attitude. It was a mantra. A fucking anthem. Rhett. Never. Quit. But that was a lie. The truth was that he quit a long time ago. The reason he took all of those slams and bumps and chairs right against his cranium and kept pushing himself for a victory was that if Locke ever wanted anything in his entire fucking miserable life, it was the acceptance and adoration of others. To hear a crowd of thousands chanting his name right after he performed a bump in the ring... for people to address him in public and tell him how much they loved his match with whatever opponent... Fuck. That... that right there was better than any cocaine. It was better than any pussy. It was even better than the cuts he gave himself so frequently he wore black sleeves on his arms to hide them. He grinned at his next thought. Devin Shakur cuts his wrist because he's a little emo bitch (he assumed, since the guy looks emo enough). He cut them because he truly enjoyed it. That wreckless, masochistic side was what not only propelled him further into addiction and mental instability but also every victory in every match he'd ever won. In the morning, he'd tell Laura to go home and he'd hop in his beat up truck and drive to West Virginia for the next Revolution. He would reach in the center console, take two xanny bars out of a little envelope, swallow them and grind his teeth all the way to the arena. There, he'd wrestle and confront Bryan Dawkins once more. Maybe even throw a fist or two at the motherfucker. But at that moment he was wiping the blood off of his arm. He moved to the coffee table, blood still trickling down his left wrist. Four rails sat on the table beckoning him. He obliged, lifting the rolled up hundred dollar bill and snorted them all up his right nostril in what seemed one great inhale. When he exhaled, his head felt wonderful. The drip tasted great. He crawled to and sat in the corner of the room closest to Laura's head. He placed his hands on his knees and he watched coverage of Barack Obama. "Now I'm staring at the sun, waiting for it to explode. Because a day is gonna come... don't know when but it will come. And we'll finally know the way out of here." -- 'From a Balance Beam' by Bright Eyes
