The Glaring Differences and a Lesson Learned about When to say When(UV vs. Youngblood, Final Copy)

4 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Friday, May 4, 2007 Friday, May 4, 2007 Don’t ever let anyone tell you the life of a professional wrestler is easy. Most of it is smoke and mirrors. The good surrounds the bad effectively closing it off from the outside world. Take Killean Sirrajin as an example. Sure the guy has money, fame, a beautiful daughter and close friends. But think about all the bad that comes with it. There’s… ummm… … and there’s… Yeah, that too. Look, just trust me that a wrestler would nine times out of ten like to take a break from the world they live in and become just an average Joe. They’d like to be able to walk around and blend in with society without having to deal with autograph seekers and the media twenty-four hours a day. They’d like to become… normal, for lack of a better term. Every day… except today that is. Smoke was billowing from under the hood of his 2006 Dodge Charger. His cell phone was lying on the drivers seat flashing the “Low Battery, Unable to complete call” display on the screen. But what was Killean actually doing? Well he had his thumb out trying to flag down the nearest driver who would stop and give him a hand with his vehicle problems. Yes, today, Killean Sirrajin was a common man, cut off from the luxuries and the attention that a star gets and it was just about to get more common too. After numerous cars honked to get him to back off from the road, paying no attention to the problem he was having, a tractor trailer unit finally put on his four-way flashers and pulled over onto the shoulder behind his car. The driver opened his door and stepped out and down from his unit. He was a middle aged man, possibly in his early forties although the gray hair that took up fifty percent of his head hid the fact. He was a moderate build with a small belly on him. He was basically your typical truck driver. “Havin’ some difficulties?” He said as he pulled up his jeans higher on his waist. “No, I’m doing this shit for my health…” was what Killean really wanted to say. “Yeah, I think it’s the oil pump.” Was what he actually said. He hated stupid questions. “I’m David.” He said as he walked to the front of Killean’s car. “And who might you be?” Glorious! This trucker didn’t recognize him at all. Finally he wouldn’t have to deal with the attention. “Killean. Killean Sirrajin. Nice to meet you Dave.” “Likewise.” Dave said as he leaned in to take a quick peek through the little smoke that was still emanating from the engine compartment. After a few moments of looking and fiddling, he wiped his hands and sighed. “You’re right about the pump, tha’s for sure. Although the lack of oil might’ve caused some extra damage to the block.” “Heh, well that’s what a warranty is good for.” “Call a tow truck yet?” Dave asked, pointing to the cell phone on the seat. “Erm… my cell died before it could complete the call.” Killean was embarrassed. He had never let his phone go dead in case of an emergency. But when you have a huge match to prepare for and you forget to pack the car charger… “Alright, come with me and we’ll send a message to my dispatcher. They’ll get a truck out here soon as possible.” Dave offered while motioning to his truck. Killean hesitated for a moment and then followed the man, getting into the passenger door of his truck. He looked around for a moment in the front and saw pictures of what looked to be his wife and three young kids. He had two girls and one boy. He then looked to the back into the sleeper part of the truck and noticed some dirty dishes, a microwave, a hotplate and anything else that could make the life of an over the road driver easier. Indeed, it looked like he lived in his truck for a couple weeks at a time at least. Dave typed in a short message on a computer screen that was in his truck. About a minute later, the unit beeped and a red light came on. Pressing another few buttons, the light went away and he nodded his head. “A tow will be on its way soon. Where ya headed to?” “St. Louis.” Killean replied, running his hands through his hair, trying to get it out of his face but the breeze coming in through the window was not helping. “I’m headed right through there. I could go through the city instead of going around to give ya a lift if ya like.” “Well I was going to wait for the truck, but what the hell? I needed time to go to the gym anyways.” Killean said, opening the door to the truck to get his bag from the car. “I’ll get them to tell the tow guy to take it to one of the Dodge dealers there. That should work out for ya.” Killean nods to Dave to confirm that would work for him. As he runs to his car to get his bags, he silently wonders if what he is doing is a good idea. He hears all kinds of horror stories that include people asking for rides being kidnapped. But what was he thinking? Dave was an older man and Killean was a twenty-eight year old, two-time Universal and Intense Champion in PRIME. Really, did he have anything to worry about? He grabbed his couple of bags out of the car and closed the trunk lid. He ran back to the truck and got inside. Within a minute, the eighteen wheels were rolling down the road, on their way to St. Louis. |~*~| The breeze was coming in through the partially open window, blowing Killean’s hair all over his face. His repeated attempts to push it away were futile as it just kept blowing back. He loved the open highway, even more so when he wasn’t the one doing the driving. It was just relaxing to lean back and take in the country and all its glory. It was so much different than the city. It was one of Killean’s goals to move out to the country to get away from the constant hub-bub of the city. “Hey guy,” Dave started while switching lanes to move around a car. “Can you go back there to the fridge and grab me a Diet Coke? I normally have to pull over for that kinda stuff so I’d appreciate it.” Killean nods and walks back the the fridge in the sleeper bunk area. He pulls the door open. “Grab yourself one while you’re at it there guy.” Killean did just that. He handed the drink up to Dave who thanked him. Killean came back to the front and caught glimpse of the picture of Dave’s family again. They looked to be a tight knit bunch, although he found it rather symbolic that none of the few pictures had the older man included. Dave apparently wasn’t home much, so being out of the pictures could show that he is always away. “Your family looks sweet. How do they feel about you being away so much?” Sirrajin asked while cracking open the can of Diet Coke and taking a long sip. He rests it in the cup holder as Dave smirks, moving back into the right hand lane after safely passing the four-wheeler. “Needless to say my boy, it’s a constant argument. Every time I go home, I don’t feel like doing anything around the house because I’m tired. I miss plenty of family events because there is a very important shipment of paint or bras that needs to be delivered to Chicago or Phoenix. My oldest daughter just turned seventeen and I missed her birthday.” The older man had a rather sad look on his face for the shortest of moments, but he quickly perked up. “But hey, my calling is out here, doing exactly what you see me doing. I could never find myself behind a desk or having the normal nine to fiver that the regular folk have. My heart is on the highway and with my family, but there is bickering of which should come first.” Relaxing into his seat again with his drink, Killean began to combine Dave’s issue with his own current situation. The thoughts led him into a daydream about his future with Desiree. |~*~| “I hope Dizzy likes the cake I got for her.” Killean said as he finished frosting her name on the top. It was Desiree’s seventeenth birthday and since he tried, sometimes failing miserably, to spend each birthday of hers at home, he was putting more effort into this one. He had even offered to take her and her friends out to the movies. Either way, he hadn’t been home all the time to watch his little girl grow up. Killean had still been involved in wrestling on and off. Being a Hall of Fame wrestler in PRIME earned him plenty of appearances around the world in Indy federations and some bigger ones too. But when he had the chance to get out of the business all those years ago, he turned it down, opting to stay with it. But where had it brought him recently? “Dad, have you seen my phone?” Desiree said as she walked out of her room into the living room. Killean was frosting the cake in the kitchen and closed the lid of the box to prevent her from seeing it before the time came. Killean pictured her as a beautiful girl, grown up to look a lot like her mother Stacey. It was hard for him to believe that Stacey was indeed her mother but he had settled with the fact that she looked plenty like her birth mom. Desiree made her way over to Killean, standing in the kitchen in front of the cake box on the counter. “Dad, I’m gonna go to the mall with the girls.” Dizzy said as she checked her phone for any calls. Killean was confused, thinking they made arrangements the day before. “I thought we were going to spend part of the day together hun? We talked about it yesterday.” “Yeah well you didn’t seem to really be too excited about it since you were busy responding to e-mails offering you an appearance spot in matches. I mean how old do you think you are? Contrary to popular belief, not many fans these days know who you are. A forty-four year old man is not exactly what people want to see.” Desiree responded, putting on her coat. Killean seemed a little hurt. “I was doing this for us. I was really excited about spending the day with you.” “You know it’s impossible to read you sometimes Dad. What did you expect me to think when you rushed the conversation and then rushed me away so you could finish what you like to call important work?” Now she was upset, not so much at her father, but at the decisions he made for himself. “I just thought that I’d try and make good this time. I know I’ve been away for some of your past birthdays and last years was a fluke. I wanted…” But he was cut off. “YOU wanted. Y-O-U wanted! Why is it about what you want all the time Dad? What about me? When you didn’t come home for my birthday last year, I gave up on it all. When you showed very little interest yesterday, I made plans. A nod and a shove out of the room is not my way of agreeing to plans. You and your damn wrestling is ruining my life!” What a common line coming out of the mouth of a teenager. Just imagine an older person saying something like that. Desiree whips her purse from the counter, still not seeing the cake behind Killean. She hastily turned towards the door and marched off. Killean tried to stop her. “My wrestling ruins your life? MY WRESTLING MADE YOUR LIFE WHAT IT IS! Would you rather me have taken up the offer from the mafia from way back that I told you about? Yeah, I didn’t think so!” The door slammed shut just as he finished his sentence. His head, held up stiff at this point, dropped hard. He sighed deeply and turned to open the cake box. He stared at the words he put on the cake himself and lifted it from the counter. He turned again and placed it on the counter so it would be the first thing Desiree saw when she walked in the door. Killean wiped a lone tear from his eye as he walked off to his room to lie down. He began to think about his meeting with Matt before he brought the Tchu persona into PRIME. Some may not know that Matt was Killean’s therapist for a time and they discussed many of The Supreme Machine’s problems. One such worry was the offer that Killean had from mafia like organizations back in San Diego when he was still with his late wife Kira. Sometimes he regretted informing Dizzy of that information, but they made a deal a long time ago to be totally honest when something was bugging them, and Killean just happened to let slip about Kira. So he went into the entire story. Dizzy was surprised to say the least but was proud of her father for being so honest and reliving the terrible memories in his mind one more time. He let out a deep sigh as he made his way down the hall. Shutting his bedroom door, he remembered one last time what the words on the cake were. “Happy Birthday Dizzy, Love Always, Dad.” |~*~| The sun was shining brightly in his eyes as they fluttered open. He covered them with the back of his hand and slowly rubbed them. He bounced in his seat as they went over a bump in the road. “Hey kid, ya been out for about an hour now.” Dave said as he turned down the radio a little bit. It sure didn’t feel like he fell asleep for that long, but dreams suspend disbelief. “Where are we?” Killean asked, clearing his throat as he sounded a little hoarse from his nap. “About an hour outside St. Louis now. I saw the tow truck drive by with ya car a little while ago. Being limited on what speed I can go sure gives others the advantage of getting there sooner huh?” Killean rolled his eyes back a little as he ran his hand through his hair trying to untangle it. It was a pretty realistic dream for him. He swore that he had already lived most of his life and witnessed Desiree grow up. At least she wasn’t screwed up in his dream. He wouldn’t have been able to bear that. With the pressure of wanting to spend much more time with her, he didn’t need any more stresses, not to mention stresses that revolved around fiction. “There’s a restaurant up here in the truck stop. Ya hungry? We could grab some food before St. Louis if ya aren’t in a rush.” Killean readily agreed, not having eaten in quite a few hours. Normally when he went on his road trips in the car he was like that. Most of the time, he would live on two meals a day instead of three squares. Pulling into the truck stop, a parking spot was readily available. Dave switched off the engine, but that only served to make the many surrounding engines more audible. |~*~| Lunch was quite uneventful. Both men were steadily stuffing their faces and there really wasn’t anything interesting to converse about. Killean asked a few questions about Dave’s job and what is all involved, having a mutual respect for the men and few women that are in the industry that keeps Canada and the USA moving at their respective paces. “I know it can be hard to fathom, but in truth, everything that ya use in your everyday lives is in a truck or being hauled by a truck at some point in its transport. Heck, that plate and fork you’re usin’ was brought here in a truck at one time.” Killean listened to the stories that Dave told, having never listened to someone he just met so closely before. Perhaps it was because Dave was in a similar circumstance in travelling a lot and being away from family and friends. “Dave, you have to tell me how you do it. How can you be away from home so much? When I’m gone for more than a few days, I need to get home.” “It’s an acquired taste kid. Kinda like tastes for different foods. Some people just feel that their lives are better when they are on the road sometimes, just like people who eat broccoli. I can’t touch the damn stuff but you get the idea. You’re kinda a different person in that aspect. You don’t mind bein’ away from home but you don’t wanna be gone for long. You’re stuck in the middle of the two extremes and that’s not a bad thing.” Dave paused, letting Killean absorb the information. He understood where Dave was coming from. Here was a man that was away from home for a couple weeks at a time and enjoying it. Meanwhile, Killean was complaining about a few days. It kind of humbled him. “But do it while you’re young kid.” Dave continued, taking a sip from his drink and placing it back on the table before looking back over to Killean who was taking a bite out of a dinner roll. “That’s the key. When ya get older and your daughter gets older, things will begin to happen stemming from being away from home. I’m sure ya knew about that already, but it’s just a warning from someone who lives it.” “I hear that.” Killean said. He thought about it briefly as he put another forkful of potato salad into his mouth. When he was finished, he continued. “But when do you think a decent time is to cut it off?” “Ahhh see, that’s a decision you’re gonna need to come to my boy. If ya feel like ya have done what you needed to do and ya really want to be home with your daughter every night, there is no point in riding it out. It’s time for it to end right there. By the way, what do you do that keeps you away from home?” Uh oh, here it comes. Killean wasn’t going to lie, but he wanted to. This man didn’t recognize him at first and he was rather enjoying being a regular guy, mingling in a crowd without constant attention. “Uh… professional wrestling.” “Ah, never watched the stuff.” Dave replied and Killean sighed in relief. “Never really got into it, but that sure will keep ya from home. So it’s quite similar to what I do. All I can offer in advice is to take it one day at a time. Don’t make a rash decision in an instant, cause you’ll live to regret it. Think about it, think about your friends and your family and then come up with the best solution. You’re young kid, but that job is physically draining. Retiring early is always common in shit like that.” Killean had thought about retirement before. He battled in GTT 5 and then made a return to PRIME as Tyler Nelson’s hired hit man, his only goal being to capture the Universal Title from Walkins. Before that, he had basically said he was done. He was going to stay home, live life, take care of the little girl who showed up on his doorstep and run his business with Vegas. But then the wrestling world came calling again. He was tempted to return earlier but never bent and pulled the trigger. From that point, it continued to eat at him inside, knowing that he had time left to accomplish more. When he came back, he captured the Universal Title for the second time and he had his one-on-one match with Tchu that he never had. Even though he lost that match, he was still happy to have it. Now he is aligned with Tchu instead of being at his throat and he is going for the 5-Star Championship against a wrestler he hates more than anything in life. Brandon Youngblood thinks he is so good, but he needs to be brought down a notch. But say all of that happens. Say that Brandon does lose Sunday… say Killean does in fact capture the 5-Star title. What else is there for him in PRIME? The first push to his first Intense Title was tough. He was undefeated going into the first Colossus against Poet, Shadow and the champion at that time, Kincade. It was a Syndicate Structure match, a match never before seen. He was willing to fall from scaffolding through a glass ring to get the Intense Title. He did just that and walked out of Colossus undefeated still. He then went on to lose the title to Shadow at The Great American Nightmare. But he came back, recapturing the title again. Then he rolled on to Ultra Violence 2005. He was focused as ever, winning the Universal Championship and ending Karina Wolfenden’s record tying streak of ten wins. As the future goes, it was repeatedly up and down. But starting with Ultra Violence 2005, he won the Universal Title. Ultra Violence 2006? Another Universal Title victory, this time over Clyde Walkins, was in the cards. So what could Ultra Violence 2007 hold? A 5-Star title? Defeating his most hated competitor? It could all come to fruition. But what is left after all of that? Killean is getting to the point where he wants to be home more. When he goes home after Ultra Violence, does he really have anything left to accomplish? However, given all of that, given the fact that Brandon Youngblood is standing in his way of a fifth singles title in PRIME, will Killean have the push to win? Will he walk out of Ultra Violence in 2007 still undefeated at the event? Damn rights he has the push to win. Ultra Violence has consistently been the Killean Sirrajin show. Afterwards, it would be time to discuss his home time. But not yet. Dave finished his meal and actually took care of the bill without Killean knowing. He was appreciative, but really didn’t want the older man to pay for the meal. They settled back into the truck. The last leg of the trip to St. Louis was short, but Killean decided to get in his final question for the family man. “Tell me Dave, if you had been to everywhere you wanted to be, delivered every load there was and in the end felt like the push wasn’t there to drive…?” He paused and looked at the driver as he pulled out of the parking lot. “If that was me, right now… this would be my last trip my boy. You just need to know where to end it.” How correct he was. |~*~| What are the differences between Brandon Youngblood and Killean Sirrajin? Brandon dropped the ball the last time he had the chance at the big prize. Killean picked up the ball and ran with it… twice. Brandon doesn’t know his own flaws. Killean has had his flaws exploited and has filled those holes. Brandon wouldn’t know what hit him. Killean knows the values of family and friends and the value of being away from the ring. Brandon Youngblood’s life is inside that ring and he doesn’t know when to rest up. One day, he’ll end up a crippled mess, unable to feed himself cause he just didn’t know when to quit. Perhaps Ultra Violence? Killean Sirrajin has done it all in the world of PRIME. Brandon has done all he can hope to. But the biggest difference of all? Brandon Youngblood is not Killean Sirrajin. There is only one… … and he’ll stand at Ultra Violence, one more time with a Championship held high, with his daughter by his side, supporting him every step of the way... at much as a toddler could. It was his drive to survive. Do you have any of that Brandon? Didn’t think so.
