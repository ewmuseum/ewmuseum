A Poor Time To Have A Crisis Of Faith

25 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/25/07 The following is part of a taped conversation with client Danny Ferguson, dated 8/25/07: "So you understand my problem, then?" "Well, one of your problems, Danny, is that you assume it's singular." "Although I appreciate your constant fixation on the big picture, I'm thinking small." "This is not a surprise." "This is also not the time, doc. So if you would pleas-" "I know, I know. I'm sorry. Your problem is that you've been trying - and very diligently, I might add - to break this habit of projecting." "Which I never thought I had to begin with." "You're taking my word for it." "I stopped paying people to tell them they're wrong a while ago." "Another progressive step." "Anyway-" "Anyway, you came to me so I could fix you, and I tell you to stop projecting your problems onto other people, like that friend of yours who-" "He wasn't my friend." "Really? It sounded like he was a friend." "He betrayed me. Screwed me over. How is that friendly?" "I never watched your show, Danny. I only know what you told me. The only way to hate someone as strongly as you claimed you did was to have trusted them initially. If he wasn't a friend, maybe he should have been." "Whatever. Alia iacta est." "You a big Caesar fan?" "Not anymore." "Fair enough. So we were talking about projecting..." "Yeah, you said I can't keep trying to manifest my problems in other people." "Something like that." "And what's my biggest problem?" "My answer probably isn't yours, so let's assume that was rhetorical." "Wrestling, doc! Everywhere you look, they're saying how I let this wrestling thing kill my career." "Is that what you believe?" "I...no, I don't....the point is that they, the sheets, the tabloids-" "Danny, I don't read articles or watch TV shows about people who I see in person regularly. That stuff is a secondhand source. If you want to talk about the articles, there's a little box inside the front cover that tells you where to send Letters to the Editor. Do YOU think wrestling is affecting your life negatively?" "Let's put it this way - it's doing a lot better for me right now than acting is." "But you still consider yourself an actor instead of a wrestler." "Well, yeah. If you were a famous psychologist and you took up bartending, would you be a bartender first?" "You're a little loose with your comparison there." "You get my point." "Ok, so you want to keep wrestling, but you consider yourself an actor, and clearly you can't survive in both worlds at the same time." "Right. I mean, no, I can't." "And a lifetime of having decisions made for you means you're leaning towards the public opinion, which is to stop wrestling." "That's a little harsh, bu-" "Except you can't do that because for the life of you, you can't pull away from wrestling, and not just because it's the only thing giving you steady work at the moment." "...yeah." "So you're projecting because you're still wrestling?" "Not just that. I've got a match this week against, like, the epitome of wrestling. Like, this guy eats and sleeps the sport. Actually, I have no idea what his personal life is like, but he carries himself like he's in the gym 24-7, trying to be the 'greatest of all time.'" "Ah." "Yeah." "So this is just, like, begging for you to misplace your emotions." "Seriously." "You can't just forfeit? Avoid trouble altogether?" "Ego aside, doctor, I haven't lost a singles match this year." "How is that 'ego aside'?" "Well, it's a simple fact." "It's a simple fact that you can't bear to walk away from a hobby - because that's what it is when you work a second job you don't need, a hobby - because you're doing really well?" "I'm doing well to the point that I can make the transition to acting with some buzz." "Kinda like the buzz you got when the bottom dropped out of your previous acting work?" "..." "Danny, this is obviously unhealthy. You know this. You're aware of the hurdle with this wrestling guy, so that's a good step, but we don't seem to be any step closer to breaking the cycle. You have opportunities to get yourself back on track professionally and you're not taking them. Why?" "...I just can't, doc. I am enjoying myself. I haven't enjoyed my work once in Hollywood. I do it because I'm good at it. I didn't ever enjoy getting up to shoot some movie bullshit. I'm neck-deep in this wrestling stuff, though, and I'm loving it. I don't understand why I have to choose." "No one who is important is telling you that you have to." "...yeah, I guess..." "Time's up, Danny." "Huh?" "I said, your time is up." END TAPE.
