Redemption

26 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26/09/05 “It’s pretty appropriate that PRIME offer you redemption and we’re standing outside a church.” Paul Cain and Damien Kahn have been on a journey of discovery and this is the second leg of that trip. With the victory at Revolution 76 in the record books, Cain had proved to the doubters that maybe the issues with his personal life had been well and truly laid to rest. For the first time in a long while, Paul had felt his mind was clear and his heart was no longer as heavy as a lead weight. Damien used the word ‘redemption’ in many senses now for Paul was no longer a guilty and anguished man; thanks to his grandmother he had experienced redemption. PRIME were offering Paul the second stage of redemption by giving him a chance to show his real potential against thirteen other men. His fight with Nerezza was dogged by his battle against his own personal conflicts and it had forced the real Paul Cain into the shadows. However Paul was now ready to shine but first he had a job to do. “D-Money, take a good, long look at this place…It’s MORE than just a CHURCH…What do you see?” Paul looks like an artist painting an imaginary picture as his arms wave around dramatically. His friend stares at him and then the church, clearly confused by what he sees as a normal everyday place of worship. There is a deep sigh of disappointment from Paul who shakes his head at Damien. “It’s just a church, the same place that my family would take me on a Sunday as part of our ritual. I don’t see what the big deal is.” Paul looks aghast at this and his mouth is hanging open as if Damien has been shown a huge card with the answer on but still missed the point. “Just a church?! Are you out of your mind?! This isn’t just a church; this is where we will find the answers to the end of Imperium!” Damien Kahn had always been a realistic man and never believed in daydreaming. The concept of Paul Cain destroying Imperium as well as winning the Pulse Title AND getting back into the Jewel tournament seemed completely ridiculous to Damien. Although he was pleased to see the return of the Serial Thrilla, Damien truly believed in the idea of you should never run before you can walk. His reply to Paul’s proclamation was flat and sounded like a father reprimanding a child who had been told off countless times about the same subject. “You won’t destroy Imperium, PRIME won’t let you anywhere near Hoyt Williams. Stick to what you can realistically gain and that’s a spot in the Jewel in the Crown tournament and a shot at the Pulse Title.” Paul adjusted his suit jacket and straightened it as he stared straight ahead at the ornate church in front of him with a slight smile. “Oh ye of such little faith, D-Money.” With that, Paul began his second long walk in recent days as he headed towards the church. It seems that Cain likes long walks and they always seem to be very revealing. Onward, down the path of redemption. ******************************************************************************************************************************************** He stood at the back of the church leaning one of the pews, looking down to the front where he can see the priest busying himself. Paul could not recall the last time that he had been in a House of God but he did think that with all the sins he had committed, there was something inappropriate about the Cain and God combination. As he looked at the large image of Jesus on the cross which formed part of the stained glass windows at the front, he felt like he was waiting for a bolt of lightning to strike him down. The priest was a balding but grey haired man dressed in the traditional black robes with white collar. He seemed oblivious to the fact that a stranger was in his midst as he tidied the altar with a cloth. Paul began to make slow movements down the aisle towards the altar, his boots clumping against the wooden floor. “I wondered how long it would take you to approach me.” The priests’ Irish accent in his voice was calm and serene as he spoke peacefully to Paul who made his move towards the man of the cloth. Cain frowned in some confusion as he was sure that the priest could not possibly have heard him come in as the door was already open. Once again curiosity grabbed him and his enquiring mind had to know the answer. “How did you know I was here Father O’Brien?” There was no response as the priest waited until Cain was behind him before turning slowly around to face this newcomer. Paul noted immediately that this man was not looking at him but was instead looking behind him. “I do not have to see you to know you are here my son. For although my eyes cannot see, I have the sharpest vision.” Paul dared to laugh slightly to himself as he mused upon the fact that nothing in his life is ever simple or straight forward. Instead of getting a priest, he got a blind priest who spoke in riddles. It seemed to be just another typical day in the eventful life of the “Serial Thrilla” Paul Cain. The priest picked up on Cain’s scoff and smiled to himself. “You were not expecting a blind man in robes with the hearing of a bat then?” Having realised his faux pas, Paul cleared his throat nervously and tugged at his collar slightly. Cain was never intimidated by anyone but when it came to his grandmother and members of the clergy, he maintained a healthy and cautious respect. After recovering from this embarrassment, Paul looked around the church and sighed to himself. “To be honest your holiness, I wasn’t expecting to find myself in one of these places again.” The priest chuckled and set his cloth down on the altar. He then turned around stretched his arms out using them to guide himself to the front pew so he could sit himself down. Patting the seat next to him, he encouraged Cain to join him. “So if the church is so alien to you my son, what brings you here and what can we do for you?” Paul decided that sitting next to a priest would be pushing the weirdness factor just a little too far. Instead he stood in front of the pew with one leg resting on it and his arm draped across his knee. “I have a friend and he has a problem which he needs resolving…” The priest again smiled to himself as he listened to this stranger tell his tale. “In my trade my son, when someone starts their story in that manner, it normally relates to something personal or a sin of the carnal nature.” It certainly appeared that this member of the cloth was a modern thinking one as he snickered at what he saw to be Paul’s embarrassment. Instead Cain raised one eyebrow at the clergyman in an ‘action can be louder than a word’ demonstration. The priest composed himself and gesticulated for Cain to continue in his tale.“Father, as much as I appreciate your liberal attitude, this isn’t a laughing matter. My community has a serious issue that needs resolving before things get out of hand. I need your help to find a way of doing that but if you can’t or don’t want to, then I’ll just go and do it myself anyway.” O’Brien leaned back in his seat and folded his arms with one finger against his chin. Although he could not see Paul, he mentally studied him with every word spoken. He noted that this man was full of fight and determination however he was not surprised by this for it ran in the family. “Just like your grandmother, fire in the belly and a strong iron will to carry through any task that you start. Her daughter may not have had that spirit but her grandson most certainly does. Doris always impressed me and she still does because she is determined to see her grandson succeed even if she is not physically strong enough to be there. I knew you were coming Paul because she told me you had come home again. Now let me see whether I can help you with this problem of yours.” Father O’Brien had been solemn and calm yet the smile on his face showed his fond memories of Doris Jones. She had resided in his congregation for many years up until she left for the nursing home and she had given her all to her family and the church. Whilst she was forced to live her final years in confinement, he felt it was his duty to protect her grandchild and do whatever was necessary to help him. The silence that filled the church was enough to convey the emotion that Cain feeling at the mention of his grandmother. “She will be fine my son. God is watching over her and will make sure she is protected and loved during this difficult time. What is most important is that you make her proud and show her that the wishes she had for you are going to come true. Let God do the rest for you both.” Paul sighed heavily and looked at the ground for a moment. He then looks slowly up at Father O’Brien and confesses his problem. “There are some that use the power of faith to manipulate others though. There are some people that claim that they have the knowledge and power that God himself has. The group known as Imperium are two people that believe they have that sway over others. In recent weeks they have gone around trying to assume control over people’s lives and now they have chosen one young man to shape and mould into a disciple of theirs. They are a cult and they wish to add to their numbers by making him a believer. I truly think that they will not stop until they have the full control of the community that we are in.” Nodding knowingly, O’Brien reached for a bible which was next to him and placed it into his lap. “So you wish to save this young man do you, my son?” Paul looked up to the heavens as he paced slowly up and down in a small line. His mind was working over time as he considered the power that Imperium could hold if they had someone like Blackwolf under their control. He then stopped in his tracks and turned to O’Brien with a look of determination on his face. “I WILL save him because he cannot fall into their hands. IF he does, then they will use him to hurt others around him. However they have already poisoned his mind against me, I can sense it, I need something else, something stronger to SHOW him the truth. Come with me Father, help me show Mark the light, please…?” Slowly the priest took to his feet and edged forward towards the altar holding his bible under one hand. His tone was cautious as he spoke. “If the Devil has servants that are doing his bidding and they have this young man’s ear already, then it WILL be hard to stop them. They shall already have planted the seeds of control in his mind and it may be too late for either of us to influence him. IF that has happened then all you can do is contain him and ensure that he does not do to others what you have already seen him do before. I fear it may be already too late for him.” Cain looked exasperated and strode over to the priest, taking hold of his arm lightly. “I MUST try Father, I must! My grandmother could have given up on me but she didn’t. Damien could have given up on me but he didn’t. Even Claudia, the woman whose trust I abused did not give up on me until the bitter end. I have to repay the faith they put in me and put it into someone else now. He is a young man growing up in a business that will stab you in the back the week after it praised you as being the future. I WILL NOT let him be taken by them. I WILL NOT let Imperium win that easily. Come with me, Father…Help me…I HAVE to try.” The words of Paul Cain echoed throughout the church as they faded out and the priest stood motionless with his head down for a moment. He then gradually turned around to face Cain with his eyes looking in Paul’s direction. “I will do what I can but remember; you may already be too late.” Cain took a deep breath and exhaled heavily. “Then I’ll see him in Hell.” ******************************************************************************************************************************************** As Paul Cain strode out of the church, Damien glanced up and flicked his cigarette to the ground. He stomped on the butt hard and jammed his hands into his pockets as he watched his friend come towards him. He noted that Paul seemed intensely preoccupied as he walked with his head down and arms folded tightly across his chest. Damien called out to him in an attempt to snap him out of his reverie. “So will he help us then?!” Paul glanced up and nodded his head slowly as he approached the car. He fumbled in his suit pocket for his car keys and flicked them out. “He’ll help us but he thinks it’s already a lost cause. I don’t believe that though.” Damien wore a concerned look on his face as he watched his friend open the car door on the drivers’ side. “The journeys over now man, you’ve done your bit. Let the Rev do his thing. You got thirteen OTHER problems to get through on the way to YOUR redemption.” Paul smiled as he leaned on the drivers’ side door, with a big confident grin. “Didn’t I tell you? Thirteen is my lucky number.” Scene fades out to black.
