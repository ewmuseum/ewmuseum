This is what happens when you spend five hours staring at a Word document and can't finish it 'cause you'll be awake 'til like 8am

8 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8th April “Friends may come and go, but enemies accumulate.” - Thomas Jones. I Standing in the back room of a typically faceless chain record store, Jason Natas cocked his head back and allowed a disgruntled sigh to rise through his windpipe. With shoulder blades pressed back against the wall and hands stuffed into the pockets of his blue jeans, he was far too preoccupied with mentally going through a list of places he'd rather to listen to the pre-programming corporate blurb spewing from the man in the middle of the room. He wasn't missing much. “Twenty for a picture, ten for a signature.” “No longer than forty-five seconds with a single fan.” “Keep it clean.” “Try to look interested.” Typical pre-signing spiel. He huffed as he lit his cigarette, trying his best to blot out words he'd heard a thousand times before. The room wasn't anything of note. Small, cramped and windowless, it housed only a couple of tables, a few sets of chairs and a smattering of PRIME alumni and officials. Unlike Jason, the others in attendance at least feigned interest in the name of professionalism as the monkey in the suit rattled-off an uninspiring brief of trite clichés and overused figures of speech. A poster - “Meet the stars of PRIME!” - hung overhead; a hyperbolic title for the two men who hadn't wrestled in PRIME since 2004 and the tag team grappler that joined him for this insipid public relations stunt. Often it perplexed him why he was chosen for such events. He was one of the most reviled men in the business with a rap sheet longer than Garbage Bag Johnny's “pharmaceutical” bill. He'd made his name through his infamous detestation for all forms of fanfare in the sport, so why would anyone put his name on a poster to try and attract a crowd? He wouldn't waste any more of his time thinking about it, dismissing the signing as another pain in the ass contractual obligation. Taking a long, therapeutic drag on the cancer stick, Jason noticed the bodies in front of him begin to shuffle. Perhaps noticing the stale funk of cigarette smoke hanging in the air, the suit turned and scowled at Jason. “You know this building is no smoking, right?” he asked, folding two skinny arms over his chest. Jason smirked as he exhaled. Holding up the cigarette momentarily, he flicked it across the floor. It sent up a small cluster of sparks as it collided with the concrete surface and skittered across towards the suit's shoe. “Who's smokin'?” Jason croaked, flashing a yellow-toothed grin. Knowing better than to trifle a man of The Anti-Superstar's reputation, the brown-haired executive only sneered and turned his focus back towards the group. “Alright people, doors open in two minutes. Take your places.” Jason joined the slow shuffle from the briefing room to the store proper. Mingling somewhere between a dude he couldn't remember the name of who hadn't wrestled in four years and someone's manager, he strolled out onto the stage that had been erected at the very back of the barren store. Behind him were hung posters of some of the company's biggest stars. Bryan Dawkins chowing down on a slice of pineapple. Jason Snow flashing a sly grin. Tyler Rayne and that smile. … none of whom were in attendance. Taking his seat at the table and grabbing a pain, he sighed and watched the army of security men begin last minute preparations before opening the doors and unleashing the fanboys. This wasn't going to be fun.
