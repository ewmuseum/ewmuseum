Into The Abyss: Part XXIII - Charlotte Has The Answer

14 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14-09-09 A fine drizzle greeted him as he coasted through the Motor City. Quietly scanning the streets of his old home town, Von Kelsig kept his foot hovering over the accelerator as the F-150 rumbled over the macadam. The tension enveloped him like a vacuum, sucking the life from him as he silently studied the environment. It had been a long time since he stepped foot in Detroit. This used to be his town. As a world class wrestler and a native he practically held the keys to the kingdom. Most doors were open to him, and those that weren't he wouldn't venture near anyway. Gripping the wheel and clenching his teeth he focused on the traffic and the pedestrians power-walking through the misty downpour to get to where they were going before the rain got heavy. Nothing was going to keep the bad memories at bay. Dusk had fallen and only the amber haze cast off by the street lights illuminated the way. In the low light the looming shadows lunged at him from the sidewalk, creeping up the sides of buildings and causing him to flinch every time they swept over his truck. Cooped up inside the cab of his F-150, Von Kelsig struggled to maintain his composure as he rolled deeper into the concrete jungle. A red light brought a lump to his throat. Slowing the vehicle to a halt he made sure the windows were wound up on both sides and sat patiently, waiting as a crowd darted across his path. Several heads turned to the Ford puttering next to the crosswalk, and Von Kelsig froze. The fear of being recognized by the wrong person crawled up his spine and shook him raw. In a heightened state of paranoia he couldn't fathom that they were looking away to avoid the rain. At the same time Von Kelsig watched as not a one of them noticed who was behind the wheel of the truck, helped in part by his trademark beard being neatly braided. Comforted by the shielding rain, Von Kelsig pushed on. I-75 was fast approaching, the final stretch of his journey for the week, and the most daunting. Normally he was comfortable navigating across America to reach each ReVolution, but when that road took him within shooting distance of Reuben Blanco's old haunts, well...even giants have to fear something. Why Detroit? Why now? he thought to himself as the F-150 approached the on-ramp for 75. With a hundred yards or so to go Von Kelsig swallowed hard as the final junction approached. To his right, a dark maw seemed to beckon him. The buildings lining the cracked tarmac seemed to slouch, as if being dragged down by the state of the neighbourhood. Past the flickering lamplight into the darkness beyond lurked the greatest Demon of Von Kelsig's past. Checking his mirrors, Von Kelsig took a deep breath and cut across the lanes, veering straight into the void. The rain pounded against the truck, rattling like bullets against the cab. Latching onto the gear stick, Von Kelsig manoeuvred around corners and roundabouts like a rally driver. Taking advantage of the quiet streets he throttled the Ford over the blacktop until at last he came to an all-too familiar street... ...Which was a surprise given how much it had changed, one building in particular. He refused to stop, instead slowing the truck to a purr and rolling past the old factory building. At first glance he noticed the massive steel shutter was vandalized with graffiti and had been badly dented. The side entrance had been boarded up and many of the windows high above the street level had been smashed. Von Kelsig almost couldn't believe his luck. In his awe he hadn't realized the F-150 wasn't moving any more, sitting in the middle of the road in front of this derelict building that once held him prisoner. Squinting as though to part the heavy raindrops he noticed little black dots peppering most of the factory wall. "Jesus..." He said under his breath. The entire side of the building was riddled with bullet holes. Glancing up and down the street Von Kelsig reassured himself he was in front of the right address before he let his Ford crawl over to the curb side. Holding the biting point on the clutch with one foot and precariously complimenting it with just enough acceleration to keep the engine grumbling he examined the old headquarters, ready to flee at a moment's notice. He never stopped to realize just where he was or why he had come here, but all thrill seekers can't ignore their fear forever. As he drank in his surroundings Von Kelsig was glad he confronted this particularly horripilating consternation. He couldn't begin to deduce the real reason why, but in his own mind this looked like the aftermath of a gun fight between Reuben Blanco and either a rival dealer or the cops. That fat lowlife son of a bitch made himself out to be a God in the criminal underworld, and with that in mind Von Kelsig imagined this fight would have involved him somehow, given how lucrative he saw business being in parts of the country like this, he wouldn't abandon these headquarters. An old strip of police tape wriggled under the force of the rain battering the face of the factory, slithering under the gap of the side door like a writhing tongue. The corner of Von Kelsig's mouth turned up, stuck in a half smile as he imagined a day gone by when the man who tortured him was finally confronted by justice and taken down in a hail of gunfire. Had he failed in his escape attempt years before, he would have been around to witness the betrayal of Reuben Blanco by his supposed business partner John Doe. After escaping with Gloria from this brutal criminal overlord, Von Kelsig sought refuge with an old comrade in Bruce Shanahan, later to be discovered by John Doe himself. Why a conspiring mob dealer would personally seek him out confused Von Kelsig until the man revealed himself to be Tyler Rayne. Rayne's extra-curricular quest to seek out the Se7yn had brought him to Reuben Blanco, who worked under the Se7yn's umbrella chiefly as a trafficker. Once Blanco divulged the information he needed to pursue his nemeses, Rayne was gone. Unbeknownst to Von Kelsig's tormentor, the Detroit P.D. received an anonymous package full of evidence of what was going on behind the closed doors of the factory. Subsequent to that, a small army of officers accompanied by S.W.A.T. swooped on the factory. Reuben Blanco went down fighting. But he went down, for a long time. He wanted very much to get out and explore the old hell hole, but as satisfying as the dishevelled state of it was, he had no idea how the neighbourhood had changed in the years since he had been here. He held a hand up and winced. The tips of his fingers bore the mark of Blanco, horribly disfigured. The indescribable pain of having his prints erased by blowtorch lived on in his memory, and looking past his hand to the building beyond he found a small measure of solace in the knowledge that some comeuppance had found Blanco. With his fears gradually subsiding Von Kelsig took his leave, content that this chapter in his life had a definitive end to it. The threat of Reuben Blanco hung over him for the longest time, and seeing his criminal empire lying in ruins on some forgotten backstreet in Detroit was a palatable statement of finality. As he did a U-turn and drove back out towards I-75, Von Kelsig failed to notice the car hiding in a dark side alley. As soon as his Ford roared past, the wiper blades on the hidden vehicle began thrashing back and forth against the rain before the driver pulled out onto the road and began tailing the F-150, hidden in the spray kicked up from the tyres of Von Kelsig's truck. Checking his rear view mirror Von Kelsig couldn't see the car through the mist of rainwater, unaware of any other presence on the road other than his own. Merging with the traffic on the Interstate he shifted on his seat before cracking his back, trying to work some feeling into his numb backside and legs. "Almost there..." He sighed, cruising along behind other cars as thunder rumbled in the distance. With Blanco fresh in his mind, Von Kelsig's thoughts turned to Gloria. Her spectre haunted him at every turn, and in his home town especially her ghost lurked around every corner. When he initially learned of Gloria and Larry's demise, he shut down. So much emotion had been spent on coming to terms with the affair that when their deaths came out of nowhere it stunned him into silence. Von Kelsig was no scholar but he knew through the circumstances that the police would suspect some kind of involvement on his part. What struck him as odd was the fact they died in his house. What kind of beast showed up unexpected to someone's home and killed the occupants then disappeared without a trace? The idea that his love life could be There was no hard evidence at the scene to suggest he or anyone else had been there at the time of the murders. Forensics were baffled by such violent deaths occurring in such a clean environment. No DNA, no prints...nothing. Just two grotesque cadavers and the only suspect was a jilted lover, albeit an innocent one. Realizing this, Von Kelsig went in for questioning adamant to prove his innocence. Enough shit was happening in his life without a jail term being added to the list. Convinced he had proven himself, Von Kelsig left the station with only his conflicting emotions to bother him. Every time he saw her face in his mind and remembered the times they had shared together his heart sank in his chest. He wanted so much for her to accept his lifestyle. In his head she should have been content with any life that wasn't like the excuse for an existence she had under Blanco's roof. Alas, a life without him around proved to be too much to bear. He accepted that, and deep down would have dealt with a break-up if she had dumped him before finding new love. As it was the way he was betrayed became the one reason he wasn't a complete wreck. She had paid for her infidelity, even if it was a high price. He couldn't for the life of him comprehend how they had come to be slaughtered so soon after the discovery of the affair. There were no co-conspirators, no other love rivals, nothing. As unbelievable as it was to assume that a murderer had randomly stumbled upon his home and killed his ex-girlfriend and her lover, Von Kelsig had to believe something. With that belief in place, nothing else mattered. As tough as it was to cope with, he could live with the idea that things had happened the way they did. He didn't care about the house, and he certainly didn't give a shit if there was a madman on the loose chopping people up for fun. What's done is done. Throughout their relationship Von Kelsig made it clear to her that wrestling was in his blood. Her inability to understand this never fazed him as his run of success proved, but when he was dragged out in front of his peers at the mercy of bumbling police officers and had his personal life spilled out for all to see...that was enough to throw him off guard. His career was his life. He accepted that long ago and no matter how hard he tried to shy away from it and lead a normal life, he always found himself losing what was important to him as a man and being forced back to the squared circle. He couldn't put his finger on why. He wasn't a mystic, wasn't spiritual or religiously inclined – even if he made his name being a supernatural dark power in the old days of wrestling when fantasy and fiction were acceptable realms of reality. There was no heaven or hell, at least in a literal sense, no angels and demons, just the real world and the life he lived. He relied on his senses just like any other person...but something kept pulling him back to the wrestling world and it wasn't something he could sense. It was like his destiny was to be a legend of the squared circle, and it tore him up inside. ReVolution 205 was a massive fuck up. Having to deal with police and his colleagues gossiping about him behind his back riled him up. Who were any of them to judge him? He was a wrestler, and a damn fine one at that. Outside of his character no one had the right to talk about him unless he was there to defend himself. He didn't chastise Jay Phoenix for his indiscretions. He didn't ignore Michael Sloan because people thought he took the Chainz character a little too seriously, in fact he found Sloan to be the one man he could turn to in this time of need. The industry was full of such a rich variety of people and equally unique characters. This was his business; show business. It took all sorts to make a place like PRIME what it was, and to be talked about behind his back by a bunch of freaks who probably had more skeletons in their closets angered him. Consequently he suffered a loss at the hands of Tony Gamble. No matter what problems arose in his life he always found that as soon as he walked through that curtain he was Hessian, the one-man army. There were no whores to fuck up his heart, no torturers or failed business ventures. Just one man and two simple words to sum him up. Murder. Show. When Hessian stomped out into the RBC Center to face someone literally half the man he was, a sliver of Von Kelsig tagged along with him. No amount of power in the world could silence that little niggling shard of reality lodged in his brain, feeding on the gossip and the doubt and the paranoia before it became too much to handle. If he had dropped a loss to Snow or Tsonda or Vashaun he would have rolled with the punches and recovered. But to lose to Gamble? 'The Grin'?! His face contorted into a snarl and he pressed the accelerator to the floor, his frustration boiling into bloodlust. Gamble had capitalized on his vulnerable state, but with all that behind him now he was ready to walk into 206 with Sloan and remind people just who the fuck they were dealing with. The fury stayed with him all the way along I-75, creeping through his veins and pumping him up for the fight ahead. With a day before the show to get his aggression out at the gym he spent the rest of the journey looking forward to redeeming Hessian's name. A red Pontiac GTO pulled out to overtake him. Von Kelsig turned to see the car run level with him for a moment before drawing back to his rear. Thinking nothing of it he pushed on, desperate to reach the hotel and shut his thoughts off with a hard drink. Meanwhile, the driver of the GTO followed him closely. Reaching to the passenger seat he grabbed a cell phone and held it to his ear while concentrating on the F-150 ahead. Ever since Von Kelsig had passed over the state line into Michigan he had been followed by the man in the Pontiac, who expertly tailed him without notice the entire way. "Greco...I got your man sitting right in front of me.........yeah, he's making a beeline for the next venue.........no, he stopped by an old abandoned factory on Charlotte Street.........Four-sixty-six Charlotte, yeah. Run a check on it. I'll check back in when we get to Auburn Hills." ---- Placing his phone back in his pocket, Detective Greco grabbed a file from his desk and jotted down the address across the top. Darting between desks he left the room and ran excitedly down the corridor, coming to a door windowed with frosted glass before knocking sharply once and diving in to the office. His partner Barnes was sitting at his computer, a cigarette smouldering away in his mouse hand while he held his head up in his other hand leaning on the desk. Dark circles stained the armpits of his shirt, while the bags under his eyes confirmed the many pages, files and documents on the monitor screen were taking their toll on his patience and nerve. Glancing up stoically, Barnes took a drag and let the smoke waft over his face before he exhaled and stubbed it out in the ash tray. Before Greco could speak his partner sighed and rolled his hand, gesturing a string of dead-end leads. "The cross-references all checked out. There's not one bad word on Von Kelsig. You got anything?" He asked frustratedly. "Lyons just checked in, said Von Kelsig stopped outside an old factory just off the Interstate. It might be nothing, but run this address and see if anything comes up." "Shoot." Barnes replied, sitting up straight and poising himself over the keyboard. "466 Charlotte Street." The keys chattered as Barnes entered the address into the database and waited for something other than a dead-end to load up on the screen. In a moment the screen flashed as a list of results popped up, all related and with the address highlighted in bold. In that instant both detectives stared in disbelief at the welcome results, Barnes's eyes lighting up immediately. "Holy shit......" He gasped before clicking on the first heading. Both men read aloud for a few minutes before Barnes slapped his thigh and wheeled back from his desk, pumping a fist in the air and grinning confidently. "Jesus," Greco muttered, reading off the page, "this could be our lead!" Barnes began furiously scribbling down details while simultaneously printing off the results. "Shit this could be it. Get on the horn to Terre Haute, we need to find Reuben Blanco!"
