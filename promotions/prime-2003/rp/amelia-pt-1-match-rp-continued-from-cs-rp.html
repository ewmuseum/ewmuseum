''Hey. Uh...just tell me when to start apologizing.'' ''You did nothing wrong.'' The difference between Jeph and I was absolutely startling at times. We were the same on the outside - the piercings, tattoos and funny hair - but he had such an intense calm and level-headed nature that I'd grown to be almost jealous of, it was nearly psychotic. I always loved to watch his reactions to things; A shrug, that little smirk and maybe even a frustrated sigh...if he was really upset, of course. I'd brought him past 'really upset' at various points throughout our lives, but we'd been friends since I could remember so, really, that wasn't saying much. For me to piss someone off intensely only a few times in a lifetime was actually rather miraculous, and if it hadn't been for Jepha...well...I'd be a hell of a lot higher than I was at that moment. He'd helped me so much, but there was an awfully long road ahead - his recovery and mine now took the stage. He was physically slighted by a car accident that would be healing quickly, or so we hoped, but my problems weren't only fixed by exercise and time...they were a lot harder to shake, especially with one of my two sources of strength being weakened and medicated. One look into his eyes told me that he was well-aware of the painkillers I'd just lifted from the tray his nurse was wheeling out, and knew moreover that I'd had a small feast of them between the airport, a quick stop home and then the hospital. He was far too tired to fight me on something so comparatively small, though... and had absolutely no clue about how badly I'd fallen since Friday afternoon. I'd known that all the training Gibson had given me would pay off and every ounce of my confidence would show through in the Dual Halo match... but my guardian angel fell as I was leaving the gym that morning. When I'd heard about Jeph's wreck, everything fell apart; that training, practice...all the confidence in the world wouldn't have helped me at all in the ring if I couldn't get my mind off of the boyfriend I'd left in the hospital. If I'd abandoned him for that match, I figured, I'd better perform... but with my head on his well-being, I absolutely couldn't and that's where my dust of choice came into play. You'd be surprised how easy it is to get coke backstage at a wrestling show. I didn't know if it was good, bad or laced with some god-awful drug I actually wouldn't want in me, but the seller was an ancient contact I'd had from back in OSW. He used to do their lighting, and now designed PRIME banners and entrances pretty damned well, mostly thanks to the amount of illegal substances running through his blood and brain at a given moment. Cute little fella, and always willing to discount Aimzy. Sadly, that seems to be what I'd become backstage... 'You know, the druggie chick.'. It was a serious piss-off, up until the point that I realized how accurate that description was, of course. I'd been sitting there, show after show, neatly arranging my lines with a Hoyt Williams Outreach Program business card and approaching my peers with the filmy white leftovers hanging onto my nose ring. Embarassing as it is, that's the only way I can continue to fight...at least, until Jeph's around to help me. Beating cravings on your own is a lot harder than people think, and dealing with the shit-talk I get from some of the greener fools backstage can be a thousand times worse...but it all fuels me, just the same as the reassuring words Jeph let play out. ''Really, you didn't.'' he said, ''You followed what you needed to follow and, from what your mom said, you did damn well. You did fine by me, Ames.'' I couldn't stand seeing this guy, normally dancing or contorting himself to get the 'perfect' shot from his camera lens, so crippled suddenly. He'd get better soon, but it was hard to watch him just laying there with as much medication in him as was in me. This wasn't supposed to be the way things were... not at all. I don't know, maybe it was just the part of me that wanted to squeeze and love him to death in some weird way...but this situation was entirely too fucked up to contemplate. I was the strong one now, and that put me in a really bad position... one where I needed to think things through and actually, well, assume responsibility. ''So... you saw?'' was all I could say without choking at the sight of him, knowing I'd left Jeph alone to do what...come in fifth place? It'd have been one thing if I'd won, but I couldn't even make top three for him. It seemed he knew what was going through me, too. ''Fifth is incredible, Amy. You just had a bad run of luck with Youngblood. You were tired, and I don't blame ya...you're just coming into your sport again, and it's a demanding one.'' ''I don't need to be patronized. I didn't win, and that's what I was gonna do.'' ''You didn't give it a hundred percent, either,'' was his sharp response, ''Think of what could've happened if your head had been entirely on the match. Technically, it's sorta my fault you didn't make it all the way...y'know, getting into a wreck and all.'' He winked when shifting the blame, but it didn't make me feel any better. I'd choked in that match. The coke didn't last like I'd needed it to, and everything was getting blurry by the time I came against Youngblood and got shoved straight to hell. It was an embarassment after all of that training, all of the work and effort...to have everything fall apart in one fucking day. One.Fucking.Day. One accident, two lines of dust and three Vicodin... The fault was entirely my own. I'd let Jeph's pain fuck me up. I let my heart get ahead as usual, and it tossed a wrench into the works. It always did.
