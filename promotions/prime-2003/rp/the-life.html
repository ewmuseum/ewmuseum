The Life

Date: Today Kenjiro Ito is many things. Arrogant. Dickheaded. A prodigy in the squared circle. A proud Japanese citizen. But above all else, Kenjiro Ito can be categorized by his love of The Life. What, you may ask, is The Life? Simple. It’s what Tyler Nelson lives every day. It’s what a rock star lives. Or a movie star. The best thing about The Life, though, is that there are different ways to live it. Karina Wolfenden travels the globe, from the peaks of Nepal to the shores of Oah’u, looking for the next high it can bring her. Danny Ferguson jet-sets wherever he damn well pleases. He’s got the money, the looks, and the fame to live The Life to its fullest. But they’ve had a chance to grow used to it. They’ve been doing it for a while now. Ito, on the other hand, is still new to The Life. He doesn’t make as much as Ferguson or Wolfenden. But he has just enough to be able to fit in at the bottom scale of The Life. Kenjiro Ito wears nice clothes. Maybe not the $1000 suits yet, but certainly the $500 ones. He can’t quite eat at five star restaurants, but he’s a regular at four star establishments. He calls himself the Bringer of the Ice Age. The Cocky Osakan. The Reason To Watch ReVolution. But behind all the names, all the flash, and all of the insults is a man who grew up with a dream. A man who, like so many others, wanted to make it in this world. Wanted to be wealthy. Wanted to be famous. Wanted to be able to walk down the street and have people know who he was. And now, at the tender age of 25, he had a bit of it. A taste. A taste of the most tantalizing things in the world. Moreover, he knew what it was like to lose The Life. When Jolt Wrestling closed, and when his contract was cancelled, Ito had to go back to Japan. No strip clubs. No suits. No Cuban cigars. One of the rules of The Life is you spend like it’ll never end. There is no preparing for retirement, no thinking about anything other than how much ass you’re gonna get tonight, or how good the next meal’s gonna be, or how fucking sweet it is to be you. The future doesn’t matter worth a good goddamn. Kenjiro Ito may hate men like Chandler Tsonda. But he respects the man’s desire for The Life. But consider the case of one Kyle “Velocity” Lamen. A man for whom life is short. A man for whom the sands of time are ticking slowly away. In Ito’s mind, Lamen shouldn’t be wrestling. He should be out boozing, and whoring, and spending every last dime that he has. Dine, dance, and screw till you drop. That’s how Ito would want to go out. But, no. Kyle Lamen is an honorable man. Honor. Pssh. Kenjiro Ito cares not for honor. The Life is not honorable. The Life is about excess. About living life to its fullest. What did titles matter? Accomplishments? All would be lost soon, to the permanence of death. In the face of eternal night, playing the hero wasn’t just imprudent, to Ito, it was downright illogical. Why love when you could lust? Why covet when you could have? Why watch when you could do? Part of Kenjiro Ito wanted to show Lamen the error of his ways. For once, he wanted to show his fellow man what life really WAS about. Ito wasn’t usually a charitable sort, but the man was ill. He could make an exception. But Ito knew that Lamen wouldn’t accept it. Too stubborn. Too proud. Too full of high and mighty bullshit that didn’t work in real life. And, essentially, that was the difference between Kenjiro Ito and Kyle Lamen. They would meet on ReVolution 91, and compete for anywhere from 7-15 minutes. One would win, the other would lose. In Ito’s mind, he would drop Lamen with Ito’s Ice Age, get his hand raised, and move on. Kenjiro Ito would continue to live The Life, in hopes of becoming famous and wealthy enough to do it forever. And Kyle Lamen would continue to live until he could respire no longer. For while Ito lived The Life, Lamen would continue to live according to his own principles. His own thoughts. His own noble ways. That was fine with Ito. Because to him, The Life beat The Death any day of the week.
