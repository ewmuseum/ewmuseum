It’s been a long time since I’ve been in the wrestling ring; cocaine addictions and a broken ankle had sidelined me for about two months. But now, I’m back, and I’m ready to compete and do what I do best: wrestle. Coke addictions and a severely broken ankle won’t keep this monster down, no...you’d have to kill me to be done with me, but I digress. Eventually, everyone will compete in the same halo where all hell breaks loose. This is my observation since I hadn’t been in such a match before, but I know I’ll have my fun with it. Whether I win or lose, I know this will prove that I’m ready to come back, or it’ll put me back on the shelf. Everyone is going for the win. Everyone is going for the ultimate wish, whatever it may be. If I’d won, I’d go after the Universal title, as would anyone else in the Dual Halo Match. From what I’ve seen in PRIME, Clyde Watkins defeated a very promising superstar in Tchu. Who da thunk it: a jobbing, retarded man winning the biggest prize in any promotion. I almost hanged myself after witnessing such an atrocious act. But it looks like Tchu will be getting his title back soon. My apologies, ladies and gentlemen, I’m rambling. I hate it when I do that; gotta work on reducing my rambling. Anyway, before I make my return to Culture Shock in lovely Tokyo, Japan, I want to fill you in on everything that has transpired in the past two months. Yeah, I’m going to bore you with my life story, as usual, but it’ll end quickly, unless you blow your brains out in the middle of my story, I wouldn’t blame you. I suck at storytelling. It’s onto new beginnings...and everything else in between. “So, everything looks good? All the bones in my ankle have healed?” It had been eight weeks since I had my ankle broken. In those eight weeks, I’d been worried about the strength of my ankle, how well it healed and how well I could walk on it without any problems. I suppose I could say that I developed a sort of phobia: if I took a step, my ankle to shatter into a million pieces. And I had been worried about the soft tissue, ligament tendon and muscle damage I received while breaking several bones in the ankle. After awhile, my fears slowly evaporated. Two weeks ago I started walking. The first few days were tough trying to navigate with a cane, but I managed to do it. After ten or so days, I weaned myself off the cane and started doing ankle stretches to regain strength back in my ankle. I did several repetitions a day every day. Things were definitely looking up for me and a possible return to the squared-circle. Determination was a definite must for me if I ever wanted to return to the wrestling world. Doctor Henry Haynes, the man who operated on my ankle, was looking on the monitor screen, studying X-rays of my ankle. It appeared that he wasn’t listening to me when I asked him my question. After a few moments, he nodded, swiveled around in his chair and looked up at me. “Yeah, the bones in your ankle have healed completely,” Dr. Haynes said. “You don’t have to worry about re-breaking the ankle again, unless you do something stupid.” I chuckled a bit at what the doctor said. “What about the muscles and such?” “Those are still tender. When you had your ankle broken, it tore up a lot of muscle in your ankle. Right now, half the battle is won with the bones being healed and sturdy. Now the other half of the battle is the full recovery time of your ankle, which is another five to eight months. After that, it’s smooth sailing after that. I was happy to hear that. Bones have healed now I have to wait for the ankle to heal in its entirety. But one question remained on my mind: “Am I able to compete this Sunday? I’ve been booked in a match for the Pay-Per-View.” “Oh yeah,” Dr. Haynes replied, nodding. “You can go back into the wrestling ring. But the only thing I’m going to ask you is to wear a brace or an Air Cast when you perform. The best recommendation is an Air Cast. It’s lightweight, comfortable and, of course, keeps your ankle straight. If you perform without some sort of ankle protection, you run the risk of twisting your ankle, and in the state it is now, you don’t want to do that.” (Air Casts are definitely comfortable. I should know, I’m wearing one now.) “Excellent,” I said, with the utmost satisfaction. “Just as long as I can compete in the ring, that’s all that matters to me at this moment. He tore off the piece, then handed it to me. “I’ll do one final X-ray on your ankle and we’ll go from there. Bright and early. I thought she would’ve left if I hadn’t gotten my cocaine addiction under control.” I looked at the doctor and shook his hand. “Anyway, thanks for everything and I’ll see you next month.” Dr. Haynes nodded. “Take care, Simon.” I left the doctor’s examining room, met with the secretary to set up the next appointment and left the Healthcare facility of the hospital. Harvey Leeds could depict different nuances in a person’s voice, whether the person was telling the truth, or what the state that person was in. He had plenty of years of experience to back it up. I’d be taken away, Yuriko’s lawyers would suggest to fight against me, encourage a divorce and encourage sole custody of our son. Not exactly the path I want to take in life. Going through rehab was my only answer. I was taken aback by the immense size and shear beauty of the building. But it also had a sinister, macabre feel to it as if it once was an asylum back in the early twentieth century. Whatever the ominous past might’ve been for the old building, it no longer matters. What matters was getting myself clean and sober. The life I’m living now was leading me nowhere but six feet under - cold, deceased and alone. It was time for me to make my journey inside the large house. Again, thanks for everything.” “Just doing my job,” Harvey replied, flashing me a smile. “Good luck with your future endeavors, and hopefully, you won’t be coming back.” I nodded to him. Using the crutches the hospital prescribed for me, I made my way towards Yuriko. Yuriko got out of her car, ran towards me and gave him a tight embrace, kissing me on my lips. I apologized for everything I’d done to her. She told me not to worry about it; we’d work on it when we got home. She helped me into her car and we went home. Next stop...the Dual Halo.
