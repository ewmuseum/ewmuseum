What would Rampage do?

28 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/28/2005 “Can’t believe there was air in the brakelines… again. Sonofabitch.” The voice was muffled, mainly be the engine block that was impeding the vision of the speaker. “How the hell did that happen…” he muttered again. With a heave, the man in question pushed himself up and out of the inner guts of the Jeep Wrangler and groped around blindly. The big man’s face was grimy, matching his hands and the shaggy golden mane that hung around his face. His hand finally settled on a rag and snatched it up, only to bring it to his face and prove that his grime would be matched. “Great,” the big man muttered. He turned around, grabbing an old toolbox and hoisting it onto the Jeep’s frame. He muttered to himself, frustrated as he rifled through the wrenches, speaking ill about the arrangement of those steel tools. With a sigh, he finally pulled the desired size out and leaned back down into the greasy depths of his work. Life never had been easy for Scott Matthews, not since he took up the job of being the traveling independent wrestler. His life was one of constant wandering, driving and working. Every day, night after night, he’d work in a big arena or a dingy hall, trying to carve out enough cash to send home and get him to his next show. His mind flitted to that thought as he tried to reach for the detached brakeline. The next show. He’d always been able to keep his schedule mostly full. Always been able to find some place that needed a big beast on the card and he’d go and hit the ring. That’s just what he did. But not lately. Lately the shows seemed to have been drying up. No, they weren’t drying up, but they didn’t want him. Promoters and bookers had declined his offers to appear, rejected his calls. He, former RPW tag team champion and a fairly notable name, had been blown off by no-name promoters left and right lately. And then his Jeeps damn brakes had screwed up on him. So he had headed home, to good ol’ Pikeville, Kentucky, to park his Jeep and try to fix his brakes. A miracle he hadn’t died swinging around the familiar old curves of the mountains, but he still wasn’t dead. And the phone rang. In the garage, the phone was mounted over on the wall, hanging there with an extremely loud ringer and a speaker so that you could not only make sure you hear the ringing if you were doing something like welding, the person on the other end could join in like he or she was right in there with you. Okay, not really, but it was just easier to have the damn thing be a speakerphone than a cordless or one of those long-ass cords. Scott didn’t want to bother right now. “Will someone GET THAT?” he bellowed around the engine block. The shout was partially that he wanted to make sure to be heard. The other part was that he was getting real frustrated with how his big hands couldn’t get to the position he needed them in. “How the hell is anyone other than a midget supposed to work on these fucking things?” he growled to himself. “I’ve got it,” a female voice shouted back. That would be his mother, Cathy. Scott never called her that, though. Just didn’t work to him, so he didn’t. “Scott, this is someone about a show they want you to work. You want me to hit the speaker for you?” “No,” he shouted back. “Damn hose…” he muttered under his breath. He took a moment to extricate himself from the frame of the vehicle and kicked the groundcart at his feet. He hated using it; it was too small and hurt his back, but he didn’t really have a choice. “Let Aly talk to ‘em,” he shouted to his mother. Aly, or Alyssa as her full name would be. That was Scott’s ability to keep going. Sure, he had a reason to keep living the life he was; his sonofabitch father was dead and he had to support his mom and Lacey, his fifteen year old sister. But Alyssa Sanders was what kept him going. He’d met her in RPW and after a stormy first few weeks, they’d settled into a happy, if difficult relationship. She didn’t need money, but she also couldn’t run with him to every show. Not that he wanted her to, really. After that first greasy worker had tried putting his hands on his brunette baby, that had really been enough. So Scott lowered himself on that hateful cart and pulled himself up under the engine of the car. For the millionth time, he was glad that the Jeep rode high; working on it was much easier. From the bottom, he could reach the hose, but the cart was already digging its edges into his broad, shirtless back. Still, he jammed the end of the hose into place and tightened it down, twisting the wrench with long strokes. “Scott-ee,” came a sing-song call. It made him smile in spite of himself. “Scotty, come out from under there.” “Just a second, Aly. I’m almost done.” He strained his right arm, locking the nut into place. Finally done, he took a long breath. “No, I need to talk to you now,” she said, grabbing his legs and pulling him. The cart slid along and Scott instinctively tried to look where he was going. The mat of dirty blonde hair on his head managed to pad the blow of his head hitting the axel pretty well. It just still stung and pissed him off. “Shit, Aly, you know better than to do that.” Unphased, the lithe beauty smiled down at him. “I’ve got news for you, mister,” she said with a grin. “I know why you haven’t gotten any bookings lately.” “I bet it was that fatass Beau James,” he growled, putting his hands down on the floor to get off that stupid groundcart. “He’s been pissed at me since…” He was cut off when Alyssa swung a leg over him and sat down his stomach. She put her hands on her chest and dropped her chin on her fingers, staring him right in the eye. “No, no, it’s got nothing to do with him,” she said, still smiling. “That was PRIME, honey.” “PRIME?” Scott asked, more than a little confused. “What do they…” “Alyssa!” came the shocked gasp from behind the girl. Scott’s mom still wasn’t used to how… forward Alyssa was with Scott. And it would look somewhat bad, wouldn’t it? She’s straddling this woman’s son, and no matter what he might have thought of it, this was still her oldest child. Aly shot a wink at Scott and pulled herself up. “Just trying to find a good way to break the news,” she said. “Sorry if it bothered you, Cathy.” “I just didn’t expect…” she trailed off. “Well, what do you think, Scott?” “I don’t know yet,” Scott replied, pulling himself off the groundcart by the bumper of the Jeep. The sharp points of the damn cart finally quit digging into his back. “That’s why you haven’t been getting bookings,” Alyssa said with a smile. “PRIME’s been on you.” “I don’t get it,” Scott said, shrugging his massive shoulders. “Look, they turned down my singles application. Why are they hitting me up now?” “They want you to tag again,” she replied. Scott was silent for a moment. “Oh wow, it’d be great to work with Gabe without all that invincible crap getting in the way.” For a moment, a wistful smile almost crossed the man’s face. “Not quite,” Alyssa said. “I hate to break up your reverie there, baby, but it’s not Gabe. Gabe’s actually leaving PRIME.” Scott’s brow furrowed. “Then who the hell do they want me to tag with?” he asked. His mind tried to recall the guys on the PRIME roster. Gibson was tagging with Azala. They had a team of lucha guys… “Vangellus Oxios, the partner Gabe’s leaving.” The world stopped for just a moment. Memories came rushing back. Oxios was a loud-mouth, rich-boy, obnoxious jerk that had wanted to have sponsorship on his moves. Scott looked at Alyssa with a very irritated glare. “You’d better not be screwing with me honey.” Alyssa smiled and bit her tongue from saying something with his mother standing right next to her. “No, Scotty, they’re serious. You want to know the best part?” Scott shook his shaggy head, so Aly leaned in and whispered in his ear. For the briefest of moments, his hazel eyes widened. “Well, mom…” he started, but she cut him off right away. “Oh no, mister,” she said putting an edge to her voice. “You’re not sending all of that here. I already know and you’re going to keep some of it for yourself.” ”The hell I am,” Scott retorted hotly. “You guys should be doing better. If I’d landed something like this earlier, you could’ve gotten that new car a year or two ago instead of last week.” ”Scott, I don’t care. We live comfortably because you work so hard. I want you to enjoy that yourself.” “I don’t want you to live comfortably,” Scott shot back. “I want you to live well and you’re not going to be doing that if I start indulging myself.” Alyssa and Cathy turned their eyes towards each other. Scott was impossible when he got started like this and there was really nothing that either one could do. His determination to give as much money as possible to the lives of his mother and sister was admirable… but sacrificial almost to the point of frightening. Alyssa knew something Cathy didn’t, though. Every so often, Scott would hold onto a smaller check and simply drink until he passed out. It was how he dealt. It wasn’t a healthy way to do it, it wasn’t safe, it didn’t even really work, but for Scott, it was a full night of escape and apparently it was what he needed. “You do what you feel you have to, then,” his mother sighed. He wouldn’t rest at all. He could be making what folks at the top of PCW, OSW or even PRIME made and still wouldn’t be happy. “However, you are not giving me your signing bonus.” Scott’s eyes widened. He’d heard of signing bonuses… but… “A signing bonus?” he asked quizzically. “Yes, plus you’re going to start letting me do things for you,” Alyssa cut in. Right now the two of them had him on the ropes and couldn’t let up. “You need a cell phone so we’ll know how you’re doing and that Jeep? Scott, it’s going to die on you sooner or later. It’s at least ten years old.” “Now wait a minute,” he said quickly. “No, I don’t think I will,” Aly replied tartly. She crossed her arms across her chest. “That phone will be paid for by me, and you will carry it.” “Mom could use it more than…” he tried to begin. “Oh no, I couldn’t,” she cut back in. “You’re the one on the road all the time and I worry about you. You’d better carry that thing.” Scott looked back and forth… it was a two-front war that he just couldn’t win. “Alright, alright,” he assented, “you win.” “But tonight,” Alyssa said, “we’re going to go celebrate.” Scott gave her a sideways look, his hazel eyes betraying him. “Yes, we’re going to go to Lexington, mister. We’re going to have a nice dinner, enjoy a night out and stay in a nice place.” ”I think that sounds marvelous,” Cathy added. “You earned this.” “But… but…” Scott stammered. “What’s the card for the next show?” He racked his brain, trying to remember if he’d seen it at any point. “There’s a battle royal… and Gabe was supposed to have a tag match…” ”Yes,” Alyssa replied slowly. “You’re in both of those.” “I gotta train. I’ve got to talk to Oxios and see if Chuck and the boys around here will help me with the battle royal thing. They’ve got a small show going on. Maybe I can…” “No,” came the firm reply. “You and I are going to celebrate. You’ve been pushing yourself so hard, if you don’t take a break you’re going to get hurt.” The big man known as Rampage was outclassed and beaten. He knew it. He was simply done. With a nod of his shaggy head, he turned around to grab the filthy rag he’d left on the Jeep’s black frame. ”Oh, and Scott?” Alyssa asked sweetly. That made his spine stiffen; she usually only did this when she wanted him to do something. “Please wear something nice. Some khakis and a buttonup shirt. We’re celebrating you landing a big contract.” “I think that’d be great,” his mother added. “He’s got stuff in his closet like that he’s never even worn.” But Cathy stopped and glanced at the old clock on the wall. “Kids, I’ve got to run by the bank before it closes and pick up Lacey.” Scott tried to hug his mother, bug Alyssa stopped him. “You need a shower, Scotty.” With a wink to Cathy, who was heading out the door of the garage, she turned back to her man. “You know,” she purred as the car outside started up, “I just love how you look in buttonups.” “No, I didn’t know that,” he replied. “You rip ‘em off too fast.” Alyssa’s throaty laugh made his blood boil. “I know. But for now, let’s get you cleaned up,” she said, walking back into the house. “Let’s?” he asked. “What’s this ‘let’s’ stuff?” The answer hit him in the face. In the form of Alyssa’s shirt.
