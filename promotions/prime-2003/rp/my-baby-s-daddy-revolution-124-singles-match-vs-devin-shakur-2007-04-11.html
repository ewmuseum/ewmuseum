My Baby's Daddy (Revolution 124, singles match vs. Devin Shakur in defense of the Intense Title)

11 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 11th, 2007 The Sultan of Soul slammed a brown leather suitcase shut and clicked the gold locks into place. He thought to himself, making sure he had everything he needed for the trip to Salt Lake City, where the one-hundred and twenty-fourth PRIME ReVolution would take place. He grabbed the two identical cases and carried them out of his room, walking toward the kitchen and setting them down with a huff. He cracked his back and took a peak at the clock behind his glasses. Five o’clock in the pm. "Hmmm…plane leaves in the mornin’ ‘round seven a.m…what’s a brotha to do for the evenin’?" Asa paced a bit, scratching a leg through his jeans and scratching his stomach through a white t-shirt. A light bulb clicked on above his head. "Liquor store ain’t closin’ for another hour…better go pay n’ ol’ friend a visit." That said, Asa snagged a black denim coat from a nearby hook, a pack of Marlboro Blend 27s, and stepped out into the driveway, lighting a smoke and ambling down the sidewalk into the streets of Los Angeles, California. * * * * * * * Asa set a bottle of Basil Hayden’s bourbon whiskey onto the counter. Chuck, the fat, old, balding store clerk, smiled at the Ayatollah of Funk n’ Soula, beeping the bottle with one of those beeper things to ring up the order. "I trust you actually have some dough this time?" asked Chuck. "Hell yeah I do! I ain’t lettin’ them bitches take my cards n’ money anymore, Chuck. I cleaned up a bit, got myself back in gear, cat daddy!" replied The Fro. Chuck snorted. "Yeah, I’ve seen you on T.V., but it looks like you haven’t cleaned up THAT much," said Chuck, pointing to the bourbon. Asa laughed and straightened out his jacket. "True ‘nuff, brotha. But hey, never said I was gon’ be perfect." Chuck returned Asa’s credit card and bagged the booze, which Asa quickly gripped in his big black hand. "Congratulations on getting back on track, Asa. It was pretty disheartening seeing you in the state you were in." "Brotha, life is real damn fine. Catch ya later, pops." * * * * * * * Asa padded down the street. He walked just like any brother should, but when you see Asa walk, you can’t help but think he’s dancing a little, even when he isn’t. With a smoke in his hand and big smile on his face he headed up-street. He soon took a quick turn into an alleyway, and it wasn’t long before he found an old black fellow, passed out asleep, leaning against the alley wall. He had tattered clothing, and gray, patchy hair, drool dripping out of the corner of his mouth. Asa smiled and kneeled next to the old man. He tapped him on the shoulder. "Wha! Jesus fuckin’ Christ! Keep ‘way, cops! I ain’t breakin’ no law!" yelled the old man, flailing about in an attempt to stand. Asa laughed until the old fool realized who he was. "Oh, it’s yo’ funky ass," said the old man, taking his seat against the alley wall once more. "Good to see you too, Roy," said Asa, sitting next to him. "Whaddya want now, boy? I already give ya plenty o’ advice, I ain’t got mo’ ta give!" "I ain’t here for advice, brotha. I’m here to say ‘thanks.’" "Thanks? Nigga, fa what?" "For gettin’ me off my lazy black ass!" Roy stared at Asa for a bit, and soon a smile came across his toothless mouth. "Ye went n’ got back into the wresslin’, didn’t ya?" Asa nodded. Roy slapped him on the back, letting out a hearty laugh. "Good fer you, boy! Glad to hear ya gone back to whuppin’ cracka-ass!" "No doubt, pops. Just got done whuppin’ one particular cracka’s ass. Got a shiny belt outta it too. Oh, n’ this is for you." Asa handed the bottle to Roy, who, like an excited child at Christmas, promptly tore into it, revealing the bottle of Basil Hayden’s. He stared at it for a moment, his face in a half smile of disbelief and joy. "Ya know, I used ta drink Basil Hayden’s with ma poppy, way back, years ago," said Roy after a moment of reflection. Asa smiled as Roy cracked it open and took a swig. "Well, it’s a small token, my man," replied Asa, patting Roy on the shoulder. "Ya can’t put a price on what ya done for me." "Bah, horseshit. All I did was slur words atcha. You was the one who did somethin’ ‘bout it." Asa nodded, then checked his watch. He let out a sigh then heaved himself to his feet. "Well, let’s get goin’ Roy." Roy stopped mid-swig. "Wha?" he asked. "C’mon! We goin’ hom!" "Nigga, I AM home!" "Not no more, jive turkey. C’mon, you comin’ with me." "Boy, I ain’t goin’ ta live witcha. Last thing ya need is my crusty ol’ ass sleepin’ on ya couch," said Roy, returning to his bottle. "I got a shower, n’ you’ll be sleepin’ in a bed, cat daddy." "Lissin, ye don’t have ta…" "I know I don’t have to, Roy. But I am. You think I’ma let you rot out here in the streets? You must be straight outcha mind! You gonna live with me, travel with me, and live a god damn life, instead o’ gettin’ drunk out in the streets every night. Nigga, you ain’t got a choice." Roy hesitated for a moment, took a big swig from the bottle, then lumbered to his feet. "Alright, youngin’, but lemme warn ya, the moment my fat ol’ body falls into a warm bed, you sure as shit ain’t gettin’ me outta it." Asa laughed and clapped Roy in the shoulder as they headed out of the alley. "That’s fine, jive turkey. I’ll deal with it." * * * * * * * Roy looked up at Asa’s large, white home. His eyes fell across Asa’s two cars; his olive green, 1970 Boss 429 Mustang and his gold Cadillac convertible. Roy shook his head a bit, then turned to Asa. "If I’d known you was livin’ ‘ere, I woulda passed out on ya couch weeks ago." "Ha! An’ I woulda whipped ya ass! C’mon, lemme show ya your room." Asa led Roy through the mahogany front door, the two stepping into the kitchen. Asa spread his arms wide, presenting his home. "Here ya go, Roy! Welcome ho…who the fuck are you?" Asa was referring to the suited white man sitting on his couch. The man slicked back his brown hair and stood, extending a hand to Asa. "Mr. Fountain? Peter Webber, good evening." Asa tentatively shook the man’s hand, an eyebrow raised at the intruder. "I ain’t in trouble with the police, am I?" "Oh, no sir," answered Webber, laughing a bit. "Not at all. Shall we take a seat?" "Yeah, fa real." The three took a seat on Asa’s white suede couches. Roy gazed around, flabbergasted, at kung-fu paintings and Asa’s huge-ass TV. "So, what’s goin’ on?" asked Asa, leaning in toward Mr. Webber. "Alright, here’s the story, Mr. Fountain. I’m from child services, and we have a matter to deal with that occurred about four years ago. Do you remember a woman by the name of Shanda Russell?" Asa’s eyes lit up. "Hell yeah! I damn near fell in love with that girl! She was a backstage interview suga mama back in Old School Wrestlin’!" "Exactly," said Peter, smiling. "Now, you admit to having relations with Shanda, correct?" "No doubt," replied Asa. "But it didn’t last long. When OSW died, we kinda got split up." "Well I have what may be some shocking news for you, Mr. Fountain." "What, she dead?" "No, no. Actually, we’re unsure of her current whereabouts, but she happened to leave something behind that has to be taken care of." "Alright, tell me what’s up, brotha." "Okay, now brace yourself, Mr. Fountain. After spending some time tracking you down, and studying DNA evidence, which we found was a match for you…" Mr. Webber waved in a "come on over" kind of way behind Asa. The Funk Doctor’s eyes nearly bugged out of his head as a small, four year old boy stepped around the corner of the couch, curly hair on top of his head, clad in a little blue jeans and a green and white striped shirt. Asa looked up to Pete, eyes wide and mouth half open. "Mr. Fountain, I’d like you to meet your son, Miles." Miles stood with hands behind his back, big brown eyes looking up to his daddy. Roy took a look at the little boy and turned to Asa, tapping him on the shoulder. "If you think I’ma take care o’ this li’l runt, you straight outchya mind." To be continued...
