[Endsong] – Flying High

18 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 18 February 2009 Hawaii – three months ago "C’mon, Rick, we are nearly there." "You said that an hour ago, Jay" came the reply. "So either you’re lying to me or your definition of ‘near’ is very different from mine!" "Every step taken is another step nearer the destination, Rick!" "OK – that’s it!" Ankle deep in rich verdant green vegetation, Rick James suddenly stopped, glaring up at the muscular back of the man he had always counted as his best friend but – recently – it was a friendship that had been strained at best. At worst it had been something that Rick tried not to think about. The two men had spent most of their lives together, growing up as boys on the Native American reservation that Phoenix had always called home and that Rick – when his father took up a medical position there – had grown to cherish too. Moving on they travelled to the same college, studying very different fields until, finally, the wrestling industry called one of them. Jay Phoenix, the man that would become known as the Eternal Flame in the ring, answered that call and Rick James went along with him. Through thick and thin the pair had remained friends, as close as brothers, until Phoenix had succumbed to either a spiritual possession or a psychotic break – depending on which ‘medicine man’ you asked – and become the man known only as Ember. For two years Rick James searched for Phoenix – thinking that he was dead but hoping … always hoping … that he was alive. When he found him he helped bring Phoenix back to the reality of his self, banishing – killing – the persona of Ember at the same time. The memories though, the shame of what he had done – and what had been done to him – haunted Phoenix. Depression, guilt and self-loathing filled his days while terrors filled his nights. Thoughts of suicide became verbal requests to die until, finally, Rick forced him to accompany him to Hawaii to try to put the past behind him and achieve some level of spiritual well-being. Six weeks into the stay and nothing had worked; nothing had changed. Until two nights ago. Returning from an early morning walk into the deep forests of Hawaii, Phoenix had – for the first time – looked like a shadow of his former self. A light burned in his eyes, a smile hovered on his face and he had been able to look Rick directly in the eyes. Rick hadn’t asked what had changed – Phoenix had only volunteered that he had been thinking of his father – but it didn’t matter; he could see the vestiges of his friend in front of him, the man that he had thought that he had lost. Lost to the memory of Ember and his own careless tongue. When he had found Phoenix – when he had helped nurse him back to physical … if not mental nor emotional … strength he had slipped up and said the one thing that he had never dared tell his friend. The one thing that he had been too afraid to say. He had told Jay that he loved him. In that moment – when Jay looked at him and into his soul – he knew that the true meaning of the words had been heard. Had been felt. He didn’t bother trying to pretend that he meant only as a friend, only as a brother. In that moment they both knew the truth – Rick loved him; was IN love with him. It may have been simply bad timing – Phoenix had suffered so much, had felt so violated – or it may have been that he simply didn’t feel the same. Whatever the cause he just turned away and, in doing so, broke Rick’s heart. The next day they flew to Hawaii and neither of them had talked about it since. In fact Phoenix had done little to no talking at all. Until tow nights previously, when he said that he needed one more day on his own. At the end of that day he returned – tired and sweat soaked – but looking happy. Looking resolute. Then, this very morning, he had been waiting for Rick at the front of the house a smile on his face as he simply said that he had something to show Rick – something that he needed to talk about. When Rick had asked what he had been told not here, not now. Simply pleased – and amazed – at the changed in Phoenix Rick had been more than happy to go along with his friend – now that he recognised him as the man that WAS his friend – and so they began to walk. Three and a half hours later, however, after walking through thick vegetation, heavy forests and over dark volcanic rocks that led up a stepper and steeper incline, Rick had had enough. "I am not going another step," he stated, resolutely, "until you tell me what this is about!" "What this is about?" Jay stood there, hair long and auburn - red and blond streaks flashing with natural highlights as he turned to face Rick - flowing freely down to his shoulders. With a quick motion, he reached up and pushed the hair away from his face, looking up at the frustrated man with a growing smile. "This is about what you wanted for me, Rick." "What?" "Listen," Jay said, stepping down until he was only a few inches from Rick, the incline making his look down at his friend. "I promise that we are nearly there, just a few more minutes at most and then …" "Then?" "… then it will all be clear." "It had better be, Jay," Rick snarled, smiling in spite of himself at his friend’s easygoing nature. "If not I am going to just sit down and you will have to call mountain rescue to come and get me!" "… or just leave you out here all night." "You wouldn’t dare!" Rick spluttered. "You know that I hate sleeping out doors!" "That is the white man talking," Jay laughed. "You would never believe that you basically grew up on a reservation!" "If God had wanted us to sleep out of doors," Rick stated, amazed at hearing Jay laugh after so long. "He wouldn’t have invented hotels!" "We won’t need mountain rescue," Jay reiterated. "Or a hotel. I promise." With another smile he turned back and started walking back up the slope and, with a sigh, Rick followed him. Less than five minutes later Rick pulled himself over a steeper part of the slope, nearly a ridge and, with a gaps of relief, threw himself to the grass as he saw Phoenix sitting only a few feet in front of him. "What are you doing, Jay?" he breathed. "Trying to kill me?!" "That is so weird Rick," Jay whispered, catching his friend’s eyes. "Are you reading my mind?" "It would be a short read, Jay," Rick quipped, breathless. "Not much more than a short story!" "Funny!" "Why was it weird, though?" "I was just thinking about another climb, another mountain is all." "Really?" "Yeah," Jay muttered, half to himself but keeping his eyes locked on Rick’s own. "with my grandfather. The first time I climbed a mountain – I thought that he had died." * * * Arizona – twenty one years ago1 "You see, there is Abedabun, the place we call the ‘Sight of Day’, and we really are nearly there Little Flame" The old man smiles at his grandchild as he beckons him to keep walking, pointing ahead to no more than a few hundred feet away where a large grey butte, bigger than any other that is visible, rises up from the desert floor, reaching up into the lightening sky as if it was a finger pointing to the heavens above. Seeing that his grandson is about to say something, the older man laughs out loud, forestalling him. " … and until this ceremony is over, and you take the first step into manhood with the first light of the day, the same way that our People have for many years, greeting Father Sun from the top of Abedabun, you are still Little Flame to me. I may be the Shaman of our tribe to everyone else, but you are my grandson Jay, blood of my blood, life of my soul, and even after you have your adult name today, and even when you have journeyed into adult hood, many years from now, you will ALWAYS be my Little Flame. Now stop your arguing and hurry up, we still have a way to go, and a climb ahead of us, and not much time left to do it … if you are not on the top of the Sight of Day when the first ray of the sun strikes, you will have to be called Little Flame forever!" Seeing the look of horror on his grandsons face, the Shaman turns his head away and swallows back a laugh as his grandson, Little Flame … Jay … overtakes him with a burst of speed, moving straight towards the butte, looking worriedly at the lightening of the skyline to the West. Following behind his quickly moving grandson, the Shaman sighs and looks up to the sky, relishing the freedom and cleanliness of the night, smiling to himself as he watches the butte approach, becoming larger and larger, a slight shadow enveloping both him and his grandson as they finally come to rest at it’s foot. Jay is looking up at the butte, suddenly looking … and perhaps feeling … a lot smaller as he is dwarfed by the rocky formation that stretches up above him, the top almost out of sight as it blends in with the darkness above it. A few beads of sweat appear on his forehead and he staggers a little backwards, his face becoming a little paler. The Shaman reaches out one hand and steadies his grandson, while kneeling down on one knee beside him, staring intently into his face. "Are you ok Jay?" With gulp of air, Jay attempts a smile, but it is tight and drawn. "Sure I am grandfather, I just never realised how high up that is … it looks bigger than I remember" With a small squeeze of his grandson’s shoulder, the Shaman smiles. "Things have a habit of looking different when seen with eyes of fear Jay, but when …" The Shaman is interrupted as Jay vehemently argues. "Hey, I never said I was afraid grandfather ... I NEVER said that … you know that nothing scares me!" The Shaman’s eyes widen slightly, and another smile is held back, only a twitch of his lips betraying his inner emotions, but thankfully for the agitated grandson, he doesn’t notice. "You are right my child, you never said that. … though there is nothing wrong with being afraid you know, as long as you don’t let that fear rule your life, because once the fear rules you then your life will never be the same, you will never be able to take a step without checking over your shoulder to see if it is there. Fear is like a living, breathing thing Jay, it is like an animal that refuses to be tamed, but also refuses to stay in the wild. It will follow you always, and will haunt your nights as it takes up your days … but only if you allow it to … only if you allow it into your heart in the first place instead of facing it down and telling it that you refuse to be ruled by it" Jay doesn’t seem to have heard anything that his grandfather has said, as he eyes have never left the rising butte in front of him, and with a small voice he whispers to his grandfather. "It is so big grandfather, so high and so dark … what if I fall?" Shaking his head slightly, as if realising that his words were not enough, the Shaman gets to his feet, brushing the red sand off his knee. "If you fall Jay then you will get hurt, it is a long way down after all, and Mother Earth may nourish us but she is less than forgiving when you drop on her from a long way up. If you are careful you will not fall though, if you concentrate and climb you will be fine … it is only the fear that can stop you, only the fear that can push you off your perch" Staring at his grandfather, the words of reassurance he obviously wanted to hear not there, Jay looks more and more worried. "You mean that the fear is there … it is real? It can push me off the mountain and make me fall?!?!? … I don’t want to do that grandfather, I don’t want to go up there!" The Shaman shrugs, his eyes bright with an internal fire, and a look of though plays across his face, internal dialogue almost evident there. "Well now Jay, that is your choice to make after all. You don’t need to do this ceremony, you don’t need to take the first step into adult hood, and you don’t need to replace your child name of Little Flame with another do you? … how about you just stay down here while I go up and greet the Sun and send him your regrets?" Jay looks down at his feet, shuffling them through the sand as he moves from one foot to the other, looking very uncomfortable, as with a very small voice he replies. "Ok grandfather … I will wait here. … maybe we can come back sometime and do the ceremony then … maybe some other time …" His voice drifts off, but still he doesn’t look up to meet his grandfather’s face, otherwise he would have seen the look of both tenderness and slyness crossing over it. The Shaman turns to the rock face, and with a few quick moves starts to climb upwards, his movements sure and confident, as if he has done this many times before. As he gets about ten feet up the face, his voice floats back down to his grandson. "I won’t be too long Jay, it is only a short climb really, so you just sit there and wait for me. … and remember to watch out for rattle snakes, they get hungry this time of the day, and one bite and you will be unconscious in seconds!" With that the Shaman continues his climb, quickly moving upwards as Jay watches. Not quite disappearing into the darkness, which is becoming lighter and lighter with each passing moment, the Shaman reaches the top of the butte, and with a grunt of effort pulls himself over the top and out of sight. Still looking disconsolate, Jay keeps his eyes on the top, waiting to see his grandfather reappear, but suddenly has to step backwards as a chunk of rock is dislodged from the top as a hand falls limply over the edge, and a small grunt of something … pain? … is heard from above. Dodging the rock, Jay looks upwards, his eyes wide as they focus on the unmoving hand, and with worry evident in his voice he shouts. "Grandfather … what is it … are you ok?!??!" Waiting for a few seconds, Jay listens for a response, but gets none, and the hand still lies limply over the edge, unmoving. With the sound of worry starting to sound more like panic, Jay shouts again. "GRANDFATHER!?!?!?!?" His eyes becoming wider with fear, Jay looks around him, seeing nothing but miles upon miles of open and empty landscape, no sign of another human being within sight. Looking up at the top of the butte, seeing the hand there, sweat again breaks out on Jay’s head as he takes a couple of steps forward and grabs the rock face, leaning his head against it as droplets of water roll down his face. It is tears though … running freely from his eyes to make their path unchecked down his cheeks … and not sweat. With a choked voice, Jay’s knuckles whiten as his grip on the rock tightens, and he pulls himself slowly … ever so slowly … up the rock face, his voice coming out in small, choked breaths as he talks … whispers … to himself. "I am coming grandfather, I am coming … you just stay there, I will be there in a minute" Getting about thirty feet up the rock face, Jay pauses for breath, looking down as a gasp escapes his throat, his hands locking on to the rock as they almost form claws that refuse to let go. His eyes widen in total terror. "Oh no … grandfather … I am going to fall … I am going to fall … help me please … help me grandfather." There is no response from above, and with movements that are so slow as to be almost unnoticeable Jay looks upwards as his hands un-claw themselves, a small rivulet of blood running down from his left one as the pressure on the rock face is released, reaching for another hold. His grandfather’s hand has still not moved, and so with small movement after small movement, Jay pulls himself up until with an explosive gasp of relief and tears he pulls him self over the top of the ledge and collapses against the unmoving body of his grandfather. Panting, Jay stares at the man, looking for signs of damage … even for signs of life, and then gives a small, high-pitched shriek as his grandfather’s left arm … the one that is dangling over the edge, wraps itself around his shoulder, pulling him into a close embrace. "Glad that you could join me my Little Flame, and proud I am too" Jay pushes back from his grandfather, his fist actually striking out to pummel the older man’s chest before the Shaman grabs them both in his own hands and listens as Jay almost explodes with words. "You were faking! There was nothing wrong with you! How could you do that to me grandfather … how could you make me think that you were hurt … or worse Why did you trick me like that … why?!?!?" Brushing his hand through his grandson’s hair, reassuring him and quietening him at the same time, the Shaman gives a small smile as he answers. "I didn’t trick you Jay, I just decided that I would have a lie down up here and watch the stars. … it was your own mind, your own fears, that made you think anything else" Seeing the anger reappear in his grandson’s eyes, the Shaman quickly keeps speaking. " … and think of this Jay. You had two fears trying to make you their own tonight; you had two fears competing to rule your life. You had the fear of making this climb in case you fell … and you had the fear for me and my safety." Tears again fall down Jay’s cheeks as he speaks, his voice soft. "I thought that you were hurt grandfather, I was so afraid. I am a coward!!" The Shaman’s voice is equally soft, but filled with authority, and at his tone, Jay looks up into his face. "You were not a coward Jay, a coward is someone that refuses to face his fears and instead let’s them rule his life for every day that he walks the land. He never truly lives though Jay, because allowing fear to rule you is not living at all. Also someone who refuses to admit that they are afraid is just as much of a coward … or an idiot … because no one can be totally free of fear, and trying to pretend otherwise is just another form of fear itself. It is only a brave man that can face his fears my child, can face them and stand up to them, admitting that they are there but staring them down and putting them to one side. Only a brave man would have made that climb tonight Jay, only a brave man would put his fear for someone else ahead of his own fear. You could have fallen Jay, but you kept trying. You beat your fear Little Flame. You climbed the mountain. I am proud of you, so very proud!" Realisation dawns in Jay’s eyes, the tears drying on his cheeks as no new ones fall, and with a quick movement he throws himself into his grandfather’s arms, hugging him tight. "I love you grandfather … and I don’t mind if you call me Little Flame really you know?" With a laugh the Shaman ruffle’s his grandson’s hair, standing up as he points to the West, where the sun is beginning to crest, it’s first rays crawling over the land towards the butte. "Well, in a few minutes you will have your new name Jay, your spirit name" Watching the sun rise, the Shaman begins to chant, his words forming almost a song of words that cannot be understood but still seem to resonate with some long forgotten memory. At the first light touches Jay’s head, the Shaman throws back his head and shouts out. "AIYANA HAKAN … I name you Eternal Flame!!!" As the sun continues it’s climb into full daylight, the Shaman reaches down to his newly christened grandson, smiling at him as their hands join. "Nayeli my Little Flame, Nyeli … I love you too. * * * Hawaii – three months ago "Wow," Rick breathed, when Phoenix finished speaking. "You never told me that before!" "I hadn’t thought of it in a while" Jay admitted quietly. "But so many memories, so many thoughts, have been running through my mind recently that it was bound to crop up …" "No, not the story," Rick interrupted. "Though I kinda meant that too … I meant your name. You never told me about that name, before!" "Oh yeah," Jay smiled, blushing slightly as he looked at Rick. "That is sort of why I wanted to bring you up here." "You made me climb a mountain just to tell me your grandfather’s nickname for you?" "I made you climb a little mountain, Rick," Jay joked, "for two reasons. My name – that thing that you call a nickname – is only part of it. You see that was my spirit name, my true name. It is something that I was meant to keep private and secret – with a true name much harm can be done. Grandfather told me to never tell it to any one …" "… but you told me?!" "You are the exception to the rule, Rick," Jay stated so softly that Rick had to lean forwards to hear him. "You didn’t let me finish you see. Grandfather told me to never tell it to anyone apart from the one …" "The one what?" "Damn it, Rick, but this isn’t easy for me!" Getting to his feet Jay walked a few feet further onto the grass and, with a shake of his head in confusion, Rick followed him. Reaching Jay he – gently – reached out and placed a hand on his shoulder, waiting for him to speak. As he did so he looked past him and gasped. A small fire was laid out, surrounded by rocks, beside a deep blue pool of water. Beside this rested a blanket laid out on the grass and a covered basket. Staring at it Rick didn’t realise that his hand had tightened on Phoenix’s shoulder. "What’s this?" Rick asked, forgetting what they had been talking about. "This place is beautiful!" "This," Jay stated, quietly, "is why I wanted you to come up here. This, Rick, is for you." "For me?" Rick asked. "Why?" "To make a start," Jay answered, reaching up and taking Rick’s hand in his own. "To try to begin to say how sorry I am for … well for everything …" "Jay," Rick started to argue, "you don’t have to …" "Yes," Jay interrupted, squeezing Rick’s hand. "I do Rick. I do. I have so much to apologies for, so much to make up to you for. I have realised just how selfish I have been, how much I have wallowed in my own self-pity …" "You had good reason, Jay," Rick pointed out, staring at his hand held in Jay’s. "With everything that Ember did …" "This wasn’t him, Rick," Jay stated, adamantly, "everything that I have said and done to you in the last few months has all been me. I was hurting and, selfishly, took that out on you. I don’t expect you to be able to forgive me, at least not right away, but I wanted to try to at least start to show you how sorry I am … how much your mean to me." "By making me climb a mountain?" Rick quipped again, his voice hoarse as he tried to swallow a lump in his throat. "From the top of the world, Rick," Jay whispered. "You gie me wings and, with you, I feel like I am flying high. I always have, I always will – it just took me this long to realise …" " … realise?" "What you said to me," Jay turned to look at Rick, holding tight to his hand. "In Detroit when you told me that you loved …" "Don’t worry about it Jay," Rick interrupted, embarrassed. "You don’t have to say anything … I will be fine … I will get over it …" "I hope not, you idiot!" Jay laughed, softly, stepping closer. A matter of inches separated the two men, their eyes locked as the moment stretched into eternity. "I told you that my grandfather told me to never share that name with anyone apart from one person, didn’t I?" "Yes …" Rick felt his heart beating, sure that it would burst out of his chest at any moment. "Who?" "With the one person that I would trust my life with, Rick," Jay breathed, the words leaving his mouth and entering Rick’s own on his breath. "With my soul mate. With the person who loved me, wholly and completely and who I loved in return." "… but you shared it with me?" Rick asked, his vision swimming in a rainbow haze of tears. "I know, Rick. I realised yesterday, when thinking about my father, that everyone that I loved had left me - had died. Then you told me that you loved me and I was afraid. I was afraid of what I felt, of what people would think and that you would leave too," Jay whispered. "I'm not going to leave you, Jay," Rick sighed, softly. "I looked for you for so long, I never gave up hope and I went to Hell - literally - to get you back ..." "I know Rick. That is why I am not afraid. Not anymore ... and I hope that you won't mind spending one night out doors. With me." Jay breathed, indicating the blanket and fire, before leaning forwards until his lips barely … softly … gently … touched Rick’s. "I love you." * * * 1UT Flashabck
