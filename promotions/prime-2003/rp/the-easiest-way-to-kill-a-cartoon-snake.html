The Easiest Way to Kill a Cartoon Snake...

April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/9/08-4/23/08 Prologue: Two weeks ago. Wichita, Kansas. A woman dressed like Lindsay Troy and a man with a drawl so thick it could barely be pierced were talking in the hallways backstage at the Kansas Coliseum. "Yes, and some day, you'll be a real boy, Pinocchio," Cozen told Wade Elliott -- right before her pocket started to buzz. As the Faceless Fighter made her excuses and stepped away from the 'Bama Bruiser, she answered that telephone. What happened later is more noteworthy, with Cozen's defeat of Lindsay Troy. What happened then may end up more important. ---- The yellow legal pad gets a liberal workout from Cozen's chewed on tiny nub of a green pencil, picked up from a waitress outside Beatrice, Nebraska who did not have a life worth living. I asked him how he got this number. My number. Mine, not yours. What's yours is mine and what's mine is mine. I tried to be all slick and controlled. I'm fucking Hannibal Lechter. "How did you get this number, Clarice?" The speaker on the other end was an asshole my enemy a cartoon snake. (He wasn't really a cartoon snake, though I talked to one in my dreams that night. We were in the Garden of Evil and he asked me to take down the apple. I shoved it down his throat and he stopped talking.) I like to picture people when I talk to them. It's part of what I do, what I am, I am nothing, am I evil? The man on the other end of the line would have made a good Disney villain, I think. Probably a snake, with a fat face and a Fu Manchu mustache -- perhaps a fez. I HATE HIM. "Does it matter?" he asked me. Of course it matters. Everything that matters, matters. Everything I am, I was. Everything I was, I did. Everything I did mattered. I told him so. "Of course it matters." <--See? "Focus on the present," he said. His voice was soothing, calming, like a babbling brook. I wanted to lose myself in it, couldn't. Wanted to be like it, wouldn't. "The present matters." Santa never brought me any presents. "The present matters, Ruse." I came up short. I'd like to claim it was a victim an innocent a pussy an intern that halted my progress. I wanted to hit him tear out his throat and eat it shout at the kid, but I just smiled my Lindsay smile and he blushed. Why does everyone love her? In truth, it was that word. "Ruse." (n.) A crafty stratagem, a subterfuge. I am made of lies. I am made of ruses. I am made of Ruse. No. I am Cozen. "Cozen." I corrected him, my words clipped like a hedge, clipped like a British person. I remember the Brit lady I met...she liked square-toed shoes and cursed like a sailor. "You arse-bandit. Don't badmouth th' dead." "My apologies." He sounded gracious; I bet he spread his hands when he said it. "Haven't answered me question, bumtag." "I represent certain... interests who would like to see you succeed." The pause was probably more than three dots. It was more like, "I represent certain...........interests." I was supposed to ask what kind of interests. It wasn't inferred implied, it was outright, all in my face. "ASK ME WHAT I'M TALKING ABOUT!" he was shouting without shouting. I don't respond well to peer pressure. "Tell me what your name is, sugar." "My name is Khenti al-Alwari. Though you do not truly wish this information, you are just making conversation, trying to get me to let down my guard." "No," I told him. "I asked because I like to know who I'm hanging up on." And I did... well, as viciously as you can hang up a cellular telephone. ---- Khenti al-Alwari was a tall man of Egyptian (he would never say "Middle Eastern") descent who lived a life that many of his countrymen would have given their left testicle to enjoy. That was the very metaphor-slash-bad joke that Al-Alwari was telling at a dinner table of some of the most reasonable ne'er-do-wells he knew, several days after the woman going by the pseudonym "Cozen" hung up on him. "--their left testicle!" He chortled to himself, raising his glass of thick, purple wine to his thick, (now stained) purple lips. The laughter from the rest of the table was not as uproarious, but Khenti al-Alwari was a man known as much for his risqué sense of humor as for his many jowls. Plus they'd heard the joke before. "Welcome to my home, my friends!" he exclaimed, wiping off his lips with the back of his forearm and slamming the goblet back onto the white tablecloth, hard enough that it would have sloshed had he not drained it dry. Khenti's guests bowed their heads respectfully. The rotund Egyptian clapped his hands together, summoning the serving girls, who came with large plates of beef (no pork). The bodyguard of his guest of honor clapped thick hands together; it was the only sound other than the clinking of silverware against plates. Dinner conversation was dull, drab, and uninteresting; a careful façade. "If you will excuse me," Khenti said, pushing away from the table and throwing his wadded-up cloth napkin atop the haphazard stack of forks and knives on the table. "I have to piss." Khenti strode briskly through the hallways of his palatial mansion, stopping only to adjust the positioning of a tall vase. Al-Alwari's servants had long since learned to keep their eyes down as they bustled through the hallways -- that was how he liked them, quiet, pretty, and of Egyptian decent. The sons and daughters of Cleopatra, waiting on him, hand and foot. It was good to be a king, even if most people had never heard his name. In retrospect, he should have known something was up, because he didn't have a serving girl that tall. In his defense, however, he really had to pee. ---- Have you ever listened to Guns N' Roses' "Sweet Child o' Mine"? "Her hair reminds me of a warm, safe place where as a child, I'd hide." Her hair. Reminds him of a warm, safe place. Where as a child, he'd hide. Her hair. And they say I'm crazy. My hair. It was dark, black. Black like the abyss, black like death, black like shadows that fall and dance. Not like hers. Hers has character. Hers has life. Lindsay Troy has such pretty hair. No, it was a nice shade of black, like I'd pulled the sky down around my head. It was what had attracted me to her, rushing away from the market with a bag of fresh bread. It was a rush job, like with Lindsay. (STUPID IDIOT! WHY DIDN'T YOU TAKE YOUR TIME??) But it would last, and I wouldn't forget. He came around the corner and I dropped my eyes, obsequious subservient his bitch. "Good evening, lord," I said as he walked past. He did not look twice at me -- no one does no one ever should. Once he passed, I turned. I smashed his face into a marble wall. Again and again beat it like a drum. I dragged him, tugged and pulled, into a spare room. When his blurry eyes refocused, I was there. Standing over him. "Hiya," I said. It wasn't the most intimidating greeting I've ever come up with. ---- "Hiya," the girl who was not Khenti Al-Alwari's servant girl said to him. "Mind telling me what's up, son?" "I... was not expecting to see you." "Lindsay Troy didn't expect to be so tired." "I do not know of what you speak." "You don't watch wrestling?" "No." Cozen tipped her head to the side, eyes narrowing in consideration. "Oh," she said at length. "I am not interested in you because you have a pretty belt buckle." Khenti fairly spat the words, his disgust ruining the round lines of his face. She drove a knee into his nose. "Do not -- " A second knee cracked a tooth. "Talk bad!" The third made him spit blood. "About my Lindsay!" "You maniac! What, are you planning to beat me into unconsciousness?" "To death, I was thinking." The fourth blow from her bare knee crossed Khenti's eyes, slumping him to the floor. "Why did you call me break my train of thought! I had to beat Lindsay, break her, make her cry. You could have ruined everything and you don't care about my career?" She put a bare foot to the man's Adam's apple. "Here's a hint, jerkwad. Wednesday nights are a bad time to bug me!" Lips painted a faint shade of red curled away from a face that was more reminiscent of Lindsay Troy's Biopic with an all-Middle Eastern cast. "Who do you work for?" "I don't... gak... I do not work for anyone." "Lies! I hear them, speaking them, your lips are moving, so you must be lying." She drove a knee and her shin into the man's ribcage. "You work for someone all of us do everyone but not me not anymore. I'm the boss of me." He doubled over, crossing his arms over his prodigious belly. "You are mad." "Mad-angry or mad-crazy? Isn't it great that the word means everything and nothing and everything that is nothing?" "You are an insane person and I want you out of my house." Cozen's eyes narrowed. Her lips pursed. The tips of her toes pushed on his lips, stretching his mouth into uncomfortable positions. Her voice gained a whisper-faint quality, steely. "You are not in position to negotiate, sugar. Way I see it: you're on your back and I could kill you seventeen ways and be gone before anyone else knew I was here. Way you see it: I am all-powerful and godlike, no one could ever harm me. Which one of us is right, do you figure?" "What do you want?" Khenti's eyes were wide; he looked up to the Faceless Fighter. "I can give you money... power..." "I want answers. I want the truth! I can't handle the truth, can I?" She tipped her head to the side, considering again. Studying him as a young child might study a fly right before they ripped off its wings. "No, I think I can handle the truth. So tell me who you work for." Khenti's lips pulled away from his lips. "You are -- " She put one leg across his chest, the other knee across his throat, holding herself aloft by a shelf in the closet. "Your next words had better be an answer, snake, or I lean down and silence you forever." The door to the maintenance room creaked open, drawing Cozen's attention to the figure standing within. "He works for me," the intruder said. "But feel free to kill him if it makes you feel better." Lindsay Troy's hazel eyes (Cozen couldn't bring herself to change the pretty, pretty color) locked onto the slight figure on the other side of the door, wide with shock. She did not like not being in control of the situation. "You don't think I'll do it?" She leaned back, forcing her weight down onto her left knee, pressing against Khenti's trachea. "I'll do it! I'm fuckin' crazy, lady. Everyone tells me so." "I am certain you will do it," the speaker said calmly, even matter-of-factly. "It is how you removed Lindsay Troy from her Universal Championship." "Well, that and a booby-trapped box, because I knew she'd try to turn my weapons against me. It's what she does." "Indeed. I was hoping we could... chat, though I must admit I was not expecting you to find your way out here to Khenti's abode." "Yeah, well. Momma always said I'm creative." "That you are. I will be in the dining room. Come and visit when you are finished here. I am certain we can benefit each other." Khenti al-Alwari's face had turned a mixture of red and white, but he was able to turn his head to the side. "Dir -- " He broke off, scratching at Cozen's thigh with hands far too well-manicured to do any damage in that manner. If one were to lean down quite close to Khenti's lips, they might hear one softly sighed word escape his lips. "Why?" His guest-of-honor did do so, however (instead choosing to close the door to give Cozen some privacy), so he did not receive an answer. Minutes later, Cozen stood, nearly skipping through the door. She tipped her head to the side, like a bird or a dinosaur, looking back to the dining room and down to the exit. She chose to walk to the dining room. ---- I have met the best person in the world. We sat down, we chatted. I think we share outlooks on a lot of things. (Is that a good thing?) I haven't agreed to anything, can't make my mind up, can't think gotta think. I have met the greatest person in the world. Claims she wants to be me kill me save me help me. I don't like people who want to help me. Help has always been a steady diet of tranquilizers and antipsychotic medication; they give me a headache bouts of sleepiness RAGE. But she let me do whatever I wanted, let me break the man who nearly broke my concentration, so hard to be Lindsay, gotta think, sarcasm pop culture love life he loves her. I have met the finest example of humanity. And she is just like me. Isn't that great?
