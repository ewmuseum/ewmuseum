Unchained

4 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/05/2007 Today The Stakiri Building New York City 6:30 am Despite the other seven people in the bedroom with him, it was the sunlight bounding in from the uncovered window that first woke Jonathan Winters from his much needed slumber. He allowed a length yawn, before even attempting to open his eyes. “Wake up,” the familiar voice ordered. Finally opening his eyes and allowing them a vital few seconds to focus he found his illustrious employer, Cornelius Stakiri, looming at the foot of his, the man he recognised to be Dr. Medelov standing next to him and five of Stakiri’s well-armed men, each point heavy-set automatic weapons at his head. Winters pushed himself up from the mattress and rested back upon his elbows, now gaining a better view of the room and the danger that lay before him. He grinned and rubbed the sleep away from his eyes. “You don’t seem surprised to see us, Winters,” Stakiri observed. “Precisely why is that?” “Because I’m too tried to even care why you’re here, Stakiri,” Winters replied. “Something’s obviously gone wrong and I’m the first one to blame.” Agitated, Dr. Medelov lunges forward at Winters with fire in his eyes. “Enough of this! Tell me where she is! Tell me now!” Stakiri rolled his eyes and held back the good doctor with a single arm. “Don’t so dramatic, Medelov. He’ll rip you in half, you moron.” Dr. Medelov ceased his laughable tirade and turned to Stakiri his face red with rage. “But he kidnapped her! He kidnapped Aeryn!” Winters raised a speculative eyebrow. “Aeryn’s gone?” Stakiri sighed. “And why is she so important to you, Medelov?” Dr. Medelov grew quiet and left the room, whilst Stakiri sighed and glared at Winters for the longest time. Winters cast his eyes of Stakiri’s heavily armed men in turn and then stared back at Winters questioningly. “And you accuse Medelov of dramatics?” “I thought a sufficient display of power would gain your attention,” Stakiri explained. “And yes, Aeryn has gone missing. Wouldn’t happen to know anything about that, would you?” “The last time I saw Aeryn was two weeks ago,” Winters informed him, sitting upright and leaning back against the wall. “I tried to see her a couple of days back, but if you recall, I wasn’t allowed to see her.” “Tell me where she is, Winters!” Stakiri demanded, finally losing his cool. Winters chuckled and rested both hands behind his head lazily. “Just what are you implying, Stakiri?” “Cute,” Stakiri said, turning to leave. “Very cute.” “Now if you don’t mind,” Winters began, throwing his legs over the side of his bed, “I got Lavelle at a PRIME PPV this week and I got a feeling it ain’t gonna be easy.” “When you’re willing to set your terms,” Stakiri told him, “Then you know where to find me.” Winters ginned. After what they’d done, how could he even consider negotiating. Two Weeks Ago The Stakiri Building New York City 14:02 pm Winters stared at her, his mouth wide agape and stance defensive. Tears began to well up in his eyes. He could tell just by looking at Aeryn that something was wrong. Her facial expression was that of puzzlement and her eyes were searching. “Hey,” he greeted, desperately trying to keep it together. “Do-do I know you?” She responded, innocently. Winters’ heart sank and his gaze lowered. Unsure of what to do or to say, he simply remained quiet. Confused Aeryn allowed a brief glance to the doctor stood to the left her and, simultaneously, the doctor laid a gentle hand upon her shoulder and shot Winters a sympathetic smile. “I’m afraid she’s very disorientated at the moment,” the Doctor explained. His voice calm and his face bizarrely unreadable. “Winters,” Stakiri began, gesturing grandly toward the doctor. “This is Dr. Medelov. He’s in charge of Aeryn’s recuperation.” The doctor smiled, realigned his spectacles and then extended a hand in a feeble greeting which Winters promptly ignored. Dr. Medelov developed a confused expression and glanced questioningly at Stakiri, who rolled his eyes in response. Slightly abashed, the good doctor continued regardless. “Uhm…the extent of the enemy mind programming was extensive and hard to eradicate.” Winters shook his head, so overcome with emotion he could no longer think straight. “What does this mean?” “It means,” the doctor began, pausing to remove his spectacles, “we don’t know to what extent her mind has been damaged.” Displeased with the answer, Winters pushed for instantly clarification. “Will her memories ever return to her?” The doctor winced. “It’s hard to tell at this stage, but that eventuality doesn‘t seem likely.” Winters punched the wall to the side of him, leaving a sizeable dent in his wake. “I’m sorry,” Aeryn called after him, as he stormed away, “I didn’t mean to upset you!” Five Days Ago The Stakiri Building New York City 18:06 pm “Ugh,” Winters allowed, slipping into the comfortable embrace of the waiting area’s luxurious leather sofa. He rested his travel bag upon the floor next to him and grinned as he caught the elderly receptionists disapproving glare in doing so. “Tell Dr. Medelov I’m here to see Aeryn.” The receptionist did not attempt to mask her disgruntled sigh as she lifted the receiver to her ear. Within seconds she was connected with good doctor. “He’s here. Yes, him. Okay.” The receptionist replaced the received and cast her eyes back over the paperwork she’d been sifting through as Winters had entered the research lab waiting room. “The doctor will be with you in a second.” Winters nodded a response that the receptionist couldn’t possibly see and gently rubbed his eyes. It’d been the better part of a week since he saw her last. It’d been a week since his world fell apart all around him yet again, since he discovered the love of his life barely even recognised him. After that he simply couldn’t stick around and let the unbearable pain dig deeper. He needed the time away. He needed to get his thoughts together. He needed to find the strength to deal with this. Luckily, he had his wrestling career occupy his time -- and luckily was a word used liberally in that sentence. All of that shit with Charity, Lavelle, Dusk and Shakur was beginning to get to him like nothing else within his relatively short-lived wrestling career ever had and the cracks were beginning to show-- Fuck that! Dismiss those thoughts here and now, you fucking pussy! He silently demanded. You’re here to see Aeryn and your problems are the last thing she needs piled on top of her own right about now. Get a grip-- The heavily fortified lab door hissed open and Dr. Medelov emerged, making sure to heave shut the door behind him. He composed himself for a few seconds further, before heading over toward Winters. Winters was already on his feet and wearing a decidedly questioning expression. Greetings were the very last thing on his mind. “Where is she?” Dr. Medelov produced a soothing smile and held both of is hands defensively. “Jonathan, I--” Winters ignored him. “Where’s Aeryn, Medelov?” Dr. Medelov sighed. “I’m afraid you can’t see her, Jonathan.” Winters face visibly enflamed with anger, but he managed to maintain his composure. “What?” “I’m afraid our psychologists believe that your presence may serve to further confuse, Aeryn,” Dr Medelov calmly explained. “Rest assured, they only have her best interests at--” Before the good doctor can even finished the sentence, Winters had him up by the collar of his immaculate white jacket. “Best interests? Best interests?” Winters screamed. “You don’t even know the woman!” “With the greatest respect, Jonathan,” Dr. Medelov began, “neither do you. Not anymore.” Winters grunted something under his breath and released Medelov from his vice-like grip. It would do no good taking his frustrations out upon this poor deluded fool. He was taking orders and just like everyone else in this damn building, he was taking the orders from but one man. Winters left in silence. --- “Calm down, Winters,” Cornelius Stakiri told him, his voice gentle and desperately attempting to sooth his anger. “You need to just calm down.” “You presume to tell me what I need, Stakiri?“ Winters kinda figured his illustrious employer knew him better than that. “Well, I--” Winters ignored him. “And you want me to be calm? How the fuck am I supposed to be calm about this after everything?” Stakiri rubbed his forehead and slowly bringing his hand down to the bridge of his nose. “You’re not, I guess.” “You guess? You fucking guess?” Winters screamed, unable to control himself any longer. “Winters, what do you want me to say?” Stakiri pleaded, subtly backing away from the much bigger man. “I’m just as cut up about this as you are!” “Yeah,” Winters responded, allowing an angry chuckle, “sure you are!” Stakiri’s eyes narrowed. “And just what are you implying?” “Nothing,” Winters muttered, turning his back to Stakiri and making his way towards the exit. His mind was already made up and it was abundantly clear he would receive no help from his illustrious employer. “Nothing at all.” Yesterday The Stakiri Building New York City 00:06 am “Jonathan,” The delicate voice softly whispered, “is-is that you?” Winters allowed a smile despite himself and their present circumstances. The last time he’d seen her, she didn’t even recognise him and now, she at least knew his first name. It was progress and at that point, he couldn’t ask for much more. Quickly, Winters righted himself and focused his back on the task at hand, placing a finger over his mouth, effectively calling for silence. Aeryn, still bemused, nodded in acceptance and Winters handed her a rather sizeable black cagoule. She took it reluctantly, unsure as to what Winters intentions truly were. Winters glanced around and, once confident nobody was within listening distance, decided to break the silence, “Put it on.” Aeryn looked at him. “Why?” “Because I’m getting you out of here,” Winters told her, his eyes still darting all around. “Won’t Dr. Medelov be angry?” Winters grinned again. “I hope so.” “But I--” “Aeryn,” Winters began, pausing to consider precisely to put this to her, “Dr. Medelov and Stakiri don’t want to help you get better.” “But they are being so nice to me…” Winters nodded. “Perhaps so, but to help themselves. They want to know what you remember from your time with the enemy. They want to know precisely what you learned before you were captured and mentally altered. They have anything but your best interests at heart.” Aeryn developed a thoughtful expression. “But you do?” “Yes, Aeryn,” Winters said with conviction, suddenly unconcerned about the volume of his voice, “I do.” Aeryn simply glared at him for the longest time, before finally climbing into the overbearing cagoule. “Let’s go.” Winters grinned. “Follow me.”
