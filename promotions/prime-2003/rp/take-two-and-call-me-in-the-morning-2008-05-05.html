Take Two and Call Me in the Morning

5 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 5, 2008 "I don't like the way these MRIs look, Troy" Troy Douglas had probably heard Dr. Susan Owens say some derivation of that sentence at least fifty times over the last ten years. He could keep heading into her Center City office for the next fifty years, and he'd hear it even more. Hippocratic Oath, and all that. "What's the damage this time, Sue?" "The ligaments in your knee are pretty badly damaged, Troy. They've been repaired and replaced so many times that the stress is beginning to warp them. You can only delay major reconstructive surgery so long. If you don't get the full procedure done soon, you're seriously at risk of blowing out the knee for good." "Oh. And the bad news is what, exactly?" "Ooooh, Big Mister Sarcasm, here." "C'mon, I thought I was at least a little bit subtle with that one." "You were, are and always will be as subtle as a brick to the head." "Hey. Don't knock it till you try it, Doc." This was why Troy still shipped himself back to the City of Brotherly Love whenever he needed to be looked at by a doctor. He'd developed a rapport and a level of trust with this particular physician that he wasn't going to find with any in-house doctor. Plus, she'd kinda performed the procedures that had allowed him to walk, let alone wrestle, so you can forgive him for playing favorites once in a while. "This is serious, Troy. What happened to you last week against Crucifix, it only served to quicken the deterioration in your knee." "I'm fine. I told you that already. The knee's just a little sore. You know me, Sue. I've gone through a hell of a lot worse than this. We'll wait until after UltraViolence, you'll go in and clean everything up and I'll go back to normal." "Not this time." "What?" "Arthroscopic surgery isn't going to work this time. The ligaments are too badly damaged. If we don't set a date for reconstructive surgery soon, you won't make it past UltraViolence." Considering that he hadn't been able to put any weight on his right leg since last week's ReVolution, he didn't doubt her sincerity. Troy knew his ligaments were hanging on by a continually fraying thread. He knew that every time he stepped into the ring, he was moments away from being taken out on a stretcher. He also knew that a surgery like this could keep him on the shelf for months, and losing all that time meant losing a whole lot of opportunities that he wasn't willing to pass up. After rocketing into PRIME with his performance in the Dual Halo and his win over Tony Rolo, the last month hadn't been all too kind to him. Maybe the fact that it felt like flaming knives were driving into his knee ligaments was finally coming back to bite him, but Troy knew that if he stopped now, there was a good chance -- surgery or not -- that he'd never be able to recover from a sudden, violent loss of momentum. "Are there any other options?" "Nothing certain. This is the only way you know your knee'll fully recuperate." "Anything, Sue. You know what I went through to get this far, you know that I'm willing to take any chance. Right?" "Right." "So, I'll ask you again. Are there any other options? Anything at all that can keep me on my feet." "There are some doctors at the University of Chicago that've been developing some experimental treatments. They're looking for people with damaged ligaments to be a part of their alpha testing phase." "Is it legit?" "I'll admit to not reading all of the research to this point, but if it works, it could replace a number of time-tested surgical options." "Is it safe?" "This is medicine, Troy. Nothing's 100 percent sure, nothing's 100 percent safe. It's a cutting edge procedure that's barely been used on humans, and there's a good chance you'll end up needing the surgery anyway." "I'll do it. I'll be in Chicago next week before UltraViolence. Get my name on that list, Sue." "And if it doesn't work?" "I'll go in for the surgery." "That's a good boy. Now, I'm keeping you off your feet until you--" "No." "What?" "I'm not taking a week off, Sue. My name's on the card, and whether I've got ligaments or not, I'm going to be in that ring." "This is a bad idea, and as a licensed medical expert, I'm telling you right now that I don't endorse this." "Duly noted, Doc. But, when have I ever stopped going through with my bad ideas?" ******************************************** From the Files of Troy Douglas: See, couldn't get rid of me yet, PRIMEates. I'm kind of like a cockroach that way. No matter how many times you think you've got me down, no matter how many times you think I can't possibly come back, I keep dragging my busted up, tired ass back to the ring week after week. Kind of pathetic when you think about it, huh? I came in here with a bang, ladies and gentlemen, but in the past few weeks it has been much more like a whimper. Maybe I've let pride get the best of me, maybe that busted up, tired ass I was talking about early has finally betrayed me, but as you've all seen, the last month hasn't been the kindest to me in the old win-loss column. Last week, when I stepped in against Crucifix, it all came to a head. I had the guy right where I wanted him. I had him in the spot that was going to get my PRIME career back on the right track, and then two things happened. First, I made a mistake and gave a dangerous man a giant opening. Second, that giant opening happened to be a knee that has a bad habit of being like a bungee cord that can snap away at any moment. Bit of a double whammy, if you don't mind me use of hackneyed phrases. But, my knee's in fine enough shape this time around, so this week at ReVolution in Milwaukee, Jason Natas is going to have a hell of a lot of trouble on his hands, no matter how many good legs I'm operating on. Besides, why not keep scaling the Cliffs of Insanity until that bungee cord eventually snaps and sends me hurtling into the Pit of Despair? Hey. "Princess Bride" references. Ten bucks to whomever had that in the "Dated Pop Culture Jokes" pool on the PRIME message boards. Don't think I don't read those things, you weird little trolls. Well, the wonderful little pills that my doctor gave me today are starting to kick in, so I'm going to have to wrap this one up a little short today, before what I'm writing becomes even more disturbing and nonsensical. Looking forward to seeing all you Cheeseheads this week at ReVolution. Hope you're not tired of me yet. Oh, and please don't bring anything capable of cutting bungee cords. I'd really like to hold onto my knee for a little while longer. I've grown attached to it over the past 32 years. Okay, that horrible pun was probably the painkillers typing. Onward and upward, kids. See you at the end of the road.
