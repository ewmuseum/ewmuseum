Put The Goddamn Gun Away, Fritz

9 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/09/09 It was another long, slow day. For over a year now, the economy has taken its toll on Fritz Kaeding’s Mercedes Benz dealership on the outskirts of Dallas, Texas. Sure, cars were still rolling off the lot, but the pace was little more than a crawl compared to the boom of a few years ago. Back in 2006 he could barely keep enough cars on the lot. Now, he’s had to cut down his staff by half and maintain reduced inventory levels in order to keep overhead down. The namesake of the dealership, Fritz Kaeding, leans up over his desk grey steel with a sigh. The fluorescent lighting reflects off his shiny bald head as he buries his face in his hands, rubbing his eyes with his fingers as if trying to gouge them right out of the sockets. A red and black striped tie hangs loosely around the neck of his short sleeved white dress shirt. The desk is a mess of inventory sheets, manifests, and other miscellaneous paperwork that makes it appear that he had a busy day. You know what they say about appearances, though. The slightly overweight man retrieves a pair of reading glasses from the breast pocket of his shirt, flicking his wrist to open them before shoving them onto his head. He next reaches and pulls the top side drawer of the desk open, reaching in and fumbling around until he produces a bottle of Tums. Fritz pops the top and pours four or five of the antacids directly into his mouth, pruning his face as he chews them into chalky submission. He tosses the bottle back into the drawer and slams the door shut, the steel against steel sound echoing throughout the empty dealership. “Three cars?” Fritz looked over his sales report for the day. Another heavy sigh leaves his lungs as he tosses the sheet haphazardly back onto the desk. “Fuck.” As he sits there in the solitude of his office, slouched over dejectedly, Fritz suddenly perks up. His head snaps up and he cranes his neck, hearing the sound of footsteps walking across his showroom. The portly man quickly reaches under the desk where his chair is situated and carefully peels something away from some Velcro underneath. When his hand becomes visible again, it holds a leather gun case with a .38 revolver inside. With a stealth that belies his size, Fritz gingerly moves around the desk and takes a position just inside the door to his office. He nuzzles up close to the wall as he removes the gun from the leather case. As the footsteps draw closer, Fritz winces as he quietly cocks the hammer of the gun. Holding the weapon tightly with both hands he raises it up in front of his face, his wide eyes darting back and forth as if they are looking for the sound waves of the footsteps coming through the door. He can almost feel his heart jumping out of his chest as the footsteps shuffle to a stop just outside his door. Beads of sweat form on his forehead as Fritz anxiously awaits the intruder. He takes one big breath, holding it in for what seems like an eternity as he sees his hands start to tremble in front of his face. “Put the Goddamn gun away, Fritz.” Fritz closes his eyes and exhales loudly, relaxing the tense muscles in his body. He leans his head against the wall as he uncocks the gun and drops it limply to his side. “You almost gave me a heart attack you son of a bitch!” The mixture of anger and relief in Fritz’ voice is awkward, enhanced by the irregular breathing of a near panic attack. Peeking around the door jam, dressed in a nice looking tan suit, is Tyler Nelson. His brow is furrowed and lips pursed tightly together in a scowl. Seeing that the immediate threat of hot lead has passed, the man known as The Greediest Player in the Game enters the office. Fritz still tries to compose himself as he makes his way back around his desk. “What the hell are you doing here?” Fritz carefully flings the gun onto the desktop, causing a dull thud as it lands on top of a stack of papers. Tyler looks around the office. It’s rather plain in décor, with dull white walls and a book case covering the wall behind the desk. There are several pictures hanging on the wall, most of them framed posters of current and classic Mercedes models. Tyler’s eyes do come across one picture of interest, however, and he strolls over to the wall to get a closer look. Fritz plops down into his chair, still trying to regulate his breathing. “You still have that picture on your wall, huh?” The question is obviously rhetorical. Fritz looks over to where Tyler is standing, squinting his eyes momentarily before nodding his head. “Yeah, sure. No reason to take it down that I know of.” The photograph, an 8x10 glossy of the dealerships ribbon cutting ceremony, features Fritz holding an oversized pair of scissors in preparation of cutting into a large red ribbon. There are several other people in the photo, but one person of interest stands in the back of the crowd. Tyler Nelson. “I thought you said I was bad for business?” Tyler turns around, a smirk on his face. “You were, at the time I said that.” Fritz aims a finger at Tyler. “Even you have to admit that.” Tyler unbuttons his suit jacket and makes his way over to a chair in front of Fritz’ desk. He eases into the chair, adjusting his tie as he tries to get comfortable. “There may have been a time when the name Tyler Nelson might have cast a negative light on things,” Tyler concedes. “Negative light?” Fritz scoffs. “You were like Kryptonite to Superman for a while.” Nelson shrugs his shoulders. He knows how things work, and as much as it pains him to admit it, Fritz was right. When Tyler was having his issues with the IRS, which ultimately led to a prison sentence, there weren’t many people who stood by his side. In fact, no one stood by his side; not that he really expected anyone to. If the roles were reversed, Tyler would have done the exact same thing. Business is business, after all. Fritz spins his chair around toward the bookshelves behind his desk. He reaches and pulls a large, empty binder from its spot, revealing a bottle of scotch and a couple of glasses hiding behind it. The portly man grabs the bottle and glasses then spins back around, placing them on the desk. Fritz pours some scotch into each glass, then holds one out for Tyler. “On the house,” Fritz quips with a smile. Tyler takes the glass, clangs his against Fritz’, then raises it to his mouth. The alcohol flows bitterly over Tyler’s taste buds and down his gullet as he takes a sip of the single malt beverage. Tyler’s sinuses start to drain and his eyes water a bit as he chokes down the scotch; it certainly wasn’t champagne. Fritz, on the other hand, downs the entire contents of his glass like it was water and quickly pours himself another round. “So what brings you in?” asks Fritz. “I know you ain’t in here lookin’ for a new Benzo.” “Why not?” Tyler does his best to seem offended. He’s sure that Fritz knows exactly ‘why not’. “Your money problems aren’t exactly a secret.” There is almost a hint of joy in Fritz’ voice as he talks about someone else’s money issues for a change. “The feds took you to the cleaners, pal.” Tyler frowns, placing the glass down on the desk. “You don’t have to remind me, Fritz. Believe me, I’m reminded of it every single day I have to wear cheap suits, sleep in some God awful no-star motel, or eat greasy truck stop food. I’ve got things moving in the right direction, but I need a few more irons in the fire in order to build myself back up.” “Yeah, I saw you got back in on the wrestling gig. My son watches that crap.” “Wrestling used to be a hobby for me…an outlet for aggression that wasn’t quite appropriate in the boardroom. Now, it’s much more than that. It’s my only means of survival. And that is absolutely unacceptable.” Tyler rises from his chair and begins to slowly pace the office. Fritz screws the cap on the scotch and puts it back on the bookshelf, sensing the gravity of the conversation. “My options are severely limited by having to play by someone else’s rules, Fritz. I need room to move…to breathe.” Tyler turns to face Fritz, who is leaning back in his chair listening intently. “That’s where you come in.” Fritz’ eyes widen slightly. “Me? I’m not a professional wrestler, Tyler. I don’t know what you expect me to…” “I don’t need you in PRIME, Fritz.” He walks back over to the desk, this time taking perch on the corner of the desk. “Quite the opposite. I need you outside of PRIME.” “I don’t understand.” “As you’re well aware, the IRS so graciously relieved me of all my assets. They’re also keeping close tabs on me since I’ve been out of prison. It’s hard to make moves when Big Brother is looking over your shoulder, so I need you to get some things started for me.” “Gosh, Tyler.” There’s extreme hesitation in Fritz’ voice. “I’m having a hard enough time keeping the dealership afloat. I’ve even sold off a lot of my side projects so I can focus on this. The environment for guys like us is really tough right now.” “Just relax, Fritzy.” The smirk on Tyler’s face is as devious as ever. “You just need to sign some documents, and I’ll take things from there. I only need your name to keep the feds off my case. Of course, there will certainly be some bonuses in it for you should things go well. And they will go well.” Fritz sits there quiet for what nearly becomes an uncomfortable amount of time. His business ideology is very similar to Tyler’s, but they broke the mold when they made Tyler Nelson. Fritz knew that if he got in bed, so to speak, with Tyler it was either going to be very good to him, or very bad. There wasn’t going to be any in between. “What about start-up money?” Fritz asks. “They don’t take IOU’s for this kind of stuff.” “We’ll start off small and work our way up.” Tyler explains. The confidence in his voice is convincing. “With this new contract I received from PRIME, I figure I’ll be able to get a few stakes in the ground and see what happens. So, are you in?” It wasn’t as much of a question as one might think. The expectation was there for Fritz to help, and the car salesman was cognizant of Tyler’s ability to parlay small amounts of money into gigantic piles of money. He may not do it legally, but then again, what successful businessman really does? Fritz’ hands weren’t clean by any stretch of the imagination. “Okay,” Fritz concedes. “I’ll do it.” Fritz rises from his chair and extends his hand. Tyler shakes it, a cunning smile on his face. The Greediest Player in the Game was about to embark on a mission to rebuild himself from the ground up. Hopefully no one would be dumb enough to get in his way.
