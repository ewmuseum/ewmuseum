Date: Ten to the deadline 'Oh, COME ON!' Vander Jung was doing his best, and failing miserably. He was trying to do something, anything, to get ahead in the federation. Right now, he was just a road agent. But he was more than that. He was a great manager, and an even better wrestler. But nobody wanted a 6'2' 134 lb. wrestler. Not even one who could fly, at that. Blame it on cancer; chemo takes the weight off. As well as the hair. And the tan. Fuck, he just looked messed up all around. There he was, end of the show, still without little else to do. Taun Pham shot him down for a tryout match. Of course he did, the guy isn't stupid. Nobody would react to a guy like Jung. Well, not in a good way. The last thing PRIME needs is to have a wrestler come out and get laughed without that being the intention. Not good for busMariselas. Pham rolled his eyes, shoving Vander out of the door. 'You're a good agent. Just stick to that.' And the door closed. Jung wasn't going to give up, though. He had to do something. If not wrestle, he had to latch onto somebody he could coattail to the top. Maybe then, after being visible, he could make a better case for getting back into the ring. But who? He needed a mark. Wearing his black shirt and red pants, Vander looked more like a towel boy for the Chicago Bulls than a road agent. But his backstage employee's pass let people know who he was. Not that anybody cared. The wrestlers were the stars of the show. But who to pick? Then, it hit him. Chet Worth. Someone like that would certainly need a mouthpiece. All the new guys who don't speak when they first get to PRIME must suck at it, that was the operational policy backstage. Luckily, Jung wasn't too far away from the lockers. But standing in the way, two female wrestlers who had wrestled in a tag team match against each other. 'Hello, ladies,' Vander began, realizing too late that he sounded far too much like Val Venis. 'I need to find Chet Worth. Have you seen him?' The thicker one, a dark-skinned beauty about 145 pounds on a 5'5' frame, nodded, but not in a good way. 'He left already.' 'Dammit...' Jung covered his mouth with his left hand. He always did that when he released minor curses. The skinnier one, 5'10' and only 109 pounds, blonde and even darker, being Jamaican, asked him, 'Excuse me. You're an agent. How did you think we did in our match?' Vander really hadn't watched their dark match. He was too busy with other things. But it's the same with every dark match talent. Positive criticism, tell them to stick to the basics and don't get ahead of themselves. 'Too many highspots, not enough psychology. Stay on the ground. Stick to the basics.' The thick girl rolled her eyes. 'Told you, Teq.' Jung arched an eyebrow. 'Huh? The Jamaican girl had enough of the I-told-you-so's. She was glad to have something else to explain. 'My name is Tequila,' she said, fixing her hair behind her right eat. 'Baby sister Margarita. Older brother Sake. Guess who my father was?' Vander scratched his chin. 'An alcoholic?' 'No,' Tequila huffed. 'Jumpin' Jack Daniels, from the Pacific Northwest circuit.' 'Ooh. I remember him. One of the progenitors of the high-flying style back in the 70s and 80s. Good man, I met him backstage at a PWC show.' 'And this is--' Sarah-Plain-and-Tall interjected. 'I can introduce myself. Marisela de Las Casas.' At that, Vander slapped her across the face. She was shocked by that, as was Tequila and the Amish folk who came to watch the event. They had to wait for all the cars to leave, otherwise the crazy drivers would ram through their horse-drawn carts. Marisela exclaimed, 'Hey!' She reserved herself to strike back, though. 'Oh,' Jung seemed as though he'd been unable to stop himself. 'Um... do you happen to be related to Negro Casas? 'No...' Vander slapped her again. Tequila stepped in the way. 'Why are you hitting her?' 'Sorry. Just... whenever I hear the word Casas I lash out.' Tequila demanded, 'Why?' Vander shrugged, chalking it up to 'Bad childhood memory.' 'Like what?' A slap to Tequila. 'BAD. CHILDHOOD. MEMORY.' A slap to the back of the head. Vander held the spot. Turning his head, Skylar Green and Shawn Stewart, dressed in incidentally-matching black coats, long pants for the weather. It's still winter, still very cold. Though it was Stewart who hit Vander, Skylar was the one to say, 'Don't hit a lady.' The girls were impressed of both of them. However, Stewart and Green were both spoken for, and nothing else would come of that. Jung instantly had a better idea come to mind. Shawn Stewat and Skylar Green were a better team than the men they were replacing, Scott Matthews and Vangellus Oxios. If he could manage THEM, then he'd be in a much better position to get attention for himself. Then he could get to the point that he wanted to be at: wrestling in the big time. But they were heading out the doors, and now was not a good time. He'd wait until next week. After all, big names like them... they couldn't remember him after more than a few minutes, right?
