Missing Pieces: Kairi

Date: ReV 224 Tokyo, Japan- 02:34 a.m. 09/05/93 "Sniper on the roof to the east. Prone. Lax guard. Chatting with the spotter. Spotter is eating dinner. Squatting. Oblivious. Estimate... " "I got 'em." The voices were soft. Near inaudible. Less than whispers spoken into the sparse movements of wind. "Mark as targets one and two." His fingers flexed straight for a fraction of a second before curling back around the rough plastic of the binoculars. The turn was microscopic. A slow and deliberate rotation that followed the general path of the sniper's scope. Careful that moonlight did not reflect across the lens. That an errant or wandering gaze did not fall upon an unexpected twinkle in the motionless night. The binoculars returned focus to a warehouse just short of a half mile distant. The same warehouse Targets One and Two were keeping a rather poor watch over. He blinked. His chest pressed into the sheet of little pebbles spread across the rooftop. Breath, muted and contained. Regulated. Each breath was a count. Three full minutes passed before he spoke again. A final confirmation of the past half hour's worth of observations. "Main entrance. Due north. One on the roof above the entrance. Alert. Mark as Target Three. Two at the door. Lax guard. Three on patrol. East-West about ten meters from the entrance. Lax. Smoking. West-East... twenty-five meters from entrance. Alert. North-South. Forty meters. Lax. Movement of shadows inside... estimate between six and ten more guards. Side entrance. Two on the roof above loading dock. Chatting. Oblivious. Mark as Targets Four and Five. One in the entrance to loading dock. Distracted. Too much movement to get an accurate estimation of guards inside. At least a dozen. Two on patrol. East-West about five meters from entrance. Alert. West-East is twenty meters from entrance. Sitting on a stack of... something. Covered with a tarp. Smoking. Lax. Two more guards at the far back of the roof. One on either corner. Backs to us. West Corner could be sleeping. Mark as Targets Six and Seven." He lowered the binoculars to the pebble-strewn roof. His hands returned to almost the same position, steepled in front of his face, but with the thumbs extended to form a perch for his chin. Fingers folded down and his face slumped forward, nose hovering just above the knuckles. He took a handful of deep breaths and then rolled to his back. "So what's the plan?" The question came from his left. He sat up, confident that the movement would go unnoticed. Each of the four men on the rooftop was dressed in full black. Long black t-shirts, black gloves, black commando pants. Black military boots, unshined and further dulled from the dust of the roof. All four had a black holster strapped to each thigh. Black nine millimeter snapped into the holsters. Standard issue for most law enforcement. Each had a six inch combat knife slipped into the right boot. Black, ridged handle. To conceal their identities, the four men wore black cloth masks over their heads. There was about an inch or two of space between the bottom of the masks and the necks of their shirts. The sparse amount of skin that was not covered in cloth as dark as the night itself. The man who asked the question was far from inconspicuous, despite the stealth attire. Six and a half feet tall. Weight well over two-fifty. Muscles bulged beneath the tightness of his t-shirt. Built like a brick wall. He was identified, as each was, by the white kanji embroidered onto the forehead of his mask. Famine. The answer came the man with the binoculars. The smallest of the four, he stood at just over six feet tall. Not much more than two hundred pounds. Still, he remained the most calm. His breath even. No nervous twitch or excited jitter in anticipation of what was to come. The question pointed in his direction had been asked with a hint of impatience. His answer was even. Smooth. Matter-of-fact and devoid of emotion. He was the Angel of Destruction. The execution of righteous retribution rested upon his shoulders. He carried the burden of designing the methods with which men met their fate. The others looked to him for guidance. Carried out his will. The blood of fathers and brothers would flow beneath the tred of his boots that morning. The point of his blade would send souls to the waiting arms of a welcoming Hell. He was Death incarnate. Nothing greater on this Earth was to be feared. "There's one guard at the fence on the northeast corner. I'll take him. You two follow after." His gaze shifted to the left, where Famine sat with War. He was the tallest of the four, at an estimated height of six feet, eight inches. Though he weighed less than his compatriot Famine, the musculature was no less impressive. Taller and leaner, more like a basketball star than football, he sat quiet. Did not speak. Just listened... and prepared for what was next. "Take the loading dock. Keep it silent until we've breached the warehouse. After that, we've got fifteen minutes at the most before the Ninth Clan catches up. I'll take the front. Jason follows after he eliminates all targets. We meet central. Start the fire, head out the back." "Don' suppose we know what they're usin' it for, eh?" From his right. Jason. Prone. Stretched out behind the scope of his own sniper rifle. He does not turn from his watch to voice the question. Famine scoffed and clenched a fist. "Does it matter? We fuck 'em up just the same, right?" The fourth Horseman, Pestilence, shrugged from behind the comfort of his scope. The rifle provided a fine excuse to not face his friends. Perhaps, then, no one would notice the reluctance with which he approached the coming storm. He remained flat across the roof, moving his rifle to focus on Targets One and Two. Death and Famine stood. War continued to kneel, though now moving a large black duffle bag in front of him. He unzipped the bag and proceeded to issue arms. Death slung the katana over his shoulder. Strapped across the back. Famine and War had a short and silent debate over the remaining choices. Each settled for a pump-action shotgun and a semi-automatic. Without a word, the three Horsemen slipped from the rooftop, quiet as the still night that surrounded them. ************************* Tokyo, Japan- 03:03 a.m. 09/05/93 The first few kills are the easiest. His hand touched the guard at the same time as the point of the knife. Fingers clasped tight around the forehead. Pulled back as the blade pushed forward. The steel edge slipped through the flesh smooth as butter. A slight snag when the point reached the front of the guard's throat. The slightest twist and application of extra pressure pushed it on through. The hand of Death moved down from the forehead to the mouth, muffling the gargled pleas. The guard began to slump. Death guided him down with care. All six inches of the knife pushed through the guard's neck. Twist to the left. Twist to the right. Blood spurted and gushed from the wound. Dark crimson splattering in the shadows of the concrete. Death removed the knife. His hand moved from the mouth to the side of the head. Continued to press the face against the concrete. Seconds ticked. A full minute. Death rose and slinked into the shadows of machines and tarp covered towers of equipment. He listened just long enough to ensure that two pair of boots followed behind, then diverted upon their own path around the warehouse. Death continued on. Quick. Silent. His head continued to count the seconds since leaving the rooftop. He had measured the gait and pace of each guard. Estimated the length of each patrol quadrant. Even allowed himself variables for distraction and laziness. So when he came to pause behind the cover of a forklift, crouched near the rear tire, back pressed firm against the cool metal, he expected to hear the footsteps of the North-South guard moving closer. He was not disappointed. His grip around the knife tightened. He shuffled through the various descriptions in his head. The estimated heights and weights stored for the short term. North-South was about five and a half feet tall, though his legs were longer than most men of such height. Thus, his stride was longer and would arrive at the confrontation point a handful of seconds sooner than Death might have otherwise expected. A shadow stretched across the concrete at Death's left. The legs of the shadow shortened as the guard approached. The toe of a boot about nine inches from the back end of the forklift. A bit farther than anticipated. Death held his breath. Counted the fractions of a second in his head for the next foot to drop. He timed his movements with the guard's stride, rising just as the heel planted. The point of the blade was piercing under the chin as the rest of the foot dropped. Before the toe hit the ground, the knife was through the bottom jaw, slicing the tongue in half and dividing the upper jaw into the sinus cavity. That last step had taken the guard clear of the forklift. Death put his free hand around the back of the head, fingers wrapping around the forehead, and wrenched back while still pushing up with the knife. The guard twirled backward, guided by the unexpected strength contained within the small frame of his assailant. Death stepped forward as he pulled the guard down, vacating his hiding spot as a final resting place. He pulled the knife free, twisted his wrist so the edge of the blade was now across the throat... and slit. The guard was placed face down, blood forming an ever enlarging pool beneath the corpse. Death slinked along to the front of the forklift. The next one could prove to be more difficult, as he was the most alert of the patrolmen. The task was meaningless to most of these men. Guarding a warehouse, the contents and purpose of which most of them were unaware. Of course, performing such a menial task would prove how loyal and dedicated such men were. Hopes of promotion with the ranks of the Council of Twelve were more than enough motivation to keep a man on task. At least, it had been at one time. Until the whispers and stories began to float through the scattered gatherings of lower Yakuza. Tales of blood and dismemberment the likes of which had not been seen for centuries. Someone had come to wage war against the Council of Twelve. More so, it seemed the Dozen were helpless to stop the bloodshed. Their grip on the Japanese underworld was slipping each month. Eight of the twelve had been found beaten. Tortured. Eight councilmen secured in the basement of a hospital, kept alive with feeding tubes and breathing machines. None of them would wake again. At this point, fear had turned to paranoia. Panic. The remaining Yakuza heads had placed their faith in the newest of their members. Young Fuijin Kurisawa. All except the ninth member of the council. Yamato Tagashi. The Tagashi family had long been rumored to head one of the few remaining ninja clans in all of Japan. Ruthless and calculating, as the oldest member of Clan Tagashi, Yamato himself held control over an unstoppable force of assassins. He did not fear the gaijin piercing the heart of his beloved council. He did not place faith in the charismatic Kurisawa, rumored to have been the cause of this plight upon the Council in the first place. He trusted in himself. In his clan. In the centuries of training and discipline honed to create the most perfect killing tools. He did not fear what was to come. Thus, neither did those under him. That overconfidence would be his undoing. Fear could prove to be a powerful motivator. The instinct for survival as well. The men guarding this warehouse were filled with bravado from the Ninth Councilman. Brash. Invincible with the might of the Ninth Clan behind them. Where other Yakuza were preparing for war, steeling themselves for the massacre to come... these men expected to watch a massacre themselves. Death was willing to give a modicum of respect to the West-East guard who continued to remain vigilant despite the lax and indifferent attitude of his colleagues. It would not save him. No more than it had saved the others. Still, though, it was nice to see that some men heeded the warnings. He came to rest behind a tarp covered stack longer than it was wide. He positioned himself within the shadow of the crates and wrapped his left hand around the hilt of his katana. This would have to be a quick strike. He tried to remember the exact paces of both West-East and East-West. He tried to estimate the distance between the two. The placement of the burning cigarette in relation to his current shadow. He calculated the amount of time it would take Matt and Alex to traverse around the back of the warehouse. Two guards, there. One kill each. Alex would not wait long before resorting to firearms. The timing would be close, but West-East was passing a careful gaze across the warehouse exterior. There was no chance of sneaking around before War and Famine unleashed the firing squad. West-East was deliberate. His steps were slow, giving him ample time to absorb his surroundings. He saw the attack coming. The movement in the shadows behind a stack of crates. He focused on that portion of the darkness, narrowing his gaze. He heard the distinctive and surprising sound of steel. Sword unsheathed. The glint of light reflecting on the metal. He raised his weapon... but was not fast enough. The point of the blade pierced him just below the sternum. The figure holding the sword seemed to form from the shadows themselves. Blood spurted from the guard's mouth. The katana slipped through intestines and vital organs. Sliced across the spinal cord before protruding through the back. The shadow assailant continued to rise. Continued to push. The guard was lifted from the ground. Impaled upon the splintered wood of a light post. He gasped. Gagged. Confusion clogged his mind. Vision blurred. He blinked. Tried to focus. Gunshots from the back of the warehouse. More blinking. His vision cleared enough to read a single kanji, blazing white in the shadows that rushed in to swallow him. Death... ...left the West-East guard impaled upon the light pole. He dropped to his knees while spinning, shifting the momentum he had used to stick the guard. Skin rubbed off his knees. Friction against concrete. He did not seem to notice. One hand reached to his boot. One hand to his hip. The three remaining entrance guards had been shocked to hear the retort of gunfire. The knife and pistol were in hand at the same time. Raised in the same motion. The knife took flight at the immediate instant his finger pulled back on the trigger. The bullet arrived first, splattering pink brain matter all across the warehouse door. A moment later, the knife plunged deep into East-West's chest. Before the patrolman had fallen, a second shot burst from the nine millimeter. A second set of brains splattered across the warehouse door. Death strode quickly to cover the distance between himself and the East-West guard. A third bullet stopped the guard’s attempts to crawl to safety. He recovered his knife. Reholstered his gun. Returned to the impaled guard to remove his sword. This would be needed. There were men inside awaiting his arrival. ************************* Tokyo, Japan- 03:14 a.m. 09/05/93 “Bloody hell.” He counted eight bodies laid out across the floor. The scene outside had been as expected. Precise kills. Quick. Efficient. This… this was something different. Eight bodies, but only… six heads. Not all of those were still attached. The number of hands and limbs littered about the pools of blood looked to have come from more than just eight people. There were… four handguns. A shotgun. One automatic rifle. Three switchblade knives. These men had been armed. Prepared. Trained. Still… Pestilence sighed. This crusade had turned into something… different. Something wrong. He understood the reasons when it started. The club. Lin. All of them had wanted revenge. Ninth months later, though, and he just could not see it. Whatever light had been at the end of this tunnel… they were all shrouded in darkness now. At this point, they were killing just to kill. The purpose had been lost somewhere along the way. It wasn’t just the Yakuza, though. The eight members of the council eating through tubes in a hospital basement had deserved their fate. Their wives… their children… none of them deserved the things that had been done. Not that Jason had participated, but he had not stopped it, either. Wives tortured. Children crippled. It was beyond anything he would have imagined. Tyler was out of control. None of them could reign him in. Too deep to get out, so everyone just kept… …the blood trail led off to the right. An open door that looked to lead into some sort of corridor. The flickering lights illuminated the sheen of thick liquid splattered across the walls. Pestilence shook his head. The rendezvous was supposed to be central. That hall did not look like it led to the center of the warehouse. He sighed and pressed forward. He opened a door at the far end of the room and slinked through the darkened hall. Someone had cut the power. At least to this portion of the warehouse. He met no resistance as he pushed further into the Yakuza compound. He stumbled into a well-lit room and shielded his eyes from the unexpected brightness. Famine and War were standing at the other end of the room, each waiting near a closed door, shotguns at the ready. Famine noticed his arrival first. “Jase.” “Alex.” “Where’s Ty?” The question from War. Concern in his voice was quite evident. “Dunno. There were bodies… everywhere. Listen, Matt, he’s…” “I know.” The strain of emotion in his voice indicated that he did, in fact, know. He was just as concerned. The slight tinge of despair in those two words also meant he had no better idea how to regain control than Jason did. There was no moving forward without Death, though, so Pestilence let his gaze wander about the room. An operating table was bolted to the center of the floor. The brightness of the lights now made some sort of sense. That blinding surgical illumination. The cold metal table was stained with blood. There was a drain near the base of the table. The floor there had been discolored. Blood encrusted scalpels and forceps laid in various places across the room. Stacks of what he assumed were surgical supplies, all covered with tarps. An open box of syringes. A sink full of pinkened water. “The fuck is this place?” “No idea. Ty said he heard a rumor that Yamato was letting Raijin use it for… God knows what. Bitch is fuckin’ crazy.” “Aye.” His attention wandered once more. There was a sound nearby. Like scraping. He tried to place it. Somewhere… behind him. He turned, perusing the covered stacks. Strained to listen. He heard it again. A scratching sound from one of the stacks. He took a careful step forward. The room was silent except for their breathing. Even still, the sound was almost impossible to hear. Perhaps he had imagined… …he knew better than to look up. The sound of shattering glass exploded upon them from above. Pestilence ducked, tucked his arms up over his head, and awaited the downpour. Shards of broken window rained upon him. A particular large shard sliced through his forearm. Not too deep. Painful. The glass tinked and twinkled around him. Something heavy slammed against the stack he’d been listening to. The impact knocked Pestilence back on his heels. Down on his ass. The glass seemed to have stopped. He risked opening his eyes enough to stare dumbfounded at the black-garbed body. A headless corpse that now toppled the scratching stacks of medical supplies. Everything tumbled to the floor. Crates. The tarp somehow tangled around the body. “The Ninth Clan is here. We need to move.” The voice came from above. From the same broken window through which this… Jason assumed now that it was a ninja of Clan Tagashi… had fallen. The three Horsemen looked up to see their fourth, Death, standing at the edge. War came forward first, placing his shotgun on the operating table. Death lowered himself from the window, hanging from the open ledge before dropping into the waiting arms of War. Like always, Matt was there to save him. The best of friends. Whatever fight Tyler had gone looking for, he had certainly found it. His gloves had been removed, as well as his shirt. A tattered remnant of that cloth remained dangling from the waistband of his pants. Two fresh cuts bled long but lean across his abdomen. The scars were collecting quickly, Jason noted. There was blood and torn flesh on his left shoulder. Probably a bullet graze. His guns were gone. His knife was gone. Nothing but the sword strapped across his back. “How much time?” “Minutes. Maybe. Anything here we can use as an accelerant?” “All sorts of medical shit. Gotta be something we can— ” “Shh!” Jason hushed them. He heard that sound again. The scratching. The crates had collapsed in the fall. Scattered across the floor. One of them, though… He approached with caution. He could feel the others watching him. The crate moved. “The fuck?” “Careful, Jase.” “I got it, Matt.” His hand descended slowly. He grabbed the lid of the plastic crate. It did not seem to be fastened. The scratching again. It sounded desperate somehow. He couldn’t explain it. Jason’s movements hastened. He stepped up to the crate and wrapped both hands on either side of the lid. Removed in an instant. Something jumped at him. A blur of skin. It latched around his shoulders. He could feel the creature’s bones. So frail. Then he felt the teeth sink into his neck… and the instant relief as his attacker vanished. Jason whirled. Astounded at the scene before him. Famine and War both stood back. The same astonished expressions flashing through their eyes. Death loomed above the operating table, the point of his sword pressed against the neck of a little girl. She couldn’t have been more than ten. Malnourished. Disturbingly thin. Her head had been shaved. Fresh stitches across the right side of her skull. Dried blood still crusted around the closed wound. Another stitched incision on her left side. Just below the ribs. She had no clothes. Spittle collected at the corners of her lips. She growled, but knew better than to fight against the blade. The girl was barely more than a skeleton. Even through the sunken features and discolored skin, though, it was obvious she was of Japanese descent. “Ty…” “We don’t have time for this, guys. Let’s just get the fuck out before the pajama party gets here.” “Shut it, will ya, Alex?” The girl opened her eyes. Bright green eyes. Death lost his grip on his sword. The steel clanged against the operating table. The katana clattered to the floor. As quickly as he had pulled the girl from Jason, he now pulled her to him. A shocking embrace. An arm around her too small waist. A hand at the back of her shaved head. He gently pressed her face into his shoulder. Whispered in his ear. “It’s OK. It’s OK. We’re not going to hurt you. I won’t let anyone else hurt you.” Famine was pacing. Agitated. He knew the Ninth Clan could not be more than seconds away. So did everyone else. Tyler sank to the floor, the little girl clutched in his arms. He continued to whisper. She started to cry. Those eyes. Rare amongst the Japanese. It was part of what had made Lin so special. Her bright green eyes. “We gotta fuckin’ book.” “He’s right.” “Yeah, but…” “Ty, we gotta go.” “We can’t just— ” “Bring the fuckin’ girl then, but we gotta move.” “Alex is right.” “We’ll never make it. Not if we have to carry her, too.” “Then we leave her. We don’t even know—” “No.” It was the first time he had spoken to them since she opened her eyes. He looked up to Jason with tear-filled eyes. For months, those brown orbs had been blank. Devoid of anything but the cold call of death. Now there was compassion. Love. Despair. Fear. “I can’t leave her.” “I’ll stay.” The words left Jason’s mouth before he even realized he had come to the conclusion. Someone would have to hold off the Clan while the others made their escape. Shadows descended from above. Through the open window. Two shotgun blasts left gaping holes in three of their chests. Tyler stood, the girl clutching tight around his neck. Sword in hand. Jason clipped off three shots. Three dead assassins. Tyler whirled. Even with the extra weight of the girl, he was the graceful incarnation of Death. One through the chest. A severed arm. A decapitation. Two severed legs. Through the jugular. Another shotgun blast. Jason put a bullet through a man’s heart. Another sword through the heart. An opened abdomen, intestines spilling to the floor. Shotgun to the chest. Another blast removing a ninja’s leg at the knee. Jason finished him with a bullet through the skull. “Go.” Alex did not wait to lead the way. He pushed through the door, dropped another Clan member with a shotgun blast, and continued on. Matt hesitated for a moment. Jason nodded. Matt returned the gesture and followed. “Take her, Ty.” “I’ll be back.” “You know I won’t be here.” “I— ” “Save her. Save… yourself.” Tyler nodded. He lingered for a moment, the two men exchanging a thousand emotions through their eyes. Tears glistened in the corners of his eyes. A final, heavy sigh pushed him to turn away. Toward the door through which the others had exited. A small chuckle from Jason brought him to pause. “You know, if I ever had a daughter… I wanted to name her Kairi. You remember that restaurant down by the arcade? Sushi place with the red doors? We had a waitress there once named Kairi. Thought it was the most beautiful name. Lin used to tease me about it. Thought it was hilarious that one of us would settle down long enough to have a family, let alone think of names for our children. Guess in some ways, she was right.” “Jase…” “Just go.” So he did. Jason was alone. He stood with his back to the operating table, a gun in each hand, and waited for the shadows to come…
