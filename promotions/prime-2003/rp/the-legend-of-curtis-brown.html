The Legend of Curtis Brown

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: The distant past. To tell the story of Allen Brown, known to many as "the Codemaster", one had to go back in time. Many years ago, when the Codemaster was only ten years old, he got his first Nintendo Entertainment System. It was great and wonderful, the type of thing that all kids, when they were in that era and that age, wanted. It would lead to his obsession with video games, which would lead to how he presents himself to this day. However, he wasn't always meant to be like this. He was meant to simply be Allen Brown, the one who would carry the legacy of the Brown family name in the wrestling world. His father was Curtis "the Hammer" Brown. One cannot tell the story of Allen "the Codemaster" Brown without first telling the story of the Hammer. In the late 1950s, Curtis Brown traded a potential career as a baseball player in order to take up professional wrestling. Though a black man in a predominantly white wrestling world, Curtis succeeded where many of his collegues failed due in part to his natural athleticism and his high amount of charisma. Though he had success as a face, Brown became notorious in the 1970s for his dirty tactics and his creatively inspired methods of cheating in order to win his matches, and continued his success well into the 1980s. By then, after almost thirty years of wrestling, he had been broken down enough that he was forced to retire in 1984 at the age of 48. There were at least three primary legends surrounding the Hammer. - The first was the fact that he was a lecherer. Although he was married and had three children with his wife shortly before his divorce in the 1988, it was rumored that Curtis had slept with dozens of women both before and during his marriage. It's also rumored that Curtis had fathered numerous illegitimate children and, therefore, Allen Brown probably had a lot of half-brothers and half-sisters that he doesn't know about. One story can be related from former wrestler and wrestling trainer, Kyle Morris. Morris previously wrestled as "the Beaver" or, more commonly due to his crazy antics in his heyday (the 1980s), the "Nitro Beaver". Kyle Morris had been a tag partner to the Hammer during the latter days of the Hammer's career, and was in fact trained by Curtis. However, during his travels on the road, he had encountered some of Curtis's less than honorable behaviour concerning women. Curtis Brown thought of women as sex objects, rather than as people. He would sleep with one of those ringrats every now and again, usually not enough to raise suspicions. Then, after the night was done, he often was the first to awake and he would leaves before the other woman awoke as well. It's rumored that he was gifted with what could be known as the "pimp slap of doom and despair", which instantly made whatever whore do whatever he damn well wanted to do. Eventually, however, his wife found out about Curtis's many affairs and left him, and even left her children, behind. Curtis continued his activities long afterwards, even into the 1990s and into his 50s. - The second was the fact that he was egomaniacal. When Curtis's stock began to rise in the wrestling world, his ego rose exponentially more. It's thought by some people that knew him that if Curtis had been more modest, he would've crushed the glass ceiling and he would've also been the World Champion that he wanted to be. He often thought he was a bigger star than he was, which caused him to often refuse to job important matches. This had the unfortunate effect, for Curtis, of causing him to eventually become blackballed in major companies like the AWA and quite a few members of the National Wrestling Alliance. This was particularly why Curtis found himself only able to work in the western wrestling territories of the United States, where he was still a big name. He hated the fact that he wasn't a big star, and often sought to make examples of other younger wrestlers. He was rough on training new wrestlers (apart from his twin sons, Robert and Jordan) and was often known to rough up such newbies akin to how Bob Holly once roughed up Matt Cappotelli on Tough Enough. His wrestling school had a notoriously small graduation, and those wrestlers who survived it often also worked a stiff style just like Curtis Brown himself. Yet, because of his ego and his desire to see his sons succeed where he could not, he didn't give his sons the same treatment. This led to jealousy against his sons, compounded by the fact that neither Jordan nor Robert had inherited any of Curtis's talent or charisma, and were forced to wrestle much like the Harris Brothers as an "evil twin tag team". - The third was that it was rumored that he had ties to the mob. This is a small rumor, though one that's been plaguing Curtis Brown since he left the major wrestling organizations in the middle of his career. It's thought that he could have been kicked out of even the regional territories if it weren't for his friends in the mob, who kept Curtis with a job. These rumors were unsubstantiated, no proof had ever been delivered of his actions. Neverthless, they have haunted Brown's reputation many years after his retirement. Even though Allen Brown enjoyed his new Nintendo Entertainment System... at the age of ten years old, he had no way of knowing just what kind of man his father truly was quite yet. He was simply a young kid with a brand new toy, and couldn't care less about the worries of his world. But reality has a tendency to smash the walls of a child's mind, and sooner or later, Allen would have to confront his father's sins... first hand.
