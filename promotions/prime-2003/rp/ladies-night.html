Ladies' Night

Date: ReV 137 Angelica Brooks is, admittedly, a young woman with little worldly experience. While work at PRIME has provided hundreds of opportunities and experiences she’d have never had on her own, those opportunities are almost exclusively limited to tours of North America and the general fanfare of working for a nationally recognized sports entertainment company. Beyond the doors of professional wrestling or the borders of the United States, she is, for all intents and purposes a naive and metaphorically virginal human being. That being said, it should now be pointed out that Ms. Brooks hasn’t the foggiest fucking clue what she’s doing in Tokyo. Of course she knows why she came to Tokyo. There is a definite and easily defined reason for the journey. From there, though, it’s pretty much just one big impulsive mess. Things had been kind of wild after Colossus, and with ReV 136 playing as the follow-up, well, she just hadn’t had much time to do the little things. Like catch up on her mail. After the show, however, a few minutes were spared to do just that. That’s when she found the postcard. Ask us about Lin. At first, the name didn’t mean much to her. She’d almost discarded the postcard as trash, but a nagging thought crawled through the back of her mind. A second inspection pulled her attention to the photograph pasted upon the postcard; a large, brightly lit Tokyo nightclub located on a bluff overlooking the Pacific Ocean. The Katana’s Point That’s when it hit her. The Katana’s Point was the name of the nightclub Tyler Rayne owned in Tokyo. The name, Lin, she had overheard during a conversation between Tyler and his friend Katterina. She remembered it as one of the first times she’d heard Tyler sound…serious. Somber, even. Anything less than the usual confidence. She’d left the arena in search of a hotel room, a quiet evening and the opportunity to sleep in a bit. The next day was a much needed break, mostly filled with a lot of shopping and appreciative glances at cute guys. She booked a late evening flight out of Kansas City, and arrived in Tokyo right at prime clubbing hours. For whatever ridiculous reason, she’d not bothered to reserve a hotel, or even look for one. She couldn’t make herself wait. Fortunately she’d brought the postcard, a picture of her destination helping to break the language barrier that might have prevented her from getting there. Now, forty-five minutes later, she’s still standing in line to get in, a cold wind whipping her hair and pulling at the bottom of her leather miniskirt. She’s wearing a white halter and a pair of pumps, something simple but efficiently sexy. Angelica looks up and down the line of patrons, thankful that she’s relatively close to the front of the line. The line, she realizes, is composed almost entirely of women. From what she can see, she’s the only one of non-Japanese descent. Or, at the very least, non-Asian descent. For all she knows, any one of them could be this Lin. Finally, she’s up to the front. The bouncer, she notices as a few inches shorter than herself, waves her in without much of a second glance. She’s almost insulted by the dismissive way he passes her toward the door. “Wait. I’m looking for—” He cuts her off with a cold stare and jerks his thumb toward the door. “But, I—” The woman behind her whispers something in Japanese, causing a small murmur of laughter around the front of the line. Even the bouncer smiles. Angelica suppresses the urge to argue further, emitting only a frustrated grunt before turning to enter the club. “Holy shit, this place is huge.” Somehow, standing outside, flanked by the most lights she’s ever seen and the enormity of an entire ocean, she just hadn’t noticed. It’s about the size of an airplane hangar. Almost immediately to her left, a set of three stairs, probably close to four feet across, lead up to a raised dais that composes that entire left quarter of the club. It is populated with dozens of tables and chairs, a half dozen various sized screens on the wall flashing different movies and sports events. Behind the bar, stretching almost the full length of the wall itself, a staff of seven bartenders is working diligently to keep up with the demand of their patrons. The dance floor stretches from the bottom of those steps all the way to the right wall, though there is a narrow corridor of space allotted to the entrance, where she’s now standing. From the far right wall juts a stage, a variety of instruments left abandoned and unplayed. Giant vertical screens stretch the height of the club’s walls, each of them flashing a psychedelic silhouette of one of the dancers on the floor. It’s kind of like watching the intro to a Bond film. Finally, smack dab in the center of the building, a large pillar stretches upward, widening into a semi-circular collection of mirrors that reflect the images of all the dancers back down on them. Around the base of the pillar, another bar, this one tended by a few less bartenders, but no less busy than the other. There must literally be hundreds of people in the building, an overwhelming majority of them female. Finding one girl in all the lot was going to be beyond impossible. “I need a drink.” She marches up the steps, a hard rock anthem blasting through the speakers. She doesn’t recognize the song, though she assumes it might be Manson. A group of brightly dressed young men catch her eye, and Angelica takes a small detour from the bar to stop at their table. “Excuse me, do any of you know a girl named Lin?” The four men turn to her, each taking a moment to give her the once over. One of them even has to lower his sunglasses to do so. They glare at her for just a moment before turning a look to each of their companions, and then proceeding with the conversation as if she’d never spoken. Angelica gives an aggravated smile, and the finger, before turning to stalk off to the bar. “Jack and Coke.” Despite the mass of bodies surrounding the bar, a spot of good fortune graces Angelica with absolutely no wait whatsoever. It’s an odd bit of luck, but certainly welcome. With no one to tend but her, the little Japanese man’s attention shouldn’t be so easily distracted. “Hey, I’m looking for a girl named Lin. You know her?” He stops mixing the drink, if only for a second. “You do know her. Who is she?” The bartender passes her the drink, and, without thinking, Angelica hands him a twenty. “Is she here?” “No good.” “What?” “You in Japan. You use Japanese money.” “Shit.” Needless to say, she didn’t have Japanese money. Yen, was it? “Look, I know Tyler. If you could just—” He’s not even paying attention. The bartender’s eyes are focused more on something to her right, and as she turns to follow the gaze, two bouncers appear from amidst the crowd. “You’ll have to come with us, ma’am.” “What? For a drink? You can’t be serious.” “The manager would like to see you. This is not…a request.” More like a threat, apparently. The unforgiving looks of the bouncers don’t leave much room for argument, though. Against her better judgment, Angelica pushes herself from the bar and falls in line behind the bouncer she’d spoken to. The quiet one steps in behind her, obviously to ensure she doesn’t stray from the path. There’s an elevator on the back wall, beneath one of the large projector screens. They ascend to an upper level Angelica didn’t even realize existed. She is led down the hall and into a semi-circular office. The bouncers leave her alone in the office. It has no walls except the one on which the door is positioned. All around are windows affording a view of the entire club. She realizes these must be the mirrors she noticed on the way in. The ones at the top of the pillar. Interesting. There is a large desk to her left, the large black chair turning in dramatic fashion to reveal a beautiful Japanese woman in a business suit. “I would have thought Tyler more careful in his choice of friends.” “Excuse me?” “Most of them prove to be quite more intelligent than you.” “Who the—” “I’m the manager. You can call me Kairi. I will call you a cab.” “Okay. Hold on. What the fuck? I thought Tyler owned this place.” “He does. But with all his sordid…hobbies, you don’t really think he has time to properly run a nightclub as well, do you? Are you really that naïve?” “Where do you get off—” “You need to watch your tongue, little girl. You get off lucky, using it here. But you drop her name in the wrong part of this town, and you end up dead. Or worse.” “Right. Lin. Well you’re the ones who told me to come here and ask, so—” “I didn’t tell you shit, girl. Especially not about her.” “Yeah? Then what the fuck is this?” More than fed up with the hostility, Angelica slams the postcard down on the desk. Kairi turns an unamused eye at the girl, but nonetheless takes a moment to study the postcard. “Where’d you get this?” “I thought it came from you.” “No. No one here would have sent this.” “But—” “Who else knows about this?” “Nobody. I just—” The door to the office opens, and in steps Katterina Wylde, not so surprisingly squeezed into yet another pair of tight leather pants. The black matches her bikini top, which she wears under a short denim jacket. “Katt, you look like a slut.” “Yeah, well, I’m tryin’ to get laid. What’s your excuse, Brooks?” Angelica opens her mouth to respond, but nothing comes to mind. Instead, she hangs her head in defeat. Kairi motions for Katterina to come over, passing the postcard along for the Dark Angel’s perusal. “This is fucked.” Kairi nods. Katterina sighs, tossing the postcard back onto the desk. She turns her eyes to Angelica. “C’mon, sugar bear. Let’s go.” She considers her options. Either go with Katterina, the bat-shit crazy lesbian she knows, or stay here with Kairi, Queen of the Ice Bitches and probably get herself kicked out of the club. Only one choice offers her the chance to answer some questions. She follows Katt out the door. “Where’d you get the postcard?” “It was in my mail. I figured—” Katterina stops, turning to Angelica with a disturbingly serious demeanor. “You’re in some shit, Brooks. I’m not gonna lie. Someone’s tryin’ to fuck with Ty, and they’re using you to do it. You have a hotel?” “No. I didn’t have time to—” “Good. One less hassle. You stay here for the night. Tyler has a room. We’ll figure shit out in the morning. After I get some poon.” “You’re…who is she? Lin. Who is she?” It was Angelica’s turn to halt their progress with the serious expression. She’d come all this way for an answer, and by damn, she intended to get one. “She’s…they were engaged. Her and Ty.” “What happened?” “He killed her.” At first, she thinks it’s a joke. But nothing about Katterina’s face implies amusement. She can’t fathom it. Can’t believe it. There are a handful of attempts to respond, but Angelica simply doesn’t have the right words. Or any. “Look, Brooks, it’s like this. You wanna be Ty’s friend, you gotta accept one thing. The world’s not painted in black and white. Hell, we don’t even deal in shades of grey. It’s all just dark. Only thing that matters is how deep you go, and how much you like it.” Katterina opens a door, revealing a small apartment. Bedroom, little kitchenette. Bathroom to the side. “There’s the shower, if you need it. Food and drinks in the fridge. Phone by the bed, you want anything else.” “Where…where are you going?” Katterina smiles. “There’s an entire club full of women down there. Surely one of them would like to fuck the shit out of a hot little number like me. Get some rest, sugar bear. We’ll get it all straightened out in the morning.” Angelica nods absently, finding herself unable to keep from watching the wiggle of Katterina’s ass as she goes. Shaking the odd thought from her mind, Angelica steps into the room, immediately locking the door and stripping off her clothes. Suddenly a shower is the best idea in the world. The water is hot and comforting. There’s even a pressure adjustment on the shower head. It’d almost be perfect, if it wasn’t so quiet. Too quiet to do anything but think. And right now, all she could think about was the revelation that Tyler had killed his fiancée. He was a murderer. It was… She notices the shadow only a second before the blade of a katana slices through the shower curtain, the point piercing tile but an inch from her ears. A blood soaked hand grasps at the end of the curtain, ripping it from the rings and revealing her to the sword’s wielder. She screams. The katana pulls from the wall, gripped tightly by an arm soaked to the elbow in blood. A scarred but physically impressive chest is splattered with crimson droplets as well. She recognizes the scars, though the black mask covering his face denies her confirmation. “Jesus fucking Christ.” The voice is familiar, and for a moment, she is relieved. She is not dead. But she is still naked, which presents a whole other problem. He turns to leave her, disappearing for only a second before he comes back to toss a towel in her direction. She doesn’t remember having turned off the shower. “Rayne, is that…” The blood-soaked hand moves to pull the mask from his face, revealing his identity as that of Tyler Rayne. He looks exhausted, or perhaps maybe saddened. Weighted, as if he were carrying some emotional burden. He does not turn his eyes back to her, even once she’s wrapped the towel around her naked body. He simply turns on the sink faucet, and begins to scrub. “What happened?” He doesn’t answer her question. She waits a moment longer, debating on the next move. She is young and impulsive, and sometimes, less than tactful. “Why’d you do it?” Seconds pass before he responds. “Do what?” “Kill her. Kill your fiancée.” The flow of water stops. He turns to her, and it’s the most heartbreaking look she’s ever seen. It almost makes her want to cry. “Who told you?” “K-Katt. She…I…” “I loved her. I would have died for her. Would have killed for her. But…this madman comes. Yakuza punk trying to make a name for himself. Impress his father. I…we were in the office. You can see the whole club from there. Everything.” “I saw it. I mean, I was there. In the office.” “I don’t know how he did it, but he got his men in here. Points them out. A dozen, at least. Armed with semi-automatic weapons, judging by the bulge in their suit jackets. So he gives me a choice. Kill her, and spare the people in the club. Or don’t, and watch as his men open fire on hundreds of unsuspecting innocents.” “Oh God…I…Ty, I’m…” “Don’t. I’m not worth it.” “Ty, you…don’t say that. You just—” “Do you see this?” He raises his arms to her. Blood-stained hands, even after the scrubbing. Hair and skin beneath his fingernails. ”DO YOU SEE THIS?” She jumps back, shocked at the unexpected outburst. He puts his hands to his head, passing frustrated fingers through his hair. “I’m not a hero, kid. I’m not some tragic character you’re supposed to pity and embrace. I’m just the guy that killed the only thing he’s ever loved in this entire world. Just another guy, can’t wash the blood from his hands.” She steps out of the shower, slowly stepping across the few feet between them. Her arms reach up, around his shoulders. She falls into his chest. For a moment, he does not move. Slowly, his arms drop, wrapping themselves around her hips. His head falls to her shoulder, and he weeps.
