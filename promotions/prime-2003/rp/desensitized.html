Date: Winter January 19th, 2008. Early Afternoon "Would the defendant please rise." 2007 had unquestionably been the worst year of Kaiser Vashaun’s life. Just a week in, 2008 was looking to dethrone its predecessor. He pulled at the bottom of his suit jacket, straightening its pinstripes as he rose to his feet. Keeping a stoic face, Kaiser turned to the twelve jurors that would decide his fate. Given the magnitude of their soon-to-be impact on his life, he was surprised how little attention he paid to their faces and any emotions behind them. Instead, he just counted their heads. One, two, three, four… "On the sole count of Murder in the Second Degree," the judges booming voice betrayed his gray hair and frail frame. In his long black robe, he sounded like James Earl Jones, but looked like George Burns. "how do you find?" "We find the defendant… not guilty." A concoction of shocked whispers, sighs of relief, and the unmistakable silence of outrage filled the space around him, the weight of each reaction smashing against the courtroom walls. His attorneys shook hands, and exchanged hearty laughs through their large grins, very much pleased with their performance. Kaiser didn’t share in the smiles and handshakes. He just kept counting heads. …five, six, seven… ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ March 4th, 2008 Ronald DeBrosse wasn’t exactly the sort of gentleman Kaiser had expected to find the first time he’d sat down across from the man who was going to hand him his new career. Sports gents, Kaiser thought, were supposed to be young, good looking guys with short dark hair, who walked around in taylor-made suits and sunglasses that cost more than most people’s first car. Ronald, or "Debo" (as all people with the last name ‘DeBrosse’ like to be called), waas the Mr. Hyde to the typical agent’s Dr. Jekyll. At 5’6" and maybe 130lbs, with balding red-hair and more freckles than sharks have teeth, Debo looked like a lot of things. ‘Agent’ wasn’t one of them. It occurred to Kaiser, as he plopped down in the cheap folding chair across from Debo’s desk, that the guy could have looked at least a slight bit the part. But, his "I support single moms" stripper T-shirt, tucked into a pair of slacks, did nothing but promote the image of a middle-aged man trying to be far cooler than his existence would permit. "So what's up Kais?" "I'd really prefer you didn't call me that." "Sorry, man. It's just... we've known each other how long now?" "Less than two months, Ron." "Well, feels like a lot longer than that." Debo scooted his leather chair forward, pulling in close to his desk. For added emphasis, he folded his hands in front of him and leaned forward. "So you want to wrestle, huh?" "That's the idea. I don't think they'd have me back at my old job." "Probably not." "So, what can you do for me?" "I can get you in somewhere good... not much garunteed money, though." "That's fine. I can start at the bottom and work up." Kaiser folded his arms across his chest. Wrestling hadn't been his first career choice, but it wasn't out of disinterest. He'd watched the stuff since he was six years old and loved every minute. Still, it had never crossed his mind to actually pursue it as a career. Lots of kids tossed a football around in the back yard. They didn't tryout for the Cowboys. The recent events, however, had left him in dire need of a new direction. Returning to the JFD was out of the question. Pro wrestling seemed like just the place for a story like his. "Ok. Good attitude to have. Slightly harder than that to actually... ya know... accomplish, but that's the spirit." "Where're you planning on getting me in?" "PRIME." "You're kiddin' me?" "I know Lisa Tyler. We go way back." "Is that the same as how you and I go 'way back'?" "Sorry. Like I said... seemed more like a couple years to me." Debo attempted to straighten up some papers on his desk, more to keep his hands busy than anything, but his efforts only made a bigger mess of the stack. "Anyway, she'll listen when I tell her I've got a hot prospect. Look at ya… chisled, nice-tan, dark hair… well, what little bit you have." "I shave mine. What’s your excuse?" "Point taken." "PRIME's the real deal. You sure you can get me in?" "Yeah. You sure you want in? I was a little surprised when you called me up the other day and started to hash this out. I'm still not entirely sold on it." "What else can I do?" "Just in case you've forgotten, Kaiser... they did find you innocent." "Twelve people's opinion can't change the minds of millions. It'll only make 'em wonder why a dozen idiots get to play God, and not them." "Did you do it, man?" Again, Debo leaned forward, a strand of his thinning red hair falling into his eyes. "Did you really fucking do it?" "Doesn't matter. I think enough of Jackson, Mississippi's already decided I'm a bad guy." "And you think it won’t follow you?" "Maybe. But that’s what’s different about the world of wrestling. They just call you a heel and tell you to make the crowd hate you." "Well," Debo leaned back in his chair, folding his hands behind his head, "based on all my pleasant dealings with you... I'll think you'll do just fine with that." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ January 19th, 2008. Early Evening He hung his head, shielding his eyes from the sun as it melted the horizon. It’d been at least four hours since the verdict had been rendered, and Kaiser had spent the better part of it sitting in the middle of the courthouse steps, until a couple police officers with not a whole lot else to do, had made him move. He hadn’t made it far since then, moving twenty yards to a bench near the fountain at the base of the grounds. For the past hour or so, he’d sat there, listening to the splash of water, feeling the burn of the sun move across his shoulders and sink slowly into his field of vision. Sitting there, with his head hung, alone on an old bench in need of a fresh coat of paint, he must have looked like a man who’d been through hell and returned only to find the earth ablaze. A fitting thought for the orange hue that saturated his surroundings. "Kaiser." Like a dog, conditioned to respond, he lifted his head in the direction of the voice. Through squinted eyes and distorted shadows, he couldn’t make out a face, but the voice had given him his answer without looking. "Hey." "Kaiser, come home. It’s getting chilly and your sitting there dressed like that." Laren Townsend rubbed her forehead as she pointed to her fiancé, sitting in shirt and tie, temperature dropping steadily. "You could at least put your suit jacket on." "I’m fine." "Sweetie, its probably not even 50 out here. Put the jacket on." He didn’t say ‘Ok’, just grabbed the coat, but didn’t put it on. "Not really that cold." "What’s on your mind, Kaiser." "Pretty much nothing." In the the three years he’d known Laren, he’d never once found it difficult to talk to her. It was the first thing he’d realized he loved about her, though she swore it was her long blonde locks. He wasn’t much of a talker and often found himself forcing out the shortest responses possible while engaged in chit-chat. With Laren, he’d spouted off more in one evening than he had in a hundred prior conversations. They’d only been dating a month when Kaiser had realized he was in deep, and though he waited almost two years to propose, he’d known the third week they’d been dating that he would marry her. "I don’t believe that." "Ok." "You’re telling me you’ve been sitting out here in front of the courthouse for four hours, and you’re not thinking anything? C’mon, sweetheart, I’m not dense. What is it? What’s wrong?" "I’m fine. It’s nothing… I swear." His negative response stirred her insides. Kaiser could tell by the look that jolted across her face for a brief second. It was the look of sick anticipation, the same one that hits at apex of rollercoaster, just one moment before the world drops out from beneath. Her guts were lodged amongst the various ducts of her throat. She was about to take that first drop. "Kaiser… I have to ask you something. I let it go during the trial… I didn’t know, maybe you couldn’t talk about it or something… I dunno. It all seems stupid, but I have to ask now that its over." She paused, a heavy pause, "Did you do it?" "Why do I have the feeling I’m gonna get that question a lot?" "So?" "Jury says I didn’t." "The jury wasn’t there. Only you two were in that building when it was collapsing. Only you walked out. You know the truth." "What’s it matter?" " ‘What’s it matter?’ It matters whether a firefighter died tragically doing his job, or whether he was murdered. It matters, Kaiser. It matters to me. And if you can’t think any more of it, any more of us than to give me an answer… then don’t bother coming home tonight, cuz I don’t wanna share my apartment or my life with a man who can’t even give me a straight answer to something this important." "You think I did it." "I don’t, but your refusal to answer doesn’t sound much like innocence." The vitriol of her voice subsided as she took a seat next to him on the bench. For the first time that day, she touched him, wrapping her arms around his bicep, clenching it like child would a teddy bear. "In my heart, I believe you didn’t do it. I just need to hear you say it." "Ya know, the last year has been pretty fuckin’ awful. I’ve watched my sister turn into a shell of herself after having her dignity… her entire being just ripped away from her. I’ve been stabbed in the back by the guy who was supposed to be my best friend. I’ve been tried for murder. I won’t be able to go back to the department. And all things considered…" he trailed off mid sob-song. "I know it’s been tough, baby. I just…" Without warning, Kaiser jumped to his feet, tearing his arm from her grasp with little effort. In three large strides, he’d already put the dying day between them, the last remnants of the sun burying her under thick shadows. "Where are you going?" "Motel." "What?" "I’ll be by tomorrow morning to pack up my things." Laren said something else, but the distance and the fountain made it impossible to hear, and he didn’t have much to say anway. He dug through his pocket, searching for his keys, keeping steady pace over the Court grounds. Halfway across the parking lot, peppered with the occasional car, a shiver raced down his spine. As he approached his truck, Kaiser swung his jacket around, sliding his arms in the sleeves. He was colder than he’d thought.
