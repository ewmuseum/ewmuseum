Lose Yourself

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: GAN Marquis Peeples stood at the edge of the building, looking out at the city that seemed so full of life even now at this late hour. With his arms folded across his chest, he thought back on where he came from. A life that most people end up losing their life over, and here he was with a second chance. Did he really deserve it though? What made him any different than the other nine people in that one out of ten statistic he had been a part of? Why did it even bother him so much? He really had no clue, and he doubted he ever would. He just knew that he had the opportunity to start over, and he was going to make sure that he did not squander it. “What are you doing up here?” Marquis turned to find Tyrell Dawkins there with a puzzled look on his face. “What?” “What are you doing up here, man?” Tyrell repeated. “Just thinking,” Marquis answered. “About what?” Tyrell's curiosity was peaked. “Everything,” Marquis replied as he turned back to stare out at the city. “Well aren't you the conversationalist tonight.” “Man, I don't even know what the hell you just said.” Marquis and Tyrell shared a laugh. “All your answers were like one or two words,” Tyrell said as he patted his partner on the back. “Something bothering you, bro?” “I've been thinking about that cop a lot,” Marquis answered, his head down in shame. “Dawson?” Tyrell said the officer's name, unsure if that was the same police officer Marq was talking about. “Yeah,” Marquis said with a nod. “It's like no matter what I do, I'm never really going to leave that life behind me...you know?” “Come on now,” Tyrell was surprised that Marquis would still be beating himself up about something that happened a few months ago. “You can't keep beating yourself up about that. Dawson is doing a lot better now that he is out of the hospital, and those thugs that were responsible for it are locked up behind bars.” “I know that,” Marquis pulled away from Tyrell's sympathetic pat on the back. “But it's my fault that you and the cop got involved in this. It was my choices that put you in harm's way.” “You made a mistake,” Tyrell tried to reassure Marquis that this is something he does not need to dwell on. “Everybody makes mistakes, Marq.” “Yeah, but my mistake nearly cost two people their lives,” Marquis shook his head. “How can I live with that?” “Because we're both alive,” was Tyrell's answer. “You can't blame yourself for something that didn't happen.” “That's easy for you to say,” Marquis took a few steps away from the edge of the building, his eyes set on his feet as he walked. “You don't know what it was like to walk into the gym that morning and see the glass from your office shattered all over your desk, or to walk out back and see the crime scene tape on both ends.” “I'm not going to lie and say that I know exactly what you're feeling,” Tyrell approached his partner, the sound of small rocks crunched beneath his feet with each step. “But you have a chance to make something out of your life, so stop being so hard on yourself and focus on the good things.” “Good things...” Marquis laughed. “Tell me one good thing in my life.” “We have a chance to become the PRIME tag team champions at Great American Nightmare.” “Really?” Marquis shook his head. “That is the best you could come up with?” “What's the matter with that?” Tyrell stretched his arms out at his side. “Do you really think we have a chance of beating the Wolves?” Marquis turned to face Tyrell. “Forget the fact that I couldn't wrestle my way out of a wet paper bag for a moment, because it means absolutely nothing when you take into account that they've beaten us every single time we've faced them.” “You're not serious,” Tyrell shook his head. “You can't stand there and tell me that you're scared to face them.” “Scared?” Marquis shook his head. “This has nothing to do with being scared. What it does have to do with is the fact that we have no chance in hell of beating this team. All we're doing is proving their point.” “And what point is that?” “That there is no one that can beat them.” “You really think that?” Tyrell turned away and shook his head. “I can't believe you're standing there saying this right now.” “Why not?” “Because it isn't true, that's why.” “You're out of your mind,” Marquis answered him as he kicked at a patch of rocks. “They're going to retire the tag team titles the week after GAN, and there is nothing we can do to stop them.” “That's a load of crap,” Tyrell snapped. “We can do anything we set our minds to do.” “Except this.” “Don't you think like that,” Tyrell shoved his partner from behind. “Look at me when I'm talking to you, dammit.” “What!?” “You give me one night,” Tyrell grabbed a hold of Marquis' shoulders. “I'll show you what we can do, but you need to promise me that you're willing to lay it all out. You leave nothing to chance, because we're going to prove the Wolves wrong by taking those damn titles off of their shoulders after we knock their heads off of them.” “You're out of your damn mind.” Marquis laughed. “Just promise me.” “Alright,” Marquis nodded. “I'll give you one night.” “That's all we'll need.”
