War (Part 7): The Cost of Mindgames (Will finish before deadline)

5 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 5th, 2009 Steven Steven Connors always acted with extreme precision in every facet of life. Big or small, the former detective was meticulous when it came to details. His hair never grew past a certain length because he was fearful of public perception. Stubble never grazed any portion of his face. He felt those who allowed facial hair to cover their skin weren't trustworthy individuals. When it came to organizing his home, even greater measures were taken to ensure cleanliness and perfection. Every room of the two story suburban palace could be featured as a model in home magazines, nothing short of an immaculate masterpiece. A mantra Steven placed on the kitchen wall just above the sink read "A man's home is his castle," and Steven treated the lap of luxury with the utmost respect and dignity. Should anybody close walk into the living room at the moment, they would be befuddled by the scene in front of them. The massive fifty inch flat screen television which normally donned the wall decorated the mahogany floor in a sea of glass. Two couches were toppled, cushions stabbed with a kitchen knife and stuffing strewn throughout the room. The custom Egyptian hieroglyphic glass table, the centerpiece of his living area, was broken in several different places. Some of his sports memorabilia encased on the seven story glass shelf in the left corner punctured the floorboards while a few collectibles overflowed into the kitchen. Steven wound up and pierced the kitchen table with a hammer. The viscosity of a single shot blasted through the home, so loud it could be mistaken for a gunshot. His left leg swung around and clipped an antique chair at the base, bouncing it off the drywall next to the sliding glass door. Clutching the hammer even tighter, Steven turned his attention to the cabinets stacked from base to ceiling with rare plates and kitchen accessories. Soon, they would be fragments covering the eloquent black and white floor tile combination. His German shepherd Maverick peered around the dining room corner, concern flooding perplexed brown eyes. While Maverick knew Steven to be an eccentric owner, he never saw him in such a disturbed state. Destroying the creation of his hard work was so off the spectrum, Maverick grew frightened and let out an audible whimper. "Shoo, Maverick, get out of my damn way!" Steven shouted, his right foot recoiling back to kick the dog if he didn't obey the command. Maverick darted away and pawed up the stairs, nestling himself in the closet of the master bedroom. If the dog had the ability to reach up and call someone, anyone, he wouldn't hesitant.   Minutes ago, Steven received a threat on his life from Christian Daniels after the mammoth biker figured out the latest scheme Steven displayed in trying to win their war, hunting down Christian's son and using him as a negotiator. Steven honestly never believed the boy would calm his father to the point of even considering a truce, but he also assumed the shock value would be enough to hold Christian in a state of limbo and confusion. The window of opportunity would open for Steven to even up the odds and eliminate a few of Christian's followers.   The tactic failed horribly and Steven was backed into an unenviable corner. Christian did not recede, but instead grew incredibly violent and vowed to make amends the only way he knew how. The likelihood of a bluff was virtually non-existent. Steven knew Christian's track record of carrying out threats better than anybody. All the results were in one column. A frightening thought panged through his mind. Christian always killed systematically and loved taking death slow. He would draw out the anguish and revel in the pain of his victims, even more so for Steven considering the crime committed against him and their rich history.   With his actions over the past year, there was little place to hide from Christian's wrath. Representatives of Death, from actual members to local paperboys and all the way up to political officials, showered the entire state of North Carolina. Christian's ability to locate someone was unparalleled. Steven also couldn't rely upon his former police brethren because the only service they would provide involved a psychological facility and evaluation. They wouldn't believe it odd for a man to grow paranoid after losing his entire family in the span of a few months, but he knew nobody would believe accusations of Christian stalking him. The call wasn't recorded for proof so the odds of tracking down some former colleagues in the department to come over and watch his house were slim to none. It wouldn't be a stretch to assume that even if he did call them, Christian's crew would show up. Word recently spread through the underground that Christian was seen with a few high ranking state police officials, looking to get them on his side.   Steven had to do something and fight back before being swept up in the murderous rampage. The impulsive plan conjured up moments after hearing Christian's seething voice was nothing short of egregious and dangerous, but options were scarce and nobody had the stomach to stand by him during such treacherous times. Once the first classic painting from his collection was destroyed, there was no going back. After fifteen minutes, the lower level of his home was a complete mess, comparable to the damage a hurricane might cause. Steven glanced around and gave a nod at his handiwork.   Stage 1 was complete.   Now he headed upstairs where the more complex and strenuous Stage 2 would take place.   Christian   Christian slammed open one of the drawers on his master bedroom dresser, rummaging through the grab bag of weapons. He grunted and groaned while flinging rusty old knives on the floor, a few containing drops of crimson on the tips. They weren't what he was looking for. He wanted a fresh blade, one that would pierce deep but not immediately kill. He wanted – no, he needed- to watch Steven Connors suffer. He needed to see the look on his face when the rest of his body caught up to the realization that he was dying. He needed to see the agony in his bloodshot eyes when the blade twisted around inside of him. He had to hear the screams elicited when the knife danced centimeters away from vital organs, but wouldn't answer the cries for mercy.   Nobody played mind games with him and got away with it. Nobody used his son as a pawn. He continued to look for the perfect tool while muttering under his breath.   "Gonna fuckin' kill that piece of shit, motherfucker wanna play? Bitch gonna find out what happens when you pl..." Christian's angst brought the rest of his body up and he screamed down the hall, "DEVIN, IS THE FUCKING CAR READY?"   Disposing of another cigarette in the trashcan, Devin Shakur apprehensively walked into his brother's bedroom. If the situation was a bit lighter, he would inform Christian that the knee jerk reaction wasn't going to go over well. Usually, Christian held all the power and dictated every situation. He intimidated those who walked into his path and always stayed one step ahead of anybody nipping on his heels.   Now, he was being played like a fiddle. Steven employed one of the dirtiest tricks in the book, but it was also the most effective. Nobody in the house ever saw Christian fly off the handle at the drop of a hat. Once in a while he would head out to the shooting range and let off some steam if something didn't go his way, but being suffocated by a desperate man with nowhere to go but down was an aberration. Although, Devin wasn't ready to negotiate with Christian's temper. If he were in Christian's shoes right now, he would probably act the same way.   Devin nodded, "The car is ready, boss."   The words did nothing to soothe the ailing Christian, who grabbed the top and bottom of the drawer and launched it over the bed, ignoring the loud thud the box made when colliding with the wall. He took a step left and plundered through another drawer. Everything appeared to be identical to the previous drawer, a vast collection of steel and aged metal devices cramped into a tiny space. Christian lobbed a pair of inch thick steel chains over his shoulder and dove into the pile. He felt around like he was looking for the trick card in a deck or rigged lottery ball with a specific weight texture.   "Come on goddammit, can't be this fuckin' disorg - Ahhhhh"   Yanking his arm from the bottom of the drawer, Christian's eyes lit up like Christmas trees when he found the Japanese knife. A flick of the wrist sent the blade nine inches into the air. Christian saw a hint of his own maniacal eyes in the reflection of the slick and flawless steel.   This would be the device Steven Connors would be maimed and tortured by.   "Let's do this shit."   Steven   Underneath an almost unbearable amount of weight, Steven's arms and legs vehemently shook. The defense mechanism was a futile attempt to keep upright while completing the task at hand. His body hadn't gone through a rigorous workout in quite some time so holding such a hefty amount of weight against his being wouldn't be an easy and fluent job. He was attempting to do the work of two or three men.   A break was required in order to continue. He turned his body and let the pressure rest against his back while observing the damage done to his hands. The abrasiveness of the bed frame cut through his skin, leaving small trails of blood around his wrist. His hands were swollen to a severe extent, throbbing like beating hearts, and he winced while shaking the limbs out. Perhaps he could find a bowl of ice before the cavalry arrived.   But this needed to be done. Time was his enemy.   With a roar, Steven spun back and pressed with his leg muscles, bringing the entire frame up vertical. He couldn't let it rest a second time or the consequences could be excruciating, and he wouldn't have enough reserve available to bear the brunt again.   He pushed through and managed to flip the bed over. After letting out a massive exhale, Steven was satisfied and got the respite he deserved, doubling over, grabbing his stomach, and resisting the urge to vomit. His hand reached over and nabbed the bottle of water from the nightstand. With the moxie of a professional drinker, he downed the bottle in a matter of seconds due to the severe dehydration endured over the past few weeks. Keeping pace with Christian was arduous on the mind and draining on the body.   Maverick emerged from the closet with a moan, trotting out to his master and brushing up against his leg. Steven looked down with an exasperated sigh, nudging the dog away and pointing toward the closet. Maverick's head rose and met the eyes of his owner, a confounded look on his face. He couldn't understand what was happening. Steven's face tensed, not fond of dealing with the confrontation, and he urged his loyal companion back to the closet with a more authoritative point. Maverick whined and returned to his resting place, walking around in a circle before plopping to the ground.   Steven walked over and slid the door shut, locking the dog inside. He needed to stay focused. Worried eyes browsed the bedroom, looking for anything else that needed to be decimated for plausibility purposes. His mind pictured the scene which would develop once Christian arrived. The downstairs area was set to a tee and now the upstairs was finished as well.   The two hardest parts plan were yet to come. With a grumbling stomach chock full of unsettled nerves, Steven walked over to the bedside table and crouched down, opening the compartment and staring at the only object inside.   His Colt 45, the one that had been passed down to him from his father and to his father from his father. Other than Maverick, it was perhaps the most cherished item in the house.   And he would use it for a purpose befitting such prestige. He didn't have to check to see if the gun was loaded because he already knew that it was. Steven dug into his bottom lip and stared over at the closet, sighing deeply. Sweat already soaked the gun and it wouldn't be long before his hands were trembling with overwhelming fear.   He looked down at his watch and grumbled under his breath. Time was still ticking against him. If he was actually going to go through with the presentation, the anxiety must cease and desist. Christian wouldn't slow down because of an inconvenience so he couldn't either. Picking up the pace, Steven grabbed the cordless phone from the computer table and slightly hung his head. He eyed the bathroom and almost followed the straight and narrow, but deviated to the closet and popped open the door.   Pointing the gun away from his body, Steven crouched down and let Maverick lick his face. All the fond memories with his pooch rushed through his head in a few fleeting seconds. He never liked saying goodbye to someone, especially one who tugged at his heart strings with a simple bark, but sacrifices had to be made during times of warfare.   Maybe one day they would meet again.   He patted the dog on the head, shook his hand, and fed him a treat from his pocket. Maverick munched on the delicacy while Steven closed the door and forged ahead to the bathroom. The burden of suicide almost buckled his knees, but he caught himself against the elongated handle of the door at the last moment.   Nobody wanted to go out like this but it was better than the alternative.   He looked down at the phone and back at the gun. All that remained was to dial three numbers and put on the performance of a lifetime. Christian   The sound of a Bic lighter connecting against Devin's left thumb indicated his descent onto the first floor. Christian hovered a few feet behind, chomping at the bit to devour Steven limb by limb. Everything always went according to his terms and conditions. Tonight would be no different.   Christian's fingers synchronized into a pattern and ran along the dragon handle while staring at the back of Devin's head. He pictured the crop of hair belonging to Steven's disheveled form and fantasized about all the places he would plunge the knife, how long the blade would be kept in specific wounds, and what he would say while watching Steven squirm. This wouldn't be a moment to savor but a form of closure. Steven was the last Connor alive and putting him six feet under would bring an end to the saga that had clouded the last eighteen years of his life. It would also mean that he could finally plot his exit from the country without having to look over his shoulder.   Four heads from the nearby kitchen table perked up, escaping from their laptops. The rest of the Smith brothers. The tallest of the group, Michael Blaze, rose from his seat and joined the march toward the door. He would drive them to Steven's home and serve as the getaway driver once the job was complete.   Swinging open the front door was Jason Hardy, standing with keys in hand to another vehicle equipped with police scanners. His mission was to be on the lookout for any officers patrolling the area. He would have direct contact with everyone on the team. If everything went according to the blueprint, Michael would drive Christian and Devin to a predetermined location for a vehicle switch so any police inquiries would only find one person in Michael's car. Afterward, both vehicles would be ditched in favor of a plane and the bright lights of New York City. Michael took a green John Deere tractor hat off the bookshelf and retrieved a pair of Oakley sunglasses from his back pocket. Jason tilted his glasses down and threw on an NC State cap.   Devin glanced over his shoulder at the three remaining individuals pounding away on the computers. They were booking the reservations in New York and were scheduled meet the foursome at the spot in a few hours. Everybody gave an encouraging thumbs up. Devin responded with a nod and escorted Christian out the door, who gave a violent pound against the steel before slamming it behind him.   Steven   Steven heard the claws of Maverick scratching against the closet wall and placed the 45 at his side. His index fingers reached up and shut his eyes, trying to fight back tears that wanted to stream down his face.   He really didn't want to go through with the scheme but Christian was on the warpath and Steven couldn't blame the man. He looked like the villain for invoking such dubious tactics, using the crux of an abandoned son as a stepping stone for the sole purpose of amassing an upper hand.   But a hint of masochism shone through in the way Steven acted. Three years ago when Christian and the crew killed his father, law enforcement would have gone to hell and back for his scalp. They would have taken anything Larry or Steven said at fact value, no questions asked. The evidence was sky high and only escalated when Devin Shakur annexed himself into the picture. They confronted Christian twenty one months ago and everybody still wanted their heads.   Yet, family pride kept Larry and Steven from turning over the massive stacks of hard evidence. They wanted to be the ones who ended the war and who could make their relatives proud. They allowed obsession to get the better of them. Their reputations dwindled and they lost their jobs. No longer was either brother seen as a credible source of information and the evidence disappeared.   Christian drove them further up the wall, knocking loved ones off one by one until only the brothers stood. They should have packed up and gone to a remote location, counted their losses while they had the chance, but they couldn't go. Too much was invested in the endeavor. Before Steven could contemplate a new line of attack, Christian forced Larry to place a gun against his own head.   Steven could have admitted defeat and gone away, but he called Caleb and baited the biker. Now, he faced the biggest dilemma of his twenty six years. All eyes were focused on him, and like an army Christian's entourage was descending, hell bent on leaving nothing more than a corpse. Steven wouldn't see tomorrow, one way or the other.   The fact such an elaborate was committed indicated Steven wasn't ready to pack his bag and raise the white flag. He still wanted to fuck with Christian one more time while the chance remained.   Two doors slammed, breaking Steven from his introspective trance. He rushed over to the bedroom window and stared outside. A car sped off down the road and his two assassins were already storming up the driveway. Steven knew he had to make the call. It was imperative. There was no going back.   He pushed the three buttons and held the receiver up to his ear. In an instant, a voice on the other end spoke.   "911, what is your emergency?" The dispatcher said.   This would be his Oscar winning performance, "1913 Macey Lane, there are two armed men inside my home..." Steven made sure the dispatcher heard the pants of fear, although she had no idea it was an illusion.   Steven violently kicked at the bathroom door, pretending to be Christian and Devin attempting to break through.   "HELP, THEY ARE RIGHT OUTSIDE!" He shouted.   "Alright, give me a second to get the information out. What is your name?"   "Steven, Steven Connors."   "Steven, hang in there for a few moments. You are doing great."   Steven cradled the phone against his neck and recalled one of the many tricks learned on the force, the art of throwing his voice. He put his best effort in and put on a heavy Southern accent, "COME ON OUT THERE, MOTHERFUCKER!" It wasn't the best Christian Daniels impersonation, but the dispatcher didn't notice the difference.   "Ok, Steven, we have units on the way. I'd like you to stay on the phone as long as you can. Is there anything you can place against the door to prevent the intruders from getting inside?"   "No...I'm locked inside the bathroom." Steven slammed his foot into the door again and slid his shoe up the door to fiddle with the knob, straining his hamstring to provide maximum effect. A cramp was digging itself up from the base of his leg and soon he would have to rest, but the act was going off without a hitch.   "If you can, Steven" The dispatcher interrupted, "Look for anything that will stall them getting through the door."   Steven muttered "OK" in a nervous tone and kicked the middle hinge on the door. The dispatcher worked frantically to keep the incoming patrol cars updated with information.   "I can't find anything...Please hurry. I don't have anything to defend myself!"   Steven returned to a horizontal position and nudged the door opened with his feet, creating the myth Christian and Devin finally forced their way through.   "AHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHH!"   "Steven? What happened?" The dispatcher responded.   He continued to let out the shriek and angled the gun at the precise degree, knowing what his fellow law enforcement officers would look for to declare the situation a homicide. He threw the phone into the air, adjusted his left hand and pulled the trigger.   "Steven..." The dispatcher said, waiting a moment before repeating herself, "Steven..." She got no response the second time.   Blood spattered against the wall of the bathtub and Steven Connors slumped to a final resting place just in front of the tub.   As they were making their way around the side of the home, Devin reached out and grabbed his brother by the arm bringing both of them to a stop. His eyes glanced toward the second floor and heard the furious barking of Maverick.   "What the fuck was that?" Devin said, reaching into his back pocket and pulling out a handgun of his own.   "Fucker's probably watchin' TV on a loud set, now let's go, goddammit!" Christian roared back, practically dragging Devin around the corner.   "No..." Devin's eyes scanned around the neighborhood and up the street, "Something doesn't feel right. We gotta jet."   Christian shot a glance over his shoulder, sizing himself up in a confrontational manner toward his brother. Devin tried to hide the gulp and looked up into Christian's harrowing eyes, holding a good amount of his own discontent.   "We done come this fucking far, I ain't gonna have one of yer bullshit hunches fuck this shit up." Christian said, extending his arms out, "I need to remind you of what he did?"   Devin sucked air through his teeth and turned his head away, disgusted by Christian's subjectivity. If this were 99.9 percent of other jobs, he would err on the side of caution, but the burning desire to even the score drove him. He wanted to end Steven's life in the worst way possible.   "Sides, if activity picks up, Jase and Mike will hit us up." Christian responded while power walking around and up the back porch. Devin stood in place and swore under his breath, reluctantly agreeing with the point. He didn't take his hand off the 9 millimeter while scaling the stairs.   The small tidbit of information he didn't know was that Christian turned off the cellular phones. Jason and Michael screamed concern to both of their brothers, encouraging them to leave the house at once. Jason's scanners were going off at an alarming rate. Michael moved into place two streets over and watched through binoculars as Christian muscled open a door leading into the laundry room.   He tried waving his arms to Devin but wasn't able to be located in time. Michael slammed a fist into his own leg and let out a string of profanity. If they didn't get out soon, he would have to risk his own head and save them. The cops weren't that far away.   Immediately upon entering the kitchen and spying the wreckage, both men knew something was terribly wrong. Christian placed his hand against a small space of kitchen counter that wasn't covered in the destruction of dishes. He reached into his pocket and pulled out the blade, fully expecting Steven to pop from around a corner and look for a free shot. Devin tightly clutched the trigger of his weapon while the two moved into the living room.   Christian said "Hang close" and dashed around the corner, knife extended into the air and ready to strike. The room was ruined but empty. Christian stepped around glass and left a footprint on the Egyptian table. Devin forgot to look down and notice the incriminating piece of evidence because he didn't know what the hell was going on.   "Could someone have gotten to him, first?" Devin said, eyes darting around the ceiling and looking for some kind of video camera.   "Fuck no," Christian responded, "Nobody's stupid enough'ta come round and take this fucker out head of me."   The growls of Maverick grew louder and louder. Christian reached out and placed his hand on the railing, eyes shifting to both sides. Around the left corner was a small work office and to his right was a small closet. Christian looked over to Devin and ordered him in that direction. He walked across the hall and threw open the door, almost firing a shot off on instinct. Nothing.   "It's clear."   "Goddammit."   Outside the house on their cellular phones, Michael and Jason threw frantic dialogue at one another.   "You gotta them the fuck out of there!" Jason shouted.   "There are too many goddamn kids out here, man." Michael responded, anxiously tapping the steering wheel with one hand and looking through the binoculars at another. A horde of kids lined the street. On one end a group was playing stick ball and at the other a few were chalking the street in preparation for a game of hopscotch.   "Well can you at least see something in there?" Jason asked.   "I can't see shit. The prick must have shut everything up when he heard we were coming. How much time do we got?"   "Not long..." Jason paused and went to his scanners. The new information dribbling across staggered him, "Oh fuck me rotten. Fuck! Fuck! Fuck!"   "What?"   "They are coming in force because the dispatch says C was in there, remembered his voice from television."   "The...WHAT?" Michael screamed, trying to get a better position on the house.   "The bitch claims C's voice was heard in the background when some shot went off."   "HE WASN'T IN THE HOUSE YET!" Michael replied.   Jason despondently responded, "Well the cops don't know that."   "So they think..." Michael got hit with a tidal wave of realization, "Oh...FUCK! NO!"   "He's setting them the fuck up! You gotta get them out of there however you can."   "I can't just fucking get out of the car. Too many people will make me."   "Go around the fr-"   "We both know the cops are gonna shut down both ways out of the front."   Jason jammed a fist into the steering wheel, startling himself when the horn went off, "You know what, fuck this shit. I'm on my way. Try calling the house line, maybe that'll get through to them."   Michael pulled out another cellular phone from the glove compartment and hit a series of buttons. He put the phone up to his ear and discarded it just as fast when a pulsating beep invaded his ear, "Shit is busy."   "I'll be there in a minute. We're not leaving them in there." Jason said, dropping the phone back into its holster and screeching from the alley about three minutes away. He'd need to get in there a fourth of the time to safely get everybody away.   Christian popped from the guest bedroom, unaware of the dire straits he and his brother were in. Steven's car was parked out front so he knew for certain the man was home. Word on the street was he only left to get the dog some food. He hadn't even left the house for his own nourishment, electing to order in every night for the past two weeks.   A trembling hand led Devin in following his brother's footsteps back into the hallway. Maverick's barking picked up more of a nervous tone. It had been minutes since he heard the shot, but Christian and Devin presumed the dog's cries were nothing more than a decoy.   Now there was only one room left to check, the master bedroom. Devin and Christian stopped outside the door and looked at one another. Throwing caution to the wind they barreled through the door and sent it sliding across the floor where it collided with the upside down bed.   Devin checked around the room while Christian eyeballed yet another wrecked room with confused eyes, "What the fu..."   The overwhelming smell of gunpowder perked Christian's nose and he followed the trail. Devin continued to scan around for any place Steven could be waiting. He checked the ceiling but it wasn't false or made of tiles. He grabbed hold of the closet and slung it open, gun pointed down.   Maverick looked at him and growled furiously. Devin slammed the door and made sure it was locked.   Meanwhile, Christian finally discovered where Steven was located.   "GODFUCKINGDAMMIT THAT PIECE OF SHIT!" Christian screamed and pounded his fist into the door with all his might.   "What? What?" Devin asked but received no answer. He scurried around the corner to the sight causing Christian's anger.   Steven Connors body couldn't have been positioned better if Steven Spieldberg was directing a movie. His head tilted over the left side of his body. The phone used to call 911 rested in his right hand. His left middle finger was extended toward Christian. The coup de grace was in his face. Even though there was a giant hole in his forehead, Steven managed to etch a massive smile on it before the bullet struck.   It was the ultimate fuck you to Christian. Steven took his own life so Christian wouldn't have the satisfaction of making him suffer.   When Devin turned around, he saw the angry form of his brother heavily heaving behind him. With a shove, he moved Devin away and got into the impassible face of Steven. Bits of his teeth were grinding on one another while he tried to hold back emotion. His fingers slowly balled into fists while his eyes got bigger and angrier. The breathing reached a fiver pitch and his face had turned the darkest possible red.   He no longer had control over his own body, slipping into a madness induced trance and grabbing Steven around the throat. If there was any life remaining in his body, Christian was going to remove it.   "You son of a bitch!"   Christian squeezed tighter, numbness creeping into his hand while the rest of his body leaned forward to exert more pressure.   "YOU SON OF A BITCH!"   Christian used the leverage and slammed Steven's head against the front of the tub four times. Each shot resonated louder and louder through the bedroom. Devin could do nothing but watch in astonishment at the sight of his brother snapping.   But no matter how much force Christian applied in banging Steven's head against the plain white tub, his middle finger did not go down and the smile did not decrease.   In fact, Christian would swear the grin had gotten bigger.   "GRAHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHH!"   He smashed Steven's head into the tub a final time and doubled over momentarily, catching his breath. Devin thought about intervening because he believed the sound of sirens to be closing in. He took a step forward, only for Christian to rise up inches away from Steven's grin.   "You think you fuckin' won this don't you asshole?"   Christian stood up and chipped the end of the wooden frame around the sink.   "You ain't won shit! You couldn't even be a goddamn man and FACE ME!"   He rifled a kick into Steven's head, trying to wipe the smile from his face.   "You ain't NOTHING but a GODDAMN CU-"   The sound of police sirens roared ever closer, catching both Devin and Christian's ears. Before they could exchange a glance, the back door flung open and the pitter patter of footsteps crunched glass on the kitchen floor.   "C, D!" Michael screamed, looking down at his gloves to see if they had been cut.   Jason picked up where Michael left off, "We gotta get the fuck outta here or we're gonna be surrounded by pigs!"   Part of Christian did not want to leave the scene even though the pieces of the puzzle were rapidly falling into place, and more would if he did not vacate. Devin, no longer willing to let Christian's rage act as a liability, grabbed a hold of his ear and screamed, "MOVE YOUR DAMN FEET, SOLDIER!"   He gave a disgruntled nod of the head, leaving Steven behind, and made it his mission to get all of them out.   Michael and Jason listened to the rumbling of the footsteps pushing down the stairs mingled with the screech of police vehicles dragging across the pavement outside. Michael encouraged Devin and Christian forward, "HURRY UP GODDAMMIT!" Christian and Devin nearly toppled over one another to get around the corner and through the living room. They ignored the glass gnawing into the floor and plowed through the back door, running incredibly fast across the well manicured lawn.   The only thing stopping them from getting off the property was a ten foot high fence, which didn't even register to Christian when he took a portion out with his giant frame.   "Don't look back." Christian said, his body not used to running so hard. He wasn't a twenty five year old buck who could handle such strain on his muscles, but he fought through. There was no way he was going to be pinned for something he actually didn't do.   Police officers kicked open the front door and poured through the lower level.   On the other side of the giant fence, Marcus Ferguson was building a sandcastle in his backyard under the watchful eye of his mother. She wasn't particularly fond of the other children in the neighborhood and their aggressive tendencies, so she opted to keep Marcus away from them and allowed him to explore his creative side by building sandcastles. His natural talent would more than likely lead him to a career in some form of art.   Her nerves reached an unmatchable apex when the fence crashed down and four shady individuals plowed into her yard. She leaped from the porch in a single jump and made a beeline for her child because they were doing the same.   "Stay away from my baby!" Teresa shouted, pumping her legs furiously.   "We don't want shit to do with your kid, woman." Michael replied, maneuvering toward the other side of the yard where the Civic was parked.   "Wash yo mouth out with soap you criminal!" Teresa replied, snatching her child from the sand and holding her in his arms. She would defend her baby until the bitter end if any of them were lying. She cradled his head against her body, not wanting him to look at any of them pass.   But he glanced underneath a little crease in his mother's shirt and watched all of them intently. He remembered the lines in Christian Daniels face. He remembered the awkwardly shaped hair and black dress attire of Devin Shakur. He remembered the stubble and facial texture of Jason Hardy. He remembered the gray hair and startling height of Michael Blaze.   "Fucking witnesses" Christian muttered under his breath. They would be seeing one another at some point in the future. No doubt the cops would spot the hole and inquire about whether or not the Ferguson's saw anything noteworthy.   The quartet broke up into pairs, Devin and Jason bolting across the street into the car parked further away. Michael and Christian were about to collapse when they reached the Civic, forcing open the door and flooring the car. Jason followed one second later while Devin hung onto the door and shut it just before colliding with a parked pickup truck.   They had to get out of the area before the cops decided to start any kind of dragnet. They would have to find a place to ditch the vehicles because there wasn't any doubt in their minds a few people saw the auspicious vehicles going four times over the speed limit in a neighborhood littered with children. Michael chimed in, "We need to get out on the highway so move toward there." Jason replied, "Yeah, I figured that much out." After dropping from 95 to 35 miles per hour, Jason saw Michael's car fall in line behind his. The sound of Christian chugging a bottle of water was heard. They snapped around two other corners and were running parallel with the highway. It could be seen just over a series of trees. Jason slid over into the correct lane and prepared to speed up. Only one stop light separated them from going 75 without raising eyebrows. Now the backlash from Christian's decision was brought down upon both he and Devin. Jason looked over at Devin, arms raised into the air, voice full of intensity, "WHAT THE HELL WERE YOU THINKING?" "The hell are you talking about?" Devin snapped back, "I was the one trying to get him the hell out of there from the START!" "I was trying to fucking wave at you before you got in the damn house!" Michael said. "How was I supposed to see you from two damn streets over? My eyes don't work that well!" The stoplight impeded them and so both cars came to a halt, waiting while a few others maneuvered toward the area of the crime scene. Police still roamed through the home but weren't yet canvassing the area for witnesses. An unmarked policemen saw the four arguing and slowed down, a bit perplexed any of them were in the neighborhood. This section of town mostly held families and none of these guys fit the bill. To say he was anxious would be an understatement, anger rescinding from his body and turning into fear. Christian The entire plane ride up, Christian thought long and hard about his decisions. Jason was right, emotion snagged and manipulated him to the point where all four of them were in immense danger. No evidence from past crimes remained and ever since they came back to the country, they were only under suspicion for their activities. Now, multiple eyewitnesses could place them fleeing the scene and more than likely a few saw them entering the home. Christian replayed the scene over and over in his head. It wouldn't be hard for an attorney to convict them of the crime. His fingerprints were on multiple locations throughout the house. He wouldn't have needed to go there for any other reason but murder, the prosecution would say. The biggest regret was plowing through the fence because a mother never forgets, especially when she fears her child to be in danger. If some kind of blockade wasn't placed, soon they would be located. Devin and Christian wouldn't make it to ReVolution 200. Steven got exactly what he wanted. His credibility was restored and Christian felt the harsh pains of being on defense. Christian took a long chug of his beer and placed it on the small glass table. What he would need to do in order to have a chance at redemption was staggering. Never before was he in a position where the odds were stacked against him. He wouldn't mind borrowing a gun and blowing his own brains out now to rid of the troubles. Nobody hunted a dead man. Which, he knew, sooner or later, everybody in the hotel had a high probability of becoming.
