A Common State of Inner Violence

undated

Date: Halo time “It is not the strongest of the species that survives, nor the most intelligent, but the one most responsive to change.” - Charles Darwin. I The musky funk of a hundred sweating bodies together in a sardine-like squeeze clogged his nostrils as he dropped his bulky frame on the bench with all the grace of an M1 Abrams. His chest heaving with every lungful of the thick, muggy air and beads of perspiration seeping from the pores on his forehead, he wondered if this incommodious atmosphere was the most unpleasant he'd ever found himself in. After dragging the back of his hand and wiping off some of the sweat he looked up, squinting in the filthy light that struggled to penetrate two dusty light bulbs that badly needed replacing. The basement was small and choked with a lot more people than it's architect had ever intended. Ventilation was poor and the brown brick walls were coated with a sticky, sweaty glaze, immediately soaking into his black Agnostic Front t-shirt and causing him to recoil as he leant back. Not exactly the most aesthetically pleasing surroundings – certainly not for a man who'd spent the best part of the last year touring the country's finest wrestling arenas – but the ramshackle room provided him with more solace than any sold-out stadium ever could. Leaning down and dropping one of his calloused mitts to the floor to retrieve a ream of white grafter's tape he took a few moments to soak in the vibe of a domain that he'd been away from for long it almost felt alien. The mob before him buckled and swayed as one, parting only to open up a small bit of space in the centre of the room to make for a gladiator's stage. Across the room a tattooed twentysomething in a wifebeater bawled obscenities at another for standing too close while the vast majority of the crowd grew impatient and restless. Even at the best of times a fighting crowd was never the friendliest group of people in the world and the cramped, sweltering conditions weren't helping much. Unravelling the tape, Jason carefully began to wrap it around his hands, starting at the palm and working his way up to the wrist. He took his time. Wrap them too loose and the tape's gonna come away and you've got not protection; sweat's gonna loosen the adhesive. They'll fall away and you're dealing with bloodied, skinned paws for a couple weeks. Wrap them too hard, though, and you're not even gonna notice the broken bones until you unravel the tape and your hand collapses like a gutted fish. He finished up and bashed his fist into his palm. Extinguishing his cigarette and rising to his feet, Jason pulled his damp tee over his shoulders and blindly tossed it to the floor. As soon as he reached his feet a familiar sting in his ribs stung his nerves and he grimaced. Even after two months the results of Jay Phoenix's assault still hadn't fully healed and the lurid reminder of his tattered wrestling career had lingered in his chest for far longer than he'd thought. A shot to the ribs and he'd go down like a sack of potatoes. He'd considered taping his chest up too, but that'd only paint a target. Slowly he pushed his way through the throng, on his way to recapturing a thrill that no amount of dancing around a squared circle with Bryan Dawkins could ever provide. He parted the bodies like strips of Velcro and stomped his boots on the floorboards as he reached the centre. The bear that stood across from him was big, no doubt about it. At least two inches taller than Jason's 6'4” and with maybe a ten/twenty pound weight advantage, the kid looked custom-built to absorb a punch or two. He sized him up, Jason; trying to build a mental picture of what he was up against. His face was far too pristine; his face almost entirely free of blemishes and imperfections and his nose yet to be broken. There was a youthful exuberance behind his eyes that existed in no man who'd brawled for money on more than a couple of occasions. The kid was inexperienced, definitely; might even have been the first time he'd done this. He was build like a brick outhouse, sure, but there's no use in having the goods if you don't have the know-how. The roar of the impatient crowd raised a few decibels and Jason put up his dukes, slowly arching towards the kid on his tiptoes. As soon as he drew close to him the kid swung a haymaker than glided harmlessly passed the ducking Jason's ear. Natas drew closer, inviting another mistimed punch from the kid before taking a step back. Fuckin' rook, he thought. Gon' be easier than I thought. Slowly Jason backed away, keeping his guard up and the kid was after him like a bull seeing red. A couple of jabs smashed against Jason's raised forearms and an uppercut was swiftly dodged. Planting a stiff one in the exposed rookie's own ribcage, Jason stepped to the side and watched as the brown-haired greenhorn winced and looked into the eyes of a man who'd done this many times more than he had. He continued to invite the offence, tiring the kid out. He was big and full of anger, sure, but his chest sagged like an alcoholic's and there was little tone to his arm muscles; the kid's conditioning was poor and Jason knew exactly what he was doing. His opponent was all over him like a nasty rash and blindly threw furious fists at Jason with little success. A quick side swipe was swatted away by Jason struck like a cobra as he threw a taped fist into the kid's nose. A crimson eruption burst from his nostrils and the kid raised his hand to his nose as an unfamiliar rusty taste sifted through the corner of his mouth. Jason's got him reeling and he knows it. Abandoning the softly-softly approach, Jason strode forward before the kid had a chance to see red and planted a left hook on his chin. The kid staggered back towards the crowd, almost rebounding from the swell, and he's wide open as Natas powered forward with two jackhammer blows to the stomach. The crowd were rabid; calling for blood and the kid's head and Jason was only happy to oblige. Just a couple of shots away from a reasonable win bonus, Jason hooked both of the kids arms and tried to force his torso down. He was pushing down on the boy with all his might but the kid was stronger than he looked and desperate not to let Jason put him to see. So Jason leaned forward, putting all his upper body forward as he thrust his knee upwards into the kid's stomach. Now he was really squirming. The heat was getting too much and both bodies were far too sweat-lubricated for Jason to keep the hold on for much longer. He re-thought his strategy, but before he could do anything a sudden burst of power from the kid ignited beneath him. Suddenly, swiftly, his opponent brought his head up and Jason had no time to react as the back of the kid's head smashed into his jaw with the force of a freight train. The last thing Jason Natas remembered was a dull thud as his body hit the ground and everything went black. II “Aidan, what are you doing?” his girlfriend queried from the passenger's seat of his beaten-up 1994 Chrysler as it pulled up curbside. A well-groomed gentlemen in a white shirt and waistcoat strolled up to the car, reaching the for the door handle. “Parking lot's 'round the back...” “I know.” The muscle-bound twenty-two year old winked as he slid his athletic anatomy from the vehicle, stepping out into the cool, New York City air. Rubbing his hands together as an icy burst of East coast wind breezed through him, he handed the keys to the valet and warned him about the problem with the right indicator. She'd only been out of the car for a matter of seconds and already Amanda was shivering, clutching her small, black shawl tight around her petite figure. Immediately he swooped around the hood and left the car to the valet, taking his girlfriend by the arm and leading her under the canopy and through a set of double doors into the restaurant. What a place, she thought as she first laid her eyes on the lavish interior. With deep, wine red coloured carpets, rich mahogany furniture and gold coloured fittings Spencer's certainly looked as classy as it's reputation suggested. A soft score of classical music was just audible over the faint chitter-chatter of a higher class of patron than she was used to and penguin suit-wearing waiters attended to the clientèle with charm and grace. For a girl used to to eating out at Dennys and TGI Fridays it was a lot to take in. Her time to gawk in awe was restricted though as Aidan took her towards a short, plump lady manning the front-of-house desk. “Chase. Table for two. Think we're about ten minutes early,” Aidan said to answer her inquisitive gaze. Soon enough they were whisked away towards the table that had been set aside for them. A small, round surface with three sets of silver cutlery already set out and a candle melting wax down the sides of the wine bottle it had been jammed into. Their waiter pulled out her seat for her to sit down and she cast Aidan a curious glance as he ordered “the most expensive bottle of Champagne you've got”. “Aid, what are you doing?” “What am I doing?” he shrugged. “It's a crime to treat my girlfriend to some fine dining now, is it?” “No, no. Of course not. It's just...” Her voice trailed off; she was still under the spell of the exquisite surroundings in which she found herself. Aidan had really pulled out all the stops this time, even if she did feel a little out of place sitting among such obviously wealthy patrons in her $20 Wal-Mart dress. “This is going to cost at least half of this week's paycheque.” “This week's paycheque, maybe. But next week's? That's a different matter all together.” He leaned forward across the table, taking his partner by the hand and looking into the olive green eyes he'd fallen in love with. He smiled. Again she flashed him that curious glance, is if he was up to no good. “Is there something you're not telling me?” He leaned backwards, breaking his hold on her hand and ruffling his dark blue shirt against the back of the chair. “What if I told you that my days of wrestling in front of fifty people for thirty bucks a pop were coming to an end?” he chirped in his light, airy New York accent. “What are you talking about?” Amanda said as she narrowed her eyes. “You're finished with it?” “No. No, nothing like that.” Aidan couldn't help but chuckle to himself. Delighted himself and grinning like the Cheshire Cat, he felt like the most important man in the universe all of a sudden. “You ever heard of a little thing called the 'Dual Halo'?” Dual Halo. The term was completely alien to her. She'd followed her partner to a couple of independent shows, sure, but this young lady wasn't a hardcore wrestling fan by a long shot. “A what?” He smiled as the waiter returned with an already popped bottle of the fizzy stuff and placed a couple of menus down in front of him. Picking the glass up and waiting for the fizz to die down, he looked across at his red-haired partner who was still completely puzzled. “Well I can't say 'til it's signed, sealed and delivered, but let's just say our lives are about to change, 'Manda. For the better.” Smiling and swallowing a mouthful of the overly-expensive beverage, Amanda knew Aidan was up to something. Finally she began to relax in the splendour of her surroundings, her boyfriend's infectious bliss made it hard for her not to smile. III Jason Natas – twenty six years dying and not yet dead- felt like he'd been dragged through the deepest reaches of hell from the very moment he awoke. Trashed, scattered, battered and bruised and still not over last night's concussion, his immediate attempt at rising from his bed was thwarted as a white hot bolt of pain shot up from the back of his neck and into his skull. He rested his head down on the pillow and took a couple of minutes to breath and regain focus. Slowly he slid up against the white wall behind him, propping his lifeless carcass up and closing his eyes. It ached; from the tips of his toes to the top of his head. Every muscle in his body felt stretched to breaking point and his brain felt swollen and ready to pop. The jaw was the worst though; God, how it stung. Rubbing the back of his hand across the rough two-week beard that adorned his jawline, he forgot how painful the shoot fighting business was. They came flooding back to him; memories of the previous evening. The kid with the power of an ox yet the elegance of a newborn foal on ice. It was supposed to be a quick, simple mop-up job. Jason used to eat punks like that up in seconds a few years ago and by all accounts it looked light his first straight-up brawl in over a year was going that way too. It was a stroke of luck, a complete fluke. He could fight a thousand times in a row and that blow to the jaw wouldn't have happened again. The kid was trying to break free, the kid; he didn't have a clue what he was doing, and yet he knocked Jason Natas – a man who'd fought the likes of Troy Douglas and Xavier Kannon for a living – the fuck out. Losing like that pained Jason, and he recalled why he stepped away from the dirty business of fighting guys for money in a disused basement or a murky back alley for the bright lights of PRIME in the first place. But Jason Natas was fighter, not a wrestler. He didn't have a clue how to “entertain” and he couldn't “cut a promo” to save his life. A brought a stiff, abrasive style to the ring that didn't sit well with the high-flying spot monkeys he found himself surrounded by, and on a roster full of Hollywood pretty boys and “sports entertainers” he felt like the blackest sheep in the flock. Jason would never win any accolades for his technical wrestling skills, no; his style was much more suited towards a less-stylised approach to violence. His greatest talent was one that he could not justify in PRIME, and so he'd drifted back towards the underground fighting scene in hope of recapturing a thrill that he thought he'd lost. Slowly, carefully, Jason slid his bare feet over the side of the bed and pulled a dirty tee shirt from the floor. Pulling it over his bruised frame, he hopped to his feet too fast and stumbled straight away. Stabilising himself by putting his tattooed mitt against the wall, he shook the dizziness from his aching head and stumbling through towards the kitchen. There was no doubt that the previous night was a complete and utter disaster, and the pain that shot across his jaw as he parted his lips to swallow the painkillers only amplified that feeling. Dragging himself around the breakfast bar and sitting himself down in front of a ridiculous LCD television that was more than a little out of place in his unspectacular apartment, he felt for the first time like a man without a reason for existence. His PRIME contract was up for renewal in a couple of weeks and he hadn't spoken to his agency since Jay Phoenix had put him out of service back in January. On that front, things were looking bleak; he knew fine that Najera, Loftman and Jewel had tried to get hold of him on numerous occasions and yet he'd shunned their calls. Unsure of whether or not he wanted to go back into a world he never really cared for in the first place, PRIME's Anti-Superstar chose instead to disappear within himself. Fighting wasn't a viable option, he knew that now. There's no way that a man can sustain a living fighting bare-knuckle for any length of time realistically. Sure, he was good at it, pitbull-esque in his ruthlessness and machine-like in his efficiency, but if last night told him anything it was that it simply wasn't worth it. The cash wasn't bad for what the job was worth, but if it meant waking up feeling like this every day of the week then Jason's thirst for blood suddenly evaporated. But was wrestling really any better? The money was immense, yeah, and the perks were phenomenal, but if Jason had to spend any more time in the presence of Chandler Tsonda, Bryan Dawkins and Tyler Rayne then he might end up jumping off a bridge. And the publicity that being a part of the biggest wrestling promotion in the continent? Unbearable. Potemkin too. Jesus Christ. Those guys were supposed to be his agents, they were supposed to be his guiding light through all of this, and yet they'd turned out to be as big a hindrance as anyone he'd ever met inside the squared circle. This was a jam, no doubt. He sank down into the leather couch and sized up his options when the sting in his ribs returned and the visage of the man who did it reflected on his inner cortex. Phoenix. “Fucking cunt,” Jason grumbled. Brock Shepherd, Bryan Dawkins, DUI, Jimmy Bonafide, Troy Douglas. Compared to Jay Phoenix, every other enemy he'd made in his time as a professional wrestler seemed irrelevant. Dawkins may have defeated him and Shepherd may have humiliated, but none of them had put him on the shelf, none of them had deliberately put him out of commission like Phoenix had. Jason could handle losing. As long as he put up a decent fight he could reluctantly accept his failure and move on, but to lay down without fighting back was something he couldn't deal with, and that's exactly what he'd be doing if he didn't come back to PRIME and bite the decorated veteran in the throat. He knew then exactly what needed to be done. Jason sighed, brushing his tongue against a tooth that had suddenly began to work it's way from the root. He made a mental note involving dentists. Shuffling across the room, he picked up his bulky, outdated cellphone and keyed in a number he hadn't even thought of dialling in months. IV “So Jason. Predecessor. V Something succeeded or replaced by something else. He'd rattled the word's meaning around his skull for longer than he realised and after almost an hour he hadn't even gotten as far as sticking the key in the ignition. He wasn't exactly the deepest of thinkers, Jason; but Jewel's lexical choice and unintentional lack of discretion had him stumped. Ruffling his thick brows into a steely gaze, he swiped the back of his hand against Chase's own, sending the pad skittering across the soggy floor. Aidan glowered at him, took a step forward. “Hey man, what the fuck is your problem.” “Heh.” Suddenly Jason pulled his hand up and clasped it round Aidan's neck. Brute force and the element of surprise allowed him to slam the smaller guy against an alley wall decorated liberally with crude spray paintings, but the kid was putting up a struggle. Arms clamped around Jason's hand trying to break the grip, Aidan wildly kicked his feet out, one catching Jason in the shin. Immediately Jason balled his left hand to a fist and slammed it hard into the side of Chase's skull. The blow was enough to convince the kid to quit it. “Now, you gonna tell me what's goin' on now?” Jason's nicotine-ravaged tones hissed. Chase was squirming, wincing though the disorientation brought about by the sudden blow to the temple. “I don't know what you're talk-” “Come on!” Jason squeezed, tightening his grip around the boy's throat. “Ain't fucking 'round, here.” “I swear man!” barked Chase, his voice hoarse and his face growing red as it gets harder and harder for him to take oxygen into his lungs. “I don't have a clue!” Jason shrugged. “Fair enough.” With his right hand he briefly slackened his grip, offering Aidan Chase a brief respite and a hope for a quick release. This was all but shattered as Jason, with his free hand, plucked the cigarette from his mouth and slowly, agonisingly, brought it towards Aidan's neck. It extinguished with a faint hiss as the hot ash met with the skin on Chase's neck but it did just enough damage the draw a bloodcurdling scream from the kid. Aidan dropped to all fours as Jason released his grip and then kicked him behind a nearby dumpster so as not to be seen by any passers-boy. Grabbing tightly to the singed skin on his neck, Aidan looked up as Jason sparked up another cigarette. “You're out of your fuckin' mind,” he forced from a sandpaper-rough throat. “Answers, fucker.” Jason said, leaning in menacingly with another cigarette. With the threat of another burn looming Aidan cowered, raising his hand to his face. “Alright! Alright!” he wailed. Jason backed-off just a little. “We're working out the details on a contract. I'm a wrestler; I was there about a job. You-...” “... my job.” Aidan gulped hard, resting his back against the wall as a trickle of blooded oozed between his fingers. Jason bit down on the cigarette and exhaled. Of course he was Aidan's “predecessor”. It was as clear as crystal. He cursed himself for not figuring it out earlier. Before Jason had the chance to plot further Aidan was on the defensive, making his plea. “I swear, man; I had nothing to do with this! I didn't wanna take your job,” his voice trembled. “Oh yeah?” Jason raised an eyebrow. “Seems to me like yer right in the middle 'a this fuckin' thing.” “No. NO! I'm just a broke indie kid looking for a job, man. I didn't know that this was gonna put anyone outta work!” He was honest, sincere. If Jason were a man of more remorse he may have felt obliged to cut Aidan Chase some slack in this situation, because, truthfully, he hadn't done anything wrong. “Please man, you gotta believe me...” Jason paused, pondering his options. Shifting his glances around the empty alley, his silence only increased the apprehension swelling inside Aidan Chase and Jason could see that the kid was breaking into a sweat. Chase had given up; Jason had him at his mercy and it took a lot less effort than he thought. Typical career-wrestler, Jason thought. Wet fuckin' blanket. “Alright, here's what you're gonna do,” Jason said as he knelt down. “Got a cell on ya?” Chase nodded. “Good. Pull it out, get those fuckers on the line, tell 'em you ain't takin' the gig no more. Say you've got cold feet, somethin'.” “But my gir-” “Shove yer sob story up yer ass,” Jason bit back with a snarl. “You take yer fuckin' phone and dial that fuckin' number.” “Y-you can't make me!” Chase managed to summon a final drop of bravado. “I'll call the cops, man!” “Heh.” Jason snorted; chuckled, almost. Slowly he rose to his feet. “Well, ya could do that, yeah. But you ain't the one in the drivin' seat here, kid. Like to see you sign a contract if I break both yer fuckin' arms, and besides...” he reached into the depths of his pocket, pulling out the sheet of paper adorned with Aidan Chase's home address. “... I have this.” Jason smiled. Not knowing what the man standing before him was capable of, Chase was unstable to stop the acidic bile torrent that suddenly exploded from his stomach like a geyser. He doubled over to the side as his stomach emptied itself across the concrete. Jason shook his head, watching carefully as Aidan wiped the vomit from his mouth and pulled his cellphone from the pocket of his soaked black dress pants. “Y-you're...” He gave up and dialled the number. Trembling, lifting the phone to his ear, Aidan Chase made the call that would drive a stake through the heart of a lifelong ambition. “Hello. Put me through to the Potemkin suite, please. Thanks...” VIII “Aidan! Wait a minute!” Mr. Loftman shouted into his silver Samsung as his oblivious co-workers continued in a state post-Natas revelry behind him. “This is PRIME! This is your shot! You can't be doing this!” he protested, but it was far too late. Aidan Chase had already hung-up. To say that Mr. Loftman was stunned would be a gross understatement. The plan was supposed to be foolproof, and yet it had collapsed spectacularly before him in a way even he couldn't have predicted. Clenching a fist with his right hand, Loftman drove it violently into the wall and skinned his knuckles. “FUCK!” he roared. Najera and Jewel jumped. “What the fuck?” the Hispanic mumbled. “Uhhh, Nate. You okay?” quizzed Jewel, completely bamboozled by his colleague's sudden change in attitude. “Yeah, who the hell was that?” enquired Najera. “It was Aidan,” Loftman said in a glum, defeated tone. His whole body seemed to sag as he let out a frustrated sigh and aimed a stress-relieving kick at the leg of the table. Jewel rose to his feet, holding his hands to his side in effort to curb his employee's sudden anger. “Whoa, man! Seriously. Calm down...” “'Calm down'!?” Loftman screamed. “We're fucked, man. Completely.” “Wait. Why? What did he say?!” “He's out.” Jewel raised an eyebrow. “He's... out.” “Yes, he's fuckin' out!” Loftman stormed around the table, fuming. We don't have the time! We've got a thirteen day deadline and we need to get something inked NOW! There's no time to scour the indies for another guy...” Loftman stopped in his tracks, shuddering at the mere implication of what Najera was suggesting. “Jason...” The word seemed to fall from Jewel's mouth like a domino. “Oh no. We've got an agreement; they could sue us, Loft...” Nathaniel Loftman stomped a foot into the floor and clenched his fists. Breathing heavily through his nose, his face the colour of beetroot, he knew that there was nothing he could say to make the words coming from his co-worker's mouth anything less than the cold, hard truth. “That Bronx fuck is behind this, I'm fuckin' tellin' you...” “It doesn't matter now,” said Najera coldly. “It's ove-” “What, you're just going to brush this off?!” “Nate. Pulling his shoe from the dent and uttering every four-letter word he could thing of in his angered state, he stormed out of the room. IX A few blocks away, Jason Natas turned the key in the ignition to pull his damaged car away from the alley with a smile on his face. Uncontrollably it spread to a full, ear-to-ear grin and it wasn't long before a low chuckle segued into a deep belly laugh that he was helpless to curtail. Thinking nothing of the life he'd left broken and beaten in the alleyway or the pain in his chin, Jason Natas, delighted, turned on the radio on to a station of hardcore punk rock and immediately began scheming the demise of Jay Phoenix. The Anti-Superstar would ride again.
