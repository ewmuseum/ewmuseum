"...mommy, too?"

8 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/08/07 * * * * * Amanda was asleep in the bed, her blonde hair spilling over the pillow. Adrian stood by her side, towering over his wife in silence, a half grin appearing as he watched. To him, she was the second most beautiful thing he had ever seen – and only second by mere inches – just behind their 6-year old daughter, Isabella. Adrian knelt down, as delicately as his near seven-foot, 350lb frame would allow, and sat beside her atop the bed. The frame and box spring groaned under his weight, but Amanda remained under sleep’s spell. He smiled as he traced her features with his eyes, from that flowing blonde hair (which she never could seem to keep contained), to her cute little button nose that had a slight point to it (which she hated, but he absolutely adored), and down to her slightly cleft chin. She was his angel, and mother to another angel-in-training. “Mandy,” Adrian started, but stopped at the sound of his own voice. It was barely above a whisper, but even in the silent room it seemed to boom and echo off of the walls. His voice was deep and carried as much weight as his frame, so whispering for Adrian Bastille was pretty much on the same level as talking for everyone else. But he felt guilty for a moment, a guilt that he had felt before, but continued anyhow, “Wake up, babe.” She remained asleep, her chest slowly rising and falling. Those tan eyes remained hidden behind eyelids, which never twitched even as Adrian spoke. They were the color of sand, a beach he would gladly spend the rest of his life lost on. He sat there for a minute, waiting to see if she would stir. The guilt and shame continued to race through him like rain-swept rapids, drowning the clarity of his thoughts. The loss was going to be unbearable on some days, and Adrian just wasn’t prepared for it. How could he be? Over the course of the last few days, he repeatedly told himself that nothing was his fault, and that these things happen. But it was Adrian’s nature to blame failures on himself. Must be woven into his genetic code, he figured. Gently, he pushed some of Amanda’s blonde hair away from her face and could smell the pear-scented shampoo that she always washed her hair with every morning. In the coming weeks, Adrian knew that he would pause a moment any time he detected the particular fruity scent, saddened by what memories it unearthed. He sighed. The machines monitoring her health quietly worked their magic, keeping Amanda alive. Every day for the last six weeks, Adrian came into the hospital and tried to wake Amanda up, perhaps only lying to himself at the chances of it actually occuring. But the accident left her brain dead, kept alive only by the miracles of modern technology. Until today. Today was the day he would say goodbye to his loving wife of six years, when her life support would be disconnected so her organs could be donated to those who needed them. Had Amanda’s parents not passed away amid the tumbling towers of September 11th, they undoubtedly would be alongside Adrian on this horrible, but unavoidable day. Mentally, he had accepted this fate weeks ago when the doctors informed him of the severity of Amanda’s injuries. Thrown from the vehicle through the windshield, she had slammed into the telephone pole that had suddenly stopped her car, and then subsequently, suddenly stopped her as well. He had cried many nights after the accident, but once reality set in, the tears slowly gave way to a brave resolve. He needed to show his little daughter, his little Isabella, that it was all right to cry and that her mommy would probably not be waking up from this very long nap. Adrian found it odd, a morbid oddity in fact, that tears no longer formed at the thought of losing his soul mate, but as soon as he thought about their beautiful daughter losing her only mother, his eyes quickly shimmered with tears. Adrian knew that she could hear him. He knew God existed, and that even though Amanda would soon be singing alongside His angels, that even while she lay here catatonic, anything he said would somehow, someway, get to her. Wherever she was. Her chest continued to rise and fall with slow, steady breaths. Commanded by technology, she lived on. Adrian just sat in the quiet, and continued to watch her as his thoughts roared back through the years since they had dated, married, and started a family together. All of it beyond promising, destined for a future that most people would cherish having. It was never supposed to point towards a telephone pole, nor a hospital room. Never towards a motherless daughter. He stood, carefully getting up as to not disturb her, lips pursed as his mind continued to wander. After a few minutes, he bent down and placed a light kiss on her warm lips, then lowered his head next to her ear and whispered into it, “God might’ve made me bigger and stronger than most, but you were the one who truly gave me life. I’m going to be so lost without you, Mandy, so lost.” He paused a moment, once again tracing the features of her face for the last time, “I love you.” Adrian closed his eyes before turning away from his wife, thoughts once again roaming over the last few years, from their first date to their first fight to the house they bought only last year. When he opened them, he was only a few feet from the door of the hospital room, and a stride later, it was open and he was out in the hallway. Isabella, hair as blonde, curly, and unruly as her mother’s, sat between her grandparents. A white dress with red spots all over it matched the slightly flush look of her cheeks, and at the sight of her father, she hopped off of the chair and ran over to him. ”Daddy, is it my turn to say goodbye to mommy?” He nodded, and opened the door for her. She disappeared into the room, and the door clicked shut behind her, leaving Adrian in the hallway with his parents. “Thanks for coming” he said, his booming voice still fairly weak, but still commanding. Around them, the hospital was booming with life, or death. Nurses roamed, Doctors rushed, and patients slowly meandered through the halls. His father nodded without saying a word, but Adrian’s mother looked at him with such sympathetic eyes. When she spoke, her French accent was still heavy, although slowly weakening with every passing year in the United States, “You think she’ll be alright? Isabella, I mean.” For a moment, Adrian saw the pained look across his mothers face, a result of the anonymous question. “She’s held it together for the most part. I’m not sure it’ll really sink in until these daily visits stop.” The finality in his voice was mildly disturbing. He supposed it had to be. “Don’t you want to be by her side when she says goodbye to Mandy?” Adrian shook his head, “I want it to be private. I’ve had my time, she gets her own as well.” Adrian’s father smiled through tear-glazed eyes at the thought. Always quiet and a man of few words, Adrian knew that his father approved of the way he was raising Isabella. Whatever mistakes he had made while raising Adrian, Adrian had failed to make while raising his little Bella. And just then, the door opened and Isabella stepped out into the hallway. Her eyes were pink, and cheeks slightly more flushed than when she had entered. Her cheeks still had the tracks from the tears, but the tears themselves had been wiped clean. It broke Adrian’s heart to see that Isabella had cried while in the room, and tried to cover it up before she came out to rejoin her father and grandparents. Trying to be strong, when showing weakness was fully acceptable. All four remained quiet for a moment, before Adrian picked up Isabella and hugged her in the middle of the hallway, not caring if they impeded the path of doctors, nurses, or other patrons visiting their loved ones. This was their moment, and nothing else mattered. “Daddy” Isabella asked, her voice filled with innocence and slightly muffled by his shoulder, “Are the angels still going to sing with us at church?” ”Yes they are, sweetie. Mommy, too.” Isabella said nothing for a beat, before finally facing Adrian. Her blue eyes stood out from the pink surrounding them, but no tears fell, and they lit up with the thought of her mother in heaven singing alongside heaven’s other beautiful angels “…mommy, too?” He nodded, “Yes, dear. Mommy too.” Adrian held Isabella the entire journey out of the hospital, and out to the car, where he hugged his parents goodbye and glanced one final time up at Chester County Hospital. Till death do us part, he thought. Keep it together, Adrian, keep it together. It wasn’t your fault. * * * * *
