Mid Life Crisis - 'The Pitch'

27 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27/03/07 The dull drill of the hotel telephone echoed around the minimalist room. I rubbed my heavy eyes roughly and scrabbled to look at the nearby clock. Nine in the morning, but it felt like it was still too early to be doing anything productive. The thought of rolling over and falling back into the peaceful slumber was pretty tempting, however I had a meeting with some of the bookers from PRIME. I wasn’t quite sure what it was for but they had said it was pretty important. I pulled myself out of the bed and grumbled my way to the bathroom. The leaky fluorescent light bulb flickered into animation and jolted my own desire to wake up. The usual routine of trimming the beard and washing my teeth soon passed before I hopped into the shower for a quick one. All done, all clean and presentable in the space of less than thirty minutes. I’d plucked my formal and yet casual attire for the day (and honestly I wish I could tell you what that phrase meant, but Jessica just shushed me and pushed a shirt and pants combo towards me in the shop at the time) with which to deal with my employers. I knew it wasn’t meant to be anything really serious like a reprimanding, so this kind of dress would be suitable enough to hopefully give off the right vibe. Being where we were and the amount of time we had been given off to relax, they should be in good moods to talk to me, no matter how important their business was. I grabbed a gym bag for the trip, I figured while I was out I may as well get something done with the day. There was a serviceable gym in the hotel and I could work things out…especially if things didn’t go well. *** On the way down to the lobby area (that’s where I said I’d be able to meet them), the missed opportunities of the Halo came back to my mind. Overall, that entire tour had been a missed opportunity; for whatever reason I hadn’t had my head right, I had been distracted and listless, not at all like I usually am in wrestling. For the first time in well over twenty years I had lost sight of the goals that I had set out for myself. Almost every day during that tour I struggled to care about what was happening. Almost every day I fought with myself over what I was doing: whether I was right or if something was odd about the whole affair. I felt tangible guilt for Sully several times during the tour, largely because I was letting him down. I don’t let anyone down. Certainly not in the business. It was all very autopilot from the start on the tour and I should have recognised that something was going wrong right from the beginning when I just accepted into things without analysing them. I was laissez faire and that’s just not my character. For the first time in a long period, I was endangering my own livelihood without a care and this was something I had only done maybe once or twice before. Back then, it was only to get out of an unwanted contract or intentionally look bad so that they’d fire me. It came down to asking myself the question again on the Halo night; was I trying to get myself out of PRIME before my run had even started? With that came interminable questions, most of them centring around the one term which I always aspire to recreate over and over within myself: drive. Did I still have it? Were the injuries that had been stacking up since the run in AWC finally coming to catch up with me? I did admit to myself that the knees were rustier coming out of the championship match on the first night. Then again, I had been in the tag-team wrestling championship bout on the first night. It perked me up but I remembered the words which had haunted me earlier in the week: I was one of the misfits now, someone who wasn’t able to climb any higher due to any number of factors placed about them. That kind of thought scared me, despite my friend’s attempt to reconcile the mould with which he had entered into. The difference between hi and me at the time was that I still had the drive to want more than just a sideshow to the main event. I still believed that I could make it into that main event and show them all what I could do. It wasn’t that I didn’t have it, I didn’t believe I had the drive right now. It then came to me as I was exiting the elevator that perhaps I was heading to a meeting where my fate would be sealed. Up until now I figured it was merely something about a program from now till the next pay-per-view. A program that would hopefully be re-establishing myself with the crowd and giving me the chance to make amends, to restore faith in those who had hired me despite my age and baggage that came along with it. Anxiety crept into my head. This wasn’t going to go well, I could tell. *** Barney Anderson and Dave Price were waiting patiently in the lobby way, sipping on cups of coffee in the shade of some palm trees that had been transplanted indoors. Seeing me walking out of the elevator (Dave was naturally the one to spot me first), they stood up and waited for me to approach. Barney Anderson, lifelong fan of the business and a man I had encountered numerous time over the past twenty plus years in this business. He had a passions that was ever present in his demeanour and interaction with the boys, almost to the point of obsession. Sadly, I think he secretly wished he was one of us, not working for us. Barney never got that opportunity that he wanted, largely due to the spinabifida that he had since the day he was born. An under-developed leg and a weak back are not things you can really use to advantage in the ring. He was a hard worker and constant presence if you needed to talk to anyone, someone who was able to listen intently and not look like a pussy doing it. He understood the tough times back in the old days and was able to relate to the journey that a lot of wrestlers had gone through. In fact, Barney had only been hired a couple of months previous to me to be part of the booking committee of PRIME. After much the same amount of toiling it in the indies as a head booker, Barney had finally been recognised and given the chance to work with the big boys. He’d been tirelessly working since his arrival and a lot of people had been whispering that maybe he was working just a little bit too hard for himself. A trip to the hospital was what some people were prescribing. This also didn’t help with his last run-in with the indies before his call up. A particularly precocious arrogant prick of a wrestler had threatened Barney with some significant pain if he wasn’t given a chance to highlight the show. Barney had refused in his own polite manner (I heard it was ‘fuck off you douche bag’ but eh) and the guy came after him with a ten inch blade after the show. They found Barney running down the mainstreet of the town they were in, pants round his ankles and screaming bloody murder. Right behind him was the prick, cycling slowly on a push-bike with the blade being twirled in the air. I’d like to say I haven’t heard stranger things, but this is wrestling and most certainly stranger things have happened. Barney greeted me with his effortless smile, the wrinkle lines travelling all the way up from his lips right to the shining forehead. Just like every overweight man, he was sweating profusely and with no hair to speak of on the top of his head to halt the flow, he was cascading rivulets of sweat downwards. Every now and then you’ll see him secretly lapping it up, enjoying the salt, but not right now. To add to the entire appearance, Barney had some fascination with dressing eternally like he’d fit in at a luau. Today was his sky blue flowery shirt and the fluorescent pink flip-flops suited it if you were blind and therefore didn’t have to look at him. He gripped my hand firmly and shook it. ‘Good to see you again Jack. I trust you’re recovering well from the Halo?’ ‘Yeah, bit by bit. This body doesn’t recover like it used to when you’d have me wrestling every night Barney but it’s getting there.’ ‘Well, those were different times then Jack. I’m not paying twenty bucks for a fifteen minute match am I?’ ‘No, you’re paying me fifteen for a twenty minute match!’ We grinned at each other and clapped each other on the back with our free left hands. It was only then we heard the audible cough and we had to abruptly halt our parley. Barney admonished himself internally and put his hand to his lips to hide his cough. ‘But of course, Jack I’m sure you remember my friend on the committee, Mr. Price?’ ‘Dave Price, Barney, you can call me Dave you know. Pleasure to meet you Jack.’ Dave Price, or stiff ass depending on who you talked to. The words college boy would probably be a calling card for the man and he would bound and leap from whichever corner he was infesting. He was somewhere in the middle of his twenties and he looked the part. Straight out of college, PRIME had plucked him for his degree in script writing and saw the potential to breed a new type of booker who would understand the new crash television and high drama aspect that they wanted to push above the old-school feel of the old shows. Price was perfectly suited for this kind of work, politely dressed in a smart business suit, he never switched off from his up-front and important voice. Despite his approach, it was definitely taking its toll. Already you could see the sprouting silver hair from all corners and it would only be a matter of time before he either began plucking them out or dying them to make sure that no one could see he was under pressure. Pressure was a bit of an understatement however as everyone knew in the back that he was the poster child for the new movement that PRIME wanted to install; if he failed, then the movement failed. As such, few people were willing to work with him other than when they had to. This meeting was one of those occasions for me. There was one reason and one reason only that Barney was here for this particular meeting and unfortunately it was for a reason that I really didn’t agree with. I had even hinted to Barney about it before because he could do so much better, but he hadn’t picked up on it. Price for all intents and purposes was using Barney as his own personal lackey to do random servile tasks and as a pin cushion to bounce his insults off of. Barney for what it was worth was oblivious as I’d said and felt that Price was his chance to make it to the top while he still could but it was obvious that Price had no intention of bringing him with him. As soon as Price got he wanted, Barney was on a one way ride back to the bottom. Sad, but true. His hand looked weak and fleeting, almost like it was disappear were I touch it, but I shook his hand nevertheless. It felt soft and numb, cold and devoid of emotive gesture. His sickly smile as I looked at him made him come across as some unlovable tax man. Not a bad nickname. That could stick alright, but I’d have to run the joke with the guys in the back first. I’ll keep that one for later. His demeanour changed and now looked very pressing for time, as if he had another appointment to go to. ‘Well, shall we get this meeting started? Jack I have made a reservation in the restaurant if you’ll join us?’ ‘I guess I don’t have a choice do I?’ ‘No, I guess you don’t.’ The retort was dry and held by a thin lip which quivered at the bitter quote. He smiled so very falsely and then extended his hand for Barney to lead the way. He did so with quiet unknowingness, plodding along and beginning another one of his stories. ‘Jack, do you remember the time we were in Mexico and I met that intriguing woman…?’ *** The brunch we had was adequate, with watermelon and basic choices on the menu to whet our appetites for the day. Nothing on the menu was particularly filling by the look of it but Barney seemed determined to find out whether or not the statement was true. So, while myself and Dave finished up our polite meal etiquette, Barney was stuffing his face full of watermelon. Price placed his implements to the plate and looked at me directly. ‘I know we’ve been dancing around the subject now for the last hour and a half but I think we can stop being idiots about this: why do you think we brought you here Jack?’ Prompt and forward, just like I expected him to be. The knot in my stomach also seemed to feel this way and decided to rear its head right now. I tugged at my beard lightly to muse over the question, as if to feign dumbness but I wasn’t really expecting him to buy it. ‘Honestly? I haven’t any clue.’ ‘Really? Because you…’ Barney had perked up to inject some life into the conversation but a simple hand from Price had halted the interjection. He pursed his lips before wearing a thin smile to greet me. ‘I appreciate the deference but I’m not here to pull the rug over anyone’s eyes. I’m trying to give you a chance to get back in the spotlight. I don’t think anyone can deny that you haven’t been on form of late. Frankly, some people have been calling you a disappointment. A bust.’ Now that I did not like. ‘You think I’m a bust? I wouldn’t go that far.’ Restraint was not my forte but I held it in as best I could for this one. Such a barefaced insult was disgusting and were it in any other place than this public locale, I probably would have hit him. As it stood, I took it and looked the worse for it. Price knew this and attempted to press even deeper. ‘Perhaps, but it doesn’t change the fact that what you have done in the past month or two is paltry in comparison to the other AWC refugees that have found their way to PRIME. Chainz? He’s all over the news for his on, and off, screen engagements. Pierce Lavelle is consistent as ever, winning over fans with his no nonsense style and honesty. Garbage Bag Johnny? He just finished fourth in the Halo. Even your partner has gotten more press than you for his public stance with politics. Looking at them all, you haven’t got anything going for you.’ ‘That’s not true, I have many talents that I can contribute to…’ ‘Then why haven’t you shown them Jack? We’ve given you the opportunity in many different situations so you can’t lay the blame on the committee. You certainly can’t lay the blame on people like myself and Barney.’ The dagger twisted that slight bit more as he backed me into the proverbial corner. I had no legs to stand on right now because there was no excuse I could give to get me out of this situation. The slick-mouthed college boy had got to me and now I was his whipping boy. Fucker. ‘So, what are you getting at?’ It was the only out. I had to take the bait and give him his soapbox. He’d won it without the slightest bit of contest and that pissed me off right now but damn it he had to be given it. Otherwise I might be kissing my job goodbye. He grinned like a Cheshire cat at this, confident in his victory. With minimum fuss he pulls out a dossier which is pretty plain and clean. He flicks it open and lays out one of the most complicated looking profiles I have ever seen. Even Barney looked impressed, his jaw slacking and letting some mangled piece of melon fall carelessly on top. Price quickly swats it aside, glowering at his bumbling, if quiet sidekick. ‘This…this is a simple organisation of how you can maximise your potential Jack. It’s nothing over the top or extraordinary, it’s just simple things that you can work to your advantage. Take, for example something as basic and helpful like advertising. Is there anything you do that nobody else does outside of the ring to make you jump out?’ ‘Not that I can think of.’ ‘Exactly. Now, for someone like Garbage Bag Johnny, he has his stoner mentality and all around hoboness. He has something to connect himself to the fans: the power of his bong. Meanwhile you’re just a roided up monkey who can wrestle.’ ‘I don’t take steroids.’ ‘And why don’t you? You’d have a more defined and busted physique.’ ‘Do you want me to walk out of here? Because that could be possibly the most insulting thing you’ve said to me so far.’ His grin widened just that little bit more as I stood to make sure my stance was clear. Barney looked at Price who was happy enough to let this happen. It fell to Barney to pick up the pieces. ‘I’m sure Dave only mean that as a joke, and you know that he’s only trying to help Jack. Please, sit down and hear what he has to say. I know he’s talked me through a couple of the basics and it seems like it could be something for you to get behind. Honestly Jack, would I bring you here if I didn’t think I could help you out?’ And then came the hammer blow. The son of a bitch college boy had me hog-tied and collared. Unsurprisingly, but much to my chagrin, I sat down. ‘I knew you’d make the right choice Jack.’ His tone was so………patronising. ‘Regardless, that’s just a minor example. What I can offer to you Jack is something that could be the saviour of your career. Hell, it can and should bring you right to the top of the heap and I know it’s no secret that you want that more than anything else.’ He flipped the dossier over to my side of the table and it landed perfectly in my lap. Dabbing his mouth with the napkin, he promptly stood up and looked towards Barney who was in no way near finished with his mountain of food. ‘Take a look and think it over. You have all week to figure it out Jack. Just remember we’re trying to help you.’ He left without another word and Barney only then began to finish his serving. Looking up from the plates which littered his part of the dinner, he tried to reason with me sincerely. ‘Jack, I know nobody can tell you what to do, but a lot hinges on this. I would be lying if I said I didn’t want this to go ahead. It’s probably my only shot to solidify myself in the company. If this doesn’t work, I’m out and that’s it. No second chance, no three strikes you’re out. I’m done. I only took this particular one on because I know you and I know that you’ll make the right choice and I know that I can make you look good. You know?’ The eyes slightly bulged out of his head and he looked pleadingly towards me. My heart sank and I sighed. I couldn’t respond to him audibly, I just shrugged. ‘It’s up to you Jack.’ Barney stood and waddled his way out of the restaurant. My eyes dropped to the dossier which lay neatly in my lap. I didn’t know what to do right then and there… …Aww crap, Barney left me with the bill again. *** ‘So, what did you say to him?’ Dave Price stood outside the restaurant waiting for Barney Anderson to make his way outside too. Price was impatiently tapping his watch when he wandered over and was in no mood for Barney’s antics. The stench of stale food off of him was annoying to say the least. The sooner he could get out of here and begin the plans, the better. ‘I made the plea just like you said.’ ‘Are you sure?’ ‘Yes, god! I don’t know why you made me do that though, he’s my friend.’ ‘A little emotion to the bargain will help tip the scales Barney. My own part of it might have been enough, but yours was icing on the cake.’ ‘Well I do like cake…’ ‘Not literally. Regardless, I think we’ve found the one who we can utilise the best in this path. Now I just need to make some phone calls. Barney, I think you know what you have to do.’ ‘Yeah, yeah. I have the numbers right here. You sure that you want me to make the call though? Do you really understand what you’re getting into?’ ‘Of course.’ ‘I don’t know, he does hate his guts, especially after the way things ended. You never know how long grudges can last.’ Price brushed him aside and began to walk towards the exit. ‘Trust me Barney, with the things that he’s going to read in that dossier, Jack Murphy would even work with the Illustrious Face-Eater, let alone who you’re going to call.’ Again, Price made his exit with a point and now Barney was left alone to ponder things. He idly thumbed through his black book looking for numbers from his old contacts. The pudgy thumb lingered over AWC. *** This dossier had everything. And I looked at it, everything didn’t just mean the map of the future, it had everything from my past, everything that had made me what I was today and how they could be used to further maximise my potential. He pointed out so many different things, things that I didn’t even remember quite well that I was stunned. Flabbergasted. Hell, he’d even managed to find something positive about the body-builder gimmick that I’d been pushed into during the eighties. It was comprehensive, it was complete and it was thorough. Laid out before me was a structure to my career and all the possible paths I could have taken and I could still take. To look at it all made my head spin but there was one thing I was certain about. I think they’re right. A lot of my talents were being hidden because I was too stubborn to let them shine. I was being arrogant in thinking that I didn’t have to give it my all from the beginning. I wasn’t fulfilling the drive I always aspired to. If nothing else I could finally pay back an old friend who now needed me to launch his own career into something more and solid, to something that he always knew he could do but only now had this once chance to prove it to the rest of the community. And who was I stop that? Change was afoot, but I was finally starting to see that I needed to go along with it if I wanted to survive. It was now or never, before it became too late.
