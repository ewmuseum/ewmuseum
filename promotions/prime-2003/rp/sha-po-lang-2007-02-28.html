Sha Po Lang

28 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/28/07 Nothing. That is what Sun Tzu saw when she looked out her balcony at the night sky. Not exactly and odd sight considering the time of the year. The dark hid the dreary clouds of gray, but unfortunately those same clouds hid the stars from sight. Not to mention the fact that the lights of Manhattan tended to overwhelm the glow from tiny specks countless miles away. Still, Sun Tzu looked out at the empty expanse with some joy in her heart. Soon, after all these many months, she would be returning home. It would be Chinese stars she would be looking at in a Chinese sky. Sun Tzu recalled her lessons in astrology and the three stars Sha, Po, and Lang. Sha, meaning Seven Killings, is the Power Star. Po, the Army Breaker, is the Ruinous Star. And the Greedy Wolf, Lang, is the Flirty Star. Depending on where they shown in the sky, the trio could bring either good or evil. The situation was not unlike Sun Tzu’s own. In America, she was jeered in every arena she wrestled on. But when she was back under a Chinese sky… nothing but adulation. Strange how much the perception of an entity can change depending on the position of the observer, thought Sun Tzu. Such musing reminded her of another time she looked out into the night sky… Hong Kong had one of the most famous skylines in the world. But Sun Tzu really wasn’t looking at it as she stood in the ally while dragging on a cigarette. General Chang would have a fit if he knew what she was doing. But Sun Tzu’s opinion was that her line of work pretty much trumped nicotine as the likely cause of her eventual death. After one last drag, she flicked it away, watching it ship along the concrete made slick by an earlier rain. Then she turned and entered an old building through a door that groaned in protest when it was opened. The large room was empty except for a single chair. That chair was occupied by a very distressed young woman. Her face was quite pretty in spite of her red eyes and wet cheeks. She glanced up when Sun Tzu entered but did not rise. She could not, because she had been tied the chair. Her expression showed nothing but despair, even as Sun Tzu smiled sweetly at her. “Oh, look at you!” exclaimed Sun Tzu. “Did my absence move you that much, Xiu-xiu?” The girl took a few halting breaths before finding the power of speech. “W-why, Fei?” Fei. That was the name Sun Tzu ha given Xiu-xiu when they met. Her cover had been that of a student at the University of Hong Kong who had just transferred into the history class Xiu-xiu was taking. Of course, “Fei” was a bit behind and had to borrow some lecture notes and sweet Xiu-xiu was more than happy to accommodate. Thus was the start of a friendship some weeks ago. “Well, my dear,” replied Sun Tzu. “It really has nothing to do with me. Or you actually. The two of us are mere bit parts in this tragicomedy. But they say there are no small roles, just small actors, so let’s just still try to do our best, shall we?” “I… I thought you were my friend…” “Oh, but I am! Or was. I’m guessing that the whole knocking you out with a backfist and tying you up bit has soured our relationship. Or did it just take it to another level?” Sun Tzu slowly ran her tongue along her teeth as one hand had slowly circled her breast. She laughed when she saw the look of horror on Xiu-xiu’s face. “Kidding. Sort of.” Sun Tzu may have lied about many things to Xiu-xiu, but she was honest about being her friend. While it may have been her mission to keep tabs on the girl, Sun Tzu did enjoy her company. It was not unlike what she felt with Andre a few years back. The afternoons studying, the nights at cafes and clubs… it gave Sun Tzu a glimpse of what life would have been like if she had been raised in a far different environment. And the best part was that the glimpses needed before the novelty wore off and the truth of such a mundane existence revealed itself. So while Sun Tzu felt a slight twinge of regret when she got the call, it faded rather quickly. “Well, I guess I can clue you in a bit,” sighed Sun Tzu. “As you know, your bright father has been working at the atomic lab in Los Alamos, located in a quite dreary part of America. What you might not know is that he was supposed to be passing information back to Beijing. But for whatever reason, he chose not to send anything of value.” “You wanted to betray his employers?” “Betray? The only betrayal here was that of your father towards his country! Don’t make him sound like some sort of hero because he’d rather buddy up to a pack of capitalist that make racist jokes about him behind his back instead of protecting his homeland!” “S-so…” “So eventually Beijing caught on. I guess your father wasn’t that bright in spite of that Nobel Prize. Some men are currently having a discussion with him. And you are here as an incentive to loosen his lips. I took a picture of you earlier with my phone and sent it to one of your father’s visitors all the way in Los Alamos. Isn’t technology grand? I’m just glad I don’t have to pay the bill.” “You… you’re a monster, Fei.” Sun Tzu’s eyes grew cold as she looked at the terrified girl shivering in the chair. “No. I’m a soldier. I have a sense of duty and a sense of patriotism, two things that your father lacks. Along with some teeth and fingers by now, most likely.” Those words sent Xiu-xiu into hysterics. As she sobbed, Sun Tzu knelt down and slapped her sharply. “How can you do this to me?” blubbered Xiu-xiu softly. “I was your friend and you just throw it all away…” Her words faded into more sobs. Sun Tzu gently raised Xiu-xiu’s face by her chin and gently looked her in the eye. “No, no,” said Sun Tzu, almost whispering. “I didn’t throw our friendship away. I sacrificed it. Even though it was dear to me, I surrendered what I felt towards you for something greater… my country. Now we just need to find out what your father is willing to sacrifice and for what cause.” There was a moment when not a sound came from either of the two young women. Then it was shattered by the buzzing of a phone. Sun Tzu took it form her pocket and answered it. “Yes. I understand. Thank you.” She put the phone away and looked back at Xiu-xiu. Again she raise the girl’s face so it looked at hers. “Hey there.” The gentleness in Sun Tzu’s voice was almost startling. “This is almost done, all right? I just need you to be strong for a little bit longer. Can you do that? If not for me, than for your father?” Slowly, Xiu-xiu nodded. Sun Tzu smiled warmly at her. “Good girl. I’m proud of you and so is your father. Don’t be sad, this be all be over before you know it.” Sun Tzu slowly wiped the moist cheek of Xiu-xiu with one hand as she stroked her dark hair with the other. Xiu-xiu exhaled loudly as she finally began to relax. Then, swifter than a cobra striking, Sun Tzu jerked her jaw while pushing the back of her head. There was one single sick snap, then Xiu-xiu’s eyes rolled up lifelessly. Sun Tzu rose, no expression on her face as she took her phone out once more and dialed. “It’s done. The girl is dead too. I’m leaving now.” Turning away from the corpse slumped in the chair, the ropes the only thing keeping it from falling to the floor, Sun Tzu walked away. She went out the old creaking door without looking back once and vanished somewhere in the night... And another night, years later, Sun Tzu thought of the dichotomy of friend and enemy, loved one and traitor, patriot and murderer... two polar opposites in the same being. It all depends on perspective, just like Sha Po Lang. But there was one absolute truth… in China, Sun Tzu would be a winner.
