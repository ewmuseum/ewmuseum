Date: 11/05/06 A History Of Violence.Written: 11th May, 2006. 'There's the moon asking to stay,Long enough for the clouds to fly me away.Well it's my time coming, I'm not afraid... afraid to die.' - Jeff Buckley. I The moon imposed on the last remnants of lingering daylight. With its cratered face skulking under a veil of deepening blue, the thick brushstrokes of twilight smeared peach and pink across the horizon, while the departing sun backlit the sparse, low clouds in mauve. As the stars began their nightly coup of the skies, the faint, almost apologetic breeze barely tickled the tips of the blades grass where two childhood friends lay. The humid week had left the grass a lush green, providing downy bedding opposed to the harsh prick of strawy yellow. Snug in the meadows cushion, they lay stretched in opposing directions, joined by the gaze between adjacent faces. As the grass around them gave one last reach towards the dwindling light, the blades underneath lay fallen, flattened where the pair had spent the last 5 hours. Two people at perfect ease with one another. 'So,' he let be drawn out, filling a rare pause. 'Sure you don't fancy shelving Europe and staying here for the summer?' Perfectly groomed, if a little thinly-spread facial hair bordered the subtle appeal of his smile, while a bar-pierced eyebrow was cocked a little as an exclamation. A white, short-sleeved shirt that hung loose on his skinny frame was smudged with green from the seeping chlorophyll, while a brown pair of Safari shorts showed-off bruised and grazed shins. The question was posed by Alexander Yeo, student and potential future valedictorian of Bishop Maginn High School in Albany, New York. And to whom the question was posed? That would be one Miss Karina Wolfenden, failing, occasional pupil of Bishop Maginn High School in Albany, New York. 'I would stay here,' she teasingly fostered hope with. 'But I don't want to.' A roll of the eyes followed as he was wriggled free of the baited hook. 'There's something pretty wrong in the universe when the girl who's permanently grounded gets to spend summer across the pond,' Alex sighed, masking the obvious injustice with his usual good humour. 'While I, the guy who tries to play the Angel on your shoulder, is stuck here, probably mowing lawns and cleaning gutters.' With a sigh that foretold the boredom that lay ahead in the summer months, Alex pondered. 'How did you get out tonight, anyway?' he then enquired. 'Reduced sentence for less-than-infuriating behaviour?' '1st floor window,' honesty blurted out with a wink. The sun finally lost its grip on the landscape, and slipped from the horizon's ledge. Subconsciously, Karina slipped her light jacket onto bare shoulders, while overhead the sky dimmed to a subtle backlit state. 'How long exactly are you grounded for, anyway?' Alex followed up, having lost count of the tally almost as soon as he'd begun keeping it. 'I usually have at least a rough idea until report cards come out, then it all just goes haywire.' 'If I assume they're running consecutively,' Karina pondered, counting off on her fingers - of both hands - before starting over again, 'then technically I'm grounded for about 3 evers... and a half. So, every now and again, I just assume they'd want me out of the house to comply with the Geneva Convention or something.' Reclining flat against the grass, Kari blew her purple fringe to the side. 'And what happened to all this Enter the Dragon stuff you were meant to be doing?' she asked, gesturing to his shins. 'Or do you keep kicking that tree out the back because it looked at you funny?' He auto-replied with the same 'ha-ha' fleeting grin he always did. Alex sat up and spun around, sitting cross-legged above the girl he'd grown up next to. 'I've got enough saved up to finally start classes at the New York Jiu Jitsu school,' he was eager to reveal, although slightly surprised that Wolfenden had actually remembered something he was interested in. 'And... no laughing... my cousin is dragging me along to some wrestling school he's wanted to go to since last summer.' 'Wrestling?' was thrown back at him with a stifled laugh. 'The kind with guys in leotards rolling about in mats, or the one with... well, guys in leotards pretending to roll around on mats?' 'The latter,' he answered with dwindling enthusiasm, while Kari's body almost convulsed to keep it in. 'If that was your plan to get me to stay and watch,' Wolfenden managed to force out between bouts of laughter, not aided much by his punishment in tickle form. 'It... it almost worked!' Rolling free from his nerve-triggering finger tips, Kari too spun through to sit in the grass, picking the flicking a buttercup his way in revenge for the playful hostilities. Shuffling her feet back towards her, Karina hugged her knees, using them to disguise her still quivering bottom lip, threatening to breach with another bout of laughter as she pictured him in luminous spandex. She rubbed her eyes. Thoroughly scrubbing the image. 'And I get all this from the girl who was actually asked to LEAVE self-defence class...' Alex quipped, shaking his head as rosy cheeks betrayed the fact that he'd wished he was more secretive. 'Oh, please!' Kari instantly protested. 'I did exactly what they showed us. Grab, spin, knee, mace. It was a class I was actually good at... well, one I wasn't failing based on attendance.' He just sat, looking out the tops of his eyes at his childhood friend. 'What?' she self-consciously appealed. 'What? Oh, don't you get started again on the anger issues...' II 'Frank...' Lawrence Wolfenden was able to make even such a dour name come to life, the warmth and charisma breathing new life into it. 'How've you been?' In his element, the Wolfenden patriarch made it his mission to meet-and-greet all who had accepted the invite. Every couple of months, the cream of Albany's business world and society life was skimmed from the top of the county's pale and invited to his home. A parade of all those who had either made a success of their life, or failed to ruin the success passed down to them. 'Max should be here later,' Lawrence continued, able to gesture coherently to others while never losing the attention those he was conversing with. 'He hasn't stopped talking about the work experience you lined up for him. Sadly, Karina can't be here tonight. She's... feeling poorly.' With the waiters scurrying to and from the kitchen, conversation buzzed around the room. But while respectability was ever visible, in the corners lurked those whose vices were amplified by the occasion. One such man was Mitch Fuller. Looking as if his collar was choking him, the swollen man wheezed as another glass was dropped down onto his table. Timid in his shadow, Mitch's wife - Claudia - rested her hand upon that which he was using to lean on the table for support. 'You don't look well,' nervous words could barely rise above the ambient conversation. 'Maybe you should have a glass of water... or two?' As soon as the words trembled off of her lips, Claudia looked down to the floor... too downtrodden to even realise quite how stunning she looked in the dress that hung from her willowy frame. 'I never listen when you scream 'no' or when you say you'll leave me,' he seethed, looking at his wife of 22 years as of she was a faecal stain on his shoes. 'So you think I'm going to listen when you say I've had enough? Go shove the tits I bought you in another waiter's face, and keep reminding me why I sleep in the pool house unless I feel like degrading you further.' She looked around skulkingly, praying nobody heard. The scotch barely had time to warm the throat as it was knocked back, then slammed on the table where his wife sat humbled. He loosened his tie, trying to flush the blotchy red from his perspiring face, before patting his brow with a napkin. 'If it wouldn't make me sick to my stomach to let you take half of everything I've worked for...' Fuller went to begin another rant with, before his intoxicated worlds tailed off and he staggered away from the table. Those guests who carried themselves with a little more class and decorum looked at him with soured expressions as he stumbled through the obstacle course of waiters and guests. With the sweat cascading down his brow, a thinning fringe became matted down against the wrinkling, leathery skin, aiding the salty droplets to sting his grey eyes. 'Mitch, he-' one guest went to greet him with, hand extended. 'I need to take a piss,' alcohol let the honesty flow in announcing. As the man and his group exchanged disapproving looks upon resuming their conversation, Fuller dug a hand into his collar and tried to loosen it. With the air tasting stale and warm, he yearned for a breeze as the doors to the hallway were shoved open. Eyes winces as sweat was spread under their lids, a problem remedied as he used a wrap hanging up in the hallway to wipe the sockets of each eye, then to mop his face once more. 'The flash fucker can't afford air conditioning?' Fuller slurred, resting a hand on the walls of the hallway as he guided himself into the bowels of the house. With slapping footsteps, he carried his hazy head towards the bathroom, when a scratching sound caught his ear. Juddering to a halt, Fuller gave his head a short, shrift shake, eyes blinking to refresh as his senses settled. After a wrenching sniff, he cocked his head to the side, letting his right ear drag him towards the scratching. Dropping his inebriated mass against the wall, he wiped the hair from his face, letting just his right eye peek around the corner. Struggling to focus, he just about made out two sets of fingers cupping the sash window. Gently, they eased it up just enough for a slender frame to slither through. Clad in a turquoise summer dress, denim jacket tied around her waist, Karina Wolfenden had returned home. Before he could even realise it, Mitch Fuller’s jacket began to slide along the wall, and he staggered around the corner, startling the daughter of his guest. 'Well, well, well...' he sleazily slurred, cheeks creasing with a lop-sided smile. 'Looks like I caught me a burglar.' Eyes narrowing, Karina shared the look of mild disgust that the other guests had displayed. 'Don't you look at me like that!' a shortened temper hurriedly reached the end of its fuse to exclaim as he lumbered forward. 'Or do you want me telling Daddy that his little princess has been sneaking out? I bet he'd like to know what you've been doing, huh? Oh, don't worry though... I'm sure you can buy my silence.' Even without taking a sniff of the stale, yeasty stench that was being expelled by Fuller, Kari almost dry-heaved. 'How about a kiss for your Uncle Mitch, huh?' he oozed, eyes running up and down the queasy Karina, before he puckered up. 'Or how about I walk right past, and tell your wife she's married to a fat, sweaty perv?' Wolfenden's rash mouth was never going to be able to resist hitting back with. 'Now, breathe in, I need to get past.' Hoping the less-than-flattering rejection would have sobered the man up, putting him back in his place, Karina went to victoriously stride by... only for Mitch to make a grab for her. Catching his wrist, she secured her grip on his clammy skin, and switched to self-defence auto-pilot. But her by-heart sequence was interrupted as he grabbed one of her braids, swinging Karina face-first into the wall, then pinning her to it with his girth. 'You think I haven't learnt how to avoid that mass-taught shit by now,' he wickedly hissed into her ear. 'It doesn't work for my wife anymore, and it isn't going to work for you.' As his flabby torso engulfed hers, Karina shivered, repulsed. Eyes screwed shut as she felt his hands reach around, un-tying her jacket and tossing it down the hallway. 'As I'm sure your Daddy will teach you one day,' the lecherous whisper continued, 'once you reject an offer, you can't change your mind. Now, I just raised the price a little.' Her whole body squirmed within its creeping skin as his sweaty palms ran up her chilled arms, before resting on bare, lightly freckled shoulders. Feeling his salty ham hocks squeeze in what he though was a massage, Wolfenden began to struggle, only for his weight to push harder onto her, making it hard to breathe. 'See,' he switched ears to remark, prompting Kari to fling her head to the other side, not wanting to look at him, 'this thing never happens to good girls. Does he know where you sneak out to? What spotty streak off piss has probably had his filthy hands all over you... and more... tonight? But, hey, don't worry... now you're with someone who knows what they're doing.' As a tear squeezed out through screwed-up eyes, his thumbs hooked the straps of her dress, slipping them off her shoulder. 'Your arrogant prick of a father will probably thank me for straightening you out,' his stomach-churning words continued to spill out. Not-so-gently nibbling around her neck, his hands began to stroke through her hair, infectious touch ridding it of natural shine. 'Now, tell your Daddy you're sorry...' With the tears about to breach their restraints as the feeling of helplessness set in, a primal, animalistic roar deafened her. 'I... thi-' she heard his panicked words stutter in a fluster. Hurling himself down the passageway, Lawrence Wolfenden threw a row of knuckles with all the force he could muster. In a fruitless attempt to try and explain the indefensible, Fuller's hands shot up to try and beg his host off... but it only took his two best defences out of play as Lawrence's lead knuckle ploughed through his cheekbone. As Kari spun round, startled, relieved, her weakened frame slid down the wall as Fuller's bloated frame was dropped to one knee. Face flushed as red with rage as Fuller's was with gluttony, Lawrence's teeth ground enamel dust as steam could almost be seen expelled from his nostrils. Eyes, warped in anger, deepening moisturised wrinkles, locked onto the rabbit-in-the-headlights glare of Mitch Fuller, whose words, along with his morals, had deserted him. 'Law-... look... she came ont-uph...' Before he could force a whole lie out, Lawrence tossed his jacket over Fuller's head, then once again flung a punch at the shrouded head of his now former friend. As the blow knocked a squealing Fuller down onto the carpet, Lawrence tumbled forward onto him, the raw passion behind the blow overloading him with inertia. As he touched down, Lawrence's knee drove into Fuller's groin, again letting loose a squeal akin to a pig being herded into the abattoir’s slaughter room. Face obscured by the fine-woven silk and cotton blend, Mitch Fuller flapped his limbs frantically as punch after punch rained down into it. His screams lowered in tone as a wrenching gargle took over. The fury of the punches and the lashing of arms conspired to send to jacket falling from over Fuller's face, showing a bloody mess of broken skin and collapsed facial features. Grabbing Fuller by his loosened tie, Lawrence yanked his limp head back up, then smashed it back down against the hallway's floor with one final blow. With deep, panting breathes, Lawrence brought the brutal onslaught to a halt, slowly lowering the furious fist that had recoiled for another punch. Beneath him, the unconscious Mitch wheezed each breath, blood streaming from nose, lips, and eyebrows into his airway and gullet. Mixing with the sweat, the diluted crimson not running back inside dripped down onto the carpet beside the discarded jacket, which was the only thing having prevented Lawrence's knuckles being flayed raw. And then, as realisation hit just as hard as he'd dealt out to Fuller, Lawrence turned to his daughter. Huddled into a tight, shivering ball, Karina couldn't even force her eyelids down to blink, such was her fixated glare. Shirt sprayed with coughed-up blood, and fist still clenched, sticky and warm, Lawrence could match the stare, but couldn't even fathom matching the emotion. He was the man who still kissed her on the cheek every morning before he left for work, despite the faux-struggle of resenting remark dished out through embarrassment. The only thing she thought he knew how to punch was a stapler. 'You... I... you shouldn't have had to seen that,' Lawrence spilled out, breathless as he came down from the primal high. 'Is he..?' Karina couldn't even bring herself to finish asking. It could have been to ask if Fuller was dead... or it could have been to ask if the man she'd recognised as her father was anywhere within the man she was staring deep into. And if she couldn’t even be sure who he was... what did that say about the strangers obliviously enjoying their drinks and canapés, out of sight. 'He'll b-' Lawrence tried to assure her, reaching out, only for Kari to freak. Shuffling away from him with a whimper, she pushed herself back up against the wall, before turning and rushing down the passageway... hand struggling to restrain a mouthful of vomit. In vain, Lawrence extended his hand further, but the slamming of the bathroom door may as well have severed his outstretched fingers. A choking cough then sent a plume of blood up into the air as Fuller came back to. 'You.' Again yanking Fuller up by the collar, Wolfenden waited while panicked hands tried to hold him at bay. 'You didn't get hit in the ear, so you'd better be listening to this.' Whimpering, Fuller nodded, every drop of bravado having leaked into his underwear. 'The doors onto the back patio are open,' Wolfenden began, every word barbed so that it lodged in the brain. 'You're going to drag yourself out there, take a walk to 'clear your head' then make a phone call.' Plunging his hand into Fuller's inside pocket, Lawrence retrieved his cell phone, turning it off and slipping it into his own back pocket. 'Then you're sadly going to have your phone stolen, resist, and get beaten to a pulp,' the Wolfenden patriarch's orders continued. 'Like the big, brave man you are, you then manage to crawl back here where you'll be found in this convenient location. Are we clear? A nod means you do this, word for word.' Struggling to breathe, Mitch frantically nodded, as if the first ten times may have gone unnoticed. 'And then...' it went on, Lawrence leaning over Fuller, as if he needed to exert another sign of dominance. 'Your account will be cancelled on Monday, and you will pay up in full by Friday. There is nobody you have to explain it to, so you will not even make up a reason... you will say nothing. Then, whether you're paid up until the end of the year or not, you will remove your son from Bishop Maginn... just on the off-chance he is yours, and is unfortunate enough to the riddled with your genes.' 'O...okay...' Fuller wheezed, as terrified by the calm as he was of the violence. 'And if my wife ever has to hear Claudia reduced to explaining away another bruise...' Wolfenden growled, releasing Mitch so that he thumped back against the floor. 'She'll be having to explain away your disappearance next. Now, leave.' Rising, Lawrence stepped back away from Mitch, watching his bulbous, whimpering form skulk up and ooze out into the night. With the primitive buzz having subsided, he watched Fuller slip out through the shadows, then scooped up his jacket. Concern hastened his strides as he followed his daughter's exit route, before using his unblemished left hand to knock at the bathroom door. 'Karina,' he began, needing a place-holder while he tried to formulate his next words. 'Look, what you saw...' Not hearing any noise from inside, Lawrence instead felt a draft slithering through the gap to the frame. Tentatively turning the handle, the door swung open before him, showing the low full moon balancing on treetops through the open window. III Alexander Yeo's eyes resentfully crept open, pulling apart the glue of sleep that bound them. Not quite remembering exactly what it was that had woken him, Alex took a few seconds to fully emerge back in the waking world, rubbing his eyes and letting out a laboured yawn. A sharp tapping noise prodded his sedated senses, before its repetition attracted his attention to the window. Letting out a weary sigh, he parted with his sheets, shuffling through the carpet towards the windows, where another bout of tapping emanated. Eyes still dry, and contacts dwelling in their solution, Alex squinted as he pulled up the window, despite knowing who the call sign belonged to. 'Any room at the inn?' asked Karina, voice sapped of all vibrancy. 'Yeah, sure,' Alex welcomed her in with, always going to have been obliging, then polished it off with a little white lie. 'I couldn't nod off anyway.' Face still pale, and shoulders slumped forward, Kari hopped in, dragging her soles against the carpet before perching on the edge of the bed. After pulling the window shut, Alex looked across to Karina, where the red rings around her eyes stood out from even behind her fringe. 'Are you okay?' he felt awkward in asking, arms acting as if they wanted to reach out to Kari as she looked back up at him. 'What happe-' 'When I get back...' she ignored his concern with, needing to let her selfish intention for being there take centre stage. 'I want you to teach me how to fight... all of it.'
