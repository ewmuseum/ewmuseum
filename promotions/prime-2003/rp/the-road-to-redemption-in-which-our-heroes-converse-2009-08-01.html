The Road to Redemption: In Which Our Heroes Converse

1 August 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 1, 2009 From the moment he stepped through the revolving door of Christus St. Joseph's hospital, the strong scent of disinfectants had refused to release their hold on his sense of smell. Still, Tyrell Dawkins felt he had no choice but to put aside his dislike of the sanitary aroma and venture forth to the third floor where Officer Caleb Dawson was recovering from his near fatal gunshot wounds. It was the least he could do for the man that risked his life to save his own. He smiled and nodded toward a young nurse wearing dark purple scrubs, her black hair pulled back into a loose pony tail save for a loose strand that framed the left side of her face. She smiled in return, shifting the clipboard in her left hand to her right so she could tuck the loose strand behind her ear. “Excuse me, “ Tyrell hesitated a moment as the nurse stopped her forward progress and turned to face him. “I'm sorry to bug you, but could you tell me where Officer Dawson's room is.” “Oh yes,” the woman with the magnetic name badge that read 'Sherry Pirote, R.N.' pointed down the hall with her free hand. “He's in room 315. Are you family?” “No ma'am,” Tyrell turned away, suddenly overcome with a tinge of guilt. “I'm the person he was trying to help when he got hurt.” She giggled softly, and a nod soon followed. “So you're the one.” “Excuse me?” His eyebrow peaked a bit while his lips crinkled in surprise. Her remark caught him a bit off guard, and he hoped that his sudden refusal to make eye contact did not reflect toward her instead of the comment made. “Officer Caleb,” she pointed in the direction of his room again. “The moment he came out of recovery, the first thing he asked was whether the guy being chased in the alley was okay.” “I...” Dawkins was a bit choked up, unsure how he was supposed to react to something like that. He could feel a flutter in his gut as he contemplated turning around and leaving. “I didn't expect that.” “It's not something I hear every day, so it was a nice surprise.” The remark did nothing to quell his nerves. How could he walk into a room with a man as selfless as that, when the first thing he thought about was being thankful he was alive. “There's a woman in there with him now,” Sherry continued, her left eyebrow peaked with . “But you can go in if you'd like.” “Thank you.” * * * * * * The room was loud, as children ran around in the background screaming and hollering. The majority of them could care less to sit down and talk to an absentee father that will spend most – if not all – of their childhood years living a life behind bars. It was a lifestyle Marquis Daniels had grown up around, become accustomed to, yet he could not see how these children would not follow in their father's footsteps. That is usually how things turned out when a young boy or girl were raised without a father figure to instill discipline and authority in the household. There is a fine line that separates discipline from abuse, and Jerhiah Peeples could have starred in a three ring circus with how well he walked it. The father of Marquis and Dyson Peeples hardly ever gave them the love that they deserved, and only showed his love for his wife, Cashelle, in a sexual manner. Of course, that was only on the rare occasions that he was sober enough to realize what he was doing. The rest of the time he spent stumbling around the house in a stupor, forcing himself on a wife that had grown weary of his drunken advances. To say that his father was not much of a role model was an understatement, and quite possibly why Marquis had done everything in his power to make sure he did not make the same mistakes in life that his father had. “This mother fucker.” The words took hold of his attention and lifted his eyes to the big barrel of a black man wearing an orange Harris county jail jumpsuit. Gone were the set of three karat diamond stud earrings that he hung from his lobes, the ruby and diamond crested grille that he would proudly flash with every slick grin, and the wads of cash he always had in his pocket. “Sandman.” Marquis motioned for the man known as Sandman to have a seat across from him, knowing full well that the man wanted to reach across the table and beat him until he was seconds away from death. Sandman smacked his tongue against the back of his teeth as he reached for the seat at the table Marquis had pointed at. The legs of the chair scraped against the tiled floor as he pulled it away from the table, pausing slightly before dropping himself in his seat. “You got a lot of fucking nerve walking up in this bitch like you got balls the size of Texas.” Sandman leaned back in his chair, throwing his arm across the back as he sneered in Marquis' direction. “Besides the fact that there are ten armed guards just itching for a reason to put me on my ass; tell me why I shouldn't take that chance and tear your fucking heart out.” “Because we both know who the real snitch at this table is.” * * * * * * Tyrell Dawkins pushed the door of the hospital room in slowly, careful not to make too much noise. He peeked into the room, sticking just his head into the small gap to announce his arrival. “Hello?” The woman seated in the uncomfortable looking chair by the hospital bed turned slowly, the illumination from the muted television dancing across her face. She raised a hand to her face, her index finger extended and pressed to her lips. “Shhh.” “Sorry,” he whispered. He entered the room and slowly closed the door, careful not to let it slam shut. “I can come back if you...” “No,” she cut him off in a hushed tone. “Please, come in.” The room was exactly what you'd expect, sterile white walls with plastic covered posters that did nothing more than advertise medicines and showed examples of faces in different stages of pain. The small twenty six inch flat screen television was mounted to the wall, one of the many upgrades that hospitals had partaken when trying to make things a bit more comfortable for their patients. All it really did was spend the money they were overcharging them anyway, because the money sure as hell did not go toward the food. Three square meals devoid of any taste whatsoever, they probably served better food in prisons. Still, it was one of the few creature comforts the place offered that anyone could enjoy. Unfortunately, it was on the Weather Channel. Tyrell made his way toward the bedside of the officer that had risked death to keep him from losing his. He nodded toward the woman, unsure of who she was or what her relation to the man was. He only knew the officer's name was Caleb Dawson because of the reports in the news broadcasts that made it one of their top stories. It was right up there with the President drinking a beer and a woman dragging her child across the floor of a store in importance. “How is he doing?” Ty inquired as he finally turned to take stock of the woman. She wore a white floral patterned skirt that reached about mid-calf and a solid salmon top that matched one of the flowers on her skirt. Her hair draped over her shoulders, and looked as if she had not kept up with it in a while. Her face was devoid of any sort of make-up, but still she had a natural beauty that cosmetics probably only enhanced even further. “Better,” she responded with a curt smile. He must have made her feel a bit self conscious, because she pulled a rubber band from around her wrist and pulled her hair back to tie it up in a pony tail. A few loose strands managed to stray and frame the features of her face. The light brown eyes that stared back at him had a tinge of pain that screamed out for comfort. It was as if she did not believe the words she had said. “Tyrell Dawkins,” he extended his hand toward her as he introduced himself for the first time. She took his hand in her own and gave it a soft shake as the name skipped around in her mind as she tried to place where she had heard it before. “Annabelle Edwards,” she managed to reply before it dawned on her just who the man she had just met actually was. “You're the man those thugs were after when Caleb showed up.” He wasn't sure if she was asking a question or just stating what she already knew, but he answered her just the same. “That's right, ma'am.” A simple nod, as her attention went back to the man laid out before her with a bullet still lodged less than an inch from his lungs, was all the reply he was offered at the moment. She was unsure whether she should hold some sort of animosity toward the person she held sort of responsible for the condition Caleb was in. 'Was it really his fault though?' she wondered as he stood there staring at her expecting some sort of reaction other than a nod of the head. “And you are?” he inquired, though he did not mean to pry. She knew full well what he meant by his question, but even she was clueless as to what the proper response should be. She knew they did not have a relationship you could consider normal, so calling herself his girlfriend would not be true. She was sure that she was more than just a friend, so there was no way she would respond with that. She settled on, “It's complicated.” Satisfied that the answer was vague enough to keep him wondering while she figured out what the right answer should be, she continued to speak. She hoped that her question did not appear to be prying into his affairs. The last thing she wanted to do was invade the man's privacy, but no one had given her any answers. Surely the man that was there would be able to tell her something other than 'We're still investigating.' which happened to be standard fare despite who was at the front desk when she asked. “What happened?” It was a question that he had been asked many times the evening of the incident, and as much as he would like to explain how things had turned out the way they did; he was unsure about what exactly had happened. He meant no disrespect when he answered with the answer he had received not even a minute before. “It's complicated.” he swallowed back the lump stuck in his throat, but it had grown roots and decided to set up residence. He could tell right away that she was not pleased with the answer he had given, but he was unsure what she expected him to say. “I guess I deserve that,” she said with a slight smile, a hint of a younger woman crept into her voice. Her hands were clasped together on her lap, but her thumbs circled each other nervously as she bit on her lower lip. “We were going to go out on our first date the day after the incident.” She stumbled on her words a bit, evidence that she was doing all she could to keep from breaking down into tears. “We've known each other for a while now, but neither one of us had the guts to actually take things to the next step. I think that is what made me fall in love with him.” “You were in love with him?” he winced slightly, and immediately regretted not only the surprise in his tone but the comment being made in the past tense. “I still am,” she said, her attention back on the man who laid prone on the slightly inclined apparatus. “Nothing's changed.” “I'm sorry. I didn't mean for that to come out the way it did.” “You have nothing to be sorry about,” she told him, those light brown eyes moistened from the tears she struggles to keep refrained. “Still, that wasn't what I meant.” he stood there in silence for a moment, the television casting shadows on the walls behind them. He knew he had to tell her more, that he had to explain to her as much as he knew about what happened that night. “They thought I was someone else.” “What?” she seemed shocked at the comment, even if it was brief. “The men that were chasing me...that shot Caleb,” he added that last bit in there at the last minute, because that seemed more important than a couple of guys shooting at him. “They thought I was someone else.” “So in your own way, you saved someone's life too.” “I never really looked at it like that,” he replied, suddenly a bit more comfortable around the woman he just met. “Especially when all I did was run away.” “You're alive.” A slight smile creased her face. “Caleb is alive too.” The smile on her face doubled in size, wider than before. “Maybe you should look at it like that.” “I guess I could.” he returned her smile with one of his own, how could he not return that smile. “It beats taking all the blame.” She was right, and he knew that she was right. * * * * * * “You're full of shit, you know that,” Sandman said with a smug grin on his face, still confident and cocky. “The name Jack Connors ring a bell?” It wasn't much, but the twitch on the corners of the Sandman's lips was enough to give Marquis the upper hand in the route this conversation was headed. “You don't know what the hell you're talking about.” Sandman leaned back in his chair and tucked his hands in his pockets. Gone was the smug grin, replaced now by an awkward smile that looked as if he had painted it on with crayons. “I was there, remember?” Marquis leaned forward, his hands flat on the table as he shakes his head. “What you did to that cop was personal, plain and simple.” “You can't pin that shit on me,” Sandman starts to get a bit nervous, the shiftiness in his eyes like a pair of spotlights shining through a dense evening sky. “Talk any louder and I won't have to say anything.” It was true, the sudden change in the Sandman's voice had garnered him quite a bit of attention he had been able to avoid up until now. Even the little kid running around in the background got quiet and looked at the big black man in the bright orange jumpsuit. “What kind of deal did you two have going on?” Marquis inquired, knowing full well that he wasn't going to get a direct response. “Yeah, suddenly you don't have much to say.” It wasn't that the man had nothing to say, he just did not know how to say it without incriminating himself even more. He especially did not want to rat himself out on the murder of a police officer. “I don't know what you think I'm going to tell you, but you're barking up the wrong tree.” “I don't want you to say anything,” Marquis replied without missing a beat. “I just want you to leave me the hell alone. Don't be sending yours goons to take me out, and don't send anyone to collect money that I don't owe you anymore.” “Just like that?” Marquis nodded in response. “I'll give you your space,” Sandman leaned forward, which in turn made a few of the guards pay more attention. “But you better believe this isn't over.” “Oh, it's over.”
