I'm Home, Son (Born Again, Round II and III)

26 May 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/26/10 The Good Life Victory was merely the icing on the cake. The return to the sport itself was unimaginably wonderful. …We’re speaking of the game of darts, of course. More specifically, Emilio’s first game back after his return to Mickey’s Pub, the finest establishment in the greater Boston area. “Bull’s-eye.” Emilio went up to the board and momentarily admired his handy work before taking the dart out and placing it back in the case. “I ain’t never playing Around the World with this asshole again,” Mike remarked in between sips of his lager. “He’s a damn darts shark and that’s the God’s-honest truth.” Emilio smiled, shrugging his shoulders. “If I didn’t play with such incompetent amateurs, I might rise to the challenge and find my full potential someday. As it is, I’m off a bit tonight.” Mike beamed back at Emilio. He chuckled, realizing for the first time this evening just how improved his friend’s English was. He remembered back to 2004 when he first met Emilio. The poor guy could hardly order a cheeseburger from the dirve-thru, let alone hold an intelligent conversation. And yet here he was now, poking subtle fun at his bar buddies. Mike half expected Emilio to launch into some philosophical rant at some distant point in the evening. “Well ain’t you goddamn pretty,” said Mike. Emilio turns and flexes his muscles. “Yes. That’s what the ladies say.” “And I say get me anothah fuckin’ beer,” a voice called out behind him. Emilio winced as Patch, his other longtime friend, came up and punched him in the kidney. “This liver ain’t gonna drink itself dry. But I guess that’s what I get for drinkin’ with ‘incompetent amateurs,” Patch explained. “Maybe if I drank with some guys in my own league it might even up my game a little.” Emilio couldn’t help but chuckle at his friends parody. “Maybe you should up your game in the bedroom,” said a small blond woman, stepping onto the scene. Emilio and Mike shared a laugh at Patch’s expense as the man shook his head. His wife, Sheri, playfully tugged at his shoulder. “Maybe I’d reach my full potential. Or at least a goddamn orgasm. For once.” “Yeah, man,” Emilio quipped in, “maybe you should step up your game, kid.” Patch feigned anger, throwing his fist softly in wild archs, but swinging at no one in particular. This caused Emilio a brief moment of déjà vu. Standing there laughing, drinking beers with his friends; it all felt so right. Although it pained Emilio to be away from his daughter for any length of time, hanging out with Patch and Sheri and Mike was like a home away from home. Maybe it was the similarity between the verbal beat-downs they engaged in with each other and the physical beat-downs he took part in the ring. Maybe it was the way they made him relaxed and filled him with a sense of serenity. Or maybe it was just the getting shitfaced. Either way, Emilio enjoyed these moments. They distracted him from his “day job” of getting pummeled at the hands of behemoth athletes, and sometimes getting thrown off PRIME-a-Trons. Unlike professional wrestling where the highs were high and the lows were low, here in his realm of social nirvana, the intensity was tapered back, the sharpness blunted. Whereas a loss in the squared-circle was horrifying, a loss at a game of darts or poker was a minor inconvenience, a benign statistical anomaly in an otherwise immensely enjoyable evening. “So what do you think, champ?” Patch began to ask, snapping Emilio away from his thoughts. “Are you gonna beat these guys or what?” “Fuck yes,” Emilio said quickly, surprising even himself by the confidence in his voice. “You know this Garbage Bag guy ain’t no Cuban slut like Elise Eres, dude,” Mike said. “I’ve seen his stuff before. Pretty good in that ring, I tell ya.” Emilio nodded. Indeed, Garbage Bag Johnny was quite the competitor. From the Global Title Tourney to the Dual-Halo, GCW’s current commissioner had made quite a name for himself. Emilio had been studying his opponent for the last two weeks and he was certain of one thing: even if…scratch that, when he took down the G-Bag, he probably wouldn’t be in the best condition to compete later on in the evening. But that’s pro wrestling for you. A week off and then two matches in one night the following card. No matter. Emilio would surely step up to the plate. He always did. And if the underwater aqua-cardio training was half as useful in the long haul as it was intense, his heart wouldn’t be the only thing to keep him going. His body would be ready as well. Even if he was forty years old. Or “seasoned” as Emilio liked to think of it as. “Ah, pay no attention!” Patch said with a dismissive gesture. “The dude’s a freakin’ hobo wanna-be, man. His greatest accomplishment is running that joke promotion with all those mental characters.” Mike laughed. “Yeah. I heard about one dude who performed a live penal enema on World Wide. You see that shit, guys? I mean, this horned-freak just funneled a goddamn Mountain Dew through a straw in his cock…I know, right!?” Emilio raised a fresh glass of beer to his lips and took a long, deep sip. “What people in other promotions do with their genitals on live television is none of my concern. I’m focused on wrestling, guys. Winning matches. Winning titles. That’s it,” Emilio declared, dissecting the air in front of him with his hand. “I don’t care about any of that drama. You know what happened last time around.” Emilio trailed off, averting his eyes as frowne spread across the faces of his three friends. “Eh,” Sheri said, breaking the tension, “cheer up champ. You’ll have that Universal title in no time.” “Yeah, buddy,” said Patch. “Fuckin’ no time.” Emilio’s three friends gathered around, lifting their beers up to his and clanging them together in unison. “To Emilio,” Mike said before they all took a sip. “May he win the title, find true love, and stop bein’ such a stupid asshole when it comes to darts.” “To Emilio!” they said in unison, sharing a quick laugh. “With friends like you guys, who needs enemies?” Emilio asked. “Oh, you do, man. You do,” said Patch. “Huh?” Emilio raised a brow. “I’m just sayin’ you’re at your best when you got somethin’ to fight for. You know, when you’re really pissed.” “I’m not sure I follow,” Emilio said. Patch cocked his head. “Not saying you need to get back in PRIME and start making enemies, bro. All I’m sayin’ is that you shift into a whole ‘nother gear when the wolves are at your throat. It’s human nature, I guess. But especially with you. I ain’t ever seen nobody more stubborn in my life.” “It’s true, Emilio,” Sheri added in her raspy tone. “You’ve just got a switch inside ya. And when someone turns it on, watch out.” “So,” Emilio said, raising his mug back to his lips, “you’re suggesting that I just walk back in and start making enemies? Start taking names and kicking ass? Should I root out all the assholes in PRIME, chase them back to their locker rooms and stomp them out? Maybe I’ll even catch someone’s attention, get thrown twenty feet into a production table, and get branded on live television. Or maybe someone could even fuck with my family again. Yeah. That’d be awesome…” There was an awkward pause before Emilio let out a sigh. Mike came up and patted him on the back. “You know we don’t mean anything by it, guy. We’re your friends, Emilio. We don’t want anything to happen to you.” Patch and Sheri both nodded their heads in agreement. “But we also know who you are, what you stand for. And well…you, uh, just have a way with people…” Emilio frowned. “You mean I attract flies like a steaming pile of shit?” “Well, not exactly,” Mike began. “No way,” Emilio said in a low, rumbling tone, cutting his friend off mid-sentence. “Maybe in the past. Maybe I used to have a penchant for bringing the plague on myself. But not anymore, guys. Not anymore. I ain’t no holy crusader. I know that now. I ain’t carrying the weight of the world on my shoulders. There’s more than enough people on the world to carry that burden. I’ve paid my dues,” Emilio said, taking another long gulp from his mug, “and now it’s time to cash in on my investment. I’m bringing bck that title if it’s the last thing I do. The rest of the locker room can suck it.” By now the tensioned had lessened considerably. “Sounds like a plan, brotha!” Mike finally said. “Yeah, Rage. Go get ‘em,” said Sheri. “I intend to,” Emilio said, finishing off his beer. “I intend to.” “Intensions do not always become reality, my friend.” The four of them turned at the sound of a strange voice. Strange, Emilio thought, yet somehow familiar. A short man with dark skin and a black stubble goatee meandered over to them with a martini glass in hand. He was a sharp dressed man, far too dressed up to fit in with the Mickey’s Pub crowd. He wore a jet black suite with a light green shirt under a black sports jacket. Although he wore dark sunglasses, Emilio imagined a twinkle in the man’s eyes as he approached the future PRIME champion. “Excuse me?” Emilio asked. Instead of replying, the man simply held out a hand. After a moment, Emilio reluctantly accepted the shake. The man’s grasp was both firm and confident. “Allow me to introduce myself,” he began, nodding toward each of Emilio’s friends. “You’re allowed to do whatevah you freakin’ want,” said Patch. “It’s goddamn America.” Sheri and Mike laughed at this, but it was more forced than genuine. The man simply let out a slight sigh but his expression never changed. His gaze was still concentrated on Emilio’s, and Emilio’s on his. “My name is Ishmael Fatwah. And I have a message from your father.” Emilio’s heart skipped a beat. He felt like reaching up and unclogging his ears. Similarily, his companions shifted nervously in place. Mike opened his mouth as if to speak but then thought better of it. Over the course of his life, Emilio had mastered the art of maintaining a calm demeanor even if he couldn’t control his emotions. Although the bile in his stomach was building up, his facial expression remained unchanged. He kept the same assertive stare into this man, Ishmael’s, eyes. After a moment, a crooked grin spread across the man’s otherwhereise passive face. If he was surprised at Emilio’s controlled and passive stance, he wasn’t showing it. “And just who the fuck do you think you are?” Patch finally asked. His tone wasn’t as angry as it was assertive. Nonetheless, the off-duty copy took a stride closer to the man even as his wife put his arm on his shoulder. “These ears would be deaf to whatever the old bastard had to say,” Emilio said. He finished his beer and walked over to gently place it at the bar. Then, quietly, he pulled out a stool and took a seat. Ishmael walked up to the bar and took a seat to Emilio’s left. Patch went to sit beside his friend but was pulled back by his wife. “Hone…” Sheri pleaded, tightening her grip on his shoulder. Patch let out a sigh of protest but remained in place. “I’m not here to play games,” said Ishmael, quietly. “Nor am I,” Emilio responded. “But my father, well, whatever it is, I’m not interested. If you’re his messenger, go back and tell him this. My days of taking part in his…extracurricular activities are over,” Emilio continued, choosing his words with the careful meticulousness that he had developed over years of intense verbal battles. “I’m not a messenger,” said Ishmael. “In fact, I’m not even a friend. I’m merely a business associate.” Emilio took a sip from his freshly poured beer. He swallowed hard. He turned his gaze upward and store into the man’s eyes with a hint of spite beaming forth from under his furrowed brow. He knew what kind of “business” his father was. And he wanted no part of it. Not anymore. Not when he came this close. Not when he was this clean. A new lease of life, a clean slate, and the reunion with his long lost daughter was something that he was very thankful for. He praised God every day in thanks for these blessings. To Emilio, at this point in his life, his professional goal of becoming the PRIME universal champion, while finally in his sight, was no longer something that he needed to fill the empty gap in his heart. Instead, it was more of an auxiliary extension of the joy he had recently found. An extra. And none of those things were worth sacrificing for a wad of dirty cash. No matter how much, and no matter how much he could use the money at this point. “I’m not interested,” he said after a moment. “And I won’t tell you again.” “Not interested in what?” asked Ishmael. He opened his arms and rolled his shoulders. “Not interested in whatever the offer is.” “I have no offer,” Ishmael began, reaching into his jacket pocket. Instinctively, Emilio sat back on his haunches but leaned his upper body closer to the man, ready to attack in the event that it was a weapon. “I simply come with a gift.” “I don’t want it.” “I didn’t ask if you wanted it or not.” Even beneath his tanned skin Emilio’s cheeks went red. His muscles twitched and his heart began to pump. He was preparing himself, mentally and physically, for whatever this stranger had in mind. Ishmael, however, either didn’t notice Emilio’s distasteful reaction, or simply chose to ignore it. Probably the latter. But instead of presenting a defensive posture himself, the man simply shook his head and released a deep, guttural laugh. “A beer for my friend, please,” he instructed the bar tender. “I’m not your friend.” “Ah, maybe so Senor Ragerio….Jr. But either way, I’m going to do what I came here to do…” He reached back into his jacket and pulled out the hidden object. Emilio jumped up from his stool. “For you…my friend,” Ishmael said, tossing Emilio a small black cell phone. Emilio caught it in one hand. He squeezed the small object in his giant palm, turning it over in his hands. Emilio’s raised his brow again. “Why this?” he asked. But before Ishmael could utter a response the phone began to vibrate. “Well,” Ishmael started, “aren’t you going to asnwer?” “Why would I?” “Because it’s for you?” “Is that so?” The man simply shrugged. “If you want me to answer it, I can.” “Be my guest,” Emilio said, tossing the phone a little too hard back toward the man. Despite this, Ishmael caught it gracefully with one hand and flicked open the receiver in one swift motion. The phone was on speaker. “Hello?” A familiar voice asked. “Is this my son? Emilio, is that you?” Emilio bit the bottom of his lip, and his shaky nerves that were once flustered began to settle in as a wave of anger descended upon him, catalyzed by the voice of his father. “Yes, Senor Ragerio, your son is right here.” “Ah! Emilio!” the voice on the other end of the line chirped. “How are you, my boy?” “I’ve been better,” Emilio said, snatching the phone back. “But if you don’t hang up, get your crony out of my city, and leave me and my family alone…you’re not going to have a very nice night. That I can guarantee.” The old man chuckled. “Yes, yes. Always the sparkplug, you.” The man’s chuckle was quickly replaced by a much more somber, sad tone. “But Emilio…son…you are my family. Just as my granddaughter is my family.” “Sylvia will have nothing to do with you!” Emilio shouted with a little more poison than even he expected. “Mention her name again…and I’ll kill you,” he finished, in a hushed tone that only Ishmael and the old man on the other end of the line could hear. Undeterred, the old man laughed. “Enough with the dramatics, my boy! Save it for the ring, no? I hear you’re having quite a time in PRIME. A contender for the title? I must say, I’m impressed. But pleasure aside, my son, I’m not interested in talking about that right yet. There’s plenty of time for that down the road. My son-” Emilio cut off his tirade with a stark utterance of his own. “I’m not your son you dirty old bastard. Now make your point before I hang up.” “You are my son,” the voice asserted, “and Sylvia is my granddaughter. I haven’t seen her in years, and my how she’s grown..” Emilio’s heart began to pound. “You….you?...” “Yes, I’m looking at her as we speak, son. Such a beautiful young woman she has become.” He paused to the let his son interject, but Emilio was too shell shocked to come up with a response. “Anyway, I’m at your house now. Cooking up dinner, actually. Sylvia is in the other room. Why don’t you come home from the bars a bit early tonight, son? Have a nice family dinner?” With that, Emilio hung up the phone. He squeezed it hard until the screen cracked and then chucked it back in the direction of Ishmael. Yet again, the man somehow managed to make the catch with surprising ease. “If anything happens to her, I’m holding you responsible,” he threatened, putting a finger in Ishmael’s chest. Ishmael simply nodded his head. “I’m coming with you,” Patch said, putting on his jacket. “No can do, buddy,” Emilio said as he tossed a crisp $50 bill on the bar. “This is a one man show.” He motioned for the bar tender to keep his change before heading for the exit. Grandpa Rage Hector Fernando Ragerio was a man of intrigue. He was an old man now, sixty-two years of age, but moved about with the confidence and ease of a man in his twenties. With his tall six-foot-two frame, and deep set brown eyes, he was both physically a man of a lesser age but with the knowledge and wisdom of someone beyond his years. A rare combination in human beings. He was a sharp man, too. Conniving, greedy, unforgiving, to be sure. Yet to his friends he was intelligent, loving, caring, and above all else, loyal. If he was in your corner you knew he had your back, one-hundred-percent. His enemies despised him and his friends looked up to him. He was, in all respects, the perfect cocaine dealer. The archetype henchmen, much like a character straight out of a Hollywood movie. Emilio didn't remember his father much, really. And for that matter, he hardly considered him a father. Emilio had been adopted at the age of ten. After his real father had died in accident, working in the coaca fields for Mr. Ragerio, the drug dealer had taken it upon himself to raise the young lad. Emilio was a wild-eyed, adventurous youth. He was six-foot by the age of ten and one of the fiercest competitors in the regional soccer league. He was temporarily set back by the death of his father, but as is often the case with children, he recovered quickly and bounced back strong, becoming a man quite advanced for his age. He was strong-willed, passionate, and had a righteous streak of justice in him, too. There were many occasions when the school bullies would be sent home in shambles, beaten down at the hands of the fiesty child, Emilio, who was much younger than them. Naturally, Mr. Ragerio took a liking to this kid and officially adopted him at age eleven. However, personal and political differences had set them on opposite ends of the fence when Emilio turned fifteen. The wild teen had gone off to join the Sandanista rebels while Mr. Ragerio took comfort in the proection and implicit consent of the Contras, who allowed him to continue his illegal operations throughout their reign. That, however, is a story for a different day. Needless to say, besides for his heart which pumped overtime, often skipping a beat on his frantic cab ride home, Emilio was also seething in an ocean of anger. The thought of this dirty old man, a man who once allowed men like Emilio's real father to be worked into the ground until they literally collapsed out of exhaustion and died, hanging out in his home, making conversation wit his daughter was unbarable. Even if he was technically Sylvia's grandfather. Emilio could never forgive the old man for what he did. For how he treated his enemies. For how he treated his workers. For how he treated his own family. It was only five years, in fact, since the time that Senor Ragerio tried to play Emilio into coming to work for him. The old man was aware of Big Ben, the Japanese mafia man's, attempt to kidnap and ransom Sylvia. In fact, he knew the dirty details of plan, rigth down to the exact time and location of the kidnapping. And yes, to be fair, he did save his granduaghter. But he had done so only as a ploy to regain Emilio's affection. Instead of preventing the whole ordeal, as a man with his wealth and unlimited resources surely could have, he let it play out, sending in his men only at the last minute before Sylvia was lifted off in the plane and would have been sent to God only knows where. As grateful as Emilio was for the eventual rescue, he was just as infumed at the very notion that the old man had not acted sooner. For what is she was hurt? What if, God forbid, something did happen to Sylvia? Then not only would Big Ben and his cronies have been killed, but Senor Ragerio would have suffered at the hands of his only son as well. Of that you can be quite sure. So as Emilio quickly exited the cab as it pulled up to his doorstep, flinging the cab driver a crisp bill, not waiting for the change, he clinched his fist together and let the pain seep through him like an ocean of unreleneting rage as the past fears and anxieties mixed with the uncertainty and spontanaity of the present. This wasn’t going to be pretty. A Father’s Pride Emilio was surprised, really, at how he took his time as he came into the house. He calmly entered his quiet abode, shutting the door easily behind him before kicking off his shoes. He even managed to turn and hang up his jacket. However, as the brisk smell of his father’s familiar cooking wafted in through the kitchen doorway, instead of inciting a pleasant memory of the finer meals he had dined on as a youth, the odor brought him nothing but anger and with each scent pouring through his flaring nostrils, his blood began to boil. Without worrying about the possible consequences, Emilio rounded the corner and leapt through the kitchen doorway. “Good evening so-” But the old man’s words were left unfinished. He was thrown down to the floor in a manner of violence that not even his battle tested body was keen to. Emilio mounted the man in a single bound and drove one well aimed fist into the man’s face, bloodying his nose. That’s all it took though. No vicious beating. No malignant outburst or uncontained rage. Just one solid punch, one bloodied, face and then it was all over. Settled. Emilio stood to his feet, wiping the invisible dust from his pants. He then reached down and helped Senor Ragerio to his feet. The man was smiling. He turned his head up, swallowing blood, and held the tip of his nose. “Very good, son. Your much stronger than I remember.” Emilio was silent. “However, that’s no way to treat a guest. Especially one who has prepared such a fine meal as this,” he said, motioning toward the dining room table where plates were set out in a fine display and an array of authentic Latin American cuisine was set out in steaming bowls in the center. “At least that’s my opinion. As a gentlemen.” Emilio ignored this. “Where’s my daughter?” he asked quietly, directly. “She’s in the living room, I believe. Either watching the Sox game, or perhaps playing on the new laptop I bought her. If I had to guess, I’d say the latter, though. She’s quite keen to it.” “Why would you do that? What are you doing here?” “Well it’s the least I could do after not seeing her in almost fourteen years. I’ve missed so many Christmases and birthdays. It’s nothing, really. And besides, she needs it for school. That old Gateway you bought her for her birthday last fall isn’t worthy of a woman of her caliber. Do you not approve?” Emilio let out a sigh. “That’s not the point, Senor Ragerio. And you know it.” Before the man can respond Emilio cups his hands and yells, “Sylvia!?” A moment later a soft voice from upstairs replies. “Da…Daddy? Is that you? Are you home?” “Yes, sweetie. I’m, uh, back early for a few days. Resting up before the big night…Are you okay?” he finally asked, all the while sending his adoptive father the evil eye. “Yeah. Why wouldn’t I be? Are you here to see grandpa?” Emilio cursed under his breath. “Uh…yes, hone. I am.” Emilio turned as he heard the familiar footsteps coming down the stairs. But then he turned back and looked at the bloodied sight of Senor Ragerio and shook his head. “Uh…wait a second, hone...” But it was too late. Sylvia shot out into the kitchen like a lightning bolt and flung herself at her father, wrapping her arms around him a warm embrace. It had been a full eight days since they’d last seen each other. The longest period of time she had spent away from her father since their reunion. Meanwhile, Senor Ragerio turned away and pretended to be fiddling with something on the stove. Sylvia had apparently been so enthralled by the presence of her father that she had not noticed her grandfather’s disposition. Good Emilio thought to himself, taking her by the arm. “I’m only back for the night, hone. And then it’s back to Boston for two days of training before the fight.” “You’re gonna kick Garbage Bag Johnny’s ass, huh dad? Takin’ out the trash!” Emlio frowned. In the background, Senor Ragerio laughed. “Watch your language, young lady.” “Sorry, dad,” she said, blushing. “And you,” Emilio continued, turning toward his unwelcomed guest, “we need to talk.” Senor Ragerio simply nodded, cutting away at an onion as the blood in his nose had finally begun to clot. “Slyvia, me and…gramp…need a little alone time, okay? Why don’t you go down street and pick up some desert.” “Ice cream?” “Sure.” “Ben & Jerrys’?” “Fine.” Emilio took out his wallet and handed his daughter a $20. “F’in sweet,” she said. Emilio raised a brow. “Language…” “Sorry…” Sylvia grinned, extending up on her tippy toes to plant her father with a kiss on the cheek. “I’ll be back in a jiff!” “Take your time,” he said. Emilio waited until he heard the sound of the door shutting behind his daughter before he turned back to face his guest. “Alright, Senor Ragerio, just what the hell is going on?” His father stopped cutting the onion for a moment and turned to wipe a tear from his eye. “Must we discuss it now, before dinner?” “Oh, gee! I’m sorry, is this bad time?” “Yes, quite.” “Well I want answers.” “Son, we’ll plenty of time to reminisce about old times.” For a moment Emilio thought that the old man had gone crazy. “What are you rambling on about? Had too much tequila already, did ya?” Senor Ragerio shrugged. “No, I’m just in no mood to argue. Besides, did you hear the good news?” “No. What?” Emilio asked, lowering his guard for the moment. This is going to be good, he thought. Senor Ragerio turned away from the counter and flashed his son a shit-eating grin. “I’m moving in.”
