Date: ReVolution 201 Another week had passed. David walked into his small apartment, one not fitting a man who made as much money as he did, and threw his black bag onto the ground. He couldn't bring himself to go back to his house, not after the scene he had made. There would be questions, questions he wouldn't want to answer, and looks that he couldn't bear to handle. He wanted to be left alone, but that apparently wasn’t in the deck of cards for him. “What’re you doing here?” David didn’t care if his question was blunt. His precious blue eyes looked at the woman who sat on his piano stool. There was sadness in her eyes. He had to try and hide his concern. “Wanted to see you and I knew that you would come back here instead of going to your house.” Philadelphia’s voice sounded like a soft rain falling on the earth; a perfect symphony of quiet and wet. “I just need some time to think.” “I understand that, David. Trust me, I do.” “So, I think you should leave.” She just shook her head though. David quelled the need to fight. Instead, he walked to his kitchen, not large enough to hold more than him in it, and pulled out a bottle of vodka and poured himself a glass. “David, you need someone in your life that is going to look out for you, that’s going to have your best interests at heart. I might not be that person, but I’m going to at least try. Your family was left in shambles when you left this time, and yes, I know you had to due to work. They need answers most importantly.” There were no answers that he felt he could give that would fix their pain. He walked around to the edge of the kitchen. David took a gulp of his beverage. It didn’t have the kick that he was looking for and it showed on his face. “I don’t think they want answers, Philly. At the end of the day, I think they want an explanation as to how I hid this from them for so long. Then, they’re going to want to sling me up on a cross and crucify me for besmirching their valiant father’s legacy. At the end of the day, they’re not going to believe me.” Philadelphia shook her head. David didn’t care. Instead, he longed for a quiet evening at his piano. Away from the noisy distractions that seemed determined to break him into a wallowing mess. She stood up and walked over to him, draped his arm across his chest. A light kiss on the back of his neck cooled him. “You try to carry the weight of the world on your shoulders, David, and you’re not going to be able to do it forever. The amazing thing is that you tried to protect your father from this, protect your family from this, without even a hint of what was truly going on. I think that’s truly honorable. It came out though. They’re still your family.” His eyes closed. Slowly. Another kiss, this time on his right earlobe. David liked that. “I’m sorry.” His words were equal in weight and yet powerful in meaning. Philly smiled at this gesture and wrapped her arms around the man she had slowly fallen for. David felt the small protruding object against the small of his back, much like a woman in the club grinding into your typical red-blooded male. “I don’t know how you do it, David. I just don’t know. It’s truly jaw dropping to watch you be more of a man than should be expected of you.” She paused. David turned around and looked her in the eyes. “You fear being a father, not because you want to shun your responsibility, but because you want to do right by your daughter. It’s beautiful. Just trust me that I know you’re going to be a great father.” David shook his head, unbelieving of the sentiment. He gently grabbed the glass and finished it off, his tongue dancing wildly inside of his mouth. “I have trouble believing that. I think being a good father is encoded into our DNA and based off our experiences in life.” I think Shawn gave you the best experiences you could have possibly had. I look at your sisters and they came out great. You came out great.” “Then why don’t I feel great?” “Once again, because you put so much pressure on yourself, pressure that no one can possibly bear for long. The problem is that you’ve carried it for years now, and it’s slowly going to wear you down until you’re unable to stand any longer. Why don’t you put it down? Why don’t you move forward without it?” Her questions begged to be answered, worse than a dog when it wants to be fed. David looked into the mirror that hung by his front door, an old one that he had found in a dumpster while he moved in, and saw a man who looked like hell. Worse even. “Because I don’t think I can. I don’t think God wants me to.” Her eyes were filled with sadness, and he knew it. She pitied him and it made David feel even worse about everything. Philly was trying so hard and instead of helping her out, he was making it just that more difficult. “Will you at least talk to your mother?” David wondered what he would say to her. “Sure. Yeah, I guess I can do that.” His words lacked any enthusiasm in them though. Philadelphia wrapped her arms around David and brought him into her, wanting him to feel like there was someone there for him. He welcomed the gesture, but deep down, he wanted to just be by himself. Drinking a bottle of scotch, until there was none left, except for black. *** The PRIME Headquarters is your typical low-key building, complete with a receptionist when you walk in through the front door, offices littered for a few floors, and then some production areas to film different pieces for upcoming shows or commercials. Most PRIME wrestlers never even step inside of the building, having no reason to go to Chicago, or wanting anything to do with the place unless it’s a Wednesday or Sunday evening and for a show. Andrea Noble though found herself in the building two days before ReVolution 201, and had a large smile on her face. After a successful discussion with Lisa Tyler, she felt like David’s career was ready to take the next step, and all that remained left was walking out of Colossus VI as the Intense Champion, something that she knew deep in her mind that David could do. “Evening, Martha,” Andrea said as she walked past the frumpy receptionist who was the first person everyone saw when they walked into the building. As she pushed the door open and walked through the threshold, she accidentally bumped into someone and nearly knocked the person over while various papers dropped on the ground. “I’m so sorry.” Andrea immediately dropped to her knees and began grabbing the various items off the ground and began to hand them back to the woman when she noticed the manicured nails and the lively red-hair. A pair of glasses rested perfectly on her nose and as Andrea stood back up, she realized that it was Mary-Lynn Mayweather she had ran into. “Andrea, it’s so good to see you!” The two hugged, awkward at first, but quickly warmed up as Andrea broke the embrace. “What’re you doing here?!” Andrea’s voice had more excitement in it then she had intended and her eyes shot to the ground. “Here to get Jack’s paycheck. Even though I’m wrestling more nights than not, he seems to think that I’m still his assistant and errand boy, er, woman. But, I still owe him so much for getting me in this business that I guess I’m not really going to complain.” “Jack will figure it out sooner or later.” “I hope so. So how about you? What are you doing here in Chicago?” Andrea shifted slightly as she closed the door and began to walk to her car with Mary-Lynn walking right next to her. David’s younger sister noticed the elegant pantsuit that Mary-Lynn was wearing and it made her smile slightly. “Oh, you know, stuff.” Andrea’s voice tried desperately to hide her true intent. “Listen, you can mention David’s name when you’re around me. It’s not you that hurt me, it was him.” Her voice sounded a little edgy and Andrea made a mental note of it. “Well, here to just talk to Lisa about a contract extension and things along that line. He had a pretty successful ReVolution 200. Plus, I needed to sign the contracts for the change in his title match at Colossus. Not quite sure if David’s all there when it comes to that.” Mary-Lynn nodded her head. She readjusted her glasses, the awkwardness starting to settle back in. “Yeah, not sure what David thinks he’s doing with that. As if Rhine isn’t going to be tough enough.” Andrea was surprised to hear Mary-Lynn had been keeping up with everything David was doing. More so by the fact that she was concerned about it. “Lisa asked me if he was doing okay.” Not so much okay, rather, if he was out of his fucking mind. Lisa wasn’t happy about the abrupt change that hadn’t even been run by her. Andrea liked her totalitarian-like attitude when it came to running PRIME. It was refreshing to see, especially from a female in the position that she was in. Mary-Lynn stood there, silent, with her eyes looking towards the gravelly ground. “How is he doing?” She sounded sincere and Andrea put her hand gently against her shoulder, but Mary-Lynn shied away from her touch. “I’m not sure David is doing too well. Between the pressure he’s putting on himself inside of that ring and then everything with Philadelphia and the family, I think he’s starting to lash out. Maybe even be losing it a little bit.” “I think so too.” Andrea stood there, shocked for a moment, and looked at Mary-Lynn with keen interest that at first she didn’t come right out and explain. After a few moments though, the red-head cleared her throat and looked at Andrea. “Look, I still really care about him. I just can’t get in the middle of this thing with Philadelphia and him. He’s about to become a father and all of that, cool for him. Can’t mess with that much like you can’t mess with global warming. But, I kissed a guy. Out of spite, but I still kissed him. David lost it and decked the guy. Got into a fight with me.” “He’s been getting into fights with everyone.” “I’ve seen him, Andrea, when he’s not around other people. He drinks, a lot, and just seems on the verge of self-destructing. I’m really scared for him.” Andrea’s worst fears were starting to come true and she felt this sinking feeling in her stomach. She reached for her keys and unlocked the doors to her car. “David’s always been an interesting soul, ever since he was little. He doesn’t like to talk to people and he just sort of bottles everything inside of him. It’s his biggest flaw. When he was fifteen, he started to drink. Nothing serious, but it’s only grown in the years since. It takes him away from his problems, but he doesn’t know when to stop.” “He needs help.” “I agree, Mary-Lynn. I just don’t know what to do. But, I think you should come by the house soon, maybe we can figure something out.” Mary-Lynn smiled. “Sounds good.” With the pleasantries exchanged, Andrea got into her car, and sat there for a moment while Mary-Lynn walked away. A fear, a fear that she would lose her brother, took over her and it brought Andrea to tears. *** Rebecca sat alone in her room. Over the past few days, it had felt like it was closing in on here, even though it was the largest in the house. Sitting on a quilted blanket, her tears stained the quilt even though they were invisible. Questions had flittered across her mind, like butterflies in the middle of spring, but had disappeared to the dark recesses of her mind. The tips of her fingernails grated softly against the edge of the quilt. In the nook of the room, stood a man, unknown to her. “Hey.” David’s voice interrupted the quiet moment that Rebecca had decided to have. Her eyes looked over at her son and she felt a well of tears attack the gates, but she managed to hold them back. “Hey.” Her voice was meek, nothing more than a whisper. “Didn’t know you were here.” He nodded in her general direction, unable to muster up a response to that. David’s eyes swayed from side to side like a hammock on a breezy day. Glasses rested on her nightstand, one right next to another, and could tell by the general look of his mother that she hadn’t left the room in days. “So, we should probably talk,” David began. She looked back at the bed. For David, this was uncomfortable. “We don’t have to.” Rebecca was sincere. David knew better though and walked to the edge of the bed. He folded his leg under him as he sat just inches away from her. Yet, those inches felt like miles and he wondered if they would ever be able to fix the chasm that separated them. “I think we need to.” “I can’t believe you’ve held onto that weight for so long. I’ve thought about how you left, and now I know why. I understand the fights you two had. Instead of helping you, I pushed you away, and blamed you for things.” David hadn’t imagined it being this easy. “Dad wasn’t a bad man.” Rebecca scoffed. “Shawn had another family, David.” It was the first time he had ever heard his mom refer to his father by his first name. It sent a chill down his spine. “But, I think he still loved you. I don’t know if he was still involved after I left. Maybe thought that with me gone that he could continue to live that part of his life. I don’t know.” She looked at him and brought her hand behind his ear. “You’re such a sweet child, David. I’ve been so angry at you, that I failed to see the things that you’ve done, the responsibilities that you’ve taken to help this family through everything. It shows a special characteristic to see someone able to handle it with such strength as you have.” David wanted it to end. His skin itched violently and he felt like it never would end. “Mom, stop. This is too much.” He hadn’t felt like this since the tenth grade when Emma Green had told the whole school that she had slept with him and it was the most amazing moment of her life. She wasn’t even sure that it could be topped for the rest of her life. “I was so horrible to you.” “Mom, you weren’t.” “David, just let me speak, please.” He didn’t object. “I just want to say I’m proud of you. I’ve had a lot of time to think these past few days, and I just want to say I’m sorry. I know that I have hurt you these past few months, and I will do everything in my power to fix our relationship.” David stood up, his voice suddenly dry, and his body screaming out in pain. He wanted to say something to make this better, but there was nothing that came to mind. Her arms wrapped around him without warning and he allowed his mother to have that moment of relief. When it was over, he left the room without saying another word. His feet hit the stairs harder than he could imagine. A buzzing noise started to irritate him and he looked around for the source. David walked into the kitchen and opened the refrigerator and pulled out a bottle of vodka. He looked around desperately for a glass within arm’s reach, but found none. Instead of wasting another moment, he unscrewed the cap and threw it onto the ground. The liquid his tongue, a splash of it at first, much like the first sip from a Sprite bottle, but it didn’t stop there. As the minutes went by and David took more and more of the poison into his body, he couldn’t find the strength to stop. He felt everything around him get slower and slower, until he had finished the nearly full bottle and sat down. No one was there with him; he was left all by himself. He pulled open one of the cabinets and found another bottle to drink from. David did just that, this time warm bourbon, and felt it bubble in his throat, much like his own personal bubble bath. A few more minutes went by and that bottle had passed into his system. Things felt even slower, tougher, and more painful. David scratched his arms violently and felt the strips of skin come off of him. Yet, they didn’t. His eyes flew across the room and back to the stove to the right of him. Something wasn’t right, yet it never dawned upon him. “You doing okay, David?” The voice startled him. He dropped the bottle and it crashed, splintered into pieces. The shards of glass laid about him, but no one came for them. He was alone. Yet he wasn’t. He looked around for the source of the voice and found himself looking into a tall mirror that he had never noticed before. It was there that he realized where the voice came from. It was Shawn.
