Gun. Part One.

17 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/17/09 Knoxville, Tennessee The Chevrolet Silverado's large tires came to a stop on the quaint suburban street, headlights already turned off. It's low rumble came to a halt, revealing the eerie silence of the night. The front door swung open, and a heavy steel-toed work boot made contact with the pavement. Moments later a cigarette butt exploded on the ground into a small shower of sparks. Wade Elliott slammed the door shut, steely blue eyes, though hidden in the dark, were certainly staring forward with conviction. He spat, and followed his own heavy, thudding steps down the street. * * * * * * * “Please...please pick up...” Ellis Sherman fidgeted with the phone against his ear, tapping his free hand against the wall of his small home nervously. Finally, a soft, proper voice came through on the other end. “Hello?” “Richard?” Ellis asked quickly, the hope in his voice clear and evident. “Speaking, who is this?” “Ellis,” he replied. “Ellis Sherman.” “Ahh, the master gunsmith. What do I owe the pleasure?” “Richard, please tell me you still have the .357 I sold you.” “Of course, a piece of perfection like this doesn't leave my side. You don't want it back, do you?” “No no,” said Ellis, speaking quickly. “I'm calling to warn you. Someone knows about the Twins.” Silence. “I see. And who would that be?” “I'm not sure, but at the same time, I'm positive.” “Who, Ellis.” Sherman swallowed the lump in his throat. “Does the name 'Wade Elliott' sound familiar?” “I know the name. Tell me, why would Mr. Elliott know about the Twins?” “Because...” Another pause. “...because he has the other one.” Ellis squeezed his eyes closed, sweating beading on his bald head's forehead. An irritated sigh came from the other end. “You told me the other one was hidden away, Ellis.” “I'm sorry, Richard. I didn't want to tell you. Wade came along when I first made the pair. I was able to hide the one I sold you, but he took the first one! Richard, you need to keep yours safe.” “Yes, Ellis, I well know that,” he snapped. “But rest assured, if this Wade fellow comes, he'll find himself lying face down in the dirt. But, so as to hope it doesn't come to that, you need to keep my whereabouts confidential.” “Absolutely.” “Stay in touch, Mr. Sherman.” Ellis set the phone back on its hook, removing his circle bifocals and rubbing his beady black eyes with his thumb and forefinger, taking deep breaths to calm his nerves. He took a deep breath and replaced his glasses and turned... ...to find the last man on the planet he'd want sitting in his kitchen. Ellis froze up. Sitting at the table, one boot resting on his knee, was The Bad Dog himself. He locked. Sherman in that heavy stare, and the gunsmith could do nothing. Not even breath. “Ya know, Ellis,” started Wade after a few moments of very uncomfortable silence. “Might start thinkin' 'bout lockin' yer door. Even Ol' Wade can sneak 'round quiet like when he feels.” Ellis' mouth could only shift open and close slowly as Wade rose from his chair. “Swung by this way 'bout thirteen years ago. Remember that, Ellis?” No answer. “Figured so. We had a bit've a business transaction. I know ya remember that. But if I remember right, you weren't all that truthful with me, Sherman.” Ellis could only try to flatten into the wall, try to mold into it as Wade moved closer. “See, I took that pretty li'l gun ya made. Hell've a gun, Ellis, it really is. But ya didn't tell me it weren't the only one.” Sherman started to stammer, Wade leaning down, snarling visage square in his face. “Ol' Wade don't like bein' lied to, Ellis. But that was a long time ago, an' I'm a fairly reasonable fella, so yer gonna give me the other one.” “I...I...can't...” Ellis stuttered. Wade nodded. “Yer gonna give me the other one...” Wade wrenched Alice, the masterpiece .357 magnum made by the hands of Sherman himself, from the back of his belt and pushed it hard against Sherman's forehead. “Or I'll BLOW YER FUCKIN' HEAD OFF!” “His name is Richard!” came a soft voice. Wade slowly turned his head to the right, the wife of Ellis Sherman standing in the kitchen's entryway, scared and stiff. Sherman's head dropped, mouthing the words “oh no.” “His name is Richard Hildebrant!” she continued. “He bought the second one a few years after you took the first! We don't know where he lives and where he spends his time, he never told us! That's all we know! Please! Please don't hurt Ellis!” Elliott squinted at the frantic wife, then turned to the prone Ellis Sherman, who could only hang his head, the stiffness of fear had left when the name “Richard Hildebrant” fell from his wife's lips. An indication that her words were, indeed, truthful. “Looks to be yer lucky day, Ellis,” said Wade, giving Sherman's forehead a nudge with the gun's barrel before returning it to the back of his belt. He stepped away, leaving the terrified wife and the solemn gunsmith to their own. “Always a pleasure, folks.” He swung the door open and stepped back into the night, the thuds of his boots fading in the distance. After many moments, Ellis turned to his head toward his wife, eyes glistening with failure. “What have you done...” he whispered. * * * * * * * Elsewhere... Richard Hildebrant tapped his soft, delicate finger against his chin, legs crossed in a large, posh leather chair. His massive, ornamented office sat very quiet, yet was screaming with smarm. Marble pillars in each corner, a gold framed mirror on one end of the room, a red elaborate rug over a glistening hardwood floor. He sat in the center at a colossal oak desk, clad in a disgustingly expensive suit, black shiny hair combed to one side, brown eyes staring off with thought. “Wade Elliott.” Richard lifted his fair, gangly frame from his chair, reach under his arm and retrieved a fantastic silver .357 magnum, holding it in front of his face to inspect its perfection. Shadow black handle fading into bright silver. Like thousands of times before, he read the small, eloquent black engraved writing along the barrel. It read: I come from a place whither I desire to return. He looked up, and placed the gun back in its holster. “I'll be waiting.”
