Kindred Spirits? (Part I. Global Title Match)

15 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/15/05 Detective mode. He would never understand American culture. It was several months past Christmas, yet holiday ornaments remained atop the desk. A porcelain figure of Santa held a sleeping baby Jesus. Bundled up in a thick yellow garment, Christianity’s icon looked peaceful in the loving arms of December’s jolly Mr. Capitalism. A homemade Christmas candle lit the neo-Angelic scene with a flickering glow, and shadows danced off Santa’s rosy red cheeks. Less than a candle, it was more like folk art: a tomato jar half filled with red wax, white streaks along the outside. Indeed, the mouth of the jar was stained a chalky black and gray, compliments of a quick five-minute lighting, victim of a candlelight dinner. “Dinner” in the loosest sense of the word, however, with Chinese takeout serving as the main course. But such was life on the road, her soul a perpetual convict of motion, a motion so unyielding that the traveler might not breathe, or in her case, have a quiet meal. Ironically, Emilio had met her at a McDonald’s...in Tokyo. Maya was an ardent political science student at Boston University, majoring in international diplomacy. But she spent this semester abroad, cooped up in a junk apartment near her grandparents. They never made the voyage to America that their daughter, Maya’s mother, did. A conservative Japanese family, they were deeply troubled by her outwardly American habits. And playing off from their fears, Maya fed the flames by landing her summer’s job at Micky D’s. With this in mind, Emilio quietly chuckled to himself as he rummaged through her drawers in tacit amusement. Forest Gump said that you can tell a lot about a man by his shoes; where he’s from, what he’s done. And Sam Keen said that if you empty the contents of a person’s pockets, you can glimpse into their soul. Yet, Emilio, ex agent of Nicaragua’s special forces, stuck to his own philosophy. He indelibly subscribed to the age-old wisdom that you can learn a great deal about a person by going through all their shit. Especially their undergarments. And most importantly, you can see what they have to hide. If anything. For as beautiful as his new object of infatuation was, as pure as her heart seemed, Emilio couldn’t afford to take any chances. Especially in the position he was. Indeed, very recently, Emilio had gone toe to toe with a myriad of the best competitors in the business. And while Jolt’s Triple Crown champion, Brandon Youngblood, never found a way to pin him, and Angelo Deville, a former PTC Global champ, had failed at convincing him to submit to The Soprano twice in a row, and while neither Chris Sloboda or Vampir Nosferatu could break his spirit...Emilio’s languid frame could no longer endure such punishment. Emilio had to reach down deep. His visceral mind told him he needed heeling. So he bought a one-way ticket to Japan, his second home. He drank bottles of the coach wine along the way, and gargled down the small samplers of vodka after as if they were after-dinner mints. Anything to help cope with the pain. You see, the big match was approaching. It had yet to register on his conscious radar, but remained an indelible force under his skin. If it didn’t register in his brain, it was encoded in his DNA. Every cell seemed to be breathing “Global Championship, Global Championship” in an unconscious mantra. He was vexed at the recent happenings at PRIME- the new stables, wild card egos, and the chest-beating of two dozen professional apes- but instead of reviling in self pity and taking the luxury of placing himself on a mental pedestal “above the fray”, he decided to step back and view the world from afar. A soul-search in the Land of the Rising Sun, his indy circuit stomping grounds, seemed a reasonable solution. Because if you couldn’t face yourself without bias in the mirror, you didn’t have the intestinal fortitude to wear PrimeTime Central gold around your waist. However, he soon found that he had fallen into his own conceited trap. He was ambushed by a wave of unsuppressed thought with all the bad memories suddenly rushing back to him. The more he actively searched for the missing answers, the key to his success, the more elusive they became as his soul clamped shut like an oyster after an onslaught from his own logic. He picked at his brains like berries in a briar patch. And more than once, he was pricked. And more than once, he found things he didn’t want to find and buried them deep beneath waves of his conscious ocean, tying them to a stone and tossing them overboard. But the memories would not subside. The failure to make his mark on PTC during the preliminary match, instead falling under the crazy frame of Mal Somers, the way in which he narrowly lost to Angelo Deville, and the umpteen embarrassment at the hands of Vampir’s pack of wild dogs....they held water in his mind. They refused to drown. So finally, Emilio was forced into submission. He began to quit thinking critically and instead indulged in simple pleasures. He stayed at a familiar hotel, drank Saki at a familiar bar, and relived battles once fought at the same underground venues he toured as a fighting adolescent. The more you know, so the cliche went, the more you realize you don’t know. But the more Emilio learned he didn’t know, the less he cared. For all he had to know in his heart of hearts was that he was good enough to compete for the biggest prize in the game and strong enough to proudly represent one of the greatest federations in the sport. So after forty-eight hours of mental misdemeanors, he began to enjoy his vacation. Emilio felt rejuvenated as he stood out of his seat at the old indy league arena, whooping in excitement as one competitor mounted the other, mauling his opponent’s face like a rancorous mother bear in the defense of her cubs. At this, Emilio felt the fire in his belly rise. Inspiration lifted his heavy heart until it almost begged to be swallowed back into his chest. Emilio Rage, the PRIMEal Rage Legend, would return to PTC as if he were the natural heir to the Global Title throne, and to PRIME in similar fashion. The date was set, and he’d be ready. ...But then of course, fate wouldn’t have it. No, it wouldn’t be that simple for a man who faced an entourage of controversial choices at every major step of his life. So on the evening before the morning of his flight, he met her. And everything changed. The burden of proof. So there he was, restlessly shifting through her personals without so much as a second thought. He picked through her belongings as if he had a vendetta, as if her attraction were but a small, unearned flicker of his potential respect for her. Yes, she needed to prove herself. He ravaged through drawers and cabinets almost as if he wanted an excuse to tell her, sadly, “no.” But apparently, Maya Hananoki had nothing to hide. Nothing besides boredom. Nothing besides the daily gloom of plainness and relentless predictability. She must have felt attacked by her sourly uneventful schedule, an “X” marked on every day of the same boring calendar with a picture of a different breed of dog for each month. Coffee stained photographs, crumpled romance novels, and newspaper clippings of her cousin Jed, the baseball player, lay strewn about. Her personals were nothing more than the good ol’ ingredients that made up American pie. The bland 50's picket fence kind. What Emilio found fascinating, however, was the shape in which she left her otherwise unamusing apartment. If it wasn’t for the fact that her uncleanliness reared its ugly head across every inch of her bachelorette’s pad, he’d call her a “closet” slob. But the war zone extended far beyond even the shadow of the closet. Dirty clothes piled over a broken television set, a stack of old magazines collected dust on a three-legged chair: awkward feng shui for an Asian beauty. Emilio loved how the lone red drape blocked out the golden rays of the sun...except where there was a small slit in center. And the other drape covering the dusty window, tattered, hung slanted toward the fish tank on the desk, half dragging on the floor. All the while the gray kitten, perpetrator of the insidious structural damage, lay curled up on a brown yard sale-caliber couch, which was laden with hair-balls. “Spike” had ignored Emilio the whole time. And best of all, the murky aquarium with belly-up fish serenely lining the surface, seemed to add the defining subtle touch that read, convincingly, “slob.” Classic. Just the type of person -judging by her environment- that Emilio could see himself spending some time with. A lot of time. Finally, as Emilio rummaged through a box of old jewelry, he pulled out an old gold necklace with a porcelain figure of a unicorn. He set back, resting against the mundane brown-colored couch, and grasped the pendant in his hand. Emilio didn’t know Maya liked unicorns. He did too. So he closed his eyes and began to consider how he felt sometimes as if his own life were a myth, and began to consider the week’s events leading up to his meeting with this young lady... The Meeting The Hispanic man pondered what Mal Somers’ head would look like as a trophy head on his cabin wall back in Nicaragua, or a stuffed Nick Worrel on his sofa, or a Latch Key Kid rug, as he roamed through the streets of Tokyo, desperately looking for a place to eat. Emilio was in desperate need of chow. (Not be confused with “ciao, “ especially not in Asia, dummy.) The restaurant beneath the Americanized hotel that PRIME set Emilio up with just didn’t touch the spot. Emilio, needless to say, had disappointed thoughts about Hin See and the front office as he threw on a company sweater and hit the busy streets of Tokyo. As he aimlessly wondered Tokyo in search of grub, it soon dawned on him: If an aerial shot had been taken, one could have played “Where’s Waldo: A Dark Hispanic in White Japan.” But he would have been too easy to spot. So ashamedly, he eventually dragged himself out of the inner-city, no longer wanting to look like the near-seven-foot, dark-skinned orphan in search for his mother. Even in quiet neighborhoods, after too long, the buzzing crowds approached him from all sides. The major disadvantage of being a professional wrestler instead of the indy-hopper that he one was, was that now it wasn’t just fans of the “sport” that made their way up to him. Even in shy Japan. Oh, no. Now it was everyday folks with little or no regard for the sport, but a longing desire to be in the bless-ed company of a celebrity nonetheless. Unable to shrug them off, Emilio almost gave up and decided to head back to his hotel in defeat. But on a whim, he turned down a side street... When he had lived in Japan, he had always felt more comfortable in the suburbs. The backwater retreats of the world’s second largest city were full of intrigue, and above all else, housed no Westerners. The tourist attractions, business communities, and historic sites were well out of sight, and out of mind. Here, people spoke Japanese, Emilio’s second language. Here, you could witness real Japanese culture where the people were generally light-hearted and soft spoken, not with the bustling buzz of the soft-market, consumer electronics culture they were labeled with. But most importantly, the suburban Japanese kept to themselves. That is, except for this one girl. By seven-thirty in the evening, having ate nothing sense morning, as you can imagine, a double-cheeseburger didn’t sound too bad. So with melancholiness strides Emilio grudgingly stepped through the doors of the McDonald’s. And store directly into the eyes of a beauty. Watching her every action as she fidgeted with the buttons on the register -obviously a newer employee- Emilio’s heart began to glow. She peered up into his eyes, her long dark hair pulled back in a tightly groomed bun, and she blushed. The rosy red of her cheeks forced a shiver down Emilio’s spine, permeating his soul with school-boy passion. But when she spoke, Emilio’s mouth dropped. “That’ll be $3.79.” Had Emilio come all this way to fall head over heels for a woman who’s culture he’d just attempted to escape? Her accent could only have been one dialect of English. “American?” Emilio asked, letting out a slight sigh. She didn’t respond. “I’m making an informed assumption,” he added without much thought. “There’s no such a thing as an ‘informed assumption’,” she replied with a roll of her eyes, tossing him a pair of hot cheeseburgers. Emilio caught them in surprise and held them to his chest. “And besides,” she added, pouring salt to the wound with the cliche, “ ‘assume’ makes an ass out of you and me.”. Outwardly, Emilio forced a smile., but on the inside he was slightly offended. “Sassy, free-spirited, and stubborn?” he wanted to ask. Who knows what she would have answered. Needless to say, a shake, a large fry, and several flexes of the biceps later, Emilio ended up in possession of her keys with swift instructions to guard her apartment until her shift ended... Score. Direction...Anyone? So there he was now, standing erect in the center of Maya’s livingroom, having turned over every chair, table, and household decoration in search of something to convince himself not to love her. Or, maybe it was just an unconscious guise of action to quell his lust, his yearning to learn every inch of her. To know her. Either way, later, when she returned home, the fling was finalized. She lured him in with her incantations of good faith, and Emilio was hooked. She wrapped her tender arms around his large, beefy frame and bent him down so that he was at the same height as her, and their eyes met. Maya’s warming embrace seemed to feed and replenish every aching bone, every open sore, every tired cell in Emilio’s body, and filled him with joy. He pulled back after a tender kiss and looked at her in wonder. Her fain smile and dark eyes became permanently etched in his soul as his eternal sanctum, a place to wander back to when he was in need. A loving home forever. Something that, until this very moment, Emilio had never even dreamed of. “Well hone,” she asked while pulling back on his arms, bending her chest back so that it rose in his face, “are we...‘we’, or what?” “Yes dear,” Emilio replied as his fingers went numb, “yes dear, we’re we. “Well good,” she said, smiling, “because I think we should spend a night on the town.” Adding, sharply, “...You’re flight can wait until tomorrow night.” And with that, she reached out and planted him with another kiss on the cheek. “Yes, yes it can,” he thought as he mind went shot. So he answered, plainly, “Uh...okay.” That evening, as the floodgates to his heart opened, and Cupid’s spate rushed through his body, piercing his nerve endings, Emilio knew he was in for a long, long ride. Whether or not their lives were congruent enough to allow such a sporadic co-existence in love, was beyond his concern at the time. For at that moment, he had no mode of contemplation. His mind was intertwined with the beating of her heart. All he could do was feel. Soon, the question would be forced upon him. But for now, she was coming back to America.
