Joshua Taro Freedom, A victim?

26 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26 Nov “Why?” Joshua said more loudly then he meant. Several other patrons turned their heads and looked over at Joshua and the women he was talking to. “Because no American wants too hear how much you hate them.” She said for the fifth time trying to hide the frustration in her voice but failing. “God,” Came Joshua, he too growing frustrated with the circler conversation. “You Yanks, have you never thought that maybe it isn’t my problem. America the riches most powerful nation in the world! That’s all I ever hear, yet eighty percent of you have never left the country. Half the world hates you, the other puts up with you because you pay them too. By the way, your country is a couple trillion dollars in debt, but hey, who’s counting.” Joshua used the pause in the conversion to call for a third refill of his coffee. They had been sat going over the same point for three hours now, and both of them looked tired of it. “Let me just say this.” Joshua continued. “If I’m a voice of hate, why is it only Americans who loath me? I was in Japan just over a month ago. They loved me. Sure I’m Japanese, but not every American cheers for every American. I was mobbed everywhere. I’ve been to Italy, Germany, Spain, England, France, Mexico, and China. I can’t say they all loved me, but they didn’t all hate me.” Joshua stared across at her and waited. She seemed to be pondering his argument respectfully which Joshua knew not everyone would have done. “Okay, that’s fair. However, you’ve been publicly showing dislike in America for many years. Can you say the same about those countries?” She seemed pleased with her counter point and it was clear to Joshua she knew the answer already. “Of course not. I don’t have to live in those places. I take it you’ve done a lot of research, so you’ll know it was the fans that turned on me, before I them.” “I was aware.” She replied simple. “But I and my problems are a tiny, practically insignificant piece of a much greater puzzle. World politics while a large issue isn’t the reason you came here I’m sure. Seeing as we’ve already spent a number of hours talking, why don’t we cut to the chase? I believe that’s the saying and get to the point.” Joshua waited drinking the fresh coffee that had arrived a minute or so ago. “Here’s the thing Mr Joshua Taro Freedom. Politics and Social issues are linked to everything. You’ve spent almost five years in the America Wrestling World. Being Japanese can’t have made it easier. You, yourself say as much. The past three hours we’ve been talking say as much. What I want to know is this. Is Joshua Taro Freedom the mad monster people say? Or was the opinion on him already made before he even started.” Joshua stared at her. He was a little taken back by the reason she had sort this long, in depth chat. She however didn’t wait and continued onwards. “See, I’ve interviewed overseas actors, musicians, artist, postmen, and policeman. I was almost ready to rap things up when your name popped up.” Joshua gazed at her and briefly allowed panic to take over him. Had she heard stories and rumours about those sixteen days of drug, alcohol and sex parties? However she continued without showing any indication she had seen this reaction in Joshua. “Joshua Taro Freedom, that Japanese warrior was heading back to where it all began. Looking to not only repeat feats deemed at the time impossible, but to sore further and become the undisputed best.” She looked up into Joshua eyes in time to catch the fading relief then the uncontrolled chuckle Joshua allowed himself. “Maybe some people had me right. I must be a little crazy for trying.” She too allowed herself a chuckle. “Maybe?” She echoed. “Didn’t you leave because you where over looked for a Title shot and felt greatly undervalued?” This piece of information placed the cherry on the impressed cake. The number of people who knew Joshua had been told he was getting a Prime World Title shot in his first run numbered just three. How she knew Joshua didn’t care, the fact she knew was “Impressive” Joshua said aloud before he could stop himself. “Thank you, I do take pride in my work.” “The Title Shot was the straw that broke the camels back yes. I’d had been lied to a number of times and just spent the last six months in matches I shouldn’t have been in. I wasn’t a tag team wrestler but found myself holding the titles through mismanagement. Losing out on the biggest prize and my life’s dream was just, too much.” “You think race had anything to do with it?” She asked an eager glee in her eyes. “Ha.” Joshua laughed in a whisper. “Racism in wrestling, only since the drawn of the sport. However you start making the nice white owners enough money and you can be green and purple for all they would care. It just a lot harder to get in a position to try for those not of Caucasian descent. However it took a year, but I finally got noticed. I’d spent most of that time flirting with death, and had probably been given more pints of blood then recommend but, finally I was their. “What happened?” She asked seeming generally interested. “The usually thing.” Joshua said offhand but continued to explain. “I was told soon, and then a couple old faces returned. Of course the money men pushed them to the forefront. Old Stars brought in old fans. Still I was told soon, really soon. Then when I felt I’d missed my chance as can happen. Which I would have accepted a hell of a lot easier. I got told however, point blank, in a month. You’re headlining the next PPV. You can imagine the reaction, I told my father and we kept it to ourselves. Then everything went bad, The Tag Titles were lost. My head was of course elsewhere. Two days later I get faxed the Title Match contract. I had it in paper; I signed it and sent it back. I figure at this point everything’s fine. Only for a week to go by and the PPV card to be announced. “What happened?” “I wasn’t on the show. Anywhere. I went from Headline to after thought in a week. I was upset; my father was asking me what happen. I was calling the office hourly but no one would say why.” “What did you do?” She asked keen to hear the story in full. “I did what I always do. I made someone find out. Prime is more then just a couple dozen wrestlers. A lot of staff are required to run this company. Sometimes they hear things, and information can travel. It was about three weeks later when I found out. A change in policy was ordered. They wanted a friendly American face on their Posters. The country was not in the best shape, and they felt a hero was needed. I wasn’t the all American hero, so I was tossed aside.” Joshua still looked hurt by this outcome. Many years had passed but it seemed the pain was ever present. “So.” She asked, filled with curiosity. “Why return, why come back?” “I’ve not found the success I crave outside of Prime. I’ve won many matches, made more money but something was always lacking. I felt let down by the arenas I walked into. Felt the competition was softer, it felt easier but less rewarding. I took little pleasure from my time away from Prime and made the choice to relight my own passion by returning to were for me, it began.” “Has anything changed with how you are treated?” “I’m not sure yet. I’ve only wrestled once, but that was in a huge tournament. So I was given a chance. Perhaps it was a little early in my return but a chance none the less.” “You lost that match, correct?” “I did, I wasn’t expected to win, but I believe I showed a lot of people more then they expected. I’ll get better; I’ve never failed to go unnoticed.” A smirk crept its way onto Joshua face. “Though not everyone agrees with the way I conduct myself.” “So a man calling himself Codemaster is next inline for you. Perhaps delivering a comeback victory.” “Really, I hadn’t heard. I wish I could tell you a host of things about him. Wish I could honestly sit here and count the many ways I am his better. Yet I can’t. I’ve been to focused on getting my own life and career on track. So focused on my own world I have not yet scouted the roster I find myself a member of. When the match comes I’ll be prepared, I’ll be in the best shape I can. I’ve never doubted myself much. I’ve had moments but those are fast become a piece of Freedom history. So we’ll see.” “Joshua.” She said standing up preparing to leave. “I want to thank you for the chat. I enjoyed it and hope one day to do it again.” She smiled at him affectionately. “Good luck with the match Joshua. I’ll send you a copy of the story.” Then she walked away, leaving Joshua to call for a fourth and final refill.
