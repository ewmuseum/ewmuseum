I ain't thinkin' about any of that. But I am. It's right in the back of my mind. Everything I got, it needs to be focused here...but...but...' 'Look, okay...it will take care of itself. 'Yeah...I'm glad you're back.' *** Ashes to ashes. Dust to dust. He never cried. Not a drop. In his mind, he could not. He hid his eyes behind the frames of Oakley sunglasses. That way, nobody would be able to look into his eyes and see the overwhelming sadness. This way, all he could do was react to what was going on around him. His younger sister, Lindsay in a beautiful skirt with a black sweater over top, in black pumps with nylons, her hair done up in a conservative bun. The polar opposite of what she would do on any other day. She tried to keep her emotions bottled up. He had other things to worry about. Texas High School football to be exact. Tommy looked a lot like his brother, and seemed to be built just as strong. As a two way player at fullback and middle linebacker, Tommy was all the rage. The current number two national rated (by most scouting magazines, anyway) middle linebacker prospect in the entire country. He drew comparisons to Ray Lewis because he was someone who was just so emotional on the field. All the top programs had been seeking him, from Tennessee, to USC, to Florida State and Miami. But he had given a verbal agreement already. He was going to be a Texas Longhorn. On Friday, he picked off a pass late in the forth quarter of a game against a rival and took it back for a touchdown, securing the win and keeping the team’s undefeated record intact. After the game, Tommy broke down and cried, dedicating the game to his father. 'I felt him watching me up from above, telling me that I could do it! This one's for you Dad!' He was in a suit, his long dirty blonde hair somewhat unkempt. He'd taken a vow with his fellow linebackers to not get a haircut until they had won State. The playoffs were right on the horizon. Candace was the older one, holding hands with her husband. She was in a long black skirt with a black sweater, her hair tied in a ponytail. She could not help be cry during the entire proceeding. His wife was in his other hand, dressed in a pair of black dress pants and a black shirt, her hair cut short and her glasses on her nose. She did not know how to react, so she remained stoic with her husband. The eulogy was given by Jeff Bunting, an friend to Ben from the Smith-Barney days. His story was that he quit when he saw what was happening to his friend, and helped him Benjamin get back on his feet by getting him a job in a fishing shop. Jeff spun tales of the good times, of the dedication his friend had to what he was doing, trying to crack as many jokes as he possibly could. But even her broke down up there. 'I...I can't believe you're gone.' He said, then looking at the family. 'I'm sorry. The world has lost a great man, and none of us can even possibly understand what you're going through. You've lost a father...a husband...a friend.' That was enough to get Tommy to cry. But Brandon could not. He could not let himself. He had to remain strong. The priest continued on his diatribe, something of which Brandon did not pay attention to. He was not a man of faith. But he did not deny his family this. It was only right to respect their beliefs and pay tribute to the man who had raised him in the way he chose. It was agonizing. But when it was finished, the crowded funeral home filed out, getting into their vehicles. The pallbearers took the casket. Brandon. Tommy. Jeff Bunting. And Benjamin's brother Chris. The drive to the cemetery was odd. Different for everyone close. The realization had finally sunk in. The rest was a complete blur for Brandon. He was laid to rest, the family paying their last respects. And then, it was on to the Youngblood home. It was time to eat. To drink to the memory of a man, while flawed, was no worse than anyone else. Just like all of us, he could only admit to his gravest mistakes when forced. Maybe it is something about the specter of death that allows us to let go. To let us admit to what we did and what we should have done better. Or maybe it is simply understanding that there is only so much time left before you can not try and do something about the past. It was hours. Brandon could not leave. Instead, he stood there for a good long while. He owed it to the man. Maybe this was his penance for what took place all those years ago. Maybe, in that mind of his, he was having a discussion with his father from afar. Melissa came back for him, walking through the grass as Brandon stood there, the wind kicking the flaps of his suit and tie around. She drew close to him, standing there for several minutes before speaking. 'How are you doing?' 'Alright.' 'Patch things up with the family?' 'Yeah. Shocking that it took this to get us back together. It's kinda weird Missy...for so long I wanted this. I wanted it because he disowned me. But after all this time, after all this bottled up hatred...I'm just happy he is in peace now.' 'It's interesting how that can happen.' 'Yeah...I guess. It's just a realization that maybe if people weren't so damn pigheaded, they could get along instead of butting heads. We went at each other a lot then. But I understand now. I understand why he did it. And it's something I can respect.' He paused. 'There's something I've been meaning to say. All this...all this training...all this working out...it was because I was afraid. I know I said it was about getting better, and it was all about wrestling, but that's a lie. What happened...what happened with Vetra...I'm not strong enough to go through that again. I'm not strong enough to lose you again. I know it's sappy as shit, but I rather be filled with concrete...I'd rather be dead inside then let you get hurt because of my actions ever again.' 'You can't always be afraid Brandon. If you were, life wouldn't be worth living.' 'I don't need to dedicate my entire like to wrestling anymore. If it's what you want...I'll do it...but if not...I won't.' 'It's not the job that has me worried. It was the obsession. It was always the obsession.' A moment of silence came. Melissa broke it. 'You know...I didn't get a chance to say this to you before...' Melissa trailed off. She gripped his arm tightly, before forcing her emotions out, a tear falling down from her left eye. '...But you remind me of someone I once fell for.' 'Is that right?' Brandon responded. 'Three weeks of absolutely hell...when did you change?' Brandon looked at her, pulling his glasses up. He wanted to look at her, eye to eye. 'When I realized what it took to be a man.' *** Showtime. The City of Angels. Finally...a break from the constant struggle that has become the Youngblood way the past couple days. It's a welcome change of pace, one where all the frustration I hold for what happened can come out in a fit of violence. It is not just for me. The family will be in Staples tonight. Ironic that on this night, we're all going to get a chance to punch God in the mouth and make him sorry he put us through this. We drove from the hotel on over. I dressed for the occasion; baggy slightly worn and faded blue jean shorts falling below the knees (just seem more comfortable), a pair of white K-Swiss shoes (pure white, if there is a mark on them they just don't look right), USC jersey with the number eleven on it (Leinart is a man amongst boys), a USC fitted hat turned backwards on my head, and my trademark Oakley’s. I had my iPod blasting Jay-Z in my ear as Melissa and I made our way into the arena. Los Angeles. My home away from home, at least in the wrestling world. I plied my trade in independents around here. We worked the Grand Olympic Auditorium, which was a place that could only hold about seven thousand people. It is a far cry from the arenas I work today, but the atmosphere in that dingy place was something that most of these arenas lack. Staples has an aura, yes, but compared to The Great Western Forum, it's nothing. The Forum might as well have been the Madison Square of the west. It's like the old arena in Chicago, the name passes me by, that was replaced by the United Center. There is a certain appeal that a building has had special things happen inside it that draws me. To be in a building that champions have been crowned in. However, there is also an appeal to going out and creating history. The Staples Center does not have a tremendous amount of wrestling history, only a handful of events have taken place here. But the Lakers won championships here. And going into a Universal Championship match with Hoyt Williams? I feel more than confident that by the end of the night, the big belt I've been working so hard to get will be in my hands. The thing with training constantly is that you are in tremendous shape. A byproduct is a feeling of fatigue after the match. I trained to failure often, and it was to make sure that I could keep myself going even if my body wanted to quit. However, it takes a lot out of a person to do that. Doing all those reps and doing all that hard work caused me to be sore going into a match. It was a sore I could overlook with my pain tolerance. But it was the fatigue. It was something that a great athlete knows not to do, yet I did so anyway. After all, no pain, no gain, right? Wrong. I busted my ass and sweated bullets, constantly trying to maintain my weight as the workouts I did shed pounds like nothing. I was screwing with my body big time, putting it through hell and demanding so much from it. In this couple months span, I've probably put on more than three years of wear and tear just from my workout regimen alone. Coming into King Of Kings, I did not feel that way. I felt extremely fresh, somewhat rested even. The match with Vetra played more like a simulated contest, used to loosen up the muscles and work yourself into top shape. And I'd be lying if I did not think that I was in the best shape and in the best mindset I had ever been in. I just felt...different. Maybe losing him was working in my psyche. Maybe it was the feeling that I had to impress my family. And who knows, I might have a large contingent of fans here from my independent days, wanting to see me take the next step. All I knew was that there was an ominous feeling in the air. Maybe the winds of hope. Maybe the winds of change. Who knows. Going through the locker room quickly, I sit down, talking with my wife. Then, it's focus time. Time to get yourself psyched up. This is what I wanted to do when I came into wrestling. I wanted to wrestle the big matches, to be in main events and wrestling in the high pressure situations. Thinking about Hoyt, I'm trying to run down what I know about him. About his style. He's strong. Has huge holes in his technique. Exploitable if you can take a pounding. Next to no technical ability. No ability to fly. Brawler. Limited move wise but what he has can and will hurt you. He overwhelms opponents with his strength. Next to no true stamina. If a match goes past ten minutes he struggles. He's not what I would consider being in 'gym' shape. Hell, even the fact that he has long hair and that scruffy beard plays into the thinking because it gives me a lot to grab at. It's why I shaved my head. People grabbing me by the hair was frustrating and it hurt. I just remember back to Randalls in the Circle, grabbing him by the beard. I have to do that to Hoyt. I have to fight through whatever Hoyt can dish. If I can, then he is mine. Melissa changes from her casual clothes to a beautiful white sequin evening gown with stilettos. She looks like an absolute goddess, and having her back in my corner and actually into it is something I have going for me. She can be a real cerebral bitch when she needs to be. And in the heat of battle, anything she sees can give me an advantage. It is like having a coach on the sidelines, looking at you and telling you what you need to do to make a crucial adjustment. In wrestling, that is something that we do not have the luxury of having. But you got a smart manager, and it works beautifully. And Duke Williams, even with what he has done in wrestling, is not exactly that sharpest knife in the kitchen. I unpack my singlet for the night. The usual order, since my other white one got stained horribly with blood. But the white boots, those are special. The white boots are undefeated in title matches. Jeff Garvin and Nova. Both times, I walked out of the building as a champion. And when it came to mojo, I could use any bit that I could get. I grab into my bag, grabbing my white athletic tape. I wrap it around my wrists, making sure that I get it around before tearing it. Melissa hands me a black marker. On each wrist, I write something simple: BFY. For him. I grab my black mouth piece, which is something I am going to start using. Seeing Kurt Angel use it was something I thought was smart, especially when it comes to getting your head or jaw jammed. The damage in moves such as jawbreakers and such are somewhat lessened with this. I walk out, into a long corridor. Get myself stretched out, do some quick power sprints. Get a good sweat going before the big contest. And then, it is truly show time. Universal Championship on the line. A new Jewel In The Crown has been made. And that person is the next number one contender. I lose tonight, and another chance might never come. Melissa comes with me, handing me a bottle of water, which I slug down as quickly as possible. In the gorilla position, I think about the journey here. Would I have changed it? Sure, but wouldn't we all? The fact is, you change something, and you aren't who you are today. You're completely different. The mistakes in life, they are made for a reason. You just got to learn to roll with it. Took me this long to realize it. This is it. The crescendo. And I don't know about you...but I live for this. *** You know, all things considered, I shouldn't be here. I shouldn't be standing here on the gorilla position, waiting to burst through those curtains here in the Staples Center. Right now, I should be training at some remote US Olympic Team outpost, gearing myself for a Gold Medal in Beijing. Right now, I should be getting inducted into the Amateur Wrestling Hall of Fame. But life doesn't work that way. Life rarely works out how you plan it. But one thing I do know, is that no matter what anyone says about karma or destiny, none of that matters. Not a single bit of it. I don't believe in karma. I don't believe in fate and I sure as hell do not believe that cosmic forces are at work. I got to this point all on my own. I did not need help. I did not need some higher power, some God to get to this stage of my career. But, you know, there is oh so much that I've been forced to realize. The cycle. It's always been about the cycle. It's how things are coming around, and right now I have blown right past Vetra and am at the spot I was just before. Inches from the Universal Championship. But after the Dual Halo, after Vetra took Melissa, something snapped inside. I was lying about training my ass off to better my career. I trained, became as strong and as much of a machine as I could to make sure that I would never have to be afraid of losing her. I went cold and dead inside, just to make sure I would not have to feel the sinking guilt I did when Vetra took her from me. I cried, I was upset and angry and sad and hopeless. Before then, I was a hardcore alcoholic. I had a hole to fill inside. I had no family, in my mind. I had no friends, in my mind. I just felt empty. Vetra took Melissa from me, and those sorts of feelings, the ones I had to drink into submission, came back. I did not ever want to deal with it again. I never wanted to feel like that again. Oh, don't get me wrong. I'm not suddenly a good guy. I'm not an upstanding man. Frankly, I'm an asshole. I'm one of the sickest people this sport has ever seen. I will never not be that person. But at least I am not lying to myself. I'm not holding onto some ideas of grandeur which I can never attain. Brandon Youngblood is who he is. It's just that sometimes, a person stands back and realizes what they are wrong about. I've come to terms with the family issue. Took my father to his death bed too, but I finally got what I had been seeking from him for years. I realized that there was a place for me in the family, I just pushed them away. I realized that I lack true strength. I'm a sell out. I have a wife, who I love very much. Yeah, chokes you up, don't it? It's an albatross around my neck, something that someone can exploit. At least, that's how I used to think. Now? I frankly don't care if she is something that others can exploit. I don't even think about it now. Jeff Garvin dared to touch my wife. You know what happened to him? He received the beating of his lifetime. And then he tapped out. For the first time in his career. For the only time in his career. Jeff Garvin can never show his face in the same arena as me again without a certain fear encompassing him. Peter Vetra? Yeah, it hurt. It hurt a lot. And I did not snuff him out immediately. But look at Petey now. From the sounds of things, the punk is hurt pretty bad. Each of these times, every single damn one of them, it happened because I slipped up. Tried playing mind games. I don't play mind games anymore. I just go out there and beat the holy hell out of whoever I want to, whenever I want to. Things have changed. I don't have to be afraid of losing her, because if I do, she knows that the person avenging her is going to more violent, more insane than anything the violator has ever seen.
