Date: Chapter 3 of "Five Years in the Making" Douglas International Airport Charlotte, NC Three days ago... For an airport in his home state that happened to share his last name, this sure was a place filled with bad memories for Troy Douglas. He'd flown to and form Douglas Airport countless times over the years, but there were always going to be two flights Troy would always associate this place with. One was five years ago, on the worst day of his life. A flight where he'd written the eulogies for the two most important people in his life. A flight where he'd been forced to come to terms with a harsh reality. There weren't going to be any more pancake breakfasts on Saturday morning with his dad, and he wasn't going to have Lauren next to him to provide comfort when it was 3 a.m. and he felt like his back was going to rip apart. The other landed ten minutes ago, this one leaving the funeral of the man who'd given him a second chance in life. Two flights. Two funerals. One coming. One going. And he would have given anything for none of it to have happened at all. ***************** Five years earlier... The ride from Charlotte to Greensboro was silent and somber. Alan had picked him up at the airport, but aside from a conciliatory hug and a few words of greeting, neither said a word. Mostly, Troy just stared out at the road, watching the asphalt below him race by, thinking about what he'd written while he was 30,000 feet above the Atlantic Ocean. For two days, until he was finally sitting in the front row at the funeral parlor, those words were pretty much all he thought about. Thinking about what he was going to say seemed to be the only way to avoid thinking about what had actually happened. If he could conjure up all those good memories, maybe he could somehow push out the searing heat in the back of his brain that had been building ever since the phone call three nights before. But, as hard as he tried, he was failing spectacularly. Lauren's funeral was first. His father's would be two days later. Troy wouldn't pay attention to much during either, listening just enough to know to rise when he was called to speak. When the time finally came, he walked slowly to the podium, trying with each step not to completely lose his composure. He turned to face the audience, and reached into his pocket. But, instead of grabbing his notes, his left hand clasped around a small piece of metal in his pocket. At that point, he didn't need any notes. "I, uh, obviously this is something I'd never imagined doing. When you lose the person who means absolutely everything to you, a lot of things go through your mind." That was an understatement. "I don't think we need to talk about what happened. Not now. There's time for that later. What we've -- what I've got to mourn is not how this happened, but the simple fact that the world is a worse place without Lauren in it. Something I knew from the time we were kids..." ... ... They were seven. The Bennetts had moved in directly across the street a week before, and Jack Douglas had kept up with traditional neighborly custom by inviting them over for dinner. And while the adults were busy playing cards in the living room, Troy was on the swingset in the backyard, swinging back and forth in the balmy Carolina evening next to this strange new girl he'd just met. If only he'd known then. Considering he was still in that awkward "girls are icky and have cooties" phase commonly known as elementary school, Troy wouldn't normally spend all that much time talking to a new girl, let alone feel so comfortable around her. But, there was something about this particular girl that changed things. Something in her smile. Something in the way she laughed. Something in her eyes. Talking to her was easy. "So, where did you move from?" "Michigan. My dad got a new job down here."" "You like it here so far?" She waited a moment, her eyes glossing over with the beginnings of tears. "It's nice ... but ..." "But what?" "I miss my friends, and now they're all so far away!" Now it was Troy's turn to wait and think. Even at seven years old, he knew what he was about to say was important, although he didn't quite understand the magnitude until years later. "Well ... I - I'll be your friend." "Really?" "Yeah! Dad's been at the school like, forever, so I'm not going anywhere. I'll always be right here," he said, slapping the metal pole on the swingset to emphasize his point. Of course, when he did that, he completely lost his balanced and tumbled backwards off the swing, taking a spectacular fall onto the grass. When he opened his eyes and spit out a mouthful of turf and grass, the first image he saw was light brown hair hanging down in front of him, framing a smiling, freckled face. And those eyes. You could describe them in any hacky, grocery store checkout line romance novel terms you wanted, because even to a seven year old Troy Douglas, he'd just seen his personal version of perfection. There was something deep within those brown eyes that made a kid who'd just made himself look like a complete idiot ten seconds ago feel completely at ease. Something there made him felt at home. And for 21 years, he relished that feeling for every single day. ... ... "She was a light on everything she ever came near, and I don't think you'll meet a single person that met her who didn't love her. I know I did. Lauren, I miss you, I love you, and there's not going to be a second that goes by for the rest of my life when I don't remember that." As he walked away from the podium, he opened up his hand to look at what he'd pulled out of his pocket earlier. A small gold band. The wedding ring he would've put on his finger permanently just three months later. He'd put that in his pocket earlier in the day, not really knowing why. When he looked at the engraving on the inside, it became instantly clear. T- I'll always be right here. -L ***************** Three days ago... The ring was still in his pocket to this day. It sounds sentimental, but he'd never put it on his finger. He'd just carried it with him as a symbol, a representation that the engraving on the band really did mean something. It didn't make him feel any better about the death of Jason Reed. The day he'd gotten that phone call, he wasn't sure what to think. Ever since Jason had told him about his ALS, Troy knew that one day he'd probably have to be at his mentor's funeral. But, in nearly two months of phone conversations, e-mails and text messages with the man who trained him to wrestle, he'd never heard anything but a positive lookout. The medication was working, the physical therapy was helping him make progress and he was still healthy enough to get in the ring and teach aspiring wrestlers a thing or two about working on the mat. Of course, no amount of medication, no amount of physical therapy, no medical miracle known to man or God was going to stop the brakes from going out on that city bus and crashing into the driver's side door of Jason's Toyota. If he could've run from Atlanta to Philadelphia, that's what he would have done. Less than an hour after the final ReVolution before Colossus, he was on a plane back up north. And now, just days away from the biggest match of his PRIME career, on the biggest show on the wrestling character, he had to deal with the fact that the man directly responsible for Troy ever going anywher near a wrestling ring had his ashes sprinkled over the Atlantic Ocean just 18 hours previously. It wasn't something he'd ever really imagined. He had no desire to try and recall the funeral right now. Whereas five years ago, two deaths had made him want to quit wrestling forever, this passing made him want to do the exact opposite. Mainly because he was pretty sure that if Jay was still here, he'd kick Troy's ass if he didn't stop moping over his own death. But, sitting outside this particular airport waiting for a cab and looking down at the one ring he did keep on his hand, Troy couldn't help but remember the one other funeral that brought him to Douglas Airport. ***************** Five years ago... It was the ring that triggered him again. Two days earlier, a ring in his pocket did the job. Now, standing in front of a crowd at his father's funeral, it was the ring on his finger. The ring that he knew meant more to his father than anything he'd ever done professionally. 1994 North Carolina State High School Basketball Champions. He didn't need notes this time, either. "Anyone who knew Dad knew he could be a little bit obsessive about things. About teaching, about reading the Sunday paper cover-to-cover without any interruption, and about winning State. He knew when he had a team with a chance, and when he had won, he was going to ride it as far as he could." ... ... He was seventeen. And for a seventeen year old kid in North Carolina, playing in the state championship game at Duke's Cameron Indoor Stadium should have been as good as it gets. Although you might not be thinking that when you're in a timeout, down a point with seven seconds to play in overtime. It had been a long, grueling game, but the East Lake Knights just couldn't seem to pull ahead of Raleigh Central Catholic. Every shot was contested, every passing lane was occupied, every set play was disrupted by brutal, physical defense. But, by bullheaded determination and Kenny Morgan's sharp shooting, there was still a chance. 7.1 seconds to play. 81-80. Knights ball. Jack Douglas was in his familiar position in the huddle; sweaty, shouting and gesticulating at his clipboard. It was always a wonder that the other team didn't know exactly what the Knights would do before they took the court. Normally, the man who'd manned the bench at ELP for 25 years had an intricate plan set up for any late-game situation. Apparently he'd had a change of heart, because this time, the plan was decidedly simple. "Funnel the ball to Kenny. Ken, if you get the shot, take it. If not, get the hell rid of the ball, and get rid of it quickly." "That's it?" was the universal response from the team. "Strategy's only going to take you so far, men. You want this one, you go take it yourselves. Got it?" Everyone nodded in agreement. "Alright. State on three... ONE. TWO. THREE..." "STATE!" Despite a double team, Morgan managed to work his way to the ball and grab the inbounds pass at midcourt. But, as he started his drive towards the basket, the Kentucky-bound guard felt the defense collapse on him. With the lane closing quickly, there was only one option left. Four seconds... Troy saw the minute opening at the exact moment his teammate did. He spun away from his man and darted to the foul line at the exact moment the ball arrived. Three seconds... A defender swung right to him, but Troy faked, got the man off his feet and drove to his left. Two seconds... For a second, he had a clear shot. But, as he took to the air, RCC's 6-foot-10 center spun and soared through the air. Troy adjusted his body desperately and... One second... From the moment it left his hand, Troy knew the ball's fate. The buzzer sounded as the orange orb was still floating, but it didn't matter. The swarm of teammates an instant later confirmed that this wasn't just some dream. 82-81. State. In the mob scene on court, he only looked for two people. The first was Lauren, who had sprinted down onto the floor, grabbed Troy and kissed him like the universe was about to collapse upon itself. The second was his father, still standing on the sideline, apart from the entire near-riot. He smiled. He nodded. And, despite all the racket, Troy could still read his father's lips to make out what he was saying. "Thank you." "No, dad. Thank you." ... ... "People are going to remember a lot of things about Jack Douglas. A legendary coach, a beloved teacher, a staple of the Greensboro community. Trust me, he was all that and more. But, to me, he was my father. He was my teacher. He was my coach. He was everything I wanted to be. And if one day I ever become one-tenth of the man he was, then I'll be happy with my life." Then he looked at the ring, smiled and whispered. "Thank you, dad. For everything. I'll miss you." "But I'm still not running those windsprints."
