Four People You Meet in Japan

24 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/24/06 A guttural explosion erupted from somewhere inside causing a crude crimson mist to speckle the sink, giving random splashes of color to the black marble surface. It had been weeks since the Dual Halo and he was still coughing up blood. The odd part was that, despite a violent fall through the failing roof of the structure, he had suffered no internal injuries… a fact that had been verified by two trips to separate doctors. Still, that hadn’t stopped the explosions of blood to rip through his throat and stain every bathroom fixture in his hotel room. It was rather unsettling. For what it was Worth, there was some peace of mind that came with the fact that he had never truly lost in the Dual Halo and a reel of Danny Ferguson’s favorite item provided the visual proof; however, that tranquility had taken ten hours to settle in, and about ten seconds to vaporize. The scenes of Culture Shock raced through his mind at the speed of dead. He saw himself pushing through the doors of the entrance set, well before the buzzer had sounded to signify his official turn in the Halo. Then there was the image of The Supreme Machine marching down the aisle; an image that was burned into his mind. There was the blur of the arena shooting upwards as the roof gave way and he crashed to the canvas far below. And of course, he saw the blinding lights as he laid flat on his back, feeling his foot pushed off of the bottom rope, raising up at the sound of the bell only too see his nemesis giving him a cold stare that made everything far too clear. He slowly lifted his gaze to the mirror above the mutilated marble in hopes of that his own reflection would break his manic thoughts and that he might actually look a little better than he felt. No luck. The view in the mirror was caught somewhere between rage and exhaustion. What was typically a face that had seen a day, two at max, of not shaving had now evolved into the result of nearly two weeks without grasping a razor. And, what was once a simple shadow had grown into a beard, perfect for holding the remnants of blood from his latest enigmatic explosion. His hair was getting longer, starting to develop that shaggy appearance that had always prompted Mary to suggest he “get a haircut…please”. It hung in his face partially concealing his eyes… but not quite enough. Even through the dark strands of hair, that fragmented the picture of his face, it was easy to see the disturbing change that had occurred in his eyes. Despite the easily apparent fact that they were very much bloodshot, most likely due to the severe lack of sleep the nights had been serving him, it was something else that caught his attention. The cold and disturbing stare that usually graced his brow when he stepped into the wrestling ring had somehow found an undeviating home in his eyes. What had once been reserved for ‘business’, turned on and off at his will, was now a permanent fixture… even a month after he’d last been anywhere near a ring. He tried to flex a few muscles, move his eyebrows around, anything to find the simple and human look that he had remembered seeing in the mirror at home countless times before, but all the twisting and contorting only caused the emotionless cold stare to mutate into the same look of rage he had worn at the conclusion of Culture Shock. It was certainly a look full of emotion, but not quite the one he was looking for. He needed a shower. Something. * * * The Hotel Employee Smelling better, but feeling the same, Matt grabbed his room key from off the side table by the bed, then headed out into the hall, pulling the door shut behind him. The elevator was closer, but he opted for the stairs, noticing a crowd standing impatiently in front of the doors… and he had no desire to find himself packed into an elevator like the canned goods aisle at the grocery store, fighting for the little available oxygen with a bunch of strangers who have to laugh and scream with their friends despite being locked up in a box where even whispers resonate. Six flights of stairs wasn’t that bad… wasn’t that bad at all. The stairs were much more his style…empty… only the sound of soft scuffing as his feet lazily slid off the edge of each carpeted step filled his ears. Otherwise, he had all the silence his mind desired and all the air his lungs could handle. On the other hand, hotel lobbies tended to more closely resemble crowded elevators than desolate stairs. As Matt pushed open the door from the stairway and stepped into the lobby, his silence evaporated. People pushed through the main glass doors of the hotel, slamming their luggage into those trying to exit into the Japanese night life. A handful of teenagers hung out on the arrangement of furniture that sat plastered in the middle of the room. They seemed to be listening to one of the boys telling a rather funny story as he rambled on and on while they all sat with ears peeled, occasionally chuckling in unison. Simply wanting to get to the front desk as easily as possible, Matt slowly made his way past the bustling people, turning his shoulders and leaning one way or the other whenever necessary to slide through the mass of life without repeated collisions. Once at his destination, he braced himself for a second potential difficulty. Over the past week, he had begun to notice that a fair amount of the hotel employees spoke at least a little bit of English, some being even rather fluent. But as he stepped up to the check out, he noticed that the gentlemen behind the desk wasn’t a familiar employee face, and had no idea how difficult it was going to be to request a wake up call for the next morning. “Uhh… can I… get a wake up call… tomorrow morning?” “Of course.” The man smiled and stepped over to the computer. “Room number, sir?” Through a sigh of relief, Matt responded with ‘612’ “And what time?” “7:30, please” He watched as the kind gentleman pushed a few keys, then lifted his head and smiled again. “Done.” No sooner had the word parted the lips of the man, than his look changed to one of contemplation… as if he were thinking through some sort of math problem. “Y… you are Duar Haro runner up!” “Yeah.” He gave a small wave as he started to step away from the desk. “Thanks for uh… taking my wake up request, sir.” Matt nodded his head, trying to be nice, but in no hurry to recount the ending of Culture Shock with anyone. “So crose” the man now held up two fingers, maybe an inch apart, “but not quite! Haha.” So much for that idea. While trying to maintain his composure, Matt began to think how entirely inaccurate first impressions of individuals could be. The ‘kind gentleman’ who had politely helped him with his request for a wake up call was now having a good laugh at his short comings in the Dual Halo. Worse yet, Matt was a guest at the hotel, and somehow… mocking the failures of guests didn’t seem like it would fit under the ‘good procedure’ category in the company’s employee handbook. “Yeah, so close.” He cut a path straight for the main entrance now, not wanting to hear anything more to change his opinion of the ‘kind sir’ behind the desk, but his ears were greeted with one last remark before they found sanctuary in the buzz of the Japanese streets. “Better ruck next year!” * * * The Man at the Restaurant It occurred to him as he strolled past the endless lights that his stomach ached from its entirely empty state. It had been nearly twelve hours since he’d last eaten anything, so without giving it much thought, he stopped in the first restaurant he came across… at least, he thought it was a restaurant. Inside, he slunked down into whatever barren seat he could find and slid as close to the window, away from the center of the room, as he could. The restaurant was fairly crowded, not enough in the way of empty seats to allow him any real privacy, so he did the next best thing… losing himself in the world outside the window, focusing his attention on those who were fractured from his existence, a plane of glass guarding him from their disruptions. Still, he had to turn his attention at some point towards his indoor surroundings, at the very least in order to actually get something to eat. And as he searched for something that sounded… looked appealing… he was left vulnerable. It was about the time he was losing hope at finding something he recognized or that would satiate his hunger that he heard the annoying, piercing sound of realization that preceded being smothered by the excitement of some crazy fan. “Whoa! You him, Tchu! Tchu!” As the words bounced off the walls of the establishment, a teenage Japanese boy rushed across the floor, practically crashing into Matt’s table, slamming on the breaks just before the potential disaster “Tchu! Tchu!” The boy glanced over his shoulder, back at a couple of friends who had remained in their seats across the room. They seemed rather excited as well, but appeared to be younger, and every bit as nervous as they were excited… probably why the remained at a safer distance. “You’re awesome!” “Thanks. I appreciate it.” He gave a half-hearted smile, hoping the brief exchange would satisfy the teenager, but he knew no exchange with a fan was ever that simple. “Can I…autograph?” Now making an actual request, the boy’s enthusiasm eroded and an underlying nervousness of his own peeked through. “Sure” Matt reached into his pocket, searching to see if he had a pen on him, but it wasn’t necessary. Having expected a positive response, the boy produced a pen and a piece of paper , what appeared to be a flyer for a concert, from his pocket. He took the object from the boys shaking hands and quickly scribbled Tchu across the middle of the paper. The boy reached out in hurried state and snatched away the paper practically before Matt could finish his short signature. Immediately, the teenager turned and started to sprint back towards his comrades when he realized he had forgotten his pen. Turning on his heels, he spun back around and took two large steps back towards the wrestler. “I um…” Before he could finish explaining his embarrassing state of forgetfulness, Matt held out his hand, pen in grasp, and again forced a smile, handing the utensil back to its rightful owner. “Um… Thank you!” As the young fan returned to his side of the room, Matt took a deep breath, rubbed his eyes, and then continued with his useless search for something to ease his appetite. His moment of peace didn’t last long, and his thought that the fan would be satisfied was nothing more than wishful thinking. “Tchu…” When he turned his head this time to try and muster one more difficult smile, he was greeted by the same teenage boy, this time with two friends in tow. “Can we um… get… a picture?!” It occurred to him at that moment, he probably should have been flattered. The occasional mugging by a fan when out on the town for the evening wasn’t the sort of thing that typically tortured him to a frustrating extent. He had gotten used to the level of fame that came with being a professional wrestler, and if anything… the continuous admiration of this kid and his friends should have been a welcomed change on the heels of the sarcastic hotel employee. Yet, for some reason, most likely the fact that he felt like shit, the prolonged, though polite, pestering from the teenager was more than his hollow stomach could handle. “No thanks, kid.” One of the boys behind the teenager shook his head up and down, trying to change Matt’s mind without having to actually open his mouth and verbally beg. “No, not tonight. Sorry guys.” “Just one?” The teenager once again took control of the groups desires, echoing the plea that his friend couldn’t spit out. “One?” The boy held up a single finger. “Yeah… ok. One.” The boys pumped their fist in excitement as one of the friends behind the teenager grabbed the sleeve of a nearby little old man and asked him if he would be so kind as to take a picture “Just one!” he assured him in elation. * * * The Woman at the Restaurant Despite the low roar of his stomach starting to feed upon itself, he couldn’t bring himself to eat what sat on the table in front of him. After the picture, it had taken him another ten good minutes to finally order something, and even after the food had been laid in front of him he had done nothing but stare blankly at it for another ten minutes. By that point, a whole new rush of people had moved into the restaurant, replacing the crowd that had finished… including the trio of teenagers and the elder gentlemen who had struggled with the camera for nearly five minutes before finally taking the picture. The boys had wished Matt luck in his next match, then gone back to there table to leave him in peace. His next match. To be honest, he had no desire to step into a wrestling ring any time in the near future, so the fact that PRIME had bookedhim in a Falls Count Anywhere match that was bound to see the action spill everywhere but the ring should have suited him just fine. But there was more to it than actually managing to avoid a ring. There was a problem developing on two fronts. First, he was growing increasingly frustrated by PRIME, and life for that matter, and the way things were happening. He had practically killed himself in the Halo, just to see his potential victory snatched away from him by The Supreme Machine… and did PRIME do anything to address the outcome? Naw, of course not. Video footage, strolling across the big screen live for everyone in attendance to see had clearly shown his foot on the ropes and the interference of Killean Sirrajin, but the decision was upheld and just like that… it was on to the next show. And then, ReVolution 90, another match against another champion, and a lethal one at that, in another non-title match. So, the potential reward for the match was nothing more than another check in the win column and another opportunity to become a champion passed by, but the risks… only a potential ass beating of the career ending variety at the hands of a proven hardcore machine. And there was the second problem. As his head became more and more of a disjointed horror show and his focus turned off and on like the kitchen light… he was developing more of a target on his back. End up on the right side of a few too many three counts, and you become a walking benchmark. When he had joined PRIME back in September, he’d been the new guy, flying under the radar, nobody giving a second thought to his performances. A JITC tournament victory and Universal title win later… and now, he was getting to that point where his name on a card meant the chance for someone else to prove something. Ever heard sports analyists dismiss a near loss by a great team to a much lesser team by saying “hey… they’re the best, so they get everybody’s best shot… there’s gonna be some close ones.” That was becoming his plight, except in PRIME, there weren’t really ‘lesser teams’, just a small margin between the good and the great. Now, Matt had gotten to that point where everyone gave their absolute best effort when they saw their name across from his on a piece of paper. As was always the case, somewhere along the way, the juggernaut stumbles. And his scenario was no different. It was the way of the circle of life, or some shit like that. The difference was, a stumble in abusiness like pro wrestling, in a fed like PRIME meant potential career collapse and severe injury. Becoming a ‘benchmark’, a juggernaut, took consistent focus. Remaining that way in the face of everyone’s best efforts took even more focus. And he wasn’t sure just what sort of grasp he had on his focus anymore. As he stirred the food around on his plate, he noticed a similar population had filtered in to replace the previous. There was an older man, just the kind who looked like he wouldn’t be able to operate a camera. There was a table of three teenage boys, another filled with young adults… ones who appeared to be the group from the hotel lobby. With this crowd, however, his presence didn’t seem to be catching anyone’s attention. “Not really hungry.” Spoke too soon. He looked up from his meal and saw the face of an attractive American woman peering over her shoulder back at him. Her tan skin was contrasted by the vivid white the separated her lips into a large smile. Her dark hair hung past shoulder length, a slight curl giving it a spring that was just fading from her prior movement as his eyes lifted to hers. All in all, she was simply beautiful… in a very different way from Mary he thought to himself. Mary had always been the attractive, down home, girl next door type. She looked her best in a t-shirt and jeans, and opted for a pleasing beauty that was accented by minimal amounts of beauty. Her dark blonde hair and fair tone gave her an unmatched simplicity that had always struck him as stunning. This woman, however, was dressed in a fancy top, far from the realm of t-shirts and hoodies, and it was obvious by the amount of accessories she wore and the intricate make up she had applied that she was far more the ‘high maintenance’ type of attractive than the ‘girl next door’ variety. They were very much opposites at first glance. Except the smile. The smile… was a mirror image. “Not hungry for this” he finally located an answer. She laughed and stuck out her hand, reaching across his table. “My name is Liz.” Her smile widened as she waited for him to accept her offer of a handshake. “Nice to meet you, Liz.” He took her hand in his, hoping his grip wasn’t so tight as to be painful in its nature. Then he smiled back and pulled his hand away awkwardly letting it rest on the table. “Uhhmuhmm” She cleared her throat to snap him out of his dumb state, but he just stared at her in slight confusion. “Aren’t you going to tell me your name?” “Wait… you mean you don’t know who I am?” “Should I? Are you a celebrity or somethin’?” “Haha.” He laughed. Despite her high maintenance appearance, she seemed to be quite the opposite underneath. Funny, easy going… much more like Mary than he had first assumed. “Naw, not really.” Matt leaned back and smiled, unforced, for the first time all night. “Sorta, I guess.” “Sorta?” “I’m like a pseudo-celebrity.” “Well, pseudo-celebrity or not, I’m guessing you have a name…” She raised an eyebrow inquisitively. “Matthew. The name’s Matt. “Well Matthew Matt, if you’re not hungry for that,” she pointed playfully at his dinner, “then what are ya hungry for?” “I’d kill for a burger and fries.” “Burger and fries, huh? I can get ya that and you don’t even have to kill for it.” “Really?” “Yup, all ya have to do is follow me.” * * * He finished off the last of his fries and laughed as she concluded her story about her crazy aunt that lived in a trailer home in Kentucky. “What’s so funny? She reached across the table and gave him a soft shove in the shoulder. “I’ve got a goofy Uncle who lives in a trailer home in Kentucky.” “No way! Maybe we should try and get them together!” “I don’t think so. I’m not the matchmaker type. Besides, I think he’s married.” “Think?” “He goes through marriages like cars. Everything runs smooth for a few years, then things start to screech and sputter and the next thing ya know, he’s out on the hunt for a new ride, test driving a few along the way.” “That’s the most insensitive thing I’ve ever heard.” Just as he was reaching for his drink, his hand stopped inches away, frozen by her sudden change in attitude. “I’m sorry… I…I don’t think of women… he just…” “Hahaha, I’m kidding! In fact, I know some people who treat their cars better than they do their spouses. I was just messin with ya.” The ease of his manner returned as he laughed and shook his head. There was something about Liz that just clicked. An hour with her had made the hassles of the rest of the evening disappear somewhere into the electric hum of the Japanese lights. It had been a long time since he had sat down and enjoyed pointless, light hearted conversation the way he was doing now. A long time. “So why are you in Japan? I told ya I was here on business, but you still haven’t told me why you’re here.” “You never told me what ‘business’ you were here on?” She took a drink of her soda and winked at him. “But I told you it was business, that’s more than you’ve told me!’ He picked up a forgotten, cold fry and tossed it at her. It sailed aimlessly over her left shoulder and landed on the seat of a nearby unoccupied table. “You have terrible aim, and I’m here visiting a friend who I went to college with.” “So why aren’t you out with your friend?” “Why aren’t you out with your co-workers?” “Hmm,” he thought about how to phrase his response, “not the kind of business where you hang out with your fellow employees. Problems at the office tend to make us not want to spend our free time in one another’s company.” “My friend is busy.” She stuck out her tongue in sarcastic fashion. “See! I can be vague too!” Again he laughed, leaning back in his chair. “Ya know, this past hour has been awesome. I really like you, Liz. You’re smart, cute, funny, kind… and best of all, I think you actually enjoy my company.” “And why shouldn’t I?” “A lot of people don’t.” “Are they right not to like you?” She leaned forward, a more serious tone slipping over her for the first time that night. “Most of em… most of the time.” He stood up from the table, stepped back and scooted in his chair. “I guess that’s part of the reason I really had a great time here tonight. It was nice to sit down with someone who didn’t already have a reason to hate me or a preconceived notion of who I was. It was nice to be a stranger. To get to make a first impression… its been a long time since I had that opportunity.” “Well, for what its worth, I very much liked that first impression.” “Thanks” He gave her a small smile. “I should probably be going.” “Wait…that’s it?” She quickly jumped to her feet. “Whaddaya mean?” “I mean… no email address, no phone number? Am I ever gonna see you again?” “Count on it.” He turned and headed towards the exit, her voice stopping him just as he started to push open the door. “When… Where?” He turned around, leaning his back against the door, choosing to push it open with the weight of his torso so that he could fire one last smile in her direction, and hopefully accept one in return. “On TV… I told you… I’m a pseudo-celebrity.” * * * The Forth He could see the sign for his hotel down the street, maybe a block away. His face still bore the lingering remains of the smile from his unexpected encounter that evening. There had been some peaceful aura about their exchange, mostly due to the fact that Liz had allowed him, not only the rare opportunity to make a first impression, but to forget… even if just for one night… what he was. He hoped he could continue to lose that knowledge away from the ring. The more he submerged himself everyday in what he had become, the less he had to feed on when he stepped between the ropes where inhuman nature and discomforting rage and aggression were key to thriving. As he arrived at his hotel and pushed his way through the main entrance, it occurred to him that his main problem had been his inability to separate his life inside the ring from his life outside. And their mixture was causing things to shatter on both sides of the line. Liz had driven a distinct wedge between the two. Inside the lobby, his steps came to an abrupt stop once inside the door as he noticed a mirror sitting over a small table in the far corner of the room. For the second time that night he turned his attention toward the reflective surface, wondering who would meet him this time. From the distance, he couldn’t make out the look in his eyes. His hair, however, was still nearing a shaggy state, and the beard still remained, but at least the blood was gone. He turned and made his way across the lobby, his steps carrying him quickly. As he passed the front desk, he heard a familiar voice, though he didn’t bother to stop for it. “Heeyyyy! Duar Haro roser!”
