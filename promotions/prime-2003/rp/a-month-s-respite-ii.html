Date: The Not-So-Distant Past Any longtime Lindsay Troy fan worth their crown knows the following to be absolutely, infallibly true: I. Lindsay Troy, by nature, is very distrustful of people she doesn't know and isn't friendly with. She either prefers to associate with known acquaintances in small numbers, or to not associate with anyone at all. She is a highly motivated, highly driven, highly successful loner and is quite comfortable as such. II. Keeping Roman Numeral I in mind, to be called a friend by Lindsay Troy is a distinguished honor. Conversely, to be called an enemy is equivalent to Troy wishing you death in the most painful way possible. Like being screwed in the back of a Volkswagen while listening to Tomato Day carols as part of the ambiance, then being shot in the face and the chest with a nail gun and having your body left in the middle of nowhere to be devoured by wild coyotes. Or something similar in premise. III. Lindsay Troy does not get along with people from the Winnipeg, Manitoba area. Since the events of PRIME's ReVolution 112 telecast, wrestling fans and journalists have succumbed to lots and lots of head-scratching over unanswered questions; questions which severely contrast the social and behavioral patterns of the Queen of the Ring. When explanations are asked for, the demands are ignored. Theories, both practical and outrageous, rise up and are given audience in everyday conversation and on Internet discussion boards. Historians turn to the books, pour through tape and website chronicles to find the justification in Troy's unexpected return and alliance with Tchu, Worth and Sirrajin. But maybe, quite possibly, it's not about truths at all. For any longtime fan of Lindsay Troy worth their crown also knows that despite conventions and expectations, the Queen of the Ring takes the path of most resistance. It's the thrill of the challenge, the incomparable chase and the breaking of protocol that has made her one of the most talked about figures in the history of the industry. And she wouldn't have it any other way... * * * * * November 22, 2006: 2:30PM CST For every stressful day, every grueling contest, every long night away from home, the simple moments spent with loved ones make everything else worthwhile. Lindsay Troy dozed in the sweet inbetween of sleep and consciousness, rubbing her niece's back while the toddler laid against her shoulder. Cecilia Marie, pacifier in her mouth, was six months old and had no idea how precious this time spent with her aunt really was. Troy didn't remember the last time she wasn't on the road for the Thanksgiving holiday but, thanks to a term of personal leave due to family crisis, she would remember this year's downtime for more than just her uncle's illness: it would be the first holiday spent in her sister's home outside of Houston. While Brent's recovery from his stroke was slow-going, he was making better progress by the day, and was hopeful that he would regain full use of his left arm in time. The doctors were optimistic, which gave the rest of the family a better mindset. Lying on soft, broken-in leather, Troy's silently thankful that Dan Ryan, brother-in-law extraordinaire, is as tall as he is to have a custom-made couch as long as the one she's currently stretched out on. Now at an intimidating six-foot-seven, Ryan had joked that the couch is a product of too many embarrassing overhanging feet incidences as a youth and refused to have the same problem in his own home. Being the height that she is, Lindsay couldn't blame him. Alaina entered from the kitchen, her bare feet padding along the polished wood floor and her light taupe shirt covered in flour. "Your phone's been buzzing non-stop for the past five minutes." The time to doze is over. "Huh?" "Phone. Buzzing. It nearly fell off the counter when I was rolling out the dough for the pie crust." Lindsay sat up, careful not to disturb the slumbering Cecilia. "Did you look to see who it was?" "Hey, not my job. I was content to ignore it the first two times, but after the third and fourth I figured it'd be best for you to check it out." Alaina handed over the phone and watched her older sister curiously flip the top up to inspect the urgent message that surely awaited her. "No voicemails," Lindsay said with a murmur. "And whoever it is is calling from a 204 area code. Isn't that Connecticut?" "Connecticut's 203. Amanda and Justin moved to New Haven after she got tenure at Yale. That's their area code." "Hum..." "Wait...," Alaina squinted, trying to recollect. "Isn't that a Canadian exchange?" Lindsay visibly tensed up. Cecilia stirred, her tiny hands grasped a bit of cotton shirt, then settled again. "Can't be." The phone buzzed again, a mini-time bomb ticktickticking away in the palm of her hand. She looked down again. "Same number," she confirmed with a whisper. The phone returned to stillness, but her eyes remained on the color screen. "Maybe you should call it back, Lindz," Alaina reached for Cecilia, who still remained in her childlike slumber. "Whoever it is must need you for something." Alaina exited, presumably to put her daughter down in her crib, leaving her sister alone in the large living room. Minutes passed with Lindsay still staring at her phone, not making a move to return the call in kind. Finally, with a look of anguish on her face, she scrolled to her missed calls list and pressed Send. Ring... Riiiiing... Rii-- "Hello?" The voice is male, deep and familiar. "Um, yeah. Hi," she started, already fumbling over the words. "Someone from this number has been trying to ..." "Lindsay?" Troy froze. "Hello? Shit, don't tell me the call dropped again." "Who is this?" she demanded. "It's Kill." "Who?" "Killean." Lindsay yanked the phone away from her ear, staring at it in disbelief and attempting to process the information. On the other end of the phone, Killean Sirrajin heard nothing but dead air and wondered if maybe she hung up on him. "Troy, are you still there?" She reluctantly returned the phone back to the side of her head. "I was trying to process if this was the one phone call you're allowed or not." "That's not funny," he replies with a sigh. "How did you get this number?" Lindsay stood up and started to pace, the couch no longer offering her comfort. "Be serious now. Did you harass Blaine for it?" "No. Chet gave it to me." "Chet?" "Yeah." "Chet Worth..." "How many other people do you know named Chet?" "..." "Well?" "I'm mentally running through all the ways I'm going to hurt him if I see him again." "Don't get too mad at him. He didn't want to give your number out, but I asked him to." "You asked him to?" Lindsay suddenly realized that she's beginning to sound very much like a parrot with all the repetitive questions. "Yes, I asked him to. Maybe even pleaded with him a little. Point is, I wouldn't have asked if it wasn't important. And the reason I'm calling is important." Troy stopped in front of the window to look out across the sprawling ranch. The still-lush grass rippled in the late November wind. "I know you've been gone for awhile," Killean continued. "I don't know why, no one knows why really, and Chet wouldn't say when I asked." "That's because it's personal." She could sense the nod on the other end of the phone. "I respect that. But things have become personal in PRIME as of late. I don't know if you've been keeping up with things, but if you have you'll know what I mean. I called - - - I called because I need a favor. Both of us, Matt and I, need a favor." "Why didn't Ward call then?" "Because you've given me the crystal-clear impression that I'd be one of the last people you'd ever want to talk to unless forced. And if I called, I'd get your attention." "Smart thinking." "So it worked, then. I've got your attention." "Briefly, yes. But it's starting to wane..." Behind Lindsay, Alaina re-entered the room. She leaned against the door jamb, arms folded across her chest, and watched her sister's rigid body language react to Killean's voice. "Then I'll get to the point. Matt and I started something last week, but we can't do it on our own. There's not enough manpower to combat the virus that's slowly starting to take over the company, and a two-person front won't be able to withstand the long-term barrage." The Queen of the Ring shook her head, her ponytail sweeping between her shoulder-blades in an attempt to dust off the burdens long carried there. "Trying to find my sympathetic side?" "It may be there. It's worth a shot and you can't fault me for trying." "You don't know me very well, Sirrajin." "You won't let me know you well enough. I tried, despite my actions towards others at the time, but the efforts fell short. But now I'm trying again, even though I'm getting the same treatment as before for whatever reason." "Like I just said..." "I know what you said. Now listen to what I'm saying. Matt didn't want to try and force you, but I don't give up that easily. We need another, and we want the other to be you. If you can't respect that, then I don't know what else to say." She banged her forearm against the windowpane. "I'm in Texas with my family. It's the day before Thanksgiving. You want me to explain to people that I need to grab my shit and fly to wherever the show is to get involved in this?" "Listen, I fed you some bullshit when you first came to PRIME, about your indy street cred and how you can't just waltz into the company like you own it. But you did waltz in, and it may have been hard for some of us to admit but you are a part of PRIME and you are one of us, or at least you were before you left. Swallow your pride and at least think about it. That's all I want." Before Troy could protest, Killean hung up the phone. It took her a moment before she slowly lowered the phone, clicking the top over the numerical keypad and heaving a troubled "ugh." Alaina walked over to stand next to her and the two stared out of the window for a time. Finally, the younger spoke. "Go."
