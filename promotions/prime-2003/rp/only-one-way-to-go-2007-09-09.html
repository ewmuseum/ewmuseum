Only One Way to Go

9 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 9th Big Bear Ranch Big Horn, Wyoming 2:18 AM Big Bear Munson Monsoon, born Michael Munson, sits in the dark inside his Wyoming ranch’s training room. A towel is covering his head as he slumps over, elbows on knees. He is silent and motionless. What’s the big deal? It’s just Jason Snow. Yeah, well Jason Snow is 3 and 0 against the “Big Bear.” That’s nothin’ though, because those matches were all chump change compared to this. This is for the Golden Flapjack, for the Big Taco… this time, Snow was the onlt thing between the Big Bear and the 5-Star Title. Big Bear picks up head and walks into the next room, using only the moonlight to guide his course. He walks into the bathroom and turns on the water. While waiting for it to get cold, he looks in the mirror. There’s only one thing that needs to be done. The very same thing that Big Bear realized after watching Snow beat Hall, the same thing he realized when he won the 5-Star Challenge, and it’s the same thing that Big Bear walked out of CIV understanding…he has a 5-Star Title to win. Reagan National Airport Washington, DC 5:39 PM Mike Munson sits inside Reagan National Airport, arms crossed on the counter of Joe’s Pub & Grill in the C corridor. His hair is held back by a pair of sunglasses on top of his head and he takes a swig of his ice cold Yuengling, watching the college football highlights for the week. The bartender wipes the counter in front of PRIME’s newest official 5-star contender, grinning to himself. “You look familiar, fella. Where ya from?” Big Bear pulls the mug away, saving some of the frothiness for later in his rich, thick Bad Ass Mustache. He pauses for a second, locking eyes with the stranger, and then nods slightly. “Alaska. Got family out there?” “Nope, that’s not it. You look real familiar, television kind of familiar.” The bartender looks again, nodding his head and going back to wiping. “Yeah? Well, I just... have that kind of face.” “Nope. You’re that wrestlin’ fella, Big Bear Munson Monsoon. No need to say anything, just wanted to tell ya I’m a fan. M’boys are, too. We’re rooting’ for ya, Mr. Monsoon.” The barkeep goes back to wiping the bar as Big Bear shows some remorse for turning the fan away without batting an eye. He takes another swig, this time pouring the entire mug down his throat. Taking a long look at the mug, Mike lets out an Ahhhhhhh and holds it up toward the man again, who is already walking down behind the bar with another full mug. “Thank ya, sir. For the kind words, too. It’s been a while since I’ve actually gotten out to meet the fans. You’d think I should have been training all this time, not being at the recent ReVolutions, but nope; I was just sitting there, thinking about everything ahead of me. So, again, thanks for bein’ kind.” The barkeep grins, tossing the towel over his shoulder now, and leaning up against the bar. He pulls over a stool and pours another Yuengling, setting in front of Munson. “You probably get this all the time, but…protect yourself out there.” “What?” He glances down the bar leaning in closer to Munson, noticing a few lonely souls who could care less what they’re talking about. The two lock eyes. “Just be careful. I know, I know; I’m just some bartender in Virginia while you’re PRIME’s next big hope… but it’s not worth it if it takes everything out of you and leaves you with nothing, you know?” Big Bear takes in the advice being given to him, most symbolically by taking a swig of the beer and staring for a while at the bartender. He nods. “I think you’re wrong there…” “Joe.” He’s the owner. “…Joe. In fact, if I worked that way I might be in your line of work; safe and sound, inside an airport with level orange security. I’m sure you feel safe, all the time, but I couldn’t live like that. I’ve got to put it all out there… so that way, I take it all home, too. Not just for glory, but so you don’t have to. So you can cheer for a respectable guy, somebody with values and nuts to take on those psycho nut jobs like Chainz.” The barkeep didn’t smile, he didn’t frown – he just poured a shot of Johnny Walker Blue label into a glass that he pulled up form underneath the bar. Big Bear looks at the drink, back at the ‘keep, and then shakes his head no. “Mister Big Bear, go ahead. On the house, to the future.” Big Bear holds up his hand, extending two fingers. He motions to the keep for another glass. The bartender agrees, pulling up another glass and pouring it. The two hold their respective glasses in the air. “A toast?” George looks at his soon-to-be drinking buddy, whose eyes are running through his head. “Of course, a toast!” Big Bear pauses briefly. …Jason Snow was days away, with such a reward that Big Bear’s life would change forever. Dice, Dollars, & Tragedy will bring forth one of two paths… the least desirable would be a loss, again, to Snow and missing his prized opportunity for the coveted 5-Star Title… Big Bear raises his arm, high and proud. “To hard work.” Joe raises his glass, smiling proud as he admires his own achievements around the bar. …The second option would put Big Bear in the one position he’s never been in – a champion. Beating Gamble, beating Walczak, and beating Rayne would all mean nothing if he didn’t walk away the new 5-Star Champ. Mike Munson didn’t devote his mind, body, and soul to this sport to be called an almost champion. No way. “And the pride within.” Joe’s eyebrow cocks a bit, still smiling and admiring this charismatic customer’s ability to string together words better than he. ’Big Bear’ Munson Monsoon was PRIME’s Jack of Hearts, a fan favorite. Jason Snow was no villain though, since it appears every woman seems to cream herself at the mere sight of him, and recently being the victim of a Michael Sloan rage episode at Colossus IV – the fans were behind him too… “May our ups and downs come…” …but Big Bear is in this, this fight of two strong-headed heroes, for the fans. Kicking ass and taking names is the biggest part of Big Bear’s résumé. Sure, part of it is because he’s big enough to always have something to say and be heard, but most importantly; Mike Munson does this for the little guy who won’t get the shot nine times out of ten. This time, at DDT, Big Bear is the little guy. The underdog. The moment he steps into the ring with Snow in Atlantic City, he’s going up against a Unified Champ. The 5-Star Champ. A legend. A proven warrior inside the squared circle… A lock of the eyes with the owner and operator of the airport bar comes quick, putting both men in an armed and ready position. Big Bear raises his glass a bit higher, clinking it against Joe’s. They share a grin in what some call those ‘still moments’ in life. Big Bear realized, through this drink and talk with Joe, a long time ago what had to be done. Even though the only training Big Bear got done was with the dumbbells at home, even though Snow’s streak as of late has been plated in gold, there was only one thing to do. At Dice, Dollars, & Tragedy, it will be Big Bear’s moment in glory, his moment in the spotlight, his turn to shine, and the most dominant reason of all – to become the 5-Star champion. “Like a fart in the wind.”
