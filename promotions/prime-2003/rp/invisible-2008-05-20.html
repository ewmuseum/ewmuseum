Invisible

20 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20th May 2008 OOC: Yeah, sorry this is so poor. Finals this week. Not good. “Don't walk away, Don't let your weakness show, It's such a journey to the promised land, When you arrive learn to take control, And do your living by your own command.” - Crowbar, To Build A Mountain *** Things hadn’t exactly been going the way that Jason had planned. By now he thought he’d have notched up at least a couple of victories, maybe a draw or two, but no, here he was sitting on a 1-3 record. Not exactly the type of start to make everyone else sit up and take notice, huh? He thought it’d be so easy; he thought he’d waltz into PRIME and leave a path of destruction behind him that the company hadn’t seen since the debut of Tchu. He’d been a street fighter for as long as he could remember, how could any of these jumped-up little wrestling pussies even stand up to him? Obviously the gulf between an unsanctioned fistfight and a singles contest in the world’s most visible professional wrestling promotion was something that Jason Natas had completely underestimated. To anyone else such a mediocre start would be completely understandable. After all, Jason Natas had barely two months worth of training experience under his belt when he first signed up to compete with PRIME, and he was stepping into the ring, week-after-week, with guys who had been doing this for years. Guys like Troy Douglas, who, just over a week ago, handed ‘The Anti-Superstar’ his most recent loss inside the squared-circle. He wasn’t at a level where he could compete with the Troy Douglas’s, Crucifix’s and Union Jack’s of the world, not yet. Trouble was, Jason was the only person in the world that couldn’t see this. His failure to find consistent success had left him completely baffled; he knew he must be doing something wrong, but he couldn’t pinpoint it. The fact that he simply wasn’t that good at wrestling didn’t cross his mind for a moment. That’s not to say he hasn’t been doubting his own abilities recently though. He genuinely felt that the part he’d played in the six-man tag victory a couple of weeks ago would be a genuine turning point in his young wrestling career, which made the loss to Troy Douglas a hard one to take. He didn’t even want to think about how he might react if he lost to Bryan Dawkins at UltraViolence. For the first time in his life Jason Natas felt like a failure. A never-will-be. A flop. A loser. A reject. Even when he was living in a cold, damp, roach-infested squat he felt better about himself. Regularly getting his shoulders pinned to the mat in front of a worldwide audience might have something to do with it… *** “Horse-fuckin’-shit!” Jason spat the words across the table like a cobra, the vitriol in his voice all too evident. He slammed a gloved fist down on the oak surface to accentuate his point, as if it wasn’t clear enough already. “I’m afraid not, Jason, its right here,” Najera announced dispassionately as he juggled a piece of mint chewing gun around the inside of his mouth. “Paragraph 43, subsection 2. Plain English.” The suit-clad Hispanic slid a stapled bunch of A4 sheets across the table, straight into the waiting hands of Jason Natas. It only takes PRIME’s Anti-Superstar a matter of seconds to read the clause with his own eyes. “This is fuckin’ horseshit!” he snorts, repeating himself as Najera adjusts his relaxed seated position into something that looks a little bit more attentive. Jason somehow resisted the burning urge to tear his Potemkin Management contract to shreds; instead opting the throw it violently against a nearby wall. “Careful with that, now… it is the only copy we’ve got,” retorted Najera as a slight smirk began to draw itself across his features. Fortunately for him he managed to wipe it away before Natas had taken notice. “Yeah? You give me one good fucking reason why I should just tear yer precious little contract to shreds right now, huh?” Najera snickered condescendingly; he found Jason Natas’s lack of eloquence and manners nauseating, not that he’d ever tell him, though. “Well, you could do that, Jason, but that’d mean no more paydays from PRIME – or us - ever again, comprende? Plus we’d be able to take you to court and milk you for all your worth; breach of contract isn’t a mere misdemeanour, you know.” Jason clinched his teeth together. He wanted to leap across the table and rearranged Mr. Najera’s photogenic features, but knew that that wasn’t within his best interests. “So yer goin’ ahead with it? 30% is a pretty big chunk of salary to be deductin…” underneath the table he clenched his fist; it seemed as effective a method of stress relief as anything else he could come up with. “Mr. Natas, if you’ve got issues with the content of the contract itself then I’m afraid you should have raised them before signing it,” he said as he reclined in his leather chair. “It explicitly states that if you don’t maintain a 50% winning rate at any point, we’ll take some of our money back. Simple economics.” This time Jason had to gently bite his tongue to suppress his anger. He knew that every single word that spilled from Mr. Najera’s mouth was nothing but the cold, hard truth, and it sickened him to the very pit of his stomach. A scowl was Jason’s only response. The pay cut would hit him hard; he wasn’t exactly on a Prince’s salary as it was. He needed that money to fund a lifestyle that had grown ever more elaborate since his emergence as a professional wrestler. “If you beat Dawkins at the pay-per-view we may be willing to reconsider…” “Well ain’t you just the fuckin’ saint,” scoffed Jason. “Come on now, Mr. Natas, we’re only trying to protect our investment here…” And that’s all he was. An investment.
