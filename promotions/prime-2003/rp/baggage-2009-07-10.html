Date: 10 July 2009 If there was one thing Kazys could say about getting into this wrestling gig, it was that the hotels had improved. No more run-down Cuban Arms, across the street from a Texaco and a woefully understocked market. No more noisy cleaning ladies deciding to vacuum every room within a fifty yard radius of his at six in the morning when he was trying to get to sleep after the bar closed. Still, he reflected, as he glanced around, this was only a Ramada. At least it was a nice one. No dingy carpet, though that hotel freon air smell was ever-present. He'd hardly had time to unpack much, and was still regretting the fact that most of his clothes right now still consisted of Hawaiian shirts and loose-fitting slacks. He could have played a seedy Miami gangster. Then, it might have been more interesting to do that than to play bodyguard to a woman who didn't seem to know how to keep her mouth shut. As though on cue, Kaz's phone started buzzing on the nightstand. He knew it was Elise before he even looked at the screen; as though anyone else really called him these days. Text message. Yet another one of those things he wasn't really fond of that he was having to get used to, being around her as much as he was. “Hey Kaz, need you to come down here to my room. I'm having a total dilemma about what to wear out there, and I need your... help?” He sighed as he read the message aloud, breaking the almost church-like silence of his room. It was unusual for Kaz's eyes to widen any more than about half-opened disinterest in most things. Now was an exception. He was about to enter into every man's worst nightmare. Again. It certainly wasn't the first time that Elise had put him through this, and it wouldn't be the last. She was so concerned about their image – well, more her image, but then, in a sport like this, how could he have blamed her? He wasn't here to be the eye candy, he was here to take care of the business end of thing when Elise, by far the charismatic member of the team, spoke a little too hastily for her own good. That was safe. That was something that he could handle. It was just that it was an adjustment, was all – an adjustment to a life that looked very, very different than it had when he was closer to Elise's age. ---- 8 January 1991 Eyes that were wide and blue peered from behind the thin green and white curtain covering the dirty kitchen window. Outside was the typical gray January of Lithuania, cold outside, and not much to look at. Certainly nothing of interest for a thirteen year old boy who would rather have been listening to his rock music or playing a video game (a rare treat). The flat that Kazys shared with his parents, aunts, uncle and three cousins in Vilnius was not exceptionally nice, but it was home. It was cramped, the walls the typical hard concrete cinderblock of Soviet construction. Not engineered for comfort, but for convenience, for the express purpose of cramming as man people as possible into one building. It was entirely built for efficiency, but efficiency for who was perhaps the question – certainly not for the families who lived there, considering how all the warm air seemed to flow right out whenever the temperature dipped under thirty. “Kazys!” his mother hissed in a low whisper. She'd been talking like that for the past week or so, and it was starting to drive the young man crazy in ways he couldn't even begin to understand. He only knew how nervous it made him, the way his guts seemed to twist inside him each time she did it. “How many times have I told you to stay away from the window?” Elzbieta Jankauskas was a slim, wiry woman. Looking between her and her youngest son, it was entirely obvious that he had not inherited his build from her. He had, however, inherited from her the delicate cornflower blue eyes that seemed to focus on everything at the same time they looked straight through whatever they saw. “Ma, I'm just watching the planes, I told you, my hands are too cold to help you cut radishes.” In a way, Elzbieta felt sorry for her boy, having, as he did, to sit tied up in the apartment on these cold days with nothing to do but schoolwork. But things were happening in their country, things which hardly anyone seemed to understand, much less to be able to control, and Elzbieta was frankly terrified for her family. As her son spoke, those same blue eyes widened until they seemed to be the only distinctive feature on her face. “Planes?!” Thirteen year old Kazys rolled his eyes and nodded. “Yeah, ma... planes.” On any other day, his mother might have slapped the taste out of his mouth for being so disrespectful. Elzbieta brooked no disrespect, not even from her nieces and nephews, and was known by far as the disciplinarian among her siblings and in-laws. Suddenly, Kazys found himself pushed out of the way by his mother. He stumbled nearly into the stove as she looked for herself. She was rewarded for her efforts. Just as she peered out, a low-flying MiG shot over the building. It was only visible for a moment, but Kazys knew exactly what it was – he had loved to see the planes and military trucks rumbling around in the streets ever since he was a small child. “That's a MiG29 Fulcrum,” Kazys said, recognizing the camouflage patterns and the needle-shaped nose. “Kazys, hush!” his mother exclaimed. Suddenly, her tiny feet were tap-tap-tapping across the floor of the apartment, towards the bedroom she shared with Kazys' father Darius. Kazys wanted to tell her to come back, to scold her for going to wake up his father over what was probably nothing, after his father had been working all day in the streets, loading and unloading the shipments of overpriced food that had caused such unrest in the Lithuanian capital all month. “Darius! Darius wake up! There are military planes...” Kazys was surprised to see his father up in moments. The stress of trying to support the family in such an inflated economy had begun to take its toll on Darius, even though Darius was still a relatively young man. His father's face had started to sink into lines over the long Cold War years, lines that seemed entirely out of place, though not unbecoming. Another plane zoomed over as Darius himself went to the window, brushing past his son without so much as a glance. For a long few moments, dark-haired Darius simply stared up at the sky. He swore softly under his breath. “This can't be good...” Only later would they discover the truth of that pronouncement, when the truth came out that the Soviets had flown in the brutal counter-terrorist Alfa Group to the Lithuanian capital, anticipating trouble. ---- Kazys frowned, distracted from whatever it was that Elise was saying. He didn't know why he was supposed to care about this, really he didn't. Elise knew damn well that she had much better judgment about what to wear to a wrestling match than he did. 'For Christ's sake!' he wanted to shout, 'I've been doing this for two months and you've been doing it for how long? And you want me to tell you what to wear?' Not to mention, he felt it was rather silly for a woman to be asking for fashion advice from a man who looked like he'd stepped off the set of Miami Vice not five minutes ago. “Uh, it's not bad,” Kazys said, eyeing Elise with what he hoped seemed to be an attentive expression. The shirt was a neon blue, cropped and spandex (of course). It had only one arm and was cut low across the chest on the other side, seeming to hang on her figure only by the tightness of the material. The shorts matched, and were indeed very short, though that was something Kaz had gotten used to relatively early on in his partnership with Elise. Elise twisted in front of the mirror, apparently trying to look at her own ass. “Not bad? I was hoping for a little better than that. Not bad doesn't stop people in their tracks. And since I know how awesome we're going to be, I'd like to look the part, you know?” “Uh... yeah,” Kaz responded, seeming much less sure of the certainty of that statement than she was. “I mean, I dunno, I dunno about this kinda thing. I don't even know what else you have.” “So you think I should put it on standby or something? I mean, I always thought that I looked good in this kinda aqua color, but if you don't think so, I guess I can see what else I brought with me. That's a good idea.” He hadn't realized he'd actually had an idea. Apparently, though, Elise had found one somewhere in all his mumbling. He wasn't sure if he should have been thankful or concerned about that, or if Elise was even actually listening to what he said at all. She seemed almost to be having a conversation with herself at all times, even when she was looking straight at him. “Be right back!” Elise chirped, heading for the bathroom with a small tote bag in hand. “Shit...” Kaz murmured, shaking his head as she shut the door. He'd never met a woman with as many bags to tote around as Elise Ares. It was, at least, a good excuse to flex the bodybuilder muscles he'd managed to grow into over the course of his life. He looked like his father, he reflected, catching a glimpse of his face in the mirror, only his muscles seemed inflated, like they'd been pumped up with a bike pump. It had been a long time since Kazys had seen his father. One of his last memories of Darius was the day he'd gone off to boot camp. ---- 15 May 1997 Kazys stood, awkwardly tall, over his mother. Two duffel bags dangled from the ends of his long, gangly arms, which seemed almost too slim to support the weight of his belongings. The train station was crowded today, one of those days when Vilnius seemed somehow more alive than normal, somehow like it was one of those bustling European tourist cities, like it was more than just a place where people came to see the TV tower and marvel about the brutality of the Soviet Union and those poor people who had to live behind the Iron Curtain all those years. Kazys' blue eyes, hardened over the years by the things he'd seen his countrymen suffer, narrowed. He was only nineteen now, but felt he'd grown up fast. Faster than he wanted to, sometimes, but there was no room to complain about his life, only to live it. His mother's eyes were still red-rimmed with tears. She hadn't wanted him to go, even though his father had worked and work to make her understand his choice to enlist. Every time his mother looked at him, Kazys knew she thought of Rolandas, his cousin. Rolandas had been killed at the TV tower in 1991, and since then, his mother had jealously guarded her son, as though one day, Kazys too would just keel over dead, unexpected. It was because of Rolandas that Kazys was here. The events of January 1991 had made such an impact on him as a young teenager that, as he was growing into adulthood, he couldn't ever entirely shake them out of his mind. It wasn't a fear, no... but a desire for some sort of closure. To know that his homeland would never be trampled again. The only way he knew to ensure that was to do what he had done, not five weeks ago. Army recruiters were easy to find in Vilnius, and Kazys was an attractive prospect. Tall, with the obvious potential to build a lot of muscle on his thick frame. A no-nonsense boy with slim to no romantic delusions about army life, or life in general. A series of interviews and a physical, and Kazys had signed the paperwork to join. He remembered his mother's heartbroken face when he had brought the papers home. But Darius Jankauskas had supported his son, and ardently. He had been there to parry each of his mother's thrusts with a sword of reason, telling Elzbieta that it was important that Kazys be allowed to make his own choices. The train was pulling into the station now, the one that would take him, for the first time, from his parents, and towards an uncertain destiny. He could see the label, RN7101, on the front. He knew his mother was hoping that it was the wrong one. Kazys shouldered one of his bags, digging his boarding pass out of the pocket of his neat khaki trousers. He glanced back at his mother, who was silent, pressing her lips together in a thin line. He leaned over, wrapping one arm around her in a young man's awkward embrace. “I'll see you in a few months, ma,” he said quietly. “Don't forget to write us... and your grandparents,” his mother responded, her voice quiet. He thought he could hear the hint of a tremble, and he tried to resist the urge to roll his eyes. “I won't, I've already told you that like ten times, ma...” “Kazys,” his father said, by way of warning. “Let's go get your things on the train, son.” His father's voice was always stern when his mother was concerned. And, Kazys reflected, perhaps he really did spend a little bit too much time needling her, but wasn't that the job of every teenage boy? Then, he wasn't much of a boy anymore, and hadn't felt like one for quite a long time. His father grabbed one of his bags and Kazys shouldered the other as they started for the platform. The queue wasn't terribly long, and Kazys saw a handful of other young men with their relatives, and even some all alone. At least, then, he could probably find someone to talk to for the long ride to the training site. He was at the door sooner than he realized he would be. He looked down at his father, who caught his eye, as though he wanted to speak. Darius had a certain manner about him that made it obvious when he was about to say something – as a man who always seemed to measure his words, those were the times when people paid attention to him. “Son, I want you to know that I'm very proud of you today. You do the whole family a credit with this decision.” Kazys nodded slightly. “Thanks, dad...” That was the last thing father and son exchanged before Kazys had gotten on the train to become a member of the Iron Wolf Mechanized Infantry. ---- It took Kazys a moment to realize that Elise was back, this time in all yellow. The top was similar to the first one, at least as far as Kazys could tell. Matching it was a short yellow miniskirt, obviously entirely centered around the attention it would get in-ring. And Kazys had no doubt she'd use that attention to her advantage – meaning to cheat – if she could. What did he care, though? Except... well, it was pretty heinous. He made a face. “Elise, my God...” “What?!” Elise asked, turning over her shoulder to look at him. It surprised him that she'd actually reacted to what he'd said. “It's...” “Cute? Because I mean, I think I look really good in this.” “It looks like a highlighter...” Elise paused for a moment, seeming to consider this. She turned back to her reflection in the mirror, staring at herself from what seemed like every possible angle. She smoothed down the little flip in the fabric of her miniskirt, frowning. “Ugh, you drive a hard bargain. Maybe you're right. I don't want anyone else to say that, no matter how silly it is.” She flounced past him again, the determined step of a woman on a mission. Kazys leaned forward, pinching the bridge of his nose between his fingers. He'd have wondered how he got into this situation if he didn't already know the answer. ---- Two months ago Kaz's eyebrows were about as high as they could have gone. They might have migrated off his face if they'd had the option. “Wrestling? Elise, you know...” “Yeah, wrestling. I mean, haven't you watched it before?” Kazys gave her a very non-plussed look. “No. It wasn't exactly popular in Lithuania, and I kind of had other things to worry about, you know? Like the whole Communist thing? I didn't exactly get to hang out and watch stuff like that on my leisure time.” Elise sighed, cocking her hip to the side with her hands on her hips. She rolled her eyes. “You know, you should live a little, Kazys. Wrestling's very fun, you know. You've got the build for it, and... and you could be a superstar. I mean, maybe not quite like I could, but I've kind of got a pedigree, you know? But you can learn how to wrestle, and I mean, how many people with no personality have been carried through a job with a partner who's got one?” Kazys frowned, giving Elise a rather unamused look. “You know... when you put it that way?” Elise's eyes brightened. “Forget it.” “Kazys, come on!” It seemed to be taking her a moment to figure out what she'd done to piss him off. That was... entirely expected, he supposed. At least now that he'd gotten to know her a little bit better. “Kazys I didn't mean it like that, you know I don't think you're some earthworm or something with no personality or anything like that, it's just that... well I mean, I'm a show-woman! You're a bodyguard, and I'm kind of... used to being in the public eye. I'm ready for this kind of thing, I know what they expect out of you when all eyes are on you... you don't. That's all I meant, okay?” Kazys nodded, grunting. “Yeah. But that doesn't mean I'm gonna do this.” Suddenly, Elise was rather indignant. It occurred to Kazys that she seemed like the type of girl who had no idea what to do when someone told her no. “And why not?” Kazys was about to show his stubborn side. His mother always said his face would stick that way – mulish, chin jutted out, eyes narrowed obstinately. Around a girl like Elise, Kazys wondered if that might not be something useful. A permanent “no” to her permanent “but whyyyyy?” “A better question... why -should- I? It's not really my realm of expertise. I don't even really find the idea interesting.” “Kazys,” Elise said, giving him a withering look, “I'd appreciate it if you didn't trash talk my family's business, okay?” Kazys visibly rolled his eyes. “Sorry.” “Look, all I'm saying is that this might be good for you. I know it's a full time job you know, making sure that the public doesn't get to me and all that-” At this, Kazys gave Elise a rather withering look of his own, but she didn't seem to notice. “But I mean, this will give you something to DO instead of just follow me around all day. I mean, not that people wouldn't kill to be doing that, but I'm sure you'd actually like to... think about something else? Do something else?” She had a point. And Kazys hated that she had a point. He sighed. “What kind of commitment are we looking at?” “Pff, I don't know. That depends on what kind of contract we can squeeze out of PRIME once they see how awesome we are.” Kazys raised an eyebrow. “Uh...” “Don't -worry- about it, okay? Everything's gonna be fine, and you are gonna love this.” Kazys had the feeling, at that moment, that he was getting himself in way, way over his head. ---- “I know you're gonna like this one,” Elise said from the bathroom. Kazys was still in a world of his own for a few moments after she spoke. As soon as she reappeared though, he had to pay attention. Elise Ares seemed to be rather adept at creating those moments. All he saw was pink. Bright pink. That is, until he got to the booty shorts. He could have sworn half of her ass was hanging out, and she pranced in front of the mirror like she knew it. “Um...” “You know, I really think this one might be the best. I mean, I like the skirt on the yellow one, but you really can't beat something that shows off your figure. Every woman wants to be you when you wear stuff like this, you know. And every man wants to date you.” Kazys couldn't suppress a sigh, or a roll of his eyes. “I guess...” “You don't like it?” Elise turned to him, hands on her hips. Kazys made a face. “Highlighter.” “Ugh, you're impossible to please,” Elise said, turning back to the mirror and fussing with her hair, pulling it up into a ponytail and yanking some strands down expertly to frame her face. “I hope you never try to find a girlfriend, Kaz, because with an attitude like that... Anyway. I think this is the best one of all.” “Sure,” Kazys said. “I was just giving you a hard time.” 'And if you believe that, I've got some desert property to sell you in the Amazon.' Elise didn't seem to even think about whether or not she believed him, passing over his response with a cursory nod. “Pink makes tanned skin look great, anyway. I'm going with this one. Thanks for your help, Kaz, you're the best.” “I'm something anyway,” Kazys murmured. His eyes turned to Elise as she bent over, rummaging through another bag. In fact, as he looked around, he noted the presence of far more bags than he could remember hauling up to Elise's room. It was like they were multiplying on their own, like tribbles. Then, he thought of the fact that he'd be the one hauling them back down to their car whenever they had to get ready to go back to the plane. Kazys frowned, then sighed.
