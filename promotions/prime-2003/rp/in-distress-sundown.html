In Distress: Sundown

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 21-26, 2007 LOCALE: Albuquerque, New Mexico DAY: June 21, 2007 CUE: "Corner Story" by Del tha Funkee Homosapien ---- "Look, I just don't think this is a good idea," Alex said pointedly as Easton stepped on the accelerator and blew past the just-turned-green light. Dry wind whipped through the open windows as Easton cruised through the streets, both hands on the wheel of his rented sedan. His hair was pulled back in a ponytail, reducing the effect of that wind on his hair, but he still squinted as if going through a sandstorm. Alexander Shaw was seated next to him, a LIONS F.C. jersey hanging on his frame, his own dirty blonde hair catching the wind like a low rent rockstar. Future Development blasted tinnily through the speakers. "You've got to do radio soon," Easton's protégé said. New Mexico was neither man's favorite state, but they weren't here to see the sights. Tucked away in the duffle bag in the trunk was Easton's 5-Star title, a constant reminder of their reasons. Easton pulled around a turn a bit harder than he meant to, exhaling. Shaw glanced at him and rolled his eyes. The younger man let his arm dangle out the window, reclining in his seat as best as he could. Easton was really anal about people not leaning the seats back too far, Alex had found. "It'll be fine," Easton responded. He came to a stop at the sign, glancing about curiously. He was a cautious driver, something that made Shaw want to rip the steering wheel out of his hands. He started up again, pressing his shoulders against the seat and letting his brow settle into a focused curve. "Yeah?" Alex said, lazily watching the buildings pass by. "I can imagine what it'll be like talking about Winters while stoned out of your skull." Easton chuckled, rolling his eyes as he made an easy left. "Like there's a ton to say." Alex laughed as well, drumming his fingers on the outside of the car. Easton could outwrestle Jonathon Winters, that didn't need to be said. Neither man in the car doubted it. "Anyway," Easton continued, "I'm not smoking before radio. We're just gonna pick up and then head back. It'll be quick." The car fell into silence as they pulled out onto the highway, leaving the city behind them. The early morning sun warmed their limbs and faces as they slipped into the sparse out-of-city traffic. Easton took the opportunity to remind himself not to underestimate his opponent this week. After all, it was a mistake he warned everybody else not to make – not that his warnings had ever gotten across – and it would be kind of embarrassing to make it himself. Losing this week would be a step backwards. "Who are you buying from again?" Alex asked. "I wasn't aware you were familiar with New Mexico." "I wrestled in the southwest with AXIS for a few weeks." Easton adjusted his mirror, watching the silver-white convertible behind him. "Made some contacts. This kid's a suburban white kid. Just safer than messing with city dealers." "Oooh, that's racist," Alex winced. "Shut up. I haven't gotten my shit laced yet and I don't want to start." Alex smirked, glad to see that his barbs had dug under the skin. "Does he use it for meditation, too?" Easton shook his head, switching lanes deftly and speeding up to pass a hulking SUV. "He's just a stoner," Easton said, deflecting the verbal prod. "Listens to a lot of Sublime." Alex furiously worked at the handle of his door. "Let me out," Alex demanded. "Don't make me go in there. I'm beggin' ya." "I make him shut it off." Easton's lips pursed. He was about as thrilled Alex was concerning this kid's musical taste. "Smart." Alex allowed himself to relax again, running his hand over his hair in relief. The conversation drifted into silence once again as Easton drove. His thoughts became consumed with PRIME once again, swirling around the match to come. A non-title matchup, something that Easton couldn't understand. He didn't think Winters deserved a title shot, but the man had held the belt before. Besides, Lisa Tyler hated Easton. He was sure of it. Why she wasn't trying everything at her disposal to get the belt off him, Easton couldn't figure out. What would he say at radio? He had to be civil, but at the same time, he couldn't pretend this was going to be anything but choking Winters out. Easton wasn't sure if this was some sort of god complex that had been brought about by his 5-Star victory, but he just didn't feel threatened. Really, he was feeling good about himself. His marriage aside, he was feeling better than he had in weeks. Well, besides his marriage and Chainz, to be accurate. Before long, Easton was pulling into a small court, double-checking the address he had written down. He brought the car to a stop and Alex immediately opened his door, stepping out of the sedan. Easton followed suit, finding himself in the second position as he slammed the door and hurried to catch up to his trainee. Shaw glanced back at him, hand poised to knock. Easton waved him off and knocked himself. Almost instantly, the door swung back to reveal a lanky man with black hair hanging around his earlobes. A wide grin came onto his face. "Hey, man," he drawled, giving a breathy laugh. "Wow… I didn't think you'd really come." "Yeah, well, sometimes you're in need," Easton said. "D'you typically deal on your stoop?" The man looked at Easton, the sarcasm obviously having gone over his head. "Nah, man, I'm not an idiot," he protested. "Come on in… but not you, man," he said, lifting his hand up to Shaw. "You gonna fuck him?" Shaw snapped. He slapped Easton on the shoulder. "Hey, you didn't tell me you were gonna fuck him when we were coming or I would've stayed back." "Shut the fuck up," Easton growled. "I just don't know you, man," the dealer said, shrugging his shoulders. "Sorry. Unless you're buying. That's different." Shaw shook his head, turning away and lifting a hand, frustrated. "Whatever." "It'll just be a second," Easton said, sliding into the house. Alex turned, walking slowly back towards the car and getting back inside, leaving the door open and letting a leg hang out. He watched the door of the house and, indeed, it wasn't long before Easton was re-emerging. Alex pulled his leg back into the car and slammed the door closed as the PRIME superstar got into the driver's seat. Easton tossed his cellphone onto the seat as he slammed the door. "We're outta here." "Where's your stuff?" Alex asked. Easton patted his pocket quickly before putting his seatbelt back on and starting the car. "To radio!" Hall cried, pulling out of the driveway. "Time to challenge the old sticks-and-stones theory." Hall shot out of the sleepy neighborhood like a rocket, not particularly eager to be back in traffic, but less eager to be late. CUE: "Bush Boys" by Danger Mouse & Jemini ---- "Holy fuck!" Easton yelled, scratching his head furiously. "Nobody saw this!?" Alex stood beside him, peering at the smashed-in window of his car. "At least this is a rental, right?" he said. "It'll suck without tunes, though." The sedan was thoroughly keyed and glass both seats, but a particularly big shard lay between Shaw's feet. He sighed, looking around as Easton kicked the car's hubcap. Radio had gone great. Everything that Easton had wanted to say, he'd said. Put himself over as a deserving champion, made sure people knew Winters didn't stand a chance, talked about his future with PRIME, with the business, everything. And he comes back out to this. "Who even does this shit anymore?" Easton demanded. "Stealing fuckin' radios and nothing else. They could have at least taken the goddamn car, made it worth it." "And you would have loved to lose that belt, huh?" Alex leaned against the car, careful to avoid the jagged edges of what was left of the driver-side window. "Don't they make you put a deposit on that?" "Get offa there," Easton snapped, swinging at Alex. Alex backed off of the car, snorting contemptuously. Easton rubbed his forehead with the heel of his palm, quieting his fury but still harboring it. He shook his head, turning around towards the station. "Where you going?" Alex called, following slowly. "To get a fuckin' broom or something," Easton growled. "I'm not sitting on glass the whole way back." Alex nodded, picking up the pace to catch up with Easton. DAY: June 26, 2007 CUE: "Escape Artist" by Sage Francis ---- "Look, all I'm saying is that Charlie is the best character on the show," Alex said, twisting so he could look at Easton from his position on the couch. Easton sat on a chair in the kitchen, swallowing a mouthful of Raisin Bran. It was a lazy afternoon for them; training had already been done, so all that was left was recovery. "Come on," Easton replied when he could, punctuating that by draining half of his glass of orange juice. Alex rolled his eyes every time he saw that TV commercial breakfast that sat before the champ. "Mac is pretty excellent. He fucked Dennis's mom, and that's pretty solid right there." "Yeah, but he did it like a bitch!" Alex pointed out. "Remember, he goes to Charlie for confirmation at first, then he acts like a girl when it's all over! Plus, she pretty much tosses him aside." "Okay," Easton conceded, "but Dee—" His thought was interrupted by the hotel phone ringing. Alex rose, but Easton waved him off, getting up and hurrying over to the phone to pick it up before the third ring. He lifted it off the receiver quickly. "Hello?" "Easton, so glad I got you, man," the voice on the other end breathed. "It's Brian." "Brian, hey," Easton said. "How's it going?" It had been a long time since he'd heard from his former cameraman; last he had heard, Brian was wrestling on the East Coast. "I'm fine, but Jeanne isn't." Easton's face instantly became concerned. Alex lifted an eyebrow and Easton lifted a hand, staying his words. "What's up with Jeanne?" "She called me last night. She said she's been getting some weird calls from your phone." Easton blinked. "I'm not calling her…" He should have been less proud to say that, but in context, he was glad he wasn't. "She says it's not your voice, E, but… I dunno." "Listen, you gotta know I wouldn't do something like that. I wouldn't tell anybody to do something like that." "It doesn't sound like you, man. I mean, like, not something you'd do. She said the guy on the other end—" "Guy?" "—has been making some weird… I dunno. Threats? Said he's watching her, that he wants to see her. It sounds really weird." "Do you know if it's Chainz?" Easton's voice was urgent and his eyebrows furrowed accordingly. "No idea." Easton grunted in frustration. "She didn't tell me, so I'm assuming it wasn't. Maybe she can't recognize his voice." Easton knew that was bullshit, but he couldn't read Jeanne as well as he thought he could. Maybe she thought not saying anything would protect his frame of mind. "Where are you?" Easton's fist clenched and unclenched as he cradled the phone against his shoulder. "I'm in Pittsburgh, I'm driving up to New York now. I just stopped to call you." There was a pause and then Brian continued, "Justin's still in Tennessee, Easton." Satisfied, Easton nodded. "Get there as fast as you can, Brian. I'll be there when I'm able to." "When's that gonna be, Easton?" Easton could sense the accusation in Brian's voice and he swallowed hard. "Soon, Brian. I'll talk to you later." He hung up the phone and gritted his teeth. He should go now. He should go be with her. And yet he knew he wasn't going to. This was his break in PRIME. This was going to get people to shut up, quit doubting him. Jonathon Winters obviously wasn't worth sticking around, but this wasn't about Winters. This was about Easton's career. Brian could handle things in New York for a few days. "What's up, boss?" Alex asked, his expression adopting Easton's former concern. "Get your shoes," Easton said, clawing at his beard. "We've got business." There was one thing he could do, though. He could find that cellphone. He knew exactly where it had gotten to. CUE: "Rumble" by U-God ---- Alex glanced around as they pulled back into the weed dealer's driveway in a new black rental. "Tell me again why this is a good idea." he said, looking over at the more experienced grappler Easton put the car into park, stepping on the emergency brake. "The day we did radio?" Alex nodded, his eyes squinted with curiosity. "We came and picked up from here." "Yes, I was awake," Alex said dryly. "Well, when I was in there, I got a call from HQ asking if I was gonna make radio like scheduled," Easton explained. "That was the last time I remember having my phone. Seeing as I didn't really do anything after radio, it had to happen before that. I'm thinking this creep pickpocketed me when I was in there." "Alright." Alex took a deep breath, looking at the ranchhouse before them, and then looked back at Easton. "So what's the plan?" "We rip his house apart." Easton opened his door and got out of the car, slamming the door behind him. Alex exited after him, falling a few steps behind the Canadian as he walked towards the door. Easton pounded on it, muscles tensed. There was no answer until Easton knocked again. "Coming!" was the faint cry from inside. Same guy as before. Easton lifted his chin, his features settling into a hardened scowl. The door pulled open and the black-haired dealer blinked. "Hey, man! Uhh… what's up?" "We're searching your place," Easton said matter-of-factly. "What?" the man croaked, taken aback. "You’ve got something of mine," Easton told him. "Let us in, I'm taking it back." "I don't know what you're talking about, kemosabe," the stoner protested. "You know damn well what I'm talking about!" Easton snarled. His hand shot forward, grasping the man by the collar of his t-shirt. "Hey, if you're tryin' to get your fifty back, that was fair and square!" The dealer's hand drifted behind his back, but Easton let loose with a rocket of a punch that smashed square into the man's face. The dealer, released from Easton's grip, reeled backwards against the wall, where Easton caught him with both hands around his throat. "Rip apart his room!" Easton snarled to his protégé. Alex slipped into the house, slamming the door shut and heading off down the only direction possible. "Which one is it?" Alex called, turning a corner. He stood there, watching as Easton choked the dealer. Both men were flying on adrenaline at this point. "Down the hall, on the left." Easton pushed forward, putting his weight into squeezing the man's neck, but before he could ask any questions he felt a sharp pain against his stomach, driving out his breath. Easton stumbled back against the door and the dealer whipped out a switchblade, the thin slip of the blade sliding out drawing Easton's eyes down to the weapon. The dealer snarled and made to lunge forward, but Alex was there just before he could make a thrust, barring the man's arm and forcing the switchblade out. The dealer cried out as Alex slammed him back against the wall. With two hands gripping the stoner's hair, Alex pulled him into a succession of fierce knee strikes to the stomach, each accompanied by a helpless groan from the dealer. Alex pushed the man back against the wall and stepped backwards, watching as the black-haired man slowly slumped to the ground. Easton shoved Alex by the shoulder. "Get going," he said, his eyes still boring holes into the dealer. "You know what my phone looks like." "Yeah," Alex said, sparing the dealer one final look before heading off to search for the phone. Easton brushed some hair out of his face, his green eyes smoldering with fury. "What kinda sicko are you?" Easton snarled, poking him in the ribs with the toe of his sneakers. The dealer was too busy trying to recover his breath to reply. Sounds of things crashing to the ground filled the dead air. "You steal my phone. Then you make calls to my wife? You threaten my wife!? For what? Shits and giggles? Huh!?" Easton's whole body sprang into motion as he pulled his leg back and then slammed it as hard as he could into the dealer's ribs, forcing an agonized groan from him. "For fuckin' laughs with your stoner buddies, you fuck!?" The dealer lay down on his side, curled up against further attacks from the Canadian wrestler. Easton felt like smashing this guy's knee in with a baseball bat. He glanced up as he heard footsteps and Alexander Shaw came back around the corner, shaking his head. "Didn't see anything." "You look hard?" Easton demanded. Alex scowled. "What do I look like?" Easton nodded, looking back down at the dealer. "Check the kitchen." Alex took his turn to nod and turned to go. "Wait!" the dealer croaked. Four sets of eyes centered on the fallen man as he stretched his body out just a bit, getting his elbow under him. "I don't… I don't have the fuckin' phone." "Bullshit," Easton snapped. "Go look, Alex." "No, you fuckin' assholes!" the stoner snapped. "You think if I had your phone, I wouldn't fuckin' tell you? I don't have shit." He turned on his side again, looking up at Easton. He was obviously angry. Apparently, he had gotten over the pain enough. "You got a choice. Two thousand dollars and you leave now, nothing'll come out of this. You go into my kitchen and the second you get out of here, I'm callin' the goddamn cops." Easton rolled his eyes and said in a condescending staccato: "You deal. Out of your house. You idiot. Nobody. Is going to do anything." "This weed," the dealer responded in similar fashion. "Can be gotten rid of. These bruises? A bit tougher." Sure enough, there was some swelling on the dealer's face; both Shaw and Hall could see that. "And why shouldn't we just walk?" Shaw asked. "Uhh, 'cause everybody heard you making jackasses of yourself when you were coming in," the dealer pointed out. "And 'cause I'll sue you monkeys for every goddamn cent you have. Let's not make this legal." Easton and Shaw exchanged glances. Easton sighed. Why couldn't stoners just be stupid? "Two thousand, you said?" The dealer nodded. Easton slid his checkbook out of his back pocket slowly. Even if this dealer did still have the phone – which Easton no longer thought he did, especially in light of him not talking after that beating – it wasn't worth getting sued over. The phone's service could always be canceled. Easton tore out the check carefully after writing and signing it and dropped it onto the dealer. The dealer pulled it to his eyes, inspecting it. "I don't like dealing with the law, so I'm not gonna pull a fast one on you," he said, groaning as he pulled himself up into a sitting position. He glared at Easton. "But if I see you around here again, I'm gonna shank you." Easton lifted his hands in surrender, turning around and exiting the house. Shaw followed him, shooting the dealer a disgusted look as he pushed his way out of the house. "Well, ain't that a bitch," Shaw said loudly as they walked back towards the car. "That asshole got one over you." Easton shook his head, pulling open his door. "Whatever," he grumbled. He looked at Shaw over the top of the car. "Dude sells shitty weed, anyway." Alex blinked. "So you paid fifty dollars for shitty weed. Explain to me how he didn't get one over on you again?" Easton scowled, not wanting to get into this right now. If he didn't have a reason for beating Winters before, he had one now. Just one night and Easton would be getting paid to smash someone's face in. If only he could do radio again now. It was a good thing that Winters probably had very little to live for, too. Easton didn't feel like having pity after working off all that frustration.
