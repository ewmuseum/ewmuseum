Date: 1/24/07 As Andre Spilling unlocked the door to his apartment, a wave of relief came over him. He had such a bad day at work. Well, maybe not actually bad. Boring… definitely, bad… not so much. Deadlines, overtime, demanding bosses, angry clients… pretty much the stuff of legends to Andre, thanks to the fact he shares a last name and several chromosomes with the head of the company. Still, any of that would be a nice change of pace from mornings at the gym, three hour lunches, and afternoons spent trying to think of reasons to call his secretary into his office simply so he could watch her walk way. Just a little bit of stress to get the heart pumping, that’s all Andre wanted. Anything that could delude him for a moment into think his so-called career wasn’t built upon who his daddy was. Nepotism. The word even sounded vulgar. “Have you heard? That Spilling lad actually enjoys nepotism!” “Nepotism? That pervert! The sound of John Coltrane’s “Favorite Things” was the first thing to greet Andre as he opened the door. Rather odd, since he didn’t recall having that CD in the stereo, let alone how it would be still playing at that time. And had he really left all those lights on when he left that morning? When his nose was tickled by the aroma of garlic bread in the oven, a contraption Andre had never used in his life, he concluded that he was not alone in his apartment. “Honey, you’re home!” While Andre would normally not object to an attractive young Asian woman stepping out of his kitchen, he did have a bit to this particular attractive young Asian woman. Hell, sometimes she even scared herself. She just needed a moment of escape, a second away. Mei closed her eyes, the lack of vision like a cocoon of sable silk… … and as she slowly opened her eyes again, Sun Tzu was gradually, but still harshly, brought back to reality. This had been such a simple mission: an Egyptian employed at the American consulate in Cairo has overheard a conversation between two of Washington’s finest agents. Like typical Americans, they had a strange outlook on the linguistic skills of the citizens of other countries. They are convinced that their tongue is as alien to the natives as that backwater gibberish is to them. That is, until the Americans want something from one of those funny little foreigners.
