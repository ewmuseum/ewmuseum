Benevolence

21 February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/21/2007 The night was chill, scathing in its frigidity. Things were made no worse by the ghostly stirrings of the shadows within the crypt, the howls of the voiceless dead trying to rend apart the fabric between the realm of the living and that which they now called home. "The spirits are restless tonight," called Tully across the span of the subterranean tomb. "They cry for redemption yet there is none. As foolish in death as in life." Ethan Knight frowned, as he had from the moment they entered this ghoulish expanse beneath the earth. It was difficult to smile when a hundred dead cursed and defiled your name with their enmity. "What exactly did you expect, Brother Tully?" he replied, his voice echoing off of stone walls overlaid with frost. "Most of these cretins still refuse to accept Death as their new master. Besides...who shall they curse if not the ones who helped put them here?" The occupants of the crypt had known nothing but violence and agony in their final moments. It was in essence a mass grave carved from the Earth by the Sect of Black Wisdom, a chamber to deposit sacrifices made in the name of the Great Old Ones. A chamber adorned with nearly as many tapestries of blasphemous things as Wyatt Manor itself. Father Shanahan called it his theater of the damned, a place where the crucified would dance on the strings of Nyarlathotep and his kin until the end of time. And a place where the spirits' essence could be harnessed as a source of power for the living; lost souls raped and in eternal servitude to their slayers. The tomb running beneath the manor knew no rest. Hearing their curses and cries ring out from the land of the dead, Horace Tully felt no remorse for those buried in this hellish crypt. They had mocked the Sect and their masters when they had been denied their opportunity in the GTT6 final match, celebrated when their triumph became defeat. He couldn't stomach the impudence of nonbelievers and heretics. Doing so when dead made it all the worse. Just because they were passed on it didn't exactly mean they were inanimate. A shadow shifted to his right and Tully's eyes darted in its direction. The spirits in this place, restless and vengeful they may be, were powerless to cause hurt. They could voice their rage all they wished but never could they make their desires for physical harm come manifest, or so Shanahan said. The chorus of a hundred phantoms reverberating in his head, it could sometimes be hard to hold to that conviction. Horace knelt, his hand coming to rest on a rough stone marker. The earth beneath it was relatively fresh, as was the corpse the primitive headstone crowned as Bonnie Keller. Her fury pelted his mind, spewing her damnation from the grave. But it it was inconsequential to the sinister right-hand of Bruce Shanahan. She would help empower him even if it were against her will. She little more than an ethereal battery in his soulless eyes. "Weep and shriek all you want, my dear," he whispered, caressing the headstone like one would a traumatized little girl. "You have no choice in this matter." His eyes closed, and the world around him faded to the insubstantial. A mother holding a dead child, its flesh decayed and rotting. Her own face was pallid, eyes like cold obsidian as she stared into her mortal antagonist. Hatred and vengeance tainted her soul, none of the purity remaining that she once knew in her God-fearing life. The core of Horace Tully stood poised over her, a giant, looking down on the pathetic wretch as she glowered at him, wrapped in tendrils of the ethereal mist he'd come to know upon entering this world between worlds. She was been among the most recently deceased, and among the most turbulent in death despite her painless demise. "You took everything away from me in life," she said coldly, her face still etched with disdain. "What more could you possibly do?" Her aura pulsated with a mix of despair and bitterness, a martyr wanting to weep and exact vengeance on one of her killers. She gazed down at the baby in her arms as if it still lived, still crying and uttering its feeble laughter. Perhaps she still beheld that image in the afterlife whereas he was blinded to it. What humanity remained in Tully thought it just; the dead should be rightfully relieved of the pain of life, especially those sacrificed for such a glorious cause. He circled the motionless woman as she embraced her her child, a predator stalking wounded prey, sizing it up for the kill. His face was blank, he took no pride in what was being done. Regardless, the end would justify the means. "I assure you that I don't take delight in suffering. But you're now in service to the Old Ones. I'm merely your taskmaster, come to collect the services due." "Don't you ever question what that monster commands you to believe?" she asked, pain wracking her voice. Her allusion to Bruce Shanahan's gospel brought pause to the brooding cult lieutenant as she shook her head, tears welling in her eyes as she bounced the child cradled in her arms. "Can't you see the madness of it? Of following such cruel gods, whether they'll even offer you the rewards they promise?" Fire leapt into the eyes of Horace Tully as he squared himself onto the beleaguered spirit. "I won't tolerate your blasphemy. The same can be asked of you and your kin, the followers of a debauched god that allows his supporters to be slaughtered in genocide. Who are the blind zealots of Christ to question the champions of the cosmos?" He peered at her as she continued to rock the baby in her arms, a cold expression carved in stone. "You are all doomed to follow your savior to the grave. How's that for a truth your Bible so conveniently omits?" He turned, trudging to the inner bounds of the mist that ringed them. He didn't know for sure what lurked beyond and had been told it was unwise to venture further. His business was with the disheveled soul of Bonnie Keller, the only thing in his mind as he began to pray. "You'll never get away with this," she hissed, seething. "Death will come to claim you as it has us. But God won't be merciful on your souls then, I guarantee it. Not as long as you walk this path." He ignored it, his blackened prayers echoing through the Limbo-like realm. Petitioning to be energized by the trapped energies of the dead, the sacrificed that lay beneath the earth in the name of Nyarlathotep, Yog-Sothoth, Azathoth... "His deceit will never die as long as men like you continue to abide it," she said, refusing to fall silent. "Follow your heart, Horace Tully, and deny his evil. I pray that someday you will undermine his wickedness, and come to affirm the righteousness he avails you to fight." He grew cold as his words traveled the umbra, the icy sensation that gripped his heart to assure the melding of his spirit and the other. It would be temporary, but the power would make their impending victory al the more pronounced. "I'll pray for you, Horace Tully. God loves all his children, and I cannot turn my back on my brother, lost as he may be." He shifted uneasily as she her voice fell to one of sorrow, like a mother mourning her son's tragedies. His prayers receded as he uttered the ending, then turned to face the woman holding the baby. To his shock, it began to kick and squirm in her arms, as healthy and full of life as the day it was born. "I forgive you, Horace." The voice had changed, from initially vengeful and sad to merciful and sympathetic. He collapsed to one knee as the smiling infant blinked at him. Horace Tully had seen enough pictures from his childhood to recognize the dancing blue eyes leveled on his own. "I forgive you, my son," the voice whispered as she stared deep into his own eyes. The child that was once him, so many years ago. Tully shivered, his breathing raspy. The spectre faded away as a halo hovered over its head like a crown and a smile spread across its lips. The child, the reflection of the benevolence he knew in youth, lifted its tiny hand and waggled fingers at him, saying goodbye... He was notably shaken as the brilliant light lifted, plunging him back into the frozen recesses of the tomb. Ethan Knight crossed through the shadows of the crypt, misted breath fading in his wake and booted feet crunching on stale, frosted soil. His comrade was kneeling, pale as a ghost with his brow wrinkled in worry. He had an inkling of what had happened; Shanahan had warned them of what forces might find their way into the ethereal domain, past the darkness of the unhallowed crypt. "Forgiveness," he said, dropping to one knee. "After all we've done, all we've taken from them....yet still some choose to forgive and absolve us of the torment we bring onto them. Is that what happened, Brother Tully?" Shaken, the other nodded. "It was so abrupt a change," he replied, remembering the shift from malice to mercy. "Unnatural. It wasn't her. It couldn't have been..." "Benevolence is our enemy, my brother," sighed Knight as he looked on his trembling accomplice, turning and stalking toward the door. "Father Shanahan warned of us of these tactics. The sacrificed that attempt to become saints and rattle our nerves. Give it no more thought. The meek will never overcome the powerful through their hollow charity." "I saw myself, Brother Knight. She held a child....and it was me. It became me, right before my eyes." The crunching of earth ceased. He could feel Knight's eyes on him, mortified. Even he knew something wasn't right. What did it mean? In Cthulhu's holy name, what did it mean?
