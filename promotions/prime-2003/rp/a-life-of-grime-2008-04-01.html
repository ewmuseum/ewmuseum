A Life of Grime

1 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1st April 2008 THREE MONTHS AGO This was an unusual situation for him, sitting as the head of the table in a boardroom twentysomething stories above the New York City streets. Around the table sat three high-flying self-made millionaires; each one of them seemingly squeaky clean, silver spoon poster boys for upper-class, corporate America. They dressed in fine, tailor-made suits and carried combs in their pockets just in case – god forbid – one tiny hair in their impeccable haircuts sprung out to make a bid for freedom. Their teeth were perfect – so bright that they redefined the meaning of the word “white” – and their chins looked smoother than a baby’s backside. Each of them probably live in nice homes in the suburbs… you know the type; picked-fenced garden, two stories, perfectly-mowed lawn. He bet that when they finished work today they’d drive home in their brand new Porsche and park it in their two-car garage. They would step through the door and dinner would be sitting waiting on the table for them, fresh out of the oven. As they dined their wives and kids would gather round, listening intently as Daddy ran through the ups and downs of another day in the life of a high-rise executive. Jason Natas couldn’t have been more out of place if he’d made the effort. He stuck out like a sore thumb in his well-worn, faded blue denim jeans, scuffed Dr. Martens and dull, scratched leather jacket. Tufts of his short, dirty blonde hair stuck out from the edges of his trucker cap, and the thick layer of stubble that lined his face gave the impression that he hadn’t shaved in days. It was at moments like this that Jason thanked his lucky stars that he wasn’t white collar. “So what’ll it be, Mr. Natas?” the guy with the revoltingly-perfect sideswiped blonde hair asked with in his upstate New York accent. Jase couldn’t remember his name… Jewel… Duel… something like that. It didn’t really matter; he hated the guy on principle, and yet he found himself in his boardroom, about to conduct business. The guy slid a document across the table towards Natas, along with a ballpoint pen. Jason picked the sheets of paper – which were bound together by a single stable – and skimmed through them briefly, not really taking in any of the words written on them. “We can make your dreams come true, Jason,” Mr. Loftman promised with a flash of those oh-so perfect pearly whites. The only slight imperfection in this man’s appearance was the fact that the top button was missing from his sky-blue coloured shirt. His flawlessness made Natas sick to his stomach. The roughneck New Yorker continued to offer no response despite the best efforts of the businessmen. The final member of the trio, Mr. Najera – who, as a Hispanic, was the only non-Caucasian in the room – piped up; “This is the only way…” He said nothing more. This is the only way… Jason pondered Najera’s choice of words a couple of times in his head. *** FOUR DAYS EARLIER “Look, you gotta understand somethin’…” Darius Payson thought he said “understand,” but in his rather inebriated state it came out more like “undeskamd”. “… this fightin’ thing, you can’t do it forever, na mean? There’s only so much blows to the head a guy can take before he starts to go all nutty. Shit Jason, you could end up vegetable because of this crap!” He slammed his shot glass down on the table to emphasise his point; it worked. The impact of the glass hitting the table sent shockwaves around the piece of furniture, causing a couple of the other glasses to tumble over. One rolled off the edge of the table and tumbled to the ground, clashing with the bar’s wooden floorboards and shattering into a million tiny pieces. “I know man, but I’ve got nothin’ else,” a tipsy Jason Natas responded after polishing off the last of his Jameson. “Look at me, for fuck’s sake, who’d employ a guy with tattoos on his face?” he paused for a moment to point at the tiny star tattoo underneath his left eye, “fighting’s all I have.” “What, hanging around in alleys, beating the snot out of some guy you’ve never met just so that a bunch of rowdy hooligans can get a cheap thrill? Ain’t no way to make a living…” Jason was somewhat surprised at the level of confrontation in his old friend’s voice, but he figured the alcohol had something to do with it. Whether or not Darius’s level of intoxication was affecting his behaviour was irrelevant though, because Jason knew that was he was saying made perfect sense. He knew that he couldn’t go on living like this for much longer; it might not be the death of him, but street fighting was too much of a weight on him, mentally and physically. It was a shit business. Every other night Jason would prowl the streets, looking for someone who might give him twenty bucks to fight with another guy. As ludicrous as that may sound, such people are relatively easy to come across, especially in a city like New York. There was something about seeing two men physically taking each other apart that just “clicked” with a certain type of person. The same type of people who’ll gather in a disused warehouse and place bets on a couple of fighting greyhounds. Replace the dogs with humans and you’ve got it. And this is what Jason Natas did for a living. It wasn’t pretty and it certainly wasn’t anything to be proud of, but it brought in just enough money to pay the rent and put a modest amount of food on the table. It’s not like he had a family to provide for anyway. He couldn’t remember how he’d first gotten into the business of illegal street fighting, but he didn’t care enough to recall, but put it this way; a man with a perfect upbringing does not end up as a street fighter, that’s for sure. “Seriously, you should give this wrestling game a shot,” Darius suggested in a deadly serious tone of voice. “I’ve told you, I know these three guys, they can make you a star…” “Nah man, that game ain’t for me,” Jason said as he shook his head dismissively. “Too much fairies, flying about the ring and posing to the fans like a bunch of suck-up bitches, know what I mean? I figured that if I ever stopped doing this I’d give that MMA thing a try…” Payson scoffed immediately. “MMA? Shit, that’s just a glorified version of the stuff you’re doing right now. Give wrestling a shot Jase, the industry is crying out for guys with your tenacity and street toughness at the moment… you’d be snapped up at the blink of an eye! And I already told you, I know a few guys who can help make this happen for ya…” From the pocket of his black trousers Darius pulled a small business card. He handed it over to the tipsy Natas. “Jewel, Loftman and Najera,” uttered Jason as he read the card out loud. “Professional Sports Agents?” He raised an eyebrow at his friend who responded only with a nod. “I dunno man, sounds too fuckin’ prissy to me…” “Fucking HELL Jason!” roared Darius. He startled Natas as he slammed his fist down on the small table without warning. “This is a fucking way out, and you’re not going to take it!?! You’d be an IDIOT to pass this up! You could get away from this life of crime forever… move out of that roach-infested swamp you call an apartment… you could take care of yourself Jason… take care of yourself forever…” Again Natas found himself surprised by the confrontational stance that his friend was taking, and he didn’t quite no how to react. It was easy to go on the defensive and bite back at a guy you hardly know, but what do you do when your best friend has a go at you like this? He found himself warming to the idea though. “Well, you know I can’t afford all that training…” “No need to worry about that, these guys will handle that for you… all you gotta do is give them a call and arrange a meeting. They’ve got contacts in ALL of the country’s top promotions…” Darius paused for a second and looked Natas straight in the eyes. “This’ll work for you Jase, I promise.” *** After a few days deliberation he finally decided to act upon his friend’s advice. He paid the agency bigwigs a visit. He needed to do this; he had to escape his life of grime, and this was the perfect way out. He couldn’t end up stuck in a rut for the rest of his life, just like his brothers and sister. If Darius was true to his word then Natas figured he’d be able to turn things around in no time at all... plus, wrestling wasn’t so bad. Sure, the bigger promotions of the world may be populated by all kinds of egocentric, self-loving pricks, but that would only enhance the pleasure that Jason would get from beating the living daylights out of them. This is the only way. “See, thing I don’t understand is this; how do you guys benefit from this whole… situation?” the puzzled Natas enquired roughly as he slid back in his chair a little bit. The “three stooges” (as Jason referred to them in his head) were well-prepared for this kind of response, years and years of negotiating multi-million dollar business deals made this seem like a walk in the park. “It’s as we explained earlier,” started the blonde one that Jason didn’t like too much, “we take twenty percent of all your earnings. I know that may seem like a lot, Mr. Natas, but I can assure you that you won’t miss it when your first paycheck comes in! We’re talking megabucks here; you’re going to be loaded…” “This is PRIME we’re dealing with!” Mr. Najera enthusiastically announced. “The number one wrestling promotion in the Northern Hemisphere! With our help, you can be on their television shows in a matter of months. You’ll be made for life; you’ll be able to move out of that grimey apartment you live in, you’ll be able to provide for your family… everything a young fighter could ever dream of!” Najera leaned a little bit closer, lowering the volume as he spoke his final words; “… and all we ask is a meagre portion of your salary… and that you carry out various other contractual obligations, of course…” That grin again… Jason had to look away, the urge to punch each of these guys in their pretty little mouths was growing so strong that it would soon be too great to resist. Jason was never one to pause for thought, and this situation was no different. “Other contractual obligations”? That couldn’t mean anything too serious… … could it? “Fuck it,” blurted Natas without thinking. He’d made up his mind. He grabbed hold of the pen and haphazardly inked his signature and the day’s date at the foot of the last page in the contract. The three other men in the room collectively breathed a sigh of relief; the deal had been sealed. They had Jason Natas exactly where they wanted him.
