Date: May/June All day every day. An endless assault. My comeuppance for daring to take a stand against the powerful. It seems I have been handed a sentence to leave me broken, wincing in pain as my guts force the steely taste of blood and regret into my mouth. I am to be the penitent man, humbled at the feet of my God. I am not. ~ The Patient~ Late May “Your PSA is down considerably.” Doctor Millette tapped his finger against the test result, pointing to one of a thousand tiny numbers no one else in the room would understand. “I’m really surprised they’re this low.” “What does that mean?” “Mrs. Ward, that’s a very good sign for your husband.” A smile spread across Christine Ward’s face. The first of genuine nature in weeks. She placed her hand around her husband’s arm, giving it a squeeze, hoping to elicit a similar response. But Dean’s stern look never wavered. “What do you mean by ‘very good’?” “High is bad, low is good. Prior to the surgery, your PSA was through the roof. This number is pretty low. More so than I expected. Lower than some guys six weeks removed from this surgery. Actually, we don’t typically even test for PSA levels until twelve weeks post-operation. If we’d waited another six weeks to run the tests, it might look even better.” “But I’m not outta the woods, right?” Christine’s grip slid from her husband’s arm, her hand slumping to her side. It took everything she could muster not to sigh or scream, maybe even slap the man she’d married nearly 40 years ago. The low PSA may have been good news, but his low level of hope was not. Dean’s attitude was in the pits, completely bottomed out, and it wasn’t going to help him fight. Nothing seemed to pick up his spirits, and all he could focus on was the worst case scenario. He wasn’t interested in any good news short of “congratulations, you’re cured”, and his lack of physical strength left him armed with only his will. But with each passing day, it seemed that this cancer… this cancer that never stopped attacking his body… wouldn’t have to face the wrath of a positive spirit. Dean didn’t know how, or want, to wake up every morning and fight. “No, not out of the woods yet. It’s a good sign, but there’s cancer I wasn’t able to remove with the surgery. No discernable mass showed up on your latest CT Scan… the cancer that’s left is what we call ‘free radicals’. Tiny bits that survive the surgery and kinda hide behind other organs. The way we kill those is with radiation treatment.” Dr. Millette folded his arms across his chest, the test results crumpling under his armpit. “But first, we start the hormone therapy.” “Awesome.” Dean’s voice did little to hide his sarcasm. “Can’t wait.” “It isn’t the most pleasant thing in the world, but its typically very effective at denying the remaining cancer it’s fuel source. Without fuel to feed on, the cancer has a helluva time trying to grow any further. It’ll go a long way until we can get you fully recovered from surgery so that you’re strong enough for the radiation.” “Whatever it takes to beat this, Doctor.” Christine spoke for her husband, strong, confident, hoping it was far more contagious than the cancer. “Easy for you to say.” Dean glanced at his wife, then at the doctor. “You’re not a guy who’s about to have his testosterone blasted away.” ~ The Patient’s Son~ Forgive me for my lack of sorrow. This tired, broken body has felt so much regret that it has little left to offer. I am not, however, completely without apology. I’m quite sorry, Mr. Shakur, that I have not, and will not, be mentally or emotionally crushed by your plans to cripple my body at any possible moment. What my children had to witness did not leave me feeling regret, but anger. Where you hoped to create fear and paranoia, you have given me only a chance to release the compounded aggressions of a lifetime. You have given my worried mind a pure, simple, physical form to defeat. And I will. Your mistake was an easy one to make. I am not the penitent type. I’m prepared. Endless battles are not new to me. I’ve grown use to fighting every hour of every day. My career has been an uphill battle, never the tallest or fastest or strongest. My marriage has been a constant struggle, affording no rest. I lose Mary a little bit more every time I back out of the driveway and head for the airport. While I’m away, traveling the road, I wrestle with the burden of an abandoned wife. When I sleep, her lonely face fills each dream. When I’m home, I fight to make up for lost time, for the many ways I have hurt her. And that imbalance only strains things further. Much of my life with Mary has been wonderful, fulfilling, but not a minute of it has been easy. And there is no relief in the other directions I turn. For years, I have supported a younger brother, covering his needs while he squanders his life on hookers and 10 to 1 odds. We butt heads, going to war over his irresponsible behavior, his problems becoming my daily concerns. My father fights his own continuous battle, and I work tirelessly to find a way to be his rock, to return the favor of raising me for more than half my life. I fight to be strong when I feel so exhausted. I struggle to smile when I want to weep. And nothing I do, no matter how positive or full of strength, does anything to kill his cancer. There is no plan of attack I can muster that will achieve victory. Luck will have to take care of that. Still, I seem to try anyway, forcing myself to keep at it. So, the concept of twenty-four seven is not new to me. The idea of fighting constantly, never knowing where the next assault will come from... it’s an idea that’s not nearly as unsettling as I’m sure you had hoped, Devin. This is what I’ve come to know. This is what my life is. I wake up and I fight. So know that I mean no disrespect when I say that your plan of revenge has not made me regret my choices. A war with Hush has not left me pained. Nor do I fear Chainz, Tyler Rayne, Olsig, or a thousand other radicals you have set free to maim me at a moment’s notice. The obstacles I face every day have left me able and willing, prepared for another constant battle. I still wage the wars of a struggling marriage, of supporting a reckless brother and a sick father. They have not overwhelmed me. They have not beaten me. Neither will this.
