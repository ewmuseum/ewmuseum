There and Back Again (Dual Halo '07)

9 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/09/08 Nova had begun to wonder in recent years if it was still such a good idea – as a matter of policy – to purchase drugs in person, in broad daylight. It’s not that he thought he was such a big shot, the scent of his celebrity would draw paparazzi out of the woodwork like cockroaches (an effective analogy, no?) or anything like that. It’d just be more foolhardy than modest not to concede that in the world of professional wrestling, or sports entertainment, or whatever the hell it had all become, he was a ‘name;’ a marquee player in PRIME’s phenomenal ascent, a force in the New Frontier’s next generation, an indefatigable fellator of his own accomplishments… …you name it. The Risen Star pondered the legality of his circumstances – a rarity in the admitted partier’s daily routine – as he waited in the hallway outside Apartment Three of the G Street Apartment Complex. It struck him as appropriately depressing that everything in the tiny border town of Harlingen, Texas seemed to be dispassionately labeled with a letter or a number. It struck him as quite awkward that he was sharing the hallway with another waiting customer. “That’s a nice stucco print on the wall,” Nova commented, shattering the silence, “I’ve, uh, seen a couple of decent stucco attempts in my day, and I gotta say, they really spared no expense with this place.” “Uh, yeah,” the man replied with a shrug, his voice low and quick. His face was almost completely obscured by the hood of his sweatshirt. “Yeeeeah,” Nova said slowly, nodding his head. He opened his mouth again, but opted out of a another surely-masterful stroke of comic genius. Shhhhhkkktt. Metal shrieked against metal as the peep-area’s metal door slid open and two aggressive brown eyes peered out of Apartment Three’s door. “Just you two?” Nova nodded, and a moment later the sounds of door-locks unlatching could be heard. The door swung open and a lean young man motioned for Nova and his mysterious fellow consumer to come inside. They stepped into a living room area, and the Risen Star’s eyes immediately began adapting to the dim haze of lamplight and smoke. Faces were amorphous in his unadjusted vision, but he could make out a woman in a recliner holding a small child, and several persons seated on a couch in front of a glass coffee table. It wasn’t the first time the former Universal Champion had stood in the low lighting and sweltering early spring heat. The girl in the chair with the kid was…Christine? Either Israel’s woman or his sister, making the admirably quiet kid in her arms either his kid or his nephew. Growing up the son of a drug dealer would surely be an interesting experience, Nova thought to himself. “What’s up, Nov?” Israel said at last, turning and grinning at the Risen Star. Nova interpreted the smoking cigarette hanging out of Israel’s mouth as an invitation, and lit one himself. “Heard you had some good herbs, my friend. I’m not interested in rolling hog-legs out of Mexican shit-weed, but that’s all there seems to be around here. Hook me up with some o’ dat STICKY!” Amused as always at his customer’s vivacity (and who wouldn’t look energetic compared to the lumps on the couch, silent, unmoving, fixed on the TV), Israel slid a black lockbox off the top of a stereo speaker and motioned for Nova to follow him into the kitchen. The mysterious man in the hooded sweatshirt paused a moment before following as well. With a THUNK! Israel hefted the box onto the wooden tabletop of his kitchen-esque table, removed the key from his pocket, and unlocked the steel lid, pushing it back. The aroma that struck Nova’s nostrils was stiffer than the majority of clotheslines he had received in his wrestling career. He almost staggered, his knees growing weak from a sensation that a normal, drug-free man might feel at the sight of a nice car…or something. Peering into the box through misty, incredulous eyes, Nova saw the baggies of fluffy dankness. The crystals. The red hairs. The crystals “Okay,” Nova said, nodding, “that’s what the fuck I’m talking about.” “That’s why it’s sixty-five,” Israel replied, leaving the box’s lid open tantalizingly. “Sixty-five?!” Nova gasped, “dude…dude. Seriously now, don’t split my wig here…sixty-five?” “Sorry, bud,” Israel offered, shrugging his shoulders, “gotta make money, and gettin’ dope that solid down here in fucking Texico is a miracle in ‘n of itself. You’re not plannin’ to take any across the border, are ya?” “Goddamn right I am,” Nova grinned, the white streaks in his blonde mane catching some light from the lamp nearby. “Right into Hell, Israel…Monterrey, Mexico, and a big fuckin’ steel cage. You do me a solid on an ounce?” “Four-fifty,” Israel replied, though it obviously pained him, “only ‘cuz I hate lookin’ like a douchebag for markin’ it up on my boys, but you gotta understand the mileage on this grass It’s like fuckin’ bottled water, dude, seriously.” “My man!” Nova pumped his fist. “Bag it ‘n tag it!” “And you?” Israel said, cocking his eyebrow, and Nova became aware again that there was another person in this little circle of commerce. “What’s good with ya, Matt?” In an all-too-rare moment, memory collided with the Risen Star’s brain like a freight train, connecting the sound of the voice in the hall to the name…to the face revealed as the sweatshirt hood dropped back, revealing… “…Matt? Matt…Mills?” Nova said, peering over at the stubbled pallor-ridden face of the one-time PRIME backstage interviewer-turned-budget cut. “Holy shit, Matt Mills! Dude, what the fuck is going on?” “Thought there was a chance with enough time you might hazard a guess,” Mills replied, his voice immediately more rough and despondent than Nova’d ever heard it. “Dude, this is hilarious,” Nova went on, “I never knew you got blazed, man, I would’ve been chongin’ out with you years ago!” “He ain’t here for smoke,” Israel chimed in, casting a light on the Risen Star’s moment of uncharacteristic naiveté as he removed a baggie of pills from the lockbox and handed them across the table to Mills, who quickly shoved them into his pocket. “Oh,” Nova replied, slightly embarrassed for his inferential error, “well, uh…” “I actually gotta be lockin’ the place down for a little while,” Israel went on, finishing up the bag-roll on Nova’s ounce, “got a buddy comin’ over, so you guys oughta bounce. Swing back through after Mexico if you’re still alive, Nov…we’ll roll up some of that shit. Be careful, though, shit’ll fuck you up, for real. It’s that Piney-Cone-Coneski.” “Ah, the Piney-Cone-Coneski,” Nova repeated lovingly, holding the bag up to his nose and giving it a hearty whiff. “Well alright, man, I’ll see you later.” Mills nodded to Israel and Nova followed him out of the apartment, neither sparing goodbyes for the couch zombies. Out in the hallway Mills immediately started off down towards the darkness of neglected overhead bulbs. “Mills!” Nova called after him, “hey, hold up! I thought…” “Oh, what?” Mills spat, turning around, “what, you wanted to ‘hang out?’” “Well, hell, I just…,” Nova began, but the former interviewer cut him off again. “Why? You bored, Nova?” Mills mocked, “No celebrities around Harlingen to hobnob with? No ladies to call on for a place to sleep?” “I just thought,” Nova replied slowly, “You look, ya know…kind of rough.” “Fuck you,” Mills said before turning back. “Wait now, c’mon!” Nova called again, “listen, I got a big bag of funk here, and you don’t look like you’d turn down a little smoke.” Mills paused. His shoulders softened. He turned back again, the defensiveness gone from his eyes and voice. “No, I wouldn’t.” The sunset cast odd shadows off the playground equipment in one of Harlingen’s small parks. Nova watched the joint smoke drift up into the air, caught in the dying light before disappearing into the night. He passed it over to Mills, practically lounging across the top of a picnic table cemented into the earth. “So Matt…what the hell happened?” Nova asked, smoke shooting from his nostrils in little jets. “I’m honestly shocked you noticed anything was amiss, or that it would be strange to see me buying pills in southern Texas…,” Mills replied, no small amount of bitterness in his voice. “Yeah, Mexico is, like, a hundred times cheaper,” Nova replied, but quickly added, “sorry…” “You know how many times you passed me in the hallways in the weeks and months leading up to my release?” Matt asked, handing the joint back, “how many times I chased you and Deville and Snow, or Rollins, or you and Silver, or any of those assholes down the halls – at my own bodily peril – trying to grab a segment, or a sound byte, or anything? You didn’t care. You were the champ, you had the big names backing you up, and all the fucking air-time you could gobble up terrorizing people in the ring, or shit away sitting in your locker room smoking joints and crying about your life to the whole world. You didn’t notice when I loosened up on the five o’clock shadows, cigarettes outside the back entrance…when I basically trudged around like a zombie for a few weeks after learning that I was getting the axe like some greenhorn no-show.” Nova ignored the might-be shot at his personal life. “That was a while ago, Matt. I was different then.” “Yeah, me too,” Mills replied, spitting. “I had a job.” “I don’t understand that,” Nova said, shaking his head, “with your credentials as an interviewer in PRIME, I’d think you could go anywhere you wanted.” “You’d think…,” Matt shrugged, “but SCCW and GCW are a lot more hungry for PRIME’s talent than they are for the interview team…as awesome as we are. I just wasn’t getting big offers…and I know it’s retarded for me to live like this when technically, I could find a job…somewhere. But that’s the thing, Nova…that’s the real curse with PRIME. Ask Brandon Youngblood. PRIME’s like a big fuckin’ Lotus flower.” Nova was relatively certain the high-octane chiba had gotten to the lightweight, but he was willing to indulge. “Oh yeah?” “Once you’ve had it, really experienced it…,” Mills went on, his eyes staring off at nothing, “…it’s impossible to go back to anything else. I didn’t last more than a couple of weeks anywhere I went. Started drinking a lot. I just missed PRIME. I missed my home. Nothing else was gonna compare to it. And that’s what kills me about all you guys, you, Deville, Wolfenden, Killean, all of you…you just leave. You leave knowing you could come back whenever you wanted, open arms, probably get a fucking World Title shot at the next ReVolution. Do you know what I’d do to get my job back? If management told me I could have my job back if I entered the Dual Halo, I’d be out there in spandex in a heartbeat, gratefully taking a beating from every guy on the roster. But you…” Mills paused, taking the joint back from Nova, hitting it several times rapidly, and handing it back. “You…I bet you got an invite from PRIME to compete in the Dual Halo, and I bet you thought it over. You obviously decided to go for it, otherwise I doubt you’d down here in Shithole, Nowhere. But you thought about it, and I bet you almost tossed that opportunity into the trashcan, didn’t you?” Nova was grateful his woolly beard shielded his reddening cheeks. Truth be told, he had actually thrown one Dual Halo written invite away in the trash, and only decided to compete after receiving a second and drunkenly signing on the dotted line one night. “Yeah, sure you did,” Mills said, nodding. “But I’m telling you, man, coming from someone who’s been washing back oxycodones with whiskey for a week or two…or three…you need to realize what you’ve got – a permanent foot in the door to the greatest wrestling company in the world – and you need to live it up, for all of us out here who would love to…but can’t.” By this time the sun had gone down completely. The only light came from the joint’s cherry, fast diminishing itself as the weed burned down and the boys got lifted up. Suddenly Nova reached into his pocket and removed two tickets, holding them out to Mills. “What are these?” Mills asked. “Those are two VIP passes for Night Two of Culture Shock,” Nova answered, lighting a cigarette, “they, uh, they give ‘em to everyone who competes, ya know, to give to family or something. I don’t have any family, and I figure maybe if you cleaned yourself up, you might be able to grab hold of someone backstage and make a pitch about why they shouldn’t have gotten rid of you. Use the spare ticket and - God forbid – take a woman with you.” Mills rolled the tickets over in his hand. You’d think he was holding the Golden Ticket promised to the winner of the Halo match. “I…thanks, Nova.” “No worries, Mills,” Nova waved dismissively, “sorry for being a dick.” “Meh, you’re not a dick,” Mills grinned, “Silver’s a dick.” Nova laughed. “Alright, I can’t see shit out here anymore, let’s bail. Somewhere you need to go?” “The bar,” Mills replied, sharing the Risen Star’s mirth. “Sounds good,” Nova agreed. As they walked towards the car, Nova felt a vibration in his pocket. He motioned for Mills to head on. “Think I’d better let it go…looks like another love TKO…(oh oh oh)…think I’d better let it go…(What you think about it, girl)…looks like another love TKO…” The smooth sounds of Teddy Pendergrass’ “Love TKO” piped from the tiny speakers in Nova’s phone and he stopped in his tracks, flipping up the receiver. “Yo!” “Hey,” came a female voice from the other end, “you aquí yet, Starboy?” “You hold your horses, chica,” Nova replied amusedly, “I get in tomorrow.” “Tomorrow!” the voice protested, “but these margaritas taste awful when you drink them alone!” “You’re probably the only woman I don’t worry about sitting alone in a bar in Mexico,” Nova grinned, “I PITY THA FOOL WHO MESS WITH LINDSAY TROY!” “Damn right,” Troy affirmed, “weeeell, alright. Guess I’ll get back to fending off well-intentioned Mariachi now. See ya tomorrow.” “Hasta mañana,” Nova replied before hanging up the phone. He turned back to the last rays of pink and purple light protesting their way across the dark sky. The sky would already be black over Monterrey.
