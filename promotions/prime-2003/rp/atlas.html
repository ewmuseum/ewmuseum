Date: Now and April 2007 April 6th, 2007 “Jesus Christ!” His hands trembled by his side, and each small jolt shook free drops of blood that crashed in tiny spots and vanished in the filth of the bar room floor. “What the hell was that?!” Robbie looked up from the broken pieces of table and human body, casting a stare of horror towards Kaiser. “What in God’s name is going on?” Kaiser surveyed the situation, looked his arms up and down, and felt himself get queasy at the sight of all the blood… none of it his own. John and Robbie knelt over the debris left in his wake, and as he watched them tend to their friend, Kaiser could think of no response that would satisfy everyone, so he just yelled “Shit!” “Kaiser,” Robbie started his way, looking left and right to see that nearly everyone in Finny’s Pub was watching him. “What are you thinking? You wanna spend the night in jail? You know he’s gonna press ch…” “He’s not going to press charges!” “What makes you think that?!” “He…” Kaiser stopped, thought better of answering the question, and pulled out his wallet, digging for some cash. “Tell the barkeep I’ll pay for the table.” “What…” Robbie tried to get answers, but Kaiser stuffed a wad of cash in his hand, then turned and walked out the door. The lights of the city pierced his eyes, even with the night’s black sky, and the drizzle of rain, the street was much brighter than life inside the bar. Kaiser again looked down at his arms, saw the blood running in slow trickles around his fingers, falling into the crevices on his palm. Everything was his fault. He’d allowed it to happen. And now… he’d snapped and beaten a guy he’d thought was his friend half-to-death. John and Robbie would have to pick sides, and they wouldn’t pick his. Hell, his own family probably wouldn’t even be able to glance at him without their stomachs churning. One last time, Kaiser looked down at the fists he’d lost control of, then wiped them against his chest, painting his shirt with a violent hue. Still, his hands didn’t come clean, just turning to a blurred mess instead. With a pained sigh, he lowered himself to the ground and took a seat on the wet curb, not ready yet to head home to the family. As he watched a small stream of water run along the edge of the street, carrying small items to the gutter, he cursed the last hour, cursed that night at the pizzeria two months ago, and he wondered if things could possibly get any worse. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Now “You could have transferred. There are numerous stations in the Jackson Fire Department.” Dr. Eric Trowbridge crossed his left leg over his right and pressed his notepad against his thigh. “Why were you and Anthony still working together?” “Why should I have been the one who had to transfer?” “No one said you should, Kaiser. He could have done the same. But if you and others were uncomfortable with the idea of the two of you working together… why not take the necessary steps to remedy the situation?” “I did… eventually.” Kaiser cracked his knuckles as he sat on the tan leather couch. “But a transfer request isn’t processed and approved overnight. As far as I know, it was in the works for me to move to another station when the Jackson Arbor Apartment Complex went up in flames.” “And in the meantime, you and Anthony were simply supposed to keep your distance?” The doctor wedged his pen in the spiral binding of the notepad, and folded his arms across his chest. “That was the idea, but sticking to it when you’ve got dozens of people rushing in and through and around a big-ass blaze… not that simple.” Kaiser’s toes sprung up and down, shaking his legs beneath him. The sessions with Dr. Trowbridge had been his father’s idea, and though Kaiser had initially scoffed at the idea, the common-sense of his father had finally seeped into his skull and led him to seek help. In ninety days, and a half dozen or so sessions, the bouncing of his feet hadn’t subsided a bit. He must have looked like an idiot, sitting there in front of a well-respected psychologist, springing up and down on his toes like some sort of eight year old. “So the two of you wound up going in to the apartment building, and you were the only one to walk out.” “And next thing I knew, I was on trial for murder.” In three months, they’d hardly talked about anything that seemed the least bit relevant. It hadn’t been till the previous session that the two discussed the events that had turned the last year of Kaiser’s life to hell. He wanted to get to the point of something. He wanted the doctor to cut the bullshit. And at a hundred-fifty bucks an hour, his wallet couldn’t afford to let thoughts marinate. “Guilty or not, you must have known as soon as you walked out of the building without Anthony that there would be an investigation.” “ ‘Guilty or not’?” “I’m not implying one way or the other, Kaiser.” “Ok. Well, I’ll tell you this…” For the first time since he’d sat down in the office, Kaiser stopped bouncing in place. With his legs steady, he leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees and clasping his hands. “…I’m not disappointed he’s dead. I don’t miss him a bit.” Kaiser took a deep breath and enjoyed the air that filled the new space in his lungs. “Strong words towards a guy who was your roommate and closest friend at one time.” “He was an act. Truth is… Anthony Barker was a piece of shit in disguise. He was a cocky, arrogant fool who talked too much and thought he was infallible.” “I see.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ April 6th, 2007 “Hey guys! Sorry I’m late… lost track of time.” Anthony yelled over the room as he let the door shut behind him and shook the rain from his hair. Across the pub, Kaiser flipped-shut his cell phone without saying a word. He stared at Anthony, watched as the drops of water flung from his mane, and he waited for his friend to come his way. Tony bounded across the old dirty floor, heading towards his “three amigos” at a table in the back of the bar. “Hey boss!” Robbie slid a Guinness across the table. “Beer’s been waitin’ for ya.” “Yeah. Sorry ‘bout that.” Anthony reached for the beer, but before his fingers could wrap themselves around the jet black bottle, he felt himself shoved sideways, staggering to his right until he heard his ribs crack against the edge of something hard. “What the hell?!” Kaiser removed his shoulder from the sternum of his old college pal and pinned Anthony’s head against the sticky surface of the bar. Holding his target steady with a palm across the neck, Kaiser turned his right fist loose, driving it repeatedly into Anthony’s face. The impact was so savage that his hand bounced back for another swing under its own momentum. “Kaiser… STOP!” Robbie’s voice was loud, the type of scream that popped veins from the neck, but it shrunk into silence under the choked cries of Anthony and the unsettling sound of bare knuckles opening up gorges on a man’s face Each punch found its mark. And like a hammer driving in a railroad spike, each swing was harder than the previous, adrenaline taking over. “Stop!” John put a hand on Kaiser’s shoulder and tried to pull him away as he pleaded. This time, Kaiser heard the pleas and he relented his assault just long enough to grab the bloodied Anth by his shirt collar. Tony’s face was a mess, cut in several places. Blood flowed from every wound and puddled in his eyes, causing him to blink in rapid fashion to try and flush his vision clean. With a guttural noise rising through him, Kaiser tossed his friend toward the round table where his Guinness was waiting, and watched as Anthony fell across the flat surface, busting the table and collapsing into a jumbled mess of blood and beer and splintered wood. With each chaotic breath, Kaiser’s chest heaved as he staggered back a few feet, distancing himself from the carnage. “Jesus Christ!” His hands trembled by his side, and each small jolt shook free drops of blood that crashed in tiny spots and vanished in the filth of the bar room floor. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Now “I want to talk a little bit about you and your family since the events that transpired last year.” Dr. Trowbridge plucked his pen from the pad of paper. He had talked with Kaiser in detail about his relationship with his parents and siblings growing up, but this would be their first conversation about the current state of Kaiser’s family. “I speak to Dave quite a bit. Mom and Dad were pretty supporting… all things considered. Dad especially. Up until he died, he and I talked every couple days.” “And your sister?” “No.” Kaiser dug his fingers into the couch, feeling the leather tear under his nails. “We don’t really talk much anymore. Haven’t seen her since Dad’s funeral. Even then, I think we exchanged ‘hellos’ and a sympathetic nod of the head.” “Why have the two of you slipped away? In our previous conversations, you mentioned that you and Michelle were very close growing up, arguably more so than you and your brother.” “Everything was kinda my fault.” “You didn’t…” “No,” Kaiser bit off the doctor’s thought, and as he did so, he once again began to bounce his toes beneath him, “but it all started with me. And afterward, it all ended with me. Long before Anthony’s death, we got into it at a bar. I just tore into ‘em, and no charges could even be pressed because of everything that would have come to light. I got away with assault to keep everything and everyone else shut up.” “That’s a helluva weight to carry on your shoulders.” “I deserve it. Just like I deserved to stand trial for murder, guilty or not. And just like I deserved to have to quit my job.” “But you quickly found new work.” “Yeah.” “It was an interesting choice.” Dr. Trowbridge jotted something in his notebook, his eyes avoiding Kaiser, watching the pen roll across paper. “Professional wrestling isn’t the typical nine-to-five.” “I enjoy it.” A thought occurred to Kaiser, and in a change from the norm, this time he asked the question. “You actually watch the stuff?” “From time to time. More so recently, since you’ve returned. I have to admit, the first time I saw you on TV, it was a bit of a surprise for me.” “Why’s that?” “When I watch ReVolution, the Kaiser Vashaun I see in PRIME is not the Kaiser Vashaun I meet with in this office.” Dr. Trowbridge looked up from his notes, once again focusing his eyes on his patient. “For weeks, I had the hardest time getting you to open up about any topic. You gave two and three word answers to everything. I see a guy in front of me now who’s private and reserved. I see a guy who’s nervous and burdened by a heavy sense of guilt and responsibility.” “That’s just who I am.” “Then why isn’t that the guy I see on television?” “Who do you see on TV? “I see a guy I’d best describe as a cocky, arrogant fool who talks too much and thinks he’s infallible.” The twitching of Kaiser’s legs stopped abruptly, and he leaned back, allowing himself to sink into the overstuffed comfort of the couch. He didn’t have a response that would please anyone, so he just said “Shit.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ April 6th, 2007 “Check this out. 4 ball, into the 7, corner pocket.” John leaned over the faded green cloth of the pool table, lining up his shot. “If you actually pull that crap off, I’ll buy all your beer for the rest of the damn night.” Robbie leaned back in his chair at the table a few feet away and waited for his chance to laugh. “You better pay up when I make it.” “Just shoot.” Kaiser stood with pool cue in hand, patiently awaiting his turn. “You talk too much.” “Alright, alright.” John sized up the combination, missed, and cursed to himself. “Beautiful shot, Johnny! You’re a regular Mike Massey!” “Whatever. Hey, where the hell is Anth? He was supposed to be here a half hour ago.” “Boy, you sure didn’t waste any time changing the subject.” Rob laughed as he took a drink of his beer. “Anthony went to see a movie with Michelle.” Kaiser took his turn at the pool table, rattling the 4 ball in the side pocket, but failing to sink it. “He should be here soon. They may have gone to grab a bite to eat afterward.” “He better hurry up.” John chalked up his cue. “He’s the only one of you jackasses I can beat at Nine Ball.” “You can’t beat any of us.” “Ehh, piss off, Rob.” Kaiser was about to join in on the banter, but the muffled sound of Bob Seger’s “Hollywood Nights” halted his intentions. Reaching into his pocket, Kaiser pulled out his cell phone and checked the caller I.D. Dave He flipped open the phone, cutting off Seger’s voice mid-chorus. “Hey, what’s up, bro?” “Kaiser…” Something about Dave’s voice was unsettling, and Kaiser took a couple steps away from Robbie and John in an effort to find some sort of privacy. “What’s goin’ on, David?” “Where are you at?” “I’m at Finny’s with John and Robbie. We’re waitin’ for Anth to show up.” “I think you should come over to Mom and Dad’s.” “Why? What’s wrong, David?” “Michelle just got home… she’s a mess, Kaiser.” “What the hell happened?” Kaiser turned and looked over his shoulder. John had abandoned the game of pool and had joined Robbie at the round table. Neither seemed to be paying him any attention. “Did her and Anth get in a fight? Aren’t they too early in the dating process to be having blow up fi…” “Kaiser…Anthony raped her.” “Anth… h…he what?” “Come home.” “Are y..is…is this…?” David started to say something, but his words were lost on Kaiser the second the door to Finny’s swung open. “Hey guys! Sorry I’m late… lost track of time.” Anthony yelled over the room as he let the door shut behind him and shook the rain from his hair. Across the pub, Kaiser flipped-shut his cell phone without saying a word. He stared at Anthony, watched as the drops of water flung from his mane, and he waited for his friend to come his way.
