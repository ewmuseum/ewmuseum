A False Idol (ReVolution 70 - Handicap Match v. The Covenant)

undated

Date: Timeless Dear Karina, I hate myself for writing this, for being reduced to putting to paper words that should flow freely between us, thoughts that should be shared almost telepathically. I feel like I’m in junior high again. I’m too embarrassed to call you, too afraid of your reaction – of finding knives in your eyes – to say it to your face. Besides, this way you can’t interrupt me before I’m done. This way you can’t infuriate me to the point where words won’t come. How did it come to this, Kari? I know how it came to this, Kari, and I’m sorry. I know it's my fault. Like you, I’ve never been very good at this sort of thing. Love Trust doesn’t come very easily to me. It’s an alien emotion, culled out of me by a lifetime of knowing it as the quickest path to pain and death. When I first pursued you, my goal was to make you trust me, but trusting you never entered into my mind. It was a scheme, a ploy to make hate blossom from falsely requited love. Born of the Whirlwind Romance you levelled me with during the Dual Halo, revenge was my motivation. Romance, so to speak, was the furthest thing from my mind. I thought as others did, that you were a callous ring harlot singularly focussed on yourself. I saw manipulating you to feel love and compassion, making you surrender to something more than your pride, as a conquest worthy of my attention, a superfluous feather for The Deville’s cap. People said it was impossible, but that only made the prospect all the more endearing. They were wrong. I was wrong. As I came to know you, you enkindled within my heart and soul a wild fervour of doubt and misgiving, a rapture of hope. I found in my arms the most precious person I’d ever encountered. You challenged and intrigued me. You were genuine. When we spoke, eyes entwined, I knew the trust was real, knew beyond doubt that I’d found someone interested in ME. Not my name, not my wealth, not the grandeur of crushing me underfoot, but me. I’d planned to deceive you with my feelings, but in the end deceived myself. Within that first week the charade was gone. I clung to it like life itself, trying to destroy what we had, but I never stood a chance. It conquered me. You conquered me. I fell for you, Kari. I fell in love with you. My ultimate goal was always the match, and up until a couple of weeks ago, I’d been pursuing it despite my feelings, deluding myself that they were mere consequences of the act, forcing myself to adhere to the plan. When I talked to the doctor and forced his hand, I knew it would piss you off. I banked on it. It was designed to be the ultimate blow to your self-reliance, the major step in the breakdown. In the execution, however, no matter how I tried to convince myself it was the plan, I knew it was out of fear . . . fear of seeing you hurt. The penultimate step was the bloodied shirt, and the final . . . I couldn’t bring myself to the final step. When it loomed ahead, I jumped ship, deciding for the first time in longer than I can remember to trust my heart over my head. I couldn’t go through with it . . . I realized I couldn’t hurt you so deeply . . . because I love loved love you. We’d established a covenant, a bond I couldn’t bring myself to break. But you . . . you snapped it like a twig, smiling at me the whole time. Now I find myself questioning if those people were right all along, if my first instinct was the one I should have followed. Have you been playing me this whole time, as I originally sought to play you? It serves me right, I suppose, but I can’t believe you’ve been deceiving me all along. I can't believe our relationship was fake. I thought we shared more than stubborn pride, more than a desire to dominate and usurp. I thought we were more than a match. Now I’m not so sure. Now . . . Now I think I want to kill you. The high-backed chair clattered to the floor as Deville surged to his feet, angrily crumpling the letter in both fists, savagely tearing it apart like an animal. The shredded fragments of his heart made a neat pile on the desk, but they only survived long enough for Angelo to pull a lighter from his pants pocket and set them ablaze. ... Sorry guys. I had a pwnerful idea for a story to capitalize on the idea, flesh it out, and bring it home, but the motivation to write it went the way of Justin’s workrate. >=) I’ll make up for it with Colossus. Promise.
