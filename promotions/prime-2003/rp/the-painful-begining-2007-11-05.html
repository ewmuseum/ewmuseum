The painful begining

5 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5th November, 2007 If there was anyone outside of Joshua’s family that cared, they might have asked him why the last sixteen days had been so bad. They may have even cared enough to help in his recovery, however no one did. Joshua was unliked, and avoided by almost everyone that knew him. “It’s still a blur.” He had told his father again. The question of what his son had been doing was becoming the only one Joshua’s father seemed to ask. Yet despite the clear concern, despite the fact Joshua’s memory was almost ninety percent complete, he remained consistent in his answer. Both he and Jessica had decided this episode would remain tightly guarded. Better yet Joshua had thought after the fourth painful conversation about it, burned, beaten and buried was how he wanted those sixteen days. So now, three days later and over the painful come down of a reckless attitude to cocaine and pain pills, Joshua began to once again rebuild his controversial career. He needed a new Job having quit his old one in disgust. The company had been almost as happy he was leaving and did not challenge his request. So for the next few weeks at lease, he and his family would be fine. Joshua wasn’t poor by any stretch of the imagination. He had, from the home he kept, cloths he worn and cars he owned saved a lot of money. Joshua’s share of the family gym made him almost as much as his old basic wrestling wage. However, Joshua girlfriend was planning a not at all small wedding. Along with hers, and his daughter obsession with cloths, shoes and small hold nothing at all hand bags, finding proper full time employment was prudent. This is why Joshua found himself sat in his office staring at a number he had long since forgotten. He clearly didn’t like being in this position, the pain of what he was about to do was not only showing on Joshua’s face. It seemed carved into it, like a hundred thousand year old cave after the waters had dried up. Even as Joshua picked up his phone he felt like slamming it back down, but, he push on. Slowly hitting the required keys and listening with dread as it rang. “Hello, Prime Wrestling head office, this is Stacy how may I direct your call?” The voice came bellowing out, perky and high pitched. “I’d like to speak to the director of talent relations.” Joshua answered with all the dullness of a man accepting an unwanted short fall. “Whom may I say is calling?” The perky voice asked a note of anticipation in her voice as if this sort of thing didn’t happen at lease five times a day. “It’s Joshua Taro Freedom.” Joshua again answered in the dull voice. There was a large intake of breath on the other end of the phone and Joshua guessed Stacy had worked for Prime long enough to remember him. Joshua’s last tour with the company lasted just over a year, and was flooded with controversy and a brutality many had called unnecessary and obscene. “Are you really him?” She asked, and Joshua noted all of the perkiness had left her voice. It had also seemed too dropped a little from its peck bring it to a normal human level. “Yes” Joshua answered almost in a sigh. “I’m really him.” Hearing her shock had done nothing but stamp firmly into the ground how badly Joshua position was. “Please Hold.” Was all Joshua heard before Prime’s Revolution theme music came on the line and Joshua pain was giving time to stew. What happened next was too awful for Joshua. It remained the opinion of Prime’s head office that his walking out on the company was not to be forgiven. The resulting request for a contact from Joshua had been met with laughed. Joshua had expected it and knew he would have to do the only thing that he hatred more then anything else in the world. Joshua begged for a job, he had called up his first full time employer and had begged for over an hour for them to forgive his past and rehire him. The reality of what Joshua was doing hurt more then any match he had ever been in. Even in his old Prime days, when he gleefully took himself and Mike Wade off the Big Screen he hadn’t felt this bad. No cut, not even the one he had gotten from the Ambulance match went this deep, and that had needed more stitches then he dared remember. “Okay” Joshua finally said after almost two hours on the phone. “I’ll accept that deal.” With all the strength of a man who had just spent the last two hours bent over the table getting painfully shafted. “Of course you do, but don’t worry. If you draw you’ll get a fair wage. Eventually!” Came the cold reply, the last word filled with the arrogance of a man who knew he was in a powerful position. Then before Joshua could even say goodbye, or thank you, the phone was put down on him. Joshua knew the attitude he had been met with would surely be echoed in the locker room. Joshua was realising he was in real danger of become a whipping boy. Those were the wrestlers that never got a chance and only kept a job so the other works could have someone to bully. Placing the now dead toned receiver down Joshua sighed a resonating sigh. The worse place to be and Joshua had learnt this fact before he could even speak English. The worse place to be within a company was the pocket of people who hatred you. The attitude Joshua felt from the telephone conversation and consequent contact negotiations told him how hatred he was. Joshua knew, knew more then he honest wished, that his next move would set the course for this entire Prime career. Joshua sat back in his chair his mind racing. How to make an impact, someone, someway. Joshua knew the first time they saw him would have to be something special. Prime would have to see him as the man he was, not as a man who begged for a job and was currently in a weakened position. As Joshua left his office, he wondered how far he would finally go and if it would mean someone would quite familiar, find themselves in a hospital.
