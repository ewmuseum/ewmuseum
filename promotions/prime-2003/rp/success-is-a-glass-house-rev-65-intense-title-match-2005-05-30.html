Success Is A Glass House (ReV 65, Intense Title Match)

30 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/30/05 Come See the Stars ofMid-South Championship WrestlingMain EventSupernova vs. “Psycho” Sam AdkissonOther Exciting Matches Include:SWAT vs. Dr. Blood“Hillbilly” Hank Higgins vs. The NaturalAlexa Danger vs. Sharon TateJanuary 15, 2000Canton Civic Center, Canton, MS Well over four years before he will officially drop the prefix of his wrestling moniker, Supernova stands in front of a MSCW promo poster in the backstage area of the Canton Civic Center in northern Mississippi. “Nova! Hey!” Nova turns his head to see his long-time best friend dating back to childhood jogging up to meet him. It will be five years almost to the day before SWAT, angry, hurt, and filled with a jealous rage over Nova’s rising popularity and success in Razor Blade’s Advantage Wrestling, turns on his friend and demands that he choose between his new friend Big Poppa and his tag team partner and childhood compatriot. The steel chair embossed with SWAT’s facial outline will provide the symbol of Nova’s decision…but that is the distant future, a more complicated time. This is the past, and in the past, Supernova and SWAT, collectively known as Lethal Enforcement, are young, hungry, poor…and gifted. “Hey, SWAT,” Nova says with a grin as he shuffles a cigarette out of his pack and lights it, “you seen the ring setup yet?” “Shitty as usual,” SWAT replies, returning the grin. “Ropes too tight?” “Absolutely.” “No give in the ring?” “Of course.” “Extreme risk of serious injury that the proprietors of our thousand-dollar medical insurance policies would laugh at us if we asked them to redeem?” “It’s almost a certainty, I’d say.” Nova grins widely and flicks his cigarette towards an ashtray. “I feel the endorphins buzzing already.” SWAT chuckles, but the mirth evident on his face is quickly replaced by a serious frown. “This could be it, you know.” “What could?” Nova asks absently, his eyes locking back on a gritty black and white photo of himself in some goofy pose adorning the right side of the promo poster on the off-white cement brick wall. SWAT puts a hand on his friend’s shoulder, and Nova turns around to look at him. “Tonight could. Tonight could be it.” Nova scans his partner’s eyes. “Are you high?” “What?” SWAT says with surprise, “No…” Nova breathes a sigh of relief. “Good, ‘cuz neither am I, and if you snuck off to roast a joint without telling me, I’d be righteously pis-” “Come on, man,” SWAT interrupts irritably, “be serious for five seconds. There will be talent scouts here tonight. You know that. You’re the Main Event…the dork on the promo poster.” The comment warrants a laugh from Nova, but SWAT continues. “If you go out there and do what you’ve been doing, they’ll notice…and just maybe you’ll blow this Popsicle stand and head somewhere where your talent isn’t going to waste in un-televised First Blood matches in high school gymnasiums.” The grin disappears from Nova’s face as he claps a hand on SWAT’s shoulder. “And maybe I’ll take your big ass with me.” This time it’s SWAT who laughs, before producing two beers from his pockets and handing one to Nova. “Right, then…” he holds his can up. “To getting out of this redneck hell-hole!” Nova touches his can to that of his boyhood compatriot. “To getting out.” ------*~*~*------ As Nova yanks on generic black kneepads over red tights with single tattered silver lightning bolts on each side, he can hear the crowd of about a hundred cheering sporadically out by ringside. A slightly overweight middle-aged man graying brown hair approaches him. “Hey, kid…you, uh, you Supernova?” “On my better days,” Nova replies with a wink, even at age twenty using the same tired witticisms that he will recycle for the rest of his life. “You’re on in about ten,” the man says plainly before walking off. Nova nods, scratching his chin, still a couple of years away from growing the thick beard that will become his trademark. He looks down to examine tights and his long golden blonde hair falls over his face, unstrained by the bandanna that he hasn’t started wearing yet. He thumbs the “N” of the “NOVA” that’s stitched, in gray felt, across his waistband; it’s coming unraveled. He’ll have to sew it back on after the show. An audible round of cheers and applause causes Nova to turn his head towards the curtain separating ringside from backstage. A moment later, SWAT appears from the other side, panting heavily, his massive frame covered in sweat. More notably, one hand is pressed against his bloody forehead. “What the hell happened?” Nova inquires incredulously. “Fucker tried to bite me!” SWAT growls. “Tried?” Nova repeats, amused. SWAT shoots him a dirty look, but Nova shrugs it off. “So where is Dr. Blood?” “Probably still in the ring,” SWAT says with a grin, “I closed my fist and punched his fuckin’ teeth out.” “Nice,” Nova replies, lighting a cigarette. He offers one to his bleeding friend, but SWAT motions it away. “You know I don’t smoke cigarettes.” “Never too late to get addicted,” Nova says as smoke shoots out of his nostrils. “Isn’t that the antithesis of those smoking ads on TV?” SWAT replies. “How would I know?” Nova fires back, “I’m too poor to afford cable.” This gets them both laughing hard. SWAT wipes a tear from his eye, one hand still on his forehead. “You’re up soon, eh?” “Eh, indeed,” Nova confirms before exhaling a plume of smoke. SWAT claps him on the back, and Nova winces in pain. “Everyone knows you can do it. Everyone’s betting on you to beat that pussy Adkisson’s ass.” “Monetarily?” Nova questions, raising an eyebrow, “because I better see a cut of the…” “Figure of speech, Nova,” SWAT interrupts, rolling his eyes. “Don’t Tread On Me” by Metallica hits with all the audible power of the boombox in Nova’s apartment. “Looks like that’s your cue,” SWAT says, stepping out of the way. “Looks like it,” Nova agrees as the two grip hands and snap. “You got it, baby,” SWAT says. “I know it,” Nova replies, popping his neck and flicking away his cigarette butt. He moves toward the curtain, but a voice stops him in his tracks. “You! You there!” Nova turns around to see an old man dressed in dirty, ragged clothes standing at the base of the stairs. He sports a patch over his left eye, and stares at Nova cautiously with his good one. “You the one they’re cheering for?” Nova hadn’t noticed, but now he realizes there actually are some cheers coming from ringside. “On my better…oh, fuck it. Yeah, that’s me.” “Success is a glass house.” Nova isn’t entirely sure he’s heard correctly, as evidenced by his raised eyebrow. “Begging your pardon?” “They once told me I would be the greatest thing that ever happened to pro wrestling,” the old man goes on obliviously. “Yeah, ‘they’ will say things like that, won’t ‘they’?” Nova says dryly, “listen, old timer…my music is playing, and…” “Goddamn accident,” the old man continues, grumbling, “tornado tag match in ’66…my partner goes down and I don’t see…” “What did you say before?” Nova asks, trying to get back to the old man’s first comment. “I trip over his outstretched feet and hit the mat,” the old man says, ignoring Nova’s question, “one of the guys we were facing was running to drop a leg on my partner and didn’t stop in time after I went down…” “Supernova’s up! Get out there!” a man dressed like a technician yells from a few yards away. Nova pays no attention to him, his eyes fixed on the old man. “He didn’t stop himself in time,” the old man repeats, “and mid-jog one of his boots caught me here…” He points to his temple. “Lights out in my left eye…for good. Success is a glass house, kid. Not so hard to build up, but all it takes is a wrong move and you’re fucked, left standing amidst a pile of broken dreams and goals.” Nova stares at him, entranced. A voice shakes the cobwebs clear. “Supernova, get your ass out to that ring!” He turns and pushes the curtain out of the way, and as he walks out onto the small wooden ramp, he thinks he can hear, faintly, the words of the old man. “Success is a glass house.” ------*~*~*------ Nova reels backwards, catching himself in the ropes and trying to hold himself up. Blood pours down over his face. That son of a bitch! That fucking…son of a bitch! Other than generalized curses directed at the man quickly moving in on him, Nova can barely think. His head is groggy from the botched leg drop that saw “Psycho” Sam Adkisson’s heel land squarely on Nova’s forehead, opening him up and leaving him possibly concussed. Man, I’m fucked…I gotta….man… Nova springs off the ropes with a clothesline, but Adkisson reads him like a junior-level American History book, hooking him under the arm before hoisting him into the air… Fuck! Gotta… …and planting him on the mat with the impact concentrated on Nova’s upper back and head. Lights out. ------*~*~*------ Nova sits on the back steps of the Canton Civic Center smoking a cigarette, his head wrapped in a thick bandage. The talent scouts had ushered Adkisson away to chat it up after the match, while techs dragged Nova out of the ring and helped him to the back. He had looked around for the old man, but hadn’t seen anyone. SWAT had gone to get the car. A man dressed in a worn suit wearing a cowboy hat pushes open the door behind Nova and steps out. “Hey, Nova…next show is tomorrow night in Birmingham at New Life Christian Auditorium…you, uh, you gonna be there?” Nova doesn’t immediately answer, watching his smoke rings climb into the sky. “Oh,” the man continues, “and if you know anyone who needs a job…um…Sam Adkisson got picked up by a guy scouting from Texas…some Lonestar Wrestling or something. So you gonna be in Birmingham for the show?” “Yeah,” Nova replies bitterly, flicking his butt away and watching the last of the scouts make his way out to a black Lexus, “Yeah, I’ll be there.” ------*~*~*------ Nova sits forward in the recliner, watching Lulu rub her chubby fingers across the surfaces of the PTC Extreme Title and PRIME’s 5-Star Title on the floor. He had often thought about that old man from backstage in the Canton Civic Center almost six years ago. He had never seen him again. A week after Sam Adkisson’s upset win, Nova and SWAT were picked up by a fledgling promotion that would quickly shut down, but pave the way for the duo’s eventual introduction to Razor Blade, a man who, before succumbing to paranoia and turning on his friends, played a crucial role in Nova’s professional development. Nova heard some years back that Adkisson didn’t last long in Texas. A coke problem led to missed shows and fights backstage, and shortly after being canned from the promotion, “Psycho” Sam was found spinning from a ceiling fan, a leather belt clamped around his neck and a poorly-written suicide note on the dresser that no one bothered to read besides the police. “Success is a glass house.” Watching his daughter delight in the golden shine of the belts, Nova can still hear the old coot’s sage-like…well, Nova had never quite figured out what the words were…possibly advice, perhaps a warning, or maybe a little bit of both. Nova can appreciate the contextual importance and coincidence of the old man’s words in his current situation, as he is slated for ReVolution 65 to face TCM, Aimz, Lopez, Nick Brocken, and Mystery Excitement inside a structure dubbed “The Glass House.” “Goo…goo-ahheee!” Lulu squeals with delight as Ariel walks into the room and scoops her up into her arms. “Oh, God, you need changing. Phew…” Nova gets out of the chair and opens the front door. He walks out onto the porch and sits down in a rocking chair, pulling a cigarette out of his hard pack. The words of an article he had read online had been echoing in his head for days. He knew not to let net columnists get to him, but the problem with this particular article was that it echoed his own fears about his success in PRIME. He can’t remember it word-for-word, but the gist of it was that Culture Shock 2005 had determined the ultimate hierarchy of the new PRIME. It wasn’t universal; the elimination order in the Dual Halo didn’t number-for-number determine where the superstar(s) in question would wind up on the pecking order, but it gave a decent outline. Nova was focused on particularly in the article because of his recent rise in stock with the company. He was one of the hot names on the mouths of many of PRIME’s insiders and outsiders, as a two-time 5-Star Champion, a worthy PTC representative, and possibly a future Universal Title contender… …but, this article claimed, Culture Shock showed where Nova was destined to remain in PRIME…near the top, respectably, but never the number one guy. Why? The article listed five reasons: Aimz, Emilio Rage, Brandon Youngblood, Angelo Devillo, and Karina Wolfenden…the five superstars who outlasted Nova in the Dual Halo. The writer admitted it was really four reasons, as Rage had fallen out of the picture. In his tenure with PRIME, Nova had yet to beat any of the people who outlasted him in Halo One. Ultimately, the article claimed, he probably never would. The writer commented with “all due respect” that Nova was a great athlete, a sure asset to PRIME’s roster and one of the few names that had built himself up from no recognition to stardom in PRIME without a pre-existing reputation in that circle of talent… …yet again there was a “but.” The article cited the people that Nova had beaten in his career with PRIME…Ozric Mortimer, Dani Furher, JTF, Tyler Lopez (the article went as far as to claim that Tyler Lopez was far and away responsible for bolstering Nova’s win-loss record, and this got a laugh out of Nova). Hoyt Williams was a solid victory, but as for the rest…they were talents, all of them, but not the faces of the new PRIME, not the future Universal Champions and Hall of Famers. The writer felt confident that his theory about the Dual Halo’s hierarchical significance would be proven at ReV 65 where Aimz will pin Nova for the second time and walk away with the Intense Title, one of the top five (or now four) in the company…remaining that way. I can’t get the words of the article out of my head because I’ve already made this connection to those who survived me in the Halo. I know I haven’t gotten a win over the K-Wolf or the Deville. Same with Youngblood…and it was Aimz who pinned me in the Halo match. I know Aimz is the biggest threat to me in the Intense Title match. She and I both picked up momentum heading towards Overkill…I managed to keep it, and she fell victim to the K-Wolf Onslaught…but that’s irrelevant. If you’ve been with the company for four months or longer and haven’t lost to Wolfenden, raise your hand… …and no, Deville, you don’t count. I can’t let myself think for a moment that Aimz is less dangerous because she lost at Overkill. If I don’t keep at least one eye on her the entire time, I’ll be staring up at fluorescent hospital head lamps before I know it. Truth be told, I probably will be anyway. Brocken…shit, I don’t know what to think. Oz was my boy. I know that. I haven’t met “Nick” face to face yet, so I don’t know if we’re still cool or not. Who knows, I may extend my hand and get Babe Ruth-ed into next week with a steel chair… …it’s happened before. Time will time if friendship is stronger than gimmick change. Brocken’s damn good, but this isn’t his time…yet. Trashcan Man…so close to breaking out after a disappointing string of losses, and getting better every week. Not taking him seriously as a contender is potentially lethal, but again, I just don’t see this as his match. I could see it as his rematch, however. Tyler Lopez…umm…right. If I had a quart of ice cream for every time I’ve kicked Lopez’s ass, I’d be the fattest mother-fucker alive. As for the Mystery Opponent, I don’t have time to come up with possible identities and attempt some sort of theoretical strategy. For whoever it is, let ‘em be content to know that there’s a pair of size thirteens waiting to bruise that tailbone. “Success is a glass house.” That may be true. On Wednesday, at least one glass house will be broken. I’m hoping the right one stays in tact. “I was thinking about what a friend had said,I was hoping it was a lie.” - Neil Young, “After the Gold Rush,” After the Gold Rush “Rumble, young man, rumble!” - Muhammad Ali
