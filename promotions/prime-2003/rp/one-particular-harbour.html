One Particular Harbour

Date: March 22, 2009/April 3, 2001 There was this vista. This one, perfect, arrow-shaped rocky outcrop carved out of the coastline by the combined forces of wind, lava and raging ocean over the course of countless millennia. Look straight down, and you find a beach. The kind of small, castaway inlet that was too insignificant to be overwhelmed by resorts and hotels, swarmed with beach chairs, umbrellas and tourists or cheesy, open-air tiki bars. The only sign that this little cove hadn't been transported back in time to before the Islands were settled was the occasional sound of a fishing vessel off in the distance or a lone surfer paddling by, trying to find the perfect swell. Beyond the white sand on the shore was the endless, crystal-blue expanse of the Pacific Ocean, undisturbed except for a few foamy whitecaps poking out from the surface. It was a hike to reach this place, off the road and through some dense, sticky Hawaiian jungle that was chock full of bugs in sizes that are normally reserved for 'B' horror movies. But, with the right effort, and the right map to guide you, there it was: an unspoiled, uncut diamond in the midst of commercialized tourist traps. Of course, the man standing on the vista at this moment didn't need a map. He knew the way from memory. ***** Eight years earlier... "You're sure this is the right way?" Troy was sure. "You do know that's the fifth time you've asked that, right?" Answering a question with another question was a surefire way to annoy her, and Troy knew it. Knowing someone for 18 years tends to reveal a few personality quirks. "Pardon me for doubting your faultless sense of direction, Mister Got-Us-Lost-In-Florida-When-He-Held-The-Map-Upside-Down." Troy just smirked. Didn't really have a good comeback for that one. Didn't matter, either. They waded their way through yet another grove of palm trees, Troy taking the lead as he saw the final marker on the way to their destination. Breathing in deep, Troy wiped the remaining sweat off his brow and turned left to the last clearing. "And you doubted my ability to follow directions, Lauren." His girlfriend, his best friend, stepped through the clearing onto the vista, and by the look on her face, Troy knew the hike had been worthwhile. He saw the flash from deep within those brilliant, chocolate brown eyes, the captivated smile that had entranced him since they were seven years old, and any questions about taking his girlfriend on a trek up a small mountain in the Hawaiian jungle leapt from his mind. This was the spot. "Not bad, huh?" "I'll say," she answered. "How'd you find out about this place?" "Hula Bowl week, senior year of college. They took us on a chopper tour around the islands, and I saw this spot. Tour guide said people almost never came up here, but the trails were all marked. I came up the next day, and that's when I knew I had to bring you up here one day." "Aww, always the hopeless romantic." You have no idea. "So," she said. "What else makes this place so special?" Troy smiled at her, looked at the watch, then out to the horizon. "Wait for it..." "For what?" It didn't take long for her to find out. The seconds ticked away on Troy's watch, and when he saw the clock strike 6:40 p.m., he was ready. He'd gone over this in his head a million times before. "For this," he said. Then, he pointed her to the horizon, and she saw it. The sun dipped down over the Pacific Ocean, and the clear, bright, sky blue day turned to a brilliant shade of purple, dotted by the gold sun bouncing off the surface of the water for miles and miles. She only had one thing to say. "Wow." As she gazed across the ocean, Troy fumbled in his right pocket, securing his hand around the most important piece of the puzzle. "You know, that's what I see every day when I look at you." The inherent cheesiness of the line broke Lauren from her mesmerized state, but what she saw when she turned around stunned her even more. Troy Douglas, all six feet, five inches of him, kneeling on the ground, holding out his right hand, in which he held the small velvet box he'd pulled from his pocket, a brilliant gold ring with one diamond set into it sitting right in the middle. It was cliche, and Troy knew it, but for all his football player-turned-pro wrestler tough-guy exterior, he was a gigantic sap at heart, and this was too perfect. He'd realized it three years ago when he first made the hike to find this place, and when he knew he'd be getting some much-needed vacation time, he booked the trip to Hawaii immediately and surreptitiously went to the jewelry store the very next day. He'd been writing this speech in his head for three years, but now that he was finally giving it, the words came out breathlessly. "Every morning I wake up next to you, I already know it's a better day than the one before, and I never want that sensation to end. Not a lot has been constant in my life, but for as long as I can remember, you've been there. You were there when I was stuck in that hospital, you were there to help me through months and months of rehab, and somehow you stuck by me when I decided to reject a surefire job offer and make a living wearing spandex and rolling around with large, angry men. "I decided a long time ago I wanted to spend the rest of my life with you. I was just ... err ... waiting for the right moment to ask you. So, how about it? Lauren Bennett, will you marry me?" There weren't any words. There didn't need to be. ***** The present day... Eight years, and he still new the way. The yellow flags marking the way had frayed and faded, but Troy didn't exactly need them to find the spot. Still, he paused to rub his thumb and finger against the final marker before taking the final step of his journey. He hadn't returned to the islands since that day, but when the winds of PRIME sent him back to Hawaii, he knew there'd be a certain stop to make. As he stepped through the clearing onto the rocky point, he reached into his pocket, just like eight years before, and pulled out the same thing he had that time. The ring had sat in a drawer, untouched, for just over five years, and it was only when he found out he'd be heading back to Hawaii that he pulled it out of mothballs. Just as before, he waited for the sun to set over the water, then pulled out the ring. And then... Well, he wasn't sure what to do. When he'd taken the ring out of it's resting place, for some reason he thought he'd emulate the end of Titanic and toss the ring into the ocean as some kind of parting gesture. Standing there, on the very spot he'd proposed marriage eight years ago, he realized he wasn't quite ready to do that. Plus, he'd always kind of hated that movie. So, he looked at the setting sun refracting through the brilliant diamond, set the ring back in his pocket, leaned back against a boulder and watched as the daylight made its final journey across the horizon line and evening began to fall on Maui. "The feeling's still there."
