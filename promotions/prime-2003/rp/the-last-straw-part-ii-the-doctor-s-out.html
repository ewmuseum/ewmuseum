I can’t believe how comfortable I feel around her. I swear, I act like a goofy six-year-old when I’m with 'er sometimes.” “Nothing new there. You’ve always been like that.” “Yeah… around the family… you… Michelle… that’s it. But I don’t even give it a second thought around her. And for whatever reason, she just laughs and keeps agreeing to go on dates with me.” “Guess she’s just outta her mind.” “Well, that’d make two of us.” Kaiser grabbed a hoodie that was balled up on the arm rest of the couch and gave a quick wave to his brother. “You can hang here if ya want… I gotta get rollin’ though. I don’t think we’ve been on enough dates for me to show up late.” “How many is this?” “Six… I think?” “Nope, not enough yet.” “Later, Dave.” Kaiser slammed the door behind him. Kicked back in the recliner, David waited till saw the headlights from Kaiser’s car turn and vanish up the road. As he pushed himself out of the chair, Dave immediately felt his intestines turn to lead. He hadn’t even done anything yet, and already the guilt was heavy. Not heavy enough to weigh him down, though. Based on what he knew now… he wanted answers. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Dave Two Days Earlier “Excuse me, ma’am.” Dave Vashaun looked around the Psyc department office. It’d been nearly eight years since he’d even set foot on a campus, and never UM’s, but immediately his mind was plain of stampeding memories. All the cubicles were occupied by women, and no one working in the office was between the ages of 30 and 60. Upper classmen... or near retirees. The room even smelled like college. “Yes, sir?” An elderly women, looked up from the copy machine she had been hovering over. Her hair was solid grey and heavily permed. She wore large glasses with the creepy beaded chain attached to them, but her smile was kind, and ridded of the uncomfortable feeling Dave had been swallowed by when his brain had kicked into memory-overload. “What can I do for you?” “Uh, I was hoping you could help clear a couple things up for me. Was there a… uh Dr. Trowbridge who worked at the University? Eric Trowbridge?” “Of course. Dr. Trowbridge spent a great number of years with this university… many of them as head of the Psychology department. It was a bit of a sad day around this place when he retired.” “Yeah,” Dave removed his coat, tucking it under his left arm. As he did so, he blushed… wondering if it appeared like he planned on staying a while to chat it up with some total stranger about a former teacher. “I heard he retired in 2007.” “To the disappointment of many. The students loved him. And no matter their background or personality, he always found a way to connect.” The women chuckled, and again, her response put Dave at ease. “Not surprising for a doctor of psychology, I suppose.” “Hehe… I suppose, not.” “So was there anything else you wanted to know about Doc, or the UM Psychology Department? Do you know someone enrolled in the program?” “A good buddy of mine… his sister, Kylie Kenderson… she’s a grad student hear.” “Oh… I know Kylie! She used to work Tuesdays and Thursdays hear in the office. Very intelligent young lady.” “Yup. Her brother was the one who was telling me how Kylie had been disappointed when Dr. Trowbridge had left the university. To be honest… that’s why I’m here. I was surprised to hear that he’d retired. I have a… friend who claims he’s been seeing Dr. Trowbridge for the last few months. I was wondering if you know whether or not the doctor has continued any sort of private practice since retiring from UM?” “Oh.” The elderly woman let her head drop for just a second, then looked back up at David… leaving her soft smile behind. “I’m afraid Dr. Trowbridge passed away at the beginning of this year. He’d been battling cancer.” “I’m sorry to hear that. I must have just misunderstood my friend.” Dave fought to keep the words protected from the seething nature that once again returned the red hue to his face. “Thank you for all your help…” “Mary Ann.” “Thank you, Mary Ann.” Dave shook the woman’s hand, then turned and headed for the staircase. His firmly planted footsteps echoed up and down the steps, and as he reached the exit to the building, he left his jacket tucked under his arm. With his blood popping and bubbling at a near boil, he wouldn’t need the coat to keep heated. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Couple Wednesday, December 3rd “They don’t make these chairs with guys like me in mind.” Performing a cautious balancing act with a large bag of popcorn in his right hand and a 32oz Coke in his left, Kaiser shook and shoved his way into his seat, stretching the plastic arm rest at his side. “I think this place has unusually small seating. Well, this ogre is going to gorge himself on popcorn… so I guess you’ll have to go get your own.” “This is date number five… six? At this point, I’m allowed to call you anything I want.” Ellie stuck out her tongue and made a rather goofy face. As Kaiser laughed, she took the opportunity to grab a fistful of the forbidden popcorn. “Seriously. Get your own.” Kaiser moved the bag into his left hand and nudged Ellie away with his right arm. “You better share. In fact, you better be grateful I came to see this with you anyways. Vince Vaughn is creepy. And Four Christmases? That doesn’t even make sense.” “Yeah, but it’s got Tim McGraw in it.” “Your man-crush on Tim is kinda weird.” “What’s weirder is that you’ll still date me.” “Not if you don’t let me have some of the popcorn.” Again, Elle reached, this time stretching over Kaiser’s frame, and again… she came away with the goods. “Hey,” The guy behind Elle leaned forward. “could you two keep it down a bit.” “Geez, pal. That way… I don’t have to yell at you and that guy can listen to his Akon. “Fine.” “So how’s work?” “I love that you call it ‘work’… like it were any old nine-to-five.” “Ok… how’s your wrestling job?” “Haha… better.” Kaiser took a drink from his Coke. “And I told you, my therapist says its better not to bring ‘work’ home.” “How’s therapy goin?” “Good.” “You sure?” “Yeah. Why?” “I dunno, I just thought I heard Dave say something at work the other day about it.” “What did he say?” Kaiser’s left hand tightened, clenching the guts of the popcorn, causing a few kernals to spill over top and land on the floor, joining a month’s worth of filth. “Didn’t really catch it. He was talking with Jerry. Something about some problem with the doctor you’re seeing.” “Oh.” “Is something wrong?” “No.” Kaiser stared, almost blankly, at the glow of the theater screen. “But I should probably tell you something.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Dave Wednesday, December 3rd David crossed the living room and headed for Kaiser’s make-shift office.
