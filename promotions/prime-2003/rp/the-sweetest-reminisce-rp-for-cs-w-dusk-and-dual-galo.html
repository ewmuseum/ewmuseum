Date: Today Grotesquery Less Than Two Weeks From Now… Jonathan Winters finds himself in complete awe as he stands in the Culture Shock arena, utterly overshadowed by the monstrous Dual Halo construct. It has been a rough few months for Winters outside of the wrestling ring, but staring at grotesque structure he shakes his head with apprehension, silently concluding, and things are about to get a hell of a lot worse. But that’s okay. Everything that’s happened with his former love, Aeryn, and his on/off employer, Cornelius Stakiri, has drained him and competing in the Dual Halo event will only serve to regenerate him. A strange concept to fully consume, but Winters’ outlook nonetheless. Most entering the competition see it as a means to an end, as task they must overcome in order to achieve their goals. That simply isn’t the case for Winters. He enjoys the fight of it, the competition and more predominant, the violence that comes right along with it. It’s a joyful release, a cathartic exercise that always serves to relax him. This is his reward for two solid months of hell and he intends to savour every moment of it. It’s early afternoon now and the arena is still empty to a degree, with PRIME technical staff scuttling around, making their final preparations. Soon every seat in the building will be occupied and the carnage can begin. Soon dreams will be created and shattered, all within a blink of the eye. Winters’ general unwavering disposition has thus far prevented him from making any kind of allegiances within the organisation and has attracted a substantial amount of enemies. Even though the very nature of the Dual Halo event adopts the well cliched philosophy of every man for himself, certain well placed and well timed allegiances will undoubtedly make all the difference. Will or can anyone trust me enough to make any such tactical venture? Winters chuckles to himself at the very notion. His reputation has been firmly entrenched within the locker room for a long time and trustworthy wasn’t exactly the best characteristic that could be attributed to him. Fuck ‘em, Winters silently declared. I don’t need anyone’s help. I don’t want anybody’s assistance. And I sure as hell have no intention of performing an archaic ‘team up’ routine. Fuck. That. Winters turns his back to the manmade atrocity and heads back toward the backstage area, muttering under his breath, “See you in a few hours.” *** Stratagem Yesterday… The Stakiri building, located deep within the heart of Manhattan, was not how Winters remembered it. It’d only been a matter of weeks since he last stepped foot in the building and already the security guards, systems, codes and routines had changed to what Winters had memorised them to be. Winters nodded his head with approval, as the security detail lead him along the elegance marble laced corridors, directing him towards one of Stakiri’s more secured offices. Winters grinned. Stakiri message was clear. He still didn’t trust Winters as much as he once did and his arrogance and general cocky attitude had obviously evaporated in the midst of war. It’s about damn time, Winters silently exclaimed, making sure to stay a few steps behind the last security guard, just in case something unexpected were to happen. Winters relationship with his former employer had been strenuous over the past couple of months, hell, it’d been strenuous ever since the two of them met, over two years ago when Winters first awoke in that damned Kansas cornfield, without a memory to his damn name. Much later, he discovered much later that Stakiri had access to his previous life and could’ve been responsible for the loss of memory in the first place. It was a long story, but in short, there had always been a certain distrust amongst the two, it’s just that war had heightened it to intolerable levels. Winters idly suspected that it was the rival underworld organisation that’d sown the seeds of doubt in both of their minds, but he was too proud and stubborn to admit such a truth and so was Stakiri. He realised the tension between the two would never truly evaporate, but that was not his main concern right about now. Aeryn was. The security detail shepherded him into the space office and closed the sturdy oak doors behind them. Stakiri was already sat behind his delicately carved desk, his eyes never leaving Winters even for a second. Winters eyed the table up warily. No doubt there was a panic button carved into the underbelly of the table with an entire squad of his combat ready men, just waiting to take a chunk out of Winters’ ass. After a few seconds of awkward tension, Stakiri smiled and directed Winters to the chair directly opposite him. “Please Jonathan, take a seat.” Winters nodded a reluctant response and slumped down into the hard wooden chair, squirming a little in order to get comfortable. In the end, he stopped, convinced there was no comfortable position to sit in the damned things. “So,” Winters began, establishing a reluctant eye contact with his former employer, “how fares the war?” Six months ago, Stakiri had attempted to undermine a rival business organisation’s research project because it‘s progress was far ahead of Stakiri Corp‘s own. It didn’t turn out too well. Similar to Stakiri’s setup, the rival organisation had strong links with the underworld and they didn’t take too kindly to industrial sabotage. There was no talk, no negotiations. War was declared right then and there and it’d been raging ever since. The first victim in the debacle was Aeryn. She was sent to disrupt the rival research project by Stakiri and had come back a changed woman. Literally. The enemy had used memory manipulation techniques so often utilised by the United States government to turn her against her employer and help with the rival organisations war effort. She betrayed Stakiri and, more importantly, she betrayed the man she’d once claimed to have loved; Jonathan Winters. Stakiri rolled his eyes at the question and provided Winters with an already perfectly translucent notion, but followed it up with details, regardless. “Perplexing. They’re still playing to the minor hoods and racketeers greed, funding them to destabilise my power base with frequent attacks. There’s a new play for my territory on a daily basis and there doesn’t appear to be an end in sight. The streets are at war, Jonathan, I can hear the gunfire even from here.” Winters fought the urge to roll his eyes, dismissively. Stakiri had always withheld a certain panache for melodramatics and Winters found himself somewhat unsurprised the habit had not been lost in short absence from his employ. It was just another one of Stakiri’s less than subtle ploys to appeal to Winters long lost sense of loyalty and to, hopefully, coax him back into the fold. Truth be told, there was no need for his former employer to hide behind poorly crafted pretence and Winters quickly became sick of it. “We both know why we’re here, so let’s get right to business.” Stakiri nodded solemnly. “Aeryn.” “I believe she is the key to tipping the tide of this little war of yours,” Winters told him, his facial expression purposefully unreadable. “I think she should be our first target.” Stakiri grinned and leant back in his chair. “But of course you do.” Winters ignored the jibe. Aeryn was his one weakness and, what’s more, Stakiri knew precisely how to exploit it. “Are we in agreement?” Stakiri’s grin widened. “Yes.” “How shall we proceed?” Winters questioned. “I’ve already assembled a team and instructed them to follow your exact orders,” Stakiri told him. “My intelligence operatives have already been issued their mandate to find her. We should have some preliminary locations within the hour.” *** Hunt Yesterday… More than aware that the team surrounding him breathed heavily, Winters grinned to himself as they grouped together around the target apartment door and made their final preparations. Minutes earlier, he had fully prepared them for what they were about to face and in doing so put the fear of god in them. Scared is good, Winters silently deliberated, as the apartment door flew open, half ripped from it’s hinges thanks the sturdy boot of the squad point man. Stakiri had passed the location onto Winters as soon as his employers intelligence units had reported it. Apparently, a number of reliable sources noted a woman meeting Aeryn’s description entering and exiting the building on a regular basis over the last five days or so. They mobilised immediately, making use of Stakiri’s overly expensive personal helicopter. The team of five quickly filtered into the apartment, securing each room before moving onto the next. Winters already knew Aeryn was long gone. If she was there, the screams and gunfire would’ve already sought his attention. Winters promptly entered in after them, ignoring established protocol and the fervent signals of the last man guarding the door. He sighed as he walked past, waiting for them to finally draw the same conclusion he had. In the end, he got tired of waiting. “It’s okay, boys,” he declared, not even trying to hide the disappointed buried deeply in his voice. “She’s long gone.” “Sir!” One of them emerged from a back room, his face contorted with transparent concern, pointing back toward the room he just exited. “You have to see this.” Confused, Winters followed the squad member into the back room, hit instantly by the foul smell of sweat and vomit. After taking a moment to adjust, he saw the body lay prone upon the bare wooden floor. She had a slender frame and long black hair. The resemblance was undeniable. No! He silently exclaimed. It felt as though somebody had reached right inside his chest and held his heart with a firm, undeniable grip. It can’t be her…! It’s not her…! Aeryn…? Winters drew closer and knelt beside the body, examining it closely. “Do you think that’s wise, sir?” The squad member questioned. Now he was certain his warning regarding Aeryn had struck a chord, but very much doubted Aeryn could pull this off. At least not intentionally. Winters nodded toward woman’s chest. “She’s not breathing, kid. Hasn’t been since we entered the room.” “Dead?” Winters shot the man an icy glare. “I should hope so.” Winters turned his attention back toward the body, desperately building up the courage to look at it’s face. It’s not her, it’s not her, it’s not her, he repeated over and over, quickly flipping the body over onto its front. He stared at the dead woman’s face for the longest time, make sure the featured were firmly chiselled into his mind. “Well?” Stakiri’s solider questioned. “Is it her?” Winters closed his eyes and stood. “No.” He had to admit, the resemblance was uncanny and could fully understand how the link was made, but this woman’s features were not as pristine as Aeryn’s and the skin colour was a couple of shades darker. Thank god! He breathed a sigh of relief. “What now, sir? Winters opened his eyes and snapped back to the task at hand. “Move out. We’ll throw an anonymous call to the police later, to pick up the bo--” Winters face scrunched up as other, less obvious details within the room suddenly leapt out at him. The emptied candy wrappers lay strewn all over the floor, white chocolate Crunch bars, Aeryn’s favourite. Coincidence? Then, an open copy of Empire film magazine, lay open at the picture of Aeryn’s favourite movie, Fight Club. His eye’s wildly scanned the room and more and more links to Aeryn jumped out at him all at once. This was no coincidence. He was supposed to see this. And that meant somewhere, deep down, there was still a small part of he left. There was hope. “Sir?” Winters broke free of his reverie. “The Intel isn’t inaccurate. She’s been here.” He glared at the man sternly. “Aeryn’s been here.” *** Manifestation Today… Winters bolted upright in his bed, all of a sudden, his eyes encrusted with sleep and his forehead dripping with sweat. Rubbing his eyes clean with clenched fists, he idly wondered what had caused him to wake so abruptly. He couldn’t recall any such nightmare, nor was there anything lingering at the back of his mind. He pushed the covers away and threw legs over the side of his bed, promptly participating in a lengthy yawn. However, mid-yawn, he heard a noise. Eyebrows raised, he pulled on a pair of jeans and stood, wandering out of his bedroom door. As he walked, his eyes darted from side to side wildly, desperately searching for it’s source. He entered the living room and still found nothing. Or so he thought. A cool breeze from the left of him altered his senses and immediately, he knew something was wrong. Preparing himself for action, he whirled around toward the open window. What the fuck…? He silently questioned, taking one last glance around the place. It was still clear, which could mean only one thing; whoever the hell had been in here had already left. But why? He silently questioned, unable to accumulate a discernable answer. Quickly, he climbed out of the window and stepped awkwardly onto the adjoining fire escape, wincing at the cold metal surface under his bare feet. Despite the exterior darkness, there was enough moonlight for him to see the alleyway below was clear. The only option left was upward, But where could the perpetrator go from there? He silently questioned, already bounding up the steps. Unless it was a trap… He paused mid-step and glared upward, before continuing his journey. Then I guess I’ll have to spring it! Upon reaching the building rooftop, his eyes scrutinised the layout, searching for something, anything that could be described as a threat. The last team this happened, he found a team of Stakiri’s best men ready to take down. Turned out Aeryn had been the one to paint a target on Winters back. It was one the very last acts of her betrayal, before the truth finally came out. However, this time, there was nothing to be seen. That illusion faded fast. As soon as Winters turned to leave the area, he heard another noise; the sound of scuffling feet against sharp gravel. His eyes darted back towards the building top and quickly picked up on the darkened figure emerge from behind a release funnel at the centre of the construct. The slender frame as unmistakeable to Winters, even at that distance. “Aeryn…?” He muttered, apprehensively rushing onto the rooftop. The woman remained silent, but walked forward into the undeniable glare of the clear moonlight glimmer. It was Aeryn, but recognising the stern expression etched into her face, he already knew it wasn’t the Aeryn he remembered, it wasn‘t the Aeryn who’d left the clues all around with the dead woman, in the apartment he and his team raided the previous night. Or perhaps it was, he grimly concluded, and that was the whole point.I’ve been played by her. Again. Aeryn leapt forward, relinquishing a powerful right hand that struck Winters hard across his jaw. He stumbled over to his right, keeping himself upright with an outstretched hand. The last time the two of them met, things had turned out pretty much the same as this. The only difference was, Aeryn caught him by surprise. At the time, he didn’t actually believe that she’d hurt him, but now he was under no such illusion. As Aeryn thrust forward with a follow up attack, Winters blocked the wild boot and used her lack of position to formulate an appropriate counter attack. He surged forward, catching his former lover around the jaw with an abusive forearm, sending the woman sprawling to the lose gravel flooring. “Perhaps this time,” he began, looming over her, “I’ll put up a better fight.” She glared up at him and hissed at him, before getting back to her feet and lunging at him a second time. This time, he was equally as prepared as before, catching her first with ease, and used his superior strength to overpower her. “Give it up now,” Winters told her. “Stakiri can help you. He can undo all of the false memories. He can restore you to the woman I love.” Finding him to be sufficiently distracting, Aeryn reached inside her pants and produced a nasty-looking blade and threatened to wield. However, she paused before propelling it forward and developed a confused expression. Winters glared down at the knife and then established eye contact. He knew then she was back with him and for the first time in two weeks, he was looking at the woman he fell in love all of the months ago. The innocence, the love and the fear were buried deep within her eyes. Winters released his grip and took a few steps backward. She glared at him confused at first and then noticed the knife in her hand. She dropped it immediately, covering her wide open mouth with both hands, the tears already descending her delicate cheeks. “Jonathan…?” He rushed forward, scooping her up with both hands. It’s her! It’s really her… “It’s okay, it’s okay…” “What happened?” She sobbed. “Why--” Winters withdrew his embrace, wondering what in the hell happened to the end of that sentence and saw it in her eyes again. The innocence had evaporated as though it were a mere dream and all that remained now was anger. She drove her head forward, connecting soundly with his nose. He reeled backward, already feeling the sticky wet substance stream from his nose. “What the fuck…?” “You’re a fucking pussy, Winters,” she accused circling him, aggressively. “I can’t believe you fell for that old trick.” She was lying, he could tell it in her voice. The old Aeryn had forced herself to the surface and now this cheap façade was desperately attempting to cover it up. Despite the relapse, Winters knew then there was still hope left and that’s all he really needed to hear. Aeryn lunged forward again, but this time Winters sidestepped and honed a well-placed elbow to the back of her neck. She fell to the floor immediately, consciousness leaving her completely. He hauled the prone body over his shoulder and descended the fire escape. Soon, the woman he loved would return to him and his life could recapture the briefest glimmer of normalcy once again. Or, so he hoped. *** Vigil One Week From Now… Winters stands over her bed, glaring at the colour display of the medical equipment at the same time desperately trying to hone out the annoying sounds that accompany it. Stakiri had told him her recovery would be long and arduous, but the wait did not concern Winters in the slightest. The doctors had already explained that her false memories are losing their potency and her old ones are fight to the forefront of her mind. There is more than hope now, there’s certainty and it will be this certainty that will carry him forward. Although Aeryn will not make it to Culture Shock, she will still be with him, providing that extra energy to persevere, to continue the fight. And for now, that’s good enough.
