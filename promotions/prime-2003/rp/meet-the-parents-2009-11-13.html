Meet the Parents

13 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/13/2009 "You know, the more I think about it, this really is one ugly damn tie." Troy Douglas slouched in front of the full-length mirror, parsing the image he saw with disdain. Nearly 34 years old, and he still couldn't make himself feel comfortable in a suit. Something about it always made him feel completely awkward and cramped. Craning his neck, Troy yanked the blue silk tie from his neck, flipping it recklessly across the room. He ducked down and opened his suitcase, flinging socks and underwear haphazardly across his hotel room until he fished out his quarry, a dark red tie that happened to be the only other one he'd packed -- and only because he'd forgotten to dig it out of his suitcase when he got home after the book tour. When he poked his head back up to look into the mirror, he was greeted to a much more pleasant sight. Standing behind him, looking breathtaking in an emerald green dress, was Samantha Wade, engagement ring shimmering mischievously on her right ring finger. "That tie better?" she quizzed him, batting him lightly in the small of the back with her left hand. "All kinds." Quickly, he slipped the tie around his neck, fingers fumbling nervously as he completed the Windsor knot. He spun around, finished, only to see an embarrassed grin plastered on his fiance's chuckling face. "What?" Troy threw his arms into the air, perplexed, nearly tearing the right shoulder off his jacket in the process. "A grown man, and you still can't get your tie on straight. Tsk-tsk, Mr. Douglas." Troy flushed, bowing down so Sam could fix the tie with the skill and dexterity of an experienced surgeon or master painter. "Exactly how did you get so good at this?" "Dad could never get this done, either," she said. "Mom used to handle it, but when she was out of town, it was up to me to make sure he looked prim and proper for the boys in the board room." "Speaking of momsie and popsie, shouldn't we be going down to meet them?" "Yeah, seems about time to face the firing squad." With that, Sam tapped Troy softly on the cheek and started to stand up, but was pulled off her feet before she could straighten up, swept straight into a quick kiss before Troy let her back down. "We face it together." ***** When the elevator door spread open 23 floors later, Martin and Catherine Wade were there waiting, looking eminently impatient. Catherine, at least, forced a slight smile when she saw her daughter. Martin, on the other hand, stood so stiffly that it seemed he'd been starched along with the rest of his suit. If it weren't for a wayward glance at his ornate silver pocket watch, the honorable Mr. Wade might well have been mistake for a department store mannequin. "Mother, father, it's --" "You're late, Samantha," Martin said, not even taking his eyes off his watch. "I'm afraid that's my fault, Mr. Wade," Troy piped in. "I had a bit of a wardrobe snafu up there. Couldn't quite locate my tie." Troy tried to smile and coax something out of Martin Wade, but he couldn't even get him to look up. It was only the second time he'd met Sam's parents, but Troy could very easily discern exactly how the Wades had earned their reputation as more than a little cold and distant. Every time he saw the kind of family Sam had grown up with, Troy was more and more amazed that she hadn't ended up as an intolerable stick in the mud. "Carter's been waiting with the car for 15 minutes, Samantha. I'd like to avoid a ticket." So, wordlessly, they stepped through the Hyatt's revolving door and one by one felt the brush of the cool New England evening. Sure enough, standing right at the curb, holding open the door of an impeccably kept limousine, was Carter Hallowell, the Wades' driver since before Sam could even remember. Troy and Sam entered the limo first, Troy crouching down to fit his 6'5" frame underneath the low doorframe. Once Martin and Catherine had neatly slid into their seats, Martin, whose conversations seemed to tick by with military precision, broached the next subject. "So, Samantha? Where is this restaurant we'll be dining at?" Sam suppressed a smile, and Troy's left knee began shaking so violently he thought he'd soon jackhammer through the floor and start dragging a brand new pair of shoes along the streets of Boston at 45 miles per hour. "It's a pretty new place in Chestnut Hill called Telemachus. I've heard some great things about it." Troy stared down at his fiance, her eyes glinting wickedly even in the low light of the limo, thinking back to the first time he'd heard the name of that particular restaurant. ***** Two weeks earlier... An hour of unanswered calls and busy signals had Sam and Troy nervous beyond the point of repair. From the moment Sam checked her voicemail, to the agonizingly long cab ride back to her apartment, to call after call that went without reply, she was fraught with apprehension as to what could cause her twin brother -- even with all of his vices -- to call on a Saturday night, panicked and out of breath. Hovering over the speaker phone with Troy clutching her shoulder, Sam pointed her eyes skyward and pressed the redial button one more time. Brrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrriinnnnngggg! No answer. Brrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrriinnnnngggg! No fucking answer. Brrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrrriinnnnngggg! "Hello?" The voice on the other end, quivering and unstable as it was, remained the most comforting thing Sam had heard in the last hour. Her brother was still there, although know she had the unpleasant duty of finding out just what in blazes was going on. "Tommy, it's Sam. You've had me worried sick for the last hour! What's going on?" She could here her brother grousing over the phone. Tommy was never the kind to part with information, good or bad. Right now, it seemed like the latter was the only option. "Ummmm..." "I'm your sister." The ensuing pause was so pregnant it could've put Octomom and Kate Gosselin to shame in one fell swoop. "Thomas Gabriel Wade, tell me what's going on ... now." For a brief second, Sam was sure her brother was going to hang up the phone and run away forever. Then, she heard a deep exhale on the other end of the line. "I ... uhh ... I," he stumbled over his words before finally getting them to ramble out coherently, "I got a job." Part of Sam was overjoyed. The other part wanted to reach through the receiver and wallop her brother in the head with his own phone. "What? Where?" "New place in Boston, called Telemachus. Guy who owns it knew my work from out in California, his last executive chef bailed, he needed someone in a hurry and gave me a call tonight. I start Monday, and I've got my own kitchen again." "That's ... amazing! Tommy, I'm going to have to get there as soon as I can!" "Yeah," Tommy Wade said. "Amazing. Uh, I've got to run, Sam. New boss is calling." Without hesitation, he hung up the phone. It had been a hectic night. Having a loaded gun poked into the back of his neck had the unfortunate side effect of amping up his nerve quotient by a few degrees. ***** The starkly modern dining room at Telemachus was packed this Saturday night, full of Boston's biggest foodies feasting on braised short ribs, chanterelle mushroom risotto and roasted rack of lamb with asparagus and garlic confit. The food was so good, that in a corner table, Martin and Catherine Wade showed their first glimpses of actual emotion in what seemed like an eternity. "Samantha, this sea bass is simply divine," Catherine said. Martin, the top button on his double-breasted suit threatening to burst away and launch into the stratosphere, didn't say a word, but nodded his satisfied assent. As Troy cut another piece of his lamb, he felt a nudge on his thigh from a very familiar knee. It was his cue, he knew, but he didn't want to take part in this little game right now. He chewed, swallowed another blissful bite, and took a long drink from his glass of pinot noir before Sam nudged him again, even harder this time. Grudgingly, he set his glass down and robotically issued his oft-rehearsed line. "Maybe we should give our compliments to the chef." "In person," Sam added. "You know," Catherine said, "that sounds like a brilliant idea, doesn't it Martin?" Again, Martin Wade only nodded, consumed in his meal. "Why don't you tell the hostess, Samantha, while your father and I both go to freshen up?" "Of course, mother." The elder Wades rose from the table and headed for the bathroom, leaving Troy Douglas alone with his fiance and one abiding thought. This won't end well. ***** "That's two scallop, one filet medium rare, and will somebody tell Gary the Pastry Douchebag to get those goddamn desserts moving for table nine!" Thomas Gabriel Wade was in his element, alright. He might have ulterior motives for being at Telemachus, but, to hell with it, he was back in his own kitchen, and from five until midnight, he was the king of his own world. The hustle, the bustle, the harmony of sizzling pots and pans, the heavenly smells, the danger of getting your finger lopped off by an errant cleaver or scorching yourself on an unattended burner at any time, this was the life he loved. He had his addictions. This was the one he liked the most. Of course, then came Kyle the Obscenely Gay Floor Manager to utter Tommy's least favorite sentence in the English language and ruin his good time. "Some diners would like to meet you." Kyle LeClaire was glad that the chef had laid his knife down a good ten feet away a few moments ago. "Not now, Kyle. In case you can't tell, I'm a little busy running my fucking restaurant!" "She says she's your sister." Grabbing a nearby towel, Tommy wiped the sweat off his brow and stepped towards the door, signaling to his sous chef Ray to take command in his stead. He swung open the dining room doors, making a mental note to never let Kyle speak to any of his customers again. It didn't take him long to pick his sister out of the crowd. It wasn't exactly hard to find one's female doppelganger in any situation, even disregarding the much-vaunted twin telepathy. He slid up behind Sam and Troy, patting his sister on her shoulder. "Why didn't you tell me you were coming?" Sam smiled, looking back at her brother, finally returned to his natural element after years wandering the metaphorical wilderness. "We thought you might get nervous," Sam said. "Also..." Troy had started to speak, but the pained look on his face indicated that one of his sister's heels had just driven itself into his shin. Tommy was confused for a moment, but then looked at the two empty plates on the other side of the table. Quickly, he turned from excitement to bitterness and resentment. "Sam, tell me you didn't--" He didn't need to finish the sentence. Martin and Catherine Wade were striding mechanically back towards the table, and if anything was going to break Martin's staid expression, it was the presence of his estranged son. His eyes narrowed, and before steam could begin to emerge from his ears, the cause of the problem was removed. Tommy Wade was already gone, leaving his trusty silver tasting spoon trailing in the wake of his angry march towards the kitchen. "Samantha..." Her father glowered, ready to melt diamonds with a laser glare. Sam stared straight back, not backing down an inch. Without hesitation, Troy put his arm around her shoulder in support. Well, he thought, we tried. ***** Tommy didn't stop in the kitchen. He stamped straight through what had been his domain just minutes before and threw the door open, stepping into the alley behind Telemachus. Reaching into his pocket, he grabbed a cigarette and lit it, kicking the overfilled dumpster as he took his first drag. Shaking his head, he turned around to see the barrel of a very familiar handgun smiling at him. It wasn't the first time he'd seen this gun. It wouldn't be the last.
