May 27th 2008, 7:37 PM Los Angeles, California Brandon Youngblood couldn’t have felt more uncomfortable than he did inside the atrium of Walgreens, his thumb rubbing over the wrinkles of the crinkled note in his clutches. The texture of the paper was all-so-smooth, the touch relaxing the screaming whispers inside his brain to toss it away. Burning the note would actually be more reassuring, because then the pangs of want undulating through his body would become silent. His eyes were hidden behind wide rimmed sunglasses, the rest of his face a mess of scars and cuts held together with black thread. It had been days since the BACW legend had violently assaulted him, and the swelling of his left eye had yet to die down. The longest gash was hidden underneath a black bandanna tightly wound around his head, preventing others from seeing the most horrific of Dagger’s autographs. The recent volley only pounded home the reality of his diminishing appearance; three years had passed since the time Brandon could be considered a pretty boy, the ugliness of his arrogance having fully manifested itself in scar tissue and facial features that warped into unflattering positions. People were now able to poke fun at his Leno-esque jaw, commenting that he could find work down the road once he was finished wrestling as a hammer cracking cement at construction sites. For a man trying to find a way into an alternative career as an actor in Hollywood, it played as a painful reminder of the handsome appearance eroded away through time. Conventional thought was that a man who acts so jaded to believe his own self-hype is compensating for his inadequacies. Brandon was no different; his face was an irreversible causality from his approach to wrestling, one of which brought only embarrassment. He never dipped his toe in the water, instead choosing to crash headlong into blood feuds and opponents that he stoked like a flame through his chest pounding sense of entitlement. Brandon fidgeted in his chair, his glance cast downward towards the ceramic tiles of the floor. His big toe dangled haphazardly along the grout line around his seat, his brain transfixed in a migraine induced fog. He let his index finger slip the folded paper open, and his eyes catching the roughly scribbled chicken scratch of Doctor Radanovic. One word was clear as day: Oxycodone. Prescribing a pain killer addict his preferred drug of choice was an extremely alarming practice because if caught, he stood to lose everything. But Brandon did sell his pain a bit more heavily than he should have, carefully dancing the lying line he’d grown so well practiced with so he could get his prescribed fix. He had been making do on Bayer and Advil since he had faced his addiction head on. Years had passed since he had taken a tablet of Oxycontin. His mouth watered at the prospect presented to him on the sheet of paper. He looked up towards the glass partition, watching as a middle aged man clad in a lab coat fiddle with his graying mustache as he slowly ambled through aisles of pills. Youngblood, during his worst times, day dreamed of slipping into one of those impeccable white coats so he could line his pockets with thousands of dollars worth of chemical bliss. Youngblood gave himself roughly eight seconds to let a bad thought linger about getting a fix. He could easily punch his way through to the back and have his fill of whatever he desired. Eight seconds passed. The idea still was too appealing. A cold sweat developed on his brow. The thought hadn't escaped his mind. He was in trouble. “Seventy-three.” There was nobody else in the general vicinity of the glass partition. Youngblood's tongue came from between his dry lips, instinct causing him to moisten them as he stared down at the numbered ticket he had pulled off. There was still time to get out of this place. But Brandon was frozen, cemented into the floor and set in his ways. The number was his. The electronic tally on the wall above changed from seventy-two to a red blinking seventy-three which seemed far too intense even through his sunglasses. Brandon rose from his seat, not directly looking towards the parted glass windows of the partition as he cautiously walked on unsteady footing. His knuckles were white from grip tension. Drawing close, his fist opened, his other hand opening the crinkled paper and placing it on the stainless steel cut out in the middle of the white table separating pharmacist from patient. His hand continued trembling, his fingers tapping against the steel in uncontrollable spasms. The pharmacist looked at the note for a moment, then towards Brandon's face. His left hand snaked slowly beneath the table, his gaze slowly leaving Brandon's sunglasses and more towards the vacant distance. Brandon saw the hand slowly moving, and understood what was going on. “Can you please remove the glasses, sir?” Brandon thought his body language had given him away. Maybe it was the wearing of thick sunglasses as the sun was going down. It was Los Angeles however, and sunglasses were not exactly considered strictly daytime wear in this city. Brandon pursed his lips together for a moment before craning his neck upward, his shoulders rising. Youngblood's first reaction always focused on uncoiling, of looking physically threatening enough to stop lines of question before they became too personal. “Excuse me?” Brandon's lips were curved in a disgusted grimace as the words escaped between the faint spaces of his clinched teeth. His hands grabbed hold of the table, his upper body slinking downwards to meet the pharmacist at eye level. Youngblood was significantly taller. He was tempting fate with his threatening demeanor, because Brandon didn't know what sort of tolerance the pharmacist had to violent outbursts. His hand was near a security button. One false move and he was in trouble. But the pharmacist didn't back down quite as easily as Brandon had expected. “You need to take off your glasses sir.” Brandon sighed, peeling off his sunglasses. The prominent swelling of his eye was the first thing the pharmacist could see, and his instant reaction was to wince. Unprompted, Brandon reached around to the back of his head and untied the bandanna, peeling it off his forehead. Youngblood stripped himself of his dignity, all for a prescription bottle that was going to cause a relapse. He felt like a whore. “I got in an accident.” The pharmacist's hand rose from beneath the table, resting next to his other as if it had always been there. “I am sorry for the trouble. Just, you know—” “Addicts.” Brandon finished the pharmacist's thought for him. Brandon brought the glasses back towards his face, fitting them so that most of the swelling was covered. “Don't worry, you ain't the first. I ain't exactly want this stuff, I'm more into natural healing.” “So you're one of these, uh, you know?” The pharmacist paused for a moment as he looked through his many drawers to fill the prescription. “You're one of them spiritual guys. Like one of those Wiccan people?” Brandon rested his elbow on the table, turning to his side. His other hand reached upwards, his fingernails delicately scratching the stitched wound on his forehead. “Not exactly.” Uncomfortable minutes passed on by at an alarmingly slow pace. No small talk was exchanged. Brandon always felt extremely queer whenever he talked with someone at a retail outfit. The candor felt disingenuous. “Here's your prescription sir.” Brandon felt his stomach sinking as he eyed the white paper bag, a gnawing desperation to return the bottle while he still could pulling at his senses. It would have caused a scene, but the moment he took the pills into his possession, it was only a matter of time before he was down the slippery slope again. April 18th 2007 Bexar County, Texas The aversion to hospitals was not a Youngblood trait exclusive to Brandon. Hatred for them seemed to be fixed into the blood of the Youngblood clan. His mother was just better at hiding her complete disdain for the staid trappings housed in hospitals. “How's your hip, ma?” Brandon was seated as far away from his mother as he could be while still remaining within the confines of the box. “You can talk better than that. Speak clearly.” Even with a morphine drip, Abigail Youngblood was able to reel her son in like few others were capable of. Their surroundings were nondescript based on repetition. Every other unit in this wing was sure to have the same thrice painted over sky blue walls with ceramic tile floors and white ceilings. All of the units surely featured blinds that were massed ordered. There were no paintings on the walls. The television had only five channels to choose from, and one of those was a channel to show what was going on on other cable network channels which couldn't be watched in the hospital. A little effort may have made the place a little more bearable. “How's the hip?” “That's better,” his mother sarcastically praised her oldest son. His mother's mood was as patronizing as ever. “It's broken. I don't need help, I can take care of myself.” “I wasn't even offering you help,” Brandon flatly stated, doing his best to match her childish behavior. “I wasn't asking for it.” Minutes passed with an uneasy silence shared between the pair. Brandon's heart rate began accelerating. It had been over a year since the two had been together, a reality which came with being the black sheep of the family. The only reason he was here was because his little sister, Lindsay, said it may be a good idea to go southwest and see her. Brandon chalked that attitude to her age. But Brandon decided it was best to broach her. He was sick of the animosity. And if she was incapable of making the first step towards healing, then he was going to have to take it. “I came to see how you're doin' ma. I didn't come here to start no fights. I didn't come here to start a pissin' contest between us.” “Brandon—” “Now I know we ain't seen eye to eye for quite a while. But if we are ever gonna band together like we need to after what happened with dad, we need to put our differences aside.” “Brandon.” Her teeth were turning a tinge yellow, and skin hung loosely from her bones. Her husband's death from brain cancer had drained the life from her. “What ma?” Brandon rose up from his seat, walking towards his mother from across the room. He stood over her hospital bed, looking down towards her. For a moment, Brandon felt her resistance crack. He reached his hand towards hers and gripped slightly. Abigail pulled out of his grasp the moment he gripped. She looked him in the eyes, and Brandon could see that the skin on her face showcased the outline of her skull. “You don't have the right to do that Brandon. You gave up that right the second you walked out of our home the way you did. You gave up the privilege to be part of our family.” Brandon turned away, unable to look his mother face to face. The words hurt, the pain remaining just as severe as the first time he had heard it. “And why Lindsay tolerates you I will never know. She knows what you are.” “I made my mistakes ma. And I'll pay for them.” “Mistakes? Mistakes? Brandon, you forfeited your soul!” Abigail's faith was strong. The way of Christ was the only true salvation for her. She had forced the doctrine upon her children from the second they could breath. Hellfire and brimstone preaching of family values was the way to deliverance. “To become a wrestler?! What, you people are still that upset that I left to do something I had my heart set on? You and him were unreasonable then, and you are unreasonable now! At least dad forgave me before he died!” Abigail merely shook her head. “What ma? Huh? What? I done the drugs ma. I hit my wife. But I'm a changed man. I'm clean. I'm completely clean. It's like you ain't ever gonna forgive me for just walking out for wrestling? I mean, shit!” His mother's breathing was heavy, her response somber. “What you've become isn't something I can approve of. I know what you are. We could’ve forgiven our boy if he wouldn't have pushed us away. But he pushed us away. And he rubbed it in our faces. I will never forgive you for what you did. For what you put your father through.” Those words gave no hope of reconciliation. Brandon's entire body drained of color. He felt naked. Defenseless. His motivation in his wrestling career always was focused on his parents casting him aside. The simplicity of their belief system made them easy targets. But now, the rift was shifted; the onus was around his neck solely. Brandon didn't want to hear another word. “We raised you better than this.” Brandon's nostrils flared. Seeing her in such a state, seeing the effects of what his father's death had wrought upon her—“Ma?” Sin was not inclusive to Brandon. It flowed freely through the bloodstream of all those cursed with the surname of Youngblood. “It wasn't the tumor. It wasn't cancer. Dad killed himself.” Silence. Brandon didn't even look towards his mother. For a woman of faith, who hoped beyond hope that she would see her love again on the other side—“How you gonna deal with that?” He knew she wanted to respond, to come back questioning the accuracy of his claim. Even if it was a lie, the seed of doubt had been planted within her head. Brandon didn't stay for the aftermath. He quietly exited the room, leaving others to deal with the damage that had been done. May 27th 2008, 9:08 PM Los Angeles, California Sister Mary Ann was an all too stereotypical name for a career nun. The old woman, now delicate and frail with wrinkled porcelain skin, had given fifty years of her life to the Lord Jesus Christ. The decadent trappings of Los Angeles only spurred her on in her devotion, her life a testament to culling temptation in all its forms. Brandon felt uncomfortable as she took his hands into hers. The black attire she wore as classical, but the Church had undergone tremendous renovations to make it anything but. The pews had a mahogany shine reflected by the soft lighting above. The Stations of the Cross were all predominantly displayed with sculptures carved into the marble walls. Even the stain glass of the windows had undergone a significant make over; a visage of Jesus now shining brightly on both sides of the altar. The altar itself featured a pristine ciborium of white with gold trim, which would bathe the priest in shadow if not for well placed light fixtures for when the sun did not shine through the windows. The place was a gaudy representation of faith, fixated upon the features and less upon the actual substance of mass. It was a feast for the eyes. “How has the day received you, my son?” she asked through wisps. Her bifocals showcased green hued eyes that seemed part of a husk that was incapable of hatred. Even for someone as brazen and irreverent as Brandon, her gaze struck a sentimental cord. Youngblood's straining glance was placed upon the much smaller woman, a smile crossing his lips as the discomfort from her clasp dissipated. “It's been a long time since someone has asked me that, Sister.” She released his hands gently, bringing them to her breast and placing her palms against one another. She looked away from Brandon as she shuffled towards the altar. Brandon followed behind her. “We did not have your presence in Church this weekend.” Brandon pursed his lips together, thinking of any of a multitude of lies he could tell the old woman as to why he hadn't joined Melissa for mass this past weekend. “I got hurt. Was in the hospital for a spell. I prayed there.” Brandon was never the praying type. “I hope you were looked after well.” She ambled slowly up the stairs towards the altar, lightly draping copper covers over the incense laden candles off to the right. She needed to use an extension just to reach them. “Father Faber said you were quite unpleasant earlier. Is everything alright?” Her questioning manner reminded him of his mother. “I'd say yes, if only to not trouble you with my problems. They are always a bit on the weighty side.” He had another man to weigh down with his burden. “Ah, my friend!” Father Clayton Faber shouted across the way, his youthful exuberance bursting forth in a bout of excitement. His face was young and fresh, the jawline was perfect and his skin a comfortable tan. Well groomed and parted locks of curly brown hair framed his shining face. Faber was a young priest, but was someone who took great care in the job he did. His hands reached out for Brandon's, taking hold of them. Brandon wondered how Faber was able to keep his hands so soft and smooth. They looked and felt as if they had never felt the strain of a hard day's work. “You look to be very raw, Brandon. Perhaps it is time to find another career path?” He chuckled to himself, looking towards the ever mindful Sister Ann. He flashed her a reassuring smile, his hands reaching towards hers. “I believe it is alright for you to go home Sister. I believe Brandon and I have quite a bit of catching up to do.” She nodded to him, slowly making her way towards the exit. Father Faber looked to her back as she walked away, waiting for a sign of her departure. Brandon's eyes were focused solely on him. “We got to talk Father.” “Brandon, you must give Sister Ann time to leave. The possibilities of her knowing your secret might have horrible repercussions. She may be old, but she still has hawk-like instincts. Nearly impossible to slip anything by her.” Brandon walked into Father Faber's field of vision, obscuring his bead on the old nun. “Now'd be as good a time as ever.” His foot was strumming against the hard marble floor, an echo reverberating through their solitary acquaintance. “In all the time we've known each other, have you known me to not be discreet?” Faber's glance slowly rolled towards Brandon's, his mood changing briefly towards condescending arrogance. “Sister Ann doesn't know you beat your wife.” Faber was speaking an octave louder than usual. Brandon's impatience turned quickly to dismay as now he was looking to see if anyone had heard him. “See, Brandon, you always rush things. Now let’s go to my office.” He offered his hand to the left. December 12th 2005 Colorado Springs, Colorado The walls were bright white. Everything was bright white. Brandon slunk back in his chair, looking upwards. His skin had turned rough with goose flesh and sweat was pouring profusely from his forehead. The straps around his chest kept him in place. Brandon's teeth collided with each other in rapid-fire succession, his muscles tensing at the forearm and bicep. Brandon was in a state of euphoria. His body language would not denote this, but his mind had been scrambled on a skillet, and he couldn't distinguish anguish from joy anymore. His world was blurred, the barbiturates force fed to him placing him into a tensionless state. Everything was too bright for his eyes. They began to water just from surveying his surroundings. “He's doing fine. This is all a byproduct of clearing his mind. He has to fight the cravings.” Brandon's mind was on a cloudy autopilot. Motion was a blur. Reality became one disjointed mess of puzzle pieces that were being forced into tightly wound spaces that didn't seem to fit quite right. The voice was deep and laborious; it came from the right. “Dear son, how are you feeling?” “I feel lightheaded.” Brandon puckered his saliva coated lips, looking lazily towards the blurry outline of a human being. “These are extreme times, my son. You must understand that what we do here is reassess your identity to fully achieve your goals.” A hand patted his chest in a sign of reassurance. Brandon's isolation from the rest of the world was less than a week old. Brandon tried to return the touch, but was unable to thanks to the leathers straps holding his wrists. Stammering, he tried to speak clearly, but anxiousness got the better of him. “I think I, I, I don't know.” The sun was burning his eyes. He tried to look away. He couldn't. “Look, we will have you on your way soon. You are doing just fine Brandon.” This voice came from the left, and was rich in texture. This was an adolescent voice. Brandon's eyes opened, a flood of tears slowly falling down the contours of his cheeks. “Before it is over, you will praise Jesus Christ. He is the giver of miracles. He died for your sins. Your sins especially Brandon.” The voice was so sweet. Brandon smiled simply hearing it. He became comfortable in his lack of functionality. “Jesus loves you Brandon.” The right said. He tried to look towards him, but all that was there was a swirl of degenerating color which caught his attention. The words the right was speaking had lost their meaning. They were simply gravelly vibrations that caused harm to his eardrums. “Yes Brandon, Jesus loves you. He does not judge. He is beyond the need for that pettiness. If you are to serve Him, then you must come willingly into the light.” The left gave meaning back. Brandon's brain, however, was having tremendous difficulty with the continued strain. “You are a good son Brandon. We are here only to help you.” The pair moved away from Youngblood momentarily, either beginning or continuing an earlier discussion. “So why did he need to be strapped down?” The gravel voice was the one asking the questions. “Intense withdrawal symptoms, Father. He was hurting himself. He was quite rabid.” A moment passed before the left moved on with his mode of thought. “I think a lot of it has to do with pent up aggression. He is used to being in control of himself. But, as he has shown me, I believe that to be a very thinly veiled cover hiding something.” “And that is why he needed to be sedated?” “Yes Father. Our medical staff believes that this is the only way he can become docile.” The two were silent again. Brandon tested his bondage to no avail. His skin was suffocating. Youngblood suddenly felt the urge to breath through his forearms. “Make sure he knows up from down. If we are going to convert him, he can't be apprehensive.” The left was reassuring in his candor. “Relapse is never an option, Father. I will do my best to nurture him.” “Now, Brandon, you understand that we are only trying to help you, yes?” The two shifted back into focus. Their facial features were still distant fuzz, their voices echoing in echoing chants. Had they switched places? “I, I dunno. What the fuck?” Brandon's body chemistry was dangerously skewed. There was more in the cocktail than simple sedatives. Brandon was swimming in a sea of psychedelics. Those weren't his drug of choice. “It's okay Brandon.” Left said. “What the—what the fuck am I doin' here?” Brandon's arms flailed against the leather restraints, his fingers nervously fidgeting in a failed attempt to release himself from his bondage. He suddenly felt extremely cold. His neck began to fidget and ripple with each shiver. His head was swaying from side to side. The right side took its leave. All Brandon could see was the left. “It's alright my son. It's alright. It's just you and me now. It's just me and you. My name is Clayton Faber. I'm going to be helping you through this tumultuous time.” “What the—who the fuck are you? My eyes. My eyes man. I can't see nothin' but white, man. We got to get out of here.” “It's okay Brandon. You are simply confused. I am here to remedy that.” Faber's hand gave Brandon's lower abdomen a soothingly soft pat of reassurance. The tips of his fingers began to rub in a circular motion around the area. “It's okay. And that has been part of your problem Brandon. You push things too far.” Father Faber's hands were clasped together, his thumbs twiddling with one another without true purpose. Faber's work with a Christian Center in Colorado Springs was not placed as prominently, a lone certificate for his service in a neat picture frame that hung over his head like a guillotine. Brandon often wondered if the placement of the certificate was a product of intent or simply random chance. Knowing Clayton Faber, he thought chance had little to do with anything he did. Youngblood also could not see Father Clayton Faber as a man around his age because of his very youthful appearance. Physically he looked several years younger, more akin to a developing young man than someone who had passed into adulthood. Appearance acted as a veil for Faber. He was able to pass himself off as a much simpler and inexperienced person thanks to his dimpled smile. Those who bought into his callow aspects would likely never see his darker side peak to the surface. Brandon tasted truth; he knew Faber's personality was a parlor trick for his flock to lap up like ignorant dogs. “Addiction is a very horrible thing, Brandon. It causes your path to become wrought with peril. It turns you inside out. Suffocates you.” Brandon grunted through Faber's lecture, interjecting with words that tasted bitter against his tongue. “Finally. Something we can agree on.” Youngblood craned his head upward, blowing smoke directly towards Faber's face. Father Faber sighed as he waved the fumes away from his nostrils. “Look, Brandon, I know what it seems like to you. But the reality is, you found salvation in the arms of Jesus Christ. You've made such great progress over the years with your addiction that it makes it quite difficult to see why you would regress back to that dreadful state of dependency.” “I ain't dependent on the painkillers. I've done well enough without 'em.” Brandon's glare became fixated on the carpet. There were little hexagonal designs tinted blue and mustard woven into the lavender rug. Don't feel ashamed at your false assumptions. God has a plan for you. And while it may seem like you are unable to cope without these things, I promise you, you can.” “Father, you're not understandin' me.” Brandon doubted Faber ever really understood what he said because his detached droning rarely conveyed passion. Rather, everything had a practiced precision. A raw fire became kindled in the pit of Youngblood's stomach. “I understand you full well Brandon. We've been through a lot the last few years. Brandon didn't push him away when his head lowered downward. He never fought the hot breath on his cheek. He never said a word of defiance. He never bit the tongue slipping into his greedy maw.
