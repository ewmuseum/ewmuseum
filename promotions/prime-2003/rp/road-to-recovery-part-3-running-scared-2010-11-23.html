Road to Recovery Part 3: Running Scared

23 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23/11/2010 Sam’s Steakhouse The Bronx, New York 21st November 2010 Flanked in all directions by glittering distant stars, the moon broke through the darkness of the night, its silvery glow almost making it the perfect night for the hopeless romantics of the city to whisk their lovers away for evening of reckless abandon. The beauty was lost on Jacob McKail. Instead, his attention was focused upon the bitterly cold night air biting at his face and hands and his primal instinct to find warmth and embrace it for all his worth. But he resisted, instead deciding to take a long drag of his cigarette whilst massaging away the nagging pain in his shoulder. It’d been bugging the shit out of him ever since kicking Big Bear’s ass up and down the Rushmore Plaza Civic Center. He was still massaging the pain away when he saw her entering the restaurant ten yards down the road. Elle. Shit! He silently screamed, taking another drag of his cigarette and wondering just the hell he was going to do. Just last week Elle had wandered back into his life, declaring her undying love for him and expecting everything just to be okay. It’d been more than a year since they were lovers and a lot had happened. To both of them. McKail had been through all kinds of shit and Elle got herself engaged to respectable man, someone who can look after her. What the hell can I do? He asked himself. I can barely look after myself. But it was his fault. The drawback of getting himself clean and himself back together was there was nothing to block out all of the bad shit he’d done; his guilt was overcoming him and he needed a release. He thought apologising to the people he hurt the most was the best way to start. Elle was first on the list and six weeks ago he found her and made his apology, but she’d been struggling to control her feelings for him ever since. At the time he told himself he was doing it for her benefit as much as his own. He was a complete bastard to her; he lied to her and then just ran away like a damned coward when she caught him out. But he could see now that was bullshit. He did it for purely selfish reasons; he wanted to alleviate his own pain and beat back his guilt to more tolerable levels. Now he was wondering just what in the hell to say to her. He didn’t know how the hell he felt about anything, much less about the only woman he cared about since his late wife passed on. Come on, McKail, he told himself, throwing his lit cigarette on the floor and heading towards the restaurant. Man the fuck up! You’ve run away from her before, you sure as hell ain’t gonna do it again. *** Lebanon Hospital Center The Bronx, New York 26th June 2010 “Hey,” Jax began, a wearing a distinct look of concern upon his face, “you okay?” McKail managed an unconvincing and weak nod, his hands rooted to either edge of the cold, stainless steel propping him up the best they could. “Cuz you look like shit!” Jax added, slapping him on the back and heading for the bathroom door. “See you at lunch, man.” McKail couldn’t manage a goodbye to his friend; his was body was still worryingly weak and his throat was still stinging. He managed to raise his head just enough for him to be able see the ghostly pale reflection of a face he once recognised in the mirror. Jax is right, he decided. I do look like shit. But it was hardly unsurprising. An alcoholic deprived of booze and then locked away in a minimum security rehab facility didn’t make for a pretty picture and two days into his six month sentence at Lebanon, he’d spent the better part of thirty-six hours hurling his guts up. “McKail!” The sharp yell echoed throughout the bathroom, somewhat amplified by the stainless steel interior, damned near deafening McKail. There was no doubt in his mind who the perpetrator of the artificial sonic boom was; Buzz Copeland. “Get your ass out here!” Buzz was the charged with making sure the facility ran smoothly and without incident and it seemed that the facility administrator didn’t much care how he did it. Buzz took certain liberties with the human rights violation laws and, if rumours are to be believed, had thoroughly been investigated by the government on more than one occasion. If there was any credence to these rumours, then McKail found it somewhat disturbing the malevolent prick still had a job. McKail found the thirty or so other ‘patients’ gathered in the common area; they formed a semi-circular audience in which Buzz seemed to be the main attraction. He forced his way to the front of the pack and slumped down into the old, worn, floral patterned couch he favoured, nodding a weak thank you at Jax for keeping the place for him. “Now that we’re all here,” Buzz boomed, eying him warily. McKail noticed two of his security detail, otherwise known as ‘enforcers’, flanked Buzz at either size. They already had their hands grasping the handle of their nightsticks and it signal to McKail that whatever the hell this was about, it wasn’t going to end well for some poor bastard. McKail had a horrible feeling he knew who. “Let me begin. It has come to my attention that narcotics have been found on the premises. God only knows how you desperate fucks manage to sneak this shit in, but it’s in here nonetheless and needless to say, I won’t tolerate that in my facility.” Buzz cast his gaze slowly across the patients, drinking in their fear with noticeable delight. “So I’m giving the person responsible for this one chance – just one – to own up and to tell us precisely how they managed to smuggle the shit in or the consequences will be dire.” A chainsaw would have difficulty cutting the tension in the room, as Buzz eyed the patients up a second time, this gorging on terror. Jax rustled in his seat next to him uncomfortably, whilst Buzz’s eyes latched on to McKail. “I believe you have the answers I require, Mr McKail,” Buzz declared, slamming his nightstick down on the arm of the couch with considerable force. “Don’t you?” *** Sam’s Steakhouse The Bronx, New York 21st November 2010 McKail tried to hide his nerves as the waitress led him over to his table and to Elle, but he could tell all of efforts would inevitably prove useless. Elle always could see right through him. Upon seeing McKail, Elle’s face lit up like a pinball machine and it damn near broke his heart. She stood from the table and embraced him for what seemed like hours, but could’ve only been mere minutes. McKail didn’t much care that the waitress was still there waiting for them sit back down and Elle didn’t seem all that concerned with it either. He would never admit it, but he needed that hug. The embrace lasted way beyond all comfortable levels, but when it did finally finish each of them took their seats and the waitress handed them menus and took their drink order. “You’re late,” Elle said, wearing a playful smile. McKail couldn’t hold back his own smile; he felt like a real fuckwit. He didn’t expect any of this; his feelings and his reaction to them were entirely foreign concepts to him. It felt like someone else was in control of his body. “I know,” he managed. “Sorry.” “Plucking up the courage to come in here, huh?” She asked. He nodded. How did she know? “Me too. I sat in the rental for five minutes before I could face this. Sounds stupid doesn’t it?” McKail shook his head. “Not really. This could be a really good, or a really bad idea.” “Bad?” Elle asked. All of a sudden she abandoned her playful nature and set up her defences in full stall. “Our previous attempt at this didn’t end all that well,” McKail reminded her. “And getting clean didn’t automatically make my life a whole easier, nor did it change me all that much from being the irritable bastard who walked out on you.” “So that’s it?” She asked. The tone of her voice changed to that of anger and she didn’t seem to care all that much that the other patrons of the restaurant had started to look over in their direction, curious and looking for a little entertainment to go with their evening meal. “You’ve already made your mind up, haven’t you? You’re just gonna run away from me, from this like you did last time?” McKail reached across the table and held her hand. “I ain’t running no more, I’m done with that.” “Then what’s this all about?” Elle asked. “What’s stopping us from rekindling what we had before?” McKail sighed. He was hoping he wouldn’t have to have this discussion tonight. “Mary.” Elle scrunched her face up confused. “Your late wife?” McKail nodded. “But I thought she passed on six years back.” “She did, but I—“ “Excuse me,” the voice interjected. McKail had assumed it was just a waiter coming to take their order, but when he saw Elle’s face contorted in horror he realised that something was wrong. “...but what are you doing with my wife?” Wife? McKail silently glared at the man questioningly and saw a man standing at the end of the table, wearing an exceptionally tailored suit, numerous gold rings on either hand and a tan too convincing to be fake. He looked back at Elle who’s eyes had already begun to tear up. “I’m sorry,” she said, rising from her seat. Her ‘husband’ grabbed her under the arm before she could linger any further and dragged her towards the door. Stunned, it took McKail a few minutes to fully appreciate just what in the hell was going on but when he did, he jumped up from his seat and followed them outside. “What the hell is going on here?” He yelled after them. Her husband turned at the question and by default so did Elle. “I’m taking my wife home, if that’s okay.” “Seems to me she don’t wanna go any place with you,” McKail told him. “She ain’t got no choice,” her husband sneered. McKail grinned. “Maybe I’m here to give her a choice.” “Jake, just leave it okay?” She pleaded. But the fire in McKail’s heart was not yet put out and what’s more, she could see it. “I have to go with him. Just let me go. Please?” McKail grunted away the anger under his breath and shook his head. “Whatever.” Her husband had already heard enough and without another word had resumed dragging her away. McKail watched them go, wondering just what the hell he’d walked into and wondered just what the hell kind of game Elle was playing with him. But just before they disappeared from sight, she glanced back over her shoulder and told him all he needed to know. She was going back because at this point she had to. Mikey, he silently pondered. It had to be Mikey. Mikey was Elle’s son from a previous marriage and she loved him more than anything in the world. It only stood to reason she was going back to her husband and their clearly dysfunctional marriage because she was afraid of what he might do to Mikey. This ain’t your problem, McKail, he told himself. But he’d already decided that it was and he’d already decided he was going to stop running from his problems. He owed Elle that much. *** Lebanon Hospital Center The Bronx, New York 26th June 2010 All eyes darted towards McKail and Buzz developed a grin upon his face, barely noticeable under his sizeable moustache. “Come on, Mr McKail,” Buzz prompted, his voice calmer now but no less menacing, “you know who’s narcotics we found, so just tell me and let’s get this over with.” “I don’t know what the hell you’re talking about,” he lied. “You don’t?” Buzz questioned somewhat perplexed, or at least acting so. “You mean to tell me that your roommate smuggled illegal narcotics into my facility and was using said substances right under your nose and you had no idea about it?” “Hey man, I don’t know what you found, but that shit ain’t mine,” Jax said, even more agitated by this point. Guilty was written all over his face, the poor bastard just didn’t realise it. Buzz angrily pointed his night stick at Jax. “Shut the fuck up or you’ll lose all your damned teeth, boy.” Buzz turned his attention back to McKail. “Answer the damned question. Did you know or didn’t you?” McKail shook his head. “I didn’t know a damned thing.” Buzz grinned. “Okay, I can buy that.” He grabbed Jax by his collar and hurled him onto the floor. “You don’t look like the sharpest knife in the draw, so I’ll let you play the dumb card. But truth of it is, I just wanna know which way your bread is buttered, boy. I wanna know just how committed you are to getting clean and getting the hell outta this place.” McKail stared at him blankly. Buzz took his nightstick and cracked Jax in the ribs. Jax cried out in agony immediately, barely able to breathe and blood already trickling out from the corner of his mouth. “Now you can report me and get your ass thrown back into jail,” Buzz warned Jax, “or you can take your punishment like a man and remain at my facility. Hell, I won’t even increase your sentence.” Jax spluttered something under his breath and Buzz took that as the latter choice. He then drew closer to McKail, the grinned still firmly planted upon his face, and handed him the nightstick. A little puzzled McKail took it and Buzz’s grin widened. “You got a pass on denying all knowledge of the narcotics,” Buzz told him, “but you fail this little test and I’ll know for certain just how much of a model patient you are.” The true meaning of his words was clear; he wanted McKail to choose a side; with him or against him. Fuck! “I want you help your roommate out. I want you to use it to remind him that he wants to get clean and he’s not going to use drugs anymore.” McKail stood, his eyes darting between Buzz, Jax and the nightstick. He was all out of options, that much was clear. There was only one thing to do now. He loomed over his friend, the nightstick raised high above his head. Buzz glared up at him admiringly, but not for long. McKail brought the nightstick down across the bridge of his nose and managed to get another body shot in before Buzz’s enforces restrained him. On a better day, McKail might’ve put up more of a fight, but he was still weak as shit and it felt as though Buzz’s enforcers diet mainly consisted of steroids and red meat and not a whole lot else. The enforcers roughed him up some as the rest of the patients cheered him and chanted his name, before dragging him to east wing of the building and locking him away some place away from the main population. Later that night, lying on the cold floor of the tiny room, McKail smiled to himself. For the first time in a long while, he hadn’t needed booze to face his problems. He hadn’t simply run away from Buzz or the seemingly unwinnable dilemma that was thrust upon him and he felt damned good about it too. I’m done runnin’
