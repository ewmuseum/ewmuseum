It is a way of life

23 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/23/2005 ~When opportunity knocks at your door, you must answer to get anywhere in this life. If you don't answer, you may as well kill yourself now because sometimes the opportunity never comes again. Opportunity comes in different forms and sizes. It could be a new promotion at work, a new job, a new love interest, or a number one contendership for a title match. Some people never get the opportunity of a number one contendership, others get it too such for no reason, and then some others get the opportunity and seize it with a vengence. Kano is such a man. He sees his opportunity, whatever it may be and knows this may never come again. He realizes that this match could, theoretically, save his ailing career. I mean lets be honest here, he is in PRIME, but it almost seems like he isn't quite where he was during his glory days in PCW. He knows this could be the turning point and his stepping stone back to his former greatness of yesteryear. He knows that if he fails, it could send him back to being lower than whale sh*t. He never wants to go back to that. He has a business to run, a multi-million dollar business. He has obligations to his fans, his stock holders, his family, his friends, and to himself. He will not fail his obligations. He has to much to lose, including his self-respect and his personal fortune. He values his personal fortune and his expensive way of life above just about everything in this world. He has everything to lose, and very little to gain, except respect from his peers. Kano can gain respect if he pulls out a win in this upcoming match.~ *The scene opens up in a large conference room full of business-looking men sitting at a long table. These men are all wearing expensive suits (Armani, Versace, etc.). These men are varying in age and race, but all of upper class. At the head of the table is a large, flat-screen. Suddenly, in big, bold print 'Transmission coming through,' appears on the screen. Almost immediately, Jon Kano's million-dollar face comes into focus on the screen. Kano has his hair slicked and tied back, like he is all business for this conference call. This crowd of seated businessmen are now silent, anticipating Jon Kano's words.* Kano: Good afternoon, gentlemen. Welcome to our annual Kano Enterprises, Inc. stockholders meeting. I am proud to announce for the fifth straight year, our stock prices have continued to rise, due in most part to my involvement with PRIME, a fine wrestling promotion. When our corporation went public in March of 1998, our stock price was $3.12, it is now $42.18 per share of stock. This is outstanding. Many of you have voiced concern over my career in professional wrestling, I assure you that my career has helped our growing corporation. I plan in the next year to continue to invest in small residential and commercial developments in and around Las Vegas and Reno, Nevada and to expand into Phoenix, Arizona and its spawling suburbs. I will now open up the floor to questions and concerns about Kano Enterprises, Inc. *A large man of about sixty years of age and caucasian descent stands up and adjusts his fine silk tie.* Man: Mr. Kano, I know I sound like a broken record, as every year I voice my main concern of you still practicing this hobby of being a professional wrestler. I really don't understand your position on this matter. You need not do this, and I am concerned as I am afraid of you being hurt in the ring and not being able to perform your duties as CEO of Kano Enterprises. *Kano just smiles on the screen.* Kano: Mr. McCormick, you are right in the fact that you are like a broken record, and I will respond the same I do every year. This isn't a hobby, it is a way of life. A life I am perfectly comfortable with. How many people do you think across America would know the name Kano without the association with professional wrestling? Not a whole hell of a lot. I am a walking marketing machine. I don't see what your major concern is. You seem so determined that I am going to get hurt so badly that I cannot perform my duties to the corporation. Have some f*cking faith. Next year will be my tenth anniversary in the wrestling industry. I have been one of the most dominant big men for several years now and that trend will continue. Mr. McCormick: Mr. Kano, I have the utmost faith in you and your abilities but you cannot control what others do. Kano: Quite true, but Mr. McCormick, do you even watch professional wrestling? Mr. McCormick: I watch enough to know that your next match is a street fight tag match, what kind of behavior is this for a CEO? *Kano is obviously getting agitated with this stockholder.* Kano: Yes, a street fight, for the sake of having a street fight in the grandest arena of them all, Madison Square Garden. Now, my partner is the hardcore expert. Trashcan Man was hardcore before being hardcore was cool. He is the only person I have ever met in my life who would take a punch in the mouth, spit out two teeth, smile and then beat the living hell out of the person who did that. He has a tolerance for pain that I have never seen before. I think the sick bastard enjoys being in pain more than inflicting it. The son of a bitch has issues, but I like him. Now as far as my opponents go, to hell with them. I don't have a particular problem with either one of them, but I also don't have a problem beating the living hell out of them. That is what I do. We will be having a good old time at ReVolution as old school PCW runs amuck once again in PRIME as two hall of famers go wild. I can't wait. This is going to be fun. Now gentlemen, if you would excuse me, I have a match to prepare for. I say good day. Several stockholders (in almost perfect unison): Wait!!! Kano: I SAID GOOD DAY!!! *The scene fades to black as 'Transmission Ended' comes across the screen.* ~Knock, Knock, Knock. Will Kano be able to answer the call of opportunity or will his struggles in PRIME continue?~
