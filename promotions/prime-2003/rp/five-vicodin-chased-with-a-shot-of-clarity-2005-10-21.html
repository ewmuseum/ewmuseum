Five Vicodin Chased With A Shot Of Clarity

21 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 21st, 2005 'There's a light at each end of this tunnel,You shout 'cause you're just as far in as you'll ever be outAnd these mistakes you've made, you'll just make them againIf you only try turning around.'-Anna Nalick, ''Breathe'' Chapter OneGOOD FORTUNEFortune is defined as the chance happening of fortunate or adverse events in someone's life. It also represents success when at least partially resulting from luck. A man is fortunate when he's rolling in money, success and pre-written stories to be used for future success without the sacrifice of time or effort when others around him would be doing actual work. The man who thinks ahead and uses his methods to trick people who would worship him for what they thought to be an upstanding and current effort is fortunate, because nobody's caught onto his unethical ways of business yet. Unfortunate in that situation would be those who were using their time and effort in the present to make their own fortune, the people who only slip and slide because of someone's pre-prepared body of work to be used whenever and wherever he may see fit. His story doesn't have to relate to anything else, because it's good. And it's good because he's had years to work on it, while you're banging your head on your desk trying to pound out something worth looking at on a week to week basis. Beyond definition, fortune is a foretelling of ones' destiny. An unfortunate woman may become inclined otherwise out of little more than her ability to adapt to new or changing situations. Unfortunate, as a word, is characterized by undeserved bad luck. An unfortunate woman would be a person stuck in a rut. She'd toil and pine for the creativity and freedom of expression sought and found in earlier endeavors, questioning down to her very core what would bring that spark back to her heart. She wouldn't be lost anymore, but she'd still be searching for a purpose and a method to the madness around her. She would call her own sanity into question. She'd be promiscuous without being slutty, and lovesick without loving enough. Unfortunate is causing misfortune. It is disastrous, and the opposite of fortune. By any stretch of the imagination, someone like Amy Campbell would be naturally unfortunate. Mention of her name would equate to being hapless, ill-fated and unlucky. She was, by definition, a victim of bad luck; And that said a lot, for someone who had never been so easily defined. Fortunately, she had learned all about adaptation and now, she was perfectly capable of taking on a man with pre-designed and time-perfected tactics. She wasn't yet fortunate, but her luck was changing minorly with every passing day. For instance, she'd been offered a movie role and, despite the fact that it was a small speaking part that garnered her little money or creative freedom and happened to perfectly play into her 'skater chick' stereotype, it was a decent accomplishment for a girl of 22 who had - for all intents and purposes - given up on attempting to do anything in life beyond charging her fists into the flesh and bone of others. She felt fortunate and quite pleased leaving the LAX airport in a Lincoln with tinted windows and a kind driver sent by the agency that would also look after her accomodations and any other needs while she shot her small two-day scene in an upcoming Cameron Crowe production. Ecstatic would describe her in the moment when she arrived in Los Angeles, her second-favorite American city, with the promise of meeting her favorite director and earning herself the right to be immortalized on film by blowing everybody away with what would be the most brilliantly acted three lines in the history of cinematography. The driver, who hadn't introduced himself as anything but 'I'll be taking you from here, Miss Campbell', hadn't seemed to take an interest in his client beyond glances into his rearview that displayed the little redhead as a nervous and bubbly girl whose appearance was as mismatched as the color in her eyes. She was mumbling to herself, most likely reciting her lines, before her eyes met his in the mirror. She smiled sheepishly at him. ''You caught me talking to myself. What's your name, anyway?'' ''Tyler, actually.'' She grinned, ''You Tylers are good luck, y'know.'' ''I noticed that. We drivers aren't as high-class as our clientel, yaknow. I still watch TV,'' he quickly adjusted his seat and focused eyes back on the road, ''But I'm not even s'posed to be talking to you past this. We don't speak until spoken to, and we're almost to the set anyway.'' They'd hired a driver to take her two or three miles that a taxi would've gladly done for under ten dollars. For the first time in a long time, Amy felt like practical royalty. When they stopped, Tyler The Driver was rather surprised when he circled the car, noticing that Campbell had opened her own door with no objection. He was used to working with celebrity, but even the F-listers who had walk-on parts on afternoon soaps wouldn't open their own doors. The girl was impressively modest for someone whose appearance was so loud, brash and confident. She stepped out of the car and into the wide world of Hollywood, and he silently wished her godspeed as she smiled to him and walked into the den of wolves that she'd so eagerly anticipated. Immediately recognized, all the little redhead could do was bask in the glory of knowing she was taking part in something large. Bigger than most wrestling shows. She'd be rented out at Blockbuster and, though customers would only see her for mere moments, she was satisfied knowing she would be remembered forever for what she was about to do. Yeah, it was the tiniest of parts... but that wasn't the important piece of the puzzle for Amy. She was just happy to be recognized as a talent, however minor, in something other than ripping people to pieces. So, script clutched tightly to her, she staved her way through a sea of assistant somethingorothers and was eventually lost beyond belief, only to be found - not by herself, but one of many strangers. ''It's new to see you looking so confused. I like it.'' The voice had come from behind her somewhere, and at first it wasn't acknowledged in the least. Amy could only assume that it was directed at one of the other peons roaming relentlessly. She heard indistinct shouting over a megaphone and decided to wander to the voice of command, instead of the one prodding in behind her ears. ''Damnit...'' the voice continued in the background, ''AMY.CAMPBELL.'' Finally, she turned around. The voice had some distant measure of recognizability to it, but she was more concerned with the point to the man attached to it yelling to her. When she looked around, though, it became apparent. Staring at her were several confused cameramen and boom-mike operators, fairly irritated by the fact that she was walking against the flow of people. She hadn't realized that the indistinct shouts were actually from the director's assistant, calling for a scene full of extras to begin working and, for this take at least, the newcomer to the set had ruined it. ''Err...'' she muttered as the man grabbed her by the arm, pulling her near a booth of lit computers, ''...Sorry? And who're you?'' ''Your agent, remember?'' ''I have an a--'' In that moment, she recalled a sleepy morning months before when the same question had been on the tip of her tongue. ''...I do have an agent! Sorry for hanging up on you that time,'' she shifted her eyes, not wanting to tell him that she'd thought he was a prank caller, ''I, uhh... I didn't think you were serious about this.'' He smiled at her, knowing precisely what had happened that morning - but the redhead was unpredictable, and most likely drunken when she hung up on him if anything he'd heard about her was true. At that moment, however, she was perfectly sober and pretty as a tropical dream. Well, a pale tropical dream. With a lot of scars and tattoos. Oh, and then there was the nose ring and the mismatched eyes... Okay, so she was far from a stereotypical dream girl, but she'd serve most men just fine. Her body was tight, her face was beautiful and, as her agent, he knew that she'd always have work in some beauty or entertainment-related industry. Of course, she'd never been so sure. Even then, she cast her eyes downward in a strongly lacking confidence - and her strides showed muscle strain from workouts and training that were obviously linked to the fact that she was strongly considered the underdog of her next match. ''Well, you just made your first leap onto film... but I'm guessing that intrusion'll probably get cut, eh?'' he winked, causing Amy to blush at her prior mistake, ''Anyway, you've gotta get into makeup before anything else. I got told to take you straight there, kinda like a handsome police esco--'' ''I'll be fine,'' the redhead insisted, cutting off his attempted pass, ''Just point me in the direction and, uhh, make sure I don't walk across any extra scenes?'' Meekly, the unnamed agent pointed across the heated concrete lot and Amy nodded knowingly, beginning her trek further into an industry that had previously only existed in her DVD player on late, lonely nights. It was the beginning of something wonderful... ...Or, as was the case with most of her stories, it was the beginning of something wonderfully tainted. InterjectionDeath At Your Door-------------------------------Wednesday, October 19thShe expected nothing walking back into her apartment. At the very least, she didn't expect what she saw. The family was in trouble... not just a single person - half of the family seemed to be in turmoil over individual problems, and Amy had come home after work on a small movie role to help sort out everybody's anxieties. Her father had always said 'It never rains, but it pours', and that was ringing terribly true. After going to LA for a two day dream job, she'd been worn down to the last of her nerves by phonecalls from family who needed her help. The problem was that Amy was the absolute LAST person a normal human being would turn to for help - so she knew they were on their last legs solution-wise. The problems of everybody else overshadowed hers, and she was thankful for that... but her family problems were still her problems. It wasn't like she lacked at all in concern for her brother, her sisters... She rolled her carry-on through the door of the loft and ignored closing the door in favor of making it to the kitchen to get her car keys as soon as possible - after all, she wasn't the only impatient member of her family. Rush and hurry were ever-apparent in her steps as she sucked harshly on the water bottle that she'd plucked from a vending machine at the airport, not wanting to wait in any food service lines. Stepping into the apartment, nothing was more prevalent than the scent of alcohol and sweat - not terribly uncommon for her home, but her sister wasn't there. In fact, nobody had been there in the past two days... yet the scent of something was thick in the air. A mixture of something chemical, and human perspiration. The sweat could've easily been coming from her - she was anxious, after all - but the artificial, sterile and chemical smell... not normal. Regardless, she was in a hurry to get the keys to her Audi so she could make it to her parents' home before their supper hour, where one of her older brothers and pregnant sister would be waiting. It might have been a hurried homecoming, but it was abnormal... Mainly because Amy Campbell never made it to the kitchen. Chapter TwoLosing It------------------------------- The cameras weren't rolling quite yet, but excitement was peaking as Amy sat in the makeup chair, cheeks being lightly dabbed at by a platinum blonde woman who wore rather inhuman looking leather pants. Her thighs had the little redhead entranced, and not in a sexy way - the cow skin creaked and crackled with every motion that the woman's torso made. Every time she leaned in toward Amy's face or neck, Campbell was overcome with wonder at how she could bend her legs even slightly in the outfit. It was the same perdicament she encountered with the viewing of any Matrix movie; The story got left out in favor of her sitting in awe of the fact that anybody - Keanu Reeves or no Keanu Reeves - could move in such constricting garb. The Leather Pants Perdicament had consumed her to the point that she didn't notice the MIDI ringtone of her cellphone kick in until it was tickling her leg from the not-so-subtle vibration in her pocket. The makeup artist didn't say a word, apparently used to her clientel ignoring her in favor of the latest communication technologies, and Amy quickly picked up the phone with a thousand apologies to those around her and a quick shake of her head, finally able to divert her attention from the creaking thighs. ''He--whoa whoa, calm down, dad... what's wrong?'' On the other line, her father was sighing and out of breath thanks to a heightened anxiety. He was a hyper man by nature, but never to the point of breathing as heavily as he was. ''Your brother... Chris... he's, he's just lost it.'' It was urgent, that much was apparent. Her father continued without any response from Amy. ''He thinks there are cameras everywhere, and says the income tax people are after all of us. He's convinced himself that someone is watching him and everyone else, and... we just can't deal with it here. We were thinking of calling your therapist, whatshername. Can you talk to her? Can you talk to Chris? He kept saying you knew what he was talking about...'' ''I have no clu--wait... call Viv, okay? Adult Mental Health at the hospital, it's in the phonebook. I... I've gotta try to process this before I talk to anyone, alright? Just tell Vivian I need her to help us with this. I'll head home early, but there's stuff I need to do today, okay?'' ''Alright, just call me back as soon as you can. Your mother's wild about this, and you know how she deals with things...'' ''Don't worry,'' Amy reassured, ready to press the power-off button on her Nokia phone, ''Look, I've gotta go handle this stuff so I can head back, but I'll call...'' ''Love you. Bye.'' With that, it was through... and Aimz was struck dumb. The quietest of her brothers had lost his mind? Was it drugs, schizophrenia...? So many questions littered her head, but the least of them were what her makeup artist had in mind. It seemed she'd ignored the call completely in favor of small talk. ''Soooo... big match later this week, huh?'' When the woman backed up for a moment to clip part of the Canadian's blaze red hair up, Amy shook her head slightly to rid it of the cobwebs that her family interaction had caused. ''I, uhh... not really. It's a 'curtain raiser', which basically means I get to go out and give eight minutes of my time to help the audience warm up their 'boo's for the night. They never fail to give me the most insignificant part of a show.'' ''Don't you win some big prize if you beat the guy you're after?'' ''I win if I beat Ferg in my first match, and then the winner of ANOTHER match in the semi main event... if I make it that far.'' ''Why wouldn'tcha?'' Amy's brow raised just as the woman took a pencil to it, ''Uh, do you watch much wrestling?'' ''My fiance does, so I've seen ya a few times. Worked with Danny Ferguson once, too.'' As far as Campbell was concerned, that was a perfect segway. She practically hoped that the woman would get a raise after this project, if she answered the right questions. And by the right questions, she meant ''Really? Can you tell me anything about him?'' ''Not much more than you'd know. 'Cept he complained about always getting a kink in his right shoulder if he slept on it. That was before he tried getting me to bed, though, so it could've been bull,'' she snickered, ''Actually, most of what he said seemed to be blowin' smoke... but the kids love him. And he's pretty damn good at what he does.'' ''Wrestling fans seem to believe he's the second coming,'' Amy huffed and corrected herself, ''The stupid ones, at least.'' ''Explains why my fiance likes him, then. He's not a big fan of you, though. Something about Karin--'' ''Sorry to cut you short and all, but that's a bit of a sore subject... especially if --errr--when I make it past Ferguson.'' ''All I'm sayin' is that Ferguson's a big star here, girly. A big star.'' The intention was to have been subtle, but that hadn't worked so well. Campbell didn't seem to be brought down at all by the emphasis, and the hired makeup assistant was phased slightly by that arrogance. She dabbed some rose onto Amy's cheekbones and sighed, bitterly noticing the glare the redhead gave her. It was the same glare that so many upstarts who wished they were more would give her, trying to assure her of their confidence. It was never the confidence that they lacked, though... She sighed again, ''Just be careful who you talk to about wanting to kick his ass around here. Especially if you ever wanna get into any decent party in this city.'' Amy sneered, but stayed silent until the woman directed her to the door, backing away from the collapsable makeup chair. ''Wardrobe's in the next trailer, they'll need ya now,'' she mumbled, ''And, uhh... not to pry, but you were a little short with your dad on the phone earlier. I wasn't just staying quiet to be polite, yaknow... it was a little shocking, and kinda rude if I can be honest. Might wanna call him b--'' She never expected to be so quickly and, again, rudely cut off. ''No, you can't be honest. I'm a wrestler - unarmed people aren't honest to us. I'll be off to wardrobe now...'' Aimz snarled, catty side kicking into full throttle, ''...and I hope you never try giving Russel Crowe family advice, 'cause you're already pretty lucky that I've got a bad throwing arm.'' With her phone placed securely in her pocket and out of her temper's range, Amy practically stormed out of the trailer, much to the surprise of the artists and bigger stars in the next chairs. Nobody looked particularly impressed, even when she'd puffed out her chest for those last few threats. Of course, neither Amy nor her ego had been around to see that part. InterjectionDie By The Sworn----------------------------------Wednesday, October 19thShe'd been headed for the kitchen, but stopped at an unusual scent and the light noise of fabric shifting against fabric to her left. Stepping back to close the front door she'd left ajar, she determined that there was an intruder and, more than likely, they would try to escape - so she kept the door covered, and made certain to lock it. Who was there, though? Danny Ferguson, Tchu, Ellis Easton, Karina Wolfenden... Dave Gibson? Hopefully, just a burglar she could maim in 'self-defense'. Reassuring herself that it was probably just Killean trying to surprise her, Amy turned the corner that lead to her livingroom and found a body on the couch. Nobody dead, but he hardly looked alive and he mumbled a half-hearted greeting. It was the body, voice and meek attitude of none other than Jeph Howe, her ex-boyfriend and always an unusually jilted lover. He was exposed, not physically but in the sense that his eyes gave away everything he thought. Wearing his usual tight jeans and a silky button-up shirt that kept all but the tattoos on his hands and throat covered. He looked weakly at her and chuckled... or, at least she THOUGHT he chuckled. That chuckle turned out to be only an attempt that lead into a cough escaping his beard-scruffed lips. As she got closer, the smell of alcohol grew bigger. ''Jeph... what the hell? What're you doing here?'' ''Came to see you one last time... nothing more...'' Howe coughed again and made the air thick with the scent of alcohol. Amy covered her mouth and looked back over her shoulder at the kitchen area, ''Are you drunk?'' ''N... nah... juss livin' without... need not to die? Mebbe?'' He was drunk, and it had better not have been her booze. She immediately turned and stormed into the kitchen, ignoring the odd visit almost entirely as she looked through her cupboards only to notice a half-emptied quart of dark rum in a corner of the countertop. That explained why everything smelled like booze, but beside the bottle was a MUCH more disconcerting discovery... Jeph needed a hospital, and fast. Chapter ThreeWardrobe Malfunction---------------------------------The chest was puffed out and her shoes were platformed, but walking into the second trailer, Amy felt that the odds were stacked unevenly against her - especially when the first person she saw immediately stood up and announced his exit. According to his lisping dialect, he was 'Off to talk to Danny F', his supposed best friend, and couldn't 'waste another minute around here'... as if he was looking at a small time movie or stars. In Amy's head, he wasn't looking at anything small-time. Unfortunately, not everybody agreed with her... and by that, it meant nobody agreed with her. Stepping up to the stylist that had wandered over near her, she mumbled something about what problem the man who'd just left might have had, and the woman - who had already brought over concept art from the film for Amy's split second scene - explained that he was one of Danny Ferguson's inner circle and had a problem with mostly everyone who worked on small budgets. Of course, Amy's answer was ''I guess he doesn't know how big this is gonna be.'' and, in a move equally as expected, the nameless stylist chuckled to herself and spoke until she HAD a name. ''I'm Grace and, all things considered...'' she looked Amy up, then down and up again until she came to one conclusion: ''We're not gonna need much work to make you look the part of a grungy young skate punk clerking a record store.'' Given what she'd heard, the world around her at that moment belonged to people like Brad Pitt and to people like Danny Ferguson. Not to people like Amy Campbell, and that's the only reason she didn't snap at her stylist. She opted to wait until the second day of the shoot to piss people off too badly. ''I'll... try to take that as a compliment,'' she surrendered mildly to the other woman's insult, ''It's good that we don't need to do much, I can get to work early and not be pressed for time.'' Grace laughed aloud, ''Pressed for time? Sweetheart,'' she cackled, ''Hate breakin' it to ya, but you've got two days to do about three seconds of filming... you ain't gonna be pressed for time.'' Amy looked around to the Hollywood types who populated the rest of the small room. Some were snickering in her direction, while others were too wrapped up in their T-Mobile Sidekicks to care and, given the way Grace was looking at her, the little redhead was overcome with a rather sudden sense of inadequacy that she tried overshadowing with hard stares and a tall stance. Why were they all trying to be cruel? Didn't they know what wrestlers do? Regardless, she was under the gaze of the stylist once more. This time, Grace appeared ENTIRELY confused. ''Are you...'' she began, befuddled, ''...wearing your own shirt?'' And she didn't mean simply a shirt that Amy owned. The shirt Aimz wore... well... had her name on it, right alongside a cartoon chariacture of her. It was topped off by a PRIME logo on the back of the tattered, cut-up and entirely too self-serving to be truly 'punk' t-shirt. In Amy's mind, it was the way people would remember her for sure when they saw the movie. In Grace's mind, it was a travesty of redneck wrestling egotism and it had to go. ''Get that thing off before someone smacks you, honey.'' It was time for Amy to put her foot down... kind of. Maybe. ''I, uh... why does it need to come off? What's wrong with the shirt?'' ''Tom Cruise is a dick, but even HE doesn't walk onto a set holding a sign that says 'I banged Nicole Kidman'. Understand?'' ''Not enti--'' ''Well, you don't have to. This isn't a movie about wrestling, and you're not a wrestler in it... so change your god damned shirt, girl.'' Amy grew lightheaded. Had that woman really grown balls so big? Did she have any idea what Aimz did for a living? The little insiders were still snickering, and Amy was only growing angrier. Finally, she really stood up for herself - for her innocence and for her talent - by speaking up. ''Who the hell are you to be insulting me like this?'' she growled, ''I don't see security in here, and I'm pretty fucking sure you couldn't take me fist for fist, y'know.'' Instead of wide eyes, her threats were met by an unexpected reaction... ... Jolly laughter. ''Have you got any idea who this lady is?'' one of the hangers-on chuckled, pointing to the stylist, ''Gracie here could buy and sell your ass, sweetie. She's got more pull in tinsel town than you ever will... so back your ass down if you wanna stay on this movie.'' Aimz' head grew even lighter. She'd officially been targeted by the biggest bullies around. Though they were skinny and tight in the pants, they stood FAR above her because - quite simply - she was on their turf. She was a stranger there, and she elected to make herself scarce. Grabbing the shirt Grace had held to her and sneering, the little redhead left the trailer almost with tears in her eyes - still trying to look upstanding and intimidating but knowing that she was failing with each slowed step. When she finally did make it out the door to stumble down the steps of the trailer, the Hollywooders broke out once more in laughter. ''Can you guys believe her? Wearing her name on her damn shirt?'' Who did that little Canadian girl think she was, anyway? Did she seriously think she had pull in that town? Laughable. InterjectionI'll Kill You If You...---------------------------------Wednesday, October 19th''Jeph!'' It was frantic. He needed help. She was nearly crying, and he looked calm as an autumn morning. In her kitchen, Amy had found a bottle of Ibuprofen painkillers next to the open bottle of rum and examined it quickly, finding it nearly emptied. Seconds later, she was shaking her ex-boyfriend on her couch, shouting at him. Her mind raced with a million possibilities, and none of them involved the word 'Why?' - she was concentrated on the fact that there was no way she was letting Jeph die. Not at his age. Not on her couch. ''Jepha,'' she hugged him ''C'mon, we've gotta go to the hospital... you're gonna go comatose.'' He nodded slightly, consciousness quickly evading his grasp as the amount of pills he'd taken took their footholds in his brain - trying as hard as they could to shut it down, to relax him once and for all. Luckily, he'd come to the couch of a fighter. A fighter with a cell phone that she KNEW how to use. Nobody would die today, not on Amy Campbell's watch. Maiming people in a ring was one thing, but this situation was something she refused to be responsible for. Chapter FourChild's Play--------------------------------Who needed scripts when you had fine, and free wine in catering? Despite what occurred back in wardrobe, she'd changed into a bright pink tanktop provided by Grace, who'd mocked her until the very end. However, the wine would make it better; Martini & Rossi, Asti... the very best of the best, at least in Amy's mind. She let it swirl around her tongue, having taken the bottle as opposed to the pre-filled glasses provided by the catering service. Around the room were various assistants and low-level extras, as Amy'd avoided the star suite at all costs - not needing her treatment of their supposed favorite wardrobe queen to spread around too quickly, or tarnish her already mild reputation on the set. Unbeknownst to her, she wouldn't have been allowed in the suite for stars anyway. Despite what she might've thought, Amy was one of the lower level workers. She was a level above an extra, with the tiniest of speaking roles which - in her mind, at least - was one of her biggest accomplishments. Immortality on film. Enjoying the fruity taste of the white wine, she grinned and swirled it around her mouth before swallowing with pride - proud to be presumably the only one in the room who understood the finer points of the drink. That didn't change the fact that she was alone in the crowd, however. Alone only, however, until a child approached her. A chubby boy of about eight or nine years, he grinned up at her relentlessly, waiting for her to smile back until she finally did. ''Can I help you?'' Amy grinned to the boy, always entertained by youngsters. ''Yer Aimz!'' he beamed, ''My daddy said he seen you. When Tchu wins the crown, can you get me an autograph?'' Amy cocked her head to one side. When Tchu wins what? JiTC?! She cracked her neck, but couldn't take any action against the kid. He was just a child, and an apparent fan of her direct competition. She couldn't really blame him, considering the looks and glory of the older men in comparison to their young admirers. Guys like Danny Ferguson and even Tchu had the ladies, the muscles and the power in the eyes of these kids. She just had a push-up bra and sexual appeal that little boys were a little too young to grasp quite yet. But, the kid was still pinning her rival to win. That would require someone beating her in the finals, be it Tchu or Ferguson. Maybe he'd even pick KARINA over her, god forbid. ''I'll, uhh... yeah. You want one from me?'' ''Naw, I gotta get back to daddy.'' He said promptly, running in the opposite direction with a measure of glee she hadn't experienced since... well... since she was that old. Of course, it was hardly the response she'd expected - and the nobodies crowding the room who'd been eavesdropping were already giggling over the could shoulder Aimz had just gotten from a boy who wasn't even a decade old. Ignoring their stifled laughter, Amy glared at her own reflection when she met it in a mirror set beside the door. Her eyes were set on her arms, her shoulders and the meanest stare she could possibly conjur. Sadly, that stare was more pathetic than Amy herself felt at that point. She felt faded and possibly too caught up in the movie role, despite its immensity. In all of her time in Hollywood, she'd ignored her physical appearance almost entirely... and that needed to be remedied. Right in time for her stare to grow weaker, the little redhead sat down against the wall and stretched her legs out in front of her, silently stretching and - maybe a little secretly - attempting to astonish those in the room with her flexibility and physical skill. Her chin met each knee once, twice, ten times before she brought her arms over her head and began creaking her neck to each side, limber in her movements and smiling at the sight of the muscles in her shoulders flexing out and in. In her eyes, she was the toughest looking human in that room - despite the pink tanktop and lipstick. She raised an eyebrow and looked up to the people who'd previously been chuckling over her, only to find that... ...Well... ...They were still laughing at her. ''What in the hell is wrong with people around here?!'' It didn't take long for her to get stand up, glaring all the while, and snatch a bottle of wine from the main catering table as she sneered at each and every single person in the room, side-stepping toward the door. As Amy made her exit, they took to talking and, once again, laughing. ''Looks like someone's finally seeing her place on the food chain,'' one quipped. Another piped in by citing that he hoped ''That she's better at acting than wrestling.'' while another chimed her gruff response to the presence of Amy 'Aimz' Campbell on their set... ''She's not. The girl's hopeless. She's got a shit match and a shit role in a shit film. The chick's fighting Danny Ferguson, she's doomed! She shoulda stayed home in her igloo, 'cause this world is way too real for her. She's gonna realize this week exactly how insignificant she is... and it's gonna be funny as all hell to watch the fallout.'' The naysayers kept chuckling heartily and exchanging insults amongst themselves, entirely aware - and happy - that Amy was halfway across the set at that point, probably getting ready to shoot herself between the eyes. If there was one thing to be said about 'Tinsel Town', it's that it might love its Danny Fergusons and even Ellis Eastons, but it would NOT give Amy Campbells any breaks. InterjectionYou Have No Strength To Squander-------------------------------Wednesday, October 19thIt wasn't going to happen. Amy smiled when the paramedic looked up to her, nodding that her friend would be fine. Jeph was looking at both of them with glassy, pleading eyes. It was obvious enough that his original intentions when consuming rum with an overdose of painkillers weren't the intentions he had at that moment. He wanted to live, and not to come so close to unconsciousness. He was faint, and couldn't entirely make out what Amy was saying... ''It'll be fine,'' she grinned sympathetically at Jeph, then looked up to the medic riding in the back of the ambulance as he rechecked Howe's vitals, ''I've overdosed a million times, Jepha... it'll be fine.'' The paramedic raised an eyebrow, but Amy continued. She was breathing heavily, but tried to remain confident - reassuring them all, ''It hasn't killed me yet, no matter how hard I've tried. I dunno why you wanted to do this after seeing me try it so much and living through the bad times with me, but you did it... and it'll be fine. You're stronger than me so if I can live through it, you know you can.'' How many times was she going to reference herself? Once more, the medic looked at her with questioning eyes that she was ignoring entirely in favor of trying to reassure Jeph. He was hazily taking it all in, but nobody in that ambulance was quite sure who was hearing what... ...or why Amy Campbell had to respond to anxiety by not shutting the hell up. Chapter FiveMe, Myself and I'm Kinda Hot--------------------------------The lines were small, but they were intense. They required the utmost of acting talent, and the director had called upon none other than Amy Campbell for that talent. It was bigger than any backstage sketch, it was the BIG big time. Hollywood. Tinsel Town. 'Fergieland', as some had noted to her... but that was to be no more. This was the Amyville Horror - the movie that would make her - and the lines had nothing to do with cocaine, proving that her life had truly turned around for the better. Sure, she only had a couple of words to say... and she might only be on film for two seconds... ...But it was the exposure that counted, right? She was going to kill the part. It was time to show the acting world exactly how to channel emotional turmoil and a life of pain through to a couple of simple lines. That's right, she'd make the audience cry their damn eyes out. ''You sure you want that record? It's a little... dee---- err--- shit, sorry guys!'' A secondary actor shook his head, and so did the woman beside him. The director went through Amy's line once more with her, and Campbell sighed. ''Alright... ready now. Let's do it.'' Amy stretched her neck and cracked her knuckles as they called out that the cameras were still rolling. She was NOT going to screw that up again. She cleared her throat, ''You sure you want that CD?'' ''NO.'' the actor beside her growled, ''The line is 'Are you sure you want that record?'. For the love of Christ, who is this chick?!'' The lady who stood next to him touched his arm lightly, ''Now, calm down. She's just a wrestler. She's nervous.'' Just a wrestler? You couldn't kill people on set, right? Too many cameras. Amy'd have to take it. ''I'm ready. Roll.'' ''You don't give that direction,'' shouted the director, ''I do. Now... ROLLING!'' Amy cleared her throat again, looking increasingly nervous. She washed her face of the anxiety and transformed back to looking casual within a moment. Now, THAT was acting! ''You sure you want this record? It's a little... deep.'' ''Of course I'm sure.'' one of the actors muttered and met her eyes as she handed it over the fake record store counter, ''It's for my lady friend here.'' CUT! ''Beautiful, guys. Apparently not, as a security guard and production assistant quickly came up behind her. At least they threw in the security guard to feed her ego, it made her feel dangerous. And those hadn't been any secondary actors, they were the LEAD actors. Amy just avoided recognizing that fact, as she wanted to save any importance found on set for herself. She smiled a little to herself as they took her back off of the immediate set, happy to have done two days' work in one and happier that she acted the hell outta that line. Why was everybody looking at her, then at one another? She heard a softly spoken 'newbie' comment, which was a given... but didn't they know just who the hell she was? She laid bodies out for a living, and she didn't HAVE to pretend when she was backstage - unlike the rest of the PRIME crew. Obviously, these people were ignorant to the world of professionalism in fighting and wrestling. That was ever-apparent when one man shouted to her in passing: ''Hey! Tell Danny to call me back, I'm one of his agents!'' One of? Okay, it was a given that Ferguson was big in Hollywood. He was great at pretending in PRIME that he was actually worth a second of people's time, and he brought most of his fanbase from movie geek fanboys who were so engulfed in their IMDB message boards that they wouldn't have known the meaning of an unscripted fight if they got booted in the mouth by The Rock. The thing was, he didn't have that much fight in him - not truly. He was an act, and little more. At least, that's what she'd be hoping on the eve of the Pay-Per-View. It was twisted and defaming, and didn't do anything to boost her confidence given the match she was headed into. Spend a couple of days in Hollywood, then go face Danny Ferguson? As Angelo Desoprano would say, fuhgettaboutit. With confidence hitting an all-time low, and concern for her family starting to resurface now that her work was done, Amy stayed in the free hotel room all morning - entirely ignoring any extra casting calls or the promise of a breakfast with her all-too horny agent, whose next appointment was most likely a wannabe Jenna Jameson, given his far too casual and cheap method of going about things. Not with her family - that was too depressing - but with a variety of people from Killean Sirrajin to Tyler O'Neill and eventually an apologetic voicemail sent the way of Dave Gibson. Final ChapterEscaping Early----------------------------------The flight attendant was pissing her off. The uncomfortable chair was pissing her off. The pillow that was sticking itself to her leg pissed her off. The fact that her can of Coke had given her a shock pissed her off. SHE pissed her off. All through her time in Hollywood, she'd been entirely self-serving. She'd practically ignored her family problems in what she thought was a noble act to complete an obligation to a production company she'd been happy to get hired by. She didn't even buy into Jeph's attempted suicide when he was laying on her couch, steady in the process of dying. She'd only had concern for herself. Even at that moment, she was only thinking about herself. Everything Aimz thought was about her or how the world around her related TO her. Wasn't that the human condition? Could it really be THAT bad? With those questions, she thought back to her history in PRIME. fWo. Her divorce. Her addictions. Her admissions. Her ridiculously self-righteous glorification of rehabilitation and 'getting help'. Everything she'd done in and out of a ring. All of the people she'd shunned, and even justifying the existance of a human being like Karina Wolfenden as someone who was born to BE in her life... He was right, and the overwhelming sense of that fact made her want to cut that Coke can open and slit her throat with it... But that was the selfish answer, wasn't it? This flipped everything she'd ever thought sideways, and then raped it. The purpose to existance became to find out exactly why she was the way she was, but even THAT was incredibly selfish. In all of it, Amy realized that she'd never gotten used to thinking or talking about something that didn't relate directly to her success or downfall. With that in mind, she was entirely lost - and no longer comfortable in her own skin. Jeph was right, and she ran away from that. She hadn't even called her parents yet to explain why she wouldn't be stopping by on her way to LA, or why their daughter was a completely egocentric bitch. She had a feeling they already knew the answers. Welcome to a new way of thinking. A new girl. It had to happen, or she'd crumble into herself and lose everything - everyone - she had. Aimz was heading straight for a Pay-Per-View without passing Go, and only collecting knowledge to better herself... or so she'd hope. She needed to be a part of an entirely new school of thought, but she needed to FIND that thought first. Training was only fuel for her self-induced hype machine. Flexing wouldn't solve this, and neither would kicking someone in the face. She had fortune down, but what was the true definition of selflessness? That left yet another question... What the hell happens now?
