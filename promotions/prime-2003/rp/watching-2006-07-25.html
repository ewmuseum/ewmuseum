Watching

25 July 2006

Date: 7/25/06 “Yes, Comrade, we do this for Lenin, we do this for Stalin, and for our Motherland! We make an example out of Gamble this week, and we take down the only wrestler capable of staring you in the eye, St. James, in very near future!” The gigantic Russian next to him unleashed a titanic laugh. “Lets get to America. It’s time to smash an upstart.” Profile 21 lowered the headphones from his left ear and powered the ranged ear dish down. He carefully set the small apparatus into a nondescript briefcase and shutting it. He felt the butts of a pair of .22 caliber pistols against his ribcage underneath his long jacket. He always preferred the smaller caliber for handguns; with one of those, he could, as a native of Savannah might say, shoot hairs off a gnat ass. But today, he was hoping for some more information. Carefully, he leaned back into the rented room he was staying in. The two Russians were retreating now in the pouring rain. He'd had to calibrate the ranged listening device in order to compensate, but it was also good cover. So, there he sat in the dark, considering. Why was he even here? It wasn't that he didn't think Titan could handle himself. In fact, Titan had made some rather cagey moves and managed to take down Stanislav at the one hundredth show. The move with the ring ropes was inspired and cunning. That wasn't to take much from Stanislav. The big Russian would have had Titan at his mercy had it not been for that one caveat. But know 21 knew who Ruslan was. September, 1972 Back at that point, 21 had gone by a different name, but he'd had basically the same work. And Ruslan had given him implicit instructions about a base to hit. It was supposed to be a special target that the South and the US weren't going to hit, but Ruslan wanted it blown up to anger the Viet Cong. It had been an easy insertion. He'd laid the bomb down, walked out and picked up the radio transmitter when he got far enough. In addition, he'd laid out claymores at the tunnel entrances and entrances to all the structures. So, when he hit the button, the big boom would've brought people running. Right into a hail of US explosives packed with high velocity ball bearings. But it was Ruslan that had struck him. The little man was ruthlessly efficient, every report, every job was a recitation of body counts and reactions. This reaction was one of the dominos that pushed the VC even harder into South Vietnam. Ruslan had seen it as a necessary step for the Soviets to take. While other, more idealistic communists might find the tactics to be distasteful, Ruslan had encouraged him, even suggested ways to up mortality rates. So, the explosion had gone off. Most of the camp was dead from a hail of claymore shrapnel. In fact, most of the people in the camps were sure it was an attack by Americans. The dead Green Beret that 21 had left there, blown from the bomb site, only confirmed their suspicions. Now, he looked up at where the small man had walked away. Apparently, it had been his task to handle the giant Stanislav. That was frightening. It was apparent that Ivan had been indoctrinated into the communist way of thought, but with Ruslan now in the picture and Nelson gone, the whole dynamic changed. Before, Nelson had been the real focus of Ivan's rage. Things had changed. Titan was strong, tough, all of that, but if he wanted to win, things needed to change. Slowly, he picked up his case and headed for the door. Titan would need to hear about this as soon as possible.
