Their Story: Part III - An Examination of Dreams (match RP for ReV 89)

6 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/6/06 His It had happened several times. Two versions. Always the same… one or the other. On a Thursday, he would dream of that frozen night in mid-December where he had captured the Universal championship… and then the face of PRIME had suffered a terrible death, changing everything in an instant. And in the dream, Hin See, the unfortunate subject of the grotesque occurrence, would avoid the flames, avoid the bang, and avoid the eternal nothingness that followed for the victim of any such violence. In fact, the dream would be stunning in its simplicity. There was never even a need for Hin See to avoid the explosion that had reverberated in the in the minds of PRIMEates everywhere and in the eardrums of those in unfortunate proximity. The dream would lack the boom, the flames, the terror and tragedy. Instead, Hin See would make his way across the lot, dusting off the newborn flakes that had fallen from the winter sky and come to rest on the sleeves of his coat, so that they wouldn’t melt and dampen his attire the second he took his seat in the warm confines of the limousine. He would sit, listen to the hum of the engine, think about the new direction of his company and hypothesize what 2006 would look like for PRIME and its devoted members and followers. He would sit, wait patiently for Pham to join him so they could congratulate each other on the successful close of the old year, and begin to welcome the new. At no point would there be any horrific cloud of smoke and fire erupt into the sky, defying the gravity that carried the snowflakes down to earth with gentile care. And that would be it. A dream mostly composed of by the lack of anything. It couldn’t be said, however, that this dream was the grieving of a man who was tormented by the loss of a great figure and close friend, and who wanted nothing more than the happiness his companion brought him. The dream was as sick in its foundation as the act of violence itself had been. Hin See’s survival was the key to Matthew’s respect, the way to his moment in the spotlight. The death of one man had stolen the thoughts and attention of everyone under the very sun he hoped to bask in the glow of. If there had been no explosion, no attempt, successful or not, to take the life of Hin See… then the highlight of the night to carry PRIME into 2006 would have been of the newly crowned champion. The picture to fill the minds of every PRIME member and fan alike should have been that of The Inhuman Being, championship in hand, standing over a broken and beaten King of Kings. It should have been the thought that preoccupied everyone as they tried to ignore their family for the holidays. It should have been the vision that filled the eyes of everyone as they bowed their heads and closed their eyes for grace before Christmas dinner. Instead, their thoughts and visions were of the tragic news they’d heard regarding Hin See. Their prayers were with his family and closest friends. With one explosion, in a singular boom, the significance had seeped out of his success as quickly as ash had replaced the falling snow. And no sooner had Matthew acquired the victory than it lost its relevance… until it just didn’t matter. In a moment, that very same moment, his success died, but without the tragedy, the bang, or the importance. And then, no one remembered. So the dream wasn’t about miracles, or grieving for a lost life. It wasn’t a dream that made real the impossible wishes of an individual unable to handle the loss of his friend. It was simply one man, unable to cope with the insignificance of his successes, dreaming… lusting for an alternate moment to fill the reality… a moment that would allow him to bask in the glory he had achieved. It was selfish. Then, on… say a Saturday… he would dream of that same night. The explosion that was the finale to The Great American Nightmare. The flames that extinguished his place at the top of the mountain. In this version, Hin See wouldn’t avoid anything. Things would occur just as they tragically had in actuality. But afterward, after the fire department had suffocated the burning wreckage, after nearly every soul at the show had gone home… or wherever they had to go… his dream would continue with a strange encounter. In the parking lot, just a few steps away from the clear spot on the asphalt where the limousine had sat parked, blocking the snow, he would somehow find himself standing across from Hin See. And under the luminous beam of the parking lot lights, Matthew would notice that his employer looked very much dignified, unscarred, as if he hadn’t been phased by a moment in the pressure and heat. And without a word being spoken, after just a few seconds face to face, in a simplistic rage, he would unleash a furious swing towards Hin See. The bone of his fist would connect with the cheek of the man standing across from him. Lifelessly, Hin See would fall into the thin layer of fresh snow that covered the coarse blacktop. But the assault wouldn’t cease. Without giving it a second thought… without giving it a first thought… he would drop down over his victim and continue to brutalize the man who had offered him the opportunity to obtain great success, then stolen his thunder. Between the sick sound of bone breaking bone again and again, Matthew would scream of lost glory and success going unnoticed. He would question, repeatedly, who would remember his victory after this? Then he would answer his own question… no one would remember. And then another crushing blow. Soon, the snow around the men would start to turn a pink color, then deepen as the trickle of blood spilled more freely, until each and every flake around the head of Hin See was stained red, the purity of white nowhere to be found. Finally, the violence would stop, and as he would rise to his feet, the lights in the parking lot would, curve, shift, contort until they all aimed his way, spewing their glow on his shoulder, leaving him standing in their light. Alone. Funny that he would dream about killing the man whose untimely death he blamed for the lack of attention paid to his successes. Sensibility wasn’t frequenting his dreams lately. Just loss and destruction. There were occasionally dreams of losing the Universal Championship. Sometimes he would dream of submitting to Walkins. Other times he would be knocked senseless and be down, out cold for the three count. Once, he had even dreamed that before the match had started, while he had stood on the second turnbuckle, title raised proudly above his head… that it had simply vanished. And when he had lowered his right arm, it had come down light, nothing in its grasp. Gone. All before the bell had even rung. Lost. Undoubtedly, there was a great amount of blame to be passed around for his collapse. The most appropriate thing to do would be to take the bulk of that blame on his own shoulders. After all, he was the one who had remained rather silent in PRIME, and he was the one who had lost the match to Clyde Walkins. But for Matthew, it didn’t seem quite right to shoulder all the blame himself. And slowly, with the passing of each day, he found himself dishing more and more blame out to other individuals. The dreams of Hin See’s death were example enough of the blame Matt tossed his former boss’s direction. As disgusting as it might have been to actually give the fact a thought, Hin See’s death had stolen the attention away from the crowning moment of Matthew’s professional career. Then of course, was the general cast of PRIME executives, who could be blamed for booking him for a title defense the first show after he had captured the Universal Championship, knowing full well that he wasn’t physically fit to be competing. For that lack of consideration given to his physical state and capability to properly do his job… Matt blamed anyone and everyone who could call themselves an executive of PRIME. The fans could be blamed, they were the ones who never seemed to fully recognize what he was accomplishing in a fed as supremely talented from top to bottom as PRIME. Somehow, his undefeated streak, JITC victory, and Universal title win all seemed to fly under the radar of the general fan. And of course Walkins could be blamed. As much as Matthew had himself to kick in the ass for perhaps not taking his opponent seriously enough… it had been the Clyderman who beat him for the title. Walkins was the one who had walked away with his Universal championship in hand. Walkins was the one who had left him with a foul taste in his mouth, one unique to his tenure in PRIME. The taste of loss. And such was the case in another dream he would have… one of an entirely different night, an ambiguous evening, far from the confines of a PRIME ring. Though there were no flames, no ashes, no death or championship belts, the themes of loss and collapse were highly present. Unlike the two versions of the GAN dream, where he passed blame around to the fans who forgot, the man who died, everyone but himself…in this wondering journey of his scrambled mind, he took full responsibility for the way everything had fallen apart. He would meet Mary in a nice restaurant, a small little steakhouse tucked far away from the food-row that lined the interstate at Exit 83B. Under the dim, forgettable lighting (the best kind. Who wants to be constantly noticing the hot lamp swinging overhead while trying to enjoy a romantic dinner?) they would discuss the last few months of their lives. She would ask questions about all the days she had spent in a rare amnesic state; he would halfheartedly recount a few specific events, then say “nothing much really happened”. Then, they would pause to order dinner. He would get prime rib… medium rare, with sides of fries and applesauce. She would order a filet, medium, a baked potato and a salad to go along with her meal. A root beer for her and a coke for him and then the waiter would be on his way with a small smile and nod of his head. From there, with a little hesitation, she would mention the wrestling. His face would immediately contort to show his concern and lack of comfort with the subject. In a hushed, barely audible tone, Mary would tell him about all the reasons she didn’t like him wrestling… reasons he already knew... and then she would ask why. Not why he had gone back to it, but why he wouldn’t quit when she had recovered from the amnesia. As they waited for their dinner, he would remain silent, as if he hadn’t heard the question, or didn’t care that she had asked it... and at no point, would he answer the question. Not while they waited for dinner, not after the waiter had brought them their meals. Not during dessert, or as they crossed the parking lot and headed for the car. It was at that point, she would decide enough was enough. Under the lights of the lot, much like the scene that followed The Great American Nightmare, a different sort of explosion would occur. Mary would yell, scream, cry… he would do the same, and despite the fact that the dream would begin with the two arriving together in his car, she would choke out one final ‘goodbye’ through her tears and head for her car, leaving him standing alone at his own vehicle, under the glow of the parking lot lights. Alone. * * * Stacey’s Stacey didn’t dream all that much, at least not while she slept. She was more concerned with making things happen, and to be honest, she had been growing frustrated by the fact that Matt hadn’t answered any of her calls, or returned any of the messages she had left on his machine. It had been several weeks since she had showed up at his doorstep… almost a month… and she hadn’t seen or heard from him since. Given the fact that she had made the journey specifically in hopes of talking with him, it was safe to say things had been generally unsuccessful. Three years before, it had occurred to her that Matt had been one of her closest friends in life. Despite the fact that they had known each other only a little over a year at the time, Matt had gone out of his way to help her on numerous occasions. Of course, at the time, there had been no Mary in the picture. She had been back home in Ohio with her fiancé, an old friend of hers and Matt’s. So, as Stacey had found herself quickly falling for Matthew, she had decided there was no reason not to make that fact known; however, before her innocent flirtations could evolve to an advanced state, Mary… in some twisted irony… made her triumphant return to Matt’s life. He had reacted immediately, leaving her sitting alone on the edge of the bed in a small hotel room. It turned out, as she would later discover, Mary had come back to Matt after her fiancé had come home late after a long days work one evening and taken out his frustrations on her face. That evening in the hotel room was the last she had seen of Matt up until this last month when she had shown up at his door. The difference this time, was that she wasn’t concerned with whether or not Mary was a part of the picture. But it seemed, as luck would have it, Mary wasn’t going to be around to spoil things. Still, regardless of who was or wasn’t involved, she had to get in touch with Matt, and it was becoming clear that the telephone wasn’t going to help with that. She would sit on the couch in the afternoons, while her cousin was at work, and she would daydream that finally, even if just once, Matt would hear one of her messages and actually pick up the phone to call her back. Stacey would imagine all the details. She would be watching television… probably a soap opera. The skies would be partly cloudy, just enough to block the sun in a fashion that would cast strange shadows across the living room. Right as the show was going to commercial, the phone would ring. She would sigh, figuring it to be just some telemarketer trying to sell something no human being could possibly need, and answer the phone with an annoyed ‘hello’. Stacey’s surprise at hearing Matt’s voice would cause her to practically drop the phone, fumbling to catch it before it could spill to the floor. Once recovered, she would put the phone back to her ear and give a much more appropriate greeting. From there, conversation would flow smoothly from one casual topic to the next, until finally… he would ask her to dinner. But that phone call hadn’t yet come, and was beginning to seem like it never would. “Maybe I should go over there tomorrow.” Stacey curled up in the recliner, pulling her knees up to her chest. Despite the fact that it was only eight o’clock in the evening, she was already dressed down for the night, in an old baggy t-shirt and a pair of gym shorts that were barely long enough to peek out from underneath the shirt. “What do you think, Em?” “I think that’s a bad idea.” Emily laid her book across her lap, turning her attention to her cousin’s question. “There’s a reason he’s not returning your calls. He clearly doesn’t feel comfortable with this.” “I want to talk to him. I need to, even if just to get some things off my chest. And if I sit around and wait for him to decide to pick up the phone and call me back… I could be here forever. I feel like I’ve already been enough of a burden for you. I don’t want to become a permanent one.” “Stacey, you’re family. You can stay as long as you’d like. You haven’t been a burden at all. Actually, its been nice having someone to joke around with after I get home from work. I was headed down the fast track to becoming one of those creepy ladies who lives alone with her eight cats, all of which she treats like her real children, and never leaves the house except to go the store.” “You only own one cat, Em!” Stacey picked up a pillow from the end of the couch nearest her and threw it over to the other end towards her cousin. “Yeah,” Emily caught the pillow and tossed it back down to its previous spot, “but I was thinking about getting another one.” Stacey shook her head in amusement for a second, then returned conversation to the original topic. “So, you think I should just keep playing the waiting game?” “Yeah. No need to push things. Just be patient.” “Patience isn’t my thing, Em.” * * * Hers Mary had her own dreams, much unlike her husbands. One night she would fall asleep and immediately dream about things having never fallen apart in the first place. Perhaps, it could be said that it was her equivalent of Matthew’s What if Hin See had never died? dream. There would be no fight. There would be no wrestling. Mary would have never suffered the traumatic events of the summer and Matthew would have never joined PRIME. She would dream about autumn passing the same way it did every year, marked only by cool weather and the changing of the leaves, instead of coming and going without notice under the guise of amnesia. In the dream, the holidays would be as pleasant as the year before. Family and friends gathered round, giving each other a hard time, everyone laughing and talking at the same time so that the walls of dining room shook from the garbled roar. Killean would be there. Because Matthew had never gotten back into wrestling, no bad blood would have developed between the two. Thus, Killean sat at the table, next to his old friend, patting Matthew on the back and telling him all about his personal successes in his young wrestling career. Matt would laugh and recall some old memories… having no new ones of his own to share. It was a dream filled with all the happiness she had grown accustomed to, the routine and events she had come to love. In another similar dream, Mary would concern herself with the well being of their baby girl. Georgie would roll on the floor, laughing with her father… one who had been there every day, every step of the way. Matthew would pick her up, lifting Georgie above his head, kiss her on her tiny belly, tickle her with his goatee. Mary would witness the events from the kitchen, watching her husband and child through the opening above the bar area as she worked on preparing dinner for the evening. Matthew, noticing Mary’s smile in the distance, would hop to his feet and carry Georgie with him into the kitchen. In the dream, Matthew then kissed her and handed the baby over to her mother, offering to finish the preparations for dinner. Carefully, he would take over, picking up where Mary had left off, letting Georgie calm down from all the laughter in the comforting arms of her mom. There wasn’t even a mention or thought given to the idea of wrestling in this dream. There simply wasn’t any need for it. Matthew’s efforts and attentions were dedicated to the right thing… his family. Instead of concerning himself with being a dominant champion, he concerned himself with being a good father. Instead of focusing on unleashing his inhuman side to all those who didn’t know him, he focused on displaying his very much loving and human side to the person who knew him best. Those two dreams carried an aura of amicable togetherness. Husband and wife sharing all the moments that make a life. Mother and father with child, embracing their own blessings of family. They weren’t all illusions of a perfect life that never crossed a rough patch in the road. They didn’t all ignore the current difficulty of her relationship with Matthew, or the nightmare that had been the last five months of her life, recovering from amnesia only to watch things fall apart with her husband. Some nights, her mind would confront the reality of her situation. She wouldn’t dream of ‘what if it never happened’ scenarios. Instead, the truth of her problems would dominate the dream. It would start with the fight that had led to her moving in with her parents. From there, Mary would pack up her things, grabbing the necessities, clothing, shoes, a few things for the baby… then leave the house, only a note to signify her whereabouts. Then the dream would flash forward several weeks… the present most likely… with Mary just sitting on the couch at her parent’s house, the television on, but serving only as background noise. Soon, another sound would add to that noise. The doorbell would ring and she would let her father answer the door, choosing instead to remain curled up on the couch, reading an edition of Newsweek magazine. The conversation that followed between her papa and the visitor was short, but its content was enough to tip her off. An instant later, Mary’s father would step out of the way and Matthew would come into view. A small smile would spread across his face, causing the same to happen to her. He would make his way over to the couch, take a seat next to her, and lean over to kiss her on the cheek. A few minutes would pass, and they would find themselves walking around the half frozen pond behind her parents’ house. Matthew would talk about the mistakes he’d made and how the last month had passed in slow-motion. Mary would agree, confessing she shouldn’t have been so quick to anger and admit to waking up some mornings wishing she had never moved out. The dialogue was vivid. She could remember every word of the end of the conversation, as if they’d actually been said and not just dreamed. “Its basically been hell, Mary. I wake up, eat lunch, watch tv, work out, dinner, more tv, go to bed, wake up, eat lunch…” “Eat lunch?” “I don’t wake up in time for breakfast.” “That’s no different from before I moved out.” “I guess it’s just… things were routine before, but I was happy. Everyday might have been basically the same, but every day was…nice, enjoyable. Take away the good things, and you realize just how routine things are. And then it starts to drag, and drag you down with it, and…I’m sorry.” “I know, Matthew. So am I. I just want to get back to that good routine.” “Me too. Me… SHIT!” Just like that, he would slip and fall on the muddy banks of the pond… purposely, just to make her laugh. And then, that was it; she would wake up. Laughing. * * * So, while one person on the outside readied to inject herself into their lives, a husband and wife spent their nights dreaming, and their days examining those dreams. They both wanted things to be fixed. They both wanted to go back to the smiles and laughs, and lovable routine they had settled into. But, while one of them dreamed of those very thoughts, and imagined the happiness they both desired… the other could dream only of the violence and loss that ripped them to pieces in the first place, obsess only with his professional failures And though they both just wanted to talk things through, just wanted to remind the other “I still love you”… unlike Stacey… neither of them just picked up the phone.
