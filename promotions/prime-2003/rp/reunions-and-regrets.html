Reunions and Regrets

Date: Dual Halo (2016 words) -Seattle, WA February 22, 2009 A chill breeze swirled down Dog Alley, cooling the steaming chowder that rested in a spoon before Tyler Rayne’s lips. He swallowed the bite with little more enthusiasm than he had the ones before. Despite the quality of the chowder itself, and some would argue it was the finest to be had in all of Seattle, it was obvious that Tyler was simply eating for the sake of it. He took no joy in the delicious contents of the bowl. He stared long down the alley. Toward the Irish pub he couldn’t see but knew damn well was there. Just across the cobblestones and brick. A bulbous man with a ukulele sat against the wall. His deep baritone voice bellowing a poor rendition of Tiny Bubbles for the huddled masses. One of the young girls passing by dropped a bill into his top hat, for which the man gave a thankful nod of his balding head. “Little cold out here for you California boys, isn’t it?” The voice was smooth and surprisingly confident. Tyler was caught off guard by how confident he’d become. How much he’d grown up in the years since they’d first met. He stood to welcome the portly blond man with the wide smile and bright pink cheeks. Black gloves matched the North Face jacket. The vigorous zeal with which the man shook Tyler’s hand matched his enthused smile. “Been forever. Gotta be… what? Two or three years?” “Rena’s funeral. So-“ The smile immediately faded from the man’s face. His entire countenance drooped and even the pitch of his voice fell an octave or two. “Damn it, man. I’m sorry. I didn’t mean to.” “No worries. Have a seat, Kenny. You want some soup?” “Nah. I’ve eaten so much of that damned chowder in the past ten years. Amazing though, isn’t it?” Kenny Webster pulled out the chair across from Tyler and lowered himself. Years ago he couldn’t have even imagined sharing a meal with the ominous and frightening MDK. That was back when Kenny was doing television. Backstage interviews for one of the hundreds of wrestling promotions that were sprouting up at the time. OCW folded almost as quickly as it sprung to prominence. Tyler Rayne had put that little promotion on the map with his violent antics as the masked anti-hero. Competition was fierce, though. Budgets and production values skyrocketed as the industry boomed. OCW just didn’t have the funds to keep up. After that, Kenny tried his hand at hosting. Landed a few gigs for some syndicated dating shows. Nothing he was overly proud of. Eventually he had moved back home to Seattle and stumbled into a well-paying job with a marketing firm. “Not bad.” “This whole street, man. The market. I just love this city. Every little kiosk here… man. I’ve seriously spent weeks walking the same three or four blocks every day. Never once ate at the same place. It’s amazing. Really wish you would’ve gotten to spend more time here.” “Me too, actually. City always has a good vibe. Speaking of… did you see the place?” “Yeah. I drove by after I heard. Still had that spare key you gave me. It was… I’m sorry, man. Just… ash. I’ve never seen anything so… how have you been holding up?” “Certainly seen better days.” “I’d imagine. Did they get all of ‘em?” “Everything except the Manor. Guess I got lucky in that regard.” “You’re a lot of things, Rayne. Lucky’s never been one of ‘em.” “No shit.” “You, uh… you find out who it was? Who did it?” “Have my suspicions. Other things on my mind lately, though. That’s kinda why I called.” “Right. The mask.” Kenny unzipped his jacket and reached a portly hand inside. A second later he produced a ball of black fabric. He set the mask down on the table and tapped a finger against the white kanji printed in the middle of the fabric. The only identifying feature of the mask at all. “You never did tell me which one it was.” “Pestilence.” “You’ve got Death. Michelle’s still got Famine. So who’s got War?” “Always the reporter, eh?” “You’re an interesting guy, Rayne. Hard not to be intrigued. Man with so many mysteries is begging for questions.” “You think you could do me another favor?” “I could try.” “See if you can find Michelle for me.” “Hopin’ for a little romantic reunion?” “No. I… she… I’d rather her not know it was me asked you to look. Just… make sure she’s all right.” “There a reason she wouldn’t be?” “Knowing me isn’t enough?” An awkward silence falls between them. It should have been a joke. Both of them know it wasn’t. “Who is it?” “Se7yn.” “Jesus. How long’s it been now? Seems like he’s been after you forever.” “Met Katt in the summer of ’01. Woulda been six months or so after that.” “So seven years.” Tyler scoffed. “Yeah. ‘Spose so. Look, I’m gonna uh… I’m going out of country for a bit. Kinda why I needed the mask. I’ll call ya when I get back. See about ‘Chelle. Um… if you don’t hear from me in a week or so…” “Last time that mask was in Japan a lot of people died. I don’t know what happened there. I don’t even want to. But you take it back there… you intending the same thing to happen again?” “The man who wore this mask… I… I made a promise. One I didn’t keep. Now… Jesus. Everything is fucked. It’s all coming back, man. I can’t put the pieces together. I just know I’ve got that feeling. This… I’ve put years between me and this mask. Been around the globe time and time again. Never felt so close to… I’m not gonna make it through this one, Kenny. I’ve done so much stupid shit but it always… I’ve always been invincible. Untouchable. Now? I just… it feels like it did back then. Like there was no way I’d make it through.” “Just lose the mask, Rayne. Let it go. Let them all go. You can’t keep living back there. That’s not you anymore. You’ve got this girl, right? Lindsay? Just live your life, man. In the present. Not the past.” Tyler’s fingers slide across the black fabric. There’s a power in that fabric. The power of memory. His fingers curl around the cloth. Closing into a fist. “I made a promise, Kenny. I don’t… I failed. I … there are things that must be done. Things I said I’d never do. You… thanks. For holding this. I’ll call you when I get back.” He rose from his seat. The chowder was forgotten. The Irish pub across the street was forgotten. He couldn’t even remember the face of the man sitting right in front of him. There was only the mask. The memories. The broken, shattered lives hidden inside the white curves of that kanji. “Tyler. I… be careful.” “It’s probably not me you have to worry about.” “I’m not worried about you dying. I’m worried about you living with what you’re about to do.” ---- -February 23, 2009 The bright glow of the computer monitor seemed to wash what little color remained from his face. The pale, almost sickly, complexion whitened under the pulsing light of the Internet. His head lulled back, similar to the zombie he resembled, and came to rest against the back of the office chair. A near empty bottle of Jack Daniels slipped from the loosened grip of his left hand. It hit the carpet with a dull thunk. His right hand still clutched tightly to a bolt of black fabric. Tyler Rayne closed his eyes and wished like Hell he could forget the face. If even just for a moment. One blissful second to relieve him of the guilt. The failure. The computer monitor sat idle on the bruised face of Drake Roberts. A rap sheet pulled from the Raleigh police department’s information logs. The mug shot was unflattering, as were most. This was a few years ago. Drake’s brown mess of hair was much shorter than. Blond frosted tips. He was disheveled. A spot of blood dried just under his nose. Dirt smeared across his cheek. A knot swelling on the right side of his forehead. He and about two dozen other kids had been hauled in on misdemeanor mischief charges. There were rumors of more serious offenses to come. Possible assault charges. Maybe even city litigation for damage to public properties. There were a handful of concerned citizens pushing for jail time. Insisting that Drake and his cohorts had started a riot. The judge, however, was a bit more lenient. Deemed fit that the altercation was nothing more than some rowdy football fans getting a little out of control. The incident caused quite a stir with the locals, but Drake walked away with nothing more than a shit ton of community service. Most of which he spent repairing the damages made during the brawl. Not one of the proudest moments in his life… but he did meet Summer Scott while sweating away his debt to society. A picture of the two in Tokyo just hours before she was murdered rested against the bottom left corner of the monitor. Pictures of Drake were scattered all across the desk. There were a half dozen photos of him with Summer the week before she died. Visiting tourist attractions and laughing at the strange marvel that is Japan. Pages torn from a family scrapbook. Drake and his adoptive parents standing beneath the monolith of Mt. Rushmore. Posing in front of the MGM Grand. A camping trip on the banks of a raging river. There was a youthful exuberance to all of the pictures. The way Drake would carry himself, even in his youngest years. That twinkle of innocence and naivety in his eye. Like he knew for certain the inherent goodwill of the world. The love that made life possible. Like he could never be convince otherwise. It reminded Tyler of a man he once knew. Long ago. A man he lied to. A man he failed. Beneath the pictures was a litany of various legal documents. Copies of deeds and transfers of ownership. A handwritten list of adoption agencies from both Japan and the United States. Crumpled pages torn from over a dozen years’ worth of calendars. A viciously scrawled and discarded ledger of every trip Tyler had made to North Carolina since ’93. There was even a picture of Tyler himself from what had to have been that same era. At least fifteen years ago. Even that long ago he was still sporting that same irresistible smile. He occupied the photo space with three other men. Of particular note was the man standing to Tyler’s right side. Slightly larger in both height and width, the man’s square jaw and sharp nose were distinctly familiar. Almost identical to the features of a young boy scattered across the desk top. The bruised face from that mug shot. Tyler’s right hand slipped from the arm of the chair. It slapped dully against his thigh, jarring the fingers loose. He shivered once before a blackness darker than the cloth he’d been holding swept in. Waves of nauseating nothingness took him from a drunken stupor to a drunken coma. The black cloth fell listlessly to the floor. The white kanji lost within the folds of the mask fell to rest against an aged document. Leaking shots of Jack Daniels stained the edges of the yellowing paper brown. One corner already curled slightly inward. Probably a remnant of extreme heat exposure. Some of the ink had even faded after years for poor care. A useless piece of paper stuffed in moldy cardboard boxes and dank file cabinets. A worn and tired birth certificate that still managed to serve its purpose despite such a poor condition. The letters stood out bold and proud. The proclamation and acknowledgement of a small boy’s existence. A name passed on from father to son. The continuation of a bloodline. A lineage. Drake Alexander Logan
