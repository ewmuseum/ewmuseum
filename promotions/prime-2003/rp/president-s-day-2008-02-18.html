President's Day

18 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/18/08 Even among a crowded street in Green Point, New York one man stands out. Tom Walczak towers among the crowds, which are filled with school children and their parents. They walk in and out of stores having nothing better to do than shop on their day off. However, among all the smiling faces Walczak’s is one of bitter disgust. With each store he passes his face only shows more revulsion. As we look closer it easy to see what has gotten Walczak so upset. Almost every store has a sign advertising a President’s Day sale in the center of their windows. A holiday of shameless self promotion for the United States, and one that Walczak feels has no place in this predominantly Polish neighborhood. Walczak makes sure that no one mistakes him for an American sympathizer. He proudly wears a white t-shirt a Polish Flag plastered in the middle of it. His head is adorned with a bandana that proudly displays the White Eagle, the national symbol of Poland. There are very few places in the United States that proudly display Polish heritage, and today one of those places have been overrun. Finally, it seems as if Walczak has had enough, one store has gotten more than under his skin. The store is covered in red, white, and blue and the American Flag looms above the entrance of the store. It was a vision of American pride in all but name, Murawski’s Electronics. Walczak storms into the store, unsure of what to expect. “Hey, you, are you the manager?” Walczak’s anger seemed to be let out in one yell, at a tiny young man standing behind the register. The man seemed visibly shaken, and uncertain of what to do next. He fixes up his blue vest, and glasses trying to find to courage to answer Walczak. “Uhh…I…uhh…no…he…uhh…is in the back…” The young man swallows his breath, feeling relieved that he had just managed to put together a sentence. Walczak heads towards the back of the store leaving the scared little boy to gather his bearings. As he reaches the back of the store he sees a wooden door with a sign that reads ‘Manager’ written on it. KNOCK KNOCK KNOCK Walczak’s fists slam against the door nearly breaking down the door in the process. “What do you want Janek?” The man opens the door looking at the man in front of him, but seeming to be shocked at what he sees. This is not the small little boy at the register, but instead a lumbering near seven foot monster with a look of anger plastered on his face. “Who are you?” The man crosses his arms trying to show Walczak he runs this place, but all this does is add to the man’s already funny appearance. He stands nearly two feet shorter than Walczak, and his gut nearly comes across to touch Walczak. He tries to cover the fact that he is bald with a terrible comb-over that seems to keep falling over his eyes. “You don’t have to worry about who I am, but I have a couple of questions about how you run your store.” “This should be interesting, because I have gotten nothing but compliments on the way the store has been run today.” “That is just the thing. Why is the store run any differently today? I used to walk by this store and beam with pride every time I saw the Polish Flag waving above the entrance, and why did that have to change. Why did you have to replace it with that symbol of gluttony and evil that hangs there now?” “It is a national holiday, so why should I not display the flag of the country that made this possible?” “Because this country puts down our people making them feel like an inferior race.” “What are you talking about our people? I was born in this country.” “What about your father?” “Straight off the boat, came to this country around 1950.” “Why do you smile when you say that? Do you know how disappointed your father would be if he saw this store now?” This seems to have struck a chord with the man, as his arms now come unfolded. The man even points a finger towards Walczak showing his displeasure as he searches for words to come out of his mouth. “My father was a proud American, and if you are so disappointed with the way I run the store that why don’t you just get the hell out of here.” “Proud American…I highly doubt that. He had to come to this country because America gave him no place else to go. They gave his country away to people who subsequently slaughtered millions of his fellow countrymen. So what was his choice stay at home and be slaughtered, or leave and hope his son would be smart enough to come back one day.” “GET OUT!!!” The man now points towards the front of the store slamming his foot down in the process, but all this does is bring a chuckle to Walczak’s demeanor. Walczak leaves the store and in the process tears down the American Flag hanging above the entrance, leaving it now flat on the ground. How have I let this happen? Now even my own people are now siding with this country of infidels. This holiday does nothing more than further promote the high horse that America rides on. It doesn’t celebrate independence, nor does it celebrate a great victory, all it does is revere the men that made this country the way it is today. They call these people the forefathers of America. These people laid down the foundation for a country that would terrorize the weak of the world for centuries never lending out a helping hand so that others may prosper. People in my country praise me for the things I have accomplished, but here they laugh at me, and I will not be able to go back until I make them respect me. It started with the Freak Himself, and now it will be dDusk.
