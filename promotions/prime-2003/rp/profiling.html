Profiling

24 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May, 24 For years, it was Titan's job to profile, to watch for what didn't fit and figure out why. He was trained to profile people, determine what they were doing, why they were doing it and what they intended without ever talking to the person in question. And he learned to do it by not directly looking at the person. It was a training trick of Profile 21's. He would sit Titan down with a book, an article, something and then proceed to randomly chuck objects in the big man's direction. Titan had to be able to disseminate the information presented in the subject material and still be able to keep enough situational awareness not to get smacked in the temple with a stapler. Irritating, but effective. From that point on, Titan profiled people as a habit. Even now, sitting in the airport waiting for his flight, he was reading through a magazine and profiling people that went past. He'd picked out about twenty people that were terrified of flying, but were going anyway. He'd bulls-eyed three men that were sure they were going to be joining the Mile High club. He flipped the next page, going on to the continuation about the new prospects that Israeli Military Industries were attempting with hollowtip rounds when another person caught his eye. She wasn't terribly tall, about five foot five, he estimated. She was dressed casually; pair of khakis and a conservative blouse. But as she went, he started picking up tells. Her hand trailed at her side, almost as if grasping for something around waist level. She's walking quickly, as if she's got someplace to go and wants to get there quickly. The last detail is what sealed it for him: the way she carried her shoulder bag in such a way on her hip that looked like she was almost cradling something. He smiled inwardly. A mother heading home, quickly. Her hand was grasping for that of a child at her side and her shoulder bag was cradled on her hip like an infant. The next person walked up. Titan's eyes took in his well-trimmed, blue pinstripe suit. The man looked to be in his mid-twenties and movie at a fast walk. He had a shoulder duffel across his shoulders and his briefcase clutched in one hand. Titan watched him as he went by, continuously checking the Blackberry in his hand. The giant smiled to himself. Just another yuppie, running along, hurrying to his next destination because, damn it, the world depended on it. And people thought that wrestlers needed to learn to deal with stress issues. A pair of people went the other direction. It was a young couple, about mid-twenties, the same as the yuppie had been. They're holding hands and wedding rings glitter with that shine that only white gold can bring. His cold, blue eyes examine them; they're dressed comfortably. Nothing too tight, no possibly stiff materials. They're awfully close together. They move past him, closer than any of the others and he gets a good look at the rings. There are still ribbons tied on them. This revelation doesn't incline him to smile. They're newlyweds on a honeymoon. The big man sighs. Marriage. The idea has never scared him, but to be honest, it's never really presented itself. The big man wasn't all that well versed in dating. It couldn't be all that surprising. Titan was sitting in an airport discerning a great deal about these people as they went by. He was dissecting them by body language, determining what could be considered fairly private aspects of their lives with just a look. He can't imagine what it'd be like for a woman to have to deal with something like that. And not just deal with that aspect, but the aspect of having a gigantic boyfriend or husband or whatever. He'd been told by guests at his home that it felt like being a kid or being in an oversized dollhouse, where the chairs were terribly high, the tables made to match. To Titan, it was home, but to everyone else, it would be a land of novelty. The other problem was, for him, it was a necessity. He couldn't just buy regular furniture and squeeze into it. The stuff would collapse, sure as taxes, and then he'd be out, what, another couple hundred dollars for stuff that could be very well-made, but didn't hold up because he was... well, himself. He couldn't imagine a woman being happy with him in the casual aspects of a relationship. Sitting in a movie theater, driving anywhere, even eating at a restaurant. None of it would be normal, acceptable, or even just mildly interesting. Every bit of it would be a trial. In a way, he was almost the inverse of Chandler Tsonda. Tsonda was a former model and had plenty of experience with women. Not that it was something Titan held against the man, just a fact of life. Why be mad at him for something like that? Much better to kick the snot out of the man because of who he associates with and what they did to someone like Kyle Lamen. Regardless, it was a difference. In the ring, Tsonda was quick, he could fly and he did damage by using both of those attributes. Titan was obviously the stereotypical giant, thudding around the ring following the opponent he'd just pitched that direction. In some ways, being Tsonda was probably easier in the ring than being Titan: smaller target, faster reaction times, easier to dodge attacks, harder to anticipate. But Titan had plenty of time to profile the man. He was arrogant as hell and supremely confident. In some ways, he had rights to that, but in others.... well, being honest means that he wasn't as good as he claims. Else he wouldn't be shacked up with Ferguson and Face Eater to deal with insecurity issues and he sure as hell would be higher than the 5 Star title by now. Titan held no illusions about his own capabilities: he was big, strong and he could fight. That got him to the tag title match. It may have lost it as well, but sooner or later, being one man against two (three in that case) was going to catch up with him and drag him down. In a one-on-one environment, though, things might well turn out to be very different than the tag scene had been. His eyes shot up. New man was walking by. He was taking several small, shuffling steps but was doing so very quickly. He was very nervous and he was trying to get somewhere quickly. His eyes were darting back and forth, almost like they were looking for a series of people that should be closing in on him. It reminded him of the several bombers and attackers that he'd dealt with in his past. It's odd that he would just be that kind of paranoid, but then again, he is in an airport, which many people find to be terrifying places. But the man exhibited other signs that bothered the giant. He kept wiping his palms on the sides of his slacks, over and over again. That was a classic sign of nervousness. Titan looked at him more closely. There was a great deal that wasn't coming together right. He glared, closer, trying to figure out what exactly was going on... Then he saw it. The man's walk was irregular, like he wasn't used to the weight that seemed to be bearing on his hip. It made his gait irregular, his leg a little stiff, moreso than it should have been. Titan looked at that hip and his eyes went wide. The one word flashed in his mind. GUN. Titan stood up, slowly, and deposited the magazine in the seat he just vacated. He ambled up behind the offending man. It seemed he was nervously surveying everything in front of him, but nowhere else. He had tunnel vision towards his goal and was only concerned with what might get into his way. Titan stood there behind him, taking a deep breath to focus himself. It had been a long, long time since he'd tried to disarm an armed man by himself. “Excuse me, sir,” he said calmly. From there, everyone else might say all hell broke loose. For Titan, it was exactly as he expected. The man spun, pulling a firearm from the hip holster. It was a shining Colt .45 1911 model. As he turned and drew, Titan's big hand caught his wrist and forced the weapon aside and downward. The weapon was fired twice, with both big rounds digging their way into the carpeted floor of the airport. The other man's arm reached for the massive paw that had a grip on his wrist. Titan's other hand struck the man right behind the jaw and the big man could feel the man's jaw separate under the impact. He sagged and the weapon dropped from his hands. Airport security dashed up to see a man slumped on the floor and a giant standing over him. The offending firearm was lying away from both of them, where Titan had very pointedly kicked it away. “Who are you?” one of the security guards asked. “Former IDF,” Titan responded. That got a few raised eyebrows from some of the guards and puzzled glances from the others. “Well, listen, we need to get a statement from you,” he said. “Flight 186 now boarding.” Titan reached into his pocket and pulled out a slip of paper with his name and his cell phone number on it. “Reach me there, I'll give you a statement.” With that, the big man walked over and picked up his magazine and shoulder bag and headed for his boarding ramp. He handed over his ticket and headed down the always-too-short rampway. With his slow, lumbering steps he made his way onto the plane with an exaggerated care, making sure not to bump into anything and angled his way into the cramped plane cabin. He looked around and took his seat, directly on the aisle before reaching up and stuffing his bag into the overhead compartment. He pulled the lid back down and laid his head back. “Hi,” came a voice, “I think I'm sitting next to you.” Titan opened his eyes and turned his head to see a smiling faced framed by red hair. With a sigh, he pushed himself up from the seat and attempted to move himself in such a fashion to allow her to pass by. She was small and slipped right by him. She was small and slight, standing only about five foot, six inches and quite a slim little woman. He probably would have made up about three or four of her. But she happily dropped into the seat next to him and turned to face him. “So I saw what you did in the terminal,” she said brightly. “That's one hell of a conversation starter,” he replied earnestly. He was greeted by a slight laugh. “Well, maybe it is. Were you in the military?” “Israeli Defense Force.” “I've always wanted to go there.” Titan looked at her with a slight grin. According to her body language, the enthusiasm was genuine. She wasn't throwing herself at him like some of the “ring rats” he'd met. In fact, she seemed like a fascinating woman. It might be an interesting flight.
