Date: 8/26 and 8/27 In four days, I'll be thirty-one years old. Thirty-one, with a divorce under my belt, all the anger and resentment that's come along with it and a bum knee going into my ReVolution 138 title defense, thanks to the Fuckhead and the Midcard Bit Player. When I was younger, I never imagined still doing all this by the time I reached this age, and every day that's passed since my thirtieth birthday I've realized that it was only a matter of time before my body started to not work the way it used to. I had been mostly lucky up until last week, but then again how many women past thirty are still in the business and are still going at 100% all the time, every time? I can count all the ones, to my knowledge, on one hand. There's no irony in the fact that Sonny knew just which knee to have Donnelly target: the one where I had to have surgery to take out bone chips thanks to a fire extinguisher shot 5 years ago. And, just like Chameleon, Donnelly blended right into his surroundings, striking when the moment was right. Silver and GOLD is turning out to be more of a curse than I ever expected it to be. The eyes in the back of my head were closed for seconds too long on Wednesday, and I'm suffering the repercussions as I hobble up the gantry and off my flight. I've never liked traveling into Logan Airport, a fact I should have considered before buying a house outside Boston, but unfortunately failed to remember before signing the paperwork. It's never not a mob scene, whereas Tampa International always has its downtimes and they're always predictable, at least to me. Flanked by security, I'm not halfway down the terminal's corridor when the mob scene slowly migrates to me: young and old alike, teenagers, small children, adults, those with the knowledge of who I am leave their gates, the stores and watering holes and follow along like the children of Hamlin. I don't have a pipe, but I do have a Sharpee. I sign everything as I limp closer to the exit, look over at people with cameras and cell phones and muster the best smile I can. The older crowd whoops and cries that I'll be kicking the Fuckhead's ass twice in one month and keeping the Strap, then throwing Donnelly onto the Churchill Downs track to be trampled by million-dollar racehorses. The youthfully innocent tell me that I'm the best wrestler ever, their hero, their role model. Their role model. I'll be ashamed to called that after ReVolution 138. Role models don't maim their fellow man... ~**~**~**~**~ August 27, 2007 Boston, Massachusetts: Casa di Troy, Northeast. 2:00AM "Where the fuck did I put that stupid brace?" Surrounded by boxes and bags, Lindsay Troy muttered out loud to the empty room, annoyed and wishing she was tired enough to sleep and could worry about finding her fancy-schmancy knee brace at a better hour. But Lindsay Troy is an insomniac, and had been since she was a young girl, and knew that no matter how hard she tried, there was no way she'd be falling asleep anytime soon. The whole not-sleeping business started when she was ten, not long after her father had been killed in a plane crash and she and her sister started living with their aunt and uncle. The girls had always slept with stuffed animals since they were babies and while Alaina would continue sleeping with them up until she graduated high school, Lindsay pushed them all aside and refused to even have them in the same room as her. Brent and Elise thought it was because they were given to her by Marco and Cecilia, so they bought her new ones, thinking that a change would do the youngster good. They too, were rejected. The girls' doctor, after careful consultation, thought it was due to the loss of both parents and the resulting trauma that came with it. That very well may have been a part of it, as from then on out, Lindsay Troy only slept well when she was holding something...or someone...she truly cared about. The inanimate creatures of fluff became too hard to feel any sort of childlike sentimentality for once the older Strozzis were gone. At that young age, Lindsay felt that she needed to grow up and be the parental figure for her sister despite having two new parental figures in her life. Whatever this was, it was a different kind of insomnia and Troy was still living with it at nearly thirty-one, especially since her separation and divorce from Joey Melton. Sometimes she wished she didn't have this night-owl disposition. While it was good during her college years, it was not so much now, and with another huge match in just a few days she really, really wanted to get some sleep. She decided instead to look for her knee brace, hoping that the search would result in her being calm enough to snooze for a few hours before her 12:30 flight to Kentucky. Sleeping on the plane would be futile. The brace was packed somewhere...she just had to remember where she tossed it. Unfortunately, the growling of Ciro downstairs made Troy give pause, and she shuffled over to the door to quiet the guard dog down. "Ciro, knock it off!" But he didn't stop. Blended into the threatening snarls, she managed to hear a knock at her front door. When the knocking persisted, Ciro started barking dangerously. Lindsay sighed, slowly maneuvered her way down the stairs and hop-stepped over to the entertainment center where she pulled a bat out from behind the thick wood. She half-figured that a burglar wouldn't be knocking on her front door, but in this day in age...who knows? Moving as stealthily as she was able, Troy stopped by the front door, flipped on the porch light and peered through the eye-lens to see the person outside. The bat clattered onto the wood floor. Lindsay threw open the door and saw, in full-view, a very familiar man with very familiar buzzed-blonde hair, smoking a very familiar Camel cigarette. "Caes?! What are you...how did you even know where I was?" Caesar Vega smiled softly, taking a drag off the cig. "I made Ryan tell me after our match in the Frontier." Troy returned Nova's smile, looked down to reassure Ciro that all was well, then gazed back at the Risen Star. "There's no smoking in the house." The Camel burned out in the dirt of her small front garden. ~**~**~**~**~ "Earlier, three little girls told me I was their role model," she told Caesar as she eased herself on the couch next to him, two glasses of water in her hand. "No bourbon?" he accepted one with a smirk. "What do you think this is, a bar?! Heh. Being on the road, I haven't had time to replenish my stash. I only came back here to get my knee brace. You can thank Sonny and Donnelly for that." "I heard," Caesar replied. "Yeah." A pause. "So the thought of being a role model...what, shocks you?" "No. It doesn't. Under normal circumstances. But lately I'm all 'murderdeathkill' because of Fuckhead...I think anyone who wants me as their role model is crazy." Caesar shrugged, not wanting to delve too deeply into the Sonny issue given their sortakinda friendship. "You've done a lot of good for a lot of people, you know." "And a lot of bad for a lot of others. I'm a lot more infamous than those little kids know or want to believe. It's only going to get worse after 138." "Or they just want to see the good in you." He took another swig of water, ignoring the silence until she quietly broke it. "Why are you here, Caesar? Really. It's 2:30AM on a Monday morning and you just randomly showed up on my porch, hoping I'd be here and awake no less?" "I didn't like the way I left things," his head turned lazily to look at her, "trying to sneak out the back door like I did. I know I explained and all but with Randalls there, it just seemed cheap. You've done a lot of good for me, Lindz, and I'll never forget it. We're both hard on ourselves, not without reason, but with apology. You've helped me see some things more clearly. Maybe it's time I return the favor, in some way." She nodded in acceptance and he put his arm around her, drawing her close. Unable to help herself, and not really wanting to, she nestled into the crook of his arm, her own draping itself over his torso. And that's exactly the position she woke up in, seven hours later.
