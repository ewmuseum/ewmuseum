A Sign, and Some Loss... But No Whores.

6 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 6, 2006 Uno: El Principio [Puerto Rico, 1982] Maria cradled her head in her hands, a feeble attempt at silencing her feverish sobbing. With an even more feeble attempt at comfort, Dominic rolled the palm of his hand along the small of his wife’s lower back. For what had seemed like an eternity to the couple, they had made attempt after attempt to consummate their love in a child. Nearly giving up on not only motherhood, but very nearly ending her own life, Maria and Dominic arrived at the fertility specialist in hopes of an answer. As Maria struggled to choke back tears, she thought about her place in life. More over, her purpose. She believed that she was put on this earth to bear children and her biological clock was wildly spinning out of control. They say time flies when you’re having fun, then for Maria sitting in a fertility specialist’s office in the traps of Puerto Rico must be a fucking party. No wait, a fiesta. She struggled to focus on the words spilling from the handsome man sitting before them, her fingers slowly grazing over the set of small scars on her left wrist. Hell, she couldn’t even do that right. Maria blinked tears from her eyes as the news came, sending a series of momentary tremors through her body. “I’m sorry Mister and Misses Delgado,” he cleared his throat before continuing, carefully running his eyes over his report one last time. “It just seems you both are not destined to have children.” A deep, guttural moan escaped the grasp of Maria’s larynx. Apparently he had chosen the wrong words, round dos coming up. “Have you ever thought about adoption?” Maria’s petite hands lunged through the air towards this source of unnecessary suffering, she imagined ripping his eyes from their sockets, and attempting another fertility test inside his empty cranial cavity. As she slid calmly back into reality, she realized they were no longer in the office, but Dominic was carrying her through the threshold back into the torrential downpour erupting from the black cloud covering San Juan on this night. Or maybe the cloud was just for her, crying for her. Either way, she didn’t care anymore. She ripped away from Dominic’s arms, and ran into the night, letting the rain wash over her skin. Maria wasn’t sure what she was running from, or running to for that matter, but she knew she needed to escape. Dominic knew better than to stop her, he watched as her frame disappeared into the night. Dos: Una Maldicion Exhausted, soaked, and with tears streaming ferociously down her cheeks, Maria sat huddled at the base of the metal dumpster. With every heave of her chest, she felt the last few specks of her useless life exiting her body. She had to do it. Her body now shaking uncontrollably and her breathing strained, Maria placed her hand inside her purse. For a few tense seconds she became rigid, before removing her hand from the purse, slowly revealing a sleek black Beretta. For years she had hated her husband’s alter-lifestyle and all that it stood for. But now that irony would kill her, the very gun she hated more than anything would be the embodiment of that of which would set her free. Death’s cruel hand arriving in an extension of her husband, this time the message was sent for her. Maria folded her fingers and placed her closed fist softly to her own lips. She tightly closed her eyes and began to whisper quietly, her prayers lost within the wind patrolling though the alleyway. As the sounds of her whispers faded, the stream of tears followed suit. Maria was focused for the first time in her life and she knew what she wanted, no, had to do. She slowly raised the black pistol towards her face, carefully extending her open mouth towards the black hole that would soon swallow her life whole. Having watched Dominic unwillingly so many times previously, she hated that the sight of a gun had no effect on her anymore. The rain continued to pour down, managing to slowly slide down the barrel of the gun into her open mouth, leaving her last thought in this life reminding her of how much she wanted to die. Maria thought of Dominic, she thought of what could have been. The utter disdain she felt for his lost soul was the last feeling she would take from this cruel world. She burst into an explosion of tears as she squeezed the trigger, sending an eruption of fury flying into the storm. ... ... The alley went quiet, the rushing gusts of wind retiring for the moment, the rain momentarily clearing. Quiet, dead quiet. Then the crying came, not a sobbing as before, but a high pitched whine. Maria’s eyes shoot open, unaware of her current state. She ran her hands over her face and body, suffice to say that she was alive. And pissed off. The pistol lay in a puddle in front of her, the tip of the bullet meant for her barely extending beyond the barrel. Her eyes lay focused upon the misfired bullet that saved her life, as she is immediately brought back to reality by the gentle crying behind her. She tentatively made her way towards the far corner of the alley, the apparent source of the tortured crying. As she removed the plastic bag from atop of the cardboard box, the weight of her life suddenly whisked back into her lungs. She began to cry again. Tres: Un Regalo Maria burst through the front door to her residence, leaving mud and dirt imprinted into the white flooring. And for the first time in her life, she didn’t care. She continued to run through the house, a bunch of clothes bundled tightly in her arms. Room after room she quickly scanned before moving on to the next. Finally she arrived at her final destination, the large mahogany doors leading to Dominic’s office. Without knocking, she entered. Per his usual, Dominic was seated in a large black chair behind his desk, only this time he wasn’t facing the door. “I knew you’d be back,” he always seemed to talk down to her, like a child. “You couldn’t pull the trigger, could you?” She ignored the condescending chatter, her news nearly bursting through her teeth. “Look! Look at what I have!” No movement came from the powerful man. “Dominic, we did it!” For the first time in a long time, she seemed happy. “I named him Diego.” Dominic sighed. “Who did you steal it from?” He said without turning towards her, he always seemed to know everything. “You’ll have to give it back, you just... don’t deserve it.” “It?!” Maria seethed. “Look at him you fucking prick!” He spun from the chair, with every intention of striking the ungrateful bitch. He had given her everything; money, cars, clothes, and a meaningful life to live... but she always wanted more. His blow was immediately halted as if by the hand of God. Dominic hulked his massive frame to one knee, and carefully removed the small child from the grasp of his nearly hysterical wife. “L.. l... look,” he stuttered, his eyes stretching wide. “It... it... it’s... a sign.” Dominic rotated the infant towards Maria, the small birthmark on the child’s neck catching her eye for the first time. “It’s a... a crown,” the words were barely audible. Not because she whispered them, but because of the growing commotion in the hallway outside the room. The first intimate moment shared by the couple in days is quickly destroyed as the door to the office is slammed open. ‘It’s happened, we must go now.’ Was all the man said, and yet somehow threw the room into turmoil. The smile quickly faded from Dominic as he turned his attention to his wife, he drew her in close and kissed her like he hadn’t since their were growing up. She pulled away. “What’s happening?!? Dominic! Dominic talk to me!” “We have to go, it’s happened.” “What’s happened? Where are we going?!” Maria bent over to take Diego from the top of the desk. “We? I’m sorry Maria, but there is no more you and me.” She turned to a solid flash of white, and a searing pain tearing through her brain. The pain lasted only a second, but the blood slowly filtering out of Maria’s skull onto her newly claimed son would torture his world for the rest of his life. Cuatro: Sueno [Chicago 2006] Diego suddenly awoke, finding himself lying upside down in a black recliner that wasn’t even in the reclining position; in fact, the recliner was lying on it’s side. His shirt was littered with debris from whatever snack he has devoured the night before, his breath sinking of stale beer. With the yellow bandana laying on his chest, he managed to wipe the crud from the corner of his mouth before exhaling a painful sigh. Unlucky for Diego, the sudden realization just after waking that a dream was just a dream didn’t pertain to him. He had already lost so much, with only folk tales and outlandish stories to keep the shallow memories of his mother alive. At many times in his life these painful memories shared during his drug induced coma he claimed was sleep became so lifelike, he believed them as reality. This dream came so often, every single moment exactly the same as the time before. Maybe one day he would find answers, eventually starting his search for his past. But, hell, that would take some effort and that shit he was running low on.
