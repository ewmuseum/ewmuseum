Date: 166 TheBLOG Mathematics. Eli VanNess Entry: 7/6/08 I am not who I was. The life of a carefree child cannot continue without unavoidable bumps in the road. In my case, the train has been derailed. I am continually amazed at how much difference only one year can make in a lifetime. Only 365 days ago I was a naive college student, foraging my way through the "real world". And in only one short month's time after that, thirty short days, I was expelled; thrust without a choice into a completely different world. I went from frat boy to homeless, jobless, and entirely useless waste of societal space within a window of 720 hours. I spent the first eighteen-fucking years of my life to get to a spot and in what seemed like a blink of an eye I was back at square one. And through this whole ordeal that prick of a best friend Felix didn't have a care in the world. He didn't care that he was the one who had actually agreed to buy the underage student the alcohol. He didn't care that he was ACTUALLY the person who had accidentally set fire to the dormitories. And he didn't care that his silence about these matters would drag me down to the inescapable depths of a lengthy criminal record at eighteen years old. The bastard didn't give a shit that with the charges stacked against us it would be impossible to ever achieve the goal of higher education, nor would it be possible to ever land a semi-respectable form of employment. Although, Felix "thought" that he had it all figured out, "Listen, man, you get used to the smell of gasoline after a while. Look on the brightside, all these cute chicks driving by get to see you whip a fat stack of bills out of your pocket every time you service someone's car." Yet, through this whole situation I made my best attempts to act as if the whole thing didn't phase me. I remained Eli VanNess, the notoriously good-looking party-goer whose only exercise was landing and then bagging sexy bitches. I did this not for Felix's sake, nor for the sake of any of my other friends; but for my own sanity. If at eighteen years old you look yourself in the mirror and say "I have fucked my life up, and I will never be somebody", your parents will likely find your lifeless body choking on its own vomit after passing into a vodka induced coma. Yet when the opportunity to compete as a tag team in PRIME arose, I grabbed it; seeing it as the only worthwhile thing I might ever be able to do again. So, I continued my self-destructive habits, partied harder than I ever had, and continued my friendship with Felix. The latter was getting increasingly difficult to deal with. But true to form, Felix fucked everything up for us again. In two matches we were 0-2. I may be the only wrestler in PRIME history to say he has competed in two matches and has never been pinned, yet still has never achieved victory. Two locals came out of the crowd and defeated us in what was supposed to be our profession. I'm not sure if you're beginning to sense a pattern. After that match we disappeared from both the PRIME backstage and on-air crews. I could not deal with the embarrassment of losing anymore, especially when there was nothing I could do to stop it. Felix had gotten me kicked out of school and in the process made us the laughingstock of our families and communities. Now, he had shamed us on the national stage. It was still too much to take. Few people are aware of exactly what happened on our last night as a part of the PRIME roster, and even fewer are aware of the year of hell that followed for both myself and my bastard best friend. ------------------------------------------------------------ PRIME ARENA DIRECTLY POST LOSS Felix Young stalked up PRIME's entrance ramp drenched in sweat, chest heaving up and down in rhythm with the generic entrance music the technicians in the studio had selected for their opponents celebrating in the ring. In tow was Eli VanNess, bearing more resemblance to a deer in headlights than to a professional wrestler. His deep brown eyes darted back and forth through the crowd, his brows becoming more and more twisted the farther he made it up the ramp. To Eli, the arena was swirling around him, a litany of laughing, ridiculing faces pointing and laughing. "Why shouldn't they laugh?" he thought; they had just been beaten by two locals who came out of the crowd in wrestling trunks. Felix paused in front of the curtain, waiting for Eli to catch up. He brushed by Felix aggressively and returned his bewildered gaze with a look of sheer rage. The bump had been no accident. Eli pushed by and nearly ripped the curtain off its hinges on his way through. This mere action fueled his rage, causing his to remember that he had actually jerked the curtain earlier that night, not even garnering enough respect to wrestle outside the programs first scheduled half hour of television. He continued to storm through the backstage as Felix caught up with him, forcefully placing his hand on Eli's shoulder. "Dude what the fuck is wrong with you man? Chill the hell out." Felix said, unaware that he was taking the entirely wrong approach to smooth the situation over. "Chill the hell out? Chill the hell out?! I was just embarrassed in front of how many millions of people for the second week in a row? I might as well be out there tagging with a kid that's still in grade school." Eli was no longer able to censor his anger. "Listen Eli I -" "Better yet I might as well be out there all-fucking-alone!" ------------------------------------------------------------ ....Mathematics I might as well be out there all fucking alone. Harsh. It was at that moment that our days of competing together in PRIME (no matter how short-lived they were) were effectively over. I gave my two weeks notice to the front office who kindly thanked me (however sarcastic it may have felt, or come across) and released me. Felix, who was now out of a tag team partner, didn't have the same appeal. To them, two stupid, sadistic, simple-minded party boys were better than one. While Felix called me multiple times begging me to give the business at least one more shot with him, I refused to answer the phone, until finally he left one last voicemail. ------------------------------------------------------------ ONE MONTH POST FINAL LOSS "One new voice message" croaked the digital women living inside of Eli's phone. He knew if was from Felix, and although he had refused to answer his phone calls, felt like he had to at least hear this message. For all the times Felix had called, he had never left one, just hung up and tried again later. "Enter your password and press #." The phone clicked away as Eli found himself surprised to be so worked up by the voicemail. The feeling caught him off guard and he shifted uneasily in his chair. Eli was now living in an apartment in the Red Hook section of Brooklyn. He was paying only pennies a month to live here while the landlord constantly remarked that this was "no place for some pretty white boy to be living". The room was not so much decorated as it was littered with newspaper articles. Headlines like "PRIME COMING TO ATLANTA THIS MONTH" and "NEW SIGNEES FOR PRIME LIVING UP TO HYPE!" rested all over the tables, chairs, couch, and floor. For Eli, following PRIME after his departure was not just a hobby; it was his life. Not an event went on within PRIME that Eli was unaware of. Just a month ago wrestling was a way to make a quick buck and move one, but now, it was everything. Eli entered his password and listened. “Hey man, I don’t know if you’re going to get this, let alone listen to it, but I figured I’d give it a shot. For all I know you’re at the bottom of a lake somewhere, ha.” Hearing his best friends voice for the first time in over a month irked Eli. His face contorted into a tight grimace as if he had just caught a whiff of something that was sticking to his shoe. “Anyway man, I just wanted to let you know that my mom died a week ago. I called you to invite you to the funeral and see how things were but you didn’t answer and I gave up after a while.” Eli’s was suddenly aware that his heart was in his throat as he struggled to swallow. Mrs. Young had been like a second mother to him. She even took him in when he was kicked out of his house for a week when he was sixteen. It was remarkable how small that incident seemed now. “I’m moving west, somewhere in the area of Oakland or California. Who knows what I’m going to do once I get there, but at least it’ll be a fresh start. Maybe I’ll get discovered by a movie agent while I’m waiting tables or something, wouldn’t that be crazy, ha. Anyway, you have my phone number man. If things could have happened differently you know I would have wanted them to, but sometimes things just seem out of your control.” ------------------------------------------------------------ …Mathematics Sometimes things seem just seem out of your control. For six months that followed, no saying, no sentiment, no cliché could have better described my existence. I ate, slept, and breathed PRIME. My life revolved around the weekly Revolution schedule. For some reason I created this far-fetched idea that one day I would make some sort of triumphant return to PRIME in which I’d ride in on no hype and all and ride out on a wave of expectations. Then it happened. ------------------------------------------------------------ TWO MONTHS POST PRIME EXIT Location: Red Hook’s Gold’s Gym Eli sauntered into the Gold’s Gym that was located only two blocks from his apartment. Dressed in a gray nike tracksuit with black aasic track sneakers he made his way into the locker room. His eyes darted around the air-conditioned changing space. He quickly spotted a deserted locker with an empty chair propped against its hinges. Carefully he stepped over the bags of the people exercising and proceeded to place his bag into the locker. Upon unzipping it he removed his track top to reveal a white tank top clinging tightly to his chest. He glanced over his shoulder and caught his reflection in the mirror. He spun around and allowed himself to examine his still body. Slowly his eyes made their way from toe to shoulder and he grinned. Two months away from exercise of any sort and he still looked good. Although he had lost some weight, he was still around 200 pounds and felt that he was still as diesel as when he originally left campus. That was about to change. With his chest puffed out he entered the gym, only to be barraged by what seemed to be a million people. Each was more or less twice his size. Eli’s chest quickly deflated as he made his way over to the free weights. With each repetition his eyes wandered to himself in the mirror and then another man doing the same exercise only with twice the weight and triple the muscle mass. A look of disgust crossed his face as he caught sight of himself in each mirror that seemed to be surrounding the whole gym on each wall. After a few more quick exercises he resolved to buy some sort of home gym that he could fit in his apartment. Although he was a professional wrestler for only three weeks he felt destroyed by all the men in the gym. He even thought he caught a few guys in the corner smirking; they obviously must have recognized him. Eli pushed past his “fans” and reentered the locker room, followed by the man who had been doing the same workout as him. Eli sat down and proceeded to put his jogging top back on. When his head popped through the opening he caught sight of the monster standing right in front of him. “You didn’t look too happy out there champ,” The man said in a deep, bass-like voice that seemed to echo throughout the whole room, “the name’s Tyler. Tyler Thompson, but everyone calls me TT.” “Nice to meet you.” Said Eli, as he offered his hand. TT’s palm nearly engulfed Eli up to his forearm. In an instant the inadequate feeling from the weight room returned. Instantly aware of Eli’s reaction, TT offered some advice that would change Eli and the upstarts forever. “Listen, I recognize you form the TV man, I hate to admit it, but I’m a closet wrestling freak. Always wanted to be one. You were part of that not so great tag team that was out of wrestling just as fast as you entered,” he paused and said through a slight chuckle, “you even got beat by those locals.” Eli aggressively shot out of his chair, but TT put his arms on his shoulders and extended them. “Listen broski, I’m not here to ruffle your feathers, I’m here to offer a solution.” With those words he gave a quick glance around the locker room. Then, in one swift movement he scooped up his gym bag with one hand and unzipped it with the other. Eli peered inside to see a few clear bottles with what looked like prescription stickers printed onto them. Next to the bottles was a plastic baggy filled with individually wrapped syringes, with the date of usage marked clearly on the underside. “What are you some kind of junky?” “Nah bro, my brother is a doctor across the country in California. I used to lift casually but never go to the place I wanted to be physically. I go into his office, give him some cock-and-bull story about my back hurting and he prescribes beneficial “performance enhancers” to help me “heal”.” Said TT, a slight grin spreading across his face. ------------------------------------------------------------ Mathematics… That right there was a turning point in my career, maybe my entire life. For nearly two decades I had tried to be successful at anything and had failed each miserably. Now, as if by some sort of miracle, a man was placed into my life that would give me the tools to advance. Whether that will be a blessing or a curse has yet to be determined. To say that I was addicted to steroids would be incorrect. I am a control freak and I do not have an addictive personality. I could have stopped at any point during each cycle. The problem was is that I didn’t ever want to stop. When TT was being cracked down on by gym officials, I kept my cool. He never gave up my name and even gave me the phone number for his brother’s practice if he was ever to leave the gym. When TT later had liver and kidney failure and held on for only three days, I didn’t stop. I was not addicted. I simply didn’t want to quit. The funny thing about “performance enhancers”, i.e. steroids is that once you begin to see your body change, you become obsessed with it. Never once in my life had I been careful about the food I ate or the liquids I drank. Yet the second I plunged that needle into my thigh, I knew the physical and chemical makeup of every single thing I ingested. Alcohol, my main rehydration method, became a non-factor in my life. It seems stupid, almost suicidal (in a business sense), that PRIME has decided to allow both Felix and myself to compete again. I almost broke into tears when they returned our phone call about making a comeback. PRIME has us on a short leash, I will not beat around the bush when it comes to that. Booking us to work as a team with Troy Douglas was a bold move. I for one have a lot of respect for the Intense Title holder, and am ridiculously motivated by the chance to compete alongside him. If I were to say I had scouted our opponents…I’d be lying. This match is about me. I have put eleven months of body-destroying work in to put myself in the upper echelon of physicality in the entire wrestling business. It may sound cocky, but this is a personal test for me. Whether we are victorious or not, I want to come out of that ring knowing that I can compete in PRIME with or without Felix dragging me down. On Revolution the questions I have been agonizing over for eleven months will be revealed. Am I cut out for PRIME? Can I compete? Will I be great? In the eleven months that have followed my meeting with Taylor, I have grown two inches and put on forty-seven pounds. I am only 6% body fat. I will not stop. I cannot stop. I need to be great. I will probably suffer severe side effects long after my wrestling career is done. It will probably kill me. But I was faced with an equation when I made my decision to head down this road. I will be great. Eli + Natural Physical Skill = Mediocrity At Best Eli + Doing whatever necessary to advance = Champion To me, it was simple mathematics.
