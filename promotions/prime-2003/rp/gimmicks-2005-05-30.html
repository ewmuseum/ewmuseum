Gimmicks

30 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 30, 2005 February 18, 2001 You knew you’d have to start somewhere. But HERE? You shake your head as you walk through the door, surveying the narrow ceiling. A few guys are busy setting up chairs… and they’re not using many racks to do it. With an outfit this size, you figure some of the talent has to be doing this menial job. That realization worries you. A couple of them looks to be north of 40. Most of them have eaten too many Whoppers… or not enough. Christ, how old is that one?! MAYBE 14? And then there’s the ring. The mat might as well be paisley—it’s got more stains than it does clean spots. Some of them are the familiar rust-colored stains of dried blood. You’d rather not think about those other stains. The ropes are sagging badly. The top one looks to be barely attached to the turnbuckle in the northeast corner. “Help you, son?” You’re snapped out of your reflections and see a skinny man wearing a black T-shirt eyeballing you. “Um, yeah… Name’s Dave Gibson. I got contacted about being on the show.” “Oh right,” the skinny guy said. “Sam’s in the back.” With a nod of thanks, you get your feet moving, past the ring and the black curtain that you assume will be used as the entranceway. A man with bleached-blond hair and broad shoulders is crouched over a stereo. “Dammit, I can’t believe this,” you hear him say. “Can’t believe we don’t have P.A. equipment. How’s anybody sposed to hear the entrance music?” Past the amateur sound technician, you see him—“Sexy” Nick Stevenson—and repress a hysterical fit of giggles. This is an interesting definition of sexy, to say the least. Stevenson is leaning back in a director’s chair that seems ready to snap asunder under the strain of his bulk. A large belly is dropping over his belt line, and he’s got a towel wrapped around his head. A discarded box of hair dye lies next to his right foot, and he’s snacking liberally from a bag of Doritos. This is the man you came to see. Like it or not, he’s the one who was willing to give you a shot, give you a crack to step inside the ring for the first time and take a step toward achieving your dream—to be a professional wrestler. “What you want?” Stevenson says. “We don’t start sellin’ tickets till an hour before showtime.” “Um… I’m Dave Gibson. You called me? Wanted to book me on the card?” Stevenson shrugs and drops the bag. He stands, placing one hand on his towel. “I got this new kid… real potential as a babyface, I think,” Stevenson said as he turns and rummages through a gym bag. “Probably gonna book him to go for our heavyweight title soon.” “Lookin’ for a new champ, huh?” “Naw, just a new challenger,” Stevenson said. “Fans are gettin’ tired of watchin’ me beat all the bad guys. Think I’m gonna turn heel soon so I can start beatin’ the faces, too.” “Hey, it’s your show. Say, how many people fit in here anyway?” Stevenson shrugged. “We usually average 50. Sometimes 80,” Stevenson sheds the towel revealing a freshly-dyed black mullet. Last summer we booked Griff Truxell and had 112. That’s our biggest crowd ever.” “Lovely.” “Hey, if we’re too small-potatoes for you rookie, you know where the door is…” Stevenson’s pudgy face had started to turn an unsightly shade of red. “No. No, it’s fine. I just want a break.” “Good to hear it,” Stevenson said, continuing to rummage through the bag. “Anyway, like I said, you’re my mystery man.” “Right… so where do I change?” “Hold on, I’m looking for your mask.” “My what?” “Your mask.” “I don’t wear a mask.” Stevenson looked up. “You do tonight. You’re gonna play a character I’ve used for years. Always gets a BIG pop. Beatin’ him will really help my new babyface, too. Let me just find… AH!” Stevenson pulls out a mask. It looks pretty standard… then you get a look at the design on the front and feel your stomach twist. There, criss-crossing the face, are the stars and bars of the Confederate flag. “What. The hell. Is that.” “It’s your gimmick!” Stevenson said. “The Redneck Ninja! Like I said, it gets a BIG pop.” Your head is swimming—more from the idea than the image of 50 people being capable of giving ANYTHING a big pop. “Here’s what you do, see? You’re a good ole boy trained in ninja martial arts.” “I think that’s called ninjitsu.” “Whatever the hell it’s called, yer good at it. Got it? Except, you use that redneck touch, right? When you do a chop… ?” Stevenson brings his hand down in a vertical motion, rather than the knife-edge or overhand chops you prefer. “Ya yell ‘Yee-HAH!’ Get it? We figure that’s what a redneck ninja would say.” You pause, still letting the information digest—and it’s doing so badly, like a salami sandwich with extra thumbtacks on top. “Listen… I, I don’t think this is such a good idea.” Suddenly, Stevenson is in your face. It isn’t until you’re eyeball to eyeball—well, eyeball to forehead—that you see how short “Sexy” Nick is. And people at how many wrestling schools told YOU that you didn’t have the look of a pro wrestler? “What the hell you tryin’ to pull? You gonna come in here, and start tellin’ me how to run my show? I’m Nick Stevenson, boy! Champion AND owner of Mountain States Wrestling. You wanna work with me? You wanna break into this business? Then you… are gonna wear… this… mask.” Stevenson holds it up to you, then slowly lowers it. “Like I said, if you don’t like it, you know where the door is.” You’re surprised how little time the decision takes. Before you can convince yourself otherwise, you shoulder your bag, turn and walk back out of the middle school gym without a second look. “You’re gonna regret this!” Stevenson yelled. “I know people! I’ll make sure you never work in this business!” * * * * * Present day You refused to sell out that night—if a $30 paycheck (hardly enough to cover gas to and from)—could be called selling out. Kinda ironic, since you built a character around doing just that for almost two years as a wrestler and manager. But even then, it was different. You were still you. Sure, they were different incarnations of you… but they were still you. And unlike Trashcan Man, you didn’t need a Rolodex to keep track. There was the man who appreciated the history, skill and technical heritage of wrestling. That was “Old School” Dave Gibson, back when he was a babyface. There was the man who wanted to advance his career, whatever the cost. That was “New School” Dave Gibson. And then there was the man who—above all else—valued money and fame. The Sellout as art imitated life. They were all part of the same man, when you got right down to it. Different facets of the complex structure of his personality. And none of them… needed a gimmick. Gimmicks… hell, who needs them? Nobody talented, not in your opinion. Now you’re facing a pirate and a clown. From outer space, no less. And your only ally is a gigantic warrior from Malawi, Africa. Hearing the breakdown of the match makes you want to pitch it to Roger Corman. But for you, it’s reality. You turn over in your bed, sighing as you try to get some sleep. One thought gives you some solace. Whether it’s a wrestler, a pirate or a redneck ninja… They all bleed the same.
