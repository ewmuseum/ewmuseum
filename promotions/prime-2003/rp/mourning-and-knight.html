Mourning and Knight

Date: Great American Nightmare -October 9, 2008 Pittsburgh, PA "This is probably illegal, isn't it?" The words rolled off her tongue with just a hint of amusement. She turned to look up at him, the man walking next to her, and let a twinkle of hopeful excitement flutter through her light green eyes. It was a silly thing to get so excited about walking through a park. She knew that. But Angelica Brooks had not left the Jennersville Regional Hospital since she'd arrived. Everyone from her mother to C.P. Cantrell had insisted she stay until the doctors were one hundred percent assured of her health and recovery. So stay she had. Locked up and caged until this cool Thursday afternoon in October. When her shiny white knight had rode in to secret her away from the overprotective eyes of parents and hospital staff. "Probably a little. Yeah." Tyler Rayne felt the heat of her gaze. The youthful exuberance beaming at the mere thought of doing something wrong. He smiled. She melted but tried not to show it. The nipping cool breeze had flushed her cheeks pink. Red tresses drooped lazily from her scalp, framing pale skin that had seen too much recent action in a hospital bed and not enough in a tanning bed. His eyes returned to the park. The path set before them. The hustle and bustle of Pittsburgh natives. She had to force herself to look away. To watch the dogs romping through the grass. The old man reading the newspaper. Anything but him. She willed herself to keep from reaching for his hand. Arms swinging so intimately close as they walked. He was unusually quiet since returning from ReVolution 175. In fact, he had been growing increasingly less talkative for, at the very least, as long as she'd been awake. After the incident. The induced coma... Given recent events, however, he could hardly be blamed. "How's Chandler doing?" "Good enough, I suppose. He wasn't exactly forthcoming with the details." "And you didn't ask." "Not my place." "But you wanted to. You can admit you're worried about him, you know." "Tink's a big boy. He can take care of himself. I just... I don't wanna see him laid up, too. Another friend in the hospital. I... it wouldn't be doing Dawkins any favors to see his best friend all... you know... not after Nikkie." "That's what you're worried about? Dawkins?" "God damn it, kid." The next few minutes are walked in silence. The urge to reach out for him tingles at her fingertips. She'd almost forgotten what it was like to be around him like this. "I... I honestly don't know what I'd do if Tink... well, if things went south. With you and Uni out, him and Dawkins are the only two people I can trust in PRIME. Only friends I've got left in the place. It's just..." "Just what?" "I... don't even know, B. I really don't know. I just... hmm. Can't say I've felt real fuckin' helpful lately. Everyone just keeps gettin' -" "This wasn't your fault, Ty." The reply was quick and sharp. The tone angrier than she had intended. "Not what your mother said." "Oh fuck my mother." "Can't say I hadn't thought about it." The most natural reaction is to lash out. It is a reaction she did not suppress. She playfully backhands the leather jacket covering his bicep. He responded with feigned objection, soothing the spot of attack with his 'uninjured' hand. The two share a much needed laugh. His smile fades soon, though. Hers remains for far longer. They walk. "I mean it, Ty. It wasn't your fault." "Sweet of you to say, kid, but we both know that-" "We both know that if I'd been thinking even a little bit, I wouldn't have gone out there alone. I knew the Hand was dangerous. I knew Desade was looking to make an example. And I went anyway. Yeah, sure, maybe it wouldn't have been me if you hadn't gone and pissed the bitch off..." He winced at the accusation. The lingering unspoken gnawing at his guilt now revealed. His shoulders sagged. His head hung in shame. Everything descends. She stopped. Put a hand to his arm to stop him as well. The diminutive redhead looked up into eyes that refused to meet her own. "...but if it hadn't been me, it would have been someone else. Mary-Lynn or Mills or Hoyt knows who. She would have gotten her hands on somebody, Ty. This sucks and I hate it. I hate that fucking hospital. I hate not being able to do anything. I hate Desade and the whole Dead Man's Hand for doing this to me. I hate PRIME for ever hiring me. I hate SCCW for ever hiring her. I hate myself for being so damn stupid. And yes, for a little while... I even hated you. But I've had a lot of time to think, and in a way... I'm glad it was me. It hurts. And it sucks. And I hate it. I don't cry in front of you. I don't want you to think I'm a pussy. Stupid, I know. But... I don't. When you're gone... it's all I do. I hate this so much. I hate being here. I hate... all of it. But if I wasn't here, someone else would be. If I wasn't going through this, someone else would have to. And as fucked up as it sounds... I'm okay with that. If I had to be the victim to save someone else... that's something I can live with." A full minute passed between them. He just stared blankly at her expectant face. Awestruck. Completely. Fucking. Awestruck. "You've come a long way from that annoying little model I met a year and a half ago." "Lot has changed since then. I've made some new friends. One of whom seems to fancy himself a hero. Keeps thinking he can save everybody. Guess maybe some of that rubbed off." He smiled. So many nights spent lying awake, predicting this very moment. Guessing at her response when it all finally came through. He had expected the worst. Hoped for the best. This was more than he had ever anticipated. "Yeah. I guess maybe it did." "Ty?" "Huh?" "Can you do me a favor?" "Anything, kid." "Don't come back." "Yeah, su-- I... what?" "When you leave tonight. Don't come back." He stared at her again. Looking for the twinge of a smile. Waiting for the coming punchline. It never came. "I don't want to be the reason you lose the Roulette." "Jesus, B. You know I could give a shit less about that da-" "But I do, Ty. Ever since you got here, you've been scratching and clawing to get respect. You've been this close that spot for months now, but you still haven't gotten in. You're amazing, Ty. You are, without a doubt, the best performer on that roster. But all anyone ever sees is smile. The charm. They only hear the cursing and the funny remarks. No one really looks beyond that. To the heart. The dedication. The loyalty. I mean, for fuck's sake, Ty, if you'd spent half the time you'd spent with me at that hospital actually training, you'd already be Universal Champion! This is your shot. Your one chance to prove you're everything you keep saying you are. All that bullshit you keep talking about... now's the time to back it up. You could do this, Ty. You could-" "Alright, kid. Jesus. Enough with the Rocky routine. I get it. If it means that much to you... I'll leave." "And train?" "And train." "Good. Now don't come back until you've got a Uni Title to show me." He laughs. "Right. Look, we, uh... we better get going. Wouldn't want your mother calling the cops on me." "Again." "Yeah." "Ty, um... one more thing?" "Gods damned you're a greedy little bitch." "Fuck you." "You'd love to." "Tell Dawkins I... shit. I... don't even know what to say." "No one does." "Just... tell him something comforting. But cool. Whatever you'd say. Tell him that. And then tell him I'd say it." Another chuckle. "Sure, kid. Sure." ---- -October 12, 2008 Tokyo, Japan "Finally decided to take a break, eh?" He casts an unnecessary look over his sweat-beaded shoulder, the warm smile of a dear friend there to greet him. Kairi slides her lithe figure through the big double doors of The Katana's Point's office. Thick black ponytail sashays behind her as she marches to the desk. He watches for all of half a minute before remembering why she took care of the day-to-day management of the club for him. Shit was fucking boring. Tyler Rayne let his eyes turn away from the Japanese beauty and her sifting drawers, rather more interested in the complete emptiness of the nightclub below him. He stood still, affixed to that single spot, sticky arms crossed over his bare chest. "It's been awhile since I've seen you train this hard. What's gotten into you?" "Figured it was about time I started taking this wrestling thing serious." "Please. I couldn't remember the last time you took something serious." Her tone is light and teasing, but for once, he is not in the teasing mood. The jab goes unanswered, and Kairi pauses from her search to look up. She's known him for nearly fifteen years. Probably knows him better than anyone. Even Katterina. Thought it wouldn't take a genius to figure out something was up, a good decade or so of experience will help cut to the root of the problem. "What's up, Ty?" She doesn't need to ask. But she will. For his sake. "I've lost myself in... days of training now. Haven't thought about much of anything except that fucking match. Purpose, I suppose. If I'm thinking about the Roulette, then I don't have to think about-" "Lin." "Yeah." "Have you told Lindsay?" "Jesus, no." "You need to." "Like fuck I do." "She deserves to know." "She'll hate me." "Not if she loves you." "I can't, Kai. I just can't. If I lose her, too..." "You won't." "The fuck would you know?" "I'm Asian. We have our mysterious ways." He can't help but laugh at the absurdity. "Brooks made you come out here, didn't she?" "How did you-" "I know you, Ty. You wouldn't have left her side if she hadn't made you." "Fuckin'... god damn you." "Yeah, yeah." "I'm going to get back to work. Ten minutes in here and you're already in psychiatrist mode. I'd rather just go back to kicking things really, really hard." "You are good at that." "Better than most." "You should tell her." "I know." "But you won't." "Probably not." "At least think about it." "What else am I going to do?" "Kick things really, really hard." "There is that." "If she loves you... she'll understand." "That's what terrifies me, Kai. I don't deserve to-" "Yes you do. Fifteen years and you still haven't realized that. It's time to let go." "I can't." "Try harder." "Wasn't I going to go kick some things?" "You had mentioned." "Thanks, Kai. Even if I don't listen. Thanks." "Someone has to straighten you out every now and then. Lord knows you can't take care of yourself." "Fuckin' A." He exits the office. She waits a handful of moments, herself now staring at that spot on the floor. The very spot where Lin had died. The very spot where he had... ---- -October 16, 2008 Boston, MA "Are you sure you know how to turn on the stove? It's a little more complicated than opening a Chinese take-out box. I don't want you to hurt yourself." The very thought of Tyler Rayne in a kitchen, doing something as domesticated as cooking dinner was laughable. Downright unbelievable. To see him there, though, physically standing behind the stove, vegetables laid out across the granite countertop... it was more than she could handle. The Queen of the Ring sat high on a bar stool, looking down on the proceedings with a condescending (yet somehow inviting) smile. "Oh ye of little faith." The oven burner roared to life. A much brighter and higher flame than Lindsay herself had ever needed to crank from the appliance. He set a large black wok within the flame and turned his attention to the vegetables. While his culinary skills may still be in question, his handiness with a knife could hardly be doubted. Unknown sauces boil and simmer within the wok. He pops a head of cabbage into the air. Over his head. He winks at her. She rolls her eyes. She watches the cabbage dropping behind his back. He watches her. Catches the cabbage effortlessly. Back on the countertop and he begins to tear. "You really do feel the need to show off whenever possible, don't you?" The corners of her lips twitch, smile growing. From the living room, the clicking of toenails against hardwood floor approaches the culinary sanctuary; Ciro and Isis, Doberman bodyguards and canine companions to the Queen, smell food. "Part of the job requirement." A bowl of noodles find their new home in the wok. Steam rises from the steel bowl. Everything hisses. The cabbage is added. Some spices. An ample helping of some dark liquid. His hands move so fast she can hardly keep up. Isis takes a seat on the hardwood floor, barely inches from the Queen's stool. Protecting. Guarding. Her dear counterpart, Ciro, however finds the smell of Asian delicacy far more appetizing than duty. The Doberman plops itself down near Rayne's feet, looking up expectantly with, what else... puppy dog eyes. "'Sup, pooch." Where once there were vegetables, now there is meat. Chicken breasts. He cuts them longwise, then sideways. Little chunks that disappear into the mounting concotion within the wok. The poultry is gone in an instant. He takes a moment to toss the contents of the wok. Add more of those mysterious fluids. She spies a shrimp and wonders when the hell he had a chance to add that in there. "So... how's the knee?" He doesn't look up, but rather proceeds to chopping a rather sizeable portion of beef. Incidently, a chunk of the raw meat finds its way to the floor. Ciro snaps it up in an instant. "Do you want the truth, or a lie so I don't wreck the moment?" He shrugs. "Moment? Look who you're talkin' to. If there was one... I'd be bound to ruin it sooner or later, anyway." Small chunks of beef begin an aerial assault on the wok. He is errantly throwing the beef in all sorts of ridiculous manners, clearly just to amuse himself. Over the shoulder. Behind the back. The faux basketball shot. A flat flick of the wrist, like skipping a rock. Surprisingly, a few pieces of meat miss the mark. A handful of strips that tumble down to the hardwood floor and are greedily consumed upon their arrival. Even in the most candid, private moments, Lindsay Troy has always had a tough time admitting a weakness or a fault. Years of fashioning herself into a tough-as-nails, take-no-shit juggernaut of the sport have left her this way. But there's no need for pretenses here. Tyler knows she's hurt, knows she's been laid up at home with a severe case of cabin fever and longing for the road, know she's been putting herself through grueling hours of physical therapy to get herself back faster, stronger, better than before, because surgery was absolutely not an option. "Well, it's not the worst injury I've had to it. This'll be number three. But it's in a bad way, Tyler. They're not even considering a possible time-table for return at this point. I'm hoping before the end of the year but..." Lindsay trails off. Optimism's never been her strong suit. "I don't want to disappoint myself." "So don't." For a man so long-winded, so... obscenely eloquent at times, he sure had a knack for short and blunt responses. He shoveled genereous helpings of food onto two plates, pushing one steaming mound of noodles in her direction. The other he held firmly in hand, snipping as much of his own meal as possible between two chopsticks. He leans against the back counter, facing her, and chews. She takes her own set of chopsticks to hand, snares noodles and chicken betwixt them and lifts the food to her opening mouth. It's not long after Lindsay starts chewing that she's murmuring her approval of the dish. "This is amazing, Tyler. I honestly expected you to walk through the door tonight with PF Changs' carry-out." "What can I say? I'm good at what I do." She smirks while taking a piece of chicken between her thumb and forefinger. "Some things more than others." Holding it over Isis' snout, the dog takes the offering and licks her jowls. Tyler hardly notices, watching Ciro eat the bountiful helping from between his own chopsticks. "I, uh... I take it you heard about the Dawkins thing. With Nikkie." The sudden change in atmosphere comes with an immediate change in demeanor. A small but palpable level of tension rises within the kitchen. Even the dogs seem to perk. Noticing. "Yeah." Her lips purse together, eyes relegated to her steaming plate of stir-fry. Fingers absently click the chopsticks together. "I got his number off Tsonda. Left him a voicemail. Seems very...there's really no comfort in a situation like this." He laughs. It laughs all amusement. "I should know." "So should I." Chopsticks poke lazily into two separate plates, neither of them attempting to grab at anything at all. Lost. In memories. In forgotten grief. In pain long buried. "About that. I should... tell you something. Before we go any further with... whatever the fuck it is we're doing." "Well, we were eating." He offers a half-smile in apology. "Told you I'd ruin the moment." "You're good at what you do, after all." "It's just... this Dawkins thing. I've been thinking about her, ya know? Lin, and... I figure you should know the truth. All of it." The nod from Troy is so slight it's barely noticeable. She slides off the barstool, hops over to the refrigerator and pulls out two beers from the holdster on the door. She hands one to Rayne, then opens a drawer and pulls out a bottle opener. "Figure we'll need these, if you're about to get heavy on me." "Fuckin' A." Bottles open, they both drink. Here's hopin' she's got more in the fridge. "We, uh... we were engaged. This was... fifteen years ago, give or take. Her uncle owned the club at the time. Had a bit of rough business with some renegade Yakuza. Couple a punk kids lookin' to make a name for themselves. Tryin' to prove they were tougher than their fathers. We'd been dickin' around with these kids for awhile. I was just workin' the door, tryin' to pull in some extra cash while I was wrestling. Anyway, these punks show up one night. New Year's Eve. Dozens of 'em. Jump us. Fuckin' just... the numbers. There were too many. There's maybe twenty or so of 'em works their way in. Lingering just outside the dance floor. I go upstairs. To the office. This... fuck. He's got Lin. Gun to her head. Nothin' I can do. So he gives me a choice. He can let her go, and every man down on that floor unloads his semi-auto into the crowd. Couple hundred people caught in a crossfire. Fish in a fucking barrel. Or I can kill her, and he'll order his boys to leave. Every single one. Without a bullet being fired. The love of my life or the lives of four hundred strangers. Make a choice, he says. Time's a wastin'. Funny thing about Yakuza. They don't lie. For all the digusting shit those fucks will do... there's an inbred honor somewhere lost in there. He knew and I knew that his word was gold. So... there I was. Left to make a choice." Lindsay says nothing, unable to respond for longer than either are probably comfortable with. There's no making sense of a revelation of this magnitude, no real, discernable reply for brutal, heartbreaking honesty. She finishes her beer, reaches for two more and pops the tops off. "Why are you telling me this?" "You need to know. Or deserve to know. Or maybe I just need to finally admit it. Fuck if I know. The thing with Brooks. Now you're laid up. Nikkie... It just seems like ever since that day, I've been cursed. Like... this ghost is haunting me. People close to me get hurt. Die. Sometimes worse. I'm... I just think you should know what you're getting into. Before we get too far. She didn't die. I killed 'er. Saved a whole lotta lives in the process. Went and ruined a whole lot more after the fact. None of it was right. None of it has ever felt right. But... you should know. What I am. What I've done." There is no proper response. So none is given. They sit quietly. And they drink.
