Memories [2]: Admiration and Disappointment (Rev 166: v. the Upstarts and Troy Douglas)

undated

Date: February 19th, 2001 It's sometimes said that admiration is the state furthest from understanding. Admiration, in its truest sense, is to look at someone or something, blinding oneself to their negative qualities. To admire means to forgive the past transgressions, past mistakes, and past sins of a person... to put them up on a pedistal higher than anything else. Admiring someone means to highly support the positive things they do in life. Their talents, their skills, their accomplishments... all of them are sugar-coated to be even more important than they already are. The funny thing about admiration is that everyone does it at least once in their lives. People find someone in their lives to admire, and cherish it for years until time and circumstance finally betrays that admiration. The problem with admiring someone is that you lose a sense of self when you so blindly support someone. When your admiration for a certain individual begins to define who you are, what happens when that someone passes away? What happens when they do something that even you can't ignore? What happens when they betray that admiration? * . * February 19th, 2001; Providence, Rhode Island For Simon Knox, the man he admired the most was his father, Earl Knox. A son can't really help but admire his father, that strong male of the family that serves as the pillar of support. Simon, twelve years old at the time of February 19th in 2001, would wander into the gym every day he had a chance in order to watch his father train his brother, Steve. He watched not to see his brother essentially take the beating of a lifetime, although that was certainly an amusing reason for him to come... but to see his father do what he did best. Today, Steve Knox was being trained in a bit of submission wrestling. Although his skills have improved a bit since a month ago, he was still weak in this regard. Earl himself was more of a brawler than a technician, he admitted that becoming knowledgable in technical abilities and becoming more well-rounded in the ring. And right now... "Boy, you ain't gonna do much lyin' around on your belly." Earl Knox said to his second son, caught in a half Boston crab. One of those Lance Storm-esque ones where the knee was elevated higher than normal. Steve, who had clearly managed to progress past simpler stuff, was having a bit of a harder time trying to figure his way out of the hold. Simon smiled upon entering the room, seeing his father in action in what Simon always pictured to be his natural enviroment. Much like usual, he was wearing a T-shirt depicting his father. As he walked towards the bench, his eyes remained transfixed on his father's training, so much so that he didn't even notice that the bench was already being occupied. In fact, he almost sits down on top of the person occupying it, causing the occupant to groan in annoyance. Simon turned and saw that it was Lieselotte, his strange German cousin. Her pink and black dress nearly ruffled from the awkward start of their meeting for today, Lieselotte's surprised look soon faded to what one would consider to be her typical poker face. Grumbling, Simon simply sat next to her. He had long forgotten the incident with his father and that producer, Willy Wilkerson, and thus he had also forgotten that Lieselotte had heard something from that conversation that Simon hadn't. However, there was something about this girl that bugged him, perhaps it was the language barrier between the two, or perhaps it was the fact that she wasn't even paying attention to the greatness of his father even despite the fact that she was in his gym. After seeing that his brother wasn't getting out of his predicament any time soon, he turned to look at Liese. "What're you doing here?" Simon asked. Lieselotte didn't immediately notice that Simon had said something to her. However, after a couple of seconds, she looked up from the book she was reading. She thought about his question for a little bit, processing it, and then she answered, "You here." Simon blinked at her, but figured that she couldn't really tell him what she was talking about. The bridge between their language was still much too hazardous to cross. That being said, Simon turned to see what Lieselotte was reading, and found that it was a book about speaking English. With a "hmph", he turned back to the ring to find that Strve had managed to break out of the half Boston crab, and was getting to his feet. "Hmmm." Earl mused, "I might make somethin' of you yet, Steve, m'boy." "You know," Steve said, trying to catch a bit of his breath, "For someone who didn't originally want to get into the business, you're sure good at drilling it into people's heads." Earl Knox was originally from south Texas, and didn't originally have aspirations to become a professional wrestler. He wanted to be a football player, and even briefly played for Texas A&M. However, he suffered an injury during his college days, and his attempts to rehab the injury eventually led him to being noticed by wrestling promoters in the area... having already had the look of a wrestling star. Ironically, the exact same thing would later happen to Soren Knox. Earl Knox was eventually directed to Harley Race, who trained him. He didn't originally like the idea of becoming a wrestler, but gradually warmed to it within two years of working in the business. Initially as a heel, he eventually became famous through feuds with wrestlers such as Kyle Morris and Bob Woods, delivering virulent promos that eventually caught on and added to his hip factor at the time. By the mid-90s, Earl was so popular that he became the top face of his promotion, and it wouldn't be long before Hollywood came knocking. Earl offered Steve a chuckle, "Those days... they're probably not long for this world." Steve was confused. Earl leaned against the ropes of his ring, looking up at the ceiling, "I'm gonna start shootin' a movie, some time in April. Ain't gonna be around at home much after that." Steve raised an eyebrow, "So... what? You're gonna up and drop me in the middle of training?" "Naw," Earl said, "Nothin' like that. I'm just passin' you to a buddy of mine until I get back so I can beat on ya some more." Steve made a sound that seemed like a part sigh and a part laugh. He brushed back his sweaty brown hair, and looked at his father, "You're strange, dad. Most fathers these days don't like to spend time beating up their kids. In wrestling rings." "That just means I'm just old fashioned, boy." Earl said, "Anyway, guy's name is Jeremy Odessa. Bit of a strange one, that guy. Always serious, even at times where you can just relax. He ain't the type of clown you'll find on a unicycle, that's for sure. He'll pick up where I'll leave off, so expect a whole hell of a lot of kickings of your ass, son." "Great to know." Steve said, sarcastically. Professional wrestling was a test of endurance. The main goal of training to become a professional wrestler was to basically punish oneself so much so that they grow used to it and can, theoretically, last longer in a professional wrestling enviroment. From an outsider's perspective, the act of training to become a professional wrestler either showed a lot of love for the profession, or it showed that the people training to become wrestlers were quite masochistic. Steve Knox, the Knox son who would go on to have the most talent, would have you believe that he did it all out of love for the profession. Simon Knox, later in his life, would claim that he did it only because his father did it as wel, and that if his father was never a wrestler, he'd never be one either. Soren Knox, the oldest brother, actually didn't think about wrestling as a career until long after both Steve and Simon entered the business. Steve Knox endured so that one day, he would be known as a great wrestler like his father. Simon Knox, however, just wanted to be in Steve Knox's shoes at that moment. After an hour of watching Earl continuing to torture his son under the guise of training him for professional wrestling, the session was over. Steve would eventually stagger back to his bedroom, sleep the rest of the day, and wake up so sore that he could barely move. And then he would do it over again. Simon, however, wasn't interested in his older brother. Rather, he was interested in his father, which was why he followed his dad out of the gym and into the main lobby of the mansion. His dad was probably thinking of a trip to the kitchen to get a drink. "Hey, dad." Simon called out to his dad. This caused Earl Knox to stop dead in his tracks and turn around to see his son standing out in the doorway. He offered his son a weary smile. "Hey, Simon. What can I do you for?" Earl asked. Simon Knox was every bit still a child, particularly one that had grown up around the success of his father. He can seem spoiled, but as his father was one who grew up in a poor family, he was raised to not get everything he might want. However, what Simon was about to ask for, though completely unreasonable from the standpoint of anyone else, was something he had long desired. He looked up at his father with his hands on his hips. "I want you to train me." Earl Knox was a man who always thought lightly on his feet and absorbed such shocking blows with the greatest of finesse. This comment, however, came as suddenly as a speeding bullet, struck with the force of a locomotive, and might have even been able to leap tall buildings in a single bound. Earl stared at his son for a good long moment, and raised his eyebrow at him, "What." Simon repeated his statement, "I want you to train me." "To... do what, exactly? You havin' trouble with your homework or somethin'?" Earl asked. Earl might have been a bit sarcastic, however... he had a gut feeling that he knew what his son was going to ask. "I want you to train me to be a wrestler. Like Steve." Simon repeated a third time, only with more clarification. Earl thought the boy was kidding when he heard the his request the first time, but then he saw that glimmer in his eyes and the utter seriousness in his son's voice. Earl hadn't felt so nervous in at least a month, when Willy had pitched the new movie to him. He walked towards Simon. "Never heard ya so serious before, Simon." Earl said, "Even Steve wanted to wait until he was a wee bit older, though. I can't in good conscience train ya." "Why not?!" Simon asked, the manner of which was like a typical child. Earl kneeled in front of Simon and put his hand on his son's shoulder. As a father, Earl knew that he must occasionally battle his son's admiration for him with what was sure to be obvious disappointment. "Don't be so hasty, son. Wrestling ain't exactly what one would consider to be the most kind of professions. You gotta understand that it's a world of pain and misery before it's a world of glamour and fun. It's a business. People use other people's success to fill their own coffers, people get hurt, people might even die because of this profession. You're young. You still have time to decide what you wanna do." Earl said. He wasn't normally the one to mince words, but he thought he'd try to explain himself. Simon Knox didn't get it. "I don't get it." he said. Somehow, Earl Knox knew that he wouldn't. He stood up and looked down at his son, before sighing to himself. "You're too young. I can't train ya." Earl said, flatly. "But... I want you to train me." Simon said. There was a hint of wavering sadness in his voice, as if his boat had been rocked, people were jumping overboard, and then a god damn Leviathan (not Batista) came out of the water and ate the boat, "You, and nobody else." Earl had been a wrestler for a very long time. He had many, many fans over the years. He would meet, greet, shake the hands of, and sign something for his fans, and he did this for more than a few years. He'd met many impassioned fans who loved his work, be it his wrestling or his promos. However, what his son said did strike at him deeply, like a spear being lobbed into his heart by a tribal spearman out in the jungle. Earl paused, then he knelt down in front of his son again. "Tell ya what," Earl said, already deeply regretting what he was about to say, "When I get back from filmin' this movie, I'll start the ground work for trainin' you. It ain't gonna be easy, my boy. I figure that after a week, you might give up." "No, I won't!" Simon argued. He was shaking in nervousness and despair, the idea that he wouldn't be able to start immediately hurt him on the inside, and the effects were starting to be seen visibly on the outside. Earl chuckled, "We'll see, Simon." He stood up, and believing that he was done here, started to walk back towards the kitchen to get a drink, and then he might shower and settle into a good book. "You promise, right?" Simon called out. This caused Earl to stop, pause, and then turn his head towards his son. "Yeah. I promise." Earl said. He then continued his walk, and eventually disappeared behind the door that would eventually take Earl to the kitchen. Simon, shaking from both disappointment at being rejected from immediate training and overall nervousness from having this talk, couldn't really do much except stand there like a deer caught in headlights. Simon's father made him a promise. Unfortunately, it was a promise he was destined to be unable to keep.
