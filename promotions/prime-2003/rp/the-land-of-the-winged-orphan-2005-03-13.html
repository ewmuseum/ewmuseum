The Land of The Winged Orphan

13 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 13th, 2005 The Land of The Winged OrphanMarch 13th, 2005Springfield, Illinois This shade of grayThis certain sadnessThis cold morning lightThis silent madnessIt hangs in the airIt hangs like a memoryIt hangs like a cloudIt hangs on desperatelyBetween dark and lightBetween was and beBetween young and oldBetween me, myself and.. I. The doctor had come to realize that he was Ozric's one and only friend while clean. He also figured out, through time, that he was, to Ozric, just another sobbing citizen spitting in his face and in his way, while high. - The Psychiatrist - A hub of government action, business activity, arts and cultural affairs, Springfield's beauty went beyond the velvet rope of parks, recreational activities and sunshine. It went beyond the corner parked hot-dog stands and devoted citizens that fancied walking to work rather than jumping into the proverbial automobile and being sent through a flurry of traffic that would stab you with a jittery, horn-punching blur of anguish. This city's beauty was commerce and the color 'green'. This city's beauty included the stir-crazy cab drivers, the homeless who continuously walked the incoherant pathway of wretch and the towering skyscrapers that seemingly acted like guardians throughout the skies. Banks and extremely expensive clothing stores impulsively took over most of Springfield's inner-city which granted the drug-dealers and criminals a one-way ticket to anywhere but here. This, also, turned out to be where Jack Reginald Klingman made his final stop. After most of New Jersey learned that he was the heartbeat in the act of letting most of the psychotically enhanced individuals loose into the real world, they threw him out in the middle of the road with his luggage. They let rumors of his death fly into the open air where they caught onto a gullible wing of media who truly believed the infamous doctor had mysteriously died which, in return, gave newspapers and news reports galore an interesting story to break some of the moldy, tasteless gloop they were normally accustomed to posting. In actuality, the doctor had never died. Within each step he walked, the more gasps for air he would take into his lungs. The doctor had walked for hours on the same blunt stretch of road that the sun seemed quite attracted to. After three dangerous encounters of hitchhiking, the doctor had made it to Illinois without even noticing. It took three different signs and a traditionally deep-dish pizza restaurant to convince him that he was so far away. It felt wonderful, though. Becoming part of a town that had more businesses than civilization could comprehend. There were markets for absolutely everything and anything in Springfield. Surprisingly so, it wasn't much different than Chicago. It only took the doctor two and a half weeks to buy out an office and begin classes. If he wasn't working, he wasn't living. What's life without the suspense? What's life without the drama? What matters is not how much you use the drug, but how much you exploit it. It all felt normal to Jack. The desk, the paper, the notepad, the open discussion, the questions, the answers. It almost felt as if he was still in his stuffy 'Jersey condominium, listening to Enigma as he watched the television with the sound turned down. Washing his worries away with a glass of scotch every night and putting his own words in the actor's mouths by using the music to fill the void in his imagination. Mentally, everything would tend to black out and burn to a crisp as his job involved listening and writing; nothing else. Drugs, alcohol, schizophrenia, marriage problems. Drugs, alcohol, schizophrenia, marriage problems. Drugs, alcohol, schizophrenia, marriage problems. It would slowly start to feel like a broken record and right before the process of emotionally cracking would sink its teeth in, a day-off from work would arrive. Though his schedule was getting infrequently busy with the usual suspects of counselling, the doctor insisted on pulling Ozric back into his embrace, free of charge. This would be the fifth installment of counselling-sessions for Ozric and the doctor was becoming more and more pessimistic of it all. Though he kept it hidden from the drug-sicken wrestler, a look of displeasure seemingly never left his face. Jack would never be able to look at him the same again. For he disobeyed everything he taught him in their classes and lost Jack's trust completely. For some strangely mis-informed reason, the doctor refused to let up in saving what was left of this man's life. - The Addict - The coagulated voice deep within himself wasn't enough to sieze the antics he suddenly started to live by. It didn't serve as a shock to Ozric. It was as if he intended on overdosing on the drug. As if this was to be the natural cause of his inevitable death. Young and groggy with angst that touched the pit of his stomach and bubbled throughout his body, Ozric couldn't detail how last week's ReVolution went. It was an absolute blur and for what he gathered from those around him, it was absolutely life-threatening to go out there and wrestle, just three days before he nearly died from his drug habits. Being a freshman inside this style of entertainment, he was unsure on how friendships and trust would go. It wasn't until he noticed this week's card that everything started to become crystal clear to him. Friendships were useless at this point and he had no need for them. Hell, even drugs turned their back on him this time around. Even associates were far and wide in detection. So where would he go from here? Back to an emergency room where he can be revived time after time again until his heart finally gives up? Give up the hard-drugs and settle for alcohol? Drive out of town and re-begin his life? There's absolutely no reasonable option that could turn his life around for the better in the process. He felt lost in a haze of misdirection. Locked away behind a steel curtain of depression that seemed to never budge. It world felt so God-damn unfair to him and that was the thing, the world wasn't being unfair to Ozric.. Ozric wasn't being unfair to the world. 'You're such a loser. You absolutely suck at life. What are you waiting for? Just pop a couple more and that's it! All of those problems and stressful bullshit creating a storm cloud over your head will finally disapear.' Ozric shouted towards the vanity mirror inside the Meridian Hotel suite he was staying in for the night. He could envision small phrases on the mirror, written in blotchy black lipstick; 'Give up', 'Eternal loser', 'Freak'. For this meager amount of time Ozric felt like an artist. He could see snow-capped mountains concealing the ridicule and fireworks of hope and happiness bursting into the sky. A mental piece of history had been displayed in front of his eyes and the only scary part about it all was that... He wasn't on drugs. The mental machine that lie inside the cranium of this clown had been working at full speed and it showed no signs of slowing down. Sure, the drugs were gone for the moment and he could finally think clearly, but everything else wasn't. He could've sworn last week's match was the biggest of his career. He could've sworn that everything was to be put on hold after he won this match. His concentration had seemingly been foiled by Hin See once again as he was thrown into the squared circle against his former stablemate, Emilio Rage. The future was moreso an arcane oracle. Impossible with spasmodical failure. Built like a fortress without an entrance, nor an exit. He'd rather continuously cough up bulky torrents of blood than take on a friend, no matter how former their friendship may have been. They were an alliance together and fought side-by-side to defend the better half of what PRIME stands by. So, what exactly happened? Ozric never intended on understanding Emilio's motives and ended up drugging himself to a blur the night after the carnage occured. The only thing he could remember doing was staring at his hand, which transformed into a gestural abstraction that slowly altered to his uppermost immersions. Needless to say, his hand turned into a giant catfish that sang Gene Kelley songs from 'Singin' in the Rain'. He had nothing but the best things to say about Rage, for he literally saved him from what Furher was slowly morphing into, a wagon-pushing vedge-head. It was 'The Enlightenment' that slowly created protection for him; a second skin, per say. This developmental plan of being an opposition against the forces within Dark Age lifted Ozric up to the highest he's ever been mentally. He felt at ease with himself and stronger than ever. He walked into each show with one intention finger-painted in curvelengths along the walls of his cerebrum: 'Dark Age'. All he wanted to see was Dark Age crumble and all he wanted to be was the man who did it. Unfortunately, the best things in life are earned. Apparently, Ozric hadn't reached that part of the obstacle course before the opposition he was involved in permanently closed its doors. - The Obstacles - 'So glad to see you, my friend.' Dr. Klingman said through inflamed adnoids. He could almost be taken as Bugs Bunny; if Bugs Bunny was a doctor who studied psychosomatic illnesses and drug theory. Was bald with hairy arms and wore bi-focals, of course. 'Likewise, doc. It's been a while...' Ozric said as he shook the doctor's hand and walked past him. The room had an aroma of cheap cologne and an incense of Strawberry-odored airspray that was slowly hitting the side of the room Ozric was standing in. It all seemed too new to him, as the furniture was of a different texture and a different color. There was two lamps struggling to ignite the extent of the room and Dr. Klingman's desk was chipped and stained in so many different places that it was rather annoying to count. An open, foam container lie in the middle of the desk with grains of rice and small pieces of pork scattered around it. 'Chinese food?' asked Ozric as he walked over to the asian cuisine and sniffed the spice of Kung Pow Chicken which nearly sent Ozric over the chair behind him and onto his back. Both of his eyes starting to tear as an uproar of coughs came out of him. 'Give me some time. I've only been 'back on the job' for two weeks. Finally getting some clients and working out a few sessions here and there.' the psychiatrist explained as he pointed towards the couch. Ozric turned his head and nodded as he walked over and sat down, pulling his black windbreaker coat off and rested it on the coffeetable in front of him. 'So where do we start? I mean, I'm still in shock to see you actually taking me in again. After the fuck-up I pulled with the drugs and all...' Ozric articulated as he rested his head against the back of the couch; closing his eyes and inhaling heavily. He couldn't believe he was back to 'square one': Sitting in front of a shrink, about to tell his life-story, or lack there of. He felt comfortable though, as this was one event much too common to him. He was satisfied with spilling his guts to the doctor, yet, he still felt the need to be back inside the ring. He couldn't believe the two bandages on his forehead and the black and blue eye were courtesy of that backstabbing motherfucker, Dani Furher. He was no threat and Ozric was quite irrefutable on this. At one point in his tenure at PRIME, Ozric was able to say Emilio and him had eachother's backs. Now? It wasn't as believable a story. 'Before we go on, I'd like to forewarn you the only medication you shall recieve out of me is verbal treatment. This is your last chance with me...' explained the doctor as Ozric nodded. He was deeply distracted by something else. Worries? Most likely. Failure seemed to crawl on both of Ozric's shoulders so easily on so many different occasions that it was tough for him to commit to what the doctor was leaning towards. Then again, when has Ozric committed to anything? Half of the matches he's wrestled in he can't seem to remember the outcome of and he's been on such a heavy guard pattern of defense against The Sentinels that it's been tough to keep pressing forward. Come to think of it, 'reverse' had become Ozric's favorite point of direction. The berate of destruction seemed to compress much easier this way. 'I don't think I can agree to any of that right now, Doctor. I'm a recovering drug addict trying get past this phase in my life.' Ozric said as the doctor shook his head whilst laughing. 'This isn't a phase, Oz. This is a mental and emotional illness. You are addicted to methamphetamines. I handed myself the job of healing the wounds and furthermore cleansing you...' Ozric nodded and closed his eyes, grinning to himself. 'Like the wingless orphan...' Ozric stated as he glided his fingers through the strands of black hair that nearly hid his whole face. The doctor placed his notes down on the coffee table and gave Ozric a painful look of perplextion. 'The wingless orphan. I'm afraid to ask...' the Doctor said as he fixed his glasses and took a sip of his lemon-flavored tea. 'I suppose it is my job, and I suppose I have to hear this one? the doctor asked as Ozric nodded his head. The doctor sighed and crossed his legs. 'Go on then...' 'Have you ever had a feeling of euphoria? Where you can turn a drop of water in your palm into an ocean? Where you actually feel the swaying of waves against the sheath of skin on your hand? Where you can hear waves brushing against rocks and boat docks on a stormy night?' Ozric asked as he paused for an answer. The doctor looked up and nodded his head and pulled his glasses off. Truth being, he had felt the exact thing for the past couple of weeks right before he'd go to sleep. The doctor nodded his head again; his mouth half-open. 'Well, imagine that along with this ample piece of land that stretched for miles and was completely abandoned. Imagine being on this piece of land with the sky smiling at you, the water singing towards you and the most phenominal painting coming to life in front of your eyes. For that split moment in my life, the world seemed very small and very fragile. The world morphed into a sea of tranquility; and I was floating amongst it..' - The Vital Painting - There wasn't any form of calamity between Emilio and Mortimer. If anything, it was more of a blessing to him. This was to be a remarkable outing for the both of them in every aspect of the mind. This was going to be war without the pre-match and post-match beatings. Doll parts lay scattered amongst my bare feet. The sky was the color of Mercury The sun wasn't visible to the human eye. The water surrounding the doll-limbed infested radius of land was transpicuous and absolutely dazzling. Heaps of waves curled over and bashed into the shore as half the island was being battered with terrential downpours of rain. The other half had a thermogenic effect to it. With an apricot sun attacking the unpaved ground. Ozric's right hand gripped tightly to a woman's left hand as they both looked at the world that physically looked miserable, but mentally felt like a world of pure serenity. Curiosity struck both of them at the same time as they plowed their feet through the black sand. 'Mother, is this hell?' questioned Ozric seemed to be much younger than now. The woman, who was evidently Ozric's mother, looked down and placed her index finger against her lips, doing her best in an act to keep Ozric calm. They continued to walk as they noticed fish flapping on the ground, straining for air. A mountain was crumbling to the right of them and a massive tornado was forming in the middle of the body of water surrounding the island they were occupying. 'Not yet, Ozric. Not yet...' answered the mother as she noticed a flim-like wall that covered a large fraction of the island. Turning her head, she noticed that it stretched for miles. She then quickly shifted her head to the right only to find a gold-painted lamp; the bulb obviously shattered. She could see a portable television lying at the edge of the shore, being destroyed by the waves. She coughed back her tears and whimpered as she turned her head, trying her hardest to make sure Ozric didn't know she was crying. 'This is our house. This is our neighborhood. This is our life. What did you son-of-a-bitches do to our home?!' the mother screamed as she fell to her knees. As his mother lost complete control of herself, Ozric became incredibly interested. Was this some sort of catastrophic storm that was slowly ending civilization? When did it start and where was he when it all unfolded? He felt like he was in a science fiction thriller as he reached the curtain that blinded the other side of the island. The doctor continued to listen to Ozric's dream as perspiration started to fall from his forehead, back and chest like bullets. He couldn't resist listening to this, no matter how fictional it seemed to him and no matter how terribly frightened it made him. He couldn't stop wondering whether this was all just some sick dimension in Ozric's mind or if this disaster happened when Ozric was just a little child. Not to mention the fact that he felt completely honored to be in the presence of a man with such an amazing figment of mind. Through all of the damnation taking place inside of him and through the cerebreum battle that never seemed to end and through an emotionally twisted drug-overdose, this man was to report in Anaheim to take on someone he thought of as a friend and a comrade in the company. The doctor seemed to tear himself away from all that was real only to listen to the tales spitting out of Ozric's mouth like a spree of bullets exiting an automatic-rifle. A young Ozric suddenly grew into that of an adult as he stared at the wall in front of him. Reaching out to touch it, he heard the shriek of his mother which alerted him enough to turn around for a split second only to turn back around and encounter the presence of the winged orphan. Flying just a few feet above Ozric, the 'winged orphan' looked down at him, flapping her wings and smiling. 'Why so blue, young lad?' the orphan said as she let herself float down to the ground. Walking up to Ozric, she placed her hands on his shoulders. 'Who.. what... what are you?' asked Ozric who couldn't understand any of this. Even if it actually made any kind of sense, Ozric wouldn't dare try and put some kind of density to it. The orphan giggled as the curtain dropped, showing the better half of the island. The sunshine blared beautifully and the rain had disapeared. 'I am here to guide you to happiness, Ozric. You are a strong, brave and bold man and I am here to support you. Do you understand?' the orphan asked as she walked away from Ozric and entered the world. 'No.. I don't, actually.' answered Mortimer who turned his head and noticed his mother straining to get her son's attention. 'That's good. The best things in life come out of nowhere and you shouldn't have to know or understand it. Let this be a lesson for every obstacle you encounter. Understand?' Ozric nodded as the orphan grabbed Ozric's hand and brought him into the better half. It was quite evident that Ozric never knew his mother through past history. He barely knew his father, either. Family wasn't a stronghold in Ozric's life and he accepted it like it was anything and everything. Obstacles are molded out for each life that is created. Ozric's job was to climb over them and journey onward. ..Up until now, he never understood that. Gripping with trembling hands, Ozric held a photo in front of the doctor's face. For some reason, the doctor couldn't look away as Ozric handed it to him. It was a piece of art that lies in The Louvre; an incredibly famous museum. The photo showed, in perfect detail, the two divisions of an island. An celestial being flying high above the divided-island with hands clasped together. Its eyes closed as one half of the land lie in splinters of broken doll limbs and the other half lie in perfect wonderment. Dysforia took on euphoria in the fashion of a poetic piece of art.. ..And it was the vignette of an unbalanced clown. 'My God, Ozric...' the doctor commented as he placed his hand against his chest. Wiping away tears that jammed up at his lids, the doctor couldn't swallow it all. For everything sentimentally meaningful in that piece of art suppressed Ozric's yesteryear. The doctor felt selfish for holding Ozric up like a rat in a cage. Always convinced that Ozric was risking his life everytime he went in that ring, the doctor finally punched a hole through his ignorance and realized that he was breathing much easier in this bussiness. Not only did Mortimer mean something to PRIME fans, but he meant something to management and for that, Ozric was no long afraid to express himself. If anything, he was gathering people that understood his difficulties and people who shared the same hardships. 'Doctor, I've come to realize that you don't agree with what I do for a living. What you don't concieve is the fact that this is what's keeping me alive. I grow to be stronger mentally and physically through each match that I'm in and I become human once again. If I don't have this to restore myself with, I may as well just jump back into the hole I was burying myself in and keep up with the drugs...' The doctor noticed they were an hour over their session time but decided not to barge into Ozric with it. This was one of the most paramount classes they've ever had together. For once, Ozric was flushing out his own internal mess and the doctor appreciated it. He had written three sentences down on paper before coming to the conclusion that some things are best left to be recorded mentally, for this was all as much a dream as it was a reality to Jack Klingman. 'I figure my life was constructed through problems and stress. Why leave all of that behind now? I wouldn't of been born had that not been in play at the time. Wrestling is stressful, painful and can latch on to you emotionally. I've had all of these done to me numerous times and I'm not about to have it all go to waste. I'm going to Anaheim and I'm going in that ring to be the best I can. To many, tomorrow is just another day in their lives. To me, tomorrow could be the last. It's time to cut the uptight bullshit of living life to a limit. Hop like a bunny on the edge of the Grand Canyon, bungee-jump at Niagara Falls... Sieze the motherfucking day! We're only here for the moment and who knows how much longer?' The doctor stood up and clapped his hands. He shook his head as he finally broke down in a neverending series of emotions. He couldn't believe what he was heard come out of Ozric's mouth. Hope. Buoyancy. Promise. A utopia of dependence. 'Carpe Diem, Ozric.' the doctor said he embraced Ozric with a hug. 'Carpe Diem, Jack.' Ozric followed up as he put both of his arms around the psychiatrist as the scene faded to iminent caliginosity. The final key struck a chord deep inside of Ozric. It was plain and simple. Clear and absolutely fantastic. A brilliant expression of pride formed on Ozric's face as he promised himself that Arrowhead Pond would never look the same after this week... I had so much to sayBut no words to speakThe feelings were strongBut I was only weak So there we were againTragic and absurdChoking on every lineAnd every final word A few pages from an old diaryA dusty shelf, an unread historyAll these words and memoriesAre all of you that's really left for me.. Good luck Ryan. It was blast writing against you, man.
