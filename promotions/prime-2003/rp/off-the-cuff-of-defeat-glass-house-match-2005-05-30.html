Off The Cuff of Defeat (Glass House match)

30 May 2005

Date: May 30th The sight of Amy Campbell that evening was unforgettable. She was dressed brilliantly in a scarlett blouse that covered all but the tiny tattoo on her throat. Plain black slacks backed up the all-too proper sentiment of the outfit, which was topped in its formality by her tightly tied hair - locks that were used to falling around her ears and shoulders were trapped by an elastic ponytailer, only the strands fringing her face able to wriggle free. It wasn't the outfit that made her image burn into the minds of any who knew her, though - the broken, defeated expression she wore atop that formal garb when she walked back into her apartment that evening shattered any semblance of contentment or beauty in the minds of those who saw her. Staring at her normally bright and beautiful features, you couldn't have appreciated her perfect face if you wanted to - you'd feel guilty thinking any positive or happy thought anywhere near the woman that night. Her hips swayed from one side to the other as she hurriedly prowled to her bedroom to shed the formal wear in favor of a more characteristic pair of jeans and a pressed grey tanktop. While she rubbed her scalp, sore from being constricted that entire afternoon, Amy fell into one of the cushioned wicker roundchairs she'd only recently adorned her environment with. Completely detached from the earth and truthfully a little buzzed from the wine at the reception she'd just attended, the little redhead stood again with little hesitation when she realized that the key ingredient to such a heavy session of brooding was missing; There wasn't a drop of alcohol in sight! That had to be remedied, and was when she limped to the kitchen cupboards and revealed a stash of bottles only Paris Hilton would have the room to keep. With a plastic party cup, she began a vodka tonic... extra vodka, of course. The drink ended up being... well... just vodka, but that was fine by Amy's standards. One sip and she nearly gagged, but built up her tastebuds' defenses for the next swig of the liquor. With an agonizing shake of her head, she dealt well with the drink's assault on her senses and finished half the glass with a grin on her face, knowing that her painkillers would soon kick in alongside a nicer buzz. Instead of spending the week visiting with her friend Dave Gibson, Amy opted to stay at home and sulk alone. She'd given up on drugs, assuming that they were the cause of her overt confidence at Overkill... the cockiness that cost her two falls against Karina Wolfenden, and found her with a heart broken worse than it had ever been. The spirit that everyone had been talking about had been stifled - she was no longer 'up and coming', simply beaten, and couldn't stand the thought of stepping into a ring again in only a few days. What if she couldn't avoid Karina like last week? How could she face her peers, let alone the enemies she'd need to one-up for the Intense title? It seemed like a worthless battle to undertake, but it had to be done. Without PRIME, there was no paycheque... especially with fWo cracking down on her for 'rumored drug use'. Flashes of her past were hitting her randomly - drinks to mix, floors to sweep and poles to dance on. Things that shouldn't have been coming back were, and it had all started with that sick little overdose the week before. She'd become reflective, and unintentionally so... it was a waking nightmare. Speaking of which... 'I should've done more, gone farther. I never do enough, and that's why I lose out...' The day she'd gotten home from Overkill, the world knew Amy Campbell was injured. All the wrong people knew Amy Campbell was injured. Her neck was weak and her body was numb, it made for the perfect opportunity of revenge if anyone sought as much... and Adrian, her ex-boyfriend and constant plague, always looked out for it. He'd come over that night, excusing the visit as collection of money she owed him, but things hadn't stopped when she gave him the measley fifteen dollars - they only got worse when she relented the sum. He'd kicked out her knee and managed to wrangle her down to the couch, but soon found his head going through one of her livingroom end-tables. Her rage surmounted, and the redhead couldn't stop beating him - throwing him against the wall and kicking his ribs in with a fevor he'd never imagined could boil within her. Everything Amy had felt in defeat came out in that glorious self-defense. It was no-holds barred, and Adrian ended up stumbling out with a broken nose and ribs among other scrapes, bruises and a concussion. Despite putting up a splendid fight against Karina and then Adrian, this wasn't the confident and self-destructive girl that anybody was used to. Instead of pride, you found a certain shame and embarassment that she wasn't able to follow up on everything she'd ever said or believed in when it came to fighting - her livelihood. This wasn't Amy, but a puppy whimpering and licking its wounds from a tussle gone awry within the litter. She went at Karina with everything there was within her - all of the drugs, all of the hurt - and had come out the loser in two straight falls. The LOSER. It was plainly unacceptable, and nearly impossible to swallow down when she thought of returning to work that week. If she saw Karina, Amy wasn't entirely sure of what she'd do... or if she'd have a job afterwords. It was such a tippy fight and an uneven decision that she couldn't help but think that a little more practice and tuning into her body would beat the K-Wolf easily. 'Just a LITTLE more effort,' she thought, 'An extra few seconds. That's all I needed...' She'd just returned from her parents' anniversary, having narrowly escaped the small talk and ''It's okay, wrestling's just a work anyway''s that were swimming quickly her way, but even that small accomplishment barely made a difference in her mood. The only things helping at all were the continuous sips of her drink, warming her chest before adding to the alcoholic high she'd become trapped in. Scraping and scraping, nothing was going to bring back that win. Nothing would make up for the loss, no matter how hard she wished it away. There had to come a time to give up... Chapter One: Love is a Rhythmlast night...They weren't quite sure if she was dancing to live or living to forget... The sight of Amy Campbell that night was unforgettable. Her mind was out of the world as she knew it. Swaying in the middle of a crowd that made Aimz at her worst look like a nun, she was gliding in between people she'd never met before as others watched on from the sidelines, not nearly as drugged as the mass on the dancefloor. Weaving by shoulder after shoulder, Amy was entirely alone in that sea of bodies... She was the only one who appeared sober, for one thing. It was needless to say that the rave scene wasn't entirely familliar with wrestling or its stars, and that hall was one of a very few places Campbell could disappear - she was nothing more than a face prettier than the usual there. That's what she'd found solace in, letting the rhythm heal her body while she wound her waistline to the bass line, sober as the day she was born... but freer than any high had made her. It was curious, and piqued the interest of the few people at the party who did recognize her. They'd known Amy as brash, angry and never without bloodshot eyes; This woman was entirely unknown to them. She looked healthy, despite a few obviously tender joints that bent with a gentler motion than their counterparts, and she appeared to be entirely happy. So much so, in fact, that they'd had a few minutes of debate as to whether or not the redhead they beheld truly WAS the fighter they'd seen a mere week earlier on their flatscreens. They weren't at all sure until the beat stopped its relentless assault on her body and, free of the hardhouse track that had bound her for a solid thirteen minutes prior, Amy left the polished floor to refresh her senses. ''Water,'' was the extent of her dialogue that night, at least before she corrected herself to the bartender who'd eyed her ages ago, ''Err...what's that new stuff? It's flavored or something... the citrus stuff, that. I'll have that - the fruity water.'' ''You sure?'' ''What,'' she cocked an eyebrow jokingly, ''Is it poisoned?'' ''No, but...'' ''Yeah yeah, let's just have the water. I'll save getting loaded for home.'' ''Whatever you say.'' It couldn't be Amy Campbell. She was smiling... As she stood at the bar and gazed around the room, though, it became apparent that every facial feature matched the wrestler... right down to her mismatched brown and blue eyes. What, then, had suddenly possessed her? The eyes she knew were on her, that's what. Not those few fans or the drunken party-goers, but the man she knew frequented the place every Tuesday night - always perfectly sober and looking for a new trend to photograph. She hadn't even looked up to the balcony seating to see him there - he just was - sitting at the same little table maybe with a martini or, failing that, iced coffee drink. Amy was a great actor, and it was meant to be indistinguishable tonight. After seeing her loss, she'd known that he'd worry for her breaking down. She didn't want back into things, but she did want him to have the impression that she was okay - she didn't need his help or worry - so he could exist content. She hadn't, however, intended for him to leave his position on the club's upper level... ''Now that's something I'm not used to seeing.'' That's what you get for assuming he was up there. She turned around quickly, shocked at first, but met his eyes with a staggering smile. Remember... act happy. ''You used to hate coming here,'' he said, ''You'd say 'Bah! The only thing worth anything there is the booze'.'' He was laughing, having picked up on her flighty sense of glee by simply watching. But the cocked eyebrow and hesitation in his smile only served to remind her that he hadn't forgotten, and wasn't entirely convinced by this sudden chipper version of who she was. He remembered everything all too well. ''And I'm kind of surprised you haven't sworn at me yet...'' Amy met him halfway with a grin, ''What can I say? Everybody's here for a good time, and I'm having one.'' Something had happened in that moment, something neither were sure of. For whatever reason, they began a walk in-sync back toward the table Amy'd known he'd be at. Maybe it was the past, maybe it was the atmosphere... maybe it was just Jeph. Chapter Three:Real-timethere, in her reality...Two tablets helped her system wind down. She'd thrown up already, knowing it was the result of these painkillers cocktailed with her favorite drink, but it wouldn't stop her. Right now, with all of her bones and joints seemingly dangling from the same frayed thread, Amy needed those painkillers. The problem wasn't the drug, but the method she didn't see as being nearly as harmful as it was in reality - the mixture of those medicines with alcohol. The buzz was nice, the sickness wasn't... and the addiction she didn't even clue into was worse. Every few minutes, she'd build herself up and pull through one of many moodswings that straying from 'real drugs' was throwing her way, but then the tiny redhead would unknowingly self-medicate - replacing her first addiction with one that was a little more legal. She didn't care what Jeph had tried to pull, or how disastrous the prior night had been. He was far too ignorant to real pain and the needs of those who experience it. That's right, a day in her shoes would kill a lesser person... Chapter Four: Wrong Turnlast night, cont'd...Two hours had passed. Amy'd only drank water. Jeph didn't drink anything, and they were already coming through the door of an apartment they'd shared not three months before. They were smiling, laughing and unusually close to one another in a physical sense. Her fingers ran down his arm when she laughed, and he unknowingly backed her into the wall while stroking a strand of hair behind her ear. Neither were aware of their actions - of acting as if they were together again - but the body language was a clear indication that they truly wished it to be. Unfortunately, Amy wasn't exactly receptive to the idea of their union, and hadn't even intended for this much to happen. She didn't yet see that she was dragging him back in... Their little dance continued when the door shut. They were taking playful jabs at one another regarding any number of things from her drinking water that night, to his pants being tighter than he normally wore them. It was a conversation of fragments, but these two knew one another too well to need full words and meaning. It stopped short, however, when he leaned in for a kiss he'd been waiting for months to feel again. That small touch woke everything within Amy up, and the hair's on the back of her neck stood at full attention as she regained her senses enough to resist and put pressure against Jeph's chest to keep him at bay. ''Shit!'' she growled and, when her personal space was regained, nearly flew across the living room to the open kitchen. As Jeph watched on, the little redhead went right back to hastily pouring herself a glass of straight vodka - using the liquid to slip two white tablets down her throat. Howe, meanwhile, had lightly slammed the back of his head against the wall in a frustration he hadn't experienced since their first breakup. Instead of questioning her motion away from him, he responded to those sips of vodka without a hint of subtlety to hide the sarcasm to his tone. ''I didn't know Bacardi had started selling water,'' he sighed, ''Or that you were still such a slave. That helps you fight too, right?'' Amy's fist met the counter in an uncompromising slam, ''For fuck's sake, Jeph. You're not even supposed to be h---shit.'' ''No no,'' Jeph cocked his head, ''Go on...'' ''You...'' she took another mouthfull down, ''I was supposed to convince you I was okay. It was supposed to end there, right? I was supposed to leave you back at the club, thinking everything was fine with me. You were supposed to feel good...'' She collapsed her upper body onto the counter, holding her head in her hands and staring plainly ahead at nothing. ''...Not like this...'' You were supposed to raise him up, let him feel good knowing YOU are - not drag him back to it... ''...Nothing like this,'' finished the broken redhead. His heart sank for her as Jeph watched the woman he loved crumble into a thousand tiny shards. Physically, mentally... she was just out. That one fight - that one person had taken so much from her. To Karina Wolfenden, that match was just another win but to Aimz, it was everything she'd ever promised herself that she could do. It was her proving to the world that she could really do what she claimed... In the end, it was her falling just short of all of those things. Chapter Five: Reflect on Direction If I fucked up in trying to assure just Jeph, I'll fail miserably with my parents and close friends. Nobody's gonna buy that I'm okay, that I'm perfectly alive after all of that... it doesn't matter how hard I dance or smile, it just won't go over. If I thought anything was going to be easy from here on in, I was really fucking horribly wrong. Everything's just become harder since losing to her, I don't even feel like I've got a purpose in wrestling of all things. There's a match this week, there was Brandon Youngblood verbally tearing me limb from limb last week, and eventually there'll be a rematch against... yeah, a second chance to beat her into a pulp and still come up just short of winning because of my stupid fucking ego. If it wasn't for that last line before the match, or if I'd spent an extra minute stretching... it was so close, I could have had it. I could've had it. ME. I could've actually followed up on a promise. I would've been their dream. Now I'm just some crestfallen bitch. If they even keep buying my t-shirts, I'll be surprised. If I can muster up the willpower to throw a single punch, I'll be surprised. I've been sick since it happened, and my stomach's only getting worse as this week approaches. I can't avoid her forever, and I know that... but fuck me gently, I don't want to see her. I can't face that. Even if I mutilated her on sight, it wouldn't make it better. Looking into that woman's eyes just once will destroy me, because now she's sure that she's some kind of superior to me. I know it, she knows it... meeting would only solidify that one dreadful thought that she might be better. She isn't, but now she's convinced of it - now it'll only be harder for me to prove that notion wrong. I've gone down once. Why am I even bothering to try making people feel better about this? Everybody's convinced I'm going to hit a low, maybe try to kill myself off... they probably aren't wrong, but I hit that low two weeks ago - and forget about suicide, that only says 'Hey, I was too weak. Deal with it.'. I can't afford to fail this week's challenge, but I've got little doubt that I will. People in glass houses aren't supposed to throw stones, but that's all I've done in the past months. If that thing doesn't shatter above me, I know I'll end up going through its roof. My luck took me over the likes of Trashcan Man once; Hell, I even handed Angelo Deville his first loss here... but if I had ANY luck, it left me when Karina took that first fall. I can't walk into this match or even the BUILDING with my head held high - or even level with others. It'll just hang there and get fed defeat once more. That's the way things go for me - little highs, huge comedowns. I overdosed on my own pride, and I'm still choking on it - trying to get those last pieces free from my throat before I lose another match. Whoever Nick Brocken is, whoever Nova thinks he is... whoever this mystery opponent is... they all saw what happened, and they know what they're walking into when it comes to me. You can blow through me like a ghost, and I'm tempted to let you just to end the fucking struggle. The bigger a fight I put up, the bigger the letdown when someone else gets lucky. Why do I bother? I guess it's because this is literally all I have left. There's no husband anymore, no steady relationship. My adopted son lives with my real parents because I'm unfit to see to him. My past is a terror and even my drugs have left me. I can't stand tall above Karina Wolfenden, I got lucky with Angelo Deville and now I'll be another number underneath the Intense Title. Really, all I'm trying to do is hold fast to the life I have... if I can even manage that much. There's a purpose in here - deep down somewhere - that I need to find. Dearest little journal, this is my life. - Amy Chapter Six: BraveheartShe closed the tiny book with a thud and crawled from her seat on the flood all the way back to her feet. She stretched, not unlike she would at any point before that, and then tossed her journal back onto the bed's pillow. She turned the music low and stood staring out the window for one endless moment. ''It's all what you've made for yourself,'' she spoke in scolding to herself, ''Don't let some athletic trendwhore take that from you. Existance is predictably unpredictable, but you don't have to fall alongside the scoresheet.'' Amy Campbell's remaining purpose was to make the most of... well... whatever it was she actually had left. It was a waste of time to try convincing those around her that she was fine or anywhere near to being happy after a loss like the one she'd sustained, and her mistake with Jeph had said that much plainly. Karina had succeeded in making her entire life look like little more than a whimper veiled by pride, but all that meant was that Amy just had to try harder this time. Picking up a win at ReVolution would be a great start to that, if she was ready for it... Instead of taking another drink, the little redhead grabbed her militia coat and circled the address on a Nubody's Gym card. If she couldn't have a fresh start, she was going to force an upside into whatever it was she did have.
