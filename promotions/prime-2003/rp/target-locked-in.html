Date: 6 months ago Six months ago… Special Agent Finn Roberts picked the Polaroid up off the table and looked over at the man who’d called him in with a bemused look on his face. “Polaroid? They still make this shite?” FBI Special Advisory Agent and Unit Chief of the Organised Crimes Unit, Salvatore Bonanno, didn’t find any humour in the situation. He glared Finn’s gaze back down to the Polaroid in his hand. It was a picture of a rather fat man. On the verge of four hundred pounds. He sipped on some small, posh coffee cup with a Siamese cat in his lap; free hand resting on the cat’s contented head. “What am I lookin’ at, Chief?” “Lou ‘Kitten’ Coppano. Don of the infamous Coppano family. You heard of ‘em?” Bonanno’s gaze never wearied as he stared an inquisitive hole into the face of Special Agent Roberts. “Yeah. I heard of ‘em. ‘Specially heard of Kitten.” He lifted another Polaroid of the infamous Kitten Coppano, this time he held a Burmese under his right arm and pointed at something. Another man in expensive suit looked on as Kitten pointed in Polaroid. “Heard he’s a mean motherfucker.” “He is a mean motherfucker, Roberts. Meanest there is. I’m sure you’ve heard the stories.” Sal’s gaze never lapsed. The man could make a statue turn away. Roberts grabbed his Styrofoam cup of coffee and took a swig, swirling it in his mouth to whet his whistle before swallowing. “We here to talk ‘bout some big, bad fucken goomba?” “No, Roberts. Did you get the brief?” Sal picked up a bunch of paperwork and tapped their end on the table to neaten the pile. “I’m sure you’ve read it.” Roberts nodded. “I’ve read it. Wasn’t much to it, bein’ confidential and all.” “Can’t trust UPS, Finn. We’ve brought you in here coz we need a man to get in and under. A man who can afford to disappear off the face of the earth for a while.” Bonanno put the stack of neatened sheets back on his desk, resting on his elbows as he spoke. “How long is a while?” Roberts could tell there was no answer, but figured it was a question worth him asking. He figured he needed to understand how long he put his life on hold for. “Period of time cannot be known. But it’s all relative, Finn. We need the best and we got sent you.” Bonanno eyeballed Roberts. “That you?” “Yeah. That’s me. What’s the score?” Roberts pulled a pen out of his pocket and began to take notes on a yellow jotter. Chief Bonanno reached into his desk drawer and pulled out five more Polaroids, skidding them across the mahogany top perfectly to land right by Roberts’ hand. He picked up the first to see a brash, younger version of Kitten Coppano. Fit and healthy looking. Even the still emanated confidence. “Giancarlo Coppano. First born son of Kitten Coppano. Fast moving ranker in the Coppano Family. Moving from Soldato to a Capodecina within six months. Next in line as Consigliere. Next in line to be Don. We need you to get in close to Giancarlo.” Roberts looked down at the picture again, pulling it for closer inspection. Finn tucked it behind the other four to inspect the next photo in line. A picture of Giancarlo in a wrestling ring, clotheslining someone in the confines of what appeared to be a gymnasium. No big crowd or flashy photographers. Something Independent. Finn Roberts was familiar with wrestling. Had it not been for the shove into the family business, the federal agency, Roberts would have liked to try his luck going pro as a wrestler. “They say you’ve done some wrestling, Roberts.” Chief Bonanno was emotionless as he spoke. Finn found him to be one of the single most interesting persons he’d come across. A psychiatrist would have a field day with the Chief of the Organised Crimes Unit in the way he bottled up everything. Roberts nodded. “Aye. I’ve done a bit.” “Coppano Junior is in the midst of going pro. Several big-time promotions have identified him as a star of the future. Talent scouts and lawyers are throwing contracts at Giancarlo’s management trying to get him signed by the handful, Special Agent Roberts.” Bonanno plucked a handful of paper off of his pile and dropped them in front of Finn. “Why hasn’t he signed then?” Roberts looked over a contract from Global Championship Wrestling followed by another from All-Star Championship Wrestling. “If there’s so many scouts, what’s goin’ on there?” “Coppano’s management is co-operating due to a scheme they were runnin’ that got foiled by the IRS. Turns out they was shavin’ funds left, right and centre. They were very accommodating when we told them we could make this go away. ‘Specially since we seen Giancarlo’s name on their books. It’s mutually beneficial to everybody.” Bonanno let a flicker of a smile grace his lips. Even if it were subconscious, Finn could see the smile. He’d watched enough Lie to Me to pick up on this stuff. That and the training the Bureau had given him over the years. Roberts smiled at the idea of Bonanno fighting his grin. “So they’re holding out on Giancarlo?” “Exactly.” Chief Bonanno rose to his feet, grabbing a glass off his table and heading to the water cooler, stopping half-way. “They’re working on a deal with a company called PRIME… heard of ‘em?” Roberts nodded. “Sure. Who hasn’t? Not nothin’ I’d watch myself… I’m more of an Empire Pro fan.” “I don’t care what you do in your time, Roberts. I asked if you heard of ‘em.” Bonanno poured some water from the cooler into his glass. He drank half right away and topped up the glass. “I heard of ‘em.” Bonanno rounded the desk to his chair again, resting his glass down but not taking a seat. Instead he leant on the palms of his hands to speak to Roberts. “Finn. His management is working on a contract for a tag team.” Sal widened his eyes expectantly at Roberts, hoping he’d figure out the insinuation. “Ah. I get it. That’s where I fit in.” Roberts nodded, rifling through the rest of the Polaroids. Various shots of wrestling action and Giancarlo at the same restaurant Kitten drank the little coffee from. “You need someone on the inside to get close to Giancarlo. Work their way into the family through the son, right?” “Right.” Bonanno nodded, finally taking his seat. As he sat, all his weight expelled the air in the cushion making an awkward fart noise in the rush of ass. Roberts shook his head. “It’ll never work.” Bonanno double-taked at Finn’s response. “Why not?” “Coz I’m Irish. How is an Irishmen s’posed to infiltrate an Italian Mafioso?” Roberts threw his hands into the air, for extra melodrama. Bonanno let the smile cross his lips this time. He looked through a deck of Polaroids like a ten year-old with their baseball card collection. He found the one he wanted and tossed it across the desk like Gambit might have. It flew and hit Finn right in the chest, landing picture side down in front of him. “Who’s this?” Roberts asked, picking up the Polaroid, looking over the picture of the pale Irish-looking gentlemen in the leather jacket. Bonanno’s grin didn’t lapse this time. “The current Consigliere, Roberts. Keegan O’Doherty.” “An Irishmen,” Finn muttered under his breath, in almost a whisper. Bonanno repeated him, with smugness engulfing the words. “Fancy that. An Irishmen.” One week later… Italian Stallions reads the sign by the door. Wrestling school by the Italian Stallion Jimmy Riscitelli. Certain it was the place, Finn Roberts stepped through the threshold to be greeted by the stale stank of sweat and muscle ointments. There was no reception area. Just a hall of lockers for those who weren’t using the locker rooms. A sign read you could see Jimmy about renting one of those lockers for five dollars a month. There were framed wrestling pictures of who Finn presumed was Riscitelli. The words Italian Stallion emblazoned up and down the legs of the trunks were the give away. Finn figured the photos were taken in the late seventies due to the workmanship and style of the trunks. At the end of the hall it opened up into an area big enough to fit a wrestling ring and various exercise machines. Over on the far left was a trio of guys skipping rope. Someone shouted at them to go faster. Ordered them to do a criss-cross which one of them failed to do. Upon failure he was asked to drop and give me twenty. He delivered on the order. Far right corner were some guys who looked juiced up on anything that would make those muscles shine. They curled with dumbbells and watched each other, envious of the largest for lifting so much. Though it didn’t look as if he could lift with ease. Finn thought to himself the guy should lift a little lighter before he shat his pants. Throughout the gym the sound of a group of guys doing a bike class whizzed loudly. Yet the main hub-bub seemed to be coming from the wrestling ring. A group of nine or ten guys had gathered there, all looking up at who Finn presumed to be Jimmy Riscitelli. The man to his right had his back to him. Roberts shouldered his gym bag and strolled up to see what all the fuss was about. “…like I tells you, the only ways ya gunna get the experience is ta get in the ring. We promise he’ll go light on yas, yeah?” The old man patted the guy to his right on the shoulder. As Finn got closer, he could see the guy was on his cell, speaking to someone in hushed tones. “Who is gunna step up and get that valuable experience? Who got what it takes? Who gots the balls to face him?” The group of guys gathered about ringside all looked to one another. None of them seemed willing enough to get inside the ring with the guy. None of them looked particularly interested in being embarrassed in public, at least, that was the feeling that Roberts got. The guy finished up his conversation on the cell and folded it up, handing it to Riscitelli who pocket it in his breast pocket without batting an eyelid. The guy turned around with a big shit-eating grin strewn across his face and Roberts recognised him right away. It was the face he’d seen in the polaroids. It was his target. Giancarlo Coppano. Arrogantly, the man held his hands out as if asking the question. Who would step up to face him? Finn Roberts stepped through the crowd, pushing them aside with one arm and wriggling through. Eventually, they began to part and Roberts was ringside with his bag still over his shoulder. Riscitelli looked down at him, a little frustrated that an outsider would enter his gym. Roberts dropped his bag by his feet and looked Giancarlo in the eye, while answering Jimmy. “I will.” Roberts turned his gaze to Jimmy. “I’ll face him.” Riscitelli snorted, obviously unimpressed by the stature of Roberts. “Look, little man, wrestlin’s for us big kids, capische? You ain’t even a member here. Ya ain’t covered by my insurance if my friend here cripples ya.” Stuffing his hand into his pants pocket, Finn returned his gaze to Giancarlo, looking him dead in the eye. He retracted his hand and threw a handful of twenty dollar bills at the ring. Some of them landed inside. Some of them drifted, delicately like a leaf falling from a tree, landing ringside at Finn’s feet. “How much for a membership?” “Forget it, kid. This ain’t for you.” Riscitelli leant down and collected the notes that Finn had tossed in. Roberts didn’t move a muscle. “Let’s see, Jimmy,” said Giancarlo, with cocky written all over his face. “Let’s see what he got.” Riscitelli snapped to attention. He took Giancarlo aside and began whispering angrily at him. Finn picked up words like green and shark and stiff. Heard the phrase could be someone out to get you right before the pair of them turned and gave him the once over. Giancarlo turned around, looking down at Finn but responding to Jimmy, speaking out of the corner of his mouth. “It’s alright, Jimmy. I got this.” Finn smiled. Target locked on.
