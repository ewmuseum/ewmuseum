"Roots" Prologue: Irony

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Rev 142 “Jason Snow, eh?” Walter began, his smile beaming brightly for anybody and everybody within the immediate area to see. But Jonathan Winters could only note the last remnants of the man’s undigested lunch wedged between the numerous fissures his crooked teeth afforded and it made him more than a little queasy. Although, he reasoned, that could just as well be the current topic of conversation he’d somehow found himself caught up in. “Wouldn’t it be ironic if he had seven dwarves accompany him to the ring? You should definitely pitch him the gimmick…” Winters simply rolled his eyes and refused to respond to him, a tactic that had thus far been proven to be woefully ineffective. Walter just sustained his rambling regardless of the silence, throwing the odd of question his way every now and then, but mostly Winters figured he did it just to entertain himself. Despite the annoyance of the act itself, Winters found himself sympathising with Walter’s need for stimulation, no matter how meagre, after all, they had been sitting in the StakiriTech waiting room for the better part of forty minutes already and it was quite obvious both of them had already become a little stir crazy. “What are you going on about?” The receptionist suddenly questioned. The elderly lady lowered her spectacles a little and shot Walter a rather abrasive look. “Ignore him,” Winters chimed in, “his mouth is a full three minutes ahead of his brain.” “I wish I could ignore him!” She yelled, losing her cool completely. Less than a minute before complete breakdown and raised voices, probably a new record for Walter. He’d be bragging about this for days, not that Winters would indulge him. Walter’s eyes darted between them both in quick succession, a bizarre combination of shock and hurt spread across his face in equal portions. “What? What the hell did I say now?” “I’m trying to work over here and all I can hear is you, going on about somebody called Jason Snow and the irony of him owning seven dwarves.” She paused momentarily and purple-faced, gasping for oxygen. “What the hell is that even supposed to mean?” Winters held back a sly grin the best he could and could only hope Walter hadn’t noticed; he loved seeing the old trout flustered like this. She was of that age where she didn’t much care about manners anymore and absolutely redefined the adjective ‘battle-axe’ for a new age of pissed of elderly women looking for blood. “Ya know, like Snow White?” Walter replied. He didn’t even attempt to disguise the patronising undertones to his voice. “I know it’s probably been a long time since you’ve seen the movie, but correct me if I’m wrong, Snow White did have seven dwarves right?” The receptionist’s face contorted with confusion and she remained silent for the longest time, as Walter flashed her his usual moronic toothy grin. “And what does any of that have to do with this-this Jason Snow person?” Walter rolled his eyes and then shook his head. “Jason Snow and Snow White.” “So it’s because they both have the word ‘snow’ in their names?” The receptionist questions, rhetorically. “That connection’s loose at best, Walter.” Walter opened his mouth, eager to reply, but the receptionist denied him any such opportunity. “And even if the man did have dwarves for some incomprehensible reason, it certainly wouldn’t be ironic in any sense of the term!” This time it was Walter who developed a look of complete confusion. “Then what is it?” “Bizarre,” the receptionist promptly replied, “perhaps even warped, but definitely not ironic.” Walter did take time to digest the information, but clearly it wasn’t nearly long enough. However, as soon as he’d opened his mouth to further argue his imbecilic point, the StakiriTech lab doors smoothly slipped open interrupting the heated debate and their illustrious employer, Cornelius Stakiri, accompanied by a group of scientists Winters did not recognise, emerged mid-conversation and quite obviously in the throes of excitement. It took a while for Stakiri to acknowledge them completely, but when he did he shot them both the widest of grins. Winters couldn’t remember the last time he’d witnessed his employer this happy, but he knew it was long before Stakiri’s underworld gang war. It was understand. His nemesis OmniTech had won the last couple of rounds of the fight after all; it hit Stakiri hard, perhaps harder than anyone ever before, but it was about time for him to retaliate. Perhaps he was smiling because he’d found the means to do so…? “Excellent news,” he beamed, quickly diverting a hundred percent of his attention to the document held within the lead scientists hands. The lead scientist highlighted a number of points, of which Winters could hear nothing, and Stakiri nodded along with him. The other scientist, realising Stakiri’s didn’t plan on following up on his elusive comments, glanced up from her own paperwork and shot them both a smug self-satisfying grin of her own. “We need to run more tests, but it seems the patient’s previously lost memories are, well, returning.” “You mean Aeryn,” Winters corrected, not bothering to hide undertones of anger in his voice. “Well, yes, of course,” the scientist responded apologetically. “In my line of work it’s sometimes best not to make a personal connection with the patient. It can make things…complicated.” “Whatever,” Winters muttered in response, her clinical explanation somehow making him feel slightly worse. Winters watched on as Stakiri nodded and barked a final command at the lead scientist, who then turned right back around and scuttled off back within the deep confines of his lab, whilst Stakiri turned his attention back to the two of them. “Great news, boss!” Walter exclaimed. “Now we‘ll get those bastards back!” “Of course we will, Walter,” Stakiri confirmed, in an utterly patronising manner. If Walter realised it, he didn’t let on. “Would you do me a favour sit tight here, whilst Winters and I talk in private?” Walters nodded. “Sure thing. I was in the middle of an argument with the Ice Bitch Queen from Hell here anyhow!” Stakiri rolled his eyes. “Great.” “How the hell can I be an ‘Ice Queen’ and be ‘from hell’ too?” The receptionist asked, whilst Stalkiri placed an arm around Winters shoulders and directed into the StakiriTech labs. The female scientist followed closely behind them, her attention already embedded into the data from the reports contained upon her clipboard. “It’s more irony ya dumb bitch!” Walter yelled. Stakiri shook his head as he and Winters continued further along the corridor, but in the distance behind them, the raised voice were still difficult to ignore. “It’s not irony at all,” the receptionist responded, “it’s idiocy in it’s truest form!” Walter paused for the briefest of moments, before retaliating, “Ah! But it’s just like that Alanis Morissette song ‘Ironic’! What do you have to say about that? Eh?” “The thing about Alanis Morrisette is, she’s an idiot too,” the receptionist promptly replied. “Nothing in that song was ironic at all, it was just a list of grievances that--” The lab doors slid shut some distance behind Winters and that was the last of the conversation they heard. Small mercies… “When those bastards from OmniTech fucked with Aeryn’s mind and made her turn on us the way they did, they did a real number on her,” Stakiri said, studying Winters reaction. “As you recall, the only way we could stop her from both us and herself was to fry the programming, which didn’t leave much of the old Aeryn left…” “…and then you wanted to dissect her brain for potential information,” Winters interrupted bitterly. “I remember all of this. I was there, remember?” Stakiri sighed. “You’re not going to let that go are you?” “It’s not something one easily forgets,” Winters sharply responded. He still hadn’t warmed to Stakiri’s rather warped sense of humour and he doubted he ever would. At least, not in this life time… Winters acid tongue does not seem to phase Stakiri in the slightest. “Since then I’ve spent a fortune calling the leading scientist’s in the field of memory regression and although there was less than a forty percent chance of success, last night the scientist’s uncovered some positive signs.” Winters raised a speculative eyebrow. “Meaning…?” “She’s remembered an important experience from her past,” Stakiri replied. “And it could be just the opening we’ve been looking for.” Turning right into an enclosed lab at the end of the corridor, Winters came face to face with Aeryn, who immediately pushed herself from her chair by the door and wrapped her arms around him. His heart sank and he kissed on the cheek. “You okay?” She nodded a teary-eyed yes. “Jonathan, I remembered something!” “That’s great,” Winters responded, holding back the tears himself. Stakiri patted Winters firmly on the back. “I’m glad your pleased, because you’re going to help her develop it further to see where the trail ends and what else you can unearth. But I warn you, Jonathan, in the process don‘t try to look too closely into your own lost past.” He turned and made his way back into the corridor. “You won‘t like what you‘ll find.” To Be Continued…
