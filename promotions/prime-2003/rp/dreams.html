Dreams

Date: ReVolution 198 Millions of dreams, millions of people. It's how the world truly turns. A man in the middle of Detroit, his dream is just being able to provide for his family of four in these difficult economic times. Meanwhile, a man in a Penthouse apartment in Manhattan wonders if his investments will allow him another month of feeling comfortable. Down south, in the surly heat of Tuscaloosa, Alabama, a woman looks at her son as he leaves for college to hopefully become a legend unlike any have ever seen. In the great west, a man will into his eighties dreams of the next step in life. Dreams that once meant something eventually will fade into obscurity without tenderness and love. However, dreams that die can be reborn filled with life, joy, and happiness. Never was any of this a dream for David Noble; at least, not the dream he wanted. "Now, this is going to be cold," the piercing, nasally voice of Doctor Rubenstein overcame any thoughts that David might have been having at the particular moment. His eyes shifted towards the monitor just a few feet away from him and watched as the odd-shaped device in the doctor's hand touched Philadelphia's stomach. A sickening motion overcame David as he watched the first images of his child before his very eyes. Some would say that this was the proudest moment of their lives. David would disagree. "Oh my God!" she shrieked as she saw her baby for the first time in her life. Tears of joy welled up in her eyes as the doctor beamed brightly. "Now, it's a little early to truly determine the sex of your baby, but it looks like the two of you are about to be parents to a beautiful girl." The words shook David slightly as his hand gripped the steel metal and it felt like he was going to tear it off with sheer strength. "David...," Philadelphia looked at him with a weird sadness. He didn't know what to make of it and instead of questioning it further he looked over at the doctor. "So, Doc, what do we do from here?" Philadelphia's eyes returned to the monitor as joy overtook once again. "Well, just keep her healthy. Philadelphia here informed me of your recent notification of the impending delivery. So, I know this is all very new to you. However, there's nothing to be worried about. There's still time left to figure out how everything is going to work. As I explained to Philadelphia, the key here is not to stress the body out too much. Eat healthy, stay out of arguments, and just generally keep her in a positive state. Judging by her general demeanor though, I suspect that won't be too difficult." The doctor was indeed correct. The past few days, David had learned more about Philadelphia then he had in the entire time beforehand. Her temper and attitude towards life greatly differed from David's and it was almost like a fresh day of spring had overcome him. "God," Philly remarked in a soft, hushed tone. "We're going to have a baby girl." David looked over at her and smiled. It was somewhat false. The reality of everything had hit him strong the past few days and he still didn't know how to truly handle it all. Part of him yearned for the old days when he could see whoever he wanted to, drink whenever he felt like it, and be wherever he chose to be. Those days though seemed long gone. "OH!" Philly's eyes perked up as she eagerly grabbed David's hand. It moved to her belly before he had the chance to figure out what was happening, and felt something slam into his hand. A kick. "She knows you're there. She's happy her father is here," she offered him and he could only smile. Okay, maybe this wasn't going to be so bad. *** Rebecca Noble is a simple woman, always had been, and always would be. When Shawn had first met her, he remarked on her plain nature, and it was something that attracted him towards her initially. In her older age, it seemed like the things that interested her had faded away much like a butterfly does in its last stages of life. Her family, thankfully, provided the warmth and love she needed after the unexpected death of her husband. Yet, there was still a coldness there, a need that couldn't be filled. "Hey, Mom," the voice interrupted Rebecca's thoughts as her eyes rose from the book in her hands. Standing in the threshold of the doorway was Andrea who looked absolutely tired and beaten. "Andrea, come in," Rebecca put down her book and took a long look at her most successful daughter. Andrea stepped into the bedroom and sat on the bed as her mother wrapped her hand around Andrea's. It was a subtle gesture, one that Andrea appreciated. "So, I guess we need to talk." Andrea's voice showed the exhaustion that was written across her face. It hurt to hear her speak like that and Rebecca showed it. "Don't worry about me. I'm fine. We need to talk about you." Rebecca cleared her throat. "About?" "You and David. Look, you might not think that I'm aware of things, but I do get things far more than anyone else in this house." Confident. Sure. Unwavering. "It's just how I work. David and you have barely spoken in the past few weeks. His looks towards you, they're those of a person scorned and hurt. Yours is of a woman whose heart has turned black." An uncomfortable silence took grip of the room. "Listen, there's nothing between David and me, I promise you that." The lie matched the tenor in her voice. "Mom, I don't want to sit here and listen to you try to cover for things straight to my face. Not interested in that. Have a little bit more respect for me than that." Andrea's voice had hardened over the last few seconds. The lawyer edge in her had come out, her mother noted, and it was one that was deeply tenacious. Rebecca sighed. A painful sigh. "We had a fight. Probably a few weeks ago." Andrea's eyes peered into her mother's. "Let me guess, it was about Dad." Rebecca nodded her head, giving Andrea the confirmation that she needed. "I told him," she began before her head dropped and her voice softened. "That I hate him. That I'll never forgive him for what happened to your father." It took Andrea by surprise to hear such hatred come from her mother. "Shawn was his father too, lest you forget." "I know, I know." "I know not everyone gets along with David all of the time. He's quiet and to himself, but he could be worse. Trust me, I've seen the casualties of kids who were orphaned. But, you have to remember how much he loved you and Dad. Worshipped and respected the ground you two walked on. On top of that, look at what he's become. Successful. Driven. You can't blame David for what happened though." Rebecca felt the wave of emotion take over her and she fought with all of her might and strength not to succumb to tears. "I was just so angry. He had been gone for years and then came back one night. The same night that your father died. I couldn't live with that! In my mind, I equated the two being connected, even though I knew better. Then, he took me away from my girls." She stopped. Her breathing became heavier. "He took everyone I loved away from me." It was a somber thought and one that hit Andrea squarely in the chest. Sweat began to compound in the palms of her hands. "He needs you now more than ever, Mom. He doesn't know what to do. This girl, Philadelphia, is carrying his baby supposedly. He needs you there to guide him." Rebecca's posture slumped as she felt hopeless. "And how am I supposed to forgive him?" Andrea looked at her mother as her brow furrowed. "Would Dad?" The answer was obvious. *** "I think you should leave now." David's eyes met with those of redhead. He deserved those words and he knew as much. Yet, it didn't mean they didn't hurt any less. Somewhere in his scattered brain there was resemblance of a checklist of how he had hoped this encounter to go. Making it to her house had been first on the list, but being kicked out ruthlessly hadn't appeared on it. He would have to make amends. "Mary-Lynn, please, give me a chance to explain." Mary-Lynn felt the desperation as it rolled off of David's tongue. Her glare towards him though didn't soften in the least bit. "A chance? To explain? Please, tell me, what you would like to explain. You knocked a girl up! You're nothing but a dirty slut. I thought you were better than that, but I see now that I couldn't have been more wrong. Dinosaurs aren't as bad as you and they eat people!" "Wait, what?" "Don't try to change the subject. Get out of my house." David looked over his shoulder and contemplated the thought. For so long, he had only searched for happiness. In the briefest of moments, he had it, and he shared it with this woman. The softness of her lips against his was still fresh in his mind and how he longed for those moments again. They were in stark contrast to the environment he was in now. "Mary-Lynn, don't you understand? I want to be with you. This, with Philadelphia--" She held up one of her fingers and stopped him cold. "Oh, so she has a name now." "Mary-Lynn..." David pleaded now. There had to be something that he could do. Even as her mood turned harsher, he refused to give up hope. Not after everything he had gone through as of late. "I can't believe I ever had feelings for you, David. I thought you were better than this. I knew that you had a thing for the ladies, but I never realized the true depths of how far it went." Her words were cold and calculated. In ways, David was certain her heart was broken from the sheer shock of seeing Philadelphia sitting on his couch, pregnant. David refused to let go. He couldn't. "Mary-Lynn, why can't you let me speak? Let me explain everything to you. I screwed up, yes. But, that doesn't change the fact that I care about you, that I want you in my life. Do you know how happy you make me? I was flirting with a darkness, a black abyss, but you pulled me out of it." "That might be the case, David. Yet, it still doesn't change the fact that you're about to have a child. I'm not about to get mixed up in that turmoil." David felt the frustration build up inside of his chest. "I still don't even know if this is my child!" "You haven't denied it though, have you?" There was no answer he could give her, none that would make this better "...No." "Exactly. Get out of my house." "Mary-Lynn!" "I want you out, now! Just go!" No longer could he fight with her. Mary-Lynn tucked a lock of her hair behind her right ear and her fiery eyes gave defiance a new name. His right hand, callous from the weeks of figthing, reached up and grazed against her soft cheek. He wanted her more than anything. In a day that he felt his child kick for the first time, it had ended in a blaze of fire. It was never meant to end like this. He turned away from her and opened the door. David wished it would just be stuck and give him more time. Unfortunately, the door did not have his best interests at hand and before he knew it, he was outside of the apartment with no chance to turn around and see Mary-Lynn again. Anger took the best parts of him and he walked outside into the cool air. For minutes, he walked as the cool sunset gave life to a new breed of people. David felt the eyes against his skin and darted down a side street until he found a door with a sign above it. BAR He slipped in and dodged a woman who was exiting. She looked at him, but continued along her way as David walked up to the bar. With his thoughts racing a million miles an hour, he needed the chance to get away from everything. As the cold glass met his hand, he knew that the night would turn for the better in just a few moments. The liquid hit his lips and he felt the weight of the world come off of his shoulders. His eyes glanced around the room before he found a piano on the decpepitid stage. At one time, this place had been the spot for celebrities to come and just have a good time. In the years since though, it had turned into a mess where only people who stumbled upon it drunk. Yet, most of the people here tonight were here for the first time. Much like the people who had been here the night before and those on their way for tomorrow night. The difficulty in a bar with no name and no exact location that you can recall after a long night is that the patrons were always fresh. David sat behind the piano and began playing. At first nonsense, but towards his third drink he felt the music come to him a little more naturally. The sixth drink is when everything in the room felt more vibrant, more alive, almost like a symphony playing before his very eyes. The people in the audience were engaged by him as he continued to play, and with each ounce of liquor that entered his body he became better and better. Some in the audience remarked at how sad his music was and the dualing dialouge that was evident in what he was playing. David thought nothing of it. A young woman, roughly 22 or 23, walked up to him and looked him in his beautiful blue eyes. "What's your name?" He smiled. "That doesn't concern you." She returned the smile and sat next to him. The rest of the night was a blur.
