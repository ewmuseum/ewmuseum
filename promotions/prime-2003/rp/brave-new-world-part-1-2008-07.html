Brave New World, Part 1

July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 2008 Toby Jenkins was walking into the hornet's nest. Torrey Pines High School duffel bag slung over his right shoulder, he peeked inside the door of the brightly lit school apprehensively. He'd spent most of his formative years in Japan while his father lived there on business, but after his parents divorced, Christine Jenkins wanted her son back in San Diego, so that's where he spent his high school years. Now, he was back, but what he saw in the gym was reason for both great excitement and untold fear. A dozen young men, none of them looking older than 25, going through the most frightening workout regimen Toby had ever seen, putting nearly everything he'd ever done in nine years of competitive gymnastics to shame. Some were bunched in a corner, doing a seemingly infinite number of free squats, sweat dripping from their foreheads like a sputtering water fountain as they went through the militaristic ritual. Others were in the ring at the center of the complex, flopping to their backs at the cue of a whistle to the point of utter exhaustion and beyond. All unfamiliar faces, all Japanese faces. Except for one. Off to the right, next to the ring, was one other American face, working his way through a set of sit-ups that would tire a Navy SEAL. Somewhere between numbers 599 and 603, his eyes perked up as he saw Toby standing wide-eyed in the doorway. Finally, he paused, propping himself up on the ring apron. "So, the much-ballyhooed other gaijin finally arrives," he shouted across the room. Dumbfounded, Toby pointed to himself, nearly dropping his bag in the process. The other American smiled and nodded, waving him over. "Didn't know there was another American crazy enough to come over here for this," Toby said. "Hey, I was surprised, too. Dexter Carroll," he said, extending a hand. "Toby Jenkins. Nice to meet you." After a brief handshake, Carroll, who stood a couple inches taller than the 5-foot-6 Toby, surveyed his countryman, giving him a wayward glance. "How old are you, 16? How the hell'd you end up in Japan?" Toby smiled. He was young, but he new he looked even younger. "Eighteen," he said. "Just out of high school. And I didn't end up here. I started here. Dad was a legal liason for a few U.S. companies based in Tokyo, so I lived here until I was 13, then moved back to the states. Now that I'm out of school, I had to get back." "To this? Probably would've gone better if you weren't late. Koji, the head instructor, he's gonna FREAK." Toby glanced sideways, and he could already see that the stocky, middle-aged Japanese man at the center of the room was giving him a look that could melt granite. "Better get into your gear. Don't want to piss him off any further." Toby gathered his bag and hopped to the locker room. He didn't need any further motiviation. *** Six hours later... The restaurant was crowded and boisterous, but the two young Americans were sequestered in a corner, putting the finishing touches on some tempura and trying to ignore the increasingly loud sounds of bad karaoke coming from the bar. They were aching, there arms sagged, but on the other side of the world from their home, they were smiling and laughing. "I thought my ribs were going to crack," Toby said. "Your ribs? I'm surprised my brain didn't stop me halfway through for the sheer stupidity of it all. I'm pretty sure no human being is supposed to do that many squats." Toby laughed, pushing the last remaining shrimp around his plate. "What the hell is wrong with us? We fly thousands of miles across the world to learn to wrestle. I'm three weeks out of high school, and from what I've gathered you know about six phrases in Japanese, most of which involve locating the bathroom." "And the phone. Don't forget about the phone." Toby nodded dramatically, nearly causing Dexter to swat a ramekin of soy sauce off the table in a fit of laughter. "But, seriously, this is crazy." "Don't I know it. My parents are both political science professors at Ohio State. They think I'm out of my damn mind." "We are." Dexter pondered that for a moment, but could only nod in affirmation. Traveling to the other side of the planet just to have grumpy Japanese wrestlers put them through various forms of medieval torture was not exactly normal human being. But, to a professional wrestler, what's normal anyway?
