Tell Me a Story, Tell Me a Lie

22 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/22/08 Getting writer's block before you ever started to write was generally considered a poor way to go about things. He'd tried to write at the airport terminal in Greensboro before flying out to Omaha. He failed. He'd tried to write while cruising at 35,000 feet. He failed then, too. He'd tried to force himself to get words to flow out of his keyboard at 3:00 in the morning in his hotel room. Sensing a pattern here? Troy Douglas wasn't used to being at a loss for words, but ever since he'd agreed to write a blog for the PRIME homepage, his fingers and his brain had flat out refused to be on the same page. He'd have an idea, but as soon as he opened up a document on his laptop, the words just couldn't come out. His mind was a river storming downhill, but somewhere in his nervous system between his brain and his hands was the Hoover Fucking Dam. Which is why Troy was sitting in the corner of an Omaha Starbucks, sitting in front of a blank Word document, trying to bring that dam crashing down with what was currently his third ludicrously large cup of coffee. "Can I get you anything else, sir?" The 20-something blond with the pixie haircut and the nose piercing behind the counter had asked him that same question about six times in the last two-and-a-half hours, and frankly, it was wearing on Troy a little bit. "Another one of these, if it's alright." The same answer one more time, and it didn't expect to change any time soon. Not unless he could finally get this damn blog entry to type itself and he could get himself away from overpriced java, indie music and pretentious 30-something businesspeople. This is taking so long, it's getting funny. He didn't particularly like coffee, especially Starbucks coffee, but after staring at a computer screen this long, if he didn't constantly innundate himself with copious amounts of caffeine, Troy was at great risk of falling into a trance because of the hypnotic effects of a blinking cursor. He was just about to give up, hurl his coffee across the room and smash his laptop repeatedly against against the window just for the pleasure of seeing the device crumple into various tiny pieces, but the voice of one of his high school English teachers kept poking into the back of his head. When you can't think of anything to write, just write something. Well, it had worked before. So, just before he turned a $1,500 computer into a piece of artwork on the lines of Homer Simpson's barbecue pit, Troy took another sip of coffee, inhaled deeply, and let his fingers finally get to work... ****** From the files of Troy Douglas: Hey there, PRIMEates, it's one of those strange, new people that's been around since the Dual Halo. I know you've been a little hesitant with all of us, but trust me, you can come closer. We don't bite. Much. Well, everyone except for Cozen, that is, but I'm not going to touch that situation with a ten-foot pole, at least not for a little while. Anyway, the good folks at PRIME asked me to write a little something once in a while for the main page, which, frankly, has me questioning their judgment just a little bit. Especially as I'm currently sitting in the corner table of a Starbucks here in Omaha, sucking down my fourth cup of coffee and trying to rouse myself from a three-day case of writer's block that'd make J.D. Salinger flinch. But, I'm here, and since I haven't said a whole hell of a lot on TV since coming over to this company, I figured that, at the very least, I owe a few of you guys the courtesy of an introduction. Those of you who know me, those of you who know my work from elsewhere, you pretty much know what I'm about, so I've got no problem if you go back to checking stocks and porn. But, if you haven't had enough of me already, feel free to stick around and enjoy the party. We'll be passing around the free punch and pie in a moment. Those of you who are still wondering who this big quiet guy is and exactly what I'm doing here, do me the favor of sticking around a couple more minutes before you get on the boat with the rest of them. And I lied about the punch and pie. Sorry about that. To start, I guess I have to tell you the story of the best day of my life. That might sound a little third grade-ish for some of you, but bear with me for a moment or two. Trust me, PRIMEates, this isn't your normal story. Anyone who's ever taken a peek at my bio on the Web site of any company I've ever worked for knows at least a little bit about my football career. Even if history has all but forgotten my days on the gridiron, there's still one that'll always stand out for me. It was a Thursday night, December of 1998. Second or third-to-last game of my rookie season with Philadelphia. We sucked that year, and we had a national TV date with the St. Louis Rams, who frankly weren't all that much better. You want to know who won, how many yards Duce Staley rushed for, et cetera? That's what Wikipedia is for. Considering I wasn't there after the middle of the third quarter, I wouldn't exactly be the most reliable person to ask. I played mostly in a situational role that year, playing some linebacker, some defensive end, getting in wherever I could. This particular game, I'd drawn a regular third-down matchup directly across from the man for whom the phrase "pancake block" was popularized, Orlando Pace. I'd gotten into the backfield a few times, but the massive left tackle had kept me at bay for the most part and kept his signal-caller clean. Of course, on this particular play, I wasn't going for the quarterback. I'll set the stage. Third and 15, Rams just short of midfield and down a touchdown with a little less than six minutes left in the third quarter. Our offense had been moving pretty effectively, and a stop here was going to give us a chance to go up two scores headed into the fourth quarter. We got the call in from the sidelines to dial up a zone blitz. I was going to drift back into coverage, and our free safety, the Brian Dawkins not currently in PRIME, was going to come from the secondary on a blitz. We'd run the play time after time during the season, and it had worked its fair share. It worked this time, too. Just not for me. The ball was snapped, and I floated off the line of scrimmage into the flat. I could see Brian fly into the backfield, forcing the quarterback to scramble. I curled back to the other side of the field, ready to give chase. And that's when I felt it. To give you a little background, Veteran's Stadium in Philadelphia was home, in case anybody didn't know, to perhaps the worst playing surface ever known to man. They called it Astroturf-8, and it had ended the careers of people like Michael Irvin and Wendell Davis. It was just about to end mine. The field was notorious for the seams that were produced by the patches of turf that covered up the Vet Stadium infield. As soon as I felt my knee twist, I knew I'd caught myself on one. Of course, if that's all that happened, it might've just been a torn ACL at worst, and I'd be able to get back in training camp the next year. But, the quarterback was still scrambling, and his blockers were scrambling to clear a path. I turned, and a receiver was right there to give me a hard shot right across the chest. And even if it ended there, I could've just been carted off, gotten my knee scoped, maybe had some knee ligaments from a monkey or a cadaver spliced in, and I could've gone along with my life. I fell backwards, and unfortunately, as the tape showed, I stumbled back just a a 320 pound offensive guard was stumbling forward at full speed. Now, football helmets are used for a reason, folks. Because they are very, very hard. And when a giant man running like a freight train accidentally drives one into your unprotected back, bad things happen. I collapsed, and I knew right away I couldn't move. But even then, if the bizarre chain of events had ended, I might still have a career. Then I saw it out of the corner of my eye. One more stumbling man about to land. I blacked out beforehand, but from what I've been told, he landed on his side, right on my neck. I was semi-conscious for a little while, but I had no idea what was going on. I remember being the hush of the crowd, I remember being rushed to the ambulance, and then there was nothing. I woke up sixteen hours later, my dad and my girlfriend looking down at me. For the life of me, looking into the bright lights, I thought I was dead. Apparently, I'd gotten lucky. I'd just avoided complete paralyzation, but I'd still suffered a brutal combination of knee, back and neck injuries. My football career, I was told, was unequivocally finished. I didn't take two steps for about six weeks. That was the best day of my life, PRIMEates. Because, without that day, I'd never be here today. Even though football was out of the question, I still rehabbed like I was getting ready to climb Mount Everest barefoot. I went through more physical therapy than I can even comprehend, and since then, my back has healed to the point where it's no longer a risk to go out on the ring and take the impact that I absorb on a regular basis. There's pain, sure, but it's nothing I can't live with. That day was the best day of my life, folks, because, after another series of strange coincidences, I ended up in the squared circle. And, regardless of the shit I've taken in more than eight years in wrestling, there isn't a place I'd rather be on the planet than in front of you people. Alright, I apologize if that sounded as horribly sappy as I think it did, but give me a break, okay? I'm working on about four thousand CCs of pure caffeine and I'm not sure how much longer I can keep typing before I just collapse and starljksdkjfskhffdddddddddfs Oops. Give me a minute while I wipe the slobber off my keyboard, okay? You know what, sarcasm works a hell of a lot worse over the internet. I could keep going on and on, but I'm pretty sure most of you have long since either stopped reading or be lulled to sleep, so I'll just say thanks for stopping by, and I hope you all tune in to see me and Logic this Wednesday at ReVolution. I plan on giving you all an even better reason to keep watching my tired, boring ass when I step into that ring. Until next time, I'll see you at the end of the road. --Troy ****** With that, it was done. When he'd finally started, the words flowed like a rushing stream, and finally getting words like that out onto a page were an exorcism. He'd told a lot of bullshit stories during his time in wrestling, but this time, he could honestly say that it wasn't one of them. Of course, it was up to the rest of the world to like it or not. He still had no idea why they'd asked him to write, but as he sent his e-mail to the PRIME web staff, for the first time he thought that it might not be the worst idea. And, just as he closed the laptop, the same girl came back with the same question. "Anything else I can get for you, sir?" "No more coffee. Just the check, please. I'm finally done."
