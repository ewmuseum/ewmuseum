Problems Next Door

19 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/19/2005 “No, don't do it,” Scott said, his hand held up in what he knew was a futile gesture. “I'm coming with you,” his sister said, her arms crossed over her chest. “No.” “Yes.” “No.” “Yes.” “I'm not taking you.” “Yes, you are.” Scott's hands balled into helpless fists. This wasn't the kind of argument he needed to be having and certainly not over anything that would be any manner of discussion. It was something that should be quite simple. “No, stay here,” Scott said, pointing back at the house. “I'm not staying here,” Lacey replied. “Lacey... we don't know who these people are.” Scott threw up his hands in desperation. He was losing, he knew it. It was the classic argument that they always had: he couldn't stop her and she wouldn't listen. So, he opened the door to his Wrangler and his sister climbed into the passenger seat. As Scott fired up the engine, he did smile inwardly. In the past few weeks, ever since Lacey and Alyssa had sat down and enjoyed a heart-to-heart, her taste in apparel had definitely changed. She sat primly in the passenger seat, dressed in khakis and a conservative blue blouse. Scott was definitely pleased with the attire change, especially regarding the way she used to dress. Which was probably for the best. The people in Pike County weren't quite open to the new arrivals that were getting set up not too far away from his mother's house. So what was he looking at? Well... he was going to find out who exactly these folks were, but he had a good suspicion. He hit the bottom of the hill and pulled a left, onto a gravel road and splashing through the small creek that the road ran... under, for lack of a better term. There, at the base of the sign, was a jumble of vehicles and several very unhappy people. The sign was arranged in an arc that bridged the road and had the words “Followers of the Pious Tree.” It wasn't hard to see why they had been labeled otherwise: The Freaks of the Poison Tree. Take it as you would, but you would basically be looking at a cult, as people perceived. It wasn't that much of a stretch, really. They were standing there, by the sign, all wearing robes of various colors, none noting any particular pattern. Scott stood there, his booted feet shuffling uneasily. The gaze of these people was unsettling, glassy and never blinking. “What the hell is this?” Scott muttered to an older man standing to his left. The man hefted a very solid-looking cane. “These folk are tellin' us they're here to reach out to the heathens and all. Apparently we're the heart of unbelievers.” “I honestly have no clue how exactly that works,” Scott said, shaking his big head. They were in the heart of Appalachia, where Pentacostal churches weren't out of place and hellfire and brimstone preaching wasn't unheard of. But these freaks? He shouldered his way forward, his big frame parting those around him easily. He didn't realize that Lacey was following in his wake. There, at the head of the crowd, stood the local district magistrate as well as three local preachers. It was quite apparent that none of them were terribly thrilled with what was going on around them. Scott stood there, listening to them go back and forth with the apparent poobah of the Poison Trees. “What you know is wrong!” he shouted, his face as red as the robes draped over his body. “You do not know what you think you know!” “Your reasoning is circular,” one of the preachers responded. “Silence, infidel!” he shouted back. “We are adherents to the Pious Tree of the cross, blessed by the golden tablets of Joseph Smith!” “Oh great,” Scott heard Lacey mutter, surprising him that she was with him. “We've got a mixture of Mormons and...” “The messiah has returned and he will begin his work here!” “...David Koresh, I guess,” she finished. “Whore!” he shouted, pointing at Lacey. Oh hell no. “You are dressed as a whore of Babylon, presenting your wares for sale to the other sinners!” the man screamed. His finger's target was soon eclipsed by a larger frame. “Just who the hell do you think you are?” Scott responded. “I do not cower before a minion of the Beast!” he screamed, the capitalization coming through quite clearly in his voice. “I don't even fucking think so,” Scott say, smacking a fist into a palm. “We will not fall to one man,” the Grand Wizard or whatever responded. “We are many, you are but one, even one bold enough to try to defeat those of the Pious Tree.” “I ain't afraid of a slew of people, even crazies like you in your paper pants,” Scott responded. “You wanna take me down?” It was a mass of confusion. Police sirens cried as the flashing lights shone on the bedlam ahead. It wasn't that Scott threw the first shot. In fact, he ate a shot right on the jaw and cracked the ringleader in the skull with a fist the size of a canned ham. It was hysteria that had everyone flowing inward around the big man... and one person outward. Lacey Matthews had sense enough to get out and lock herself in her brother's Jeep. The fight had ended pretty quickly... for the residents of the area. The new arrivals had kept fighting, going toe to toe with law enforcement officials and the massive man that their leader had attacked. Scott sat on the back bumper of the ambulance, holding a cold compress to his forehead. Someone had grabbed a branch or some such to crack him directly in the skull. Needless to say, someone with a rage complex hadn't taken kindly to it. But there he sat, Lacey next to him, when a police officer walked up. “Mr. Matthews?” he asked, putting a foot on the bumper. He dropped his elbow on his knee to lean. “You were the one struck first in this, right?” Scott cast a cynical eye up through the curtain of lion's mane that fell in his eyes. “Wasn't exactly the plan, but yeah. Mr. High Falutin' popped me in the chin.” “Well, sir,” he said, taking a moment to scratch at his forehead. “I ain't real sure how to tell you this, but we kinda owe you a bit.” Scott turned to look at his sister. She shrugged helplessly at him. “Y'know, sir,” Scott started, “I'm not really in the mood for jokes right now.” “No joke, son,” the officer said, pulling his hat back into place. “Turns out these Poison Tree Freaks was just a cover-up. Son, I don't know how to tell you this, but it was the setup for one of the biggest drug smuggling ops that was ever gonna be around here.” “Oh, bullshit,” Scott muttered.
