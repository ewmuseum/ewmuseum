The Assignment

Date: ... The Nokia model 5600 vibrated upon the glass surface, producing a slight buzzing noise that jarred him from his google search. The sleeve on his white terry cloth robe receded (it was a little small for him) as he reached across the table, over a white file-postage envelope containing a half-exposed folder. Bringing the cell phone to his ear, he pressed the button to accept the call, recognizing the number and in fact expecting the call. “Here,” he said, his right hand completely covering the phone which was normally used with a Bluetooth headset (that was broken and therefore he temporarily had to return to the awkward handheld method of phone conversation). “Hey, it's Van,” was the answer that crept into his ear. “Got it?” The man nodded. “Finished it,” he said, referring to a download of the map of Pikeville, Kentucky. “All the specs. Still need to know about the family.” “Well, I gave you the address,” Oxios said. 'They can best let you know what they need from you.' As he held the phone to his ear, he released a sigh. His left hand was occupied with his dual-google search: one search for anything related to the Cult of the Poison Tree, the other for weapons distributors in the area. “Right, I'll be there soon. Leaving tonight.” He kept his answers short. Sentences were never more than three words long. It was a quick dialogue, but foolproof. Anyone who might have tried to listen in would have no idea what they were talking about. It wasn’t code, but this phone line was only used by clients, and he liked to keep convesations short so that they didn't distract him from business. They knew what they were talking about without saying anything specifically about it. It also cut down on walk-ins from learning anything that they weren’t supposed to know. The reply on the other end of the line was, “Good. Thanks, Cell.” Darcellus Wallace nodded, saying, 'It's my job,' to Vangellus Oxios on the other line before ending the call. Darcellus Wallace hit ‘End’ on his phone and set it back on the table. Vangellus Oxios’s best friend to this day was six feet, seven inches tall, weighing a solid and intimidating 266 pounds. He had the physique of a professional bodyguard, and indeed he was one. A former boxer (that's how he met Van), Darcellus concerned himself more with finesse and maneuverability than plowing through opponents, even for his size. Wallace was sometimes seen on television back when he was the bodyguard of Oxios himself, part of a two-person contingent in Rush Pro Wrestling along with Teshigahara Emi Sachiko. Of course, he didn't mind Van, unlike his diminutive counterpart. He respected her ability, though, and the two bodyguards did get along. That would be good, because he knew that they'd be working together again on this assignment. Having found what information he could (the Poison Tree was mentioned in passing, mostly they were considered quite harmless, actually, compared to other millenial cults), he saved the pages for offline viewing and triple-filed them within a series of folders linked from one directory in the computer to another, meaning that no one would find the information whom wasn’t deliberately looking for it, and even then it would take an exhaustive search to find it. The computer was turned off. His “home business” required security measures like that. He knew much more about this client's lives than most people would ever need to. Safeguarding their privacy was his responsibility. That's why after he's done, he completely wipes the files. That was the way of things, the nature of the game. As Wallace finished with his typing, his wife Rhonda walked by still wearing her pink silken house robe that he'd bought for her for Mother's Day. An image of ebony beauty, even with bed hair and no makeup. She still wasn’t completely awake, but had enough awareness in his present state. “Morning,” Darcellus smiled. She mumbled back, 'Hey, baby,' not intending to start any conversation until she had a cup or three of the coffee brewing in the Black & Decker appliance on the kitchen counter. The mugs were directly above it near the fridge, and the Maxwell House was across the gap over the sink. A can of coffee was already sitting on the counter, but that was the green Maxwell House: decaf. Rhonda could never grasp the concept of decaffeinated coffee; after all, the caffeine content was the reason that coffee was even mass-distributed in the first place. 'Where's the regular?' she asked. Darcellus simply raised his cup to her and said, 'Good to the last drop.' Marriage is about sharing, so Rhonda had no problem with stepping over and taking the coffee mug in her hands, gulping down the rest of the half-full cup. Darcellus was already awake, so he was okay with it. After gulping down her first cup, Rhonda was a little better. Her metabolism was rather quick, the caffeine took effect soon; the warmness sensation coursing through her helped as well. She poured another cup of the straight black coffee (she drank other drinks for taste, he drank coffee to wake up, and the bitterness of black coffee helped her to jolt her system) and walked over to the table where Darcellus was packing away the computer. Outside, the sun was peeking in through the forest that provided a natural border to their backyard up outside of Nashville, Tennessee. “Early morning?” she asked. Wallace nodded. “Just getting some last minute research done.” She knew that he'd been contacted by Vangellus. It wasn't a problem; while she didn't want Cell on the road all the time as a bodyguard, that was still his job and right now local work was sliding with the coming winter months where not many celebrities would book for the area. As Darcellus got up from the table, he took the computer and file with him to the black leather computer bag that came with the computer (the library likes its things to be in working order and kept safe). It was outside of the kitchen, resting in a high-back stool sitting by the counter that faced the kitchen with the opening provided by the sink. Rhonda came back out of the kitchen, giving her husband his first hug and kiss of the day. 'When are you going to be leaving?' 'Tonight' he said, the side of Rhonda's head pressed against his chest. 'Van's got a flight out arranged.' She met his eyes with a grin. 'Then I'd better give you a proper send-off. Good thing I've got you all day long.' He knew that look in her eyes. She knew it too. Darcellus took her on the spot. Van: Wrestling. It as a lauded athletic competition, heralded throughout the ages. Though its roots go back to the days of pre-civilization as the most base for of combat between two humans, we can trace the origins of wrestling Wrestling was very important to ancient Greece, both as a form of sport featured prominently within society, it was also physical training for soldiers. Even the women (of Sparta, at the least) were taught to wrestle so that they could increase their physical strength and combat ability, so as to protect the home front while the males were at war. Sexist bastards, take note. The ladies have wrestling in their history, too. It's only 'a man's sport' because the whole history of the modern world has proven to us unequivocably that white guys are jackasses. Wrestling is a large part of the African tradition as well. In Things Fall Apart, the main character Okonkwo is a former wrestling star among the various villages. In the United States, wrestling started off as a carnival sideshow attraction, but when 6'4' 260 pound combatants became more common among the population (and stopped seeking the presidency of the United States), the sport became larger and more organized, wider-ranging, and all around a greater spectacle. And in this spectacle, there are three kinds of athletes: the savants, the entertainers, and the superstars. The savants just have the physical part of wrestling in their blood. Whatever they do, they do it WELL. They've got the physique, they've got the athleticism, they know what they can do and how to shape a match around that. They work to cover their weaknesses and highlight their strengths, and are excellent at forcing opponents into their style of competition. The entertainers are the ones with all the charisma in the world. They can make a person feel whatever they want with their words and actions. And really, that's quite hard. It's easy to get the crowd to pop for a high spot. But if you don't have highspots and still can get the crowd behind you, you're golden. And finally, there's people like me, the superstars. I've got the talent and the charisma. The total package, if you will. While the Tyler Brigade doesn't have this for them, maybe they at least have the talent part. We gave them a shot because we're bored. So that's that. The soft twitter of the bird was a good sign. Nothing startled the little bird here so close to twilight. The woman ran a gentle hand over the wooden weapon in her grip. Vangellus had asked her to come here to help Scott. While Van aggravated her, Scott seemed a rather likeable fellow, despite his reputation of a hot temper. She had observed him with his girl, Alyssa, and his family. His great size was belied with the gentle nature he showed around them. The birdsong stopped and her ears almost perked. That was a sign. Here in this continental climate and seasonal forest, signs were different than many other climates she’d worked in. She moved deftly, her feet slipping through the dead leaves at her feet with an unreal silence. Her quarry, though, did not have the same ability. While he was very quiet, he still rustled the leaves underfoot. She watched him, wanting to find his intention before she took him down. In his left hand was a small pair of binoculars, in his right was clenched around an object that was somewhat hard to distinguish. He moved and the dwindling sunset caught exactly the right angle. It was a machete, coated in black teflon. Apparently these cult members were not fools. Not all of them anyway. The man was garbed in matte grays, but still the traditional robes favored by the members of Poison Tree. Her mouth twisted at that thought. From everything she’d been able to find on the cult, their practices were disgusting and infuriating. The man moved forward further and lifted his binoculars. She could tell that Wallace was making a circuit around the house, as he tended to do a few times during the day. He simply didn’t like sitting still for too long. Wisely, he never practiced his art in any kind of view. The cultist was studying him intently, very cautious of an element he didn’t know. The woman altered her opinion again. These cultists were very intent on what they intended on doing and were not going about it in the random, reckless fashion with which the massive man had first encountered them. Either that brawl had been a ruse or they had specially trained men as part of their congregation. That first brawl, though, had cost them dearly: many of their number had been arrested when the police had found evidence of prescription drug trafficking. Hence, their reason for hating Scott so. She watched the man observe Wallace return to the house. With a small smile to himself, he continued forward. She trailed him, moving deftly from tree to tree. He slipped his way down the slope, making his approach down into the valley hidden away in the Appalachian Mountains precarious. But he moved surely, not planting his step without making sure he could move surely from that spot. She felt a rise of pride within her: she had spent her first days here ranging everywhere In sight of the home learning the terrain and how she could move surely and quietly. The man made quietly, careful steps that took him to a well-trimmed backyard. He lowered himself to a crouch and began a steady creep toward the house. She had to wait before exposing herself. If he saw her, then all her stalking would be have been for nothing. She kept to the edge of the trimmed property, watching him intently. She hoped that Wallace was still on edge. The cultist made his way around to the side of the house and raised from his crouch, peering carefully through a lit window. He dropped suddenly and moved away. No doubt he was afraid of almost being seen. So he moved again, around to the front of the house. She made her move then, as he turned the corner, making a dead run for the other side of the brick structure. She stopped, pivoted and glanced around the corner. Had he heard? The man hadn’t moved from where he was when she started her sprint. He crept onward, though, vanishing on around the corner. She followed, turning her own corner and very nearly cursed. He’d left his binoculars there along the edge of the house. Was it a trap for her? Was it just a measure to leave behind a piece of equipment he didn’t need? She gently stepped around and moved to the corner. She saw him, looking in that front picture window. Her blood ran cold. He was intent on doing damage, else he wouldn’t have come this far. That’s when she heard Wallace bellow from inside. He had been alert, but this man was armed. So was she. Wallace was not. She moved in, abandoning stealth for speed. Her weapon swung upward and struck right against the back of the man’s knee. He turned, his face pained but he swung his machete. She turned her wooden weapon to meet the blade and the edge bit in. They were locked there for a moment, eye to eye, and she saw the fanatical hate burning in his eyes. But there was a factor that they hadn’t counted on. The door to the garage burst open and out came the very man that the cult hated so much. Scott Matthews had been there, not in the house proper. His broad chest glistened with sweat, probably from his insanely heavy weightlifting routine. The cultist’s eyes widened and he tried to pull the blade free. She wretched hard, turning her weapon and in turn, his. He couldn’t keep his grip, and so focused on his weapon as the equalizer that he didn’t realize how quickly the Manbeast had closed ground. Scott’s big hands grabbed by the shoulders and hefted him off the ground. Scott turned and swung, throwing the man straight against the brick exterior of his mother’s home. The man hit with the horrifying impact of meat falling to a tile floor. Scott followed, not wasting time, but slinging a heavy fist into the man’s diaphragm. He doubled over and fell to the ground, curling into the fetal position. The big man turned to her and exhaled. He seemed to almost deflate, letting so much intensity out of him. “I’m really glad you’re here,” Scott said to her.
