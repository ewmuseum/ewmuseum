numbing cuddles from infantile happiness

4 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4 nov 08 You tripped up again, you tripped up again, oh no. You know better, don't you? Free to believe in, oh no. Tick-tock again. Tick-tock again, oh no. Waiting, Waiting. Waiting, Waiting. Adam throws me a toothless smile that melts my heart within an instance. His tiny frame wrapped in chubbiness and innocence. His right hand flickers his fingers over his lips and he makes a “beeble beeble” noise as the fingers rise and fall. The noise amuses Adam, and he begins to fall into a hysterical and high-pitched laugh. The laugh is contagious and I myself begin to chuckle. Being a parent gives you the right to be lame and enjoy those clichéd simple things in life. Hanging around with your kid is one of them. April paces back and forth in the background, chewing on her fingernails. Something strikes within her. I assume it’s courage but you know what they say about assuming. She bursts into the lounge, standing over Adam with tears running down her face. “You can’t be a part of our lives, Brock!” she blurts all at once. Taken aback, my hand rises to my mouth as it tries to amble out the words yet I cannot get my voice box and lips to become a cohesive unit. Stammering and “wh-wh-wh”-ing, April talks over the top of me. “Adam doesn’t deserve this!” “Deserve what?” I finally reply. “THIS, Brock. THIS! For fuck’s sakes, Brock. You know EXACTLY what I’m talking about.” “I’m afraid you’re wrong, Ape. Whaddaya mean deserve this?” Gently yet abruptly, she plucks the babe from the floor mid-hysteria, putting him on her hip as he tries to “beeble” her lips. Tenderly, she paws his flickering digits away from her mouth. “This constant unknowing, Brock. Do you really want to bring your son into a world where his own father can’t even control his own actions?” Flabbergasted by the accusations, I rise from my seat with my hand and prosthetic begging the questions before my words arrive. “Are you talking about this thing with Samantha Gideon?” Samantha Gideon was the personal assistant who met her demise at some stage in the duration of celebrating my recent Just Wrestling Championship victory. A matter the law were taking with grave seriousness and their fickle finger of fate pointed directly at me as their only witness and suspect. Like the law, I was trying to piece together the puzzle yet my memory seems somewhat murky at this stage as to the how’s and why’s but that’s another story for another day. “I didn’t do it, April. You gotta believe me!” Her head shakes with frustration as her free hand tries to gather the courage she needs to continue. “Of course I believe you, Brock, but Adam does need all of this. It’s ALL of this, Brock! ALL of it.” My heart feels like a lead weight being tossed into the ocean, plummeting to the pit of my gut as April continues. “Think it’s good to have cops crawling over the joint for character points on you? Think it’s nice being stopped in the street so people can get a photo of themselves with the Just Champion’s kid? Is it nice for a kid to grow up with a father who’s constantly touring around the country and barely there for them?” “April, I spend any God damned fucking minute I can with Adam. You know that!” “I know that, Brock, but we just don’t need the stress that’s caused by having you involved with our lives!” “What’re you tryin’ to say, April?” I ask. Hoping it was more rhetorical in her eyes than something worthy of an answer. “Are you saying that you want me out of your lives for good?” Her silence resonates within my soul and it feels like an atomic bomb has gone off inside my heart. Clutch it momentarily, feeling the aftershock of her silence. April’s eyes are filled with tears and her face withdrawn and sullen. Sunken shoulders and sunken heart, she turns the babe to clutch it closer to her hugging him tightly for her own comfort more than his. “You want me… gone?” The descent and rise of her head in the nodding capacity seems like slow motion as April’s sobbing begins to echo in her chest, heaving it as she tries to cover it up with Adam. I could feel my body tensing up as though it were attempting to cry. A bad case of cottonmouth and a lump in my throat as I could only respond with a brooding nod. My hand and prosthetic outstretch toward my son as I choke on my words. “Can I… c-c-can I hold h-him one more time?” April’s sombre head bobs back and forth with a nod as she passes Adam into my arms. Pressing my son to my chest, I feel his tiny heart beating against mine. The feeling of your own flesh and blood pressed to yours was a sobering, numbing feeling and the rest of the world seems to vacate from your being. The sweet smell of milk and talcum powder. His warm breath in the nape of my neck and he seems to understand what was happening. He did not wriggle and writhe, trying to clutch at body parts he was yet to understand. Adam seemed to soothe within my grasp, just breathing with me and enjoying what might have been the last time in a long while before we would meet again. With her arms outstretched once more, April comes to take Adam from my arms, which I find near impossible to let her do. Not promoting the removal nor blocking her attempt, I force her to pluck him from my clutches. I cannot move my arms from their hugging position as Adam’s tiny legs are the last things to brush over my forearms. I am crying. Tears stream down my cheeks as a lump has formed inside my throat and my gut, casting a daunting emptiness throughout my existence as April leans in and kisses my damp cheek. “I’m sorry, Brock. I’m so sorry.” I cannot speak. I nod to April and try to move but my walking is more of a stumble. My bearings are lost from emotion. My sight blinded by my tears. Clutching the walls for support as I head toward the door. I can hear April still talking to me but those words never enter my ears. I am hurting. My eyes sting from the tears and as my ears begin to decipher the noises around me again I realise I am howling with my sorrow. Hyperventilating like a child who has scraped their knee on the bitumen. Never have I felt pain like this. Never. Pull the door open and step out into the mocking sunshine outside, turning to close the door behind me to see Adam is crying too. They say they are too innocent to understand what is happening in their lives. That they don’t have the capacity to know. The moment I pull the door shut on my son is the moment I feel I could argue with the behavioural scientists and doctors forever more. Adam knows his daddy is going away. And by this he is saddened. My aunt emerges from the lounge room, her eyes like that of a raccoon’s. Caused by the mix of tears and mascara, there are large black rings around her eyes. She’s been crying. “Where’s Dad?” “Brock, you should probably come take a seat,” she advises, trying to guide me to the lounge with a gentle hand upon my elbow. Shake my head. Her words are like bullets in my heart, striking my happiness and knocking the breath from my lungs.
