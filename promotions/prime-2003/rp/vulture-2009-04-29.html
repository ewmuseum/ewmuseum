Date: 29th April “Collective fear stimulates herd instinct, and tends to produce ferocity toward those who are not regarded as members of the herd.” - Bertrand Russell He dropped his copy of this morning\'s edition of the New York Times and took a sip from the foul-tasting cup of Joe in his right hand. Washing the overpriced brown liquid down his gullet (and cursing Starbucks for being the only coffee outlet to set up shop on the block), Detective Stone plonked his bulky self in front of his desk. Chomping down on the lukewarm egg muffin that was waiting for him, Stone sifted through the batch of case files he\'d laid out the night before. Letting out a hefty sigh as the reality of the volume of work ahead of him began to sink in, Stone cracked his knuckles, looked skywards, and started digging into another day of solid graft. The first few files weren\'t of any note. A petty theft. An assault. Textbook stuff of no real interest to Stone whatsoever. Typical NYPD Detective fodder. The type of case that doesn\'t score you any points with the higher-ups for clearing, but if you don\'t close that shit? You\'d might as well walk through the office with a bag over your head for the next couple of weeks. But the third case file, that was a different story. Pinned to the front of the brown sleeve was his face. Rugged and carved in stone, with a thick layer of lazy stubble spouting from his jawline and a small star tattoo underneath his right eye. Stone paused and glared at the black and white polaroid for a moment. What kinda nutjob gets his face tattooed? the detective asked himself. He knew that these wrestling types were a little off-centre, but this guy looked like he\'d been dragged through Crazyville ass-backwards on more than one occasion. He pulled the sleeve open and rummaged through the folder\'s contents. There were a few priors on his record. Two possession busts and an assault that was settled out of court. Nothing to write home about, but his alleged involvement in an underground fighting ring was enough to pique Stone\'s interest. He was just beginning to get into a groove with the file when the ringing of his phone derailed his train of thought. “Stone,” he answered gruffly after pulling the receiver to his ear. “Harry, your ten-thirty\'s here.” Surprise stretched it\'s way across Stone\'s face. He huffed. “Alright. Send \'im through, I guess.” The man with the tattooed face was early. * * * * * “You\'ll, uh, have to forgive me for my lack of preparation,” Harry said as he ushered the burly New Yorker into the floor\'s sole interview room. “Wasn\'t expecting you for another hour and a half.” “Got shit to do,” Jason Natas said as he padded his way around the heavy table built to separate detainer from detainee. “Flyin\' out t\'Kansas this afternoon. Gotta get this shit over an\' done with.” Jason – dressed in a black hooded sweatshirt and a pair of loose-fitting blue jeans, sat down in the uncomfortable plastic chair and cast his eyes over his surroundings. Cold, steel-blue walls and a gray concrete floor, the monotony only broken up by the beaten down table and chairs in the middle of the room (and who could forget the small security camera perched in the corner?). Once you\'ve seen one interrogation zone, you\'ve seen them all. Stone turned to close the heavy wooden door behind him, wondering if this was going to be harder than he\'d thought. “Can I get you anything?” Stone asked, taking a sip on his almost-depleted cup of coffee, only half-expecting a response. “Yeah. Bottle of Scotch an\' a couple \'a strippers.” Jason smirked, folding his arms over his barrel chest. “Oh, an\' Troy Douglas\'s balls on a platter.” Charming, Stone thought. “I\'ll take that as a \'no\', then?” Jason sniggered. “Heh. I can tell you\'re real fuckin\' good at yer job, Sherlock.” Gritting his teeth together, pulling the hefty wooden chair away from the table, Harry Stone took a seat across from Jason Natas. For a moment he examined this most rugged of men. Dressed in an outfit unfitting of a man of his relative wealth (but perhaps appropriate for a man who\'d just had his home burned to the ground) and with an obvious regard for any form of personal hygiene, he was even more unpleasant looking in the flesh. His face was an Atlas of bumps and bruises, with a cut here and a welt there, and he clearly hadn\'t had a shave in at least a week. Hell, Harry pondered, this guy even makes a buzzcut look unkempt and messy... Locking his fingers together and leaning across the table, Stone began to consider how he was going to play the game. “I assume you know why I asked you to come here today?” “Yeah, an\' I ain\'t got much t\'say on the matter either, so keep it snappy.”Jason growled. “Well if you don\'t want to talk about it why did you bother to come along?” PRIME\'s Anti-Superstar looked up momentarily. “\'Cause I figured if I didn\'t, one \'a you fucks\'d jus\' track me down anyhow. Talkin\' wi\' cops ain\'t my bag, but I ain\'t a fuckin\' spastic either.” Harry watched as Jason tightened his arms around his chest and frowned. He was right. This was going to be harder than he\'d imagined. * * * * * “So you can\'t think of anyone that would want to show up and burn up your apartment, then?” Harry quizzed. “Nope.” “Not a soul?” “Not a one.” “That\'s kinda funny, Jason. I would have figured that a man in your profession would have a host of enemies queueing up to take a shot. I mean, you do beat people up for a living, right?” He snorted, Jason; as if the mere suggestion that being a professional wrestler involved “beating people up” was humorous to him. “Wouldn\'t call it that, exactly. Shit\'s about as real as Vanilla Ice. S\'all flips and spins; ain\'t much actual head-knockin\' goin\' on.” Detective Stone nodded his bulbous head. He knew fine that calling wrestling a “sport” was a stretch of the imagination in the first place, but he needed to try and make Jason feel more at ease with his surroundings. He needed to lure him in. “Surely you\'ve ruffled a couple of feathers over in PRIME though, eh? I know what goes on on those shows. When you\'re not fighting in the ring you\'re just fighting outside it, right?” “Well, yeah. \'Course there\'s some cunts I don\'t get along to well with. Seriously doubt Bryan Dawkins or Hank Cobb\'s gon\' wanna come up to New York jus\' t\'throw a match through my letterbox, though.” Harry sighed. “You know, you seem incredibly relaxed for a man who\'s just had all his worldly possessions torched.” “What can I say?” Jason shrugged. “Ain\'t really a material kinda guy. S\'all just replaceable shit anyway.” The Anti-Superstar felt guilty red heat brimming behind his facial features. Fortunately, the cop seemed to be buying his story. He did care about what had happened, yes; but the last thing he wanted was for the police to keep sticking their noses in. With Stone lurking around, how was he ever supposed to gain a measure of revenge on the foolish soul who\'d decided it\'d be wise to empty a can of gasoline and strike a match. “Mmmhmm,” hummed Stone as he nodded. “Well the results from the lab were pretty inconclusive. Certainly not enough to point a finger at anyone; anything pertinent got torched in the blaze,” he said, as if Jason cared about finding the culprit. Natas didn\'t respond. At least not verbally. He did care about finding the culprit. He just didn\'t want Harry Stone to be the man to do it. “Going back to the \'enemies\' thing,” Stone began. “We\'ve ruled out any links from your day job, but what about your past?” This seemed to strike something with Jason, whose eyes suddenly bolted upwards, meet Stone\'s. “Let\'s see...” said the detective as he thumbed his way through his notes. “Ah yes. Says here that you were involved in some kinda underground street-fighting group for a number of years. I can imagine there\'s be a few unsavoury characters lurking around that scene, no?” Suddenly Jason looked to the floor. “Ya don\'t know a fuckin\' thing \'bout my past, pig.” This is it! thought Stone. He looked up, leaning over the table. “I don\'t know about that, Jason. I\'ve got a whole file here that sug-” “I said you don\'t know a fuckin\' thing!” Jason snarled, balling a fist and crashing it down against the table with such force that Harry jumped. Immediately Jason tried to calm himself down, knowing that a violent reaction to any kind of suggestion from the detective wouldn\'t look good. “You know what?” Jason began, rising from his chair as Stone parted his lips to speak. “I\'m outta this fuckin\' dump. Already half a day late for this fuckin\' show. Any more an\' I\'ll have Tyler breathin\' down my neck. Fuckin\' dragon.” With that he stomped his way around the table and, without pausing for thought, slammed the door behind him as he left. Harry Stone was good, and Jason knew that if he spent any longer in that room he\'d end up divulging information that he wanted to keep to himself. Because if he told Harry Stone who\'d torched his apartment, he\'d never be able to seek bloody vengeance. * * * * * When Harry Stone finally emerged from the interview room he walked with his head high and a spring in his step. Interviewing Jason Natas had been almost like having an argument with a brick wall, but despite the wrestler\'s reluctance to talk, Harry felt like he\'d made waves. Inside the interview room everyone has an act, and Jason Natas was your typical, by the book hard-ass. He played tough at first, sure, but Harry had seen such behaviour from so many suspects in the past, and he knew exactly how to play him. You feed them lines. Give \'em bait. Toy with them a little and eventually they snap. Jason Natas had kept his cool until the moment he was asked about his past. He toyed around with the potemkin village, fed him all that nonsense about the apartment arson (a case that wasn\'t even his to investigate in the first place), and then he rolled out the big guns. Harry\'d struck a nerve. Shuffled across to his desk, he loaded up Jason\'s file on his computer and begin punching letters into his keyboard. His investigation into the Anti-Superstar had only just begun.
