A Matter of Fact

30 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-30-06 ~<>~<>~<>~ Tonight… His hand rang and his muscles sang with pain as Rich Rollins stirred from sleep, shaking off the aftermath of one hell of a hangover. Everything was black. Flexing his shoulders, he tried to rustle himself out of the subconscious and back into the world of waking, but he couldn’t. He blinked. Everything was still black. "So, you’ve decided to join us, then?" came a vaguely familiar voice. "It’s about time, home wrecker." Moving his arms, Rollins found they were bound tightly against the small of his back. He blinked again. Still black. The musty catch in the air told him his head was stuffed inside some kind of sack. A low growl emanated from his throat. His tormentor continued. "It isn’t wise to be so callous, Mr. Rollins. People have feelings. And lives. You understand the meaning of family, yes?" A sharp pain flared across The Renegade’s stomach, courtesy of a steel-toed boot. He coughed, choking on the bag that cut him off from the world, falling to his side. A heavy weight settled at his elbows as the sound of chain link scraped against concrete. "You have to be able to trust someone to call them family," Rollins said as steadily as he could manage. "There’s only three folks I trust: Me, myself, and I. So fuck you." Another kick. Another searing pain in the dark. "Your mother must have skimmed over the chapter on manners, my friend. Obviously she did; after all, you burned her body along with that pretty pregnant woman." The speaker was restrained, but Rollins could hear the biting edge in his voice as the blood boiled in his ears. "I loved my mother, Renegade. Just like I love my wife. Do you love my wife?" Rollins snorted. "Which one?" Groaning into the black abyss, Rollins was introduced to a new friend; a steady barrage of fists beat his head against the pavement, lighting up his dark world for brief moments at a time as his temples met with invisible knuckles. His heart beat fast, but not fast enough to keep pace with the rhythm of his spineless assailants. Finally, it stopped. "That’s enough Markus. We wouldn’t want our special guest to miss the point now, would we?" Struggling back to his knees, The Renegade straightened his screaming backside. "I wouldn’t miss it for the world," he spat. "And I’ve loved a lot of wives. What makes yours so special?" There was silence. Then: "The difference? The difference, Mr. Rollins, is that my wife is the reason you’re going to die tonight." ~<>~<>~<>~ Yesterday… The pale blue turtleneck hugging Lori Goodwin throat did little to hide the fact that, despite her vehement claims to the contrary, she was seeing an abusive man. As a secretary for the political offices of ex-Illinois Governor hopeful, Benjamin Holly, Lori was constantly berated by a slew of stuffy beurocrats who thought they knew best. In public they’d smile, pretending not to notice the dark marks under her chin and the bags under her eyes. In private they would confront her directly, seeking some explanation as to how such an intelligent woman could fall in with some violent heathen. She lied, of course. Not the usual I-fell-down-the-stairs, tripped-and-bumped-my-eye-on-the-counter kind of lies. Lori finally told them exactly what they wanted to hear; yes, she was dating a crude moron who took his aggressions out on her face. Of course she didn’t like it. No, he didn’t have a job. Yes, his mother was nice. No way could she tell them the truth. The truth was, Lori wasn’t dating anybody. She was married to Benjamin Holly, their boss. She was the first-wife to the highest-profile closet polygamist in Illinois, sharing his bed with three other women, one of whom was in a coma after suffering some kind of violent seizure. Ben got angry with her because he was stressed; the weeks leading up to the elections had been hard, and after he had lost to the incumbent Ron Blagojevich, Lori didn’t think things could be any worse. She was wrong. Sitting in a comfortable office chair, making copies of a dozen finance reports and filing them in their proper drawers, Lori had muted her tiny desktop television but kept an eye on the Dow Jones indexes out of old habit. When her husband came on screen to field some questions about his upcoming plans to run for state Senate in lieu of losing the governor race, she hit the volume and sat back, smiling. "… ten percent of what they were, and half of what they will be if Blagojevich listens to anything I’ve said in the last year," her on-screen husband said, dictating to some nameless journalist. "If we can keep a camera on every corner, an eye in every alley, the rate at which fellow Chicagoans are killing one another is going to drop like a cow girl at the ho-down." Lori rolled her eyes. Ben liked to throw out the cheesy catch phrases. It kept his public opinion polls healthy. After a ten minute back-and-forth debate over whether the reigning Serbian select could keep the state of Illinois together for two more months (let alone two more years), Lori began losing interest and turned back to her filing. Her friend Shari Crane from third-floor accounting dropped in to say hello, and they shot the breeze about a new restaurant that had just opened downtown. Lori’s suspicious turtleneck was never brought up. Then, without warning, the last thing Lori ever expected came out of the lips of that nameless journalist: "Are you a practicing polygamist, Mr. Holly?" Lori stopped mid-sentence and turned to her television, mouth wide enough to swallow the Sears Tower. On-screen, Benjamin Holly looked shocked but regained his composure as quickly and professionally as he possibly could. "Do I look like a practicing polygamist, sir?" Ben grinned, one of the best weapons in his political arsenal. "If I had to deal with more than one wife, you think my hair would look this good in the morning?" The journalist allowed a polite laugh, but Lori didn’t find anything funny about the situation. How the hell, how in the hell had anyone…? "Excuse the bluntness, Mr. Holly-" "No offense taken, son." "Right." The journalist cleared his throat. "It’s just that there have been rumors flying around about some of the residents of the infamous Pangea Compound south of town. Some of those rumors have pictures attached to them, and some of those pictures -- allegedly -- are of your vehicle. Do you entertain potential constituents at the Compound?" Sheri Crane hadn’t noticed the horrified look on Lori’s face. She was too busy laughing at the screen. "Are you kidding me? You get a hard-working man like Mr. Holly at your press desk and you waste his time with crap like that?" Lori ignored her. She was suddenly too frightened to breathe. Maintaining his cool façade, Benjamin Holly clapped the young journalist on the shoulder and sighed. "One of these days, son, you’ll learn that it takes more than a single man to keep hold of any one woman, not the other way around. I’m no more a polygamist than I am a dentist!" Laughter from the screen. Lori hit the mute button again, forcing her face to unfreeze. "So," Sheri said, "want to hit up that new place for lunch?" ~<>~<>~<>~ Two days ago… "Fuck! You fucking fuckhead, what the fuck?! I leave you alone two fucking seconds and fucking disappear! Fuck, Rollins! When are you going to fucking learn to keep your fucking comments to yourself and… gahk!" Grabbing a handful of the kid’s hair, Rich Rollins was ready to wind the punk’s arm like a jack-in-the-box, just to see how long it would take before his eyes went pop. He knew he should have gone with someone more professional, but hell, he was all about giving money back to the old neighborhood. Just because the little rat was going to turn around and spend his hard-earned cash on drugs and pussy didn’t mean The Renegade was supposed to feel responsible for his moral well-being. Hell, what was money for anyway? "I didn’t hire you for a lecture in fuckin’ gutter-speak, Teake. If I wanted to hear you use four-letter words, I’d start doing this." Now he started twisting arms. Shrieking like a scalded dog, Teake hopped on his tip-toes as Rollins wheeled him around like a top. "Ow-ow-ow-ow! Fine, I said fine, okay? Fuck, lemme go!" Jerking his arm free, Teake cradled his elbow against his chest and scowled. "You sure got a hell of a way of making friends, don’cha?" "I don’t have friends," Rollins replied with a smile. "I surround myself with people I respect enough not to kill." From the look on his face, Teake believed him. Rollins just shook his head, sighing. "Okay, pipsqueak. Did you get those glossies?" Fishing a manila folder from an old backpack pockmarked with band buttons and a few faded stickers, Teake grinned, albeit gingerly, since he had to root through the thing with his throbbing arm. "You know me, boss. I never let a paying customer go out empty handed. ‘Specially not an old neighbor like you." Rollins snatched the folder before Teake’s toothy yellow grin could grow any more obscene. Unwinding the elastic tie, he groped inside to see just what kind of juicy contents his degenerate private eye was able to squeeze. A thin stack of photographs met his fingertips; unable to control his curiosity, Rollins pulled them free and immediately started flipping through the evidence. What he saw, he surely didn’t like. "That lying…" More importantly, he surely didn’t like anything he saw. "… that lying bitch!" The pictures were perfect, all right. Perfectly viable evidence that the woman who claimed to be his long-lost sister, Lori Goodwin, was nothing but some kind of decoy. Snapshots of her and that sleazy Republican piece of shit politician were everywhere; shots of car rides, shots of private dinners, shots of interlaced fingers as the two walked down a grassy lane; it was almost enough to make Rich Rollins want to snap Teake’s arm anyway. "Most of those were taken at the Pangea Compound," said Teake. "I managed to muscle my way over one of the gates. Destroyed a perfectly good PRIME tee-shirt in the process, in case you cared." "Not really," Rollins replied, still flipping through the pictures. "Figured." Teake sighed. "So, can I go now?" Rollins blinked, finally registering what it was Teake just said. "Wait a second, these were taken where?" "That tight-lipped Mormon cult colony an hour south of here. You know the one; those conservative bible thumping types are always on the news, picketing the place. They’re polygamists." An unusually thoughtful expression breached the surface of Rollins’ brow as he placed the photographs back in their folder. Teake began to say something, but hesitation proved the better part of valor and he kept it to himself. Absently, almost unconsciously, Rollins dipped into his jacket pocket and produced a wad of Benjamins that lit the kid’s face up like a Central Park Christmas tree. Teake took the gangster roll eagerly. "Holy shit, dude! Those pictures were good enough for you, huh?" Already ten paces away, The Renegade shot a look back over his shoulder and said, "Good enough for me? Kid, these beauties are good enough for US Weekly. I’ll teach that bitch to yank a man’s chain, ‘specially if it ain’t the one that counts." Watching Rollins go, Teake frowned. "Sure as hell beats breaking their arm," he mumbled, rubbing his shoulder gingerly. ~<>~<>~<>~ Tonight… Things hadn’t exactly gone as planned for Rollins. Sure, the local nightly news had loved the scandalous possibilities his pictures more than implied; Teake’s stack of glossies earned him a few good favors with some of the columnists at the Tribune, one of the few places in town he had never been able to sink his fingers into. After showing a few young and hungry journalists his juicy payload, he’d been able to have a word or two with one of the editors. They told him the fact checkers would do what they do best, and off Rollins went, feeling more productive than he had in weeks. What he hadn’t counted on, however, was the fact that he wasn’t the only one playing the old cloak and dagger. Benjamin Holly, after his confrontation with the Tribune representative on live television, knew exactly who to go after, and why. Benjamin Holly thought his first-wife was cheating on him, using Rollins as a way to act out against him. He never trusted women, especially the ones he was married to. Rich Rollins thought Lori Goodwin-Holly was playing him for a fool, setting him up for another public crucifixion. He never trusted women, especially women with political ties. Both of them couldn’t have been more wrong. Unfortunately for Rollins, his mistake still found him sitting on his ass, blindfolded and bound, chained between two Hummers and absolutely pissed as hell that he hadn’t figured Lori for a leech sooner. "Stand him up," Benjamin Holly barked. Twin engines roared to life, drowning out any snappy comeback Rollins could have mustered. It didn’t much matter; as the massive suburban tanks began pulling away, the chains at Rollins’ elbows grew a lot tighter than he was comfortable with. Squinting his eyes against the pain (which was also redundant, considering he had a smelly sack tied around his head), The Renegade rose to his feet as the slack in the chain disappeared. Holly gave a slight nod. The Hummers stopped. "I never liked you," said The Prophet. "Good," The Renegade replied, "I never voted for you." Holly slammed his fist into Rollins’ solar plexus, sending Rollins into a painful back-and-forth swing as the taught chain suspended him. He quickly realized what a piñata must feel like. Grabbing a fist full of burlap sack, Holly ripped the shroud over Rollins’ head away, taking a few hairs along for the ride. The Renegade winced, blinking against the sudden stabbing light of the Hummer’s halogen lamps. Finally looking his enemy in the eye, Benjamin Holly shook his head sadly. "You obviously know who I am," said Holly. "And I know who you are, so that should take care of our introductions. Now the real question is, do you know why you’re here?" Trying to shake a tangled mass of sweaty hair from his eyes, Rich Rollins sneered. "Yeah. I figured out your little secret, and now you’re gonna give me a splittin’ headache to put whatever those wives of yours shove up your ass to shame." His sneer turned sour; with a dangerous tint coloring his hoarse speech, The Renegade continued. "You think you scare me, Benny boy? You really think you can get away with murder?" "Why not?" Holly smiled. "You did." Any and all trace of boastful heroism drained from Rich Rollins’ face. "The fuck you know about murder?" he whispered. Clasping his hands behind his back, Benjamin Holly began to walk a wide circle around his captive. Rollins watched the man closely, imagining more ways to break a man’s neck than there were days in the year. "I know more than you may think, you ignorant piece of trailer trash shit. You see that greasy spot underneath that grimy boot of yours?" Holly indicated the pavement for emphasis. "That spot is all that remains of my son. He betrayed me, his faith, his family, his everything. I loved him. I don’t love you." Now standing directly behind Rollins, Holly leaned in close enough to smell the fear that had begun pouring from his captive’s body like emotional sweat. Whispering into The Renegade’s ear, The Prophet practically seethed. "You don’t love anything, least of all yourself. What I’ve built, what I’ve done, is more important than you’ll ever understand. Do you believe in God, Richard?" "As a matter of fact," Rollins said, "go fuck yourself." "I thought not." Pulling back, Benjamin Holly nodded at his sons in the jeeps. "Kill him." Rollins grit his teeth and closed his eyes. The engines screamed, revving with unimaginably painful implication, pounding the thoughts right out of The Renegade’s head and squashing them in a roar of pure horsepower. He squeezed his eyes tight, and for the second time in his life, Rich Rollins knew he was going to die. And he would have, too, if Lori Goodwin-Holly hadn’t smashed her prized Denali Envoy into the back of one of the Hummers. Somehow escaping being torn shoulder-to-chin, The Renegade gasped as the huge rolling suburban tank lurched forward, motivated by the large utility vehicle that was now rammed so far up its tail pipe that he couldn’t tell where the Hummer ended and his rescuer began. Narrowly avoiding a crushing confrontation with the first Hummer’s grille, Rollins dove to the side as one vehicle careened into the other, severing his steel tether like a piece of twine. Even more shocked than Rollins was Holly; he stood by with his jaw slacking in surprise, both palms pressed firmly against his scalp. The Denali’s driver side window rolled down an inch or two as a familiar voice screamed: "RUN!" Rollins didn’t have to be told twice. Pulling himself out of his shocked stupor, Rich "The Renegade" Rollins tore off into the night, leaving ten feet of steel chain in his wake. ~Fin~
