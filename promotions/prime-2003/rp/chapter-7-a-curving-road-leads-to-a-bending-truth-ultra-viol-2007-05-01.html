The aroma of freshly cooked dough electrified her taste buds as she pulled up a seat at the counter, spreading her briefcase on the floor beside her. Ordering a muffin and coffee, Nora Patterson, stared with her gorgeous green eyes into the bottomless pit of her handbag, searching for her mobile phone. Perhaps searching for an escape from the dreary and docile world she’d embark on. “Morning Nora,” a voice called behind her. Twisting her head ever so slightly Nora recognised the scent of Jasmine and Lavender grace upon her nostrils, a smile lacing her lips as Hailey Rose pulled up a stool next to her. Ordering the exact same dish, Hailey turned with a smile and a twinkle in her pale blue eyes. “Shouldn’t you be in San Francisco?” Nora raised an eyebrow and poured a satchel of sugar into her coffee, stirring it absentmindedly she turned her attention to Hailey. “I was supposed to take a sabbatical, but work is just hectic right now.” “Supposed to … being the inauspicious word in that sentence,” Hailey verbalized, and drummed her nails impatiently on the grey counter-top. “And it’s not work, it’s your client or clients. Perhaps her boss, Joseph McClain, was right. She needed a vacation of sorts. Placing the white cup onto its saucer Nora dipped her head and let a sigh emerge from her thin rose lips. “Law is my beat so to speak, I live for the thrill of a good case, the win … a lot like you and journalism!” Hailey sighed, almost mournfully. “Don’t remind me. I had the draft all done, then I turn on the news and …” Hailey’s mobile suddenly sprung to life, cancelling her train of thought as she answered to an unrelenting voice on the other end. Hailey’s eyebrows furrowed and her muffin dropped to the plate. She grumbled an incoherent curse and shut the phone; it seemed her lunch hour was to be cut short by the persistence of her boss. “Bad news?” Nora asked, seeing the expression on Hailey’s visage. “You could say that. My meeting with the Editor is in an hour.” Nora patted Hailey’s back in a reassuring and friendly manner. “Alright, no more work related talk, we’ll keep it casual …” Nora suggested with a calming tone, and downed the last drop of coffee. “I guess, but unfortunately I must pry with one question. How are things going between you and Pierce?” Nora’s eyes went wide and her face slightly reddened. “What?” “I meant the case, his case …” Hailey replied, noticing a slight redness in Nora’s pale complexion. Embarrassed by her reaction she took a bite of muffin and waited till she’d swallowed it. “Not too well, he’s a closed book and that doesn’t sit well with the judge, especially when bits of his past could come up.” Hailey nodded her head as she listened to Nora vent, seemingly happy with the discussion, Nora relaxed a little and let out an exasperated yawn. Hailey checked the clock and rose off the stool, placing a hand on Nora’s shoulder. “Give him time, Nora. With Pierce, sometimes his past takes time to uncover,” Hailey declared. Hoisting her briefcase and handbag up off the floor, she continued. “Try to relax a little more.” Nora turned sharply on the stool, regretting the movement as she strained her neck. Rubbing it therapeutically she followed Hailey having paid the bill. “He’s got trust issues?” “From a psychological perspective, yes, you would call them ‘trust issues’!” “He hates psychology,” Nora stated, moving alongside Hailey and rubbing her neck with the tips of her fingers, massaging the strained muscle. Hailey smiled at the statement and plodded along to the cab stand. “Give it time, it’ll all work out.” Hailey stuck her hand out and hailed a cab. “Just that we’ve less than a day till court and …” Nora paused as the cab pulled to a screeching halt beside them. Hailey opened the back door and slid into the passenger seat, a defeated look in her eyes. “Give it time, Nora. I’m off to meet my executioner …” Nora thanked Hailey and watched her drive off. She stood alone on the side walk as chilled air fluttered down her back. Jones gulped, hoping this question would be avoided. “A year and four months, your honour.” The court room fell silent, both sides waiting for the Judge to utter a word. Jones mumbled to Lillian. Rush produced a manila folder from under his jacket, a beaming smile across his face as he kissed Calleigh’s forehead. Nora turned to Pierce with a smile. “Don’t worry. He should grant equal custody, they usually do in these circumstances.” Don’t worry, easy for you to say Pierce thought as he twiddled his thumbs under the desk and bounced his feet up and down, impatient as he sat in the uncomfortable wooden chair. “Unless there is a reason for this withholding of information, I see no reason as to why both parents shouldn’t have time with the child.” Jones stood up, objecting to the Judge’s prior speech. “The reason she left him, your honour, was because he was on drugs.” “OBJECTION!” Nora bellowed. “This has nothing to do with a custody hearing.” The Judge turned his attention to Nora and looked at the father, Pierce Lavelle. He looked worn, lethargic and beaten-up. He inched closer, his hammer in his grip. “Denied, care to rebut, Miss Patterson?” “My client was working on a case pertaining to the selling of an illegal sports enhancement drug. Wherein he was injected, against his will, with a lethal dose and spent six months in rehab for this. He has been clean a year and managed to help stop the selling of this drug, your honour.” The Judged nodded his head, impartial to both sides, he pondered a moment. “Would the mother or father like to add a closing statement?” Lavelle stood up suddenly, shocking both Nora and the defence. “I would like to say something, your honour,” Pierce replied, wobbling a little as the dizziness ensued. “Proceed.” “I know my job plants restrictions and requires a lot of my time, but I love my daughter, and she needs to be with her father. All I ask is that I be granted time with her, please don’t deny me rights to my child.” “She’s not yours …” The entire court room, which contained a small amount of people, turned toward Lillian as she stood up and slammed her palm into the table. Lavelle was stood in shock, mid sentence. “What …? What do you mean?” he stammered. The Judge ordered both Jones and Nora to the podium. Jones brought with him the manila folder, Nora looked worried. “Care to explain the unnecessary outburst from Mrs. Rush, Mr. Jones?” “I believe I can. I hold in my hand a paternity test, taken recently, in which it states that the child belongs to a Mr. Tony Rush.” Nora grabbed the documents and looked stunned; she continuously flicked through the pages. Unable to bring herself to say a word she turned to see Lavelle sat in shock. “Well I see no need to prolong this hearing. Upon the review of new evidence, I grant full custody to Mr. and Mrs. Rush.” The slamming of the hammer against the wooden block echoed coldly through the room. Lavelle turned with venom in his eyes, and jumped out of the chair toward Lillian. “Why? Why the hell did you make me believe she was mine?” Lillian remained still. “I’m sorry, I didn’t know till now.” “Till now? You said she was …” Lavelle moved forward but was held back by the bailiff. “I’m really sorry,” Lillian muttered and ran off after Rush and Calleigh. Lavelle turned to the bailiff and shrugged him off. “I’m fine, alright.” The bailiff reluctantly let Lavelle go and walked off; Nora moved up beside him and placed a hand on his shoulder. “I’m so sorry.” Lavelle looked toward the exit, distant and weary. He watched Lillian take Calleigh in her arms, she didn’t look back. Of all the feelings, confusion was the most evident and he doubted whether he’d ever understand why Lillian had done this to him, but it wouldn’t matter to try, he doubted he’d see her again. “Me too …” he stated. Moving toward the exit, Nora followed him and remained silent; she looped her hand in his for comfort and gazed toward the morning sunshine. In all the years of family law, this wasn’t the first time she’d seen this. A mother’s confusion always hurt one party member. Lavelle felt her hand in his and it calmed him. Despite the proceedings his life would move on, whether he was ready or not and one event coming up on the horizon was Ultra Violence. All he knew now was that he would face Jonathan Winters willing and able and burning for revenge … … He would be ready …He won’t stand down … …He’ll get his revenge
