Catching Up - Chapter 3 - The Trial (Part I)

14 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 14, 2009 THURSDAY MAY 14, 2009 The remnants of sleep were still in Bryan’s eyes as he sat in the last row of Room 4 inside the Hilo County Courthouse. The beard was still present, the hair still uncut yet more tidily kept than it was a week ago, and the eyes were still covered by the same monotone black sunglasses. His attire wasn’t much better, either. A signature “BRUH” t-shirt overtop of a pair of tattered jeans and flip flops was the clothing choice that morning, and it was clear that even after Dawkins’ meeting with his lawyer a week beforehand, Bryan wasn’t feeling all that confident. As the judge handed out his final verdict to a low-life drunk driver, bodies began to shuffle from bench to bench, and it seemed as though everyone in the courtroom knew each other. Bryan, however, stayed put on the bench furthest back from the judge, who had already given out multiple jail sentences and other probationary sentences. He wasn’t anxious to be there at 9:30 in the morning, but he couldn’t really think of anyone who would have been. His head hung low and eyes continually drooped as he fought off the urge to fall asleep… “Bryan!” …When his lawyer (kind of) had finally arrived. “Yeah bruh?” With a sleazy smile on his face, Harper took a long swig of whatever he had inside that brown thermos and took a seat next to his most famous client. Harper was the kind of guy who loved the smell of convictions in the morning, and his demeanor definitely showed it. With that crooked smile on his face and an entire bottle of gel in his hair, slicking it back, Harper was in his comfort zone. “You ready to get your kid back?” “First off, bruh, it’s Nicholas. And second, yeah, if you can do it, I’m ready.” Harper took another deep gulp from his thermos and smiled. “Good, ‘cuz we’re next.” Within seconds, the bailiff’s voice echoed throughout the courtroom. “Next we’ve got a child custody case brought forward by the plaintiffs, Kenneth and Sarah Dawkins and Michael and Pamela Sivec, against the defendant, Bryan Dawkins. Please rise for the Honorable Judge Raymond Mayhan.” Everyone in attendance rose to their feet as Judge Mayhan entered the room. Surprisingly to Bryan, this was a different judge than the one who had been throwing out harsh sentences left and right since Bryan had arrived in the courtroom. Mayhan stood about 5’6” and maybe 150 pounds, and wasn’t intimidating by any stretch of the imagination. His snow white hair was thinning considerably and the wrinkles on his face were deep and telling of his older age. He raised a hand to the mass of people and sat slowly. From Bryan’s point of view, all that could be seen of the judge was his head. Peering across the way, he saw his family, both immediate and in-laws, for the first time in months. His mother nearly burst into tears upon seeing her obviously distraught son, while Kenneth simply stared straight ahead. Meanwhile, Mike and Pam Sivec looked at Bryan with deep, caring eyes; he knew they didn’t want this situation to occur. At times, to Bryan, it seemed like his father was the only one who was adamant about this whole thing, because it didn’t appear that anyone else wanted to be here at the courthouse fighting over Bryan’s first son. As the parents’ lawyer Melinda Anderson addressed her opening statements, Bryan’s gaze was fixed on his mother. Her eyes were filled with tears, causing her eyeliner to run down her cheeks, leaving a black trail from her eyelids to her jawline. Bryan felt bad for her; he knew she wanted to have nothing to do with this situation. She was just doing what she was supposed to do: stand behind her man, support his decisions, and assist him in any way possible. She was being a good wife. Sadly, in being a good wife in this circumstance, she wasn’t being such a good mother. “My clients have all of the right in the world to seek joint custody over Nicholas Chandler Dawkins, because his father, Mr. Bryan Dawkins, wanted to take his newborn son out onto the road with him, traveling along with his WRESTLING company!” Bryan began to stir in his seat. A cold sweat was developing, and slight twinges of rage began to overcome his body. “I think we’re all in agreement when I say that taking a newborn child out on the road for over 40 weeks per year is completely unacceptable. Your honorable, I personally think this is an open-and-shut case. Mr. Dawkins has no business fathering a child…” Dawkins, very discreetly muttered. “Shut up…” Immediately snapping his focus over to Dawkins, Judge Mayhan scowled at the young Hawaiian. “Have something to add, Mr. Dawkins?” Dawkins shook his head slowly, avoiding eye contact at all times. Anderson, on the other hand, was staring bullet holes into the defendant. “No? Well then I suggest that you keep your mouth shut until asked to do otherwise. Mrs. Anderson? Please continue.” Anderson nodded, while Dawkins slumped in his chair. “…like I was saying, Mr. Dawkins doesn’t have any business fathering a child, ESPECIALLY if he’s planning for Nicholas to live out on the road with him. He’s unfit to be a father, and it’s sad that we all have to be in this courtroom during such a pointless hearing—“ “You don’t know my motives, so I suggest you shut the fuck up…” Again, Bryan opened his mouth. And again, Judge Mayhan wasn’t having it. “MISTER DAWKINS.” Dawkins’ head lifted a little, while Judge Mayhan continued in a harshly stern voice. “This is your final warning. Unless you’d like to spend the remainder of this trial in jail, I suggest you keep quiet.” Again, Bryan couldn’t quite figure out when to keep his mouth shut. “I’m already in jail at my house, bruh…so it’s no difference.” Mayhan’s face turned bright red and the stern voice became a billowing shout. “MISTER DAWKINS, I’M HOLDING YOU IN CONTEMPT FOR THE REMAINDER OF THIS TRIAL! BAILIFF, TAKE HIM TO THE HOLDING CELLS.” And with that, the bailiff removed the handcuffs from his belt and placed them around Dawkins’ wrists. Dawkins was now getting what he wanted. He had no desire to be at the trial, and had he not been at the courthouse that day, he would’ve ended up in the same place he was headed. Jail.
