An Empty Glass (Revolution 190, Tag Team Match with Jason Natas vs. Tyler Rayne & Chandler Tsonda)

8 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/8/09 "Another round, Wade?" He blinked, pulling the plug on the daydream behind his sharp blue eyes. An empty tumbler sat comfortably between his rough paws, one ice cube melting away in the bottom. He shook his head, and heaved himself to his feet with a groan. "Naw, that'll do it." "Thirty-five dollars." The drifter known as Wade Elliott pulled a fifty dollar bill from his wallet, dropping it onto the the modest bar top. "The Dead Goat Saloon." The name itself had caught Wade's attention upon traveling into Salt Lake City, Utah, location for PRIME's 190th installment of ReVolution. Of course, as per the usual of bars, taverns and dives in 2009, the public consisted mostly of young college kids and ski bums, slurring pick up lines, passing out in their stools, or puking their guts up after too many green martinis, soda and vodkas, red-headed bitches or whatever kids drank these days. Whatever happened to cowboy bars? Wrong state, apparently. But lucky for Wade Allen Elliott, a dark, tired, thirty-seven year old sore thumb in the midsts of young adult bliss, the The Dead Goat still had a bottle of Jim Beam to keep him company. "Twenty-five's your change, Wade." The Bad Dog waved it off, leaving the cash on the bar and turning away. He breathed in deep, tucking his wallet into the back of his worn out blue jeans. He went to adjust his drifter's cap, but soon realized it wasn't there, having left it on the floor to the hands of Lindsay Troy at a Revolution some weeks ago. Instead, he ran his fingers through his unkempt brown hair, exhaled through his nose, and stepped toward the exit. "Leaving so soon?" Wade's eyes shifted to the soft sound of a feminine voice to his right. He turned, and then froze. Boom. Bombshell. Jesus fucking Christ. Glorious, shining, straight brunette hair falling over soft, delicate shoulders. A pair of beautiful brown eyes and a set of lips that would make Sean Connery start to stutter. A rack that could kill a lesser man pushing through a black top leading down to a pair of flared out blue jeans that fit in EVERY right place, the hems resting over a pair of shiny leather high-heeled boots that should really be reserved for the bedroom. Of course, Wade was speechless. She smiled, taking a step closer to the wide-eyed Blue Collar Brawler. "My name's Rochelle, you are...?" Elliott cleared his throat. "Wade," he managed to push through his lips. He brought a finger to tip his non-existent drifter's hat, eliciting another smile from Rochelle. "You drivin' home, Wade?" she cooed. "Not home," he said. "Wherever the road takes me...I s'pose..." "I just watched you drink seven whiskeys tonight," she laughed. "Are you sure you're okay to drive?" "Oh, don't worry 'bout that, darlin'. My bloodstream's prob'bly a good 75% Jim Beam at this point." She giggled, resting a dainty hand on his shoulder. "Funny, but maybe I should drive you home. I've got a comfortable spot, quiet..." Wade coughed into his hand, stepping uncomfortably to the side. "Appreciate it, Ro...Roch...." "Rochelle." "Right, Rochelle. But I think I'll be just fine." He offered a forced smile, eyes darting from her face to the floor. She stared at him, mouth slightly open and eyes squinted in that "is this guy joking?" kind of look. But she closed her eyes, smiled again, and grabbed his wrist with one hand, retrieving a pen from her pocket in the other. "Suit yourself, Stud..." she said, scribbling into Wade's bewildered palm. "...but if you manage to come to your senses, don't be afraid to give me a call." She winked, and patted the side of his face with her hand, then sauntered away into the crowd. Wade stood transfixed a moment, shook his head, breathed deep, and took off out the door. * * * * * * * The back of his skull hit the head-rest as he slammed the driver's side door to his truck closed. He dragged a hand down his face, and shook his head left and right. He looked downward, and opened his palm, revealing the seven digits that served as a key to what would no doubt be the most ecstasy filled night of his god damn life. He stared, then closed his fist. Then reached upward with the other. He plucked a photograph from behind his sun-visor, flipped on the interior light, and held the photo in front of him. In the photo stood himself and Lindsay Troy, arms over each other's shoulder, backstage at the end of a ReVolution months and months ago. She was smiling brightly, eyes closed from the laughter of something said that Wade couldn't remember. Even he smiled in the picture, a rare grin next to the only human being on this earth that he ever truly gave a damn about. He closed his eyes, bringing that hand to his forehead. He exhaled deeply, returning the photo to the visor before turning the key and bringing the Chevrolet to roaring life. Angus the Bluetick Hound, no longer asleep, popped his head off the passenger's seat. "Sorry, Pup." Angus rested his head again. Wade pulled out of the parking lot and onto the main road, headlights from traffic whipping back and forth. "Go back to sleep."
