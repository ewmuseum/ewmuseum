The Origin Of The Species: Part I - Life & Death

11 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-10-09 Thick treads thrummed over the blacktop as I pulled the F-150 into the entrance of the New Park Cemetery. The sun shone brightly in the sky but a cold wind betrayed its warm glow, moaning hauntingly through the tombstones and throwing up dead leaves from the well-maintained grass. They blew onto the road so I could only just hear the crisp crunch beneath the wheels over the purring of the engine. The graveyard was almost empty save for a few couples and one family mewling over their respective dead. Cruising over the first hill I gazed over the vast stretch of land dotted with those forlorn markers. On the crest of the next knoll ahead the groundskeeper tottered along in his ride-on tractor mower, hunched over the wheel half-heartedly as though each day on the job was sucking the life from him little by little. I reckoned he was in the right profession at any rate as I let out an anxious sigh and slouched against the backrest, resting my hands at my groin and thumbing the steering wheel around the wide meandering macadam. I greeted the melancholy gardener with a slight nod, at which he must have surely grunted because he never returned the gesture, instead curling his lip and grumbling off away on his lonesome. I noticed upon entering the cemetery that the gravestones were mostly carved of stone and pockmarked with little acid rain craters. The messages which once upon a time had been cleanly chiselled into the stones were now faint indentations in the rock, barely distinguishable from a natural groove. The further I travelled the newer the tombstones appeared, the more well-tended and colourful the plots, decorated with lively little bunches of flowers. Eventually the crowded rows of tombs petered out into grass land all the way to the boundary which still lay a few miles beyond the last grouping of plots. Turning off the main road I continued along past the latest row of tombstones and slowed my truck to a snail's pace so that I could read the names as I drove. Teddy bears and various other personal trinkets lay at the heads of the tombs, and before I even arrived in front of the one I was looking for I could tell I had found it. It was the only tombstone on the row without any form of decoration or memorial other than the paltry gold lettering scribed into the black marble. Braking the truck to a halt I killed the noise and sighed heavily. Gloria Carmichael 1981 – 2009 “How the hell did a two-bit whore wind up in the same resting place as Bukka White?” I asked myself. Stepping out of the F-150 I pulled the collar of my coat up and tucked my chin down in a feeble attempt to cover myself against the nipping wind. Shoving my hands into my pockets I plodded over to the plot and felt my soul sinking into my cold feet. The earth over the coffin hadn't quite settled yet and lay in a mound before the tombstone, topped with sprigs of growing grass. At first I drew a blank on emotion. By rights I guess I should have started blubbering and wailing over my lost love. Perhaps if the shrew hadn't ripped my heart out I would have, instead I found my mind wandering back to a phone call from one Beth Marquis. Let me explain... In the run-up to PRIME's Great American Nightmare I spent a few days more than usual in Waco preparing to annihilate Devin Shakur. The reason for my lingering presence boiled down to an aimless outlook on life after Gloria's death, if I might be frank. I had nowhere to go, no home to return to, at least not one I wanted to visit. Apart from Michael Sloan and his wife I was pretty much alone on the road, living out of my truck and bedding down in whatever hotel I fancied the look of. My reason for being was simply to compete in PRIME. I had no life outside of wrestling. Sure, I met new people in each town and had a laugh, then went to work and met up with all the other PRIMEates, but there were no mainstays in my life outside of Gloria, and when she went behind my back with that prick hairdresser and got herself killed I was on my own yet again. Beth Marquis first introduced herself to me under the assumed identity of “Luanne”. As usual I was holed up in some dive letting time pass me by and getting bevvied when she approached me, exuding confidence and a boyish attitude that I found endearing. She was stocky but muscular, with a mane of wild blonde curls. She told me she was a bodybuilder and spent the night knocking back shots with me. I thought I'd found a friend, and in retrospect I had, however I later learned she was an undercover cop. Two detectives name of Barnes and Greco were assigned to Gloria's case after she was found murdered in my home beside the body of her lover, Larry DeLancy. I had few dealings with the detectives, meeting with them firstly to identify Gloria's corpse and thereafter going down to Union Station in Memphis to answer questions. As far as I was concerned that was where business finished between us, I explained that on the night of her death I was hundreds of miles away at Fenway Park winning the Elite Title, thus clearing my name before the investigation got under way...or so I thought. What I didn't know was that after turning up absolutely nothing in terms of a lead or a suspect the detectives went about trying to pin the double homicide on me anyway. They figured with lack of prints, DNA or any evidence whatsoever it had to be me, what with Gloria cheating on me and the killings taking place in my own home. They spent two long months tracking down character witnesses and affiliates in a bid to figure me out, even visiting a drug dealer called Reuben Blanco in Terre Haute in a bid to dig up some kind of dirt on me. Blanco and I had history dating back a few years after I laid out one of his loan sharks. He tried to recoup the money he lost and when his dirty tactics failed I ran out with Gloria and went about getting my life back on track. Anyway, Blanco lied that he knew I had hired a contract killer to do the job for me, turned out the so-called hitman was a mob doctor called Guillermo Menendez, one of Blanco's close associates. The only blood on his hands was that of his patients. With the case on the verge of being closed regardless of whether an arrest was made or not, the detectives tailed me to Waco and staked me out. They offered up Marquis as bait to either get me to confess or fall prey to my murderous hand, or so they thought. I just thought she was a honey with a good head on her shoulders and a heart as big as her biceps. After getting to know her I asked her out. We had a pretty fun time until we got back to the hotel and she told me she had to run out for something. I waited a few hours in the bar of the Hilton and even caught up with Sloan and Tracy and had a few drinks with them to pass the time and ease my nerves. Eventually I gave up hope and the night was a bust. A couple of days later I got a call from “Luanne” who spilled the beans on the whole plot, what her real name was and what the detectives had planned for me. Needless to say I was shocked to discover I had been deceived, and horrified to learn that while I was on my lonesome propping up the bar she had gone to check in with Barnes and Greco and discovered local law enforcement surrounding their stakeout vehicle, an old beat up RV. Inside the camper van lay the bodies of both men, their throats cut and their lives taken from them. Being that I was with her at the time of the murders I had an irrefutable alibi, which gave the Captain of the Memphis precinct due cause to shut the case. I was officially in the clear, while somewhere out there lurked the madman that butchered my girlfriend and her lover, and now two homicide detectives. I shuddered at the thought that someone had randomly entered my home and committed this terrible crime, and somehow by coincidence the trail of death had followed me to Texas where the detectives were slain. I didn't feel pain or guilt or sadness for Gloria. I stood over her grave thinking about those two detectives that had lost their lives trying to find her killer. I thought about Beth, who I still couldn't decide if I liked or not. I thought about how close I had come to falling victim to the stranger that had mutilated Gloria under my roof. In fact the last thing on my mind was the dead thing lying six feet beneath me. I tilted my head back and closed my eyes, taking in a breath of fresh air in a bid to calm myself. For as long as I could remember my life had been one wrong turn after another, leading me down a bad road I never intended to travel in the first place, but I dealt with it anyway. In the stillness of the cemetery and with only my thoughts for company I had time to reflect on everything. I wasn't sorry for Gloria, in fact I was more concerned with myself and what I had lost, and her tomb was perhaps the greatest symbol for just that. Wrestling was my life. I excelled at it and I was loved for it. I had worked damn hard over the years to get to where I am today. And it was all I had to my name. Ask anybody about Hessian, they all said the same thing. Legend of the ring. Giant among men. One of the most brutal competitors ever to step foot in the squared circle. Ask them about Von Kelsig? You got the same answers. To the people, Von Kelsig was the man behind Hessian, the soul of the Hardcore Monster. To me? Von Kelsig was a prisoner trapped behind the demon eyes of a tyrannical heathen. Nobody knew the real me, knew who the real Von Kelsig was. Gloria did. But she was the only one. Michael Sloan had scratched the surface but he didn't have much of a better clue than Adam. Truth is, there wasn't a Von Kelsig. I didn't have a life beyond the ring. PRIME and PTC were my life. Other wrestlers had an easier time of it. Some had businesses outside of the industry, some were property magnates and others got by on their model looks. Me? I had nothing. When I stepped away from the business to try and open my own bar in Detroit I had it taken from me by Reuben Blanco who wanted to get even after I took out one of his loan sharks. When I tried to settle down with the love of my life she was taken away from me by a sleazy fuck, and why? All because I dipped my toe back into the industry because apparently I wasn't fit to work anywhere else. Like I said, wrestling was my life...I wasn't trained for anything else. Everywhere I went I was haunted by Hessian. My business and my woman were taken from me and afterwards I found myself drawn towards wrestling, as though it were some kind of calling. No matter how hard I tried to stay away I always got pulled back in. Call it destiny or fate or some derivative of the inevitable, that was what I felt and the proof of it lay in my success throughout 2009. Never before had Hessian seen glory as he had in this year. An Intense Title, an Elite Title, a high ranking in one of the toughest tournaments in the sport and on top of that...a new-found respect from my peers. The Murder Show barely saw the spotlight of the main even shine on him before this latest run in PRIME, and already I was being touted as the man who would dethrone the almighty Jason Snow for his precious Universal Title. I ran my tongue over my lips and pursed them in a bid to fend off the biting cold. Reaching into the deep slash pocket of my coat I removed a single black rose and knelt over the grave, placing the slightly crumpled flower on the dirt. In silence I bid her farewell and rose to my feet, grunting as my knees popped on the way up. With one final look at her tomb I turned and took leave of her once and for all. Plumped firmly back in the driver seat I fired up the engine and set the air-con on lobster cycle. A few revolutions later I was warm and cosy once again. I never looked back once as I drove away from the graveyard and left that heartbreaking chapter of my life behind me, dead and buried. The F-150 had barely trundled out of the cemetery when my cell rang. Rather than fumble with the wheel and take the call simultaneously I let it ring out and focused on putting distance between myself and the graveyard. I was scheduled for a guest appearance on a radio station in Chi-Town and still had a day or so to drive up to Illinois. Thoughts of success and titles and glory echoed in my mind as New Park disappeared under the horizon of the rear view mirror. If wrestling was such a strong force in my life I was glad that I stood tall as one of the most dominant forces in the industry, rather than be a cop-out like so many others who tried and failed before me. I was a two-time PTC Champion, I upheld the honour of PRIME by wiping my ass with Desade and Wyatt Connors after my home took a pounding at the hands of SCCW last year. Sure I dropped a loss to Tony Gamble a few weeks ago, but given the circumstances of my personal life at the time who could blame me? About time the little bastard got his act together instead of getting under people's feet anyway. That one fluke aside he only individuals to defeat me in singles competition this year were Jason Snow, Chandler Tsonda and Jonathan Rhine. Everyone else was lucky to walk away from a match with the Murder Show. The daunting prospect of the next few months crept into my brain as I reminisced about my achievements. As Elite Champion I seemed to be in for quite a wait while contenders skittered out from the woodwork and fought for a chance to face me for the big one. Business in PRIME was as usual, with Devin Shakur being the latest prize dick to piss me off, and with the Jewel in the Crown fast approaching I felt more than ready to lay the smack down on the other unfortunate entrants into the tournament. Then there was that other tournament. That cursed behemoth that once in a while reared its ugly head and sang its siren song, luring all and sundry to its warm embrace. The Golden Turnbuckle Tournament. Or was it Global Title Tournament? It had been so damn long since I participated in it I couldn't even recall the name...all I knew was that with an exemplary record in the second GTT and a monstrous streak behind me since my return at the start of the year I just had to see what all the fuss was about. I knew a lot of the names entered into the tournament, recognized a few more and ignored those I didn't. One name especially rang out, belonging to a man I had encountered before. Jesse Jamester. Coupled with an unknown called Steve Harrison those two made up my opposition in the first round. I chuckled at the recollection of standing across from Jamester in my first Global Title match, how he had fought tooth and nail against myself, Chris Storm and a couple others I forget. It was a ladder match, I remembered. He tried his best to upset me, but it just wasn't enough. “Ladder match. Haha, how the hell did a seven-foot, mask-wearing monster pull that one off?” I muttered under my breath as the first sign for I-55 came up on my right. Jamester had been on as much of a hiatus as me, if not longer. As far as I knew he hadn't been in active competition in a long time, and he certainly hadn't achieved the kind of success I was relishing. In a way I almost pitied him. Through the grapevine I learned this first round match was to be his chance to leave a mark in PTC one more time, for maybe the last time. Same thing he said the last time before that. He lingered on my mind as I pulled onto I-55, blasting the horn at some asshole in a Mercedes who thought he was too good to switch lanes and let me on. Maybe I was being too harsh on Jesse. Who was I to judge the wants of others, or their needs when I was wide open to judgement on my own? I remember clearly on my first night back at ReVolution 185 thinking the same thing he was now, and look where I ended up. Maybe all he needed was that first victory to get the ball rolling again. He certainly wasn't a curtain jerker, Christ he held his own against me in our altercations. A win over the Elite Champion would have put him on the map. It went without saying he needed this more than I did, hell I only entered to show PTC just how dominant their Elite Champion was. Losing wouldn't affect me in the slightest, I still had the belt and the respect and the power. I could have given Jesse that leg up, the chance to prove himself to even tougher opposition as the tournament progressed. Or I could have rained hell down upon him and given him the fight he desired. Maybe that was what he wanted all along. Maybe that was what I would give him. He deserved that much. “Shit.” I said aloud, remembering suddenly that the phone had rang when I left the cemetery. Checking my mirrors I pulled onto the hard shoulder and parked up, grabbing my phone out of my pants pocket and flipping the facia open. Sure enough I had one missed call and a voicemail waiting. I didn't recognize the number, and hoped nobody at PRIME management had been trying to get a hold of me only to be ignored. “...Hey big guy it's Killer the ringpost-phobiac here...” the message began, and I knew at once it was Killean Sirrajin, greeting me with an inside joke pertaining to a spot we shared during a match at ReVolution 190 where I ripped off one of the ringposts and went to town on him with it. Good old Killean, always up for a laugh. “...Listen, a young man named Ryan Trent contacted me through one of my charities asking for you, he heard a PRIME wrestler was their patron but he didn't know that that was me. Apparently he needs to see you about something important, a family matter of some sort. Says it's imperative he speak with you. Don't worry it's not a charity issue, he just went through that channel to try and find you. I told him your schedule is hectic, so he gave me an address you can find him at. 5200 Park Avenue, Detroit. He asked for your cell but you know how people are when they get a celebrity's phone number, I said an address would be preferable. Anyway if you're gonna be in Detroit any time soon stop by and give him a shout, would ya? Thanks, and hey hit me up if you're ever in my neck of the woods, huh? Don't be a stranger. Later man...” The automated voice asked me if I wanted to replay or delete the message. I ignored it and closed the cell over and pocketed it. “Park Avenue?” I said to myself, unfamiliar with the name. I tensed for a moment, then quickly relaxed when I remembered that Reuben Blanco and all his cohorts were dead or behind bars. Jesus, was that going to haunt me forever, every time I thought about or went to the Motor City? I didn't recall any Ryan Trent either. Maybe I had met him at some point, signed an autograph or shared a beer, hell if I knew. Why would he, or anybody, want me for family business? Did he have a Hessian mark for a kid brother? I was intrigued to say the least, and with that radio slot booked in Chicago I would be in the general area. I would coast up I-55, then onto the 57 outside of Sikeston, Missouri and ride the Interstate all the way to the Windy, fulfil my duties there then take 94 over to Detroit and find out what Ryan Trent wanted. All in a day's work for a world-class wrestler. Concentrating on the road I waited for my opening and tore out over the macadam back into the traffic flow, roaring up the Interstate towards whatever lay ahead of me. Even if it wasn't good news, maybe I had dipped the sister of some overprotective sibling, it couldn't be any worse than the shit storm I had just rode out with Blanco and Gloria. Could it? ---- GPS was a wonderful thing. Normally if I were travelling from venue to venue I wouldn't need directions, but for side-jaunts like the one I was now on it came in very handy. I had left Chicago shortly after my appearance on the radio, just another typical interview to plug PRIME and the Elite Title. I wondered how often Connors had hyped the belt when he had it, after all I only learned he was champion when Tyler Nelson rewarded my loyalty with a shot at it. When the monolithic skyscrapers and clogged freeways gave way to crumbling apartment buildings, derelict sites and potholed streets lined with beat up old cars I sensed something might be fishy about this. Regardless I pushed on, I had seen plenty of rough neighbourhoods in my time and this was no different. The F-150 chugged along, happily bouncing over the rough tarmac and taking the jagged surface in its stride. The streets were almost empty, though there was an abundance of vehicles parked up on either side of me along Henry Street. To my left I saw the Comet bar, a desolate looking joint amongst the concrete with a lovely view of several dead trees and a few burned out wrecks lining the rear of the parking lot. The GPS still read Henry and I was glad not to have been headed there as a group of three black men poured out of the entrance, two of them pummelling the third as he fell away from them pleading with his hands held high. I passed by unnoticed and sighed. What a sorry state of affairs. Rubbing the film of sweat off my palms onto my jeans I hit the next junction and saw the finish line before me, just as the frigid automaton confirmed it. “You have reached your destination.” it announced. I pulled up outside the front entrance and studied the building. BAR*MUSIC experience detroit punk here read the faded banner draped above the segmented window. Awesome, at least this Trent fellow had the decency to meet in a bar with good music. I got out of the cab and stretched my arms in a V, happy to be out of the truck for the time being. Leaning on the hood I bent my legs a couple of times each and rotated my ankles working circulation back into them. Giving the bar another once over I clucked my tongue against my teeth and made my way in. The building itself was an old dilapidated brown-brick affair, with red guttering lining the parapet walls. Some kind of crumpled silver bunting hung lifelessly underneath the windows, each one framed, the white paint flaking off. The sign jutting out over the door was equally inviting, an ancient looking arrow lined with broken bulbs pointing inward that read: 2500 Club LIVE ENTERTAINMENT BAR drink specials nightly HAPPY*FUN*TIME Who were they kidding? The place was a dump. I held no high hopes for the interior, and pushing the door open to be greeted with the stench of cigarette smoke and stale liquor I was glad to be a pessimist. As I suspected the bar was a reflection of the exterior. Dark, dank and full of shifty people. There was a notable absence of the “detroit punk” advertised outside. Seeing a seven foot Viking ducking into the room had a couple of the punters rubbing their eyes and blinking in disbelief. There were several patrons enjoying beers, five propping the bar up and a few others seated at one of the rickety tables at the side of the room, their dejected gazes hidden behind the cloying plumes of tobacco smoke. “Ryan Trent?” I asked loudly and authoritatively. Big white dude with a beard and scars criss-crossing his body like a road atlas? Of course no one spoke up. The bartender stared at me as he cleaned out a pint glass and placed it amongst the others beneath the sluice. While his customers were mainly clad in garish shirts and cargo pants or ripped jeans, he was well groomed, his wiry afro cropped close to his scalp and a bushy moustache hanging over his top lip like a wary caterpillar. His chequered shirt was tucked into his waistline and a surprisingly clean apron hugged his torso. “What bidness ya got here boy?” He asked in a gruff tone. The bar boys parted gingerly as I approached the bar, noticing the old tube television up in the corner was showing a baseball game when the flickering static would allow it. I struggled to hold back a cough in the miasma of smoke and a growing reek of body odour. The creaky ceiling fan did nothing for the circulation in the room. Standing proudly over the bar I placed my hands onto the scratched, stained oak and looked him dead in the eye. “I got business with Ryan Trent. Where's he at?” I growled. The scraping of wood on wood drew my attention to the three gents sat at the table, who promptly got up out of their seats and made their exit. Weren't I trying to make my presence felt I'd have let loose a bellowing laugh at the sight of their cockroach-like scuttle to safety. I must have looked like some kind of badass biker hitman type to them. The bartender grumbled something I couldn't quite make out and returned to serving up another round to the stiffs on either side of me, too afraid to move in my presence. “Ain't bringing' no trouble to young Ryan is ya?” one of the eldest of the bunch asked me, his bug eyes twitching nervously, though I noticed that almost as soon as I walked in. He was too far gone to be nervous of a giant like me. “No trouble at all my good man. He wanted to meet me here, I take it you know him?” I replied politely, surprising them all with my sudden soft tone. “Hell we all know each other 'round here.” Another piped up between drags of his roll-up. “He in the bathroom.” As if on cue a gurgling flush rumbled through the room as the toilet door squeaked open and a man emerged fiddling with the fly on his pants and mumbling to himself. He appeared as well-groomed as the clientèle, though much much skinnier and a lot whiter. His hair was tousled and greasy, his black overalls smeared with an even thicker, blacker oil. His arms and face were scored with streaks of the stuff too, and at one time he would have been strikingly handsome before weary creases and lines marred his fair complexion. He took one look at me and froze, his Adam's apple bobbing nervously. “Uhh...” He gaped. “Don't worry Ryan, he's cool.” the bartender assured him. “Yeah he looks cool...how's the air up there big guy?” Ryan replied, assuming a cocky expression and nodding to me. “About as fresh as the air down there, scooter.” I said, shooting him a wry grin. He smiled and approached the bar apprehensively, watching me out the corner of his eye as he feigned interest in the game on the TV. “So, Ryan Trent I presume?” I asked after a short silence, relaxing my posture and doing my best to appear less threatening to the guy. “That's me.” He nodded, eyes fixed on the television even as the static crept over the screen and fizzed incessantly. “You wanted to see me about something?” At first he narrowed his eyes, unsure of what I meant. A moment later he turned to me, his lips parted as though speechless before waving an oily finger at me. “Von Kelsig. Angelus Von Kelsig, right?” He muttered. “One and the same,” I smiled, “so what brings me to this pleasant little nook of Detroit?” His eyes darted between the other guys at the bar. None of them were interested in our conversation, favouring the bottom of their glasses to the grease monkey and the giant. Looking back at me he bit his lip and furrowed his brow, his pleasant demeanour slipping once more. “Can I uh...can I speak to you outside?” “Sure...” He led the way back out of the bar, and only when my back was turned did the bar proppers relax and continue their grumblings in peace. In the light of the day Ryan appeared younger, and I couldn't help but think that more than the dark atmosphere of the bar put years on the men inside. He noticed the F-150 almost immediately, his eyes popping wide at the sight of such a magnificent specimen. “Holy shit, that your ride?” “Hell yes it is.” I replied with an air of stoicism about me. “Niiiice,” he continued, “I'm a mechanic man, case you didn't catch that from the get-up...” “I had an inkling.” I added over his voice. “...man that's a beauty. Don't see anything like that around here dude, it's all throwback biscuit tins and souped up pieces of shit, y'know those gangbanger rides that look like a two-dollar whore dressed up like a million dollar supermodel?” “I can imagine.” He marvelled at the truck in silence, pawing over the bodywork and dropping to the pavement, cooing at the metal under the body. I watched him with a half smile plastered across my face, and it struck me that perhaps he was a little antsy. Eventually he got back up and brushed the dust from his overalls before he drew a long breath and exhaled sharply. “Look...I didn't think you'd show up so soon. I mean, to be honest I didn't think you'd show up at all. The guy I spoke to on the phone said you were dead busy...” “I was...in the neighbourhood...” I interjected. “Oh, cool.” He mumbled again, scratching the back of his head and gazing at the ground. “Soooo?” “Well...I-...see...” He was stuttering, his nerves getting the better of him. I leant against the bed of the truck and folded my arms, watching him fumble with his words. “Take it easy man. There was some kind of family matter you wanted to discuss with me?” I said, easing him into the conversation. “Oh yeah...family.” He wheezed. “Well, reason I asked you out here Mr. Von Kelsig...” “Angelus.” I corrected him, raising a finger. “Angelus, right. Reason I asked you out here Angelus was......fuck I need a cigarette.” He groaned, thrusting his hand into his pockets and producing a crumpled pack of Marlboros and a silver Zippo with a Bald eagle's head emblazoned across the side. “No thanks.” I said as he offered me a smoke. Shrugging, he put one to his lips and lit up, leaning against the truck and taking a long drag. I leaned against the bed of the F-150 by his side and stared off into the distance, giving him all the time he needed to collect himself. He took a few more drags and I could tell just by his breathing he was beginning to relax. Standing in the middle of the ghetto with an oily stranger wasn't on my to-do list, but nevertheless I was there, so I guess all I could do was go with the flow. “I'm your brother.” Finally he spoke up and I breathed a sigh of re-WHAT?!! “What??” I echoed my own thought, both confused and a little shocked at once. “I'm your brother.” He repeated, “That...that was the family matter I wanted to discuss with you. It's us. You and me. We're family.” “This some kind of joke?” I asked, almost offended by his statement. “No!” He yelled, the cigarette dropping out of his hand as he raised both to stop my doubt in its tracks, or maybe stop me from leaving. I was still leaning against the truck, rigid with shock now. My history was well documented and my past was plastered up on my bio at PRIME for all to see. Those who indulged knew the fate of my family. All of them. “It's the truth. I can prove it. We're brothers.” He said again. Now I was speechless.
