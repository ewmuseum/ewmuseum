Chapter 1: A Cold Awakening (Rev133 Lavelle vs. Dusk)

4 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4 - 07- 07 Manhattan, New York Midnight The moon left a blue-grey tint on the Empire State building, as it shone onto the city of New York. He strode through the streets with finesse. His slight limp went unnoticed by passer-bys as he fumbled through the waves of people, his eye on one thing. Jessica Thompson Her blonde hair fluttered like bees around her head as the wind swept it violently. Gripping his carrier bag with a tight and sweaty palm, he continued. The long tails of his trench coat billowed out behind him, wrapping in the wind. I must have her. She’s the one. I will have her. He stayed behind a few feet, keeping a close watch on her through his tinted glasses. She disappeared down Eleventh Avenue and a smile etched onto his split lip. He began to move briskly and looked down the abandoned street, nothing but the remnants of construction work. There were no workers. The street was quiet, desolate, the perfect place to enact his passion. He listened intently for the sound of those red high heels on the pavement. Within moments he heard them – clip, clop – and looked down behind a black dumpster, the smell of putrid garbage vile against his nose, unlike Ms. Thompson’s scent which allured him like a child in a candy store. There she was, making her way to Broadway, heading west on 23rd Street. He moved with a ferocious pace. His heart raced. His limp ached but he didn’t care. He was going to feed his hunger. Reaching into his trenchcoat he pulled out the syringe… he stopped. Panic coursed through him. She turned around slowly, cautiously. He was in the shadows, watching her as she stared toward him. The moon had lit her eyes, those beautiful blue eyes. Thompson shook her head and a moan left her lips: “I’m imagining things. Get a hold of yourself Jess.” She thought and turned around, but stopped … a bottle scuttled along the pavement. The glass rattling against the concrete till it collided at her feet. “Who’s there?” she asked, wearily, her breathing increased. She began to take slow steps backward, moving closer to the Chelsea Hotel. Damnit, he chastised himself. He’d become careless. He moved too quickly. He was supposed to be silent and deadly. Instead he was loud and passive. His first victim in Central Park made him see his earlier mistakes, he was a novice, but this… this was to be perfect. His chest heaved with a deep and wishing breath. Slipping the syringe back into his pocket, he pulled out the hunting knife. It would have to be quick and efficient. Jessica Thompson saw a figure move from behind the dumpster, shadowed by the darkness of night. Shivers ran through her to the tips of her fingers as she saw the sleek silver implement glisten by his thigh. She began to run, panting helplessly as she struggled in her high heels. ‘I hate it when they run’, he muttered and began to chase her. His legs lacking the stamina she had. He dragged his left leg behind him as he jumped forward. She could see the lights of the Chelsea Hotel in front of her. She was almost … An arm gripped her stomach, yanking her backward. The steel, cold, jagged edge of the knife was taut against her neck. She choked on her tears. Fear enveloped her. The figure inched closer, his body pressed hard against hers. He sniffed her hair and could feel every muscle inside him tighten. “Jessica. Jessica. Jessica. I will make this quick, if you behave.” Jessica’s eyes went wide. She recognised the voice. “I know y- …” She felt the blade rip through her neck. She began to gag. Death’s clutches creeping up quickly. He watched as she fell to the floor in a heap. He shook his head and grabbed her ankles. “Oh, Jessica, we could’ve had something good if you’d only listened.” ---- Manhattan, New York Chelsea Piers 9:00 a.m. Marcus Tyler sat in the small boat house and gazed out into the Hudson River, the deep brown liquid sloshed against the sides of the pier as the wind picked up speed. A storm slowly circling off in the distance, ignited with a rumbling moan. A weather report slowly murmured in the background. We’re in for a storm today, New York, so wrap up. It’s going to be raining, but expect to see clear skies in the afternoon, with a temperature of 65 Fahrenheit. Straightening his back into the chair and letting out a yawn he rubbed his gloved hands together for warmth and watched the monitors. An odd static filled his two-way and a voice came over the other end, his fellow co-worker and deck hand, Chris Schomburg. “Tower, we got some slack on our lines. I can’t shift ‘em.” “Roger. I’ll be there in a minute.” “Pier four, boss.” In for another busy day, he sighed and grabbed his two-way and engineering belt. The cold gripped him instantly; the chilling bite stung his cheeks. Pulling up his scarf, he walked with a careful pace over the slippery wood. He grunted as he stepped on some broken planks and felt his ankle give way. He approached pier four and looked toward his co-worker stood at the boat and arguing with the fisherman. Pulling his belt down from his shoulder he rubbed his hands together: Time to get to work. Tyler turned and slipped, tumbling down the steps and onto the plank, landing with a thud in front of his co-worker and the fisherman. Both raised their eyes in disbelief as they gazed toward Tyler, laced in a dark substance. It was thick against his jeans and coat as he stood up and looked at it, twiddling it between his thumb and forefinger – it wasn’t oil. “Is that …?” the fisherman paused. “Its blood,” replied Tyler. “Eh, boss, it’s all over the platform…” the co-worker shrieked in disbelief as he circled. Tyler turned around and saw the wood was oddly darker than the others, the rain had washed a lot of it away, but the evidence was there. “Call the police,” he ordered his co-worker, turning to the fisherman he said, “Your boat will have to stay here until the police deal with this.” The fisherman raised his arms in defeat. “Wow, fair enough. I ain’t got anything to do with this.” “We’ll let the police handle it, alright.” Tyler turned and shook his head: This is going to be busier than most days. ---- Manhattan, New York Twelfth Avenue, Chelsea Piers, 10:30 a.m. He had received the call as soon as he touched down in Newark and he wasn’t pleased, the lack of sleep he’d received on the flight over and not to mention the bruises that still lay visible from his confrontation with Dusk had put Pierce in a bad mood. He watched the city pass by him, his mind on the case that had been thrust into his hands, and he wasn’t sure he wanted to work another homicide so soon after his father’s death but he had little choice in the matter, life was moving on whether he was ready or not. The cab came to a screeching halt and he looked out the window at the scene around him, rain dotted the window, reflecting the flashing lights of the cities finest police force. Stepping out he paid the cab driver and watched him speed off. Heaving a sigh he shivered in the cold chill. Pulling the USA Today paper up above his head, it offered some shelter from the torrential storm. The scene before him was a nest of activity, six patrol cars and a coroner van blocked the plateau to the docks. The flashing lights cast a disturbing glow against the rain soaked city. “Good morning partner.” Pierce turned to see Ignacio Booth stood beside him, brandishing a grin, and dressed in a suit and heavy leather jacket. He was holding a coffee and sipped it slowly. His brown eyes looked absently onto the scene as a squadron of cars lined the path, his hair soaked to the skin as beads of rain water dripped off the tip of his crooked nose. “Morning, Iggy,” he replied, groaning as he felt the water trickle down his back, cold against his skin. “Shoot, he drag you out of bed too, bru?” Ignacio asked. His eyes gazed toward the scene. He began to click his tongue off the palette of his mouth, a distracting habit. “No, he dragged me out of the plane.” “Damn. You only just here?” he asked, Pierce nodded his head. “Well, hell of a way to start your New York life.” Lovely start to a Monday: Pierce thought as he turned to Ignacio and routed through his pockets for his cell phone. Looking through soaked eyelashes he surveyed the police officers around, checking the uniforms for the Detective. “So, what we got?” Pierce questioned, softly. “Female. Early twenties. She was found on the pier. Engineer, fisherman and a deck hand are being questioned. Found the blood,” Ignacio answered with a direct tone, stating the facts as he knew them. “Police found the body.” “OK, good. So is this the scene of the crime?” Ignacio shrugged his shoulders, causing the rain to glide off the leather jacket. He turned on his heels. Pointing up the road he said, “Nah, not here. I came here from Broadway, and there were troopers near Chelsea, about 3 minutes up the road. Looks like its all part of the same murder, bru.” Pierce folded his arms and puffed his chest out, thinking. He could almost hear the cogs of his mind turning in the deathly silence around him. “So the person kills the girl and brings her all the way down here to finish the job …” he pondered. “Seems like that’s the case, but he couldn’t carry that … no blood between here and Chelsea.” “He drove,” Pierce suggested, stating another possible solution. “He’d have to have had the car waiting then, right?” “Yeah, meaning a second helper.” Ignacio’s eyes lit up, he said in a quiet tone, “Oh shit, that just makes this more fucked up.” “Let’s not jump the gun, my South African friend. Talk to the detective on scene. I’m going to scout the coroner van, see if I can get a glimpse at the scene.” “Right. Right. You want lime-light or facts?” Ignacio questioned as he pointed out the two vans and the glaring lights of the cameras. Pierce nodded his head, wherever there was a story to be told or blood on the streets, the media moguls would flock like vultures to a fresh kill. “Facts only!” “OK.” Pierce patted Ignacio’s shoulder and strolled down the slippery paving stones, the stone soft under the soles of his shoes, but Pierce stopped moving, a question had come to mind. He turned to Ignacio. “Iggy, did we get the all clear on this case or are we gonna be bombarded with the entire newsroom?” “Who the fuck knows, we’re his slaves, bru. I just do as told, pays the bills.” “Right!” Pierce watched Ignacio saunter off toward the line of police cars, pushing his way past the Times news crew. Pierce moved quickly down the grass lawn, weary of the cobbles as they were extremely slippery. Stepping onto the flat ground, he noticed instantly the stench of death – a putrid and vile odour that curled the nostril hairs and caused the acids in his stomach to flutter and rise. Taking a deep breath, feeling a little nauseous, he moved forward. In the distance he saw the fog begin to creep up the Hudson, shading the police boats in the distance. Nothing more than blinking lights in a dark and perilous river. He followed the path toward the grey coroner’s van, the back door opened and a young crime scene assistant was stood beside the door, lighting up a cigarette. “Hey, no reporters, you heard the police officer,” the crime scene man said. “And is lighting a cigarette allowed during a crime scene?” “Touché, you can stay if you don’t tell my boss!” Pierce managed a smile and looked out into the distance as he saw two small body bags being removed; the coroner was supervising the removal. He was a short man, bulging at the belt as his stomach slopped over the side. His comb over soaked to his head, as patches of scalp reflected the morning glow. His large lab coat dripped water as he yelled orders. “That the victim?” “What’s left of her,” the crime scene assistant announced. “You want a smoke?” “Nah, gave up,” Pierce lied. “Yeah, been meaning to give up myself,” he said with a smile and took off his latex glove. “I’m Scottie.” “Pierce. So, Scottie, how’d she die?” The crime scene assistant, Scottie, hesitated a moment and looked toward the floor where the rain had washed away most of the blood. He looked back up and blew out a ring of smoke toward the clouds above. “You want the facts?” “Yeah, that’d be great, Scottie.” “Then talk to my boss, I don’t know nothing, sorry.” Pierce cursed as he watched Scottie move off and hop into the driver’s side of the van. He stubbed the ground with the tip of his shoe in frustration. The coroner approached wheeling the gurney with the two body bags. Pierce felt a thud of uneasy recognition as he realised the body had been separated, he couldn’t shake that irritating itch that there was something familiar. “You better clear the scene reporter,” he directed in a serious tone. The coroner was middle aged and balding; wrinkles lined his forehead, curving as he stared toward Pierce with steel grey eyes. Pierce nodded his head and backed up slightly. “I guess that means you won’t be privy to answering some questions?” Pierce asked, sheepishly. The coroner hissed, water spluttering from his purple coloured lips. “You’ll get answers in my report later, just like everybody else,” he replied and slid into the passenger seat. He shook his head, aggravated at the lack of enthusiasm and answers being given. Deciding to try a new angle he turned around and saw Ignacio Booth waiting at the top of the hill, beaming. Pierce clambered up the slippery slope. “Any luck?” he asked. “Yeah, I got some juicy information from a female copper.” Pierce laughed. “Please tell me it has something to with the case!” “Of course, bru. I’m a married man,” Ignacio stated clearly. “Seems the victim was killed by a slit to the neck, but the killer had stripped her and cut her body in half from the waist. Left her in the fisherman’s boat.” Pierce stared wide-eyed at Ignacio. “You got all that?” “I’m a charmer, bru, a big charmer.” “In future, you work the cops.” Ignacio laughed and turned when their cell phone’s ignited, the annoying Ring Ring shot through the silent crime scene. Pierce quickly answered, pulling the phone back from his ear instantly. “Pierce Lavelle, where are you?” boomed the voice of their Editor, Joseph Wallace. “Eh, at the crime scene, boss,” replied Pierce with raised eyebrows, curious as to why his boss had called. “You’re at the crime scene …” Wallace said in a sarcastic tone. “I got Times, Washington and USA all gearing up for a lunch hour report and you’re still at the crime scene. Get your skinny white ass back to my office.” “But boss, we’ve just -” “Now, Pierce, now.” Pierce hung the phone up and sighed, Wallace wasn’t one of the nicest people to work for, but he held a certain dread about him when his blood boiled and his eyes glazed. He knew he was in for a rough ride working in New York. ---- Manhattan, New York Inquisitor News Room, 12:00 mid-day Morningside Heights, Amsterdam Avenue The mid summer weather in New York was taking its toll as another misty day rolled in off the Hudson. His eyes cast to the window, moist from the condensation in his small office. Joseph Wallace was tense, more so than any other day, his pits were wet as he pulled his back straight. The head of the Inquisitor was breathing down his neck for turnovers, big cases and the ultimate page one headline. Instead, he had nothing to offer. Leaning into the chair, he found little comfort as his back clicked. His eyes cast to the elevator as it pinged open. A grin laced his crinkled lips, pushing his moustache into a loop: ‘About damn time!’ Pierce Lavelle and Ignacio Booth moved through the newsroom, their shoes squelched, causing the eyes of their fellow employees to wander in judgement. They moved toward the Editor’s newsroom and Pierce couldn’t shake a feeling of unease. Joseph Wallace swung his wooden door open, the blinds rattled with the force. He gazed toward them and ordered, “My office, now, you’re both late!” Slumping his shoulders, Pierce was last to enter and closed the door firmly behind him. He turned around to see Ignacio, rather relaxed looking in a chair. On the other hand, Wallace was sat upright. Clasping his hands tightly together, he looked at Lavelle with a stern gaze. “Do you want to explain why it is my top two reporters are late?” Pierce shrugged, dropping into the chair. “Traffic!” “TRAFFIC? Now, you listen here you skinny white boy, I got word from Lindsay Fallen that you were one of the best. All I have seen so far is bone idol laziness, I gave you part-time but I can easily make sure you’re fired.” Ignacio remained in his position and looked around the room, feeling no need to speak up. “With no disrespect to my former employer, I only cracked that one case,” declared Pierce with a forceful tone. “I am aware as to the circumstances of that, so why aren’t you putting in the same effort.” “I’m a little jet lagged. I’ve been flying all -” “No more excuses,” Wallace directed and turned in his seat grabbing the television remote, he turned on the television with one push of the button. The screen flickered to life and in front of them was the CNN broadcast and Times reporter Angela O’Brien, announcing the findings at New York’s latest murder. Wallace turned, muting the television. “Explain to me why it is that Times and USA have both reported the murder and have handed in finished pieces for this evenings post?” Ignacio now inched closer, a grin on his lips. “Cause boss, we aren’t a newspaper that seeks make-up and lime-light. We go by the details and to help solve a case.” Wallace chuckled. “You have been spending too much time with our resident Jew.” Pierce watched the debate about his situation in the newspaper, with a passive attitude. Wallace didn’t comment further, instead he sat back and opened up his cigar case, lighting one up he blew a ring of smoke toward the air-conditioning unit. “What did you learn about the case then, Booth?” Booth shuffled in his seat and pulled out a soggy notepad, his brow creased in embarrassment. “It got a little wet.” “I can see that.” “Look, I’ll get you more than Times and USA Today have,” Pierce reassured. “Oh yeah, and how do you plan to do that?” Wallace questioned, sarcastically. “Remember two months ago when my editor sent me up here to help, because this paper was short-handed?” he asked, inching closer. “Yeah, I remember.” “Well, you partnered me with Iggy and we worked a case in Central Park.” Ignacio turned, recognition hitting him in the gut. “Jesus, that girl had the exact same M.O. as our recent victim.” Pierce slouched into the chair, folding his arms. “I got your facts and I got leads none of these lime-light reporters got. So, you want us to work this case or not?” “You think the Central Park murder and the Chelsea Pier murder are connected?” Wallace asked. Pierce raised one eyebrow. “It’s that or we have a copy-cat.” Wallace emptied the ash of his cigar into a tray and turned with a gleam in his eyes. “Alright, I’ll give you boys the resources you need. If this turns out to be more than a gut feeling, then go with it.” “Thanks boss,” Ignacio said, kindly. “But, if it’s nothing … I want a report on my desk tonight, ready for the morning’s edition. You got that?” Pierce stood up and pulled the shirt from his back, he moved closer toward Wallace and looked at him. “You got yourself a deal, boss.” “Good, now get going. Time’s a wasting.” Ignacio and Pierce left the room and sighed with relief. The meeting had started off on a rocky road, but Pierce could see things turning out a lot smoother. “Iggy, you still got that file on the victim in Central Park?” Pierce asked. “Yeah, I think so. I’ll run it up now and print it.” “OK, I’m going to go and take lunch, get some dry clothes and see if I can pull some strings with the local police.” “I’ll ring you when I get this done,” Ignacio said and began to type and scroll, his eyes glued to the flat screen monitor. “You want anything while I’m out?” “Pick up a pizza will you, this could take a while.” Nodding his head he hit the button for the elevator. ---- Manhattan, New York Park Heights Apartments, 1:00 p.m. 3rd and 72nd Street The cab pulled to a stop outside a series of apartment buildings and small store fronts. His eyes glanced over at Central Park, calm and serene as the wind swept its green branches and shades of coloured petals. Months ago it had been wrought with grief and anguish. He stepped out, the rain had subsided and in its place a covering of puddles and misty sunshine, a fire engine was parked next to them. He didn’t give it a moment’s glance. Instead, he looked at the apartment building he was staying in. As he moved closer he could hear indistinct chatter. Pierce opened the double glass doors with the brass handles, only to find the foyer alive with activity. People stood arguing. He felt a cold substance brush off his ankles and he looked down, standing ankle deep in an inch of water. “What happened here?” he asked the young man, stood with his hands buried into the pocket of his New York Giants sweater. He turned to Pierce, raising an eyebrow at the man in the soaked suit. “It’s like Niagara Falls in there man, everything’s ruined.” Another stepped forward, an old woman, cradling a small case for her glasses. “Yeah, the pipes burst on the fifth and sixth floor. The fire department is fixing it.” “You’re not joking?” Pierce asked in disbelief, he didn’t have a view of the stairs but looked up; the ceiling above them had wet patches in the dried paint. “Nah man, she ain’t kidding. Whole place is crumbling.” He shook the kids words from his mind as he waddled through the water and crowds till he found the superintendent that he’d dealt with, Mr. Lee Cusa. “What’s going on Mr. Cusa?” Cusa turned, gazing up at Pierce. He was a small man, his eyes were bleary and blood shot and his glasses sat delicately on his nose. “Pipe problem. You come back later,” he ordered. “No, this isn’t good,” Pierce retorted. “Go to motel, come back later, I fix … OK?” “No, this no OK. I just moved here and you’re telling me my apartment has three inches of water cascading down the stairs?” Two firemen came down the stairs, shaking their heads. They approached Mr. Cusa and Pierce Lavelle with frowns. “You’ve lost the piping on floor’s five and six. We’ve turned off the mains. You’ll have to call in a plumber.” “I call department. I fix. OK.” The fireman moved off back toward their other colleagues and headed for their truck. Pierce flung his arms in the air and moved to the front door, the kid with the New York jumper held the door open and said in a quiet tone, “This place is old, but the views good.” Sighing he left the apartment and looked around as the rain clouds began to circle, a deep and rumbling thunder in the distance. Slapping his hands into his pockets he looked toward the pavement. This is just great. Fuck! I’m having a great start to this week: He groaned. “You Mr. Lavelle?” asked a deep voice. Pierce looked up to a man, small in stature but well built, dressed in a delivery uniform. “Yeah, why?” he said, coyly. “We’re the moving guys from D.C., we have your stuff?” answered the delivery man. Pierce saw the truck and kicked the pavement. “No shit! Yeah, you’re going to have to re-route, I haven’t got a place to live anymore.” Pierce looked back at the apartment and the residents leaving and then toward the delivery truck. His week had been bad enough after his father’s death and his encounters with Dusk, but his moving and job arrangements in New York weren’t working out either. “Excuse me?” “I know its last minute, but my apartments been turned into a water park.” “Well, we got to drop ‘em off, sir. You’ll have to sign the papers,” stated the delivery man and pulled out a document and a pen. “You’re serious?” Pierce asked and signed the document. The delivery man didn’t have an answer, nor did he care. He turned and pulled the page out handing it to Pierce as a receipt. A few cars pulled to a stop, he could hear the doors slamming and some of the residents leaving. Closing his eyes he felt a hand delicately touch his shoulder. Turning around he gazed into the eyes of his girlfriend, Dawn. She stood in her detective attire, the glow of her badge shimmering in the lights of the truck. He kissed her passionately on the lips, relief and peace fluttering through him. “Pierce, I got a call from Tracy, said you ran into some problems,” Dawn said and looked around the place. “Oh yeah, things are going great! Welcome to Casa De Water!” he grunted and watched as his boxes were piled onto the side walk. She approached him and noticed he was soaked to the bone and he had a black eye, worry had overcome her. “You’re soaking,” she said softly, holding his hand, it was cold to touch. “As is my apartment, my stuff is here and I haven’t got a place to stay … on top of that I got to work a double homicide story.” Running his hands through his soaked hair he sat down on the wet sidewalk. Dawn smiled, an idea coming to mind. “You can stay with me, if you behave.” “You serious?” he looked up, smiling. “Definitely. Gives us more time together,” she said, winking. Removing her badge she approached the delivery men with a confident stride. “My name is Detective Stanton. I’m going to have to ask you to move this property off the side walk.” “We don’t have anywhere else to put it!” he stated nervously. “There’s a storage unit in Hampton, called “Quick Fix”. Leave them there in locker 24.” “Uh-huh, will do m’am!” Quickly they began to put all the boxes into the truck and drove off as instructed. Pierce smiled, pausing. “You do realise all my clothes are in that truck!” “We’ll follow them and pick some up. There was no way all that stuff would fit in my apartment. I’m on a cop’s salary!” He smiled as he sat into her police issue car and breathe a sigh of relief as the warm air danced over him, drying the beads of water that lay on his skin. ---- Manhattan, New York Rosie’s Bar, Upper Westside 6:00 pm Rosie’s bar was filled with the evening commuters as they watched the news and played darts. Ignacio Booth sat into a small seat in the corner, sipping on a beer and lighting up a cigarette. Placing his cigarettes and lighter down he took a sip of the beer and basked in its soft taste, sloshing it around his mouth he smiled as the door opened. Pierce Lavelle entered wearing jeans and a Steelers jumper, his hands delved into his jean pockets he approached Ignacio with a grin. “So it’s a done deal?” he questioned as he slid into the seat opposite. A hostess approached and he ordered a beer and another for Ignacio. “Yeah, Wallace is giving us the go ahead,” Ignacio answered. “Brilliant. Did you find the file on the first victim?” Pierce pondered. Lounging back into the leatherette chair he glanced around the bar. “Ja, I did,” Ignacio said and pulled a small document from the seat beside him. “Her name is Martina Roberts.” Pierce rubbed his temple as the smell of the cigarettes rose up his nostrils. “Did you find anything about her death that was similar to the one today?” Ignacio raised his eyebrows and said, sarcastically, “Aside from the fact they were both in two parts?! No, I haven’t managed to get the coroner report for the first victim.” Shrugging, Pierce took a deep swig of the beer that had been placed for him and put it down heavily on the table. “Not to worry, Dawn is going to look into both files for us … off the record stuff. I’ll wait to see what she finds but meanwhile we should see whether both knew each other from something as simple as going to the same grocery store.” Ignacio was nodding his head and jotting notes next to the small information file on Martina Roberts. Basic information he had acquired that day two months ago when he’d been called to Central Park for anything but a picnic. He looked up with a raised eyebrow. “So that why I never got my pizza?” “I lost my apartment and we went out for lunch, I’m sorry.” “Uh-huh. That what you call quality time in this country!” he said laughing. “I heard about Park Heights. That place was run down anyway; you should look at a town house or something.” “I’ll buy the Property magazine tomorrow. Next time I’ll get you two pizzas and we’ll call it even, partner.” Ignacio flicked his lighter and lit another cigarette. “That’s great bru. So this bird, you been with her long?” he asked. “Yeah, she’s a big part of my life now. It’s going really well.” “Ja. Ja. Wait till you get married bru, a whole ‘nother story there,” he said as he wiggled the gold band on his finger and smiled. There was a long pause as Pierce listened to the Creedence Clearwater Revival version of “Proud Mary”, tapping his fingers off the wood. Ignacio leaned forward, blowing a ring of smoke into the greased light bulb, it shimmered above them. “So… What now, bru?” “We find more about these two girls and connect the dots,” replied Pierce with a tired yawn. “Well then partner, welcome to New York,” Ignacio declared, raising his pint.
