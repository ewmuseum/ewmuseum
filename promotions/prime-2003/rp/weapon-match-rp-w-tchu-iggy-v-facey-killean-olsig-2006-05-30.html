Weapon (Match RP - w/ Tchu & Iggy v Facey, Killean & Olsig)

30 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30/05/06 Weapon.Written: 30th May, 2006. 'A compromise between honesty and lies,To lead me past their sly disguise.My moves are slow... but soon they'll know.' - José González. I My name is Karina Marie Wolfenden, and, today, I killed a man. Through my I-Pod's earphones, the singer from At The Drive-In advises me to press eject, and give him the tape... if you know what's good for me. May isn't so much burning out into June, as wading through a muggy fortnight. The early morning rain reaches a higher state, hanging as steam, and leaving us gulping in air like a caught fish. Give me crisp sub-zero, or arid, baking heat... but don't give me humidity. Even within its band and clips, I can feel my hair frizz. Waiting way back in line, I switch stance, back and forth. Standing still, my right knee aches, leaving me with awkward posture. It can withstand a morning run, and the best kick-bag work-out I can give it, but standing still the thing resonates a dull throb right up into my lower back. I must look like I'm on an imaginary step-machine. Only for me can standing still be the hardest thing I've done in the last few days. Its probably all good practice for when arthritis cashes in our life-pact, moving in with me for my later years. My hope is that painkiller manufacturers are churning out some good shit in 20 years time. At the counter, an elderly lady drops coins, one by one, into her tatty purse. Spending five minutes scraping change from her piggy-bank of an account wasn't quite inconvenient enough, apparently. Shaking fingers send a dime rolling around the purses rim, before it drops in to a muffled chink. Finally, incoherent fingertips button the purse closed, and her feet shuffle her away. If I thought I had a snowball's of making it to that age, I'd have to go and actively hunt more ways of chipping away at my life expectancy. I can see a mischievous smile grow in front of me as the guy ahead in the cue sees her approach the DANGER! slippery floor sign. Through my earphones, José González assures me that we'll cast some light, and I'll be alright... for now. We all shimmy forward, and the half-man, half-giraffe standing 3rd in line blocks the cool current of the air conditioning. Watching Apocalypse Now last night is beginning to seem like a pretty shit idea now, as my mind transporting itself to sweltering, tropical climes doesn't do a lot to cool me. In fact, it does pretty much fuck all. Leaning over the ropes that we're herded between, I swipe a brochure on investment advice to fan myself with. For the briefest of blissful moments, the air-con wafts its teasing, refreshing breeze across me, for as long as it takes giraffe man to tie his shoelace. As the breeze is again split both sides of me, the sour odour of stale sweat, mixed in with the harsh sweetness of someone's deodorant, makes me wince. Even through the increasing volume of my I-Pod I can still hear the pointless chitter-chatter, like crickets, where the line snakes back on itself four times. I'm so bored I almost, for a fleeting second, think about work. I take a deep breath, through my mouth, and press my thumb down on the volume button until my nail bleaches white through pressure. With a hint of a smile, I close my eyes, and time speeds up for me. Through my earphones, Thom Yorke tells me he wishes it was the sixties, and that he wishes he could be happy, and he wishes... he wishes... he wishes that something would happen. I'm also counting on a decent hearing-aid to go with my uber-painkillers. I feel the vibrations through the floor tiles, and shuffle a foot or so forward. Something distant loses its joust against jagged guitars, and is forced back out my resistant ears. I feel the cool breeze of the air-con use my own fringe to tickle my nose, but as my eyes open, it's not just giraffe-man whose ducking... everybody is. As I blink, refreshing my vision screen to see if I'd just experienced a random download error, I feel my left earphone ripped out. Instinct pitches my head left, where I look down the barrel of a gun... dominantly imposed on me like a pornographic, gun-metal phallus. I emerge from my daydream in laboured fashion, like wading through treacle, eyes rolling towards the ominous, shadowed exit of the barrel... oblivious to the person clasping it within his shaking grip. 'ARE YOU FUCKING LISTENING?!' less-than-subtly slaps my attention back into waking-world sync. 'GET ON THE FUCKING FLOOR!!!' He plays conductor to the potentially lethal symphony, orchestrating my descent to my knees with the barrel of his gun. Through my right earphone, DJ Shadow urges me to be still now. He is with me, he is deep within me; I am at peace. I cannot be harmed; I will not suffer. Breathe deeply. Breathe in the healing love of the universe. Anyone who calls me a daredevil can cash in their option of retracting the accolade as I shuffle back against the wall, arms crossed defensively. With every stroke across the slick tiles, his stride fills my wake, until he can loom over me, the control-freak in him cocking the trigger to match the erection he's probably getting from this. As he backs away, puckered lips force a blown kiss upon me. From the tops of my eyes, I finally look past the black hole of the weapon. Joining the dots across the bank's floor, we pass on messages of emotion through eye-contact. Nearly all of them are terrified, a few refuse to look up from the floor tiles, just hugging their knees and rocking back and forth, muttering mantras of faith. As border-line retarded as robbing a back at 11am is, I find a brief moment to admire the flecks of intelligence. They let us see nothing that doesn't have the ability to plough a white-hot bullet through our flesh. Faces are masked by balaclavas, eyes obscured by mirrored sunglasses, and even the counter has all the 'position closed' blinds pulled down. The amount of people staring at nothing but fabric betrays that what they don't know is a welcome distraction from what they do. Through my right earphone, Ashlee Simpson protests that she didn't steal my boyfriend. I wonder how the fuck that got there. They don't even need to share the briefest of words. It's all planned out. Before we can even ask ourselves how much time elapsed while our senses were scrambled by panic, they've removed the wall clocks, turned off the digital clock at the counter, and are demanding we toss our watches into a refuse sack. Not wanting even his shadow to touch me again, I toss mine from a good 15 feet away. It misses, and he grinds it under his heel. Tying off the sack, it's tossed into a vacant corner. I've forgotten which song just ended... and which went before that, making 2 seconds and 10 minutes indistinguishable. On the ocean of blue floor tiles, land masses of scared humanity have huddled together, just leaving a few of us stray islands at the end of the world. Within their groups they whisper, those spreading assuredness slowly being swamped by those overcome by panic. Those landlocked, a human-shield coast to protect them, naturally seem the more vocal. There are three knocks on the counter's glass, and for the first time, the two masked 'crowd-controllers' look to one another. They communicate in digits and directions, Code, as if they were machines, dehumanised by their outfits' detachment from the outside world. As the stockier figure backs towards the door, he relays a departing message, obliviously swearing in sign language. I only know the bad words. And 'off'. The door locks shut behind him, and right away the now lone gunman seems shakier. His smooth pacing is replaced by sudden turns, gun ensnared tightly by his fingers. The two men to my left nod back and forth, heads twitching to the sides. 'You,' one urges. 'No, you,' he stubbornly gets in reply. I hope they keep it up for the next however long, to keep us all safe. A collective gasp then almost sucks me into a vacuum as a fierce bang, muffled by brick and glass, sounds out. For an all-too-brief, denial-ridden moment, some try to convince themselves that it was another knock on the counter glass. I pray, in my own way, that it was part of the script... immersing myself in the stream of faint hope. Devoid of time, what's left in its place drags. The floor is making my tailbone ache, and a dull throb bounces back and forth along my right leg. My hair begins to itch at its roots, and I can feel a bead of sweat trickle down into my eyebrow. Clammy skin leaves my clothes sticking to me, and I find myself out of breath. I can't remember when the sensations began, or when the battery of my I-Pod ran out. The gunshot seems to have rattled our captor. He's no longer happy targeting the floor, instead turning with his weapon raised, the fearful believing he's thinking eenie, meanie, miney, mo... Behind the gun, I convince myself he's nothing. The weapon is his strength, his will, and has replaced his need for smarts. I loathe the contraption, instigating my plummeting opinion of its wielder. I tell myself I could take him... stiff leg and all. Despite my glare being reflected back at me, I sense his gaze lingering on me as he again sweeps the room. He passes within 6 feet of me, attention elsewhere. I'm non-threatening, compliant... nothing to differentiate me from the pack. I have anger problems, which I keep locked up 6 days, 23 hours, and 45 minutes of just about every week. They went up-tapped last week. All he has is a gun, and he's convinced that's all he needs. But more shots have been missed than kicks, knees, or elbows. I remind myself that I shouldn't even be telling myself such things. All I need to do is sit on the floor, against the wall, and keep quiet. I have no right to make any choices for all these people. I tend to listen to myself about as much as I listen to others. My feet creep back along the floor, closer, until I can tuck them under me. I'm no longer seated, I'm crouched, poised, wound. I tell myself to sit back down. I ignore myself. I tell myself to purge whatever thoughts are over-riding my common-sense controls. I ignore myself. I tell myself to ignore myself. I ignore myself. He's set in a pattern, not even realising it, such is his mind's need to set a rhythm in its erratic state. In 10 seconds, he'll be close enough for me to tell if he showered that morning. In 8 seconds, he'll be looking to the pair of men to my left, who he knows hush whenever he gets within earshot. in 6 seconds, he'll turn his back to me, gun pointing between the islands of captives... harmless, open ocean. His route is my only grasp on time. In 8 seconds, my foot can smash against the joints of his fingers. In 9 seconds I'll drastically reduce his chances of becoming a father. In 10 seconds, he'll be biting into his tongue as his teeth slam together. I begin my countdown, the sweat from my brow having dried cold. The glass of orange juice swilling around in my stomach sets like concrete, trying to anchor me. Pins and needles sabotage my toes, the dull paralysis that follows the acidic sting. I hold firm to my policy of ignoring myself. My toes wriggle, and I wait for my green light. Then, just as I load my toes with pressure, something shoots through my peripheral vision, and I abort. From the extremities of one of the islands, a suited man bursts up. In his haste, the worn treads of his dress shoes lose traction on a slick tile, and he's sent sprawling forward. Panicked, the robber brings his weapon around, and matching the man's premature action, pulls the trigger before taking aim. We're not fortunate enough to have a silencer make a whisper of our gunshot. Everything goes silent, save for the ringing in our ears. The would-be-hero's eyelids almost peel back around the balls themselves as he looks into the terrified eyes of the bullet's victim. Shock fulfilling its role of nature's best painkiller, he simply slaps his hand across his chest, blood bursting the banks of his fingers. I cut the strings connecting me to the present as the screams start, blind-terror mixing liberally with accusation. I slide my feet back out from under me, and sit, as I was told... a good little captive. Shaking his head, the would-be-hero wallows in denial, wondering how he could be so stupid... despite those around him posing the question in far more forceful terms. He believes he's the only one who had such thoughts. And nobody else would ever claim them as their own. Retreating behind my eyelids, I allow my grip on time to falter as the panicked screams blend into one layer of white noise. My name is Karina Marie Wolfenden, and, today, I killed a man. And I got away with it.
