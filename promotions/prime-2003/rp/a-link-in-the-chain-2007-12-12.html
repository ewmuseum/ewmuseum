A link in the chain

12 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/12/2007 Fusenshoff is surrounded by empty beer mugs and disgruntled citizens having a great time passing around shots and yelling loudly. He’s by himself, growing more and more irritated by the minute. Normally either because he’s not so inebriated or he’s focused on something else, Fuse ignores the pet peeves that come from spending most of your time alone in a bar. Instead, tonight he’s mixing very hard liquor with his brews and has every intention of drinking his way into a hospital. The nights are few and far between when Fusenshoff chooses to push the limits of his alcohol tolerance well past its breaking point. It always starts the same. He wakes up in a cold sweat scared out of his mind, shocked beyond any comprehension. Despite the nightmare he awakes from being a recurring nightmare, it still takes several minutes to pull himself into normal consciousness. This is especially unordinary due to the fact that his normal life isn’t much different than a nightmare to begin with. Over the past couple of years he wakes up like this about once every ten months or so. The nightmare occurs far less often than it did just over half a decade ago, probably because drinking as much as he does keeps him as far from REM as the American government is from Osama Bin Laden. Another argument could be made that time has healed his wounds to an extent, though they’re far from being just permanent scars. Killing your sister tends to have that affect on people. But Fusenshoff couldn’t escape it this morning. He could’ve run a marathon and excreted less perspiration. The first thing he does on mornings like today’s is reach for the fifth he usually leaves on an end table next to the bed. As one might guess, five swigs of whiskey before hopping in the shower or brushing your teeth usually starts bad and ends ugly. Fast-forward twelve to thirteen hours and Fusenshoff has no hope of asserting any self-control over his body. All day the only thing he’s been able to think about is how to end his life without taking the coward’s way out. He’s been able to last over five years without committing suicide. Still, getting yourself killed without directly acting on its behalf is more difficult than one might think. Fusenshoff is counting on getting so drunk that he’ll get hit by a car on the way home. That’s if he doesn’t fall off his stool here at the bar, convulsing uncontrollably, until someone unfortunately calls 911. That’s when six bikers all the same size as Fusenshoff or larger walk into the bar. Suddenly a gameplan falls right into his lap and he realizes that tonight just might be the night. Fusenshoff immediately orders three more shots from the bartender and a brew before the man cuts him off. Fuse turns sideways on his barstool and the college kids next to him all immediately turn to stare. The guys who’s been doing nothing but throwing back shots and leaning his forehead on the bar counter suddenly shows signs of life, they notice. The future PRIMEate completely ignores the coeds as he focuses all his attention on the bikers. He notices immediately that this isn’t their first pit stop. They’re loud, screaming at waitresses and passers-by, and having trouble putting one foot in front of the other as they take a seat at a large booth. Thirty minutes passes as Fusenshoff has finished all three of his remaining shots and is half-finished with the beer in front of him. He lost count of how many founds of Jim Beam the bikers have had, but he can tell his opportunity is minutes away. He waited to make sure they’re drunk enough to have a great deal of difficulty refraining from beating Fusenshoff past that last inch of his life. Four of the bikers rise from the booth and grab pool sticks when Fusenshoff decides the timing is perfect. He pounds the last half of his beer and leaves a fifty for the bartender as he approaches the bikers. One of the guys is racking as the other three check out the waitress with the heart-shaped heiny taking a drink order across the room. Fusenshoff isn’t in the mood for chit-chat and since there’s really no point anyway, he gets straight to business. Fuse stumbles over, bleary-eyed, to the pool table and grabs the cue ball while walking over to the biker racking the rest of the pool balls. All four men watch him as he tosses the ball a foot or so up in the air and catches it a few times. As he approaches his target the biker stands up tall and puffs out his chest. Fusenshoff just grins. The biker announces in his most intimidating tone, “You hand me that ball or I’ll hand you your ass, son!” A split-second later the biker is on the ground clutching at his orbital bone, which is pouring blood like a leak in a canoe. Fusenshoff grabs his pool stick leaning against the table and positions it like a baseball bat as the other three bikers run at him. He cracks its over the head of the one coming at him from the right of the table before launching himself at the other two approaching from the left. It doesn’t take long for the last two bikers sitting in the booth to recognize what just happened. The crack of Fusenshoff’s pool stick roused their attention when they heard it shatter over their buddy’s head. Fuse holds his own pretty well two-on-one, but that lasts about twenty seconds before he’s getting stomped by four men at once. His plan is working perfectly when fate decides to intervene. While Fusenshoff was staring with a determined concentration at the bikers kicking back shots about fifteen minutes before the scuffle began, two off-duty cops sat down in the far corner after an especially long shift. The two officers were clad almost completely in their casual attire, save one glaring difference. Unlike most civilians, these men carried large shiny metal draped in black leather that gave them an all-access pass to intervene in any situation they deemed unlawful. Too bad for Fusenshoff, just such an occasion was about to arise before these two officers became preoccupied with the conversation of two incredibly intriguing women. Needless to say, the officers prevent the bikers from assaulting Fusenshoff so severely that he misses his date with the coroner and instead finds himself in a little white room that smells like a medicine cabinet. The morphine drips and searing pain running through most of his body clouds Fusenshoff’s head to the fact that just nine months from today he’ll find himself on a path to recovery and tonight was one big link in a chain of events that leads to a life worth living.
