Date: Various When David Kellner raised his glass of champagne, entire rooms of people usually felt compelled to follow along in fealty. The editor extraordinaire had a commanding presence, there was to be no doubt of that, one he'd made good use of in hundreds of toasts at hundreds of parties for the release of hundreds of different books. Twenty-three years at Plainwell & Marks Publishing had made him among the best in his field, bar none, and now, only those most sacred of manuscripts found their way to the mahogany desk in David's corner office. Which is why for David Kellner to be giving this toast, for the release of this particular book, was so far out of the norm. The man who had final say over the words of intellectual giants and literary titans shouldn't be bothered with the autobiography of a guy who got slammed, suplexed and hit in the face with steel chairs for a living. Yet, here David was, smile on his face, champagne flute tilted towards the ceiling. "When this book came to my desk, first thing I wanted to do was fire my assistant for even bringing it to me ... and no, Jonathan, I'm not kidding." Many of the gathered chuckled, but in the back of the room, Jonathan Daley could only roll his eyes. Barely a year out of college, he'd already come to know that his boss was dead serious about his job. "Then," Kellner said, "I followed the Golden Rule our the publishing industry. When in doubt, just read the damn manuscript and figure out for yourself whether it's any damn good or not. I stand here today extremely thankful that I turned to page one and kept on reading. Troy, I know this was an extremely difficult process - the writing and the editing - but I'm proud to say, as you've become so famous for saying, that we've reached the end of the road. In two weeks, this story hits the shelves. Tonight, we celebrate. To Troy." The fifty or so camped out in the Plainwell & Marks conference room raised their glasses and repeated David's assertion, all save for the man who was the evening's center of attention, who nodded politely, shared in a drink and shook any number of offered hands. Truth be told, Troy Douglas didn't exactly want to be at this party. Writing his autobiography was a long, emotional and extremely personal process, and while he appreciated the work done by every single person in the room, insincere and impersonal revelry wasn't exactly his cup of tea. So, when several pairs of hands started nudging Troy towards the front of the room, urging for a speech, he recoiled. Begging for escape, he leaned down and whispered to the pretty redhead next to him. "Sam, any ideas?" Samantha Wade grinned, leaning on her tiptoes to reach her date's ear. "I think you're out of luck, buster." Troy shook his head, refusing until the last moment to go along, but finally gave up and assented. He looked down at the floor, searching for words and stalling for time, flicked a few microbes of dust off his charcoal gray jacket, cleared his throat and eventually looked back up to the small crowd. Here he was, a man who made his living on national television, who cut promos in front of cameras and live crowds on a weekly basis that had earned him a great deal of praise, and he couldn't find a few words of thanks at a small party. Such was the way Troy's brain worked. When that little red light on the camera went off, so too left his desire to perform like a dancing monkey for an organ grinder. "Umm, I'm not really one for giving these kind of speeches..." ***** 15 years earlier... He had only one thought on his mind. "I still can't believe they're making me do this." Troy Douglas fidgeted in his cap and gown, shuffling the small stack of index cards nervously with his right hand. Inside the gymnasium that bore his father's name at center court, the crowd was still, but buzzing. It was at about this moment Troy decided to bludgeon each and every person who had voted for his as senior class president. Sure, it looked good on his college scholarship forms alongside his achievements on the gridiron and the hardwood, but had he realized that he would have to speak in front of 1,500-plus parents, friends and family members gathered at his own graduation, he would've run so fast in the opposite direction that the Earth's rotation may have reversed itself. Troy bobbed on his feet, waiting for the headmaster to call his name so he could get the damn ordeal over with, get his diploma, toss his cap in the air and get on with the rest of his life. Right now, though, all Troy Douglas wanted was a bucket to vomit in, or at the very least a way out that wouldn't set off a massive chain of fire alarms. The butterflies in his stomach were dancing in a conga line, and he was certain that, were he not leaning against a cement wall, his knees would buckle and he'd flop to the grimy tile floor like a dead fish. Luckily, there was a 5-foot-3 brunette confidence boost standing right next to him. "You'll do fine, you know," Lauren Bennett said. "So says you, Miss Valedictorian." Lauren flipped her patented coy smile, batting Troy playfully in the thigh with her bundle of notes. Which, as Troy made a mental note of, were significantly longer than his own. "You planning re-reading Lincoln's second inaugural? Or Gone with the Wind, maybe?" Lauren chuckled, her face twisting slightly into a wry but defensive scowl as she slid next to him on the wall. "I am not! I just ... had a lot to say." "I'm sure you do," Troy said, reaching an arm around her shoulder and leaning down to place his chin on the top of her head, turning her into a makeshift headrest. "You'll be fantastic. 'Specially with me as your opening act. Can't help but knock 'em dead after that." She stepped away from him, both of them now smiling widely. They were so comfortable together, like opposite parts of a clasp that fit together with perfect precision. No matter how many times they used it, it still clicked. Just like clockwork, he always said. "God," she said. "We're really leaving this place, aren't we?" Troy looked through the small window on the gym door to see it decked out in red and black. At the front of the small sea of humanity were his classmates, all dressed in their academic regalia. "Yep. End of the road, I guess." They looked at each other wistfully. For their high school career, this was the end, but they knew it was far from the end for each other. In 10 weeks they'd head to college together, and beyond that, bright visions of a future life together. Neither was sentimental nor obsessive enough to talk about plans for five, ten years down the road, but even without saying it, both Troy and Lauren knew that they couldn't see a future without the other involved. They held each other for a moment, smiling, until Troy finally heard the words that he'd been waiting for echo from the gym. "...please welcome, your senior class president, Troy Douglas." Troy looked down at Lauren, still nervous. She gave him a curt nod, almost ordering him to go forward, and he quickly shuffled his feet to the door. "C'mon, off with you, then." "Once more unto the breach, I guess." He gathered his notes once more, pushed the door open and walked out into uncertainty. ***** "...it's been an absolute honor to work with all of you on this, and I just want to say thank you. Cheers." The applause was light, and once more, champagne glasses tilted skyward. As he walked back to Samantha, Troy polished off the rest of his glass in one gigantic gulp, then groaned as he set it down on a side table. "Thirsty, are you?" Troy looked down at Sam to see her grinning widely. She was almost always smiling, even when she was in a less than stellar mood, and it was one of the things that endeared her most to Troy. The smile changed from time to time as her demeanor did, but the grin was almost always present, and Troy was rapidly falling in love with its many forms. "I can't stand these parties," he said. "Yeah, not exactly my cup of tea either. Still, you worked hard on this book. Time to celebrate, right?" "I really don't need imported champagne and speeches to celebrate. Or a few dozen people I've never met and likely will never meet again." Sam's smile quickly turned mischievous. "Then, let's blow the damn popsicle stand!" Troy feigned shock and horror, but his expression became conspiratorial as the wheels began to turn in his head. "Why the hell not? Let's let the corporate types have their bash and we find somewhere far, far away from Jan from Accounting." "I've got just the place in mind," she said, her green eyes twinkling. "Lead the way then." A quick excuse and a handshake with David got the couple out of the room unhurried, and soon it was just a 20-story elevator ride back down to the streets of Manhattan on a cool Indian summer night. Of course, if Troy had known where they were headed, he might have preferred to stay at Plainwell & Marks. ***** "I can't believe you brought me to a KARAOKE BAR." Sam winked as she put the finishing touches on her margarita. The two of them were drunk, no doubt about it, if the raucous laughter and the way they were leaning against each other for support weren't dead giveaways, not to mention the free-flowing alcohol. "Come on, everyone loves karaoke!" Troy leaned back from the table, looking Sam dead in the eyes. He might be drunk off his nut, but he was still lucid enough to state his long-held mantra. "The two greatest evils ever foisted upon the universe are organized religion ... and karaoke. I do my best to avoid both." "But, you like music!" "I like listening to music, and I took guitar lessons for six months in junior high. This isn't music. This is making an ass of myself in public for no good reason." "For me?" Through her tequila-induced state, Sam Wade still managed to pout innocently, pleading Troy to go on with it. She'd already made the rounds twice, including a rather stirring rendition of "Copacabana". If she was willing to do it, why shouldn't he? One look into her eyes, and the battle was over. "Well," he said. "You just gave me a good reason." "Good. Now shoo!" He balanced himself for a second before regaining his equilibrium, then walked up to the empty stage. For a moment, he had no idea what to try as he leafed through the catalog, but then, out of the corner of his eye, he saw the song. For her. A quick tap of some numbers, and all of a sudden the music began to swell around him. Maybe it was the tequila - or more likely the look in Sam's eyes as he focused on her from the stage - but he quickly threw himself into the song. Didn't even need to look at the screen. Though the lights pointing down at him were bright, they broke at just the right point so that all that he could see was the tired, smiling face of Samantha Wade. He stepped down from the stage, walked slowly back to their table, and stuck his arm around her. "There," he said, "you got me to do karaoke. Chalk one up for you." "Gladly," she said.
