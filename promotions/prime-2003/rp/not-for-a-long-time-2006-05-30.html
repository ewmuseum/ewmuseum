Not for a long time

30 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/30/2006 Well... crap. Titan eased himself back down into his seat on the airplane with a sigh. Once more, hitting the A-List where it counted had escaped him. Sure, he had wins over them and had gone the distance with two of them at once, but he still hadn't taken away their prestige. Someone had, which he noted to himself with small satisfaction, but he had wanted to do it as well. He eased himself into the wide frame of the first-class seat, a necessity for him. This time, he wasn't lucky enough to land the outer row seat, so he squeezed into the middle seat. Even those wider seats weren't quite big enough for him, but they were as close as he could get. So he took a deep breath and leaned his head back. Crap. “Well, I didn't think I'd see you again,” came a bright voice. The big man's head came up and he turned his eyes to the aisle. There, stood the slim redhead he'd met on the flight in. She plopped down in the seat next to him, dressed in a light gray suit that complimented her dark red hair. He smiled at her. “No, I have to admit, I wasn't expecting it, either,” he replied. She crinkled her nose in response. Now that was interesting. The nose-crinkling was something Profile 21 had taught him was an unconscious signal that a woman wanted a man to find her cute. Titan considered this a moment; she was certainly attractive, slim but still having full curves, and very obviously intelligent. Her bright personality was refreshing as well, in the face of the constantly guarded, sometimes paranoid attitudes of a backstage wrestling atmosphere. Her bright, blue eyes were trained on his face continuously, signs of direct interest in the response of the subject. Titan was taught to not look at a subject while interrogating, so that responses seemed unimportant and the subject would be more desperate to give up something of importance. “So what are you doing flying back to Savannah?” she asked with a smile, turning her petite body in the seat to face him. Titan shook his head. Every signal he was picking up was totally genuine. “Is something wrong?” she asked. “Okay, listen, I have to tell you a few things,” the man called Shadow Mountain said slowly. “I was in a pretty rough division of the IDF MP. I learned how to profile people based on nonverbal signals and such, and every signal you're sending me is completely genuine. It's something I'm really not used to in my new line of work.” “What kind of work is that?” she said, putting an elbow on the armrest between them and plopping her chin into her palm. Another signal of interest. “I'm a professional wrestler,” Titan responded, bracing for the response. “I started in the FBI in this country and, thanks to some somewhat crazy stuff happening, I ended up on PRIME's roster.” “You know,” she said, her eyes drifting towards her upper left. That was a signal of attempting to remember, whereas lower right was attempting to lie. “I've never watched that, but I might have to, now that I know someone on the show.” She smiled broadly at him. “And as for your profiling, of course I'm interested. I don't get to meet many giant Israelis. Now I know a giant, Israeli wrestler. It's fascinating.” Titan is just completely taken off-guard. “Okay, miss...” “Stephanie,” comes the quick reply. “Okay, Stephanie,” Titan says slowly, “I'm trying to figure out how to approach this. This conversation isn't the norm for me. I'm usually picking out deception and lies when I talk to people and you're just being so blatantly honest, it's really throwing me off.” “I like to be honest,” she replies. Her eyelashes flutter for just a second and she brushes some hair back over her shoulder. Ah-hah, she's attracted. WHAT? “I don't know if you realized it, but you just basically sent a signal to me that you're attracted to me.” The big man says this frankly, not knowing how exactly to handle the situation. “So you're not used to honesty and you're not used to having women be attracted to you?” She smiled and sat back in her seat, crossing her legs and putting a hand on her knee. Yet another pose of nonverbal attraction. The big man rubbed his forehead. This was... just bewildering “That's pretty much it,” he said to her. “Well, when was the last time you went on a date?” she said. This conversation was real direct. “That'd be back in my early IDF days. That's been a long, long time,” he almost muttered the last to himself. “Well, then, it seems to me that you just need to start again. So tell me why you're going back to Savannah, how long you're going to be there and we'll figure the rest out.” She smiled at him, a wide grin of very white teeth. “My parents live in Savannah,” he said simply, “and I figured it was as good a place as any to live. Pretty moderate temperatures, nice enough place, all that good stuff. As for going back, heck, the place I bought still has boxes stacked up.” “Well, I live in Savannah, too,” she smiled back, “just outside of town. So maybe I can help you unpack or something.” “Okay, Stephanie, don't take this the wrong way,” the giant said, holding a hand up, palm outward, “but why are you so interested in me? I don't get it.” “You don't?” she said, inclining her head to the side. “Let me just come right out and say it. You're a handsome man, you're very smart and observant and you're not taken. Plus, you're a big, strong type. I've always loved a tall, dark, handsome, available man.” Well, that was about a direct an answer as someone could hope for. “Besides, I like honesty in a relationship and I want someone that can call me on it. I've always been able to call my boyfriends when they were lying, but they never could pick up on any signals I gave them.” “So the, ah, logistics of this wouldn't bother you?” “Why would they?” she said with a laugh. “Men serve lots of functions. Carrying things, getting things off the top shelf, killing things, being soft to lay on. I can't think of a thing in that list you wouldn't be better than most men at.” “I guess that might be right. So you need to tell me what exactly you do, Stephanie.” “Oh, I just work in the historical district in Savannah,” she replied with a wave of her hand. “The historical district?” he asked, intrigued. “Let's see... Masters or PhD?” “What?” “People that work on historical districts don't get sent on plane rides first class for work for no reason.” “I should already know better than to try and get anything by you,” she smiled at him. “No, I have an MBA and a Masters in American history. I got hired to run the historic district about a year ago.” Titan smiled. “You sure you're not fishing for a big guy to move things?” “Oh,” she laugh, patting his forearm, “no, we have whole crews of people for that. You, I just want to move around things in my house.” Titan couldn't help but smile broadly. Not a date for years, and this was already starting off well. God bless America.
