Snowfall: Kings Over Aces

Date: March ~DEAN (DAD)~ March 12th Ohio seasons were pronounced, covering the spectrum of weather. Summers were often filled with blistering heat and humidity, covering everything in the sweat of the earth. Winters were cold, occasionally capable of freezing the snot in a man’s nose. And there was snow. Sometimes a few flurries. Sometimes several inches. Or, like 2010, several feet. The start of March had brought the first warm temperatures of the new year. High 50s and lots of sun. The blankets of white had been vanquished by the sun, and what remained were dead yards, grass as brown as the mud. It was amazing what horrors lied beneath an otherwise perfect surface. As he headed up the drive of his parent’s home, a toddler in his left arm, and his little angel holding his right hand, Matt marveled at the mountain of snow piled up near the curb. It was misshapen, turning an ugly black around its base, and no matter how much radiant glow the sun cast down, the mound of grotesque snow wasn’t going anywhere. Like a fucking tumor. “Gampa!” Georgie released her father’s hand and ran up the driveway to her grandfather’s waiting arms. “Guess we know which one of you she likes best.” Mary took her daughters place, grabbing her husband’s hand as they followed behind. “That’s cuz he spoils her.” Matt watched as his father kissed Georgie on the cheek and swung her around. “Too much.” “C’mon inside.” Dean Ward shifted his granddaughter in his arms. “Your mother made Pizza Caserole for lunch.” “Does Mom make anything other than casseroles these days?” “No.” He shot his son a look of annoyance. “I made the mistake of recommending we order a pizza, and this is what we got.” Everyone had a quick laugh as they pushed their way through the front door and into the living room. Things were a bit disheveled, unusual, considering his mother could have been a professional cleaning champion. She carried a can of Pledge and a tattered old t-shirt, converted into a rag, the way a painter carried a brush and palette, creating brilliance anywhere they could find a canvas. A couple articles of clothing sat scattered across the back of the smoky blue love seat, a TV Guide and Newsweek laid on the floor, and layers of dust covered what were usually glistening table surfaces. Given the events of the past week, it all seemed painfully acceptable. “Pizza Casserole smells good, Mom.” “I made plenty of it.” “I’ll bet.” Matt handed Hunter off to Mary, kissed his wife on the cheek and crossed the living room, heading into the kitchen. “Haven’t had any in awhile.” “You probably don’t eat nearly well enough on the road, Matthew.” “Jesus, Christine. He’s 34 years old. I’m sure he can manage his own diet.” “Thanks, Pops.” He gave his father a quick pat on the shoulder. “And I’m 33.” “Oh.” “So how ya doin, Dad?” “He’s managing. It’s been tough on both of us.” “I can answer myself, Christine.” “She means well, Dad.” “I have Prostate Cancer. It’s not like I’ve become a def mute. Still quite capable of answering ‘fine’ when someone asks how I’m doing.” “Except ‘fine’ isn’t the truth.” “Yes, it is.” Dean rolled his eyes. “How long till lunch is ready?” “Probably ten minutes.” “Come to the garage, bud. Your ol’ man got himself a new toy.” “No kiddin’?” “Yup.” The two generations headed through the kitchen, connecting with the garage just behind the laundry room. Matt flipped the light switch on the wall to the left, and immediately, his eyes were hit with the hues of ocean waves. “Wow.” “ ’67 Corvette.” Dean turned back to his son, a smile wide as the car. “In marina blue. Your dream car.” “Had to sell the Camaro, but when I saw this on the internet, I had to have it. Wanted to own one of these things since I was 15. Drove all the way to Tennessee to pick ‘er up.” “She’s gorgeous, Dad.” “Hope I’ll be around long enough to really enjoy it.” Matt didn’t know what to say, and so he gave his dad a pat on the shoulder the same way he had for forgetting his age. "Lunch in five minutes, boys" Father and son both turned toward the door to the kitchen. “Delightful.” Dean folded his arms across his chest. “So, I go in for surgery at the end of this month. Goin’ to Cleveland. They’ve got a good cancer treatment center there.” “What’d the doc say?” “It’s fifty-fifty.” “How complicated is the surgery?” “They said it could take four hours or more.” “This shit ain’t right.” “You’re all angrier than I am. Stuff happens all the time. Hell, I forget how many men are diagnosed with Prostate Cancer every year. It’s a lot, I remember that much.” “But it’s supposed to happen to guys in their sixties. You’re barely the other side of 50.” “I’ll be fine.” “Think I’m supposed to be the one reassuring you.” “Don’t worry about it, Matthew.” There was a moment of silence, before Matt finally mustered something worthwhile. “Love you, Dad.” “Love you too, bud.” “Car sure is beautiful.” Matt folded his arms in the same manner as his dad, and looking down at the glimmering blue surface of the ‘Vet, he couldn’t tell their reflections apart. ~THE POKER TABLE~ A Couple Night Before Pulling at the corner of his beat-up old cards, their colors faded and layers peeling, Matt peered down at a suited 9-3. Without dismay or gusto, he flicked the cards across the green felt of the table. “You gonna muck every hand tonight, Tchubert.” “Listen, hippy, you’d be foldin’ too if you weren’t catchin’ any cards. I’m beginning to forget what those one’s with the faces even look like. Think I’ve seen one queen all damn night.” “You talkin’ bout this one?” Nova scratched at his beard as he tilted his head, nodding in Lindsay Troy’s direction. “Yeah.” “Hasn’t seen much of me lately. Matty’s become quite the hermit ever since that whole retirement thing.” “Will you three shut up and play poker. And I raise.” Sonny Silver pushed a stack of chips forward, tripling the size of the big blind. “Besides, what’s all this ‘retirement’ talk? Last I checked, The Inhuman Being was back in PRIME, failing miserably. Just let the conservative pussy fold his cards and stink up the ring in peace.” “Dude, everybody knows retirement in pro wrestling is like blowjobs on the dance floor at clubs. You hear about it all the time, there are websites that swear its true, but has anyone really ever seen it with their own eyes?” “Is The Risen Star hanging out at a bunch of dance clubs?” “You know it, Wardsy. How I keep my ring agility sharp in my off-time.” “And I wouldn’t exactly say Matt is stinkin’ it up in the ring. He made the semi-finals of JitC.” Lindz called Sonny’s raise as she came to her friend’s defense. “Semi-Finals… somebody throw this man a parade!” Silver laughed at his own joke while he waited for Nova to burn a card and lay out the turn. 9-3-2 “Shit. Figures.” Matt slouched back in his seat and folded his arms across his chest, watching the hand play out while he listened to three of his former co-workers bicker about the state of his career. The back of his brain pounded against his skull, yearning to scream out, echoing Sonny’s complaints about too much chatter. He didn’t want to be hearing a play-by-play breakdown of his career. Poker night was for Texas Hold ‘Em, beer, March Madness predictions, and brief discussion on the best tits in Hollywood. He looked across the table at Troy, and she gave him a half smile. Maybe not so much conversation about tits tonight. Grabbing a chip in anxious boredom, Matt flipped it between his fingers, walking the blue ceramic circle up and down across the width of his hand. Troy won the pot, pairing her Ace on the turn, and Silver spat and cursed his misfortune as he gathered up the deck. “My deal.” “The Guy does have two Universal Titles to his name.” Putting his hands behind his head and kicking his feet up on the table, Nova continued the defense Lindz had mounted moments earlier. “That’s pretty impressive.” “Plus the Jewel in the Crown in 2005 and the Dual Halo in 2007.” “Word. Queenie is right. He’s won both of those. I was there, dude… I saw it.” “And Matty was PTC Unified Cha…” “Christ,” Silver cut off his former partner-turned-nemesis as he glanced at his hole cards and folded them, “do you two nutjobs own stock in the guy’s career or something? Listen to yourselves. ‘2005 this… 2007 that…’ what has the guy done lately? Ward hasn’t been the Wrecking Ball since I beat his ass at King of Kings. “Guys, Sonny is right.” For just a moment, Matt thought he could taste the pungent familiarity of vomit rising up his throat. Looking down at Jack-Deuce, and committed as the big blind, he checked, going heads up with Nova. “I’m a shell of my former self out there.” Silver burned a card before turning over the flop. A rainbow J – 8 – 4 “Twenty bucks” Matt placed his bet and watched in anticipation while Nova peaked at his cards. “I raise.” Nova put out four small stacks of chips and grinned. “Make it eighty if ya want to play, Wardsy.” Running his fingertips along the two-day stubble of his chin, Matt stared down the man who had stabbed him in the back and taken his Universal Title when the Team PRIME/FU battle lines had been drawn. He watched the way Nova breathed, looked for anything in the often-stoned gaze of the Risen Star. Taking in everything he could, Matt propped his elbows on the edge of the table. “I think you’re bluffin’, Champ.” Nova may have smiled, it was impossible to really tell in that jungle of a beard. “So then call me.” ~GEORGIE (PUMPKIN)~ “Is Gampa sick?” Letting his eyes wander from the road for just a moment, Matt glanced up into the rearview mirror and watched his little princess in the back seat, as she scribbled in her coloring book with a yellow crayon. “Yeah, pumpkin. Grandpa’s sick.” “Is he gonna die.” Matt’s hands tightened around the wheel, the leather squealing under his grip, and the torrent of questions made him thank God that Hunter wasn’t old enough to understand any of it. Even in kindergarten, Georgie was too young to even know what cancer was, or what it meant to die, but she knew the words, and while she couldn’t understand their severity or permanence, she knew enough to be curious. He didn’t know what to say, or if it even mattered, with so much of it over her head. So much of it was over his head. Locking his eyes back on the road, Matt tried to focus on his driving while he searched for anything to say. Suddenly, he felt his wife’s hand squeeze his thigh, letting him know that it was ok, she would handle it. His death grip on the steering wheel eased as she held tighter to his leg. “Grandpa will be ok, Georgie. But he might be feeling icky for awhile.” “Like when I ate too much candy on Christmas?” Matt smiled, the first time in at least two days. “Yeah, kinda like that, angel.” “Will Gampa go to the hospital.” “Geez” He couldn’t help it, hard as he tried, and an exasperated sigh escaped his lips. Again, he felt Mary squeeze his thigh. “Did I do something bad?” “No, pumpkin.” Matt glanced his glance to his two children in the backseat. “Daddy’s just tired.” “I never been to a hospital. Are they fun, Mommy?” “ When we go to visit Grandpa Ward, we’ll go down to the cafeteria and you can’t get all the chocolate milk you want.” “Really?” “Absolutely.” Mary took over fielding the questions, watching as Matt propped his left elbow against the window and tucked his head into the palm of his hand. “Can you have chocolate milk for lunch at a hospital?” “You sure can.” “Gampa is lucky.” Georgie scribbled away in her coloring book, dropping the yellow crayon in favor of purple. “Not that lucky, pumpkin.” Matt rejoined, though he never moved his head from its spot propped next to the window. “Grandpa doesn’t like chocolate milk.” ~THE POKER TABLE~ About an hour later “I can’t believe that wench called my raise with an inside straight draw. Seriously?!” “No big, deal.” “Speak for yourself, hippy. It was a bad beat. And bad beats are bullshit when they happen to me.” “Poor Mr. Silver’s just grumpy cuz he was busted by a girl.” Under the table, Nova felt the toe of a boot slam into his shin and a weak grunt escaped his lips as he looked across the felt, catching Troy’s glare. “Not that there’s anything wrong with that. Chicks are awesome.” “Yeah, me being a ‘conservative pussy’ doesn’t look so bad with you sitting over there outta chips and licking your wounds.” “Shut it, Ward. Like you’ve got room to talk. I’m not the only one who lost to a girl recently.” Silver raised his hands in the air, framing a make-believe marquee. “Tyler Rayne defeats Tchu. Ring a bell?” “We get it, Sonny. The guy came up short at King of Kings. He’s still got a pretty good track record.” “When dinosaurs roamed the earth. What does any of that count for nowadays, Lindz?” “Guys a champ, that’s all that matters.” Nova tugged at his beard, contemplating his next move. With trip-queens after the flop, he felt like making back some of the money he’d slowly been losing over the course of the night. “I’m gonna raise.” “With what? You’ve got three bucks left, stoner.” “I’m all in. It’s four bucks.” Nova shot a nasty glare Sonny’s way. “Plus, the very first bong I ever owned.” Nova reached under the table and pulled forth the bong, setting it with his chips in the middle of the pot. “Dude, are you kidding?” “What?” “That thing looks like it was made from a can of Quaker Oats and some hollowed out Lincoln logs.” “So?” “So it ain’t worth ten cents.” “It has high sentimental value.” “Gimme a break.” “You were a lot cooler when you were doing the Mr. Silver schtick.” Nova turned his attention towards Matt. “Why did you invite this douchecanoe?” “Seemed like a good idea at the time.” “You’re all just pissy because I’m over hear tellin’ the truth while you two play kiss-ass with the host. Like just because we’re sitting in Ward’s basement, that means he hasn’t lost a step. “Never said he hadn’t lost a step, but you don’t have to keep harping on it, Sonny.” Troy’s plain-spoken words struck Matt in the same way her boot had Nova’s shin. With a perplexed look smeared across his face, for the first time in the evening, he found himself becoming defensive. “Now you’re piling on too?” “No. It’s not a big deal, Matty. Everybody loses a bit. You’re not twenty-something anymore.” “It is a big deal, Lindz.” “Is anybody calling my raise?” Nova checked his cards, making sure nothing had changed since he’d made the gamble and put his prized bong up for grabs. “This poker game went to hell when that witch busted me with a lucky card on the river.” Sonny flipped the bird in Troy’s direction. “Ya know, it’s easy for you guys to sit there and say it’s no big deal and tell me all about my accomplishments.” Shaking his head, Matt let loose, no longer biting his tongue. “Each one of ya assholes beat me in some of the biggest matches of my life.” “I beat you two or three times. I think.” “You beat me like twelve times, Star.” Matt picked at the corners of his cards. “Fact is, I know I’m not 28 years old anymore. I know I was away from the ring for nearly two years. But that doesn’t mean I can’t still be a king in this business.” “Yeah, and that’s nice and all, but there are a handful of kings. You used to be the fuckin’ ace of spades. There’s only one of those.” “What’s your point, Sonny?” “Point is, you don’t have that same physical ability ya used to have. So if you still wanna be the best, you better decide you want it and step it up in other ways.” “Who said I didn’t want it?” “You did… when you lost to Rayne and disappeared for the next month. I mean, when you lost the Universal strap to Clyde Walkins, you came out the next week, and you threw the gloves off, challenged the whole fed, and the anti-PRIME was born. When you came up just short in your first Halo, you opened up the next ReV by beating Vangelus Olsig up one side of the arena and down the other. This time, you took your ball and went home. That’s not the Matty I know.” “Lindz is right, Tchubert. When I beat you for the Universal title, you made it your mission to kick my ass and destroy FU.” “I was faster and quicker than you, Matty. Nova was at his peak. Sonny wanted it more than you did. Ya enter every match with disadvantages. Now, you’ve just got to deal with the fact that the day has arrived where, every time you step into that ring, the mileage on your body is a disadvantage.” “It’s like you wanna be in that ring, wanna win, but you expect it to come as easily as it did four or five years ago. And that shit ain’t gonna cut it.” Silver slapped the table, causing stacks of chips to topple over. “You gotta think smarter, want it more. If ya don’t, you’re gonna have a lot more people who’ve kicked your ass to invite to the next poker game.” “Thanks, Sonny.” Scratching the back of his head, Matt let out a deep sigh and looked at the mess of cards and chips on his table. “What the hell happened to our poker game?” “It was lost after I went all-in with the bong.” “No shit. I…” ”Hey, bro.” Matt’s head snapped around, following the booming sound of the voice as it traveled down the stairs. Immediately, he turned back to the table, and took in the emptiness. Five unoccupied chairs sat around barren green felt, a tiny tundra void of any action. All the chips were tucked neatly in their case. In his hands, Matt rolled the brown glass of a Blue Moon, just a last bit of backwash swishing around the bottom, completely unaware that he’d even consumed the beer. He bit at his lip and narrowed his eyes, as if squinting would bring back everything he would have sworn was just there. Again, he heard the voice. ”Bro, you down there?" “What’s goin’ on, Scotty.” Matt’s younger brother thumped down the stairs, joining his voice. “Just got off the phone with the guys. Should be here in twenty. Chuck says his wife wants a nice vacation, so he’s gonna take all our money tonight.” “Really? Chuck?” “Guys got no poker face.” “Ain’t that the truth.” “So what the hell were ya doin’ down here? Playin with yourself?” “Just talkin’ to ghosts.” “I’ll grab ya another beer?” Scotty nodded towards the bottle in front of Matt as he headed for the mini-fridge behind the custom bar. “Yeah,” Matt looked around the empty table one last time, tried to hide any tremble in his voice “better keep ‘em comin’ tonight, Scotty.” ~MARY (BABY)~ “I just washed up the kids and tucked them in for a nap. You wouldn’t believe the places pizza sauce can get.” Mary slipped into the bedroom, wrapping her arms around the waist of her husband, resting her cheek against his broad back. “I can believe it.” Working around his wife’s embrace, Matt grabbed a stack of t-shirts and dropped them into the corner of a suitcase that sat sprawled atop the mangled sheets of their un-made bed. It was the picture of perfection, his packing job, everything tucked away in perfect alignment, the right corners of this folded clothes matching perfectly with the corners of the suitcase. Socks layered atop underwear. Shirts stacked according to color, all the warm hues together, the cool one’s just beneath. His mind worked in shifts, trying to see a step ahead, as if he were solving a Rubik’s cube. “How are you doin’, sweetheart?” “I’ll be fine.” “Why don’t I believe you?” Stepping out from behind her husband, Mary grabbed his left arm and spun him around to look into his eyes. It wasn’t until she had him halfway around that she noticed the neatly organized luggage. “What’s the suitcase for?” “I…” Before he could respond, she figured out the answer herself and gave him no chance to finish. “You can’t honestly be thinking about going overseas right now?” “I have to, baby.” “No you don’t. You can stay right here in Ohio and I’m sure the world will still turn. How can you even be considering this with your father scheduled for surgery in just a couple of weeks?” Matt took a large stride toward his chest of drawers and grabbed a small bottle of pills. “What’s that?” “Oxycodone” “You’re taking painkillers? You don’t even take Advil for a headache.” “I’m not a young buck anymore. My body can’t take the same punishment.” “Even more of a reason to stay home, Matthew.” He folded the main flap of the suitcase over, and grabbed the zippers on each side, sealing the luggage. “This is what I need.” “What about your family? They need you to be here.” “Every day?” “If that’s what it takes.” “I can’t do it. Growin’ up, I don’t think those eyes ever showed any fear, and today, they’re suddenly bloodshot and aching to cry. But he won’t, cuz he doesn’t want to make it harder for anybody else. I can’t do it, Mary. I can’t sit there and watch him die. Something’s got to keep my mind occupied.” “So that’s your answer? Fly halfway across the world and leave your mother and father to deal with this without their son? You’re supposed to be their rock. Your father would be there every step of the way if it were you.” “He’s a good man, better man than I am. All I ever wanted growing up was to be half the father and husband that he was.” “So then do this. Be there for him.” “I don’t have it in me. Wish I did.” “Yes you do. You’re so much like your father.” Mary reached down to take her husband’s hand, but he was already reaching for the suitcase, hoisting it off of the bed. “Not enough.” He pulled the handle free from his case and rolled it into the living room. Mary bit at her fingernail, then followed behind him. “You’re really gonna leave?” “I need to do this.” “Unbelievable. Just go ahead and hide on the road, Matthew.” She spun on her heels, trudging back toward the bedroom. A couple seconds later, the sound of the door slamming rattled the picture frames on the walls, and woke the children. Matt lowered his head and drug his baggage behind him. As he approached the front door, he hesitated briefly, making sure this was the right course of action. It was. Anything to keep his mind from thinking. He’d fly overseas, step foot inside that ungodly structure, board the plane to head home. And he’d hope the blackened mounds of snow will have burned away.
