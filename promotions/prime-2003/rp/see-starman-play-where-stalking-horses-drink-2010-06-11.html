See Starman Play: Where Stalking Horses Drink

11 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 11, 2010 See Starman Play: Where Stalking Horses Drink By William K. Spades Orlando Vacant Staff Writer Friday, June 11, 2010 HOLLYWOOD – It was a motel room night’s sleep. You know the one- stiff fabric cotton blanket that’s reached it’s rewash limit, gives you an itch, and at times scratches you where the semen hadn’t or couldn’t be washed out properly. Beneath that, cold white sheets and a bedspring that leaves your back feeling numb and crooked when you wake. Motel room night’s sleep: the closest thing to sleeping in a coffin without being dead. I regret not having dreamt anything fit to be described on these pages, but it’s the honest truth. People I know are always telling me about weird and lucid dreams they have, and I normally have nothing to contribute. Shit, I’m violating that old rule, huh? “Don’t make yourself the story.” For a moment, then, I’d like to address the issue of redundant motel catch phrases. “We’ll leave the light on for ya!” Of course you will, otherwise I’m gonna think you’re closed and drive right the fuck by. You’d be goddamn wise to leave the light on. How about the motel I’m staying in now, Super Eight: “Destination Super.” First of all, there needs to be a colon between “Destination” and “Super.” Second, if it’s called Super Eight, then I’m already assuming the destination is super. “Hey, where we going?” “Super Eight.” “Oh, I heard its Super.” “Yeah…I know.” Does that bug anyone else? I woke up from my non-dream, confused but with a heightened sense of…clarity maybe? Audio clarity. Maybe hearing, but I don’t think that’s it. A didgeridoo was playing somewhere close, perhaps outside, and its every change in pitch was distinctly caught by this new awareness of mine. It’s like I could see it, too. Shit’s weird when you’re waking up, isn’t it? My eyes opened despite the urge to roll over and force another sleep. Where the hell is that didgeridoo coming from? The clock says 9:31 am; is this person serious? Obscure Australian instruments should be illegal to play at such an early hour. Sleep has been shaken off, and I sit up to find the sound emanating from my room. It took a second to register, but yeah, this is real: Castor Strife is sitting cross-legged in a chair across from bed, playing a didgeridoo. “Hey dude,” I said, laughing a bit while do a yawn/stretch. “Nice flute.” I knew what it was, but I wanted to give him the satisfaction of correcting me. He was beginning to get more and more familiar to me. Silence was the retort. Well, not silence, he kept playing that instrument from hell while staring directly into my eyes. Is that level of oddness really necessary? “Wow, alright cool. You’re killing me with that thing, seriously.” For some reason my vocabulary drops to Keanu Reeves level until I’ve had some caffeine. This was the first I met Castor outside his home, and I must admit- I am much less intimidated and afraid to speak freely when we’re in an equal setting. The music stopped. “I’ve been reflecting lately. Until this morning, my place among my PRIME contemporaries had been unclear.” Castor spoke with the sureness of a sermonizer. “Other promotions regard me as nonpareil, a star among craters. They call me “oddity,” they call me Avant Garde.” “What changed this morning?” “…but what am I here? Not here, but HERE, in PRIME, what am I? Can you answer that? Can anybody?” I tried to answer, but he cut me off again. “Well I can. Among a catalog of men, even women, that include or involve kidnappers, murderers, armed forces, SWAT teams, police chiefs, more police chiefs, bitter ex-wives, doting girlfriends, police chiefs...I’ll TELL YOU what I am! I…am…normalcy. The rhythm to the wave, the calm weather, the changing of the calendar…I am MUNDANE!” He shook off my every attempt to ease his mind. “No, that’s what I am, don’t tell me otherwise. You ask me what changed this morning, Mr. Spades, and I shall now tell you. Only a few hours ago, after happening upon this revelation, I doused my property in gasoline, took a match, and set fire to my house and all my belongings in it. Hear me, now and forever, I BURNED MY OWN HOUSE DOWN AND I’D DO IT AGAIN!” How’s that for a kick in the teeth? News like that takes a second to register, but his cacophonous laughter robbed me of that second. “What will they say now?” he blurted out, barely able to contain himself. “Castor Strife won’t be called mundane again, I promise you that!” I paused, trying to capture a thought or a phrase, something with which to frame the moment before he ripped it off the wall and ran down the street with it. “You really did it, huh? Burned your house down?” “Mmhmm. To it’s very foundation. Oh you could visit it any time,” he said shaking his head, “it’s nothing but ash.” “Level with me- you need the insurance money, don’t you?” “Come on,” he spoke excitedly, “I have a table waiting for us. We’re getting breakfast, and we are going to celebrate this exciting metamorphosis of character. Let us dine, and toast to the PRIME UNIVERSAL TITLE!” Whatever, you think I’m about to argue with the guy? If he wants to burn down his shit, be my guest. Have at it, Hoss. Enjoy prison, unless you’re able to scam the insurance company somehow. And I have my doubts about that, considering he was out there at 6am in plain daylight pouring flammable liquid all around his house before striking a match. You’re going to need some world class bullshitting to get around that one. We weren’t even in the parking lot before the day manager came up and notified me of the complaints I received. “Were you playing instruments this morning?” he asks me. Yeah, sure, fine, it was me. I wake up to Castor Strife doing his best to fuck my day up. Free breakfast though- killer. Before we get to his car (his revelation, his breakfast idea, his fuel), he has something to tell me. “I didn’t really burn my house down, Mr. Spades. I wanted you to think I did. Let’s go.” “Uhh, ok then. Cool.” No music, we ride in silence, for the most part. Here we go, breakfast! …at the Waffle House? Come ON! “This is where I choose to eat this morning. Also on mind is why you don’t answer your phone. Why don’t you?” “I don’t know, I was sleeping I guess,” I say, pulling the phone from my pocket to check the deal. “Man, what THE FUCK?! 142 missed calls? Are you kidding me?” “Can I ask you something, Mr. Spades?” “Ugh, WHAT? What do you want to ask me? I just wanna eat, can you dig that bro? I don’t care if you burned down your house, or blew up my phone, or metamorphosis this and that or whatever…I’m hungry and I need caffeine!” “I did burn down my house.” “You did?” “Yes, I lied before. It’s burned, the whole thing, and we shouldn’t talk about it anymore. It’s gone, now and forever.” “…” “…” “So what do you wanna ask me?” “Which PRIME wrestler do I most remind you of?” Somewhere, deep down, I think he’s trying to goad me into quitting the story. “I really, really don’t fucking know. I don’t watch PRIME, I don’t watch wrestling, I DON’T CARE. Does it matter?” “No, because if you answered, I would have corrected you. There is no one in PRIME like me, nor will there ever be.” Alright, to make a long story short: we sit down for about a 40 minute breakfast. In between intermittent bathroom breaks to inject GHB into his dick vein and make calls on his cell phone, Castor admits once more that he did not, in fact, burn his house down, but he would have no problem doing so if it came to that point. Once we got around to talking about the upcoming pay-per-view event (at his insistence, not mine), he admitted to having a feature film to premiere, live in front of the audience. As for the title, well…he left me with this before dropping me off: “Capturing their glory isn’t the point; I’ve already done that. Leading a horse to water- ah, now that is quite the sleight of hand. If Castor V. Strife is to be their next Universal Champion, it will be unprecedented. Olsig and Emilio, they may be here to settle old scores, finish what was left incomplete, or simply fulfill a dream. I was brought to PRIME because I was told they needed a new page scratched into their tabula rasa. My presence is an investment into the future. The past…I’ll leave that to Olsig and Emilio. That page will have turned, and they will be left behind. For me, this title is power, and PRIME will never be the same for it once it’s around my waist. Never before has it been done, and never again will it be repeated.” If that seemed forced, that’s because it was. He insisted, and I’m an easy bribe. Orlando can fire me once I’m home, see if I care. I came to Hollywood for Dustin Diamond, and they gave me Castor Strife.
