Tabula Rasa: Interrogation

5 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 5th, 2007 Tabula Rasa: Interrogation Part One of Thirteen Monday August 7th, 2007 – 5AM PST Eight hours. That's how long I've been sitting inside of this small, dank room. Moments after I had suffered another defeat, which was starting to become commonplace, at the biggest PRIME PPV of the year my entire life came crashing down. After eight months of thinking I was in the clear my past had finally caught up to me. Silence fills the room and my red eyes scan the room, wondering to myself when it would all begin. I can feel each second passing by, the anxiety crawling across my skin. I can still remember every minute detail of that night, even eight months later. My blood was boiling from the reality of being betrayed and lied to. So many memories had hit me after months of not remembering everything and even though so much tore at me, the memory and feeling of being betrayed hurt the worse. She hadn't been my wife or the love of my life. She had been the bane of my existence and a blight on the world. One of my earliest memories was that of Jocelyn killing my wife and my daughter. I had no choice. I had to kill her. I had to know that eventually things would catch up to me. The life I had lead was far from clean, and sooner or later I would mess up. I hadn't clean the scene, instead leaving my blood and prints everywhere. I wasn't in my right mind that evening, but I also didn't regret it. She deserved what was coming to her. However being charged with her death couldn't of come at a worse time. I had so much to do and I could feel the clock running out on me. How could've I been so blind and naive? I need time and yet there isn't any for the taking. Then, finally, the door swings open and in walks a man that I can't despise. He's just doing his job, even if it means taking me out in the process of doing so. He wears a rather sharp suit and I notice almost a bounce to his step. Something tells me that this case hadn't been as simple as it appeared to be and that leaves questions as to why a case that seemed so easy had become that difficult. Yet, I push that thought out of mind as two other people walk in behind him. Two new players to the game. Daniel I had figured out a year ago. These two I would have to learn and learn fast before I was blind sighted by them. One of them has dark auburn hair while the other one is a blonde with a pair of glasses on. It's evident to me that the auburn-haired woman must be Daniel's assistant because of the stack of folders in her hands. The other woman looks rather professional. As her hazel eyes land on me, I can see a flash of hatred towards me in them. That throws me off guard for a fraction of a second. What had I done to her to warrant such treatment, such contempt? Daniel then pulls out the chair sitting across from and sits down in it rather slowly, never removing his eyes from me. I return the exchange by never removing his eyes from him as well. There is obvious tension in the room, a hatred exuding from Daniel that has seemingly transferred through osmosis to the others in the room. It's rather unsettling. I can feel my stomach churning from the anxiety when finally Daniel speaks. "My apologies for keeping you waiting for so long," he says and I have to fight back the urge to laugh in his face. Instead I keep my face straight and show not one ounce of emotion. My eyes remain locked on Daniel while I have three pair of eyes focused upon me. "I'm sure you are," I respond sarcastically. He smiles back at me, but I don't return the favor. Instead I keep staring at him as if being here is an inconvenience to me. I then can feel the woman, his assistant sitting down next to him, and as I glance over at her I notice that her eyes aren't filled with hatred, but intrigue. My eyes narrow slightly at the thought of this and then look back at Daniel. "You'll have to also excuse my rudeness. I've just realized that you haven't met these two women. The woman sitting next to me," he then nods toward the auburn-haired woman, "is my assistant Lindsay. The lovely woman standing behind me," he then turns in his chair to the blonde-haired woman, "is US District Attorney Madeline Young. She will be trying your case," he finishes telling me. As I look at Madeline, I get the feeling of her rather snooty attitude from the pure puss face she has on. Yet, I also get the feeling that she is a rather intelligent woman who is rather good at her job. This worries me some. I once again push the thought out of my mind. "What case are you talking about? I don't recall you telling me what I'm being charged for," I respond, my voice not showing any reflection in it. When Daniel escorted me out of the arena he had told Lisa that what I was being charged with was confidential. While I found that odd at the time, I hadn't thought much of it until I found myself sitting in the interrogation room. Why were the details of my case confidential? None of this was making much sense to me. "You know what case I'm talking about Craig, don't play dumb with me," he says and I once again fight the urge to smile right in Daniel's face. Of course I knew why I was here; I had known that this day was coming for the past eight months. But, I needed to deflate his ego, and begin stalling for time. Even in his words I can sense the irritation beginning to seep in. I know though that the time I would need to flip Daniel isn't there though, and that Daniel would throw everything he possibly could at me. "Your circumstances are unique, Craig," Madeline begins to say in a rather silky voice. I turn my eyes toward her and raise my intensity level up a few notches. "The courts are starting to feel that the celebrity trials are becoming detrimental to the country, the justice system, and to those who're being charged with crimes. There are celebrities who're found not guilty in a criminal trial, but have their very livelihood destroyed because of the charges levied against them. That's why your case is confidential to the degree that it is. The judge presiding over your case would rather not make her court into a circus event," the woman continues to tell me. With each passing word, it all becomes crystal clear to me. My trial is not going to be known to the world. What I've done won't ever be known. It's partly calming while also terrifying. But, at the very least, it’ll let me continue my work even though I have to know that there will be people watching my every move. “We’re waiting on your lawyer to get here first. We’ve already explained to him the special rules to your case,” the lawyer tells me, keeping the same tone in her voice. Then, there is a tapping sound. I glance over and noticed that Daniel is tapping his fingers on the steel table. From the look in his face, I can tell that he needs to ask me something; something that is bothering him. “What does a guy like you, a person who travels the world and wrestles people, need to pace a high-powered criminal attorney on retainer?” he asks me after letting the silence stew for a few seconds. I look at the attorney and can see the interest in her eyes as well, and then I look at Lindsay who is interested at well. There is something disconcerting about looking at three different pair of eyes with the same intent in their eyes. “I’m pretty certain that I’m not supposed to answer any questions until my lawyer gets here,” I respond. “You know who utter those words?” he asks me and before he gets the chance to answer his own question, I cut him off with words of my own. “Guilty people. Look, I can see that you’ve watched plenty of Law and Order episodes, but you could at least come up with something a little more original. If this is what I’m to expect when you start interrogating me, then I’ll get you one of my action figure dolls, and let you question him. That might actually be entertaining,” I say, knowing that my words are going to goad him. I can the vein in his forehead throb, and I know I’ve hit a rather nice spot. After a few more moments, he begins to calm down, and then the door to the room opens. In walks a man that I’ve only met once. Everyone in the room look at him and Daniel immediately gets out of his chair. “Good morning, ladies and gentlemen, I’m George Long, of Long, Rubenstein, and Long,” he says before shaking Daniel’s hand and then Madeline who slowly rises out of her chair. I look at George and nod towards him who flashes a smile back at me to try and calm my fears. The only problem with that is that I’m not scared; not of this. In the world, there are many more things that I’m scared of, most of all, myself. “Mr. Long, how nice of you to join us. We’re sorry to have called you at such a late hour and for you having to have travelled all of this way,” Daniel says as he shakes the lawyer’s hand. He simply nods his head before walking around the table. “Now, if you will excuse me, I do plan on taking my client out of here,” he tells Daniel and Madeline who immediately look ready to object to this piece of information. Yet, he stops them with simple rise of his hand as he continues on with his statement. “We’re all aware of his special circumstances, and our meetings are to be held at specific times and dates that aren’t detrimental to my client and allows the presiding court to move forward in a timely fashion. So, there is no bond being set upon my client nor is he being taken to a jail cell. So, if you’ll excuse us, we’re leaving,” he finishes telling them and Madeline closes her mouth. However, Daniel doesn’t. “That’s all good and well, but I would like to ask your client a few questions pertaining to the murder of Jocelyn White, which is what he’s being charged with at this time,” he says, the last of his words catching my attention. I look at George who shows no expression on his face, which is what I’ve been told, is a sign of a good lawyer. I glance over at Madeline who is firmly looking at Daniel now. “Then, you will schedule an appointment with my office and the courts, and we’ll do it at that time,” he responds and Daniel shakes his head. Yet, something intrigues me about Daniel’s persistence to this ordeal. Deep down, I get the feeling he is hiding something, something that he is just itching to throw into my face. Before Daniel gets the chance to say anything, I interject. “No, it’s fine. Daniel, you can have some time to ask me a few questions,” I tell him and he’s frankly shocked at this piece of information. He looks at Madeline who nods her head and begins to walk out of the room, followed quickly by Lindsay. I look at my lawyer who has a rather stern look on his face as he takes one look at me. “Let us talk alone.” He then walks out of the room and I know all three will be standing in a secluded room watching everything that goes on inside of this room. Yet, this is my time with Daniel, to pick his brain and find out what he knows. If there is one thing that I’m certain of, it is that Daniel underestimates me. I attend to use that to my full potential. "So, let me start off with asking about your alibi on the evening that Jocelyn was murdered," he says as he sits back in his chair, his elbows on top of the table as he leans towards me. I lick my lips slightly and then move forward as well. "That was eight months ago, detective, where were you?" I ask him, needing to know how soon he found out about the murder. "I was in Los Angeles on my way to see your wife," he says and I simply nod my head. "Were you having an affair with her, detective?" I ask him and his face turns to stone. Then, he laughs at this notion and shakes his head. He's rattled slightly, I can tell that much, and decide to continue to pursue this avenue that I know isn't really there. "Oh, come on, she was an attractive woman. So, I couldn't blame you, especially with the type of schedule I had. Tell me, how many times did you sleep with her?" He then leans back in his chair and stares at me. "You must think that you're so smart, trying to turn the tables on me Craig, but no, it's not going to work. You know full well that I wasn't sleeping with your wife. Trying to deflect my questions, but let me set the scene up for you a little bit. Twelve hours before she was brutally murdered, she gave me a call, and told me that she was worried for her life. I asked her why she was so worried, and she told me that it was because she was certain that you were going to kill her. Now, tell me, why would she think that?" he asks me and I don't have an answer for that. I pause for a second and can see a flash of victory in his eyes. I then clear my throat, knowing that I need to answer much quicker than this. "You'll have to ask her that, detective. I wasn't with my wife that evening or the evenings before that," I tell him and he once again nods his head. "So, where were you? I mean, it was around Christmas time, and I know for a fact that you weren't on tour as you had just been fired from Global Wrestling. Were you having an affair?" he asks me and the answer is a clear as day in my mind. I was in Europe, but then that would open a whole can of worms. "Needed some time to myself to be honest. Went travelling. You should try it some time," I tell him and he nods his head before he gets out of his chair. He then opens one of the folders that are still sitting on the table and begins to read from it. I wonder to myself what it is that he's reading, but then I know that eventually I'll know. Daniel couldn't hold all of his cards for long. "So, who's Jack White?" he asks me and I can feel my heart begin to race slightly. Yet, I don't let it show, not one bit. Instead, I remain calm as his words are fresh in my mind as he names the alias I had been using whenever I travelled to Europe. "The guitarist for the White Stripes. I didn't realize you wanted to play music trivia. Is that why you asked me here? Okay, I can play that game. Who's Michael Jackson?" I ask him, continuing to deflect his questions. However, now I've underestimated his focus, not realizing that he might be more honed in then I'd give him credit for. "Amusing. I figured you would know him since you purchased airline tickets for him," he says before dropping the folder in front of me. Right there before my eyes are tickets that I had purchased what felt like an eternity ago. A set of tickets under the name of Jack White. I glance at them before looking up at Daniel. "You'll also realize that the credit card that made these transactions is now cancelled. That's because the card was made in my name without even knowing about it. Apparently someone stole my social security number, and when I found out about this card I cancelled it," I tell him and he simply nods his head. There's something annoying about the way he nods his head, but I decide not to think about it any longer. I have to remain focused at the situation at hand. He's slowly starting to show me his cards and I know eventually the Ace is in there somewhere. "So, explain to me then how the flight attendant to the airport that the tickets are for remembers your face on that flight each and every time?" he asks. "Wow, I guess this guy really took identity theft a little far," I tell him, not even missing a beat. I then sit back in the chair and smile back at him. "Why don't you flip over those tickets and check the last one out?" he asks me and I wonder what else he has hiding there. I move forward towards the folder and flip past those tickets to find the bombshell; the ticket I had purchased the evening I killed Jocelyn. I swallow hard and look back at him. He had me as clear as day. "Well, that does look a little suspicious," I tell him and he nods his head. "It does, doesn't it? And surprisingly, the flight attendant on that flight remembers you sitting in your chair drinking quite a few of those fancy little bottles they like to give to first class customers. Were you drunk that evening?" he asks me and I look back at him. "I don't remember the flight very well. It's a long one and considering the fact that I do a number of flights a year, they all start to blur together. But, I guess since you have an airline ticket and testimony from the attendant that it might be true," I tell him and he just continues to look at me. "So why were you heading to Los Angeles, Craig?" he asks me. "To see my wife I would imagine," I respond. "For what?" he asks. "Look, my wife was a rather attractive woman. What do you think?" I ask him back. "I think it was to murder her. The attendant on that flight remembers you being rather grim and dark, not an ounce of happiness on my face," he tells me and I lightly lick my lips. "I also think that the woman is a dolt, but I don't know much about her just like she doesn't know much about me," I tell him. "Did your wife know you were coming out there?" he asks and I just simply shake my head before answering his question. "I was going to surprise her," I tell him. "Then why did she think you were going to kill you on that very evening?" he asks me and I take a second to respond. I glance away from him, breaking my trance for the first time in a little bit. I think about it all and realize that I don't remember much about that evening. Frankly, it was all a blur, a series of events that barely made any sense to me. Then, I look back at Daniel who is waiting for my answer. "You'd have to ask her," I respond. "Kind of hard to do so with her dead, don't you think?" he rhetorically asks and I don't have an answer for this one. I can tell by the way he looks at the file that he registers this and pulls the folder away from me. "So, what was it that you were doing in Europe, Craig? Having an affair?" he asks and I shake my head. "That would've been nice, but no," I tell him and he nods his head. "So, tell me about "hell mountain" then," he responds and then I begin to wonder who it is that he's been talking to. "You're going to have to go into more detail," I tell him and he stands right back up and begins to pace around the room. He looks at the mirror in the room and I can tell frustration is starting to set in. He then looks back at me and shakes his head. "Never mind. We've got time to go through everything at another time," he tells me before looking back at the mirror. "Lindsay, come in here and gather this stuff!" he yells and then opens the door and walks out. In rushes his assistant who is followed by a security guard who looks like he could fight. He moves around behind me as Lindsay takes a quick glance at me before starting to pick up the files. The door then closes on its own as she gathers her files, her eyes flickering towards me every few seconds. "I'm going to guess I'm not allowed to look at those files," I tell her and she looks at me with the same contempt in her eyes. Then, she sits down in the chair and looks at me. I can almost see the questions burning in her eyes. She couldn't be any older than twenty-one and already she was having to deal with a case of this magnitude. Part of me felt sorry for her and could only imagine what she had learned about not only me, but the world she had been thrown into. There is a certain sorrow in her eyes that softens my heart as she tries to find the words to speak to me. "Why'd you do it?" she asks me and her question, as simple as it is, baffles me. "What do you mean?" I ask her, trying to figure out what exactly she is talking about. "Why'd you kill her?" she asks me and the question is so pointed, so sharp, that it takes me aback a little bit. "Excuse me?" I ask her and I begin to feel my anger rise, suddenly being put on the defensive. I had given her an opening by softening up and she had taken it. I keep trying to fight my anger, but find it increasingly difficult. She sits back in her chair and continues to look at me with that same contempt but naiveness in her eyes. "She loved you and you murdered her. Now, you sit in this room, and so many consider you to be the man just because you wrestle. Yet, if they knew the truth about you then they would want to throw you into a lake of fire in a heartbeat. You're a celebrity so you're treated differently, but did you treat her any differently? Did she deserve what you did to her?" she asks me and the anger continues to rise. I begin to lose sight of myself and my fists start to ball up as she continues to hit me with accusation after accusation. "You don't know what you speak of, do you understand that?" I ask her through gritted teeth and I can see her face begin to tense up. I can see the anxiety and pressure of this job starting to get to her. "I don't?! Don't you see the damage you've done to people?! Daniel has been working at this for months now and yet you just make jokes in his face! You killed a woman and you don't show any remorse? You're just so damn lucky. You're not a man, or even human. You're a beast. A beast that doesn't deserve to live, much less the special treatment you're getting here," she says, each word rising in volume to the point that she is basically yelling at me. I begin to rise out of my chair as she backs up slightly. I can hear the blood pumping in my ears, nearly blocking everything else out. "You think you're so smart? Understanding everything? You don't understand a damn thing about this world, little girl. So, before you start judging me on assumptions and false lies that your boss has fed to you, why don't you ask yourself this? Why is your boss so hellbent upon taking me out? Did he tell you about how he tried to pin the murder of another woman on me, a woman I tried to save?!" I yell back at her and it all happens so fast that I have no choice, but to react. She continues to move away from me, and I begin to wonder as to why that is. Then, I realize that the guard behind me is moving before I even realized it as he goes to wrap his arm around my throat to subdue me. I immediately react as I grab his wrist and wrench it backwards, immediately snapping it into two or three pieces. I spin in place and thrust the palm of my hand onto his chin, snapping his head back into wall before I grab the collar of his shirt and flip him over my entire body onto the metal table. The door that was once closed flies open as I glance up and I can feel the fire in my eyes. The fear in Lindsay’s eyes nearly drowns me and I snap back to reality. At the threshold of the door are Daniel and Madeline, Daniel rushes up towards me and I back up. Even before Daniel is able to reach me, George enters the room with fury that I hadn't expected from him. "What the hell are you all doing in here?!" he yells at Madeline, Lindsay, and Daniel. "Trying to goad my client?! Oh, I will not have this, no I won't! You know the state that my client is in after having to battle in the ring earlier tonight, and you hit him with accusations and questions that will only anger him." He then turns towards Madeline. "I expected better from you and you should learn how to handle your detective and his assistant." Daniel turns towards George and begins to yell back at him. "Your client attacked an officer! I want him in handcuffs and his previous deal thrown out the window! He is not a man to be trusted!" he explodes in George's face and I can only look at everything that is unfolding before me. "Get out of my face before I charge you with conspiracy. We'll see you all in court one week from today," George tells him before moving around him and motioning towards me to start coming with him. I begin to move towards him when Daniel cuts me off and stands between me and him. I look at him, tempted to hit him, but am now in control of my anger. "No! I'm tired of playing these games with him. Craig, you're going to get the needle, and trust me I plan on putting it in you myself! Tell me about the two hundred people you murdered!" he yells at me and the words shock me as I take a step back wondering where he had found that out. I look at George who is looking around furiously. "What?" I ask him, the shock obviously written across my face. "You killed over two hundred people, Craig! Over two hundred people!" he yells back at me and it all becomes clear to me. Santiago’s endgame. I nearly throw up on Daniel's shoes as nausea washes over me. Yet, I'm able to maintain my calm and cool as I look him in the eyes. "Prove it," I tell him coolly and he turns around and grabs a file from Lindsay's hands. "I don't have to," he tells me as he slams a file on my desk and I realize it all. Daniel had documented everything. As I look at the file, I can feel everything coming at me, the walls closing in, and I look back at Daniel. Immediately, my lawyer grabs me by the wrist and begins to drag me out of the room. "We're leaving," he announces to the room. "Go ahead, leave. We'll see you soon, Craig, and the next time we do, you're going to be charged with the deaths of every single person in our files!" he yells at me as I walk down the hall with him. I'm still startled by all of the revelations and stop for a few seconds to catch my breath. My laywer looks at me and walks up to me to make sure I'm okay. "Is it true?" he asks me and I look up at him. "What?" I ask back, the room spinning around me. "Did you do it?" he asks me and I immediately know that he means everything that I'm being accused of. I look around and see that no one is around us and know that any conversation that George and I have is attorney-client privilege. "...yes," I say finally, knowing that eventually George would have to know. He sighs as he looks around, and I'm almost certain that he's going to be sick. I want to apologize for dragging him into the mess, but I know that he's dealt with cases at this level. It's the reason I hired him. It's his job to try and prove my innocence, and with the amount of money that I'm spending on him he will do everything he can to save me. "Is there anything that can save you?" he asks and I try to think if there is any evidence that could prove otherwise. However, from the mere sight of the files I've seen I know that Santiago took his time to put everything together, to make sure that I would be punished for my crimes. Even though I didn't remember anything as a result of him wiping my memory time and time again, I can't prove it. I had killed those people, I knew that much. The proof was there and there was nothing I could do to prove otherwise. "...no," I tell him and he just shakes his head. I know I've just made his job that much tougher. "What do you want me to do?" he asks me, almost as if I might as well plead guilty. I know my answer though immediately. This isn't about staying alive. "Stall for time. That's all I need," I tell him before I start walking down the hallway. I know he has more to say, but not tonight or this morning, whatever time it is. For now, I need to get out of here and get as far away as possible. As I walk towards the front of the door, I see a face that I desperately need to see. Katie. As she sees me Katie runs up to me to give me a hug. My eyes though are staring at the rising sun and see the red sunset that I’ve missed so often. With time slowly ticking away I know that this might be the last time I ever get to see this sunrise. I let that settle inside of me before turning towards Katie as she wraps her arms around me. I return the favor, happy to finally have a friend by my side. I’d taken her friendship for granted the past few weeks, pushing her away from me, and not trusting her. She came to me to help me and in the end I would end up helping her. I need her by my side if I intend to finish everything that I’ve started and learn everything I need to know. “As soon as I heard I rushed over here,” Katie tells me as we break the hug. I nod my head as we start to walk down the stairs towards my rental car. I can see that she’s already gathered my items and packed them up so we could get out of here. “They know everything. Jocelyn. Everyone I’ve killed. Santiago screwed me,” I tell her, using short sentences to get my message across. At the realization of all this, I can see Katie’s eyes get wide, but I ignore them. For me, I don’t have time to let my mind wander about what I’ve done. Instead, I have to act, and act fast. For now, I’m a free man, but how much longer could that possibly last? Something tells me not for long. As she gets behind the wheel of the car, she speeds off into the night as I start to lay everything out. “So, what do we do now?” she asks and I stall for a minute, letting the silence give me a few more seconds of thinking. Everything is connected, my mind starts to put the pieces together, and I try to find the best starting point. In all reality though, there is no good starting point. I can see the finish line, but I don’t know where I’m starting from and how long the race is. I look out the window, my mind not even registering the buildings or the people on the sidewalk. Instead, I’m in another world. And that’s when it hits me. “What we do now is stop letting these questions haunt me and start answering them. There are forces here that are even bigger than me, things that I can’t even begin to solve. Yet, there are those who understand me better than I understand myself. I know where one of those people is,” I tell her and she nods her head before turning the car down another street. I can only assume that she is heading towards the airport, but choose not to ask. With a limited amount of words left, I can feel the pressure starting to mount upon my shoulders. I don’t have time to talk. There is too much left to be done. “So, where does the story begin?” she asks me and I know that she wants to understand everything that’s happened since I’ve been arrested. Yet, I can’t answer that question now; in due time I’ll let her know everything that I know. For now though, I continue to think about the possibilities at hand when the very real reality of death hits me. I begin to ask myself what should happen if I don’t finish everything and what if I fail before I have the chance to succeed. I know that my work will never be done and I look over at Katie who looks she’s lost in her thoughts as well. “Katie,” I whisper and the mere sound of my voice is enough to jar her from her thoughts. She glances over at me and doesn’t even have to say anything for me to know that she’s listening to me now. “I need you to promise me something.” “Anything,” she tells me and I hesitate, and almost change my mind, to put such a large weight on her shoulders. However, I know that she can handle them and decide to trust her with this. “I need you to be able to finish what I start if I can’t. I need you to be able to remember my story because this story, I think this story is critical to the past, present, and future, and someone needs to be able to tell it if I can’t any longer. This way, someone can help you finish it all,” I tell her and I look back towards the window knowing that my last testament is safe with her. I don’t even need her response; I already know what it is. The background that is Seattle passes me by. Just like the world has. Just like life will. To Be Continued
