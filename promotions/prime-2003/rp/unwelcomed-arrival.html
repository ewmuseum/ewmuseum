Unwelcomed Arrival

Date: ReVolution 192 The Jazz chord filled the small apartment with sheer eloquence. As the chord lingered in the air for a brief moment, David's eyes were closed as his fingers skated against the glossy keys until he found what he was looking for and augmented the chord in a way that added dissonance and a richer tone to it at the same time. David shivered slightly, partly from the dynamic of the chord as well as from sitting at the piano with no shirt on. His mind was filled with simplicity as he began to play a song that had no title due to the fact that he was making it up as he went along. Fingers danced across the piano with grace as he felt the next notes before he even played them. As it switched to a minor and diminished tone it was evident the angst that he was feeling as his brow furrowed and the intensity was written into his face. "You're good," a voice came from behind him. David's eyes snapped open and he sat there for a minute as his chest heaved. Steps were heard inside of the room as a woman wrapped her arm around his shoulders and sat down next to him. A red silk sheet was the only thing that covered her body and it was appropriate considering that it matched her hair as well. "Thanks, Mary-Lynn," David said to her as he glanced over at her. She was beaming with happiness and beauty; months of flirting had lead them to this point. Deep down, David felt regret though as she had been a means to not feel alone in these very moments. His lips softly kissed her on the cheek as his left hand kept playing a few notes. She blushed from the attention he showered her with and craned her neck to show it. "Who wrote that song you were playing?" she asked him and he shook his head. "I did. I'm supposed to write a song for this composer named Eric Whitacre. He wants to publish it and do it with this choir he's working with. Just can't figure out the progression as it falls apart and turns into this weird Gregorian Chant feeling thing. It's not clicking as well as I would have liked," he informed her as he continued to toil away at the piano. Mary-Lynn smiled at him and shook her head as she realized there was so much about David that was a mystery to her. Her eyes wandered around the piano before she noticed something missing. "Where's your sheet music? I'd love to take a look at it," she asked of him and he grinned like he was a little kid. "There is none," he revealed and she looked astonished at this revelation. "What do you mean?" she questioned him with this shocked tone in her voice. David closed his eyes for a minute as he played the same chords he had played earlier, but still wasn't happy with the result of how the sound came out even though Mary-Lynn swore it was beautiful. He opened his eyes again and shook his head as it was evident he was disgusted with the way the song was coming out. "It's all in my head. Once it's perfect, then I write it down. Until then, I'm not going to waste paper on a potentially ruined idea," he told her and she just laughed at the notion. Even having known him for months, David never failed to amaze her, and it was that which drew her to him like a moth to a flame. "Why don't you come back to bed?" Her intentions were both meant for selfish reasons as they were for caring. "Can't sleep. Too nervous. Too...," and then his voice trailed off as he looked at the front door with apprehension. She rubbed his shoulders gently as Mary-Lynn felt the tension there. "Scared?" she asked him and David nodded his head. Pity slipped into her mind as she could see the trouble and pain he was experiencing from the moment she stepped through the threshold to his apartment. "David, I know that taking this step is going to be painful. Maybe too painful, but it's a necessary step. Your mother reuniting with her daughters. You get to see your sisters. It's going to be good for you. It's going to cleanse your system." David moaned as he closed the lid on his piano. Fear had overtaken him by this part. "She lied to me," he told her as he could feel his body rebelling against him. His head smashed into the piano and it just remained there as she held him, unsure of what ailed him. Yet, he just repeated those same words over and over again. "She lied to me." *** “Hey, can I get your autograph?!” Walking through the airport had grown to be more of a pain than anything for David. As weeks had passed since he signed contracts with fWo and PRIME, victories started to mount, and with those victories came fans. As David was gracious for anybody who supported him, he stopped and signed the autograph for the teenager who looked much like he did when he was that age. His mother stood behind him with a smile on her face. “Look at my son,” she said in a low tone. “Never thought I would see this day. Becoming a superstar right before my very eyes.” David rolled his eyes before he thanked the fan and proceeded down the long corridor towards their gate. Some people walked past him, oblivious to him, while others just gawked at the rookie. David, not one accustomed to the new found fame, pulled down his black ski cap to the point where it was nearly covering his eyes. As they reached the counter and checked in for their first class seats, Rebecca took a long look at his son and noticed something odd about him. “David, are you feeling okay?” she asked him before she ran her hand against his cheek. He shuddered away from her before he walked over to a seat in a nearby corner that would allow him some privacy. “David,” his mother pleaded with him as they both sat down and David looked over at her. “What, mom?” he asked her in an annoyed tone. Her eyebrows raised at hearing her son respond to her in that light. “David, your eyes are red. Bloodshot red. What the hell is wrong with you?” she asked of him, her anger slipped into her voice. David just shook his head as he laid his head back and closed his eyes. Having an in-depth conversation about his personal demons wasn’t something that David was particularly interested in, especially in the middle of a crowded airport. Mary-Lynn had tried to pry them out of David the night before and she had little luck in figuring out the puzzle that was David’s mind. As Rebecca decided that she’d rather just talk to her son instead of fight with him, Rebecca took a deep breath in. “Excited to see your sisters?” she asked him and at first he didn’t respond. His right hand twitched slightly as his breathing became heavy. Rebecca looked at her son with slight apprehension before he sat up in the chair and looked over at her. “Oh, yeah,” he began sarcastically. “Beyond thrilled. I’m sure it’s going to be a rosy family reunion.” David’s left hand this time squeezed as hard as it possibly could into a fist and it was obvious to Rebecca that something was deeply bothering her son. She tried to place her hand on top of his, but he shied away from that as he just wanted to be left to himself. The tension in the air was undeniable, but even David’s own mother was clueless as to what truly bothered him. Over the intercom, they called for any passengers that would be sitting in first class, and David took no time in getting out of his seat. He walked towards the attendant and handed the young woman his ticket. She took it and scanned it, and David bolted down the long hallway and into the plane where he gladly took his seat. His mother followed shortly after and sat on the inside closest to the window as David relaxed in his chair. “I’m certain your sisters can’t wait to see you, David. You have to think about it, they haven’t really had the chance to talk to you in years. Now, they’re getting to see you on television all of the time and they want to know who you are now,” Rebecca continued to nag him, but David remained silent. His right hand twitched once again and he looked around frantically before his eyes landed upon a young, pretty blonde flight attendant who could tell from David’s eyes that he needed her. As she eagerly walked over, his mother continued to try and talk to him, but he managed to zone her out. The woman kneeled in front of David with an eager look on her face to please. “Yes, sir?” “Alcohol, please,” he told her and she was more than happy to oblige his request. She stood up and began to turn away from him, but paused before she looked over her shoulder. “Anything in particular?” she asked politely. “Surprise me,” David responded quickly, not wanting another minute to pass by without something in his system. She smiled at him and walked away as David awaited his drink that would allow him a few hours of sweet serenity before he had to face the horrendous hell that awaited him. *** Returning home had proven to be easier the third time around. Hours had passed since the requisite greetings and pleasantries had taken place, and now the house was eerily silent. David walked through the kitchen and looked inside of the refrigerator before he grabbed a cold beer and put it on the counter. His head had ached for the past few hours and a nap didn’t help in the least bit. He looked around at the kitchen and felt the history, remembered the memories, of everything that had happened here. David opened his beer and turned around only to be met with Samantha who looked wide awake. David offered a sip of his beer, but Samantha just shook her head before she took a seat at the round kitchen table. David decided to take a seat with her and the two just looked at one another. “I can’t help but feel like you don’t want to be here, David,” Samantha began, and David sighed as he took a swig of his beer. With his mother asleep, the claws had come out, and it was time to have the conversation he dreaded from the moment his mother decided it was time to head back home. “You starting the conversation off just like that is the very reason why I don’t want to be here. I don’t want to be talked down to or chastised for the decisions I’ve made. Things happened and I’m ready to move on, plain and simple,” David replied, his tone a little more forceful then he had intended. Samantha took a long look at David before she took his beer and took a lengthy chug from it. As she pulled it away from her lips, she groaned for a minute before handing it back to her younger brother. “Oh, really? Ready to move on? Is that why you sat in the corner silent for hours on end? Or why you retreated to your bedroom and only came out when you felt like everyone would be asleep?” she asked him and David showed exasperation on his face. “Don’t even try to play that BS with me, David. I know you better than that.” “So, what, you were waiting for me?” David quizzed her and she shook her head. “Waiting for Samantha.” David finished off his beer before he tossed it into the nearby trash can. The beer hadn’t done the trick he had intended though as he rose out of his chair and walked over to the liquor cabinet his Dad had kept for years before his death. Sure enough, it was the way he had remembered it, and could tell from the dust that had settled on the various bottles that no one had bothered touching it in quite some time. He removed a bottle of whiskey and grabbed two glasses before he returned to the table. “Alright,” David said as he began to pour the liquor into the glasses. “If we’re going to do this, might as well be a little drunk so we’re not holding back in the least bit.” She frowned at the whiskey as Samantha was not a huge fan of it. What disappointed her the most is that David knew that and still brought down the bottle. She took a sip of it as David downed an entire glass in mere seconds. “Fine, David. Let’s do this. When are you going to stop running away from the very people that love you? Maybe Dad was wrong in the way that he talked to you, but he did have a point. Three years without hearing from you? Then he passes away and you don’t even show up to his funeral? What kind of shit is that?” she asked him, her voice nearly raised to a yell. David held up his hand as he poured another glass of whiskey for himself. “Okay, rule #2, Sam, no yelling or screaming. You want to belittle me? Fine, but I’m not going to let you dress me down like the little boy caught with his hand in the cookie jar before dinner,” he told her frankly. She nodded her head in agreement as David took another drink of the Jack. “I left, Sam, because as much I loved you guys, as much as I wanted to see Sarah and MacKenzie grow up, I didn’t want to subject you to the fights any longer.” David grew quiet as his eyes flittered down towards his glass. Samantha took another sip from her glass. “What made you hate Dad so much? One day, you loved the guy, and the next minute you’re just up in arms with him,” she asked him softly. “I never hated Dad.” She scoffed at the suggestion. “Bullshit, David, and you know that. Rule #3, you’re going to be honest. Something happened and I’ll be damned if you’re going to sit here and lie to me in the face. You saw or know something, something that changed that ideal in your head that you had in Dad, and it hurt you,” she informed him and David remained silent as he finished off his second glass of whiskey. “What do you know, Sam?” David asked her sadly. “Nothing.” David looked up at his sister and his eyes narrowed. “Bullshit, Sam. Rule #3.” “David--,” she began, but he immediately cut her off as his eyes moved towards the living room. As his thoughts formed in his head he was sure he heard something outside of the house. “Sam, where the hell is Sarah?” he asked her and he could feel the tension rising in his chest. Samantha looked at David with tepidness. “What do you mean?” she asked him and David shook his head. “You said you were waiting up for Sarah. It’s nearly 2AM in the morning. Where is she?” he asked her once again, but before she had the chance to open her mouth, the front door opened. David immediately heard footsteps echo throughout the household and fear slowly took over him. He heard the sound of a bag moving through the house and then the door closed again. David’s hand wrapped around his empty glass and waited for what felt like an eternity. Entered the room was a young woman with a sharpness to her. Dressed in all business clothes, black skirt, white blouse, and a black jacket, the woman’s look cut right through David as he looked at her. “Hello, David.” David swallowed hard as he looked up at the eloquent woman standing above him. “Hey Andrea.” His tone was short and filled with venom. She took her jacket off and slung it over the back of a vacant chair as Sarah entered the room and stood in the threshold with careful eyes. “So, I guess it’s time we all had a talk,” she said, her voice filled with vibrato and confidence. David’s eyes looked at the bottle of Whiskey before him and knew immediately that it wouldn’t be enough to help him escape from the violent reality that awaited him. “Fuck.”
