Redneck Zombie Massacre!

29 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/29/06 “Okay, now what did you want me to do?” Shawn asks, setting the thick, bound stack of papers down on the table in front of him. He honestly hadn't expected to get an invitation to Logan's Roadhouse on New Circle Road from someone he'd never heard of. How could you, really? But the purpose is what really baffled him. “We want you to be in our movie,” came the response from the pimply-faced, college-age looking student across from him. There were two, one ridden with acne that would make the boy's mother flinch, the other with a hunch posture that made his teeth seem to hang over the front of his jaw. “And why is it you want me?” Shawn said, slowly reaching for his glass of unsweetened iced tea. He took a careful sip and shuddered. They'd brought him sweet tea. Again. He sat it at the edge of the table and looked back at the two of them. “I'm not an actor, never have been.” “You're a local celebrity, and we're hoping that this movie would be a local cult hit,” said Bucktooth, as Shawn had dubbed him in his head. “Redneck Zombie Massacre should do really well in Kentucky and we want a local celebrity to play the hero.” Shawn tried very hard for his teeth not to grind. Oh, he'd read the script, and there was no way it'd market well anywhere, especially in Kentucky. “Where are you two from, by the way?” “I'm from Pennsylvania,” said Acne, “he's from Illinois,” he said, pointing a thumb at Bucktooth. “And you think people in Kentucky are going to respond well to being portrayed like...” he waved a hand at the script, trying to find words. “Like the most undeveloped, backwater, idiotic...” he trailed off, grasping for more adjectives. None came. “Isn't it true? I mean, you know, somewhere in the state?” Now Shawn's teeth DID grind. “Stereotyping lives on,” Shawn said, waving down the waitress. “What?” said one. He wasn't paying attention to which one said it, and their voices were so nasal he couldn't tell them apart. “Did you just call us bigoted?” “Yes, ma'am,” Shawn said politely, gesturing towards his glass, “this is sweet again. I asked for unsweetened.” “Oh, I'm sorry!” she said. “You want me to bring you another one?” Shawn bit his lip. Time to play it safe. “Tell you what, just replace it with an ice water and a cup of coffee, please.” “Cream and sugar?” “No,” Shawn said pointedly. “Just straight out of the pot.” He turned back to his two... antagonists seemed to fit well. “And yes, I damn sure am calling you bigoted. You got a problem with that?” Both swallowed, their over-sized Adam's apples bouncing visibly. “No, no, Mr. Stewart.” “Good. Now, walk me through what it is you want me to do.” “Okay,” Bucktooth said, enthusiastic to recite what he thought would apparently be a takeoff hit. Shawn had supposed it was somewhat flattering that they wanted him to be the Bruce Campbell to their Evil Dead, but it was insulting that they wanted him to be in this script. “First, there's a mental hospital. We're going to call it the Centretown Home for the Viciously Abused.” Shawn blinked. Was that supposed to be funny? “Then, we zoom in with some scream effects until we come to one door. There's a girl inside in a straight jacket not moving and two doctors walk up.” “We'll be the doctors,” Acne responded. “I'll ask what her problem is, he'll say he doesn't know, I'll offer to go play a round of golf and he wants to go to my club because his doesn't allow Jews.” They both started rather irritating guffaws. Shawn stared at them, his mouth slightly open, his eyes wide. They apparently thought it was funny. “So, anyway, there's this barrel of toxic nuclear chemical warfare waste,” Bucktooth went on. “There's a black guy in a Jeep that's driving the barrel to the base. And he wrecks because he's smoking pot and this redneck runs him off from the barrel. He takes it to make a whiskey still out of it.” “Whoa,” Shawn said, slapping a hand on the table. “You really don't know what you're doing.” “Sure we do! We're making a zombie cult classic!” protested Acne. “No, you don't make whiskey in a still,” Shawn countered. “That's moonshine.” There was an awkward pause. It stretched on. It wasn't terribly awkward for Shawn, but for the two college kids, it was more awkward than... well, seeing tits in real life. “Sir, here's your coffee,” the waitress said, walking up. She sat a white mug of black coffee down in front of him and a glass of ice water next to it. “Your food should be out in just a few minutes.” “Thank you very much,” Shawn said, still staring at the two. “You do too make whiskey in a still,” protested Acne. “No, whiskey is in barrels, moonshine is in stills.” “Okay, well, moonshine, then,” Bucktooth said. “So, anyway, he makes it and it comes out green, so he goes to start selling it. We've got some great jokes lined up. One woman has her baby standing in a washing machine and puts the stuff in its bottle.” “Okay, see, that's exactly what I was talking about. People aren't going to think that's funny, they're going to find it insulting!” Shawn said to them. “Oh, come on! It's funny!” insisted Acne. The woman behind them in the booth turned around. “Mr. Stewart there is right. That's horrifying and you ought to be ashamed of youselves.” The two college kids traded looks, confused. “What is it you want me to do in this?” Shawn said, trying to push the conversation along. “Oh, well, you're the hero. You show up in a ninja outfit. You're a ninja zombie hunter, and you got this feeling about zombies showing up.” Shawn was immediately shocked again. “So, you show up with swords and stuff and start killing zombies until they shoot you with their shotguns and the movie ends.” “That's it?” “That's it.” Shawn was utterly baffled. “You want me to show up in an utterly ridiculous role that, as you said, would actually not show me as the local celebrity because you want me to die without apparently accomplishing anything?” “Yeah, it's a commentary on life,” said Bucktooth. “Life is pointless, so why fight it? Roll over and die.” “Oh my good GOD!” Shawn belted out. The entire restaurant went quiet. “You HAVE to be kidding me!” “You don't like it?” “I think I might sue you for defamation of character if you even get my name near this damn thing,” Shawn retorted. “If you want someone for this, go find the Illustrious Face Eater. Or maybe Danny Ferguson. God knows he doesn't get work anymore.” “He's in prison!” Acne whined. “I don't care,” Shawn said, getting up from the booth. “Okay, guys!” comes a new voice. “Hey, Shawn!” comes the same voice. Yup, you guessed it, Ashton Kutcher steps around from another room. “Hey, guess what?” Shawn walks right over to Kutcher, grabs a drink off the table and throws it in Kutcher's face. The Ms. Demi Moor looks at him, somewhat baffled. “Come on, man, it's a joke.” “I got the last laugh,” Shawn said with a grin.
