The Happiest Place on Earth

21 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 21, 2009 "I can't believe we came here instead of going training," Jack grumbled as they walked along. "We were supposed to be making a huge impact in the company, and we're supposed to be building up our mysterious qualities, and instead, we're walking around looking at Walt's Magical World." "Hey, dude, stop harshing the mellow," Luis said. "The reason we're here is because it's totally chill." The two were walking along Main Streeet, dressed in some regular street clothes, and the dialogue seemed a bit off for anybody that didn't know the two men. Luis was shorter, and definitely Hispanic, while Jack was taller, and although definitely gaelic, his skin seemed tanned by something other than the sun. Jack seemed to have a permanent scowl, while Luis was standing, enjoying himself. "As long as we don't go on 'It's a Small World'," Conway said. "I really don't want to get stuck sitting in a boat that rides on three feet of water - especially not by that part where every single language in the world is singing the song at once." Gallegos chuckled and said, "That's happened to you before, has it?" "In Orlando, not here," Jack replied. "It was a waste of good vacation time." "Well, at least it was paid vacation," Luis replied. "You gotta' admit, this whole place is designed for the middle and lower classes to enjoy themselves." Conway laughed, then said, "Yeah, sure, if you call spending eight bucks on a hot dog and a soda enjoyable, I guess you've got a point." "The only thing is, the hall of presidents isn't up to date anymore," Luis said. "How do you know?" Conway replied. "It ain't like they haven't known that Obama wasn't going in to office yesterday. I bet they managed to change it during the inauguration." The two kept walking, and Luis finally spoke again. "Hey, you wanna' check out California Adventure first? I hear they have some wicked rides over there." "If I wanted 'wicked rides', I would have gone to Magic Mountain or something," Jack replied. "Besides, we should be training. There's a couple of rich punks that..." "Not rich," Luis corrected. "Punks led by a rich woman of questionable morals." Conway almost cracked a smile, but stopped himself and said, "You were being pretty literal about that, weren't you? I mean, you weren't..." "Nope, no euphemism. She thinks that she can buy the world, and that includes buying a tag team that's worth a damn," Luis said. "It's the worst kind of inequality. It doesn't matter what skin color you are - it just matters whether you're rich or poor, and if you're the latter, she figures she can buy anybody at all." "Hey, if she pays well enough, I imagine they'll work as hard as possible for her," Conway said. "I'll admit that she could be a bit more respectful of the guys working for her, but they're just doing their job." "True enough," Luis said as they turned in to one of the subdivisions of the theme park. They saw the rides, and the people enjoying them. "Ain't it grand," Conway said. "All of the huddled masses, and they're waiting to ride in a fake flying elephant." "C'mon," Luis said. "Let's take that Winnie the Pooh ride. It looks like fun." The two men walked off, enjoying one last day of freedom before they began their new existence as traveling pro wrestlers.
