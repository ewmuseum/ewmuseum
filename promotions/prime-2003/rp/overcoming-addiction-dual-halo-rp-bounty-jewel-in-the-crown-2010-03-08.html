Overcoming Addiction [Dual Halo RP - Bounty : Jewel in The Crown 2010]

8 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/8/2010 “Hello… my name is Chris, and I am an addict” I swallowed hard the first time I said that. Here I was, in a room full of strangers, telling them I had a problem. I mean, come on…. I’m Boda. Former NBA and NCAA star. Wrestling Legend. Son. Father. Husband. As I looked up at the room full of fellow addicts I heard a collective…. “Hi Chris, welcome.” That was April 5th, 2008. I would proceed to hear this many time over the next 21 months of my life. I’d attend weekly AA meetings from that point out. But lets flash back to the circumstances that led me to these rooms. February 2005 After a successful run in many feds, I had found myself in PRIME. I was determined to be the dominate force I was in other companies. I was always the ’go to guy’ for every company prior to PRIME. And I started out with a bang. I had won the chance to face Killean and Black Angel, the Universal Champion, for the top prize in PRIME. I gave the fans what they wanted, hell, I gave myself what I wanted. A 5 star match. That was the only way I knew how to go. I didn’t believe in 4 stars. I held my head up high that night. I bested the champion. I went toe to toe with Killean, a man, whom not only became to hold the same title I won that night, but was one of the best feud partners I’d ever had. That night, was probably the best night of my career. It all went downhill after that. I started to notice minor health issues. Trouble sleeping, sweating, night tremors. It was affecting my in-ring performance too. A month later, I lost that title. I slipped, and let Karina take the gold from me. I thought it was a fluke, but low and behold, it was my health. But, being a big, stubborn Alpha Male, I didn’t think anything about it. After all, I assumed I was at the top of my game. I thought I was in my PRIME. I couldn’t have been more wrong. From that lost to Karina, I sauntered in a mire of losses, with an occasional win thrown in. I was also getting hurt more. Finally, I broke down and saw a doctor. March 2006 to June 2006 “Mr Sloboda?” said the voice on the other end of the phone. “Yes?” “Mr Sloboda, this is Dr Brooks. Listen….the biopsy came back. It’s not good news. You’ve got thyroid cancer, that’s spread to your lymph noids in your neck. We need to operate. They’ll be a long recovery, chemo therapy, sickness, medicine for the rest of your life. Chris? Are you there? By the time he said ‘cancer‘, I was already spinning out of control in my mind. I barely heard the rest of the conversation. Cancer…. Up until this point in my life, the scariest words I had heard were ‘Honey, I’m pregnant’. I don’t care how tough you are, when someone says you’ve got cancer, you’re going to cry. And I did. I cried just like I did when my kids were born. Yes, for a big bad man, I am a sensitive son of a bitch. But, dammit, I was just told I need to go through a life changing experience. “So, Mr Sloboda. I’ve booked you tomorrow for surgery.” Dr Brooks’ voice was now piercingly numb in my left ear. “What… what… about my next match?” I stumbled to find the words. Here I was, being told I needed surgery to remove ‘DEATH’ and I was worried about my career. “I’m sorry. You’ll need to take time off. You’re not going to be able to wrestle for at least a year.” A year. That’s okay. I can do a year. At least I thought a year would be easy enough. “Mrs. Sloboda, the surgery was a success. We removed the tumor on his thyroid. We also had to remove the complete thyroid, and a big chunk of muscle and fat around his neck.” Dr. Brooks explains the outcome to my wife, as I slowly drift to consciousness. “Now what?” Stacey asks “Well, in a week, he takes radiation treatment. He’ll need to be isolated, no family around” “Okay, we’ll stay with my parents. I only want to see him get better” That was the beginning of the end of my marriage. Sure, I loved her. But come on…. Traveling the roads 300 days a year puts a toll on anyone’s marriage. I was in California, she and the kids are home in Florida. I’m horny, and cyber sex wasn’t an option. So why not get busy with one of the ring rats that hung around PRIME shows. You get the picture, I didn’t think she knew about it. But I guess I was wrong. A week after taking radiation, the doctor cleared me to let the family come home. I was tired of staying in the big house alone. I was used to spending nights alone on the road, in hotel rooms, but when I was home, I wanted my family around me. And here I was, home for a week, and alone. It didn’t sit right with me. “Stacey” I spoke into the phone. “Hey, the doctor said you and the kids can come back” There was an uncomfortable silence on the other end. “Did you hear me? I’m cleared, ready for you and the kids to return.” Stacey finally responds. “Yeah, I heard you. I was thinking we might stay here a little longer.” I didn’t need it spelled out for me. I could hear the unhappiness in her voice. After 11 years together, I could sense things about her. And now, I could sense what was going to happen next. “Chris…. I need some time. I love you, but I can’t go on right now. I know it’s bad timing, but you’ve taken us for granted too long. We’ll be at my parent’s. I’ll call you later, when I come get our stuff.” ………….. Wow. In less than a month, I’ve lost my career, parts of my body and now my family. I simply hung up the phone. I was speechless. I’d been to blind with my lifestyle to see my family being pushed away from me. Now, I’ve been in physical pain for as long as I can remember. I’ve broken bones, torn ligaments, separated shoulders. But , the pain I was feeling…. I’d never felt before. I was alone. And my heart was broken. I needed something to dull the pain…the pains. I could feel the spiraling trails of emotions twirling around inside of me. I needed help to take it all away from me. “Hey John… it’s Chris…..listen…. I need a little something…. To dull some pain.” Being a big time athlete, I had made many connections over the years. I didn’t have to ever really ‘want’ for anything. I could secure what ever I wanted, when ever I wanted. And shortly after Stacey took the kids and herself away, I called in the favor. “What you looking for Chris?” John replied on the other end. “What ever man, bring a mix of everything” And everything he did bring. There were pills, of every color. There was marijuana, there was cocaine. All those were all right, but there was two drugs John brought, that at that time, I did not realize would be the beginning of my fall from grace. For some reason, taking a hit of crystal meth or crack was the only was I was able to forget about my life. “Thanks man….come back tomorrow. I’ve got chemo in the morning, but then I’ll be looking to ‘expand’ on today’s transaction.” I walked John out the front door of my home in Orlando. The next 24 hours became a tizzy of drug use and alcohol induced comas. I literally fearing and loathing, and I was no where near Las Vegas. I spend that night alone, dulling the pain with hit after mouth watering hit. When the rush wore off, the pain of my life returned. So I would proceed to inhale enough of the toxic vapors to mask my feelings. The morning after John first provided me with what I needed, I went to the hospital for a round of chemo. I can remember the drive home, I made my agent , Stan, who decided to drive me, pull over, so I could vomit. Now, it was a normal thing to be sick after chemo. But, remember, I was just on a drug bender, so that didn’t help any at all. As I spewed alongside I-4, I couldn’t help but somehow text message John with my ‘take-out’ order. Climbing back into my car, I motion for Stan to continue on. “Chris… you going to be okay? You need me to stay with you? I’ve been meaning to talk to you, you seem really depressed lately. I’m worried about you.” I was partially listening to Stan as he expressed his concerns to me. All I could think about was getting high. Chemo always drained me, always pained me. The whole 2 hour treatment session always left me time to reflect on my life. As we meandered down I-4 and then down the long road to my residence, I was lost in thoughts of better times. Times when I was still in the league. My debut in PCW and my success in that and many other federations. Thoughts of my two daughters, their births, the birthday’s I made….. Then the birthdays I missed. And then the anniversaries I was not home for. A myriad of good and bad events spewed like a dull painful enema through my brain. Finally, I was at home. Stan helped me to the door. “Are you going to be okay?” Stan worried about me. Sometimes I wonder if it was because of the money I made him. “Go home Stan….I’m fine. I’ll head to bed while this round wears off. Thanks pal.” I sent Stan on his way. I knew John would be here momentarily. I headed to the bedroom. It was dark, curtains been drawn shut for months now. I sat on the edge of the bed, and opened the nightstand. Looking into the drawer, I reached down and pulled out my recently purchased .357 Magnum. I had been doing a lot of research as of late. I visited every website I could find about suicide. I was depressed. Hell, my whole world came crashing down in a matter of months. I wanted to turn back the clock, but I couldn’t. I turned the gun barrel towards my face. I stared down the deep abyss of the steel killer in front of my eyes. “JUST DO IT…. YOU FUCKING PUSSY. SHE’S NOT COMING BACK. YOU’VE BEEN ON THE ROAD, YOU’VE CHEATED ON HER. AND SHE KNOWS IT. YOU BARELY KNOW YOUR OWN KIDS. JUST PUT THIS GUN TO YOUR HEAD, AND PULL THE FUCKING TRIGGER. IT’S OVER. DO IT.” As I sat there, getting myself psyched up to end my life I decided I needed one more hit. John was on his way, but I had a stash in the drawer next to where the gun was. I sat the gun down on the bed, picked up the glass pipe in the drawer. Loaded the end, with some sweet, white nectar of the Crack Cocaine Gods and sparked my BIC lighter. I drew in the vapors in the pipe, my ears ringing, as they always did. All senses went into overload at that moment. Thoughts of suicide dissipated. The memories blanked from my mind. I took another hit, then followed it by some tequila that was sitting on the dresser. The effects started to wear off, and the suicidal thoughts crawled into the numbness of my cerebral cortex. “FUCK THIS. I DON’T WANT TO LIVE ANYMORE. I CAN’T WRESTLE. I’M ALL ALONE HERE. FUCK THIS TO HELL.” As the depression darkened around me, I wanted one of two things. I wanted to get higher, or I wanted to get dead. I thought, in my twisted mind, that the only thing I could do to stop was to just end it all. I swigged from the bottle once more, than took one more blast from the glass pipe. As the ringing returned to my head, I stood from the bed. I somehow managed to walk to the curtains that were drawn shut. Yanking them open, I let the sunlight in. And then nothing. Everything went blank. It got really dark, really fast. I don’t even remember if my heart was beating, or if indeed I was not breathing. All I knew, was one minute I was looking out into the bright sunny Florida sky, then nothing. ………………………. ………………………. ………………………. “Chris….Chris…. Shit…. He’s dead……CHRIS?!?!?!” ……………………… ……………………… ……………………… All was black. Pitch black. I could hear someone yelling at me. But he sounded so far away. He sounded shallow and empty. I knew it was too late. And I was glad. “Christ…. What the hell did you do? You big fucking asshole.” As the voice was fading out, my life flashed before me. In a matter of seconds, my 33 years of life on this planet flashed by. It was like watching your life on fast forward. I could feel myself floating, moving. I was still waiting for the light at the end of the tunnel. But hell, I knew I wasn’t going up. I wasn’t a GOD fearing man, and with all my infidelities and bad behavior, I knew I was heading downstairs to party with Satan. And you know what….I was ready. I was so fucking depressed, so fucking angry, I wanted to die. After all, who the hell in their right mind would smoke crack cocaine and drink tequila hours after taking a round of chemotherapy. “Christ Chris….. Come back…. You can’t die” I could barely hear the voice crying out to me. This was it. Farewell world. Suddenly a rush of cold came over my body. As I was fading into death, something snatched me up and whipped me back to life. I was soaking wet and cold. My eyes opened and I staring at water crashing down on me. “Fuck! I’m alive?” I shout out. “Thank god man…..” the voice I had been hearing became clear. It was John. “I need to get you to a hospital.” I started to get up. Still a little fuzzy. “Don’t bother. If overdosing didn’t kill me…. I don’t need some ER doctor to do it.” Standing up fully, I see what the cause of the coldness was. Somehow, John managed to get me into the bathtub, turn on the shower and dump a few buckets of ice on me. I told John to leave. I proceeded to take off the wet clothes and climbed into bed. As I dosed off, I knew John wasn’t going to leave me alone, not after the stunt I pulled. I woke up the next morning, and continued on the path of self destruction for the next 2 years. May 2007 to April 2008 Stacey and the kids came back, only briefly. I had become a functioning addict and alcoholic. I made promises that I couldn’t keep. I wanted her and the girls back, so badly. But, the drugs…. Ahh, the sweet allure of the drugs kept pushing them away. Don’t get me wrong. There were good times. I finally convinced the family that I was a new man. They moved back home in November 2007. Of course, it was partially due to the fact that Stacey and I, in a moment of trying to work things out, got pregnant. We were expecting our third child in March 2008. I was actually able to keep myself clean too. It was hard, but I was doing it. I was actually done with my chemo treatments. I was spending time with the kids, as I was trying to build my strength back. When I left the business in 2006, I was 350 pounds. After all the chemo, the surgeries …oh, and the drug abuse, I was down to a sickly looking 230 pounds. I truly did look like a crack head… albeit a 6’11”one. Life was actually turning around for me. Until I got the phone call I wasn’t expecting in January of 2008. “Chris….I’m in labor….. Come to the hospital.” Stacey didn’t even give me time to respond to her. Stacey had gone shopping, baby stuff of course. She left me home with the girls. They were busy with their Nintendo DS playing and their Facebooking. I peeked in on them. “Kenz… Car….we got to go. Your mom is going to have the baby” Corbin Christopher was born January 15th, 2008. 6 weeks premature. He was 5lbs, 20 inches long. The biggest premie on record in the hospital. But, his lungs were not fully developed, so he was rushed to a neo-natal intensive care unit. He spent the first month of his life there. I blamed myself for the fact he was born sick. I couldn’t help but think that maybe the cancer made this happen. Or worse, the drug abuse. What if Corbin doesn’t make it? I stated thinking the worse. This was the nature of the beast called addiction. I loaded the girls up and we left for the hospital. We spent hours there. Corbin was in an incubator, on breathing machines. My heart was broken. I turned on the self pity devices again. All I wanted was to take away the emotional pain that was kicking up inside me. So, once my mother and father in law showed up, I made an excuse to leave. To be honest with you…. The next three months are still a blur. I just remember making one phone call, to John and it all went downhill from there. I thought I could handle one more hit, but that one hit turned into a hurricane of binging and lying and stealing. I ignored my family, my friends, stole from people. I wanted to stop, I just couldn’t. I was hooked… again. And this time, I wanted to die more than ever before. I had a perfect family and I couldn’t enjoy it. I had burned every last bridge I had ever crossed. In my mind, I was either going to go to jail or die, just to stop using the drugs. April 5th, 2008 Neither jail nor death made me stop after all. Stacey did. She sat down at the foot of the bed on that cool Saturday morning. I asked where the kids were. She told me that her parents took them for the day. She told me she loved me… but can’t go on with the way I am headed. She told me she didn’t want to bury the father of her children. She cried, as she told me she wanted the old Chris back. Deep down inside, I wanted the old Chris back too. I was given and ultimatum…. Rehab, or our marriage, our family, was over for good. April 5th, 2008 was the last time I ever used any non-prescribed drugs. Committing to rehab was easy. Actually doing it, that was the hard part. Let me tell you something….. I’d of rather gone an hour long match with Ivan Stanislav, Meanstreak and Angelo DeVille, then to experience, again, what the next few months of my life had in store for me. I checked into Spirit of Life Men’s Recovery Center on April 7th, 2008. It wasn’t a huge fancy club med style 5 star rehab most famous people attend. Nope, this was a run down, old motel. One that I used to go to, with a crack whore, and fuck and get high in. A pastor from New York bought it, kicked out the users and hookers, then opened a men’s ministry. I was put in a room with 2 other addicts. Both redneck hillbillies. There were 2 set of bunk beds in each room, a television, a fridge, and a bathroom. The rules were simple. No family interaction for the first 30 days. They state it’s because in the first 30 days, you have a higher chance of relapsing, and with family visiting, it makes you want to even more. For the first 60 days, I took a beginner class, where I learned about addiction and why we are addicts. We also took the 12 steps of AA. Now, granted, I was there for drug addiction, but the programmed was based on the Big Book and the principles set forth by Bill W. Now the 12 Steps were written as a guideline that would help me, as well as the thousands and thousands that they have helped before me, find a way , a path, to getting over my addiction. And they did. Over the next 3 months, I learned the 12 steps of AA. I knew them front wards and backwards. But, it wasn’t a rosy , easy 3 months. I went through a lot of withdrawal symptoms. I was shaking constantly. I couldn’t eat for a week. And I couldn’t stop shitting for two. I was coughing blood, then black shit, that I could only imagine was the inside of my lungs, coming apart. I was having ‘dope’ dreams almost every single night. And, it didn’t matter that I was a Hall of Fame wrestler, multi-time champion from many organizations. In Spirit of Life, I simply an addict. Of course, that was how the staff members treated you. The other addicts, they knew who I was. And on a few occasions, I’d be stepped too. A lot of the residents were either straight out of prison or had spent a considerable amount of their life in and out of prison. And to them, the recovery center was merely an extension to their prison life. And since I was always the biggest dog in the yard, I was tried on many the occasion. I never backed down before in my life….but while I was trying to get better, trying to beat the monkey off my back, I avoided all confrontations. While going through the first three months, doing the 12 steps, I was building relationships. First one with myself. Second one with God, and third one with my family again. It was baby steps but it was steps in the right direction. While in rehab, I seen a lot of men come in, wanting the same thing I wanted, but not make it. They let the addiction get the better of them. Reverend Terry use to tell us that for every ten men in the program, 1 makes it. And as determined as I was to return to the business I loved, I was determined to be the 1 in 10 that makes it through the program. And so, over the next 7 months, I progressed. I was working out, getting my body back in some resemblance of what it once looked like. The dope dreams were becoming less and less. The family visits were becoming more and more. I was learning more and more about myself and why I was addicted to drugs. As I became less addicted, I became more dedicated to getting back into the ring. As I learned more about myself, and what my purpose in life was, I became more ambitious in my quest to become a world class athlete again. February 2009 “The next graduate has come along farther than any other man that has taken this program. When Christopher first came to Spirit of Life, he was a complete mess. He was suicidal, depressed and in desperate need to rid himself of addiction.” Pastor Gary, dressed in his Sunday best, addressed the room full of church goers, recovery center supporters, young guys in the program, and family members of those graduating on this date. “Now, when I met him, I knew he was going to be my biggest challenge to date. Granted, he’s almost 7 foot tall, and was once on top of the world in his business. But, as we all know, addiction will take even the strongest man and break him. And Chris, he was broken. But, it is my pleasure to present this ‘certificate of completion’ to Christopher Sloboda.” I stood up from my seat and blushed as the church was filled with cheering and whooping. I sauntered up to the podium and took the Pastor’s hand. We shook hands, then hugged, as the flashing of cameras lit up the moment. I had a tear or two in my eyes, I won’t lie. My two daughters were cheering me on. My son, who was only 15 months old at the time, was babbling in the front row seats as Stacey smiled, a tear in her eyes too. “Thank you Pastor. 10 months ago, I sat on the edge of my bed and looked in the mirror. I couldn’t stand the person I saw looking at me. I was once a world class athlete, at the top of my game. In a matter of a few years, I self destructed. I hated myself, I wanted to die, and I was selfish to my family. I had lost all hope for living. My career as a wrestler was over, I had multiple surgeries for the cancer, I had become depressed. 10 months ago, I was a hopeless dope feign. And now…. Thanks to this program, I’ve found a new lien on life. I’m now able to look in that mirror and smile. And dammit, if the man looking at me doesn’t smile back. And, because of this program, I’ve gotten the ambition, the drive, to get back in the ring. I can now get back into my playground and once again become the king of the mountain. I’m now a dopeless hope feign.” I took a moment to clear my throat. "I never thought I'd ever become an addict. No one wakes up and think, today, I want to destroy those that love me, those that care the most about me. It was something that just happened. I was once on top of the world, the one others looked up too. But I digressed and became a monster. I let the drugs change me. I let myself get pinned by this monkey. But today, I stand here telling you that I once beat cancer, and I'll be dammed if I was going to let addiction beat me." The church erupted in cheers for me. My wife and kids were in the front, not a dry eye to be had. The pastor handed me my certificate. There were camera flashes everywhere. I somehow, in the middle of this all, flashbacked to my earlier PRIME days. I was somehow being gravitated towards a possible return. As the flashback ended, I sat with my family. For the first time in years, I was happy. And for the first time in years, I knew I was ready to return to the ring. All it took was one phone call to make that happen….. January 1st 2010 “Hello Jason… it’s Chris…. What do you think about shaking up PRIME a little?” And so it was set in stone. January 8th 2010 “Hello, my name is Chris and I’m an addict” 2 years ago, it was hard for me to admit this. Today, it’s never been easier. “Hello Chris.” the other members of the group responded. “I wanted to share with you all the joy in my life. 2 years ago, when I finally realized I needed help, I was so depressed, so suicidal that I didn’t want to be in this room. But, I’ve gotten a new lease on life, thanks to this program. Thanks to the 12 steps, the principles of AA and learning that I don’t need addiction, I have learned to live life on life’s terms. Thanks to everyone’s support in these rooms, I’ve become reunited with my family. And even though that’s what gives me the most joy, it’s what’s going take place in two weeks that fills my heart with jubilation. In two weeks, I am returning to wrestling. I am returning to PRIME and because I don’t need drugs and alcohol in my life, I will once again climb that mountain and become the king.”
