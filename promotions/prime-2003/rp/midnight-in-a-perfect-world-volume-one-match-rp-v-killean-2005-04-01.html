Midnight In A Perfect World : Volume One (Match RP - v Killean)

1 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/04/05 Midnight In A Perfect World : Volume One.Written: 1st April, 2005. 'Where can I run to?Where can I hide?Who can I turn to, now I'm in a virgin state of mind?' - K's Choice. 0 - Thru' The Glass. '...this is a piece of Karina you wouldn't dare deny,' were the words that flicked off Angelo Deville's forked tongue. Looking down at the business card which was presented to Karina in honour of her Universal Title win, the K-Wolf's precise eye tried to find something distinguishable... words concealed in the card, only visible in the right light. But after ten minutes of study, it was still just the same card, on the same, unremarkable beige, with the same, unremarkable golden text. Not even a hint of lavish embossing. 'Why do I feel tempted to ring the number,' she bemoaned, denying all the instincts that told her anything gifted from Angelo Deville was far more likely to be a curse than a blessing. Despite her acute mistrust, Kari had kept the card close to her for the days following ReVolution, and each peek seemed to tempt her into dialling its inviting number, unlocking its mysteries by unravelling just what The Deville was so keen to reward her with. 'Hmph,' she exhaled, twirling the card between her fingers, almost wafting up the stench of Angelo himself. He knew her better than she was comfortable with. He'd known that flowers would most likely be left to wither out of water, or that even the most delicate and caringly crafted of chocolates wouldn't tantalise her palette anymore than a cheap slab of from the local store. The K-Wolf wasn't about gestures, she was about actions... and something told her the card was the looking glass, tempting her into the world beyond it. Somehow, the touch of the Crimson Angel had made it more than just pulped wood and ink. 'What's to lose,' she submitted, swiping her cell phone from the arm of the chair her curled-up body was slowly being absorbed in to, showing that even PRIME's most daring daredevil needed to crash in a rather more comfortable sense. Her life was lived walking a tight-rope, flicking danger's ear when he wasn't looking to rile him up, and goading the Grim Reaper to take his best swipe. To her, alive was a feeling she couldn't recognise unless her body was one slip away from being parted from her soul, left ravaged by rock, crumpled at ringside entwined in the guard rail, or pummelled by mounds of compacted snow. She wasn't going to let an indescript business card intimidate her, despite the shadowed intentions of the man who presented it. Tucking her knees up inside the striped sweater that engulfed her slender form, Karina punched in the number and held the phone to her ear, nestling it into the cascade of lilac that bordered her plain face. As soon as it began to ring, the tone shifted repeatedly, as if the call was being relayed through a series of diverters, losing any eavesdroppers who clung desperately to its tail. Eyebrows were sucked down towards the high bridge of Karina's nose in suspicion, as she waited for an answer that would satisfy her amplified curiosity. Under the frosty, unimpressed exterior, there was a part of the K-Wolf, tucked deep away, that felt like a child on Christmas Eve. Her attraction to the unknown helped propagate the repressed tingle, letting it crawl achingly slow up her curled, foetal spine. If Deville was going to spite her with a prank, it would have been before his cold blue eyes, a performance for his own sinister satisfaction. 'Please state your full name,' a crackly voice then suddenly spoke, sounding as if the tone and character had been lost weaving through the call's diversions. 'I think I should get to know who this is first...' Kari hesitantly replied, her ear being opened further by suspicion to try and hear the muffled background noises. 'Please state your full name,' it repeated back at her, causing the K-Wolf to crack a slight smile across her thin, pale pink lips... an element of tease drawing her in. 'Karina Marie Wolfenden,' she answered, seeing where it would lead. 'Thank you,' the voice then politely replied, before a final crackle killed the line. Curled up in the arm chair, Karina seemed caught unawares, rarely one to be dumped out of a conversation and left with a look of perplexion. Enticed, she punched the number back in, only for the line to go dead. Karina trusted her fingers and thumb enough to know that the fault was not hers, and instead that one of the diversions had turned into a blockade. 'What are you up to...' Kari pondered, her words hushed and speculative. As a mixture of prophetic visions began contesting for attention in her effervescent imagination, Karina was jolted to attention as her apartment was buzzed. She exhaled in laboured fashion, as if the exertion of standing was too much for her weary body, muscled having tightly contracted back following an hour of high-impact aerobics and the shower that followed. Strides shortened as she approached the intercom, looking back to where her phone sat on the chair's arm. 'Wait...' she let creep out, turning her attention back to the intercom. Wondering if the door slammed shut on the phone was to allow the more literal one before her to open, Kari plunged the intercom button in, letting loose the familiar, static sizzle. 'Coming out to play?' I - My Life, A Dream. 'Urrrgh...' It was the oblivious groan of a girl dragged prematurely from her slumber. Every week, thousands of adoring men tuned in to see Karina Wolfenden, despite there never being even the slimmest chance of any risqué, exposed flesh. Admittedly plain features were complimented by vivid emerald eyes, while a mane of glossy, lilac hair sucked in attention like a vacuum. The looks of a lightly freckled girl next door, with an attitude that could cut your tongue clean off if you uttered the wrong word. 'Gnuh...' Needless to say, they'd never seen her in the morning. Eyes could barely stay open, a dry ache tugging them back down, wishing eyelashes would tie into knots. The hair that usually seemed to cascade effortlessly down past her prominent cheekbones was dry, like tinted straw, and not far from resembling a haystack as no two stands seemed to be going in the same direction. Lips hung open, as if in shock at the sudden exposure to the waking world. 'Erggggg...' was then expelled as she yanked the covers sharply over her face, hoping that the world wouldn't have noticed her waking. But alas, the moment her weary eyes opened, Karina was anchored in the day, unable to retreat back to her dream world. Grumbling a mixture of blasphemy and obscenities, Karina hurled the covers from her curled up body, before slowly extending her six foot frame out, toes dangling over the end of the bed. 'Uhhh,' she again groaned as her feet swung onto the floor, elbows resting on knees as Kari rubbed the sleep from her eyes. With a yawn, she rose, arms held aloft and lifting her spaghetti-strapped vest up to show the butterfly tattoos on her midriff. Pulling her white pyjama bottoms up out of overly-conscious modesty, Karina strode over to the kitchen, grabbing a hair tie on the way to restrain her wayward strands. 'Psylocke,' Kari bellowed, spitefully ensuring that if she had to be awake, she'd at least make sure her cats shared the inconvenience, 'Betsy, breakfast!' Having not yet heard their paws pitter-patter along the wooden floors, Kari crouched down by a cabinet where their food and bowls were stored. 'Shiiit,' Kari groaned, slapping her forehead then dragging the palm down her souring face, 'out of food.' Just as she was about to make her unintelligible excuses and pop down to the store, her eyes narrowed, sensing that something was amiss. Sliding some scarcely used cleaning bits to the side, Kari fumbled about in the shadows for their bowls, but her fingers failed to probe cold china. 'Looks like you'll have to wait a little longer for breakfast,' Kari then broadcast to the apartment, although the words' targets had yet to rear their sleepy, black-furred heads. 'Must be in the dishwasher...' After scanning the rarely empty sink, Karina pulled open the dishwasher door, where a week's worth of her own dishes were stacked, but not those of her felines. 'Odd,' Kari mused, but shrugged it off as her mind not quite working anytime before midday. 'I'm sure you can hold out for 15 minutes,' the K-Wolf updated, disappearing into the bathroom, where her top and PJ's were quickly flung from, onto the out-of-control laundry pile. A few minutes later, after a few splashes of water and rapid brushing off teeth, Karina emerged. Having traded vest and pyjamas for a hooded top and overly baggy jeans, Kari separated her ponytail into two, then swiped her keys from the dresser. 'I won't be lon-' she went to say in departure, before stopping in her tracks, eyes rolling. 'Fuck.' The previous night was still a haphazard mosaic of memories. Having unashamedly indulged herself in the most embarrassing of her passions - Disco - Karina had complimented the 70's melodies with as many drinks as she could have bought for her. And, although her tall frame often looked awkward, if you saw the K-Wolf lose herself in the mesmerising 'Last Night A DJ Saved My Life', the first words that would come from your drooling lips were, 'what are you drinking?' Lost in the music, her recollection of the night before had mostly followed, tossed into the swirling Disco vortex. She remembered kicking off her heels coming up the stairs, couldn't remember picking them up, and she also couldn't remember leaving the door ajar. 'Not again,' her already weary voice lamented, now realising she had a cat hunt on the cards, given that her felines shared the sense of adventure that brewed within their owner. Slipping her feet into a pair of Converse tennis shoes, the K-Wolf exited the apartment just as her neighbour was coming back up the stairs with their mail. Despite having pretty much nothing in common with the Born Again Christian whose favourite pastimes involved coach tours to historic monuments, and board games, Kari had yet to do anything to offend him, so they were on friendly terms. 'Hey,' she casually greeted him with. 'There isn't a pair of shoes somewhere down the stairs, is there?' As his facial expression shifted to match his thought of 'so THAT's whose they were', Mark nodded. 'Yeah, between the first and second floors.' 'Exxxcellent,' she exhaled in relief, having recently begun to value things like $250 shoes. 'You haven't seen Psylocke or Betsy about, have you? Kinda neglected to close the door last night, and they tend to explore.' Not having the vaguest clue what Kari was talking out, Mark just stared blankly back at her. 'Who're Betsy and Psy...?' 'Psylocke,' the confused Karina prompted. 'My cats?' 'Pets aren't allowed in this building,' he looked bemused as he replied. Karina's prompting gaze tried to usher a punch line, or even a chuckle from Mark, but his face refused to drop the stern expression. 'Er, I picked this building because it DID allow them...' 'Look, I'm not about to judge your lifestyle or anything,' Mark seemed awkward in saying. 'But have you been taking anything?' 'I was about to ask you the same thing,' she mumbled back. Karina's piercing eyes tried to crack through his exterior, but there wasn't even the smallest hint of a fault line that would grow into a chuckle at the K-Wolf's expense. She had two cats, taken from the shelter a few blocks over. She'd named them after her favourite comic character... there was a photo of them in her wallet. Wait, was there? 'Joke over, okay?' her words stressed, as Kari retrieved her handmade, embroidered wallet from deep pockets. 'This isn't... right...' About $25 in bills, some small change, a few cards, one less than the previous night... but not a photo to be seen. 'Is something wrong?' Mark asked, concerned at her seemingly erratic behaviour. 'If you need to talk, the-' 'If this is a joke,' her potent words again threatened, 'drop it now. I'm serious.' 'Look,' Mark tried to calm her with, holding his hands between them as a passive gesture. 'I think it's best that you have a lie down, because you're starting to scare me a little here. I'll be in my apartment if you need to talk.' Left dumbfounded, Karina just stood in the hallway, staring into her wallet, where the photo used to be. Or did it? 'This is just...' she mumbled, searching between bills and cards, before probing every compartment, but to no avail. Forgetting all about her shoes that remained down the stairwell, Karina stormed back into her apartment, slamming the door behind her. Pacing around the cluttered expanse, Karina's eyes searched for their cushions and toys, eyes focussing so fiercely that they threatened to erupt into emerald flame. But no matter where she looked, nothing could be found. 'Wait...' she then advised herself, striding over to the laundry pile to swipe a black t-shirt from amidst its pungent stock. Walking back towards the morning sun, she observed the garment, which had only her lilac hairs stuck to the fabric. Desperation creeping in, she turned the shirt over and over in her hands, but still, not a cat hair to be found. 'What the fuck is happening?' the K-Wolf's bemused words gasped as she dropped down onto the sofa. 'I have two cats, I know this because...' A thought process clicked in Karina's brain, and she shot up off the couch, almost jogging around to the phone. Frantically she searched through the little book beside it, whipping each page over until the number of the local animal shelter appeared... scribbled next to a less-than artistic-doodle of a cat. Taking her frustrations out on the phone's keys, Kari pounded the number in, then waited for an answer. 'Frank's Video Emporium,' a yawning voice answered, leaving Karina's sanity stretched. 'Wait...' she stalled, trying to compose herself as hands scraped lilac hair from her face. 'Did you used to be an animal shelter? Next to a book store, just across the street from a dry cleaners?' 'Book store sold up last year,' the droning voice revealed, 'but we've never been anything but a video store for the last 20 years. Anyth-' Before he could finish, Karina dropped the receiver, walking back and forth across the apartment, grabbing her face between her palms. Looking out across the city, Karina tried to gather her thoughts, identify some rhyme or reason to them. She wasn't... well, crazy. Sure, she'd had anger management issues in the past, but the people she'd hit had certainly existed. Someone had to have been messing with her... but who? Killean? He had the most to benefit from Kari's fragmenting mind as the biggest match of his career dawned. Boda? He had promised revenge, and what better way to torment someone that have their memories feel foreign to them. But why didn't they just jump her backstage at ReVolution and put her head through the wall? 'Not their style,' Karina mumbled, dropping forward against the ceiling-to-floor windows. 'The door was open, they could have scurried out... the photo could have fallen out of my wallet... Mark could have been pulling my leg, which I'll have to break his face for later... I could have ran out of food, and broken their bowls staggering in las-... UGH! This is just...' Leaps of faith were her thing on the slopes, but not even she could make THAT jump of logic. 'Or maybe I'm cr-' Just as the self-doubt was threatening to boil over, bubbling its way up to drench her thoughts, the phone rang, its shrill tone yanking the K-Wolf over to it. 'Hello,' she hurriedly greeted the caller with, hoping it was the shelter saying there'd been a mix up. 'They're onto you,' a grizzly, abrasive voice ripped down the phone line with. 'You... you 'broke' something, and they're coming to fix it, you.' 'Not in the mood, Morpheus,' she instinctively shrugged off what she assumed was a prank call with, not bothering to even take in how the words related to her scenario before the phone was hung up. Only when the receiver was slammed down did Karina stop to think for a moment, before dismissing the fleeting thought to paranoia. 'Have you noticed history change around you,' the gravely voice continued, almost causing the K-Wolf to jump out of her skin. She stared death in the eye on a regular basis, playing chicken to avoid his scythe, but very rarely did such a shudder ever shoot up her spine, almost causing teeth to chatter. 'A memory that doesn't seem to lead anywhere? Recollections disconnected? Past and present not quite overlapping seamlessly.' With fists clenched, Karina sharply spun away from the table, trying to pinpoint where the voice was coming from. 'Not fucking funny,' escaped through gritted teeth. Narrowed, focussed eyes hunted the apartment while attentive ears tried to follow the voice as it trailed off... senses combining to settle on the stereo, where the graphic equaliser flickered back down as he ceased the broadcast. 'I don't think a pinch will do,' Kari then mumbled, before sending a clenched fist harshly into her jaw, almost knocking the K-Wolf off balance as the impact staggered her. 'Yup, not dreaming.' 'Take a look out of the window for yourself,' the voice then goaded Karina. Tearing an evil eye away from the stereo's graphic display, Karina paced sharply over to the window, looking across the cityscape that the sun had just managed to peek up over. Suspicion dominated her facial expression, before it unfurled into a mixture of disbelief and pure shock. Careering at full speed between opposing lanes of traffic was a group of 5 vehicles, all jet black with windows obscured... a fascist aura exuding from them. 'Here is where you make the choice, Miss Wolfenden,' the voice continued, while the K-Wolf began to back away from the windows, speechless, for once. 'You can either stay in your apartment, and hope to wake up before the realisation truly hits you. Or, you can trust me. I can get you out, but like everything, there's a time and a place.' Seemingly happy to drop her inherent mistrust, Karina broke away from the windows, grabbing her keys and cell phone. 'Don't take the phone,' the voice sternly advised, the second her finger touched it. Karina's head snapped, her lilac mane needing a second or two to catch up, while she scanned for a camera. 'Then how do I talk to you?' Kari asked, her words dry, frustrated. 'The same way you are now,' he cryptically informed the K-Wolf. 'Do as I say, follow the leader, and join the dots. You need to be at the Desmond Hotel, at midnight. You'll have all the answers that your brain can process then.' 'Oh, joy,' Kari sighed, recognising the name. 'Parents had their wedding anniversary there, so Dad could use the conference suites the next morning.' 'Did they?' the voice then caused her to reconsider. 'Or is that memory there to fill a space and avoid any suspicions?' With her heart racing, pockets crammed full, and eyes shocked into a fully awake state, Karina wasn't in the mood for any more mindfucks. 'Couldn't give a shit.' Fists clenched as front teeth ran down her bottom lip, releasing a scowl that could ward off even the most mischievous of enemies. 'Get me an exit.' To be continued...
