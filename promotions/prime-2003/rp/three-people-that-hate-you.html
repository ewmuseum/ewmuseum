Three People that Hate You

Date: Mid-November ~Scotty~ November 14th: Morning “You remember Leon?” “Star quarterback turned slime-ball?” “Star quarterback turned successful sports businessman.” Scott Ward brought a glass of orange juice to his lips, and as he did, his hand shook so violently that it nearly flung the glass from his grip. “Vaguely. He was a couple years behind me in school, wasn’t he?” “Yeah, graduated with me.” “Didn’t the guy become a bookie after the shoulder injury that killed his football career? I seem to remember reading about him getting busted by the feds for some shady stuff.” “They looked into it, but never got anything on him.” “Comforting.” Matt cut into his breakfast, taking a large bite, more syrup than pancake, and noticed Scott's trembling hand. "You ok?" "Yeah. Fine." “So what about the guy?” “Wanna meet him?” “What?” “I’ve been talking a lot with the guy lately, and just wondered…” “Why the hell would you be talkin’ to some crooked bookie? This is how you get yourself in trouble, Scotty. Yer fine till ya start hangin’ out with the wrong people.” “Shit, it’s like bein' back in fucking high school. Any more fatherly advice you’d like to share?” “So long as you’re comin’ to me always needing bailed out, I’ll tell ya whatever the hell I feel like.” "Whatever." Scott grabbed the bottle of ketchup and doused his scrambled eggs, missing at first, spraying the edge of his plate. "You sure you're ok?" "Yeah." "Scotty," Lowering his voice, Matt put his fork down and leaned across the table, "are you in some sort of trouble." There was a moment of pause from Scott, and as his older brother took a deep breath and straightened in his chair, he knew he'd given his answer without saying a word. As soon as he was outed, Scott's hands settled down, and he responded the only way he knew how, putting up a shield of jokes. "Ain't I always in deep shit?" "Pretty much. How bad?" "Not too... I dunno. I guess pretty fuckin' bad. Just depends on the mood Leon is in. He actually covered my ass. I wasn't placing bets with him, but I was placing them with some... equally charming people. He bailed me out... didn't say much about it at first. I figured it was just an old high school buddy helpin' a guy out." "Jesus, you shoulda known better than that." "After he'd bailed me out the third time, he made it clear it wasn't a gift. So now, he wants his money back." Scott scratched at his goatee. He could go into more detail, but he figured his brother didn't care how he'd met up with his old high school buddy, or if he'd lost the money betting on the MLB playoffs... whether it was Omaha or Texas Hold 'Em that he had stunk up miserably. And Christ, Scotty sucked at every type of Hold 'Em man had invented. "How much do you owe 'im." "A lot. I'm not even sure. Kinda been more focused on how to get it back, not so much on the exact dollar amount." "I don't believe this." Matt stabbed at his breakfast repeatedly, the fork shredding the pancakes till they sunk in the syrup and turned to soup. "Actually, I guess I can. That's what's so disappointing." "C'mon, man. I need your help." "I got my own problems, Scotty. Mary's been acting strange. I got two kids I'm trying to raise, a career I'm trying to resurrect." "I know, I know. Just meet with the guy, hear what he has to say." Scott folded his hands in begging fashion and used the same face he's used, when he was fourteen, to convince his big brother to sneak him outta the house and drive him over to his girlfriend's after curfew. "Help me out. I'll even pay for breakfast, bro." "Right. You pay for something?" Matt chuckled as he reached for his own wallet and threw a twenty on the table to cover both their meals. "Put the eight bucks towards what ya owe Leon." "So then you'll meet with 'im?" "I didn't say that." "Shit!" He watched as his brother stood, took a final drink from his water, and started to cross the diner. Scott twisted in his chair, throwing an arm across the back as Matt walked away. Not caring if every set of eyes in the place were on him, Scott slammed a palm on the table, causing the silverware to jump. "Are you kiddin' me, man? Just meet with the fuckin' guy. What's the hell's the big deal?" "Look... I'll think about it, Scotty. It's just, maybe enough is enough. Maybe it's time you grew up, stand on your own feet or fall on your own ass." “I preferred when being a big brother meant giving me wedgies and punching me in the arm if I got to the TV remote first.” Matt stopped in his tracks, fidgetting with his car keys, not surprised with the response. “So did I.” ~Matt~ November 22nd The clock said it was a little past two in the morning, but as Matt sat in the corner of the bedroom, draped across the chair like old laundry, he didn’t feel sleepy. His eyes had adjusted to the black of the room, and all around him, things began to take shape, but his gaze never left her figure, buried under blankets, hair sprawled across her pillow. He’d first met Mary in 5th Grade, back when ‘dating’ meant nothing more than holding hands in the tunnel on the playground at recess, and being dumped took nothing more than choosing to play football with the guys instead. In Junior High, he’d played guard for the basketball team, while she’d led the cheerleading squad, rallying him on at games. Then the coach had told Matt he was too soft to play defense and cut him from the team, and Mary decided shaking pompoms was kind of stupid. But it only took that one winter, traveling together around central Ohio on a dilapidated bus with no heat, to strike up a friendship. Far past curling up in tunnels on the playground, with puberty and a car now on his side, the idea of dating seemed much more enticing, and for the entirety of his high school years, Matt had spent his energy trying to convince Mary to give things between them a shot. He’d succeeded too, right after graduation, when they were both set to head off to different colleges. Maybe it had been that first year together, doing the long-distance relationship, that had soured her so much on his life spent traveling. If they could just survive college, halfway across the country, it would all get easier. He’d promised her that much fifteen years ago. And he’d been wrong about it all. “Mmmmhmmm…” Mary rolled over in bed, placed an arm where she thought she’d find her husband, and came up cold. For a moment, she moved her hand around, wondering in her slumbered state if she’d just reached and missed, but after canvassing his side of the bed, she propped herself up and called for him. “Matthew?” He didn’t move from his spot in the corner, didn’t even shift in his chair. “It’s ok, baby. Go back to sleep.” ~Kaiser~ November 14th: Morning Matt crossed the parking lot, unlockig his car with the push of a button. It was unseasonably warm, almost terrifyingly so, and as he climbed into his BMW, he decided to go topless. Another press of a button and the roof of the car folded inside itself, exposing him to the only sunny, mid-60s November day he could remember in quite some time. As he started the engine, he felt a vibration against his right thigh, and reached into the pocket of his jeans, pulling out his cellphone. NEW TEXT Matt flipped open the phone. From: Kaiser WTF?! He closed the phone and tossed it on the floor mats beneath the passenger's seat. ~Matt~ November 22nd “What’s goin’ on? Why are you outta bed?” Mary twisted her neck, peering over her shoulder at the clock on the night stand. “Don’t worry about it, sweetie.” “Are the kids ok? Was Hunter crying?” “Kids are fine.” “You’re not tired?” “Tired… just can’t sleep.” He finally moved in the chair, but made no motion for the bed. “Get some rest. Georgie’s gotta early doctor’s appointment tomorrow.” Mary made no verbal response, but showed her agreement as her head fell back into the pillow, and she pulled the covers to shield her bare shoulders. It had been less than two weeks since his return to PRIME, really only one week that he’d been gone from home, but already, she seemed worn. ReVolution 211 had been his grand return. Nearly 20,000 people had stood on their feet and screamed till the back of their throats burned, cheering him on, minus the pompoms. The roar had been as infectious as he’d remembered, but for the first time in his career, when the bell sounded, after a victory, the explosive cheers from the crowd didn’t lift his spirits. There, in the middle of the ring, fighting to stand on one leg, he’d wondered what the hell he was doing. He’d only seen the referee’s hand slap the mat three times… the ringing of the bell and the sound of the fans hadn’t made their way to his ears. All he’d heard, peeling himself off the sweat-stained canvas, was the sound of Mary’s voice when he’d left the house for the airport the day before. No matter how many times he said goodbye before hitting the road, it was never easy, but after time, it at least became routine enough that the difficulty went unnoticed. This time had been different, and at first he’d just assumed it was because they hadn’t parted ways since he’d last wrestled nearly two years earlier, but as he’d boarded the plane, ready to head to anywhere they sent him, he’d realized what had been different. Concern. Mary had never really tried to hide her frustration with him and his chosen life, or her disappointment in watching him leave for 5 days at a time. This last time, though, she’d seemed almost concerned, her frustration of a different breed. Even when he was home, she’d been acting different, especially-loving at times, distant and diluted at others. There were times she seemed almost burdened with guilt, as if she were the one leaving for some random town every week. None of it made total sense, but with each passing day, Matt became more convinced that she was being torn down, and he could only assume it was all his fault. “Baby…” Her words were soft, barely able to fight their way across the room, “come back to bed.” Matt lifted himself from the chair, trying not to make a sound, even though she wasn’t asleep. When he got to the bed, he pushed the covers back and laid down beside her. In the dark, he saw her smile as she rolled over and put an arm across his chest, tucking her head away against his shoulder. “Thank you.” Mary kissed him, her lips finding the nearest flesh. "Welcome.” “I’m glad you’re here.” He could hear that concern in her voice again, but the words were sincere. She meant them, that much he was sure of, and it made him feel like shit for ever even leaving the bed.
