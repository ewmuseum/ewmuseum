Outside The Ropes

29 January 2008

Date: 1/29/08 (by a couple minutes) "There can be no friendship without confidence, and no confidence without integrity." -Samuel Johnson April 17th, 2005 8:07 A.M. "Welcome," came the familiar voice of Nick Stuart, "to the PRIME family." There we stood, a dozen of us, in make-shaft ring gear. Next to me towered the last vestige of Neanderthal blood left on earth: seven feet tall, eyes set too close to one another, heavy breathing, the whole shebang. My eyes snapped back towards PRIME's play-by-play man as he continued. His voice was soft, but echoed in the training facility's wide open spaces. "You're some of the luckiest men and women in the sports entertainment industry right now. You have dazzling careers right at your feet." In my peripheral vision, I caught sight of the one woman amongst us. I wondered how long she would last. "You're still a step away from PRIME. You haven't made it yet, and if you think you have, your time here is going to be harder than you can imagine." The only man smaller than me, a quiet luchadore who I hadn't made any attempt to meet, was tapping his foot in anticipation. For him, for most of these never-would-be's, Nick Stuart was a reason to get starstruck. But I'd been hot tubbing with Giselle, I'd been Tyson Beckford's wing man. And beyond that, I'd been farther in the wrestling world than all of these trainees combined. Less than twenty-four months ago, I'd been rubbing shoulders with real talent like Karina Wolfenden and Hoyt Williams; now they were grabbing huge paychecks in PRIME and was in Middleburg, Florida. "Since PRIME doesn't have a feeder fed, per se, we like to use a variety of regional feds for the same purpose. Sunshine State Wrestling will do exactly that for you twelve. Your developmental deals stipulate that you'll spend a minimum of three months training here and learning from the master teachers. All of them have real world experience and whether you think you're the next Killean Sirrajin or just doing this for a paycheck, you'd do well to listen to these guys." For a guy whose life was wrestling, Stuart seemed to have an intelligence about him. He was reading us as he watched, and I didn't want some announcer sizing me up. When his eyes fell upon me, I looked out over the ring. It wasn't much. When PRIME had first courted me, by way of two stumpy white guys with glasses and bad suits, they'd shown me the royal treatment. Oh, Mr. Tsonda, we just LOVED your work for PWC. And then, like with everything else, the honeymoon was over. The people-pleasers were sent off to brownnose some other target of Motoki & Co. and all I got was a phone call about a plane ticket. Forget a chartered jet, this wasn't even first class. Just a ticket, on a tiny knick knack of a plane, to Middleburg and several nights paid in the local Days Inn. This, I'd grumbled many times already, was the definition of paying your dues. "I've got a day job that I've gotta get back to. But I came down here to be the face of this company. I've been a part of this family for many years, and while I encourage you to make friends, remember that this is a business. If you love wrestling and would be content just being around it...you can have my job." The other eleven PRIME hopefuls laughed. I wondered how anyone could stand being a lifelong bystander like Stuart. After some mild applause for Stuart, he made for the door. Not before signing some autographs. Most people claimed it was for their kids, but I saw those autographs for months, used like totems. I imagine that for those among us with no chance of ever seeing the big stage (they told me afterwards that a quarter of the guys who go to Middleburg are useless, just bodies down there to fill time on SSW shows), a reminder that THE Nick Stuart, a somebody, had been here...it was an escape. An escape from twelve-hour days with no greater goal. I never knew the nobodies in my class (and they were gone within two weeks, all three of them), but they were the ones that I often saw stealing glances at their Nick Stuart autographs. The man who now stepped to the forefront looked like a guy who'd be teaching young wrestlers in Middleburg, Florida. And by that, I mean he had a ponytail that hadn't been washed since Reaganomics were popular and arms in the middle of the brutal transition from beach muscle to globular fat. After a moment of staring at the twelve of us, he pulled off his surely fake designed sunglasses. Where Stuart had displayed some culture, some sense that wrestling was not the be-all end-all of life, this man utterly failed. "My name's Bo. Welcome," he croaked, in the key of cheap cigarettes and cheaper vodka, "to the greatest place on the planet you'll never wanna come back to." I wasn't sure he would survive the full-body cackle that followed. /~/~/~/~/ May 5th, 2005 6:19 P.M. The day was over. Thank Christ, it was over. The past eighteen days had sapped more sweat from me than...anything I could remember. I'd get to the end of the official day, only to find that it was time to hit the gym. The others all sucked down Hydroxycut and Muscle Milk in between our 9-to-4 ring work and the gym. I told myself that speeding my metabolism up anymore would just mean losing muscle, so I stayed away. But it was just another thing that I did differently. Bo kept telling me that I was a standout in the ring, but I just felt like I stood out. And alienation soured into resentment. I was mad that no one could work a fifteen minute match with me, mad that no one could sell the moves I was ready to do. And so I just stayed away from the rest of them. It was quiet in the locker room as I unwrapped my wrists. I was usually the first one to leave, in part because I felt wholly uncomfortable around the gym rat crowd. "You get done pretty quick out there, huh?" The luchadore was looking down at me. "I do okay, yeah." This was how things were. I knew I'd make it to PRIME sooner rather than later, and better to limit the number of goodbyes I'd have to say. But the luchadore, well, Sunshine State Wrestling was probably the last stop for him. "Just one of those days, ya know? Couldn't do shit out there. Couldn't even hip toss you." In the silence that followed, where I might have told him that it was okay, he wiped himself down with a towel. "Sorry, brother. Ya know, we're gonna go grab a bite and some drinks. We even got Sandy to come out tonight. I know you've kinda stuck to yourself, but we were gonna do a whole group thing tonight if you're interested." "Sandy? The hot chick?" I wasn't going to ask forgiveness for my one-track mind. We all did it, snickered about Title IX when she jumped in the ring. It was the only time I commiserated with the goons. But it was also the only time I legitimately felt bad. Because she had a ticked punched for nowhere, just like the luchadore. "Now you're all jazzed up to go, huh?" The luchadore smiled, glad to bring me into the fold. For him, this was summer camp, a good time away from the (presumed) wife and kids that he'd always remember. He wanted some nice moments to remember, for everyone to be friends. The nice part about having no hope of success was that you got to embrace the atmosphere, the experience. "My name's Leon, by the way." "I'm Chandler." "Is it true you used to work with Jonathan Conspiracy?" "Err...kinda. Bumped shoulders with the dude once in a while. Pretty big prick, to be honest." It was stupid and petty, but I liked taking the wind out of Leon's sails. If he figured out that the J-Cons of the world never ended up being the guys they played on TV, he'd be better off. I imagined that I knew better than...well, everyone. "Oh." "Yeah. Sometimes, the air up there isn't all it's cracked up to be. Smells like the same shit as this place." "That'd be the lovely mixture of ball sweat and runoff testosterone?" It was a new voice, one far too pretty to belong to Leon or any of the other goons. We both looked up to see Sandy. Her brick red hair was askew in every direction, straining to escape from the constraint of her ponytail's tie. Several loose hairs were stuck to her forehead, glued in place by sweat. Already stripped down to her sports bra, which housed small shapely breasts, Sandy was in great shape. To boot, she didn't look like a guy below the neck. Her flattened abs pulsed quickly with her short breaths; she never struggled to keep up in the ring, but I could tell that unlike some of us (see: me), she was always giving a hundred percent. "Yeah, something like that," I said, because nothing better came to mind. "Sandy! I was just tellin'...Chandler is it? Yeah, Chandler, how we're goin' out on the town. "He gonna join the hunting party?" She didn't look at either of us as she peeled off her track pants. Her long legs were shiny with sweat, and she caught me staring at one point. "I'm sitting right here." "You're usually so quiet," she replied, turning her back as she wrapped a towel around her chest and pulled her bra over her head. "Yes, I'm going to this little dinner," I said, slightly pouting. "Not much dinner to it. Maybe a couple wings in between drinks." I shot a look up at Leon, who shrugged. "Silly me, I expected we might be able to sit down for a decent meal before we get drunk off our asses." "I heard Asians can't hold their liquor," Leon interjected. "And I heard Mexicans steal a lotta shit." Leon rolled his eyes, slinging a gym bag over his shoulder. This was the type of trouble one ran into when you fraternized with these people. "He's not Mexican," Sandy replied. "He's Cuban." "Well," I said, in my best Angelo Deville voice (I'd learned a thing or two about being slimy from having to live in the shadow of the The Devil's Don), "don't you know a fucking lot about all your little friends?" "Leon," she said, in a total moment of no-sell, "I know you're trying to be nice, but some people just don't deserve it." She made her dramatic exit on that note, leaving me in the company of Leon the luchadore. "I guess we'll all be better friends over drinks, eh?" Leon was gone before the echo of his words. I was more comfortable alone in the quiet. /~/~/~/~/ May 6th, 2005 1:18 A.M. There are some stories that guys tell where they skip right over how they got the woman into bed. They do it because that's how James Bond movies work, because all women just sorta gravitate towards James Bond. He doesn't do anything, it's just the science of magnetism. This, unfortunately, is one of those stories. Drinks had, indeed, made us better friends. I'd started off by apologizing, half-heartedly, for being crude. The competition wasn't exactly Olympian; when you look like (or happen to be) a model in Middleburg, Florida, and you only know one woman, and that one woman only knows eight other men, and they're the same eight men who you've already categorized as "the goons"...well, some nights, you get to be James Bond. "Are you still awake?" We hadn't talked much beforehand. Or, rather, neither of us remembered what had been said. Same thing. "I was gonna pretend not to be," I confessed, "but no, yeah, I'm still up." "You do this a lot?" "Sex? Yeah, I'm kind of an avid fan." I flashed a smile. I didn't know why I was turning on the charm and actually meaning it, but here it was. Mixing business with pleasure, the job with friendship. "Coworkers, I meant." "For years, my coworkers were usually taller than me, gorgeous, and ate nothing but celery with a light dusting of cocaine. All female models know how to do is sleep with the guys they work with." We'd covered the whole "I used to be a model" thing. I think. Sandy, who looked as good naked as she did in her skivvies (which is, surprisingly, rare), was a pillow talker. "Why do you talk like that?" "Talk like what?" "Like you just shot a great round of golf or something. Stop celebrating." I thought about telling her that I was sorry, which I was. Letting things fall silent was like a reset button. We started over. "I've never slept with a female wrestler. You're my first." The ceiling fan shook as it tried to counter the mid-spring heat. "This," I continued, "is the part where you say that I'm your first, too." "You're not." Her voice was flat. I guess this was what my nonchalance about sex came off like, and it sucked. "O...kay." "Don't do that," she cooed, tussling my hair with the same fingers that, hours earlier, had been suplexing and headlocking, "I'll tell you a secret if you want." "I want to go to sleep." "C'mon, you go to all that work to look pretty and you can only go once?" Here's the part where you cover the kid's ears, because she was talking about sex. Specifically, the four minutes and forty-one seconds of sex that had preceded this conversation. Not my finest moment, but not my darkest either. Every night I could get this drunk and get it up at all was a victory. "What's the secret?" Three fingernails tiptoed down my chest. Sandy pulled herself close, and the secret really felt like one, as a pocket of warm breath nestled against my neck. "I love wrestling more than anything. So I do anything I have to. Pills, creams, whatever. I've done it all." "And they don't test you?" It wasn't ideal foreplay, but her loud, aggressive kisses on my neck were. She stopped only to answer, and barely even then. "Nobody wants to know if you are or if you aren't. Never ran into a standard testing policy that didn't have enough holes to skate through." There are things that your conscience rages against, times where your heart almost rises up out of your chest to compel you to action. Good guys always take the girl aside and ask, in that John Wayne moment, if you could stop, just for me, sugar. I like telling myself that it was my job to tell her to stop, then and there. I closed my eyes as her kisses descended to my collarbone. /~/~/~/~/ June 20th, 2005 9:04 P.M. "We know you've been using, you pencil-necked fuck." Bo was heavy on the saliva when he talked, and with him inches from my face, I would've needed a rain coat to handle all the spray. "Skinny pricks like you wanna play dressup and pretend to be Tony Rolo. Well, you're no fuckin' PRIMEate. You're not even a goddamn wrestler. You're a cheater, you slant-eyed bucket of shit." Fear gripped me. Not because I thought I'd been using, but because the goons resented me for consistently fucking Sandy. Would one of them have slipped something in my drink? Just enough to hit a positive? "Test me again. It was a false positive." "I don't think you get it," Bo spit, knuckles cracking loudly. "We don't gotta test you again. One positive and you're out the door. Sayonara." "That's bullshit." "No, you thinking you can slip the juice past me is bullshit, tough guy. I don't know what kinda fucked up inferiority issues you got, but there's no spots on this roster or any other for fuckin' cheaters." My mouth was dry. My nerves defied logic, defied what I knew. How could I test positive for roids? "I don't know how this happened, but I don't use 'roids. Bo, I don't care if you like me, but think about it! I need to keep my weight down to stay in the air." "C'mon, Tsonda, there's stuff out there that'll make your dick stand up and do jumping jacks. Whether you use it to put weight on or keep weight off, I don't give a sliver of a monkey's ass. You're gone." This was final. "Get outta my sight." I clenched both my fists. I was almost to the big time, almost back. What Nick Stuart had said... "Wait, wait, wait. I know what might've caused this. I know who's really using." Bo turned around, sneer curling up his face. "Answers, and make it quick, gook." If she loved wrestling that much, I told myself, she'd always be able to do Nick Stuart's job. /~/~/~/~/ October 12th, 2005 7:32 P.M. I arrived early for my first night in PRIME, expecting to scope out my locker room and maybe get some face-time with any lingering stars. The only person there was Danny Ferguson, watching as his lackeys put up posters for Hard Money. It wasn't long before I had to talk about my track record. "Wait, so even though you humped it in PWC with K-Wolf and Deville, they made you do six months of training?" "Said I was too rough in the ring, that my aerial stuff was gonna get somebody killed if I didn't learn the basics of being in a wrestling ring. Kinda right, I guess." "Bullshit, man," Ferguson replied, popping one of catering's strawberries into his mouth. "You can't listen to the suits around here. More crooked than Hollywood, and half as good-looking. Say, you wouldn't know anything about the juice scandal down at SSW, would ya?" "I know of it," I said through gritted teeth. "Some chick went all Barry Bonds trying to be the next leading lady in PRIME." "That's the story." "You know her?" "I do." Neither one of us said anything. "Was it you?" "Was what me?" "Tsonda, we all know what happened. Nobody cares. They pick one newbie, the weakest one, and they tell him he tested positive or whatever. Something to make him sweat. They just do it to break through the whole camaraderie thing. Wrestlers won't out other wrestlers unless their job is on the line. From what I hear, that's how they made Mrs. McGwire down at Sunshine State." "You think I ratted somebody out to save my own skin?" "I'm no detective, but I once spent a day on the set of NYPD Blue, and if I'm looking for motive, I'm looking at the one guy who actually made it out of that dump up into the big time. You." He finished the strawberry with another bite and pointed his stained finger at me. "I have a match in like an hour." "IT WAS YOU! You might have a future here after all. I thought you'd be totally subservient and nerdy. Ya know, the whole Asian thing." I had nothing else to say, so I turned to walk away. I could only hope that the other members of the PRIME roster were less in tune with the gossip train. Ferguson wouldn't let me leave without a final taunt, though. "Keep it up, Tsonda. You and I are gonna be fast friends at this rate!" "You don't wanna be my friend," I shot back, as coldly as I could. "It's dangerous business."
