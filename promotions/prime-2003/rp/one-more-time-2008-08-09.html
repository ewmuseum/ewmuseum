PRIME — Roleplay archive

One more time

9 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/9/08 August 9, 2008 Charlotte, North Carolina Colossus V - Night One Tony Rolo sits on a bench in his locker room, his head resting in his hands. He’s about to emerge onto the biggest stage in this sport once again, PRIME’s largest annual event, COLOSSUS. As he sits there, he remembers the times before – he thinks about how he got where he is right now – just moments away from going toe to toe with Tony Gamble. And while the task at hand is a tall one, Rolo finds his mind drifting away from the present and into the past. We’ll call it… “A Long Road Traveled” He remembers receiving a phone call in early 2003 from his friend and former Soldout Wrestling President, Sebastian Starr regarding a new federation Starr intended on starting, PRIME. Starr offered Tony a lucrative contract, making him the first member of the PRIME roster ever and even asked him for advice. Tony joked with him not to trust the Asian trio of Taun Pham, Hin See, and Toshiaki Motoki. He remembers doing as he promised Starr, becoming the biggest superstar of PRIME’s golden era. He solidified this early on in what was considered a dream matchup at that time, taking on Rock Startling on an episode of ReVolution. Startling was 9-0 in PRIME and some considered him to be unstoppable, including himself. Too bad nobody told Rolo, as he put an end to the undefeated streak of Startling. Tony was proud of himself after this win, considering he truly hated Startling. Many believed Rolo was more proud of the loss for Startling than the win for him. They’re right. He remembers shortly thereafter becoming the #1 contender for Lance Jackson’s Global Title. That’s right, Global Title – the name of PRIME’s top championship belt before it was called the Universal Title. At the first ever King of Kings pay-per-view, Tony Rolo reached the top of the mountain in PRIME, capturing the belt after executing a top rope Roloplex on Jackson. With the popularity PRIME has garnered, he knew he had just solidified his name with those of legends in this sport. And to celebrate, Joey Troy took him out for a night of drunken debauchery that ended with Tony face down passed out in a urinal. Joey has the pictures of this as proof. Every so often he threatens to sell them on eBay. Tony leaves stuff like this out of his PRIME lore. He remembers this title run being unfortunately short – just six days. Taking the belt from him was one of his all-time arch nemeses, Ian English. There was a deep distain that these two held for each other, going back to the days they shared in Soldout Wrestling. That goofy red-headed bastard always seemed to one-up Rolo, and it grated on his last nerve. It’d be many more months before Rolo had a chance to recapture the top prize in PRIME. He remembers winnings the tag team titles. His partner was, of course, his best friend Joey Troy – their team obviously known as Crudely Civilized. To this day, Tony prides himself that Crudely Civilized was, as he strongly believes, the best true tag team ever in professional wrestling. The bond between these two men went deeper than title belts, went deeper than just teammates, or even deeper than friendship when it came down to it. These two men are like brothers to one another. It’s a relationship that is rare to find in this sport, and Tony knows if he leaves this game with nothing other than Joey’s friendship that he’ll consider himself leaving as a winner. He remembers the first ever Culture Shock in 2004, and more specifically the first ever Dual Halo match. What most do not know is that the Dual Halo is a match that Rolo himself helped Sebastian Starr create shortly before he was run out of the federation he created, and ultimately bled for, by Toshiaki Motoki and Hin See. Motoki ended up taking credit for the Dual Halo, which was just one of the many reasons Tony considered Motoki to be a good for nothing, snake in the grass piece of dog shit. During the Halo, Rolo eliminated a match high five competitors and fought his way down to being one of the final two men standing. He found none other than Ian English standing across from him once again, which was only fitting. Once more, Rolo found himself at the losing end of an encounter with the British superstar, though, this was not the defining moment of the event. In shocking fashion, Toshiaki Motoki unveiled his new stable, headed up by the current Global Champion, Ignatius Lisieux. Lisieux and his new stablemates, which included Adam and Black Angel, not only cost Rolo the coveted Halo match, but laid a brutal beating on both he and Joey Troy. This would begin the feud that Tony Rolo considers as the defining one in his Hall of Fame career. He remembers ReVolution 30, then considered the biggest main event ever on PRIME’s regular programming – Tony Rolo versus Ignatius Lisieux for the Global Championship. He recalls most thinking he didn’t stand a chance. Not only was Lisieux younger, faster, and on a long hot streak, but he had the backing of the all-powerful Toshiaki Motoki. The odds were against PRIME’s Goldenboy. But as time has shown time and time again, that’s how he liked it. The night before the match, Tony had a dream. In the dream came his savior, who was ironically in the form of Ian English. Ian came in the night with a rhyming message, “Cheat to Defeat.” It was simple, it was genius. If Rolo wanted to beat Ignatius Lisieux and recapture the Global Title, becoming the first PRIME superstar to hold the coveted title on multiple occasions, then he needed to think outside the box… he needed to do something nobody would expect – and cheat was just that thing. He decided to beat Ignatius Lisieux at his own game. In true honor of Ian English, Tony Rolo used his nemesis’ move, the Tea Mist, which was just spitting hot tea in his opponent’s face. This enabled Rolo the time to hit Lisieux with a Spearbuster and make his way up the ladder, pulling the belt into his hands and claiming victory. Who’d have thought that Ian English would prove to be Rolo’s knight in shining armour? He remembers having the title stripped the next week at ReVolution, and in unprecedented fashion, the entire decision of his ladder match with Lisieux from the previous week was reversed. Never during his career had Tony Rolo been so outrage, never had he felt so robbed. As feared, Toshiaki Motoki abused his powers for his benefit, attempting the demise of Tony Rolo by defeating him mentally. This began the setup of the biggest match in Rolo’s history – a rematch with Ignatius Lisieux at the first ever Colossus. The two men considered to be at the top of PRIME would face off in a Hell in a Cell match to settle their score once and for all. It was marketed as the match of all matches. One man stood for truth and honor, while the other stood for power and ruthlessness. Good versus evil. A match for the ages. He remembers the night of that event. He remembers asking for separate locker rooms for he and Joey, something they never had done previously. He remembers sitting in his locker room, nervous and sweating in anticipation of what he KNEW was a huge match. He remembers moments before walking out of his locker room that he was almost broke down in tears knowing what he had been through to get where he was, but he somehow found the strength to focus on his opponent. He focused his rage and anger for Motoki and combined that to the hate he felt for Lisieux, and focused all of that energy on his opponent. He remembers these feelings that ran through him. And he remembers rolling Lisieux up in a small package and hearing the ref’s hand pound off the mat three times. This was his defining moment. He believed in himself. That’s all it took. And he won. This marked the end of year one in PRIME, and Tony Rolo was it’s biggest star. And he’ll never forget that as long as he lives. He remembers the details of these stories like they just happened yesterday. Only it wasn’t. Four years have passed since then. And almost everything in PRIME has changed. He does find himself in the same place, though… in a locker room on the evening of Colossus. Suddenly, a knock is heard at the door. Tony snaps out of his daydream, realizing he had been in a trance like state unaware of the time. He turns his attention to the repeated knocking. He gets up and opens the door to his locker room. Standing there is a PRIME road agent. He offers up two words to Rolo… “It’s time.” “Can I have a minute?,” Rolo responds with. “Sure,” the man replies. Tony slowly walks over to the full-length mirror in his locker room. He adjusts the knee brace he wears on his left knee and then just stands there staring back at himself. On this evening he can’t help hold back a single tear from escaping his left eye. It trickles down his cheek and around the corner of his mouth. He offers himself up some last minute words of encouragement, possibly for the final time in his career. “One night.” “One more time.” “Thanks for the memories.”
