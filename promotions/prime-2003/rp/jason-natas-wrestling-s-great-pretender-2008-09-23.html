Jason Natas: Wrestling's Great Pretender

23 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23rd Sept. Jason Natas: Wrestling’s Great Pretender by J.W. Keightly for pwuniverse.com Professional wrestling is essentially, by its very nature, a non-competitive sport (in fact, there are those that would dispute its tag as a “sport” in the first place, but that’s a whole new can of worms). Unlike its amateur counterpart, professional wrestling contests are almost entirely scripted; results are usually pre-determined by the bookers of the promotion in question, and the competitors involved are usually given a rough idea on how the match should be worked. This is nothing earth-shattering. In the age of internet smart marks, I challenge you to find a human being over the age of twelve that still thinks professional wrestling is “real”. It is a performing art in the truest sense of the phrase. Of course, you will always find smaller scale promotions who’s primary focus is to put out technically masterful grappling contests, but at the end of the day it still essentially comes down to two guys following a pre-determined plan on the way to a pre-determined conclusion. This is perhaps most noticeable at the top end of the market. Promotions such as PRIME and Sin City Championship Wrestling are essentially out there to promote a product, nothing more, nothing less. It is not my objective to disrespect the standards of the matches or the credentials of the workers in these companies (because lets face it; there’s a reason that these are the top two companies in the industry right now), but at the end of the day the focus is generally not on who is the better wrestler (pay per views aside), but on who has the ability to carry the most entertaining storyline over a period of time. Of course, the technical ability of the workers in said federations should not be discounted. Professional wrestling fans, particularly in this day and age, are a lot smarter than many bookers give them credit for. They know when they are watching a truly awful in-ring competitor and they will more often than not indicate their displeasure when witnessing a god awful slugfest between a couple of ‘roided-up meatheads with less ring acumen and athletic ability than a Canadian redwood. But that’s not to say you have to be technically flawless to get by in this business. Xavier Kannon, for example, is never going to be the type of guy to bust out a flawless five minute sequence of chain wrestling, but the guy has more than enough ability to get by in the game. Oh, and he can talk like a motherfucker too. Which brings me nicely onto another criteria that, an in ideal world, any pro-wrestler employed by one of the country’s so-called “big leagues” should fulfil; microphone skills, acting and charisma. It’s a sad fact, but in today’s wrestling environment you are going to get absolutely nowhere if you can’t get on the microphone to push yourself and your storylines. If you can’t act you’re never going to main event a PRIME pay per view, it’s simple. Similarly, if you can’t convey emotion and play your character on the microphone, then nobody is going to give a shit about you. It’s a harsh reality. Character is important as well; both on-air and off. On television you’ve got to be able to portray a character that is entertaining and believable. Natural charisma and microphone ability can always hide flaws and make average characters seem better than they really are, but there really is no substitute for a unique, engaging character when it comes to drawing heat. And if you don’t conduct yourself in the correct manner backstage, you’re going to find yourself handed a big ol’ pink slip very, very quickly. Want to know why Brandon Youngblood, a fantastically talented athlete, never reached his full potential in PRIME? Because he was a god damn scumbag. This is particularly important when you are new to the game; if you come into a new locker-room full of yourself then you’re going to alienate your peers and become an outcast, and believe me, that is not a position you want to find yourself in. Like any sport, professional wrestling is full of gargantuan egos and massive personalities, and an established star isn’t exactly going to take kindly to some punk kid rolling in, acting like he owns the place. In a sport where the backstage camaraderie is what keeps so many professional wrestlers in the business, the wrong attitude can cost you dearly. To summarise, then, the average professional wrestler under the employment of one of the company’s top promotions should prove to be competent in each of the following criteria:- 1. Wrestling ability. 2. Charisma/microphone ability. 3. Character. 4. Attitude. Of course, a lot of today’s grapplers get by just satisfying two of those points, but all the top guys in the game excel in all four, and nobody should be lacking in all of these departments. Which makes me wonder WHY exactly PRIME haven’t cut Jason Natas loose yet. This man is, quite frankly, nothing more than a cancerous presence on the roster of the world’s most popular wrestling promotion. He can’t wrestle, can’t talk… and after a recent encounter with the man, I can say, with authority, that he is simply the most odious individual I have ever had the displeasure of meeting through this great profession. During my time working as a road agent for PRIME last month, I had a blast. For the most part the wrestlers were warm and inviting, and more than keen to share a few road stories over a beer or two after the show. PRIME officials have a lot to be proud of as they have assembled a roster of competitors who are incredibly easy to approach behind the scenes (often in spite of their on-air demeanours). Apart from Mr. Natas. Usually when I’m working a show I’ll stop any workers I see wandering backstage and shoot the breeze for a little while; it helps break up the monotony of the menial bullshit a road agent has to deal with, and it’s cool getting to know the men (and women) behind the gimmicks. After ten years in this business I can safely say that up until I met Jason Natas, I never once had an unpleasant experience with a professional wrestler. Not everyone wants to talk, I can accept that, but please, if you’re too busy to shoot the shit for five minutes, at least be honest. Say “sorry, I’m a bit busy,” make up some kind of excuse, you know? Do not do as Jason Natas does. Do not threaten physical violence and slur obscenities through booze-soaked breathes. Do not blow cigarette smoke into the agent’s face. Do not attempt to stub out said cancer stick on their arm. I had heard the stories about this guy’s attitude before I met the man himself, but as usual I took them with a grain of salt; there are so many misinformed opinions in this business that is hard to know who to believe, but I can confirm that just about every bad thing you have heard about Jason Natas on various websites is 100% correct. The man is an odious, wretched smear on the reputation of PRIME. He’s alienated almost the entire PRIME roster in the five months he has been employed by the company and he’s pissed-off officials to no end. He shows no respect whatsoever to his fellow professionals and he has even gone as far as laying his hands on an un-named backstage worker, which, of course, lead to his suspension earlier in the month. And if that wasn’t enough, this guy has all the talent of a thousand year old boulder. I mean, have you seen this guy wrestle?! It’s actually painful watching him lumbering around the ring like his joints are sealed with concrete. Alright, sure, he might have a half-decent range of strikes (that’s what happens when you waste years of your life in the underground New York fight scene), but when a guy struggles to execute a fucking scoop slam… well, I don’t think I need to say much more on that. And his microphone skills? Don’t get me started. As soon as Jason Natas gets behind the microphone, he has all the convincingness of Ben Affleck playing Ghandi. I mean, this guy’s supposed to have been a hard-boiled New York street urchin all his life, but he plays the role so woodenly that it’s actually hard not to laugh. If rumours that Jason has been training with PRIME hall of famer Sonny Silver to improve his attributes are to be believed, then at least hopefully the standard of his interviews will improve. Mr. Silver always did cut a killer promo, and if some of those skills rub off on Jason Natas then ReVolution will certainly be a much more tolerable broadcast for me at least. So taking his abysmal in-ring ability, poor microphone skills and revolting attitude into account, I am absolutely dumbfounded as to why this guy still has a PRIME contract. In my eyes, he should have been given the “future endeavours” treatment a long, long time ago. He brings absolutely nothing to the product, has a criminal record, and has done nothing but cause disturbances in the backstage area since his arrival. Word has it that Jason Natas might be in “last chance saloon” with PRIME officials who are tired of his sloppy performances and poor attitude, and that one more discrepancy could see him axed from the company. I, personally, cannot wait for that day.
