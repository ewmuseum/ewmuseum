Date: September “It’s not every day a guy’s doctor tells him he raped his sister.” “Jesus, Kaiser.” David Vashaun shook his head as he crunched away on a couple of BBQ potato chips. “That’s not what he meant.” “Pretty much what he said.” “I think you missed his intended message. Based on what you claim he said, I’d lean more towards him highlighting that you act a helluva lot different at your job than you do around the house or out running errands.” “I’d get arrested for hitting people at the grocery store.” “You know what I’m sayin’. Up until you joined PRIME, you were just a quiet kid who occasionally went out for a couple beers with his buddies, but mostly kept to himself. Now, you’re one of the more arrogant and annoying… no offense… guys on TV. And that’s saying something in a business chalk full of loud-mouths and crazy characters.” “It’s a little easier to show some confidence when things are going well. Do we really need to compare the current trends in my personal life versus my professional?” Kaiser crossed his small kitchen and opened the fridge. His eyes darted around the various shelves. Nothing appealed to him. “The worst is over. Just keep trudgin’ along. I know it’s been a rough year… but ya just gotta keep your chin up.” “I dunno, Dave. PRIME’s going real good. But other than that…it’s been pretty bad as of late. There was the whole thing with Michelle. Then Anth’s death and the trial. Dad passing away. And now my shrink thinks I’m hell-bent on acting like the guy that ra…” “Stop, Kaiser. Can’t keep dwelling on it all. Things’ll get better.” “Yeah.” Kaiser closed the fridge and turned to look at his older brother. “Just would be nice for things to be going as well at home as they are in PRIME.” “Well, you can’t have a big shiny belt for everything. That’s like having your cake and eating it to.” “I don’t see what’s wrong with that.” Kaiser smirked as he leaned against the refrigerator. “Hey… can I see it?” Dave smacked his hands together, brushing off orange flecks of potato chips. “See what?” “The belt, champ. Can I see the belt?” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Things were pitch black. And stuffy. It wasn’t sure how long it’d been sitting in this…box, but it could tell the space was confined. There were others of its kind in the small tomb, maybe four or five… but none as powerful as itself. The echo of footsteps had alerted it to a change in the atmosphere. Something… someone… was coming towards the box. With each increasingly louder stomp, it waited to be released from its prison. A jolt of light shot from one single direction and illuminated the dark, revealing the beige walls of its holding place. The room was small, the ceiling arched. Suddenly, it felt itself ripped from the box, rushing towards the light. And just like that, it was free. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ “That’s badass.” Kaiser didn’t respond to his older brother’s exclamation. He simply stood a few feet back, arms folded across his chest, and allowed David to gawk over the large championship belt like a teenager watching his first porno. He’d seen enough of it already. For the first week after Colossus, Kaiser had left the belt untouched, specifically avoiding switching the name plate from ‘Troy Douglas’ to his own. He’d even refused to clean the title, leaving it speckled with dried spots of sweat and blood. Against the plating, and in its crusted state, the blood had appeared black, as if it’d been spilled by something not human. It was that discomforting thought that had led him to finally have the belt cleaned. He’d contemplated leaving the name-plate unchanged, as a reminder that the success could be short-lived, but it struck him that the Kaiser Vashaun in PRIME would never give even a first thought to that sort of humility. The Kaiser in PRIME. And that was when he’d realized… he was decomposing into two halves. “So they just let you carry this thing around with ya?” Dave ran his fingers over the center-plate. “Yeah. What the hell did you think happened with it?” “I dunno. I guess I figured the company held onto it. Ya know… kept it safe, and then you picked it up backstage before the shows.” “Naw. Put down a deposit and it’s yours to keep so long as you’re champ.” “That’s crazy, man.” “The only thing crazy was what it took to win the thing.” Kaiser shoved his hands in his pockets and leaned against the wall in his den. “Yeah. That was pretty intense stuff.” “I dunno how a guy is supposed to go out there and do that night after night.” “Just keep fightin’.” For the first time since entering the room and seeing the belt sitting on the mantle, David turned his eyes away from the prize. “Is that professional advice, or personal?” “Both.” “Figured.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The large being that possessed it, and all the others like it, headed towards a tomb of his own; this one much larger than its own tiny box it had been confined to just moments earlier. Quickly, self-awareness began to grow. It was in the clutches of its destination. It was in the hands of the man it belonged to. Though it was just a letter…its existence was damning, and its ego began to grow. It carried a weight, an importance that gave it a sense of superiority to its peers, which had sat with him in the box. And for that reason, it knew it would be selected first. As it felt the others of its kind fall away, dropping with a soft ‘tap’ on the coffee table below, leaving it alone in his grasp, it braced itself for the coming discovery. A shot of pain traversed its spine as it was torn open, revealing its innards of paper and ink. In the firm grip of his hands, it felt itself unfolded. For a moment, there was nothing. Then it sensed what it’d been waiting for. Sensed the life drain. A series of exchanges followed, between his owner and another presence in the room. It only recognized it as noise, some language it wasn’t meant to comprehend. And then there was a different noise. Something it could understand. A single sound, which let it know that its job was done… that its purpose in life had been fulfilled. As the letter closed, folding in on itself, it relished in that sound. The sound of a man’s will exiting his body in one exhausted sigh. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Kaiser reached into the mailbox and pulled out its contents, a half a dozen pieces or so, and headed back towards the house. Sifting through the mail, a specific enveloped caught his attention. He looked it over carefully as he walked into the house, kicking the front door closed behind him. “Let me guess… bills?” Dave leaned back in the couch and turned on the television, flipping straight to ESPN. “For the most part.: “Same ol’ shit. Always is.” “Yeah.” Kaiser didn’t lift his eyes, letting all the mail fall on the coffee table. All except the envelope that had caught his attention. Tearing the corner with his fingernail, he slipped his finger under the seal and tore across the envelope. “What’s that?” “Dunno.” Kaiser pulled a paper from inside and carefully unfolded it. Immediately, his eyes went to work, moving back and forth across the paper. As he reached the last of the print, he felt the life drain from inside. “So much for shit getting’ better.” “Why?” Dave leaned forward and put the TV on ‘mute’. “What is that?” “A letter.” “Little more help?” “A civil lawsuit… filed by the family of Anthony Barker.” “Shit.” “Yeah.” Kaiser looked down at the letter again. The ink as black as the dried blood that had adorned his title belt. After the criminal trial, he’d been expecting it, but in the months that followed, and in the shadow of other personal miseries and professional triumphs… the thought had lost itself in his mind. With a heaviness in his hands, Kaiser folded up the letter. And amidst the silence of Dave’s shock and the muted television, he sighed.
