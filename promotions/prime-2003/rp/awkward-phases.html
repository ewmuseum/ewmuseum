Awkward Phases

Date: Nov. 1997/Sep. 2009 November 29, 1997 Miami, Florida A football locker room is far from the best place for sappy, emotional moments. A locker room at Miami's aging Orange Bowl, where just across the bowels of the building the Hurricanes had carved out a two decade-long reputation as college football's baddest brood, even less so. So, as Troy Douglas leaned against the phone in the corner of the locker room, still soaked in post-game sweat, he was eternally grateful nobody was paying attention to him. Frankly, he wasn't surprised. The Syracuse Orangemen had a lot more to pay attention to this late November night than the romantic travails of their starting right defensive end. Not 20 minutes before, they'd walked off the field triumphant as Big East champions courtesy of their first win over the 'Canes in nearly 20 years. The bustle hadn't died down one bit. In some corners of the locker room, the celebration was still raging, the random shouting outbursts and constant loop of "We Are the Champions" were all the evidence needed for that. The media was there as well, reporters with notebooks, microphones and TV cameras in clusters around the day's stars: Donovan McNabb, Tebucky Jones, Kyle McIntosh, Dee Brown and Donovin Darius. Waiting nervously as the phone continued to ring, Troy was almost glad he'd only had two tackles and a half of a sack. Today, he didn't need the attention, and he didn't need to wait another second to make this particular phone call. The post-game phone call to Lauren had been routine for Troy at every game she'd been unable to attend since approximately seventh grade, when they were still nothing more than neighbors and best friends. Never, however, had Troy been hanging on tenterhooks as the phone rang more than this one. Once. They'd partly badly. It had happened before, but not often, and inevitably, it was over something minute and stupid. This time, it was Thanksgiving dinner. The argument had come out of nowhere. They'd known for months, of course, that Troy was going to be in South Florida over the holiday, and a mid-week trip to Greensboro for turkey, mashed potatoes and pumpkin pie was certainly out of the picture. Troy just wasn't quite prepared for home much trouble asking Lauren to put a one year halt to the Bennett family Thanksgiving ballyhoo would be. Her brother was coming into town from Memphis with his wife and newborn twins. Her 96-year-old great aunt Eleanor was being dragged out of mothballs yet again for her annual admonishments at how loud the music was these days. Even Grandma and Grandpa Bennett were flying in for the feast from their retirement roost next to the golf course in Scottsdale. In hindsight, maybe Troy shouldn't have suggested she ditch that for a three-day weekend on South Beach. Twice. He couldn't bring himself past the last time they spoke. He was getting ready to peel out the door and drive to the practice facility to hop onto the team bus to Hancock International Airport, and the yelling still hadn't stopped. "You know, I'm sorry I offended your family's delicate goddamn sensibilities, alright!" he shouted. "Pardon me if I don't want you to have to go through hearing great aunt Eleanor drone on about how things were so much better when she was growing up next door to Stonewall Fucking Jackson!" "She's NINETY SIX, Troy!" "So she claims." It just got worse from there. Their words were crashing off each other like pebbles tossed at glass windows. Sooner or later, one of them was going to crack. She did. "Just ... just get the hell out of here. Now." And for the first time in Troy Douglas' life, without a second glance, he slammed the door on his Ford Explorer and drove away. He hadn't let that moment go since. It was probably why, when he got the chance, he slammed into Miami quarterback Ryan Clement extra hard. It was why it was killing him that she hadn't picked up the phone yet. Three times. Damn. It only took a moment before he heard the telltale click that sent his call to their answering machine and her lilting, giggling voice caught his ear. "Hey, this is Lauren (and Troy, inserted his pre-recorded voice). We're not here right now, so leave a message at the beep." Beep! "Hey ..." He trailed off. He'd spent the entire weekend going over this conversation -- even between snaps -- and now that she wasn't on the other end, he had to change his playbook. "Uhh, it's me, obviously. I know you're next to the answering machine, and you really don't want to talk to me right now. Honestly, I don't blame you. I'm ... I'm really sorry about the way I left. We might not agree, but I never want to leave you like that, and I think that's what--" "Troy?" The rush he got when he heard Lauren's real, live voice in his ear was better for him than a million trips to the Fiesta Bowl. "Uh, hey. We ... we won." ***** September 16, 2009 Auburn Hills, Michigan Locker room phone calls still weren't his cup of tea. It was a different girl, a different year, a different sport and a different locker room, but still, standing in this corner in the bowels of the Palace at Auburn Hills as PRIMEates like Jay Phoenix, Kaiser Vashaun and others still milled about even though the FX broadcast went off the air two hours earlier, Troy still felt awkward leaning against the wall as he waited for Samantha Wade to pick up. Once. He hated this nervous waiting. Every time the phone rang, his heart jumped, every time the air went dead without a connection on the other end, the pickax in his stomach carved out another little pit. 33 years old, and still nervous about calling his girlfriend on the phone. Twice. Huh. At that moment, he realized it was the first time he'd used the word "girlfriend" connected to Sam. It wasn't a jarring realization, hell, he'd already used the dreaded "L" word, but it was a strange little realization he picked up on, like a loose towel left on the kitchen floor. He had a girlfriend. Again, huh. Three times. She didn't pick up, and he heard her familiar voice mail message begin a moment after. It didn't really matter to him that she didn't pick up. It was rapidly scraping up against 1 a.m. on the east coast, after all. There was time enough for this conversation tomorrow. Or the next day, or the next. --To be continued--
