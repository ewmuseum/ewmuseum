Two Steps Forward, A Mile Back

20 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/20/07 COMPLACENCY As Told by Ian Nackedy It probably only really shook us because times were bad. I mean, think about it. We were on a terrible losing streak. Blaine Blair had told us that if we failed to produce and make people laugh (because for some reason the average PRIME fan is made out of stone when we perform) then we would have to find jobs in GLOBAL. And I don’t want to take the HGH necessary to fill the “head to body size” ratio needed to be a member there. In addition, our girlfriend Nate was tired of us locking her in the supporting character crate when she wasn’t being used, and she dumped us. For reals this time. So there were a lot of problems, and when you’re that low on the ladder, a message about your father being missing and your bank accounts being pilfered is sure to hit you pretty hard. I felt like a real person then, worrying about my dad’s safety and spouting out buzzwords like “security pin” and “due compensation” pretty effortlessly. And then we won the PRIME Tag Team Titles. I could figuratively feel the euphoria wash over me. We started celebrating, buying champagne, throwing parties (our “We Are The Champions Thursday” Party was kind of lame, but the “We Are The Champions Tuesday” and “We Are The Champions Sunday” Parties more than made up for it), and we were even able to fix that whole bank account thing. Not to brag, but the incentive contracts in PRIME are rather sweet. I’ve finally got that gold-plated bath tub I’ve been dreaming about. Somewhere along the way, though, my dad slipped through the cracks. He’s still missing. We still have no idea what to do about it. That part hasn’t changed. What has changed is that me and Gil are too preoccupied with our tag team titles, with the jerks who insult us because we own said tag team titles, with the guy who likes to drink backwashed champagne – the list goes on and on. It’s been too hard to keep up with trifles such as your father’s safety. Complacency is one of the easiest things to slip into, and now that I’m in, I’m not quite ready to get out of it. Pops can handle himself, I’m sure. KING As Told by Pops Nackedy My world was black for what seemed like forever. I had nothing. I was alone. Afraid. Worthless. The world had forgotten me, and I gave it no reason to remember. Every day was darkness. And then I got word that my sons had broken into some fame. Everything immediately became light again. I had always had this vision of having power, being able to make grand decisions and see them carried out and how they would affect my immediate world. I figured they would be happy to see their father that they thought had died, and I was able to take advantage of it, using them to get me notoriety. For a long time it worked, too, and the power got to my head. I felt like I was a king: giving orders, seeing results, reaping the benefits. I operated from a small shack outside of Cleveland, but to me it was my kingdom. My sons were loyal and worked hard to please me, and I received respect from everyone around me. And then my princes realized their gold was missing. To drop another stupid and drug-induced analogy, it started to crumble after that. I was no longer a king, I no longer had power. I had nothing, and I was forced into house arrest. A dungeon, if you will. Okay, now I’ll drop the analogy. Sorry. I don’t blame anyone for what happened. It’s to the point where I don’t have the capacity to point the finger, or refuse blame, or even do anything about the situation. I only have the capacity to feel the pain. The pain of being a king at one time and losing it, of going from the bottom of the world straight to the top of the mountain, and back down again before you can get a whiff of the air. But I have a plan. My plan is to keep running, hope that my legs bring me somewhere familiar, and that they take me there faster than the past I’m trying to outrun. Not much of a plan, I know, but all I can do now is shrug and hope for the best. ASPIRATIONS As Told by The Spectator “You should totally try out the Basketweaver. I’ll give you the rights to it for one night only.” Gildenstern looked at his foster brother earnestly, waiting for gratitude. But none came. “Has that move ever worked?” Ian asked, laughing. In answer, Gildenstern sprung from the sofa in their new home and launched himself onto Ian’s back, grabbing his ears and twisting them back and forth. “BASKETWEAVER! BASKETWEAVER! BAKSETWEAVER!” “All right, all right, I tap, just get the fuck off,” grumbled one half of the Tag Team Champions. “And you expect to beat Digital Mortality? Please.” Gildenstern smiled as he moved into the kitchen to get an energy drink after that strong exhibition of manliness. “Whether he swallowed your spit or not, he is kind of a big deal. You might want to train harder than usual.” “Train harder than usual? Why surely you jest!” shouted Ian happily. He sat down on the couch and continued to object. “I’m a tag champion. I am fast and awesome. And I am one of the best wrestlers on the fly you’ve ever seen. I’m really good at dealing with the unexpected.” Suddenly Ian and Gildenstern jumped at a loud noise, as the pantry door busted open off of its hinges. Five Year Old Integer Chris, Sound and Fury’s randomness mascot, ran out, waving his hands in the air. “Where the ho’s at, playa? I’ve been locked in the crate for awhile, PAPA NEEDS TO GET FREAKY! WOOOOO WOOOOO!” Ian shot a sharp look at Gildenstern. “Did you show him that whistle tips video again?” Gildenstern shrugged innocently. “Nah, he’s been in the crate for…” “IT’S LIKE AN ALARM CLOCK! WOOOOOOO WOOOOOOO!” shouted Five Year Old Integer Chris. “Maybe just once,” admitted Gil. “Get him back in the crate, Freddie!” Ian shouted, and immediately after an American Eskimo dog ran through the hallway and began growling at the supporting character until he moved back into the pantry. Gildenstern threw a raw steak into the now-exposed pantry door. “That ought to keep him occupied for a bit.” With that randomness out of the way, Gildenstern walked up to Ian. “All I’m saying is that we’ve had a rough few weeks, and it’s just starting to get better. There’s no reason to just coast. You can go even further. You could actually win this thing.” “You think so?” Ian smiled at the thought. “It would be pretty cool.” After a few moments of reflection, Ian stood with a big smile on his face. “You’re right, Gildenstern! I shouldn’t just let this opportunity slip. I’m going to start training, I’m going to focus more, and nothing is going to get in my way!” “Guys?” interrupts Magen Nackedy, Ian’s sister-in-law. Ian and Gil look up at her, almost ashamed. It’s been a few weeks, but they still felt like strangers in her house. “Yeah, Mags?” “There’s…uh…been a disturbance,” Magen meekly replied. Ian looked at Gil uneasily. “Five Year Old Integer Chris? We’re sorry about him, but he really won’t leave us alone. Did he teach Connor how to pick up chicks or something?” Suddenly, a shout rang out from the pantry: “TRIED TO! HE PICKS LEGOS OVER LADIES! WHAT A FAG!” Scowling slightly, Magen continued. “Uh, no…it’s not that…” and Magen decided at that time it was better for actions to speak rather than words. She stepped aside as a skinny, frail older man looked up sheepishly, his breath short. “Hey, sons,” Pops Nackedy weakly said. BACK TO REALITY As Told by Ian Nackedy There’s got to be some sort of deity laughing his ass off at my life. Like, there’s probably some angel tugging on this god’s robe, saying “Uh, Lord? I know you love fucking with that guy, but there’s a war, and a cyclone just killed a bunch of people…” but the god is all like, “But he’s about to give that dawn of realization look, it’s like crack to me!” Yes, the deity watching over me is a drug addict. It would explain this, wouldn’t it? You don’t understand. I want to win. I need to win. I feel like this is my proof that I’m not just some silly, gimmicky wrestler who spits champagne and makes tasteless jokes. I’m a silly, gimmicky wrestler who makes tasteless jokes but then actually delivers when the time is right. I joke around, and I pretend like I don’t want to win, but I really do. Ever since I got here, it’s been eating me alive that me and Gil were getting fed to these teams of singles guys who are so talented, and we never really stood a chance. But I know I’m talented, and even though you could say my life is a walking distraction, I really am focused when it comes to the big stuff. Like this. And my dad had to come and ruin all of it. I know it sounds like I’m selfish, and maybe I am a little bit. But this tournament is my chance to show the world. Just like my brother showed everyone in the Chris Williams Memorial Cup. He took down a bunch of big talent until he lost to Digital Mortality. But I’m not going to lose. I have it in my mind to go through this, to win the crown, to be the king of this place, and I’m not about to let my prodigal father ruin any of it. This is my time. No one else’s.
