Buried Where it Belongs (for Dual Halo)

Date: Today Today…Now "Just lay there and shut up." He rocketed his arms forward, ramming the head of the shovel into the soft dirt, then tossed the powdered earth into the hole. "Just shut up." Another shovel full of dirt. Then another… the firsts of what would be many. And from down in the pit, there was no reply but the smoky escape of warm breaths into the cold Ohio air. * * * Yesterday Rarely, did he ever see such a side of PRIME. His time was spent amidst the violence, surrounded by thirty thousand awe-struck eyes. And, when he wasn’t working in the center of the ring, he was relegated to the often too small and strange smelling locker rooms of the countless arenas they graced as the show hit the road every week. Now, at the PRIME headquarters, listening to the mess of ringing phones and chatting voices echoing from various cubicles… and in the case of ‘higher ups’… elegant offices, he was slightly overwhelmed at just how much actual business went into making a wrestling company run smoothly. Any number of idiots could throw together a makeshift ring and beat the hell out of each other, but to make a successful, profitable product wasn’t so simple. These were the men who had to take care of all the dirty work. The marketing, the scheduling, insurance, the hiring, the firing, the retirements… … Retirement. His reason for stepping out of his comfort zone for the afternoon. The truth was, he’d come to his decision as soon as his senses had rejoined him following the assault on ReVolution 115...whenever that had been. His years in the business had led to a heavy toll in physical damage. This last unfortunate event had been terrible, though not necessarily the worst of his career. It wasn’t that the passion was gone, battered and bruised but not gone. It wasn’t the potential for lingering nasty effects, nor was it the thought of his wife and child having to witness the sheer brutality of the assault. Instead, it was a concoction of all those things coupled with perfectly terrible timing. At over thirty years of age, in a world dominated by twenty-somethings… he knew it was time. He’d just waited several weeks to force himself into the offices of PRIME to make the official announcement. "And we can’t change you’re mind? You’re sure retirement is the way you want to go?" Blaine Blair held out his hands, shrugging his shoulders a bit as the inquisitive nature of his question forced his eyebrows to arch. "Seems like the best option. The doctors seem to agree, and I know it’s what Mary will want. She’s been waiting for this day ever since my last retirement." "Well, I can’t say I disagree with your choice. I know PRIME’s gonna miss you… but it sounds like you’ve really thought this through and are making the decision that’s best for you and your family. " Blair pushed himself away from the desk and handed Matt some papers, then extended his hand for a firm shake. "You’ll need to sign a few things… and can I assume we’ll see you back in here again?" "Absolutely." Matt took the documents and the offer, shaking Blaine’s hand. "I’ll be back in here to talk to Chet personally. I just wanted to stop and give my official notice. I know the ‘retirement’ thing had been floating around ever since I was injured at 115, but I wanted to end the rumors and let you guys know the truth straight from the source." "We appreciate all you’ve done for us, Matt. Take care of yourself." "I’ll be trying." Each man exchanged a smile of the quick and polite variety. Matt turned stepped out of the sanctuary of the small office and into the noise of the bustling cubicles. He let out a long sigh, and blinked a couple times to let his eyes adjust to retirement, and made his way through the maze of cheap wall units and bad neck ties. Suddenly, he stopped, remembering one last piece of business he needed to clear up. For a moment, he debated whether to head back across the floor or just to say ‘the hell with it’ and head home. While several suits stared at him, peering curiously over and around their cubicles at the statue posed in the middle of their room, Matt finally decided that if he didn’t take care of everything now, he’d have to stay that much longer when he returned to talk to Chet personally. Doubling back, he didn’t bother to enter Blair’s office, instead, he just stopped and knocked on the doorframe to gather his attention. "Hey Blaine…" "Yeah? What can I do for ya?" "Did you get the fax about some possible future appearances. My agent and I whipped up a few ideas, just promotional stuff for the company… try to keep some ties to PRIME. Marc said he would send it over last night or early this morning and I just wanted to make sure you got it." "Hold on a sec." Blair started sifting through a mess of papers on his desk, finally locating a specific item that he grabbed by the corner and lifted from the pile. "Did get a fax late last night. Its got a tentative lay out here for some potential autograph signings, radio shows, etc. This what you’re talking about?" Blair handed it over. "No," Matt glanced over the sheet, "this says here that I sent the fax. That’s not right… didn’t Marc send one?" "That’s all we got." "Huh…" He said nothing more, though his face contorted in a manner that must have struck Blaine as odd. "Something wrong, Matt?" "No." He handed the sheet back to Blair. "No. I gotta go." With a quick wave of the hand, Matt turned and headed back across the office, his feet moving almost as fast as his mind, and though he had given back the fax, he wasn’t leaving it behind. * * * Today… Earlier Slush spinning under tires, client and agent barreled down the winter roads. Matt had called Marc an hour earlier, asking him if he’d be willing to go for a drive to talk some business. Marc had gladly accepted, hoping he’d be able to talk Matt into making a return at Ultra Violence. Matt knew Marc was aware that the doctors had cleared him for a possible return, and the sooner he could get him back in the ring, the more money Marc stood to make. But as it turned out, Matt’s idea of ‘talking business’ was turning out to be quite different from what he knew his agent had been hoping for. "I’m sick of you, Marc. I’m done with your bullshit!" As the words grew louder, his right foot fell harder into the accelerator, and by the time he finished the sentence, he was many miles per hour away from the law abiding citizen he had been just a minute before. "Jesus Christ! Slow down!" In the passenger seat, Marc braced himself in place, the knuckles of his hands turning white from the pressure of his hands plastered against the center console and dashboard. "You’re driving like you’ve got one day to live!" "Relax, I’m not looking to run myself off the road." Slowing down, Matt followed the winding curves of the road that rolled along the hills. With practically no traffic, it was looking like a short trip to their destination. "You had me fooled." The agent shook his head and released his grip as Matt eased off the gas. "And what the hell do you mean you’re done with my bullshit? What bullshit?" "You know exactly what I’m talking about Marc. I’m talking about the lies and the half truths and you trying to manipulate me into situations I’d really rather not be in." "Hey pal," Marc shot his client a glaring look and practically spit as he responded, "I didn’t manipulate you into anything! You’re all grown up… you make your own decisions. Stop blaming me every time you get your ass kicked. Which… by the way… let me remind you who’s idea it was to call out The Cadillac of Wrestling and bring Deville out of retirement. That was all you, buddy!" "It was. But who’s idea was it for me to lead the war against FU? Who decided I should give Nova a rematch at King of Kings? And who came up with the whole contractual clause that supposedly forced me to have to enter GTT?" "Supposedly? I…" "Give it up, Marc." In the process of this whole retirement thing, I got to look over my contract. There wasn’t a word in there about competing in GTT." Matt turned on his right blinker and rolled past an open gate into s small wooded area near a half-frozen pond. "You’ve been doing this for months now. You’ve been forcing your way into my head, playing mind games, talking me into all kinds of stupid wrestling bullshit that you know it’s killing my family! I was trying to work out a reduced schedule, be as careful as possible since Mary and I had patched things up, and like some sick son of a bitch dangling heroin in front of a junkie, you’ve been me pushing deeper and deeper into a mess!" "Matt, I…" "I’m not finished, Marc." The road under the tires faded from blacktop to dirt and as it did, Matt slowed to a stop, parking the car in the middle of the path. "All those things you’ve done… that’s the bullshit I’m done with. You’ve pushed your way into my head like some sort of maggot and you’ve just been feeding there ever since! Like a fucking maggot!" "Hey..." "Well, it’s done now. I’m done. You’re done." Turning off the ignition, Matt undid his seatbelt and swung open his door. "Get out of the car." "Matt…" It was pointless, he had already slammed the door shut and was sifting through the trunk. Marc let out a long sigh, and blinked a couple times to take in the surroundings, then exited the vehicle. "Where the hell are we?" "Kind of a personal place." Matt closed the trunk and stepped around the car, carrying two shovels in his right hand. "Follow me." "Fine." A bit reluctantly, Marc followed his client, staying a couple steps behind as the two trudged through the snow, heading towards a tree near the top of the incline. "I mean it, Marc." Matt turned and looked back over his shoulder to make sure he was still following. "I’m done with you." Coming to the top of the hill, Matt stopped and looked down at a spot in the ground. "Used to come to this spot a lot as a kid. Dad still claims to this day that he wants to be buried right here under this tree overlooking the pond when he dies. Don’t think Mom finds it very practical. Still… she likes to watch him smile when he talks about it. It’s nice to have your wife’s support." "Ok. Is this relevant to anything, maybe?" "Ya know, I’ve figured out your secret, Marc." It was like he hadn’t even heard the question. Or didn’t care…" "Secret? Matt, what are we doing here?" "Take this." Matt handed one of the shovels to Marc. "Start digging." * * * Yesterday Matthew kicked the toe of his shoes against the doorframe, knocking loose the four-day-old gray snow. In the middle of his living room, Georgie sat with a toy in hand and several others scattered around her. He noticed that as soon as he’d pushed the front door open, her eyes had lit up. She was getting used to her father being home. "How’s daddy’s angel?" Matthew tossed his jacket and the documents from PRIME onto the couch, and headed right for his daughter, swooping her up in his arms and raising her high above his six foot two frame. Her laughter floated around above his head and only grew louder as Matthew rubbed his beard against her tummy. "Sounds like you two are having fun." Mary stepped into the room from the kitchen, wiping her hands dry with a towel. "How could I possibly not have fun with this little ball of energy?" He kissed Georgie and pulled her into his chest, holding her in his arms. "Has she had a nap?" "Not yet." As Mary answered, she leaned in and kissed her husband, as she threw the towel across her shoulder. "Although she’s probably about due for one." "Me too." "Baby," Peering around Matthew, she locked her eyes on the mess that covered her sofa. "what’s all the stuff on the couch?" "Paperwork" he turned to follow her stare, even though he knew exactly what she was talking about, "for my retirement from PRIME." Immediately she snapped her eyes back towards him, looking intently at his face to make sure he wasn’t joking. His tone had been almost nonchalant, and she wasn’t sure whether to take that as a sign he was serious or not. "Matthew… you’re retiring?" "Yeah. I know its what you want. Besides, I’m so old and beat up and when I hit the road these days, I’m not just leaving you, I’m leaving this little princess." "This kinda sudden, isn’t it?" "I dunno. You get your butt kicked the way I did a few weeks ago and sitting at home doin’ a whole lotta nothing starts to sound like heaven." He handed Georgie to her mother and headed over to the couch, grabbing up the documents. Shuffling them in order, he smiled at Mary and held them up as if to showcase their wonder. "All I’ve got to do is sign a few dotted lines, and I’m officially retired. Again." "No." "What?" He let his hand fall to his side, the papers rustling against his jeans. "No? Why no?" "This isn’t what you want." "Yes it is." "No it’s not." She sat Georgie back in the mass of toys that blanketed the carpet, then turned her attention solely to Matthew. "Sweetie, I’ve known you since junior high, and I know when you really want something. You don’t really want this. You want to do what you think I want… but I know that if you retire today, it’ll eat at you every moment of the rest of your life." "So you’re saying you don’t want me to retire?" "I’m saying I want you to retire when you are ready to retire. I hope that’s soon and I hope that you keep yourself as safe as possible until then… but this isn’t right, and you know it." Mary took a step towards him and wrapped her arms around his waist. "I don’t want you to have to wake up miserable every morning from here on out, regretting how you just walked away. Can you honestly say you would be ok knowing the final chapter of your career was being bludgeoned half to death by a pack of wolves as ReVolution went off the air?" "Probably not." "And I don’t want to spend the rest of my life with a grumpy bastard always mumbling about what could have been." She grabbed the papers from his hand and threw them behind her to anywhere out of sight. "So forget all these. If you honestly want to bring your career to an end… I’d love to have my husband safe at home with me… but bring things to an end on your own terms." "You’re sure you’re ok with this?" Now, he wrapped his arms around her, holding her tight, as if he had to squeeze the answer out of her. "Yes. Baby, I cringe every time I see you get hit in that ring… but I don’t want you to just give up on everything because of me. That’s about the last thing in the world I want to be to you. I want you to retire when you’re ready, not when I’m ready for you. That way… I’ll know it’s for good this time." She winked, then kissed him on the shoulder. "This wasn’t exactly the reaction I’d been expecting." "Life is full of surprises." "Guess it is." For the next minute or so, neither said a word, they just stood in the middle of the living room, surrounded by the sound of rattling plastic keys and the occasional giggle. Then, he made his decision. "Sounds like I better start booking a trip to China." * * * Today…Now "Get in." "That’s… that’s murder, Matt." Marc was wrong, it wasn’t murder… or anything like it; though, not because Matt wasn’t going to bury Marc alive. That was exactly what he was going to do. "No, it’s not." He tugged the shovel from the dirt. "You’re not real, Marc. Now, get in." He pointed to the six foot hole in the earth with the end of his shovel, the air around him not quite as cold as the look that emitted from his eyes. "What the fuck are you talking about?!" "You heard me. I said you’re not real. You’re a figment of my imagination. I told you… I figured out your secret." "You’re spewing a bunch of crazy garbage, Matt!" Marc threw his shovel and shook his head, dismissing the entire notion. "What a load of garbage!" "No, It’s not." Matt dropped threw down his shovel as well, but didn’t dismiss the subject. "You’re just another side of me… some sort of representation my mind conjured up. We’re the two sides of the same coin… torn between family and business. I wanted so much to be a good husband and a good father, but there was this part of me that couldn’t let the wrestling go. That’s where you came in." "So I’m just the part of you obsessed with the rush of the ring?!" "You’re the addiction. You were always there to push me into all kinds of chaos. Taking on FU, giving Nova a rematch, competing in tournaments… just the dark side of my brain, festering into a weasel. You were an excuse, a way for me to keep getting my fix without having to be the bad guy. I could blame it all on you… ‘Marc says its in my contract’, ‘Marc says its breech of contract’… well, fuck you Marc. I don’t need the lie anymore. I thought I did, but now, I realized I can take my career in the direction I want and I can do it with my wife’s support. I could have all along if I wouldn’t have had my head in some screwed up place. I don’t have to be a wrestler or a husband… I can be both. Which means, I’m done with you." "Where the HELL is all this coming from." "Gimme a break, Marc! Don’t play fucking stupid with me! It all makes sense. The reason why PRIME reps couldn’t contact you following my injury, the reason Mary looked at me like I was crazy whenever I mentioned your name, the reason the fax you were supposed to send came from my house. It’s all because you only exist in my head. And you’re about to not exist anywhere." Matt took one large step forward and grabbed Marc by the shirt collar, pushing him towards the edge of the grave. "Now, get in." "No way! No way in h…" Marc’s defiance was cut off as Matt hoisted him off the ground and held him over the edge of the pit. "Listen to me, Marc!" "Jesus…Alright! Alright! Just let go… I’ll climb in, you sick bastard." Marc tugged free of Matt’s grip and brushed out the wrinkles in his coat sleeve, then took a step towards the hole. "You’re crazy pal. You and this split-personality, schizo, Fight Club bullshit." "Actually, I’d say I’ve got my head on as straight as its been in a long time." Matt pushed his phantom agent in the back and the dirt gave way under Marc’s feet causing him to slide into the grave he’d just helped dig. "Lay down, I can’t bury you standing up like that." "And you really think it’s all this simple?" Marc dropped to a seated position and then laid down on his back, looking up at the mind he was born from. "you really think you can just bury me like this and be done with it?!" "Yeah, I do." He reached down and picked up his shovel. "Really?!" Marc scoffed as he sunk a bit into the soil. "Just lay there and shut up." He rocketed his arms forward, ramming the head of the shovel into the soft dirt, then tossed the powdered earth into the hole. "Just shut up." Another shovel full of dirt. Then another… the firsts of what would be many. And from down in the pit, there was no reply but the smoky escape of warm breaths into the cold Ohio air. For several minutes, he just continued to fling pile after pile of dirt until he had finally filled the hole entirely, leaving a fresh patch of soil in the middle of a plain of snow. He didn’t know how long it had taken him to fill the grave… there was nobody around to time him, and he didn’t believe in watches. All he knew was that the sun was starting to sink behind the trees a bit, low enough to let him know he was going to be late for dinner. Matt dropped the shovel and turned to walk away. Behind him, the snow around the grave began to melt, the dirt turned to mud, and six feet beneath, the maggots began to feast on his mind.
