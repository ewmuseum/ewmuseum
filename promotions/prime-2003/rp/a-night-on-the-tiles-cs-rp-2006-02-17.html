A night on the tiles (CS RP)

17 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17/02/06 (submission date) NB - This is a *totally* different universe to the one most RPs were based in, certainly to the real Ignatius Lisieux one. Ideally it'd have been done sooner, run by Rich and changed accordingly. But here it is in it's full written-in-36-hours glory anyway! Another less-than-thrilling evening awaited Ignatius Lisieux as he walked out the gym, muscles tired and hair still dripping from the hot shower he’d just taken to try and ease his aching limbs. He stretched his shoulders back as he stepped out onto the street, stifling the yawn that was so eager to escape his tired body. It was two days till he had to grab a flight to Japan, and he wanted as much training in before he lost a full day and a half to travelling. Slipping back into old habits, he slung his gym bag off his shoulder and reached into it to check for any missed calls or messages on his cell phone; wishful thinking at best – about four people knew his number now, and he couldn’t remember the last time he’d actually received a call.Sure enough, he looked at the LCD display and read ‘no miss- ‘17 missed calls’ He was so close to just throwing the phone back in his bag when he clicked with what he’d just read. He blinked twice, hoping to high heaven that this wasn’t another crazy hallucination, praying that devil-Hoyt and angel-Incredible weren’t trying to call him to further guide him in his ways. Before he could check what numbers had called the phone began ringing again. He just stared at it, still thoroughly perplexed. ‘Number withheld’ It rang four times before he clicked the ‘Answer’ key, raised the handset to his ear and tentatively ventured a greeting; Ignatius: Hello? His face screws up in puzzlement, the surprise at actually getting a phone call obviously not matched by the surprise at who the caller is. Ignatius: Is that... Jeremy?! He steps away from blocking the gym door to let a short, red-haired guy walk in, listening intently to his phone the whole time. Ignatius: Yeah, I’m in New Jersey – why... He trails off, still looking totally flabberghasted. As he listens he looks up and round him, searching for something. He walks across to the nearest bus-stop, the one on the opposite side of the road – not the one he usually takes back to his cheap motel accommodation from here. He nods to himself and mumbles a few ‘mmhmm’s down the line as he crosses the quiet road. He nods and agrees a bit more as he starts to read the timetable on the bus-stop. Ignatius: Well, the next bus I could get out that way should be here in ten, and I’m guessing it’ll take about half an hour, so you just sit tight and stay calm, alright?... Alright, Uncle Iggy’ll take care of everything once he gets there... Sure. Take care of your little brother as well, make sure your jackets are buttoned up, it’s cold out... Right, see you then. One uneasy bus ride later and Ignatius finds himself in a familiar situation – standing over the sprawled body of a drunken Scot named Adam. Standing at the bus-stop next to a dime-a-dozen local bar Ignatius looks down at the mess before him; the man who pulled him (kicking & screaming) through his toughest times in PRIME, lying in a pool of his own vomit, with his two kids standing, shivering, next to him. Ignatius: How long since they threw your dad out? He bends down on one knee to be head height with the older of the two, Jeremy, who’s still clutching the cell phone he removed from his father’s pocket. Jeremy sniffs, prompting the offer of a hanky from Ignatius, before he responds. Jeremy: I dunno, about an hour maybe? Ignatius sighs, ruffles the boy’s hair as he blows his nose and stands up. He looks at the barely conscious figure of his old compadré, the guy he basically shared a life with for months but hasn’t heard from in over a year, and can’t do anything but sigh again. Attempting to remain chipper he looks down to Jeremy and the other boy. Ignatius: How does ice-cream and cable TV sound? The boys’ faces light up instantly, they answer with cheers and grins all round. An hour later, having slapped Adam sober enough to walk (sort of) and loaded everyone onto a bus back to his motel, Ignatius pops the boys in front the TV in his room with an ice-cream cone each in hand. Leaving them dripping milky, sugary goodness all over his cheap bedsheets he carries Adam through to the bathroom and throws him, fully-clothed, in the shower – turning it on full-blast cold. While Adam screams in pain, shock and disorientation Ignatius flips out his cell phone and makes a few calls. Another hour later and a happy teenager is making $20 an hour for watching cartoons with a 7-year-old and a 5-year-old in a crappy motel room, Ignatius has Adam dressed in some of the baggiest clothes he owns to try and make up for the size difference between them. It almost works. They’re sitting in the coffee shop a few minutes’ walk from the motel, drinking cheap cups of hot, nondescript brown liquid. Adam looks like he’s been drinking for hours and hasn’t slept in days – which is actually quite accurate. He looks disdainfully down at the T-shirt he’s wearing. Adam: I don’t even like... He attempts to read upside down and fails miserably. Adam: ...this band. Ignatius rolls his eyes and takes another sip of his ‘coffee’, wondering why the hell he’s subjecting himself to this when he really should be getting an early night. Ignatius: Just shut up about that and tell me why you’re in New Jersey. Adam dodges the question, hardly skilfully, but he still does it; Adam: This coffee’s shite. Ignatius: I know. But why are you in New Jersey? He watches in awe as Adam seems to fall asleep with his eyes open, totter on his seat and almost keel over onto the table before he manages to catch himself and awake, if only slightly. It seems to take a second or two before Adam manages to register anything around him, at which point he takes a quick look about and panics. Adam: Shit! Where the fuck are my kids?! He stands up, smacking the table and nearly spilling the two steaming cups into Ignatius’ lap, the owner looks up suspiciously from behind the counter as Ignatius places a hand on Adam’s shoulder and coaxes him down into his seat again. Ignatius: It’s okay, man – they’re in my motel room, I hired Katherine to take care of them. Adam looks at him blankly for a second then questions; Adam: Take care of them? You talking hooker or hitman? Ignatius’ jaw drops, at which point Adam laughs and winks. Adam: You didn’t seriously think I was still this drunk, did you? Was only ’cos it was all hitting a near-empty stomach. That shower got the last of it outta me, I’m just a little tired now. Ignatius can do little but shake his head in disbelief as Adam straightens his back up, pulls the baseball cap he’s got on forward a bit and draws a smile from the blank canvas he’d been letting his face sink into; it was definitely the same guy he knew so well so long ago. Adam: So yeah, I’m back. He takes a sip of his drink then clarifies. Adam: In PRIME, I mean. Back in PRIME. I called by head offices at the start of the year; decided I might as well get back in the game, I mean – I’ve kept myself fit enough when I haven’t been travelling for 3 days straight trying to keep two kids entertained. He downs the rest of his cup of Joe and proceeds to fill Ignatius in on the past 18 months of his life since he lost to Black Angel for the last time and walked from PRIME, apparently for good. After some negotiation with the girl’s parents, and her testimony to them that she was carrying her mace spray, an extra $10 an hour and a $50 bonus over-nighter fee secured Katherine’s services till the next morning. This left Adam (pepped from a fair few more cups of slightly better coffee & some pizza now) free to explain his cunning plan (more cunning than a fox on it’s most cunning day) to Ignatius. Ignatius: I can’t believe you’re coming to Japan. Seriously. What’re you going to do with the boys? Adam: I’m dropping them off at their mother’s – she’s just set up in New York, they’re gonna live with her now. I just had them while she handled her rehab and getting a new house with that Jamaican guy. Apparently Paul & I were good enough parents to handle them for a few months, but nothing more than temporary. This country’s fucked. Ignatius nods sympathetically as they sit down to some drinks in the nearest chain bar – solely on the condition that Adam sticks to soft drinks. Adam: I figured my best performance was at the Halo the first time, so why shouldn’t I make my return at it? Keep it hush-hush though, I’m staying low-profile there until I actually enter. Ignatius nods and takes a drink of the beer in front of him. Ignatius: Not trying to be a dick here, but what’s the point in coming back? You don’t have any beef with anyone still about and, I don’t mean this nastily, you probably won’t be remembered by many, especially not in Japan. You expecting a big reception? Adam: Not at all – I was hardly the main draw, even at the best of times, was I? He raises an eyebrow questioningly; Ignatius concedes the point with a thoughtful nod. Adam: ‘The Eternal Underdog’ they used to call me, wasn’t it? He chuckles and downs his pint of orange juice in one – three days of vending machine food on the road had left him needing a bit of a vitamin replenishment. Adam: Funny thing is, I’m probably walking into this match more of an underdog than ever. But I have a plan... With the assistance of a pen, the back of a beer mat and some explaining Adam describes his plan to Ignatius. Ignatius is so impressed he just looks Adam in the eye and tells him, straight-up, that it’s the single greatest idea he’s ever heard. Ignatius: Straight-up, that’s the single stupidest idea I have ever heard. Well... sort of. Adam: Oh, come on – it’s simple but it’s beautiful. Ignatius: It’s neither simple nor beautiful; plus the ‘random’ generator for entry is probably already run & the places set. There’s no way you could sneak in to wherever it’s held – d’you even know? – and change the order so you’re last. Adam attempts to interrupt but is cut off by Ignatius steamrollering on. Ignatius: Even if you do think you deserve the last entry spot, it doesn’t mean you’re going to get it – doesn’t matter if you came out first in the first one, things don’t work like that. This warrants a chuckle from the gruff Scot. Adam: Believe it or not I do have a slightly better plan, but I’m working on a few of the finer details in my head before I tell you it. Ignatius: I need to pee, see if you can sort those ‘minor details’ when I’m away. As soon as Ignatius is through the toilet door Adam springs to the bar, pushing past a couple ordering a few glasses of wine and physically grabs a barmaid – shoving a 50 dollar bill into her hand. Adam: Make this the quickest round you’ve ever taken – one pint of orange juice and a pint glass of half vodka, half Guinness. Now. Thirty seconds later and a pint of Guinness that most Irishmen would kill a barmaid for pouring is sitting on the table awaiting Ignatius’ return. He looks at it dubiously as he sits himself back down. Ignatius: You got me a Guinness? Adam: Aye, you drink it sometimes, don’t you? Ignatius: Not since about two years ago: remember the night where that was all we drank for Christ-knows how long and my vomit was nearly black? Adam smiles, fond memories flooding back to him. PRIME was more fun when everyone was drinking themselves into a stupor between shows. Adam: Yeah... well, fuck it – I’ve got you it now, one pint won’t kill you. Ignatius takes a sip and recoils in disgust. Ignatius: Fuck... this stuff tastes worse than I remember. Adam: Barmaids here can’t pour worth shit, good thing their looks make up for it. He winks unconvincingly, to which Ignatius can only laugh. Ignatius: Shut the fuck up, man, you’re the worst “straight” I’ve ever seen. Adam grins back and takes a gulp of his drink, prompting Ignatius to try his again to roughly the same reaction. Ignatius: Man... This’ll be my last pint of this, ever. Urgh. Adam: Apologies, I had actually forgotten. But I do have a new plan, and it’s better than the old one. I’ll tell you after this round. He smiles into his glass as he takes another drink, watching Ignatius follow suit. Another hour later, two more rounds of the same for Ignatius, and the two men are in the back of a taxi cruising across town. Ignatius is looking rather worse-for-wear with a large, white cardboard box on his lap. Adam is beginning to get merry, a quarter-bottle of Jack resting between his legs – their agreement long since voided by vodka drunk too quickly. The cab stops just outside a less-than-respectful area where the two men bundle out, Adam handling the fee with the production of yet another $50 bill. Ignatius: Y’know... I feel the need to apologise to you. I was wrong earlier, I admit that - this is possibly the dumbest idea in the history of the world, the one earlier come sin about fifth in comparison. Adam strolls across to join him on the edge of the street, looking down the road to the big house at the end they’re headed for. Adam: Mmhmm, but it’s gonna be fun. Ignatius laughs and stumbles after Adam, who’s swilling the last of his bottle – it hadn’t needed much to tank him back up. They reach the house at the end and knock on the big door; thumping beats sound from inside – getting louder and more discernible as the door opens. A huge black man, built like a tank with tree-trunk legs, opens the door – flooding the sidewalk where Adam & Ignatius stand in red light. Doorman: Whachoo wan’? Adam: We’d like to propose a deal to your management with regards to some of your staff and this... He takes a rolled wad of 50s out his back pocket, something Ignatius was obviously unaware of as his jaw literally drops for the second time in the night. The black guy nods and opens the door wider, stepping back to let the men in. Once inside the wad is quickly pulled from Adam’s hand and thumbed through, getting a rough count on it. After that the box is opened, looked at warily, all the while an orgy of blaring hip-hop, red disco lights & clients snorting cocaine off hooker’s backs is going on around them. Ignatius almost drops the box he’s holding, and has trouble keeping the little balance he still has control of. Adam looks more surprised at the lax door security. After a brief absence their friend from the door returns and nods to a small door at the top of the first flight of stairs. Adam: Thanks, man. Ignatius lifts his box again; they ascend the stairs and enter the room, knowing that knocking would be pointless with this level of noise around them. Inside there’s a black woman, built about the same as the guy they’d just dealt with, sitting behind a rather neatly-kept desk. She motions for the door to be closed then for them to join her at the table. Surprisingly almost all the outside noise is cut out by the cushioned door. Woman: Good evening gentlemen, I can smell you’ve been having fun so far. I’ve also heard you’re interested in striking a deal with me. Before you do though, remember that if you hurt any one of my girls in a way that she’s not agreed to you’ll have to deal with me after Josef. Capiché? Adam nods, Ignatius still just looks thrown by all that he’s just seen. Adam: We’ve got you, Miss...? Woman: Ms. Williams, thank you. What’s your plan? Adam: Before I start you must understand that we are 100% serious in what we’re about to ask. You already know what’s in the box, and you know that we’re packing the green to back up this request. Ms. Williams simply nods. Adam: Well... Fast-forward two hours and the men are leaving; a fair whack of bills poorer than they went in. Ignatius and Adam declined all offers to partake in the many services, lines of coke & paddlings they were offered, thinking it best to recline and shoot the breeze in the comfy chairs lining the ‘waiting room’ on the third floor. When Josef had returned their box to them it was with a look that said he was truly scared of these two men who, even combined, weigh less than him and were much less sober. Ignatius: I can’t believer we actually just did that... did we just do that? Adam: Yup – and I hope to high hell that the Child Protection Services never hear a word of it. I think all Jeremy and Franklin’s visits would cease immediately... They stagger back up to the main road and hail another taxi, difficult at this time in the morning. Roll on morning. The boys’d top-&-tails’d on one side of Ignatius’ bed, with Katherine sleeping on the other, leaving Adam to curl himself on the tiny bathroom floor and Ignatius to squeeze into the tight floor-space between one side of the bed and the wall. They were awoken by Katherine’s screams as she’d walked into the bathroom at 6am and tripped on Adam. Another $50 kept her mouth shut to her parents about the box she say sitting in the shower. She was dubious about accepting the job for the morning, but on Adam’s word that they’d be back by noon she accepted. As soon as the door closes with their exit from the room Adam receives a hefty punch to his left arm. Adam: Oww... what’d I do to deserve that? Ignatius merely nudges the box on the ground in front of them with his foot. Ignatius: This is what you did to deserve it. Adam attempts a smile, but is beginning to regret it himself. The sooner he completes this plan the sooner it’s over and done with. Adam: I s’pose... might as well wrap it up though, unless you want to leave this lying around your room for the cleaners. This warrants another punch. Adam: Okay... to the post office. You know the drill, and a post office it is. Funnily enough, with the smell emanating from their box the queue clears pretty quickly. Adam steps up to the desk where a poor girl is covering her mouth and nose with the sleeve of her shirt. Adam: Umm, hi – can I get 26 ASAP FedEx boxes please? The next day, at JFK Airport – Adam picks up a copy of the New York Times while they await their flight. He flips to the back as he sits down on the uncomfortable plastic seats in the waiting lounge and thumbs through a few pages before chuckling to himself. He folds the paper in half and passes it to Ignatius, sitting next to him. ‘PRIME WRESTLERS AWAKE TO SHOCK’by Martin LeCroux This morning the entire roster of wrestling outfit PRIME awoke to a nasty surprise. FedEx’s ‘before 9am’ business delivery service was used to ensure that everyone of the men, and the single woman wrestling for the federation, had a parcel on their doorstep this morning. Inside the small FedEx box was a single, used douchebag. It’s unknown what the purpose of this ‘statement’ has been, but police are currently following a few lines of enquiry, including tracing bulk-buyers of the ‘own-brand’ pharmacy products. The company involved has refused to be named, but it’s been reported that they are a nationwide late-night or 24-hour Pharmacy. It does appear that the ‘stunt’ has backfired on the prankster that engineered it – most of the 30-strong roster had already left for Japan, where the company is hosting is annual Culture Shock show in the Osaka Dome. This left many family members and neighbours with the displeasure of the, supposedly very pungent, parcel. With the majority of staff having left earlier this week to oversee preparations there was no-one at PRIME’s US office to give a statement on the incident. However we did manage to get a brief interview with former champion Ignatius Lisieux, who was in New York today to make his flight.“I think it’s awful, it arrived at my motel this morning – so whoever is behind it must have up-to-date information –Continued: Page 23Police investigate possible mail law breach: Page 37 Ignatius tosses the paper back into the grinning Adam’s lap. Ignatius: Hilarious, I’m sure. I’m just glad one of us had sense enough to try and cover our tracks; you owe me one... Hell, you owe me about three for this – I hate interviews, if this ever comes out my reputation’s totally fucked and I should have had much more sleep & gym time than I’ve had. For the next while you are pulling favours for me, not the other way round. Adam merely shrugs and opens the paper again. Adam: You know I’m staying with you in Japan, right? Ignatius drops his head in his hands and groans. It was going to be a long flight.
