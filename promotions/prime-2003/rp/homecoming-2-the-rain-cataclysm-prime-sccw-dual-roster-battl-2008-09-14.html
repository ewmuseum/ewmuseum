Homecoming [2]: The Rain (Cataclysm, PRIME/SCCW Dual Roster Battle Royale)

14 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 14th, 2008 September 14h, 2008; Boston, Massachusetts It was a dark night in Boston, clouds covered a mostly full moon and a light rain dampened the atmosphere. The two sets of siblings, those being Simon and Soren Knox, and Connor and Miranda O'Reily, had gotten to the city earlier in the day, having nearly overstayed their welcome in Simon's hometown of Providence. The O'Reily twins came out of their trip back home with a little more of an understanding of their mysteriously calm and collected business associate, and Simon Knox came out of his trip back home with a hint about his father. Both the O'Reily twins rather insisted that the Knox brothers stayed in a hotel. The fact that they did so puzzled Soren Knox as he laid awake in his bed, staring up in the ceiling. With the lights off and the curtains shut, the hotel room was quite dark, though Soren's eyes had adjusted to the dark by now. Soren had the misfortune of having to share his room with his brother, whom he hadn't particularly gotten along with for a long time. They slept on different beds, with Soren taking a particularly large amount of space on his. He couldn't really sleep, partially because of his discomfort of sharing hotel rooms with his cold, indifferent, unemotional brother, and partially because he'd been having trouble coming to grips with the situation that brought him to the employ of Miranda O'Reily. And, of course, it was why the O'Reily twins insisted that the Knox brothers stayed here instead of coming to their home. "Hey." Soren said, ostensibly to Simon Knox, "You awake?" "Unfortunately, I didn't bring my sleep mask." Simon Knox responded, somewhat sarcastically. He didn't move from where he laid, but Soren could tell that Simon Knox was in the same boat... too much thinking leading to not enough sleep. "I have a question." Soren said to his brother, eliciting a groan in response from the younger Knox. "What is it?" Simon asked. "Where did the twins go?" Soren asked. Simon Knox likely knew his partner better than his partner knew him, and in some cases, he knew his partner better than he knew himself. In this particular case, Simon knew exactly where Connor O'Reily and his sister were, and why he didn't want the Knox brothrs tagging along. "Connor probably went home." "He could have invited us." Soren said. "No," Simon said, so quickly that he nearly interrupted Soren completely, "He wouldn't have." "Why's that?" Soren asked. His curiosity was piqued, even if it was from an individual he could barely tolerate. Simon Knox only spoke one more word to Soren, no matter how much Soren tried to get a response from him after he said it. Simon was often a cold and inconsiderate person, but he owed much to Connor O'Reily for helping him get this far in professional wrestling, and he wasn't about to say anything to Soren that Connor wouldn't tell him, himself. "Shame." * . * On the other side of Boston from the Knox brothers was a squalid apartment building close to the coast. The building was at least six stories tall, but wasn't particularly well-cared for compared to others. It was an apartment building for people who generally had it rough, financially speaking. On the fourth floor of the building, apartment #408, there lived two people. Their names were Barry and Caitlin Blair, a couple in their mid-40s. Their apartment was a simple one bedroom place with a kitchen, living room, and bathroom. The surroundings were in rapid decay, mildew and mold built up in some of the hard to reach spots. The mildew smell was only masked by the vague fragrance of air fresheners. The living room's primary feature was a small, circular table set in the middle, with four chairs centered around it. The TV, a simple box that was in need of a digital converter in about six months from now, sat in the corner. A sofa was aligned against the wall. Near the kitchen was a window that allowed one to see parts of the Boston skyline, but it wasn't a particularly exciting view and the residents of the apartment chose to block the window with dark curtains. Despite the squalid environment, nearly everything that was actually in the apartment was nice, new, and well-maintained. Barry Blair worked as a salesman, while Caitlin mostly stayed at home and took care of their pitiful, leaking, and generally gross apartment. At least, that's what Barry and Caitlin tended to tell people. The company that Barry Blair kept, however, told a meanacingly different story. Caitlin was staying with friends that evening, which allowed Barry to play poker with three guys that had come over for a visit. The men didn't really play for money, but they had chips they could use for betting. Barry Blair sat facing the TV. His black hair was graying due to stress from what he called "work related matters". He wore a collared blue and green plaid shirt and a pair of black slacks. He hadn't been aging very well, either because of the cigarettes he constantly smoked or because of his line of work. To Barry's left was a heavy-set man, not necessarily obese, but definitely a little fat despite some muscular definition. He wore a black muscle shirt and a pair of tattered jeans, and he almost perpetually had a cigar in his mouth. His name was Benny. To Benny's own left was a tall, lanky man who very unusually wore a white bucket hat with a black suit and red tie. He seemed very drunk at the moment, not the least of the problem being the six bottles of bourbon that were scattered about his end of the table. The man's name was Coleman. To Coleman's own left was a man of average height and weight, with a heavy, rugged beard on his face. Much like the lanky man to his right, this man wore a black suit and red tie. Unlike Coleman, however, this man was not drunk. Yet, anyway. This man's name was Desmond. Desmond could never generate the poker face that Barry Blair sported. Which was a shame, because his "dead man's hand" could, in fact, crush the pair of fours that Barry was seemingly not even paying attention to. "Ya ever think that mebbe yer in th'wrong line o'work, Barry?" Desmond asked the straight-laced Barry. Desmond was an Irish immigrant who'd been living in Boston for six years. He actually came because his cousin, Barry, had work for him and he was desperate to find such work. "No." came Barry's straight-laced answer. "I fold." were the words that escaped the mouth of Coleman, who calmly set down his cards in favor of the nearest bottle of bourbon that wasn't empty. "Same." was the monosyllabic response of Benny, who simply set his cards down and then folded his arms into his chest as he crossed them. Desmond knew he was in this alone. He also couldn't hide the fact that he had at least something that could take down at least a pair. Wanting to take his mind off of Barry's glare, Desmond took a different approach, "Heard yer boy's makin' it big, Barry." "You mean Connor?" Barry asked, "He ain't my boy, we just happen to be blood related." "Still. Professional wrestler. That ain't a common job. Certainly better than us all." Desmond observed, as he slid over a couple of chips, "Call." The only thing Barry did in response was move some chips that matched the number Desmond had, plus a couple of extras, "Raise." Once Barry did this, he looked over at the television, "Supposedly, the boy's supposed to be in Boston." "Really?" asked Benny, who was already lighting up his sixth cigarette of the game, "Ain't you excited, then, Barry?" "Not really. Boy ran outta here to be a big star. Sacrificed his future to do it. Practically dragged his sister to join him. What's there to be excited about?" Barry asked, almost in a rhetorical sense. He glared at Desmond, almost daring him to make his next move. Desmond didn't like the pressure. "Fold." he said, setting his cards on the table. He looked at Barry, who simply sighed and set his cards down, "Yer sayin' he ain't gonna visit?" "Not unless his twin sister drags him here against his will, and even then, I'd probably yell at `em," Barry said, as he pulled Desmond's chips towards him. The three men stared at Barry Blair for a while, before Barry started to shuffle the cards again... all while glaring at Desmond in particular, "You guys got somethin' to say?" The three men almost simultaneously turn their heads away from Barry. "No, boss." was a low voice, belonging to Coleman. "That's what I thought." Barry said, as he began to deal out cards. * . * Not far from the apartment building, Connor O'Reily sat in a local diner with his sister directly in front of him. The drizzle from earlier in the night had now progressed to a downpour, which made young Miranda O'Reily frown. Connor wore a black coat over a "Slayer" T-shirt and a pair of jeans. He also wore a red bandana over his head, much like he tried to do when he visited the Knox family last week. Miranda, meanwhile, wore her long brown hair in a ponytail. She wore a brown lace top with a long brown shawl, a long black skirt, and a pair of black mules. She also wore a black jacket, but it was resting to her side rather than being worn like Connor wore his. Miranda stared out the window rather than keep too much of an eye on her brother. "I wish this would let up." Miranda said, mostly to herself, though that didn't seem to phase Connor O'Reily, who was busy putting ketchup on his plain hamburger. "Well, you want me to get on the line with God, tell him to cool it with the waterworks in Boston?" Connor asked his sister. He placed the bun back on top of his burger, but didn't pick it up yet so he could talk at his sister some more, "`Cause, I gotta say, I don't think God listens to `ol Connor O'Reily." "Maybe if you took off the Slayer shirt, he'd listen." Miranda said, without even turning her head to face Connor. "Maybe you should stop being my sister for a second so you could stop scolding me." Connor said, almost as fast as Miranda said her statement. Equally fast was Miranda's retort, "Connor, even if I wasn't your twin sister and you were nothing more than a stranger to me that I've met on a whim, I would STILL scold you." "Yeesh, harsh as ever." Connor observed. He picked up his hamburger and took a large bite out of it. Miranda sighed, watching her brother gorge himself on foods he probably didn't need to eat, given his occupation. There was a pause, as Connor ate his food and Miranda watched the rain. It occured to Miranda that it was on a night, much like this one, that Connor left home two years ago. He had left a note, fastened to the fridge in their guardians' kitchen, stating that he was leaving for "the big time". It took Miranda O'Reily nearly a year to finally track him down to a Texas-based wrestling promotion, calling itself "the Squared Circle", living with some near-emotionless jerk named Simon Knox and the guy who had taken the two of them under his wing. All she knew about him was that he was some fellow with a weird name. After their reunion, Miranda chose to adhere to Connor's professional wrestling dream, but to keep an eye on him in order to keep him out of trouble. Which brought us to today. It had been two years since Connor brashly left home. Any other family would have tried to look for him, but Connor and Miranda didn't have the luxury of great parenting... when Connor ran out, his uncle didn't look for him, and neither did his aunt. "You're going back home, aren't you?" Miranda asked, abruptly. "Why would you think that?" Connor asked, with his mouth full of burger. The fact that Connor would eat with his mouth full made Miranda go "uhk" in disgust. "We're in your home town. Uncle Barry's apartment is right over there," Miranda said, pointing her finger out the window and towards the very apartment building that Barry Blair stayed in, "How can you come to this diner, eat here, and not go see Uncle Barry? Why even come to this diner in particular?" "The food's good here," Connor said simply, before he looked seriously at his twin sister, "You're not gonna stop nagging until I go and see him, are you?" "I'm prepared for the endeavor, Connor." Miranda said, crossing her arms in an effort to emphasize her point. Connor stared at his sister for a long minute, while mentally trying to measure how fast he could run before Miranda caught up with him. He remembered that Miranda was on the track team in high school, had always been faster than he was, and then he guessed that she would probably kick her shoes off before chasing him. He was screwed. "Fine. We'll go there... after I get another burger." Connor said. "You've had TWO already, Connor." Miranda was quick to object. * . * Barry Blair had seen his friends out, and was content on just spending the rest of the night alone in his apartment. A cigarette in his mouth, Barry blew clouds of smoke into the air without much of a care in the world. Unexpectedly, the sound of a knock could be heard from the direction of the apartment's door. Grumbling to himself, Barry stuffed his cigarette in a nearby ashtray and walked over to the front door before going through the process of unlocking the door. However, he did not remove the locking chain from the door, instead just opening the door to see who it was. He was greeted with the sight of twin siblings, both of whom he hadn't seen in a long time. "Hello, Uncle Barry." spoke Miranda O'Reily, who stood with her arms behind her back. "Hey." was a voice that came from Connor O'Reily, but sounded low and like he didn't want to be here. Barry's only response was to shut the door. After an awkward moment between the twins, the door opened again, except wider because Barry had removed the chain from his door. He stared a hole at Connor O'Reily in particular. "Son, you got a lot of nerve comin' back here." Barry said, putting his hands on his hips. Then he turned his attention to Miranda, "And you shouldn't have chased after this sack of shit." "...Nice to see you, too, pops." Connor said while looking away. Barry sighed, and then stepped back from the door and went to his kitchen. The O'Reily twins took that as an invitation to come in, and walked inside. Connor immediately noticed the table in the living room, still in disarray from the earlier poker game, "Still playing poker with your boys, I see." Barry stifled a chuckle, as he walked back into the living room with a bottle of wine, "Somethin' like that. So, you still wastin' your life with this wrestling nonsense, Connor?" Connor's head snapped towards Barry Blair, an already irritated look on his face, "Wasting my life?" "You could have been somebody, Connor. You chose to abandon your studies, quit high school, and chase this wrestling thing," Barry said, "And for what?" "Hey, I didn't want to come back to Boston either, but I work for a nationally recognized company." Connor said, "So, I'm somebody, Uncle Barry." "You team up with that Knox boy, you drag your poor sister around all over the place now, and you ain't even a champion despite all of that boasting you do. How the hell can you look me in the eye and tell me you're somebody?" Barry snapped back, before making a sound very similar to spitting, "You're nobody, Connor O'Reily. Never will be." "I come back for a visit and your first act is to cut me down?" Connor asked, "I swear, man. You could live in any place in this town, and yet you stay in this moldy piece of crap. How are you making it any better than I am?" "Live in the high life too long and people notice," Barry said, taking a swig of his wine and sitting down in the same chair that he sat at during his poker game, "This way, I keep my head low instead of in the clouds. You should try it some time... parading around like you do, showing off along with that other kid, is just askin' for somebody to punch your lights out. You'll never be successful if you always bring attention to yourself." "Are you offering me advice on *my* business?" Connor asked. "Hey, I've been telling you that for months, myself." Miranda chimed in, drawing stares from Barry and Connor. Barry then turns back to his nephew, "You don't even listen to your sister?" "She's just acting as our public relations." Connor responded. "Public relations, and yet, apart from this region of the country, everybody hates you and your partner. Either Miranda isn't doing her job properly, or more likely, you two just don't bother listening to her," Barry said. He turned to the female twin for a brief second, before he turned back to the male, "Even though she made the sacrifice of choosing to stick by your side rather than abandoning you to the wolves like I should have done years ago, if I had known that my sister's son would turn out to be such a useless lump of shit." "Hey!" objected Connor, and he started to try and come up with various responses that unfortunately all ended with Connor telling his uncle to go fuck himself. But then Barry's expression turned solemn, and Connor was silenced solely by his stare. "Tell me, Connor." Barry said. "Tell you what? You gonna cut me down some more, old man?" Connor asked. "Who's the real star of this team of yours?" Barry asked, "Who's the one that all of the attention is given? Who's the future? I promise you, the first response out of most people's lips is not Connor O'Reily. Sooner or later, by hook or by crook, that boy's gonna abandon you." "Simon Knox is not going to abandon me." Connor retorted, although he was quickly getting flustered and losing his composure, and was pacing around the room in anxiety over the conversation. Miranda observed that Connor was getting hot over this, already. "What's his motivation in all this? Certainly, it's not to team with you out of the goodness of his heart." Barry said, "People use each other all the time. And then, when they're all used up, they're disposed of. Nature of the world, son. Seen it countless times." Camel? Here's the straw to your back. "I can't take this shit any more. I'm outta here." Connor shouted, before he stormed out of the living room and out of the hallway. Miranda called out for him, but he was already beyond the point of listening to her. She sighed. "That was poor, even for you, Uncle Barry." Miranda said, with a crossing of her arms. "The boy needs to learn not to trust everyone he meets. Only person he should trust is you," Barry said, as he took another sip of his wine, "But it ain't really my job to take care of you lot, any more." "You still do care enough to keep up with what we're doing, apparently." Miranda said, nodding her head while looking at her uncle. "I got friends in high places, as you probably know by now," Barry said, before gestured to the door, "Go. You ain't gettin' anything more outta me." Miranda nodded, and approached the door. Without breaking stride, she offered one last statement to the man who once raised the O'Reily twins, "See you around, Uncle Barry." As Barry Blair heard the door shut behind his niece, he looked at his bottle of wine and simply pushed it aside. He looked up at the ceiling and could faintly hear the rain outside, "I sure as hell hope not... you're too nice for my business or his." * . * Connor O'Reily stood out in the sidewalk in front of the apartment building, getting drenched in the rain. He loathed the rain. Not as much as he loathed himself for not being able to really stand up to his uncle even years after leaving to make a name for himself. Many of his uncle's words were still eating away at him, almost forcing him to second-guess the decisions he had made up until now. Had he not made it yet? Was Simon Knox just using him? Did Miranda really not matter to him? He asked himself these questions even as the clouds practically pissed on him. Then there was a break, not in the clouds or the rain. Rather, it was a break in the space between Connor and the rain, in the form of an umbrella. Connor turned around to the person holding the umbrella, Miranda O'Reily, who simply glared at him in that was she always glared at him when he does something particularly stupid, "I swear, you'll catch a cold if you stand out here like that." "Luckily, I have a sister who is way too nice for her own good to watch out for me." Connor responded with a smirk. Miranda groaned at her brother, "I hope you don't think I'm planning to take care of you for as long as you live." "Honestly, I always thought that WAS your plan," Connor said, "You're always nagging..." "Because you're always irresponsible." Miranda responded, almost as quickly. "You don't even bother with a love life of your own." "Because I'm too busy cleaning up the messes from yours." "You never take my side in arguments." "Because the other guy is usually right." "And yet," Connor said, "I'd have probably gone off and done something extraordinarily stupid if you weren't around to keep me in line. So thanks. At least, for making sure I stayed out of jail or out of that ditch with a knife stickin' out of my back." Miranda paused for a moment, before she let out a soft chuckle and a smile. "I do my part."
