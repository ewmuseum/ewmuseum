The Twelve Steps of Recovery: Step One [part 1 of 12]

2 May 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/2/2010 Intro……   There are 12 steps to recovering from addiction. These 12 steps have helped millions of alcoholics and addicts overcome the demons of addiction. Admitting I had a problem was hard. Destroying my family over this disease was the hardest. But, like I did in the ring, I overcame the obstacles in front of me. I admitted defeat and sought help. I was ready to make my addictions tap-out. I found a way to use the 12 steps of recovery to ‘get my wants’ back. You know, I wanted my life back, my kids back, my wife back and of course, my wrestling career back. So what follows over the next few weeks will be the story of these steps and how they changed the course my life was heading down…… April 7th, 2008 I remember it like it was yesterday. Waving to the wife as she and my Corbin, then 3 months old, dropped me off at the Spirit of Life Recovery Center. I had finally broken down and told her I needed help. That the cancer and the recovery from the chemo and radiation played a mental game with me that started me down a spiral of drug and alcohol abuse. Just a week before admitting to her that I needed help, she found me passed out on the living room couch, son asleep next to me, a bottle of vodka and a cookie of crack on the lap. What she didn’t know, was that I finally crashed after a 4 night bender of no sleep, enough dope to kill a small African village and I was only able to sleep because the hallucinogens finally subsided. So here I was, standing in the office of the place that was going to be inspirational in my turning my life around and getting my family back. “Welcome to Spirit of Life Recovery!” the bald headed man standing in front of me reaches out his hand. “I’m Pastor Gary. This is my recovery center. Let’s do some paperwork and then the intake interview all right?” Now for some reason, I was really frightened for the first time in my life. I mean, I’ve been a multi-time Champion in many companies, traveled the world and wrestled in some of the most back-assward places. My life has been threatened by irate fans and I’ve gone toe to toe with the best in this business. But it was at this moment, staring at this 5’2, late 50’s Pastor that told me and the wife two days that he had what it takes to get me over my addiction, that it sunk in to my head that ‘this is real’. That, or it was the fact that I was in the middle of some really bad withdrawal shakes. I needed, no, at this moment, I WANTED to get high. I wanted to say ‘FUCK IT’ and leave. This little man couldn’t stop me. My body wanted to stand up, but my mind wouldn’t let me. “So, Chris….I’ve got to ask you… what are you willing to do for your recovery?” Pastor Gary asks as I fill out some paperwork. “Anything” I respond. “Good…. Because here…. Who you were out there, doesn’t matter inside the fences of this center. I know you’re a wrestling superstar, but it doesn’t matter.” Pastor tells me point blank. “If you are looking for a recovery center where you past status as an athlete, superstar matter, well, bub… this isn’t it. This isn’t the Hilton. This isn’t Celebrity Rehab. There is no camera crew following you around” After an hour of being treated like an everyday crack head…. Wait…. I was here because I am an everyday crack head. After all, I was smoking dope every waking minute. When I wasn’t I was having dreams about smoking dope. After the cancer, and the drug use, I was down to 280 pounds. I was a sickly looking, 6’11 crack head. And you know what, when you have the money I have, it wasn’t hard to find what I wanted. If crack wasn’t available, I found crystal meth. I would find every excuse to smoke. So, anyways, I half heartedly listened to everything the Pastor said to me. He had some of the staff members bring me my bedding and my dinner dishes. I tried to pay attention to what was going on around me as I was shown to my room. But the urge was getting stronger. It’d been a day since my last hit, and my body was hurting. My mind was wandering. My soul was searching for some place else to be. As I made my way from the office to room 7 I saw that I wasn’t the only one feeling this way. There were other men here, apparently having the same issues as me. There was a guy picking at a spot on his arm, over and over again. “Heroine withdrawal is the worst” Ethan spoke nonchalantly. He opened the door to my room and ushered me in. “Well, Pastor Gary was right…..it definitely isn’t the Hilton.” I attempted a joke. But in my mind, I couldn’t help but think of how I got here. And I was starting to feel miserably worse. My body was starting to sweat profusely. I was getting really tired. And achy. Did I mention miserable? I stepped into this former hotel room, and grumble. I’m not happy. 2 sets of bunk beds, a TV, a fridge. My eyes roll towards the bunk beds again. “For real? How am I suppose to sleep on that bed, it’s a foot smaller than I am.” Ethan reminds me of what I told Pastor Gary only moments ago. “You said you’re willing to do anything to beat this addiction. That means living with 3 other men in a small room and sleeping on a bed made for men half your size.” Ethan turns and leaves me to fend for myself. I place my luggage on an empty bed and run to the bathroom to vomit. And boy did I vomit. And now I was sweating more than before. As the urge to purge came on stronger and stronger I hear a voice behind me….. “Withdrawal huh?” I manage to nod my head up and down, spewing stomach acid with each downward motion. I haven’t been this sick since my rookie season in the NBA. “Here ya go.” The voice behind me hands me a towel to clean myself up. I flash a thumbs up as I flush the toilet and stand up. “Wow… you really are a big bastard aren’t you? By the way, welcome, my name is Rusty. Your roommate.” Rusty was short - well compared to me he was - , bald and tattooed. He’d been in this recovery center for a few months already. Seemed like a decent guy. “So, I know who you are, so we can skip that part” I nodded in agreement, since my breath probably smelled like the wrong end of a diseased vagina. I scuttled out of the bathroom and plopped as much of my frame as I could get on the twin sized bottom bunk of the bed near the window. And before I knew it…….. DARKNESS The darkness was masking the pain…… The darkness was masking the suffering…… And suddenly, I felt nothing. It was weird. For some reason, as I lay there falling into a much needed slumber, I felt nothing for the first time. I was numb. My mind was telling me I was asleep, but it wouldn’t let me feel what was going on. As I tossed and turned, I didn’t even realize I was in motion. And then there was a flash. My eyes shuddering. The darkness interrupted by flashing lights. There I am, shaking, quivering, looking at myself sleeping. I pull a syringe from a pocket and stick it in my arm. I scream, but there is no noise from my open mouth. The ground starts to shake. The earth beneath my feet opens and I’m suddenly inside a huge crack pipe. A flame lights me on fire and I’m sucked into a pair of lungs as if I was the sweet nectar of dope. Then I’m exhaled out and I can see my physical self sleeping, tossing and turning. I yell at myself to awaken from this nightmare, but I’m to zonked. I float up into the air as the earth slowly shrinks out of view…… CRASH…… I come crashing down to earth hard. As hard as coming down from a week long binge. I was riding a magic carpet in lala land and it was suddenly yanked from under my ass. I was spiraling and plummeting back to the middle of the room where I lie asleep. “You okay?” Rusty stares at me, a look of concern on his face. “You‘ve been tossing and turning, hell, I think I even heard you scream a few time.” Sitting up, I feel my bed sheets, and they are soaked. I look at the time. “Damn… I missed dinner?” “Bro, you’ve been out for the last 24 hours. But it’s not unusual. You’re detoxing. The drugs are going to play mind games with you. And I’m pretty sure the tossing and turning was caused by dope dreams. You’ll probably have more than a few of these while you’re detoxing.” I instantly took a liking to Rusty. He was a down to earth guy, who’d been through everything and wasn’t afraid, as I would find out soon, to tell me about it. “Come on, let’s get you working on your steps. It’ll help you get on the right track to recovery.” I was handed a blue workbook by Rusty. He told me that the words inside, the method inside the covers were going to be a major push in my recovery from this drug addiction. He told me to work honestly with him on these steps and I can become a better, drug free man. We opened the book and turned to page 4. Rusty read the header of that page out loud to me. STEP ONE We admitted we were powerless over alcohol - that our lives had become unmanageable. I sat back and pondered on this one momentarily. The first thought in my head was answered before I could speak on it. “These steps were written over 60 years ago. They are just as effective on drug addiction as alcoholism.” I was beginning to think Rusty was a mind reader. “So, what do you make of this step one?” “Man, it’s pretty easy actually.” I didn’t have to think hard on this one. I mean look at me, I was a mess. I let this disease eat at me from the inside. If I wasn’t powerless, then why was I hear in rehab? If my life was manageable, then I’d be with my wife and kids at this very moment, instead of sitting in this old hotel, talking with another addict. “Let’s see…. I spend every waking hour chasing a high that I could not find. I spend more time with the drug dealer that with my children. I have sex with hookers instead of my wife. I was once a world class athlete, and now I’m a world class crack head. So, yes…. I can easily admit I am powerless over addiction. My life is definitely unmanageable. I didn’t shower for days, because I was afraid the DEA was in my shower. That’s pretty powerless. Hell, I stopped leaving the house because I couldn’t manage to find a way out of it. So, Step one…. I admit I was powerless. I admit my life was a wreck and I couldn’t manage it anymore. But Rusty… I don’t want that anymore. I want to be in control of my life. I want to beat this opponent and make him tap out.” Rusty places a reaffirming hand on my shoulder. “In due time Chris. You didn’t get addicted overnight, and now, you can’t fix this overnight. One step at a time, as the old timers said. Let‘s hit the dining hall for dinner. Next week, we‘ll discuss the second step. These get a little harder and deeper in depth with your addiction.” I shook my head as a sign I agreed with what Rusty was saying. I wanted to believe him…. Hell, I had to believe him…. My life….. My family….. My career…… They all depend on my beating this monster.
