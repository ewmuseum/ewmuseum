King of Kings

Date: Midnight The temperature inside the church was, if that was possible, a couple of degrees colder than it was outside. That was the reason, Ember told himself as he passed the threshold, for the shiver that ran down his back. He paused for a second as he entered the building proper, and stared up into the ceiling. A wry smile played across his face, underneath the mask that was hidden by the heavy scarf that protected him from the cold and people’s glances both, as he realised that a lightning bolt wasn’t on its way down to smite him. Didn’t think so, Ember thought to himself. It was only the second time that he had stepped foot inside this Church – actually inside any Church – but it was satisfying to note that he was still doing so with impunity. Obviously he didn’t think that he was breaking any cardinal laws, that he was actually committing a sin simply be setting foot on consecrated ground but the priest … the same priest that he had come to see that night … that he had first spoken to one year ago, did. ” Bless me Father for I have sinned.” Father Jonathon Simpson sat up straighter inside the darkness of the confessional box, his wandering mind brought back to the earthly confines of his body as the whispered, almost sibilant, voice broke his quiet reverie. Quickly glancing at his wristwatch he realised that midnight mass had finished over half an hour ago, and his last confession was at least fifteen minutes ago as well. Rubbing a hand over his tired eyes he gave a small sigh to himself as he felt the joint in his shoulder protest with a stab of arthritic pain. “Sorry, am I boring you?” the voiced pierced the darkness once more, this time with a slightly harder edge to it. Sarcasm. Father Simpson shook his head as he realised that he was more tired than he had thought. It wasn’t like him to ignore the needs of his flock, but forty three years in the priesthood had taken it’s toll on his body and mind and while he was in better shape than most men his age he had to admit that the late night duties in the drafty old church were starting to get to him. “I am sorry my son, forgive an old man and his fallible ways” Father Simpson said to the unseen occupant on the other side of the confessional box. “…and there was me thinking that God was infallible in his wisdom, in his impotence” came the reply. “My son, God Himself may be infallible but I am just a servant of his and unfortunately I am not … and I think that the word that you are looking for, surely, is omnipotence, isn’t it?” corrected the priest gently. “If you prefer to tell yourself that, then yes, but I prefer my choice of word to be honest” the speaker said with a throaty chuckle. The priest’s eyes widened slightly in shock at the mocking tone in his confessor’s voice. While used to many and varied people, and their multitude of sins and confessions, through the years there was something different about the way that this man spoke. Something that brought an involuntary shudder to the priest’s spine. “Someone walk over your grave, Father?” the speaker asked solicitously. “How … what made you say that?” the priest asked in obvious confusion, unable to mask the quaver in his voice completely. “The sign of fear … it’s scent or taste if you will … is something that I am well accustomed to Father, and even without sight there is vision.” Stated the speaker matter-of-factly, a small chuckle escaping him as the priest shivered once more. “I suppose if it helps we could pretend that is just the chill of a drafty building that makes you shiver, Father, and leave it at that?” queried the speaker mockingly. The priest sits up a little straighter, folding his hands in his lap and forcing himself to take a couple of deep breaths as he collects himself and regains his composure. Even though he knows that he cannot be seen through the panel that separates the two sections of the confessional box, the priest stares straight at it as if daring the person on the other side to questions his strength … or as if trying to convince himself of it. “We can dispense with talk of the temperature of this building if you prefer, my son … tell me, how long is it since your last confession?” the priest inquired, his tone suddenly formal as he settled into familiar territory. “A very good question, and one that is ever so easy to answer Father” quipped the unseen speaker. A pregnant and uncomfortable pause followed as the priest waited for the speaker to continue. After nearly two minutes he coughed slightly, unwilling to push his late night visitor into speech if that was not what he wanted … after all the sacrament of confession had to be entered into willingly … but also not wanting to spend unnecessary time in an admittedly drafty church. “You could catch your death you know, Father” the sibilant voice quipped glibly, jolting the priest as if with a physical blow. “What … what are you talking about?” the priest asked, his voice high pitched. “I thought that you didn’t want to talk about meaningless things such as the temperature though Father?” pointed out the speaker. “… what has that got to do with anything?” asked the confused priest. “I heard you cough and was worried about your health Father, that is all,” replied the speaker, again with a hint of mockery in his tone. “Well you do not need to worry about my well-being my child, I have faith that my God will ensure my health for as long as he needs me to do His work” the priest pointed out with some strength in his voice. “… and then what?” asked the speaker, almost tenderly. “What?” replied the priest as the words sank in. “What happens then … what happens when your God finishes with you?” asked the speaker, carefully biting of each word. The priest sat back against the familiar wall of the confessional, glad that there was a solid piece of wood separating him from the speaker. Small beads of sweat shone along his balding head, just starting to run down his temple … but they were ignored as the priest laboured for breath. Almost without thinking he rubbed at his left arm as if it was sore. “Did the question frighten you Father, or shall we pretend that the stench of your fear didn’t just become almost over-bearing?” the voice whispered through the panel. With beads of sweat now flowing freely down his face the priest took a small gulp of air and lent forward, as if nauseous or dizzy, the priest’s eyes widen as he caught the shadowed reflection of his crucifix dangling around his neck. Reaching his right hand up to it he grasped it tightly and muttered a silent prayer to himself, gathering strength as he did so. “Bravo Father, it seems that faith can do many things …” whispered the unseen speaker, unheard by the priest himself. “When the Father of All decides that His work for me here on Earth has been completed he will call me home, and I will sit by his side in Heaven my child.” The priest stated with quiet determination. “… and is that enough?” asked the speaker. “Enough … what do you mean?” replied the priest. “Is it enough that you spend your life as a slave to some unseen power, something that never answers when you call, and then when He decides that it is time, you die?” spat the speaker vehemently. Another pause punctured the conversation, filling the confessional with silence that almost seemed to buzz with expectation. This silence was suddenly, and very unexpectedly, broken. The priest laughed. “Oh my dear child, it is not a question of it being ‘enough’ … when my Father calls me it will not mean my death, it will not be the culmination of things, it will be the start. That is when I will start to live!” the priest smiled to himself, sure of his words now. “… but that is a discussion for another time my child, you came here wanting the sacrament of confession, and so I ask again, how long is it since your last confession?” asked the priest gently. The sound of someone moving ever so slightly in the next compartment could just faintly be heard and through the grilled panel that separated them the priest could just make out a silhouetted form as it moved closer to the piece of wood that separated them. Unconsciously, the priest moved further back against the wall. “As I said, Father, that is an easy question to answer. I have never confessed before” the speaker stated simply. “I don’t understand, you have never been to confession before at all?” spluttered the priest. “Is that a problem Father?” interrupted the speaker before the priest could continue, “will that lapse on my part stop me from confessing now?” “No my son … no of course it won’t, but tell me what is it that has brought you to my church, to my confessional, if you have never felt the need to confess before?” asked the priest with some confusion. Another shiver overtook his body as this time another laugh … a much different laugh … broke the stillness of the deserted church. A laugh completely devoid of humour that suddenly cut off as the unseen figure spoke, quietly and chillingly. “Let me tell you Father, let me tell you about sin ...” The last hour had passed relatively quickly and Ember now found himself nearly alone in the Church. Midnight Mass had finished and most people had already made their way out of the building, ignoring the figure swathed from head to foot in dark clothes. Sitting in the back of the Church, a pew to himself, Ember had found the sermon most entertaining, even if he had had to bite one of the Bibles to stop himself from laughing out loud. All that talk about Alpha and Omega, about the beginning and the end had been nauseating enough but when the priest had started to spout about the saviour being not only the Lord of Lords but also the King of Kings then Ember had nearly lost it. It was simply to serendipitous for words. He had only come to the Church because he had found himself at a loose end and had then recalled his promise to visit the priest sometime in the future. The old man, on their first meeting, had nearly had a heart attack so convinced was he that Ember was a demon sent directly from Hell to tempt him. Admittedly Ember had delighted in tormenting the old man, but only in a purely verbal way. Mental sparring was always something that he had been fond of and when he realised that the old man’s ‘rocklike’ faith had been shaken simply by his appearance then he couldn’t help but take it further. The memory of that night, the recollection of the wide-eyed terror on the priest’s face and the small trial of sweat that had run down his brow had prompted Ember to come back this night. Rather than approach the man, however, he was content to listen to his empty platitudes about salvation for all men and delight in the parallels between his words and what was going on in a place that was as far removed from the priest’s world as you could get. The wrestling ring. If only, Ember had thought to himself, the priest had been asked to write a sermon on the subject of a vainglorious idiot, a Chinese whore and a man that he thought was a demon made flesh. Would he still be as sure and calm in his words; would he still refer to the King of Kings if he knew just who and what he was talking about? The last person left the Church and Ember, from his shadowed place near the back, quietly watched as the priest knelt down in front of the alter and bowed his head. Ember quietly got to his feet and was tempted, sorely tempted, to simply tap the priest on the shoulder – or perhaps gently lick his ear – just to see if he would piss himself. Instead he turned and walked the other way, stopping only to take a taper and light three candles near the exit. “One for you Winters, one for you Sunny,” he whispered to himself as he gently lit the third. “… and one for myself.” With a small grin he blew out the first two candles and stared at the small flame that he had lit for himself. “Only one left?” he smirked, “fancy that!” Moving towards the exit he turned to stare at the back of the kneeling priest, wondering why he hadn’t gone through with his original plan and got his kicks for the night. With a shrug of his shoulders he glanced up into the ceiling and winked. “OK, you win,” he acknowledged, “this time.”
