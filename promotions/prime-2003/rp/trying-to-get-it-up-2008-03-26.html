Trying to Get It Up

26 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 26th, 2008 (It's 1,099 words without the Patton monologue, hope you enjoy.) The camera opens in the living room of the Delta Upsilon Iota fraternity house; and perhaps no habitat has ever been more appropriately titled. Because from the looks of things, with an enormous tower of pizza boxes stacked high in one corner and a dozen or more full garbage bags of empty beer cans occupying the other, there is little doubt that humans are not the only species to exist, and probably thrive, within these cracked and peeling walls. On the black leather couch in the middle of the room sits one half of Delta Upsilon Iota—the always frat-tastic Colby Korver. With an open can of Miller Lite in one hand…and an unopened can of Miller Lite in the other, Korver stares with rapt attention at the television screen across from him. An obviously expensive high definition set, it seems quite out of place when juxtaposed with the stack of old Playboy magazines wedged under the stand upon which it sits. Although the engineering behind this makeshift construction seems suspect at best, it is nonetheless doing a fine job of supporting the stand…however temporary the arrangement might be. On the plasma screen is the DVD menu for the movie Patton, winner of seven Academy Awards and instantly recognizable for its famous opening scene during which General Patton gives a rousing six minute speech, while standing at attention in front of a giant American flag. As this classic scene begins, the other half of Delta Upsilon Iota—Hank Cobb—returns from his afternoon lifting session, only to find his partner in a surprisingly unsocial mood. “Hey buddy, I picked up some extr…” “Can it Cobb. Here I am doing everything it takes to prepare us for ReVolution and you’re going to just waltz in here and talk over all the good stuff?” “Come on man, your lazy ass hasn’t moved from that couch since I left. And that was three hours ago.” “Exactly.” Korver reaches next to him on the couch and picks up a DVD case. “And you know what this is?” “Can’t say that I do Korv.” Korver turns over the case and reads dramatically from the back. “‘The inspirational story of one young man’s journey to do something truly meaningful in his life, to turn himself from an average high school athlete into something so much more…a championship wrestler.’ Damn, that’s one hell of a promo.” “I ain’t arguin’ that. But you wanna explain how this is gonna get us ready for ReVolution? Unless every copy comes with a complimentary stun gun, how is this gettin’ us ready for Team VIAGRA? Somethin’ tells me they ain’t gonna be up for a movie night come Wednesday. I mean, you’ve got to kiddin’ me.” “That’s right Mr. Cobb, while you have been merely lifting heavy pieces of metal above your head, I have been using my vastly superior intellect for something other than...or at least in addition to...getting laid. And thanks to me, we now have in our possession the secret weapon of all secret weapons...motivational videos." “Now I hate to be the bearer of bad news yet again, but I suggested that we ‘watch tape,’ I wasn’t suggesting we run up to Blockbuster and schedule a double-feature. I wanted us to start scouting our opponents’ strengths and weaknesses by watching tape of their matches.” “Maybe things are different in good ol’ North Carolina, but down here in Florida our Blockbusters carry wrestling videos.” “Yeah, if we’re lookin’ to scout the ’95 King of the Ring. Dumb ass.” “Speaking of, you’re telling me that Savio Vega wouldn’t have benefited greatly from scouting footage of the early days of Men on a Mission?” “For the love of Dixie, can someone please explain to me why we’re sittin’ around here like a couple of stooges talkin’ bout Mabel, Oscar and Mo?” Korver cracks open his next beer and taken a swig before whispered a quiet response. “You brought it up.” “Horse shit. Now get up off your lazy ass and let’s head to the gym.” “The gym? You just got back from the gym. Is there some sort of buffet there that I don’t know about?” “Have you developed some sort of allergy to hard work lately or is your vaginitis just flarin’ up again?” “Fine jackass, I’ll go. But don’t come whining to me the next time you’re lacking motivation. Patton’s got all the answers right here in front of you, you just have to ask him nicely.” “You know what Korv? Sometimes you’re a real piece of work. But hell, it’s worth a shot. Throw me a beer and turn it up.” “I knew you’d smarten up eventually. Sometimes it just takes a little longer to get my genius through that thick head of yours. Such is my burden.” “Is there a reason you haven’t thrown me a beer yet?” “Sorry big guy, sometimes I really can act like a real dumb ass.” “You’re tellin’ me.” “What was that?” “Nothin’ boss. Now press play so we can finally watch Patton kick some Nazi ass.” “Nazis. I hate those guys.” ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ “Now I want you to remember that no bastard ever won a war by dying for his country. He won it by making the other poor dumb bastard die for his country. Men, all this stuff you've heard about America not wanting to fight - wanting to stay out of the war, is a lot of horse dung. Americans traditionally love to fight. All real Americans love the sting of battle.” “When you were kids, you all admired the champion marble shooter, the fastest runner, big league ball players, the toughest boxers. Americans love a winner and will not tolerate a loser. Americans play to win all the time. I wouldn't give a hoot in hell for a man who lost and laughed. That's why Americans have never lost and never will lose a war, because the very thought of losing is hateful to Americans.” “Now, an army is a team - it lives, eats, sleeps, fights as a team. This individuality stuff is a bunch of crap... Now, we have the finest food and equipment, the best spirit, and the best men in the world. You know, by god, I actually pity those poor bastards we're goin' up against. By god, I do. We're not just gonna shoot the bastard, we're going to cut out their living guts and use them to grease the treads of our tanks. We're going to murder those lousy Hun bastards by the bushel.” “Now, some of you boys, I know, are wondering whether or not you'll chicken out under fire. Don't worry about it. I can assure you that you will all do your duty. The Nazis are the enemy. Wade into them, spill their blood, shoot them in the belly. When you put your hand into a bunch of goo that a moment before was your best friend's face, you'll know what to do.” “Now there's another thing I want you to remember. I don't want to get any messages saying that we are holding our position. We're not holding anything. Let the Hun do that. We are advancing constantly and we're not interested in holding onto anything except the enemy. We're going to hold onto him by the nose and we're gonna kick him in the ass. We're going to kick the hell out of him all the time and we're gonna go through him like crap through a goose.” “Now, there's one thing that you men will be able to say when you get back home, and you may thank god for it. Thirty years from now when you're sitting around your fireside with your grandson on your knee, and he asks you: 'What did you do in the Great World War II?', you won't have to say: 'Well, I shoveled s--t in Louisiana.' All right, now you sons-of-bitches, you know how I feel and I will be proud to lead you wonderful guys into battle anytime, anywhere. That's all." ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Korver pauses the movie and, with a howl of delight, throws his now empty beer across the room. The can ricochets loudly off the mantle, narrowly missing the 2006 Greek Week Olympic Games 1st place trophy on the mantle…as recently stolen from one of the ‘official’ fraternities on campus. Turning back to his dumbfounded tag team partner, Korver unleashes his overwhelming enthusiasm on the helpless Cobb. “Holy fuck that’s one hell of a promo. And you’re gonna sit there and tell me that wasn’t exactly what we need? Well he’s preaching to the choir big guy, because I’m drinking this Kool-Aid. Now if only we had some vodka…” “Lord knows I really hate to admit this, but I think a blind dog may have just found a bone.” “Now if you will be so kind as to type that redneck bullshit into a translator, maybe you could give me some English ‘por favor’.” “What I’m tryin’ to tell you, as difficult as that may be at times, is that we can actually use this boss. Because we got ourselves one hell of a battle comin’ up against that ink’ed up monster, and I’ll be damned if I’m get tore up. Now I reckon you done gone and got me riled up, bowed up, and hankerin’ for a good ol’ knock-down, drag-out slobber knocker.” “I have no fucking clue what you just said. But something tells me that this big hick’s ready to fight.” “Damn straight Korv.” “Damn straight.”
