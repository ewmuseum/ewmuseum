Her

26 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/26/06 The rain rolled down the hotel room window, casting pinstripe shadows onto the man sitting in the chair watching it fall. The room had grown dark little by little, and Damien had not taken the time to light it. Instead, he continued his journal entry until a slice of white streaked across the sky, and called his attention to the blackness that rose above the neon cacophony of downtown Tokyo. He was distracted, and eventually found himself staring; his thoughts brought to a very far away place. His thoughts were back to her. Her, Part I11 months, 24 days, 7 hours. When he first saw her, she was holding an IV bag between her teeth, her glasses were crooked on her nose with no free hand to adjust them, her hair was in a sloppy bun, she had the subtle hunching of a person working on their sixteenth hour of an eighteen hour shift, and her hands worked deftly to lace the plastic tubing through the mechanical pump before the annoying EMPTY CHAMBER alarm went off. Damien knew that it was the pain-killers that put a faint blue glow around her shape, and he knew that it was a chairshot to the head that made her movements ethereal and angelic. He knew that. But he also knew that when she turned to him, smiled, and asked him if he wanted an extra blanket or something to drink, that she was breathtakingly beautiful. Witty opening remarks that played at the edges of his otherwise hazy thoughts reminded him that he wanted to get to know her better, but the analgesics and exhaustion won out, and he slipped into sleep. Before he did, though, he committed her name to memory, and smiled to himself as he fell through the darkness of rest. He woke up the next morning, unrealistically hoping to see her standing over his bed, smiling at him and receptive to his attempts to make first contact. Instead, he awoke to a circle of medical students, and one chief surgeon who had been explaining to them a lot more than had been explained to him. Apparently, there is a disk-like bone that covers the ligaments of your knee, and Damien's had shattered, and fragments had radiated throughout his leg. From what he heard the surgeon explain, the plan was to remove the bone, replace it with plastic and silicone, and hope for full use of the leg within the next two years. Damien didn't like what he heard, so he closed his eyes and tried to distract himself with thoughts of her. When he opened them, several hours had passed, and he was staring up at the ceiling of the recovery room, listening to the far-off sound of a radio playing James Taylor, and the unsettling sound of a respirator. Damien took a deep breath, off-rhythm, and thanked God that it was not his. Sylvia Castelles, RN. That was her name. And for some very strange reason; one that he would probably never understand, she was the reason he stayed secure in the face of all that was surrounding him. A career in shambles, a future left in the hands of a medical staff at a hospital that's only merit was it's distance from the local gym in which the All-City Wrestling Alliance was holding a show, and the subliminal understanding that he was alone and unemployed in a city that he had been in for less than a month. In the moments before he ended up in Saint Vincent's Medical Center he was screaming and cursing in the back of a Ford Escort, with Dr. Headhunter driving, and The Black Tiger giving him directions, and trying to keep Damien calm as his leg swelled like a water floatie. In the moments that followed, he was hefted into the emergency room wait area, and the nurse at the front desk was given a note and an address in which to send Damien's medical bills. The nurse stepped out to see the man lying across three waiting room chairs, half-delirious with agony. She called for a team of orderlies, and brought the man into an examination room where he could be given the dignity of cursing and writhing hellishly in private. He didn’t realize it was her then, but was not surprised when he found out a week later. Her, Part II11 months, 2 days, 4 hours The two sat in the hospital cafeteria. He was in a wheelchair, and she was off duty. He had been discharged three months before, and had come back to the hospital for physical therapy. Of course, he had first asked her out when she came into his room to change his dressing, and she sarcastically reminded him that such unprofessional behavior, albeit tempting, was frowned upon. He smiled, knowing when the challenge had been made, and set his mind to getting her to say yes one day. And with that goal in mind, recovering became a means to a far greater end. For the rest of that week, he took every possibility he was given to learn about her. From casual conversation about her job to some heartfelt revelations about his, he made sure she understood that he was very interested in being more to her than a patient. What that was; he was still learning to define to himself. He saw her coy distance fade. At times, she would ‘forget’ a clipboard in his room, and stay an extra few minutes to talk to him. Other times, she would playfully claim to make the best muffins in New York, and then be obligated to prove it. What started out as a silly mission to get to know a woman he found pretty became a desire to share his appreciation for a human that so rare and special to him. If it was misplaced feelings because of the way she nurtured him, or if he was simply reaching out to the first person that showed his compassion, he was bound to her, and he hoped the feeling was at least potentially mutual. She finally agreed to a celebratory cup of coffee at the hospital café after his discharge, and he was grateful. Under the guise of a simple coffee date, they realized how unexpectedly and how easily they had fallen in love. Her, Part III 9 months, 4 days, 1 hour She loved the way he said her name. It was not the Anglicized version that she had come to adopt as she moved along her professional life. It was not the subjective name that she had learned to recognize in its many variations. When he spoke it to her, she found a new appreciation for it. It was a beautiful, rich, sensual, wholly personal sound that made her want to wrap herself in the musicality of his voice and never let it go. It was her name, and it was her new-found identity. She even found herself correcting those who would dare to corrupt it and try to change it. Sylvia Castelles learned to love a lot of who she was when she saw it reflected in how he perceived it. She loved the soft rise that came off of her hips, even after years on the treadmill trying to get rid of it. She loved her disobedient hair, and the way the wind sent it into her face when she was trying to look serious. Sylvia looked in the mirror; standing wrapped in his arms, and loved her crooked smile, and her left eye which was slightly lighter than her right. She loved her dark brown hair, and felt guilty for the times she wanted to make it lighter, or curlier, or otherwise not her own. She loved the natural muscle in her arms that she never worked on shaping, but that she’s had since playing softball in high school. She learned to love the way her voice cracked when she was tired, the way she held a wine glass and a cup of Kool-Aid in the same way, and the way she mumbled through the parts of a song she wasn’t sure of, but sang at the top of her lungs when she was lone. Sylvia never realized that she squinted slightly when she fixed her hair in the bathroom, but when Damien pointed it out, and told her how cute it was; she learned to love the fact that she did it. Sylvia did not love herself because Damien loved her; she loved Damien for seeing beauty in the things she was secretly proud of. For Sylvia, and Damien alike, if the rest of the world didn’t notice or care what the saw in each other, it neither mattered nor registered for them. In fact, it made them feel all-the-more fortunate that they had those secret appreciations that they didn’t need to explain to anyone. Sitting in the park at midnight, with a star-filled sky above them, and the cool grass of Central Park under them, they were silent, and a galaxy of understanding swarmed around them. He felt it before she did, and when she looked at him, he saw where the tear had left a shimmering trail on her cheek, and had landed on his hand which was crossed over her shoulders. Whatever brought about the tear also brought about a spontaneous desire to bury her face into his chest, and a flood of emotions made her cry hard next to him. He didn’t understand what had made it happen, but when she stopped crying, and pressed herself tighter against him, it no longer mattered. Whatever it was; mourning, joy, or catharsis, she now felt safe, and that was he was interested in. Her, Part IV4 months, 11 days, 17 hours. It was amazing. She had seen pictures, and had heard stories, but to be standing on the top of that hill, during sunset in Cielo Azul, Mexico was breath-taking. Making the experience even more incredible was the fact that she was there with him. He had pulled some strings and called in a few favors, and was able to get enough work to pay for that vacation. It was their first trip together since he had begun getting some work, and she knew how hard he had worked to give it to her. She never understood why he allowed himself to be abused the way he did, but had resigned herself to the idea that it was best not to think about it. The image of him in her ER only a few months before was proof enough that some things are better left out of mind. In that place, with the sun setting on the horizon, his hands resting on her stomach, and his mouth whispering silly things into her ear, it was a million miles away and a million years ago. In that moment, she was with him, and they were standing in the place that she knew was sacred to him. She would not know why until he kissed her, hen dropped to one knee, but she felt something very special in the way he let nothing stand in the way of sharing that moment. When he produced the ring, and proposed to his soul mate as his father had on that very same spot twenty-eight years before, she had to sit down. When he thought she was about to pass out from lack of oxygen, she kissed him hard, and agreed to be his wife with all that she was. As the sun set on their embrace, they were sharing a single desire to never be apart. Her, Part V2 Months, 6 days, 17 hours She was excited to finally get out of the city for a long-overdue vacation. The reservations were made at a small bed and breakfast in Vermont, and Sylvia knew that he was always the type to forget something, so she covered his tracks. “Damien, I put your laptop into the trunk of the car, but couldn’t find—“ Sylvia walked into Damien’s room, and saw him sitting on the edge of the bed. He had his suitcase opened, and his wrestling pads sat on top of a pile of hastily-packed clothes. A wave of sadness overcame her, and he sensed it. She shook her head, and turned away from the room wordlessly. Damien held the plane ticket that had come in the mail that morning, and then at the dress that she was planning to wear to dinner that night. He absently tucked the ticket into his pocket, closed the suitcase, and brought it to the front door. As he walked past where she was sitting on the couch, he tried to speak, but she simply held up her hand, and wished him a safe trip. This was not a new occurrence. If he was to be held to his word, he owed her three dinners to celebrate her promotion stemming from two months ago, a weekend away because he convinced her to take her sick time to coincide with his discharge from PT two months before that, and a romantic birthday dinner at Alfonso’s to celebrate. He had also promised to meet her parents at their home in Boston, but it was rearranged to be a video conference call from a hotel where he was staying overnight to get a try-out at a small New Jersey promotion. His heart broke each time he had to apologize, and make another promise. However, when the companies sent for him, it was hard to explain that his big chance to show them that he was marketable and available would have to wait until after he had a romantic dinner with this girlfriend. After a while, she made it easier for him by letting him leave without trying to explain. This was the eighth company to call him back, and it was something that was important to him. He was starting to get his name out there again, and it was time to move up from just occasional per-diem shows and low-paying exhibitions at the local bingo hall. If she was to believe Damien, he was doing it for her. Somehow, it was not as comforting as she was sure he hoped it would be. Her, Part VI,0 months, 0 Days, 2 hours Damien was in Glendale Falls, MA. Actually, he was in a motel three miles away from the waterfall, and he was scheduled for a wrestling match at an exhibition celebrating some anniversary. Truthfully, he wasn’t sure, but he knew that this match would be seen by many very important scouts, and he would have a real shot at a permanent contract and enough money to start the life he knew he wanted with Sylvia. He sat in that hotel room, watching as the rain rolled down the window, and listened to the sound of his own heartbeat. He was going to call home and see how Sylvia was, but wasn’t sure how to explain why he wasn’t already on his way home. It was a scheduling hiccup, one that happens when you’re dealing with middle-men and impromptu booking schedules. She had something important to tell him, and he had promised to sit with her and hear it in person. Now, with the recent change of plans, he wasn’t sure how to break the news. His uncertainty was made irrelevant when his cell phone rang, and he saw her number. He would make this up to her, he promised. “Damien? I am starting to get real impatient.’ she said, playfully whining. “I know you are, Sexy…me, too.” “I can't wait. I mean, I haven’t finished cooking your surprise, so I guess I can deal with the wait at least until it’s finished, but I really wish you'd get here already.” He closed his eyes, and could hear the anticipation in her voice. Something in his heart sank, and he hated himself at that moment. 'A suprise for me, Sweetie?' Her voice rose with giddy pride, and she could not contain herself. “Okay, I’ll give you a hint; Bring home white wine.” He cleared his throat, and did his best to stay with her in the conversation. “Baby, you cooked shrimp scampi for me? My God, I love you! I really can’t wait to get home now.” He was being sincere. He looked at the car keys sitting on the night table, and he was very close to leaving it all behind. However, sitting next to the keys was an ID card for the next night’s event, along with a letter from the IHWF stating their interest in his performance. “Well, Damien, don’t sing it, bring it. Besides, there’s a lot more than shrimp waiting for you here...Now hurry up.' She flirted playfully. That type of outright flirtation was unusual, and far outdone by her more subtle methods. Damien rested his head in his hands, and felt immediately sick. He could barely breathe enough to tell her the truth. “Sylvia, maybe you can tell me your news. I know that you wanted to let me know something important.” She was quiet, and he could hear a shift in her tone. “Damien, I told you that I would tell you when you were here. It was your idea, and now that I think about it, I think I want to do that…” Desperation started to enter her voice. “Baby, are you close? Are you almost here?” She knew. Damien knew that she knew, but was now grasping at a far-away hope. To have to remove that hope, to have to deny her one more time was unbearable, and yet, he had no choice. “Sylvia, there was a stupid mix-up with the booking. I was told that I would be on my way home by this afternoon…Sweetie, I won’t be home until maybe tomorrow night, maybe Sunday morning.” There was silence. “Sweetie, I would have called you a lot earlier, but I just got word of it when I went down to the auditorium. It’s really fucked up, and I’m so mad at these guys. It’s like the left hand doesn’t know what the right hand is doing half the time. It’s not right, you know?” Still, more silence. “Sylvia, I have a credit card in my top drawer. I had it for emergencies, but I think this can count as one. Sweetie, I want you to see if you can get a red-eye or a driver willing to take you up here. It’s not that long of a ride, and by the time you get here, we can sleep in, order some room service, and make the best of it. Then, if you want, you can see my match, and then…well, you know, it’ll be fun.” Damien heard the sound of pots being moved from the stove, and possibly dropped into the sink. He then heard Sylvia take a deep breath, and then grow silent again. “You know what, I think I can talk to these guys, maybe they’ll let me do some promo work early in the day, then give a quick appearance. If I’m lucky, I may be on my way back to New York by seven, eight o’clock at the latest.” “No, Damien. Do what you have to do. I’ll be fine. As a matter of fact, I think I’m going to just go stay with my sister for a bit. She’s been going crazy with my nephews, and maybe I can give her a hand. I’ll be fine.” 'What was your news? I think I want to know now.' She was quiet. 'I'll tell you when you get back. It's not a big deal. It can wait. Now just do good tomorrow. Be safe.' Damien knew that she was very understanding, but this was surreal. He shook off his paranoia, and promised to make it up to her. This time, he meant it. He came home to find her belongings gone. No one picked up at her sister's number, and the pregnancy test lay in the garbage blue and negative. He left three messages on her answering machine, as well as hte number where he could be reached at the IHWF HQ in Miami. Even in his most self-rightous anger, he knew what he had lost. Her, Part VII. . . They had met in a local café almost a year after she had left him. She, as he suspected, was as beautiful as ever. He was, as she expected, just as committed to his career as ever. They parted ways with none of the anger, the confusion, or the uncertainty that had been so hard for each of them to make peace with. They came to a point of closure. It wasn’t what they would have hoped for, but it was what they were left with. Happy Endings are unrealistic, but genuine closure is a good second best. And then, without him knowing it, she slipped into a cab in Manhattan, and it pulled up in front of his home in Brooklyn. She stood at his door, and without words, she was enveloped into his arms again. There was no promises made, no plans formed, and no real communication about the future, the implications, or the ‘What now?’. They simply held each other until she was asleep, and cried out. He accepted it, and didn’t ask questions. When he woke up the next morning, she was already gone. However, she had left him a note, and a plane ticket to Santa Fe, where she had started her new life. Staring out at the rain-streaked window, he thumbed the plane ticket. In the secret place of that moment, he wondered just how much he stood to leave behind.
