Training

6 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/6/9 Training A Garbage Bag Production Some time ago, a conglomerate of wealthy scientists put together an experiment that by all means should have been outlawed. Now you have to understand that I never trusted science in the first place. Never much trusted religion, neither. Come to think of it, I had a distrusting habit towards anyone or anything that tried to explain things to me, but in this case, I was hard up for cash, and when the idea of earning income as a participant in various experiments presented itself to me, I was in no position to refuse. The experiment itself was a disaster. The scientists inserted people into a pair of cages connected only by a tube that opened and shut at random intervals. They never told any of the participants what the purpose of this experiment was, but as far as my perceptions were concerned, I figured it had something to do with the behavior of people in a cage when presented with the opportunity to go through a tube to another cage. As I expected, some people went through the tube, and others did not. I think this was what the scientists were expecting, too, and who knows? Maybe there was some noble purpose to all of this that would end all wars, cure all diseases and feed all the hungry. Maybe the experiment, when properly conducted, would answer all the big questions. But during the experiment, something went horribly awry, causing the participants to break into fights, and then those fights started fights of their own with other fights that were fighting amongst themselves until it all fought into one wholly encompassing fight. With so much chaos summed between the two cages, the outnumbered proctors of the experiment could do nothing but carefully remove the fallen bodies through subsidizations of the wreckage. It was a massive failure, and I never thought that they'd attempt it again until they contacted me and asked if I wanted to participate in it once more. But things were different now. Times had changed, and they had dragged me along for the ride. I was no longer the dreamy-eyed kid that hopped at every scotch to reach for the stars and pull the veil down off of the midnight sky. I was wizened. "The fuck you mean you're going to do it?" Lucius Shazam coughed through a thick haze of freshly ripped stale smoke. "Shit, man. You ever clean this thing?" "Sorry, Lu. I haven't changed the water in months," I shrugged. "Thought you said that shit was the worst shit you ever got yourself into." "There's got to be something up if they're trying it again after the last attempt failed," I professed. "I need to figure out what's so important about it." "It's the money, ain't it?" "It's not the money." "Man, cause if you're low on dough, I'm fitting to close this bag and roll," Lucius got up, a shadowy figure through the fog. "Sit back down, Lucius," I warned, reaching into my pocket. "I've got your money right here." I tossed him a few crumpled twenties, and he straightened each one out as he inventoried them all and put them in his pocket. "I told you, Lu. Money ain't a problem right now." I protectively slid the bag towards my end of the beaten coffee table. "Besides, I was one of the last people they removed that other time. I'll make it out alright." "Got anything to drink?" Lucius asked, not relieved, but generally unconcerned to begin with. "Just water. It's in the kitchen," I pointed. "Cups are in a box under the sink." "Who the fuck keeps cups under the sink?" Lucius questioned, standing and opening the door leading back into the apartment from the rear stairwell. "That's where I keep them," I justified. "It's where I've always kept them." I heard the tap run, and it seemed like Lucius was in there for an unnecessarily long time. I lit a cigarette, and thought for a bit about armoires. I didn't know why. Nobody just thought about armoires without precursory reasons. The vision was so clear, like a medium dream, warning me about drowning. The tap turned off, and I forgot all about the armoires. "I think I gotta peace out in a minute," Lucius reentered, sparking up a cigarette of his own. "Alright," I nodded and then rotated along the axis. "Hey, uh, you got any connections to anyone who can get their hands on some cyanide?" "The fuck?" Lucius looked up, eyes wide as saucers. "I'm a drug dealer, not a terrorist." "You know a lot of people, Lu," I reasoned. "You have to know someone. It's basic statistics, man." "The hell you need cyanide for, anyway?" "The experiment," I responded, so quickly it bordered on interruption. "If something goes wrong, and I run out of options, I'd like to go painlessly." "Man, I don't know why I even deal with you." "I'm your best customer," I chuckled. "You'd go out of business if it wasn't for me." "Yeah, whatever you say," Lucius bluffed. "So you think you can help me out?" I returned. "You know, with the cyanide pill?" "Nah, man. I don't know how your wheels are spinning, but keep me as far out as possible on that shit, hear me?" "Yeah, I hear you." I kind of paused there to think if I knew anyone else with such chemical connections, and Lucius put the now empty glass on one of the steps in the stairwell. "Pleasure doing business with ya," Lucius left. I waved a couple fingers at him, and he took off down the stairs. I put the cigarette out on the wall, notching a fortieth ashen tally on the oval paint. I had a flashback of a premonition, and it became clear to me that the only way to win out would be to do so without anyone else knowing I had. I would have had to stage my own death. *** It wasn't long before the thought of faking my death became most prevalent in my mind, so prevalent, in fact, that I was no longer worried about my actual survival were something to go amiss in the forthcoming retrial of the experiment. I had done my research and was met with the disheartening conclusion that though many people had tried, there were no documented cases of anyone successfully faking his own death. That left very little room for any tried and true instructions. I was on my own. The only certainty that I had was that if I could convince myself I was dead, that meant I could convince anyone. If I was going to fool people into thinking I was dead, I would have to start thinking how a dead person thinks; acting how a dead person acts. Halfway through the bag, and I still hadn't changed the water, but that wouldn't have been very dead of me. The phone rang several times. I'd let the answering machine get it. "This is Johnny. I'm dead forever at the moment. Leave me a message after the beep," beeped the machine. "It's Lu, man. What you want?" I sprang catlike to life and snatched the phone from the receiver. "Lucius, thanks for calling back. I need you to stop by. You still delivering?" "Man, I was just over there like four hours ago," he whined. "Can't you last until tomorrow?" "Maybe," I pondered. "But I don't want to take any chances." "Shit," he elongated and paused, and I heard tapping. "Are you in a car?" I asked, and waited before continuing. "It sounds like you're driving." "Give me twenty minutes." Say what you will about Lucius Shazam, but he was the best. After you deal with Lucius, you only want to deal with Lucius. He was always there when you needed him, and you just couldn't get that kind of reliability for free. In the meantime, I had begun adhesively arranging the amassing of anatomical diagrams, various architectural schematics and the entirety of my collection of forms of identification in an arbitrary pattern about the room. If I was going to do this, it was going to have to be surgical. I heard tapping on the back door, and I knew it was Lucius. If he said twenty minutes, you could count on him being there in ten. It had only been five. "That you, Lu?" I whispered, cracking the door open a sliver. "Would've been here sooner, but I had to stop and pick up some more smokes." He packed the new box in his palm for emphasis, and I pulled him inside and double bolted the door. "Easy, Johnny." "I can't let anyone see me alive," I explained. "So if anyone asks, tell them you haven't seen me, got it?" "Do I even want to know what this racket's all about?" He took the new bag out of his jacket and placed it on the counter. "I've been rethinking the whole cyanide thing." I counted out more twenties and handed them to Lucius. "You know anything about helping someone stage a death?" "Nah, man. That's crazy." Lucius folded the bills and tucked them into his pocket. "I've given it a lot of thought, and I think I can pull it off," I beamed excitedly. "Name one person who's ever successfully faked his own death," he challenged. "Got me there," I conceded exactly. "But you got any other solutions?" "This about that experiment thing?" "The what?" I struggled from strict focus. "Oh, yeah. The experiment thing. Sure." It made perfect sense, though. If I disappeared in a storm of chaos, they could only assume I had died. The experiment would provide the perfect distraction with so many witnesses paying too much attention to their own schemes that they'd never catch on to the details of my disappearance. They'd all be left there to presume. "Hold on. I think I've got something!" I ran over to the wall I had decorated and examined a blueprint I had drawn up from memory and estimations of memories. Lucius slowly filtered in the room behind me. "Man, you got something loose," Lucius juiced. "How much would it take for you to get your hands on a coffin for me?" I ignored. "What makes you think I could get my hands on a coffin?" "Well, can you or can't you?" "It ain't going to be cheap, man," he hesitantly admitted. I knew he'd come through. He had a proclivity for fine suits. "How much we talking?" "Shit, man. Two large." "Two thousand dollars?" I gasped. "And that's with me giving you a deal just so I don't have to put up with your crazy shit anymore," he joked, the truth of his humor peeking through the thinnest of sheets. "I guess I don't have much of a choice," I considered. "I'm going to need it up front." "All of it?" "All of it." "I don't think I have that many twenties," I precalculated. "How's half sound?" "Sounds like I have to take it," he sighed as I tried to figure out whether his pun was intended, or if it was even a pun at all. Lucius followed me to my room, where I opened a shoe box kept on top of other shoe boxes in my closet and carefully started sectioning out the twenties in ten groups of five on my mattress. "I don't care where you get the coffin, but try to get some contacts in the death business from your source," I laid out in piles. "I'm probably going to need some services rendered and documents ordered to run diversions of fraudulent legitimizations in the aftermath of my carefully planned untimely undoing. The sooner we can get things to settle, the easier it'll be. You know what I mean?" "No less than usual." I handed Lucius the stack of bills, and when stuffing them in his pocket failed, he tried dispersing the bills among all of his pockets. It was ridiculous. "Get yourself a suit with bigger pockets while you're at it." I knew he would, too, that son of a bitch. *** I could tell that the experiment was drawing near, but I had lost track of any indication as to an exact time or place. I was more concerned about my own experiment than the larger scale experiment that would serve as a cover for my purported demise. I had decided that an explosion would best suit my needs, but I knew that Lucius would have to draw the line somewhere, and sticks of dynamite were somewhere beyond that line with cyanide and a mangled corpse with similar attributes I chanced requesting. I had to lie to him something fierce about cooking supplies. Lucius did manage to make good on the casket, and on my end, I had studied well for the part. I hadn't eaten for weeks, and I spent most of my time dulling around in the sanctified box. I already looked like a skeleton of myself, and I felt dead, for the most part. I was handily able to purge any thought that didn't further my ability to foster an explosion. Lucius had been able to put me in touch with a cook; an itchy man who never slept. I only knew him as the Owl for his round eyed, vacant gaze that was never really awake. Various beakers and flasks bubbled with dubious fluids that he assured me were volatile. To build up my tolerance to explosions, I had already survived three of increasing magnitude to graduate myself towards an explosion large enough to be believable. I studied the schematics for areas where I could tunnel my way to safety. Tunnels, you see, would play an integral role in both of the concurrently running experiments, so they would undoubtedly attract attention but not suspicion. As long as I could delicately balance somewhere between the two, I'd be golden. I hadn't paid much thought to obtaining a new identity after the whole ordeal went down. I figured it was best that I didn't have advanced notice, so I wouldn't drop any incriminating hints that would connect who I would end up being to who I was. I notched a final ashen tally on the wall. There were too many to count, and I don't much remember why I had started keeping tallies, but if any investigations arose, this would be sure to throw them off trail. Hell, it'd take them ages just to count them all. I looked in the mirror, and I noticed that I had probably been rotting for quite some time. I wasn't sure how I'd be remembered, or even if I would be, but I had been remembered before, and that's what got me into this whole mess. I sat at a typewriter and pecked out my obituary. I'd send it to the papers with no return address. I had paid cash for the stamps to avoid any paper trail. The thought of outlasting this experiment and witnessing its conclusion was beyond distant to me. I accepted that any last embers of hope would have to lose flame. All that was left to do was to pretend to die among a frenzy of strangers, but that's how it would've ended anyway had I any stake left in reality. I tried to keep the obituary short. Aside from my propensity to ramble from time to time, I had no grandiose statements to make and no underlying agendas to promote. There was a time when I thought that might have been the key, but nothing had ever unlocked for me. I always ended up having to slip through the keyhole. And then, after not having all too much to say, I stopped typing altogether, right in the middle of a sentence. Figured it was so I wouldn't know how it would end, but that was more a matter of convenience than anything else. It was best left unfinished anyhow. Resolution would defeat the purpose, even for me. So I took the paper from the typewriter's feed and folded it in half, put stoppers in the vials of my supply and flicked a lighter on to make sure it worked, but that'd only get me so far. They say that preparation would only you halfway there.
