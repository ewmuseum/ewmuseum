Proving it to Jay (not Phoenix)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Mid September I don’t want to be doing this. Don’t want to be sitting here, having to deal with all this pressure to perform. There’s a lot of expectations that come with this sort of thing, and failure isn’t really acceptable. You’re expected to step up to the plate, dig your cleats into the dirt, focus, and knock one clean outta the park. This is what’s expected of me. And it’s a pressure to perform that I don’t particularly care for. I put enough pressure on myself… week in and week out, trying to forge a legacy… achieve some sort of immortality that will pound people’s minds long after I’m gone. “Hey, remember that guy? He was an incredible individual. One of the best evah.” That’s what I expect of myself. That’s a pressure I put on myself week after week as I go to battle. I don’t need the pressure or annoyance of having to live up to other’s expectations. I don’t feel like I should have to prove myself to Jay Mariotti. Most of all, I don’t feel like I should have to deal with pressures and expectations away from the ring. When I come home from a week out on the road, I want to take a couple days to kick back and relax, without having to perform. I don’t want to have to worry about stepping up to the plate. I don’t want to even go to the fucking ballpark. All I want is to enjoy my free time, have a couple beer, enjoy the magnificence that is American football, grill some Strip steaks while the sun sets. And that’s the long-winded, fluffed-up version (cuz everyone knows if you keep it short that must mean you’re not trying) of why I don’t want to be sitting here in the doctor’s office. Ellie is due in about a month, just a few days after The Great American Nightmare. I don’t know if that’s supposed to have some sort of symbolism or not. Who really cares about symbolism anyways? I just know that it leaves me staring down two catastrophically life-altering events in the span of a few days. The doctor is saying that everything is going well, the baby is doing just fine. Ellie is squeezing my hand, occasionally shooting a smile in my direction, and I just smile back, make sure to nod my head from time to time when the doctor speaks. It’s not that I’m not ready to be a father, it’s that I don’t want everyone telling me I have to be one. I can handle raising a baby, but the expectations of raising a kid the way society deems appropriate… not so cool with that. With a slap of the knee, the doctor finishes his speech and sets us free. I don’t waste any time leaving the patient room, heading down the hall and across the lobby. Ellie, god bless her, manages to keep up, stride for stride. “Really don’t like this place, do ya?” “Never liked hospitals, doctors, whatever.” “You sure that’s it?” “What do you mean?” I head across the parking lot, pulling the car keys from my pocket. “I mean no one really likes hospitals and doctors, but for a guy who makes his living getting beat up and needing constant stitches, bandages, and everything… I would think you’d be a little more comfortable around these kind of places.” “For the record, I try to make a living out of not getting beat up.” “My point is, I don’t really think this has much to do with a general disdain for doctors. I think this is a little more specific, sweetie.” “Like being stressed about rapidly approaching fatherhood?” “Yeah, something more like that.” “I’m fine.” Not going to go through the whole ‘expectations’ lecture with her. She might not understand. Or worse yet, she might fully understand and tell me it’s the same thing as being nervous about becoming a father. So I stick with ‘I’m fine’ and climb in the car. “Seems like something’s been on your mind the last couple weeks.” I start the engine, but not without a hesitation I hope she won’t pick up. Ellie doesn’t know about Dave’s mistake and the… small mess it’s left me in. That’s another big thing I’ll have to worry about real soon. Someone else’s expectations to deal with. But I can’t tell her all that; she can’t handle that enormous stress during pregnancy. It’s best anyways, you’re not supposed to tell. Besides, it’s Dave fault (weird how that worked out) I’m in this mess… he should have to tell her. “Lotta stuff on my mind. On top of having a baby on the way, there’s some big stuff going on with PRIME. And ya know… I went and put my career on the line.” She rolls her eyes. It’s what she’s done every time anyone has mentioned the whole career Vs title thing since I agreed to it. “Yeah. I know.” “And there’s a pretty big match this week to boot. I’ve got reservations about my tag partner.” That’s true. Rumor has it my head may be bigger than Tony Gamble. The Grin is devious, mean, talented, and short. I’m not the most limber guy in the world, and if I can’t touch my toes, I might not be able to reach down and make the tag when the time comes. Could cause problems. Plus, I don’t think the guy likes me. “You sure there isn’t anything more than that? You’ve just seemed a little off ever since you came back from Chicago a couple weeks ago.” “I’m sure, baby. Everything is fine.” If I’m as terrible at everything else as I am at lying, I’ll be out of a job a month from now. Or dead.
