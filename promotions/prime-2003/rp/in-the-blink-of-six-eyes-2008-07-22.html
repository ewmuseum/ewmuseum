In The Blink Of Six Eyes

22 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/22/08 "There are some things you learn best in calm, and some in storm." -Willa Cather July 16th, 2008 10:37 P.M. "FUCK!" I’d forgotten what it was like to shake with rage. My fingertips danced in the air, like pressing some sort of phantom keyboard. The shivers started at my shoulders and became almost spasmic when they reached my limbs. I tried to inhale, but the only thing that came was short, hiccupped breaths. "GOD FUCKING DAMNIT!" Ripping at the black tape on my elbows, I swore to no one in particular. Because there was no one else in the parking lot yet. In fact, I’d been standing on the same small patch of worn pavement for quite some time. I’d watched Dawkins get carted into the trainer’s room, watched how he was doing until someone slammed the door in my face. "FUCKING SONOFABITCH!" I felt that degree of anger where you swear not because you care if anyone hears, but because if you don’t let out a sound, it feels like your head’ll explode. I hadn’t yet been able to wrestle my temper; there were several flipped catering tables and broken audio boxes to argue as much. But to watch Shakur’s slimy tendrils slip around the kid...damn near ruptured some vital organs. "Fuck," I hurriedly whispered, "fuck, fuck, fuck." My rage had been fueled by the adrenaline of a fucking screwjob of a loss. But now that the epinephrine was stopping its bold voyage through my veins, I felt joints creak and muscles tighten up. I was coming down from one high, finding myself in need of another. "Fucking shit," I gasped. I was so used to the post-match security of a couple Tylox that I was nearly slipping into an anxiety attack. The pain was unbearable, causing me to lose my balance and drumming up bile. Sucking down a deep breath, I stumbled back inside the arena. In the aftermath of my outburst, the halls were quiet. Mousy backstage workers scurried the other way, pretending to have something to do. Even the contracted muscle didn’t try to stop me from huffing and puffing back to my locker room. In my eyes, they could still see an animalistic glint. I heaved open the door to my locker room and was unable to be anything but surprised at what and who I saw. "Are you Chandler Tsonda?" Now, it’s one thing to ask who’s calling or who’s knocking at the door, but it doesn’t make much sense for me to walk into a room with my name in bold on the door, and be questioned about who I am. The questioner was a particularly harmless looking white guy. From his parted hair to his bland navy suit, everything about him screamed that he was some Cantrell flunkie, up in my shit for no other reason than to stroke the fire. "Are you with Publisher’s Clearing House?" He looked puzzled by my question. "What? No, of course not. What gave you the–" "Then, no. I’m a fucking tumbleweed," I replied, gritting my teeth. Whoever these dudes were, they weren’t your standard C.P. cronies. At first, I’d thought Navy Suit was a company lawyer, but there was a withdrawn confidence to his stance that nixed the theory. "Well," Navy Suit said in a nondescript, level voice, "will you let Mr. Tsonda know that–" "It’s him, Gibbons." Navy Suit, a.k.a. "Gibbons" had a cohort, who was just now emerging from an unseen corner. The second man wore a grey suit, just as predictable and risk-free as the first man’s clothes. I locked eyes with him; he was standing directly in front of my gym bag, in which there was sweet release. "Who? Tsonda?" He turned and looked me up and down. "I expected you’d be…bigger." Neither Gibbons nor his grey-suited colleague were up to any good, if they’d been expecting me at all. Sure, I’d only gotten out of the ring five minutes ago, but there was still enough piss and vinegar coursing through my veins to ugly these guys up, if not take them down completely. "I’ve had a real rough day at the office, fellas," I growled, adding a throaty edge to my voice for intimidation’s sake. "So I won’t think it bad manners if you move it the fuck on down the road." Grey Suit took a step towards me, and instinct compelled me to wheel around, putting myself at the point of a triangle between the three of us. Whether they were Cantrell, Shakur, or somebody else from the laundry list of enemies, the suits had outworn their welcome. "Mister Tsonda, we’re–" "Not here to play Tiddlywinks and make friendship bracelets, I got it." Couldn’t go letting Grey Suit control even a second of the conversation, not when he thought he could just roll into my locker room and ambush me like this. The list of who would want me put down before Colossus was short, but I didn’t take Shakur for the type to send guys in bad suits. Sun Tzu in full-on ninja gear? Absolutely, but these guys were too high-brow to be slumming with my enemy. "What we’re trying to say…" Gibbons initiated a long pause, pulling open his jacket to reveal a holstered sidearm. "…is that we’re here for some official business." "So cut the lip and let us do our job," Grey Suit chimed in. "You numbskulls got badges or are you just some good ol’ Bama boys on a vigilante kick? Gonna go give Charlie some problems? Sneak backstage and see if you can’t ‘scare the ol’ boy somethin’ fierce?’" Gibbons swallowed a word or two, allowing me to finish ridiculing him. He took a deep breath. "I," he stated, puffing out his chest, "am Agent Gibbons. This is Agent Johnsen." He flipped open a badge at eye-level: the letters ATF couldn’t be missed. I exhaled deeply, dropping my hands. I pushed silently past Agent Johnsen’s broad shoulders, digging through my bag for the familiar orange pill bottle. The measure of relief, at realizing these guys weren’t armbreakers of one kind or another, was nothing compared to what I might find inside that bottle. "Good to meet you, too," Johnsen spat. "I’m sorry, asshole, do most people whose offices you invade offer you tea and crumpets? No, so don’t mind me if I tell you that my lawyer’s not here and whatever it is can wait." Gibbons tried to recover, taking a conciliatory tone while I dumped four pills into my palm. "I apologize for startling you," Gibbons told me, in a voice that negated his apology. "But we have some questions we’d like to ask you, ones that can’t, and won’t, wait. Now, we’ve been trying to get a hold of you through a Mister Gil Lavenstein since last week." If they’d already spoken to Gil and he hadn’t harangued them into a different course of action… "And now you’re here." The pills were still in my palm, as I played with ideas of what the bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, and Firearms might need with PRIME Number One Contender, Chandler Tsonda. "Not even waiting until the end of the broadcast, and to talk to me, of all people. I’m a law-abiding citizen, which Gil would’ve told you repeatedly, so you’re in a hurry to find out what I know about somebody else." Neither of the agents said anything. Johnsen pulled up his pant leg to scratch at his ankle, casually revealing to me the service revolver that sat on his calf. He was a prick of a lawman, something that’d taken me only seconds to figure out. "So if we both know all of that, then that means that you’re going to ask about my father, with whom I haven’t communicated in several years." It was a lie, and both of these schmucks, if they knew anything about Calvino, probably knew it. But when the cops or sheriffs or even feds think you have information, but you know you’re squeaky clean, you get a healthy does of leniency in how you get to talk to them. "Well," I said, winking at Agent Johnsen, "this was a real treat. Is the ATF all done here?" I watched Johnsen and Gibbons exchange glances. "What’s that in your hand?" Johnsen was stalling while Gibbons came up with another angle. "Pain pills." "Why don’t I double-check that?" "Well, Agent Johnsen, if I had alcohol, tobacco, or firearms in this little orange bottle, sure. But since I don’t, and these things are about as harmful as a Band-Aid, I think we’ve stepped a bit out of your paygrade, huh?" Johnsen’s mile-wide smile told me that there was an element of this situation I hadn’t grasped yet. He whipped out his badge, which bore similarly bold, but different letters: FBI. Without another word of warning, he snatched the orange bottle from my left hand. I eyed him carefully. Why was the FBI working with ATF? "Agent Johnsen and I work with an interagency task force," Gibbons announced, clearing up at least one part of the mystery. "We are interested in some facts about Ettore Calvino, but you probably know less than you think you do. I’d suggest that you sit down while we ask a couple questions." I sent a suspicious eye back to Agent Johnsen, who was knee-deep in reading the front of the pill bottle. "You got a prescription for these?" "As sure as you’ve got on a clearance rack suit, sweetie." Don’t get me wrong; I’m no idiot when it comes to the law and how quickly the crooked fuckers can put you away for doing nothing wrong. But I’ve been shaken down before by guys trying to get information about my father. I’m sure that the feds have done some really great work on catching somebody’s pops, but not mine. Every couple months, federales of some kind come a-knockin’. They’re so backed up by bureaucratic nonsense that they usually have no clue just how estranged I am from my father. They’ll come in and tell me all the dirt they’ve "got" on me, before offering the one chance at freedom: giving up Ettore Calvino. It got me the first couple times, but I’m hip to it now. And once the geniuses check their facts, they realize that I know about as much about Calvino’s organized crime as an orangutan knows about Shakespeare. Merrily off we go. "You sure sound like just as much of a crook as your old man." "Funny, the only thing we share is that we both know how to irritate paper-pushing dickheads." "Mister Tsonda, could we move along with the questions, so that we might be out your hair and you might be out of Agent Johnsen’s?" Unlike Johnsen and me, Gibbons was relatively clear-headed. He could look past a person if it meant that he might do his job better; us PRIMEates might learn a thing or two from a guy who ignores those differences, but if we did, we wouldn’t be very damn good at our jobs. I looked at the four pills. The anger and then lightheadedness of moments ago had faded in the shadow of this new obstacle. But pain was surfing up my spine, starting with a flare in the small of my back. Somewhere down there was a pinched nerve. "I needa sip first. Would you mind, Agent Johnsen?" He whipped my large Poland Springs bottle at me. I ignored the continued hostilities and unscrewed the bottle. "Says the dosage is two pills," Johnsen sniped. "You got a problem or something?" Now, this was something I hadn’t expected: being interrogated about Tylox in lieu of my father. "You gonna throw me in Gitmo for a high tolerance to paid meds?" I gulped down the water and four pills joyously. "No, but I’ll be sure to let Birmingham PD know to put you away for DWI if you’re found behind the wheel. That Tylox is no joke." "Those of us who haven’t settled for cubicle life still need our physical faculties. And that requires–" "Enough," Gibbons chided. "We know about November 6th. What else is there to know about the contact you’ve had with your father?" I bit my lip, trying to hide how impressed I was. Real detective work had uncovered that I’d met with Calvino nine months ago. But if either of these two knew my role, they’d have been debriefing me until Culture Shock 2009. No broken laws on my part, but a whole lotta intimate knowledge of others’ wrongdoing. If they knew that, why couldn’t they put together a case without my help? "Nothing. On the record, I don’t have any idea what you’re talking about. I’d have to check my records, but I’d guess that on November 6th, I was visiting an old friend in Boston." Johnsen snorted out a laugh, but Gibbons stayed the course. "You’re not a person of interest. Just tell us what you know, so that we can insure your father’s safety." "I’m sure that my father has a whole army of risotto munchers to do that for him." "Not so sure," Agent Johnsen shot back. "Not since he flipped some big Lucchese dummy by the name of Stevie Wonder. Sold him to our people to keep himself outta cuffs." I raised an eyebrow at him. "No shit? Calvino’s trimming the fat and feeding it to the feds? Gotta love America, eh Agent Johnsen? He gets rid of the inefficient schmoes in his lineup, all the while taking your people’s heat off of him because you think you’ve captured somebody important. Just like the movies, right?" I’d never found myself without the mind for my father’s more criminal inclinations. It was just that it didn’t excite me. Sure, there were some qualms about being on the wrong side of the law, but I could’ve lived with that part. More than anything, I tried to make my father and everything he did as repulsive as possible. And I’d succeeded, using the athletic genes he’d donated not for the military or the Five Families, but for a lovely stroll down the runway. Irony loves my body of work. "So if you were your father, and you were in danger, what would you do?" "You’re talking like somebody’s gonna take a real shot at a dying mobster. He’s decrepit, dude. I’d probably go somewhere I knew was safe, somewhere where I was known as good people, not just because of the mobster ties. The hell would I know?" "Mister Tsonda," Gibbons started, his voice cold with certainty, "Steven Crea isn’t some dope-runner just to sate our appetites. We’ve had independent Bureau task forces trying to take him down for racketeering, fraud, and tax evasion for almost four years." "Jesus, save a spot on death row for this monster. I heard they just eat racketeers for breakfast in the clink. Almost as bad as jaywalkers and people who don’t pay their library fines." Agent Johnsen rubbed his hands together excitedly. "You know how they took down Capone? Tax evasion. Paul Castellano? Racketeering. What we know, since we’re damn good at our jobs, is that mob bosses will almost never be near enough to bodies to pin the really terrible shit on them. So you put ‘em away and hope that somebody…gives you a gift while they’re up the river." "Pin the small, but easily convictable shit, on them, and wait for any of their hundreds of enemies to take a shot while they’re exposed. I like the way you think, Johnsen. If you ever need a real job after this little summer internship, come find me and we’ll see if turn you into a fine wrestling manager." Agent Johnsen rolled his eyes, but it appeared that, with the calming effect of the Tylox, things weren’t so strained between the two of us. As I waited on Johnsen for a snappy comeback, Gibbons emerged from self-imposed silent thought. "Do your mother and father ever speak?" "Jesus fuck no. My mother would rather watch the guy caned than speak a word to him." "What if…" Gibbons was having a lightbulb moment, but he didn’t want to say anything out loud. He beckoned Johnsen to come closer and he whispered something into his partner’s ear. "Oh, boo. Secrets are for middle-school girls." "This country’s secrets," Johnsen retorted, "are what keeps it safe. And since I’d hate for harm to come to a single hair on that head of yours, it’s better if you’re left in the dark on this. Your father’s in deep." It was uncanny, the way that Calvino could pop up at the most inopportune moments. Less than fifteen minutes ago, I’d been heat-seeking-missile mad about Bryan Dawkins and Devin Shakur. Now, I was talking shop with two federal agents about my father the Soprano, wondering where he might be (and why the aforementioned feds didn’t know). Talking about a man who’d probably been surveilled one way or another for as long as I’d been alive, and two officers of the law (who didn’t appear all that inept) didn’t know where he’d slipped off to. "I think that’ll be all, Mister Tsonda." Gibbons flicked his tongue against the back of his teeth. "Aww, I was all ready to share war stories, homes." "Sorry, no girls allowed in our treehouse," Johnsen said, with even a hint of amicability. "And we’ve got a mob boss to find." "Just for the record, and since I was so mega-cooperative…" Gibbons and Johnsen both looked at me. Even though I’d offered up jack diddly, it seemed I’d built up enough trust that I had a question or two of my own to get answered. Had to choose my words carefully, if I ever wanted to figure out what was going on (did I?). "…are you telling me that the FBI and ATF have no idea where Ettore Calvino is?" Again, the two agents exchanged glances. Agent Gibbons fielded the question with considerable dexterity, his chameleon of a voice skipping around lightly. "The Bureau has no comment on Ettore Calvino. Agent Gibbons, does ATF have any thoughts on Mister Tsonda’s father?" "They’re just excited to have a lead in what they consider a paramount inquiry." Despite his playful offering of information, Gibbons’s face was blank. "Agent Johnsen, could I have back my bottle of Tylox, please?" With a lazy toss from Johnsen, both of my wishes had been granted. I had a window into why (or what, or how…I didn’t even know the right questions yet). And I had back my (quickly diminishing) bottle of pills, to cope with the next day’s inevitably head-splitting pain. I made a note to myself to re-up at the pharmacy soon. "Don’t go overboard," Agent Johnsen warned. "Those things will kill you." "Duly noted, Agent Johnsen." And so, with the agents out of my hair, I could’ve returned to the all-wrestling mindset that I’d been cultivating for weeks. But curiosity was pulling the strings now. Johnsen and Gibbons had interjected themselves in the middle of ReVolution, probably unbeknownst to the company’s fearless leader. Something in Denmark was fucking rotten. A Universal Title to win, a mystery of the missing pater familias to solve, a wayward student, and a spine that was nearing its breaking point. I would have to attack these problems surgically, one by one, or they might all come crashing down at once. I steadied my hand, wrapping long totem pole fingertips around the orange bottle. But even with a clenched fist, I couldn’t stop small tremors traveling, through achy knuckles, from wrist to fingernail.
