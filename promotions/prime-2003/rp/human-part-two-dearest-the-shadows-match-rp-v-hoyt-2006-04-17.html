Date: 17/04/06 Dearest, The Shadows.Written: 17th April, 2006. 'Sunday is gloomy,My hours are slumberless.Dearest, the shadows I live with are numberless.Little white flowers will never awaken you,Not where the black coach of sorrow has taken you.' - László Jávor. Previously... Part One - Somewhere Over The Rainbow. IV - Dreaming, I Was Only Dreaming. Dinah Carroll carried herself hunched, as if she was sodden in sorrow. A burden her body could barely withstand. Guests would be arriving that evening, but the fresh linen they would be expecting was still half-hanging out of the basket by the washing machine. Sipping from a luke-warm cup of tea, which had been sitting on the table beside her for the hour or so she'd fallen asleep in the chair, Dinah's face didn't even sour as the cold concoction insulted her palette. Behind her, the curtains remained closed, an amber glow threatening to burn through them betraying the time of day. Through the slight gap where the curtains hung, a slither of daylight carved through the shadows, giving solid form to a column of thick dust. The house was once, beside her family, her pride and joy. It was she who neighbours would come to with all their domestic emergencies. Wax dripped on a carpet, stubborn mildew between bathroom tiles, cranberry juice on a white cloth. No matter how bizarre, or sometimes embarrassing, the dilemma, she would call on her knowledge, and an array of ingenious concoctions made from lemon juice, baking soda, and club soda, to save the day. No dust mite was safe from her vacuuming, even when scurrying for cover in the trench between carpet and skirting board. No smudge or streak was allowed to blemish her furniture, or taint her windows. Now, even the family photos, which mournful eyes stared blankly at for hours on end, weren't kept to past standards. Dust amassed in the corners of the frame, where fingers left smudge marks as they wiped across the glass. Around the frames, a grimy pattern adorned the table, with coffee-rings from wet mugs forming elaborate crop-circles, while crumbs freckled the matt, dusty surface. Deeper into the chair's mould Dinah seeped, her own tired sigh matching that of the chair's creak. As the record player crackled to a halt, her right arm limply flopped over the arm, hand enveloping the television's remote control. With a crude, digital chime, light spread across the living room towards Dinah, creeping up from her toes. Against the backdrop of shadow, the screen's glow bleached her face a ghostly shade. Rhythmically tapping her index finger on the 'channel up' button, Dinah Carroll gave her optic nerve its much-needed fix, almost sending it into a fit as colours flooded through once more. As if she resided in Pleasantville, the illumination of the television brought out the colours of the room, venturing bravely into the cobwebbed corners. Continuing the cycle through the channels, her dull, laboured eyes rested upon each for only a few seconds, before switching to the next. In a zombie-like state, slouched, design shows tried to force through her tragic apathy what her house could once again be, but neither was given enough time to make their pitch. Daytime soaps and made-for-TV movies turned bitterly-recent events into cheap storylines, almost letting a little emotion escape through the cracks in dry, unhealthy skin. The time of day given to each channel was then shortened as Dinah reached the sports channels. And then, through the medium of television, salvation was beamed direct into Dinah Carroll’s living room. Just as her finger was about to obey its perpetual rhythm and switch channels, she caught sight of her, breaking the cycle. Arms tightly crossed, a subconscious display of reservation. Shimmering emerald eyes never quite managing to look straight into the camera. Hair a reflection of mood, straight out of nature. Behind her, a highlight reel played, contrasting her natural habitat to the captivity of a TV studio. 'Imogen...' Dinah gasped, the sudden shudder of life nearly leaving her breathless. Eyes rapidly blinked, refreshing her vision over and over again, wanting to see if she'd reboot back into the waking world. Leaning forward in her chair, Dinah's hand hovered out towards the TV, fingers extending as if to stroke the screen. A tear swelled in each eye as wavering lips dragged themselves out from under the tyranny of depression to display a proud smile. 'Oh, my Imogen...' spilled out, emotions unable to remained contained within her. 'I knew, I knew you wouldn't have left me...' V - And The Storm Rushes In... The cold water splashed against Karina Wolfenden's face, refreshing the skin that was still tort from the day's earlier stresses. Head still a little fuzzy from the short nap she'd taken, the K-Wolf tried to expel the sensation with a purging yawn, her whole frame stretching out on tip-toes. Left slightly light-headed from the exertion, her hands rested upon the basin, while the deep breath she exhaled hovered her fringe up just above her nose. 'From now on,' Kari mumbled, miffed at the waste of a weekend. 'I go only where PRIME goes. This stuff never seems to happen unless I go off on my own. Or listen to other people. Or fall in love. Or... well, wake up in the morning... fuck.' Realising she was a slave to the complexities of life, Wolfenden opened the closet, where perfect stacks of crisp, white towels awaited the guest. 'Someone either cares a lot about presentation, or they're one diagnosis short of an OCD,' she couldn't help but remark. Despite feeling it was a shame to disrupt the regimental display, the K-Wolf plucked a towel off the top and ruggedly dried her face, before scraping her hair back into a haphazard ponytail. With another yawn, stripy-sock clad feet began their stagger out onto the hallway. 'Bathroom...' Kari mused, trying to remember the info she'd simply nodded along to. 'Second or third on the left..?' She decided on the former, based on the tried and trusted formula that it was nearest. Supported by the newest theory of that it wasn't farthest away. Rubbing the last of the sleep from her eyes with her right thumb and index finger, she blindly burst into the room. 'Oh,' went the quick remark. 'I really have the knack for not being in the right place lately.' Opposed to finding a bath and shower, the K-Wolf found herself in the doorway of one of the bedrooms. Despite the room being warm enough to almost force beads of perspiration to the surface of Kari's forehead, in her fluster, she wasn't composed enough to find it a little odd that whoever's bed it was needed a thick blanket on top of the sheets. 'Sorry...' Wolfenden offered up with a slightly embarrassed smile. 'Bathroom is the next door along, I take it?' But just as she was shuffling back through the doorway, a lone word lassoed her. 'Imogen?' The words were hazy, a little startled. 'Oh, no, I'm Karina,' she explained, staying in the doorway, hands pressed against either side. 'I'm just a guest here. Long story. And the short version, well, it makes me sound like a weirdo.' The blanket and sheets rippled, baggy eyes peering out from the chasms between. 'Imogen,' was repeated, a sudden zap of energy no longer dragging the name out in laboured fashion. 'I knew you'd come...' 'Um, yeah, as I said, Karina,' the K-Wolf once again labelled herself, shuffling back through the doorway. 'So, I'll just go find the bathroom and leave you in peac-' With mind far more alert than body, the house's owner struggled to sit up in bed. Just keeping her head above the sheets, she pinned them down with her chin. Her face then seemed to melt with the warmth from her proud expression. 'You haven't changed a bit,' Dinah remarked, gesturing to the dresser just as Karina was about to close the door on herself. 'It doesn't matter why you left me, you're back now.' Following the hint's trail, the bemused, yet always inquisitive Karina Wolfenden strolled to the dresser. With the photo standing dominant over the table, there was little need for the K-Wolf's eyes to have to search. It was her... or rather, a possibility of her. Just like a sketch, it resembled, but with just a few differences that couldn't even be identified, just put down to individualism. Like a frustrating, long-distance game of Tetris, the pieces were spun, and had fallen into place. She wasn't one to accept coincidence as the universe's excuse. 'You're fucking kidding me, right?' Wolfenden spat, not in possession of the most compassionate and understanding of bedside manners. 'Who is this?' With the blanket as a shield, Dinah retreated behind the barrier, despite her mothering urge to reach out. 'Why are you being like this?' Dinah despaired. 'Was is something I did?' 'I said WHO IS THIS?' Kari reiterated, snatching the photo from the dresser and holding it up over the bed. 'Why would you do this to me?' Dinah sobbed, eyes bulged open. As a rapid response to the commotion, scurrying steps were amplified to thunderous stomps by the house's timbers as someone rushed up the wooden staircase. 'What's going on here?' Abigail fiercely demanded to know as she lunged into the room. 'I was just leaving,' Kari broke away from the confrontation with, tossing the photo frame onto the bed. 'Before I wake up chained to the bed with my ankles broken, or a kidney missing.' A cold look from Karina froze the eyeballs of Abigail as the K-Wolf stormed past her. Scurrying to the bed, Abigail dropped to her knees, trying to calm and comfort her mother, but attention split. 'Mom, just go back to sleep,' Abigail hurriedly softened her voice to whisper, shaking fingers fumbling the pills at Dinah's bedside. Tossing her bag onto the bed, Karina hurriedly stuffed her belongings back into whichever pockets had the room, stitches stretching to cope with the load. After tossing away a couple of bottles of water to make more room, she paused. Deep breaths stalled thoughts that were starting to spiral out of control, doing their best to screech towards some sort of halting sense. Breathe. Footsteps grew louder, the varnished wooden floor of the hallway sending the pulses down under the K-Wolf's planted feet. 'My advice,' Kari snarled, not even turning as she sensed a presence in the doorway. 'Stay back.' 'This... look...' Abigail tried to explain, going to close the door behind her, only for Kari's fierce glare to snap around and halt her. 'I know you're angry, and I would be too, bu-' Her life wasn't to be toyed with. She wasn't a pawn for anyone's game. 'You don't even want to think about the differences between an angry me, and an angry you,' the K-Wolf warned, almost ripping the zip clean off her bag as she yanked it shut. 'So I'm just going to leave, and when I'm gone, I advise you try a little something called heavy, behaviour-modifying medication.' 'I can't let you leave,' Abigail then defiantly announced, locking the guest bedroom's door shut behind her. 'Not yet.' Before Kari could even uproot a foot, Abigail flicked the key out through the gap between door and frame. 'You've got five seconds,' the K-Wolf announced, fists clenched and whole body recoiled, ready to strike. The shadow of violence so often cast over her returned, sharpening features and hardening her voice. 'And this had better be good...' VI - ...But It Makes Me Feel Like I'm Alive Again. As the sun began to descend from its pinnacle, the raw rays began to roast the back of her neck. Her sandy blonde mane was sprung from its captivity, cascading down to the small of her back. The golden sheen repelled the sun, hair the savoir of fair skin. Breezing down the street, she was almost swept up into the circling breeze by the dreamy, blissful loops that danced mesmerisingly around her brain. Pale rose lips mouthed tantalisingly along with the purring, breathless lyrics, a lullaby to send her drifting deeper into her own world. Like an intangible stream, the wind wisped across the grainy surface of the sidewalk, teasing the hem of her skirt as it flirted fleetingly with the concrete. A world drenched in a ruby hue from her sunglasses retreated into the void behind her eyelids as she almost skipped down the street she'd walked nearly every day of her life. Even blind to all before her, she could avoid the cracks in the sidewalk, just as she'd done with hop-scotch steps as a child. The shrill bark of a Terrier, its leash tied to one of the poles beside the Barber's doorway, snapped her back into the waking world. At one with the world, the birds seemed to share her tune, wildlife almost expected to scurry from the undergrowth to join her as she returned polite waves to those she'd grown up around. A wave for Mrs Harrison, who'd first taken her horse riding. A wave for Mr Lewis who'd paid her the first dollar she'd ever earned for walking his dog. A wave for Mrs Grey, whose husband's funeral was the first she'd ever had to attend. A wave for Miss Grant, who'd taught her piano. A wave for Mr Belotti, whose deli she'd once worked at on Sundays. Each wave warm, individual. All sincerity, no simple routine. Skidding to a halt, she just avoided a deliveryman, stacked boxes of peppers and tomatoes in hand, as he stepped up onto the sidewalk in front of her. With one hand securing the wavering produce boxes for him, she lip-read his apology, breezily returning the pleasantries. A purest of smiles sent him back on his way, a sense of well-being gifted upon him. Cutting through behind his van, she contorted herself around the open doors, completing a spin out into the street. A screech of the brakes, impregnating asphalt with rubber, cut through the rhythmic drumming of her Discman. The day went cold. To be concluded in Human : Part Three - Believe In Angels.
