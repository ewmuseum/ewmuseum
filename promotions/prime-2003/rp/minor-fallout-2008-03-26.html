Minor Fallout

26 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/26/08 “There is no better than adversity. Every defeat, every heartbreak, every loss, contains its own seed, its own lesson on how to improve your performance the next time.” -Malcolm X Every night since Culture Shock, it’s the same dream. It’s all a dream. I’m sitting by a river; it sneaks quietly by, but I just sit there watching. Next to me on the log is Jimmy Nguyen. Except, in real life, Jimmy’s dead. In real life, I had a hand in the injury that drove him from wrestling. Given that he became an alcoholic and sent a self-addressed bullet into his frontal lobe when he couldn’t walk anymore…well, that’s my cross to bear. So why am I sitting next to him. “It’s nice out here,” he starts. His eyes aren’t focused on one thing and his gaze spreads across the sky. We’re somewhere untamed, but not a place that I recognize. “So you lost the big one, eh?” In the dream world, Jimmy Nguyen is up to speed on PRIME’s current events. “Yeah, I guess I did.” I’m lazy with my words, spilling out unedited froth. I release myself from being eloquent. “Never had a World Title shot before this, didya? All those feds and you never got a mano y mano chance.” The words bite, but his tone is steady; the man sitting next to me on the log knows things he shouldn’t. I rub my temples until my forehead gets hot with friction. “Never did.” When Jimmy laughs, he emits a wry, scaly sound. It’s like water harshly slapping against the side of a canoe. His throat ends nowhere. “And you told yourself that when you got that one shining chance, that beacon of opportunity, that you would redeem yourself for sins past.” “I thought that if I-” “Don’t lie to me! You killed me once, don’t you remember?” For a second, I’m conscious. I realize that this haunt lives only in my own head; he knows only what I know. I can lie to Jimmy. But the moment snaps itself in half like a suicide. “You killed yourself. I was just doing my job. Besides, what does this have to do with my career?” We’re yelling and ruining the countryside. I watch the river crawl away as I wait for answers. All of a sudden, I feel much older. “Where do you think you can go anymore?” He laughs again and I retreat, shivering. I fold both arms over my chest, as moist air slides into a halo around me. Fish jump from the water and splash back down quickly, like bad omens. “What do you do when you climb the mountain and slide all the way back down?” “I suppose you climb up again.” “You won’t. Appeal to your pride and become a menace again. Make it about hurting others again. Abandon the white knight routine. We know.” When he stretches his arms out high above his head, it’s dusk. Hollow orange streaks touch the half-pink sky. “You climb up again because…” We are both quiet for longer than I expect. My teeth chatter and Jimmy slides his hands into the soil. “You question yourself and imagine what the summit really feels like. And you know you deserve to feel that feeling.” I forget the exact words when I really wake up; I only remember trying to evoke the indescribable. “Jimmy?” He coughs or laughs. I can’t see his face; I don’t know. “What will you do differently…comrade?” He sees the blind rhetoric. He knows that I’m walking without a purpose back to a woman that just threw me out. “A man creates the myth that he lives to be the best. What happens when the myth is exposed? When the blinds let in real sunlight?” “I…don’t know.” It’s all a dream.
