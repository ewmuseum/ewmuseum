Cloudy: Blue Moon and Cigarettes

8 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/8/09 He cupped his hands and flicked desperately at his lighter, but there wasn't a drop of fluid left in the thing. It’d been less than a month since he’d started smoking, and Dave hadn’t learned the ropes well enough to know that he should always carry a small pack of matches. Hopelessly, he tried a couple more times, but they failed as miserably as the first dozen. Shoving the lighter in his pocket, David looked around, then nodded his head towards the lanky guy tending bar. “Hey bud, you gotta light?” “I got ya.” The old guy to Dave’s left scratched his graying beard as he dug a Zippo from his back pocket, sliding it across the bar. Dave took the lighter, finding a flame on the first try, and lit his cigarette. With a small nod of the head, he handed the Zippo back and took a long drag from his smoke. “Whatcha here for?” Looking around him, making sure that he was in fact, sitting at a bar, Dave cocked an eyebrow at the obvious response. “To drink.” “Everybody drinks for a reason.” “Yeah. To get drunk.” “Hey, alright pal… I can take hint.” The old man pushed himself off the stool and moved down a seat. “Drink in peace.” Dave ignored the gesture, grabbing a nearby ash tray as he took another drag from his cigarette. Looking to his wrist, he pushed up his shirt sleeve and checked his watch, expecting the worst. Ten minutes late... as usual. "Prompt motherfucker." He bit his lip sarcastically as he talked to no one inparticular. "Nother Bud Light?" The bartender gave a quick point towards Dave's empty glass. Dave's eyes darted from the bartender to his drink. Grabbing the base of the glass, he picked it up and turned it in his hand, as if he hadn't even noticed it was as empty as his lighter, only a couple drops... more backwash than beer... remaining. "Actually, you have Newcastle?" "Not on tap." "Bottles fine." "You got it." The lanky kid took the empty pint glass from David, then headed to the other end of the bar, rummaging around underneath till he rose with bottle in hand. Walking back down towards David, he popped the top and delivered it with a short slide across the bar. "There ya be." "Thanks." Dave took another glance at his watch, even though not more than another minute had passed. Here he was, kind enough to agree to meet, and the jackass couldn't even show up on time. Another drink from the beer... another drag from the cigarette. Another minute passing with no arrival. "Hey, go ahead and close out my tab. I'm gonna split." "Sure thing." The bartender pressed a couple buttons on the touch-screen register. "It's fourteen." David lifted his right ass cheek off the stool and fetched his wallet. Unfolded the beat-up hunk of leather, he pulled out a twenty and tossed it on the bar. "Keep it, bud." The lanky fellow gave a nod of appreciation as Dave put out his cigarette and took one last drink from his beer, leaving half of it behind. Spinning off the stool, he let his feet find the dingy floor, his soles instantly sticking to ground. No sooner had he peeled the rubber from the bottom of his shoes than he saw the door of the pub swing open and the six and a half foot frame of his brother step inside. "Dave. Sorry I'm late, man." "Save it. Not like you've ever been on time for anything in your life." "Looks like you were about to leave." "I was tired of waiting." "You wanna beer? I'm buyin." Kaiser walked past his older brother, heading straight for the bar. Dave just turned his head and looked over his shoulder, not sure what to make of Kaiser assuming he'd just stay. "No thanks." "If you're not drinkin', why'd we have to meet at the bar?" Kaiser waved down the bartender. "Can I get a Blue Moon?" "Cuz you insisted on meeting, and thanks to the government, ya can't smoke in restaraunts anymore. Besides, I've already had a beer or three." "When did you pick up smoking? And why in God's name did you start?" "About a month ago, and because it helps my with my fucking stress levels." "What do you have to be stressed about." The bartender delivered Kaiser's beer, and the younger Vashaun brother immediately plucked out the orange and tossed it aside, then took a large drink. "While you've been out playing tough guy on TV and knocking up your girlfriend, I've been the guy left behind to hold the family together. On top of that, I've got bills to pay, a car in need of new shocks... I dunno exactly what kind of money your making now hot-shot, but don't forget that not everyone is raking in the cash." "Dave, if you need to borrow a few bucks, I..." "I don't need to borrow any money. Don't need any charity or gifts or loans. It'd be nice if you grew up and took responsibility for a few things in your life." "That's actually what I wanted to talk to you about. I've been doing some thinking over the last month or so... talking with Ellie about the future and the hurdles we're gonna need to jump and stuff. And I've been thinking... maybe before I tackle the fatherhood thing, I should try to take care of things as a brother." "What do you mean?" "I've been thinking I should talk to Michelle." "Are you serious?" "I dunno." Kaiser lifted his pint into the air, holding it towards the light. His eyes danced around the curvature of the glass. The unfiltered nature of the beer gave it an unusual thick, cloudy appearance... one he couldn't see through to the other side. "I feel like I can't let things stay like this with our baby sister. It's been over two years now. That's ridiculous. You think she'll be up for it?" "Kaiser, I don't think she was ever pissed at you. I think you were pissed at yourself and you took it out on her by shoving her away and shutting her outta your life when she needed you most. Two years ago, I don't think she'd have held anything against you." "But now?" Dave took a deep breath and headed over to the bar, taking a seat on the stool next to where his brother was standing. "Sit down. Next round is on me." Kaiser gave a brief chuckle and took a seat. "Thought you have bills to pay." "I do. Bar tab is one of them." Dave adjuste his stool, scooting it into a comfortable positin and folded his arms across his chest. "You really gonna talk to her?" "Surprised, huh?" "I believe you musta hit your head or something." "Yeah." Kaiser took a drink of his beer, then set the pint down next to the withering orange slice. "I hit my head a lot actually."
