Ozymandias

Date: Feb 28/05 I met a traveler from an antique landWho said: Two vast and trunkless legs of stoneStand in the desert. Near them, on the sand,Half sunk, a shattered visage lies, whose frown,And wrinkled lip, and sneer of cold command,Tell that its sculptor well those passions read,Which yet survive, stamped on these lifeless things,The hand that mocked them, and the heart that fed,And on the pedestal these words appear:'My name is Ozymandias, King of Kings:Look upon my works, ye Mighty, and despair!'Nothing beside remains. Round the decayOf that colossal wreck, boundless and bareThe lone and level sands stretch far away. A dusty, old ring sat in the middle of a warehouse. The warehouse had obviously lacked severely in human attention for some length of time. The only light in the place came from cracks around the plywood which boarded up the windows. Dust floated around in the paths of light cast down onto the ground. A sound shattered the sterile serenity as, with a sharp thud, the lock on the door was released for the first time in some three years. A few rats and cockroaches scurried frantically from the light that flooded into the place as the door swung open dramatically. A lone figure loomed in the doorway, a silhouette. Ghosts of the past swirled around, smells, sights and sounds of months and years gone by. Glories come and gone. Greatness then, little more than a faded, fleeting memory now. Oh, and the memories. The walls were littered with memories, memories of things forgotten. Things lost in the darkness to which they were lent those years ago. Until now— The silhouette from the doorway reached to its right and hit a switch. After a brief flicker a lone light came on, just above the ring. The light hung down about the place with a lingering, reminiscent feel. Bathed in yellow the figure stepped through the threshold and into the past – his past. His eyes were caught on many different things that found themselves at various locations within sight. The four walls were covered in bleachers, once surging with electricity, now without even the faintest spark. Once occupied by as many as 3,000 people has since become home to local vermin. Stacked and covered with overturned chairs and exercise equipment – and dust. Directly in front of him was a small video screen and an entranceway which led down to the ring. He could see the smiling facing of young hopefuls who’d almost made it and old-timers winding down their careers. They walked side-by-side through the curtain to the squared circle. They fought arm-in-arm and hand-to-hand. To his right the bar – a glass encasement filled with pictures of once famed wrestlers, the best at their sport, signed, sealed, and pardoned from recollection. He could still see ‘Iron’ Mike, the bartender pouring drinks for fans who’d come to see the shows. Above the bar a poster— Pacific Coast Wrestling Extravaganza! Professional wrestlers versus the best of Catch-As-Catch-Can! Singles MatchBuckingham Billy vs ‘Darling’ David Donaldson Tag Team MatchScotty 2000 & Rockford vs ‘Chainsaw’ Charlie and The Sasquach Submission MatchZoë vs Eve Tag Team Elimination First Blood MatchShadow & Reaper vs Adam Weir & Graeme Weir Ladder MatchFurious Frederick vs Jiant Jim Jones Three Way DanceLightning vs Draven vs Jeremy Postal MAIN EVENTTag Team MatchJohnny Postal & Brian Postal vs Aftermath & Prelude The man let out a sigh after reading that card. A haunting memory. The last big endeavor that he’d undertaken, only to end up being undertaken himself. He had a plan. A method. He’d thought it through a thousand times if he’d thought it through once. And yet, that invariable variable was his downfall. It was his biggest risk, his best attempt and his worst failure. Many records and just as many disappointments. What would make this week any different? Yes, the man is none other Our Hero, First Blood. He walks, stepping softly so as to not disturb the ghost of wrestling past that now lingered the halls and kept the building. He ran his hand along a grit-ridden ring skirt before grabbing hold of the top rope and pulling himself into the squared circle. The snap from his relinquishment of the ropes sent a million flakes of dust into the air, sifting to the ground like confetti. Brief snippets of sound fluttered through his ears. History – his story. As recent, even, as PRIME’s own Revolution 55 when he declared, officially, to have become a part of the Dark Age. Was he evil? An antichrist? Satan in carnet? No. In fact he was little more than an opportunist. He wanted to get his shot at the top. His place in someone else’s history. He wanted, right now, the Intense Title. And the Dark Age was the best way to go about it. As long as they stayed faithful and useful to him, he would stay faithful and useful to them. It wasn’t personal, just business, that’s how he operated. Emotions and business make strange bedfellows. Better to leave one’s emotions at the door. Why let yourself become personally involved? It would surely lead only to letdown after letdown. Still, he couldn’t shake the feeling that he was inadequate. Killean was younger, stronger and in his prime. A two time Intense Champion and Universal Title contender. Besides, Pierce had lost to the man thrice in as many meetings, which doesn’t include a pinfall at the hands of the Supreme Machine which would see Our Hero eliminated from the Dual Halo match at Culture Shock. The odds were severely stacked against First Blood and he knew it. Pierce stood, blanketed in cyan serenity under the sole light in the room. He closed his eyes and let his demons have their way with him. They were quick to remind him why he was a failure clinging desperately to what little notoriety he had left. Pacific Coast Wrestling – Five Years Earlier Both men laid on the mat, both bleeding and both battered and both barely conscious. One, Daniel Pierce, who had just earned himself the nickname ‘First Blood’ for drawing the crimson from his 43rd consecutive opponent – the other man on the mat – “Mean” Jerry Dean. Pierce had been the reigning Heavyweight Champion for nearly five months. He’d sworn that he’d beat Jerry ‘Atric’ Dean or leave PCW forever. The crowd was on his side. He was actually being cheered for, if you can imagine that. Choruses of “Dan-iel” could be heard from a nearly deafening crowd of 3,000 gathered in the place. There was blood all over him. In his eyes and his mouth. He looked almost as white as Dean. The two crawled, slowly and dramatically as the referee counted to ten. They aimed themselves at opposite corners. They grasped at the turnbuckles, struggling to get to their feet. After a bout lasting no less than 35 minutes the men were sapped. They’d had nothing left to give. Dean had a large gash behind his right ear that bled like lightning down his neck and across his jaw from a bare turnbuckle spot gone awry. The officials were fairly certain that he had a concussion but he refused to stop the match. As the referee rounded 8 the men found a vertical base. They stood, for a moment, collecting themselves, then lunged forward into a shoulder-and-elbow-tie-up, which Pierce won, bringing Dean up with a Fisherman Suplex. But Dean swung his weight backwards against the move and planted Pierce with a devastating Implant DDT. ONE! TWO! THREE! That three count by referee Jeff Smyth is the same one he hears every time the pinfall is counted against him. It rattled his brain. Four encounters with Killean lead to three pinfalls and one loss. He knew, or at least he was able to convince himself that this match was beyond his control, beyond his hope, beyond even a chance of winning. Pierce tore his jacket from his frame and threw it with a furious passion that ignited the room. He let out an incoherent sound, a grunt, as he threw the thousand-dollar jacket to the ground outside the ring. He knew he needed to face his demons. This thing couldn’t beat him. He hade to prove it to himself that he could handle this challenge and the he could recover from anything that was thrown his way. This time his proof was in the gold. Once he had become Intense Champion he could shove it in the face of all the doubters. Just then a knock came on the doorframe. Pierce whipped around on the spot and looked at another silhouette in the doorframe. Man: I heard some noise in here, are you guys re-opening? Pierce: Sorry, we’re closed. Man: Oh, sorry to bother you. With that the man’s figure disappeared again. Another reminder of another failure.
