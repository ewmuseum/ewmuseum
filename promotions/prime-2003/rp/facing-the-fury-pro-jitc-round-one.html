Facing the Fury... Pro - JitC Round One

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/11/20 “Bad Motherfucker baby! Bad Motherfucker!” Tyson Briggs’ right eye was swollen shut, purple and puffy with a trickle of crimson creeping down from a cut just underneath the mouse. His butterscotch skin was stained with blood, both from himself and from his opponent. His bruises and welts were numbed by the adrenaline hammering through his veins. He was still amped, walking down the hallway like a robot, fighting his muscles’ urge to burst in a kinetic flurry of mayhem. Alex Creed smiled as he walked behind his friend and training partner, safely staying out of the way of Tyson’s red-stained, taped fists that still peppered the air. A lighting-quick combo battered another invisible opponent in front of him. “Did you see that shit?” Tyson’s voice was loud and filled with emotion. Tonight had been a huge night for Briggs, a night Alex hoped would be the first of many victories for the young fighter. Creed put a hand on Tyson’s sweaty shoulder. “I was right there, you lunatic. I saw it.” Tyson pushed open the door to his very own locker room, a courtesy granted to few in the fledgling mixed martial arts promotion, Fury Pro. Alex knew Tyson would have to focus in his first mixed martial arts bout, so he pulled some strings and got him the VIP treatment. Once inside the room, Briggs was slowly starting to throttle down from his fight. Slowly. His eyes were still wide with excitement when he said to Alex, “Look at this face kid! Still pretty!” “You know why you’re pretty?” Alex queried. “It’s because you followed the game plan. We set up a strategy, and you executed it.” He tossed a towel to Briggs. “Keep that up and in no time at all, you’ll be the Number Two Light Heavyweight in Fury Pro.” Tyson used the towel to wipe the blood and sweat from his face. “Maybe then you’ll give me a shot at that pretty gold belt of yours,” he said with a cocky smirk. Alex feigned concern. “Are you kidding? You’re a bad motherfucker! I wouldn’t want you to mess up my boyish yet ever-so-masculine face.” Briggs followed Alex over to the prep table and picked up a bottle of water. A second bottle was tossed to Ivan Ostrepkov, the fireplug of a Russian who had been putting both Alex and Tyson through a grueling – yet successful - training regiment. Grappling, striking, submissions, Alex and Tyson were subjected to it all in brutal fashion. The end result was a World Light Heavyweight title for Alex, and a knockout victory in Tyson’s debut bout. Tyson poured the cool water on his face and finished wiping himself down. “Nah man. I gotta fight you. You’re the king. If I want to be the best, I gotta fight the best.” Kind words from a kid who, not four months ago, would have knifed Creed in the parking lot and stolen his wallet. He’d come a long way, both as a fighter and as a human being. Alex was happy to have given Tyson the opportunity to better himself when Briggs approached him one night after a fight. “Now let’s not make this about me Cheech. You did good tonight.” Alex toasted Briggs with a bottle of water. “Look, even Crazy Ivan is pleased.” They both turned to the Russian. His expression hadn’t changed, his face still carved from a block of unhappy granite. “You sure man?” asked Briggs, unconvinced. “He just looks pissed off.” Alex pointed at the Russian’s stoic face. “Look at his eyes. There’s a lot less menace in them.” Ivan’s paw of a right hand closed around the water bottle, squeezing it until the screwed cap popped off. Creed reconsidered his observation. “Maybe it’s just the lighting in here.” Tyson picked up another bottle of water, unscrewed the top, and poured it into his mouth just as Fury Pro owner, Ray Berg, walked in with the resident physician trailing after him. “So much for this room being an ‘Asshole Free’ zone,” Alex muttered under his breath. Ray stepped around Ivan, who continued to hold a silent vigil by the door, ignored Alex’s presence completely, and anxiously shook Tyson’s taped, bloody hand. “Good fight Briggs! Fucking amazing knockout!” Alex cringed slightly and wondered how many times Berg had practiced delivering that line with sincerity. Briggs didn’t seem to notice. “Thanks, I -“ Berg’s attention suddenly cut to Creed, and the smile disappeared from his face. “Alex can I speak to you in the hallway?” Alex watched Tyson deflate in front of him. He felt a little embarrassed, a little sympathetic for Briggs, and a whole lot of pissed off at his employer. Still, he had a hunch this conversation was coming. He’d read on the Internet that Berg was unhappy with his Champ, and it wasn’t like the internet to lie to him. After all, he was a former Internet Champion back in his “The Mecca” Alex Creed, his fWo days. “Uh, okay sure. Hey doc, check our boy and make sure he doesn’t have brain damage. Or, at least, any new brain damage.” He smirked at Briggs. “I know he started the day off with quite a bit.” Tyson smiled back and flipped him the bird as he sat down. While the doctor questioned him, Ivan pulled out a small pair of scissors and started cutting the bloody tape off Tyson’s hands. Alex opened the door and stepped out into the hallway. Ray took a final look at Ivan’s deft scissor work before following Alex out. “They teach him that at KGB school?” he asked with more than just a little patronizing tone in his voice. “I’m sure it was on the curriculum,” Alex said flatly. He leaned against the wall and looked up and down the hallway. They were alone, which seemed unusual until he concluded that Berg orchestrated the circumstances. Ray pull out his handkerchief and cleaned his hands with it. He was agitated, and it showed. “Is there any particular reason you’re pissed off,” asked Creed, “or is it just your time of the month?” Berg stuffed the handkerchief into his pocket. “Smartass. This is about you trying to ruin my promotion.” Well that wasn’t exactly what Alex was expecting. “Did I miss a meeting?” he asked. “How exactly am I trying to ruin Fury Pro?” Berg raised his voice as another fighter turned to start down the hallway. It was enough to prompt the fighter to turn around and walk away. “You’re the Light Heavyweight Champion of the World,” Ray told Alex. “It’s taken years of my life, and the blood, sweat, and tears of fighters better than you, to make that belt worth something. Now you’re trying to drag it through the mud of professional wrestling.” Leaning time was over. Alex pushed off the wall and stepped toward his boss, forcing Berg to take a quick step backward. “Mud? I seem to recall you hiring me in the first place because I was a professional wrestler. Remember? Disney was backing the fWo at the time, and their media machine was behind me one hundred percent. I was on the cover of fitness magazines, showing off the best abs in the business. I was doing sneaker ads and I had a line of fitness wear. I was partying with Lindsey Lohan back when she was both hot and cool, before she became skanky and pathetic. I was going to be the new face of mixed martial arts. What did you call me? The new King of Combat?” “And I delivered,” Ray told him. “Kid, you’re the King right now. The golden boy of Fury Pro. People laughed at you when you first started, never took you seriously because you were a sports entertainer who wanted to be a real fighter. After all those beatings you finally have the respect of the MMA community, and now you want to take the reputation of my promotion and parade it around in a circus. Is that any way for a leader to act?” The man’s duplicity was almost enough to prompt Alex to smack the ever-loving shit out of him. “A leader supports his people,” Creed explained, and realized his words were probably falling on deaf ears. “A leader puts forth a positive example by taking on all challenges, not just the ones that make him look good. That’s what I’ve done, bubba. I went to bat for Tyson –“ He saw the blank look on Berg’s face – “the guy you just congratulated, you silly bastard.” “Watch your –“ This time Alex took the proverbial piss out of his employer by cutting him off. “PRIME is giving me a great opportunity, and I’m not going to let to pass me by.” Ray seemed to struggle for a moment before finding the answer he was looking for. “I can always strip you of the belt.” Alex fired back immediately. “The man makes the belt, not the other way around.” “Did your old man tell you that?” Ray mocked his champion. The sudden change that came over the young Creed forced Berg to immediately regret that course of action. Alex grabbed Berg by the throat, spun him around, and slammed him up against the wall. “We’re not mentioning him again.” His voice was cold. “Ever.” Berg begged off, holding his hands up in front of him. “Sure sure, okay.” Creed released Ray’s throat and his expression softened. “If I’m the King of Combat, I have to compete in the Jewel in the Crown Tournament.” It was a statement of fact. “So that’s what I’m going to do.” Alex didn’t give Berg a chance to contradict him. He turned and re-entered Tyson’s locker room. When Tyson looked up and smiled, cold compress wrapped around his head, Alex knew he was making the right decision. So much was expected of him, there was no way he couldn’t compete. Heavy was the head that wore the crown, but “The Mecca” wouldn’t have had it any other way.
