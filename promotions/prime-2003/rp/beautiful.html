Date: Colossus VI Paul Johnson was not a typically happy person. On this particular day, he was only worse. I’m not supposed to be here today, he thought to himself. His white ’88 Chevy Silverado pulled up to the sterling house with a heavy look on his face. Two or three police cars sat in the driveway, several officers of the law out on the lawn talking. A fire truck and an ambulance had parked on the edge of the street and a throng of on-lookers had gathered. Paul had a bad feeling about this. He stepped out of his car and lit a cigarette as one of the officers walked towards him. “Aren’t you supposed to be in there?” the man asked Paul in a stern tone. Paul held up his finger as he took a drag off the cigarette and exhaled the puff of smoke out into the air. “I’m going,” Paul responded gruffly. He wanted to tell the man that he wasn’t supposed to be here today, but something sounded off about it. After another quick puff of the cigarette, Paul dropped it onto the concrete and stepped on it with the bottom of his shoe. “So, tell me what’s happening.” His eyes darted towards the front door where he saw a woman, a little older, sitting on the steps with tears welling in her eyes. Paul figured that she was the boy’s mother. Sad day indeed for her. His callous fingers reached into his pocket. “Looks like one of the sisters called it in. He’s in the bathroom, refusing to come out.” “Anybody in there with him?” “No. Figured that we should let you do your thing before we did anything.” “Fair enough.” Paul pulled his hands out and in his right was a white handkerchief that he dabbed on his forehead, cleaning up the beads of sweat that had formed on his forehead. A few of the officers looked over at him with a quick flicker of the eyes, but he never met theirs. He walked towards the front steps and looked at the woman who sat there. He debated saying something, but thought better of it. Stepping into the house, he saw something that you only saw in one of those fancy magazines. A pool of spit settled in his mouth and he spat it out into a plant by the front door. His dark brown eyes, hardened by a lifetime of horrors, felt a little shocked by the house. The closest he had ever come to walking into such a place had been twelve years prior when he tried his best to talk a serial murderer out of killing his latest victim. Needless to say, that had been a disaster in and of itself, and the smatterings of blood that stained the house ruined it for him. He felt a buzzing in his back pocket and pulled his cell phone out. As Paul looks at the phone, a woman frantically rushes up to him. Paul makes a mental note of her long hair and her pregnant state. “Are you here to help him? Please, help him.” Paul nods his head, ignoring the phone call in the process. “I’m on my way. They told me he wasn’t in any danger, and I just wanted to take some mental notes of the place. Trying to see if there’s anything that I can help cajole him to come out, to want to stick around.” “Please, just get him out.” His tongue ran over his lips. He looked around again and walked towards the stairs where the woman had come from. A few steps up and he saw another woman standing outside of a door, tears running down her cheeks, and her body pressed against the wooden partition. Paul figured that this is where the man was. “Ma’am, what’s your name?” The sound of his voice filled the entire hall and she turned towards him. “Andrea.” He nodded his head in response. “Give me some time with him. Go downstairs. Your mom is going to need you.” Paul expected her to put up a fight. The exact opposite happened as Andrea dropped her head and walked past him without even muttering a single word. Paul walked up to the door and sat down on the ground. He let out a sigh. “Just so you know, you’re not alone. My name is Paul Johnson; I’m with the Los Angeles Police Department, where I work as a mediator. I’m the guy that you see on television that talks the terrorist out of blowing up the buildings except I don’t really do any of that because, well, once the building actually went off. Killed men, women, and kids. Ever since then, can’t do it anymore. So they send me to these situations, where the person is on the verge of killing themselves.” Nothing from the other side. Paul expected that. “Anyways, enough about my shithole of a life. Why don’t you tell me what’s wrong, David?” *** I think I want to die. Don’t know why my family has a hard time realizing this. It’s not even a certainty though. However, I’ve also downed a bottle of Jack Daniels’ finest, started on a bottle of tequila, and am half-way done with some gin. Never liked gin. Don’t know why it’s in the house. That and the 9mm resting against my left leg would probably give them reason to fret. That would probably explain why they called the authorities and have this Paul Johnson sitting outside of my door. Most people would think that I’m a rosy person, that nothing could be wrong in my life. I wrestle for a living and live a good life. I’ve got family, a girl who I got pregnant, and am slowly, maybe even quickly, moving up the ranks in the world of PRIME. That’s what people see when they look at me. What they don’t see are my eyes, a pair of eyes that tell a story of stress, pressure, and sadness. I personally think it all started the moment Philadelphia walked back into my life. “Paul, it’s awfully sweet of you to be here, but I think you’re wasting your time.” There’s silence on the other end. Judging by the shadows entering the room, I can tell that he’s sitting on the floor, resting against the crook of the door. “You have alcohol in there with you?” I look around. “Yeah.” My voice comes out a little strained. I try to suppress the smile on my face. “I’m going to assume that you’re working on your second and third bottle now.” This guy was good. “You got a gun in there, David?” “Yeah.” “Then yeah, I’m not really wasting my time with you. So, why don’t you tell me what’s going? What’s pushed you to this point?” What do I tell him? Well, Paul, I signed a contract with PRIME five months ago, and from that very moment a shitton of pressure was tossed onto my shoulders. I started defeating some people and some seven foot goliath, isn’t that funny? David and Goliath, oh, you and me Paul could have some good ol’ times. Anyways, Hessian, he decided he was going to stick his nose in my business. Mess with me a little bit. Then, of course, some other fuckface got in my way. Chainz. Defeated him and three others to earn a shot at a title that I didn’t even want in the first place. Oh woe is my story. “Just a tough day at the office.” I know he won’t buy it. “I think we all have tough days at the office, David. We don’t go into bathrooms with enough alcohol to throw a kegger and a gun just in case we wanted to have a real good time.” I think of something smart to say. “If that was the case, Paul, then you’d be out of a job.” Good one. “Touché.” So, on top of winning that title shot? I get to go up with a Hall of Famer. Oh yeah, Jonathan Rhine. You might have heard of him. He lost his eye. So, when he wins that title, I have to hear it from everyone. All of the pundits and critics, the fans and the boys in the back, they told me I didn’t have the gall to step into that ring with him and actually take that title from him. Imagine what that would do to a guy like me, someone fresh in the sport, with everyone against me. It pissed me off, but I felt the weight on my shoulders also grow heavier. “On my way in, I met a pregnant woman. Is that your girl?” “Nope.” “Do you just normally keep pregnant women in your house?” “Not normally.” “So, your woman? Your kid?” “One of the two.” “Fair enough.” I unscrew the top of the tequila and start to drink the bottle, hoping there’s a prize for me at the end of the bottle. But, the burning sensation in my throat overwhelms me before I get a chance to find out. I pull the plastic away from lips and feel my chest start moving heavily up and down. “I think you got a pretty good reason to live. I don’t know, but you have a kid on the way. Sounds good to me.” “You got kids?” Paul remains silent on the other end. I know that I’ve hit a spot. “You want to see your kid, David?” Not really, I think to myself. I don’t voice that opinion though as I pick the gun up and look at it for a long minute. I press it against my temple, thinking of taking the coward way out. How sad is that? I know this is the cheap way out and yet I still entertain that idea. What kind of man am I? “David?” I don’t answer him. Instead, I put the gun back down and take another sip of the alcohol. You don’t have to be such an asshole about it. A chuckle leaves my lips. “Sorry, I only learn from the best.” “I’m sorry?” Paul talks this time and I try to ignore him. Do you think this is going to solve your problems? That this will let you exorcise those demons of yours?” “Look, sorry, I’m not the kind of person to have an affair with someone or have a second life. Just not my style. That might work for you, but it’s not going to work for me. So, I handle things my own way. Got it?” Paul clears his throat. I envy him in that moment. “David, who are you talking to?” “Sorry, Paul. My father decided to jump into our own little conversation.” Paul takes a moment to gather himself. “I thought you were in there by yourself?” “I am. My dad is dead.” I look up into the mirror where Shawn just looks at me, with that stupid fucking grin on his face. I pull the gun closer to me. *** “David.” I stir from my sleep. I don’t know how long I’ve been out, and my head is pounding, badly. “What’s up?” I don’t even feel my lips move. My entire body is numb at this point. I’m not sure I can feel my toes any longer. “Time to end this game, don’t you think?” I shake my head no, wanting my father to just shut up. I try to pull my body up, but it feels so done, so used and abused. I just want to roll over and fall asleep, maybe for the last time. Unfortunately, it seems like my asshole of a father is determined to make me suffer in my last moments. I think about punching him in the face when I die, because I’m certain that I’m going to the same place that he’s already at. “I’m not playing any games. Not any longer.” “So, you just plan on dying?” “Sounds like a good plan to me, doesn’t it? I can stop disappointing people.” He goes silent and I smile. It’s like he doesn’t even know what to say now. For once, I’ve shut him up. Thank you, Jesus, thank you. I slowly open my eyes, but feel the pain and want to shut them. The only problem is that I see a bottle of alcohol just across from me and I reach out towards it. Oh, wait, fuck. My arms are against me now. Fuck them. “Is that what you keep thinking? That you’re disappointing people?” I roll my eyes. He was always a condescending cocksucker. Only makes me angrier. “Yeah, I do.” “That’s just sad.” “Well, screw you too.” The vitriolic that drips from my throat is pure nastiness, an edge that I didn’t even know I had in me. It’s sad that I like it. “Do you not see what you’re doing to yourself? To the people around you? Your mom, she’s balling her eyes out. Your sisters too. On top of all that, you’re not even thinking about Philadelphia and the fact that you’re about to become a father. That’s being selfish, David, straight up selfish. I never took you to be a selfish person. I guess I thought wrong.” I roll over onto my stomach, surprised that I have that much strength left in me. “I’ve let all of them down, don’t you realize that? I can’t support them, I can’t be their emotional center and rock. I’m broken, always have been and always will be. I’ve ruined their lives, can’t you see that? I screwed up, fucked up, and that’s just the nature of being me. No wonder they left me behind.” I regret saying that last part. “They left you behind because they were fools, David. I’ve never seen a person with a better heart than you, and you didn’t get that from those jerks. You were gifted with it. You try to shoulder so much and you physically can’t do that. Instead of opening up to people, you just take more burdens on your shoulders, and turn to lonely nights of drinking. That’s why you’re here, in this room, ready to die. Time to grow up.” I hate that he’s right. “So, what do you propose that I do?” “Be a man. Sit up and open that door.” I can’t. “Can’t move.” The door shatters open up. “Well, alright, that works too. Thanks, Dad.” I look over at the mirror and see nothing there. It’s empty. My eyes strain against the very thought of moving and they see a woman with the most beautiful red hair standing there. “Mary-Lynn?” “Hey.” Her words are soft and kind, and she sits under me, rubbing my head. It’s there in that moment that I realize it wasn’t my Dad I had spent the last fifteen minutes talking to. It was her. “Why are you here?” “I guess to save you? How corny does that sound?” She smiles and with that smile brings a warmth to my heart that’s been absent for so long. I want to hug her, kiss her, and just hold her. Yet, I still can’t move my arms. “I can’t move.” My words are meant to confide in her. She looks over me and I see her eyes grow dark. It takes me a moment as she moves my arms closer to her. “Oh, God, we need help in here! Now!” Her voice hurts my head, badly. She looks at me with such sadness in her eyes, and it breaks my heart. My eyes move towards my arms and I see blood dripping out of my wrists, from cuts that I had put there. Cuts I don’t remember. *** Colossus VI. I never thought I would see this day, for more than one reason. I look at the cuts on my wrists and tears start to well in my eyes, but I know I can’t break down. Not here, not now, not ever. Keep my head up, keep looking forward, and just keep moving. The throng of fans outside of the arena when I walked in today was nothing short of overwhelming. Some of them looked at me with pity in their eyes, obviously from the story that had leaked out. I push those thoughts out of my mind; can’t focus on them either. As I sit there, a million thoughts swirling my mind, I pull my leather bag over to me and hold the roll of tape in my hands. Night one had been a success, but tonight would be the barnburner that would make this year’s Colossus one of the top ever in recent memory. The week of media had been heavy and non-stop, even when they considered me to be worse than a dark-horse in the Intense Title match that I’ll be taking part of this evening. You can’t compete with a giant and a legend, but I plan on doing that tonight. I keep saying it, tonight, I will shock the world. There’s a knock at my door. I wonder who it could be and contemplate opening the door myself. I decide against it and begin to tape up my hands, hoping to hide my scars from the world. Of course, I’m also a fucking idiot. They don’t need to see those scars, they can just see the ones in my eyes. The white shirt I’m wearing comes off easily and I look at my body. “Someone’s been working out.” Her voice cuts through like a knife tearing into a slice of pizza. My eyes shoot up and they land upon the boss that haunts my dreams. “Lisa,” my voice chortles and I reach into my bag and pull out the pants I’ll be wearing for the show. Something specially made by PRIME for tonight’s affairs. “I really hope you’re not going to keep changing in front of me. Going to make some of the boys in the back wonder about me.” “You’re not that important to me.” She laughs. It’s a laugh that actually comforts me, something that’s an oddity when it comes with her true personality. This is the side of her that I tend to see more often than not. Not sure why that is thought, but she’s always been straight to my face, kinder to me than most people think she’s capable of. “You okay?” She sounds concerned. I look up at her and nod my head. “As okay as I can be.” Lisa moves closer to me. She actually puts a hand on my shoulder. I want to move away, shudder at her touch, but I don’t. Instead, I turn fully towards her and straighten my shoulders. For some reason, I knew she was going to come see me. The past few days, our eyes had locked, but she didn’t talk to me. I don’t think she wanted to have this conversation, but knew it had to happen. “You put me in a tough spot.” I want her to just do it. “That’s not lost on me, Lisa. But, right now, I could give two shits less about the kind of position that I put you in. Nothing against you personally or even PRIME, but—“ “I don’t want to hear it, David.” There’s the Lisa Tyler that everyone knows. To the point, rough around the edges and one of the brightest minds around when you talk to her one on one. I sigh. Yet, my shoulders never slump because I know what’s coming. “Do you know that when I offered you that contract that I was putting my job on the line? I know, why would I do something like that? Because I believed in you. I made a phone call to Craig and he told me that you were going to be special. He wasn’t sure about bringing you into PRIME so soon, but I knew for some reason that you could be great. Judging from your in-ring performances, I’m right on that.” I let her down. I let everyone down. Fuck. This would be even worse than I thought. “When the story broke about your attempted suicide, my phone line went crazy. Not just from reporters, David, but from the board members of Prometheus. They were asking me how I could hire someone who could be this mentally broken, and all I can tell them is that the David that these people were talking about wasn’t the David that I knew. They wanted you gone. I fought for you, David, I fought for you.” Here it comes. I brace myself. “I’m not going to fire you.” “I’ll pack my bags.” I turn around and start to grab my bag before my mind registers what she actually said. I freeze and look over at her, and she smiles because my jaw is dropped. “That doesn’t mean you’re off the hook, and I’ve got a lot of pressure on my shoulders to get rid of you. But, I think you deserve the dignity of choosing your fate.” For the first time, I notice the black folder in her hands. She opens it out and produces two pieces of paper. “I think you know the right choice to make. One is your resignation letter. You leave her with your head held high, hoping to return to PRIME one day. The other is a statement that states that you will enter a rehab program and submit to regular drug tests and counseling sessions. At any time that you slip up, Prometheus and PRIME have the right to fire you.” Never did I expect this. I grab the pieces of paper and look at them. “I expect one of the pieces of paper to be back on my desk by the end of the show. You wrestle tonight, you do your thing with Chainz and Rhine, and then you make your decision. Sometimes, winning that gold, and hearing that crowd go crazy for you on the biggest stage of them all can do some miraculous things for you.” She doesn’t say another word and just leaves me there. It only takes me a minute to make up my mind, and I put my signature on the bottom of a piece of paper and toss away the other one. Then? Then, I smile.
