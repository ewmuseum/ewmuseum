Duke & Duchess

23 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/23/06 I finish putting on my favorite gold cufflinks and gaze right into the mirror. I’m at my best in my dapper state and I’ll be honest, the guy looking back at me is one smooth-looking brother, narcissism be damned. It’s been a while since I’ve really spent time at one of these soirees, considering all that pesky wrestling I’ve been doing. Don’t get me wrong, being part of the syndicate that is basically printing their own gold for PRIME right now has its privileges, but a model-slash-actor needs his face time with the rich and the beautiful. Tonight the event is for some charity looking to score big off of loose, deep pockets. No, I won’t be giving any more than the deposit that’s sent in with the RSVP’s. Where were these charities when my mother and I were…no, you know what, it’s not even worth it. It’s the same reason that it’s a beautiful fucking sight to watch white collar schmooze for money to the son of a forgotten immigrant and a rotten fucking loser G.I. Irony writ large, whatever you want to call it, it’s great. And speaking of great, she looks it. Ellie slinks in, wearing a satin red get-up that kicks me in the jaw from across the room. Her back is bare and I can see fluid motion created when she leans over to do her makeup. Her shoulder blades jut out boldly, giving her the heiress air. If she weren’t already living off what we in the business call Kannon money (I’ve heard the rumors; he was a big enough star in his PCW days that he was writing, booking. Supposedly he took a percent of earnings, not a salary, so I don’t bother asking where the money beyond the platinum card is.), she’d have me buying whatever she wanted, whenever she wanted. Let’s just say that she could have the most iron-willed man eating out of her hand (and…as any wrestling fan will tell you, she has). “Chan,” she coos, in the only voice that is allowed to call me by that horrid name, “who is this dinner for? The retards or the hobos?” Underneath all that screaming beauty is a refreshingly jagged, blunt view of the world. Plus, she just generally hates people who aren’t her. “Because,” she says with a slight pause as she highlights her eyelid in brilliant seafoam, “I don’t do ‘tards.” “I know, Ellie. Don’t worry, we’re not talking about the opening ceremonies for the Special Olympics,” I shoot back, now merely watching her. It’s easy to say she’s got no discernible skills, but her combined beauty and cynicism are her skills. She knows exactly to make herself the most beautiful thing in a room; in the same way, she knows (as is the prerequisite for being an A-List member) the right buttons to push on any person to bring steaming ire to the surface. These two things are intrinsically linked because when you look like that, you can get away with saying a lot more. She turns, arching her gauzy back and muttering half-at me. “You know, they’re not all winners; I don’t care what those medals say.” See, it’s hard not to laugh with her. She’s like that crazy guy in high school who doesn’t say much, but on the occasions that he speaks, hilarity ensues because what he says is so out there. Coming from the weed-riddled recesses of his mind, everything is five times funnier. There are just certain people who can bring a laugh, even when being completely inflammatory. “Good thing you look fucking great,” I add. She sells it perfectly; doesn’t turn around with a pursed mouth to fully appreciate the compliment. She’s heard it many times before, but she does eventually turn, having finished applying all her makeup. I’m going to stop soon, but it’s important (to me) to point out that she knows exactly what to do with her makeup. Now I know, hot girls perfect this stuff in grade school, but Eleanor Kannon-Hall knows exactly what a man wants out of a date at gala event: he wants everyone else to wish they were with his date. And tonight, she’ll definitely be getting high marks in that area. “C’mon, Chan, would you expect any other couple to look this good? Of course not.” She’s got a point; she’s wrestling royalty and I’m…a model-slash-wrestling champion (don’t think this invitation isn’t in part due to my successful retention of the A-List’s collective 5-Star belt). “We’ll blow the tard-lovers out of the water.” Right she is. I cock my arm at a ninety degree angle and she interlocks her arm with mine. Her skin is fair and she smells like weak vodka, all flowery, piquant, and startling. The chariot awaits. * * * * * We like to make a grand entrance. There are various cameras at the entrance to the event and we make sure to hit every single one. From behind the camera, women mutter jealousy and men’s minds churn with the dream of finding someone like her. After the initial pictures, we have to do initial introductions to the chairs. As usual, they’re dried-up white people who have no satisfaction in their real lives so they force a sad pathetic existence out of handshakes and fake grins. “Hi! I’m Barb McConnell and this…oh where did he go, that big oaf?” The middle-aged woman with the smile pilfered from Joan Rivers is already assailing us. She grabs me by the arm and leads me off towards her husband, who she has misplaced (oh silly her!). I glance back at Ellie, who is more than happy to leave the ogling eyes of the crazy old Republicans. I get tugged along until we stop at our desired landmark: a tree stump of a man; jowly and hidden behind massive oblong glasses. “Hal, this is Chan-duh-ler Tsonda and…who is the lovely young lady with you?” The words, flint on the dry tinder in Ellie’s head, almost compel her to immediately speak up and I can’t held but offer a small grin at the thought of own pride in her name. Of course, I haven’t forgotten that this imbecile just turned my first name into a three-syllable extravaganza. “This is Eleanor Kannon-Hall; she’s a business associate and close friend of mine.” Barb smiles widely again…and everyone in the room dies. Well, almost; but it’s fucking horrible. “What exactly do you folks do?” I know I’m broaching an awkward subject because Barb is just clearly the type of woman who doesn’t do anything; she just plans charity events with her husband’s money and power. “Well, I’m a vice president over at Sempra Energy. We’re a Fortune 500 based here in San Diego and we are working on-” Two things I hate: people who don’t say where they work, but give you their position and people who use the royal “we” for their company’s dealings. If you’re that in love with your work, you’re clearly fucking a secretary on the reg. He doesn’t finish because he gets cut off by Ellie. “And, like, what do you do?” See, this is the beauty of a well-placed Ellie remark; it catches the clearly-trying-to-seem-aloof Barb McConnell by faux surprise. Once the topic turned to jobs, she looked for someone she knew to drift off towards, but Ellie’s too smart for that. She even follows up with her “You’re getting prepwned” stare afterwards, as if she cannot live with the answer. Barb smiles weakly and stumbles upon an answer. “I manage the family’s assets, you know? A little investment banking here, a little real estate dabbling there?” God, she’s heading right into a trap and I know it; I think Hal knows it, but neither of us men dare to intrude on this sacred female battleground. “It took both McConnells working together to get us as far as we’ve come.” Another thing I hate: when people refer to their families like it’s some sort of tribe. If Ellie weren’t already setting this cunt up for cruel and awkward punishment, I’d be roasting her just the same. Hell, that’s the reason that she and I are two peas in a pod. “Do you have kids?” Ellie asks, still seeming genuinely inquisitive. “Why yes,” Barb responds, unaware that she’s being made. “Bryan is nineteen at George Washington and Phil is eighteen, going off to Washington University in Saint Louis.” “Yeah, I know where it is.” This would be that point in the movie where the record slips, the fun pauses, and everyone gulps in terror. But the rest of the party continues on, all unaware of the horror unfolding in front of me. I lean to Hal to make a remark, but he gives me a look and then takes a couple steps away. See, if he could’ve just shot the shit with me, maybe make a “crazy bitches” remark, I would’ve maybe pulled rabid Ellie off of her prey. But since this fucker’s going to war for his glorified housewife, all bets are off. “Do you have, like, a real job? I mean, you’re obviously not taking care of kids and from your description, it sounds like you might just sit at home and go shopping whenever you want.” Barb’s face is the-sheets-on-a-hotel-bed white. There is simply nothing for her to say in the face of Hurricane Ellie. And then comes the killer, the drop of the guillotine. “Darling, shopping all day? That sounds, like, fantastic!” Perhaps you knew Ellie in PCW or OSW or somewhere else, but this is the difference between old and new. She’s now a trained psychological assassin, having learned from one of the greatest, Xavier Kannon. She gives Barb a little pat on the shoulder and then we move into another crowd of people, as Barb and Hal sit stunned in her wake. With Ellie giggling, she departs to go find more trouble. Meanwhile, I make for the open bar. I ask for a Long Island Iced Tea and the woman behind the counter gives me a slight smile. I assume she makes her tips by offering that puny, elusive smile to rich businessmen. I figure I’ve done enough philanthropy already for the night, as she hands me the drink and then unhappily watches me walk off without leaving her any compensation. “Hey, aren’t you Chandler Tsonda?” It only takes five steps from the bar before I’m recognized. Turning, I see that the owner of the voice is a young-ish man without a woman on his arm. “Listen, the name’s Tony Meyer; I’m a closet PRIME fan, but don’t let the suits in here now.” I offer a slight chuckle, but more to the point, I want to know what Tony Meyer wants from me. “Hey, I’m uhh…well, you know who I am. Nice to meet you.” “Yeah, I think I’ve seen you around San Diego before. You’re one our celebrity claims to fame, I think.” “Sure thing, bud.” I sip at my drink (delightful, although I’m too far to rethink giving a tip at this point), hoping to gain a buzz before I have to intervene upon Ellie’s reign of terror. “Don’t worry, I’m not going to ask for an autograph.” A surprising statement, but it doesn’t mean that he won’t ask for a picture, a poster, a handshake, a piece of my clothing, you know the drill. “Just wanted to introduce myself, considering I’m a young businessman like yourself.” “Oh?” “I just finished law school and I’m looking to get into sports law; in other words, I want to be agent, but I don’t know if I’ll cut it right away.” He’s mildly intriguing, this Tony Meyer. “Maybe I’ll look you up the next time I fire an agent.” “Totally, but say, don’t you have a match tomorrow? Should you be drinking when you’ve got Titan St. James on the horizon?” I can’t go anywhere without this lecture. I haven’t gotten to where I am by being a student of the game. I don’t “prepare” for matches; it’s just not what I do. “I don’t think…oh look who’s here.” I couldn’t have planned Ellie’s entrance any better. I make fairly pathetic attempts to introduce Ellie to Tony and stimulate conversation for another forty-five seconds before fading out and walking off. The best way not to make it is to question the talent, kid. “Can we go home?” I wouldn’t have thought Ellie would be so quick to want to leave, but I can’t help also feeling bored with the surroundings. “Torturing these people is way too easy. They just squirm and flop around, but they don’t fight back.” It’s like being in the ring with Kyle Lamen. BAZING! “Yeah, let’s go, I’m tired and I do have that match tomorrow. Maybe I’ll rest up and get an earlier flight. You know, get to the arena a bit early?” This earns me a strange look from the maiden on my arm. “Then again, the match isn’t until tomorrow. How does the VIP at Rock Bottom and a bottle of tequila sound?” The idea of taking over the club’s lounge until the wee hours of the morning apparently sounds good, according to the slight, playful smile on her face.
