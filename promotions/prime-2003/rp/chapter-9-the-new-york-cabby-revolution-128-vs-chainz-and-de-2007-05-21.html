Date: 21 - 05 - 07 Chapter 9: The New York Cabby Washington, D.C. Chevy Chase Drive It was midnight when the call came in, according to the green digits on his clock, startling Pierce as he sat in the comfort of the leather recliner, a whiskey in one hand and a remote in the other. In the background “Overkill” by Colin Hay played softly. Gripping the phone, having put his whiskey down, Pierce answered. “Hello?” “Pierce, glad I caught you, did I wake you?” asked his boss, Joseph Wallace. “No, boss, I’m up.” “Excellent Pierce, because as it happens, I’m out of buzzing reporters and need you to do a favour for me.” Joseph Wallace exclaimed with enthusiasm, but Pierce couldn’t share in the joy at being roped into a late night project. Switching off his C.D. player he launched his lazy posterior off the recliner and wandered around the darkened room. “What’s the job on offer, boss?” he asked, fully expecting a simple task. “This one has ‘BIG NEWS’ in bold capitol all over it, kid. We’ll hit it big, in style.” “Caught the big fish, did we?” he questioned. Pierce opened a small desk drawer, assigned for cutlery, but now held jotters and biros. Grabbing a page and a biro he sat into the small oak table and switched on the light, the odd light from a passing car fluttered on the white washed wall like a licking flame. “You got yourself a pen, Pierce? We can’t start this off without that.” “I got one, boss. Give me some details and I’ll get on it.” “That you will,” he replied, “We got ourselves a serial killer. Just came in over the radio line that a young college girl in New York was found brutally murdered. That’s all they’ve released.” “And you want me to go to New York and try to leech some information out of the New York police department.” “You’re getting the hang of this shin-dig, Pierce,” Wallace boomed, “You won’t be going alone though. I need two of my finest on this one. Since your partner, Hailey, has been promoted, I would like you to work with Ignacio Booth.” “Is Ignacio Booth the one that got the District Judge arrested over a sex scandal, boss?” “His shining moment, play nice and score me a big headline.” Pierce scribbled down a couple of notes, nothing special, but information necessary. Happy enough with his small chicken scratch, he slipped the pen onto the polished dome and watched it slide off the table, it hit the floor like a pin in a silenced room, a quiet hum echoed discreetly. “And you will be going tonight, Pierce, we’ve got to hatch before the other maggots on the withering carcass.” “I understand, boss. I’ll stay as long as it takes. Do you want me to e-mail you in the morning with information?” “Definitely not, you will ring me. I don’t care if it’s morning, noon or night. Even if I’m on the big white phone talking to Jesus himself, you will ring me.” Hanging up the phone he scrunched the paper up and chucked it into the trash, and left a message he was sure Hailey would receive. Grabbing his laptop case and his money Pierce exited the small apartment but stopped … … He was going to be in New York a while. Flipping around on the heels of his recently polished shoes he toggled the light switch and found himself immersed in light. Bathing in the tint, he gazed at his wrestling attire and frowned at the pathetic excuse his career had become, he was now a jobber to all those higher above him in PRIME and feared his stature would only lower as the weeks progressed, but he wouldn’t linger on such a pessimistic view, he would strive to improve himself. In front of him were tickets to his next show in PRIME, a show he wasn’t enthused to be participating in. Pierce had come across Michael Sloan many times in his life and outside the ring it never daunted him, but inside the ring he was terrified. The year he’d known Michael Sloan more commonly known as “Chainz” in the AWC allowed him to know the capabilities of this beast. All he could take solace in was the fact he’d be working with Big Bear Monsoon … slightly increasing his chances of making it out of the ring in one piece. His thoughts of revenge outweighed the fear of facing Michael Sloan. Having watched the replay of his match against Sun Tzu, Pierce had one man on his mind to remove … Devin Shakur. ---- Washington, D.C. Greyhound Station, Bethesda Pierce emerged from the shadows into the orifice of steel ships, glinting in the moons blue glow like floating dreams. Stood in the silence of a harbouring night he glanced toward his shuttle bus and then toward his watch. The prospect of sleep slowly dithering into the darkness around him he sauntered up the steps in to the steel plated chasm. The interior was typical, two seats for each row and an overhead compartment. He was first to board and grabbed the front seat with every intention of using the leg room. His night was going to be long as the trip to New York would take up to four hours. Reaching around his suit coat pocket, Pierce picked out his mobile phone and slowly followed it with a piece of paper. He dialled the number and on the second dial tone, the call was answered. “Yo? Who’s this?” “Is this Ignacio Booth?” “Yeah, who’s asking?” “This is Pierce Lavelle. I’m working with you on the college murder in New York.” “You got the call too, huh? Cheeky bastard had the nerve to ring me right in the middle of –“ Pierce stopped Ignacio right there, unwilling to let the conversation go any further. “I should be arriving in New York early morning.” “I’ll be there in an hour, got a first class ticket for my troubles,” he said. Clenching his mouth, he gritted his teeth. He could’ve gone the expensive route but now he sat in the uncomfortable plastic seats with four hours of romantic comedy flicks ahead of him … while his new found partner lapped in the reclined seats of a first class jet. “So, Ignacio, where should we meet before we go to investigate New York’s latest?” “Starbucks on Broadway is perfect, brotha.” Pierce looked at his watch; he noticed the bus had pretty much filled up during his conversation with Ignacio Booth. Slipping against the window he straightened his posture as the bus began to move. “Alright Ignacio, I’ll meet you there around nine a.m.” “Sounds good and call me Iggy, all my friends do.” “See you around nine,” he said leaving it at that. No name bases to finish he shut the phone and slumped into a comfortable position as he watched the cinema of lights shuffle past him. The bus moved along and slowly with the murmur of the engine and the soft musical opening to Forrest Gump, Pierce found himself falling asleep. ---- Washington, D.C. Chevy Chase Drive Flipping the light switch Hailey entered the quiet apartment and looked around. Sighing with the exhaustion of a long day she slipped off her high heels and rubbed the balls of her feet, aching as she did so. In a slight hobble she moved to the living area and stood for a second. “Hello, Pierce, you here?” No answer. Raising an eyebrow she chucked her keys onto the small glass bowl and placed her briefcase onto the sofa. In the corner of her eyes she spotted the table light in the kitchen flickering as the fuse began to die. Moving with a slow pace Hailey gazed into an empty study and saw a small jotter with a message on it. Grabbing the loose leaf pages into her fingertips she read the message. I’ve gone to New York for a story. Will be back soon. Can you look up the name “Allan Cooper” for me around early 80’s and late 70’s? Thanks. “What am I? Your maid?” she said. Ripping the page out, she chucked it into the thrash and emerged from the study. The night gazed blankly back at her. Emotionless and callous in its appearance, like a mirror that watched and reflected the dreary image of her, reminding her of her increasing old age. Unable to let the clutches of sleep wrap around her she sat into the sofa and opened up her laptop. If she were to aid her roommate in a thankless task of finding a name she would be expecting a New York Statue of Liberty token! Search: ALLAN COOPER 1970 - 1990 Cracking her knuckles she listened to the delightful crackle as the computer whirred to life. ---- Manhattan, New York Broadway and 6th Street Pierce walked along the dirt filled streets of Broadway, gazing up at the glittering lights around him. Virgin mega stores stood out in the early morning hours like a red beacon in the misty seawaters. The tourists were out enjoying the tropical sea breeze as it blew over the quietened streets, a musky smell against their noses. Unusual for Broadway, an eerie silence drilled around him. Taxis ran through the streets without issue. Slightly less tired from his sleep aboard the shuttle bus Pierce moved toward his hotel. It was cheap enough and one of few that offered breakfast. The Super 8 hotel was on 6th street close enough to where he would need to be. The amount it cost wasn’t dirt cheap but he knew Trump Towers would’ve been too lavish. Slipping into the lobby through the spiralling doors he felt almost blown back as the air-conditioning unit was on chill. Peeling his eyelids back down, he approached the desk. “Good morning. How can I help you?” “I’m picking up my room key. Pierce Lavelle. We spoke last night.” She looked at him a moment, attempting to remember the voice from over a dozen she’d spoken to since last night. Flicking through her computers database she found the name and his room number. “You’re room number 10 Mr. Lavelle. I hope you enjoy your stay in New York.” Grabbing his key he smiled. “Thanks.” Stepping into the elevator he clicked floor six and felt the elevator rise as the cables tightened and the wheels began to churn. After a few moments of listening to the recent CNN broadcast he moved into the lobby and saw his room number at the end of the hall. Joseph Wallace had got him a room on last notice in the Super 8 hotel and he was pleased to have at least an hour to freshen up. As he opened the door he was greeted by a clean and sparsely furnished room. Relief swept over his exhaustion as he crashed into the comfort of the double bed. Something stirred him from his reverie. A vibration against his thigh. Flinging his legs off the bed he slung his torso over so he was almost touching his knees and grabbed the phone before it dropped. “Hello?” “You go to New York City and leave me here, thanks very much.” He smiled, “Hello Hailey, how are you?” “Tired and annoyed. You?” she replied in a sarcastic and usual high pitched squeal. “Pretty much the same.” Placing the phone between his shoulder and ear Pierce routed through his rucksack and pulled out a fresh shirt and trousers. No need to look too fancy for a simple coffee break with his new partner. “So I got your message and first off, you owe me.” “I know Hailey, but Wallace called me at midnight to deal with this. I hadn’t got time to deal with that too.” He listened to her sigh on the other end before a sounding “tut-tut” came from her lips. “I found two Allan Coopers from the era you requested. One is dead and died in ’76. The other has been missing since ’78.” “Tell me more about the ’78 Allan Cooper,” he requested slipping out of his dirty jeans and standing in his boxers. He shook the phone out from his shoulder and switched on speaker. “He was on the police archive,” she said. Buttoning up a clean deep blue shirt and tucking it into his trousers he listened, “Grand theft auto?” “Much worse. He was the prime suspect in a murder,” she paused as she routed around a couple of printed out pages, “Apparently he killed a woman named Mary Hume in Kansas City. He hasn’t been seen since that night.” Pierce felt the thud of an easy recognition hit his chest. Everything was adding up and it terrified him. “Pierce, you there?” she asked. “I'm here, Hailey. Thanks for all your help but I’ve got to go.” “Alright hot shot, but I expect one hell of a souvenir.” He shut the phone and felt a wave of uneasiness sweep through him. The more he dug into his father’s identity and past, the deeper he found the hole to get and he feared the deeper he got there’d be no way out. Sliding his belt buckle closed he grabbed his phone, wallet and room key and left the room with a new agenda. He had a different idea to spend his weekend in New York, aside from working. Slipping his phone open he trundled through the list of numbers till he found one. Checking his watch he shut the phone. He’d ring when she woke up. ---- Manhattan, New York Broadway and 6th Street He almost had the pick of the place. The Starbucks practically empty only filled with the patrol men getting ready to head to the precinct and the unlucky night shift waitress as she waltzed around on numb feet. He sat in a seat nearest the door, eyeing each face that entered with questionable pause. Pierce waited in the corner as he watched Ignacio Booth enter the small diner. He was a tall but stocky man. His dark skin shone against the unkind marble light. Its glow masked with dirt and grease from the kitchen. He slipped into the seat opposite Pierce and shook his hand. “No good with early mornings,” he said. “Know the feeling,” Pierce replied. “I got to tell you. Something feels off about this case.” Pierce nodded his head. There was a truth to what Booth had announced. In most cases where a serial killer was believed to be involved media was generally kept a good few paces behind as the FBI stepped into the field, but this case was silent, not a word on either front. The grave shift waitress approached from the back of the joint. In a pink dress and white apron crawled a sore eyed prune. Her hair flew around her face likes bees in a summer breeze, its steely grey tint like wires in the burning sunlight. Pierce ordered a coffee, black. Ignacio ordered pancakes to fuel his engines. “You think we’re being cut out of the equation?” Ignacio pulled out a packet of smoker’s chewing gum and took two pieces into his mouth. “I think Wallace is fishing for something that isn’t there. I’ve been on the phone to my wife in the Times and she hasn’t heard anything about such a murder.” Pierce nodded his head and sipped his black coffee, the fuel injection awakening his slack motors. “Only one way to find out,” he said. Ignacio’s brow rose. “Are we heading down to Central Park?” “Look at it this way. If the crime scene’s been cleared by the FBI, at least we’ll get some sight seeing,” Pierce replied and handed a ten dollar bill to cover the coffee and pancakes. Ignacio rose from his chair with a little difficulty. His deep brown eyes held a caring gaze, “You know John Lennon was killed not too far from there either.” “Two places to see then, partner.” Ignacio patted Pierce’s back with enthusiasm, “My bet is they’ve scooped up the kid’s parents leaving us diddly-squat to work with.” “In that case, Wallace can kiss his page one goodbye.” ---- Manhattan, New York Central Park As Ignacio and Pierce crossed the street, their initial thoughts of a boycott dissipated in an unsightly mess of blue squad cars. Lights fluttered around them as they approached Central Park. Ignacio’s eyes lit up. “Looks like we got ourselves a live story,” he said. Pierce looked around the scene, taken aback by the amount of squad cars. In the distance his eyes spotted the coroners van. Its black colour eerie as it sat amidst a scene of death. “I guess we best try our luck with the pigs. You got your phone with you?” he asked. Pierce raised an eyebrow and routed through his pockets until his fingertips pulled out his mobile. “You take the pictures and I’ll write down the scene description,” he ordered. “To think we almost didn’t come,” Pierce replied. Ignacio stood for a moment, his eyes scouring the scene around him, “Looks like we’re the first to arrive,” he said. As they approached the scene it was like something out of a movie. He stood in the chilling sea winds as the smell of flesh ran up his nostrils. Peeling away every sense of exhaustion, awakening his breakfast that now rumbled with the stench and willingness to vomit. A scene of red and blue trickled around them. The leaves on the trees creating demonic shadows around them as they stood in their finery gazing into the dark bushes as the coroner and forensic team in a picture of white innocence collected evidence. Pierce stood close to the yellow crime scene tape and held out his phone and took a picture, instantly noting the look of shock on the police chiefs face. A detective on scene turned toward them. “You got permission to be here?” he asked. Ignacio flashes his media badge and the Detective grunted a sentence and disappeared. A formidable silence enveloped them as the coroners emerged with two gurneys. “There are two bodies …” Pierce stated silently. Ignacio shook his head. “Look at the size of each black bag.” Sure enough each bag was only half and it made Pierce swallow hard to keep his coffee down. The young girl had been cut in half. Unfortunately Ignacio upon realising this and stench of death around him forced his pancakes up whether he fought the urge or not. He turned into the nearest thrash can and hurled. “That’s why I didn’t have breakfast this morning. Empty stomach is the best way to approach a fresh crime scene.” Ignacio turned, pale in the face, his eyes blood red, “Thanks for the advice.” Pierce was busy snapping shots and taking notes, he questioned a forensic technician on the findings. He was short but humble. "Young girl about twenty-two, strawberry blonde hair. Evidence of sexual abuse prior to excess wounds. It appears she was bound, kept in a safehouse for quite some time. Time of death was around three days ago. The killer did a number on her but he slipped up. Biological specimens were retrieved, we'll find the killer. Keep some of that low profile when you write it." While his partner sat hunched on the sidewalk sniffling and vomiting into a plastic bag given to him by a forensic technician, Pierce managed to obtain just enough for a page one. “You’d expect a little more media for something as big as this,” said a Detective emerging from the shrubbery. Pierce kept writing but found the statement that more curious as he looked around. They were still the only ones here and his curiousness was soon answered when a black sedan tore around the corner, a small blue light flashing in the window. A jealous grunt emerged from the police chief. “What is it?” Ignacio asked between breaths. “It’s the FBI. Looks like our information and case has gone out the window.” Two men in plain black suits emerged with a sense of pride and smugness to them. They gazed around at everyone on site and smirked. “We’ll take it from here gentlemen, leave all case files with your chief of police. Thank you.” Pierce watched as the coroner van left the scene, the young girl now finding some peace. He turned around and sat down next to Ignacio as the police began to move off. Before long Central Park became deathly quiet, a small crime scene tape cordoned off the area. “I’m going to head back to D.C. and type this up quickly before the FBI pulls the plug,” he said. Pierce nodded his head and handed him the small jotter. “That should be everything.” “You not coming?” he asked. “Nah, I’ve got something I want to do before I head back to the D.C.” Ignacio rose onto his feet, a little shaken, but sturdy. He flicked the collar of his jacket and shoved the jotter into his briefcase. “Nice working with you, Pierce, perhaps another time.” “Likewise, Iggy,” he replied. Pierce watched as Ignacio Booth moved carefully back down the street past Tiffanys. Remaining on the sidewalk Pierce opened his mobile and checked the time. He dialled a number. “Hello?” she answered. “Hey Dawn, its Pierce.” “Hey, how are you doing?” Pierce smiled. “I’m good. You?” “As good as can be expected, worked graveyard last night,” she replied. The sound of a car horn behind her. “Listen, I’m in town at the moment and was wondering if I could take you up on that coffee?” Pierce stood up and began to move away from the crime scene behind him. The stench fading as he moved further away. His reception was bad but he could still hear her. “That’d be brilliant actually. I’ve got an hour free now, would that suit you?” He couldn’t help but smile as he turned the corner. “Yeah, that’s good.” “You know Bravo on 5th Avenue?” she asked sweetly. “I’ll find it. See you then.” She said goodbye and he closed his phone. Pierce turned and without looking ran smack into a taxi as it moved off. He felt his body lift off the ground, the cars bonnet harsh against his knees as the bumper collided with him. His eyes gazed into those of the taxi drivers, almost in slow motion he watched the world spin as he collided with the windshield and flung forward. The concrete was cold against his skin, grating his forehead. An old woman saw the incident and couldn’t move from where she stood. A patrol car still remained from the scene and sounded its siren. Rising with a slight limp in his step he looked around at the quiet surrounding him. All stood still, as if frozen in time. “Damnit my car. Look what you did to my bumper …. That’s one hundred dolla …. One hundred dolla worth of damage. Look? Look damn you!” “Yeah because the car is the one suffering at the moment, you moron!” “You blind or something mister! I was right in front of you.” Pierce turned sharply, the pain wasn’t bad. Just the shock of being hit more than anything. “I wish I was, because then I wouldn’t have to look at the bump in your car!” “You pay for that. You broke it. You pay.” The policeman moved forward and the taxi man quelled. Pierce just straightened his suit trousers; wiping the dirt from his palms. The old woman moved off, unscathed by the scene prior. Pierce turned to the approaching policeman. “You better go to the hospital,” the policeman ordered. “And mister one hundred dolla here should go to jail. He drove on a pedestrian walk when his light was red.” The taxi man’s eyes went wide. “Who’s the blind one now?” he said smugly. “I’m sorry officer. Let me pay for the man’s hospital bill,” the taxi man said. “Relax, I’m fine. See all my limbs are intact and my pupils aren’t dilated,” he said. “Can somebody tell me where Bravo on 5th Avenue is?” The policeman turned to Pierce. “I’ll drive you there just to be sure you aren’t injured.” “Like I said officer, I’m fine!” “We’ll see. I’ll take you to Bravo.” In the distance a figure watched in a black BMW, his eyes a steely blue as he lifted a small walkie-talkie to his lips. In the passenger seat rested a cacophony of equipment from a camera to binoculars and pictures, weapons and microphones. “This is Sphinx.” “Have you got the boy in sight?” asked a deep voice. “Yes, and he knows more about the package. He’s getting close. How shall I proceed?” “Wait till he’s back in D.C. and we’ll go from there. I cannot stress how important it is that this boy remains alive. I want to know where the package is.” Static overpowered the walkie-talkie as he placed it back down and started up the engine. He drove off into the vacuum of smoke, a mere shadow in the afternoon traffic. ---- Manhattan, New York Bravo 5th Avenue He felt embarrassed as he sat in the back of the squad car and despite efforts to portray the fact he was in perfect health he felt sore on his shins and forehead. The cool air quivered against his head, a small headache starting. The officer driving held his eyes dead ahead until he pulled to a stop. “We’re here,” he said. Pierce’s eyes went wide as he looked in the mirror at his visage. A small bruise began to form over his forehead where a slight scratch formed from hitting the concrete. He cursed to himself and looked around. “You really didn’t have to drive me to the door,” he muttered. “I could’ve taken you in handcuffs to the hospital,” he replied. Pierce raised an eyebrow. “I was in the right -” Suddenly he heard the door open and was too terrified to turn around and explain why he was in a squad car. The officer looked dead ahead toward the person. “She’s right behind me, isn’t she?” he asked. The patrol officer nodded his head. She hadn’t spoken yet so Pierce swallowed hard and put on a smile as he turned around. She stood before him a picture of beauty. She was naturally stunning but with a look of perplexity laced across her eyebrow, causing her nose to scrunch, he liked it. Radiant in the opulent sunshine, he couldn’t help but look into her beautiful eyes. “I can totally explain this,” he said stepping out of the car. She smiled, "You making a habit of getting rides in the back of police cars?" He smiled weakly suddenly noticing a tooth was missing, “Why you like that?” The patrol officer stepped out of the car and looked toward Pierce. Dawn eyed the officer, "That's okay officer, I'll make sure he's fine." Dawn looked at Pierce with a small smile on her lips, "You mind the law now, and I wouldn't want you turning into Michael Sloan." “Speaking of the wondrous monstrosity, he’s my opponent this week.” Before Dawn could speak the patrol office stepped in once more, following protocol and covering his behind encase an issue arose. “Mister, you better go to the hospital after this, otherwise I’ll wait outside till you’re done.” Pierce turned, “I’m fine, really.” “That why you’re limping.” “I got locked joints man!” Dawn folded her arms, a worried look in her eyes, “What happened?” Frowning, Pierce spoke, “I got hit by the infamous New York cabby.” Dawn’s eyes widened and she moved close to him inspecting the cut on his head, “You alright?” “Like I told this kind officer, I’m fine. So you hungry?” Dawn looked a bit worried but took his word on it. She approached the officer and flashed her detective badge, "I'll take care of it from here. Just pretend it never happened, okay." The officer thought it over, but decided not to argue with a detective. Pierce smiled as he watched her in power mode. The patrol officer drove off in a plum of smoke, and Dawn and Pierce walked into the restaurant. She was worried, "You better not take your match with him lightly. I know what he's capable of and I don't mean to offend you or anything, but don't mess with him. I've seen many cases and many killers and sickos and none of them compare to that evil bastard." Pierce smiled as he limped slightly next to her. “I’m being serious,” she reiterated. “Dawn, I know what Michael is capable of. I’ve heard things and I’ve seen things and no matter how many times I’ve had his sick breath breathing down my neck he doesn’t scare me off. I can handle it. You don’t have to worry about me, I won’t be going anywhere.” They sat into a small table at the window, overlooking Broadway as the afternoon rush hour left a carpet of cars and people beneath them. The summer mist like a floating cloud hovered around them, covering their eyes from the unsightly city dwellings. "If you only knew what he's done. Sure you've heard the rumours, but what if I told you they were all true? That man is sick and if only I had enough evidence I'd put that bastard in jail and free my sister from his grasps. At least you two are friends; Michael won't hurt you too bad then. For someone like him he's completely under her grasp." Dawn stated as the waitress came by. Pierce went silent as he listened to Dawn’s words. They registered with him and more so than he would like to admit. He smiled as the waitress approached, a young woman with a pleasant smile and bright blue eyes. “Can I take your order?” she asked. Dawn raised the menu, but Pierce knew already what he would have. “I’ll have a coke with ice, the fillet steak medium rare, and a portion of chips. Thanks.” The waitress nodded and turned to Dawn, “And what about you mam?” It was strange as he felt nervous about being here but elated at the same time. He hadn’t dated in almost a year and felt rusty, but for a first date he felt it was going better than usual. Dawn looked through her menu before speaking up, "I'll take the Caesar salad, the lemon chicken, and a coke." The waitress wrote everything down and took the menus. Pierce was still a bit worried about Dawn's foreboding words about Michael Sloan and Dawn could tell, "Oh but enough about that asshole, what brings you to town?" “If I said you, would that be too cheesy?” he said with a smirk. She faked a smile, "Yeah, I think it would." He frowned slightly and then Dawn chuckled. "Tracy did say you were easy to mess with," Dawn said sweetly. Pierce smiled. “I’ve got to remember you’re sisters. Shoot. No in all seriousness I was here because my boss wanted me to cover a story. That’s out of my hands now so I wanted to catch up with you. What else has Tracy told you about me … just out of curiosity?” The waitress came back with the drinks. Dawn sipped on her soda with a sly smile, "Oh you know we're close, she's told me everything." He fought hard to keep his soda down, the bubbles rising into his nose. “Uh-huh, that so! Everything you say,” he squeaked, “because I know a little about you and I think it’s only fair you share some details!” She laughed, "Oh Pierce, you know I'm a detective. I've interrogated countless suspects and I know when someone's lying, but I'll play along, so what do you want to know about me?" She leaned forward, her sweet scent fluttered into Pierce's nose and making him feel very good. He enjoyed being around her, sitting here all the worries and questions dissipated. “I got a million questions, but I’ll keep it simple. Why did you want to become a cop?” He paused when his meal was placed before him; he shifted in his seat and winced, cursing the taxi driver as he twisted in his seat to get his fork and knife. Dawn saw the wince and handed him his knife. She gazed at her salad and chicken. "I don't know; guess my dad's position as a judge got me into it. Just wanted to help people out and put sickos away." Pierce nodded, "Tracy hasn't mentioned much about your family." "Well, my parents aren't happy with her, mainly for the fact that she's with that son of a bitch." “That’s kind of why I went into medicine originally and then left for journalism. My dad was a big influence in my wanting to help people,” he said trying to pull the conversation away from Michael Sloan, a man he despised. He ate his dinner realising he’d brought up a man he hadn’t spoken about in fifteen years. "Yeah I know about your dad" she said. Pierce looked up confused. "Oh come on now, I always double check who my sister is friends with and especially who I date. It was just too tempting to pass up, but you passed Pierce so congratulations" she said with a coy smile. “So it’s a date,” he said with a wink. “Only if you want it to be,” she replied and ate some salad. He looked down at his food, a smile lacing his lips, “You’re the only one who knows, right? I haven’t seen him since I was eight years old,” he said coyly and looked into her gorgeous eyes, pools of colour. "My lips are sealed, I don't discuss these matters with anyone, and Tracy has enough on her mind as is. I don't even want to think what those two get up to. I saw their toys" she said with a shudder. Pierce held his steak in mid air, just before his mouth. The mention of their toys and he suddenly lost his appetite, “Now that I don’t want to imagine. You’re brave, I couldn’t have done that. Thanks for not telling anyone else Dawn, but I’m glad you know. Makes it easier I guess knowing somebody outside my family finally knows,” he said and sipped his drink. The lunch hour had passed and the room was filling up with people coming in for the dinner and evening entertainment as a bar was above them.
