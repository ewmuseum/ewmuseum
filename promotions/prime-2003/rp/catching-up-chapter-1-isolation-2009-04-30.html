Catching Up - Chapter 1 - Isolation

30 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 30, 2009 (Note: This is the first chapter of a smaller series I'm doing that will explain what Bryan has been up to since disappearing from PRIME nearly 2 months ago. The 'Fatherhood' series will resume with the conclusion of this series.) THURSDAY APRIL 30, 2009 Men grow beards for many different reasons. Having light stubble is sexy to some women. Every winter, burly mountain men sport their thick facial hair as a way to keep themselves warm whilst stalking their prey of choice. And in spring, each and every year, many die-hard hockey fans grow the traditional “playoff beard” in hopes that the facial hair will help to propel their favorite team to victory. However, the arrival of unkempt facial hair on a normally clean-shaven individual can mean much more. His hair was much longer than usual; not in the nearly shaven, military-like cut he normally sports, but rather a frizzy mess of dark brown hair sitting atop his head. His face, no longer clean cut and chiseled, was covered with a thick beard, one that would make any hockey aficionado proud. Gone were the outlandish orange and aqua spectacles, their spot on his face vacated. Ever since being summoned to be a part of the custody battle between the super alliance of both his parents and his ex-fiancee’s parents, Bryan had become a changed man. No longer was he calm and laid back. The little things in life didn’t thrill him anymore. Life wasn’t a joy for the young Hawaiian anymore. His clothes laid strewn around the small apartment, flanking pizza boxes, plastic 6-pack rings, and empty cases of beer. The apartment itself was a mess, and to be frank, Dawkins didn’t care. Nothing was worth worrying about anymore. Which was why he hadn’t shown up for work in over two weeks. Dawkins’ cell phone had been ringing off the hook. First, the calls came from fellow wrestlers. Rayne called. Troy called. Tsonda called. Bryan didn’t answer. Next, the low-level management of PRIME was placing calls to Dawkins’ cell phone. Matt Mills called. Bernie Roberts called. Nick Stuart called. Dam called. Again, Dawkins didn’t answer. Next, you’d figure that the upper management of PRIME would check and see what one of their top draws was up to after he decided to not show up for work for around two weeks, right? Wrong. Instead, one day, a large yellow envelope arrived at Dawkins front door, confirming that PRIME had moved on without him. PRIME did not know, nor did they care, what Bryan was up to, for they had a business to run. It happens to people in this business every day, and Dawkins was just the latest casualty. With the amount of money Bryan had left from wrestling, he figured he could live in the apartment for around two or three more months, max, without needing to get another job to pay for rent. That was a long time from now, and Bryan was taking it one day at a time. The trial itself was a mere two weeks away, but his will to fight this fight was long gone. Deep inside his heart, he knew that living on the road wouldn’t have been the most ideal situation for Nicholas, but Bryan also knew that it would be good for him to grow up with his father around. Bryan didn’t know a whole lot about fatherhood, but if there was one thing that he did learn from his father, it was that being around your son throughout his childhood can make or break a man. Unfortunately, Bryan’s father was the one who was out all of the time, taking care of business and not being the family man that he should’ve been. In Bryan’s mind, the trial was already over. His parents and Nikkie’s parents had hired the most prestigious custody attorney in the state of Hawaii, and to be honest, Bryan didn’t have the money to pay for anything like that at the moment. His money was going toward rent, pizza, and beer, all of which were more important than paying for an attorney who would do nothing but waste Bryan’s money by fighting a case that was impossible to win. *BZZZZZZZZZZZ* *BZZZZZZZZZZZ* *BZZZZZZZZZZZ* Dawkins sluggishly arose from the couch and began looking through the mound of cans, boxes, and various other junk piled upon the table in front of him, before finding his cell phone. Without looking at the caller I.D., Dawkins flipped open the phone and let go a raspy hello. “Yeah bruh?” A moment of silence on the other end of the line prompted Dawkins to repeat. “Bruh? Anyone there?” As another moment passed, Bryan pulled the phone from his ear and was about to hang up when he heard a faint voice emanating from his cell. “Hello? Bryan?” The unfamiliar voice on the other end of the line intrigued him enough to engage. “Who’s callin’?” “Yes, I’m Marie, from the Hilo County Courthouse.” Bryan sighed. “We just noticed that we have no lawyer on record for you for your trial in two weeks. Do you plan on acquiring one?” More silence. “Mr. Dawkins?” “Uh, yeah…I haven’t really decided yet. I’m not even entirely sure that I’m gonna be at the hearing, to be honest with ya. Don’t really know if it’s a fight worth fighting, especially if I’ve got no chance of winnin’.” After another moment of awkward silence, the voice on the other end of the line replied. “But you do realize that if you do not show up for your hearing, you will risk being held in contempt and a warrant could be issued for your arrest? Add that to the fact that you will most certainly lose whatever legal battle you were planning on fighting, and you’ll be getting yourself into a whole lot more trouble than you think, Mr. Dawkins.” “Yeah.” “So, Mr. Dawkins, if I were you, I would get moving and at least fill out the forms for a Public Defendant if you don’t plan on acquiring a private lawyer for your hearing. And I would most certainly suggest showing up on the date of your hearing. You’ll be saving yourself a lot of trouble, sir. So when can I plan on hearing back from you?” Silence. “Mr. Dawkins?” More silence. “MR. DAWKINS?” “Yeah, uh…gimme a week.” A sigh could yet again be heard, only this time from the other end of the phone. “Okay, Mr. Dawkins. I look forward to hearing back from you.” *Click* The cell phone was thrown back onto the pile of garbage on the table, Bryan’s head crashed against the worn down padding of the couch, and his self esteem continued to plummet. His appearance wasn’t for style. It wasn’t for warmth. It wasn’t a tradition. The beard wasn’t a sign of good things in his future, like that of a hockey fan. It was a sign of surrender. Bryan had given up.
