Flashback - Destiny's Child

13 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 13, 2008 May 17, 2004 There was a slight skip in his step as David Cross approached Michelle’s apartment door. It had been there ever since Cyberslam when he told her how much he still cared for her. She was still working through some personal issues, which was understandable after what Crusader had put her through. But David was floating on the wings of Love, and Life was much better than it had ever been before. Two quick knocks on her door, and David checked his watch. Traffic was still a little heavy but they would still be able to make their dinner reservation in time. He’d even planned the route earlier in the day just to be sure they’d be on time. The florist had assured him that the flowers would arrive in time for dessert. David smiled to himself. Tonight was going to be an amazing night. The door opened quickly and David was startled to find himself face-to-face with… not Michelle. “Uh, hi,” he said to Master Hiroshi, his former instructor and Michelle’s father. Master Hiroshi stared at Cross with eyes narrowed. For a moment, David thought that Master Hiroshi was going to attack. As skilled as he was, David was quite sure that it would probably be a very one-sided battle, culminating in Cross being wiped up with a large mop. Hiroshi didn’t attack though. He hesitantly stepped aside and let David in. This is just all sorts of strange, Cross thought to himself as he stepped into the hallway. He slipped his shoes off and walked into the living room with Master Hiroshi hovering behind him like a lethal shadow. Michelle was sitting on the couch, facing the television. It was off. “I was watching this earlier,” David said as he motioned to the black screen. “The butler did it.” Michelle didn’t say anything. She didn’t move. David sat down beside her. This was all wrong. Very wrong. “Michelle? Are you okay?” The bloodshot eyes and tears on her cheeks told him that no, she was far from okay. Throwing caution to the wind, David snapped at her father. “What did you do to her you bastard,” he asked in a tone that was dangerously close to threatening. He placed a hand on her knee. “I won’t let him hurt you,” he told Michelle. “You know that.” “I haven’t caused her pain,” Master Hiroshi said as he stood in front of them. “This is your doing.” “My doing?” David stood up. “MY doing? I love Michelle! I love her more than I’ve ever cared about anything or anyone in my life.” “With your life of misery and solitude, that’s not saying much.” Hiroshi disregarded David immediately. Cross came face-to-face with Master Hiroshi for the second time in a few minutes, this time deliberately. Few men had stood this close to the elderly Asian with bad intentions and lived to regret it. David didn’t care. If he was going to be torn apart, he was going to make sure his executioner knew what the score was. “I love her,” Cross repeated. Master Hiroshi tilted his head toward his daughter. “And how does she feel?” David looked down at Michelle. “Tell him,” he said gently. “Don’t let him intimidate you.” Michelle didn’t look up at either David or her father. She stared ahead at the television as the tears continued to flow. Master Hiroshi spoke for her. “You brought her here from Japan, filling her with silly ideas of Life and Love, something neither of you know anything about. She was humiliated and brought much disgrace to her family. She is going back to Japan where she belongs. Where she has always belonged. The Temple will be her home now.” “She’s a big girl,” David snapped at Hiroshi. “She can talk for herself. We love each other.” “She does not love you,” Hiroshi stated. “You parade your feeling for her in front of the entire World on your silly television show. What else could she do? She disgraced herself. She disgraced her family. You brought her here and she was corrupted. Her shame is deep and your love means nothing to her.” David ignored him. He knelt down on the carpet beside Michelle’s legs. “C’mon Michelle, tell him. Tell him that you love me. I know you’re going through a tough time but we can get through it together.” Michelle’s voice was hoarse. “I never said I loved you. Fate was always against us.” Screw Fate. David stood up and jammed a finger into Master Hiroshi’s chest. “You did this.” Hiroshi’s almond eyes stared into David’s damaged crimson eye. “She never loved you. You’re a mongrel dog. Who could ever love you? Who would ever want you?” Word for word, all of David Cross’s lifetime insecurities were brought to the surface. He was a loser. He’d always been a loser. He’d lost friends. He’d lost family. Even his face couldn’t remain intact, always being scarred in some manner or fashion. It was like his destiny was one of solitude and pain. With Michelle, David thought things had turned around. Now they were slipping away. Cross still had his finger in Hiroshi’s robed chest. With amazing speed, Master Hiroshi brought his calloused hand up and slapped David across the face. A normal man would have been rendered unconscious from the blow, a blow from a hand that had shattered numerous cinder blocks in the past. Had Cross wanted to, he could have blocked the slap or at least evaded it. He took it. Master Hiroshi watched David stumble backward from the blow. “Get out of her dog. Michelle is leaving within the hour. You will never see her again. And if I see you again, you’ll get more of the same.” Michelle’s lips trembled as David Cross walked down the hallway in silence. She didn’t even notice that he left his shoes behind as he left her apartment. Outside her window, dark clouds opened up and shed their own tears of rain upon the city. Her heart broke as the apartment door closed. Master Hiroshi looked down at his daughter and placed his hand on the top of her head. “This is for the best,” he said in what was as close to a reassuring tone as he could muster. “You have a great future ahead of you. Your Destiny lies on a much better path. He has no future. He is nothing.” -==- Five blocks. Ten blocks. No shoes. Only socks. In the rain. How quite insane. The temperatures had dropped and being soaked to the skin wasn’t helping to keep himself warm. Right now, David Cross wasn’t thinking about his own well-being. His view of reality was like looking through a kaleidoscope of chaotic swirling colors, focusing on the black dot in the middle of it all. Shivering, teeth chattering, David pushed open the door to the old cathedral and stepped inside leaving wet footprints behind him. Surrounded by the silence once inside, David noticed that his footsteps sounded different somehow. He looked down and saw he had one bare foot. He’d never even noticed he’d lost a sock somewhere. But that was David, always losing something. Shoes. Socks. Love. Friends. Family. Sanity. He dropped limply into one of the wooden pews. With everything that had been dumped on him in his life, all the pain and suffering, all the disappointment and loss, something had to go right for him. Somewhere, David Cross had to find happiness. Michelle was his happiness, his beacon of hope in the sea of dispair. She was a perfect fit for him. They were meant to be together. Fate, it seemed, had other ideas. David didn’t believe in Fate. He looked no further than himself to see that. His name was Cross. The name he used as a professional wrestler, which he inherited from his only friend, was Crucifix. He even had a cross-shaped scar over his right eye. But what did it mean? Was it Fate? Was he destined for something great? Something divine? Nobody in the fWo could understand his suffering. As nice as they tried to be to him, none of them sat in the wreckage of the arena in Tel Aviv, surrounded by the dismembered bodies of women and small children that had come to see the show. None of them were covered in blood, listening to the haunting cries of the dying and the survivors who had lost someone close to them. None of them held a charred limb of a small infant while a bloody cross leaked through the dirt and ash on their face. None of them looked to the Heavens and asked “Why?” only to be answered with the sounds of tormented suffering. Dr. Cambridge had known that David was mentally unstable. Session after session, she tried to reach him but had no idea how. She couldn’t understand the pain of having a family that was a lie, a fact that was constantly thrown in his face by the woman he falsely called “Mother.” She didn’t have the training to help him cope with the memories of the obese troll beating him nightly, telling him that he was with her because his own family was smart enough to get rid of him. She could bring the pain out and make David face it; she had no idea how to help him deal with it. The rain pounded against the stained glass windows. David sat in the shadows where he felt he’d always been. He never walked in the light. Never. He always felt like a bug – an insignificant insect that fed on the trash that the rest of the World left behind, with a future lasting only as long as someone didn’t notice him and squash him without a moment’s hesitation. His horrible facial burns kept him in the dark. His mother’s abuse kept him in the dark. Even the plastic surgery, which gave him a normal appearance until Tel Aviv, was sullied by the fact that it came as a result of the suicide of the man who wore the name Crucifix before David did. His life was filled with Pain of every conceivable type. Michelle took that pain away. She was the reward for David’s life of anguish. She was his Savior. Now she had forsaken him. He had nothing left, not even his mind. Lighting flashed, illuminating the huge cross that was mounted high on the framed stained glass window behind the mahogany pulpit. David stood up and kicked the pew in front of him, shattering the bench beneath his maniacal force. “What!” he shouted up at the cross. “Are you talking to me, you fucking clown?” David stepped into the aisle and stumbled toward the pulpit clutching a large piece of the wooden pew. “Everybody keeps telling me that things happen for a reason. My name, this scar, Tel Aviv, Michelle, it all happens for a reason. Bullshit!” David hurled the chunk of wood at the crucifix, but the wood fell low and ended up shattering the colorful window behind it. A gust of wind helped to spread the shards of glass that fell from the window. Rain quickly extinguished the rows of candles that had been lit beneath the cross, swallowing the church in darkness. Wet, cold, empty, David watched the crucifix sway in the wind. He wanted to see it fall and crack against the cold stone floor. He wanted it to break, much like he was breaking. The cross held firm. It wasn’t going anywhere. David knelt down and picked up a large shard of glass. He gripped it firmly in his hand and as it bit into his flesh, blood ran down his wrist. The pain he felt in his hand was nothing compared to what was inside him. No pain ever would match what was inside him. He looked up and smiled. “There is no sign. There is no purpose. There is no Destiny for me,” he whispered into the night. David took in a deep breath and announced as loud as he could, “I give up the name Crucifix. I give up the name Cross.” As the cool rain touched his face, David tapped the glass shard against his scar and looked at the cross. “And this…. This you can have back too.” The long piece of glass punched through his cheek and scraped the bone beneath his eye. He tore roughly and chunks of his flesh fell away. The searing agony filled his insane laughter as he cried out for the final time, “The truth sets me Free! Free! FREEEEE!” -==- Dr. Sandow adjusted his glasses as he walked down the hallway. He didn’t even notice anymore the smell of stale air and urine that always seemed to linger in the hospital’s psychiatric wing. The occasional screams that seemed to echo down the corridor was a little disconcerting at times, but the nurses who frequented the ward understood the terror that prompted the cries. He stared at the rows of doors that lined either side of the hallway. Each one had a small black nameplate beneath the tiny observation window in the door. “Nurse,” he said to the woman walking in front of him. “How is my patient doing?” The nurse stopped in front of one of the doors and looked at her clipboard. “We’ve had to increase his phenothiazine dosage,” she observed from her notes. “His hallucinations are starting to subside and he’s becoming less hostile. We’re still not ready to take him out of restraints though, at least not until his old wounds have healed properly.” Sandow stepped up to the door and looked inside. “Still no idea who he is?” The nurse shook her head. “No sir. His fingerprints haven’t turned up anything and he has no I.D. And his little episode, we can’t even show his picture around to identify him.” Dr. Sandow didn’t feel much pity for the lunatic inside room 3A. Bound in a straitjacket, John Doe sat motionless in the corner of the empty room with his legs stretched out in front of him. His head was still wrapped in gauze from attempts to save what was left of his face. “As soon as he’s more docile I’d like to talk to him.” “Of course Doctor.” The nurse made another note. In the dimly lit room, the doctor managed to catch a slight bit of movement. “It looks like he’s still awake.” “Not likely. We sedated him pretty heavily.” Still, Dr. Sandow was sure he could see John Doe’s eyes… one in particular. “Any word on what the eye tests brought up?” The nurse flipped through the pages on the clipboard. “Severe nerve damage and leaky capillaries. That’s why it’s so red. Eerie looking thing. He hasn’t opened it much though. When he was chopping away at the tissue around his eye, he might have nicked some more nerves. It’s probably very sensitive.” “I’m sure it is. Let’s check around and see if anyone recognizes that type of eye trauma,” Sandow told the nurse. “Let’s put a name with the face.” “Or what’s left of it,” she commented. Dr. Sandow shot her a stern look. “The outside can be fixed easily enough, even for his kind of damage. It’s what is inside that I’m curious about. What could cause a person to do that kind of damage to themselves? In a church no less?” Had Dr. Sandow not left the window to continue on his rounds, he might have seen more movement from his mystery patient. He might have seen the lips move behind the mask of medicated bandages, whispering in the darkness. “I see it now,” the voice hissed with hope. He opened his eyes, the white of the left one contrasting greatly with the almost black right. "So beautiful.”
