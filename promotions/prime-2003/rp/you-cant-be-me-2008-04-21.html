You Can’t Be Me

21 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21 April 2008 Act One Under the cratered gaze of a full moon, a refreshing whisper of ocean breeze tickles the edges of floaty net curtains as it creeps into the Kannons’ Merlin Bay home. Out through the open French-doors, the plants fortifying the veranda collectively shiver in a chill skipping off the ocean, creating just enough of a rustle to stir Mr Jones, who’s curled up for the night on the sofa. Lethargically opening one eye to show a spiteful slit of a pupil slashed down his green eye, the cat stares out through the doors, where the shadows cast by the waving of palm leaves against the glare of the moon dart his focus to all four corners of the doorframe. After a lingering, disgruntled look out into the elements, the feline’s eye creeps shut once more… …but only a few tranquil seconds pass until Mr Jones suddenly bursts up onto all fours as if a frenzied puppeteer had just jolted him up by his strings. Teeth bared, his claws hook into the fabric of the sofa’s cushion, the prickling completing a harsh static sound when blended with the rabid hiss he emits. Another waft of sea breeze if funnelled by the foliage through the French-doors, blowing a curtain-hook loose so that a ghost of sheer white fabric comes to life. A panicked pitter-patter of paws replaces the hiss as Mr Jones falls back from his defensive stand, slithering through into the hallway just as the breeze slams the door behind him. Scurrying down the hallway, the retreating cat passes the master bedroom, showing the equally tattooed and bruised body of a slumbering Xavier Kannon half hanging out of the bed, naked bar a discarded sheet draped across his left heel. The fleeing feline then has to slow and weave, navigating a chicane of two bare, petite feet coming in the opposite direction. “Hey!” squeaks Ellie, dancing over the ball of marmalade fur snaking around her feet. Tightening the belt of her white silk robe, Ellie watches Mr Jones skulk into Izzy’s deserted room, disappearing under the bed. After checking that her husband hasn’t awoken, Ellie pulls the pin on her grenade of bobbed hair, exploding it and sending a cascade of platinum blonde hair down to the small of her back. Cheerily humming a tune reminiscent of the Jackson 5’s ‘ABC’, she floats across the fluffy hall carpet into the bathroom. Working the back-heeling of the door into a dance routine, she spins to the shower, unleashing a torrent that expels a ball of steam up from the shower-tray. “Mmm Mmm Mmm… MmMm-Mm Mmm Mmm Mmm…” she continues to hum in front of the mirror, topaz-blue eyes staring back into themselves. “Feel free to drop the robe anytime…“ Behind her, a dry-yet-playful voice pays her the compliment. “Shouldn’t you be asle-” Ellie warmly smiles as she goes to answer without thinking… before her rose-pink lips freeze and all the warmth is flushed from her face. That voice, it wasn’t her husband’s. “I find that I don’t need quite so many hours, nowadays…“ Perched on the edge of the toilet seat, mimicking Rodan’s ‘The Thinker’ with left hand resting on his knee and square chin resting on the back of his right, sits someone all too familiar to Eleanor Louise Kannon-Hall. “No…” A pink-nailed hand grabs a can of hairspray. Ellie spins around, her face a jumble of fear and disgust as she wields the hair product as a weapon, a well-manicured index finger on the ‘trigger’. “I’m more of a wax man, thanks all the same,” Angelo Deville icily remarks towards the can of hairspray, breaking from his statuesque pose to run a hand across his slicked-back hair. “So, life back on the road everything you hoped it would be?” His thin lips press together in front of his incisors, pushing the corners of his mouth up into a grin as smug as it is sinister. “You’re not here,” Ellie assures herself, tells Deville, and appeals to God. “You’re not here. You’re not here.” “Oh, I’m here,” Angelo counters, swinging one leg over the other and relaxing back, arms crossed over a crisp white shirt. Repelled and repulsed by the icy-blue stare of The Deville, Ellie backs away until she’s almost sitting up on the lip of the sink. Ellie’s arms wrap tightly around herself, half out of modesty to encase her robe and half to try and contain her visibly shaking body. “You’re dead,” is spat from Eleanor’s trebling lips, the tone making the accusation that it’s simply not fair he be there, exclaimated with a tear . “You blew UP! You’re in hell… we got rid of you. You’re in hell!” The shuddering of her whole body resonates through to Ellie’s fingers, and the can of hairspray is rattled loose and onto the floor. “Oh, I’m in hell,” he relishes telling her, his blue eyes glistening with glee as opposed to the tears of Eleanor‘s. “The Devil asked for me by name. Just call this a… day release. A little reward for bad behaviour. And how could I not use it to pay a little visit to my favourite family?” He was the one man, the one soul (or lack thereof) that could turn the majestic Merlin Bay into a nightmarish Cape Fear. Ellie screws her eyes shut then bursts her lids back open, but it fails to refresh Deville out of view. “Yeah, that won’t work,” he informs her, matter-of-factly. “What do you want?” is hissed with the same vitriol that Mr Jones mustered. “All this hostility,” The Deville sighs with heavy disappointment in his voice. “And to think we used to be so close. All I wanted to do was spend what little time I have ‘upstairs’ here to say just how much I admire you for letting your man return to the ring.” The resentful eyes and pursed lips of Eleanor make it plain to see she wasn’t treating it as a compliment. “After all, it’s really you who’s making the sacrifice here, isn’t it?” Deville’s sharp tongue does its best to speak softly. “You always wanted kids, didn’t you? I think I remember you telling me three… two boys and a girl, right?” “Two girls,” Ellie corrects with words that tremble of her bottom lip. “I stand corrected,” a devilishly-yet-subtly grinning Deville accepts. “To think, you could have been starting the family you’d always dreamed of right here in the hous-… no, the home you’d always dreamed of. But, to sacrifice all that for your husband, so that he can exorcise his demons and try to earn his name again… that’s love, Ellie. That’s love.” Even though his death certificate had long been filed, and the investigation into his demise signed off on and boxed away, Eleanor Kannon-Hall still regretted sharing even a hint of her hopes, dreams and fears with The Deville. “Oh, and I nearly forgot,” Angelo tuts, disappointed with himself. “Belated happy birthday. Couldn’t get the time off a fortnight ago. What is it now… 27? Sorry, rude to ask, I know. Always expected you to have had the first by now.” “Stop it,” Ellie whimpers. “Maybe have at least the first two while their Daddy is still able to run around and play with them,” his words continue to stab at her heart with. “But, yeah, you’ve got a cat, at least that’s something. Not really a substitute for a life you’ve created and grown ins-” “I said STOP IT!” Ellie’s fears and frustrations explode in an ear-piercing scream. Tears streaming down her face, Ellie plunges a hand into the cabinet and starts clawing everything off the shelf, flinging it towards Deville. As each bottle smashes off the porcelain of the toilet, Ellie mouths along, “I. Hate. You. I. Hate. You. I Hate. You.” Out of ammunition, she drops to her knees, staring though flooded-eyes at the shattered glass strewn all across the bathroom floor, save for two foot-shaped patches. “Looks like my time’s up,” Deville smirks as he looks down at the broken glass and the shattered Eleanor, respectfully holding his gaze above the view down her robe. “So many friends to catch up with, so little time…” Act Two At the very same second that Ellie first hears the poisonous words of Angelo Deville behind her, a creak winces from the floorboards of the Kannons’ master bedroom, extracting a grunt from the sprawled out Xavier. A puckering of pressured wood is forced out through the joints of the rocking chair as a shadowed figure sways in and out of the moonbeam that bulldozes through the room. “They say imitation is the sincerest form of flattery,” is scoffed in judgement. “But this is just plain insulting.” Rocking back and forth in the chair, Deville’s face is shown contorted with disgust, intensifying every time his granite features sway back into the light. “To think I once wanted to be like you.” Frosted blue eyes, narrowed in disdain, scan around the room, rendered in monochrome under the Caribbean moon. Every photo of the loving couple turn The Deville’s stomach, every blown-up, black and white snapshot of Kannon’s career leaves him feeling soiled by injustice, and the black, white and gold tights that hang from the wardrobe door have the Devil’s Don balling his fist, wishing he was in phase with the living world. He’d of rather lay bleeding and burned, choking on plaster-dust and crushed by rubble than spend one night in this room… yet still he had to visit, he was destined to “This is the mighty Lion, the King of the canvas jungle?” he reels off, derogatory drowning out the grandiose. “C’mon, Christian… at least give me a whimper? Fawn for my favour, my charity. Beg me to pull the thorn from your paw that’s left you stricken this lame.” As the last of his serpentine words flicks off his tongue, Deville rocks back out of the moonlight for a few seconds, letting the sizzle of a frothy wave up the beach fill the pause. “Or…” he probes, rocking back into the moonlight and tilting his head to observe Kannon with a curious right eye. “Since I know you can hear me, you could open your eyes.” A pair of fiery emerald eyes take a few seconds to appear from underneath begrudging eyelids. The shadows cast by the moonlight bring out every sharp feature of The Deville’s face, while bleaching the skin to leave his face a stark rendering of black and white. “Isn’t Día De Los Muertos in the fall?” is all Kannon answers back. For all the reassuring he’d given Eleanor that Angelo Deville had been exorcised from their lives, he’d seen his devilish smirk on every face, those piercing, cold blue eyes in every stare aimed his way. Nothing can creep up on you when you’re continually looking it in the face. “Oh, I’m sorry,” is offered in mock apology by Deville. “I forgot my long history of respecting Mexican culture. And it’s November, to be precise.” Not taking his eyes off Deville, Kannon brings his left heel up, flicking the sheet up over him. “Glass of water?” a faux-polite Xavier offers as he swings himself around cross-legged, sheet draped over his lap. “Or do you find it goes right through you, nowadays.” Deville can see it in him. After all the torment he’d inflicted on Kannon and his family, all the stamps he and his allies had crunched down into his bones, all the prestige he’d drained from the once mighty name, Xavier saw still having a pulse as a victory. I breathe, therefore I win. “Really?” Deville poses. “You really think there’s any humour left in your miserable existence? Hate to break it to you… Ecks Kay… but looking up at all this, well, your life makes Hell look like a saccharine Disney flick.” The tone of the Devil’s Don ices over any warmth Kannon derived from his smug satisfaction. “You picked the Dual Halo to make your big return,” Angelo adopts the role of narrator to give his slant on the tale. “Number 51, the final, extra special entrant. You strode out there in my colours. The stars fell from the sky for you as gold confetti, you said you were a Superstar, and what happened, Christian? You were upstaged by a chick with no face. You and her made it to the final three, and then your paths diverged - one place in it, but a month later she’s the PRIME Universal Champion and you? You? I don’t think I need to tell you what you are, Christian, it will hurt more when you admit it to yourself.” He knows what Deville wants. Deville wants a reaction. Deville wants him to burst out of bed and fling the rocking chair against the wall. Deville wants him to plough his fist through the plaster. Deville wants him to bite. Deville wants him to know that there’s no place, spiritual or corporeal, that he can’t pull at his puppet or his wife’s heart-strings. He remains muzzled. “And somewhere in that concussion-afflicted brain of yours, you actually feel like you’ve rid yourself of me,” Angelo blares out in bewilderment. “How do you think Ellie would react if you ever told her that you envy me every bit as much as she despises me, Christian? I was the hungry young cub that drove the old Lion from the herd and left you to wallow, picking at scraps while I took down one mighty prey after another. You looked in from the outside with envy, with regret… not with disgust. And now you’ve schemed your way back in, made a pact with the hyena that poses among the pride, and chosen Soren Knox to be the big kill that gets you back in their favour.” Casting judgement from below, Deville sets his rocking chair back in motion, relaxing into the tale. “But you can’t be me, Christian,” is delivered with a patronising tone of letting him down gently. “I broke the mould. The best you’ll ever be is a poor imitation, but, hey, when it comes to that… who better than Kannon?” “You quite finished, pink-mist?” is Kannon’s retaliation, barely enough to parry even a word in Deville’s verbal joust. “You’ve even got Cozen beat when it comes to pretending to be someone you’re not,” Angelo reels off, Xavier’s words dissipating into the ether around him. “The real you rivals even Day Lewis when it comes to method acting… you live this false character, afraid to let those know who you really are. The irony of you battling a masked man in just a few days is probably lost on you. You need their cheers, you need that validation. Just imagine it all slipping through your finger-tips if they knew you really are that man they used to jeer, who they’d loiter in packs outside the exits to hurl abuse at. Ashamed of your faith, are we? Don’t you want to burst out in mighty voice and tell the whole world about the new meaning to life you’ve discovered? Or are you glad that Silver was there to conveniently interrupt your little ‘coming out’ party?” Stone-faced, Kannon endures his words, grinding his teeth, biting his lip… like enduring cramp, you just wait for it to pass, each second feeling like an eternity. “Nothing to say for yourself, Christian?” Deville jabs. “You waiting for me to rattle some chains and take you on a visit to career-past? Silence going to be your answer when Soren finds out what you and Silver planned behind his back? Silence going to comfort Ellie when she finally sees just a hint of me in your eyes? Silence going to confront that wall of judgement that will be put up in front of you when you finally let the world know where you draw your spiritual strength from?” “Boom-boom. Boom-boom, boom-boom,” a simmering Kannon sounds out. “Boom-boom. Boom-boom, boom-boom. You miss that sound, Angie? Just listening to your heart-beat? Or how about the smell of that ocean air… it takes you a while to realise that it isn’t really a smell, it’s just how you register something that’s scentless. And that… that pulse of the night, a tree-frog. You even hear that? Go on, hold a hand out, feel the breeze tickle the hairs on the back of your hand up straight… what are you waiting for? Oh, that’s right… you’re just pretending to be alive.” Deville ponders deflating his bravado by letting him know that all the sounds and sensations Kannon is holding on so tight to are drowning out the pained screams from down the hall. “Oh, it burns,” Deville laughs his comeback off with. “And as much as I’d like to stick around up here to hear you boast of being alive past 30 as your one real accomplishment, I have to…” As the chair rocks him back and forth, Deville’s visage dissipates in the moonlight like the Cheshire Cat, leaving a lingering >=) until the breeze wafts it away. Staring through the mocking grin, he sees Ellie sheepishly shuffle in, holding a blood-stained towel around her hand. “I dropped a bottle…”
