Movin' Up?

8 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8th April YESTERDAY Jason let out a half yawn/half sigh as he rolled over in his single bed. He pulled the duvet over his exposed torso to prevent the draft coming through the gap in the window frame piercing his skin, but it didn’t do too much good. He’d had the worn out old thing for as long as he could remember and it points the stuffing was thinner than his father’s hair was. The solitary bedroom was typical of the rest of the apartment; a fucking dive. The wallpaper was in dire need of being replaced and you’d be hard pressed to find a sheet of it that wasn’t either curling up at the edges or stained with god knows what. Old, beaten down furniture was accompanied by fixtures that were falling from their placements and you could barely see the sunlight through the window, that’s how dirt-encrusted it was. It’s a good job that the floor in this place is wooden, otherwise it would really stink of shit. This was the life that The Anti-Superstar had embraced prior to becoming a PRIME wrestler. He’d lived here for the best part of seven years, figuring it to be preferable to living on the streets. The state of the place didn’t drag him down too much; it’s not like he spent a great deal of time there anyway. It’s not like he couldn’t afford a little bit of DIY here and there, street fighting did happen to bring in a nicely-sized pay packet every now and then, he’d just rather spend the cash on alcohol, cheap hookers and other vices. BZZZT! BZZZT! BZZZT! Natas could here the vibration even from his bedroom. Reluctantly he dragged himself from his pit and stepped out of bed, accidentally standing on an empty, discarded juice carton. Wiping the sleep from his eyes he made his way out of his bedroom and through to the kitchen, where he found the cell phone buzzing away on one of the worktops. It was a decent enough device, nothing fancy though. The Samsung was the first cell phone he’d ever had the pleasure of owning, a perk of the deal he’d struck a couple of months ago with his benefactors. He picked it up from the surface and held it in his palm, trying to work out how to answer the damn thing. “Hello?” he croaked shortly after pressing the green button; he equated the colour with positively. “Jason, how are you doing? Its Mr. Jewell here,” the voice on the other end of the line responded. Relived that he had pressed the right button, Jason responded; “Uhhh, right. What’s up?”. He wasn’t best pleased with hearing from one of his agents at this time of the morning, especially with the epic hangover that he felt clouding over his head. Last night had been the first real chance he’d had to go out and celebrate his first appearance in PRIME, and he’d made it one to remember. “Long story short, Jase, we need you to be in Chicago tomorrow morning, can you do that?” “Well shit, I ain’t got nothin’ else on…” pondered the PRIME newcomer. “Thing is though, how the hell am I ‘sposed to get there? I’m in New York, remember…” “There’s a flight tomorrow morning at 9:08, it’d be perfect,” replied Mr. Jewell. “If you’re on that, I’ll meet you at the airport. Some of the PRIME guys want to have a chat, I think it’d be beneficial if you could make it.” “A flight? Damn man, I ain’t ever been on no plane before,” Jason almost growled. “An’ how the hell d’you expect a guy like me to be able to afford that shit? First paycheck doesn’t come through for a couple of weeks yet…” On the other end of the phone Jason heard Mr. Jewell chuckle; Natas found it almost patronising. “Don’t be silly, Jason, it’s on us. We at Potemkin take care of our clients, just gimme the word and I’ll get it booked.” “Hell, alright.” The decision didn’t take Jason long to make at all. A nice little trip to Chicago would fill the void of tomorrow up nicely. He was planning on hitting the gym, maybe heading to the bar afterwards; it would’ve pretty much been exactly the same as every other day he’d lived for the past three months. This would be a nice distraction if nothing else, plus it would keep him out of his rancid apartment. “Good, 9:08 tomorrow, remember that. You can pick your tickets up from the check-in desk. First class, naturally.” First class? Jason could get used to this. * * * TODAY It was the second time in almost as many weeks that Jason Natas had felt unimaginably awkward. The waiting room in the Merchandise Mart was foreign to him, much like the boardroom he’d sat in a few short weeks ago to finalise his PRIME contract. The walls were brilliant white and the floors shiny, black marble; no expenses had been spared here, quite clearly. Apart from the gently tapping of the receptionist’s keyboard a few short metres away, not a sound filled the air. If the air-conditioning wasn’t so needlessly cranked, one could easily fall asleep in this room. Even though seeing professional wrestlers hanging around in the building was probably a pretty common occurrence since the big move on April 1st, he still felt out of place. Dressed in an aged blue denim jacket that had definitely seen better days, a pair of blue jeans, some scuffed and scratched Dr. Martens and with a black bandana tied around his dirty blonde hair, it would be pretty easy for an outsider to realise that this guy didn’t work there. Jason didn’t like the feeling as he sat there with just about every passing smartly-dressed office monkey taking a good, long stare at him. A few weeks ago he’d have solved such issues with threats of violence, or worse. Unfortunately, as part of North America’s biggest professional wrestling organisation, these methods were now unavailable to Jason. The walls of the room were decorated with snapshots of PRIME’s glittering history, with each individual member of the Hall of Fame having their own little bit of wall dedicated to them. An image of Ignatius Lisieux stared down at Jason from across the marble floor. “Eternal Sunshine,” a multi-time champion, the true definition of the world “legend,” and one of the most popular figures that PRIME had ever seen. From voted the 2004 wrestler of the year to bowing out after an incredible contest with Karina Wolfenden, the man had done it all. In the presence of such a looming portrait, a lesser man may feel rather intimidated. Natas took another look at some of the other pictures; Killean Sirrajin, “The Supreme Machine,” whose record-breaking haul of six separate title-reigns had yet to be beaten. Tony Rolo, the recently-returned charismatic figurehead of PRIME, holding the Universal Title proudly in his photograph. And what about Rock Startling, the first ever Intense Champion? As one of the federation’s biggest stars in its formative years, he had embarked on an incredible ten-match winning streak that has since been matched, but never bettered. In the eyes of many, these guys were immortal, most of them perfect role models for any young wrestler looking to break into the business. Jason knew that at some point, though, each and every one of them had been in this situation; waiting patiently inside PRIME headquarters while their respective agents sat in an office talking to some corporate fat cat. Even the biggest names in the business have got to start somewhere, but Jason was willing to bet that not one of them had been on the ride that he had. He knew that the business was littered with competitors with less-than-cheery histories, and that wrestling shows across the globe were crammed with over-the-top, depressing, “woe-is-me” jerkoffs, and more importantly he knew that many PRIME fans would be quick to toss him in that pile. Sure, he came from a broken home, and yeah, he may have lived in a state of poverty, but would he bitch and moan about it in national television? Absolutely not; he was so much more than a face in the crowd. The hall of fame members that surrounded him weren’t like Natas though, even in the fledgling years of their careers. In twenty-five years on this planet he’d never stepped onboard an aeroplane, for example. It was a breeze, of course, and he stepped out of the American Airlines flight in the Windy City just two hours after departing New York, but it was still something entirely new to him. Given a choice, he’d have probably preferred not to sit in about the high-class businessmen who spent the duration of the flight looking down their nose at him in the first class section. At the moment he figured that would be akin to buying a burger from McDonalds and complaining about the quality of the meat. Utterly pointless, considering the life of grime that PRIME and his three new agents (Messrs Jewell, Loftman and Najera… Potemkin Management Inc. collectively) had plucked him from. He’d been there for almost half an hour and contemplations on the achievements and careers of the PRIME Hall of Fame members had featured heavily in his mind. His near dream-like state was shattered, however, as the gentle closing of a door along the corridor shattered the serenity of the waiting room. The Anti-Superstar glanced to his right, noticing Mr. Jewell walking down the corridor towards the waiting room. With an A4-sized leather document holder under one arm, Jewell looked just as pristine as he had done when Jason had met him at the airport this morning. His grey pinstripe suit was somehow still perfectly pressed, despite the fact that the high-flyer had spent much of the day on the move. The thing that amazed Natas the most though was the fact that there was hardly a stray hair to be found in his side-swiped blonde haircut. Hairspray, it’s gotta be, Natas figured. “Well, what’s the deal, boss?” Jason asked his agent as he rose to his feet. He hadn’t spent a great deal of time thinking about what was going on in the boardroom, Jewell’s “performance evaluation” explanation this morning sufficed. The Potemkin partner sighed. “Like I said earlier, performance evaluation, it’s a routine thing; they do it after everyone’s first show…” “Well, whadda they reckon?” “Good, for the most part,” Jewell began on a positive note. “They like your intensity and attitude, and more significantly they think that you’ll have no problem fitting in with the current crop of competitors… in wrestling terms, at least.” Jason sneered; if PRIME and Potemkin thought that he’d integrate himself that easily they obviously didn’t know too much about him. “There was one thing they were concerned with though, Jase,” continued Jewell, the enthusiasm in his voice evaporating. “They think you were hitting a little bit too hard in your match, especially when it came to dealing with Bryan Dawkins.” “Pffft, little Hawaiian shithorn had it comin’,” snarled The Anti-Superstar dismissively. “This is serious, Jason. We’re talking about fucking PRIME here, the biggest wrestling promotion in the world!” Natas found himself slightly taken aback by this; it was the first time he’d heard Jewell swear. “They’d like you to just tone it down a little bit this week… not too much, they don’t want to see you lose too much of that grit and determination, but you can’t go knocking seven shades of shit out of your fellow roster members. That’s not how it works in this game.” “The fuck is this bullshit?!” barked Jason, startling the receptionist. “They hired a fighter, they got a fighter… they knew what they were getting when they offered me the contract, and now they’ve changed their minds?!” Sensing that Jason making a scene probably wasn’t good for business relations, Jewell placed his hand on the back of the PRIME newcomer and led him away from the waiting room. “It’s not like that, Jason, I’ll explain further in the cab… I’ve got some more stuff for you too. Your first ‘errand,’ if you will, as specified in the contract we discussed at the start of the year…”
