The sun hovered high in the sky behind, baking the rolling hills of the Deville estate that lay in his wake, projecting his shadow ever-forward at a skewed slant, floating toward the cliff-edge like a ghostly mist. A hellish wind ruffled the collar of his black, buttoned shirt and made his slacks, of the same colour, hug the muscles of his legs as he slowed to a stop, gazing out over the sparkling water beneath to the deciduous forest beyond. The hot air otherwise had no outward effect on him. It had been a long time since he’d last killed, the longest stretch of non-fatal violence he’d precipitated since he’d had himself murdered in order to align with the devil, a plan inspired and grounded in revenge. Executed to perfection, it had not only elevated The Deville beyond death, it enabled him to wield it as most men brandished words over a telephone or a computer screen, without threat of consequence. The murder of Salvatore, the Mafia Don who had usurped his position from Deville’s own father in opportunistic cold blood, was the climax of four years of careful planning and manoeuvring, the culmination of twenty years of deceit and betrayal. The backlash was instantaneous yet subdued. Associates of the mob boss from all across the continent tried to avenge the death with assassination, but The Deville sliced through them like a brutal bullet of truth through a spiderweb of lies, cutting a swath to the centre and decapitating innumerable mirror-eyed heads. The eventual truce was not the result of a handshake or a compromise, but attrition and fear. More men died by the sword they swore by over the course of six months than had fallen in the ten years previous. Many were nameless henchmen of no consequence, but many more were leaders and generals, and when the replacements of the latter fell as cruelly as their predecessors, the armies began to scatter. His brief respite from slaughter had whiplashed with enough force to re-make the underworld of crime, as if it had built up behind a fallacious dam destined to crumble. Most of those in the know speculated it was happening again, but others wondered if maybe The Deville really was ready to move on. Was this another calm before the storm, or would he finally quit the life he’d known since adolescence, the life he’d been bred and honed for? “Are you just going to stand there trying to look majestic,” began the light-tongued mockery from far below, “or are you coming in?” Letting his gaze drop to the labyrinth of pools he’d had built in imitation of the grotto at the Playboy Mansion, with hot-jets and waterfalls and balmy vegetation that had no business in the Canadian climate, Deville smiled at the new dam to his murderous nature. Karina couldn’t see the grin for Angelo’s superimposition of the sun, washing out his features in blinding shadow, but she rolled her eyes as if she could. Deville remained motionless for a long while, admiring the view as Karina arced backwards and submerged gracefully, using lithe, powerful legs to send a splash dangerously close to wetting him. There was no crimp in the fluid motion, no sign of the injury to her knee, and The Deville’s smile grew as he watched her skim beneath the surface, eyes closed, taut body sashaying with uncanny control. Hope that she was recovering, reduced to a muted worry in the back of his mind a moment ago, bloomed anew. It was one of many reasons he’d invited her to stay with him; his grotto was more than just a breathtaking aphrodisiac, it was a lake of recuperative hot-tubs disguised as recreation. His shirt was half-off by the time Karina buoyed to the surface, shielding her eyes in another attempt to take him in. Shedding it to reveal golden plates of rippling muscle, he crumpled it into a ball and hesitated, considering something, then tossed it down to the waiting K-Wolf. Intercostal muscles corded and twisted like a slithering snake as he stretched his arms overhead, then he was stepping off the ledge, deftly spinning slowly backward so he entered the azure oasis in a near-perfect dive. Karina had her back turned to him when he surfaced, to no-sell the spectacle, bringing a fresh smile to Deville’s dripping face. Diving again, he swam to the shallow end she inhabited and goosed her, then resurfaced with a fit of laughter only partially restrained by the indignant hands planted on his shoulders, trying to dunk him. It ended quick enough with the peace offering of a kiss. “How are you feeling?” Deville asked as they parted, prompting Karina to glance around in the adorably abashed way she still had when required to talk about herself. She began to shrug off her answer, but sudden stillness stole the gesture as her eyes fell on something and her entire body froze, as if petrified. Deville craned his head to follow her gaze and found his shirt, splayed atop the water, one sleeve sunk and trying to drag the whole thing down. Around the cuff of the other sleeve was a spreading red discoloration, the focus of Karina’s attention. Her words came as a guarded, suspicious whisper, “Is that blood?” ~><>…<><~ The Deville had been down this road before. In truth, he’d travelled it more times than he cared to count. There were subtle differences with each ill-fated journey, but in the grand scheme it was always the same. The sun was halfway up the sky and dead ahead of Deville. Had he been in a cheap car, he’d likely be squinting angrily, wondering why he was driving due east in the middle of the morning. Fortunately, the windshield of his luxurious coupe DeVille tinted automatically to adjust to varying light, acting as oversized shades for such blinding occasions. He wasn’t alone in the car, but he may as well have been. The extent of conversation over the past half hour began and ended with his passenger remarking on the wonderful weather they’d been having and Deville grunting noncommittally. He had a plethora of CDs in the car, but they were staying in the glovebox, and the local radio would have been anathema to his mood. On the surface The Deville seemed calm as a meadow, gazing tranquilly at the road, but beneath he was as molten as the earth’s core, red and black and roiling. He didn’t want to do this. He’d been avoiding it, telling himself it wasn’t inevitable, trying to fool himself into believing that once – just once – he could move onto something more. But that wasn’t meant to be. He was in a cell wrought of his own past, and every time he managed to bend the bars and break free, he found himself staring through another in an interminable line of rows. His passenger, of an age with him but aged less kindly, his dyed black hair already receding at the temples. The unfortunate circumstance was made worse by the style of hair he donned, akin to that of The Deville, and unless the latter missed his guess, adopted not long ago in a superficial ruse. He wore a grey suit, cheaply made, and as Deville’s foot turned to led on the accelerator, his nervous eyes jerked between Deville’s and the digital speedometer. He’d approached Deville the week before about a favour, a favour Deville didn’t want any part of, but he had tentatively agreed in exchange for turnabout. Being more persuasive and powerful in every sense of either word, Deville made his favour take prominence. Strangely, the man hadn’t inquired after details. Predictably, The Deville didn’t volunteer them. Suddenly smirking wryly, Deville was about to tell him to put his eyes back in his head, that the country road was barren, but the man broke the silence before he could, hoping to relieve the tension. He couldn’t have picked a worse subject. “So,” he began, trying to disguise a nervous shrug as a loosening of his shoulders. “How are things between you and the K-Wolf?” The smile he beamed was purely for show. Seeming to notice, the passenger abruptly cut his laughter short and cleared his throat. “Okay then,” he said, turning his gaze to the window. “I get the hint.” Deville nodded to himself, hoping that was true. Both sides of the road were reduced to a green blur, and although the windows were up and the AC on, the wind hurtling by was the loudest sound in the car. “Why are we going so fast?” he demanded shakily, gripping the armrest. Then, more appropriately, “Where are we going?” The Deville smiled a humourless smile, taking his eyes from the road to stare right into the hollow, deceptive eyes of his passenger. “In case you haven’t figured it out yet, I know who you are.” The man’s tongue darted between his lips, and his free arm began inching down his pantleg. “W-what do you mean?” “I brought you here to kill you,” The Deville stated simply, calmly, as if explaining they were going camping. The passenger – the undercover officer – darted for the gun fastened to his ankle, but as he did The Deville rocked him with a right hand and unfastened his seatbelt. Slamming the brakes, he fought with the steering wheel to keep the vehicle on the road as the pretender, helpless and insecure, smashed his chin on the dashboard. When the car screeched to a halt, Deville popped him again, then took the liberty of removing the pistol from the ankle holster. The gun on his shoulder went next, deftly removed as he murmured incoherently and tried to regain his bearings. Holding both guns, The Deville waited patiently for the man to sputter his excuses, his lies, to protest that he didn’t know what was going on. The Deville knew, though. He always knew. Why people doubted his resourcefulness, his willingness to act on what he knew and found, he could never figure out. “It’s not too late, Deville,” he spluttered at last. “Turn state and you won’t serve a day. I only offer option B because I like the sport of it. If you take the headstart, then when I catch you, it won’t be with a bullet. I’m going to want to relish in your misery. In all your studying, in your armchair mobbing, did you ever read about a Columbian Necktie?” The man’s wide, vacant eyes were testament that he had not. His fist white-knuckled on the doorhandle made plain the choice he was going to make. Sighing, The Deville started counting down. Before the man had gone ten feet, the window was down and a gun pointing out of it. The first shot took him in the knee.
