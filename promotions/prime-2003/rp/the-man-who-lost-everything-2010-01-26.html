The Man Who Lost Everything

26 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26-01-10 nothing left...nothing... The hulking beast bobbed dejectedly with every step, wide shoulders hunched against a wind of change and head hung low in humiliation. Gazing at the ground he wished himself buried beneath it, earth choking up every orifice and drowning out the world as the taunts echoed around in his brain. The leviathan carved a path through the flowing crowd who adapted to the obstacle before them and began filtering around either side of him. Soon he was an island, surrounded by fleeting faces and thoughtless chatter and only the occasional sliver of human contact that let him know he was still alive. The show was over. While the bastards celebrated hard, the giant just slunk away into obscurity. Away from the embarrassment and the hundreds of questions the behemoth tucked himself into a dark empty corner in the back of the arena. Lip quivering and vision blurring, he let the last of his pride crumble into ruin; a broken colossus. After some time the child happened upon him. A smile that would outlast the heavens met his gaze and with it came familiar gold. Buckled by the heart of the child, he swatted the gold away and with his last breath uttered such blasphemy to the boy. The hairless brow above those big innocent eyes furrowed as the giant tore down the mythical walls around him, laying bare the truth to the child that there were no heroes, just men who got lucky sometimes. No more a monster, but hardly a man. With the dreams of one shattered and the hopes of countless others dashed he disappeared into the night to live out his penance alone. The world was a much scarier place when you weren't at the top. Even for a monster. Gone were the intricacies of every detail, marred by a blind ignorance. All colour soaked up from his day and caged within the roiling clouds over Shanghai. Still he walked. The realization came all at once that he loved her. Before she was taken away from him. He loved Gloria, and he couldn't banish her from his head. If she were by his side he would welcome the crashing heavens with open arms. Nothing would faze the giant. Her love gave him new strength, but no more. All in the past, along with the titles and the domination and.. Survival was ensured. At worst he promised he would survive. And yet he barely felt as though he existed. Drifting on another plain now the blues turned to blacks and faces became shadows. Someone shouted his name, begging for an autograph...but he was gone. Skyscrapers faded with the crowds. He couldn't remember when the smoggy machine oil and food grease of the city became a fragrant breeze carrying sweet spices with it. Soon there were rooftops, laughter and livestock. Smoke billowed from chimneys and the world took on a soft golden hue. Tired and aching, he passed through. Something flashed from the corner of his eye. The giant sourced the flash, ambling to the glass that had reflected the sunlight. From behind it a chorus of pipe music floated in the air. Pressing on the glass the giant peered into the window, seeing his own reflection. Haggard from the travel, he sighed a fog onto the glass. Wiping it away with his forearm another face suddenly peered back at him in place of his own. The giant stepped back, startled. It was made of cracked and knotted wood. Features carved into a contortion of agony, its mouth split by tusks protruding from the orifice and snaking up its cheeks. A simple mask. Amused by his reaction, he stepped inside to find a small fat old man in a kimono with a puckered face and a flowing white moustache. He was playing a bawu in front of an old tapestry depicting a great battle. The giant waited, refusing to address the old man as he finished his song and looked up with a chubby smile. In the wrinkles of his eyes the giant saw a strange fear, as though the old man trembled in his presence and yet understood the consequences. He thought he had put the man at ease by simply pointing at the mask. The old pipe player obliged silently in the presence of the monster, unable to comprehend why he gave him the mask, nor why he didn't stop to tell the behemoth that this wasn't a shop and the mask not for sale. This was the kind of path the Chinese believed took one to their destiny. If he had stormed in and tore the house apart and still only taken the mask the old man would have let it happen. In the future he would tell his grandchildren the tale of the giant who rose from fire to take the death mask from him... There was an inscription in the inside face of the mask, discreet-like, worked into the knotted wood with a precise hand. It felt slick in his grasp as he worked it over, staring through the open eyes. Placing it over his own face he smelled a dank musk, but ignored it as he opened his eyes and saw through the mask. He saw things. Felt them. Then he remembered. From his pocket he produced feeble Western compensation for the ornament and left the old man muttering in his wake. Were he to learn Chinese he'd know that the old man had quoted the inscription in the wood, this is made for you. The giant took it with him back to Shanghai. Wrapped it in a bin liner and hid it at the bottom of his bag, remembered the old feelings he felt when it touched his face and zipped up the bag. The familiarity of it. A hint of power. The broken giant spent the better part of the week in the company of his colleagues in the gym or around promotional events. Every one of them, regardless of how long they spent around the colossus, all wondered to themselves why the man who lost everything still had a smile on his face.
