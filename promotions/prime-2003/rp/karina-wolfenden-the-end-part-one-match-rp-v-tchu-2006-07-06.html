Karina Wolfenden : The End - Part One (Match RP - v Tchu)

Date: 06/07/06 The End.Written: 6th July, 2006. 'And while the seagulls are crying,We fall but our souls are flying. And oh, my love, my love...And oh, my love, my love...We both go down together.' - The Decemberists. I - Twilight As his streaming doe-eyes look up at me, they crack red, blood-shot. Behind the glassy reflection, fear hammers against the lens, wanting to be released. I see myself in those eyes, in more than one way. Sweat glues his greasy hair down across his forehead, letting forth a waterfall of slick perspiration that forces his glaring eyes to wince. I can see a freckle wobble as his neck throbs, stressed artery thumping against stretched skin with every strained heartbeat. I can't hear the sirens yet. 'Shut up,' I curtly state as I see his lips part with more than just a quiver. 'Shut the fuck up.' His eyes roll down, dragging eyelids shut behind them. Wheezing breaths stream together apologies, profanities, and what he wished would have been the final words spoken to his mother. He needs his inhaler. He needs a lot of things, one of which I'll be happy to gift him. There's a rumble of thunder as the sky bruises a dark purple, clouds swelling ominously, before they rupture. Thick rain plummets down through the already sticky, humid air, slapping down like slackened treacle against my bare arms, and plastering my clothes onto me like wallpaper paste. 'I didn't...' he blurts out, breathless. 'I didn't mean it to go this far...' His face is reddening through to purple as his salty eyes creep back open. 'Shut up,' is again all I'll offer by means on conversation. There's a layered, distant wailing. Too sharp to be natural. Sirens. They're coming. There's a prophetic, violent discharge of lightning, the eruption cracking a blinding whip across the brooding evening sky. He shivers, despite the sweltering damp. My fingers fidget in the over-sized grip of the Walther P99. My hand is numb, fingers battling their repulsion from the weapon. The rest can't be said for the rest of my body, where the sickly sharp taste of vomit lingers at the back of my throat... like the carrot and orange soup I could barely stomach a sip of for lunch yesterday. He sees the red tip of the striker protruding ominously from the slide, and knows I'm cocked, not just the gun. 'It wasn't meant to b-... it wasn't meant to end like this,' he blubbers, before sniffing mucus back deep into his throat. 'I just... I...' It's tragic. He's tragic. I'm beginning to believe that I'm tragic. So on what level shouldn't this be a tragedy? In my head, I'm singing Grace. 'There's the moon asking to stay.' The sirens are close enough for me to know there's 3 cars. 'Long enough for the clouds to fly me away.' I'm past tired, and have staggered well into exhausted territory. In hours I've aged at least a year. Where his eyes are streaming, mine are red and raw... and the greasy shimmer of his skin contrasts with the stressed dryness of my own. My index finger strokes the trigger, subconsciously, before I snap myself back into control, and relax it. 'Though it's my time coming, I'm not afraid... afraid to die.' I'm a tease. 'My fading voice sings of love.' 'I...' it continues, as he wallows in the ruins of his dream. '... I love you.' His eyes close. 'But she cries to the clicking of time...' Forearm flames blend to an inferno as his hands slap shut in prayer. '... Of time.' If there's one thing I hate, it's having to repeat myself. 'Shut...' 'Wait in the fire.' It needs to end. I pull the trigger back over its threshold. '... up.' II - The Sands, They Are A Tricklin' As the ring pull of his 3rd can of coke hisses, the fine caramel spray is whisked away on the breeze from a trio of fans that are spotted around his desktop. The sun, nearing its summit, and melting the city below at 106 degrees, looks like it's about to burn through the over-length curtains that engulf the windows. Left hanging open, the subtle breeze bravely pushing through the muggy air outside ripples the heavy fabric, making waves of the fierce amber back-lighting. Even the booming, burley voice of the delivery driver dropping off across the street is bogged down in the humidity, barely audible to those two lanes of traffic away. 'Fuck offfff!' he lashes out in frustration, swatting at a persistent fly with a rolled magazine... the parts exposed showing little but female flesh. Seemingly enjoying the antagonising, the insect darts in for another pass, drawing a clumsy swat that nearly knocks an empty glass off the littered table. Clamping both meaty hands on the glass, his elbow nudges one of the fans, almost sending that toppling to the floor. The brief exertion breaks beads of sweat across his forehead, a couple managing to escape his fringe. He adjusts the fan, wafting more of the stale air across his clammy face. 'C'mon, c'monnn,' he urges as the download bar prepares to make its final lunge towards the 100% line. 'You've been queued for, like, a fuckin' week!' Dropping his head back, a cascade of cola is sent bubbling down his throat, where they're welcomed by the digesting jumbo-bag of Doritos that had been polished off a few hours earlier. The extended silence between songs is then shattered as over-driven guitars slash through teeth-rattling drums... before both are drowned out by a throaty roar that's welcoming the coming of Satan or something. And then it hits the 100% prime_rev98_karinawolfenden_v_silviofiore.avi Rather than leaving the file to be shared with others, he selfishly moves it to a folder within a folder within a folder. Bitten-raw nails feverishly tap away at his mouse, before the stretched skin of his flame-tattooed forearms quivers when the mouse's ball sticks to his stained mat. Another bag of Doritos is ripped open, and three heaped palm-fulls gorged upon. Stale sweat wafts around the floor like ether, huddling for safety below the currents of the fans. In the darkened, forgotten corners, life thrives in the most unsavoury of places... feeding of mould and crumbs. 'Oughhhhh-agh...' he belches, double chin resonating in some kind of vibrato. Hitting backspace, he retraces digital footsteps, showing the full extent of his library. Matches, segs, interviews, in-ring photos, posed photos, out of ring photos, misc photos, private photos, doctored photos, family, friends, significant others, fan art, fan fiction, webpages, music, desktop wallpapers, articles, angle ideas, e-mails, letters, booking schedule. All focussed forcefully on the one woman. Karina Marie Wolfenden. Unbeknownst to him, a precession of ants is doing recon under the desk, marching over a discarded strip of a furiously defaced Angelo Deville poster. 'Curtis!' The shrill voice of a middle-aged woman weaves through the frantic strands of heavy metal. Out of instinct, the kind learned through experience, he hurriedly closes two of the internet windows. As the wall of windows topples, his desktop is shown... a shrine to the woman known as the K-Wolf. Images of anguished despair and pure, unadulterated joy. Draped in gold and leaking crimson. Soaring through the skies or sprawled on the canvas. Hair dyed black, blonde, blue, red, orange, and pink. All sides of her personality brought together for maybe the first time. 'CURTIS!' Her harsh, military-esque inflection trails off amid stomping footsteps. The floorboards barely manage to endure their punishment. 'Coming, Mom!' he sharply snaps in reply, heavy fingers playing a textual game of Whack-a-Mole with the unfortunate keys. Eyes narrow in anger as the bleaching glare of the monitor highlights his face in the shadowed room, showing anger lines where bloated features collide like gelatinous tectonic plates. 'CURTIS!!!' With a forthright lashing of his right index finger, he spanks the return key. 'God damn it,' he grumbles throatily under his breath, before clearing his airway with a pair of mucus-rumbling coughs. One document remains open, which oddly energetic fingers add his final thoughts to. Then, navigating through a maze of decoy folders, to the deepest, darkest, secluded corner of his hard-drive, Curtis saves. Going through the routine he knows so well, he locks the folder, hammers in the password, and saves his masterpiece... his rapidly spiralling out of control dreams in sexual textual form. What nobody else could write for her but him. Karina Wolfenden : The End. III - Diagnosis Murder? 'Man, that Curtis needs some serious fucking help...' Cracking the window open, a young man named Jack Hawkins - better known as 'New' Jack Hawkins - leans out into the breeze and cups his lighter-clasping hands around the end of his freshly-rolled cigarette. As the cherry burns the fiercest of orange, his fills the window frame with his body, not wanting the smoke to be sucked back into is room where only a suspicious onslaught of deodorant could neutralise it... just. Dropping down into the computer chair is Derek Finch, who goes my the slightly more threatening, if infinitely more cliché name of 'The ScareKrow' Derek Draven. 'Dude,' Derek remarks, knowing all-too-well. 'He needs a lobotomy... and for the surgeon to slip.' Holding the cigarette down under the lip of the window, Jack slips back down onto the thick carpet below. 'You need to check out the shit he was posting this morning. I swear, that guy is going to have a heart attack halfway during a post.' 'Saves our brains the trauma of having to read it,' is Derek's honest opinion. 'Whoa, you weren't kidding. This shit is classic.' 'Read the bit down at the end,' Jack urges, before snaking back through the open window to taint his lungs with another barely-appreciated drag. 'He threatens to call someone he knows in Denver and have him and his gang kill that Lurker 4 Life guy just for claiming that Karina Wolfenden got knocked up by Deville.' 'I think 'kill' is a bit of an understatement, dude,' Derek almost has to chuckle in disbelief. 'You meant he was going to have him gutted and hung from a tree by his own intestines before they double-stomp his head into the curb then break his neck with a C4.' 'Yeah,' follows the pitch of Jack's rolling eyes. 'Because that move works in real life.' With an odd sense of comedic awe, Derek continues to read the deranged rantings, putting up a good fight in stifling the laughter that tries to pour out. Then, his eyelids just peel back in amazement as he sees that Curtis has replied to a post questioning why Karina Wolfenden is on TV less than the A-List by writing a booking plan for the whole of 2006, with how he'd do things. 'Does this guy even HAVE a job?' Derek's head shakes while his faith in humanity plummets through the floorboards and deep into the house's foundations. 'And how the fuck do you kill someone in the face?' IV - Momentum I've picked a fine time to get itchy feet about flinging myself from high places. 'Anyone who's thrown away anything hard is going to get killed,' I dryly mutter to myself. 'In the face.' My fingers are clamped around the rusty railing of the fire escape, feeling the grainy cancers of iron oxide turn to power against my palm. There's a definite feeling that I might not have to jump... so much as hold on for the ride. I knew that my life was getting a little too sedate for that of a wrestler. 'Okay, here we go...' I try to spur myself on with, before I convince my ever-so-receptive self that a shimmer I saw was the sun on glass. 'Or not.' Their voices pay no respect to the walls, carrying through brick and plasterboard, then out the windows into my ear. They know my name, they know everything. Except where I am. My self-conscious side begins to wonder what else gets heard through the walls. Trying to swallow any reservations with a nervous gulp, I zip up my leathers, and entwine the fingers of grease-smeared hands into the closest I get to prayer. 'Don't kill yourself,' it goes, 'you crazy bitch.' They've found the right door, they just don't know it. They even manage a dainty knock. I can almost heart Miles' thunderous pulse through the locked-down window, even above the plethora of profanities I'm guessing he's gushing in my direction. He buys me some time, turning the lock back and forth to pretend he has three of them. He could have bought a little more time by keeping it locked and letting them kick it down, though. That's what I get for forgetting to water his plants. For a month. In August. 'Sorry,' I hear him apologise through the barrier of wood. 'It's been sticking the last few days.' And it's high time my feet stopped sticking to the fire escape. Closing my eyes, I step off the edge, and wait for gravity to kick in. My body leaves my stomach behind as I plummet, leaving it to thump up against my throat and almost expel its contents out after me like a sweet acidic exhaust fume. There's a nosey side to me that takes a split-second out the rapidly escalating proceedings to check out the wallpaper through the window I flash past. As my feet touch down on the top sack in the pile of garbage, the whole haphazard pyramid gives way, rearranging itself so that I'm sucked into its middle. My zips slash the thin black plastic as I plough through them, haemorrhaging the bile of decomposition, before I come to a stop, foetal in my warm womb of waste. I'm born again in a torrent of rotting amniotic fluid, my head emerging from the plastic sacks ready to scream like a baby. 'Someone is going to die for this,' I spit, face souring as my nostrils fill with the unsavoury odour. 'And there goes my hopes of getting a cab.' Casting a nervous eye up towards the fire escape, I quickly ass through jogging, and break into a full sprint, taking the 'scenic route' through the labyrinth of allies. Making the most of my knowledge of the territory, I run a figure of eight crossing through another three of them, then double back on myself for a loop, before breaking off in a new direction. If I can get myself disoriented, they probably aren't in a much better predicament. Well, except they know who 'they' are, which you could call a huge fucking advantage as far as intel goes. I thought intel instead of intelligence... all I need now is a headband. It's very rarely that you miss sleeping beside a man who keeps an even bigger gun under the gun under his pillow. Now would be one of those times. It's getting close to 4pm, a time which just manages to show through the dank brown film that's drying over my watch, and the sun can no longer cling to the tops of the tallest buildings, giving me shadow to skulk through. As much as I hate to admit it, I need help. Serious help. The kind of help that comes with more security guards on the payroll than could ever be needed. And the kind of help that comes with grovelling. Stretching my frame out to its thinnest, I slip through the split, hanging door one of the restaurants old out-buildings, crouching in the stuffy shadows between old, redundant chiller cabinets. Speed dial #8 both gives the most 90's of hints as to the closeness of our relationship, and flashes up the less-than-informal name of 'Father'. Even those who tried to grow up way too fast revert back to the most child-like of instincts. Someone who can keep them safe. In an ideal world, of course. 'Please hold,' says even more about our relationship than his spot at #8. I shift deeper into the shadows, listening out for footsteps, but can hear little above the hostile atmosphere of the restaurant's kitchen. 'I'm afraid Mr Wolfenden is in meetings all afternoon,' his secretary, who doesn't sound like the one he'd decided he loved alongside my mother, informs me. 'Would you like to be put through to his voicemail?' 'Tell him it's his daughter,' I bluntly discard any polite telephone manner with. 'In that case,' I can imagine the sneer just in the tone of her voice, 'may I suggest carrier pigeon.' She hangs up. Perhaps it did sound like the one he'd decided he was in love with... just as I’d decided I was in love with slashing her tyres. When it comes to reaping what I sow, I never seem to have quiet harvest. 'Fuck,' is the word I choose to express myself. 'Fuck, fuck, fuck, fuck, fuck, fuck, fuck, fuck.' I pride myself on a vast vocabulary. Hunting through my phonebook, I see if I ever saved his cell phone number... ever. Drawing a blank, I choose to trump the secretary by going above her. Speed dial #2. Mother. V - Homecoming Weighed down by the bounty of an afternoon’s therapeutic spending, Sandra Wolfenden struggles up the steps towards the front door. Just about managing to keep herself balanced upright, she paces like a tight-rope walker towards the door... only for her phone to go off. Making it clear to all that a career in the circus was her life's true calling, she keeps all the bags in check, and still manages to pluck her ringing cell phone from its pocket. Stooping down towards sand-bagged arms, she answers, and I can hear the rustling of wrapping over her voice. 'I need help,' I forgo any welcome to openly admit, 'I need you to get Dad out of his meeting, and I need for him to make some calls, pull some strings, whatever... I need him, and I ne-' 'What's that?' she questions, brow furrowing as she struggles to make out my distorted appeals. 'Are you going through a tunnel?' The building designed to keep the cool in pretty much works the same for cell phone signals. Why can't they make trains out of this stuff so I feel less inclined to land one on talkative early-morning commuters. 'Try and call me from a land line,' I'm told as she struggles to pluck her keys from her pocket while juggling overloaded shopping bags. 'Just get Dad out of the meeting, please,' I force out with enough impetus to hopefully breach the barrier. 'This is urgent... and, I don't... something here isn't right. Far bloody from it. And I need his help... and I'm not talking money or a reservation when they say they're full.' 'What was that about money, dear?' I hear back. 'Yo-...n't....ain...ou?' My head thumps back against the damp wall. Somehow managing to handle bags, phone, and keys, she turns over the lock and wings the door open, dumping her shopping down on the mats... and at the feet of an uninvited guest. I hear a crackling scream, then the line goes dead.
