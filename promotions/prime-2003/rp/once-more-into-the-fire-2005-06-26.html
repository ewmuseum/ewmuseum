Once More Into The Fire

26 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26/06/05 Few people have this. Have what I have. The sights, the smells, the taste. The sense of completeness, satisfaction in more than just the fleeting present. The Caribbean’s deep, rich topaz blends in with the unblemished sky, leaving the horizon as almost a subconscious guess. It's lashings up the bleached sands add a soothing undercurrent to the melodic chorus of the birds' morning recital. The sun rises behind me as I just observe in awe, as I seem to each and every morning, casting the shadows of the lush foliage over me. If I wasn't so artistically retarded, I'd be tempted to take up painting. But in reality, I've never even taken a photo of the heavenly view, not wanting to insult its splendour in two dimensions. 'Chris?' Isabelle's voice prodded through the dense, vivid green that surrounded me. I sat up as her footsteps crackled over twigs, and scythed through long grass. Scraping the hair from my face, I turn to meet here with a smile that she already had waiting for me, in her usual, refreshingly perky way. Merlin Bay had brought out the green in her once dull, greyed eyes, and now us Kannon siblings shared the same shimmering, emerald irises. The years looking after our father had drained her physically, emotionally, and in other ways I felt too guilty to even consider. But this place is the closest man will come to the fountain of eternal life. It refreshes souls, warms spirits, and revitalises ambition. To step out the front door is to experience what most save up all year to treat themselves to for just a week. That pure, unpolluted heat of the sun that you could bask in all day. The crisp air, never heavy or smoggy, which could purify the lungs of a 40-a-day smoker. The heady hint of pollen gave it fragrance, while the ripening fruit all around us sweetened it as every day went by. 'Juice?' Isabelle then asked as her head emerged through the fanning leaves. 'The Lightfoots brought over some Mangos this morning, so we've got them, orange, pineapple, and papaya.' I didn't miss being on the road for one second. I didn't miss wrestling for one second. I didn't miss wrestlers for one second. For 3 years, bumping my body for pops, driving through the night, signing photo after photo, I'd dreamed of this. It was the end that justified the means. The bright light at the end of the long, painful, depressing tunnel. 'Thanks,' I offered with eager sincerity as I took a glass from the tray. 'Much on today?' Tucking her sarong under her, Isabelle took a seat beside me, another infatuated with the view. 'I could do with a ride down to the market if you and Ellie are going to be around?' Since I'd retired, another Kannon had emerged on the wrestling scene. One who could actually appreciate what she was achieving, and managed to avoid the aspects of the business that made me want to jack it all in every week. Throughout the wrestling world, Isabelle Kannon designs were there to be seen. Original patterns and colour schemes, and with a name-brand that was beginning to surpass mine. At least one Kannon could take something positive from the business. 'I've got a few new t-shirt and vest prints I want to test out,' she added, her inflection carrying the passion of a woman who was truly in love with her craft. 'And, well, I've been working on something for you, too...' I knew where she was going. My acceptance of Angelo Deville's offer had come with one understanding. 'We weren't going to mention that,' I groaned, not that I'd managed to keep it from my thoughts for even a second since signing. 'As Plato once philosophised, tough shit.' Maybe I'd forgot to mention that the originality and colourful designs pretty much encapsulated my sister to a tee. I'd given up trying to be protective of her long ago, and if anything, I'm surprised she didn't offer to step in and fight Deville instead. Isabelle had grown to match her surrounds, free, refreshing, and never pretending - nor wanting - to be anything else. 'This is your last ever match,' Isabelle reminded me with her typical bluntness, 'and even if that means jack to you, it doesn't to some of us. Eleanor gave Deville my design, your design. He wears it to taunt you... so... I've made a new design for you. For one night only.' As I felt the juice, heavy with fibrous pulp, make it way down my throat, I turned to her appreciatively, offering a warm smile of thanks. 'My best design, I might add.' Inspired by her surroundings, Isabelle worked feverishly, not able to bare having a design in her head that she couldn't release through a medium. Every notepad in the house was indented by scribbled patterns, while even preparing a salad would leave her scurrying off to jot down a design the different shapes and colours triggered her imagination with. I'd once been the same. Only I was far less artistic, and far more reckless. Mine used to involve something to fling my body off, or a piece of set not yet utilised to quench the demanding fans' brutal thirst. 'Well, since that whole not mentioning it failed,' I sighed, gulping down the last of her sweet, feel-good concoction. 'Are you okay with this, still?' Isabelle had walked in just as Deville had made the challenge, post-firing Eleanor as his manager. She'd heard me coughing and wheezing as I sprayed my beer out through my nostrils, than sucked it back into my lungs. And despite what I'd say to the contrary, I hadn't seen it coming. More fool me. 'Really wanna know?' she asked back as a warning, never one to sand the abrasive edges off her words. 'Probably not...' I bemoaned, sliding the white shirt back onto my shoulders as the sun began to lick at the back of my neck. 'But tell me anyway.' I could tell that she was debating whether or not I truly meant it, as it wasn't often Isabelle held her tongue. Maybe she didn't think the soothing rustle of the morning breeze through the branches, or the soft layered harmonies of the birds needed any of her scathing words to slice through their relaxing ambiance. 'Chris, no matter how much you try to deny it, you were Xavier Kannon,' she began by reminding me, not injecting the same disdain into the name as I tended to. 'People stop me and ask if I'm Xavier's sister. Some even offer double what I usually charge, thinking your name adds value. Eleanor has an audition next week, is waiting for a call back any day now, and is even having theatre scripts sent to her now. They're sent to Eleanor Kannon-Hall, not her maiden name.' I was getting special treatment. She was actually considering her words, despite the truth they carried. 'You name still sells, and you didn't get nearly enough from it,' Isabelle further reminded me. 'Your merchandise sales went to someone else, DVD sales elsewhere, likeness rights to some guy you've probably never even met before, and that's not even putting a price on your health. You were screwed, point taken. You quit and forfeited loyalty bonuses. Places closed and couldn't pay up the contract they'd promised.' In all honesty, I couldn't say I'd watch PRIME broadcasts without envy tinting my vision a stark green. I'd tuned in just to see Eleanor doing what she'd loved, and ended up watching some guys just going through the motions for probably double my OSW contract. That, or just earning their fortune with about 10 minutes of backstage TV time, coving up their inability to still work a match regularly. 'My take?' she followed on, a casual breeze to her words matching that which tickled our faces. 'This is wrestling settling its debt to you via Angelo Deville's chequebook.' 'Well, that's one way of looking at it,' I shrugged along with her words. 'Only way that makes sense to me,' Isabelle then remarked, dropping appropriately rose-tinted sunglasses down from her forehead. A serene silence was ushered in, in the wake of her words. I'd never thought that when I began in the business, when it looked like I was getting it all handed to me on a silver platter, that my last match would have such a mercenary aspect to it. And as thoughts focussed on my last match, they seemed to drag up memories of the first. The light bruising where the ropes warned soft flesh that it would need to toughen up. That sudden, mocking solidity of the canvas after the initial give... like someone pulling the mat away, leaving you to take a back-bump on wood. The tardis-like ring, always leaving you needing those extra strides to make it to the cables or buckles, nerves adding a vastness to its expanse. The lights. DAMN, the lights. Like wrestling under a grill pit, leaving the sweaty air choking you as it was heaved into your lungs. Still, when I staggered back through the curtain, I was drenched in glory just as much as I was in sweat. Scorched muscles gave a warm massage to my feeling of pride, while burning lunges panted out gasps of jubilation. I'd done it. I'd arrived. The next morning, I could barely stand. Adrenaline was siphoned off as I dreamt of stardom, leaving the pain my enthusiasm has anaesthetised. Back then, I thought of it as an honour. Battle scars. A sign of really being one of the guys. I took pride in waking up, body rigid. That wouldn't last, and neither would the honesty of my employers, my bookers, and my mentors. 'Just remember,' Isabelle then added, every silence an uncomfortable one in her estimation. 'It owes you, you don't owe it. The money's in your account whether it goes 2 or 20.' Maybe I should have had her around 3 years ago. 'This just a sibling thing,' Eleanor began to enquire as her head probed through the foliage, 'or is anyone invited?' Clad in the lightest of summer dresses, transparent in the sun to show the bikini underneath, and ravaged by the merest of breezes, Eleanor emerged through the lush green. She kissed the top of my head as she nestled down beside me, curling herself up in the long grass, then resting her head on my always obliging shoulder. Flowing blonde locks tickled my nose, but it was a miniscule distraction from the sense of comfort. 'We nixed the no talking about it thing, by the way,' Isabelle told her in the only way she knew how. Bluntly. 'Well, that lasted 4 more days than I thought it would,' Ellie shrugged back, leaving me between them like a tennis umpire. 'I think I finally convinced him that he isn't being retarded,' Isabelle volleyed back. Sighing, I waved my hand between the two women in my life. 'You can still see me here, right?' At least the atmosphere wasn't the tense, silent obstacle I'd feared it would be. We all knew the risks I'd be facing by getting back in the ring, having all feared the obvious worst-case scenario. Ignorance is bliss, however, and not a word had been spoken of it. I wasn't about to stay awake at night all the way through to Wednesday, wondering where the ramps would go so I could wheel myself back home. My back was fucked, and I'd accepted it. I'd retired because of it. I'd turned down over lucrative returns because of it. Even subconsciously, Eleanor's hand ran up and down my spine, pausing for the odd circular massage. It was the permanent wound in need of dressing, the eternal itch that couldn't be scratched feverishly enough. My Achilles' heel. My limiting factor. My off-switch. 'We agreed, all 3 of us,' I stated, knowing that beneath the banter was concern only love can foster. 'We agreed that it was worth the risk, and that I'd fight Deville. I'll walk in, and I'll walk out. This isn't some Deathmatch, or I Quit, or Last Man Standing. It's a TV match, with at least 10 cameras on the ring the whole time.' I wasn't about to be believe the hype Deville had injected into the match. To him, this was a battle of the gladiators, a clash of the titans. It was the bout he'd fantasised about, craved ever since his one-time peers left the business he'd ascended to the top of. He was fighting history, wrestling memories, battling myths. He didn't even realise how far he'd surpassed us all. 'I can tap out, I can get DQ'd, I can stay down, or I can get counted-out,' my less-than-inspiring speech went. 'Anyone wanting the Xavier Kannon of old can complain to PRIME afterwards, blast me on the internet, throw out some negative stars, whatever. I'm not going to be some part of Deville's great wrestling theatre. I'm there to make sure the million stays in my account, and land a couple of stiffs for the way he treated you, Ellie.' A life this perfect shouldn't have such a dark cloud dimming its bright future. As each day passed, I just wanted the match to come and go. With the moment drawing close, it's gravity elongated time, and I knew the flight to the US on Wednesday was going to feel longer than the week before it. The hour or so in the locker room would bring back the kind of nerves I’d felt before my debut, with each knock of the door echoing through my heartbeat. Once more into the fire I would stride, and come through un-singed. 'And come Thursday,' I continued, extending my free arm around Isabelle to hold both my wife and sister close. 'I'll still be the same man, we'll still have all this, and we'll never have to worry about money again. Issy, we can finally convert the attic into a studio, and Ellie, we'll never have to worry about how much it'll cost to fly you to readings or castings. One short week of worry, for them all to be alleviated.' Strong, leading words indeed. They even seemed to accelerate the moment, restoring the bird song to normal speed. 'We were happy,' Eleanor softly spoke, tucking her head deeper into the nook of my shoulder, 'without the million, I mean.' 'Yeah,' Isabelle backed her up with, eyes not breaking from the seabirds that danced across the horizon. 'It means a lot, you doing this, really.' 'I keep my promises,' I assured them. 'I made my wedding vows, and I told my sister that I'd always look out for her. Angelo Deville is going to help me fulfil that promise, and I couldn’t really care what he gets out of it.' And there the three of us - my family - sat, surrounded by the world in which we lived, staring out across the horizon that would meet us every morning. Angelo Deville wanted a match, I wanted this, all of it, for the rest of my... our... lives. We'd both get what we wanted. But I'd get the better end of the bargain, by far.
