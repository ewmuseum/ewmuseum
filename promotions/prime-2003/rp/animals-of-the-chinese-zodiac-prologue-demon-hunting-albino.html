Animals Of The Chinese Zodiac (prologue: Demon Hunting Albino)

Date: Who Could Truly Know? Never was I one to compliment a person. So I found it strange when I heard, "nice hat," escape from my pursed lips as I passed by an old lady while walking the cobblestone streets. She had eighty-eight wrinkles just on her face, one for every man she'd given a blowjob. It wasn't her pink, feathery hat that tipped my own to the fact that she was a real firecracker. She gave it away when she said, "thank you," all sultry and southern as though she grew up on a plantation, being served by negroes and drinking lemonade on the front porch. Any woman who talks like that is a fucking whore. I turned my head quickly to escape her gaze, which was an intense gift that Medusa must've given her, because I felt myself turn into stone. Needy of escape, I was unable to move when she approach me. "I've never asked a man for much," she lied. "But it sure would be nice if a fine gentleman like yourself could accompany me to the ice cream parlor across the street." I caught a glint of jet in her eye, "If you betray my trust, daemon, I'll banish you." Her face appeared as puzzled as my own puzzled face and I watched a tiny jigsaw, operated by invisible hands, cut pieces out of it, "Of course." I would've been beside myself but she already was. My own pigment-less hand took hers, which looked oddly similar and we walked across the street. We had nearly been hit by a '95 Acura but the driver had keen reflexes, dodging myself and my old, evil lady friend. His car made an obnoxious sound that hurt my ears. My mouth reacted with a scream that mocked it. The old lady fronted graciousness as we stepped onto the sidewalk outside of Bart's Ice Cream. I held a particular fondness for Bart's, whose neapolitan ice cream was worth murdering small children by the hundreds for. When I expressed this to the hag to my left she grinned a devilish grin, like Tony Gamble's favorite aunt. Or something far worse. "I'll buy you a double scoop of the neapolitan, if you'd like to come to my house for coffee," she negotiated. I was on to her sick, depraved idea. I decoded her jargon and read between the lines beautifully, like some sort of Mark Twain expert, and realized that her offer was of sexual intent. I may have had sympathy for the Devil, but she bore a ring around her finger to ward off spirits of my awful nature. Still, the old lady wanted to fuck... and no trinket, not one of gold nor magic can protect it's keeper from it's own desires. The question was, "Is it worth it?" I could hear the strawberry, vanilla and chocolate calling to me like a love-longing maiden, "Rhett..." I felt around for money in my pockets before I realized I had no pockets at all. Only patches of dark denim where pockets once rested long before the start of this drug binge were left. "Sure. I love coffee." There was that glint in her eye again. That jet black gaze that shined funny, like a soul turned inside-outwards. After the ice cream she took me to her home. Nearly as soon as we entered the doorway I entered her and didn't leave until I'd fucked her brains out. "Choke me," she had said between gasps in the midst of our throws. I did until her face turned blue. And then she was back to old form, returning to the role of some poor kid's grandma. They didn't deserve a demon. Not for a grandmother. Then again, who was I to judge? Perhaps they were rotten to their very core, capable of murder or rape or necrophelia and not even thinking twice about it. This was a grave time to be alive, stuck in the clutches of evil with no real way out. Some have suggested that I should've second guessed myself but I knew Evil when I saw it. And I'd stared her right in the eyes. Her shadow gave away her disposition, revealing the shape of a rebellious angel. She recognized soon after having my way with her that I'd discovered what she was. She tried to flee by running up the stairs, hissing as she did so. "You're a bastard deviant, I knew it all along! You sonofabitch!" Her foul language spilled forth from her spiraling maw. Her forked tail swaying through the air. I knew then that killing her was not an option. She was likely too powerful, being from Hell or some other equally shitty shithole. I laughed in order to display confidence, "I'll be back for you, you whore of Hades!" I'd have to trap her in something. * It's a well-known fact throughout demon hunting circles that the best container for a demon is a bottle. Not that I would know at that point in time. I suppose it was a stroke of luck that I'd read quite a bit in my high school days about Judaica and recalled the way that Jews of the days before and some time after Christ used magic as a ward against Evil. I bought a bowl made of clay from a near by pottery store. I'd detail the way that flowers were painted on the walls, the bright colors that seemed to surround you and the wonderful, lovely woman who hand made all of the pottery but in a way, I just did. The bowl I bought was of particular demon trapping importance. In the books I'd read before, the jews would carve spells in the bowl swirling from the outer rim to the center. In good faith, I cannot tell you everything I carved in the bowl that night in my small, Atlanta-area home. To do so would be to utter a tongue in which I promised long ago I would never speak again. However, I finished with a scripture that I'd read carved into those bowls a long time ago. One particular cantation that stood out, that seemed to embody my quest whole: Supressed are all demons, all no good ones, all pebble-spirits and lilliths. It was a damn fine thing to read, I thought, as I inhaled a white line approximately three inches in length and followed it with another of proper size. "No more fun and games," I said outloud at some point. In order to trap this evil spirit, which I could feel growing stronger by every passing moment, I had to bury the earthenware bowl beneath a plank of wood on my front porch. Hopefully, in leading the demon here, the bowl would trap her and she would dissipate. Hopefully. I brought a gun with me just in case. Not that I'm good at shooting or that a bullet could kill a demon, but still, it made me feel a little more safe. People pay good money to feel a little more safe. * Maybe it was the acid I'd ate just before carving the incantations in the bowl, but the world was awfully wobbly on that night. It wasn't enough to make anyone see visuals (except for the occasional flash of color in the darkness), but it was enough to still give me that body buzz... and sizeable tension. There was a great tightness in my gut, as though it were a cheating wife, caught in the clutches of her awful husband intent on strangling her for the awful way she'd hurt him. Even still, I was prepared for the fight of my life with the evil spirit if it came to it. Though the plan was to lure her to my house I felt uneasy about the idea that I could make it all the way there without conflict. Conflict was unavoidable, it seemed. I was a force to be reckoned with in mentality. That's half of the battle right there. Still, I couldn't have expected what I found in the old, migraine-spirit's home. Upon reaching the first step, through flashbacks of her wrinkly shell pressed against mine in pleasure, I felt an unwavering presence. It was frightening and intimidating, though the chants coming from inside of the house were even worse. Had demon-worshippers come to her aid? Was I being ambushed by a bunch of pentagram-drawing nerds with d20s and +2 morningstars? I creaked the door open and a red light reflected from the living room. There must've been twenty men in hoods and one without. Though the one caught my eye with his own almost immediately. I'd have done the same, but I was too busy staring at the nine-shaped scar on his neck.
