Endsong - An island of my own making

28 December 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28th December 2008 "All mankind is of one author, and is one volume; when one man dies, one chapter is not torn out of the book, but translated into a better language; and every chapter must be so translated ... As therefore the bell that rings to a sermon, calls not upon the preacher only, but upon the congregation to come: so this bell calls us all: but how much more me, who am brought so near the door by this sickness ... No man is an island, entire of itself...any man's death diminishes me, because I am involved in mankind; and therefore never send to know for whom the bell tolls; it tolls for thee." - - - - - I used to sometimes sit, late at night and on my own, and watch the sky; dark and all encompassing. The clearest nights were always the best when no cloud would get between me and my view of … well of everything. The small-minded people would look up and see simply the sky while others, with minds not quite so constrained, might see the stars themselves and imagine the countless planets that they couldn’t see. Me? I looked past the sky, between the stars, and saw the hands of the Creator that made it all just so that I could ask Him … or Her … a single, simple question: Why? You see it is a bit like the quandary that philosophy students are given when they first step foot into University – if you had one wish what would it be? It is very hard to narrow it down, if you were faced with such an overwhelming choice, to just one thing. Of course there are those that would ask for three more wishes and keep pushing their luck until the Djinn got fed up, of course, and ripped their still beating heart out of their chest so that they could watch themselves die … but those are the same people who, if faced by their Creator, would barrage Her … or Him … with question after question after question and yet, still, never ask anything meaningful. ‘Why’ may be simple, and open-ended, but it has depth, it has meaning and I am sure that if I had the chance to pose it to the Creator Himself … or Herself … then it would give a momentary pause before everything that I have ever wanted to know - needed to know - but never asked out loud would be answered; like a book whose pages are blown open by the wind the Creator would, no doubt, turn and look at me with eyes loving and terrible at the same time – the light of the Universe itself burning deep within – and the answers would pour forth. You see when I look up into the night sky, when I look past the stars and into the vastness that is everything and nothing at the same time, I imagine that I am looking into the pages of the Creator’s journal and, with every pen stroke from the unfathomable hand, another galaxy is born or another galaxy dies. When I look into that darkness I wonder how the author of all that is and all that will be can manage to write everyone’s – and everything’s – destiny and keep track of it all. I barely manage to keep myself moving, day after day, in the right direction. If I were – if I am – the author of my own destiny, the author of my own life’s story, then I was – I am – sorely in need of an editor to ensure that I dot the I’s and cross the T’s; I don’t know where I am going with the next line let alone the following paragraph. The fact that there is another chapter to follow this one, when all I see is blank pages after blank pages, terrifies me. How am I meant to fill these pages? How am I meant to write the book of my own life? Am I meant to do that at all or should I keep looking into the darkness and hope … and pray … that the Creator is prepared to write my story for me? Should I just sit back and read the words, in stark black on simple white, and let me life flow past me as the pages turn, one after the other? Do I trust the Creator that much or mistrust myself even more? I don’t know but I do know that at that time of the night – when I sat alone and searched the Heavens for answers – that most sensible people were asleep and dreaming of the life that they could have led if they had only taken that other path – turned left, if you will, rather than right - or still could if they only had the courage to give up everything that they knew – everything that they were – and take a risk on something else; something new. Become someone else; someone new. Give up what is known and safe for that which is unknowable but – hopefully – will be better than that which is laughingly called your ‘life’. Why is it that people are never satisfied with that which they have and want that which they can only dream about? I have heard people speak about the Joneses, and keeping up with them, just as I have heard it said that the grass is always greener on the other side. The thing is, though, that while people are so busy keeping up with the Joneses they lose track of themselves; and grass, no matter which side it is on, is still just grass. Don’t people realise that they can’t be someone else – they can’t be someone new? They can only be themselves and make the most of things; they can be as much or as little as they want to be but nothing more than that – nothing else. They can only be what the Creator gives them the power to be; how they … how we … use that power is the only difference. The ‘choice’, for want of a better word; is the reality behind the Joneses and the greenness of the grass. I choose to see beyond the stars while others barely see the night sky itself. Then again that is because I choose to walk with my head held high, looking up, while others watch the dirt at their feet. That is who I am, you see. I am not the man that the boy I was would have become had my parents not died when I was still so young. I choose to live through the pain, each and every day, without them. That boy, had they lived, would have been so different. But he is not me. I am not living the life I would have done had I taken the other path – turned left rather than right – and chosen the career that they would have chosen for me. I am neither an anthropologist nor an archaeologist. I do not love who I would have loved, perhaps, had that boy never felt the unbearable pain of loss that moulded and changed him into the man I am today. Had they lived, perhaps, I would have been able to open myself up more easily to those deserving of my heart, of my love. I would not have been afraid of losing everything once more – in that pain, I know, I would have lose myself, finally and utterly. Only once, in a lifetime, can any one person go through that much loss – that much pain – and walk out the other side; changed, admittedly, but still whole and living. Just. If they had lived then perhaps I would have been able to accept that I could be loved. Could love in return. As it is I hear the words ‘I love you’ and smile in return; I can’t say them back, I can’t allow myself to feel the emotion that I know should be there. … even if I know that – if the walls came down, if I were different … or truthful … I would admit that I am filled, consumed, by that love too … That is not who I am, you see. I am an island of my own making, entire within myself. I used to sometimes sit, late at night and on my own, and watch the sky; dark and all encompassing. The clearest nights were always the best when no cloud would get between me and my view of … well of everything. The small-minded people would look up and see simply the sky while others, with minds not quite so constrained, might see the stars themselves and imagine the countless planets that they couldn’t see. Me? I looked past the sky, between the stars, and saw the hands of the Creator that made it all. Now? I sit, late at night and on my own, and watch the sky; dark, all encompassing and … ultimately … empty.
