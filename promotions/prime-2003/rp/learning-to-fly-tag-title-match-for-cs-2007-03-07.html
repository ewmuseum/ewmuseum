Learning to Fly (Tag Title Match for CS)

7 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/7/07 The air was stagnant and smelled of stale sweat in Locker Room 1A of Boardwalk Hall. To most, it would have been a vomit inducing smell, one that would have made you want to vomit. To one Captain Suleimon, it was the smell of victory, a hard-fought victory over the hero, the fan-favorite Jack Murphy. Reveling in his triumph, Suleimon sat back on the bench and felt the sweet, sweet blanket of victory envelop him. Except it wasn't sweet. It was more like pain, a dull, throbbing ache that pulsed in his lower back. He put his hand there, feeling the results of the match course through his nervous system. A Pyhrric victory it would seem. Suleimon thought to himself that no one ever made him feel this bad after a match, with the exception of maybe Red Rock, but there were gimmicks involved in that. No one walks out of the Ottoman Torture Chamber, victorious or not, without a lump or two to show for it. In the confines of a regular wrestling match though, no one had made him feel the pain that The Bull did that night. Suleimon was just about to get up when he heard someone talking behind him. "Heh, if you think you're in pain, you should bloody feel how I felt after that." Suleimon turned around to find Jack Murphy standing in the doorway. "What, have you come back for more punishment?" Murphy uncrossed the arms from his chest and pointed a finger at Suleimon. "No, I haven't." "You'd be wise to point that finger away from me." Murphy feigned like he was afraid of the Captain for a second, but then kept it there. "What are you going to do, beat me up again? We're both spent, Sully. That's why I'm here." The Sultan of Smackdown furrowed his brow. "You're here just to tell me that you're tired and beaten up? I'm confused." "No, you blockhead, I'm here because I wanted to tell you that you opened my eyes in that ring tonight, and I'm pretty sure I opened yours." "What makes you so sure that you indeed did?" "You're still standing there, you haven't hit me yet and you're actually listening to me instead of talking at me, demanding that I leave your presence." Suleimon was had. He knew it too. "So tell me, how did I open your eyes?" Murphy smirked. "The way you defeated me... not with your high flying move, but before that, the low blow, the exposed turnbuckle, the choke and every other rule you broke..." "Listen, if you've come to lecture me about breaking the rules, you might as well go out to the beach and pound sand." "You assume too much, Good Captain." "I do? All you Westerners are alike, you can't take defeat to an obviously superior talent who's actually not afraid to do what it takes to win..." "Will you just pipe down for one second and let me finish what I have to say?" Suleimon shut his mouth and stared squintedly at The Bull. "I was saying... everything you did in that ring showed me something, that I was in there with someone who was willing to do anything it took to win, a man unafraid of disqualification, fine or suspension. Someone who didn't care about consequences, because he knew they wouldn't affect him." Suleimon perked up. "Sully, I've been living most of my life trying to play by certain rules. It's gotten me far, hell, I've won a lot of things here in AWC playing by those rules. But I see you, how you go out and you take things without ever worrying about pissing people off." "Yeah yeah, and what does any of that have to do with anything, seeing that you've so conveniently pointed out your successes as a prissy square?" Murphy smirked again. "Because... I feel that by playing by my own rules, I can be dominant. I feel that both of us joining together, we'd be... unstoppable." Joining together? The thought of joining with anyone gave Suleimon flashbacks to a younger day, the last time he thought of trusting someone. "I... I'm sorry Murphy. Your offer, while not as undesirable as an offer coming from someone like Mikey O'Reilly's caliber, shall not be accepted." Suleimon walked towards the door, but Murphy blocked him with a palm to the chest. "Is it because I'm not Turkish? Is this a race thing?" Suleimon rolled his eyes back slightly. "No..." Suleimon was about to answer, but the prospect of baring even the tiniest sliver of his soul to someone he didn't quite like kept him from revealing the truth. "...actually yes. Yes it is." Murphy was taken slightly aback. "Americans are horrid," Suleimon said robotically. "Irish are worse. Good day." He pushed past The Bull and headed to the infirmary to get some ice for his back. The next day, Suleimon stood on the beach, up near the water. His bare feet were washed rhythmically by the breakers as he stood in deep thought. His mind wandered back to a conversation he had with his cousin Dogan a few weeks prior. [CUE THE LOST STYLE FLASHBACK NOW, BITCHES!] "Why are you so confident you can take this company over by storm? You're a shrimp..." "And a superiorly talented wrestler..." "Bullshit, you can't wrestle your way out of a paper bag." "Oh, here you go again with your semantical arguments. So I can't chain wrestle. Big deal." "Most of wrest..." Suleimon interrupted him before he had the chance to finish. "My turn to call bullshit. There are plenty of those out there who couldn't hold a candle to the best amateurs out there, yet they succeed. Strikers, suplex machines, whatever. I'm a supremely talented 'sports-entertainer.' Are you happy now?" He cringed as he said those words. Dogan nodded. "But back to what I was saying, I'm supremely talented, moreso than anyone on the roster. Size is deceiving, my cousin." "You're crazy. There are plenty of other great talents on the roster, you know that, right?" Suleimon stared at him. "Answer me, you can't think you're the only elite wrestler on the roster." "Actually... you're right. There are more than a few who come close to me. But..." "But?" "They don't know how to do whatever it takes. They're constrained by their own inhibitions. They don't conquer their fears, and they can't conquer the physical obstacles." "There are people in the fed who have no inhibitions." "And they aren't talented enough. I can outfox and outfight them." "I see... so... what if one of these people, with the talent, loses their inhibitions?" Suleimon paused. "Then things get interesting." [END FLASHBACK!] Back to the beach, Suleimon stood deep in thought, back to his conversation with Dogan. A man with talent losing his inhibition... a threat. Jack Murphy was a greatly talented wrestler. He had proven that to Suleimon at Coast to Coast. He'd also shown his intention to follow down the dark path that Suleimon had already started down. Jack Murphy had become a threat in addition to an enemy. A wave rushed in up to the Captain's knees. He took a step back, and a thought came to him. He had barely any friends to keep close... but he had an enemy, a threat... Suleimon gave himself pause, but the urge was too strong. "Murphy... I'll accept your offer." Jack Murphy, standing behind Suleimon, was taken aback. He thought the Captain didn't see him. "How did you see me?" "I didn't." It was a full plane; every seat full up, except one, the one next to Jack Murphy. It was sold though. Murphy sat by a window and read the magazine in the bin in front of his seat. Cigar Afficianado. Not his first choice, but it was either that or Airline Media Monthly. The last one on the flight was Suleimon, clothing desheveled and a look that could kill a puppy, or something of comparable cuteness and/or cuddliness. He shoved through the aisle and stuffed his carry-on bag overhead. "Random screening my ass." Murphy smiled at Suleimon. "So did they just pat you down or did you get the full, body-cavity search?" "Ha ha ha, laugh." "Lighten up." Suleimon took his seat. Murphy placed his magazine down and turned to his Turkish cohort. "So, lay this plan on me again." "It's like this. We interfere in each other's matches, whether we need it or not. Ensure victory through whatever means necessary." "And we have to wear the grey?" "Yes, we'll become the Man In Grey, cast doubt into everyone's minds." "Why grey though?" "I have my reasons." "Alright... I trust you then." Trust... a word foreign to Suleimon. He also knew that he didn't feel the same way towards Murphy. He still wondered how he was going to coexist more than a month, but he was willing to try. The alternative was an enemy on his level. Their first attempt at synergy was a brutal failure. Suleimon challenged Gabriel Afeaki for the Relentless Championship. Nothing Suleimon could do worked against the Fijian Lion. Even a somersault Arabian facebuster was no-sold. Same with Murphy's interference. It was almost like it was some bad dream, scripted by Jeremy Jenkins. After the match, Suleimon slammed the locker room door shut. He sat on the bench, head in his hands. The ruckus startled someone out of their stall. "Man, that fecker..." Murphy, in greysuit, no mask, shook his head. "He's gotta be on Serum..." Suleimon looked up. "What are you doing? You'll blow your cover!" "Relax man, relax. No one else is in here, and no one else will come in." He sauntered over to the door and locked it. "Sorry mate." "Hey... it's not your fault. He's... Afeaki... he's on drugs. He's gotta be on that Serum." "Yeah... I don't know how he got up from your barrage. Anyone else, mate, anyone else." "Yeah, let's not dwell on it. Gameplan for next time." And they did, tweaking their plan week to week, becoming more and more in sync. Murphy and Suleimon worked night and day to get their plans down. A month passed, and now it was Murphy's turn at the Fijian Lion. Backstage, Suleimon and Murphy met in a dark corridor, so as to hide Suleimon's identity as the Man in Grey for this week. "Ready for your shot, Bull?" "Yes... and I see you're ready too." "You know it." "About that... actually... I don't want you to interfere unless I'm really in trouble. I mean, only if he's got me unconscious or in the Fijian Leg Lock. Alright?" "What? What's with the change in plans? I thought we were going to build this up more!" "I know, but... I think I can take him. Plus, I still need to keep a profile up and..." "You think you can take him? You remember what happened when I wrestled him last! The bugger's on drugs!" "I know... you have to trust me on this one though." Murphy walked away, and Suleimon was left in the shadows. "What if I can't?" he said to himself as Murphy walked away. The match was a lot more competitive than the Suleimon affair. There were times that Murphy looked like he could have gotten in trouble, but he never really lost control of the match. Some big moves and a dildo shot later, Jack Murphy was Relentless Champion. Suleimon left his dark corner and went back to the locker room to get out of the greysuit. Murphy walked in with the Relentless Championship in tow. "I did it!" "Bugger off." Murphy's celebratory mood quickly dampened, and a look of shock overcame his face. "What?" "I said, bugger off. You showed me up out there. In fact, how did I know you didn't just tank it when I was fac..." "You can't be serious! I win a title, and all you can think about is your ego?" "Why did you hurt him just now but couldn't then?" "I have no flipping idea! Seriously, he must have been off-cycle, or maybe he was on other narcotics. Hell, for all I know, he could have been a cyborg the last time and a human now, but I assure you, I didn't sell you out to make me look better." [The truth... Jeremy Jenkins didn't write the Murphy/Afeaki match, so that's why Murphy looked good!] Suleimon just sat there. "Listen, I got wind of next week's match. They're putting you against me for the belt." "Oh yeah? And let me guess, you want me to lay down for you, right?" "Actually, that would be nice." Suleimon stood up and got right in Murphy's face. "Of course it would. You don't want a challenge for your title, the only man in this federation who could give you a challenge, so just becuause we're allied, you want me to lay down for you." Murphy shook his head. "You're unbelievable!" "Then why... why do you want me to lay down for you?" "Because it would signify something. You could be pissed off at everything I stand for, and instead of fight me... I don't know the explanation. But do you really want to fight me when we're trying to dominate? Why should we destroy each other and let everyone else take the reward?" "Because... because..." "Listen... just trust me. Things will work out for you too." "What if I can't trust you?" Murphy paused. "Then... I don't know what to say. I mean, no one else on this roster would go near you, and here I am, working... we've got a plan... just trust me." "Fine... but if this only benefits you..." "It won't. You have my word." The next week, they had their charade of a title match. Suleimon came out, but didn't lay down. Instead, he forfeited and cut a huge promo about how Murphy didn't deserve his title. He then forfeited the match and stormed to the back. He didn't lay down for Murphy, but they didn't wrestle. They didn't wear each other down. Backstage, Murphy caught up with Suleimon. "Hey." "Hey..." "You didn't lay down for me." "I know. I thought that would have been better." "Oh yeah?" "Yeah. I figured that if I laid down for you, it would raise more red flags. People might be onto us more. So therefore, I forfeited out of righteous indignation and now people hate me more and love you more." Murphy crossed his arms and tapped his foot. "You know what. You're right. That was brilliant, actually. Good show." "You're being sarcastic, right?" "No. They're none the wiser." Murphy patted Suleimon on the shoulder. "Let's meet up later. We'll coordinate later, really ramp up the greysuit stuff." "Oh... okay." Murphy walked off. Suleimon paused and thought to himself, hey... maybe Murphy isn't ego-driven. Maybe he could trust him. Week after week, Murphy donned the greysuit and helped Suleimon win matches. He interfered when the time was right, was in perfect position. Suleimon did the same for Murphy. It was cohesion. Unity. Teamwork. One night, after a card, Suleimon and Murphy met up at the pub. Murphy was there first, already drinking a pint of Guinness. Suleimon bellied up to the bar. "Hey mate." "Hey." "One Guinness for my Turkish friend here." The bartender pours a dark beer for Suleimon and gives it to the Captain. "So Sully, what's up." "Nothing much. Just celebrating another week in the march towards Triangles." "Yeah mate." "Yeah." They both paused. "Bull... can I ask you a question." "Sure." "Why are you doing this?" "Hm?" "Why are you doing this? You're risking the adulation of all those fans, your good name... why?" Murphy took a big swig of his stout and smiled. "You know, I don't know. I want to get ahead. I want to dominate this company. I want to be feared, y'know? I want to bring back real wrestling, not this stupid, emo crap that people are trying to pass off." "Yeah... so what are you going to do after you get there?" "Don't you mean we?" Suleimon's eyes lit up. "We?" "Yeah... we. We're in this together, right? Plus, I'm gonna need someone to watch my back. Besides, I know a competitor when I see one." "You're not saying that just to butter me up, are you?" Murphy looked at him like he was crazy. "You're not going bloody emo on me now, are you?" Suleimon shook his head. "Alright then, now, let's raise a glass." They both raised their glass. "To dominance." "To dominance." Two hours before Triangles. Murphy sat on the bench, pondering what Triangles would hold for him. The Transatlantic Championship? The Grand Slam? His train of thought was broken as Suleimon slammed the door. "Murph, you're not gonna believe this." Murphy perked up. "Huh?" "I heard Harber's closing the company after tonight." "What?" "Yeah, Harber, that son of a bitch. He's gonna close the company before we even have the chance to claim our dominance." "Bloody hell." "Yeah... what are we going to do?" "I don't know." "Are we still going with our plan, Jack?" "Of course. We can't blow this cover. It needs to go off like we planned. But..." "But what?" "We need to stick it to Harber." "How?" "I don't know yet, but I'll know soon. You just have to trust me." Suleimon looked at Murphy. "Alright. I trust you." The rest is history. Murphy led the charge to fight Harber's crew at the end of the Triangles match. Suleimon, hardly a company man, followed suit. They battled side by side. Then they came to PRIME. ---- "So Tariq... what's with the trust? What changed?" Suleimon looked at Dogan slyly. "Everything."
