The Release(Rev 226)

July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 02 Steve Starr took a look around… he was nervous. Upon arriving to the practice facility, he was given word to report to the general manager’s office immediately. This was not the first time that this had happened; in fact it had happened twice previously. The first time occurred as a result of Steve Starr skipping a mandatory community service activity. The event was held on a Saturday morning, and was designed to foster a positive public image within the city for the minor league team. Every other member of the Burlington Royals appeared on time for the 9 AM commitment. Every member aside from Steve Starr, that is. Numerous attempts to reach him were rebuffed. When he did finally decide to show up, the coach was so displeased with his casual demeanor, sporting trendy sunglasses as he arrived accompanied by two uninvited bimbos, that he sent him home. He would later be fined a sum that would amount to a week’s worth of pay. His second transgression proved to be a bit more problematic. Steve Starr had failed a drug test. Actually, he had failed multiple drug tests. The entire Major League Baseball entity is more obsessed with catching those who abuse performance enhancing drugs as opposed to those who may casually dabble in recreational drug use. That would explain why Steve Starr was able to test positive for marijuana twice before he was ever confronted. The organization knew that a number of players dealt with various vices, but as long as it did not inhibit their performance on the field there were ways around it. Steve Starr had run out of chances however. He had been summoned into the office of the general manager, a gentleman who went by the name of Lloyd Carter. Mr. Carter had never played professional baseball on any level, he probably could not even hack it on his high school team. He was one of the fortunate individuals to benefit from the philosophy of: “It’s not always what you know, but who you know.” The lucky bastard had married the niece of the Kansas City Royals multi-millionaire owner David Glass. David Glass was a man who made his fortune at Wal-Mart, ascending the ranks and rising to a high level executive. Mr. Glass purchased the team in 2000 at a “blue-light special” price of $96 million, an extremely low figure when you consider the worth of other franchises within the same industry. Immediately upon his purchase of the team, Mr. Glass fired every member in the front office and replaced them with his own cronies. Luckily for Lloyd Carter, he just happened to attract the youngest daughter of David Glass’ only sister. There was plenty for Steve Starr to be nervous about. The main problem was that the general manager never requested to meet with players unless it concerned contract negotiations, traders, or releases. Disciplinary measures were normally handled by the coaching staff. Most of the players held contempt for members of the front office anyway. Walking down the long corridor, Steve Starr played many scenarios in his mind as to what this meeting could be all about. Staring down at the newly acquired marble flooring installed in the facility, Steve wondered if his days as a Royal were numbered. He was 21 years old at the time, and was in the middle of his third season as a member of the Kansas City Royals Farm System. And while Steve Starr’s career started with a bang… his first season saw him flirt with a .300 batting average(he ended up hitting .295) and earning a spot on the Kansas City Royals Rookie League All Defensive Squad. At 5’11 and about 205 lbs, Steve Starr was by no means a towering presence. What he lacked in size he made up with blinding speed. He covered the base paths like a cheetah escaping would be predators in the jungle. He had impressed many scouts with his acrobatic leaps and catches in the outfield, a concern many had when it was announced he would make the switch from Short Stop(which he played in high school) to Center Field. He responded by leaving fans in awe with his ability to “rob” home runs at the last possible minute. Balls would come thundering off the bat at wild speeds, soaring through the air with impeccable velocity. Fans were sure that a home run was imminent, and then out of nowhere, young Steve Starr leaps into the air and retrieves the ball safely in his mitt. Clearly his days on the hardwood had rewarded him with amazing hops. But all of that was irrelevant now. While Steve Starr had shown the potential to be an everyday player in the big leagues, he had yet to show the discipline. He was routinely late to practices and meetings, constantly bickered with teammates and coaches. He really was not cordial with fans either. The most severe situation occurred during Starr’s second season. Steve Starr was being heckled by fans in Tampa Bay, Florida. The fans were insinuating that Steve Starr had paid off teachers in high school to give him favorable grades, a rumor that began to circulate as Steve Starr prepared to enter the draft. After the game, Steve Starr had to be restrained by teammates and coaches as he attempted to confront the apparent ringleader of the heckling crew. One woman, who had brought her two young sons to the game complained immediately to team representatives. Her account revealed that Steve Starr took a baseball bat and swung it at one of the individuals in the parking lot. That story has been disputed, and no charges were ever filed against Starr so the team simply ignored the incident. They were setting a terrible precedent. Lloyd Carter would never admit it, but he was looking forward to informing Steve Starr that he was going to be released from his contract with the Royals. It is pretty harsh to think that one man could take pleasure in ending another’s career, but throughout their time knowing each other Lloyd Carter had grown a strong dislike for Steve Starr. When personal friends of his would inquire about the game’s next young stars, Mr. Carter always had this to say concerning Steve; “The kid’s got a lot of upside. He’s never going to realize it though unless he learns how to start being a professional. He’s got all of the tools, but none of the brains.” While he respected the capabilities of his young prospect, he could not stomach his primma donna attitude. It did not help that Steve Starr normally treated Carter with undeserved hostility. Mr. Carter had gone to bat for Steve Starr numerously against the wishes of his superiors. There were a few instances where ownership was ready to drop Steve Starr altogether. Yet it was Lloyd Carter who consistently defended Steve Starr. There was hope that maybe Steve Starr would mature and his talents could be realized. But Steve Starr had simply burned too many bridges. He had allowed Lloyd Carter to look like on too many occasions. Carter’s bosses were beginning to lose trust with his judgment. He had begun to come under fire and now his job was coming into question. He had no choice… Starr had to go. Steve Starr took a deep breath, and knocked on the door. “Hello Steve, I’ve been expecting you. Have a seat.” Lloyd Carter was just short of 6 feet. He probably weighed no more than 165 lbs. Born in East Rutherford, NJ, Lloyd lived what many would call a “privileged life.” His mother was an accomplished dentist who had recently established her own successful practice. His father went into the family business, serving the community as a police officer. After serving the force triumphantly for 23 years, his father was promoted to captain of the East Rutherford Police Department. With that type of foundation established by the hard work of his parents, Lloyd Carter had only one obligation as far as they were concerned, excel in school, which he did with staggering effectiveness. By the time he was a high school senior, not only was already the “consensus” valedictorian of his graduating class but he was also fielding offers from all of the prestigious east coast Ivy League Schools. After having his pick of the litter, Lloyd Carter eventually settled on Dartmouth with a major in Pre-Law. It was there that he would discover the love of his life, the above mentioned niece of the current Kansas City Royals Owner, David Glass. Lloyd Carter was never an athlete in school, he never pined to be an athletic superstar. That is not to say he did not admire athletes. He was a big time sports fan. Lloyd just never really developed the athletic gene. He spent his childhood shacked up in his room, reading and writing. While most kids were out playing Pop Warner Football or Little League Baseball, Lloyd was memorizing the preamble to the US Constitution. It was with this knowledge that Steve Starr used to form his opinion of Lloyd Carter. Steve Starr did not have the traditional childhood. This has been well documented and there is no use of rehashing his back story again. But what bothered Steve Starr more than anything, was when he thought people received benefits based on privilege as opposed to hard work. He was quite the hypocrite, he had benefited greatly as a high school sports hero in his home city. For example, Steve Starr was allowed to miss a total of 23 days of school during his final semester as a senior. The rule in the district is a maximum allowance of ten days. Starr’s various coaches were able to allow him clearance based on the premise that he was “away” on college visits, when really he was at home being a lazy bastard. Despite the animosity that had developed between the two, it still was never easy for Mr. Carter to terminate a player. Previously he witnessed some traumatic experiences when he had to let go of a player, it was not uncommon to see a player break down into tears. He normally jotted down a few of the things he liked to say during these meetings. Despite his brilliance as a reader, writer, and pretty much any other scholarly type activity, Lloyd Carter was no orator. He often fumbled through formal presentations, and it is widely considered the one setback that prevented him from pursuing a career as a lawyer. It would be tough to represent people in court without being able to enunciate confidently. Glancing over his written notes he was interrupted by a knock on the door. Surely it could not be Steve Starr, it was only 12:15 he had not been expected until 12:30. That meant he might not stroll in until a quarter to 1. But to his surprise, it was Steve Starr. He was early. Probably the first time he has been early to something since his rookie year. “Hello Steve, I have been expecting you. Have a seat.” Mr. Carter announced politely. No matter the personal dislike he may have felt towards the young man entering the room, he still was obligated to remain professional. “I hope you don’t mind me getting in early, but I was hoping to get into the batting cages. I’ve gotta do something to work myself out of this slump.” Steve replied. It was shocking to hear that Steve Starr was coming in early to put in some extra work Or maybe he had been tipped off. Maybe this was all just a spiel to get the organization to change it’s stance. These thoughts ran through Lloyd Carter’s mind before he dismissed them and returned to his original purpose. Steve Starr must go. “Well Steve that will not be necessary. You have been a member of this organization now for three years. Throughout your time here you have shown flashes of brilliance. When you first joined this organization, I targeted a 3 year window for you to make the jump to the Major Leagues. After you completed your first season, which I may add was one of the best the organization has seen in years, I thought you’d be in the bigs by the end of year two.” Steve Starr was beginning to like the sound of things. Maybe he was finally being called up after all. Then Lloyd continued and Steve was certainly not going to like what followed. “But then Steve, you see something happened. I think you may have become blinded by your initial successes. That same spunk that we saw in year one had been replaced by a new brash attitude. Teammates started to complain about your work ethic. The coaching staff started to believe that Steve Starr had developed an ego.” The truth was Steve Starr always had an ego. He had just done a masterful job of disguising the ego while he allowed everyone to focus on his exceptional play. “That was not the only issue however Steve. Your commitment to the organization has been questioned. We can tolerate a prima donna every now and then, especially one that can produce on the field. But when you start failing drug tests, and I mean multiple drug tests that’s where things become a problem.” Damn it Steve Starr thought. His addiction to the Mary Jane was starting to catch up with him. Steve Starr smoked all kinds of pot. Had done so since he was a sophomore in high-school, and it never affected his play. His high school coaches were aware of it, but they were so in awe of Steve Starr’s feats that they dare not say anything to him concerning it. They were in fear of alienating their megastar. “Steve I don’t want to waste anymore of your time. There is no denying your talents, but unless you get your attitude together you will never realize those gifts. Your repeated violation of the Major League Baseball Drug Policy, combined with your general recklessness behavior has given us no choice but to release you from your contract. You have until the end of the work day to clean out your locker room.” Steve Starr was in shock. While he had misguided dreams of one day playing professional baseball.(misguided because let’s be real, while he was talented he did not work nearly hard enough to compete with the best. He would have had to hook up with A-Rod’s cousin in the Dominican Republic to offset his horrible training regimen) But now where would he go? Could he really return to Kansas City, now? People were expecting him to be here, competing. It was the middle of June, the season was just rounding into form. What were people going to say when they saw him back at home, during the middle of baseball season? They would laugh at him. Depressed, he walked out of the facility without as much as a goodbye to anyone. He was so embarrassed he just wanted to fall off of the face of the earth.
