Facing Truth

26 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 26, 2005 Facing Truth Truth. People often hide from the truth because it takes them to a place within themselves that they fear. A place of Torture. Torment. Memories of bloodshed tears. A place that they dare not enter on their own free will, but yet have to be forced into by simple truth. People hate when it gets real...because the truth hurts. But now its time to come from behind the veil. No Dramatics... No Illusions... This is Real. Darkness beckoned, Jagged winds plagued the environment, shaking the foundations of all those who played feebly to it. Thunder roared through the ebony night sky, accompanied by the earth moving winds and the chime of porch bells that came along with it. The wind gathered a screech sound that echoed through the wretched Lynbrook community. Trees threatened fracture, Doors blown off of their hinges, and through the midst of it all stood one firm man with his family gathered around him. One firm man set to protect his family from the wrath of mother nature. One firm man. One firm black man. James Jackson always boasted his heritage as well as his race as the superior of the given. When most would shame the history of African Americans, he would find ways to boast it, pointing out that through racism and slavery alike, the black man always held steadfast in protecting his family from any danger present. Now was Jackson’s time to tear down the veils of words and embark on the sermon which he had been preaching for so many years prior. He looked at his beautiful daughter, a mere seven years of age, shivering in the warm arms of her fearful mother. The creaks of the floorboard grew with every step Jackson took, pacing himself as to how he was going to get out of this with his family…alive. He took a peak outside, the Lynbrook community invaded by the storming wraths of their heavenly God. Tears of the angels mauling the soil below, signaling their worried concern over the sinful nature that had taken over. Their attempts to wash away the sin that plagued this county fell far short, they were but spiritual angels advocating to deaf prophets. A strident sound struck the side of the house, stopping Jackson in his tracks as both the mother and the daughter rattled in fear. Soft whimpers from the mouth of the little girl signaled her discomfort. No lights, no television, utter and silent darkness, but in a time like this, the silence was even more disturbing than any other sound that could present itself. The little girl looked up into her mother’s vexed eyes with moist eyes of her own…eyes that only a child could birth in order to make your heart sink in distress. “Mommy…I’m scared.” She stated. The most cliché phrase of children her age, but the look on her face expressed her true feelings for more than mortal fear. Dread mixed in with panic, worry mixed in with fright, horror mixed in with trepidation. Little Alexis was heart-stricken with grief, fear that her short and fragile life could be altered by this experience…even more so the death of her parents. “It’s going to be alright, Alexis. It’s just bad weather.” The cracks in her voice signaled to James that his wife was just as wrought with apprehension as his daughter. The wind grew and so did its glass shattering screeches. The bells on the porch ferociously bang together, creating a dark melody that no soul could bear. Jackson feared most for his family, that is what drove him to the brink of pure rage in knowing that there was nothing he could do to tame their fear. Telling his daughter “It’s going to be alright” just didn’t cut it anymore because quite frankly, he could barely lead his own self to believe it anymore. Mother nature was terrorizing the streets of Lynbrook and the household of James Jackson. He was going to fight back. BLANG! Gasps and startles shook the room as the two females shuttered at the sound against their house once more. The look on Jackson’s face turned into one of sheer determination. No longer was fear an option. He walked over to the corner of the living room and slipped on his dress shoes to accompany his wife beater muscle shirt and his jogging pants. He marched towards the door, halted by his wife’s pleading words. “James…where are you going?” “I’m going to see what’s going on out there. No simple wind can cause all of that damage.” She reached out as if to stop him, but only fell in vain attempts as he was already out of the door and unto the porch of his one story country home. The furious wind nearly swept him off of his feet but he held his ground. The sound of murmuring caught his attention, directing him towards the far left side of the porch where he peered to find nothing. He continued hearing the murmurs as he made his way over to the left side of the porch to once again find nothing. “Time has come, Blackey…” The vibrate tone of the voice sent a chill up his spine as he reached over to grab a baseball bat laying up against the door of the house. He carefully made his way down the steps that lead up to the porch, planting his feet on solid ground, wielding the bat backwards ready for anything that happens to make its presence known. He shouted out into the darkness… “Stop playing games and get out here!” His response was nothing more than the cold howls of the furious wind. Someone was out here playing mind games with him, and in the back of his head he had a good idea of just who it might be, but in the whole of his heart he prayed to the God he worshipped that his assumption was false. His answer came shortly after in the form of another deep chilling voice. “We’re not playing games anymore…Nigger.” Thump! His body smacked the ground, forcing the bat out of his hand and unto the soil in which he laid face first. Time seemed to flow with every brutal kick he felt to his back. He could see nothing more than a swirling daze…he could feel nothing more than the rapid stomps and smacks across his back…he could hear nothing but the ferocious howling of the cold wind. Pretty soon it all came to a complete halt, and all the only thing that remained was… Darkness. They came in the form of black robes and hoods that covered their faces. An immediate speculation would figure about four of them, all assembled together with lead pipes, brass knuckles, and an iron fist. The passion placed forth in the beating of this man expressed there whole hearted hatred for him, but for a reason unknown. Moments that came more in the form of hours passed as Jackson began to regain conscious. All that he could see was the blood that stained his face, dripping into his eyes. He could barely feel or hear anything, but what he did hear was the one thing he wish he didn’t. A scream… His wife and daughter… Screaming mercy for the leader of their household. He tried to reach out to them, but he was too weak and battered to even lift them up off the ground. He tried to scream out to them, but he was too bruised to even open his mouth. All he could hear or see was the massive onslaught by the four hooded men and their screams of vulgar content, shadowed by their deep dark laughter. Fight now Nigger! Go back to the cotton field where you belong! Tubman can’t save you now, son! Scream like the punk you are! The anger boiled inside of him, driving him to the point where he managed to take a swing at one, but failed miserably and awarded only with another stiff kick, cracking his ribs. He felt the cold lead pipe fissure his skull, and the warm blood that surged from the back of his head unto his neck. All the while, none the torture he was enduring could compare to that of hearing his two loved ones scream for him in mercy. He fought for as long as he could, just for them…but fighting became too hard. Their attack was ruthless and their aggression was exalted. Pretty soon, all of the smacks across of his head, all of the kicks to his ribs, all of the screams of vulgar content and mercy…it all came crashing down by simple… Darkness. Eternal Darkness. “…Murder of James Jackson. The suspects come in the form of an elite group. Supposedly a cult, funded by their own personal religious beliefs. The attack seems to have been racially motivated.” Mayor Bobby Crown glazed over his clipboard, illuminated by the flashing red lights of the ambulances and police, all gathered around the Jackson home in true investigation style. Crown had always been a humble man, serving the small town of Wetumpka, Alabama with his heart. He hated the crime that presented itself, but was lucky enough that no crime hardly ever graced this place…but when it did, it made a bang in the community. Head of the Police Department, Johnny Moore, stood beside Mayor Crown, glancing over the information contained clipboard as well. He shook his head in shame, knowing not whether to be shocked or appalled by the killing or disgusted. He took a deep sigh and turned towards Crown. “Where are the wife and kid?” “Wife is in the local Baptist Hospital. She suffered a panic attack after watching the slaying and the overall shock that overcame her. The kid is under police custody, but we can’t get her to stop crying long enough to tell us anything.” Moore slid his finger across the bridge of his nose while closing his eyes, saddened by what had taken place in the city that he called home, but even more so by the aftermath he was sure would follow. He knew in the back of his mind that somewhere out their was a elite cult, built on racism and religious beliefs that lead them to kill. What troubled him most was knowing that the city he had long protected was in fear… And there was nothing he could do about it. “We’ve got to do something, Mayor. I’m not going to sit here and watch the good citizens of this city live in terror for the simple fact that a bunch of brats are running around slinging weapons at whomever they displease for no apparent reason.” The mayor shook his head after hearing the statement, not quick to make a response. He simply stood there, watching as the body of James Jackson was rolled into the ambulance. Inside of that body bag was a loyal citizen to the community of Wetumpka, Alabama…slain at the feet of his family for no other reason other than being black and proud of his heritage. It left a sick knot in the pit of Crown’s stomach as he turned around to address Moore. “There is nothing we can do. The only witnesses we have are the little girl who’s too horrified to do or say anything and the wife who’s in the damn hospital, hanging on to life by a thread. Unless they make their presence known in a direct location which we can easily locate them…I’m afraid there’s nothing we can do.” His mouth nearly fell from his mouth. Moore was sickened by the Mayor telling him that his city would have to live in fear because of this racially motivated cult. He slammed his fist into the hood of the nearby Police squad car, his face turning red with rage. “Technology is at its PEAK right now and you’re telling me that there’s not ONE THING we can do to stop a bunch of crazy teenagers!?!” “…Well….there is one option, but your interest in it, I’m sure, is hardly favorable.” “Name it, Crown.” “…Vangelus Olsig.” The bones in the body of Moore shook at the very mention of that name. Vangelus Olsig had made a name for himself in his hometown of Bogotá, Columbia for being a deadly vigilante before being arrested under the charges of unprovoked murder. Moore expressed his deep hatred for Olsig’s method of doing these on every turn, but now he was in a position that Olsig had been in numerous of times, and the vain in his body telling him to hunt this cult down and slay them like the dogs they are beckoned. “You’ve got to be kidding me…” “I’m afraid he’s the only person available that can fill this job of hunting down this cult and bringing them to justice, Moore. It’s either him or nothing.” “He lives all the way in Columbia. The only time he’s down here is during his little wrestling gig. You want to fly a plane half way across the country just to pick up some low life that’s probably going to end up screwing us over anyway?” “It’s a risk I’m worth taking, Chief. I’d rather be screwed over than have the people of this city slain for no apparent reason. Now I believe he’s making his way down here for another wrestling gig sometime soon…We’ll offer up the cash, he’ll accept, and we’re in business. Deal?” The mayor extended his arm out, awaiting the returning handshake from Chief Moore. The handshake didn’t come immediately, instead there was nothing more than a cold star at Chief Moore. The fury in his eyes told the story of how he was torn between hiring a deadly vigilante, or allowing more slayings to corrupt the streets of his city. Second thought rapidly presented themselves as he shook hands, basically with the devil himself. The clanging of steel hitting steel echoed throughout the center. Violent screams of threats, made by deep toned voices whom seemed destined on destroying each other, but in the midst of it all Vangelus Olsig, lying across the cold steel bunk that had been assigned as his bed, contemplating on the huge match he had coming up at Tropical Turmoil. The last thing that swayed his mindset was heading back into the mean streets, fighting crime as a side job to the profession he so adored. “Olsig” The thundering voice companied the jingling of keys that grew in sound with every second. One of the guards was coming down the hallway calling Olsig’s name, automatically signaling to Olsig that something was wrong. He sat up off the cold bunk and stared behind the bars that trapped him and to the guard who had just made his presence known in front of the cell. He drew the keys from his pocket and inserted one into the cells lock, sliding open the cell to reveal a bewildered Vangelus Olsig. “You’re out on bail…” The look of bewilderment mixed in with a look of bliss at the thought that he was finally getting out of this hell hole and back into the real world. Maybe an old friend? Or maybe a former client? Nonetheless, he gathered all of the belongings that he had along with him in the cell and slung them across his shoulder as he stepped out of the cell and made his way down the hall to loud thunderous booms by all of the guards that stood in surrounding cells. He paid none of them any attention… Because he was now a free man… The only question was how… He finally reached the main office of the building where there stood Mayor Bobby Crown with a broad smile on his face as he looked into he bewildered eyes of Vangelus Olsig. This was a man that Olsig had heard about on several occasions, but never really paid any attention to, so the question as to why he was bailing him out presented itself with much urgency of being answered. “Follow me, Vangelus.” Crown turned around and stepped out of the building. Though a bit hesitant, Olsig made his way out of the building following crown all the way to his black mustang in which Crown opened and stepped inside of. He invited Olsig to do the same as Olsig shrugged and accepted the offer, stepping into the Mustang for what he assumed would be a small chat between the two. The roar of the cranking engine made Olsig tremble, misinformed on where exactly he was going. “Where are you taking me, Crown?” “Vangelus…we sort of need your help in Wetumpka. I’m going to take you to the airport with me where we’ll both take a pla-…” “Wait…we’re going where? You bail me out of prison to haul me all the way to Wetumpka, Alabama?” “Well, we have a very serious situation going on and we need your help.” Olsig sighed and slumped down in the leather seat, holding his hand up to his head as the migraine inside began to take its furious toll. The lifestyle that he thought he had put behind him was coming back with a vengeance, and at this point he wasn’t so sure if there was anything he could do about it. It was either this, or Jail…and to be behind those bars once again was a thought that made him cringe. His choice was blatantly clear, but his desire was not. “Look…there is a huge reward in store for you if you follow through with this. James Jackson, a local citizen of Wetumpka was slain by an elite cult focusing around racism and religious beliefs. If you can help bring this group to justice, a large lump sum of money will be granted your way.” Suddenly, the pity look on Olsig’s face began to lighten up a bit. Anytime that the idea of money was offered, he was sure to jump on it. But suddenly, the thought of being involved in the 6-Pack turmoil match at Tropical Turmoil hit him. He had a huge match that he had to prepare for…he had no time to be seeking an elite cult, no matter how great the reward added up to be. “I’m sorry, but I must decline. I have a big match this week and I must prepare.” “That’s another thing. We realize how important this match is to you, so a little research has led us to one man by the name of Rudolf McNamara…one of the greatest technicians in the sports history. He’ s going to take time to train with you for the match at anytime you wish…for free. We’ve got you covered; now we just need to know if you can say the same for us.” The thought scrolled through the mind of Vangelus Olsig. These people had this whole thing figured out meaning that it must have been of grave importance. He had always adored Rudolf McNamara and the chance to train with him was a honor indeed. He looked over at Crown with the most unpleasant look on his face… “…Deal.” Signed. Seal. Delivered. “So, when should I begin?” Olsig peered into the eyes of both Moore and Crown, more so into the eyes of Chief Moore who held an ailing glare in his eyes towards the vigilante. Crown gave Moore a nudge as if to signal to him to stop it as they stood in the penthouse that would serve as the hiding camp for Olsig during this mission. “Tomorrow…in the meantime, we will be tending to the daughter of the family.” Suddenly, the cocky smile that graced Olsig’s face turned into one of deep sorrow. Being a victim of parent deaths, Olsig could only imagine the pain that the little girl must have been going through. “Wait a minute…where’s her mother?” Olsig asked. “Well…sadly enough, she died in the hospital after suffering from numerous panic attacks and breakdowns which ultimately lead to a heart attack that killed her. Now we must find a home for the heart stricken seven year old girl.” Olsig turned around for a moment, rubbing his hands through his hair as he thought over the situation. The unbearable pain that the little girl must be going through ran through his mind, and images of the pain that ran through his after his parents’ death also began to take its toll. He turned back towards the Mayor. “Give me the girl…” “…I-I’m sorry?” “Let her stay here with me. I’m pretty sure I can relate to her situation more than anyone else could.” “But, sir…who’ll care for her while you’re out investigating?” “This place does have a butler, doesn’t it?” “Yes, but-…” “Give me the girl or the deal is off.” The Mayor took a deep sigh as he looked over at Chief Moore who held a “I told you” look in his eyes.
