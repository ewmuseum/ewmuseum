The Tchuman Show

23 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23/05/06 Tuesday 23rd May 2006 To the man in the motel room Charlotte had never seemed so beautiful. Two days prior to ReVolution and the sun was still valiantly hanging in the sky, determined to shed as much light on North Carolina as nature would allow it. Children played in the streets, mothers and fathers returned home from work to their families and tourists flocked up and down the bustling inner-city sidewalks. The PRIME superstar surveyed the scene from his balcony with a warm, contented smile, all felt right with the world. In two days time he would enter the squared-circle with a fellow legend of the industry to compete for the chance of a shot at Killean Sirrajin’s Universal Title - but at this precise moment there was nothing further from his mind. Relaxation was the key to victory. The digital clock chimed out to him from his bedside table, rudely interrupting the moment of serenity and causing him to abandon his sublime post. He sighed a little and quickstepped inside to bash the device over the head – his version of punishment for the intrusion. It was 19.55. A small flutter of guilt swept across him as he recalled the reason for the electronic reminder and he almost felt idiotic for feeling the need to apologise to the clock. One soothing, affectionate tap on the white plastic of the device later and he was headed towards his television set with vigour in his aura and a hop in his step. The talking telly-box flickered into action upon feeling his touch as he turned to clear a space amongst his travelling possessions on the couch “Beautiful weather in downtown…” A local weather forecast started up, but was immediately shutdown with a flick of his wrist and some pressure on the remote control. He already knew it was fine outside; he didn’t need some square in a tweed suit telling him so. “…suffering from constipation…” He winced at the female-oriented commercial and again commanded a change of channel, horrifying images of constipated ladies unfortunately not far from his mind. Channel Two-Four-Eight seemed to represent his choice as the credits to “That 70’s Show” rolled against the audio backdrop of Cheap Trick, somehow gently soothing his weary mind and exhausted bones. “Up next on FX, our eight o’clock movie: X-Men.” The commercials that followed seemed to be over instantly and soon he was relaxing in front of his selected entertainment for the evening. Or was he? “Previously on the Tchuman Show…” A dramatic voiceover that sounded eerily like the “Inhuman Being” himself probably should have startled our protagonist – but it didn’t. He sat there as though the whole chain of events was completely normal, totally relaxed and chilled out, and simply watched… “I’m tired of THIS! I don’t have to listen to your bullshit. I don’t have to play your games! I run this damn show! You listen to me… you play MY games!” Tyler Nelson’s voice is the first to emanate from the TV set as his words to Tchu from Ultra Violence begin to draw our observing PRIME star in… “I’m gonna kick the shit out of you for this one, Nelson” Tchu’s riposte is loud and clear as images are shown of him being dragged away by Nelson’s security. Words become useless as a montage events from the PRIME pay per view flash onto the screen, captivating the attention of the silent, mystery viewer in our midst. Tchu is shown fighting valiantly in the Tag Title match before being handcuffed and removed from the arena with a smile on his face, this is our lasting image and it soon fades to black when “Pressure” by Staind starts up and an introduction for the “Tchuman Show” begins… …Ignatius Lisieux hated hospitals. He figured that he’d seen enough hospital beds already to last him a lifetime. This was where he was, though, and for now there wasn’t a damn thing he could do about. Columbia Memorial hospital in Jacksonville, Florida: Ward 3B. Adam had been great. He had postponed his outward flight to Charlotte, scheduled for Monday 15th, in order to stay and keep a watchful eye over his oldest amigo. Johnny Noble was nowhere to be seen, the lack of his presence had most definitely not gone unnoticed. Right now, the French-Canadian lay alone. No Adam, no Noble, no nurses, no doctors, just him and his trusty saline drip. Once upon a time he might have said that this is how he liked it. Perhaps back in the Global Title days, the undefeated days, he preferred his own company over that of others. He’d come a long way since then, though. The antisocial recluse had his eyes opened up to the world in December 2004 at the hands of the Dark Age and he hadn’t looked back. Noble and Adam had become great friends, at least until last Sunday, he had Hayley, albeit secretly and he still kept in touch with Killean Sirrajin, he was pretty happy with where he was at. What he wasn’t happy with, though, and I hate to be clichéd, I really do, but he wasn’t happy with the hospital food. His diet of carbohydrates in the morning and protein in the evening had been shot to pieces, his Doctor insisting that instead of building himself up he should merely be focused on recovering from his numerous post-pay-per-view injuries. But he’d been here before; he knew how it went… he knew how much it would cost - so he wasn’t getting too worked up about it. Instead he preferred to look forward to a week off from active competition and ReVolution One-Hundred and Colossus Three on the horizon. He was thinking happy thoughts… until Adam arrived, the bearer of bad news. “Awright bud?” inquired the Glaswegian, popping his head around the wood-pane door of Ignatius’ private ward, “You want me to bring you anything in before I get settled doon?” The French-Canadian smiled at the arrival of his compadre and followed-up with a hesitant shake of the head (his neck still aching from Ultra Violence.) “Awright then, nae bother” Adam responded, sitting down beside Lisieux in the process. Ignatius was contemplating him a little; something seemed off, “What’s with the accent?” prodded the Hall of Famer verbally, fingering exactly where the anomaly lay, “When I struggle to understand you, that’s when I know you’re overdoing it…” The Scotsman sighed a little, instantly caught-out by his know-all associate. Lisieux’s ears pricked up a little and he struggled to lift himself up to a more vertical position as Adam went to speak, “Got some bad news for ya…” The French-Canadian was silent in response, simply awaiting the potential hammer-blow with baited breath. “You’ve been booked this week…” continued Adam, a pained expression on his face. Lisieux instantly looked unimpressed, “For BEEP sake, are you for real?” “Aye” was the solemn reply from the “Number One Son”, little did Ignatius know that worse was to follow. “Aye, but it’s not just that you’re booked…” Adam’s face said it all. Ignatius knew instantly. Thoughts flashed through his mind, the worst of the worst of possibilities came at him from every angle: Titan St. James, Clint Poteet… handicap match, cluster match, gimmick match… Ozric Mortimer’s back with a vengeance… Vampir Nosferatu in a crucifix match… “…Tchu” interjected the Scotsman, wanting to put Lisieux out his psychological misery. “Tchu?” Lisieux pondered, his mind immediately going blank as it desperately tried to process the information. His brain struggled to function as he attempted to comprehend the enormity of the task that lay ahead of him, just one short week away. “Y’awright?” inquired Adam, placing a concerned hand on the still shoulder of the French-Canadian. “Ignatius? I said y’awright?” Lisieux did not respond verbally, the tear that rolled down his left cheek said everything and more than ever he possibly could in reaction to the news. Adam’s grip on his shoulder tightened as he attempted to console his injured friend, but no matter what the Scotsman could offer the French-Canadian in the way of support the fact remained: Ignatius Lisieux versus Tchu on ReVolution Ninety-Four next week. And for that, for the first time since ReVolution Fifty, he cried. Meanwhile, in Slumberland, North Carolina, our original protagonist has made a beeline for the en suite bathroom upon the commencement of the first commercial break of the “Tchuman Show” and for some God-forsaken reason we have followed him in! Unzipping his fly hastily and letting out a cheery sigh upon the release of some bodily fluid, he seems oblivious to our intrusion. Urine meets water in the toilet bowl as a cheery whistled tune flutters out from the lips of the man who must stand at around six-two and two-forty, but still we do not see his face. A flush of the commode later and he’s merrily on his way back to the darkening living area of the “room”, which is actually a conglomerate of three rooms and so isn’t technically the correct term for our scene here, but I digress. The former Universal and Tag Team Champion sits down (are you getting this, yet?) in his original spot and awaits the return of his show to the screen before him, giving me a little time to fill you in on some details. You’ve seen the Truman Show, right? You know? The one where Jim Carrey somehow lives his life oblivious to the fact that he’s quite possibly the most famous man in America, if not the entire world, until some enlightened dear helps him to find the truth about his existence. Well, good, that’s presuming you have, in fact, seen it. Jim Carrey was otherwise engaged this week and so I thought I’d create PRIME’s very own version of that classic and here we are… just keep all that in mind as you continue along my valiant trek to defeat the Tchuminator. Okay, good, the show’s back on… time to exit the “fourth dimension”… and… done! Relatively fresh from the body-blow of discovering that a squared-circle date with the “Inhuman Being” was a mere seven days away and with tears barely dried onto his cheeks, it was time for Ignatius Lisieux to begin to come to terms with the task that lay ahead of him and begin formulating a plan for just how to deal with the man who had only lost once in his entire PRIME tenure. Adam, of course, was ready, willing and able to lend Ignatius a hand in any way possible… most likely in ways that Ignatius wouldn’t appreciate, too. “So… it’s just a singles match then? Me against Tchu, that’s it? I guess it’s Nelson’s way of punishing us for whatever, the Dual Halo for me, I know that for sure… damn I’m an idiot” the French-Canadian drifted away a little as his mind wandered back to the events of Culture Shock Three in Japan, where he was the man unfortunate enough to find the battered carcass of Tyler Nelson laying in the rubble of the Halo’s ceiling. “Well…” began Adam, hesitant about raining on the Lisieux parade even further, “…firstly, it was Motoki who booked the match…” The Hall of Famer scowled upon the mention of his long-time nemesis’ name, anger and rage bubbling up inside him, though he controlled it so that Adam might continue… “…and secondly, it’s just not just any match… Sirrajin is reffing it… and it’s for a shot at his title on ninety-five…” Adam trailed off, leaving Ignatius with the taste of potential gold on his metaphorical taste-buds. The French-Canadian found himself speechless, again. All of this was a heck of a lot to take in for a guy who had just had his brains scrambled like egg at Ultra Violence at the hands of Hoyt Williams. Firstly he’d struggled to compute the fact that he was even booked next week; then came the news that he was facing the “Inhuman Being”… and now Adam was telling him that Killean Sirrajin was the referee and that the winner would get a shot at the PRIME Universal Title! “Are you BEEPing with me here?” was all Lisieux could muster in way of a response - the shock was most definitely evident on his kisser. Adam shook his head rapidly, “No chance, man… why would I do that to ya?” “I dunno…” trailed off the French-Canadian again, “…practical joke, or something? It’s not April 1st is it?” The Scotsman simply cocked his head sideways and glared at his compatriot in response to the nonsensical interrogative. “Well I dunno, do I?” defended Lisieux, “…I mean, Jesus, I’m hardly fully-functional as it is… then you’re barging in here telling me I’ve got to tango with Tchu for a shot at the Uni strap?! How the BEEP am I meant to deal with that?” “…You could cry again…” Adam winced, he knew he’d taken it too far and immediately tried to repent, “…Sorry…” Lisieux frowned. “BEEP you, man.” Then, thankfully, he laughed. “I’m serious though, bud…” led Adam into a poser, “…what was up with that?” The Scotsman hadn’t seen his French-Canadian buddy cry, ever, in reality. He hadn’t been there for him on ReVolution Fifty and he certainly hadn’t ever witnessed it first-hand before, he was dumbfounded by it. Lisieux did his best to shed some light, however, “I dunno…” he began cautiously, “…I guess I could blame the drugs, or the situation… or the way my body feels right now… but man, put yourself in my shoes…” Adam nodded, encouraging the “Hall of Fame Champion” to continue… “…I’ve just had a hellacious match with Hoyt, took one hell of a beating from him, just three days ago I was spewing blood all over… I figure I’m gonna be able to recuperate a little and have a nice break from competition and then you come in here with the news that I’m booked… against Tchu… BEEPing Tchu…” the fear was evident both in his voice and in his expression, the “MVP” feared the “Inhuman Being”, at least at this current moment in time. “You can beat him, man…” started Adam comfortingly, “…hey, I bet nobody had me down to beat Killean the other week, but I did that-“ Lisieux immediately interjected, “-Yeah, but with all due respect, you were pretty damn fresh heading into that one…” He had a point. Adam knew it. In PRIME the margins were so small that on any given week anyone could beat anyone else, so when you had a guy suffering from numerous injuries against a guy who was relatively fresh, not to mention when that guy is Tchu, the odds of winning narrowed dramatically. “He’s only human though, Ig…” started the Scotsman again, desperate to instil some hope into his waning friend, “…He’s lost before, to Walkins of all people… he didn’t win the Halo… he’s not invincible… BEEP it, he’s just who you were a couple of years ago…” Ignatius nodded. Adam wasn’t telling him anything he didn’t already know. The French-Canadian knew it himself, already, Tchu was the new Iggy… the similarities were remarkable. Both six-two, both around two-forty, both won two belts extremely quickly (including the biggest one), both born in August, both almost impossible to beat. “He’s who I was…” muttered the “MVP” solemnly, “…not who I am.” Again, Adam was stuck for words. This time, instead of pissing into the wind he decided it was time for a beverage break and so broke free from the shackles of tension that currently bound the atmosphere, “You wanna drink?” Lisieux was completely still, he didn’t reply. Adam, too, didn’t utter another word - instead he simply turned and made his exit from the room, leaving the French-Canadian to stare at the reflection he saw in the window adjacent to his resting place… A reflection that was eerily not his own, but that of a certain “Inhuman Being”… And fear, once again, consumed him. Obviously not a fan of commercial breaks - even imaginary ones - our protagonist is on his feet and kitchen-bound before the first advertisement has even commenced. Hungry for a snack of some description his first port of call is the dinky fridge that sits in the corner of the faux-tiled room. An electronic buzz ensues upon the opening of the bulky door and not much inside entices… a few energy drinks, some cheese, a half-eaten bowl of pasta… it’s hardly the Ritz. The muscular shoulders of the wrestler shrug a little as he closes over the fridge door and heads towards the trusty cupboards. Seeing how grocery shopping wasn’t exactly a particular hobby of our faceless buddy, his options were severely limited no matter where he looked. The cupboards held some interesting-looking potato chips, most likely leftover from a previous occupier of the motel room, and not much else aside from bread. It was post-five, though, so carbohydrates were out of the question. A glance was shot towards the infamous alarm clock, which read “8.28pm” and then his gaze was cast outside to the rapidly-darkening streets of inner-city Charlotte. The sun was hanging low in the sky, desperately gripping at the clouds to remain for a little while longer, the children were safely inside and the streets were relatively deserted. The scene had changed dramatically in thirty short minutes. Another minute flew by and – deciding he could wait another quarter hour to get some food – the brown haired superstar slunk back towards the couch in order to take in the third part of the fictional and imaginary “Tchuman Show”… Having returned a short while ago from fetching some drinks, Adam was sat in silence beside the bed of his resting friend. Ignatius Lisieux shared in his solace, both men preferring to leave unspoken words as their communication method of choice. Wild birds flocked past the window, casting a brief, fluttering shadow over proceedings and somehow, someway, breaking the tension a little in the process by prompting the French-Canadian to speak. “You really think I stand a chance against Tchu next week?” he interrogated, his right eyebrow slightly raised, desperately interested to hear the response of his companion. Adam slurped a little on the straw of his juice before delivering his upbeat reaction, “Of course, man… hell you’re Ignatius Lisieux, former Global Champion, former Intense Champion, former Tag-Team Champion, Hall of-“ “Okay, okay…” laughed Lisieux, totally easing the atmosphere, “…I get it, no need to go overboard…” “But really bud, I think you can do it.” Adam placed his juice carton down on Lisieux’s bedside table, “If I can beat Lance Jackson and Killean Sirrajin when all the odds are against me, you can do it against Tchu… Nobody had you pegged to beat Mortimer, you did it, nobody had you pegged to overcome Hoyt, you did it… Hell, nobody would’ve called you doing so bloody well in the Dual Halo either… all of this after what happened to you as well.” Adam’s supportive words were incredibly easy on the ear and belief was gradually beginning to enter the mindset of the “MVP”, the return of the “underdog” tag was something that he secretly relished, “I do sorta like having the odds against me… it’s a nice change from the norm.” “No pressure, eh?” commented Adam, thinking he understood where Lisieux was coming from, but just missing the mark by a little. “Hell, there’s always pressure…” grinned Lisieux, “…but at least this week I’ve got an excuse to lose to Tchu, and hell, it’s hardly anything to be ashamed of, is it?” Adam returned the smile with one of his own, “Aye, right! But let’s not be talking of getting beat here; let’s be talking about winning this thing… I know you well enough to be certain that you don’t wanna lose this one.” The French-Canadian nodded again, self-awareness reaching a recent high due to the amounts of morphine that had been pumped into him post-Hoyt. “Yeah, for sure… there’s nothing that I hate more than losing. Well damn man - if I’m gonna beat the Tchuminator then I’d better start getting myself prepared for him…” The “Number One Son” looked a little startled as Ignatius motioned as though he was about ready to up and leave his bed, “Wait a wee second there Ig…” he began cautiously, stretching out his right hand as though to issue a “stop” sign, “…Surely you can stomach another couple of days in here? It’s for your own good…” Lisieux shook his head, “No way man, I’m not gonna beat Tchu by lying in a hospital bed feeling sorry for myself, that’s for damn sure.” “Aye, but there’s doctors and nurses here to help sort you out… there’ll only be me on the outside” concluded Adam, making his feelings well-known. “Don’t worry; we can handle this ourselves…” Lisieux expressed, pulling the white sheets from over him and removing the drip from his left arm” …Where the hell are my clothes, anyway?” The French-Canadian stood up beside his bed and wobbled a little as Adam reached out his arms to form something of a protective-barrier around his disorientated amigo. Lisieux, though, managed to gain his composure and get his head right so that he could stand unsupported. “They’re in the bottom drawer there” answered the Scotsman belatedly, “…Nurse put ‘em in there when you got here.” Managing to keep his dignity inside of the classic hospital attire, Ignatius bent down and pulled open the drawer with a smile before dragging out a bloodied white tee-shirt, some torn jeans and less-than-clean pairs of socks and boxer shorts. “Oooooh, nice!” he commented sarcastically as Adam stood-up to give the French-Canadian a little privacy. “I’m not sure about this, bud, but if it’s what you want then let’s get you checked out of here…” Adam sighed, obviously not completely convinced that this was for the best. Whatever the weather, the Scotsman knew that Lisieux was old enough to look after himself and so just accepted his decision. Now it was time to prepare for the “Inhuman Being”. Back in the quasi-reality of our sleeping protagonist, another commercial break had ensued, leaving him with a couple of minutes to kill inside of his head. However, as what tends to happen with dreams, his mind got shifted onto another coil and before too long there entered a rather bizarre scenario involving penguins, pirates, Chandler Tsonda and Chet Worth that simply cannot be explained into words. Just think of the craziest, most random dream that you’ve ever had if you want to relate a little. Let’s just hope he returns in time for us to catch the final part of the “Tchuman Show”, because I sure don’t like the idea of giving a running commentary on what he’s thinking about now… and worse still, if he wakes up I’ll be left having to attempt to make watching X-Men on FX seem like an interesting thing to read about! Basically, this is where we’re at for those skim-readers amongst you: our protagonist is a yet-to-be-revealed PRIME superstar who stands around six-two and two-forty and who has held the Universal and Tag titles (can you tell who it is yet!?) Anyway, he was settling down nicely in the solitude of his hotel room to watch X-Men on the FX Network (plug!) before drifting off into the “Land of Nod”. So this is how we’ve ended up watching the fictional “Tchuman Show” with intermittent interruptions from yours truly while the “commercial breaks” are on. “Stay tuned after the Tchuman Show to see a special documentary on just how the program is made!” Oh good, he’s back! With your final instalment of the show, courtesy of his over-active mind, here is the mystery protagonist… Bloody shirt and all, Ignatius Lisieux proudly exits the Columbia Memorial hospital into the Jacksonville sunshine with his sports bag on his shoulder and Adam by his side. What spoils the scene a little is the noticeable limp caused by the French-Canadians left-leg, and the evident signs of pain that are prominent on his face. The “Number One Son” looks towards him with a grimace, not wanting to comment on the fact that it seems Lisieux might have signed out of hospital a little too early and likewise, the “MVP” refrains from words too, until something dawns on him… “Man I just thought - you got any cash?” the worry in Ignatius’ tone is evident. He looks towards the Scotsman with wide eyes, “I think I might have just maxed-out my card with that hospital bill…” Adam is exceptionally quick to respond, but not in the way one might expect… Without saying a word he whips two plane tickets from his inside pocket and hands them over to Lisieux, grinning all the way. The Hall of Famer looks stunned and isn’t exactly quick to respond, “How… how did you know?” The Scotsman grins again, breaking out into a laugh as he goes, “I know you too well, man… as soon as I found out about the Tchu match I knew I’d come here and tell you and that you’d want to leave straight away…” It was probably the most romantic thing anyone had ever done for Lisieux, but due to the awkwardness surrounding Adam’s sexuality he was loathe to respond accordingly, if he’d been of the female variety, though, he’d be getting pounded right about now. “Thanks man… jeez, just thanks… but what do I owe you?” “Don’t be stupid man - I know you have a struggle with your pay-packet because of Motoki and stuff, but some of us carry home a tidy wage from that place.” Adam didn’t mean to be arrogant, but it came across that way a little bit to the underprivileged French-Canadian, though he couldn’t mention it after such a gesture. “Thanks Adam, I mean it…” he started, his eyes looking a lot more alive than they had for days, “…I’ll pay you back, too, somehow.” Cash had actually become a real problem for Ignatius. Since the hefty days of his Global Championship reign and his alliance with Motoki his current plight was all the more difficult to deal with. Post-ReVolution Fifty had seen him owing PRIME upwards of $500,000 after almost a year of continued medical treatment and incarceration. GlobeTech could no longer get insurance on the French-Canadian, and so 75% of his pay went directly back to the company, he was basically working for minimum wage and he had to wrestle matches to pay back the debt. He was caught between a rock and a hard place. “Don’t worry about it, really…” responded Adam with a pat on the back, “Let’s just get to Charlotte and get you ready for Tchu… you sure you’re ready for this?” Adam didn’t even need to hear a response; he could see the fire already burning in Ignatius’ eyes, even if the French-Canadian’s body couldn’t see him through then it was a safe bet that his mind would. The “Number One Son” began to feel a lot more relaxed about what lay on the horizon. As for Ignatius Lisieux, well he was as confident as he’d ever be. He couldn’t remember waking up, or what had woken him up, but he knew he was awake. A little drool was wiped away from his chin as he stretched out his arms and legs and let-out a terrific “yawn”. It was 9.07pm according to Mr. Clock. Dr. Xavier was on his television set, bald head and all. He stood up slowly and headed towards the box, flicking it off by the spring-mounted plastic button, he had no desire to watch the last thirty minutes of a film he’d seen before, he couldn’t even remember why he wanted to watch it in the first place. Nothing better to do. Walking out onto the balcony proved to be an ingenious idea as the fresh air instantly flushed the cobwebs out of his system, he looked up at the pale moon with bright eyes and thoughts of his upcoming match began flooding his mind. He was just two short days away from facing a fellow professional that he greatly respected for the first time in singles competition, he was putting his record and reputation on the line and that worried him somewhat. There was an awful lot to win and an awful lot to lose. A date with Killean on ReVolution Ninety-Five was an attractive prospect, the chance to face an old rival in the ring was something that he relished, both men had never really got to settle their issues with one another and this would represent the best chance. Not to mention the fact that the Universal Title was on the line, it felt like a long time since he’d held the top title and there was a lust inside of him that dictated that it needed to be his sometime soon. Everyone wanted it, he was no different. Returning to the sanctuary of his dark motel room, the lack of options for entertainment and the lack of company meant that an early night seemed to be on the cards. He flicked the en suite light on and headed towards the mounted mirror on the wall. First, though, he reached down into his travel bag and plucked from it his worn toothbrush and a fresh tube of paste. You would think that a six-two, two-forty pound former Universal and Tag Champion with a birthday in August would be happy to look at his own reflection in the mirror, but it was never an easy task. He always found himself evaluating his being whenever he did so - picking faults with his lifestyle and his choices, psychologically evaluating himself, creating a persona to use in PRIME… it was hard work. As Iggy stared at the reflection in his en suite mirror, at six-two and two-forty, the world stared back through the lens of a hidden camera. That was the price the former Universal and Tag-Team Champion paidfor his success in PRIME. He lived his life through a lens for theenjoyment and pleasure of others. Everything he did, everything hesaid… even everything he dreamt, they saw it all, every week. As Tchu stared at the reflection in his en suite mirror, at six-two and two-forty, the world stared back through the lens of a hidden camera. That was the price the former Universal and Tag-Team Champion paidfor his success in PRIME. He lived his life through a lens for theenjoyment and pleasure of others. Everything he did, everything hesaid… even everything he dreamt, they saw it all, every week. They could have been looking at a reflection of each other and they would have thought nothing of it. The “Tchuman Show” was more real than anyone could have imagined, our protagonist could have easily been either of them, but it turned out to be both… “We’re not so different, you and I…” The phrase had never been truer. Tchu and Iggy, two colossal pillars of the PRIME fabric, two men who might have been legitimately separated at birth, two men with more coincidental similarities for anyone’s liking collide on ReVolution Ninety-Four in order to determine the number one contender for the Universal Title. What Ignatius Lisieux lacks in physical health, he makes up for with experience. He has been where Tchu is now and he knows what it takes to bring down the best, he’s fallen from grace himself. Tchu has the advantage physically, but, ironically for him, not psychologically. It’s been said, “To be the man… you’ve got to beat the man.” But what happens if you are, or were, the man you need to beat?
