Born Anew

5 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 5, 2005 My name is Syko Arsonist. There was a time, not too long ago, when I could use my name and fame to get anything I wanted. You’d here my name and instantly knew who I was. That was, of course, if you were an avid wrestling fan. But that’s pretty much the only medium I had any power in. Outside the ring, I was a nobody. I was just another rich man able to buy things he didn’t need. But inside that ring, I was a god. I demanded fame. I demanded adulation. I demanded the big bucks. Over time, my legend in the ring began to reflect my myth out of it. The years passed and each year I was less and less involved until I left the business altogether. It was over. I was over. Not sure why I decided to suddenly up and leave the business, but I will always remember why I started in the first place. Looking back on my life, there were probably five epiphanies I can recall that changed my life. I don’t know how many people can say that – especially at twenty-nine years of age. It is with a mixture of hatred and joy that I begin to recall such memories. Some of them are vile, remembering the darkest moments of my life; some of them are celestial, remembering the moments I landed among the stars. You are probably sick of hearing all those “rags to riches” sob stories of an abandoned youth but I have no choice but to start there. Marissa Santos, my mother, was involved in the ugly concoction of unprotected, underage sex. At sixteen years of age she managed to get herself pregnant significantly delaying her schooling and denting the purse. Abortion was not an option for her and for that I am thankful or I will not be here before you today. But where there is gratefulness there is also resentment and wonderment. How different would my life be? Perhaps I might not be rich. Perhaps I might live in South America. Where abortion wasn’t an option, neither was keeping the child. “Xavier” was the name my mother gave me when I asked whom my father had been. At twenty years of age, my “father” was nothing more but a selfish, decaying piece of shit, worrying only about his own needs and thinking nothing of taking advantage of an underage girl. He filled my mother’s head with promises of love and support, but when she became pregnant, fled and provided neither. He gave up the opportunity to become a man and have a family. Marissa says she never saw him again and neither she nor I regret that. Xavier wasn’t the only one to turn a cheek towards the problem. My grandfather, more out of embarrassment than anything, forced Marissa to give up the child. She did so, hesitantly, with the hopes that one day she could recover her son. The St. Patrick’s Orphanage in New York would be the closest thing I would call home whilst I traveled between foster homes. BECOMING SYKO ARSONIST The second incident I can describe as life-changing was the moment I was offered a chance to work as a wrestler for a then-prosperous federation. My first few months in the business were what you would describe as rock bottom. I was green, too green at times, to make any impact. Eventually, the federation went through some major financial problems and decided to cut half the employees. They kept me under the hopes that I could improve and work at the minimum salary. Following an extensive working with creative control, and my own background, I was to be called Syko Arsonist – a name and gig that would stick with me forever. Over the years the fans forgot my real name. Syko Arsonist was who I was, am, and always would be. That name became synonymous with success no matter where it was mattered. A FLAME IGNITED Well into the second year of my life as a professional wrestler, I was introduced to two things: a new employer and a valet. The new employer I could care about less for it was run by a greedy tycoon who fixed the matches and kept his pets at top tier. But it was an opportunity to make more money and I couldn’t care less about anything else. The valet was another story. Her name was Emilia. She had smooth, creamy white skin, blue eyes, and auburn hair that matched my own rage and name. Our initial encounter was awkward, me being enamored with her, though I now admit it was lust, and her so afraid, having heard of my violent behavior. But we grew accustomed to each other both on a business and personal level. Eventually, our relationship blossomed to new heights I never dreamed of. I fell in love with her. I knew that this was that second chance we are search for and few of us ever find. I didn’t have a family to go home to, or call my own, so I would make my own with her. A FLAME EXTINGUISHED Emilia’s family would be available that summer, so we postponed the wedding for the summer so that all her relatives flying in from South America and Europe would attend. Her family and friends would be the only guests. I didn’t have anyone to invite and I wasn’t going to invite the workers at the federation. As Emilia told me, this was our time. Our time to make our own family, regardless of what the past held. The months past by rather quickly and summer was soon approaching. Eventually, we were a mere two weeks away from the big day and the first waves of Emilia’s family had been flying in already. All was well, no great, in my life and everything was going great. There was only one problem: Emilia had an obsessed ex. I didn’t see it as a problem, as I trusted Emilia. I knew she loved me and wouldn’t do anything to harm me. Her ex was a different matter. His name was Sebastian Richer. He was a Canadian born entrepreneur whom had courted her in her earlier days. He was obnoxious and selfish eventually growing into his family’s wealth and making a name for himself. He owned the worldwide famous company TITAN Enterprises, Inc., whom had a stronghold on just about everything from cars to oil rigs to soft drinks. Emilia broke up with him several years later when she lived in Toronto, citing his violent behavior as a reason. Years later, her past caught up with her but she was not going to let it become her present. Sebastian Richer saw differently and could not accept her getting married. A week before the wedding night I received a message from the police. Emilia had been in an accident. I rushed over to the scene. There was no accident. Emilia had been beaten down by a group of thugs and then shot at execution range. I knew immediately who the culprit was. In an instant, my second chance was ruined -taken away from me. I had lost my family when I was a newborn. Now, I had lost another family as a man. Emilia was pregnant, I later found out. I lost two loved ones that day. FAMILY REUNION In May of 2004, the Prime Time Central (PTC) was in the middle of the semi-final round of its inaugural Cruiserweight Tournament. I found myself face to face with the accolades-spewing shrimp of a man himself: Seymour Almasy. The semi-finals were the last thing on my mind as some random lady in her late forties paid my house an unexpected visit. She showed up there being escorted by a man, who I later found out was a private lawyer, in a dull, morbid black vehicle. She claimed to be my mother. Great, I thought. I quickly sent her packing, but she remained resilient and started to cry. That wasn’t going to put a dent in me. Others had tried the same routine. Any avid follower of the business knew who I was. How else do you explain those unusual posters around the arenas? This particular lady was more ambitious than the others. She hired this man, Malcolm Kennedy to conjure up an investigation and came up with some pretty damning evidence. It was too much for me to bare, but I dismissed her – watched her leave away in tears. A few days went by and I could not get the event out of my mine. It was all I thought about, no matter where I was. If I was fighting in the ring, getting gas, going out, no matter what I did, I felt an ache in my chest. I knew why it was. Eventually, I decided to give the lawyer a call. He said that she was pretty sure I was her son and that only a DNA test could prove it. We took the DNA testing. The next day it was confirmed: Marissa Santos was indeed my mother. I cannot begin to even attempt to describe what kind of feeling it is. If you want to know, then go watch a TV soap opera. Marissa was definitely excited to have me back in her life and wasted no time in telling me where I’m from. The first thing she said was that my grandparents had died of natural causes. I was Ecuadorian and in the summer immediately flew to see the families I had never known in Loja, Cuenca, and Guayaquil. It was truly a joyous moment in my life. At that point, everything else took a backseat. My wrestling career, my money, my sponsorships – all of it seemed trivial at that point. I didn’t wrestle anywhere since that day. There were more important things here to do. At last, I had found my family. I was finally at where I wanted to be in life. BORN ANEW “That is where I am now.” Syko Arsonist stood in front of the mirror in his locker room dressed in his usual wrestling attire. The locker room was the standard for all entry-level employees at PRIME, but you wouldn’t know that by looking in. The carpeting, plastering, and lighting – all of it seemed tailored for even the most grandest of suites made for presidents, czars, and various other distinguished guests. So well-kept and inhabitable were the accommodations made by PRIME for all their employees – especially this particular locker room – that the only scar was the caretaking of the individual himself. In this case, it was Syko’s own nonchalant attitude that gave the room its identity. His belongings were tossed around the room. His duffel bag lay near his feet, opened up with half the contents pouring out. Magazines and pizza boxes were scattered throughout the floor. Syko Arsonist stared at his reflection. Only his wrestling attire personified the rage that was synonymous with him. His cold, dead stare gave off little life. His once vibrant, crimson hair now lay matted down on his head with the leftover water from the shower still sliding down his face. His hair was no longer red but now black, and a bottle of hair dye in the corner of the marble sink revealed the culprit. Syko slowly picked up a pair of hair clippers on the left side of him. In complete mechanical form, he grasped a handful of hair. Snip! Those long, wavy locks fell down to the floor like petals caught in the wind. He grabbed more of his long, trademark hair. Again, he cut it. What are you doing? “What needs to be done. I am no longer Syko Arsonist. I am no longer alone. I am Juan Santos now. I was always Juan Santos. This is me. This is my life. This is my family.” Do you really think you can make a difference? “I know I can. I believe I can. I know what it is going to take. I know where this is going to lead. But I am a new man now. A new life. I have my family. This is the second chance I have waited all my life for. God gave me this chance. I can’t let him down.” Are you sure you want to start all over again? “I am not afraid. I am not afraid of what that means. I am not afraid of what could happen. This is what I want. This is what I have decided to do.” Then if that is what you want…what you have decided to do, then welcome. “It is never a safe or easy thing to start over again. It is not easy to define and redefine yourself – leaving behind all your failures and accomplishments. To wipe the slate clean, just so you can start again, is leaving the future too open for destiny. It is never easy thing to do: to let go of what little you have and start at the beginning. I am not afraid of that. I am not afraid of losing everything I have. This is what I want. I am ready.” A loud, thunderous knock on his door was followed up by one of the backstage hands urging him to hurry up. His debut match against INSERT was up next and the technicians were ready to introduce him. “I’ll be right out,” Juan yelled, as he dried his hair with his towel. Juan Santos flicked off as much of his cut hair that he could and looked at himself one last time in the mirror. He was definitely staring at a different person. He had worn the trademark long, crimson hair for so long that he himself nearly forgot who he was. It was like your sister coloring her hair all her life that she no longer remembered what her natural hair color was. Juan exited his locker room and a backstage hand was outside ready to escort him to the wrestler’s entrance. He was a middle-aged man, bald, chubby, who apparently enjoyed his job. “Big night, huh?” he asked. Juan nodded. “This guy you’re facing is real good. Yeah, he is…” the stagehand nodded. “But I hear you’re pretty good too, no?” Juan shrugged. “Depends on who you ask.” The man laughed. “Ha-ha, we haven’t seen that kind of humor here since Token Weed thought he could wrestle. I heard good things about you. We all did. What was it they called you again? Syko…” Juan Santos stopped him before he could finish. He shook his head. “Not Syko Arsonist. Juan Santos. My name is Juan Santos.” The stagehand nodded. “Right. Well then, welcome to PRIME, Mr. Juan Santos.” All around the universe and in the myriad realms, everything continues to go on without cause or concern. The earth still spins and spins around the sun, and all the residents go on without knowing that something is running amidst. But there is a small wrinkle in the sheet of serenity. There is a wrinkle in the sea of automation. Somewhere in the world, there is something wrong. It’s not following the path. Something has happened. PRIME is no longer the same. The unthinkable has happened. PRIME has accepted the services of Juan Santos - much to the dismay of an unfortunate Chris Collins. Collins, meet Juan Santos. Juan Santos, meet PRIME. PRIME, meet Juan Santos. “This is going to be more fun than a lesbian-nun orgy.”
