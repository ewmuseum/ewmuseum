The Jig Is Up

17 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 17, 2008 "Alex, love, I appreciate the heads, but do you have any idea how many blonde twenty-somethings go about getting themselves killed on a given day in Los Angeles?" It was a rare, cloudless night. Stars blinked down from above him. A beautiful, but ultimately fleeting scene that fluttered his insides like the forgotten kiss of a long-lost lover. Years ago, he had made a point of appreciating the natural beauty of Creation. The serene wonder of something so…vast and incomprehensible. Recently, though, such vows had given way to scenes of torture and blood. Thoughts of shiny trinkets and a craving for adulation that he’d forgotten existed within. "Um…point. Fine. So maybe you do have an idea. I just don’t see how this—" Tyler Rayne sighs at the interruption, his free hand scrounging pockets for a pack of smokes. The other clasps a generic cell phone to his ear, the quiet squabbling of a female voice whispering through the speaker and into the night air. He’s noticed lately that the people around him, friend or other depending on the day, flash their Blackberrys and iPhones and various other contraptions like the proud badges of status they’re meant to be. Such simplistic and gaudy showcases never much appealed to him personally, never being one to judge a person by possessions, but rather by actions. Well, that and things he owned had a habit of getting broken. Or shot. "Is that a fact?" With the question comes an immediate change in demeanor. He no longer gazes at the stars with a flippant air to his voice and bored expression on his face. His eyes are narrowed, suddenly focused and very intent. His face hardens, jaw set around a tight lipped grimace that barely lets the question escape. "El Paso. I’m not…don’t really keep track of the week to week. I’ll let you know after that. Just…keep me informed. If something comes up in Texas, then…" He runs a hand through his hair, suddenly unable to finish a coherent thought. His mind wanders elsewhere. Away from conversations about two week old corpses in LA. Away from some backwater bar in the middle of an endless stretch. Away from the handful of miles of sand that separate him from the Mexican border. Away from everything present. He drifts to a place in the past, somewhere long ago when things were simple. Before all the bullshit and the blood. Before she died in a bathtub in… "Yeah. Thanks, kid. I’ll, uh…I’ll call you after the show." He doesn’t hang up the phone. Just slowly lowers it from his ear, dropping his entire body down into the cold sand. A crunching noise from his back pocket reminds him that he didn’t quite look hard enough for that pack of cigarettes earlier. With a sigh he lifts his cheek just enough to pull the mutilated package from his pocket, picking through the damage to pull one unbroken stick from the bunch. Now if he could only find a lighter. "Need a light?" The sweet song of her voice is usually a melody he can’t resist. Something about the playful tones and sarcastic hints that has always brought a smile to his face. Except for now, in the wake of recent conversation. He turns in time to snatch the gold Zippo from the air, giving nothing so much as a nod in thanks. Lindsay Troy appears quizzical at the unprecedented lack of interest from PRIME’s 5-Star Champion. "Must’ve been one hell of a phone call, Rayne. Never thought I’d see the day you walked away from a row of shots." He tilts his head back in response, blowing smoke up toward the infinite twinkle of space. "Ever hear the myth of Earth-That-Was? When she was born, she had no sky, and was open. Inviting. And the stars would rush into her. Through the skin of her, making the oceans boil with sensation. And when she could endure no more ecstasy, she puffed up her cheeks and blew out the stars." "I don't recall learning that in English class and I’m hardly drunk enough to start falling for poetry, no matter how provocative." "It’s from Firefly. Whedon. Wrote Buffy the Vampire Slayer. Well, at least all the really good episodes. Hell of a run on Astonishing X-Men. Probably one of the… I’m totally nerding you out right now, aren’t I?" "Just a little." Smoke curls from the cigarette in his hand as he stares off toward the border. She watches him for a half second, debating whether to tell him that the nerd thing isn't so bad. That it's been awhile since she's been around someone who has a simple passion that isn't work or society-related. Even if it is comic books... "You win the game?" His tone carries no real interest. He’s asking for the sake of taking space. Stretching the moments. Stalling. They both know it. Reluctantly, almost as reluctantly as she had agreed to come out to this bar, she sits down in the sand beside him and secretly hopes no scorpions decide to take a midnight stroll. "Wasn’t much of a game left. You more or less ran the table before you left." He shrugs. Takes a pull from the cigarette. "Spend enough time in places like this, you pick up a trick or two." She turns a skeptical eye to the less-than-savory establishment in front of which they sit. "I’ll bet you have." She crosses one leg over the other and tosses her purse in her lap. Her gaze returns to him. "The last time someone booked it out of a bar that fast, it was me doing it to you." "Yeah, looks like we’re starting all kinds of bad trends. Sorry ‘bout the rush, but Alexis usually doesn’t call unless it’s important." "It's alright. I just got tired of being leered at by Pancho Villa and the Crew." He nods while the uncomfortable silence sets in again. "So, Alexis." "A friend. FBI." Lindsay laughs. "You're so formal about it. I didn’t think you’d be the type to willingly consort with Feds, Rayne, International WhateverYouAre or not." "And I didn’t think you dated wrestlers." "Yeah, well..." she breaks off then, staring out into the vast Nothing. "Ever since Melton I've made it a point not to." For the first time, he turns to her, searching her expressionless face and seeing a portrait of resolve. "That phone call...girl died in LA. Right around the time we were there for ReV. It’s not…it’s probably nothing. Just reminds me of my sister is all." "Didn't figure you had any fam-" "She died. Few years back now. For the longest time, she was all I had. Last couple years were strained. We didn’t exactly…agree on some of the choices we each made. See this scar here?" He raises some of his hair, indicating a particular furrow in the left corner of his forehead. She glances over, the cheap neon from the grimy windows falling on the wound. "Cage match. Guy by the name of Tony Studd stuck a fork in my head. Wrestled with the thing for like twenty minutes before I thought to take it out. We were, uh…well, we weren’t exactly friends, he and I. Same ass that fucked up my left knee. Brutal, brutal fights I had with this guy. Fucking loathe him. My sister ended up marrying him, of course. Not too long after that match." "Ouch. He stabbed you in the head and she got you in the heart." "Yeah. Course he retired not long after that. Promotion folded and I moved on to the next. Rena, though, she always wanted me to quit. Retire like her wonderful husband. Can’t say I didn’t think about it." "We all think about it at some point. What made you not?" "Wouldn’t know what else to do with myself, I suppose. Never been much of a man with a purpose. You might’ve noticed. I don’t…born without a rudder, guess you could say. Wind blows north, I sail north. Not much of a way to live, but it’s done me okay so far. Wrestling’s the only thing that ever seemed to make sense. Closest thing I’ve ever come to purpose anyway. What about you? What is it about wrestling that gets Lindsay Troy’s rocks off?" "Oh, you mean besides rolling around, getting sweaty with a bunch of muscle-headed degenerates?" "I didn't see you complaining last week when I was the muscle-headed degenerate." "Sort of hard to do when a wrist-cutter kicks you in the head and you take the fall." Rayne smirks and flicks the remains of the cigarette away, the both of them watching as the bright red ash tumbles end over end before splattering in a cascade of miniature fireworks on the ground. "My father died when I was nine," Troy quietly starts, resolve cracking, "my mother in childbirth six years before that. And it was presupposed that either myself or my sister would take over the business he started once we got old enough. We certainly didn't have to; there were contingencies in place if things turned out differently, but even at that young age I knew it was what he would have wanted. I flew through school, trained in martial arts with my uncle-turned-guardian as an outlet for my jumbled mess of emotions and anger and went to college close to home so I could still keep an eye on Alaina. My best friend Jaime was training to be a wrestler and one afternoon I went to watch him. I went back the next afternoon, and spent pretty much that whole weekend there. By the time I graduated college and graduated that wrestling school, I didn't want the desk job. I didn't want the CEO corner office with the picturesque view of palm trees by sunset. I wanted the road and the adventures and the sweat and I wanted it with Jaime. I had it for awhile, then I lost him. And even though I blamed myself for his accident in the ring, I never wanted to give it up. And I don't think anyone who mattered ever wanted me to." She looks over to Rayne, who had taken to looking on intently as she spoke. Her lips turn up into a small smile. "It's not much of a glamorous story, but I figure it might wind up on Oprah's Book Club list someday." "At least it'd be better than that Frey kid. Well, don’t know about you, but all this serious talk is making me a slight bit uncomfy. Figure I’d best get back to drinkin’ and looking for a fight ‘fore I really make an ass of myself. Up for another round?" "Think I might sit this one out." He stands, wiping the dust from his jeans before extending a hand down to the Universal Champion. She offers a wry expression at the gesture, but barely hesitates to take the proffered hand. "Could be interested in another game of pool, though." It wasn’t something he necessarily meant to do. Or even really thought much about. He was just trying to be a nice guy. Offered to help a girl to her feet. Something happens in the interim, distances are misjudged and…here we are. PRIME’s Universal and 5-Star champions standing less than three inches from each other. Her hand never really quite leaves his. So they stand, staring into the other’s eyes for what feels like an eternity. They’d be breathing on each other if either of them were actually bothering to breathe at all. He moves a little closer. It would almost be comical to watch pushing up from the toes to cover the two inch difference between them. If there were anyone there to watch. If either of them could have noticed a damn other thing in all the world than… Styx's "Renegade" blaring out of her pocket. "Your phone’s ringing." "I noticed." Neither of them have moved. Still there, in that singular moment, waiting for one or the other to…back away. With an accepting, though obviously displeased, smile, he takes a step back, making quick moves to get around her and back into the bar. "You should probably answer it." "It can wait." He shakes his head, hand already on the door. "Don’t worry about it. I’ll be at the bar." And then he’s in again. And Lindsay Troy is alone in the middle of nowhere, with nothing but the stars and a purposely annoying ringtone to keep her company. With a heavy sigh she throws herself against the side of the building, amazed that it doesn't crumble to mildewed pieces, and lifts the Blackberry Pearl to her ear. "Hey Ferg…"
