Fight or Flight (ReVolution 150)

16 January 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 16, 2008 She's rose-kissed cheeks and giraffe-like legs, tanned and never-ending, cruising upwards along what he privately called his Miracle Mile. Curls coiled like tight, perfect spirals thrown by MVP quarterbacks, flowing over bare skin and covering tattoos few knew existed. Crimson red and blackest night form a silk sheath around her toned body, the tops of her breasts visible, not hidden under merchandising cotton like so many times before. For a man so used to communicating through guttural roars in bar fights and silver-tongued quips meant to either annoy or enchant, Tyler Rayne never imagined he could think such poetry. But when he saw Lindsay Troy walk across the bar toward him, wearing that dress, wearing those shoes, looking that good, the language he knew left him in an instant. "Hey," she said, pearls flashing from behind her lips. "I figured you could tell Nitz the next time you see him that I managed to not soil my dress." "Um...damn. Yeah. Wish, uh...wish I could say the...same." "You look like you've never seen a woman in a dress before," she laughed and slid into the seat across from him. "It's, uh...it's been awhile. Most of 'em just come in mini skirts." "They must not be able to afford the rest of the fabric." Rayne chuckled, mind wandering momentarily to a pre-coitus moment where that dress would be in tatters at his feet. "So, you get all dolled up just for me?" "Well, we are in Vegas. And, I wanted to disprove the popular belief that I don't know how to clean up." "Well, I always assumed, but," he looks down at his own clothes, "kinda startin' to feel outclassed here." "Don't be a sour puss," Lindsay chided with a smirk. "Besides, I figured the leather jacket and blue jeans was your black-tie attire anyway." Rayne shrugged, that infamous smile pulling the corners of his mouth. "Beautiful woman like you hangin' about, I don't suppose I've got much to be sour on at all. But, as you so astutely pointed out...we're in Vegas. What's the Queen want to do first?" She feigned deep thought before leaning forward, eyes playfully twinkling. "Beer me." ~**~**~**~**~ "Might as well drink motor oil, Rayne. Viscosity's about the same, and I imagine the taste isn't too far off." Tyler looked down at his fourth glass of Guinness and laughed. "I've had three of these already and now you choose to talk shit?" "Need to keep you on your toes," she retorted, lowering a glass of Sam Adams from her ruby lips. "You probably figured I'd say something from the get-go." "Your restraint is noteworthy and remarkable." A nonchalant shrug. "I do what I can." "Motor oil's more tart. Anyway," Tyler continued, not allowing her the liberty to question how he knew that. "Thanks for being a sport tonight. Not that I figure either of us really needs to shill for votes, but sometimes, it's nice to have some fun, ya know?" "Mmhmm. Although, I derive a great deal of happiness from caving Sonny's face in. I can think of few things more fun that that." "I have a couple suggestions," that smoldering smile acknowledged while his body lifted from the chair. "One being cleaning this place out at the tables." "And the others?" He leaned in close, hand coming to lightly rest on her knee. "My restraint is also noteworthy and remarkable." A squeeze that sent jolts down her spine. "Finish your beer and I'll be back." With a gait perfected by years of cocky swagger, Rayne exited the bar in search of chips. Troy watched him go and with an air of relief, exhaled the breath she hadn't realized she'd been holding in. Her hand came to rest over her heart, the affect of his touch producing a more profound reaction than she wanted to happen. "I can't be doing this again," she murmured, but her eyes found him roaming through the casino's crowd and she couldn't stop a shy smile from forming. "Always with the bad boys." Sam Adams was taken to hand again, drowning the shyness and desire back to the pit of her stomach. Troy closed her eyes as the liquid coated her throat and temporarily sated the need. She checked her phone, and was in the middle of texting her sister when a rowdy bunch rocketed into the area, strawberry-haired women stumbling and laughing, holding onto their man for dear life. "Hey! HEY! BARTENDER! The girls need to tell ya' somethin'." "We need some red-headed sluts for some red-headed sluts!" Raucous laughter while the women pawed the blonde-haired man. Lindsay felt her phone slipping from her fingers but, as the world slowed to barely a crawl, she could not move fast enough to prevent its plummet. Voices became distorted; people became blacked out with the background as her hazel eyes tunneled solely to that smooth-talking, baby-blued, cornerstone of her past. And when he turned his head to the right, he felt his universe crumble to ashes. He saw her grab her purse, hop unceremoniously off her perch and fly unheeded out of the bar. The women attached to his hips indignantly cursed as he pushed them out of his way and took flight as well. Because, for Joey Melton, his need for his ex-wife would never be quenched.
