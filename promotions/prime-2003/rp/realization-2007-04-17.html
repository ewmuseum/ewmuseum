Realization

17 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/17/2007 The team of Stewart and Dusk seem moments away from putting Lavelle down. GBJ is out on the apron, trying to make his way back up to a vertical base. Stewart yanks Lavelle up to a standing position and clubs him in the back. Dusk gets back up to a vertical base behind Pierce Lavelle. Dusk comes around to the front, delivers a knife edge chop, and a kick to the midsection against the helpless Lavelle. GBJ meanwhile is up to a semi-standing position on the apron of the ring. Shawn Stewart is holding Lavelle around the ribcage from behind, Dusk runs forward and attempts to nail the Lights Out, but Lavelle drops his head down, thus having the kick sail over his head and into the chin of Shawn Stewart. Stewart smacks the canvas, while Lavelle gives Dusk a low blow that sends him down into the ground. Lavelle goes over to Stewart and drops down for the cover! ONEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEE TWOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOO Dusk attempts to make a last minute save, but GBJ comes springing at him from off the top rope and nails him with a top rope teabag that sends Dusk down to the canvas as well. THREEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEE DING! DING! DING! Again, the tape squealed as it rewound. Shawn sat on the couch, staring at the television with the remote in his hand. He started at the screen, watching over and over again as the recording showed him catching a full-out standing sidekick to the jaw. Morning sunlight streamed through the windows. Nicole was off teaching her ballet class downstairs and there were no gym patrons right now. Well, Shawn could nitpick and say it wasn't actually a sidekick. It's more of a lunge kick that happens to bear resemblance to a sidekick. Sidekicks had a chambering motion, an extension to the point of impact. No, instead, most superkicks were a skip and a leg lift, combining the two forces of the forward momentum with the upward momentum of the foot. Shawn made the mental remark again that if he hadn't been doing something stupid like holding an opponent, he wouldn't have been in position to get kicked by his own partner. Of course, there's more to this situation than meets the eye. Shawn knew that he was also privy to some information that fed into the situation. Doing a proper sidekick, which he had done himself many times, did not require having an entire ring to wind up, and it also didn't travel much past the intended point of impact with any real force. In other words, he probably could have eaten his own sidekick without being knocked silly. He remembered laying on the mat and trying to kick his way out, but his body was too sluggish in responding. Shawn leaned back on the couch and turned off the VCR. Why did he still have one instead of having a DVR like everyone else? Just hadn't gotten around to spending the money on it. He let his hands rest on his knees and leaned his head back, taking a deep breath. Yeah, he definitely had a score to settle. Was it fair to blame the whole affair on Dusk? Was it fair for him to blame Dusk for learning to throw a superkick the way he did? Shawn had to be honest with himself: he didn't give a damn. This losing streak he was on was really grating on his nerves. He had made a promise to himself when he agreed to come back to PRIME that he was going to make enough to finally get a house for Nicole, to give her those things in life she always wanted. And he couldn't because he kept choking in even the most basic of matches and hadn't gotten a win since he returned. And honestly, for a man that had tried to make his name off of a Pursuit of Excellence, he was a failure. He really wanted to scream, to cry, to do something that could change the outcome of the coming week, but he couldn't think of anything other than what he always did: train and then rest up the day or so before the match. Something cold touched his hand. Shawn almost jerked away from it, but he he very calmly turned his eyes towards his right hand to see Samson there, looking up at him, his big bulldog chin sitting on Shawn's hand. Samson huffed, his saggy jowls blowing out and looking up at Shawn with big, sad bulldog eyes. Shawn pulled his hand out from under the dog's chin and scratched at his ears. It was... different having Samson around. Not bad, but different. It's not like he was some yippy dog that would interject himself into what ever was going on, but it was more he complimented Shawn and Nicole. They could be on the couch, watching a movie and Samson would just walk up and sit down at their feet. And, as all dogs do, he didn't care if you had a bad day, if you were upset, he still wanted to see you. Shawn smiled to himself as he patted the big, broad head. It actually calmed him down, too. What to do about Dusk... Samson, seeming to sense that Shawn's mind was elsewhere, plopped down on the floor and laid his chin on Shawn's foot. It was obvious that Dusk relied heavily on that superkick. When you have one move to put someone away, you go to it continuously and tend to like it quite a bit. Shawn could deal with that, especially given that a superkick didn't have the explosive nature that a real sidekick did. There were points to intercept it, and it didn't travel in a straight line towards someone's face. Actually, with it traveling upward, Shawn could hook the foot and turn it over mid-move... The VCR clicked off and the television screen flickered to a WLEX 18 special news bulletin. Virginia Tech was caught in the midst of a shooting, being one of the worst the country had ever seen. Shawn leaned forward, his mouth agape as he watched. The body count was at the high twenties now, with wounded not even tallied. There's that little part of Shawn's brain that never quit being a police officer. It happened at Thanksgiving, it's what got him into trouble with Nick Brandish and here, it kicked in again. His mind immediately went to securing the campus, locking buildings to prevent people from getting into the line of fire and to either keep the gunman out or to contain him. Definitely a him. Women, historically, didn't really go into these kinds of mass murders. Serial killing, yes, but not as much as men, and this was confirmed now as a man. Unconsciously, Shawn's hand drifted towards his side, where he had worn that service pistol so many years ago. Sure, he was still a good shot, but it's strange what comes back when the brain is running. As the coverage continued, Shawn's mouth worked soundlessly, his mind working through possibilities over and over again. Finding a mass murderer in a building would be one of the most intense experiences anyone could ever have. Someone with no compunctions about killing that is loose, somewhere unknown, with the tools to do just that. In reality, it was more frightening than facing the biggest monster in the ring. This situation could see you dead with a round in the chest or head before you knew the man was there. And then, it ended the way Shawn had expected: suicide by the gunman. Shawn leaned back on the couch, shivering with adrenaline that coursed through his body. He couldn't not think about the situation. It was something that had haunted his dreams while he wore the uniform. To make matters worse, this man seemed to have chained people in the building so they couldn't escape. He was too calm; he knew what he was doing and how he was going about it. And then the news of the university's notification about what had happened was appalling. Sending out emails?! Emails two hours after the fact? Not warning people not to leave buildings because they thought he had fled the campus? Shawn's mind went from numbness to white-hot anger. This was absurd! If someone was loose on a mass murder spree, you get the word out any way possible. The gunman wouldn't stop if people knew, he'd just continue searching for victims. And he did, armed with a pair of handguns. Samson nudged at his leg. He looked down to see those big, sad eyes again. Shawn took a deep breath and scratched the dog's ears again. And his mind shifted gears again, calming and refocusing. There was nothing he could do here. It was already too late and placing blame wouldn't help. And his own concerns... while they were still important, they seemed less all-encompassing. Sure, he was on a losing streak. Yes, he was breaking promises he made to himself. But the real concern today was for the people that lost loved ones in a senseless tragedy. Shawn patted his dog's head, realizing that he was fine where he was... and while things were frustrating, his life was not that bad.
