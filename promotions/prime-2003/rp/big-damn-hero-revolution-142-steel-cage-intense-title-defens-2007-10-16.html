Big Damn Hero (ReVolution 142, Steel Cage Intense Title defense vs. Devin Shakur)

16 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 16th, 2007 “Well…guess this is the place…” As usual, PRIME’s Southern Sparkplug, Wade Elliott, sat in his black Ford F-150, hands gripped on the steering wheel. What was unusual was that he was in a parking lot, his truck idling outside a big brick building. A large, eloquent sign reading “Radisson” loomed above him as he squinted his piercing blue eyes, a half-smoked Marlboro cigarette dangling out of his mouth. A bark came from the passenger’s seat. “No, I ain’t callin’ Lindsay!” Wade snapped in reply to his trusty sidekick, Angus the Bluetick hound. “I don’t need her to tell me how to git where I’m goin’…” Wade looked over his shoulder, staring out at the city of Providence, Rhode Island, lights burning into the night in all their urban wonder. “…even if I am in yuppie-ville…” Angus whined. “Apologize?” said Wade, taken aback. “Horse-shit! If anythin’ she’s gonna be apologizin’ to me. I weren’t the one tellin’ her she couldn’t beat somebody or stopped her from fightin’ when she had to!” Angus tilted his head to the side. Wade released a heavy sigh. “I know. She weren’t neither.” Wade finished his cigarette with a hefty inhale and pitched the butt out the window. He streamed smoke through his thick brown goatee before making a point to roll the driver’s side window down all the way. “You wait here, boy,” said Wade to his dog, scratching him behind the ears. The ‘Bama Bruiser killed the engine, stuffed his keys into his pocket and hopped out into the parking lot, slamming the door shut before marching through the brisk October air toward the entrance to the Radisson. * * * * * * * Wade took uneasy steps onto the gleaming white floor of the hotel. He drew his eyes across his surroundings. To his left: a large mahogany coffee table in the middle of what Wade understood to be a “sitting room,” filled with large leather recliners and a magnificent stone fire-place. To his right: a long, also mahogany, dinner table, bordered by elaborate dark wooden chairs. To his front: a brass trimmed stone counter with an attractive and wide-smiled young woman in a red dress with a nametag reading “Whitney” standing behind it. “Welcome to the Radisson!” chipped the young brunette. “How may I help you?” “Yeah…” mumbled Wade, stepping up to the counter. “I’m needin’ a room fer the night, first floor facin’ the parkin’ lot if ya can.” “Absolutely! Name?” “Elliott. Wade.” “How long will you be staying with us?” “…the night.” “Sure thing! Will you be in need of parking?” “Yup.” “Breakfast in the morning?” “Sure as shit.” She blinked. “Excuse me?” Wade cleared his throat. “Um…yes.” “Okay!” She tapped her shiny fingernails into her keyboard. Wade leaned and elbow onto the countertop. “Say miss…” he started. “Ya’ll allow animals in this place? Dogs an’ the like?” “Absolutely not,” she replied, eyes on her computer screen. “Why do you ask?” “Curious is all.” A moment later she turned her bright smile back to the Blue Collar Brawler. “Alright! Your total is one-hundred and forty-one dollars.” Wade nodded and dug into his pocket, retrieving his beat-up brown leather wallet and unfolding it to reveal lots and lots of green American currency. Whitney’s eyes bulged as Wade flopped two one-hundred dollar bills on the counter. “Okay…one-forty-one out of two-hundred…” She popped open the drawer and retrieved a fifty dollar bill, a five and four ones. “Here you go Mr. Elliott…fifty-nine dollars is your change…and here’s your room key.” She handed him a white piece of plastic resembling a credit card. Wade gave it a funny look before raising an eyebrow to Whitney. “This is the room key?” “Yup! Your room number is thirty-two.” Wade looked around the hotel before offering a look of confusion. Whitney pointed a finger to his left. “Down the hall on your right,” she said. Wade tipped his hat. “Thank ya kindly.” He thudded off to his left, stuffing his wallet back into his jeans. “Oh, and Mr. Elliott?” Wade turned. “It might not be a bad idea to get a credit card, just to keep your money safe.” “Thanks fer the tip, darlin’…” He continued down the hall. “…but I don’t trust the things.” * * * * * * * After much fiddling with the door’s card lock, the Drifter stumbled into his room. He glared at the white key card before shoving it angrily into his pocket. Judging eyes fell on a pristine queen-sized bed and mahogany desk. He scowled at brass-trimmed pictures of flowers and landscapes. He raised an eyebrow to the blinding white personal bathroom. He strode to one of the room’s two windows. He could see his truck sitting in the parking lot, no sign of Angus in the windshield. He spun the lever that pushed the window open. Wade let out a sharp whistle. Angus’s head popped up over the dashboard. Swiftly and silently the Bluetick clambered out of the driver’s side window and onto the asphalt. He trotted happily to the window jumped high, his front legs dangling over the windowsill until Wade hoisted him over and into the room. Angus’s tongue flopped around in joy as he explored his new environment, eventually becoming content with a red cushy chair. Wade ruffled his dog’s neck. The ‘Bama Bruiser then turned back to the window, pulling his key-chain out of his pocket and pressing a button on a tiny black remote. He grinned as his truck window rolled itself up. Angus barked. “Shhhhhh!” sounded Wade, turning to the Bluetick and pressing his finger against his lips. “Can’t let em’ know yer in here, pup!” Angus panted. “Good boy.” * * * * * * * Wade jerked out of a deep sleep to the sounds of muffled cacophony. He blinked his eyes a few times in attempt to orient himself. He had only removed his shirt before falling unconscious onto the top of his bed; jeans and boots still on his long legs. He sat up and rubbed his tired face. Once oriented, he focused on the noise coming from outside his room. “mufflemufflemuffle FUCK YOU mufflemufflemuffle WHORE mufflemufflemuffle.” “Hmmm,” Wade pondered. “Could be an argument is all.” But he perked at the dull THUD of what could only be body-on-wall. “But that’s soundin’ like a domestic dispute.” Wade pushed himself to his feet and retrieved his dirty white t-shirt, pulling it over his head. Angus’s head popped off the chair cushion. Wade knelt down next to the pup. “Ain’t nothin’, pup,” he said, scratching him behind the ears. “Just gonna go see if we got a problem.” Elliott donned his drifter’s hat, smiling at his canine companion. “Time to be a big damn hero.” * * * * * * * The Southern Sparkplug took cautious steps down the hallway, the muffled shouting drawing near. He tilted his ear as if to increase his hearing, and soon found himself stepping toward room twenty-six. Another THUD. More muffled yelling. Wade narrowed his eyes. He ever-so-quietly stepped next to the door, pressing his ear against it. “Fucking stupid whore! Who do you think you are!?” THUD. “Jerry, stop it!” Wade rapped his hard knuckles against the door. A pause in the argument. “Who the fuck is that?” came a nasty shout. “Um…house-keeping,” replied Wade in his least gruff and gravely voice possible, which was, in truth, rather unsuccessful. The door pushed open, stopping suddenly thanks to a chain lock. “Bullshit,” snarled the man behind the door, only one wild brown eye visible through the crack. Wade could smell the booze on his breath. “Howdy,” said Wade, tipping his hat. “Get the fuck out of here,” snapped the man, slamming the door shut, but not before Wade was able to slip four digits between the door and the door-jamb. The door slammed against his fingers hard. Wade groaned, curling his lips to suppress the pain. “That weren’t too polite,” he growled. He took grip of the door and pull hard, nearly tearing the chain lock from the wall, creating enough room to reach his arm inside and unlock the chain himself. He swung the door open, much to the man inside’s dismay. “I think that fancy Bible book says somethin’ ‘bout ‘honorin’ yer neighbor.’” “This isn’t your business, buddy!” snapped the shirtless drunkard, attempting to puff out his hairier-than-average chest. Wade looked around the trashed room; broken lamp, over-turned desk, empty bottle of gin, but more importantly, a terrified blonde-haired woman cowering in the far corner. Wade’s face narrowed in anger. “It is now.” * * * * * * * Through the front doors of the Radisson flew Jerry the drunk, slamming against the pavement and rolling to a stop, a blood splattered nose and multiple bruises on his face. The rugged frame of Wade Elliott followed, spitting on the ground, wiping off his hands for a job well done. Wade turned to see an old, bearded night janitor staring at him, obviously shocked and enthralled with the spectacle. “He’s just checkin’ out,” offered The Big Dog before moseying back toward his room. * * * * * * * “He was a tall man, rugged looking. He had a sort of cowboy-ish hat on, and a southern accent. Strong, too, he almost tore the door off its hinges!” Victoria Reeves wrapped a green blanket around her at a Providence police station, a hot cup of coffee sitting on a desk in front of her. A hefty police officer by name of Douglas sat across from her, scribbling notes. “And you didn’t get a name?” asked the officer. “No. He just busted in, hit Jerry in the face a few times and dragged him away. I wish I did, though! That man’s my hero!” “Hmmm,” mumbled Officer Douglas. “Is there any more information you can give us?” “I don’t think so, he left pretty quickly. I think I heard a dog walking with him…but I was probably just hearing things.” Douglas scribbled down one last note before closing his booklet. “Alright, I think you’re free to go, Ms. Reeves. We’ll have an officer waiting out front to escort you anywhere you need to go.” “Thank you. Very much.” Victoria stood and turned to leave. “Oh!” she exclaimed, spinning around. She grabbed a pen and a quickly started to write something on a sticky-note pad. “If you ever find this man, could you give him this?” She handed the pad to Douglas who squinted at her writing. “Your phone number?” he questioned. Victoria smiled. * * * * * * * “Don’t know what I was thinkin’, Angus.” The Bluetick panted happily as Wade pitched a cigarette butt out the window. He took in the view of Providence, Rhode Island through the windshield of his F-150, safely parked in a road-side rest area. He sighed, reclining his seat and retrieving a gray wool blanket from the back. “Savage place, this urban jungle,” he said, throwing the blanket over his body. Angus yipped. “’Course I did em’ a favor! Them yuppies need a good ass kickin’ from time to time.” He rested his hands behind his head, staring at the roof of the truck while Angus curled into a ball. “Them hotels ain’t my type of place anyhow, a li’l too fanciful for ol’ Wade.” The Big Dog turned onto his side, reaching over to Angus to rub his belly. “Sides,” he yawned. “I feel better gittin’ my sleep somewhere safe.” He closed his eyes, drifting off to dreamland as the heavy traffic of Providence whipped by. “Night, Angus.” Angus barked.
