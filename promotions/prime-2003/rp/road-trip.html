Road Trip

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Week toward Revolution 72 Garbage, that match was garbage. I had that match won, and then in a fleeting second had it taken away from me. To make matters worse that bitch not only cost me a victory in the singles match, but she blew it for me in the battle royal to. Not the way I wanted to start, and I cannot continue this way if I wish to make my country proud. These are the thoughts that are running through Walczak’s head. He is fresh off of his two losses at the most recent PRIME Revolution and he does not seem to be too happy about it. A look of mixed anger and disappointment is visible on Walczak’s face as he makes his way towards his newly acquired New York home. The look is accompanied by cuts and bruises delivered by Karina Wolfeden in their recent match. Walczak walks into his home and turns on the lights he mumbles under his breath as he goes into the bathroom to tend to his wounds. I can’t believe this. I go out there in an effort to make my country proud, and I come out a complete failure. All those stupid fans kept yelling KARINA-KARINA-KARINA. That’s all that I heard throughout the match, and there was nothing that could make me angrier. They kept yearning for their American hero. They hoped that she would succeed, that she would lead their country to victory. They treated her as if she was a national treasure, and had she not won they would have rewarded her with cheers as she walked away. Did one person look at how I completely dominated that match? No. Did they realize that their “American Hero” won by a fluke? No. These people believe that no matter what their kind do they are infallible they can do no wrong. There are so many American Icons, but in this country they accept almost no one outside of this country. Walczak washes his face cleaning up most of the dry blood. He looks into the mirror and shakes his head in disgust. I should really try and find out why these people believe that this country is so great. I mean these people can’t just be proud of nothing. There must be some sort of reason for their arrogance, and in order to find out what this is I should really get to know this country. That’s exactly what I’ll do. Instead of just flying over to Salt Lake City I’ll drive over, and stop over at some so-called great American landmarks. I’ll try and grasp what American pride is all about. Walczak takes a shower, and then heads to bed hoping to get an early start on his trip through America tomorrow. Day 1 4:30 am Walczak’s Apartment Walczak wakes up in the early morning and is eagerly awaiting the road trip that awaits him. He wakes up and goes through his daily routine that consists of a breakfast of fifteen egg-whites and a side of kielbasa. After breakfast he gathers his gym bag full of wrestling gear and clothes and heads out the door to embark on his journey of the United States. I can’t wait to see what this country is really made of. I want to see the pathetic people that inhabit this nation as well as the idols that they worship. Poland is a country of great beauty and history. It has been around longer than the almighty USA, and its people are prouder of their heritage. I am proud to be Polish, and I am out to prove that these people do not have the right to be proud of their American culture. Walczak heads into his car and starts to drive away. Day 1 6:00 am Ellis Island Walczak’s first stop does not take him too far away from his home. His first stop is none other than the Statue of Liberty. This is the American symbol for freedom and liberty. Maybe Walczak will see why Americans are so proud when he sees Lady Liberty standing tall and welcoming all who come to her land. Lady Liberty. Quite an amazing ideal for a fledgling country. To think how the tides have turned. This statue a gift from France, a country once revered by those in America, but now the country and its people are a source of jokes for all the American people. France is no longer revered. No, it is now looked down upon and mocked by these people. Without the French people this country would not be. All Americans look at this statue and see a symbol for their country, but most of them do not know what this statue means, and what it stands for. This is a symbol for freedom, but not for the American people. It is a symbol of freedom for those like me who come to this country looking for a new life. Walczak hears a ruckus not too far away from where he is standing and he decides to go and see what it is. There are a large number of people gathered in a group. They are holding pickets and screaming with vigor. Hoping to see what these people are passionate about Walczak makes his way over to the group. “Hey what are you guys rallying around?” “We’re trying to get congress to pass a bill.” “What bill is that?” “All the people here feel that there are too many immigrants in this country, and we want congress to put a quota on how many can be let into this country.” “TOO MANY IMMIGRANTS!!!!” “Yeah they are taking all our jobs for lesser wages. They are putting us out of work, and they are overpopulating this country.” “Maybe if the people of this country got off of their fat asses than they wouldn’t be taking your jobs. These people would give up their lives for a chance to make it into this country, and you are just scared that they will make it.” “Well I see that you don’t really agree with our views.” “Of course not. I am an immigrant, and it is because of people like you that I have had a hard time making it in this country. It is because of people like you that I hate this country. I wasn’t given a chance by anyone in this country. I had to make my own way, and even now when I am more successful than 90% of you lazy asses I am still looked down upon.” “Well than why the hell aren’t you back in your country? People like you always complain about the USA, but for some reason you always stay.” “I wish I could go back. In fact if I could go back I would, but I can’t. I can’t go back until I show the people of the USA that my country, Poland, is better than the USA.” “HA! Good luck with that one. No matter how dumb you think these people are they don’t match up to the geniuses that you have in Poland.” “Another common misconception that you people have. Poland has a 97% literacy rate, and it graduated a higher percentage of its children from college than the USA could ever hope for. The most amazing thing about this is that we are only two decades removed from the communistic oppression of our country. In a few years Poland will once again rise to power. POLSKA GURA.” The man starts to laugh out loud at Walczak. He tells some of his colleagues what Walczak has just said and they join in the laughter. “Do you realize that this country was founded by immigrants? This society was set up by people from other countries. Many of this countries founding fathers were from other countries. Your nick name was the “Melting Pot” for Christ’s sake. This country has no culture of its own it relies on the culture of other countries, but you will never admit that will you?” The man has no answer for this, and instead of digging himself into a deeper argument with Walczak he just picks up his picket, and goes over to the rally. These people want to put a stop to bringing in the people that keep this country going. Without us this country would fall behind the rest of the world. Immigrants work harder for this country than the so-called Americans do. Those men that I saw rallying did not want to end immigration. They wanted to feel superior to someone, and that someone is the immigrants. They feel that they should be part of an exclusive club. A club that should not be opened to those not of this country. Pathetic. Walczak gets back into his car and heads over to his next destination. Day 1 12:30 pm Old City Philadelphia After a couple of pit stops Walczak makes it to his next destination. He stops in Philadelphia. “The City of Brotherly Love”. Here he hopes to get a little more knowledge on the history of the USA. Walczak parks his car in a Parking garage, and heads out to view the city. Philadelphia, what a trash heap. You can sense that this city feels inferior to New York, and therefore not even worth my time. However, to gain a sense of what this country is about I will go visit one of its most treasured historical pieces, the Liberty Bell. The name itself shows that it is not fit for America. Walczak does indeed make his way towards the Liberty Bell. He makes it to Market Street the new home of the liberty bell, and heads off to see it. When he goes inside the bell is surrounded by hoards of people. The line is immense, and Walczak is dumbfounded as to why a bell brings so many people to see it. Walczak hears one of the workers talking about the bell, and hopes to get some information. “The bell was ordered to celebrate the 50th anniversary of the constitution of Pennsylvania. However, when the bell was delivered it was cracked on its first ringing. After a couple of remakes the bell continued to be cracked, and no reason could be found for this.” “So a symbol of American culture is failure?” The worker as well as many of the people inside are taken aback by Walczak’s statement. “Umm… Well, the bell is not a symbol of failure. The name itself shows that it is a symbol of liberty, and it stands for freedom as well.” “How?” “Well the bell was sounded when the Declaration of Independence was signed. This is what the bell is most well known for, and that is why it is a symbol of freedom.” “So even though the bell had been a failure through its entire existence it has one great moment, and all of a sudden it is exalted. What a typical American response.” “I don’t understand what you’re saying. This bell signifies a great moment in America’s struggle for independence. Why should it not be exalted?” “Strife? What Strife? This country does not face strife. It is the exact opposite. They make strife for other people.” “Sir you are disrespecting this country, and you are offending many of the people that are here, so if you could do us all a favor and leave that would be great.” “Yeah, no problem.” Walczak turns around and leaves the exhibit. He heads to his car and drives away leaving the city of Philadelphia behind. Day 1 7:00 pm Suburb of Washington D.C. Walczak pulls into the parking lot of a Holiday Inn outside of the Washington D.C. Metro area. He takes the gym bag out of his car, and heads into the hotel. There is a young lady waiting at the front desk and Walczak approaches her. “Yeah can I get a room?” “Sure, we just need to get some information from you, and how many nights do you think that you will be staying?” “I’m just going to be staying one night.” “Ok, well we just need a copy of your driver’s license, and how will you be paying for your room?” “Cash.” “The total is $115.” “Here you go.” “Thank you, and have a great stay.” Walczak walks away without saying another word and heads towards his room. That was one of the few times that a person of this country has been nice to me. And what did it take? Money. The almighty dollar. That’s what drives these people. She didn’t give a damn if I have a good time or not as long as she gets paid for it. That’s why this country is worthless it has no moral value. Walczak heads up towards his room hoping to spend much of his day tomorrow walking through the nation’s capital. Day 2 9:30 am Washington D.C. Walczak is ready to walk around the capital city of the USA after a good nights sleep. He plans to make his first stop at the White House. The home to the man that many people consider the most powerful person in the world. I wonder what it would be like to be the leader of a corrupt country? What kind of man can wake up in the morning and look at himself in the mirror knowing that he runs a country full of such disgraceful people? I could never bring myself to run a country that is so arrogant, so full with greed, so demonic in nature. This country is apathetic to those really in need. All that it cares about are those that inhabit its borders, and they could give a damn what happens to the rest of us. Walczak finally gets into the White House hoping to take a tour of the President’s Residence. He is softly singing the words to the Polish National Anthem as he waits to get into the White House. However, as he enters the tour area he gets pulled over to the side by security. “Sir can you please come over here?” “Why what did I do?” “Nothing we just want to check you to make sure you’re not carrying any weapons or any other banned substances.” “Why can’t I just go through the metal detectors just like everyone else?” “Well one of the people in the line said that you looked suspicious so we just don’t want to take any chances.” “Did this person give a reason as to why I was suspicious?” “Well, they said that you were speaking a language that sounded similar to Arabic, and that you constantly checking your phone in a nervous way.” “Well I was singing in Polish, and that’s a Slavic language nothing close to the language of the Arabs. Also I was checking my phone to see if there was any news on the Wrestling Show I am going to wrestle in next Wednesday.” “Well either way we have to check you just to put the peoples mind at ease.” “This is just what I hate about this country. I speak a different tongue, and all of a sudden the most normal of actions such as checking my phone becomes suspicious.” “Whatever your beliefs are we are still going to have to search you, or you will have to leave.” “You can do all the searching you want because I have nothing to hide.” The men search Walczak and indeed find nothing. He is allowed to cut a head of the line, and finally goes in to look around the White House. He opts not to take a tour and walks the house at his own pace. He moves quickly through the tour area only taking a couple of moments in each area. Such an extravagant house for a person who is running a country. A person in this sort of position should not be allowed to have such extravagance. He should focus on making the decisions that help his country, but how can he do this when this home offers so many distractions. A couple behind Walczak is also looking through the room, and he over hears a conversation that they are having about the President. “Can you believe that he is taking five weeks off?” “I know. I always thought that running the country is a full time job. I didn’t think that they president would get this much time off.” “I know, we could barely scrape together two weeks to leave the restaurant, and I still think it might fall to pieces with us not there.” The leader of this country is taking five weeks off!!! That’s ridiculous. This man is responsible for the lives of 250 million people, and he thinks that he can afford himself the luxury of taking five weeks off. Even the man that runs this country is a lazy piece of shit. That’s all that I needed to know. This man is exalted and put on a pedestal to run this country in a mansion that he doesn’t need, and then he takes five weeks off. I can’t even put words together to describe how wrong this is. Walczak makes his way out of the White House and off to visit other parts of the capital city. Day 2 12:36 pm The Lincoln Memorial Walczak’s second stop is the Lincoln Memorial. He makes his way up the stairs and looks at the behemoth statue that stands before him. Again exalting their leaders. This man was the leader of this country during a time of oppression. He freed the slaves and is forever cast into the memory of the American people. However, no one takes into effect that this man only freed the slaves to invigorate the spirit of the North before an inevitable war with the south. Also the USA was one of the last countries to ban slavery, and they didn’t end it until nearly twenty five years after the last European country did so. Walczak walks up to the massive statue of Lincoln sitting down and looking over the Washington D.C. landscape. People surround the statue and look up with awe. As Walczak is looking at the statue someone bumps into him and causes Walczak to lose his focus. “Oh, sorry about that.” “You should keep your eyes open more.” “Yeah, I’m just amazed by this statue.” “What’s so amazing about it?” “Well, it’s a symbol of America’s greatest ever President.” “Why does that make it amazing, and what did he do that makes him so special?” “He ended the era of slavery that should be enough to be remembered for.” “You just have to edit that sentence a little bit.” “How?” “Well, he just ended slavery in America. Slavery had been put to rest in other parts of the world for decades already.” “Well, I just mean that the decision he brought meant a lot to this country.” “Once again my point is proven. All you care about is this country.” Walczak walks away with a smug look on his face. He seems to be proving his point of arrogance of the American people quite well, and he couldn’t be happier. Day 2 3:07 pm Washington Monument Walczak decides that his last stop would be the Washington Monument. A monument to the first President of the United States. He wants to see why this man was picked to run a fledgling country. Walczak sees someone who looks very studious, and immediately engages the man in conversation. “Excuse me.” “Yes may I help you?” “Well, I just wanted to know why George Washington was picked to be the first person to run this country.” “Well in my opinion it was the obvious choice. Apart from being a well educated man he was also a War hero.” “How was he a war hero?” “Well, he was the biggest factor in the United States Revolution. The war fought to free the United States from the British.” “Are you sure that he was the biggest factor? Because in all of the history books that I have read they won because of the help that they received from foreign countries such as France and Poland. I believe that without the likes of Kazmierz Pulaski and Tadeusz Kosciusko this country may still have been under the colonial rule of the British.” “I think that you are taking this a little bit too far. While both those men did greatly aid the colonies in their quest for freedom I do not believe that they were the sole reason for the colonial victory.” “Well, than how can you call Washington the greatest Revolutionary hero? I believe that Pulaski and Kosciusko were of much greater importance to the States. Why is there no monument of them in the Nations capital? Without the Polish government’s help you would not have the same freedoms that you so much cherish today, but you give no thanks to the Polish people.” “There are many monuments in the name of the two generals. We definitely show our thanks to the two war heroes.” “What about their descendants? The Polish people. They are looked down upon and mocked by this country. There are jokes that are made of them, and they are not given the same opportunities as other inhabitants of this country. Do you think that this is fair?” “Well I have never heard of this, and I would certainly not condone it.” “You are one of the few.” With this Walczak turns and walks away. He heads towards his car and hopes to make at least some headway to his final destination of Salt Lake City. Day 2 9:15 pm Holiday Inn Virginia These people really have no idea what their country stands for. They think that their country just like their people is without fault. They take all the wrongs that their country has done and they turn then into acts of greatness. I am amazed that the leaders of this country can get all their people to follow them so blindly. They believe everything that is written and told to them. They are too lazy to do any research on their own, and this is the downfall of this country. It does not matter anymore I cannot change this country in a day and I must get some sleep if I want to make it into my final destination of Salt Lake City. Day 3 5:00 am Holiday Inn Virginia Walczak gets up and gathers his things together. He heads out today hoping to get a lot of traveling done, and possibly making it near New Orleans by the end of the day. What a great nights sleep. If there is one thing that I do like about this country is that their hotels are quite accommodating. Well I might as well get a move on today. There has been nothing that has impressed me about this country so far, but who knows today may be the day. Walczak brushes his teeth and heads out to his Range Rover. He drives away awaiting the long drive that lies ahead of him. He turns on the CD player and starts his day off with the Polish National Anthem. Jescze Polska Nie Zginela. What beautiful words. Poland is not yet lost. These words reign true. Poland will once again become a force that is respected throughout the world and I hope to be one of the people that get the country on the right track. Even the USA will respect the country of Poland, and then immigrants such as me will have a much easier time making a living in this country. Walczak speeds up and leaves the parking lot. He knows that today will be a day of long traveling. He does not have any major landmarks planned for today, and he hopes to travel nearly a thousand miles by days end. Day 3 9:00 pm Days Inn Mississippi No major events occurred on Walczak’s journey today. He has traveled as far as he could today to the point of exhaustion. He makes his way into the Hotel room hoping to get into New Orleans early tomorrow, and seeing what America’s famed party city is all about. Whether or not I find anything out tomorrow I’m sure that I will have a good time. As sad as it is to admit the one thing that has kept me sane all these years is alcohol, and from what I hear if I really want to drink than New Orleans is the place. Walczak heads to his hotel room tired and weary. He can barely keep his eyes open, and he passes out on the bed without so much as brushing his teeth. Day 4 12:39 pm Broussard’s Restaurant New Orleans Walczak has made it. This was without a doubt the most eagerly anticipated destination of Walczak’s road trip. Before heading out to do some drinking he heads to a restaurant to fill up. Walczak is immediately seated, and a waiter comes to serve him. “Could I start you off with something to drink sir?” “Yeah could I get a beer, and just bring out your two biggest lobsters and a large bowl of gumbo.” “Uhh, yeah sure. Is there more people coming to eat?” “No, just me wanted to get some food in me before I head down to Bourbon Street.” “Oh, have you ever been there?” “Nope.” “It’s really a great time you’ll love it.” “Yeah I hope to have a good time.” The waiter smiles and leaves Walczak. About half an hour later Walczak’s food arrives, and he gulps it down. He smiles in satisfaction, and pays the bill. Now its off to the real N’awlins. Day 4 2:53 pm Bourbon Street Ahh, I can smell the beer in the air. Nothing better than knowing you’re gonna get drunk. I just gotta scope out the bars, and then we could get this party rolling. Walczak sees a bar that seems to draw him in. Nothing to special just a little Irish Pub, but it looks like the place where the real drinkers go he makes his way in and isn’t disappointed. What a place. Even these damn American’s can’t ruin something as beautiful as drinking. Walczak walks up to the bar with a look of joy on his face. He sits at the bar, and waits for a free bartender to order a drink. “Bartender.” “Can I help you?” “Yeah can I get two beers to start?” “No problem boss.” Walczak gets the two beers and downs them. He lets out a sigh of satisfaction, and then he hears a loud chant in the back of the bar. He heads over to the area where the noise is coming from. “GO!GO!GO!GO!GO!” “Who’s next? That’s right who’s next?” An overweight man is screaming shouts of victory. He has an enormous gut, and is rather unkempt. He has beer all over his wife beater, and seems to know how to drink. “I’ll get next.” “Well, than let’s go at it boy.” The men are served up two mugs of beer. Someone yells go and the beer disappears. It just so happens that Walczak’s disappears nearly twice as quick as his competitors. “Beginners luck let’s go again.” Walczak obliges and the men drink again, and again the same results occur. “Yeah, well just cuz you can drink quicker don’t mean that you can drink more.” “I can drink all day buddy.” “Then let’s get a move on.” The men go at it. Both men down the first couple without any problem. However, as time wears on Walczak’s competitor slows down as Walczak keeps going as strong as ever. The next time words are uttered are when Walczak is on his fifteenth beer, and his competitor is on number nine. “How about we just call this a draw. It seems like you’re slowing down.” “No way man. Imma gonna catch up to you.” They keep drinking, and when Walczak reaches number twenty his opponent passes out on the table. Even Walczak has lost the sober thoughts he had before, and is slurring his words. “Who’s th-the man n-now.” The crowd cheers Walczak, and he makes his way out of the bar nearly three hours after his original entrance. Man that was great. I actually liked being in that place. I think that if we could drink all the time this world would be a better place. What a day. Tomorrow is the last leg of my trip, and I don’t think that I could have made it without having a nice day off in N’awlins. Walczak makes his way to his hotel room which is not that far away from Bourbon Street. He hopes to make it to Salt Lake City tomorrow eagerly awaiting his match this week. Day 5 5:30 am Bourbon Street Hotel Man this is the worst part of getting drunk. The morning after. Well, I just can’t sit here and sulk all day. I gotta get up and haul ass to Salt Lake City. Walczak gets out of his bed visibly groggy. He showers up and heads out with another long day of travel awaiting him Day 5 8:47 pm Salt Lake City Finally here. I have made my way across nearly two-thirds of the continental USA. I have stopped at many historical landmarks, and to tell you the truth have yet to find a reason behind the arrogance of the people of this country. Most of their symbols are twisted into beliefs that have nothing to do with the original intent, and they take other symbols that were signs of failure and convert them into exalted figures. This country is no doubt proud, but it is stupid and ignorant as well. That is all that I have garnered from this trip, and that’s really all I need. I will show this country what power is all about, but not just any power. No, I will show them POLISH POWER!!!!
