Sir Scam-A-Lot (Part 1)

26 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 26th, 2006 Darkness. A word overused by those who couldn’t possibly come to appreciate the full extent of its nature, nor could they be expected to know at just what magnitude darkness can be realized. Those who think they know darkness may never learn what it truly is, for if there is a God in heaven he will spare them that soul-destroying pain, but there is one who does know what darkness means. His black heart pumps the elixir of evil through his veins, his very existence a blight on humanity. He is the embodiment of all that is wrong with the world. He is your typical online merchant. Clyde sits in front of his computer screen; a blank stare covers what was happiness just three minutes ago. His second cheque as Universal Champion arrived earlier in the morning, and Clyde had decided to do a bit of online shopping. Searching for some fine wares on the web, if you will. What he found during his Internetscapade was not a fabulous bargain as told of in eBay’s self-published book eBay Is Your Friend, though, but rather a trap only befitting of the greatest demon in hell. An ancient Chinese sword for a buck-ninety turned into six-hundred-and-one dollars and ninety cents with a single e-mail. Having called James to his side after reading the letter a few times more, Clyde narrows his eyes in anger. This great evil could be only one thing; he had to be another demon needing to be slain. Devil May Clyde is back in business, bitch! Walkins: He scammed me, James. He fucking scammed me. Farwell: That’s no reason to kill a man. Besides, maybe his English isn’t so good and he got mixed up. He did mention something about your wife being very blandness, after all. Walkins: What’d he say about my wife?! James rolls his eyes. His cornea detaches a little more. A hand is then placed on Clyde’s shoulder. Farwell: Listen, just calm down and write him back. I’m sure it’s all just a big misunderstanding. Walkins: Fine, we’ll do it your way…for now. Clyde eyes James, then goes back to his e-mail, a response already writing itself out in his head. He’d seen this kind of thing done a million times before on HBO. Something about cappin’ the guy’s black ass, and then layin’ the mack down on his baby’s momma next to the crib. That nigga gon’ pay. Farwell: Ok, Clyde, I’ll write the e-mail. You just go make yourself some porridge. Walkins: Why? What’s wrong with mine? Farwell: Well…it’s just that it’s… James pauses, looking for the right word. Clyde interjects with the wrong one. Walkins: Perfect? Farwell: Racist. Terribly, terribly racist. Clyde’s line of vision switches from James to the computer screen, sighing as he repeatedly strikes the backspace key with his index finger. Nearly a minute of button mashing passes before James just gets fed up with the whole ordeal and pushes Clyde out of the way, holding the key down as the entire e-mail is sent straight to hell in a matter of a few seconds. Farwell: Alright, you tell me what you want to say and I’ll type out something that won’t land us with another restraining order. How the hell’d this guy rip you off, anyway? Don’t those interweb stores have the shipping price right there when you buy something? Walkins: It’s fucking eBay. Farwell: What the hell’s an eBay? Clyde rolls his eyes at James’ lack of intelligence in such matters. Surely, a man of his age and…age…would know a thing or two about the online world. Namely, that it houses the biggest collection of scammers the world has ever known. Walkins: It’s a land where everyone makes their own rules, and nothing is ever what it seems. Insanity is limitless in that place, a place where all hope has long since died, and all love turned to bitter despair. Blind men wander the streets where no one walks anymore, looking for a hand to guide them, but all they find is the cold grip of death upon their throat as it chokes the life out of another hapless victim. And, all the while, they laugh. They laugh, and they play, and they pretend everything’s ok, when they can smell the stench of rotting flesh beneath their feet. Such a place is not for the weak of heart, nor for the man who knows cowardice. Such a place would destroy any normal being. The one who can traverse the lands long forgotten by the laws of man shall be the one who brings light to the darkness. So says the prophecy. Clyde pauses for dramatic effect. James checks his watch. Walkins: I…am that man. James seems more confused than anything by the whole thing. Farwell: So…what the hell’s an eBay? Clyde ditches the theatrics in favor of a more direct approach. Walkins: It’s an online community where people come to buy and sell new and used items, based mostly upon the honor system. Sellers name their price and their shipping costs, even after you’ve won if they so deem necessary, and legal recourse against anyone involved in case you get screwed is just about nil. In short, it’s the world’s largest online scamfest. Farwell: It sounds like you really hate the place. Walkins: I do. I hate it with all of my heart and soul. Farwell: You must’ve gotten hoodwinked more than a few times. How long have you been eBaying it up for? Walkins: Six days. Last week, some guy who called himself Nova told me he found a couple nice bongs on eBay, and you know how much I love bongo drums. I couldn’t pass up the chance. But, then I saw this antique Chinese sword used by God himself once in 1432 and then again in 1996 listed by this ShoufangCurioStore dickhead, and, if there’s one thing I love more than the bongos, it’s antique swords wielded by deities. James nods in agreement. Farwell: You’ve always loved pointy objects. Walkins: At less than two dollars, it was a real steal, if you know what I mean. Clyde takes the moment to break out of his speech, winking and nudging James in the ribs with his elbow. Farwell: Meaning, he stole it from a museum. Continue. Walkins: Anyway, long story short, the son of a bitch writes me up no more than fifteen minutes ago, telling me the shipping and handling costs six-hundred bucks. Six-hundred dollars! That’s as much as you make in a month! Sadly, that sounds about right. Walkins: Then, he decides to diss on my ho by calling you bland. I can handle someone trying to scam me. I can handle someone jerking off while he does it. What I can’t stand, however, is someone thinking he can come in my house and talk shit about my bitch like that. No one gets to make fun of James, except me and maybe this Shoufang guy! James raises his fist in the air, adrenaline and anger now pumping through his veins. Farwell: Yeah! Let’s show him who’s very blandness! A moment later, the excitement passes as James realizes he was just being called Clyde’s woman the entire time. Farwell: Hey! An open palm to the back of the skull nips that in the bud. Farwell: Why don’t you just refuse to pay him? What’s the worst that can happen? The normally lighthearted tone of Clyde turns to dark and sinister once more, as he bows his head and looks to the shadows. Walkins: What’s the worst thing you can imagine? James gulps. Farwell: You mean…? Walkins: They’ll give you a strike. A shriek of terror emanates from James’ throat, then abruptly cuts short. Farwell: Wait…a strike? They give you a fucking strike?! Screw that! Just don’t pay the asshole. Clyde dives to cover the keyboard from James’ venomous e-mails. Walkins: ARE YOU INSANE?! THREE STRIKES AND I’M OUT! Clyde begins to openly sob into his hands, shaking and sniffling with half his body mashing the keys on the screen, causing the blue screen of death to appear in the background. James thinks about consoling him, then decides against it, pulling his hand away from Clyde’s shoulder. Clyde just keeps on crying. Walkins: I’m out, James…I’m out… Farwell: You’re not out, Clyde; you’ve still got two strikes left after this one. Clyde’s lower lip finds itself between a few opposing teeth. Farwell: Oh, come on! You couldn’t have possibly pissed two other people off in that short of time. Walkins: You don’t know how tempting it is to bid on things just to mess with people. It’s a rush, James. A rush that not even wiping your ass with your tag team partner’s pillow sheets can top. James nods to acknowledge Clyde, making a mental note to stop using sheets altogether. Walkins: I’m on my third strike, and, according to the eBay Charter, I’m up for the death penalty. You can’t let me die, dammit! We just need to figure out a way to keep him from giving me that strike… Farwell: The death penalty?! What the he-…why don’t you just pay it, then? You’ve got more than enough money for it now. Walkins: It’s the principle; if I let him get away with it, then the next person will, and the next after that. No, we must do something far more rash. He said he lived in China, and, since we have to return to Japan anyway, we may as well pick up my sword on the way back from ReVolution. No shipping, no shipping cost. The madness ends here. Farwell: Wait, we have to go back to Japan? Why didn’t we just stay there and save money on flights? Clyde pfft’s James. Walkins: Because, all those stuffy PRIME businessmen wanted to do was work poor old Clyde over like the government mule I am. I wasn’t having any of that, and I’ll be damned if I was gonna stay there and work while everyone else got to play! Farwell: Which is why we lost within three minutes of entering the Halo, before returning in shame to the States under the cover of darkness? Walkins: Exactly. Clyde taps his finger to his nose. Meanwhile, somewhere in China… In a small room covered with Chinese pictures and various artifacts, a little old man sits. A lime green cap covers his balding head and grey hair, while a robe of the same color outlined in gold hides his frame from the world. On his feet, a pair of white slippers. A cup of tea sits on the floor beside him, his legs crossed underneath his body, his fingers typing away at the keyboard of a laptop. A wicked grin slowly crosses his lips as he presses Send. That same smile widens when he checks his e-mail again, and sees four more unsuspecting buyers. Shoufang: Hory mory! Christmas come earry! Just then, the sound of a jingling bell in the next room causes him to stand and rush out the adjacent door. Now inside an establishment marked Shoufang Curio Store, the little old man walks behind the glass display counter housing several more wares, a warm smile on his face to greet the customer. The female customer places a small porcelain figurine on the display case, with a tag marked $1.99 attached to the bottom, then pulls out a five-dollar bill from her purse. Shoufang rings up the purchase, but wisely stops just before placing it in a brown paper bag, lifting his eyebrow as he addresses the woman. Shoufang: Gift? The woman smiles and nods, oblivious to the oncoming scammery. Shoufang: Fow smarr fee, I can lap. Shoufang’s smile widens. Back in America, fifteen hours later… James and Clyde sit in the airport, waiting for their flight to come in. James checks his watch. The plane’s late by half an hour. Farwell: Wonderful, the plane’s already late, it’s raining outside, and the ceiling’s starting to leak. I don’t know whether we’ll die from the plane crashing, or of hypothermia before it even gets here. James eyes Clyde. Farwell: And, why the hell aren’t you paying for this flight? I paid for the last one! Walkins: Now, now, James. Just calm down. The plane will be here any minute, and, before you know it, we’ll be in beautiful Hawaii! Farwell: Japan. Walkins: Beautiful Japan! James sighs, then takes a shot from a hidden vial in his jacket pocket. Walkins: I can’t believe you smuggled that in here how you did. James takes another drink, then wipes off his mouth. Farwell: The only way my life could get any worse is if someone bombed our plane… Old Woman In The Back: DID SOMEBODY SAY MCBOMBALD’S?! Everyone Else: BOOOOOMB!!! Farwell: Ah, dang… Expecting the worst, James braces for the tackle from security, but opens his eyes when he hears a loud crunch followed by Clyde’s girlish laughter. Peeking through his fingers, James sees the little old lady in the back crushed beneath a swarm of security, just as their plane lands in the background. Walkins: Hahaha! Haha! Ha! Ha…alright, let’s go.
