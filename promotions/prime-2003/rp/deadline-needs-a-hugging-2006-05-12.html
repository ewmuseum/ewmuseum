Deadline Needs A-Hugging

12 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 12th, 2006 “Five minutes ‘til air, people! Five minutes!” The shrill, obnoxious voice of an overzealous producer echoes through the small set of some unknown talk show. The Ronald Skeeterman Show, or something. The guy’s a supposed wrestling whiz that somehow landed his own half-hour slot, then decided to curse the world by hosting, directing, producing, and God-knows-what-elseing the worst thing that television or wrestling has ever known. Farwell: I don’t even know why I’m doing this. The guy phones me for an interview, and already I know how this is gonna end. All he’s gonna do is ask me questions about Clyde and his chances and blah, blah, blah, then I’ll get pissed, and someone will wind up in jail in time to get us all home for Wheel of Fortune. Maybe someone else will die. Who knows? The night is young. James draws a breath from the cigarette between his lips, leaning with his back against the outside of the set, arms folded across his chest. Eyes closed, he opens one when the highly effeminate vocal cords of Ronald Skeeterman squeak out a fake cough. Farwell takes the cigarette in his right hand, pulling it from his mouth, standing away from the tacky yellow set and towering over the host. Skeeterman: There’s no smoking on the set, you know! I have allergies that are triggered by smoke, and cigarettes cause cancer. My uncle died of cancer, okay? I don’t want to live the rest of my life with one lung because of someone like you, who couldn’t go half an hour without a fix. Ready to crush the weasel-like man with his bare hands, James stares hard into his eyes, slowly handing the cigarette to Skeeterman after an extremely long and uncomfortable pause. Almost breathing a sigh of relief, the effeminate weirdo holds the cancer stick at arm’s length, between his index finger and thumb, and clasping his nose shut with his free hand. With his nostrils pinched, his voice becomes even more bizarre, and kind of resembles Waspinator from Beast Wars. Skeeterman: Who were you talking to, anyway? His expression turning from anger to surprise, James’ eyes bug out as he quickly looks around the set. Partly bewildered, he attempts to explain himself while turning his focus back to the host. Farwell: I swear to God there was a dog here a minute ago. Skeeterman: There are no animals allowed on set! I am severely allergic to animals of all kinds, and, if you brought your pet here, he will be promptly executed! Farwell: He wasn’t my pet. Wait…what? Skeeterman: Loved and cared for until you can pick him up after the show. He’ll be promptly loved and cared for until you can pick him up after the show. Farwell: That’s not what you said the first time. Skeeterman: Yes, it is. I think I’d remember what I said. Farwell: You said you’d kill that dog! Skeeterman: No animals allowed on the set! No pets and no smoking, Mr. Farwell. This is your final warning. Still with the cigarette as far away from himself as possible, Skeeterman turns and walks almost bull-legged to his own private dressing room, which just so happens to be the only dressing room in the tiny space he leases. When out of earshot, James mutters an insult in his direction. Farwell: Puppy killer. -Three Hours Ago- Walkins: Jump! Left--no, right! Over that pit! Kill, kill, kill! The fireball! Jump, damn it, jump! Clyde wriggles in his seat, following the movements of the black and red character on the computer screen in front of him. Nearly tipping out of his chair on more than one occasion, he finally takes the tumble when James - having silently entered the room behind him - decides to open his mouth. Farwell: What’cha playing, Clyde? Walkins: G’WAHH! Noooooooooo! Holding his outstretched hand towards the monitor, Clyde hits the floor hard, while his character jumps right into a pit filled with snapping hamburgers. GAME OVER hauntingly appears on-screen, and James casually leans over the toppled chair to take a look at the game. Farwell: Making a fat kid jump over pits of carnivorous hamburgers in order to obtain his freedom…is there no greater irony? Meanwhile, Clyde sobs in a heap on the ground. Walkins: Why, Fat Albert, why? His attention span for video games short as it is, James tires of what’s on the screen before him and looks down at Clyde, who still mourns the loss of his overweight chum. Farwell: Listen, I got a call from this talk show host a few days ago about getting an interview done before Ultra Violence, and I figured you could come along with me, if you want to. Walkins: My agent says I shouldn’t do talk shows; he says they’re bad for my image. Farwell: Your image? Clyde, you haven’t won a match since winning that stupid title...just how much image could you possibly have? Clyde worms around on the ground, eventually rolling to his feet, struggling with trying to set the desk chair upright again - and, stumbling a few times in the process - before he hands James the new issue of People Magazine sitting on the table. The magazine already folded open to Clyde’s page of interest, James breathes a heavy sigh and begins scanning the pictures and headlines for anything of notice. Farwell: Britney pregnant again…liposuction for Elton John…Clyde Walkins tops the charts as hottest bachelor…what the Christ?! Walkins: Isn’t it wonderful? I’m beautiful! Farwell: It’s terrible! You’ve made a mockery of not only wrestling, but now Hot or Not lists in the backs of magazines of questionable integrity. You’re a walking plague! Walkins: Just for that, I’m not letting you accompany me to the ring for my title match. See how you like them apples…you…apple-loving…person…you… The magazine is tossed back from whence it came, and James shakes his head in quiet disappointment. Farwell: I’m not allowed to, anyway. Boss says you get to go it alone out there, or else. Walkins: Wait, I was just bluffing! Farwell: No, seriously, Clyde…no one’s allowed to come with you. You’re- Walkins: Screwed! Farwell: That’s one way of putting it, yes. Clyde’s head falls into his hands. How could this have happened? One moment you’re on top of the world, Universal Title in one hand, some stranger’s butt cheek in the other, and the next you’re giving head for pesos on the boardwalk. Walkins: They can’t do this to me…I won’t stand for it! I refuse to be Karina Wolfenden’d! Farwell: Wha? Walkins: If you will not help me, then I will need to look to a higher power. I will need to bring the strength of many to combat the forces of evil, and in the darkest hour there will be light. There’s nothing James can say, so he just stands there in a stunned silence, unable to fathom the words needed to respond to such a thing. Walkins: I must call upon the masses, from near and from far, and I shall vanquish the wickedness of those who stand before me with the will of the immortals! Farwell: …Riight. I…probably should be going now, so I’ll…see you around. Good luck with that whole biblical thing you’ve got going there. Walkins: Go. Spread the word, my son. For now, I will indulge in a little Spirit Wrestling to ease my wounded heart. James pauses for a second, as Clyde takes a seat back in his chair. Farwell: I sincerely hope you meant Wrestling Spirit. Walkins: No. Spirit Wrestling. It has all your favorite dead wrestlers in it: Andre The Giant, Owen Hart, Eddie Guerrero, my grandfather, for some reason, Yokozuna… A shudder courses through James’ body at the thought of such a game actually existing, but he shrugs it off as he heads towards the door, ready for a great new opportunity to get his name and face out there for the world to see. Finally, he’ll be free of Clyde’s shadow. Just as he opens the door, though, a final cry from Clyde sends another chill up his spine. Walkins: Fuckin’ Crash Holly speared grampy! James pulls the door shut and starts heading down the walkway, unable to hear the distinct ringing of Clyde’s new cell phone just seconds later, the theme from Clyder-Man filling the air with the sweet smell of copyright infringement. -Present Time- The audience applauds the return from commercial, while Skeeterman plasters an inauthentic smile on his face after threatening James about the whole animal thing during the break. Farwell, unable to muster the same level of fakeness as his host, just tries to look like his life hasn’t been threatened over an allergic reaction to someone’s Pomeranian. Skeeterman: And, we’re back! For those of you just joining us, we’re here tonight with my guest, former OSW, RPW and Prime Tag Team Champion, James Farwell. James, thank you for being here. James is still a little skittish over the severity of the threat, which involved a blender, a hammer from an abandoned tool shed, six exotic birds, and his left testicle. Needless to say, he’s only able to stumble through false happiness. Farwell: The…pleasure’s…a-all mine… Skeeterman: I’m sure it is. Tell me, James, how does it feel to be partner to an individual as unique as Clyde? You must have some great tales to tell our viewers. Farwell: No, not really… Skeeterman: Then, do tell…what is he really like in person? Same guy we see each week in Prime, or a complete one-eighty? Is he a Madonna in the dressing room? Farwell: A what? Look, I don’t know how this has any correlation to- Skeeterman: STDs. A star of his magnitude must touch at least three or four vaginas a day. Tell me, will his insatiable sexual appetite be his downfall? Farwell: I don’t think he’s ever touched a-…listen, are you actually going to interview me, or is this exactly what I figured it would be? Skeeterman: What did you figure it’d be? Farwell: An unending string of personal questions regarding Clyde’s lifestyle and general hygiene, with nary a mention of the guy actually on your show. Skeeterman: Then it’s exactly what you figured it to be! Right after this next commercial break, I’ll ask James Farwell the one question you’ve been dying to ask Clyde Walkins: wax or shave? A cheesy smile ends the segment, while the home audience gets treated to a slew of advertisements for yeast infection and online brothels, and Ronald Skeeterman’s nasally whine performs an encore for those lucky enough to be in the studio. Leaning over James’ shoulder, he starts talking in his ear, causing Farwell to cringe slightly and move his head away for fear of being bitten or something. Skeeterman: Show more energy! I need you looking alive and full of spunk, or the viewers watching at home won’t buy into it! Emotion! James just looks confused about the whole thing. Farwell: So, what…are you gonna actually interview me this round, or should I just go home now? Before he can get an answer, some techie with a ‘70s porn mustache gives the signal for ten seconds remaining, and James finds his eyes mysteriously transfixed on the mega-stache, along with the mullet that doesn’t just say business up front, party at the back, it statutory rapes its way into a restraining order with it. James’ mind shortly wonders how someone looking like that could land a job anywhere near a camera, but one look at the host reminds him this isn’t exactly a convention of beauties. This isn’t even The Golden Girls. Short, skinny, and with less penis than boobies, Ron Skeeterman looks like a cross between Michael Jackson and Michael Jackson, with the voice of an angel that got shot in the throat with a crossbow. And, then had his balls stepped on by an elephant. James just looks depressed. Farwell: …This is worse than the time I saw Samoa Joe wrestle. Cut to a packed arena, with Samoa Joe facing off against some unknown jobber that no one cares about. Possibly Chris Storm. Halfway through the contest, a call cuts through the rest of the buzz in the crowd, emanating from right in the middle row. Farwell: Put-a-shirt-on! Clap,clap, clap,clap,clap! Put-a-shirt-on! Clap,clap, clap,clap,clap! A single tear rolls down Samoa Joe’s face, as he runs from the ring, arms folded across his chest to shield his jiggling man-boobs from the hungry eyes of the leering male audience. And, all the while, Farwell just looks proud of his dickery. Back to the show, with one second until commercials end. Skeeterman: If you’ve just joined us, I’m about to pose the biggest question of Clyde Walkins’ life to the man who’s lived it, James Farwell. James, tell all the viewers at home…does Clyde Walkins wax or shave his buttocks to achieve that bowling ball shine? James waits a moment, neither moving nor letting his gaze wonder to the host, before leaning over and speaking into the microphone. Farwell: I believe he’s naturally hairless, Ron. Like a Chihuahua. Skeeterman: Like a Chihuahua. You heard it here first, folks; Clyde Walkins is hypoallergenic. Now even ladies with sensitive sinuses can have a piece of the Clyder-Man! Farwell: Actually, I wouldn’t suggest touching him, or even really breathing near him. He bathes, like…once a month…and he rolls around in a lot of stuff. Ronald Skeeterman blatantly disregards the comment, moving on in the ‘interview’. Skeeterman: When you first teamed up with Clyde, did you ever imagine the greatness he’d reach? And, you’ve been privileged enough to be part of it! What’s it like learning from Prime’s Universal Champion? That’s it. James has had it. Farwell: What’s it like learning from him?! I taught him everything he knows! The only reason he won that stupid belt was because he fought a severely injured man right after an almost-impossible title win. This isn’t a privilege to see! This is an injustice, and getting to sit back and take crap from some twerp like you is more than I’m prepared to handle. You can fill up the last five minutes of this show by jerking off over Clyde like you have been the entire time, because I’m done. Pulling a pack of cigarettes out of his pocket, along with his lighter, Farwell lights a smoke and exhales directly in Ron’s face. James then reaches over and knocks the microphone off the table as he stands, tossing the lit cigarette on Skeeterman’s lap.. Farwell: Is this emotion enough for you?! Turning to walk away, Farwell takes a few steps, then spins around and marches right back to the desk. Farwell: It’s PRIME, motherfucker! PRIME! Pushing the flimsy desk into Skeeterman, Farwell walks off the set, pie-facing a guard on the way out, and leaving one final insult for the host. Farwell: Skeet, skeet, skeet! The doors slam behind him, and the flustered host tries to regain his composure, but nothing will take the smile off Farwell’s face now. Not yet, anyway. -Two Hours Later- The stars in the sky shine like a fresh pearl necklace, but nothing can compare to the shine that James’ teeth give off, a smile stretching from one side of his face to the other etching lines in his cheeks never meant to be etched on a face like his. As happy as he can recall in recent memory, it’s as if nothing can ruin this wonderful moment. Before he can even get in the door, a poorly written note ends his joy. “Deer James, Hehehe, I said deer. Going away for a bit, need to find worshippers. Call me. Luv,Clyde” Farwell: The hell? Worshippers? Deer? Call him? He never gave me his number… Scratching his head, James quickly snaps out of his bewilderment, realizing a very important truth. Farwell: Clyde’s somewhere out in the world…alone. The last time he went somewhere strange alone was to sign an advertising deal, and I’ll never forget how that turned out… In a blurry flashback moment, James looks to the sky and remembers the day when Clyde got to drive alone in the streets of New York City. Grinning like a moron as he passes by tall buildings, Clyde looks ahead just in time to see a cow standing in the middle of the ring, a feminine screech emanating from his throat. He hits the brakes, but not soon enough, and ends up swerving off the street, right into a brick wall on one side of a dark alley. Seemingly out of nowhere, scavengers of the night appear, picking things off Clyde as he lies partially across the dash. Walkins: I’m hurt, don’t mug me! Having ejected from the car on impact, his wallet falls open on the sidewalk, revealing a picture of James inside. One of the muggers grabs it, prompting another shout from Clyde, but the shouts fall on deaf ears. Walkins: Leave him alone! James! As the muggers begin to flee, one of them turns back. Mugger: Quick! Get Bessie! A presumed lesser backtracks, taking hold of a rope around the cow’s neck, then shortly returns to the others in the confines of a dark and dreary alley. James blinks several times in quick succession, shaking the memory from his head. Farwell: Good God…he could already be dead. -Somewhere Else, At The Same Time- Through the darkness, a single light illuminates the way. From the earth to the heavens, it guides the way for all living creatures, great and small. It brings hope to the masses. It is light. It is good. Clyde raises the lone piece of gold into the air, standing on top of a pedestal, bowing men and women in white robes surrounding him. Rain pours down, but none respond. None care. Like a Greek God, he has a single cloth draped across a shoulder, the PRIME Universal Championship held high in the air. All around him, people stupider than he is, every head bowed; only the light is meant to see the sky. Only the light can lead the way. It is good. Only the light can banish the darkness. It is light. Only the light can save him. It is good. Only the light. It is God.
