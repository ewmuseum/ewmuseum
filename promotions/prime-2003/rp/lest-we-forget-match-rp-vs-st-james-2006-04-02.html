Lest We Forget... (Match RP vs. St. James)

2 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/02/06 Monday, February 13, 2005 1:45 PM EST The Lower Ninth Ward is an area of New Orleans that was practically wiped off the surface of the planet by Hurricane Katrina only five and one half months earlier. Living in this area were people who could barely afford to love day to day. Many of the people would not hesitate to give up their difficult lives, but they would love to get something in return. Instead, when this devastation of nature hit, their difficult lives were taken away and nothing but troubled times lie ahead. Sure you heard the stories on all the news stations. You saw the footage of people being rescued from their flooded neighborhoods. You even saw people living at The Convention Center and some people not living at all. But really no one could understand what these people have lost. Although they didn’t have that much in the first place, their entire lives were washed away in a flash. People were killed, homes were destroyed and lives left in limbo. Granted, donations were made towards the effort, but never could there be enough money raised to give these poor individuals their lives back. The only thing that is left for most of these people is the years of memories that are now buried under mud and in some cases water. These articles are just waiting to be rescued, before they rot away into nothing, Much like the family picture that Killean now held in his dirty, glove covered hand. He held the damaged picture up so he could have a better look. The image contained the family in happier times. The smiles seen were priceless, the true image of a happy family. Even though the family had little, they still made the most of it. But now it was all gone, the memories were now just that. As Killean dipped his head to look at his feet, he made his way over to Jasmine James, the owner of this now desecrated property. He tapped her on the shoulder and as she turned to greet him, he held up the picture. Truthfully, the picture was still in good shape. The frame had protected the actual picture best as it could and it was still very much salvageable. As soon as Jasmine saw the picture, she became teary eyed and she frowned. But then, her frown turned to a smile as she saw the image of her now deceased daughter Becka, who she lost during the worst flooding. Jasmine had no where to go. She was living in a hotel with her three other children. She relocated to Baton Rouge and with the large amount of evacuees she had much trouble finding work. As she was a school teacher before, she was forced to swallow her pride and work at a fast food restaurant where someone half her age and experience was her boss. But hey, whatever paid the bills was decent for her. She was never in the place to make large purchases to keep her kids happy. A few toys at Christmas were the biggest treat. Their clothes were purchased mostly second hand and their hair cut by a neighbor who used to live nearby. She truly went through the roughest of times. She was forced to make the ultimate sacrifice. Questions that should never be asked, she was forced to answer. She basically watched her daughter get swept away by the waters as she saved one of her sons. She didn’t really make a choice between her son and her daughter, but she was forced to save one and not the other just because of the sick circumstances. Days later, a helicopter finally saved her and her family, short one. Her daughter’s body has yet to be discovered to this day and she feared she would never get to give her daughter a true burial. She remembered the flight in the helicopter with the blanket wrapped around her, covering her mouth. She bit down on it hard. It was the only thing stopping her from asking the pilot to stop at every corner down below to search for her daughter, praying that she was still alive somehow… somewhere. She took the picture in her hands and gave Killean a tight hug. “Thank you for finding this. Thank you for everything you have done.” It warmed Killean’s heart to hear the words. No matter how much of an asshole he could be at certain times, he certainly was not heartless. As he turned to go back to work, two large vehicles, looking like motor homes pulled as close to the home as they possibly could. A horn sounded from one, signaling Killean to put his shovel down and remove his work gloves. He made his way over to the driver’s side window of one of the vehicles. “Killean is it?” The older man said, while shutting off the engine. “That’s right; you must be Ron from the catering company.” Killean replied, shaking hands with the driver. “Yes sir. We are happy you contacted us for this cause. You are doing some really good things around here.” Sirrajin smiled and handed the driver an unmarked envelope. “As promised… the total closing cost for the service as well as some nice tips to all your staff and drivers for everything they have done to make this possible. It’s a small price to pay if you ask me.” Killean smiled. “Certainly, you are a generous man Killean.” Ron handed the microphone to the PA system attached to the first vehicle. Workers made their way around the motor homes, opening the side doors to reveal plates upon plates of hot lunches, cool and crisp salads, hot and cold drinks and sweet deserts. Killean pressed the button as Ron honked the horn of the vehicle, getting the attention of the many volunteers helping Jasmine reclaim her memories. “Ladies and gentleman, we have accomplished quite a bit in our few hours out here. Certainly we deserve a break and certainly we deserve a treat. So if you would please form a line behind Mrs. James it’s time for lunch. Help yourselves, no one leaves hungry!” The crowd smiled with delight as they led a pleasantly surprised Jasmine James to the head of the line. Tears were rolling down her cheek. But they were happy tears. She was blessed with more generosity than she had ever hoped for. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Half an hour had passed since the motor home caterers had arrived. Many people now took their turn to sit on the small outdoor patio created by the company. Tables and chairs were supplied and they were still serving hot food to the many people just finishing their work. Killean surveyed the people, some enjoying the food and the discussions with friends, many of whom they had just met that morning. Everyone had something to talk about because they all had at least one thing in common. It was the willingness to help the less fortunate. Killean had had enough of the articles coming from the press about the future of Mardi Gras. He had enough of the articles that talked about the financial future of New Orleans. If he could make even one person feel loved and not forgotten throughout this whole ordeal, it would make him feel much better about what he could do. He turned away from the crowd and walked up the mud filled walkway and into the house. As soon as he approached the doorway, he saw the line that showed how high the flood waters were. It was basically tattooed to the outside of the house, a constant reminder of just how serious the situation really was. Making his way into the house, the smell of mold and rotting wood and drywall filled the air. It was a cocktail that seemed mixed to make anyone who was to inhale deeply sick to their stomachs. It was overpowering and yet another reason why these houses were now officially uninhabitable. The homes in this area were damaged beyond repair and were most certain to be demolished within a year’s time. Even though the scent was overwhelming, the lingering feeling of memories filled the air. It was definitely a home that had a history, be it the ups or the downs. One thing was certain; this family would miss their home. As he made his way around the broken pieces of drywall, he could hear the soft footsteps of Jasmine in the other room. Killean made his way in to see her staring out the window, no doubt remembering the good times her and her family had in the years. She was also looking back to a wedding picture still hanging on the wall but badly damaged by flood waters. He held her arms around herself, looking for some comfort. Killean was heard approaching, but Jasmine didn’t turn to make eye contact, instead she just began talking. “My husband left us a little over a year ago. He had lost his job with the local grocer only a few weeks earlier. He figured he didn’t deserve a family because he couldn’t put food on the table. He said I deserved better.” The emotion coming from her voice was thick and honest. “Really, I never wanted anyone else. I have to give him credit though. The child support has never been late.” Obviously the wedding picture was just another image of happier times in the James household. The feeling of memories was strong to Killean. But who could really understand just what Jasmine was thinking. Killean walked over to join Jasmine where she was standing. He reached into his coat pocket and pulled out an envelope. It was rather thick and one could only assume what it contained. “Listen Jasmine, my commitments do not end here, just at what you see now.” Killean handed her the envelope explaining it. “Inside this envelope you’ll find four different items. First, you will find an application form to a school system in Baton Rouge. Given their recent influx of children from other areas, I sent them a copy of your resume and they requested to meet with you. All you have to do is call them and set a time. They are expecting to hear from you.” Jasmine’s eyes lit up as she looked through the other contents of the envelope. She pulled out the application form from the envelope as Killean continued to explain the other contents. “I know you need a way to get your kids back and forth to school and to get groceries, so the title to a brand new minivan is in there. It’s fully paid for and insurance is taken care of for the next year. On top of that, you’ll see an approval form from Habitat for Humanity who will be building a home in Baton Rouge that will be your gift. Finally, here’s part of the funds that I have set aside for you to pay your bills for the first year while you get on your feet. Consider any past debts to be settled and paid and consider any bills that you will have for the next year to be paid for. This includes the furnishing of your new home when it is complete. Computers for each of the kids to help with their school work. New bedroom sets in each of four bedrooms in the home and a new kitchen and living room set. Extra spending money and other essentials will be included. This is taken from a strict donation fund that a few of my friends put together along with me. I went through rough times when I was younger. I can’t claim to know how you feel but I just want to help.” The tears continued to stream down the flush face of Jasmine as Killean spoke. She couldn’t believe what she was hearing. “I’m… speechless…” Jasmine started, wiping the tears away and looking at the envelope, a virtual door beckoning her to start her life over again. “I need to tell everyone!” But before she could go, Killean stops her, shaking his head no. “I have done many things that I am proud of. I have also done many things that I am not proud of. Being who I am, no deed goes unnoticed in the eye of the public. I’d rather be happy knowing that I made a difference in the life of someone knowing that I will not be judged by the world for doing it. So please, if you are asked, its anonymous donations. Only you and I will know the truth and that’s the way it should be.” Making himself quite clear, Killean receives a hug from Jasmine. She continues to nod her head. “You know Killean; I’m a 40 year old woman. I’ve been around longer than you have yet I’ve accomplished less. Your generosity is unmatched yet you seem so humble. You have grown up to become a good man. Thank you.” Another hug sealed the deal. As Killean was doing another look around the room, his cell phone began to ring. The first time he looked at the call display, he smirked and was about to return it to his pocket. But then he had second thoughts. He opened the phone and greeted his caller. “Hello Mr. Nelson. To what do I owe the pleasure?” ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Thursday, February 16, 2005 11:35 AM EST The phone call from Tyler Nelson gave him plenty of time to complete the second leg of his trip. The drive from New Orleans to Dayton, Ohio was actually not that bad given the circumstances of his visit. Dayton of course is the home to the Ward Family… if you could call them much of a family anymore. Killean Sirrajin’s arch nemesis Matt set up shop here with his wife Mary. They even had a baby girl named Georgie. But all was not well for Matt and his family. Matt had told Mary multiple times about his return to the squared circle, but unfortunately Mary could never remember the conversations from one minute to the next. However, after recovering she didn’t need to be told anymore. As a matter of fact, she even arrived in Lexington, Kentucky the night of ReVolution 84. She found herself walking around the backstage area towards the end of the show when the beaten and bloody carcass of a man fell at her feet planted firmly in place. The man crawled towards her and finally managed to grab a hold of her ankle. Just then, the man’s assailant came from around the corner to deliver what looked like the final death blow. All Killean wanted was to put Matt out of his misery and send him home as a broken shell of a man. All Mary could say was, “Killean… please stop.” His eyes had gone wide in horror. Never had he intended for Mary to see, let alone remember the devastating beating he gave to Matt. At the time of the attack even Matt was unaware that Mary had successfully recovered from her amnesia. To this day, Killean never apologized to Mary for the brutal beating he threw the way of The Inhuman Being. To this day, he never forgave himself for what he allowed Mary to see with her own two eyes. Matt was another story. He did that for his own good. But Mary deserved nothing less than a full apology. He pulled up in front of the quaint home of Mary’s family in Dayton. As he exited the car, his heart was pounding and sweat was now gathering in his eyebrows. He wiped his forehead as he slowly made his way up the walkway towards the front door. He constantly kept flashing back to the image of Mary crying in the hallways of the Rupp Arena in Lexington. Matt and Mary were currently “separated” so obviously Killean didn’t need to worry about coming face to face with his former friend. But he was worried about coming face to face with Mary. What would she do? Would she welcome him? Would she kindly ask him to leave? Or would she whip Killean across the cheek with a well placed slap? Either way, he was resigned to his fate and was prepared to take whatever the future held for him in this instance. He now stood on the front step, his hands shaking and his heart racing. His hands were also cold and clammy and he felt like he was experiencing a cold sweat. He wiped his hands on his coat and raised one hand, balled into a fist to the door. At first he wouldn’t knock. But then he closed his eyes… *KNOCK KNOCK KNOCK* He stood back a little and was shaking a little more now. As soon as he heard the lock on the door open, he took a big breath and stood his ground. When the door actually opened, his eyes were locked on to the space between the door jam and the door. Shaking his hands, he followed as her delicate facial features made their way around the corner of the door. He locked eyes with her, at first not being able to believe he had done this. He pulled his sunglasses off his face as a show of respect. “H-… hi Ma-… Ma-… Mary…” Killean could barely get the two word sentence to escape his lips. He was sure as hell trying to hide his nervousness, but really, anyone could tell he was shaking like a leaf. He really wanted this to go well. Mary suddenly rounded the door to come standing in front of The Supreme Machine. She reached her hand up to brush away her hair as she took a deep breath. “Killean…” was all she came out with. They stood there in silence for a moment, both trying to take in the situation as it was. Mary suddenly moved her hand from her side and up. Killean braced himself. The slap was coming; he could almost feel it as the sting was already crossing his cheek. But no, it never happened. “Would you like to come in?” The sound of Mary’s voice was soft and comforting as her hand waved behind her to the cozy home within. Maybe things would stay civilized after all.
