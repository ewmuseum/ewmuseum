I Don't Even Know What To Call This...

19 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 19th, 2005 Before I start this pile of crap I’d really like to call a roleplay (but can’t…for…legal reasons), I wanted to let everyone know that I had a dilemma while coming up with an idea for it. Now, this isn’t just any normal match that I can win and lose…or just lose, repeatedly…at will--this is the Jewel In The Crown tournament. So I need to at least win a match or two in order to show I’m not completely crapping up the roster, right? Thus, the wheels in my head began to turn, and I found myself soon coming to a crossroads: do I take a more serious route with Farwell and see where the extent of my writing ability can take me, or do I go with the tried and true comedy that has unwillingly become the cornerstone of everything I touch? I’ve mulled over this for probably longer than I should’ve, and have finally come to a conclusion, whether it’s what people want to read or not. After thinking it through long and hard, I’ve decided to go with what brought me to the game, then drove me home after I got drunk and puked in the backseat. So yeah…if you don’t usually like my stuff (meaning you have something up your ass…maybe a penis), then don’t read ahead. You’ll be sorely disappointed. But for those of you who usually enjoy (or at least tolerate) my work, read on. You’re my friends and shall thusly be rewarded. Without further ado, I bring to you the greatest comedy act in the history of anything. Forget Penn and Teller; screw you, Marx Brothers; go to hell, Bill Cosby; hang yourself with the rope of your own unfunniness, Paulie Shore! This is The Forsaken. And this kicks your ass. … Comedy ahoy! The scene opens up to a group of people sitting in a church, all dressed in black. Everyone looks solemn, save for one person, who, while trying to remain as inconspicuous as humanly possible, whispers into the cell phone held up against his ear. “I…I need to know if this is funny.” On the other end of the spectrum, a TBS official sits in one of the rows at the station’s headquarters. Typically fat, and trying in vain to look passable as anything but a manatee by slathering on layers of makeup, she types a few things into her computer to bring up the main screen. “Alrighty, then,” she speaks obnoxiously into her headset, “where are you?” Back to the man dressed in black, who looks around cautiously to make sure no one’s listening. “I’m at a funeral.” The fat bitch’s face shudders into a slight smile as her eyes light up. “A funeral…that’s pretty funny already. But everyone already knows that, so what else is happening there?” “Well,” the guy responds, “the widow was paying her final respects, and…and…she…” He pauses for a second, allowing the old whore’s imagination to run wild. “She farted.” A fat laugh emanates from her, as the man is forced to cover the phone in order to keep anyone from hearing it. “Ok, I’d say that’s pretty funny so far,” she begins, but hesitates for a moment. “Were you the only one who heard it?” “Oh God, no.” The man shakes his head, before realizing she can’t see him. “Everyone heard it. In fact, I can see a few people trying to stifle their own laughter.” The old whore types a few more things into the computer, bringing up a response of ‘moderately funny’. “Tell me about the widow,” she says, still typing away while grinning widely. “Is she prim and proper, trailer trash-…” Cutting her off, the man quickly responds. “Prim and proper.” “And has this ever happened before?” “No, never. I’d wager a guess that this is the first time she’s ever passed gas.” And then a few moments pass, while she clicks away at the keys. Finally, the response he’s been waiting for arrives. “Well, our system says this is hilarious. You can go ahead and laugh.” The sound of the guy bursting out into a hearty laugh is the last thing heard, as several heads turn to face him with shocked and/or horrified expressions, before the screen fades out to the TBS logo. “TBS is your home for comedy television. TBS: Super Funny.” James Farwell raised a questioning eyebrow towards his tag team partner. Earlier in the day, Clyde had mentioned something about needing to be at some game convention or another to once again publicly whore the Make-A-Wish Foundation. What he didn’t mention until an hour later, however, was that a three-hour trip was necessary, and that the convention began in two hours. The pedal was applied to the metal, and they were off. Avoiding getting ticketed for speeding, James found himself using the ‘sick son’ excuse on more than one occasion during their trip, and the time was nearly cut in half as Clyde only needed to stop twice for successive bathroom breaks. You see; the first time he fell in, and didn’t tell James until a peculiar odor was noticed. The second time was all about cleaning and throwing a few things away. But now they were standing outside the building set to house said gaming convention. One thing, though, would be that it’s not just any gaming convention. Oh no. For they were standing on the edge of eternity. They had finally arrived at “73}{ 1337 64//3|2’5 Convention?” Farwell’s eyebrow raised higher as he noticed something else that happened to be slightly beyond peculiar; over the heads of each person entering the building were their names…or rather, their chosen screen names. And all your favorites were there. Yes, even The Educator87. But he got busted at the gate for using a stolen ID. Upon receiving confirmation to enter, one especially nerdy character (we’ll call him…let’s say…‘Gabe’) proudly donned his new screen name of IMASTEREDYOURMOMLOL~~~!!!1111IMSOLEETITALKLIKETHISANDDONTUSEPUNCTUATIONORSPACESBECAUSEIMSOLEETDAMMITFEARMEANDMYGOLDENRAYSOFSONSHI It cut off just around there, as the name traveled further than the human eye could see. Strutting along with his fresh new name, ‘Gabe’ cockily walked right through the front door. Except not. His name caught on the doorframe, causing his neck and head to snap back with a sickening crackle, before he collapsed onto his back, lying spread-eagle in the doorway. The bouncer looked down, shrugged, and then ushered the next pair of people into the building. Gingerly stepping over him, they seemingly didn’t notice the corpse as anything more than a slight hassle, and went along their merry way. “Well,” James said, beginning to walk towards the line as Clyde followed, “at least something good came out of today.” The two showed their passes, but it proved to be unnecessary as the guard’s eye caught sight of the Chocolate Wookie in Clyde’s arms. “R-right this way, sir,” the guard stammered, pulling the velvet rope aside to let them through. As they walked through the doorway, James made a point to step on ‘Gabe’ as they entered the building. Then he came back out and did it again. Twice. Inside the building was a different story. Right off the bat, people recognized The Forsaken. Well, not so much ‘people’ as ‘nerdy Internet geeks, who pick apart wrestling matches all day’. “Hey, look! It’s The Foreskin lolz!” One of the nerds high-fived his friend. Possibly his only friend. “Yeah lolz! You guys suck! You’re not competitive at all…you’re just retarded!” “lolz! Did you see their last match? Spotfest at best lolz!” Farwell’s eyebrow began to raise even higher. “The ‘lolz’ leads me to believe this is all a sick joke, but I’m still wary…” Clyde, meanwhile, had been lured off into a dark corner by some hooded figure, leaving James to fend for himself. “Do a smart promo for once lolz!” As they circled around him, James cowered and tried to shield himself from the nerdy onslaught, howling in soul-wrenching pain as the netspeak began to flow from their mouths like pictures of nude kids onto Gary Glitter’s computer. …I feel a little sad inside that it’s the best comparison I could make, but my ‘blue whale cumming into the ocean’ line bottomed out when I remembered the four-hundred gallon myth was just that. “My child,” the voice spoke to him, raspy and withered. Possibly as old as Moses. “You hold in your hand what I have searched all these years for.” “Moses wants my giggle-stick?” Clyde asked, perplexed. The creature simply stood there for a moment, completely taken aback by what was said. “No…I-uh…your other hand, my son.” Clyde looked at the Chocolate Wookie he held, then back to the mysterious figure. “No, you can’t have Mr. CW! Never!” But the mysterious man was adamant. So much so, that he caused Clyde to back himself directly into a corner. “Give it to me! GIVE IT TO ME! GIVE IT TO MEEEE!!!” “Clyde!” A voice called out, as footsteps charged towards the two. “What the hell’s going on here?!” Grabbing the hooded figure by the back of his cloak, James pulled him away from Clyde, only for the man to completely disrobe and reveal his true self. “Oh God! My eyes!” James cried, turning his gaze from the ghastly sight. There before them stood the most heinous picture ever painted. Pale and sickly, and covered in burns and scars, the frail naked body of the old man limped towards Clyde, holding out his hands. A steel plate concealing the right side of his face, and only a deep red glow where the eye was meant to be, he coarsely pleaded with Clyde. “Please…my son,” he began, still walking agonizingly slow towards the little retard. “My time is soon to come to an end…but that which you hold in your hand will grant me life anew…please…give it to…meeeee…” Dropping to his knees and clutching his chest, the old man grabbed Clyde’s pant leg and began to pull in a vain attempt to take the Wookie from him. The pulling led to Clyde’s pants moving further down, as the man’s head inadvertently(?) neared the crotch region. Having seen just about enough for one day, Farwell grabbed his partner’s arm and pulled him away, looking disgustedly at the old man. “Molest some other kid, you shrunken old pedophile!” A boot to the side of the head sent the old man to the ground, as James and Clyde hurriedly rushed to a more crowded section, just about the time something big was about to go down.
