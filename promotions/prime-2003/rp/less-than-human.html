Date: Time he's on my side, she said, He may be on your side I said, But it makes no difference in the end, He's coming after you my friend Nestling her infant into the child seat of her Camry, Melissa diligently wrapped him in blankets before buckling him up, the tender rub of her nose against his forehead causing him to squeal as he tried to touch her face. The snow came down in sideways sheets, the gusts of wind cutting through her layers of clothing and chilling her to the bone; her child’s touch was the only thing that warmed her. He startled himself with his gurgling squeaks, lazy bobs of his head looking for the source of the noise until he gave up and giggled. Snow hardly ever fell in Texas. In Wisconsin, it was just the course of things. “Who’sagoodlilboy,” she cooed as she peeled off her gloves, combing the soft strands of his blond hair before her lips delicately kissed his forehead. “Mommy’s gonna be right back.” She shut the door, popping the trunk so she could put away the groceries. Rifling through her purse, she took a cigarette out of her paper pack, lighting it up as she put the bag of baby food and bath wash behind the mesh webbing so it wouldn’t spill all over. Cody had changed her habits; now, she was going outside, making sure second hand smoke wouldn’t touch her baby boy. It was the last hold over from her past. She didn’t consider Cody a hold over; he was the only thing Brandon had done that had come out right, so much so that she could almost forgive the gutless bastard simply because his seed could produce such a beautiful child. Almost. She’d traded away her home for whitewash oblivion, but the choice wasn’t one she regretted. Puffing through long drags, her teeth chattered as she pushed her shopping cart in with the rest, tossing the cigarette away as she made her way back. As expensive as cigarettes were around here, it wasn’t worth leaving Cody all alone for too long. Through the gray haze, binocular lenses had spotted her, remaining trained on her movement. They watched her as she backed out of her parking spot and drove away. They’d been watching her for days, monitoring her routines and scrawling on legal pads from a safe distance. Cody and Melissa’s safety could only last so long. ---- Mouthwash burst from Brandon’s lips, his shaky hands grasping the porcelain brim of the bathroom sink as he brought his head back up to stare at his mirror. There was no mirror. The shards of glass that were too large to wash down the drain rested along the basin, caked with vomit and snot that had been spit up over the months. Their gaze was muddy, but they reflected their owner well; his body was pale, his eyelids swollen to the point where he could only peer out though faint slits. His arms violently shook as he tried to steady himself, swallowing the contents of his stomach before slapping the heel of his hand against his face. All the blows to the head couldn’t jostle the splinters of his mind back into place, his breathing becoming labored as he opened the bathroom door to meet his guest. Albert Fish tried to remain calm, his fingers strumming wrinkles out of his black tie as he rested his elbows on his briefcase. After twenty years in the south Dallas police department, Fish went into private investigation because it was more lucrative, even though it forced him to wear suits more often than he’d like. The money was good at first, but that was before Jesus had come into his life. Now, he didn’t know what to make of it, lying to himself that what he was doing was for good. If it wasn’t, he’d rather not know. His skin was leathery, pockmarks ringing around his eyelids and his cheeks slumping into jowls. Given his line of work, the fact that he looked like a basset hound was all the more apt. The trappings he found himself in had long since begun to fester, the musk of the trailer choking him from the moment he walked in. Beer cans and whiskey bottles lay in piles all across the stained foxrun. The blinds were drawn; the sun peeked into the trailer through the scattershot holes left in the woodgrain walls from shotgun blasts. The shambling zombie calling this place home staggered toward his kitchen, nearly tripping over the clutter in the living room. Albert was the only one he’d had any real contact with after his stint in jail, but things had been getting progressively worse the more he was drinking. If the spots on his brain weren’t bad enough, there was cirrhosis. And psychosis. “We found her up in Wisconsin,” Fish coughed as Brandon drew near, “took a few months but we finally have her.” Limping across his kitchen, Youngblood grabbed a box of cigars he’d smuggled into the country after a wrestling tour in Cuba years before. Dust filtered into his lungs as he flipped the top open, grabbing one of the few remaining cigars before ripping the tip off with his yellow teeth. His lighter moved in jagged weaves as he tried to light it. “Celebration...” They hadn’t made eye contact; Albert didn’t want to look at the pitiful shell of a human being standing mere inches away. “Yeah. Can’t say I blame you.” Youngblood violently swung around, his right heel giving way in the pivot. Bracing himself against the countertop, the ceramic dishes cluttering the area clanged about as he tried to keep from falling. “What’s that supposed to mean?” “What?” “I said,” he stalked over the private investigator, his arms coiling as he peered through him, “what’s that supposed to mean?” His bloated face lingered above Fish, his nostrils flaring with each breath. There was something wild and unhinged in Youngblood, and even though he’d seen plenty enough bad people in the world, Fish couldn’t help but shiver as he felt the bastard’s hot breath bellowing against his cheeks. A loud belching laugh broke the silence, Brandon’s hand combing across Fish’s scalp as he sauntered away. The touch made his skin crawl. Pulling out a chair, Youngblood plopped down, snuffing the cigar out by rubbing it underneath the table. “She uh...do you know if she...uh...” Gulping, Fish flicked the snaps of his briefcase open, pushing it up and grabbing a stack of stapled papers. “There’s a detailed log of her routines,” he said as he pushed the paper across the table. “Goes out and gets groceries every few days. Lives in a condo right by the mall. Barely ever leaves. The address is on there.” “What about my kid?” “The child is in the condo most of the time.” “No...” Brandon’s voice was distant, “no. What’s my kid look like?” Fish flicked through his own copy of the Melissa paperwork. “I don’t know.” Brandon’s lips quivered as he spoke. “You guys been around my kid more than I have and you can’t even tell me what he looks like.” “He looks healthy--” Brandon pounded the table with his fists. “How the fuck do you know it’s a he?” “I can’t tell you what it--” “Don’t talk about my boy like he’s some thing! He’s got a name. Named him Cody.” Disgust blistered across his face, but the wild burst of emotions was causing him tear up as well. Startled, Fish nodded his head. “Look, I don’t know what you’re fixing to do with the address I’m about to give you. And frankly, I would appreciate it if you didn’t tell me.” “What are you worried about?” Youngblood asked as he reclined in his chair, tears trickling down his puffy eyelids. “It ain’t like she ran off on you after making a promise to stay. While you were locked up. While the only thing that kept you going was the thought of that boy.” Fish closed the briefcase, pushing away from the table. The whole situation made him uncomfortable. “I get paid to dig. What you do beyond that? That’s your call.” As he walked away, Brandon grabbed his wrist, refusing to let go. His grip trembled as he tried to appear strong. “Albert...does she look happy?” “I suppose.” The response broke his grasp. He looked down at the laminate, the warped cracks curling upward and showing the decaying floorboard foundation of his home. As he continued to stare, he tried to maintain some semblance of composure; Melissa was right to leave him after all he’d put her through. But at the same time, she’d played him, promised she would be there when he got out of jail. Promised that their son would be there. As much as he needed to see Cody, he knew something was wrong inside himself. Bouts of sanity were fleeting. “Should I go?” Albert Fish had long since left, but Brandon sat there waiting for his answer all the same. ---- The River Walk of San Antonio was ablaze in Christmas lights, carolers dressed in Dickens era attire chanting Deck the Halls at the passersby’s with all their gusto. Melissa’s hand was nestled tight to Brandon’s, their arms lightly sashaying as they made their way around the pier. Dinner was light and elegant, just like the black dress which hugged her curves and made her all the more alluring. Even in dagger stilettos, she was the one taking the lead, unknowingly letting her boyfriend’s eyes linger on her calves as she clacked forward. His skin was tan and healthy, his chiseled figure modestly contained within his black dress shirt and slacks. The long mane of dirty blond hair he sported was fastened tight into a ponytail, his free hand buried deep into his pocket as he tried to keep pace with his girlfriend. Their world was filled only with affectionate promises. “Slow down a little,” he chided coyly, dragging his feet as the carolers continued their serenade. His grasp was gentle. She swung around to meet him; their eyes locked as she playfully tugged his arm forward. “Maybe if you weren’t so slow.” “Maybe if you knew your place,” he cupped her chin between his thumb and index finger, drawing her close with his tickly touch. She couldn’t help but squeak. “Oh, and where’s that?” “Right here.” The words caused a blush to bubble up across her cheeks, the teasing face of her love turning serious as his lips cuddled against her forehead. Every nerve within her body rippled, and suddenly, her head was overwhelmed by a flood. “Brandon...” she sighed, wished nothing more than to surrender to his tide, “stop it.” He was driving her crazy, but she wasn’t alone; the massaging stroke of her nylon covered toes across his calf from underneath the dinner table had caused a thrilling chill to undulate throughout every muscle of his body. He still had yet to shake it. Not that he wanted to. “You afraid of everyone seeing you with me?” Pressing up onto her tip toes, she draped her wrists over the back of his neck, kissing him fully on the lips. “I don’t want to make the other people jealous.” Their gazes hovered in a comfortable closeness, sharing the same breath as they focused on the heartbeat of their love. Brandon broke their stillness, a smile enveloping his face as he fought back his tears. Something else was at play in his mind, his hand jostling about in his pocket as Melissa pouted her lips. It took everything he had not to release the pent up desire to kiss them again. “So what are we going to bring for your mother’s party?” Melissa smirked. “She always makes the green bean casserole.” “Good. I don’t like cream of mushroom soup anyway.” “Oh you didn’t have a problem eating mushrooms tonight.” “I did that for you.” “You want a medal for broadening your horizons? For trying new things?” Melissa’s sass was what put her over the edge; all Brandon wanted to do was bottle her up after she ran her mouth. It made it so much more rewarding when he finally did overpower her. She agreed. “How about I make some chex mix? Just put it all into one of those disposable tinfoil trays and take it?” “If that’s what you want to do. Have to use those octagons. They taste better.” Unclasping her hands, Melissa took his once again before she began to walk. He kept up with her this time, but he wasn’t thinking about what she had said about her preference with cereal. “I’ve been thinking...” “Of?” “What you get me for Christmas?” “Oh...like I’d tell you.” “Oh come on.” “Yeah. Right.” He was on autopilot, the flames inside his chest burning a brilliant blue as he tried to regulate his breathing. He swallowed, trying his best to keep a nonchalant veneer, but the cage he kept his emotions in had been rattled to the breaking point. Every breath caught in his throat. He felt light headed. He planted his feet into the pier. She jerked to keep him going. He wouldn’t budge. Another tug. Nothing. She turned around to face him. San Antonio glowed over them as if they were the only two people in the world. “You going to stand there all day.” He pursed his lips. “I’ve been thinking...” “You’re a broke record--” “Of how to do this.” One knee touched down on the pier. His hand came from out of his pocket. She had been his princess. Now, he wanted to make her his queen. “I couldn’t wait.” His fingers opened the velvet box. The ring was silver, three shimmering clear stones imbedded in its surface. “Would you marry me?” She couldn’t move. Her mouth was agape. Her eyes blinked. Her lips quivered as hot spurts of raw emotion streamed down her cheeks. There wasn’t a second thought. “Yes.” It was their milestone moment, their bodies converging in the most important embrace they’d ever share. And in that moment, they both knew they would live happily ever after. ---- The walls of Cody’s room were baby blue, the shimmer of the corner lamp bathing the room in a comforting hue. Melissa rocked tiny Cody, gingerly bouncing him on her forearms. She didn’t understand it, but her son had no trouble sleeping in the car in spite of the steady shake of the road, so she incorporated movement into her rocking. He would gurgle as he closed his eyes. He was fast asleep, the low hum of his mother the only noise in the room save his breathing. She held his warm body against her chest as her bare feet slowly padded around the room in practiced paces. To her, he was the only thing that mattered. The ring of the doorbell startled her, her next movement to check and see if the sound had woken her son. The doorbell sounded again. She sighed, putting him in his crib and nestling him just right on the mattress before she went to answer the door. It was later in the evening, but her neighbors had knocked on her door before because she’d left the lights outside her garage on. She figured she’d apologize for the inconvenience and be on her way. When she opened the door, all the color drained from her body. Brandon stood before her, the buttons of his dress shirt haphazardly fastened and his slacks filled with wrinkles. His skin was pale save for the pockets of red swelling around his eyes and nostrils. “Hi.” She slammed the door. The door slammed into his arm. She tried to keep him at bay. She didn’t weight enough. “No!” His fingers flexed as he reached for her, his shoulder colliding with the door and prying it open. He stumbled forward, mule kicking the door shut as she scurried away. “You gonna let me explain?” He looked at his left forearm; blood was beginning to show through the sleeve, a cut coming from the door slam. “What the hell’s wrong with you?” “I’ll call the police!” “No...” he stalked towards her. “You won’t.” “Get the fuck out of my house.” “Don’t talk to me like that.” “Just leave...” she was trying her best to maintain her composure, but her skin was on fire. Her legs gave way. Slumping over the couch, she pushed her head back as far away as she could. “Please.” Youngblood stomped towards her, his spasmodic twitches flinging beads of sweat all around. “You didn’t think I’d know? Didn’t think that I would know you weren’t there when I got out of prison?” She was clawing at the white carpet, trying to get away. Her back slithered up the glass of the backdoor. “I didn’t--” “I can understand you having to leave, but not like that. Not like that. Not like that. Not like that!” He towered over her, his arms at the ready. “My son!” “Calm down,” she croaked. Their son was crying in the other room. “You ran away from me and took my son. What gives you the right?” “I--” He struck her across the face with the heel of his hand. “Shut up!” She began to break down. Her cries mirrored those of her son. Brandon’s knuckles burned across her cheek. He yanked tufts of her hair backward. He threw his hand across her face once more. “Shut your goddamn mouth!” Releasing his grasp, Brandon jolted away from the scene, his ex-wife sobbing uncontrollably as she cradled her broken face in her hands. Crying. Crying. Everywhere. Crying. Every-fucking-where. His palms raked his scalp, his breaths coming out in harsh wheezes. This was wrong. He had no right. He knew he had no right. He could taste his heart as he watched his wife try to pick herself up from the floor. His knuckles had cut ribbons of flesh open on her reddened face. Her quivering fingers grasped at the carpet as she tried to move towards him. This was wrong. He couldn’t control himself anymore. “You wouldn’t even let me explain.” He too was crying. The baby was screaming. “You wonder why it gets like this. Everyone is just screaming and making it worse! All I can hear is screaming!” “Brandon--” “What am I doing?” “Brandon--” “I need to explain.” “Brandon--” He looked away, staggering toward the foyer as the screams of his son drove him mad. “I got to go get my kid. Make sure he’s safe.” Melissa couldn’t be a helpless little pin cushion in the face of her husband’s abuses anymore. The years had made her helpless, but now, everything rode on her ability to defend her son. Picking herself up off the floor, she coiled her claws as she glowered towards her husband. “Stay away from him!” “You named him Cody, right?” “Stay away--” Rolling up his left sleeve, he tried to show her the tattoo he’d gotten emblemizing his son. Blood had cascaded and pooled around the Texas Bluebonnet. All that was visible between the trickles was the ribbon with the name Cody. “I’d look really stupid if that isn’t his name.” He turned around, reaching for the bedroom door. Melissa drove a lamp into the back of her husband’s head. He fell forward into a sea of glass. Pools of blood spurted from deep black cut. The force of the blow had caused her to topple over, falling onto the back of her husband. She pushed herself up. She grabbed the door knob, stepping into her son’s room and locking the door. The entire frame of the door shook from the other side. Brandon was violently tugging to get in. She backpedaled away from the door, her eyes peeled on her son as he continued to wail inside his crib. Brandon punched the door. The wood buckled. Another punch. His fist burst through. She yelped, mortified as the following blows shattered the door piece by piece. She screamed as he stomped inside. Even so, she knew what she had to do; defend her son. Her noble stand was quickly cut down as the monster grabbed her by the head and threw against the book shelf. The shelves splintered from the force. Before she could do anything to respond, Brandon dragged her body to the radiator just underneath the windowsill. He clasped his hands around her shoulders. He violently slammed her head backward. The first crack gave her a concussion, but she tried to maintain eye contact with him. What stared back at her wasn’t her husband. Brandon Youngblood wasn’t home. The second slam caused her eyes to roll into the back of her head. He wasn’t satisfied. He kept pushing her head back, grunting and panting as he violently bashed his wife’s skull into the radiator until her body slackened and her skin went pale. His fingertips reached back into the hole he’d made, cradling her limp head forward as he kissed her lips. The grunts were replaced by sighs. He rocked her body in the tightest of embraces. “You never let me explain. We’re gonna be a happy family. You got that?” The only response he received was the uncontrolled sobs of his son. “You listening to me Melissa? You can’t just run away like this.” Skull fragments and bits of hair suck to the radiator bubbled up from the heat. “I’m gonna show you right now,” he sobbed, “just how great a father I am.” He let go of her body, letting it slump to the floor as he reached back to pull himself up. Grabbing the spindles of the crib, he began to pull himself up. The weight caused the crib to spill over. He fell on top of it, the wood giving way under his weight. Cody had fallen out, rolling over to where his mother’s corpse rested. Wild wails were all the baby could muster. Youngblood sat there in the pile of broken crib wood, laboring with each breath as he looked towards his son. He crawled towards him, scooping his boy off the carpet and cupping his head. “Shhhhhhh. Shhhhhhh. It’s okay.” With shaky hands, he rested his son against his forearms. He was unaware of the fingerprints of his wife’s blood that he’d left on his son’s face. “It’s okay. Daddy’s here. Daddy’s here.” He rocked Cody for the first time. The feeling of joy filling his soul was beyond description. He didn’t notice that his son’s right arm was limply hanging downward, the crooked bone having pierced through the skin of his forearm. ---- The Chippewa River had frozen near its banks, the icy white surface coarse yet somehow clear. Twisted roots of birch trees jutted out of the ground, lingering down the steep shoreline. Nestled tight to Brandon’s breast, his son continued crying in loud, piercing howls. They cut him right to the bone. “You have to keep quiet, Cody. Daddy’s trying to think.” As the water trembled, its moan sent ripples through its expanse; pressure crept up in wails, blocks of ice pinning the neighboring sheets in place as ridges traveled down their fracture line. He stood there, trying to keep his son warm as he thought of what to do. “Please, Cody...” his father blubbered, the drone of his son’s sobs having demolished whatever was left of his mind. “Just stop. Stop crying.” Nearby Eau Claire was visible even this far away, its orange swirls glowing to the distant north. “I don’t know. I don’t know. I don’t know.” He didn’t know how he got there, or even why he was there. Something else was manipulating him, commanding him to do the actions because they made sense. But nothing made sense. Except this. Except now. “Time is on our side,” he said. “But it makes no difference in the end. They’re never going to let us be a family, are they Cody? They don’t understand, do they? I don’t think you understand either.” The soft, pale body of his son was only months old, but he’d already seen too much. The world was too cruel for him to live in. He rested his broken baby boy on the ice, his footsteps piercing through the howling wind as he walked to the riverbank. The ice is thinner he observed. He stomped at the edge, breaking through as much ice as he could until he had created a small pocket of cold water. It wasn’t far enough. His fingernails clawed at the edges. When that didn’t work, he punched the ice until his knuckles were bleeding. Bits and pieces spreading from the shallow bay began to bob in the water before being sucked underneath in the flow. He punched the ice until his wrists finally gave way and broke. “If I can’t have you, no one else will.” Limply lurching forward, he scooped his son into his arms. He wasn’t sure if he was crying anymore. Brandon’s wilted hands hung as he rocked his son in his forearms, stumbling towards the ribbon he’d hacked into the river. He got on his knees. Freezing water seeped through his slacks. He lowered his head, removing the blankets draped around his son with his teeth. All that was left was a kiss. Cody didn’t fit in the spot he’d made for him, so he made sure to push down on him. The last thing Brandon Youngblood saw of his son were his shoulders touching against each other. After that, the freezing tide carried him underneath. Brandon crawled on his elbows, following his son as he floated away.
