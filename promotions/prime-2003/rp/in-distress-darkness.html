In Distress: Darkness

Date: July 15-17, 2007 LOCALE: Los Angeles, California DAY: July 15, 2007 CUE: "All I Need" by Method Man ---- The sound of a bag smacking dully against the floor brought Alexander Shaw's attention. The blond-haired youth watched his trainer curiously as the stocky Canadian stood over it. With a foot, Easton pushed open the duffle bag's top. There were a few pairs of jeans inside already, but it was rather bare. He would need shirts. Shaw went back to his magazine. Easton cradled his cell phone against his shoulder as he walked back to his room, letting his eyes take everything in. He usually traveled relatively light on the road, so packing wasn't too much of a problem. Impatiently, he waited for the other end to pick up. "Hey," he said into the phone once the ringing stopped. He walked around the hotel bed and rooted around in his traveling suitcase. Easton pulled out a few shirts, giving them a cursory look before letting them hang in the crook of his elbow. "Easton?" came a voice from the other side. Easton swallowed. Every time he spoke to her, his stomach was all afire. He was married to her, though. He shouldn't feel like this all the time. "Hey, Jeanne," he said, his voice quieter now. He walked back out to the living room. "How are you?" Alex opened his mouth, but Easton shushed him with his finger to his lips. His eyes smoldered for just a second before he focused on dumping his clothing more-or-less accurately into the bag. "I'm good," his wife replied. "You're coming, right?" "Just getting my shit in order." Easton migrated back to his room, closing the door behind him. "It's been a while." "Yeah." Easton didn't need to say it, but at the same time, he did. She had been calling incessantly, trying to get him to come back so they could talk. So she could see him. It wasn't as if he didn't want to see her, but it was difficult. Now that the moment was so close, they were both at a loss. Easton wished he could have said something that was actually meaningful. "We, uh, found out about your phone," Jeanne continued. "Really?" Easton frowned as he pulled up a pair of shorts. Why did he own these? "What about it?" He tossed them aside. "It was apparently auctioned off on eBay, or at least that's what the ad said. Brian's an internet freak." She said it with a hopeless amusement that made Easton smile. "No luck finding out who it was, though." Chainz was the immediate assumption that came into his mind, but he tightened his lips so he wouldn't say it. "Well, I cancelled the phone, so it doesn't really matter, right?" "Well…" Easton stopped cold. Well? "Well what?" "I got another weird call. A… a few, actually." Easton's face twisted into a scowl. "And you didn't tell me!?" He paced across the width of his bed, tugging at his beard. "'Cause you'd get like this!" She paused for a bit. Easton felt like he was dangling, waiting for the next word. "Besides, Brian's here, so he's been… watching out." "Watching?" Packing was forgotten in favor of pacing, trying to walk the worry out of his mind. How could she fail to tell him? "There's been a guy," she told him slowly. "Brian says he's harmless." "Brian says?" Easton snapped harshly. "The fuck does Brian know!?" "Brian's here! Which is more than I can say for you!" she screamed back at him. He didn't pull the phone away. Easton tilted his head backwards, forcing air out of his lungs, trying to get himself to breathe. This wasn't happening. Nevertheless, he had to remain calm. Nothing got done without calm. "Look, hun, I'm sorry." There you go. "I'm coming, alright? We'll deal with this when I get home." "Alright." The trust in her voice warmed him. "Don't tell Brian what I said." "Fuck you!" Her voice was almost a chirp. "I'm telling him right after I hang up the phone." "Oh god," Easton groaned. Jeanne laughed. "Love you." "Love you." He found himself smiling at Jeanne's audible "mwah!" before the click ended the call. He sat down on the edge of the bed and brushed his brown hair out of his face. That small joy couldn't prevent anxiety from creeping back into him, though. Someone was watching her. He was always taught to think of the worst case scenario. Problem was, there were a dozen of them, and each one twisted his insides up something terrible. LOCALE: Toronto, Ontario (Toronto Pearson Airport) CUE: "The Beef is On" by Big Daddy Kane ---- Easton lifted his duffle bag over his shoulder, glancing around curiously. No sign of Brian yet. He'd get a cab if he had to, but that was the greater evil. He'd rather pay for the gas and have a private journey. "You son-of-a-bitch!" Easton blinked, whirling around. Instinct told him to cover up and he did, his duffle bag coming to the floor, biceps just barely deflecting a set of knuckles from his midsection. Easton backed up quickly, straightening up to see a smiling face greeting him. Brian Jackson tipped his blue baseball cap over his light brown hair and booted Easton's bag. "Pick up your shit, man." "Dammit, man, you're gonna give a man a heart attack," Easton said. He reached down and hauled his bag back up. "I thought it was some crazy fan…" Brian rolled his eyes and shoved him lightly as he walked past. "You're not that famous yet," Brian told him matter-of-factly. "Yeah?" Easton said, catching up to him. "Remember Kelly?" Brian shot him a venomous look. "You mean Kelly the-girl-I-fuck-on-a-steady-basis Kelly?" Easton's eyebrow raised. "Didn't realize you were with her." Brian shrugged. "Sometimes you just need to be a good person to the fans," Brian said. "Doesn't hurt to have rugged good looks like me, either." Brian actually looked more like a 16-year old kid – sans acne, to his credit – than anyone Easton had ever met at his age. Geography homework did have a way of making a man rugged, though. He didn't have to look to know that Brian had a smug grin plastered on his face. "Anyhow," Brian went on, "I almost didn't come pick you up, you know. I was still crying over what you said about me." Easton sighed. "Come on, man, let it go," Easton said. They made their way outside. It was rather nice out; Easton didn't think he could have handled rain. "The guy around the house is harmless," Brian said. "Seriously. Don't worry about it." "Did you get a good look at his face?" Easton's voice was suddenly hard. He grabbed Brian's shoulder. "Did you?" Brian turned around on his heel, glowering at Easton. "It wasn't him, Easton. Let it go." Brian pulled away, tugging his cap back down. "But you're not sure," Easton said quickly, hustling after him. "You're not sure, are you?" "Yes. I am sure." Brian's whole body radiated annoyance, but Easton wasn't going to be deterred. "So you saw the guy's face?" Easton pressed. Brian gave a mirthless laugh. "You really like calling cabs, don't you?" He started into the street, holding up his hand to get a car to stop as he made his way to the other side. He glanced over his shoulder. "Just drop it, okay?" Easton scowled, but he fell silent. He knew it was going to eat at him the whole way back, though. If it was Chainz, the man was sicker than he thought. He would take time off just to come to his home and terrorize him? He knew what Chainz was capable of. He didn't want to take the chance here. LOCALE: Vaughan, Ontario CUE: "Jazz (We've Got)" by A Tribe Called Quest ---- The car pulled into the drive of the house that Easton called his own. HIW superstardom had bought him the house, in Toronto to be near his family rather than in the US where his commitments were. The building was three stories, quite broad, and sported a red-bricked Victorian style that Easton found a bit too fancy for his tastes, but Jeanne had picked it. That was really why he could call it home. Easton stepped out of the car and started up the driveway. He hadn't walked two feet before the front door opened. His slim, dark-haired wife stepped out from the house. She had never been shy, but that only partly explained why she could wear a long white shirt out of the house. She looked like a girl at a sleepover. Easton smiled. Every bit as beautiful as he remembered. Almost hesitantly, she stepped onto the asphalt of the driveway, unused to walking there barefoot. But soon the sight of him emboldened her and she was moving quickly. Easton dropped his dufflebag and caught her as she rushed into him, lifting her off the ground. She was only a few inches shorter than he, but she often said that it felt as if he were a foot taller. Her legs wrapped around his waist and she kissed him, hands exploring his grizzly hair and beard as if they were new. He held her tightly, remembering the feeling of having his wife close. Jeanne pulled back, fighting for breath as she rested her forehead against Easton's. Her dark eyes glowed. "Hi, Easton," she said, a wild smile on her face. Easton mirrored her, pecking at her lips again. "Hi, Jeanne." Suddenly self-conscious, Jeanne tugged down at her shirt with one hand. "Let's go inside. I bet the neighbors are looking at my panties…" Easton laughed, casting a look around to make sure that no one actually was before carrying her inside. Brian, forgotten in the couple's reconciliation, could only watch helplessly as Easton walked into his house. He grumbled to himself as he picked up Easton's duffle bag, thankful that Easton always traveled lightly, and carried it inside. He hated being the third wheel in this relationship. It always relegated him to butler. It wasn't until late in the day that Easton and Jeanne got time alone, though. Jeanne had taken it upon herself to get ahold of Easton's parents and they had driven right over. Easton had quietly mocked his older brother when his mother, still drifting along in an almost weightless dance that would have made her charming if she wasn't quite so bubbly all the time, told him "Isaac says hi." No questions had been brought up about work. He was here to get away from that, if only for a few days. Easton sat up in bed with Jeanne snuggled tight against him, her arms wrapped around his stomach, head under his arm. Her eyes were closed. His, however, were open. His body was tense. He was like a wolf now, waiting. All this time he had been worried about this stalker, been tearing himself apart about it, and now he was here. He wanted to see for himself. He wanted to get to the bottom of this. Jeanne's eyes slid open and she looked up at him. The peaceful smile on her lips quickly faded. She sighed. "He's not going to come," she said. "Why not?" He lifted an eyebrow at her. Fleetingly, the thought that this could be a ruse to get him to come back washed through his thoughts. He refused to believe his wife was that deceitful. "He just… won't. Trust me, Easton." She rested her chin on his chest, staring at him insistently. Easton's chest rose as he drew in breath. How were she and Brian so sure of everything all the time? They didn't know. They didn't have proof. He didn't bring up Brian, though. He let himself be silent. "Mmm, come on, grumpy bear. Don't be like that." She leaned up and kissed him, but he didn't put anything into it. He couldn't help being worried. He needed to deal with this. DAY: July 16, 2007 CUE: "Madness" by Deltron 3030 ---- Easton sat in the living room, feet propped up on the coffee table. The television was on, sucking his thoughts away. He needed it. Inane cartoons raced across the screen. He felt hands on his shoulders and tilted his head back, looking up to see his wife. She smirked and kissed him on the nose. "You feel tense," she said. "Can't help it," Easton replied, looking back at the television. "What're you thinking about that you can't help it?" she asked, massaging his shoulders. Her voice was soothing, and that, as much as the massage, was helping him to relax. The topic she brought up left a knot of tension under that, though. "The guy," Easton said. She stopped massaging him and pushed his head forward. Blinking, Easton twisted around. "I told you he's not coming back," she said. "And what makes you say that?" Easton challenged. She scoffed, shaking her head in disbelief. He almost liked it when he made her use that look. It seemed to bring out the features on her face he liked the best. "Because you're here," she said, turning her eyes back to him. "Everybody knows this is Easton Hall's house, and nobody's gonna fuck with a big burly wrestler." Her voice had the edge of mockery, but Easton dismissed it with a sharp nasal exhalation. "You don't know who it is," Easton said. "It could be anybody." Jeanne's fists went to her hips. This look he didn't like so much. "And who do you think it is?" she snapped at him. Easton blustered, waving his hand as he tried to bring out something that skirted what was really swirling through his brain. "A… a stalker!" he cried. "Somebody dangerous, somebody who wants to break in here and—" "Who. Do you. Think it is." Her eyes were narrowed, her hooks sunk into his cheek. This look he hated. This look said she knew too much, she'd seen too much. Easton could never guard himself properly. He hesitated before he spoke. "I think it's Sloan," he said quietly. He swallowed and brought his eyes up to his wife's. "I think it's Chainz." Her mouth worked frantically and she jabbed an accusing finger at him. Finally, she threw her hands up. "I don't believe this. She took a few steps backwards and then stabbed her finger back at him. "It has to be about you, Easton, it always has to be about you!" "What!?" Easton pushed himself up, his knee on the couch cushion. "What are you talking about?" "You think it's Chainz because then he's doing this to get at you." Spelling it out cut him deep, but he didn't let the anger he'd assumed slide out of his grasp. Where he was merely angry, though, she was livid. "Everything, for three years, has been about you!" "I'm worried it's Chainz because I know what he can do," Easton said, reining in his voice until anger only just barely bubbled into it. "Because he's sick, and if it is him, then he needs to be stopped. And if it is, then I can stop him before this happens." "You want more justification for ripping him to pieces," Jeanne accused. Easton clenched his jaw. "You want to know who's sick, Easton? You're sick!" "I'm sick?" Easton responded, eyes wide. He stood now, slapping himself on the chest. "How am I sick!?" "You're sick in the head!" Her jaw trembled as she processed her thoughts. "You think… you think that everything in the world revolves around you! Just because you have that stupid title does not make you God, Easton! Just because you were the HIW World Champion does not mean everything is about you!" This time, it was Easton's turn for his mouth to work, for no words to come. This time, though, he had nothing to say. He could only watch as Jeanne stormed off. He was just trying to protect her. He was. DAY: July 17, 2007 CUE: "Doper Skiller" by Viktor Vaughn ---- "Where the fuck are you going, Easton?" The question had caught him by surprise. Easton was sitting on his bed, repacking what clothes he'd used back into his duffle bag. He blinked and swallowed hard, pushing the flap back onto the bag. Brian's voice, full of rage that Easton could not understand, repeated itself: "Where the fuck are you going, Easton?" "Back to LA," Easton responded, not turning around. He could see Brian's shadow moving across the floor, coming closer. "I have a show." "Are you out of your goddamn mind?" Brian snapped. "Why are you going back there?" "Because I have to. I have commitments." "When was the last time you fucking walked down the aisle with PRIME!?" Brian howled. Easton felt Brian's cap smack him on the back and he shot up to his feet, whirling around to face the other man. Brian didn't back down. "When did you exchange vows with PRIME!?" "I have to earn money," Easton said. He didn't need to take this, but he wasn't about to deck his friend. Not yet, anyway. "Bullshit!" Brian stepped forward again, snarling. "She needs you here, Easton. You know that!" "The guy's not coming back," Easton said, wishing his voice didn't sound so weak. "She's lying. Can't you fucking tell when your wife is lying to you? I told her to tell you that, all of that. Those two days, that guy fucking came back. You were in your room, with her, where you should be, not freaking the fuck out. Because you always freak out." Easton gritted his teeth. He didn't press her. He should have pressed her. He let his breath go. "Well then you have everything under control, don't you?" he growled. "Don't be an asshole!" Brian shoved Easton backwards. "Hey!" Easton shoved right back, but much harder. Brian stumbled back, losing his balance and catching onto the bed at the last moment. He still hit the ground, but he managed to come to his feet quickly enough. "I have to do this. This house isn't paid off, none of this shit is secure. I have to earn the money, don't I?" "It doesn't matter," Brian told him sternly. "None of that shit matters. Fuck PRIME. You could come here and work and they'd pay you an assload of money because you're Easton fucking Hall. You can work anywhere you want and they will die to get you on their payroll. But more than that, she needs you here, and you need to be close. You remember what happened with Justin?" Easton took a step forward, fists clenching. "You son-of-a—" He reached forward to grab Brian by the throat, but Brian backpedaled, smacking Easton's hand away. "Don't touch me," Brian snapped. "Titan St. James doesn't matter. The Sound and the Fury don't matter. Jason Snow doesn't matter. Chainz doesn't fucking matter. What matters is you being here, with your wife, and getting your fucking head out of your fucking. ass." Easton glared at Brian, unblinking, not thinking about anything. "Get out," Easton said. Brian wore a disbelieving smile, shaking his head before turning and slamming the door behind him. Easton shut his eyes, tilting his head back and drinking in air. This mattered. It did. This was his future, his career. He could work anywhere, but PRIME was his ticket up. He had commitments. Easton didn't break commitments.
