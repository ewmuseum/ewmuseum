Enoclophobia: n. fear of crowds.

17 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/17/07 Standard trip, heading out for just a weekend away to the beach in Monte Carlo. Why there? Knowing a little Italian and just the ability to enjoy a glamorous place for a while was a nice diversion. Sure, he didn't do it often, but it was a nice place to go once every year and a half or so. The giant man exited the plane of Arika Israel Airlines. He hefted his carry-on bag on his massive shoulder and stepped out into the terminal. The air was dry, but familiar. He took a deep breath, getting back into the feeling of the arid atmosphere after the cloying breath of the Mediterranean humidity. His long steps took him from the terminal out through the detectors. His IDF ID was palmed in his left hand, and each time he came near a security official, he carefully rotated his hand to reveal it. They'd take one look, look up at him and wave him on by. Advantage of being the giant of the Israeli Defense Force was that security knew who you were almost anywhere in the country. His boots thudded dully on the carpet while he reached into his pants pocket for his keys. It was nice to be back from the glitz of a vacation and be getting back to the routine grind of business. It bothered him, not protecting folks in his own way. Sure, taking a break was necessary, but having a lack of purpose just didn't sit well with him. Those long strides made the crowd part behind him and left a small wake as he passed. Glint His eye twinkled. Had the vacation made him see things? The glittering lights of the casinos and the shining surfaces had given him the jitters, as they always did. He had trained to keep an eye out for the telltale signs of firearms in tight proximity, especially given how much he stood out. Glint His eyes widened. His heartrate climbed. He forced himself to breathe evenly. There was a man with a firearm in the crowd, and he had seen the light reflect off of it twice, which is more than you ever get, really, until it's too late. He glared, looking for that face he had memorized. There. It was a man in his late teenage years. He had that light sheen of sweat of nerves. His eyes were alight with demented determination. His breathing was not even. Titan dropped his bag and moved for him. People staggered out of his way. His arms reached out and he bellowed the one word that would get everyone away. “GUN!” The crowd scattered everyway at once. Titan's hands closed in, locking on the man's wrist and throat. The gun couldn't raise any higher, but it was already level at his waist height. He reflexively pulled the trigger. Titan felt the round slam into his leg and he gasped. His hands gripped convulsively, snapping his wrist and his grip pinching at the little man's entire neck. He gasped, dropping the revolver and going for his throat with the other hand. Titan heaved, lifting the man by his throat and slamming him roughly to the floor. The wood base of the floor cracked and broke under the impact, along with the sickening snap of bone. His eyes went wide and he went limp. Titan sat down numbly, like a big child, still keeping his breathing even. Slowly, the pain in his leg worked its way into his mind. He looked down at the gaping wound that a .44 magnum round had torn into his quadricep. * * * * “Titan, we never go to the movies.” “There are good reasons for that.” “Okay, what are they?” “One, I can't fit in the seats. Two, there are too many people. Three, they charge too much.” Stephanie looked up at him, her brows furrowing. She really wanted to go out to the theater and watch a movie with him, for no other reason than they never had. He gave her a patient look with those cold blue eyes. He carefully steered the Escalade around a corner and settled into the traffic. Stephanie returned his look and crossed her arms petulantly. “I just don't get it. You don't want to take me to the theater, or to a play or anything like that. Why not?” He took a deep breath. Before he had just avoided the question, diverting it, but she had also never asked it so directly before. He took a deep breath and leaned over, clicking off the radio. “You could just ask to listen to something else,” Stephanie quipped. “I know you're not a big country fan, but that's no reason to...” “It's not about that,” his rumbling voice cut in. “You asked me a question, and I wanted to talk. Listen, you already know about me. You know I'm...” “Dealing with things,” she responded. “Yeah, dealing with things,” he replied. “It goes a little deeper than that. Do you know what enclophobia is?” Her eyes looked at him, searching. “I know what a phobia is...” She let her voice trail off. “It's fear of being in crowds. I know you want to go see a movie. I know you wanted me to take you to Broadway when we were in New York, but I can't do that.” His hand tightened on the steering wheel before he forced it to relax. He was shaking, just thinking about it. She nodded slowly. “That... that explains a lot, Hunter,” she said softly. Her hand rested softly on his hand. “Waiting for it to come out on DVD won't be so bad.” “I'm sorry,” he said. “I know there are things you want to do. It kills me to not do them with you.” * * * * Titan walked up the steps to his home, a classic Southern house with a wraparound porch. He had reinforced the steps so that they wouldn't break, but really, so they wouldn't creak. Very carefully, he opened the front door, took a long step to his left and, at the same time, reached into the endtable there and a lightswitch. His hand came up empty and the light flicked on. That nondescript little man sat in one of the padded chairs, making him seem like a child. The massive .50 caliber pistol in his lap only reinforced the illusion that Titan knew was a fraud. Profile 21 looked up at him, his eyes unreadable, but his face a mask of false amusement. “If it were anyone other than you, that would have worked,” Titan said flatly. “But, it was me. I thought I'd check back in on you after your return to the IDF. That could not have been easy.” “I went to be a drill sergeant,” Titan responded. He walked away from the door, but kept his profile turned towards the small man. “I'm going to assume you'd like a glass of bourbon.” It wasn't a question, and the other man nodded, as if his mind had been read. “Obviously. It's strange. You found a vice that I particularly enjoy, yet hadn't known of.” He looked at the weapon in his hand and set it aside. “This is a weapon appropriate for you, though I don't know why you bother with such a cannon. You know very well a .38 in the right place will do the same thing.” “They don't make .38's in my size,” Titan responded coldly. The relationship between these two was never friendly. Titan was pretty sure they never actually had any kind of positive feelings towards each other except for respect. “As for your time as a drill sergeant, I see it didn't dull any of your education.” The small man spoke in a level voice, never changing pitch or inflection. “Though, I do imagine this life of competitive entertainment is more pleasant for you.” “To a point,” Titan agreed, extending a glass tumbler of dark liquid to the small man. A gloved hand took it almost delicately. “Airports are still a problem.” “Ah, yes. That interesting fear of yours. I actually find myself somewhat envious of you at times. It would have made my work to be habitually more... detatched.” He took a slight sip and nodded. “It's very true, it's not bourbon unless it comes from Kentucky. Fascinating.” “Envious? You sanctimonious little prick.” Titan took a sip from his own glass. “Yes, yes, it's a debilitating fear for you, I know.” “Actually, you don't.” That was a first. Titan knowing that 21 didn't know something. The small man's face betrayed nothing, but it was stillof note that he turned to face the big man. “Is that a fact, Titan?” “It is.” “Enlighten me.” “Are you familiar with where it started?” “It grew outward from an incident in the Tel Aviv terminal. You were shot in the leg by a terrorist when you spotted his weapon. I believe you were returning from a trip to Monte Carlo, where you had incidentally come out ahead by a good four hundred and fifty dollars, American.” “For knowing so much,” Titan said, easing into his specially made recliner, “you don't seem to understand it very well.” “Despite all the research I've ever done, I have yet to plumb the depths of the human mind. Incidentally, my research did turn up something interesting on your girlfriend.” Titan's eyes lit up. “What are you doing spying on her?” he asked, his voice grating. “Oh, calm down. She could have been an elaborate plot to get back at you, for all you know. Some amatuer is always trying that James Bond garbage. But she does spell your same... martial condition, I believe is the best way to put it.” He took a sip, watching for Titan's reaction. Titan made himself proud. It wasn't something he himself advertised. “You mean she hasn't...” “Never.” “Interesting.” “The topic never came up?” “Not yet. Regardless, you were interested in what happens.” 21 slowly tilted his head. “Was I? I don't recall saying anything of the sort.” “You didn't. Believe it or not, I can read you.” The even gaze pulled more out of him. “Sometimes,” he admitted. “There's a good reason why I agreed to instruct you. You are quite talented. Proceed.” “It's not debilitating fear. It's me stopping myself. Being in a crowd, my perception goes wild. I see the same glinting off of pistols as I did off of him. And I very nearly missed it with that piece of garbage. I seize up because, if I don't, I'm going to start laying into the people around me.” “Such a contradiction to the appearance. How do you get around the airports?” “Contractually, PRIME usually provides me with some sort of transit from one flight to another, so it's not in the terminal. But the renegotiation of my return has me on a private plane now. Even first class commercial seats just don't cut it.” “Very well, Titan. Let us drink to continued success.” “Continued?” Titan said, skeptically. “I'd be happy with starting with success.” “A like-minded woman, your parents are alive, you have a rather lucrative job. Your success has already begun.”
