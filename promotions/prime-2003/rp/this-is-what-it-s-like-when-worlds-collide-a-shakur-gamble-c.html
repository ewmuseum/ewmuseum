The young man behind the register couldn't be more than nineteen years old, possibly saving up money to buy that old seventy five Camaro he saw in the classified section – runs good, needs slight tune-up – the perfect car to impress the ladies. Well, after a new coat of paint and some flashy chrome rims. "Very well," the man replied. "Just add a medium coke to that and I'm good." Accompanied by the digital sounds of the register as the kid punched in the order, he repeated the order to the gentleman. "Turkey and ham on rye, the soup of the day, and a large coke." "Medium coke," the man corrected with a bit of attitude. "Right," the kid blushes a bit at the mistake, as a nervous twitch of his lips signals his uneasiness. "Medium coke... That'll be eight dollars and fifty seven cents." "Three oh seven," another man said as he reached for a tray of food atop the counter, more of a question than anything else. "Yes sir," came the kid's reply as he took a moment to turn away from his customer to face the five foot nothing frame of a man in a white, two button, pullover polo shirt and slightly weathered khaki pants with frailed ends that hung loosely a few centimeters above the white tiled floor of the establishment. With his black hair slicked back with an absurd amount of hair oil, the man carried his tray full of food and drinks across the fairly busy eating establishment rather nimbly, an impressive feat considering how busy the place is and how loaded the tray he’s carrying happens to be. He goes for a short while until he reaches a table where a familiar face happens to be waiting. It's the somber look of a woman with other things on her mind than food. Her eyes have bags under them that cast shadows as large as a building over her once colorful and vibrant cheeks. The woman looks up to the man carrying the tray, who has now put it down on the table and has started passing the food across the table. She accepts a salad plate and a soda, then looks up with an almost forced smile creased across her lips. "I guess they don't understand what salad on the side means?" "I can take it back," came the reply from the gentleman, but a shake of the woman's head was the only response he received. "It's not a problem, Cindy. I'll go back an-" "It's just going to end up on top anyway," she cut him off. "Don't worry about it." Cynthia Berkman has been through a lot in the last year. From having a psychotic fool that probably was not breast fed as a child, to actually losing a child... The woman known to the wrestling world as Violet Rayne has not had it easy. Then again, neither has her lunch date. "Tony." The mention of his name lifted his head away from the grilled chicken sandwich on his plate. "You called me Cindy." "Yeah," he said with a genuine smile that accompanied the sadistic grin that permanently adorns his face like an old abstract painting. "It's going to take some getting used to, but I figure I'll get used to it after a bit." 'Get used to it.' The words brought a smile to her face, a reassurance that he planned to stay around for a while. "I need to get used to hearing you say it too." The slight glow of a teenage schoolgirl could be seen beneath the shadows of death that still surrounded her. "How's your salad?" "Good," was the reply. "Mom," the yell of a child behind her caused her to whip her head around, only to find a little boy tugging at the pants of his mother. "Can I have an ice cream cone?" Her head lowered for a moment, only to lift when the back of Tony's hand brushed against her cheek. "You alright," he asked, a look of worry in his eyes as he noticed the thoughts of that night in the tears welling up in the corners of her eyes. "I'll be okay," she answered. Truth be told, she knew it would take a long time before she could ever truly be okay. She had lost a child, and everything in her head told her that it was his fault. Her heart wouldn't listen though, and the war between her heart and her mind would make her cry herself to sleep at night. "Just something else I need to get used to." She reached for her drink, only to topple it over on the table and watch as the fluid dripped over the edge and onto Tony's lap. "I'm sorry," she apologized as he jumped up from his seat. "It's okay," he assured her. "I'll be right back." "Well, I better get back to work," the kid said after he dried his hands. "Them sammiches don't make themselves." He then proceeded to pick a wedgie out of his ass before he walked out the door. Tony shook his head before he looked at himself in the mirror, an image of a man with no purpose in his life. How had he arrived at this point, where he had no friends and the one person he cared about kept getting hurt. Time and again it was proven that he could not protect her from his enemies, and that she would be a constant target for them. He wanted to turn her away, to make her believe that they were not meant to be together, and that she would be better off without him. But how could he do that now, after all that has happened. He would just look like the bastard he has been categorized as for the last few years. Was it not true though? This facade of a man that he has created of himself, was it not just an exaggerated image of the man he truly was. A man only out for himself, and to hell with the rest of the World. He only had to get what was coming to him, and screw the rest, because nothing else mattered in his life... No one else mattered. Or was that illusion created more for himself than anyone else? He needed to swallow his ego and make her feel safe the best way he could. She needed to be kept as far away from the business as possible. It was the only way to be sure she could be safe.
