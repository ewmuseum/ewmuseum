Don't Worry (DUAL HALO)

17 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/17/06 Don’t worry. Don’t worry about yourself. Don’t worry about the others. Don’t worry about your family. Don’t worry about your friends. Don’t worry about your career. Don’t worry about money. Don’t worry. Reach for the Halo. 42nd Floor, Tokyo Dome Hotel, Tokyo, Japan, World…the morning. “AHHH!! Oh, God! No! NO!!! Please, no! PLEASE! AHHH!!” … Nova hugs the walls of the shower, attempting in vain to keep his extremities out of the flow of bitterly-cold water. “GET COLD! WHY WON’T YOU GET COLD?! What…*sob*…what did I do to des-ahhhh, yes…” As warmth spreads over his legs, Nova closes his eyes contentedly and steps into the spray. “I knew it would get ho-AHHH!!!” Nope. He had just peed himself in fear. The water is still very cold. “TOUCHE, JAPAN!!” Some time later… Nova steps out of the shower, a towel wrapped around his waist. The bathroom air is pleasantly full of thick swirling steam. The kind of steam that makes it difficult to see in front of your face. The kind of steam that makes it difficult to see the rubber ducky that you just had to have at the 99 Yen store because you thought it would look really cute floating around in the bubble bath you had been looking forward to since you got off the plane…and let’s be honest, you wanted someone to talk to, didn’t you? Yes, you did. “Now, where’s my ACT mouth-wooooaaahhh!!” With a taunting squish sound, the ducky shoots out from under Nova’s foot, and said foot shoots out from under Nova’s body. The rest of him follows. CLANK!! As he falls, Nova’s head ricochets off the edge of the toilet seat, and he collapses on the wet tile. Blood begins mingling into a nearby pool of water. Fade out. What’s the matter, not laughing anymore? … Thump, thump. Thump, thump. Thump, thump. Open your eyes, Nova. Nova shoots upright, and instinctively a hand goes to his head, though he doesn’t know why, or give it much thought. After a moment of reorientation, he looks around. He’s sitting on the floor of an empty white room…empty save for an enormous flat-screen computer monitor that spans the length of one of the walls. It’s on. Nova stares unblinkingly at the multi-colored lines shifting and changing on-screen. “Disk Defragmenter…for…for my brain?” That’s correct. Nova’s head spins around, searching for the source of the answer. “Hello?” The human brain isn’t altogether unlike a computer, Nova. A constant rearrangement of files and maintenance is needed to keep you moving as fast as you possibly can…only in this case, the “files” are your memories, your thoughts. “Please come out where I can see you,” Nova asks, focusing his eyes on the computer screen for lack of something better to look at. Not yet. “Well, can I smoke in here?” Yes, but I don’t think you have any cigarettes. “Shit. You’re right. Do you have one?” There should a pack of Camel Lights on the ground by the screen. “Hey, great! That’s my brand!” I know. Nova lights a smoke and awkwardly tosses the pack back on the ground. “Where am I? I must be dreaming.” Not…really. But you aren’t where you should be. Well, you are, but you’re here also, and the you that’s here is the important you. Would you like to see yourself? “I…I guess so.” The Defragmenter cuts out, and the screen goes black for a moment before a blurry whitish-reddish image comes into focus. The white is the walls and furnishings of the bathroom in Nova’s Tokyo Dome Hotel room…and the red is the blood collecting around his head. Nova gasps as he sees himself on-screen, sprawled across the floor, unmoving. “What…what happened? I don’t remember getting out of the shower.” You did. And you slipped on a rubber toy and fell. Your head hit the edge of the toilet bowl, and you passed into unconsciousness…and came here. “I need to go to the hospital! Why am I here?!” You are here because there is a good chance that you will die, Nova. There is a high probability that you will lose too much blood before the maids clean the 42nd floor, and you will pass into a coma that will eventually sap your body of all its remaining strength. Nova’s knees give way and he sits down on the floor hard, unable to pull his eyes away from the grisly image on-screen. “I’m…I’m going to die?” I did not say that. I said that there is a good… “What the fuck is the difference?!” Nova shouts, running a hand through his hair, his eyes wide now in fear. Do not be fatalistic. There is a world of difference. With one, you have a chance to survive. With the other, you perish unequivocally. “Oh, God…,” Nova says softly, taking a drag from his cigarette, “but…but I don’t want to die! Not now! Not on the bathroom floor of a hotel in Tokyo!” And it is good that you should not. “Fuck,” Nova hisses, “FUCK! What can I do? What can I…you. YOU!” Yes? “I don’t know how you know all of this, but you can do something, can’t you?” Nova says, his eyes glowing with hope as he points a finger at the screen, “Can’t you?! Is that your purpose?” My purpose is more complex than you have the ability to comprehend. “That’s fine!” Nova replies, flicking his cigarette away, “but you have to do something!” There are powers within and without my abilities. “You have to help me!” he pleads, “I can’t die now! There’s so much for me to do still! I’ve worked so hard, and I’m so close to breaking through, I know it! And my daughter…my wife…” EX-wife. “EX-WIFE,” Nova growls angrily, “fine! Whatever! Please…it can’t end now. It can’t end now…please…” No response. Nova looks around. “Hello? Hello?!” … “Oh, God, no…,” Nova moans, burying his face in his hands, “It can’t end now…” In silence, he sits, despair cloaking him like a heavy winter coat. At the touch of a hand on his shoulder, he jerks around…and like a punch to the stomach, the air leaves his lungs. His jaw hangs agape. In front of stands a tall, beautiful woman clothed in a long white robe, light brown hair cascading over her shoulders. Her eyes seem to smile down at him. Hello, Nova. “I…I…,” Nova searches for the words that won’t come. “How do you look…” I chose this image to represent a terrestrial form, the woman replies. It is an important image to you. You know why it is an important image to you. “It’s what…what I always pictured my mom to look like,” Nova answers softly, moisture welling up in his eyes. He wipes it away quickly. Your mother’s name was Mary, the woman says. For the sake of a name, you may call me that as well. “Mar…*cough*…Mary,” Nova says slowly as his heartbeat pounds away in his chest, “Why did you choose this?” I chose this as an image of comfort for you Nova, because you aren’t going to like what I have to tell you. “What do you have to tell me?” Nova asks, standing up. I cannot help you. You will die there, on the bathroom floor of your hotel room. As though he might need a reminder of what that looks like, Mary gestures to the computer screen again, at the image of Nova’s body and blood. “WHAT?!” Nova cries, “You said there was a chance I would live!” There was a chance. However, it has passed, as chances are want to do, and you shall pass with it, out of the world and into Oblivion. “NO!” Nova shouts, “No, that’s unacceptable! Why? Why won’t you help me?!” I do not feel that our encounter will have any significant impact upon the course of your life, Mary replies, stone-faced. With the gift of renewed life, you would continue as you have, and eventually, you would die the same as you are. I see no reason to lend you my aid. “How can you know that?!” Nova protests. Because this has happened before. Nova gapes openly at his cerebral Mother-image. “This…has…” Please direct your attention back to the computer screen. Nova obliges…and watches in amazement as Mary appears to browse through the files of his memories, double-clicking folders and moving scroll bars. Tropical Turmoil, August 28th, 2005. A night you remember well, I imagine. “Of course. I got injured that night in the Main Event.” You certainly did. But there’s a part of that night that has remained up to this very point in time a secret from you, Nova…a part of that night that I would like to show you now. Mary double-clicks on the Turmoil Contender’s Match link, and the screen is replaced with the inside of the Stan Sheriff Center in Honolulu. The fans are on their feet going bat-shit as Nova stands perched atop the turnbuckle post, Aimz beneath him on the mat in a prone position. He holds up an arm before leaping off with a Shooting Star Press that never sees completion as Youngblood rams into him and halts the turn, sending the Rising Star down neck-first onto the mat. The replay is paused. You know of this. You have watched the tape countless times. But what happens next will surely be a surprise. “I know that Angelo…” Just watch. The screen is unpaused, and a second or two after impact with the mat, the arena and everyone in it disappears…except for Nova still lying on the mat…which is now the floor of a white room completely bare save for an enormous flat-screen computer monitor that spans the length of one of the walls. “Oh my God.” I think you know what happens here. We will skip ahead to the important part. Everything on-screen is suddenly boosted several notches. Nova zips around the room, examining his surroundings, and then zips back to the computer screen, where he stands, shaking back and forth for ten or fifteen seconds. Then a figure zooms on-screen over to him. “That has to be you. But…you look like Ariel!” The image of your ex-wife was what you needed at that time. The two on-screen figures appear to talk to each other in fast-motion for several seconds. Now pay attention here. The screen slows down to real-time. “Please!” Nova cries, “please, it can’t end like this! There’s too much for me to do still! I’m so close…so close to making it, after all this work! And my ex-wife and my daughter…if I die, I’ll never be able to reconcile to them! I’ll never be able to get my family back together!” Pause. Nova stares at himself in shock. You made promises to me that night, Nova. You promised that you would return, better than ever, and overcome all obstacles in the path to the Universal Title. You promised that you would reunite your family. Nova hangs his head, suddenly embarrassed. And for a while, it appeared you would keep them. You fought through the injury with fiery vigor to return at the end of the year. But after your impressive return match, you began to slow down. You became scarce, winning sporadically-booked matches, while others ran with the ball that you once only dreamt of being in the court to play with. And beyond that, your relationship to your family has become distant, almost formal. Under the weight of the truth, Nova suddenly feels several feet shorter than the figure next to him. You did not keep your promises, Nova, Mary says, turning to face him with eyes simultaneously fiery and ice-cold, so I ask you now, why should I grant you a third chance, when so many never receive a second one? Steeling his resolve, Nova turns his face back up to hers. “Because I can make it right this time.” Can you? “Yes. I know I can.” The time is now. “I know that.” If you fail again, you are lost. “So be it.” Mary stares at him, her eyes still mesmerizing in their intensity…but then they cool back into soft brown, and a small smile tugs at the corner of her mouth. So be it. Before Nova can breathe a sigh of relief, Mary’s hand is on his forehead. Above the two of them, golden flecks of light began swirling in a small circle. Nova’s eyes wander up to them, but Mary snaps him back to attention. Listen to me now! The Halo has a Dual definition…it is a symbol of extraordinary opportunity, of reward that accompanies excellence above all others…but it is also a symbol of tranquility. Serenity. Knowledge. All things come with focus, and the most powerful focus is the least focused one. To hang on, you have to let go. Close your eyes. Nova closes his eyes as the bright light above his head intensifies. Don’t worry. Don’t worry about yourself. Don’t worry about the others. Don’t worry about your family. Don’t worry about your friends. Don’t worry about your career. Don’t worry about money. Don’t worry. Reach for the Halo. Nova can feel the light pulsating above him, shaking the very foundations of the room. Reach for the Halo. Against overwhelming pressure, Nova opens his eyes, looks heavenward, and thrusts his arm into the light. 42nd Floor, Tokyo Dome Hotel, Tokyo, Japan, World…the morning. Nova steps out of the shower, a towel wrapped around his waist. The bathroom air is pleasantly full of thick swirling steam. The kind of steam that makes it difficult to see in front of your face. The kind of steam that makes it difficult to see the rubber ducky that you just had to have at the 99 Yen store because you thought it would look really cute floating around in the bubble bath you had been looking forward to since you got off the plane… …difficult, but not impossible. Nova snatches up the rubber ducky and places it next to the sink before stepping through the door into the main area of his hotel room. Light peeks through the drawn curtains, illuminating odd little sections of the room. “I bet it’s a gorgeous sunrise,” Nova says to himself as he moves over to the window. A sunrise…a new beginning, full of possibilities and hope. Nova throws back the curtains and steps out onto the balcony. But what he sees isn’t a gorgeous sunrise. It’s a vast expanse of thick, black clouds moving in. And he knows a storm is coming.
