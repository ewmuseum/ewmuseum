The Fifth Day

21 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/21/06 Day 1 Darkness. The only thing one half of the heavy metal heroes Seek and Destroy could see anymore. He felt a various number of shooting pains and aches for sure, that he could live with. The blackness, the immobility, the noises in his head screaming out all at once then going silent, those he could not. “Electric” Eric Evans, who has already been known by many names, lay in a sterile hospital room. He could feel the constraints of the gauze and bandages that covered his body. He could feel every place he had been hit. He could do nothing anymore, not even sit up and eat a meal. Jason Bass never left his side. He couldn’t see Jason thanks to the massive amount of swelling over his left eye, and the bandages over his right, but he knew he was there. He could hear the big man breathing in the corner. Never before had he felt such pain, such fear. The worst part was that it was all completely random. Evans had never met, nor spoken to, nor even glared at him. Even now he couldn’t think his name. The trauma had been set in that deep, that “The Electric One” couldn’t even think it. His breathing had gotten better. A number of bandages had been wrapped around his ribs, and while he coughed from time to time, he felt that simply breathing was a gift at this point. Eric would cough blood, and know he had because he could taste the coppery fluid in his mouth, violating whatever calm he had gained. Sometimes Jason would play Eric’s guitar lowly, trying not to disturb his best friend. He tried to stay away from the heavier songs, which always sounded goofy coming from an unplugged guitar anyhow. Mostly the Jamster just picked a few open notes and thought about what had happened…or worse what might have happened if he hadn’t come when he did. Yet it wasn’t enough. Jason kept telling himself that he could have done more. He should have done more. He never should have let Eric leave the room after a piece of tail when he needed to practice. No, Eric had done that a million times, or so it would seem. This was purely coincidence that he was in the wrong place at the wrong time, and he got caught off guard. Eric could feel everything around him, and sometimes he would try to talk, but the pain from his jaw made all his words slur. Eventually he just gave up completely and was content to lay there thinking about his happy place. The only place he could go to at this point. Visions of great shows would come and go throughout the day. Every time Eric would be fine, jumping about on the stage, playing his guitar, catching ladies unmentionables and planning for later. He loved that job, being a lead guitarist, being a ladies man. Would he ever look good again? His face was so beaten at this point that he was sure he had bruises on his bruises. Maybe if he hadn’t hit that water fountain with such velocity he would still remember the rest of the debacle. Truth be told, he didn’t remember much of it past being grabbed in the face and the entire world going dark. The entire world was fraught with pain, and right now he was in enough of it to have a degree. Eric thought a lot about the pain, and tried to focus away from it, but it never worked. He had to lay there, sometimes trying to move but failing, and take it all. He hated needles. The worst part of this entire experience was having the doctors miss the veins in his hands over and over trying to put in an intravenous needle. He didn’t really mind so much though because the morphine that came with it was a nice way to dull the sharp jabs he got whenever he took too deep a breath. That fiery liquid would burn its way up his arm leaving a trail of sweat wherever it went in his body. He didn’t really like it, but hell, it was a lot better than feeling like a piece of steak that had been hit with a claw hammer half a million times. Yeah a claw hammer, maybe that would even the score! No, he took a serious look, well not literally, more like metaphysically at the situation and realized that it would never pay back the one who had did this to him. Swift brutal justice was not the action to take this time, not against this man. Not against this Devil. Ah yes, then there was the wonderful food that he ate. Not really being able to see what you are eating is a great way to broaden your taste for cuisine. He tasted a various platter that consisted of strained cabbage, strained chicken, strained peas, and strained carrots…or so he thought. It was killing him. He needed a hamburger, a steak, anything he could sink his teeth into… Oh god, do I still have my teeth? These were the things he was forced to contemplate as he lay in that hard bed, listening to the doctors talking about his case. This was not at all the way he had hoped the night would end. He had hoped to unwind and drink a couple brews, watch the game, and well there was still the matter of that vixen. Who was she? “Electric” Eric Evans finally rested that day, after he had just plain had enough of trying to remain coherent in his living prison of bandages. Jason looked up from the guitar a bit to hear a slight snore coming from his friend and smiled. Tomorrow was another day, and another day meant he was that much closer to being better. As such, being better meant that Eric was that much closer to his sweet revenge. That was a notion that made the rocker smile a little in his sleep. Day 2 To say that this day was better than the one before, well that was a fairly accurate statement. Now instead of hearing assorted bits of mumbling and off notes through the blood clotted up in his ear, he could accurately hear what Jason was playing. Son of a bitch, the big guy was playing some song from the 80’s. Sounded like Flock of Seagulls. Figures that Jason would like them though, he is a weird one. The big man usually had a much more diverse musical taste than Eric though, who mostly stuck to his guns. Mmm. Guns. He could seriously use one of those right now to…no wait, he couldn’t move all that well yet. Dammit, he was really hoping that today would be his day too. On the bright side, he had started to finally see small cracks of light through the swollen shut eye that had been recently closed for him. The more he thought about it, the more he decided that he needed to find a way to get even. The question was though, how does a person stop a monster? One does not simply walk into the cave and slay the dragon, or in this case The Red Devil. No, a person who would attempt that needed a plan, a good set of armor, maybe a great weapon, none of which Eric possessed at the moment. Sadly he was relegated to lie on his back, his thought only his since his mouth wasn’t up to working well yet. Come to think of it, he hadn’t said a word in a long time now. That was a feat in and of itself that his adversary should be proud of. He had faced a lot of adversity in his life, and none of it had made him back up and shut up the way this had. No, normally he was chock full of witty comments and smart assed remarks. This act that could leave him flabbergasted and confused, it must have been one hell of a fight. More like one hell of a slaughter, he had to imagine. He went over the information again and again in his head and nothing ever seemed to add up. Why did it happen? Could he have let his guard down that bad? Sure he had chased a lot of tail in his day, but this wasn’t really any different. She was giving him that same come hither look that he had gotten from plenty of other women. She was wearing the slinky clothes and perfume. She was practically begging for it actually. Wait…she WAS practically begging for it. Something inside the head of Eric Evans clicked and he suddenly realized that this was no mere chance encounter like his adversary had claimed. This was indeed a setup, a prey upon his weakness. Or was it? He was hurt pretty bad in that exchange, and it wasn’t like him to jump to conclusions. Yet at the same time, the numbers just added up that way. He didn’t remember seeing her once while he was flying about those corridors, or when his head was slammed through Nelson’s office, nor when he was being carted from the arena on a stretcher. What exactly was she doing there? Would he ever get an answer? Eric tried to sit up again, and while he was pretty successful this time, he immediately doubled over in pain and coughed his lungs away. Jason stood up and put his huge meat hook on Eric’s chest to help him relax and lay back down. The bigger of the band members was forced to sit around and contemplate the vile act that had put his best friend in that cold bed. He seriously wondered if Eric could even hear the words he sang or the notes he played. He wondered if Eric would be able to compete any time soon. Ah yes there was that match this week to get ready for, and they had to split up. Oh well, that was still quite a few days away. He had almost an entire week to prepare, and what’s more he had some good partners to work with. His buddy was more important to him. From time to time throughout the day Eric would attempt to move, to be met with sharp pains, and the notion to not move again. The impulse kept being pushed by the point that Jason was watching a marathon of Maury Povich and nobody sane could handle a days worth of that crap. How many times a show could what Eric assumed to be a fat lady from the hood say “My Man Aint Yo Baby Daddy!” To be truthful, it made the grammar Nazis around the world to cry when this show was aired. Yet apparently Jason loved it. The big lug. Eric could barely keep the crack in his eye open anymore thanks to the assortment of wonderful drugs that kept him from being racked in pain. Oh well, at least he was still alive and getting better. Maybe tomorrow he could sit up, maybe he could even stand? Who knows? In a weeks time he had another match though, and there was just no way on earth he would ever let his partners down. The man with many names finished off his day the same way it had started, listening to a mix of Maury and Jason’s voice. The big galoot would laugh at the crazy people on the show, then it would be quiet for a bit and some broad would yell out some racially slanderous term that made Jason yell “OH SNAP!” and Eric would try to smile imagining what it looked like. Yeah, today was a little better than the last. He had made enough progress to be proud. Something inside him told him that it was time to rest though, and though he could barely see cracks of the lights in the room, he kept that eye open as long as he could. He never wanted to only see darkness again. Day 3 On the third day of Eric Evans hospital trip he came across an epiphany of sorts. Things up until this point in the wrestling game had gone a little different than he intended. He never intended to be laying in this bed waiting for his body to heal the massive trauma that had been inflicted on it. Hell, he had never really intended for Jason to break his bass, nor had he intended to almost be sued thanks to his own blatant disregard for safety. He had never intended for PRIME to look at him like…what was it that HE called him? A living cartoon character. That was it. A real life cartoon character eh? Well Eric had never intended to be called that or seen as that. Somewhere between the closing and slight opening of his eye again he realized a lot of things, and that he was in a world he did not understand anymore. This wasn’t a giant show, like he had hoped. This wasn’t just the next big gig. This was real, and brutal. Eric moved a little, and it felt pretty good for a change. His arm was the first thing to move, and he reached out, trying to get Jason’s attention. The larger man reached out and took his partners hand, grabbing it in his own massive paw. Jason had probably been there the entire time, and Eric was grateful, but man, he needed to be told a few things. And for the first time in three days, Eric spoke. “Jam, you really need a shower.” It wasn’t much, but hell, it was the most pressing issue he could think of wasting his words on. Eric groaned a little and tried to think of something else to say, but nothing came out. He was exhausted already, and he had done nothing. Damn that man, that devil. Why did he have to vent his hostility onto Eric? What had the heavy metal hero done to him that pissed him off so bad? The real answer? Nothing. Eric wasn’t a bad guy, he was misunderstood. Some people perceived him as cocky, maybe brash, arrogant, and headstrong. That wasn’t really true in all cases. Eric was a very smart guy, and he hid it behind many layers of the persona he had created for himself. “Electric” Eric Evans was a façade really. The real man behind that gimmick now had to reap the reward for being a big mouthed bragger. Jason yawned a bit and stood up, heading for the bathroom area. Eric was left to continue the mental debate which had plagued him for three days now. What the hell had he done? What was with that girl? What is going on? These questions, never ever seemed to add up in his head. There were just so many variables to consider, and the problem, when at an end, always popped up a new situation. Tyler Nelson could have been behind it. That almost made sense. Nelson is pretty underhanded, and he had the money to pay someone to divert Eric’s attention and send him in the path of the devil. Things started to add up for The Electric one. Tyler could very well find some woman who he knew would turn Eric’s fancy… No, that was stupid. Nelson was a lot of things, but he wasn’t a fool. Why would he go farther than to admonish a petty talent? Well, a petty talent in his eyes. Someone like Evans and Bass, they were worms to him, and he would hardly use such tact to try and remove them from his hair. On top of that, they destroyed his office. He would have probably skinned anyone else if Evans hadn’t already been skinned. Something inside of Eric wanted to believe that Tyler actually felt bad for him, but hell, he knew that type of guy. Tyler probably did in some small way, whatever way was attached to his pocketbook. If Evans can’t compete this week in that six man tag team, then he is paying Evans for nothing. That just can’t happen, not in Tyler’s world. Evans saw Jason come back over to his side and he looked as though he had tried to wash his hair by the fact that there were still some remnants of soap stuck in the thunderous hairdo. That and for once it didn’t stick out like it was inspired by Dr. J. Eric dug deep and found the power to try and sit up. He pushed with everything he had and though it hurt like hell, he leaned forward on his own power and sat straight up. Jason pumped his fist as his good buddy sat upright and clapped him hard on the shoulder, eliciting a groan from the still sore rocker. “Sorry bud” Evans tried to force a smile and looked to his cohort in crime. “It’s okay Jam. Look bro; I need you to do something for me.” Jason nodded. He would do anything for his blood brother. “I need you to go and get ready for your match next week. Look man, you are a good guy, and you have a long way to go before you are ready to be in this game without me.” Jason didn’t like the way he was talking, but he listened as Eric continued. “See, the thing is man; I don’t know if I can hack it next week. That bastard really did a number on me dude. So even if we were on the same team…” Eric pauses briefly, weighing his words. When he finally came to grips with the reality of the situation, he addressed his friend. “I’m no wrestler Jam. I’m a rock star, and rock stars aren’t meant to do this sort of thing. If I had half of your heart…If I wasn’t such a big phony. My whole getup, this stupid hair dye, everything I’ve done. It was all in the name of rock and roll.” Jason starts to say something, but Eric grimaces and holds up his hand. “This isn’t rock and roll man. Sitting in this stupid hospital because I got in a fight I couldn’t finish…That isn’t rock and roll.” Jason nods and turns, silently and slowly walking away from Eric. He knew Evans was right, but he wouldn’t admit it. “The Bad Mutha Plucka” stops by the door to look back as Eric is staring out the window at nothing. “Dude, sometimes you have to stand up and say fuck ‘em all man. You got a long way to go before you can be in this game without me as well, from the looks of things. Get some rest bro, tomorrow will be a better day.” With that, “Jammin” Jason Bass closed the door behind himself. Eric still looked out that window to the sky, wondering what the hell he was supposed to do. One thing was for sure, another day closer to being better would make a lot of difference. He lay back down and closed his eyes. Silently, he drifted off to a sweet dream that brought a smile to the corner of Eric’s mouth. Day 4 There is an old saying that things are darkest right before dawn. The fourth day of Eric’s hospital visit was as such. Whether it was the pain, the fact that he still could barely move and the next show was creeping closer and closer, or the fact that Jason was now out training...well Eric was starting to feel a lot of fear. He would look to the door, hoping, nay praying, that his buddy would come back, but he knew better. Jason was a man of his word, and if Eric told him to stay out there and get ready to compete, he knew Jason would. The rock star lay back in his bed trying to clear his mind of the troubles that had beset him now. The real problem was that he had no affirmation that everything would be okay. For all intensive purposes, if Jason hadn’t come to his help on the show last week…god knows what HE would have done. People like The Devil, they don’t understand common laws and right or wrong. They simply assume that being bigger means that they are better. Well, when Evans thought about it, didn’t his royal beat down confirm that very notion? If he could have stopped it from happening, he would have. That was a mighty big if though, and he didn’t have it in him. That brings us back to the original problem, HIM. How could Eric ever go back to this game if HE was there? How could he ever see a shadow lurking and not think it’s HIS? How could Eric ever sleep again knowing HE is out there? How could he ever hear footsteps coming down the hall and not wonder? The loss of his usually sure fire attitude was really going to kill his wrestling game. Well what little game he really possessed. He had promised himself not to go back to the events that occurred, not to try and change thing in his head. It was done, and he was a fool. He was in this hospital because he was a damn fool that had pussy on the brain. HE was just the divine lesson that had to be taught. “The Electric One” knew he had to do it. He had to get better, and in a hurry. Every bone in his body might hurt, but that was no excuse. Every shadow that lingered might be HIS but it couldn’t matter. He thought back to his past and thought about how he had faced many types of adversity. He had played gigs when he was sicker than a dog, barely making the show before running backstage and puking, then passing out on the concrete. He had taken some sick bumps before in fights, and he had doled some out. Come to think about it, this entire professional wrestling thing was his idea really. Jam was still out there. He needed Eric to be a better wrestler, and Eric needed him. They were a tag team, and they fed off of one another. The strengths of them could easily make up for the weaknesses. What Eric could plainly see now was that he had to have one driving force, one plain view. He had to have a plan of battle, not just rush headlong into the next day like he had always done. “Electric” Eric Evans sat up again, and this time it didn’t hurt near as much as before. He knew it might really have pained him deeply, but he was in no position to let that matter to him anymore. He was reminded of several great wrestlers that came before him and how they stood up to say to their enemies: You can crush my body, but you cannot crush my spirit. He clenched his left fist and reached to clench his right, only to notice it was completely taped up. He paused to look at the rest of his body and pondered to himself. Perhaps tomorrow would be a better day to get riled up Eric. Right now e genuinely felt like he had at least one more day here. Evans lay back down, but this time he didn’t simply sleep. He closed his one good eye and lay in thought. Pretty soon things would come back around, and it would be HIS turn. It was simply a matter of how and when at this point. Yeah, he knew it was going to be better tomorrow. He knew he was going to feel like fighting again, and he knew eventually the monster under the bed would die. Eric Evans had never been surer of this. Day 5 Eric awoke with a jump. He sat up in his bed and though it was painful, he decided that today was the day to test all his limits and learn what he could and couldn’t do. He was damn sick and tired of waiting for someone to come and get him. He was sick of rotting in that jail cell known as a hospital. Eric pulled his fist together in his left hand, and then pulled the right together, even though it almost killed him to do it. The shooting pain that ran up his arm was not at all dissimilar to the one he felt when HE originally crushed it. Man, what was Eric thinking, half powered trying to punch someone who was in full on kill mode. That wasn’t really his brightest moment, but then again, he had a lot of those not so bright moments lately. That all aside, Evans knew he had to get moving today. He looked himself over with his good eye and grimaces as he took too deep a breath. Had to be the ribs didn’t it? Well that notwithstanding he powered one leg over the side of the bed, then the other. Pretty soon he was sitting fully upright, and ready to try to take his first step in five days. Eric paused a minute, thinking of the implications this could have if it failed. It couldn’t fail. He placed one foot down onto the cold hard tile, and then the other, sliding off, and standing. My god he was standing! It felt pretty good, even if it wasn’t at his full height. People really take just purely standing up for granted. Eric had all he could do at the moment just doing so. He looked at his feet and wiggled his toes. Everything seemed to be working, so now there was but one thing left to do. “One foot in front of the other bro, that’s all you have to do.” He struck forward on the maiden voyage of New Evans with his right foot. It planted well, and he decided what the hell, lets try the left. Forward with the left and all went well. He was finally walking and it felt pretty damn good to do it. Well, as good as it could feel for being as banged up as he was. Evans could only feel one thing inside him right now. HIM. It was calling out to him, and the rage was starting to build. It had been five days and Eric had yet to even speak his name. Well that too had to change for this to be a successful transition. He looked at his hand and the I.V. needle attached to it and ripped it out. Small droplets of blood came out of the back of his hand, but that was hardly anything compared to the week previous. Eric looked to the wall, at the coat rack. His trusty leather jacket was hanging there, and that became his first goal. He closed his eyes and in one instant everything came together. “baltasar.” At first it was just a whisper, a voice he barely used. It was sufficient enough to start the ball rolling though. He took a step, then another one. His legs felt a little better every time. “Baltasar.” He clenched his fists and grit his teeth, seeing that spot on the wall, his jacket, and what it meant to make it there to him. Eric was damn sick and tired of feeling sorry for himself and waiting for something to happen. He had to seize his opportunity, lest it never comes again. “Baltasar!” Eric could feel the rage in him building now, as he took another few steps closer. The Rocker could feel his blood pumping; he could feel the sweat boiling as his body rejected what he was doing. He had to continue to move forward though, as much as it hurt, he had to make sure that the man who had done this to him got what he deserved. He had finally gotten the urge to fight back, and this time it wasn’t going to be a weak punch…No the devil would wish he had never ever met “The Electric One”. He came to a stop right before his jacket and opened his eye. The brilliant blue that normally hid behind the walls of red and black hair shone through, at least on one side, burning with anger. Eric had found his will to fight. With one final thought, he screamed into the quiet hospital and grabbed the jacket that hung on the wall. “BALTASAR!!!” Somewhere in the day, a man walked on his own power out the doors of the hospital, bruised, cut, hurt, but not beaten. Some people might have seen him as odd walking with a limp, his eye covered in a bandage. He had cuts upon his face and a black and blue mark on his cheek. For what it was worth, “Electric” Eric Evans had never felt better.
