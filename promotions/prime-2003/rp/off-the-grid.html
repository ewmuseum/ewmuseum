Date: Chapter 1 of "Whereabouts" Somewhere over the Caribbean Sea... Cloudless skies weren't supposed to suddenly shift into increasingly foreboding shades of grey and purple. Were they? Looking out the front of the small turbo prop Cessna plane, however, that's exactly what Ben Castor had seen in the past 10 minutes. A picture-perfect Caribbean afternoon had rapidly transformed into a skyscape that looked like it had been rubbed over with a piece of charcoal. Naturally, Ben Castor was worried. Especially considering he'd only been licensed to fly solo for sixth months. Especially considering the rather precious cargo sitting in the cabin just behind him. He steadied himself at the controls and checked his readings, trying to keep his bearings and point the jet towards Montego Bay. There were just 80 miles to go, but as the sky grew blacker and the wind kicked up yet again, Ben knew that 80 miles might as well be an intergalactic distance. Then came the thunderclap. He'd never been this close to the actual rumbles of thunder before, so when the roar coursed through the jet, Ben Castor started to shake so violently he thought he might drill through the floor and find nothing beneath him for 20,000 feet as the vast blue expanse of the Caribbean grew ever closer. Autopilot was supposed to make everything easier, at least that's what his flight instructor had told him. As the sky shook the Cessna once again, an on-board computer wasn't doing much to comfort him. It was at this point Ben made his first move towards the radio since clearing the tower in Dallas-Fort Worth a few hours prior. "Cessna 190 inbound calling Montego tower, requesting weather update. We've got some pretty rough skies up here, please advise" he said. He'd practiced the lines over and over until he knew them by rote like an Amish youth knew Scripture, but proper aeronautical dialogue wasn't his biggest concern right now. A bolt of lightning dropping his plane out of the sky like an industrial bug zapper was a slightly more pressing matter. Thankfully, a distinctively Jamaican voice crackled back over the radio. "Cessna 190 this is Montego tower control," said the voice cutting through the static. "Sudden weather shifts have caused a logjam down here, please divert to nearest possible airstrip." Oh goodie. Nerves compounding exponentially by the second, Ben quickly reprogrammed the jet's GPS computer to target the designated backup coordinates, a smaller, private airport just a bit further up the island. It wasn't 10 seconds after he felt the plane begin to bank left that the plane began to rattle like Jerry Lee Lewis one more time and the log book dropped out of its overhead compartment and directly onto the top of his head. Thankfully, that served to knock Ben out of his robotic state of fear, and that's when he decided he'd held off long enough. Switching back to autopilot, Ben reached for the cabin door and cracked it open, poking his head out to address the petite brunette in the front seat who'd been his wife for all of 12 hours. "Alicia?" "What is it, honey?" His spouse was obviously nervous, evident by the fact that she'd already set down her half-finished copy of the Dallas Morning News, and from six years close friendship and intimacy, Ben knew that Alicia Jacobs (now Castor) wouldn't put down her newspaper even if she saw the gremlin from Terror at 20,000 Feet dancing the macarena outside her window. "You may want to come up here. Weather's getting pretty rough and we're going to have to divert to a smaller airport." Silently, Alicia slipped out of here chair and moved into the co-pilot's chair, strapping herself in tight. Somehow, despite the inherent terror and uncertainty of the situation, the newlyweds managed to smile as they grabbed each others hands. "Hell of a honeymoon, huh?" Alicia asked. "Hey, it's still not as bad as telling your mother we finally got engaged. You know that woman never liked me." Then, in an instant, the darkened sky exploded in a brilliant flash of white. ~~~~~~~~~~~~ Four months earlier Somewhere over Central Texas... "I still don't know how you talk me into this stuff." Troy Douglas was no stranger to flying. His career had taken him across the globe a half-dozen times over, and he'd jet-setted around the continental U.S. on more commercial airliners than he could count. He'd just never sat in the co-pilot's chair of a plane being flown by a guy who Troy had personally seen chug four Bud Lights in 60 seconds. This troubled him in some ways. The fact that Ben Castor seemed intent on making this "practice run" as hellishly nerve-wracking as possible troubled Troy in even greater ways. But, as always, the silver-tongued Castor had an answer. "Because you're gullible enough to try anything and I'm not stupid enough to take anyone else but you up here. Also, you're the only person guaranteed not to tell Alicia." "And that matters ... why?" Then Ben Castor's face shifted into an expression very familiar to Troy Douglas. The same coy, mischievous look he sported every time he suggested another late-night pub crawl or impromptu house party. The "I've Got Something Up My Sleeve Look", as Troy had once deemed it. And this time, Troy didn't really want to know what Ben Castor had up his sleeve. "You're not ... no ... just, just no." "Yes." "NO." "Yes." "For the love of God, Ben, tell me you're not going to fly this fucking thing for your honeymoon." "Fine, I won't tell you. Doesn't make it any less true." Troy said the only thing he could really muster. "You're an idiot." "Think she'll like it?" "Sure, but it doesn't mean you're any less of an idiot." "Oh, you want to know how much of an idiot I am?" The wicked expression on Ben's face grew even wider, and then, he pushed the pilot's chair back, closed his eyes, kicked back and started steering the plane with his feet. Troy's heart jumped for a moment, until he realized that the Cessna was not, in fact, spiraling into a massive nose dive. "I really should kick your ass." "Hey, I'll turn this plane right around, Mister." ~~~~~~~~~~~~ The present day Rorschach's Pub, Savannah... When he heard the familiar tune of "Over the Hills and Far Away" emanating from the iPhone in his right pocket, Troy almost decided to ignore it. His phone had been ringing off the hook ever since he got into town from Dallas, and frankly, he was tired of nonstop calls ruining the first real night on the town he'd had on the road in months. Lindsay Troy had beckoned him to this venerable Savannah nightspot a few hours before, and Douglas wasn't one to start turning down the woman who signed his Empire Pro paychecks. Now on his fourth Sam Adams of the night, Troy was finally relaxed and having a good time when the damn phone went off one more time. He waited a second, but after his conscience won the impromptu battle royale in his mind, Troy stepped into the bathroom hallway and answered. "Yeah?" He hadn't recognize the number when he picked up, and he certainly didn't expect the voice on the other side. "Troy? It's Marc Castor." Ben's little brother? What, did I leave my rental tux at the hotel or something? "What is it, Marc? The wedding IS over, you know. No more planning or crazy, uninhibited bachelor parties. You can actually calm down." But, Marc Castor was anything but calm, and when Troy heard him mention the world "airplane" to someone standing near him wherever he was, Troy instantly snapped to attention. "Marc, what happened? Just tell me." Nothing was going to prepare Troy for what came through the phone next. "They ... uhhh ... they're still not quite sure what happened." "What happened? Damnit, Marc, what the fuck is going on?!?!" "The weather suddenly shifted, and they got caught in the middle of a thunderstorm. Last contact had Ben and Alicia diverting from Montego Bay to a smaller aiport. Five minutes later, the tower reported a lightning strike near them ... and they dropped off the radar. They never made it to the airport. ... "That was six hours ago." ~~~TO BE CONTINUED~~~
