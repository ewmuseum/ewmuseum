God Is In The TV (Prologue)

Date: Deadline day What part of life do you want to handle. Rochester, New York September 21st, 2002 Hours after the early-bird caught its first worm, the rest of the world wearily drags itself out of bed. The sun gives vitality to what was an anaemic, watercolour sky, evaporating away the thin layer of cloud as if the sky was being removed from its packaging. The squeaky wheel of him Mom’s bike announcing his arrival, the paperboy weaves back and forth across the street, hurling the morning news with unerring accuracy in each and every direction. The plastic wrapping marked 17 KANNON spins mesmerisingly through the muggy air, skidding up the dewy lawn upon landing. As he passes the home of Christian and Eleanor Kannon-Hall, he goes to ride up onto the same stretch of sidewalk he does every day, between the Escalade and the BMW 7 Series… only to find the well-polished black flank of a Mercedes blocking his route. As the bike wobbles, weaves and creaks, the paperboy somehow manages straighten it up beneath him, then dart his gaze back and forth to check none of the neighbours caught his near miss and began to ask any questions about who scratched the Cadillac Seville last week. Glancing through the Kannons‘ window, his gaze bounces back off another‘s. Standing at the window, a pale Eleanor Kannon-Hall stares out of dry eyes at the black BMW, narrowing a spiteful gaze as if to try and penetrate the ominously tinted windows. Her eyes switch down to the newspaper for just a split second, before returning to the mysterious car, looking broken when she sees its still there. The resentful gaze culminates in a shiver, prompting her to tighten her black silk robe around her body. She hears a haphazard tumbling rumble down the stairs as her weary husband descends, letting gravity do the work for his battle-ravaged body. Auburn hair frizzes out like the banister was wired up to the mains. Jogging bottoms hang lewdly low around his bruised hips. “They’re back,” Ellie snaps, wounded, as she blows past her husband without so much as a sideways glance. “Earlier.” Plunging his thumb and forefinger deep into his eye sockets, Kannon sucks a sharp intake of air through his teeth and rubs away the sleep from his eyes. His whole body tries to communicate, “It’s too early for this.” The air is expelled harshly from his nostrils, before his face retreats into a wince as Ellie sets about venting frustration and fear in equal measure by making the loudest mug of coffee known to man. “So I guess you’re just going to do… what was it again? That thing you did last time?” her voice takes on, if possible, an even more snaky tone in saying. “Oh, yeah… that’ll be nothing.” Kannon goes to offer reassurance, but sensing the words coming, Ellie slaps the radio on to cut the words off at the tip of his tongue. “I’m going back to bed,” pounces into the silence just a millisecond after she turns the radio back off. “Do something, or I‘m staying at my Mother‘s.” This was 7:03 on day three of a very, very rare four day break from Primetime Championship Wrestling‘s will-straining road schedule. Thus far, it was going the way of days one and two. “What do you want me to do?” Xavier poses, arms stretching out to barricade the doorway. “They’re just parked there. What are they going to do? We have neighbours who notice if someone‘s grass is half an inch too long!” “Fine,” Ellie huffs, deciding after all that she doesn’t want the coffee and pouring it straight down the sink. “Whatever. Why don’t you go and offer them an espresso. Maybe say they can pull up in the drive tomorrow, if they want.” She drops the mug into the washing up bowl, a depth-charge that sends a fountain of murky dishwater splashing across the worktop. “Or go ask for a brochure,” is her parting shot, fired from a sneering corner of her stressed, dry lips. “See if any of what you spout on TV is actually in there.” With a submissive sigh, Kannon steps towards her, opening his arms for a hug as he fills the exit route of the doorway. She simply bumps into him, letting out a grunt to make her point. His hands make a snap judgement and lock around her forearms, frustration giving way to restraining. She looks down to them, then up to his eyes. Her eyes bluntly ask if he really wants to do that. He doesn’t really want to do that. Closing his eyes, Kannon counts the fourteen steps up to the hallway, mouths the six seconds for Ellie to scurry along the hallway, then drops his defeated head as their bedroom door slams. “That’s it,” he decrees, punctuating with a sharp nasal inhalation. Grabbing the nearest t-shirt off the radiator, Kannon mauls the garment as he yanks it down over his tattooed, bruised and stitched-up body. Aiming a stress-relieving kick at a stack of newspapers, Kannon strides through the living room and into the hallway. Yanking the front door open, Kannon invites a waft of morning breeze in, catching the newspapers in flight and leaving them swirling in his wake. Hurdling the raised flowerbeds around the porch, Kannon touches down on the grass, a slight curling of the lip disguising a wince triggered by his right knee. ““You,“ he growls, hobbling down the lawn until his walk rectifies itself. Tugging his jogging bottoms up, Kannon descends upon the Mercedes, channelling animal instincts by puffing his chest out and spreading his frame. “You want something, you come up the path and ring the fuckin’ bell, okay?” sets the neighbours’ tongues wagging. “You hear me in there?” Kannon awkwardly hurdles the hedge, thankfully now awake enough to avoid face-planting onto the sidewalk just as the neighbours appear at their windows. Face flushed red with venting rage, Kannon steps out into the road in front of the car, swinging his arms above his head, just in case he needed to announce himself to anyone. “I’m right here,” he aims through the tinted windows, flashing a wave before slapping his chest. “I’M RIGHT HERE! You have something to say? Tired of hanging up every time we answer the phone?“ Suddenly, Kannon makes a lunge for the door, sharply yanking on the locked handle. “You’re not bored in there?” he asks in wild, defeated laughter as the engine purrs into life. “Make yourself a nice ‘gone stalking’ mixtape?” With Kannon still gripping the handle, the Mercedes bounds into reverse, sending him skidding across the asphalt as he holds on a second too long. “See you tomorrow,” Kannon growls as the car cruises off into the distance, leaving him to drag himself up, knees bleeding through shredded jogging bottoms.
