Men, or Abandoned. (ReVolution 193, singles match vs. Chandler Tsonda)

29 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 29th, 2009 Real men have dogs, and the bond that man shares with his dog is impossible to truly understand if you are... A) ...a woman. or B) ...not a real man. And in 2009, when larger and larger percentages of males are wearing makeup, frequenting tanning booths, engaging in manicures, pedicures, every other kind of \'cure,\' and willingly squeezing into jeans intended for their younger sisters, you\'d best believe that real men are few and far between. It\'s not as often to find a poker-faced fellow sitting at the bar with perma-dirt forged in his fingernails with skin like beaten leather and a five o\'clock shadow that showed up at six thirty in the morning when he punched in for work. Let\'s make a long story short. For the most part, the next generation of men are a bunch of fucking pussies. But back to the subject of real men and their dogs. If there\'s anyone who knows what pain is, it\'s the real man, but for all the nicks, dings, scratches, scars, concussions, broken bones, missing fingers, hospitalizations, amputations, and tragic accidents resulting in early retirement or death, there is no greater pain for the real man than the loss of his dog. Yes, there\'s a grieving process. Yes, it\'s okay. Step One: Denial. \"No, that\'s not my dog, my dog\'s out back where he always is.\" Step Two: Anger. \"I\'LL KILL THE MOTHER FUCKER THAT HIT MY DOG! I\'LL FUCKIN\' KILL HIM!\" Step Three: Stiff Upper-Lip. \"It\'s alright, honey, I\'ll be okay. He was gettin\' old, was only a matter of time...\" Step Four: Anger...again. \"FINE! RUN OFF, THEN! GOOD-FOR-NOTHIN\' MUTT!\" And after all that, you can bet your boots, that the real man will sit down, finish his beer, and finish the process by bawling like a little baby. * * * * * * * In the days following PRIME\'s one-hundred and ninety-first broadcast of ReVolution, \"The Bad Dog,\" Wade Elliott, found himself in a bar with a glass in his hand more and more frequently. Which, for a man like Wade Elliott, who spends most of his time on a barstool anyway, isn\'t a good omen. The death of a man\'s dog is one thing. The abandonment of a man\'s dog is completely different. The \'Bama Bruiser was thrown into a rough spot that night when Angus the Bluetick Hound refused to follow his master in favor of the heel of Lindsay Troy, and after moving through the grieving process (we won\'t go into details of Step Five,) Wade dealt with the loss of his beloved canine that only way he knew how. With whiskey. Of course, the pup\'s decision wasn\'t the only thing to be drowned out by Jim Beam\'s inviting tune. There was, of course, Lindsay Troy herself, and the explosion that was their falling out at ReVolution one-hundred and ninety-one. And on top of that, the decimating of PRIME Hall of Famer Killean Sirrajin, as Wade made the rash move to super-plex the legend through a table at the end of ReVolution one-hundred and ninety-two, which will no doubt lead to more violence. But that might have been the idea. Truthfully, for a man so foreign and opposed to change, Wade Allen Elliott obtained a different kind of glare in his piercing blue eyes in the weeks after those two broadcasts. A thundercloud gaze once belonging to the loyal protector now shifting to that of the rotten aggressor. It didn\'t take long to get used to. It\'s easy to abandon those you watched over ferociously when they\'ve abandoned you, when the very, very few allowed within your personal bubble suddenly hurl you back to an outcast\'s realm, and it\'s especially easy for one who has spent the majority of his life there. It makes this particular grieving process much shorter, and less noticeable. And as it should be, The Bad Dog sat, alone, at a ragged, round wooden table in the corner of a dingy tavern whose name he never cared to remember in a town he wouldn\'t be in for long. There was an aura about him, some sort of seething invisible glow that kept the other patrons at bay. Under the haze of a dim yellow light he sat, deep set eyes gazing at what remained of his tumbler of bourbon, resting comfortably between his big mitts. He garnered no attention. At all. It was a familiar feeling to Wade, being so alone. So many drives, so many hours to himself prior to the discovery of Angus, to the friendship of Lindsay Troy, to PRIME itself. Simple. That was the word for it. Life had become very, very complicated for a very, very simple man, so as he sat in his chair, warm bourbon swishing in his glass, it wasn\'t loneliness he felt. It was relief. But not for long. Elliott raised his head to survey the tavern, only to find it...empty. They were all gone. The folks at the bar, the good ol\' boys playing pool, the bartender himself. Even the sounds had vanished. Only the buzzing of the dim yellow light. Gone. Wade\'s facial expression didn\'t change, he only paused. His eyes started to squint, his muscles tensed, instinct preparing him for, well, anything. His vision flickered. He squinted harder, breathing in deeply through his nose. Shapes, bodies, flashed in and out in the chairs surrounding his table. He was unable to identify them, they came and went too quickly. A familiar drone invaded his eardrums as the images continued to jaunt in and out of the three chairs. He slammed his eyes shut, holding them closed tightly for what felt like minutes, then opened them once more. His vision now stable, Wade was able to recognize the three figures surrounding him, specters that had haunted, assaulted, and attempted to guide him for the past year and a half. Elliott\'s lips curled inward as he exhaled through his nostrils, the visitation of these spirits hardly a surprise. To his left, a seemingly dead Confederate soldier, wearing a heavy, blood-soaked white beard, eyes white and hollow, teeth yellow and black with decay, clad in the tattered gray uniform of the Rebel Army. To his right, a coyote. A thin, tan-haired creature that had once exposed to him bloody scenes from the past, offering him gifts of vision that Wade had denied. And sitting right in front of him, the legendary law man, Wild Bill Hickok, the first of Wade\'s visitors. A western gunfighter that had attempted to guide the Southern Sparkplug, but had long since remained absent. Yet there he sat, an angry stare just visible under a wide brimmed hat, scowling behind a thick, gray handlebar mustache. For a long while, silence. Wade\'s orbs shot from one ghost to the next, poker-faced. \"Well?\" Elliott grunted, his deep, gravely voice cutting through the uncomfortable silence. \"\'WELL\' WHAT???\" bellowed Wild Bill, bursting to life, slamming a hand into the table, eyes wide with rage. \"Si\'down, Bill,\" shot Wade. \"Ya sure as hell don\'t wanna start round two.\" \"I\'d just as soon put a bullet \'tween yer fuckin\' eyes, boy!\" the lawman replied. \"The three\'ve us show up outta the blue an\' that bullshit tongue\'ve yers has the balls to disrespect us with \'what?\' Go FUCK yerself!\" \"Quiet, the both\'ve ya...\" said the soldier, slowly extended a hand to break the shouting match. \"Relax, Bill,\" said the Coyote, a soft, human voice like that of a middle aged man escaping from his snout. \"There\'s plenty of time to sling obscenities.\" \"So what, then?\" Wade interjected, impatiently. \"Whatta ya\'ll want from Ol\' Wade this time?\" Hickok shook his head. \"You just don\'t fuckin\' get it, Wade. Ya never did, an ya never will!\" \"THEN WHY DON\'T YA GO ON AN\' TELL ME, YA SONNUVA BITCH??!!\" barked The Bad Dog, slamming his palms to the table. \"It\'s not what we wanted from you, Wade,\" said the Coyote, remaining calm with closed eyes. \"It\'s what we wanted for you.\" \"Ya had a chance, Wade,\" started the soldier, words coming forth raspy and slow. \"God knows we gave ya plenty of chances...\" \"To do what?\" asked the \'Bama Bruiser, frustrated. \"What in the hell did ya\'ll want me to fuckin\' do? Ya\'ll\'ve been showin\' up outta fuckin\' nowhere fer a year an\' a half! Speakin\' in puzzles! Whatta ya want me to DO!? \"We wanted ya to ACCEPT the fuckin\' GIFTS we were givin\' ya, Wade!\" roared Bill, teeth grit. \"WHAT GIFTS!!??\" Wade bellowed back. \"GIMME A STRAIGHT GOD-DAMN ANSWER!\" \"The gifts that would help you remember,\" answered the Coyote, his calm tone dispelling the chaos. \"The gifts that would help you see, and discover who you are, and what you could be.\" \"I know who the hell I am,\" was Wade\'s dark reply, thumping a fist to his chest. \"No, ya don\'t,\" came the soldier\'s eerie voice. \"Ya don\'t even know why ya branded that flag on yer chest...\" Wade drew his hand across his left pectoral, where two crossed Confederate flags lay emblazoned into his skin underneath his brown t-shirt. \"The gifts were key to your discovery, but it\'s too late for that,\" the Coyote said with a solemn tone, hanging his head slightly. \"Why?\" snapped Wade. \"Why\'s it too late?\" \"Because ya BLEW it! Hikock snapped back. \"An\' how\'s that, Bill?\" seethed Wade, the thundercloud rolling through his eyes. \"If ya cain\'t see why, yer a god-damn fool,\" he replied. \"Look around ya, Elliott. Look how far ya had come, and look where ya are now.\" \"Ya veered off yer path, Wade,\" said the soldier. \"You had the steady pace of a train, and ya went and jumped track...\" \"Ya\'ve done NOTHIN\'!\" Bill barked. \"You were given an opportunity to be somebody, and ya kicked it to the curb! Ya shrugged it off! Knocked away the hands\'ve those that ya coulda called friends, tore apart the only person ya ever fuckin\' cared about!\" \"DON\'T START THAT SHIT WITH ME, BILL!\" Elliott bellowed, rising to his feet. \"SHE BURNT THAT BRIDG...\" \"AND EVEN NOW YER TOO FUCKIN\' BLIND TO SEE THE GOD-DAMN TRUTH!!!\" \"Enough.\" The Coyote\'s word quieted the room, leaving Bill and Wade to a terrible stare-down across the table. \"We are leaving, Wade,\" the Coyote continued. \"We can\'t help those who won\'t help themselves.\" \"It\'s too late for ya,\" added the soldier, mouth warping awkwardly. \"There\'s no point in us comin\' \'round anymore.\" \"We tried openin\' yer eyes time an\' time again, Wade,\" hissed the law man. \"And ya wouldn\'t open em\'. Ya wouldn\'t take what we offered, wouldn\'t follow yer fuckin\' road. Hell, you don\'t even know what we are, what we stand for...\" \"Sure as hell I do,\" replied Wade. \"Ya\'ll come \'round to git in Ol\' Wade\'s way when all he fuckin\' wants is to be left to his lonesome!\" \"And so we leave,\" answered the soldier, his voice fading. \"We leave you now with nothing, and you will see what it\'s like to truly be alone in this world.\" Bill returned to his chair as the others began to fade away into the air, eyes locked to Wade\'s as they dissipated. \"Go fuck yerself, Elliott. God have mercy on yer soul.\" And in a flash... ...they were gone. And Wade found himself being stared at not by the specters, but the patrons of the tavern, dead quiet and wide-eyed at the Drifter. Taking a moment to realize the situation, Wade snapped out of his trance, eyebrows furled as his glare drew across the bewildered customers. \"Fuck off,\" he spat, as if that were the obvious answer. He sat back, and the bar returned to business as usual. He slumped in his chair, staring into space, the events of past few minutes tumbling through his skull. Alone. He had felt those feelings of lonesome as Angus left to the side of Lindsay Troy, as he destroyed the relationships of those close to him. Familiar, he had felt them many times before. But never had he felt so alone, and so abandoned, as he did in that bar, when even his own imagination had turned its back. But when you\'re Wade Elliott, you don\'t sit and stare for long. You light up a Marlboro Red. Order a fresh whiskey. And get back to business as usual.
