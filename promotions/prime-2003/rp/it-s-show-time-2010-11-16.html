It's Show Time

16 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/16/10 “None of you are worth the time and effort I’ll have to spend to train you.” The Greek bellowed, he was storming back and forth in the gym at the end of our final session of training in our first week of his wrestling school. “Now I bet you’ re all thinking I said this to Felix Red, that I tore apart the UWA roster this way…But I never did! Those boys had talent, they had a spark, you all have nothing, nothing at all. A buncha fools who think they’ll get laid if they tell the ladies that you’re a wrestler. You’re all morons thinking you’ll be in arenas with 50,000 people chanting your name or throwing trash at you. None of that is going to happen, half of you can’t even run the ropes right!” His face was getting redder and redder, he was truly out of sorts, I felt kind of ashamed that I was in such a group of losers. I’d busted my ass all 7 days of the training so far, hoping I’d have done something to impress him, if I had he sure as hell wasn’t telling anyone about it. “All any of you have done is allow me to pay for the remodeling of my bathroom. My wife’s been on me for over a year to fix it, and now we’re going to be able to get that done. Now I want to make this clear to all twelve of you morons, this week I’ll let each and every one of you drop out and get half your money back, I’m going to be merciful for one of the few times in my life. You don’t have to quit in front of everyone else right here and now, just find me this week, ask for your money back, and you’ll be all set. Good night ladies.” We all staggered out of the gym like the Confederate Army after Appomattox. I could see everyone else looking beaten, defeated, we all felt like we’d failed, were failures. I laid in bed awake that night, wondering if I’d made a huge mistake, if I’d screwed up even trying to become a wrestler. “Fuck him, he’s just busting all your balls.” Muse told me the next morning. Katie O’Connell was a sophomore at Salem High School, and my conflicted love interest. I’d liked Katie ever since I looked into her eyes and saw how old her soul was. Through a series of meditations I deduced she was in fact the bringer of music to humanity, the spirit of creativity, known commonly as a Muse. She was shy and we had some clumsy interactions to start, but we finally got through that and became friends, but that was sort of where we were stuck. I really wanted to date her, but she was always distant, she and I spent many a night with her crying over how she was chubby, ugly, all that other stuff, and how she could never live up to Kendra, who was such a sexy girl, and she’d just fail me as a girlfriend and a lover. “He seemed deadly serious about all of it, I bet half the class has quit now, they all looked like they had had their guts stomped out. It was just so ugly.” I shook my head. I didn’t know what I was expecting or thinking about, just that I knew this situation had sucked and I felt really lousy after it, which could only have been Greek’s goal. Maybe Muse was right, maybe this just was a test of us. “Look, you want half your money back, that’s proof you don’t want this that bad, that’s all it is. Is this guy a bigger dick then say, my guitar teacher? Yeah, but that’s wrestling for ya, it’s all a giant dick measuring contest anyhow, everyone’s got to be a bigger man then the next guy, it’s just how they operate, so you need to just let him throw his fit and let the weak minded sheep who’ve found out a week of hard work is too much for them and then you’ll prove him wrong and get back in there and mix it up.” Muse being so supportive was both helpful and frustrating. I wanted to hug her and kiss her, to thank her for giving me a perspective on the issue I couldn’t have because I was so close to it and my emotions were in the way. “Thank you Muse” I told her, she did at the very least like the name, and went by it, which only got me more meetings with folks from the school’s administration about my evil Charles Manson like powers of manipulation, or as my new name was known “Koresh.” She gave me a hug, but it felt off, distant, I hated that we were so close and yet so far apart, I just wanted to scream at her that I loved her and I accepted her, but it wouldn’t matter, she didn’t accept herself, I wish I could help her with her problems as she helped me with mine. Class size got smaller and smaller till only 4 of us were left. Red Mask, or “Red” as we called him, a chubby kid who was funny as hell and always wore a red mask to class to protect his ‘secret identity’ but people apparently knew he was from Danvers and his name was Jimmy, we played along because he was a goofball, and apparently one that was serious about being a wrestler. Along with him were to twenty something brothers named Dave and Leo Pawlowski, who clearly needed stage names, as clunky Polish last names were not the road to fame and fortune in wrestling. “Well, I still got four idiots left, I guess you all wanted to lose all your money, suit yourselves” was Greek’s only reaction to seeing we were not going away. He continued to beat the hell out of us class was more of a boot camp then a training seminar. “You can’t gas out in the ring, you start puking, you start sucking wind in that ring and you can’t go anymore, you disrespect this business, and nobody I train is gonna disrespect this business!” was yelled repeatedly at us. Red Mask lost about 30 pounds in 2 months. “I figure I better lose the weight, or I’m gonna die.” He told me. After 4 months, and much concern from my parents and Muse about the endless bruises, bumps, constant limping, and other ailments, finally it was show time finally we were going to wrestle in front of a crowd at the Brighton Knights of Columbus. “Maybe you four won’t be a total disgrace” was about all the encouragement we got. I found myself just totally obsessed with the match, that whole week moved so agonizingly slow, it was to be me and Red Mask wrestling the newly renamed Gold brothers, Dave and Leo got to keep their first names at least, we were told we’d have about 10 minutes to wrestle and me and Red were to be the heels, mostly since I was a pale skinny goth kid with as much hair dye as Salem High School allows without you getting suspended, and Red Mask, while slimmer than before, was still fat and he wore a mask, which is a sign of evil I guess. There was all that and the fact that the Gold brothers were two good looking guys who were in shape and looked the part of heroes. “You should wear a mask, like me, so we can be more of a team” Red Mask told me the morning of the show. “Nah man, I can’t see myself wearing a mask.” “Well then you should do something, I mean, you gotta stand out in some way.I dunno, paint your face or something.” He said with a shrug. “Face paint” I replied…I can see that, I think I’d want it to be kind of like a mime thing, black and white, good and evil…” and then I really started thinking about my image, image was so much a part of the business, and I’d just totally neglected it spending all these weeks being beaten up, bouncing around in a ring and being thrown around like a rag doll. I spend the day racing around, Muse painted my nails and my face I didn’t really like the sight of myself without a shirt, I looked to skinny, so I found a good fitting black wife beater. I didn’t like wearing trunks, and I didn’t have a pair of long tights, so I decided to keep wearing the pair of black gi pants I’d worn throughout class, I did however break out a new pair of black ring boots, I’d been saving them for this night. I remember “Enter Sandman” playing, I remember the crowd jeering me and Red Mask as we made our way to the ring, I remember the Gold brothers getting cheered, after that, the match was a blur, I remember a tag here, a move there, in the end me and Red Mask were defeated, the Gold brothers hailed the victors. I remember laying on the floor as about 25 people chanted “Gold! Gold! Gold!” Leo and Dave paraded around the ring, soaking in the crowd’s approval, small as that crowd might be, they milked it for all it was worth. And I knew I’d made them look good, made that crowd care about them. Laying on that floor in the Brighton Knights of Columbus floor, freshly defeated in my first ever match, I could not have been happier. This is where I belonged this is where I’m supposed to be. And I couldn’t wait to do it again.
