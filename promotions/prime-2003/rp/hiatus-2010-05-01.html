Date: 05/01/10 “How many?” His left shoulder rested against the aged siding of a house long overdue for repair. Routine maintenance had been forsaken long ago. Now, too far gone to ever save, the crumbling wreck of a three bedroom was just waiting for a big, bad wolf to come along and blow her on down. So there he was, shoulder pressed against the paint-chipped skin of a once beautiful home, waiting for his chance to huff and puff and blow some shit up. The simple pleasures in life really were the sweetest. The recipient of his query stood two feet away, right shoulder pressed against an equally weather worn stretch of siding. A large, wooden door (sun bleached and rotting) covered the twenty some odd inches between them. Her thick, black hair was pulled back into a tight ponytail. Each hair purposefully drawn taut to prevent a single loose strand from obscuring her vision. Chestnut colored eyes were narrowed. Tight at the corners where the crow’s feet spidered out. Lips drawn into the same tight line. She took a sharp breath in… and out. Another. Both arms were stretched below her waist. Standard issue Agency firearm gripped firmly in her hands. She looked down at the Kevlar constraining her breaths. The yellow letters emblazoned across her chest. Reassured, those brown orbs moved up to the source of the question. She should not have been shocked at what she saw there… but in the heat of the moment, Alexis Grant had entirely forgot who she was dealing with. “Please tell me you are not enjoying this.” The smile on Tyler Rayne’s face widened. He shrugged and offered her a reassuring wink. Or condescending. Sometimes it was hard to tell the difference with him. “This is the fun part, kid.” “We’re about to rush into a house full of armed crackheads. Which part of that is fun?” “One of them could be dressed like a clown.” “I hate clowns.” “Then shoot him first.” “You’re impossible.” “You really have no idea.” “No shit. Remind me to send Troy a sympathy card. If we live through this.” “We will.” “You seem awfully confident.” “Oh, we’re far too pretty to die. Well… least I am.” She broke. Couldn’t help it. He was just too damn ridiculous. She fought it for just a moment. The smile that started in the middle of her bottom lip. She tried to suppress it. Quickly looked to the termite ridden planks of the front porch. Averted her gaze from that magnificent smile. Still couldn’t erase it from her mind, though. The confidence with which he spoke. The nonchalant lean of his body. Arms loose, slightly bent at the elbows. Even if the grip on his own firearm was vice tight… the rest of him was relaxed. At ease. She absorbed the entire façade. Found comfort in it. A deep breath drew in… and when it came out, some of her anxiety left along with it. Alexis turned her eyes back to him. The smile was gone, but he still offered a soft expression. “Better?” “Yeah. Thanks.” “Sure thing. So how many?” “Six. Maybe seven.” “Maybe?” She shrugged. Double-checked to ensure the safety was off. “Ladies first.” “Such a gentleman.” One last breath of fresh air before she rotated on her shoulder. Oriented herself toward the door and launched a powerful boot into the door. The wood splintered around the knob. Rot had no hope of standing against her swift kick. The upper hinge ripped from the door frame. The oak obstruction fell in her way just as she was pushing inside. Alexis shouldered the brunt of the door’s weight. She kicked the door open with her left foot, allowing it to fall heavy on a dust covered floor. The rest of her body followed suit, dropping low. Eyes aware. Firearm trained ahead. “FBI! Everyone come out with your hands up!” Tyler took a step forward. There was no motion in the darkened shadows of the home. He remained standing, watching the high ground while she managed the low. “Actually… she’s FBI. I’m not. So if you’ve got a thing against cops or whatever… shoot her first.” “Asshole.” He stepped further into the house. Alexis remained crouched, progressing slowly in her own direction. She was shielded behind the immense floral patterns of a couch that must have been new in the seventies. Which looked to be about the last time it had been cleaned, as well. Tyler began his move toward the front of the sofa. A shift in the darkness caught his eye. Alexis noticed the slight change in his posture. She tried to follow the line of his gaze. Positioned herself at the corner of the couch. The muzzle flashed before the explosion. At least that’s how it seemed in all the dark. Lightning before the thunder. It struck him in the chest. Dead center. His feet left the floor about a second before the back of his head found it. Thunk Alexis fired off two quick shots in the general direction of the muzzle flash. “FUCK! What did I say!? THE FUCK did I tell you?! Shoot her first! Christ!” Tyler used his elbows to crawl behind the couch and curl up next to his favorite federal agent. Each breath burned in his chest. It felt like he had been hit with a fucking anvil. His finger poked around at the hole in his black tee. The bullet had drilled something fierce into his vest. The end was still warm to the touch. “That hurts every fucking time.” “Stop getting shot, then.” She popped over the top of the couch and squeezed a single shot at her target. The bullet drilled through his forehead and painted the front of the refrigerator with pink and crimson bits. A door to her left burst open. The man storming through couldn’t have been more than a hundred pounds. The Desert Eagle he brandished was almost too heavy to hold. Almost. He wavered a second before lining up a shot. A second too long. He hadn’t bothered to put on shoes, not that it would have done much to stop the bullet, anyway. He lost two toes and his balance. Toppled forward and landed teeth first on the top of his hand cannon. Tyler rolled in his direction. Somersault. Came up on the balls of his feet and launched himself into the air. Maybe a foot of clearance. Enough to gain some momentum. Enough to deliver a nose-breaking knee drop. Two. A shotgun blast from the kitchen all but annihilated the couch. That poor floral print. Tyler did not turn his attention from the darkened room ol’ Desert Eagle had jumped out of. He did fire a blind shot in the direction of the kitchen, though. The barrel of the shotgun began to trail in his direction… which offered Alexis a fine killshot through the temple. Three. The federal agent rushed for the kitchen to examine the two bodies. Very much dead. No one else appeared to be hiding in there. No one had come from Desert Eagle’s room. Alexis kept low and began creeping through the kitchen, looking for an entrance to the other rooms. Tyler kept his back to the wall and sidled along to the edge of the door. “Knock knock.” “Who… who’s there?” “Rayne. Tyler Rayne.” “Like… the wrestler.” “Yeah.” “Bullshit.” “No bullshit.” “You sure?” “…Yeah. Pretty sure.” “Why are you shooting us?” “I’m not shooting you. I shot him.” “Why are you shooting him?” “Shot. Past tense. As in not doing it anymore.” “Wha… what?” “Do you have a gun?” “Yes.” “Will you put it down?” “…No?” “Please?” “Um… why are you shooting us?” “You just asked me that, fucktard. Are you high?” “Uh… no?” “Just put the gun down, dick lick. I’d rather not shoot you if I can help it.” “Look, man… you come in here and I’ll… I’ll…” “You’ll what? Stutter some more? Cause, gotta say, not all that intimidating.” “I’ll blow your fuckin’ brains out, man!” “You and what army?” “There’s fuckin’ three more of us in here, man, and we will all—” “Kyle! Don’t tell ‘im how many of us are fuckin’ in here, man! Shit!” “What! I’m fuckin’ freakin’ out here, man! I’m tryin’ to have a conversation, so just shut the fuck—” Bang Four. Bang Five. Tyler rushed in through the open door. Alexis had her weapon trained on an unarmed woman cowering in the corner. There were two dead bodies on the floor. Both with the backs of their heads exposed to open air. Girl could fucking shoot. Tyler stepped forward and pulled the handcuffs from the back of her slacks before approaching the quivering mess of estrogen on the floor. “We good?” “Other rooms are clear. Thanks for the distraction.” “That was the plan, right? I get everyone’s attention. You put their brains on the wall.” “Seemed to work. Still can’t figure why you’re offering yourself as the bait, though.” “Working a strict no kill policy lately. You know, trying to be a better man and all that bullshit. What’s the take on this one?” “I think this one was twenty. Not a bad take.” “You did all the work.” “And can claim none of the reward.” “Sucks to be a federal agent.” Someone scoffed from the doorway. Alexis must have given the all clear when she dropped those last two. A handful of agents were now pushing their way through the house. Taking pictures. Collecting evidence. Beginning the process of identifying corpses. Two young men, including the one who had scoffed, shoved Tyler out of the way to take possession of his charge. The girl whimpered, but made no attempt to struggle. Tyler sighed and followed him out. Alexis was a step behind. “You glad to be going back?” “To PRIME?” “Yeah.” “Honestly? I don’t know.” She laughed. “I thought you loved it.” “I do. It’s just… different. No Tink. No Uni. Everyone takes it so gods damned seriously. Sometimes it’s just not fun anymore. Weren’t for Hawaii, I probably wouldn’t even bother.” “Not fun? This is, though? Shooting trigger-happy addicts in a dilapidated shack?” “Actually… yeah. That was kinda fun. Surprisingly less dangerous.” “Why go back then?” The two of them had left the foreboding shadows of the house and found themselves within the swirling, blinding lights of the law enforcement vehicles outside. Alexis took the lead now, walking toward her own black sedan across the street. “There is a rush. The fans. The crowd. I owe it to them. They can make it fun. It’s the rest of these hard asses that drain all the entertainment from it. Everything’s always gotta be dark and brooding and so… fuckin’ serious. Life and death, you know? What’s wrong with a good dick joke every now and again?” “You are so immature.” “Excuse me. Mr. Rayne?” Both of them looked up at the stern-looking figure now before them. He was an older man. A few inches taller than Tyler. Few pounds on him as well. Face worn with years of hardship. His hair was gray, slicked back. His suit was black. Pressed. Not too tight, but there no mistaking the hint of muscles beneath. He had a scar across his lip. Nothing that resembled a smile. Unbelievably, an eye patch to boot. “Ask and ye shall receive. One-Eyed Willy comes callin’. What can I do for you?” The man is not pretty. Bordering on ugly. The grimace that hardened his stone face pushed beyond that border. “They told me the eye patch would help.” Slightly ashamed, the man removes the black protective gear to reveal a fully functional and quite beautiful blue orb beneath. He shoved the patch into his inside jacket pocket… and pulled out a badge. Alexis drew in a breath. Shocked. Tyler just chuckled. Interpol. “We’ve been watching you for awhile now, Mr. Rayne. Bounties you’ve collected, such as this evening. Your personal affairs. Particularly those matters handled in Japan. You possess a unique skill set. One that we think could be quite an asset if used in the right circumstances. We’d like to offer you a job.” The man smiles. It doesn’t seem to suit him well. A foreign expression, despite his legitimate amusement at the question to come. “How would you like to become an agent of SHIELD?”
