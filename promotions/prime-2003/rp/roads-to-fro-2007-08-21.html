Roads To & Fro

21 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/21/07 I woke up hung-over off of her. My breath was still heavy, laden with vodka fumes that I could taste on my chapped lips. The sheets beneath me lay soaked, sweat outlining my pitiful frame. Looking around, reality seemed as bleak and painful as my dreams; all about her, of course. Clawing at the sleep in my eyes, I looked around my apartment. The clock teased me with its reminder that it was only seven in the morning; there were a dozen more hours of self-pity to be had before it was okay to start drinking again. Looking out the window offered no more hope; the dark clouds showed that even San Diego had turned her back on me. The thing about falling hard? You think you’re looking for that rare moment of weightlessness where you and her are caught in time…and then one day you wake up, feel the bruises, and reconsider the jump in the first place. I didn’t rouse myself from bed yet, a hangover threatening to rip out my frontal lobe at any minute. Thinking about it, I hadn’t said a word to anyone since I’d gotten home. I looked like that five minutes of a romantic comedy where the guy is falling apart because he won’t get the girl, the part right before he pulls off a miracle. My miracles were few in number. The spirit was broken and the body wasn’t far behind. My self-pity was beginning to breed its own self-pity. Stepping out of bed, I saw my phone huddled up next to the wall, below a peculiar phone-sized hole in the wall. Not able to drum up much regret for a phone, I wandered on, like a stranger in my apartment. The time for anger was spent. I used the walls to brace myself, weak from the fact that I hadn’t eaten in over sixteen hours, unless you count that time-tested Vodka & Cranberry Diet. The rain stubbornly wouldn’t come. I stepped out onto my patio, overlooking lower San Diego, and was glad that my perfect melancholy wasn’t ruined. A glorious, sunny day wouldn’t do much good for sorrow and self-hatred. Birds chirping wouldn’t work as the soundtrack to love’s cackling revenge. Complete and sudden loss is funny. When someone dies, you get time. You’ve got one day to eat morbid hors d’oeuvres, a day to look sexy in black. Before you know it, it’s time to have another dead people get together! That’s why it takes that quotidian pang; that every day moment where a space that was once filled is now empty. Something that was once there is gone, you realize. And then you cry. I had skipped the entire median step. I had gone straight to mourning, which is an argument against compassion in the first place. She was there the day before and then I woke up knowing she wasn’t and never would be. Months of carefully cultivated friendship thrown down the tubes. That’s right, wasted. If a guy tells you they’re okay being just friends with a woman, they’re telling you that she’s too ugly for him or he’s too ugly with her. Nobody in their right mind sees an attractive female and says “I’m certainly okay with never having sex with her”. If she gives you a stiffy, then primal urge, instinct, hell God (if you want to think that) says you should try to rail her. Weird how a heart-stomping fallout with a woman somehow makes you an expert on relationships, eh? The wind clawed at my face, cutting across swaths of unshaven scruff. I didn’t consider jumping, if that’s what you’re wondering. Suicide is for prima donnas. And I didn’t want to die. I wanted to live all over again, without ever meeting her. She had become sweet venom in my veins, a joyful ideal that killed me a little more every time I conjured her up. She was gone and “good” memories were false hope…it is never better to have loved and lost, proverbs be damned. I walked back inside and looked at the kitchen table, where the bottle of Svedka sat leering at me with disgust. The noticeable dent in the bottle seemed a sorrier sight in the daylight. * * * * * * * It was not the first time she had cheated. It wasn’t the first time I’d brought a woman to cheat. Read the good book; the devil made temptation stronger than God made fidelity. I was her Devil’s plaything. Her giggles between our kisses hid the darkness of the situation. We both knew that this amateur, clandestine affair was the equivalent of ignoring storm clouds overhead. The beauty of it all, though, was that I didn’t need anything else. Not common sense, not the nonexistent future. She was a beam of light, so brilliant that it would be impossible to see anything else, even if you were able to look away. I put my hand into her dirty blond hair and cupped the back of her head in my hands, pulling her in for a deep, moist kiss. It didn’t make sense; we were in a parking garage outside my office. And I was lost in everything. “Chandler…” It was that fucking hesitation. Her eyes were bathed in guilt as she looked at me, seeing all that sin in front of her. “…what am I doing?” An unanswerable question. I couldn’t give her the acquittal, the sweet escape she craved. “My boyfriend is over there…” Tears crawled out of the corner of her eyes, slowly skating to the edges of her cheeks. Her boyfriend was a fool. Bred from one of those Catholic families that guilts you into joining the army for God or country or something equally ridiculous. He wasn’t “over there” for anything other than killing some brown people. This wasn’t to pay for college or support a family; he was a selfish bastard. I wanted to tell her this…to tell her that I would run away from everything if she would. We would go buy land somewhere, where we’d build house that our kids could live in one day. And we’d never talk about back home ever again. But we would be happier that way. So, if she could be happy then that was all I needed. I would live in the Dark Ages if that’s what I took. And we could die together. “Yeah.” Nothing. I took her into my arms. This was how it would end. Not today, but in the future. She would realize that she was crying in the wrong arms. That it was me who she was killing. GI Dickwad would never know his perfect ten had been elsewhere, but I would watch things unravel. The pain would be mine. And it would be everything, like she was. * * * * * * * My hand rotated around the small, unwieldy top of the bottle. The pills inside were not a way out, but they could give me a small breath of life beyond her. A chance for that one breath without regret. I clasped three of the small white pain pills between my fingers. Nothing about them let on their powerful effect. As I picked up the Svedka bottle, I saw myself reflected on the steel of the refrigerator door. My doppelganger in the silver reflection was antsy about the Vicodine and the surge of churning wooziness that would follow. For the first time in weeks, I attended to my own, swirling pain. * * * * * * * “What are we doing this weekend?” This was during the good days, few in number and so far gone that I remembered them in pieces. Her business casual blew everyone else’s black tie out of the water. Even in work clothes, there was some secret brewing beneath the surface, a timid, yet sexy, playful side waiting to emerge. And, to the envy of all, I got to see it. And to the envy of me, it was not enough. “Name a place on the globe. I’ll take you there.” “Chandler,” she blushed, “that’s completely unnecessary. And very sweet, but kinda dorky”. She smiled and shoved me. The brown in her eyes sat back calmly, but overwhelmed you as soon as you shared a gaze. “Fine, offer rescinded. Didn’t wanna take you in the first place,” I chided “Although it just so happens that I need dinner plans for tonight. You know any gorgeous women waiting to be wined and dined?” Her smile was weaker this time, an attempt to appease me. I could read that she was uncomfortable, so I stopped trying. But I didn’t pry. She could tell me on her own time. The awkward uncertainty of our situation led me to avoid these questions. “Brussels.” For a split second, I was ecstatic. She and I would head to…and then I saw the defeated look on her face. It was not a request, but an itinerary listing for plans already made. “Oh?” “That’s where they’re flying him so he can meet me. Reward for a great half-tour of duty.” I’d made a living on one-liners about pathetic fuckjobs like him. But it felt wrong to belittle her plight right in front of her. The kind of thing I didn’t feel up to anymore. This wasn’t the wrestling ring and I wasn’t a wrestler. Businessmen don’t act like children. I was obediently silent. “Great.” Turning away from her, I saw her chest heave with disappointment. In me. In herself. In her boyfriend. It was these times when everything felt twisted around that we both should have just said no mas, given it great goodbye sex, and headed out for our lives. “Look…I really like you.” All my life, I’d used opportunities like this to rip women to shreds. Women I’d enjoyed. Maybe even loved. And I knew I loved her. This time, I needed something to work out. I needed to know that I was even capable of reciprocal love. So I kept my tongue in check, desperately wanting to confide that she didn’t “really like me” enough. Not enough to shift her mediocre world in hopes of a better one. Never enough for a gamble. * * * * * * * I sat, my jaw all fuzzy with numbness, face down at the table. It was more fatigue than continuous emotional devastation. My magic little pills were doing their job, sending me into the atmosphere. I felt like I could sense my brain matter powering every impulse within my body. Druggy talk, I know. My head spun around at the sudden noise. I nearly tripped getting up to answer the phone, with two legs asleep, and a head full of a nimbus clouds. Maybe it was Rachel. Maybe. “Hello?” The connection seemed fragile, barely a link. “Chan!” The familiar tones of Danny Ferguson, invading my ears. Unsure of how to react, I stayed silent. “Listen, I know you’re giving hell to the business world….but we’re on the brink of something big here. You should come back.” Wrestling reared its ugly head at me again, in the eleventh hour. Days before, I would never have dreamed of returning to my hobby. But now that I had taken a big l in the game of life, the structured competition and rigorous self-punishment of PRIME seemed enticing. What better penance than the climb back up the hill? “You don’t have to answer today, but Tyler’s got the papers all drawn up. I’ve even got this solid idea for a surprise comeback out of nowhere…you there?” “Yeah, I’m listening.”
