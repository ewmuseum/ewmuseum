The Origin Of The Species: Part IV - The Secret Life of Angelus Von Kelsig

10 March 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-03-10 17-02-10 The musty dinge was was almost suffocating. His breath hitched against the plume of dust kicked up from the moth-eaten Persian rug stretched across the scratched and scuffed wooden floor beneath. Sunlight poured in from a small four-pane window above the landing on the stairs just ahead of him, illuminating the cloud and obscuring his view for a moment. Squinting through the haze, the giant swatted at the murk, stifling a cough as he heard Ryan's muffled voice beyond an arched opening to his left. When he grew accustomed to the turbid air and the low light he surveyed the foyer in silence whilst eavesdropping on Ryan's conversation, softened by the thick oak-panelled walls into nothing more than muted resonance. He drew his gaze over a coat rack by the door and a bookcase set into a cove beneath the staircase which had on it two brown and brittle potted ferns. Dead, just like the atmosphere. He took a step forward and froze when the floorboards beneath the carpet creaked under his weight. Swallowing nervously, he brushed past a plastic ficus at the archway and reached out for a framed photo sat upon a shaker side table. He recognized the picture from somewhere, and it took a moment of recollection to discern that Ryan had shown him the same photo way back in Cleveland, Ohio after first hunting him down. In that picture Von Kelsig was shocked to see Ryan with his mum and dad and taller brother, but he refused to accept the sight of it. Holding the frame in both hands he studied this print and felt a lump rise in his throat when he saw that where the tall sibling should have been there was a crease down the length of the photographic paper. Von Kelsig turned the frame over, fiddling with the little hooks holding the backing in place when he heard dull footsteps coming from beyond the archway. His heart beat a little faster as he placed the frame back on the side table before spinning around to meet Ryan with a fast smile. “Dad's through in the den...you uh, you alright?” He asked, glancing at the picture then back at the giant. “Fine,” Von Kelsig replied, though he was far from it. Ten years in the business living with Hessian had kept him busy. Constantly improving on the character formula and methodically rehearsing matches and promos to reach greater heights in the industry had strengthened him from the time before he entered the squared circle. As far as he was concerned life began back in 2001 when he was enrolled in the Primetime Wrestling academy. Life began when his name meant something. A name he made by being a ruthless and steeled individual. With the last several months weighing heavily on his mind and life grabbing him by the throat once more he found himself falling into despair, characterized by bouts of drinking and depression which had heavily impacted his career. Nerves of steel had become jelly, and it was now the giant felt the shakes. Dad had set it off. Sweeping a wrist across his forehead he took a deep dusty breath and wiped the residue across the thigh of his jeans. He clenched his fists, his gaze flitting rapidly past Ryan into the hall past the archway that led to the kitchen and the den; a black tunnel in his eyes, leading to the lair of a monster that Hessian could never be. “Hey man, you sure you're alri-...” “I can't do this.” Von Kelsig blurted, backing away from Ryan towards the front door. Before Ryan could call after him the giant was out of sight, with the front door scraping shut behind him. “Fuck.” As he raced outside the giant was already at the gate. Wasting no time Ryan sprinted down the path, leaping into the air onto Von Kelsig's back. Stumbling forward, Von Kelsig swung back towards the house thinking for a split second that he would see Ryan standing there before the realization hit him. Throwing an arm back, he clutched at Ryan's shirt, hauling the much smaller man up and over his shoulder. Were he in the ring he could have easily manoeuvred the man into position for a Hellevator, but here he simply caught him in his arms and plopped him down onto the path. “Where the hell you going?!” Ryan demanded, slamming his palms into Hessian's chest to emphasize his ire. “I can't face them,” the giant grumbled. “There's things you don't know Ryan. I just can't go in there...I thought maybe I could...but-...” “Well I got news for you too ya fuck! There's things you don't know either! You think I brought you here because I wanna play fuckin' happy families? I can't barely remember shit as it is but I damn sure never missed having a brother around.” “The hell you talking about?” Von Kelsig asked, cautious now of Ryan's outburst. “For twenty-two years, not fourteen, twenty-fucking-two years I've had to watch my mom traipse through life with a permanent fucking frown on her face. Every day she dragged her ass around heartbroken because she lost her son. Sobbing all the time and depressed as hell, and those were the good days. See when she realized you were gone for good? She went completely fucking numb. Mom used to be an angel, smiling and laughing and taking care of her family. When she got it in her head you'd never be back she just locked up. Sure she still did her thing and took care of us but there was no emotion. She was like a goddamn robot, and it's all because of you she's the way she is now!” Pacing back and forth the whole time and throwing accusing fingers at the giant, Ryan vented his anger while Von Kelsig stood frozen to the spot, listening closely and feeling the bottom of his stomach drop out. “...I know how many years it's been. Fourteen was just a-...” “Just a part of the little story you made up to feel better about yourself. Yeah. I remember the day mom found out her seventeen-year old angel had flown the nest.” Ryan snarled. “Where is she?” Von Kelsig asked to the point, glancing at the upstairs windows in the house. “Same place she's been for the last three weeks. Bed.” Ryan spat, while Von Kelsig's face soured. “That's the reason I brought you here man, the only fucking reason...” he paused, choking on his words. His eyelids fluttered and he snorted back before staring Von Kelsig dead in the eye, his lip quivering as the thought burned in his head. “...mom's dying.” ---- With the weight of the world upon his slumped shoulders, Von Kelsig followed Ryan back into the house. Neither man spoke as they trudged back in, stopping beneath the archway leading to the room where a demon lurked in slacks and suspenders. “So...” Ryan turned to the giant as he shrugged, trying to find a way to ask his next question, although Ryan knew exactly what was on his mind. “Dilated cardiomyopathy. I spent most of my savings getting that diagnosis and the ACE inhibitors to keep her going. I spent the last of it finding you.” “What is that?” the giant asked. “It basically means mom's heart muscle is too weak to pump blood around her body. She's got medicine for it but she can't barely move without getting faint. She hasn't been out of bed in weeks.” “What about surgery?” “You kidding? This house has never heard the words health insurance. I had to work my ass off just to be able to afford the medicine! Shifting on his feet he furrowed his brow as things began to slowly make sense. Folding his arms he narrowed his eyes at the young grease monkey. “So you need the money for surgery? Is that what it's all about? Why the hell didn't you just tell me this to begin with? I'm worrying about the asshole through there when my...my mother's life is in danger?” “Well....yeah,” Ryan began, shrugging unapologetically before making a phone gesture with his thumb and pinky finger. “She needs the money, but I couldn't exactly just phone you up and be like, 'Hey bro! How you been the last twenty-two years? Hey I know you abandoned your family and grew up acting like they never even existed but would you mind coming home? It's just that mom's dying and I need your money because I'm a fuckin' low-rent mechanic and I don't make anywhere near the wage a famous TV wrestler does, thanks!'” he chided the giant. “Fuck...” Von Kelsig muttered through grit teeth. He never imagined that the old, forgotten existence he had left behind long ago would rear its ugly head into his new, successful life. Air hissed between his teeth and his own heart palpitated at the thought of this corner in which he had been backed into, where he would have to face what had been buried long ago. The smell of pipe smoke wafted in from the den, sparking a memory of a puckered mouth with yellowed and crooked teeth clamped around a briar pipe. A twisted laugh cackling tormentingly behind a cloud of Old Dublin huffed in his face. Shaking the image from his head he found his eyes were wet, and drew his thumb and forefinger across them to wipe away the tears. “I never thought I'd ever step foot here again...” He croaked. “Neither did I,” Ryan said, stepping forward and placing a hand on Von Kelsig's shoulder. “I mean the last thing I expected when I found you was a freaking giant. I didn't believe it was you...I thought you'd at least look a bit like me but I guess I got mom's genes, heh. But here you are. Look there's a whole other life here that hasn't been lived and you owe it not just to mom but yourself to make something of it before it's too late.” “I guess so...” Von Kelsig sighed, “but what about him?” He asked, nodding through the hall where the odorous abomination emanated from. “You want to see him?” Ryan asked, arching an eyebrow. A moment of silence passed before the giant answered. “You remember what I told you back at the industrial estate?” He asked, to which Ryan nodded. “You think I invented that, recited every detail and forced myself to believe it for the fun of it? There's a reason I left this life behind and-...” “I know man.” Ryan said, cutting in. “I know what went on, shit I was here too remember? You think things changed just because you left? If anything it made them worse. But look at you! You're not a little boy any more, you're a goddamn monster now dude! Nobody'd wanna fuck with you.” Contemplating this uplifting fact, Von Kelsig felt a chill run up his spine at the realization that Ryan was right. He was a monster. Even the image of that broad-shouldered and evil-eyed patriarch was a dwarf contrast to the colossal idol that he had grown to become. For years the sight of that man was the epitome of hatred, his sandy hair slicked back with an oil that for the longest time Von Kelsig thought oozed from the venomous pores of his father's skull. He held that image of him now, even years after he had surely soured into a bitter old man, but Ryan's words had brought him some form of solace. “Okay. Let's go.” He found strength in the first step as Ryan led him through the archway into the hall. Straight ahead at the bottom a single door led to the downstairs toilet. A similar style of carpet to that of the foyer stretched the length of the floor, while in between the tall windows along the left wall hung portrait paintings of various landscape scenery. At either end of the wall to his right two perpendicular openings led to the living room and kitchen respectively; the kitchen entrance being closest. He remembered being told that before the walls were knocked down to make those entrances they had been doors leading to the holding cells of men awaiting trial. After the renovation the upstairs portion of the house was renovated from the courtroom into two bedrooms and a bathroom. As a boy he used to play cops and robbers full of glee at the thought that real prisoners had once walked within his house, though he never knew that the alder trees outside the house were used to hang those found guilty. It was no different from the foyer in terms of cleanliness. Cobwebs sagged in most of the corners and around the light fixture in the ceiling, while dust glazed most of the surfaces. It occurred to him then that with Ryan working so hard to pay for his bed-ridden mother's medical care that there was no one around to clean the house in their absence. He didn't think a bitter old man would pick up the slack. The quieted sounds of a football game echoed from the farthest entrance, and as he walked into the kitchen a raspy guttural cough broke the still, jarring his nerves. Holding a hand to the giant's chest, Ryan peeked into the living room before giving a thumbs up. “Wait here...” he whispered. “Hey, hold on,” Von Kelsig gasped, grabbing Ryan by the shoulder. “Don't tell him who I am, okay? Not yet. Just say I'm a friend of yours helping out, yeah?” “Yeah sure man, no worries.” Ryan strolled in to greet his father while Von Kelsig waited quietly, listening to the gravelly baritone that answered Ryan's greeting, its tone tinged with the strains of age. The linoleum smacked stickily under his feet, and looking around the kitchen he saw yet more signs of neglect. Unwashed plates and discarded takeaway containers sat along most of the counter tops, and in the sink piled up to the faucet, stuck in place by wads of grime along the edges. Hundreds of cracked and chipped magnets decorated the large fridge freezer next to him, tokens from holidays that the family had never been on, and instead had asked friends to bring back with them. All in all the state of the house had left him feeling that since he had been gone the place had simply festered into obscurity. “Come on in.” Ryan said, his head popping around the corner. Taking a deep breath Von Kelsig walked into the living room, his heartbeat thumping with each step. Out of the corner of his eye he spied the gnarled form hunched into a recliner, facing a 26” tube television that flickered every so often. In an age when LCD flat screens were common as muck he felt pity that this household was still confined to last generation technology. Manky cream curtains hung askew over the only window in the room, offering only a sliver of light which only seemed to accentuate the gloom. A two-tiered coffee table sat in the middle of the room, crumpled magazines and yellowed newspapers lying in a mess while a half empty mug with a filmy layer on the surface sat among ring stains made by previous cups. As his gaze drew to the man in the chair the room seemed to darken until finally their eyes met. All of a sudden the nerves were gone. Here was the past, once a haunting memory now made flesh. Staring up at him with contempt through eyes he recognized all too well was the man who had once upon a time been his father. A pale plaid shirt hung off of his bony frame; a pair of suspenders over his shoulders and pressing the fabric of the shirt down on the bumps of his collarbone and shoulders. He wore a pair of dark slacks stained with what Von Kelsig could only guess to be smeared in ash. He was bald save for a trail of white tufty hair running from ear to ear. Hands gripping the edge of the armrests like a wary gargoyle, he glared at the giant; his features warped into a permanent grimace from the bitter years he had lived. The bone structure of his face and his hardened eyes reminded Von Kelsig of his own reflection, but after that he saw nothing more than the frail, callous husk of a once fierce coward. Ryan introduced him with a simple, “this is my dad, Jack.” “Jesus Christ A'mighty,” gasped the old man at the sight of the giant before him. “Who the hell are you?” “My name is Angelus, sir. I'm a good friend of Ryan. He and I go way back.” Von Kelsig answered, remembering his manners. He felt a smug type of scorn hearing such petulance in the tone of the old man's voice; knowing that he could easily break the bastard with his bare hands and make up for years of abuse at his father's hand. He almost wanted to goad him into a fight just to see him suffer. “It true you want to pay for my wife's surgery?” Jack asked, narrowing his eyes. “Yes sir. Ryan told me about her illness and I thought it right that she be given the help she needs, so I offered to help cover any costs.” The giant replied. “We ain't a charity case,” came the acerbity from out of nowhere. Grumbling, Jack reached to the side of the recliner and from a silver tray brought a glass of gin an tonic to his lips, smacking them together after a sip. Next to where the glass rested Von Kelsig saw the old pipe, the bowl packed with ash and the rim slicked with a foul amber stain. “Dad!” Ryan growled, taking a step forward. “This isn't about charity. Mom doesn't have long left and unless we do something soon it'll be too late. Angelus is offering to pay seventy-five grand of his own money to save her life, seventy-five grand that we don't have...and you turn your nose up at it?” “Ain't about that,” Jack said, glancing up at his son and sneering, then shifting in his seat and grumbling under his breath. The old man's reluctance to accept the offer riled the giant. His repugnance harked back to that old life; Von Kelsig could remember him being too stupidly proud to take help from anyone no matter what the problem was. It seemed that that particular idiosyncrasy hadn't diminished with time. It made sense then that, in retrospect, his nature to offer help in the capacity of a mercenary coupled with his hatred of those opposing him should stem from being an outlet for the misanthropy of a man who never once considered the needs of others. “With all due respect sir,” Von Kelsig began, “I'm to understand your wife doesn't have long left unless she can get in for surgery quickly. I'm offering that opportunity to you and asking nothing in return. To see a mother and wife given a little more time with her family is thanks enough. She's getting the surgery.” “With all due respect son,” Jack replied, sending a shiver up Von Kelsig's back at the delivery of that word, “I don't need nobody telling me how to take care of my family. I managed fine so far; ain't no big grizzly sumbitch telling me what's good fer my wife!” He exclaimed emotionally. The giant struggled to think of something to say, and instead glanced at Ryan. Shrugging his shoulders, Ryan started for the kitchen, beckoning Von Kelsig after him. Following him out, he stopped and turned back to the old man, a thought forming behind his furrowed brow. “Why don't you want help for your wife?” He asked, “You must know she has no chance without surgery...what reason could you have for not wanting help for her?” All at once Jack's features softened; the hard lines in his face melted away and his eyes glazed over with a twinkling sheen, and though he tried hard to stop it, his stiff upper lip began to quiver. Again he reached for the gin and tonic, this time taking a copious gulp as he gazed into the middle distance. “You got a family of your own, son?” He croaked. Von Kelsig shook his head. “One of the hardest things in life is watching someone you love suffer,” Jack said, still staring into space. “What's worse is putting their suffering into someone else's hands...” The old man's foreign gaze became a series of rapid eye movements as he seemed to be searching for something in his mind. A lump formed in Von Kelsig's throat at the thought of what Jack might be reminiscing about. “...I don't want Margot to end up some damn statistic in a hospital file,” the old man continued. “She's safe here, she's got her bed and her medicine and ain't nobody gonna carve her up and ask for thousands of dollars for the privilege.” “That's a medieval way of looking at it,” Von Kelsig answered coyly. “Nowadays it's easier than most manual labour to perform surgery, and if you're paying the money you're guaranteed an expert's hand. You just don't want to let her go in case...well...” He didn't have to finish his sentence, as Jack looked up at him and nodded silently. “Years back I made the mistake of letting someone go. Never did hear from them again. I don't plan on making the same mistake again. Everything will be fine, long as Margot stays at home.” Settling into his chair, Jack's head drooped to the side as he waved Ryan and his visitor off. Obliging him, they headed back out into the hallway. “What a fucking asshole,” Von Kelsig seethed under his breath. “Did he actually just bring up me running away? Or was there someone else smart enough to get the hell out of here I didn't know about?” “Man I think he was talking about you...” said Ryan. “Maybe he regretted it.” “So he realized what a dick he was and instead of doing something about it he's sat in there decaying for the last twenty-two years? Now he's developed some kind of abandonment issue about mom getting surgery?” Von Kelsig growled, a little louder this time. “I don't know,” Ryan replied quietly, pleading with a gesture for Von Kelsig to quieten down. “Maybe? Doesn't matter right now, we should go see mom.” “He's got some nerve if he does. Sitting there with his gin trying to find an answer for all his woes when it's pickling his insides as he speaks...” Von Kelsig grumbled, grabbing the banister of the stairs tightly enough to cause the wood to creak as he followed Ryan upstairs. He only realized where he was going when a fresh ray of sunlight stabbed at his face from a thin window above the intermediate landing on the stairs. The giant noticed the difference immediately. The cherry wood of the banister running along the second floor was polished clean, the ivory blush carpeting was hoovered spotless and there wasn't a cobweb to be found anywhere. “Some difference,” he remarked. “I clean up here whenever I've got time,” Ryan said with a sense of responsibility. “This and mom's room; it's the only places she's seen in the last few weeks so I figured I might as well keep it clean so she doesn't worry about it.” “Typical mom,” Von Kelsig chuckled softly. “I can just imagine her moaning about housework and stuff, even if she's ill.” “Well she hasn't said much of anything. The maroon carpet and cream walls must have been hoovered and dusted very recently judging by their cleanliness. A bureau and dresser sat either side of the window, covered with various papers and belongings, and a large Victorian wardrobe sat against the wall opposite the window. Adjacent to that was the large king-size bed, tucked underneath the flowery duvet of which lay a small, pallid figure. “Mom...” Von Kelsig whispered, walking to the foot of the bed. She had barely changed in years. Her soft chin and high cheekbones were more prominent with age, and the only lines to her credit were crow's feet creeping from her eyes. A few wisps of snowy white hair protruded from beneath a lilac night cap. She seemed to be in a peaceful slumber, though several bottles of medication sitting on her bedside cabinet could be attributed to that. “There's maybe five or six days worth of medicine left,” Ryan said, moving to her side and flicking the loose strands of hair out of her face. “After that she'll be awake a lot more...and in a lot of pain. She needs a hospital fast.” “Right,” Von Kelsig said firmly, walking over to the window and peering past the alders to the waste ground beyond. “I want her in hospital today. Phone up an ambulance and make sure she gets into surgery as soon as possible. I have to fly out for a show in Egypt but I'll write you out a blank cheque before I go.” “Sounds good,” Ryan said with a sigh of genuine relief. “When do you think you'll be back?” “Be back?” The giant repeated, forcibly digesting the thought. He had had plenty of time to contemplate his hidden past, and to confront it. Meeting his father again had only confirmed his suspicions that he was better off leaving in the first place, but the sight of his mother in her ill state had summoned that innate desire to care for one's family. Past that however he drew a blank. The thought hadn't occurred to him to stick around and try to salvage something from the wreck of a life he had abandoned. Seeing it now there didn't seem to be any harm in trying; Ryan had potential as a good friend and brother, and of course his mother would welcome him back with open arms... ...that just left the ogre in the den to deal with. “Uh...probably a week or so,” he answered eventually. “I got a show on the twenty-first and then one on the third. Between then I'm pretty much free to do my own thing.” “Awesome man.
