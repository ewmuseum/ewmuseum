Date: Culture Shock, Halo RP Prologue: Crash Into Me (Soon) None of this has happened quite yet. It was a 1997 Dodge Charger – sleek, black, and powerful. A reimagining of the muscle car; the type of vehicle that practically demands to go fast, its engine's purr an entrancing growl, the kind you feel in your bones. It was the kind of car the driver couldn't – wouldn't – resist, pressing his foot nearly through the floor. The speedometer crested seventy miles an hour, then seventy-five, then eighty, and it was still climbing. A small, contented smile crept across the man's face. When he was behind this wheel, driving at this speed, he didn't feel so sore anymore. The pickup truck ran a red light as the Charger flew past, and the heady, heavy crunch of metal on metal boomed past the next intersection. The impact happened just ahead of the driver's side door, right along the wheel well, and that's what saved the driver's life. The car revolved around like a boomerang, leaving another hundred and fifty yards of refuse (rearview mirrors and shrapnel and even a mailbox painted like a barn) before it bowed around a telephone pole. The axle broke, yellow sparks tracing a spiraling line behind the vehicle – that was all that slowed it down. The airbags and sturdy passenger cabin kept the driver safe. The pickup continued lackadaisically away from the scene, clouds of steam escaping from cracks along the hood. Police found the truck with the keys in the ignition, abandoned nearby in a dry riverbed. But then, none of this has happened yet. ---- One: The Wonders of the Internet Hunter Sabuani loved a little dong. Now, before you go starting rumors, we're talking about the chime his iMac made when he turned it on. As the machine booted up and brought his background onscreen (a grinning Jeffrey Dean Morgan as The Comedian in "Watchmen"), Hunter cracked his knuckles in eager anticipation. This was the part of the job his colleagues disliked. Some of them hired people to answer their fan mail. Others were so consumed with the competitive nature of the sport that they can't actually enjoy themselves anymore. One of his good friends (well, one of his former good friends – Hunter's ambition often outstripped his ability to be nice to people) refused to even sign onto the Internet anymore after a particularly ugly flame war with some of his fans. But Sabuani? Sabuani had always loved it. There was something visceral about logging on and gauging immediate responses. As cliché as it might be, whether they loved him or they hated him, it was these fans who bought his merchandise, who watched the television programs he was on, who sat in the stands and waved their signs at him. That didn't mean they were always respectful, of course – not them to him or him to them. Cup of tea in hand (we cannot confirm rumors that cup might have more than a dollop of brandy in it), Hunter levered himself into a padded computer chair, knobby-knuckled fingers flying with remarkable speed across the thin keyboard. If any of his fans had seen him, they might have tossed around words that only the most... intelligent, shall we say... fans might use. He opened up his MySpace page (myspace.com/[detail withheld]), and was greeted with the cranked-up guitars of Rocket From The Crypt's "Break It Up" (the Raja's first entrance music, which will put some of our viewers in the wayback machine). He gave his (ridiculously massive) friend-list a cursory onceover, marveling once again at how many people set their mood to some form of "horny", before clicking on his inbox. Now, when we say that the Raja of Roguishness checked his own fan mail, we don't mean he read it all; he didn't have the attention span for that. Some of the messages had been sent days, if not weeks ago, but all it took was a negligent flick of his mouse wheel to skim over them. But one stuck out – it wasn't a girl who wanted him to check out her webcams or a crazy stalker fan or someone yelling at him because he was a dick on ReVolution. No, it was different. His Jeep hopped the curb as he cut a left shorter than he should have. ---- Two: A Play, Interrupted Madison Sinclair still couldn't believe there was a bar open at this hour, nevermind people inside. And that wasn't even counting that she was one of those people, in a ridiculously short dress and what she'd only term as hooker boots, laughing at a joke from someone who would not only be in a bar at this hour, but would suggest it. "Are you serious?" She laughed her Julia-Roberts-you're-so-funny-and-charming laugh, drawing a wineglass up to her lips. Thankfully, the streaks on the glass (probably the only one in the place, she mused) prevented anyone who wasn't paying more than cursory attention from noticing that the level of the wine wasn't going down. For nearly a year, Madi and her ex-husband worked for one Alexandra Eloise Pierce in Sin City Championship Wrestling, which meant (other than a list of enemies as long as her arm), that the Babe was trained in some things that many wrestling valets wouldn't even have considered. She hated wigs, though. Itchy headpieces that never seemed to fit well against her head (Hunter joked that her brain was too big), they just didn't seem natural. Even the ones that Lauren Fox had made for her in Sin City (and the girl they called Savant was perhaps the best in the world at this type of thing) didn't feel realistic. Madi would rather have let her hair down, put on some music, and get her flirt on; those were things that she was good at. Even as she inched towards the big four-oh, the Babe was just what the name implied, as evinced by the fact that she kept track of Roger's eyes trailing up her legs. But she had to be careful. "So, Jenny," the man said, leaning forward. "You've never told me why you agreed to come to a bar at noon with a guy you just met." "Hrm. That's a good question," she said coyly. She took another "sip" of the swill this place had the audacity to call wine as she pretended to think about it. "Everything I do is so carefully thought out and planned and blah-blah-blah, I just wanted to be spontaneous for once. Don't you ever just want to do something, Rog? I mean, you're the one who suggested this place to start with. Which was odd for a married man to do." "Married?" His eyes widened, and his pull from his beer was a little bit more slurp than swallow. "Why would you..." Sinclair put her hand on his thigh; it was a friendly, not intimate gesture. "Don't worry, Roger. I'm not going to tell her or run away screaming." The smile she'd been keeping at "simmering" moved up the dial to "warm". "But that indentation on your ring finger is lighter than the rest of the skin on your hand. I'm not sure when you took it off, but..." "While... uhm, while you were in the bathroom." "Well..." She drew out the word as she slid her hand into her clutch. "I have a confession to make, too." The ring she removed from her bag was one she hadn't worn in years; not the one that Hunter had given her (that one was engraved with her name, which definitely wasn't "Jenny"), but it would pass inspection. "You're married, too?" Roger O'Reilly laughed. "Guilty. Matt's a controlling bastard, though, so... I don't think I feel guilty." Briefly, Madison wished for a straw in her glass that she could close her red lips around, but she settled for just meeting his gaze and taking a deep breath before speaking. "What about your wife?" When she placed her hand on his thigh the second time, it was less than an inch higher, but that's all it took. He leaned further, scooting a little bit on the barstool so their knees touched. "What Linda doesn't know won't hurt her," he said – breathed, really. "And what doesn't Linda know?" Her face was very close to his, lips barely moving. "We're just two friends having a drink. We haven't done anyth—" Madi's phone jingled in her purse (she'd even turned the iPhone's custom ringer off and replaced it with the generic tone), and she swore softly. "You don't have to answer that," Roger reminded her. She was already pulling her phone out of her purse, though. "I really do," she mumbled, slipping easily to her feet. "Is it him?" "Yeah." She brushed her lips across his cheekbone. "I'll be right right back. Don't find another married lady to hit on." The Babe Roger O'Reilly Wanted to Bang was just out of his earshot when she brushed her thumb across the screen to answer the call. "This had better be important, Hunter – I've almost closed the deal here." "I still think it's weird you're trying to fuck our daughters' teacher's husband," Sabuani said. "But that's your deal and I said I wouldn't get involved. But I got a message. We need to go." "Go?" She cupped her hand around the mouthpiece. "Where do we have to go?" "I got a message. She's coming." Madi stopped in her tracks, quick strides clicking through hard flooring and strewn peanut shells as she moved to the back room of the bar. "You're sure?" "The code checks out. I'm getting the girls – meet me at the airport." Madi was already halfway to the restroom, locking the door behind her. "Where are we headed?" "You'll hate it, and I'm not saying over the phone. Just get here." The phone beeped in her ear as the line went dead. Sinclair leaned against the sink, casting a rather dubious look on the tile floor. "If I catch hepatitis from this, I'm going to be pissed," she muttered as she tugged off her velvety boots. The wig followed, buried in the bottom of the trashcan. It took a solid jerk to force open the many-times-painted window, but Madison was still limber and was able to worm her way through to the street. Wrapping the boot around her fist, she punched at O'Reilly's window, just like a rather large man had taught her once. It only took two shots with the point of the heel to shatter the window (she was proud), and she tossed a balled up piece of fabric under the seat. Pity Roger, for he wouldn't find her little gift, so wrapped up with the blaring car alarm. Padding away barefoot, Madi hailed a taxicab. The driver looked askance at the whorishly dressed woman with the boots over her shoulder, at least until the Babe shoved a hundred-dollar bill at him. She really hoped they didn't strip-search her at the airport, because it would've been hard to explain why she was commando. ---- Three: Out of Dodge "You're not serious." Hunter Sabuani was a lot of things – not very bright, sort of a jerk, but a damn fine dancer for an old man – but even the Raja picked up some things working for Desade. So he'd been ready with a change of clothes for the Babe when they ran into each other at the airport, allowing her to trade the nearly translucent turquoise dress for mom-jeans and a sweatshirt. "Afraid I am," Hunter said as the four of them bustled through the airport, hurrying but trying to look like they were late for a flight, not fleeing. "It's out of her comfort zone and she's not likely to strike right at a show." "But she already knows we're going to Moscow, Hunter." Madi kept her voice low. "You don't think she'll be prepared to do something there?" Sabuani shrugged. "I'm not passing up a payday on account of someone being a bitch." "And what about after that?" Madi said. "Daddy?" Summer rubbed her eyes. "Where are we going?" "We're going on a trip, honey." The smile Madison had for her daughter was very different than the one she'd pulled an hour earlier for the man. "We're taking you girls to see your mom and dad in action." Hus grin didn't have any of the worry in it that hers did. Dakota brightened. "Awesome! Will Rat be there? And Unca D?" The former First Couple of the National Wrestling Council shared a significant look. Wordlessly, Hunter put an arm around both of his daughters and led them ahead as Madison dropped back, thumb flicking through her Contacts list. "Dar, it's me. You need to get out of the house. Go on a vacation, go talk to that girl you really liked in Vermont." She paused, turning her head to the side. Her heart broke a little as she formed her lips into a grin not dissimilar from the one she'd worn earlier – the one she wore when she was working the Madi-magic. "Nothing's wrong. It's just... Hunter and I decided to take the girls with us to Russia, and I don't want you to be bored. Go on, my treat – my AmEx Black is in the upstairs dresser." Another pause. "Great. But be sure to watch – Ratsy's got something ridiculous planned for the Halo. I'll talk to you later!" Madison's steps quickened, catching up to the other three at the gate. She only shared the slightest nod with her ex-husband as she presented her passport. * * * * * Twenty-seven minutes later. Across town in a small, French colonial house in the Rochester suburbs, Darien Thorsen climbed behind the wheel of a jet black 1997 Dodge Charger, grinning as he put the car into gear. He never made it to his destination. ---- Four: Reunited, and It Feels So... Good? Flying sixteen and a half hours on an airplane (counting stops in DC and Frankfurt) with two small girls was no picnic, even if you had the patience of a saint (or were the Saint of a Squared Circle), and it wasn't until sometime over Poland that Dakota finally fell asleep. When the plane landed (just after seven local time, when they'd left just after seven local time... it was very confusing), Madison breathed a sigh of relief. "You're sure we're okay?" she whispered as they stepped a subway car to carry them across the airport. Hunter shifted Dakota from one arm to the other. "Relatively." They stepped into the Moscow evening, the cool breeze tickling Dee's nose and stirring her from her nap. "Daddy?" "Yeah, hon. We're in Russia." "Russia!" Dakota squirmed out of Hunter's arms, falling in step beside her sister. "Ohmigod! Look! Russian stuff! Are there Commie Pinko bastards here?" Hunter waved down a taxicab as Madi hissed her daughter's name under her breath and large, blue-green eyes widened innocently. The four climbed into the black car, a heavyset woman behind the wheel. "I give ride, da?" "Da." The Madison game-face smile was back. "The Ararat Park Hyatt, please." "You here for wrestling event, da?" The driver made small talk as they went. "Big crowd, American TV. We are werry excited." Neither Babe nor Raja responded, Madi smoothing Lil's hair, Hunter's eyes out the window. In America, the woman would have fallen silent. "You kick people in head, yes? My son, he love American wrestling." "That's nice." Sabuani didn't look away. "I remember you, da? He have your poster on wall." "If you shut up, I'll sign it." "When my son is younger man, he love to watch Enn-Doubleyoo-Cee. Watched Internet feed when you close down. Werry mad." "Not my proudest moment ever." "You ever talk with red-haired woman, she control ewerything?" One day, the twins may wonder at their mother and father's looks at each other. But for now, they work, just like spelling long words. "Can we not talk?" Madi asked. "Not talking would be great." The cab drew to a halt, the pudgy-faced woman turning around, arm draped over the backseat. "We here. You go. Good luck at show." Her smile was gap-toothed. The cab door opened, and Hunter stood up with a grunt of a sigh. "Whatever." Sabuani felt the prick of the blade pressed between his ribs, though it didn't penetrate the skin. "Good evening, Hunter," the redhead hissed, voice as hard as a diamond. Though a scarf obscured her face, Sabuani recognized those gray eyes. He tried to keep his voice even. "What do you want?" Pleas on behalf of his children would have fallen on deaf ears. "Not what you think." It seemed she was smiling. "I would like us to work together, one last time. There are some gentlemen I have been asked to be certain are eliminated in your bout this weekend." "Asked? By whom?" He couldn't imagine Lisa Tyler would call her. "That is not your concern. Do as I ask, and I will overlook our differences." The second time, he knew she smiled and he could guess how cold the expression was. "We make a good team, hm?" TO BE CONTINUED
