Bumps in the Road

7 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7th April 2008 Mr. Najera sat the table in the diner sipping on a lukewarm cup of black coffee. He was accustomed to such “common” settings; a man of his distinction belonged in a board-room, not in a run-of-the-mill diner with the scum of the city. With his perfect complexion, impeccably well-kept haircut and $2,000 suit he stuck out like a sore thumb. He immediately noticed his business partner as he slid in through the establishment’s door. Mr. Loftman was dressed in a black pinstripe suit – much like Najera – and walked through the droves of peasants with a look of indignation that only a man of considerable wealth could ever possess. The two Potemkin Management partners chose not to greet each other verbally, instead opting for a noble handshake and nod combination. “I think Adelina was a bad choice,” Mr. Najera, never one to skip around the meat and bones of the matter, immediately proclaimed, barely allowing Loftman the time to find a comfortable seating position on the plastic bench across the table. “She’s bad news, that one…” “I heard what happened,” Mr. Loftman’s concise upstate New York accent responded understandingly. Najera let the last of the coffee dribble down his throat; he appreciated the caffeine bomb, but he certainly didn’t care much of the ridiculously amount of sugar that the waitress had liberally sprinkled into it. “Of all the girls in the world we could have paid to sleep with this guy, we picked the one with the fucking psychopath ex-boyfriend. Typical.” Loftman tittered and glanced at his business partner, expecting a similar response. His tune soon changed when he noticed that Najera found no humour in the subject. “Right. We need to deal with this situation. We don’t want or need a big old spanner in the works like this cropping up…” “Spot on,” began Najera, “from what Jason told me, he damn near mutilated the drunk bastard.” “Does Jason know we paid that two-cent hoe-bag to be with him that night?” Loftman said with a smile. “Not a clue”. Najera smiled back at his partner. “Thing is though, Jason knocked that guy up pretty good… it’s a pretty safe bet that he’ll want a little revenge, especially seeing as it happened in front of a couple of his buddies, you know? And it’s not like it’ll be hard for the guy to track Natas down, now that he’s on national television and all…” Across the table, Loftman tapped the tips of his fingers together contemplatively. “Hmmmm, yes, we might have to do something about this…” “We? We should we have to do anything? This guy signed a contract with us, remember? We can make him do anything we want. Besides, we don’t want the disposing of this creep on our record, now do we?” *** ReVolution 159 had left a bitter taste in Jason Natas’s mouth. He tried to solve that problem in the only way he knew how; by dousing them with alcohol. The Moorings Bar was exactly the kind of place you’d expect to find a man like Jason. Located at the end of one of the most uninviting back alleys in The Bronx, it was where the city’s dropouts and rejects gathered to drown themselves in an alcoholic stupor. The sheer volume of smoke hanging in the air gave the impressive that a man could contract lung cancer by simply inhaling too quickly, and the floorboards were so stained with beer and spirits that they were on the verge of rotting away. The clientele weren’t exactly high-society either; many of them made Jason Natas with his bandanna, unshaved chin, pierced eyebrow and battered leather jacket look like royalty. Shit, even the barman looked like he’d been dragged facedown through a briar patch. As for the toilets? Fucking write-off. Jason swirled the cheap, noxious whiskey around the inside of his mouth. He couldn’t believe the nerve of that little Hawaiian shit-smear. How dare Bryan Dawkins challenge him like that… how dare he? The little shit obviously didn’t have a clue what he was getting himself into. Prior to joining PRIME, Jason had made a career out of putting grown men in hospital… men much larger, stronger and tougher than Bryan Dawkins. And now PRIME’s Resident Rodent wanted to challenge him to a match at UltraViolence? Please… Jason couldn’t understand what it was that possessed the deluded piss-stain to finally grow a set and stand up for himself. Hell, if it were any other guy on the roster then the Flyin’ Hawaiian might have gone up in his estimation by the tiniest possible measure. Not this guy though; Jason Natas couldn’t stand Bryan Dawkins. Everything about the guy wound Jason up good and proper; from his general “hang-loose” attitude to his flashy, over-the-top in-ring style; Jason found absolutely no redeeming qualities in the little wretch whatsoever. He would tear Bryan Dawkins to shreds; there was no doubt in his mind whatsoever, and he would feel no remorse for the Hawaiian’s plight. Bryan Dawkins had stuck his nose in the business of The Anti-Superstar, and for that he would have to suffer the consequences, no matter how dire they may be. The little bastard needed to learn a lesson, and if it cost him his career, so be it… Bryan Dawkins was not Jason’s immediate concern though, as there was still the task of defeating Troy Douglas at 160. Troy Douglas, like Natas, had come into PRIME and quietly made a name for himself without any need for flashy ring entrances and over-the-top promos. He’d mostly kept himself to himself and let his in-ring skills do the talking. Jason appreciated that; he saw a lot of himself in Troy Douglas, and while it would a considerable stretch of the imagination to say that he liked Troy, he certainly had a lot more time for him than he did some of the prissy little prima donnas in PRIME. For the first time in his young wrestling career, Jason would be going into a match without the unrelenting desire to rearrange the facial features of his opponent. Two big, strong competitors slugging it out in the centre of the ring… he might actually enjoy it. Of course, he had been on the losing side of a Troy Douglas match in the past. This time would be different, though; this time he wouldn’t have to worry about that buffoon Simply Beautiful screwing everything up. This time he would win. He was sure of it. The tag victory over Chandler Tsonda, Tyler Rayne and a certain Hawaiian fucktard had buoyed his confidence to no end; guess we’ll all find out if it’s justified at ReVolution. All thoughts of his fledgling wrestling career soon left him as he caught glimpse of something at the other end of the bar. Necking the final few drops of his drink, Jason turned around on his stool; he recognised the guy instantly. Long, frayed black hair that looked like it had been washed with goose fat, a disgusting, near-toothless yellow smile… “Ray,” he muttered to himself. It was him; the same prick that had turned up on Adelina’s doorstep just a few short weeks ago looking for a fight, drinking in Jason’s bar? The Anti-Superstar couldn’t believe it. Either Ray had walked in here completely by accident, or he’d come seeking a little revenge for the hurting that Jason had put onto him that night. After all, Natas had threatened to carve his eyebrow out with a razorblade; the lust for revenge would be completely understandable… Jason didn’t give a shit for Ray’s motivation for being here as he leapt from his stool and strode across the bar like a lion on the hunt. He tapped the revolting individual on the shoulder of his grey trenchcoat. “Hey, fucko, I think you an’ I need to have words…” *** “Get yer filthy fuckin’ mits off of me!” Jason barked as the bar’s two in-house security guards tossed him out into the wet, New York City evening. They shook their heads in disdain as they disappeared back into the confines of The Moorings, leaving a half-intoxicated Jason Natas alone in the alley. He couldn’t understand why he was the only one getting kicked out. Alright, he had thrown the first punch when Ray had refused to come outside, but surely if a fight erupts inside a bar it’s common policy to eject both parties, regardless of who started it? “Shit,” agonised Jason as he rubbed his jaw in pain. The power of Ray’s punch had caught him off guard; clearly he hadn’t expected the guy to be that handy with his fists. This was something he wasn’t used to. Ray had officially gotten one over on Jason Natas, and immediately Jason didn’t like it one bit… he was a professional fighter for fuck’s sake, he shouldn’t be getting punked out by some alcoholic shitbag. The pain slowly began to wear off; all those years fighting in the streets had served him well. Jason would have his retribution; if was a case of when, not if.
