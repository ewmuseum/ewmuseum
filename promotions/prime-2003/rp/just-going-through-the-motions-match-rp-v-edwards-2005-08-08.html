Trapped in the echoing blur between awake and slumber, Karina Wolfenden’s red, dry eyes stared pleadingly at the television. Unable to sleep, she'd spent the last half hour cycling through every channel that she was unfortunate to receive, trying to find something that would either anchor her awake, or finally push her over the edge into deep, welcome sleep. But, alas, her melancholy state wasn't capable of forming an opinion either way, leaving her in limbo. With nothing to wake up for the next day, and nothing to keep her awake, the K-Wolf was juggled between the two extremes, pleading for one to fumble and make the choice for her. The days since Colossus II had dragged painfully. Drained motivation left her indifferent to all that presented itself to her, detachment leaving her to wander further from life's path, the yellow brick road of normality she'd briefly rejoined. She didn't even have it in her to properly mock Aimz, was pathetically lingering backstage for two hours in case she caught sight of Angelo Deville, and had nothing in the tank to fight Hin See's stark, honest words. Rather than tearing into life, fists flying and feet furiously lashing out, Karina was just rolling with the punches, being rocked by the waves of realisation. Littering the carpet around the couch were discarded bottles, an empty pizza box, paired with a similarly sparse ice-cream tub. Barely having moved a muscle since collapsing onto the engulfing couch, the churning food acted as ballast, keeping the usually active K-Wolf grounded. '-ing for a takedown, but great defence by Ch-' Yawnclick. '-hat's with all the carrots, what do they need such good eyesight for anywaaaa-' Ughclick. Fumbling around for the glass somewhere beneath her, Karina's face soured as her tongue began to break down the coating of the sleeping pill, spreading a chalky bitterness through her taste buds. Finally grabbing the glass, after a duo of fumbled attempts, Karina dropped her head down to it, dribbling Pepsi down over her bottom lip as a couple of forced gulps washed the tablet down her throat. Wiping her chin, the K-Wolf pushed herself deeper against the arm of the vast sofa, relaxing her body to feel the drugs to their trick... gradually shutting her willing body down from its extremities. II - Still, I Always Feel This Strange Estrangement. She should have been collapsing, gasping for air, while gritting teeth through the scorch of cramped muscled. A lithe body, glistening with perspiration, should have been sticking clothing and hair to it, a salty taste dripping over her lip and onto her tongue. Knuckles should have stung, feet should have had the usual, numb sensation creeping in. It should have been... different. Standing in front of the kick-bag, barely set in her kick stance, the K-Wolf seemed to toy with it, rather than explode upon the unfortunate object with every bit of frustration that had built up within her. Passers by usually winced, but were unable to look away as she all but caught fire from the intensity of her work out. But, rather than the slap of a gloved fist against the canvas, only a cushioned, barely audible thump was easily lost in the stale, sweaty air. Karina's right leg then timidly stretched out, but the contact didn't even manage to dislodge the price sticker someone had left on the bag, just by where passive contact was made. Blonde and pink hair retained its volume and style, not a bead of sweat to be seen emerging through the pores of her pale, lightly freckled skin. Figure-clinging cut-off vest top and shorts, in pale blue, kept its soft, fabric look... no signs of exertion. She'd done the same thing, twice every week when she remained in the States. The feeling of her heart barely able to remain in her humble chest was one she relished, along with the gulps of oxygen, trying to control the near-incinerating heat of her lungs. The bag was everything in life that grated her in the days before... usually enough to last for a whole hour without even having to give it much thought. Life seeped into her through every pore, and this was her cleansing. Only, lately, life was just being expelled from her path, none of it being soaked up, absorbed. Her sprit had been dampened, insulated, and no spark of lust, frustration, or intrigue could ignite it. Every day was a dead end, the countless hours of sleep just shoving her back to where she started the day before. Karina couldn't get her footing in the universe any longer, slipping and sliding aimlessly. Her career, her life, her love. Stagnant. For someone who was never afraid to hurl herself of anything tall enough to give a decent rush, the K-Wolf was shying away from shuffling forward even an inch in the grand scheme of things. 'Hey,' went a polite enquiry from the man who'd watch patiently as Karina failed to do anything but paw at the bag for the last 10 minutes. 'You done?' Blinking, Karina appeared to be awoken from conscious slumber. 'Huh?' 'The bag,' the man then gestured, patting the canvas. 'You've been, well, pretty much trying to stare it down for the last 10 minutes. And I tried that once before, the bag always wi-' Oblivious to the flirty joke that was rolling off the confident man's sliver-tongue, Karina just grumbled her answer, breaking away. 'Yeah, I'm done...' The tilting back of Karina's head helped drop her eyelid down, the powerful shower spray pummelling against her face. Fingers, for the first time in years not blighted with grazes or chipped nails, ran through her hair as Karina arched her body back, opening it up to the fierce heat of the water. She'd subconsciously hoped that it might catalyse her soul, heat triggering the hyper-active chemical reactions that fuelled her everyday life... but as pale skin began to blot with scalded, reddish-pink, all she could do was yawn. Karina's body still hadn't cooled from the shower as she strolled out in laboured fashion, steaming under her long-sleeve t-shirt and baggy 3/4 lengths. Even with the teasing breeze, the air seemed as stale as that inside, not carrying the usual, crisp taste, and chill to the moist tongue. Untied sneaker laces danced along the sidewalk as the K-Wolf slumped forward into her strides, head hanging low, content to watch her toes kick along a discarded piece of tree bark. The distraction was an overstated salvation. The grind of tyres over the asphalt failed to register with Karina, and the angered stabbing of the horn could barely lift her head. 'What'cha starin' at?' appealed the inconvenienced driver, no sooner having left the parking lot, finding himself blocked in by the plodding Wolfenden. 'The road ain't for walkin'!' As soon as she'd cleared its path, the car's tyres screeched, bellowing up smoke as traction eluded them for a brief second. A further, departing dose of horn caused Kari's eyes to narrow in spite, but her body language was far from the fiery, animated manner expected from the K-Wolf. 'Asshole.' Further down the bleak gradient she slipped... holding out for that thought that would pierce through the enveloping gloom. Her body wasn't made for being so sluggish... every fibre fine-tuned for explosive action. Thoughts were used to racing dangerously fast through her head, ideas constantly overlapping and superseding the last, rather than her mind just being a worn-out haze. Karina Wolfenden knew what it was like to feel alive, as much waking in the heat of the morning sun as when watching the ground grow in her free-falling sights. She couldn't just go through the motions, devoid of drive, drained of passion. Things weren't done just to fill time, for the sake of it. Every act, action, passion, desire, was as much a part of the K-Wolf as lungs, heart, and brain. To simply stroll was to cease breathing. To sit back and watch the mundane overtake her was to be declared brain-dead. To wallow in the negatives was to feel her heart's beat stutter and stall. To jump-start herself, to get her head back above water, Karina needed to embrace that which she'd pushed away. The one thing that kept her anchored in everyday reality, truly challenged her, always changing the rules, a moving target. The universe barely had room for just the one of them... and Karina had all but squandered it. Angelo. III - Will I Stay This Way Forever? Life was still feeling out of sync, like Karina wasn't quite tuned into the right frequency. But she had the solution. Or, rather, in her head was what she hoped would be the solution. She couldn't wake up to many more bright suns that failed to shine through her veil or grey, still tired and sluggish despite 14 hours of solid, deep sleep. It was time to fine tune what her life had before, stop the bars running down through her vision, and rid her inner ear of the dull, nagging crackle. The birds' chirping lacked any vibrancy, and only seemed to call out to the dark, brewing clouds that gathered ominously overhead. Deep in their charcoal swirls, a gurgling grumble of thunder seems to orbit overhead, calling out to her. A light pitter-patter of rain played percussion to the dull bass of her footsteps, but soon merged into a tuneless hum as the warm, sticky rain soaked through her top. All around her, people covered their heads with newspapers, scrambling for cover. Others pulled their hoods up, burrowing hands deep in pockets as they hastened their stride. 'Maybe this is trying to tell me something...' the still doubtful Karina mumbled, but remained on course. She'd spent all night, awake, convincing herself that this was the right thing to do... the nudge that her universe needed to correct its path, latch back onto the beacon of happiness and contentment. For someone who'd never grown past her care-free, teenage years that passed too fast, Karina was about to catch up in one, bold leap. Far harder than any jump her board had propelled her from. Far more daring than any building transition she'd cleared on a Free-Run. And one that made her stomach far more sickly than her first Base-Jump. To the K-Wolf, those were the ordinaries that everyone else had to tag an 'extra' in front of. It was the normal, natural progressions she found daunting. The rustling of leaves, stimulated by the swirling breeze, added a higher layer to the blur of sound that Karina was immersed in, only angry hammerings on car horns, or the pulsing of deep, passing bass able to break through it. In her head, as lyrics to go over the dull melody, she repeated her new mantra. 'It's what I need to do.' Sneakers embedded in damp gravel, the grinding muffled where each indentation promptly became a puddle. As the thick, stodgy rain continued to pound down, having felt her top sodden in under 30 seconds, Karina zipped it to her chin in a losing attempt to keep herself somewhat dry... not wanting to hurry. The longer the walk took, the longer she had to convince herself that she wasn't insane, something that had become a rather worryingly frequent task. Flared, faded lilac jeans caught under the heel of a drenched suede sneaker, embedding grit and mud into the denim. 'It's what I need to do,' intensified throughout her head, lips mouthing the mantra. Pink fronted, blonde hair was plastered down across her face by the rain, leaving strands to drop the warm water into Karina's repeating lips. A drizzle tickled around the outside of her ear, before swirling inside, leaving the K-Wolf to tilt her head to the side, agitated as it worked its way back out. 'Soooo could have picked a better day,' Karina bemoaned, peeking a look up through her damp fringe at the low sky, torn in two by a surge of lightening. A shiver worked its way down Karina's body as the harsh wind chilled her sodden form, arms hugging around her lithe body for warmth. Feet began to drag through the gravel, turning red suede to a dank, mottled brown where wet mud was dredged up through the stone. A quick look at her watch was pointless, since no time was going to be the right time. 'Last chance to back out of this...' she mumbled to herself as a pouncing stride from the gravel took her up to the fourth step. Scraping the hair from her face, high, prominent cheekbones creating a run-off for the water cascading down it, Karina just stared at the door standing before her. Left hand removed itself from its damp pocket, while the right remained, cupping around something. A glance back over her shoulder, where I lightning fork violently attacked the ground below helped make up her mind... back wasn't an option. 'Fuck it,' she decreed with a sigh, betraying the tiredness her body was feeling through to the bone. 'What the hell else is there?' IV - Sleepwalk Through My Life's Endeavour? Angelo Deville cocked an eyebrow as the heavy door-knocker pierced through brick, stone, and plaster... losing little potency before it rang his brain inside throbbing skull. 'Dramatic effect is rapidly losing the fight against just getting a doorbell,' the Crimson Angel grumbled, not raising his attention from the day's headlines... or, rather, the more tasty, recognisable little stories scattered around them, some prompting an almost sinister smirk. Above answering a door, Angelo simply slipped back deeper into the firm leather of the chair, folding the newspaper back over, and resting it on his thigh. His curiosity grew as the faint rumble of commotion wafted up the stairs, down the hall, and under the door of his study. As feet stomped up the antique staircase, a far more subtle echo joined them as the chase began. Always one to flourish in the chaos, Angelo licked his lips, fingers entwined with intrigue. '-ister Devi-' went a snippet of the conversation, able to reach the Crimson Angel without another, dominant sound drowning it out. '-cially not y-' A loud crash, mixing both the harsh shatter of glass, and the crashing hum of metal, resonated through the original floorboards, the whole house acting as an amplifier for even the slightest movement. He liked it that way... needed it that way. '-at was an anti-' the protests continued, voice struggling to remain formal under the stress. '-sed down through centu-' Another clattering sound was followed by stumbling footsteps, and a gasp of relief as an ornament was caught... before the inhaled gulp of someone walking over the catcher. 'I... don't care.' Upon hearing the voice, the welcoming smirk across Deville thin lips wavered, his often-estranged partner always able to disrupt the serenity he purposefully surrounded himself with. She'd made him punch the wall almost as often as she'd made him smile, the stinging of grazed knuckles cancelling out that warm shudder of pure, untainted happiness she'd gifted him on occasion. Angelo Deville knew people. That was his trick... simple as. He'd know the haunts you'd lurk in before you even arrived in the city. He'd know how much you'd ask for, and how much you'd settle for. He'd know the car you'd drive, the colour of your wife's hair... natural and dyed, and which flank your holster was strapped. He'd know the real emotion behind the smile, the truth behind the bluff, the heart rate within the chest. He'd know what counter you'd use for his moves... and what counter you'd use in an fruitless attempt to counter his counter. He'd know what was above the dotted line before ink was even in the pen he'd use sign on it. But Karina, she was cloaked to him. A bemusing, haphazard bundle of thought, emotions, and dreams. Ideas plucked seemingly at random from the mix, unable to ever truly make up her mind. Her face could shift through all manner of expressions in the blink of an eye before settling, while her voice would ride waves of inflections, cycling through tones. She had no rhyme nor reason to her... as enthralling as it was infuriating. But over the past few months, the latter had dominated, the novelty worn off. He could only come across so many dead-ends within the Labyrinth before the lure of its centre dwindled. Letting out a disappointed sigh, Angelo deflated, turning his chair towards the window so that he faced away from the door. 'What is it now, Karina?' exhausted words demanded to know, a tinge of weakness somehow hoping that she'd say something to give him inspiration, hope, once more. 'Just turn around,' she pleaded, for once her voice offering the apologetic softness that told Angelo that she might have learned. 'Please.' Angelo desperately tried to find something in the outside world to grab his attention, to draw him thru the glass, but the deep carbon swirling of the sky that Kari's arrival had ushered in cast a veil, down like a blind. 'Why? It's hardly like saying things to my face ever makes a difference with you.' Although his words were stern, the Crimson Angel was having to tense his very soul to keep their edge. His Juliet pulled double duty as his Achilles' heel. 'Please,' she repeated, the first time he'd ever heard her use the word twice in the same conversation. 'No, really, that funny little guy you have around the place wouldn't let me wipe my feet, so this isn't doing your carpet much good.' Despite every attempt to stifle it, a sharp exhaling through the nose hinted at a chuckle from the Crimson Angel. Admitting defeat, despite the defences he'd hoped would hold, Angelo rose from his seat, placing a finished glass of scotch down on the table beside him... barely a hint of amber remaining amidst the degrading ice. The soles of his house shoes felt tacky amidst the carpet, more due to reluctance and hesitation than any sticky substances. 'Fine,' was spoken as bluntly as the stomping step with which Angelo turned. For that briefest of seconds, relief washed over Angelo. There was no sign of the frustrating enigma that was Karina Wolfenden... until tufts of choppy blonde and pink drew his eyes down. 'What... no, K-' 'Marry me.'
