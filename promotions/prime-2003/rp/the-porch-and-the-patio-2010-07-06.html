The Porch and the Patio

6 July 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 6th I can’t remember the last time I was happy. That’s not to say I’m walking around like a character straight outta Twilight, whining endlessly about my crushed soul and eternal loneliness… I just can’t remember the last time I was actually happy. Sure, I’ll laugh at a joke, smile at an amusing story, but it’s more a courtesy or force of habit than it is genuine emotion. There’s a lot of things that are probably contributing to my general shittiness. A few months ago, I found out my wife had cheated on me some years back. My boss wants to make my career a living hell. My best friend just came back to PRIME with a new pet, and all he does is bark every time I try to go near her lawn… uhh…anyways, I’m getting the cold shoulder from the one person who’s always willing to shoot the breeze over a couple beers. But the truth is, for a 240lb badass who fights for a living and calls himself “the Inhuman Being”, I’ve been on the verge of losing it ever since my dad told me he has cancer. It hangs over everything. When I’m lacing up the boots… Dad has cancer. When I’m tucking the kids in at night… Dad has cancer. Flying across the country 30,000 feet in the air… cancer. I can push it to the back of my mind, but I can’t push it out, and even from way back there, it shouts loud enough to be heard just fine. And the problem is, I don’t know how it will ever go away. He’ll fight and fight, and It’ll always be there. And when he dies, the burden of wondering and waiting will be lifted, but all that’ll take its place will be the crushing feeling that even at 34 years of age, I’m just a scared boy who doesn’t know what to do without his Dad. So, sometimes I wonder if I’ll ever actually be happy again, capable of smiling from something other than conditioning. Content with my life. July 6th ~Doing Yard Work~ “Jesus, it’s hot.” The the sweat rolled down her face, dripping off her chin and falling between her breasts. Mary balled a fist under her tank-top and pulled the shirt up, wiping her forehead dry as she placed a hand on her hip in defiant protest against the hellacious humidity. It was just another Ohio summer day. The sun shone bright, clouds to cowardly to offer any protection, and the humidity amplified the heat, hanging in the air thick as syrup. From halfway across the yard, Matt Ward stared at his wife, neglecting his duties of spraying down the deck and patio. “Well, I could offer to spray ya with the hose, but with your shirt already hiked up over your head, I think that might be crossin’ a line.” Instantly, Mary removed her hand, letting her tank-top fall back into place. “Smartass.” “Just sayin, sweetie, the neighbors are like seventy years old. You’re bound to give ‘em a heart attack.” “It’s hot. Whaddaya want from me?” “Well, if we weren’t having this stupid cookout, we wouldn’t have to be out here slaving away, trying to get the backyard ready for a thousand guests.” “And if you hadn’t been in Arkansas on the 4th, we could both be inside right now, sitting on the couch, in the airconditioning, not doin’ a damn thing.” “What sense does it make to celebrate The 4th of July on July 6th anyways. I think this holiday is kinda locked in stone.” “To spend time with family, Matthew. That’s what people are supposed to do.” Sensing an inability to win the argument, Matt un-kinked the hose and continued spraying the patio, blasting away strands of grass and browned leaf petals. The remnants of earth trickled down tiny streams of water that flowed between the cleavage of the stone. Immediately, his mind went back to his wife’s near-striptease “We need ice.” “Huh…” his head jerked up, as if he’d been busted, caught in the act of provocative thought. “What’d you say?” “We need to pick up a couple bags of ice to fill the coolers. I figure we need to get all the pop and beer on ice by three?” “I can go pick some up now. Patio’s about finished.” “Are you sure? We can go after I get the rest of these flowers planted.” “Nah, honestly… its ok. I’ve got some other things to do anyways. I’ll just stop and get ice while I’m out.” “Where else ya goin?” “Nowhere special. Just got a couple errands to run. Washing away the last blade of grass, he twisted the nozzle of the hose, watching as the powerful jet fizzled to wimpy drip. July 6th ~Buying Ice~ Matt stepped out of his car, shutting the door behind him as his eyes darted up and down the street, rushing past the old porch of Rob Hicken’s house. It’d been a couple years since Matt had stepped foot in this part of town, even longer since he’d made it to Rob’s particular street. Moats Park, just at the dead end, looked, fittingly, as lifeless as it ever had. There’d never been more than a couple swingsets at the park, no slide or sea-saws , but now, the swings looked more like relics for an anthropologist to study than playground equipment for children to enjoy. It wouldn’t have surprised him to find that no kid had been on the things in months. Anymore, the park was used almost exclusively for softball games on sticky summer evenings, where bugs swarmed the field lights, casting eerie glows on the grass. But when it wasn’t playing host to a company softball game, or offering a teenage couple a place to make-out in private, it sat empty. Kind of sad, yet strangely impressive, the way it defied time, never changing. It didn’t have its moments, it didn’t rise and fall with economic booms and recessions, or cultural shifts. It was just Moats Park. Turning his attention away, Matt located Rob sitting on his porch, just like he’d promised when Matt had called. Once or twice, in high school, he’d sat on that same porch with Robbie and a couple other friends, talking till four in the morning… just shooting the shit about everything from the laughably stupid to the uncomfortably real. It’d been a decade and a half since Matt had sat on that porch. Almost that long since he’d seen Rob, other than the random passing at the grocery or at the Autozone. “You sure about this?” “Yeah. My prescriptions out.” Matt stepped onto the porch, dusty grey boards creaking beneath his feet. “This stuff ain’t nothing to mess ‘round with. Pain pills are for real.” “So you’re a dealer and a spiritual guide?” Matt reached into his back pocket and pulled out his wallet. The leather was worn, cracked and faded, a stark contrast to the crisp twenty dollar bills he pulled from their cave. “ Can’t be good for business, Robbie.” “I’ve known you since Jr high, Ward. My business will be fine without you as a customer. I’d rather see you not get too messed up.” “You sell this stuff to junkies, but are gonna preach like that.” “Do what ya gotta do to make a buck. Doesn’t mean I’m all about it.” “Rob, I can buy it from you, or I can buy it from someone else.” Rob sighed, but found himself incapable of arguing with the logic. His morality only ran so deep, and if nothing else, he could at least make sure his old high school buddy wasn’t getting any messed up shit. He handed over two small bottles, exchanging them for a wad of twenties. “Take it easy with these, Ward.” “Thanks, Robbie.” “Anything else?” Matt pocketed the pills “You know who’s got the cheapest ice?” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ I can’t remember the last time I was happy, but slowly, I’m forgetting what it feels like to hurt. Maybe the trade-off isn’t so bad?
