Date: Various "You're my agent, an-" "I'm your agent NOW. But once your career gets rolling, you'll drop me on my fucking ass-cheeks like you did in '04." "I said I was sorry, Walter. Thirty-five years in the business seems like a fine time to complain about how Hollywood works." "Regardless, I promised your father that I'd look after you when he passed away, so I'm doing this for him." "First good thing he's done for me." "Eventually you'll stop meaning that. Look, Danny, I’d like nothing better than to book you some mindless budget-buster where you unleash guttural screams, roundhouse kicks and automatic weaponry fire for 90 minutes straight. With this strike, though, those screenplays aren’t being written. Only art house companies have WGA deals. There's no action movies being made." "Funny, I didn’t think those needed scripts." "...says the man who’s required daily re-writes on every film he’s done." "Come on, that’s not true." "It doesn’t matter if it’s true, that’s the rep you have, and that makes you a leper at the moment. Nobody’s taking flyers on you when they can book Clooney at a low market rate." "Thanks." "Do you want me to blow smoke up your ass, or to be straight? The elite take the dream projects. There are under 100 greenlit scripts in town right now, so they ARE the dream projects. Oscar-winners are taking supporting roles for first-time directors. They’re signing on for projects that they’d NEVER consider otherwise. There's nothing out there." "Well this is turning out to bea great comeback." "Are you kidding me, Lance? I just came off chemo and you're talking comeback?" "Would it kill you to call me Dad?" "You're my father, Lance, not my Dad." "Fine, be childish. I should expect that after you ran off to be a wrestling star and got in this mess." "What, you mean got cancer?" "Danny, what I'm saying is that you reach a point in your life when you stop dreaming about who you could be and come to terms with who you are. You haven't reached that point yet, but it's going to come." "So I should shut up and go with whatever hack project you and Walter lined up." "It's not a hack project, it's the voice of a giraffe in a DreamWorks animated deal. Ben Stille-" "Lance, this is why I left acting. I want to control my destiny, not be the voice of cartoon animals." "Son, standing against the current is a fine thing to do when you're trying to change the world, but this is show business. You need to have a certain viscosity to survive; you need to be able to go with the flow." "And where's that flow supposed to lead me?" "If we're lucky, it will take you wherever you need to be." "Excuse me?" "I said I've got somewhere I need to be, Blaine." "Ok, this will only take a minute. I know our relationship has gone sour lately, but I wanted to say thanks. I got some early reports on our quarter-hours from FX, and they're phenomenal. Plus, once LA knew you were wrestling tonight, they were lining up outside." "So it's justified, then, to suspend me and then ride the comeback show to big money?" "Your steroid suspension was justified when you were caught with steroids." "Blaine, two stories came up over the weekend. One was Roger Clemens trying to refute that he was on something. Public response to that was 'bullshit, he was using.'" "Dan-" "Then Sylvester Stallone admits that he took testosterone and HGH for Rambo, and public response to THAT amounted to 'well, duh.'" "I have a computer and a television, but thanks." "My point is, why am I getting compared to the Rocket and not Sly?" "Because people want to believe that what we do is real. You can tell them that wrestling is fake six days a week, and they'll still tune in on the seventh and want to believe otherwise. They see you as an athlete, and they see PRIME as sport. It doesn't matter how indignant you get about it. As long as SportsCenter airs Wrestlemania highlights, you will be publicly crucified for the devious sin of trying to enhance your performance." "Or some witch-hunting shithead with a blog finds my name on a shipping order and sees fit to tamper with my mail publicly." "All in the name of sporting integrity." "Kid said he was trying to find dirt on Derek Jeter." "Ladies and gentlemen, the future of journalism." "To hell with integrity; I’m an actor." "Right, then...This is your copy of our now-null-and-void contract. I wish you'd have spoken to me before agreeing to the 'out' clause. Regardless of our tenuous personal relationship, this was a great business arrangement." "The thing is, Blaine...I think I made a mistake." "...Oh. I thought you had bigger and better things to do." "So did I, but right now, this is the biggest and best thing I’ve got. They say the great ones leave them wanting more, but hell, Blaine, this is goddamned coitus interruptus; I'm pulling out before the magic happens." "Interesting word choice." "I'm not gonna bullshit here. You saw all those famous faces in the front row tonight. All due respect to PRIME, but they weren’t here for the show. They sniffed a live TV broadcast that didn’t involve crossing a picket line. Half this town can't land an infomercial and I just starred on one of cable’s highest rated shows." "So thirty minutes after you walk out, I should take you back?" "They were turned back in droves tonight. We'd both be walking away from a great situation. Like you said, this was a great business arrangement." "..." "One-year deal, cut my salary in half." "Let me get the lawyers on the phone. But are you sure, Danny? You said you were done." "Blaine, you reach a point in your life when you stop dreaming about who you could be and come to terms with who you are." "And that's where you're at now?" "Not yet...but I'm getting there."
