Ghost Story: Part Two - A Friend in Need (Jewel in the Crown Round Two vs. Devin Shakur)

4 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 4th, 2007 “Shit. Ow.” The thud of his head against the cold concrete released him from unconsciousness. “The Blue Collar Brawler,” Wade Elliott brought a hand to the back of his head, rubbing it as he started to sit up. His faced winced and he slid to a seated position, attempting to obtain his bearings. His white t-shirt was stained, and his blue jeans and steel-toe work boots were dirty as usual. He peered around for his drifter's hat, to which there was no sign, and his search revealed to him his where-abouts. “Fuckin' figures.” The iron bars containing him from society explained everything. He was alone in the jail cell, in a prison he could only guess was somewhere in Calgary, Canada, where he arrived two days prior for PRIME's one-hundred and forty-seventh broadcast of ReVolution. He pushed himself to his feet, using the wall for support as he rubbed his aching head. On the other side of the bars stood a guard, dressed in full uniform, clean shaven, somewhere in his mid thirties. Wade stepped up to grip his cage and speak to his captor. “So uh,” started Elliott. “What'm I in fer?” The guard turned his head with a smirk. “Don't remember? I'm not surprised.” “Well, glad to know I ain't surprisin', but that don't answer my fuckin' question.” “Watch it bub,” snapped the officer. “You're the one behind bars here.” Wade did not reply. He simply nodded his head and scratched his scalp through his short brown hair. After an awkward pause, he decided to try his luck again. “So lemme ask again...what'm I in fer?” The officer sighed. “A bartender at Double Hockey Sticks Tavern called in around two in the morning, said there was someone causing a ruckus, got into a bit of a brawl with a few locals. We showed up to find you stumbling and shouting about in the parking lot, wasted drunk. You were heading to your truck when we grabbed you. Lucky your keys weren't in your hand, or else this would be a much bigger problem. At most you're looking at public intoxication and an assault charge.” “Lucky me.” Another odd pause. “Wait...my dog was in my truck. Where'd ya take my pup?” asked Wade with genuine concern. “We took the dog to the local pound. He'll be safe there until you can retrieve him.” Wade visibly relaxed. “And my truck?” “It's at impound, also safe until you can retrieve it.” Wade took in the information. “How long am I in fer?” he asked. “That's not my decision. I'd guess a few days, unless someone can bail you out.” “Shit,” spat Wade, well aware of his match against Devin Shakur in a few days. “You do have a phone call. Anyone you can contact to post bail?” asked the officer. Wade thought a minute. There were only two that came to mind. One being the higher ups at PRIME, but Lisa Tyler and Blaine Blair would never let him hear the end of it. The second being Lindsay Troy. He thought on Lindsay for a bit before shaking his head. “It ain't her problem,” he muttered under his breath. “Hmmm?” queried the officer. “No one,” Wade said quickly. “No one comin' to mind right now.” “Alright,” replied the officer. “You let me know when you figure it out.” “Yeah,” said Wade, walking to the small cot that served as a bed and taking a heavy seat. “I'll do that.” * * * * * * * “Nice place ya got here.” Wade's fierce blue eyes opened to the gravely voice. He breathed out his nose, well aware of who was sitting next to him as he rose from bed, swinging his legs to the hard cement floor to sit at the edge of his cot. “Evenin', Bill.” “Evenin,” replied the legendary western law man, Wild Bill Hickock, or at least his specter. He leaned against the wall, wide brimmed hat on his knee. “Why ain't ye made yer phone call yet?” asked Bill. “Ain't no one to call,” answered Wade, eyes set on the metal bars of his cage. “You know that ain't true.” “How the fuck would you know that?” “Do I need to fuckin' repeat myself,” said a stern Hickcock. “I know everythin' about you, Wade.” “Yer gonna have to explain that real fuckin' quick.” “In time, don't git yer britches all bunched.” “Can they hear us talkin',” asked the Southern Sparkplug, changing the subject. “Can't hear me talkin',” Bill replied. “But they can sure as hell hear you.” “Fuckin' wonderful. I'm sure with my talkin' to myself they'll be more inclined to let me off the fuckin' hook.” Wild Bill grinned, his long gray mustache inching upward. He paused for a bit, eyes squinting in contemplation at the Bad Dog. “Why ain't you callin' her?” “It ain't her problem.” “But she's sure to fix yer problem, and she'd do it without a fuss.” “I wouldn't say without a fuss,” Wade corrected. “But yer right. She'd be here in a fuckin' flash.” “How is it a hard-ass like yerself ends up hangin' 'round a fine lady like Lindsay Troy?” asked Bill. “Don't ask me,” said Wade, standing and walking to the bars of his cell, leaning his head against them. “She treats ya real well,” said Hickock. “Yeah, and she fuckin' shouldn't.” “What makes ya say that?” “You should know,” said Wade, turning his head back to the ghost sitting on his cot. “Claimin' to know me n' all.” “I do know,” shot back Bill. “I'm wonderin' if she does.” Wade closed his eyes, releasing a heavy sigh through his bushy brown goatee. “She don't know anythin' 'bout me.” Bill stared at the troubled Drifter. He stood up, returning his hat to his head and striding across the cell, taking position next to Wade. “Probably best she don't,” said Wild Bill, eyes gazing through the bars. “It is best she don't.” A nod from the lawman. “So ya just gonna sit in here?” “Fer a bit, I 'spect,” Wade replied, shrugging his shoulders. “Let that fuckin' cunt, Shakur take a win due to forfeit?” Wade furrowed his brow and curled his lips inward, avoiding response. Bill grinned again before turning his head to the angry Wade Elliott. “Look 'ere, Wade,” he said. “When ya can't handle yer own problems ya better fuckin' hope yer friends can, and from what I know, Troy's yer only friend on the fuckin' planet. Ye want outta this fuckin' cell?” “Naw, I like it 'ere,” said Wade, drenched in sarcasm. “Wanna beat the piss outta Shakur again?” “I'd like to stomp his fuckin' head into chunks've bloody shit.” “Then pull up yer skirt an' make the fuckin' call.” Wade snapped his head to the side at the comment. “What the fuck d'ya mean “pull up yer...” Bill was gone. Wade sighed, and turned his head back to the bars. “Fuckin' asshole.” * * * * * * * “'ello, I'm lookin' to contact a 'Lindsay Troy.'” Wade rubbed his head within the police station's phone booth. An officer waited outside, hand-cuffs dangling from his finger and ready to snap onto Elliott's wrists. Wade waited patiently until the operator could make the connection. He perked up as a familiar voice came through the other end. “'ello, Lindsay? It's Wade. I'm in a bit've trouble...yeah, that kinda trouble. I need ya to git to Calgary...The Calgary Police Station...” Wade suddenly wrenched the phone away from his ear, his face wincing in pain as his ear drum exploded, the quiet but sharp tones of the Queen of the Ring slicing through the ear piece. The Bad Dog waited a moment before putting the phone to his ear again. “So you'll git here soon? Thanks Lindsay.” Wade hung up the phone and left the booth, where he was quickly cuffed by the waiting officer. “Powerful voice on that one,” said Wade, eliciting a chuckle from the guard. Elliott cracked a grin as he was herded back to the barracks. “Told ya there'd be a bit've a fuss, Bill.”
