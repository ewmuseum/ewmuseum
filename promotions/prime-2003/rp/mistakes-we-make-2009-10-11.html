Mistakes We Make

11 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 11th The sound of the hammer cocking on Tommy’s revolver pierced Kaiser’s brain and caused his guts to tangle in his stomach. In his twenty-seven years, he had seen, and done, dozens of things he’d paid a shrink a small fortune to help him forget. But never had he seen a man point a gun at another man’s face. Standing there, taking deep breaths, trying to unknot his intestines, it occurred to Kaiser that no more than a couple small mistakes had led him to this point. If not for one or two simple missteps, miniscule errors, he wouldn’t be on the hook, helping some kid soldato pop his criminal cherry. If David just hadn’t opened his mouth, offered up a friendly betting tip to the wrong guy, Kaiser would be home watching football. Or, if Kaiser had just come through and not caused that tip to go south, none of this shit would be happening. One little mistake, getting careless with Snow, perched across his shoulders at Colossus, quickly turned into a kick to the teeth… and three seconds later, Kaiser’s fate as Sal Vercetti’s toll-collector was sealed. All of it could have easily been prevented by avoiding those simple errors. Instead, seven days out from the biggest match of his life, with his career on the line, Kaiser Vashaun was standing in a Sports Bar watching a nervous kid threaten to blow the head off of a cranky old man who seemed amusingly at peace with his impending death. Witnessing the scene, two thoughts crossed his mind. If the old man was the one staring down the barrel of a gun, and he wasn’t scared, why was Kaiser about to shit his pants? And Tommy was a horse, some cowboy boots, and a hundred pounds away from being an Italian John Wayne. Seriously… who the hell used a fucking revolver these days? ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Sunday, October 11th Night… late Big Sal’s Kaiser trudged up the stairs, his toes kicking hard against the back of each step. Just behind him, to his left and right, two Tony Reali look-alikes ensured that he had no second thoughts. When he reached the top of the steps, Kaiser turned and glared at the pin-striped suited stooges. “This is it?” He pointed to a closed oak door, and both of the Reali twins nodded in unison. With a sarcastic ‘thanks’, Kaiser turned and opened the door. “Mr. Vashaun.” A massive, middle-aged frame sat propped on the edge of a beautifully hand-carved desk. Sal Vercetti, cursing under his breath, struggled to strike the flint of his lighter, hoping to indulge in a cigar every bit as plump as he was. “I’ve been looking forward to meeting you.” “Likewise.” Kaiser stepped into the room, and the gentlemen who had shadowed him from the time they’d picked him up at his apartment, closed the door, taking their posts outside the office. “I doubt that. Something tells me a guy like you would prefer to never meet a guy like me.” “Don’t take it personally. I don’t really like anybody.” “Haha. I think I like your style.” “My style?” “Equal parts apathetic and brash. You’re not afraid. At least, not yet.” Vercetti finally caught a flame, lighting his cigar. “ ‘Bout damn time. Things a piece of junk.” “Your foyer is larger than my apartment… both of them…combined. I’m guessin’ you can afford a new lighter.” “I can.” “Figured.” “Kaiser, can I call you Kaiser?” “Yeah.” “I’d like you to meet my son, Tommy.” As Sal pointed toward the corner of the office with his cigar, Kaiser’s eyes followed, locking onto a nineteen-year old with jet black hair and a goofy looking five o’clock shadow. The kid stood against the wall, arms folded across his chest, not looking like he cared much for the proceedings at hand. “Wait a minute… his name is Tommy Vercet…” “They call me Tommy Two-fists, pal.” Sal’s boy pushed himself away from the wall and took a step forward, no longer a silent specter in the room. “And you bettuh watch your fuckin’ mouth.” “Two-fists? You an amateur boxer or something?” “No.” “Ambidextrous?” Tommy fired a look towards his father, causing Big Sal to sigh and shake his head. “Pop says ‘No’.” “Then how the hell do you get a nickname like Tommy Two-fists?” “It’s my junior year in high school, I’m dating this hot piece of ass, real freak, and she wants me to take both hands...” “OK. I think I get the picture. Appreciate it, bud.” Kaiser turned his attention back to Sal. “You should have the kid tell people he’s a boxer or something.” “Oh, I’m a ‘box’er, alright!” “Shut it, Tommy.” Big Sal chucked his crummy lighter across the room at his son’s head, sending Tommy diving to the floor. “I apologize for that.” “S’okay.” “Anyways, how much, exactly, has your brother told you, Mr. Vashaun? It occurs to me that while we’ve been sitting here bantering pointlessly, you may not even be aware of what’s going on.” “He didn’t tell me much. David showed up at my apartment a few weeks ago looking kinda nervous… panicked. Said he may have screwed up. I didn’t think much of it till I came home to my apartment here in Chicago one night and the place is trashed. So I called Dave and told him I was all ears. He just said he’d pissed off the wrong people and gave me a number to call. Said I had to help him out or things could get ugly. I called the number, was told a car would pick me up at my apartment this evening. And here I am.” “Fair enough.” Sal picked up a glass of water from his desk and took a small sip. “I don’t know if you’re aware of this, Kaiser, but your brother likes to take a couple of weekend trips to Atlantic City every year.” “Yeah, he prefers it to Vegas. Something about some “dumbass in the desert” busting his pocket Kings with a Jack/Seven off-suit… so fuck that place. Been going to Atlantic City ever since.” “Well, my boy likes to frequent the casinos as well. Tommy has a bit of a gambling problem.” “Do not.” Having peeled himself off the ground, Tommy fumbled with the dead lighter, trying to find a spark. “Like hell you don’t. Otherwise, I wouldn’t be out a hundred large.” “A hundred… hundred… thousand?” “Unfortunately, Mr. Vashaun. All because your dumbass brother and my idiot kid have struck up a nice little gambler’s friendship. As it turns out, David decided to give Tommy a little friendly tip. According to him, there was a huge wrestling match in mid-August. Seems you were a 3 to 1 dog against the champ, a gentleman by the name of Jason Snow. Snow was dominant, the heavy favorite, but according to David, you were a lock… never failing to come up gold on the big stage. It was an easy way to triple your money. Or so he told my Tommy.” “Jesus Christ. And he put a hundred grand on me?” “Yeah, and then you lost, Grape Ape. Nice goin’. How bad do you gotta suck t’getcha ass handed to ya by a Canadian?” “God damn it, Tommy… I told ya to keep your mouth shut!” Big Sal slammed his hand against the edge of the desk. “The point is, Mr. Vashaun, that was a hundred thousand dollars of my hard earned money. So here’s the deal… somebody needs to put a hundred grand back in my pocket. Now, contrary to what preconceptions you may have of me, I’m a man of some integrity. Since it was my son who made the foolish bet, I don’t feel you or your brother owe me directly.” “Somehow, that’s not all that comforting.” “What I would like, Kaiser, is for you to help my boy get that money back for me. Tommy has been itchin’ to get his feet wet in the family business, nagging me about it all the damn time. So, about twenty-five years ago, my father, God rest his soul, loaned fifty-thousand to an old family-friend in an effort to help this fella open a sports bar. That debt was never repaid. I figure between inflation and interest, I’m owed at least a hundred grand, and you and Tommy are going to collect. Do you think you can do that for me, Mr. Vashaun?” “If I think I can’t?” “I’ll bash in your fu…” Sal paused to look over Kaiser’s massive frame, “…bash in your brother’s fucking skull with my bare hands.” “Then I guess we have a deal.” “Perfect.” Sal pushed himself away from the desk and put an arm around Kaiser, cigar plugged into his lips, garbling his words. “And you’ll look after my boy, Tommy? As you can see, he’s a tiny guy, doesn’t have his father’s size. And he isn’t too bright.” Kaiser looked towards Sal’s offspring. Tommy, having discovered fire, slowly lowered a finger to the flame of the lighter till the heat was too much, then ripped his appendage away with a yelp. “Yeah. We’ll be ok. I’m used to this sort of thing. I tagged with Tony Gamble a couple weeks ago.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Sunday, October 11th Ok, technically Monday, October 12th About 12:30AM. Sammy B’s Sports Bar Kaiser stepped out of the car and slammed the door, eyeing the bar from across the street. The lights inside were dimmed, most of the action coming from a visible plasma TV screen on one of the walls. “Sam shuts ‘er down at midnite on Sundays. Place should be empty ‘cept for the old fart.” Tommy Two-Fists crossed the street without checking for traffic. Kaiser moved around the edge of the car and jogged to catch up. The kid seemed eager, probably a little too much. As Tommy grabbed the handle to the front entrance, Kaiser reached out and put a hand against the door, leaning against it with his weight. “Listen, man, let’s keep this simple. No problems, no fireworks. Just in and out peacefully.” “Hey, you think I want this to be a hassle? I got a gorgeous girl waiting at home to slam ass.” “To what?” “Just follow my lead, caveman, and this’ll all go smooth.” Tommy ripped the door open and stepped into the bar. Kaiser followed just behind, the old wood splintering in places under his feet. “I think your Gramps shoulda loaned this guy a little more than fifty grand.” Hearing the two men enter the bar, old Sam Bernard shouted across the room, not bothering to turn around as he stacked chairs on tables. “Sorry, we’re closed for the night.” “I think you can make an exception for us, Sam.” The voice sent Sammy B spinning abruptly on his heels. “L’il Tommy… I’ll be damned.” “Tommy Two-Fists, old man.” “Right.” Sam looked Tommy over head to toe, then turned his tired old eyes towards Kaiser. “Who’s this fella?” “A friend.” “You and your friend want a beer?” Sammy B crossed the room and headed over towards the bar. “I got a couple good micro brews you oughta try.” “Not here for drinks, Sam.” “Oh. Whatta ya here for?” “How’s business doin’, Mr. B?” Tommy shot Kaiser a nasty look. Apparently… he wasn’t supposed to talk. “I do enough to get by. Football season helps.” “Listen, Sam, I’m here to collect on that loan my grandfather gave you all those years ago.” “What? Your grandfather gave me that money as a gift. He was one of my closest friends… specifically told me I never had to worry about paying him back. I offered, but he flat out refused.” “Well, Pop says it was a loan and that it’s time to collect. Now, we’re figuring given the twenty or so years that have gone by, taking into account interest and what not… the amounts gone up. I’m gonna need a hundred thousand before I leave here tonight.” “Your father isn’t half the man your grandfather was.” Sam abandoned the bar, no longer interested in having a couple beers with his visitors. With his brow furrowed, Sam went back to straightening up his place, picking up empty beer mugs and wings baskets from a couple of the tables left unclean. “And I don’t have the money.” “Maybe you misunderstood, Sam. This isn’t a request. A loan was made, and now it’s time to repay it, with interest.” “I may be an old man, son, but I understood just fine. And I’m telling you, you’re not getting a dime from me. That money was a gift from your grandpa. So why don’t you just get on outta here, go back home, and tell your dad that you’re not ready to get involved in the family business, junior.” “Listen, old man, I’d hate for this to get more complicated than it needs to be. Really would rather not have to kill anybody tonight, but if that’s what I gotta do, so be it. Now, why don’t you go into the back, open up your safe, and…” Tommy reached out and grabbed Sammy B. by the arm. Showing surprising reflexes, the old man spun and broke Tommy’s grip, shoving the kid’s hand down onto the nearby pool table. With a half empty beer mug in his other hand, Sam hoisted his arm high into the air and brought the glass down hard on Tommy’s hand. Beer sloshed into the air as the heavy bottom of the mug crushed the bones in Vercetti’s left hand. Tommy screamed like a college slut being stabbed to death in a slasher flick as he slunk to the dirty floor. “Jesus Christ! Fuck… my fucking hand!” “Next time you’re going to threaten a man’s life, you better be tough enough to go through with it.” “You crazy old bastard!” He wasn’t sure what it was, but standing there watching a seventy year old man take it to some cocky gangster kid who clearly wasn’t ready, Kaiser felt compelled to speak. “So much for that nickname. Hope that girl of yours is still gonna wanna date Tommy One-Hand.” As soon as the words were floating in the air with the stale stench of beer, Kaiser saw something in Tommy’s eyes and realized he’d made another of those tiny errors. “That’s it…” Tommy reached behind his back and produced a gun, jumping to his feet. “… pay the fuck up, old man! Or I swear I will blow a hole right through your head!” Tommy pointed the revolver right at Sammy’s face, and stood there trembling, his left hand already swelling immensely. Kaiser didn’t move, practically sick to his stomach. Sam was the only one who didn’t seem scared shitless. “Go ahead, pull the trigger, boy. My kids live a thousand miles away. Wife is gone. I don’t have anyone in my life. I don’t have anymore dreams. And I don’t have a hundred thousand dollars. So just go ahead shoot. Truth is, I don’t think you got it in ya. You never sent brains decorating a wall… not like your old man. Your just a kid. A punk kid.” “I swear to god I will shoot you! Right here in your own bar!” Tommy cocked the trigger, trying to prove his point, but his hand didn’t stop trembling.” “Like hell you will. If you were going to do it, you’d have already done it.” “Bullshit, I will pull the trigger, old man!” “Then do it!” “Wait!” Kaiser stepped forward, careful not to get between Sammy’s face and the gun’s barrel. “This is fucking nuts. I’ll pay your father the hundred grand.” “What?” Tommy turned and looked at Kaiser with a puzzled expression on his face. Quickly, he realized his mistake, and turned his vision back to the wily old man. “I’ll pay the hundred thousand. This guy says he doesn’t have it… he doesn’t have it. I’ll pay it. Your father will get his money. Let’s just get the hell outta here, leave this guy alone. Both Sam and Tommy looked to Kaiser, then at each other. Hand still shaking like an epileptic dancing to a Shakira’s She-Wolf, Tommy slowly withdrew the gun, tucking it under his belt. “Ok… ok. Let’s get outta here.” Tommy didn’t waste any time heading for the door. Kaiser quickly followed suit, turning back before he exited the bar. “Dude, that this with the beer mug was awesome. I’ve hit people with some pretty messed up stuff, but that was cool.” Pushing open the door, Kaiser left the bar and stepped into the cold Chicago air. A light drizzle had begun to fall from the sky. “Hey, big man, you think you can drive? My hands kind of a mess.” “Pants too?” “Fuck you.” Tommy tossed Kaiser the keys to the car and both men climbed into the vehicle. “You’re dad gonna be ok with this?” “Should be. So long as Big Sal gets his money, he’s usually happy.” “Ok, then I’ll transfer a hundred thousand from my account.” “You really got that kinda money in your bank account?” “Yeah.” It’s everything that’s left of my Colossus bonus… but yeah, I got the money.” “Shit. Colossus bonus? They pay you way too much for losing.” “Whatever.” Kaiser pulled away from the curb and took a left at the first stoplight. “Hey, you mind if I swing by the grocery store for some late night shopping. I need to pick up a gallon of milk.” “Sure. I don’t care.” “Thanks.” For a couple minutes, the two drove in silence. As Kaiser merged onto the interstate, he let his vision lock aimlessly on the dashed white line that separated the lanes. “You ever feel like you’re killin’ yourself with mistakes?” “What are you talking about?” “I mean… like back there at Sam’s, I pushed your buttons and I shouldn’t have. It was a mistake, If I’d just kept my mouth shut, maybe things wouldn’t have gotten so crazy. I dunno, I just feel like I make a lot of mistakes, sometimes really small ones, and if I could just clean up those errors, things would be a lot different. A lot better.” “Yeah, I know what ya mean.” Tommy watched the buildings roll by outside the passenger’s window. “Can’t beat yourself up too much about those. Just gotta try to fix the mistakes. But ya think about ‘em too much and you’ll just make more. Get all stressed out. Sometimes, I like to take a night, and just let loose, ditch all that stress.” “You got any personal favorite stress relievers?” “Oh hell yeah.” “Any that don’t involve body cavities?” “Hmmm…” Tommy scratched the top of his head. “…sure. You ever chucked Molotov cocktails at parked cars?” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ A Little Later Sometime after 1:00AM. Kaiser’s Apartment Flipping on the lights in his living room, Kaiser kicked the front door closed. Finally, after what had been the longest day of his life, he was home. Sure, thanks to a few bad choices and some small missteps, he was a$100,003.69 poorer, but at least that storm cloud wasn’t hanging over his head anymore. And everyone in his family was safe. Kaiser headed into the kitchen, hit the lights and crossed the room over to the refrigerator. He tucked away the gallon of milk on the bottom shelf, and as he closed the fridge door, something caught his eye. Held in place with an old Pizza Hut magnet, was a list he’d made the day before. Kaiser stated at the list for a minute. “Shit, that’s embarrassing.” Grabbing a marker from the junk drawer above one of his cabinets, Kaiser set to work, making a couple of changes to the list. “Better.” He tossed the marker back into the drawer and once again looked over the sheet. He just needed to clean up those little mistakes.
