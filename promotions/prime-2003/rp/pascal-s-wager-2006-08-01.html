Date: 8/1/06 Pascal’s Wager Before his eyes were fully open, Damien could feel his head pounding, his joints stiffen, and the sawdust dryness on his tongue. He wasn’t a huge drinker, so when he did, he paid for it dearly the next morning. He also felt a body roll over, and drape her tiny arm over his chest. The smell of cigarettes, alcohol, and Liz Claiborne wafting with her movements. Damien closed his eyes again, and eagerly slipped back into sleep. When he opened his eyes again, Debra was standing over him with a cup of coffee, a ham and egg sandwich, and a wide smile on her face. She was wearing one of his sweat shirts, and her hair was up in a sloppy ponytail. “Do you know what time it is, Mr. Cruz?” He sat up, and grabbed his watch from the nightstand. It was twenty minutes past two in the afternoon. Damien had not slept that late in years. “I woke up at about twelve, and went for a walk. On the way back, I picked up some groceries, because you had none, and decided to make you breakfast in bed. So...surprise!” He wasn’t sure if it was the hangover, or if he had always felt that way about her, but the sound of her voice was like grinding metal. Her bottle blonde hair, fake-a-bake tan, and desperate sexiness made Damien uneasy. He really must have drunk hard, even if he didn’t remember doing so. She was more than willing to welcoming herself into his bed, and from what she was attempting now, was planning on staying there. “Listen, Debra, thank you for everything. But I don’t eat ham. I’m a vegetarian. But thank you.” She cocked her head to the side, and laughed, thinking she might have missed a joke, but not wanting to seem stupid. Damien squeezed shut his eyes, and tried his best to endure the sound of her laughter. “Well, I’ll just put your food down here. You eat up. Meanwhile, I’m going to jump into the shower...” She peeled off the sweat shirt, and stood in front of Damien in only her silk black panties. Her hair fell over her shoulders, and she held her hand across her impossibly large breasts. For what it was worth, the doctor did a pretty good job. “…feel free to join me.” It was uncomfortable in principle, but she was not too hard on the eyes. He averted his gaze, and she slithered from the room. Damien slid from his bed, ignoring the lasting effect she had on him, and made his way towards the kitchen. He put on a pot of coffee, turned on the radio, and began to make himself a protein shake. He also tried to think of a way to prevent the inevitable hard conversation he was about to have when she got out of the shower. He cycled through a list of excuses. He was vulnerable after a very hard break-up with Sylvia. No, that would be strange and might give her the idea that he was looking for a replacement. Maybe he’d explain that his PRIME career had been putting him under a lot of pressure that that what happened the night before was totally unlike him, and meant nothing. Shit, that sounded really cold, and even she deserved better than that. There was always the old standby; he wanted to be there, but he was a world-traveler. Yeah, he’d explain how he was a wanderer by nature. He was a nomad and a rebel. Getting involved with him was a road that leads straight to broken heart. The list of reasons why Damien was bad for a good kid like her was long and tedious, but she should run now and save herself the heartache. It was transparent and rather cliché, but it might be what he needed to distract her long enough to slip her out. He’d take his chances. God knows having someone like Debra around was all the incentive he needed. She was not unattractive. In fact, most men that she met were quickly trapped by her long legs, swaying chest, killer body, and pseudo-innocent giggle. She was by most accounts a knock-out. And most people understood that she was very beautiful. Unfortunately, she wasn’t among them. She wore too much makeup, tried a little too hard, clung to men a little too desperately, and made it far too easy to use her and forget about. He tried hard to ignore the fact that he was as guilty as any of the others. Damien had met her at the local bar a few months back, and avoided giving her any of the signals he knew she was waiting for. However, the night before might have thrown a huge wrench into that plan. Now he would have to get her to leave, and do it without being too cruel, or too ambiguous. He took a hard swig of the shake, and hated himself for being so careless. She walked down the stair from the bathroom, wrapped in a towel, and curious why Damien hadn’t taken her up on the offer. Standing in the doorway, she looked at him with big, blue, confused eyes. He didn’t say anything to her, and she offered to cook him another breakfast; something better than a milk shake. In her head, he supposed, she was offering him what she thought he was lacking. “No, Debra, this is fine. Now why don’t you go upstairs and get dressed. I’ll make you something to eat. Are you hungry?” Debra dripped water on the floor, and stared at him dumbly. Her mind started to work, and then she shook her head. An old rule her mother must have taught her made her hunger irrelevant. She would eat later, if at all, and hit the gym if the pangs became too obnoxious. Instead, she approached Damien, leaning up for a kiss, and allowing her towel to drop. Damien avoided the kiss by brushing a wet strand from her face, and gently suggested that she go upstairs and get dressed. Half-embarrassed, and wholly perplexed by his self control, Debra pulled the towel up to her chest, and walked away with a mischievous smile. Perhaps it was a game of cat and mouse to him, and she was more than willing to play it how he liked it. As she walked away, it was hard to ignore the heart-shaped ass that made his thoughts stutter just for a moment. The phone rang, and he was brought back to his senses.It was good news, real good news. What he thought was a pretty straight-forward match had some fresh stipulations attached to it; a ladder/capture the flag type set-up that Damien was proud to be a part of. And for extra gimmicky appeal, it was to be a national flag scheme. Well, it if sold tickets, he would be more than willing to go along with it. Olsig and Adam and himself; each representing their nations in a battle of wills…hell, the marquee wrote itself, didn’t it? That was a bit of good news in a rather uncomfortable morning. Damien hung up the phone and listened for the sound of Debra’s movements upstairs. He heard her walking around, and he wished he had stuck with the wine like he had planned. But he didn’t. As more people showed up at the bar, and more people started to get generous, he switched to harder drinks. He didn’t know why he was so easily caught in this person’s new job, or that person’s new baby, or so-and-so’s recent promotion, or the many other accomplishments that seemed so much better than Damien’s recent losing streak. So he tossed down whatever was being passed to him. It didn’t take much, but before he had a chance to take stock, he was in a taxi, with a leggy blonde’s face buried in his lap, and a spinning world of colors and sounds keeping him off-balance. It was too easy. Damien Cruz was not that careless. He was calculating, deliberate, stoic, and fully aware of himself and his actions. Damien was a man of personal economy of thought, deed, and word. He was not vulnerable to escapism, whim, or self-indulgence. He had learned early on that personal responsibility and the acceptance of the infallible laws if cause and effect are key inside the ring, and outside. That was what puzzled Damien so much about where he stood. He was painted into a corner and he had no one to blame but himself. The question played at the corners of his mind, and he tried hard to pull it into focus. When he did, it was like a thunderclap. The answer he came up with, to that question, was even more unsettling. Finally, she walked down the steps. Her hair was tied back neatly, her clothes from the night before hung on her loosely, without being unattractive, and she carried her stilettos in her hands. She smiled at Damien, and he felt very sorry for what he was about to say to her. “Well, Damien, I think I’m going to head home. I hope you don’t mind, but I made the bed. If you were going back to sleep, I’m sorry, but I figured you would be up and about now.” She was waiting for Damien to say something, and Damien knew it. The painful thing was that he wasn’t going to say what she wanted to hear. “Debra, please sit down. I think we need to talk about last night.” Her smile faded slightly, and then returned with even more vibrancy. If she could smile enough, maybe she could stay. “I had a lot of fun last night. Thank you for everything.” She said, almost oblivious to what he had said to her. At that point, it felt like a script being read. “Debra, I want to clear some things up before you go. Make sure we’re on the same page. Please have a seat; I’ve made coffee for us.” Debra put her things down, and walked to the pot, grabbing two mugs. Damien stopped her, and took them from her, pouring the coffee himself and sending her to sit again. She did, finally, and her smile was almost completely gone. Damien walked to the living room where she was, and sat on the couch opposite where she was on the love seat. “Debra, I’m not sure how to begin this conversation, but I want you to know that I really do appreciate the breakfast, and the groceries, and the fact that you enjoy my company. I really do.” “I’m sorry about the ham…I had no idea.” Damien raised a hand to stop her apology. “It’s fine, Debra. I think it was a really sweet gesture. You are a very sweet woman. You are very pretty, quite generous, and a lot of fun to be around. I just don’t know if I’m in a place in my life where I can give you the attention and devotion you may be looking for. I mean, between my career, and the time I spend on the road…you understand, don’t you?” She looked down at her hands, which played at the hem of her skirt. Then she looked up, took a deep breath, and smiled with a new-found determination. “I understand, Damien. It’s okay.” It should have ended there. Mission accomplished, deed done, loose end tied up. It was a lot easier than he thought. At that point, he could usher to the door, call her a cab, offer her a polite hug and an empty promise to call her, and then get back to the business of being miserable, insecure, and alone. But, it wasn’t that easy. She didn’t understand. She was waiting for him to change his mind. She was waiting for him to take one more look at her great tits and then be overcome with desire. She was waiting for him to succumb to her like so many others had. In the end, she knew, she would have him. He would choose her. “So you’re not looking for anything too serious. I can respect that. I mean, we’re both adults, and this isn’t the 1950’s. I am okay with a casual friendship. In fact, I’d be silly to think that you’re so easily tied down. You do travel a lot, and a man like yourself is hardly lacking of companionship. So rest assured, I understand.” He clearly read her wrong. She thought he was suggesting an open relationship as opposed to the non-relationship he was offering. Damn, this was not going the way he planned. Serves me right, he resolved to himself. “No, Debra, I think you may be reading between the wrong lines. I don’t think we should see each other anymore. Romantically, I mean. If I gave you the wrong impression, or lead you on, I am very sorry. I am not sure why or how I allowed things to go the way they did, but-“ “I’m sorry, ‘the wrong impression’, is that what you called it?” Her smile was definitely gone. ‘I don’t remember asking you to take me home with you. What I remember is you cornering me, and sliding your hand up my blouse. I remember you pulling my panties off in the cab-ride home. I remember you pinning me up against the headboard and fucking me with just a little more fortitude than you’re showing me now, Damien.” Damien’s head started to pound, and the lights in the room began to burn like phosphorus in his back of his skull. “I may be a little quick to assume some things, but I think that message was loud and clear.” His head felt like it was in a vice. Damien rose, and Debra rose with him. She dropped her shoes on the floor and stepped into them. She then kissed him hard on the mouth, and walked toward the door. “I’ll come by later. Hopefully, by then, you’ll figure out what it is that you want to tell me. If you want, I’ll bring dinner. Vegetarian, of course. Until then, enjoy your day and have fun doing whatever it is that you have to do.” “Wait.” Damien had to put a stop to this. “I have to catch an early flight. So tonight’s bad.” But, he didn’t. “Okay, then when you get back. Again, thank you for an awesome evening. Talk to you soon.” Damien locked the door behind her, and then walked up to his bedroom. He needed to wake up from this nightmare. He walked into the room and dropped onto the bed, squeezing shut his eyes. He then felt the sensation of silk against his cheek and the aroma of Liz Claiborne, without opening his eyes, he took the underwear from where she had placed them on his pillow, and dropped them into the nightstand drawer, along with the box full opened and empty condom wrappers. Fuck. That’s the word that ran through his mind until he slipped into sleep. When he woke up, it was dark, and a little past midnight. The effects of the hangover were mostly gone, and he sat wide awake, staring at the ceiling. Yeah, he thought about Debra, but mostly he thought about what events had lead to Debra. He thought about PRIME. He thought about his missed opportunities, and he thought about the match that was less than seventy-two hours away. He was having a crisis. Somehow, he had stepped into a life that wasn’t his own. It was a career he never thought he’d have to answer for; it was very bad habits that he never saw forming; it was a recklessness that seemed to spread like a moss over everything around him. He was drinking more freely, spending more money than he was earning in PRIME, wrestling like it was a chore, and letting guys with half of his talent and reputation step over him on the PRIME hierarchy. The week before, for the very first time in his career, he tapped out. He tapped out when he knew damn well that he could have fought back and turned the tables. And what was even more disturbing, he went back to the locker room, got dressed, and went home like it was just another day at the office. And to top it all off, he now had a sexual relationship with a woman with more waving red flags than the Chinese Embassy, and didn’t have concentration needed to put an end to it. If not for the box of empty wrappers, he’d be in a full-on panic. But from where he was laying, it almost seemed acceptable. Sitting up, he felt the familiar tug of fear in his gut. He felt like he was staring down a formless, shapeless, ominous fate that was not his own. Questions lingered and footholds disappeared. Scarier still was the sense of resignation that settled in like a warm blanket. Like a drug that coursed through his system, that sense of helpless acceptance was intoxicating and dangerous. Damien Cruz sat up against the gravity of that feeling, and strode into the bathroom. He threw a handful of cold water onto his face and looked at himself in the mirror. Something in him shifted, and it felt like a joint being moved after a long sleep. It ached, and creaked, and crackled, but something was moving inside of him, and it felt like a necessary pain. The Latin Assassin took a mouthful of water, and rinsed away the taste of cigarettes, and morning breath, and the protein shake, and the alcohol, and Debra’s sex, and the blood, and the shit, and the remorse, and the doubts, and the rage that curdles when one begins to shut up and collect the check as a lifestyle. The Latin Assassin cut away the hair that had begun to brush against his shoulders. He shaved off the trendy goatee that he was told made him look edgier. He ran the shower, and washed away the sleep from his eyes, the atrophy from his muscles and the helplessness from his thoughts. Whatever he had attempted to do to get by had stopped working. Whatever truths he lied to himself about were forgotten. The fear, the insecurity, the doubts, the questions, and the koans that had muddied up his singular purpose in PRIME were driven away by the simple clarity of his original purpose. If he was to survive, he would have to do it on his terms. If that meant getting up the ladder first and grabbing his flag, so be it. If it meant pinning a hundred men in one night, so be it. If it meant drawing first blood, breaking the first bone, crossing the line, jumping higher, hitting harder, or dig deeper, than he would do so. He had done it before, and he would do it again. The Latin Assassin let the water run over his body, over the scars that he had collected, over the bones that had healed after many said they wouldn’t, over the muscles that tore, the ligaments that were severed, and the spaces in between. This renewal of Fate was not the first. It was the last. If there was to be the continuity of the Latin Assassin, there could be no more questions, no more hesitation, and no more room for error. From the cradle to the grave, Damien Cruz was going to kill or be conquered, and there was nothing left to lose. Damien stood at the threshold of the doorway, looking out at the world that spread out in front of him. He looked at the rising sun, the fading lights, and the sky that seemed to beckon him. When he returned, he would put the house on the market. He would have no more use for it. Not for a very long time. He didn’t know where he would stay, but that didn’t matter. Hotel rooms, friends’ extra bedrooms, or the back of the tour bus was good enough before, and would be good enough now. If anything was poisonous, it would was the false sense of stability. So he would leave that illusion behind. In the end, he would have no choice but to do in PRIME what he knew he must. And if he failed, he would walk away. If he fell short, he would know that his time had passed. The Latin Assassin hefted his bag, and took a step into a world that he had forgotten existed. It was a familiar feeling, one that he had forgotten he loved. Now was the time to remember what he had allowed to be lost. Now it was time to embody his place among PRIME’s best. Now was the time to return to the visceral place that he had inhabited while forging his identity. Now it was time. If not now, then never again.
