Prelude to Hell

29 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 29th, 2007 Hours before UltraViolence “Do you know what the five stages of grief are?” a serene voice said from the corner of the ring. Craig, who was preparing himself for his upcoming match just turned his head slightly towards his daughter and smiles at her for a brief moment, letting her know that he was paying attention to her. “Nah, what are they?” he asks her as he goes back to preparing himself for his upcoming match. Tonight would be a night where he could redeem himself. Yet, he had been searching for redemption for nearly a year and was beginning to get to the point where he didn’t think there was any left in the world. That it was instead being used on people who actually deserved it, instead of a murderer like him. “It was a theory created in 1969 by Elisabeth Kubler-Ross. In her book, On Death and Dying, she came up with the five stages of grief on how people cope with not just death, but any kind of catastrophic personal loss like getting fired from a job or a failed marriage. Those five stages are Denial, Anger, Bargaining, Depression, and Acceptance,” she tells him as if she was reading it out of a book. Craig, with a boot in his hand, drops it onto a nearby bench and takes a look at Sarah, amazed by all of this. “Where’d you learn this?” he asks her and she just simply shrugs her shoulders. “I love to read, even when I was with Santiago. Reading kept me alive, reading kept me thinking you would one day come to get me,” she told him, her eyes darting down to the floor below seemingly retreating into her own personal cocoon. Craig had seen her do it several times as she still felt like a prisoner of Santiago from time to time. He had been impressed at how strong she was though, having fought off the temptation of drugs and alcohol after being in that environment for so long. Yet, he knew it wasn’t even easy for her after the life she had led the past few years. Within seconds, he walks over to Sarah and wraps his arms around her. She feels perfect within his arms as he kisses the top of her head. “You’re safe now Sarah, so don’t you fear. Everything is going to be alright,” he tells her and she looks up at him with a smile on her face. He notices the tears streaming out of her eyes and gently wipes them away. He then looks at her once again before walking back over to his wrestling gear. Many felt that winning championships and matches were the most important aspects to him. Yet, they were all wrong. Being with his daughter was the most important aspect to him. It was too bad that those things would all go to hell that very evening. ---- BANG! The sound echoed throughout the entire arena and as Sarah watched on the closed caption television in the backstage area, her jaw dropped, and she began to feel her heart race. With each passing second, her heart bean to race faster and faster. Part of her felt like she could die right there and then, but somehow she didn’t. Around her, people were racing around, some out of fear, others heading towards the wrestler’s entrance so they could begin to check on Dusk. Medical officials were gathering their equipment to ensure that somehow Dusk made it out of their alive. On the screen that Sarah is watching people are looking around in shock and within seconds there is a crowd around his body. She fears that it’s all over. The storybook ending that they had been experiencing would come to an end with his death and it was tearing her up inside to think like that. Her very essence was being shredded into millions of pieces. As she watches the carnage unfold before her very ideas, she can feel her heart rising in her chest and starting to creep up her throat. Without a moment’s notice, she breaks into a run, following the people out from the backstage area and into the arena. Yet, there are security guards already at the entrance, making sure that no one else gets out or in that shouldn’t be leaving or entering. They block her as she runs into them, wanting, needing to be with her father, but they have strict rules and they refuse to let her go. She tries to find the words, the word’s to tell them that is her father, but she can’t find them. She can’t voice them. She can barely breathe as she moves backwards, feeling loss in the middle of the sea of emotions. She looks around her, everything moving at a glacial pace and tries to find her place in it all. Yet, it dawns on her that there isn’t a place for her here. There isn’t a place for her anywhere. ---- “Give him another 10cc’s of morphine, we’ve gotta repair this damage soon!” a doctor yells out at one of the nurses. Four doctors surround Craig’s body as he lies in one of the emergency rooms at St. Louis Medical. He’s heavily sedated as he’s going in and out of consciousness, not even sure of where exactly he is or what is going on. The morphine that he is on is masking everything going on around him, and quite frankly at this particular moment he’s quite happy about that. He can vaguely remember the man shooting him, though he can remember the pain of the bullet tearing through his abdomen. That wasn’t exactly the most pleasant of feelings in the world. Yet, his mind keeps going to one thing. His daughter. The doctors continue to work away at Craig, hoping to stop the bleeding in his abdomen, but they seem to be having several problems with this compounded by the fact that the bullet has lodged itself into his liver after all of the moving and trauma he’s experienced. Fortunately, or unfortunately, for Craig he’s not aware of this little detour that the bullet has taken as he lays on the cold metal table with several people standing around him working away. In what seems like miles away from him, he can hear the faint beep of a machine and he starts tapping his fingers to it. The slightest movement goes unnoticed by the doctors as they keep asking for various instruments to continue the multiple procedures they’ve got going on Craig. Then, the beeping starts to speed up. Craig’s fingers tap even faster, enjoying this little game that he is playing. Yet, to his ignorance, and what blissful ignorance it is, the beeping is actually of his heart. As the beeping gets faster, he can feel his eyes closing a little more with each second. Yet, his fingers keep dancing away. “We’re losing him!” another one of the doctors yell out as they continue to feverishly work away at him. Then, the machine emits a long beeping noise. Another beep doesn’t follow it. And his fingers have stopped dancing. ---- “Is he going to live?” Sarah asks the doctor as they sit together inside of Craig’s hospital room. Sarah has been crying and can barely even look up at the doctor as they speak. Across from Sarah sits Craig, lying in the hospital bed unconscious. There are several IV’s hooked up to him and different machines checking on his well being. Yet, even with all of this, the fear starts to set inside of her mind that he isn’t going to make it. “He gave us quite the scare to be quite honest, Sarah. He did technically die for a few minutes, but we were able to pull him out of it somehow. We’re not even sure how we were able to do it and we’re not sure how much damage has been done to his brain from the lack of oxygen or blood, but right now we need to continue fixing his gunshot wounds,” the doctor tells Sarah who just simply nods her head, knowing that her father is indeed strong. “There is a time when those who are strong can’t be strong any longer,” Sarah mutters, almost undetectable to the doctor’s ears. The doctor looks at Sarah with sadness in her eyes, knowing that it’s tough for any daughter to see her father in this state. “The gunshot went through his abdomen and cracked a few of his ribs. During the moving from the arena to the hospital, the bullet went through his blood stream and into his liver where it sliced it open pretty well. He still has severe damage to all of those areas and will need to go into surgery very soon. We’re looking within the next hour or so depending on when another surgery finishes up. He’s still got a long way to go,” the doctor finishes telling Sarah before getting up. Before the doctor reaches the door though, Sarah says one last thing to the doctor. “You never answered my question,” she tells the doctor who just pauses before looking back at Sarah. “I don’t know if he’s going to live or not,” the doctor tells her before leaving Sarah alone in the room with her father. Sarah’s blue eyes look at Craig and she can feel the pain in her heart from the mere sight of seeing him in this condition. She finds the strength though to pull herself up out of the chair and walks over to her prone father. She places her hand on the top of his head and looks down at his sleeping body, wishing he was awake to comfort her. Instead, it will have to be the other way around. “Should we feel at times disheartened and discouraged, a confiding thought, a simple movement of heart towards God will renew our powers. Whatever He may demand of us, He will give us at the moment the strength and the courage that we need,” she says, quoting something she had once read in a book. Then, there is silence. A silence that is loud enough to break her heart into millions of pieces. ---- It had been a rough few days for Craig who continued to lie in the hospital bed, flirting with consciousness every few hours. He was ignorant of the world around him except for a few moments when he could hear people talking; see someone walking, or any other number of things. Once he was certain that he had heard someone in the next room over having sex. One of the things that kept him holding on though were the words from his daughter, the words she had muttered when she thought she was alone in the room and the world, about strength. He had grasped onto those words, hoping that eventually those words would give him the strength to live again. He knew he had died on the table. Several times. Four times to be exact, but it was never over for him. Not when he had so much to live for. As he slowly opens his eyes, he notices that Sarah is resting comfortably in a chair right next to his bed. He smiles as he looks at her and slowly reaches his arm towards her before touching her head. With the softest of touches, she wakes up and looks at her father, happy to see that her father is finally awake. Her spirits immediately pick up and she gets up out of her chair to hug him. “Dad!” she says as they embrace each other, but he can’t find anything to say to her. Part of it is the exhaustion and fatigue, but some of it is the simple fact that joy has overtaken him. The bond the two had was inseparable, moreso now than ever. So, he tells her the only thing he can possibly tell her, the only thing that will make her overjoyed as well. “I remember,” he tells her as they break their hug. She looks back at him with an inquisitive look on her face. “What?” “I remember everything,” he tells her, and it was the truth. His dance with death had truly brought back his memories. The year past had been for naught as he had suddenly found his way back to his past. Now, he could move towards the future. “How?” “Death. That was the key to everything. I had to be dead to have my life back again, as odd as that sounds,” he tells her, still not even fully grasping everything himself. Yet, it was the simplest explanation he had. Nothing unique had happened while he was dying on the table time and time again, nothing like the time he had seen a visage of God before him. Instead, this time was different. It was like he was in a calm stream where he could drink for days without his thirst ever being quenched, and he had done exactly that. The two talked for hours, never once bringing up the fact that he had died or the extent of his injuries. Nor had they discussed the shooting at the arena or anything like that. One day, Sarah brought him his newly won Intense Championship as a PRIME official had dropped it off at the hospital for him. Instead, they just talked about life. Sarah was intellectually above him, yet he was able to hold his own with her when it came to conversations like religion, mythology, and theology. Much of his life had been immersed in those things, things which he was certain that he needed to get to the bottom of. But, not for now. Instead, he was enjoying the moments he had with his daughter. After a few days, the doctors were ready to consider the idea of discharging Craig. He would be able to go home. “Come here,” Craig said to Sarah one evening and she came over, sitting next to Craig on the hospital bed. “Yeah?” she asked and he just smiled. “I’m going to be fine here. The doctors are going to let me out of the hospital tomorrow or the day after, and you need to get back home. I don’t want you sitting in this hospital any longer,” he tells her and she begins to protest before holding her tongue. She’s learned that in some circumstances it’s just better to shut up and do as she is told by her father. She nods her head before resting her head in his chest. The two would fall asleep like this. In the morning, he would wake up to find her gone. In the evening though, nothing would stay the same. ---- As the taxi cab pulls up to his home in Orlando, he smiles, happy to be finally home. He gets out of the cab and hands the driver some money before taking his bags out of the trunk. As he pulls them out, he notices something falls out of the side of one of his bags. A small note. He picks it up and immediately knows the handwriting. It’s from Sarah and he just sticks it in one of his pockets, ready to see his daughter and be able to sleep in his own bed for a change. As he walks up to the door though he feels like something is weird and as he gets to the front door, he realizes his suspicions are more than suspicions. They’re real. The door to his house has been kicked in and as he steps inside, he feels the weight of the world crashing down upon him. The entire house has been tossed upside down and he notices blood on the carpet by his room. He slowly walks over to the blood, hoping that it’s not what he thinks it is. As he follows the blood trail to his room, he notices that the blood gets worse and worse with each step. The blood leads him to his room and he opens the door only to find a pool of blood in his bed with a note sitting next to it. Anger resides in his chest as he picks up the note and sees a picture. A picture of Sarah, in his bed, with blood pouring out of her. It nearly kills him to merely look at the picture. Then, he reads the handwritten note. Craig – You have taken from me and the price for that was your life. Yet, you have grown stubborn in your age and instead, you have lived that which was to kill you. In that case, I decided to take the next best thing from you. Instead of your life, I took your daughter’s. Now you will understand what it is to mess with a man that’s not even on your level. You come and find me Craig, and I will ensure that you suffer the same fate as your daughter. - Daniel Santiago As he reads the letter, the tears start to stream down his face. He walks out of the room, unable to look at this sight any more with throwing up everywhere. He stumbles out of the house and collapses on his porch. He can barely contain himself as he sits there, unsure of what to do. Slowly, he pulls the note that Sarah had left for him and reads it to himself, knowing this is the last thing from her that he will ever have. It is in the nature, and the advantage, of strong people that they can bring out the crucial questions and form a clear opinion about them. The weak always have to decide between alternatives that are not their own. He reads the words over and over again, hoping that he will find life in those words, yet he can’t. Then, he remembers what Sarah told him over a week ago. The five stages of grief. Denial Anger Bargaining Depression Acceptance And it was then and there, that he refused to admit to himself that Sarah was dead. It was just a ploy by Santiago, a way to get back at him. The only problem was that it would be Craig that would get back at him. Fin.
