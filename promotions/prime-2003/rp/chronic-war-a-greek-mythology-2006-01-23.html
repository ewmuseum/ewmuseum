Chronic War: A Greek Mythology

23 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 23, 2006 Chronic War: A Greek MythologyBy Needles Scharf 39 B.C. Vangelus the Great wasn’t supposed to die. Molded in the shape of a true Olympus Champion, he maintained it all. A body sculpted in pure elaborate form, accompanied the strength of thousands of brute Spartan warriors. Not only that, but he held a passion for violence matched by none, so deep that even his allies feared it. It was said that no man could match his brute force and instead they trembled at the mere mention of his name. He was as close to perfect as such a mortal could be. But fault lies even in the most mortal of men. With the blood stained dagger in hand, chronic thoughts of the events that had brought him to this point raced through Vangelus’ mind. His wife, his children, slain at his very hands by the same blood stained dagger. In such a time as this, there was no soap opera...men were not accustomed to the butchering of their own families, and such events brought forth such chronic depression, that means of escape was boundless. For Vangelus the Great...his means of escape was death, even in knowing that on the other side was a man...no, a demon, that basked in such slain. But with every deviant action comes an even more deviant consequence. In one swift motion, Vangelus the Great buried the knife in his heart. Vangelus the Great wasn’t supposed to die. But with such events as these, the hand of fate was persuaded. Born circa 74 B.C. in the land of Negev (Referred to today as Columbia), Vangelus the Great bore great riches of the southeast upon immediate exiting of his mother’s womb. Thousands of Spartans assembled in celebration of Prince Vangelus, the son Sovereign Ruler and benefactor of the Graeco-Roman Civilization, Czar Olsig IV and his wife, Aselina. Riches of great quantity were presented before Prince Vangelus as thousands bowed before their soon to be ruler. His destination was already marked in stone, and officials of Negev were ordered to see to it that said destination was carried out richly. The years passed as the Negev Civilization watched their bred ruler grow into that of a true Spartan. Many Spartans had come and go, but none as mighty as Vangelus the Great. What was the purpose of these Spartans, you ask? These Spartans were the most established and important factors in the existence of the ancient Negev Civilization. When battles arose, they were sent to tame them. Some of the most treasured of men started off as Spartans, including czar Olsig IV himself. Brute and unrelentless training made these men chiseled arts of war. They maintained strength, agility, and a will to kill. But while their attributes were only average, Vangelus the Great’s were far more exalted. The strength of thousands of Spartans combined. The agility of another thousand. A passion for killing that even his father feared. He was the pure definition of what every Spartan wanted to be. Many heralded his claim as son of the Royal Family, Czar and Aselina Olsig, as his cause for being such a force. They believed that because of this acclaim, he was honored by the God’s with such attributes. Vangelus the Great was not the only member of the Royal Family, though...he had two sisters in Angelina and Passioni, and one brother in Cyprus. His brother, however, was cast out of the family after inheriting drunken and atrocious habits that brought shame to the family name. His sisters boasted jealousy of their underling, feeling completely abandoned in his favor. But in the midst of the Negev Civilization was a man who’s jealousy stretched to much greater heights. King Nelson of Galilee. Nelson made it his objective to rid the Negev Civilization of Vangelus’ presence. His master plan in doing so came in the form of one man...one beast. His name being simply...Ozric. While the rest of the Negev Civilization drooled over their new found king in Vangelus the Great, King Nelson spent those same hours training Ozric to become as ferocious and ruthless as the already twisted-minded individual could become. King Nelson and his group of outcasts trained greatly, all in preparation of the one event that would shape the Negev Civilization for years to come. But what experience brought forth such hate by King Nelson towards Vangelus the Great? 10 years before the birth of Vangelus the Great, King Nelson engaged in a chronic war with Czar Olsig IV. Years prior, the ruler at the time, Sir Nosferatu, granted the Negev Civilization to King Nelson under a lease term that stated that if he (Sir Nosferatu) were to die, Nelson would ascend the throne of Ruler. A carefully orchestrated plan by Czar ended those hopes, though. For it was through this plot that Nelson was exposed of being a traitor to the Negev Civilization, which at the time was labeled simply as the Graeco-Roman Civilization. Nelson’s hopes of ascending the throne were crushed and in his place, Czar was positioned. Devastated, Nelson declared war on Czar but failed miserable. That, however, only strengthened his focus. Now, in knowing that Czar’s days as a breathing man were numbered, Nelson realized that with Vangelus the Great out of the equation, he would be the most logical choice to ascend the throne. He planned on ridding Vangelus the Great with the help of his hired assassin, Ozric. Ozric was an individual whose mind could be easily persuaded...and in most cases was. It was through this attribute that the ever maniputive and devious King Nelson bred the beast he is today. 29 B.C. “Exalt! Thy name is Vangelus the Great...and by the power granted upon me, I shall rule this Civilization with an iron fist!” Cried Vangelus the Great, standing on top of Mount Judea amongst literal thousands of gathered Spartans and their wives. One week after his father’s death, Vangelus the Great gave his inauguration speech, honoring his position as the esteemed and inherited rule of Negev. In this speech, he outlined his dedication in ruling the country with sovereignty as well as his dedication in defending the country at all cost. But what he wasn’t able to acknowledge was the fact that, in the mixture of thousands assembled Spartans, there stood King Nelson and Ozric, both disguised in an effort to hide their identification. The plans were being made in full that would destroy Vangelus the Great and ultimately the Negev Civilization as a whole...simply because a Civilization being ruled by King Nelson was one destined for sheer destruction. One long year later... The Slaughter of the Innocents The invasion ten years in the making finally arose. Years of training Ozric and the mighty Oracle army had finally paid off. Ozric was as brutal and blood thirsty of a man as King Nelson could have wanted, and the Oracle followed his every command perfectly...in fear of the consequences of not doing so. Some even went so far as to say that the Oracles feared Ozric more than they feared death. Vangelus the Great was no stranger to violent uprisings, as a matter of fact he welcomed them. His army of Spartans accompanied by his brute skill was such a force to be reckoned with that the civilized world trembled at it. He was invincible, and everyone knew it. Warriors of all types fell to his mercy...even their children. Vangelus the Great marked his claim as a relentless conqueror during a battle simply known as “The Butchering”. After being deceived by several Sparta Warriors (Warriors of Eastern Europe), Vangelus the Great went on a crusade that saw not just the butchering of Warriors and their wives...but also their infant children. You see, the Sparta Warriors had pledged their allegiance to Vangelus the Great, only to betray him for 50 pieces of silver several months later. It was then that Vangelus the Great and his mighty Spartan army marched into Eastern Europe where Vangelus ordered the butchering of all children two years and under. It is said that even to this day, the bloody cries of the infants can be heard throughout Eastern Europe. This...however...was unfamiliar territory. What made King Nelson’s invasion of the Negev Civilization so perfect was that it was completely unexpected. Vangelus was certainly aware of Nelson’s presence, but was completely unaware of how far Nelson was willing to go. Things had been kept so far under wraps that Vangelus the Great didn’t even know of Ozric’s existence...and this made him even more unaware of Ozric’s capabilities. King Nelson had been so far out of the equation for so many years that many believed him to be either dead or highly sick...even Vangelus himself. Little did they know that it was only part of Nelson’s master plan. Standing by the side of a mighty phoenix warrior in “Princess Warrior” Karina and an experienced war veteran in General Evans, Vangelus the Great took on the daily activities of Civilization Ruler, completely unexpectant of the danger on the horizon. This danger came in the form of two ruthless individuals... Sir Ferguson of Gori... And Sir Farwell of Austro... Hundreds were slain at the hands of these two generals with the force of the mighty Oracle army, of course. The magic in it all was that Vangelus the Great knew nothing of the situation until one of the Spartans crawled, blood rushing from his veins and on the brink of death, into the palace of Negev. He used his last exisiting breaths to warn Vangelus the Great of the attacks before collapsing in death. Vangelus was upset to sat the least...but ready as always. Vangelus the Great sent Princess Karina and General Evans to handle the situation, watching from the mountains as the two battled Sir Ferguson and Sir Farwell to the death. Evans failed, but took Farwell with him...Ferguson and Karina battled to the death, literally...leaving both sides fallen. Vangelus the Great made the mistake of figuring that was the end of the battle, only to be proven wrong when King Nelson threw down the guns and sent out his special attraction... Ozric. And he wasted little to no time in conquering his mission. You see, King Nelson had a secret weapon in his possession by the name of O’Shea. O’Shea had battled Vangelus the Great once before...but not physically. Instead, he took notes on Vangelus’ battle tactic and sold those very notes to King Nelson for a hefty amount of silver. With those notes, Nelson countered everything thrown at him. Vangelus the Great sent 3,000 soldiers...Nelson sent 6,000. Vangelus arm his soldiers with chained daggers while Nelson armed his with not one...but two of the same weapon. Needless to say, the Oracles conquered the Spartans with ease. Which opened the pathway for Ozric verus Vangelus. The battle was intense. Blood painted the battlefield, whilst cries of terror told the story of the passion for violence that these two men stood for. In the end, though, Nelson had prepared Ozric just enough to defeat Vangelus the Great...the Negev Civilization embraced shock as Ozric stood over their fallen leader in triumph. Vangelus the Great had finally been defeated... What lay ahead was the most devastating irony of it all, though. Ozric and King Nelson tore through the Negev Civilization with their own branded iron fist, butchering innocents all because of the fact that they served Vangelus the Great. Oracles took over the city as Spartans were cast out. Vangelus the Great, meanwhile, was held captive in a cell...being left to die with no food, no water, and little to no air. Vangelus knew he was on the verge of death...but what he knew even more was that on the other side of death was his father, Czar Olsig IV, waiting to curse his name for failing in defending the Negev Civilization... It was a thought that he feared even moreso than death. Vangelus the Great did the only thing he could possibly think of...he sold his soul. King Nelson saw great potential in Vangelus the Great and knew of his capabilities, so he accepted...freeing Vangelus the Great from his death cell under the condition that Vangelus the Great would serve him forever more. King Nelson was of great greed, though, and was simply not satisfied with conquering just the Negev Civilization...he wanted all of Europe. He knew he could gain just that by combining the two most ruthless and brute men in the civilization...Ozric and Vangelus the Great. He sent them on a crusade to capture Eastern Europe in which the two succeeded at. Tragedy struck, though, after Vangelus the Great received word that his wife and two daughters were being held in Chrono...a town in the center of Eastern Europe. Vangelus the Great went against orders granted to him by King Nelson and headed to Chrono wear he tore through Oracle Soldiers who were protecting the land. Ozric followed him, witnessing the killing of his men by Vangelus the Great. Ozric immediately attacked Vangelus the Great, labeling him a traitor and vowing to kill him once and for all...but this time Vangelus the Great was ready. Ozric’s one true weakness was that he was repetitive...and Vangelus the Great knew his every move from their previous battle. He successfully defeated Ozric after yet another grueling battled and continued on to Town Square in Chrono where he witnessed his family being held captive. Vangelus the Great used his weapons and force to slay through the guards before reaching the inside of Town Square. There he saw three men...three final men in which he would have to kill in order to get his family back and ultimately escape the wrath of King Nelson. First Man...Butchered. Second Man...Massacred. Third One...Obliterated. He used his most ferocious of offense to slay through the men in an attempt to save his family...but as he placed the blood stained sword down, he realized that he’d been deceived. For these were not three men...they were three women... His Wife and his Two Daughters. Vangelus the Great fell before his knees at the side of his butchered family. Their necks had been ripped out, their eyes had been gauged, he’d completely branded his only means of love. For the first time.. He wept. It was all because of Nelson. Nelson saw his betrayal and killing of Ozric and led him into killing his own family as a means of revenge. Only King Nelson had the power to deceive the mind... And just like Modern day 2006... King Nelson had destroyed the life of Vangelus the Great. Vangelus the Great was not supposed to die... But not all things are as they should be.
