The Origin Of The Species: Part II - The Hard Sell

4 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04-11-09 Two weeks previously... “Angelus?” Ryan asked, concerned that I had frozen up after what he told me. “You alright big guy?” I took a deep breath and exhaled, staring vacantly into thin air. A multitude of emotions were running through me as I struggled to understand the situation. I had seen plenty of made-for-TV movies where long lost siblings were reunited and held one another, crying and promising to make up for the lost years. Not I. My instincts told me to get the hell out of Dodge right there and then. I'd seen my fair share of cadgers and moochers, being a world class wrestler it was obvious I had money in the bank. I could recognize the twinkle in the eye of the scammers who made a living of duping rich folk into parting with their cash. In retrospect that was perhaps the reason I wasn't as charitable as some other wrestlers out there. That said, I'd encountered a fair few frauds and dually saw them off, but this was the first con of its kind that I had come upon. “Did you hear me?” Ryan pressed on, waving a hand over my face as though to rouse me from my hypnotic state. “I heard you...” I drawled after a moment. “Well?” “Well what? I'm sorry pal but it's not every day somebody walks up to me telling me they're family.” I replied sternly, “You can't have done your research very well...” “Why's that?” “I have no family. They're all dead; long gone.” I said flatly, trying to make this kid understand that whatever he was playing at, it was offending me. My history was well documented. I made the decision early in my career to include it in my bio in each company I worked for because it told the story of how I came to be, and why. It gave the fans a better understanding of the monster running amok on their favourite television show. Ryan glanced away as I turned to meet his gaze. He was pale all of a sudden, and his hands were shaking a little. If he was smart he'd do one before I had a chance to ring his scrawny neck. He took a moment to compose himself, muttering something under his breath as he straightened up. “You don't understand.” He repeated for me as I cocked my head. “I think I do buddy.” I challenged him, “I think you're pulling one on me. So you tell me we're brothers, out of the blue, and what? We get together and go do brotherly stuff and next thing you know you need a loan or a handout or something. I'm wise to you, pal.” “No! It's not like that!” Ryan replied, “I'm deadly serious. We're related. I got proof and everything!” He seemed adamant, and the look of sudden desperation in his eye caught me off guard. It was the same look a slot-jockey gave the last wheel waiting for that last Jackpot to roll around, or so I thought given my current frame of mind. I didn't give him time to explain, pushing past him and getting back into my truck. “You know kid, it's bottom-feeders like you that give this city a bad name. I grew up here, but I never saw daylight robbery like this.” I sneered, rolling up the window and starting the engine. “I know you grew up here!” Ryan called out over the roar of the engine, “Our house is just around the-...” Before he could finish I revved the engine hard and drove off, throwing up a cloud of dust in my wake. I glanced back only once to see Ryan, much to my surprise, chasing after the truck and waving his arms furiously. He quickly gave up and I carried on regardless, quite content to leave the lying bastard choking on the dust. ---- 29th October, 2009 To say I was apprehensive would be an understatement. I expected a lot when I went to Detroit after the call from Killean, maybe a charity cause or some kind of fan-oriented activity. I wasn't of the same ilk as the likes of Sirrajin or Tsonda, who often contributed to various foundations. Not that I was selfish or ignorant, I just never really considered using my persona in such a fashion as to raise awareness or achieve donations or anything. Little did I know upon arriving in one of the more derelict neighbourhoods of the city that I was needed for something far beyond my comprehension. It had been a coupla weeks since I last spoke to Ryan Trent. PRIME's Great American Nightmare had come and gone and I was fresh off a victory against the supposedly evil Devin Shakur, in one of the most hellacious matches I had participated in to date. Thanks to that scumbag my once glorious beard was practically burned right off; I hadn't shaved since 2007. Also, my nose was broken. My bones still ache from time to time, and I wonder just how much longer I can go on doing this hardcore thing before something gives out completely. Shakur was worse for wear of course; last I heard Tony Gamble and Christian Daniels had to crash a production of Joseph and the Technicolour Dreamcoat because the Man In Black thought it was his parallel twin up on stage and tried to off the poor guy with his guy-liner brush. So sue me if I scrambled his brains a little. To make matters worse I came out of the pay-per-view with that broken nose thinking I still had to face Jesse Jamester and Steve Harrison in the first round of the GTT. I was relieved to hear Jamester pulled out but I'll give Harrison his due, he put up a fight. Still, I pulled out the win, broken nose and all. I'm not invincible. I know not to push myself too hard, especially at a time when I'm going to be competing in two tournaments simultaneously as well as working a program with whoever they throw at me in PRIME. Top that off with potentially defending the Elite Title and my plate was looking pretty stacked for the next couple of months. With that in mind I figured if I wasn't competing until ReVolution 210 I should probably take it easy. After the Great American Nightmare I sought medical attention and though the break was diagnosed I was cleared to wrestle when some idiot brought up the idea of wearing a Dmask for a couple of weeks. I know how rough it can get in a PRIME ring though, and didn't fancy putting myself at risk of further injury so requested 209 off to rest up. No sooner had I left Waco than I was involved in a road accident. Some punk street racers out in the countryside were tearing up and down the back roads and I just happened to be fortunate enough to...run into them. My pick-up was written off and I was taken straight back to the same medical facility I had only a couple hours previously walked out of quite the thing. That little episode was a story for another time. All that mattered was my truck was destroyed, I had a mild case of whiplash and a shitload of laundry to do after my belongings were strewn across the dirt when the tonneau cover broke off the F-150. Made me wish I'd just stayed in Detroit in the first place. ---- Before my arrival in Cleveland I booked into the Marriott, a swank $300-a-night hotel. It wasn't as close to the Quicken Loans Arena as the Radisson but it exuded luxury, and I planned on indulging during my short break from the squared circle. The lobby was grand to say the least. Mosaic patterns ran through the marble floor adjacent to a magnificent Persian carpet that covered half the reception area, on which sat a myriad of art deco furniture. Great golden drapes gave a radiance to the glaring sunlight pouring in through the large windows. I could only imagine what the rest of the building looked like. “Classy joint.” quipped my cab driver. The looks on people's faces when a scarred giant ambled through the front entrance was priceless, especially with a rickety little old cab driver lugging my bags in after me. After heavily medicating before the flight up I was numb to the aches and pains of the break and the whiplash, otherwise I would have carried my own bags to save the poor guy the hassle. I wasn't, however, going to risk straining my muscles any further, and so tipped the driver handsomely to help me in with my gear. “How boorish.” exclaimed a posh old bint in a fake fur. I couldn't perhaps excuse my inappropriate need for a servant to a troll like her, but at least I had the common courtesy to cover up the majority of my war wounds with a long-sleeved shirt bearing a complex tribal design. A quick glance of the lobby confirmed my suspicions that this was a local haunt for the snobs of the city; I was surrounded by botox and whitened teeth, boob jobs and nip/tucks, Gucci and Armani...and not the cool kind that Devin Shakur somehow carried off with style. If Cleveland was supposed to be one of the poorest cities in the country it certainly wasn't evident in this cloying atmosphere. Housewives, who you could spot a mile away in their bug-eyed sunglasses and jogging gear, were nattering amongst themselves as they plodded off towards the gym. A few gentlemen in sharp business suits were chortling as they discussed figures and clauses, and one waved an unlit cigar around as though he would challenge the smoking ban any moment in a show of authority. If there were any actual guests amongst them I couldn't tell, too caught up in the activities of the fat cats lingering around the lobby as I waited in line at reception. I felt as though all eyes were on me. Usually those eyes belonged to wrestling fans who couldn't believe their luck catching ol' Hessian in the flesh. The vipers of the Marriott seemed offended by my casual appearance and menacing bulk. I couldn't have cared less; I earned my fortune, unlike some of the parasites who were born into it. As I grabbed my cases and stepped forward to the reception desk I could feel my neck tighten with the slightest strain, and that was all thanks to the painkillers. “Good day sir, how may I help you?” asked the receptionist, a svelte brunette in a navy blue uniform. “Angelus Von Kelsig; I have a reservation.” I replied in as passive a tone as I could muster. “Certainly.” She smiled, tapping away on the keyboard to bring up my booking. She had the good grace to blush when I caught her eyes darting up for another glimpse at the man mountain with the broken nose. To my surprise I didn't detect a hint of contempt; if anything she seemed to resent the elitist pigs as much as I did. She hummed and hawed at the monitor as she searched, and in the awkward silence I cleared my throat. “Do you have a spa here?” I jested, to which she giggled and finally flicked a finger at the screen as my name flashed up. “We do not I'm sorry to say, however Marengo Luxury spa is an affiliate of ours and we generally send all our clients there for a deluxe pampering.” She said, and though her tone was slightly monotonous from the well-rehearsed statement, her smile widened at the thought of a barbarian like me exfoliating in a mud bath. “Sounds wonderful.” I carried on with the soft touch tone, “Where would I find this little slice of heaven?” I held her gaze to see if she would bite, and she handed me a pamphlet with my door card. “You can find all you need to know in this, and there's your key. You're in the King suite located on the seventeenth floor. Enjoy your stay at the Marriott!” she pipped. “Why thank you...” I paused to read the name tag pinned over her left breast, “Jennifer.” She clapped a bell on her desk, summoning a bellboy waiting by a pair of exquisitely carved mahogany doors. A thin head of hair combed over to one side and a large hooked nose gave him the appearance of a butler, which in this environment was quite apt. “Take Mr. Von Kelsig's bags and escort him to his room, please.” The bellboy dually complied and I followed him towards the elevators, shooting Jennifer a quick smile on my way. After the shit storm of Gloria I couldn't even look another woman in the eye. As far as I was concerned they were all heinous, soul destroying bastards. In the run-up to the Great American Nightmare however I had the pleasure of meeting a woman who made me forget all my troubles. It wasn't her body I found attractive per se, although she was pretty easy on the eye for a chick bodybuilder. Her personality was just delightful. As low as I was feeling, Beth Marquis found a way to put a smile on my face...even after I found out she was part of the undercover team trying to pin Gloria's murder on me. At first I thought she was just a loud, brash broad; your typical tomboy. When she took an interest in me and made the effort to get to know me I let my guard down. I couldn't resist her charm, and for the first time since losing Gloria I felt comfortable around another woman. We even went on a date before the pay-per-view. I thought I'd found a friend, until we met up one day and she dropped the bombshell on the sting operation. She knew I was innocent all along. The homicide detectives in charge of Gloria's case were desperate to pin something on me but after spending so much time with me undercover, Beth could tell they were just bullshitting. She was kind enough to clear the air and told me to keep in touch. As much as the deception riled me I couldn't hold it against her; else I would be advocating the criminal process. Jennifer had a similar vibe about her. I made a mental note to strike up a rapport with her at some point before I left Cleveland. ---- The bellboy showed me to my room and extraneously explained the layout to me as though I was one of the haughty walruses who couldn't discern the basic functions of the remote control. I zoned out for a while, showing enough manners to follow the general direction he was pointing as he gave me the grand tour. The suite was certainly lavish. The carpet was of the same style as the Persian in the lobby, laid throughout the den and the bedroom. The walls were cream coloured, and the paper had fancy leaf designs embossed onto it, painted gold and blue. Antique Victorian furniture lined the walls; high-back chairs that were never meant to be sat in, a drinks cabinet filled with the finest malt whiskeys that would never be sampled, and even a grandfather clock that was still set to Greenwich Mean Time. A landscape painting of water lilies by Monet seemed to be the focus point for the room. Two black leather sofas and a chaise lounge faced the painting, positioned around a heavy oak apothecary table. As I glanced back up the lilies had suddenly become sliced watermelons, Viva La Vida written into the front centre piece. “Frida Kahlo.” the concierge murmured, “Exceptional. As you can see when the television is on standby it plays a series of stills, all works of art.” I couldn't think of a response that wouldn't have a lowly bellboy turn his nose up at me so I nodded as though I was impressed. A 42” LCD television set into the wall that became a digital painting when not in use...what an intelligent waste of electricity. For the purposes of my retreat however, I would appreciate the sentiment. As the concierge prattled on about the functions of various gadgets in the room I rolled my eyes and retrieved a wad of bills from my pocket, counting out enough for a decent tip. His eyes lit up as I extended the cash to him. “Thanks,” I said, excusing myself, “but if you don't mind I'm gonna settle in.” “Certainly sir. If you need anything at all just dial 'hash-nine' for service. This suite comes with concierge lounge access so there will always be someone at your beck and call.” replied the bellhop with a queasy smile. “I like the sound of that.......” I trailed off, raising my eyebrows and gesturing for a name. “Oh, er...” he stammered, obviously not used to being treated like a human, “Harold. Harold McWhirter.” “Alright Harry,” I grinned, “any time the Man is riding you too hard feel free to come round and we'll knock off a few of those expensive little whiskies in the bar there.” Harold almost managed a smile before the bug up his ass began buzzing around again. “Actually sir...” “Call me Angelus, man.” I corrected him. “Well, Angelus...those are more for display than consumption.” “Man I'll be damned if I'm paying three hundred dollars a night to look at alcohol.” I hooted in disbelief, “The offer still stands, and whether or not you ever take me up on it that bar will be empty before I leave.” “Very well, sir.” Harold said with a wink and a bow before taking his leave. Alone again. Gazing out the bay window past the balcony to the city skyline I regarded my luxurious surroundings with a contented sigh. I was seriously buying into the whole idea of five-star accommodation. Holiday Inn had nothing on the Marriott. Not even that prick Gamble could upset me now, and he had the advantage going into our round two GTT match. Advantage, ha. ReVolution 204: Tony Gamble versus Mystery Opponent. He had no idea it was me because while I was scheduled to face him, Lisa Tyler left it a mystery because of circumstances surrounding the close of ReV 203. Gamble would happily regale anyone willing to listen of the moment he hit the monstrous Hessian with Stop Laughing At Me! He'll tell how he complimented it with a moonsault, which he botched, landing squarely on my head, then roar joyously at how he got a win over PRIME's behemoth. What he won't tell you is that Lisa Tyler held back promoting the match as Tony Gamble versus Hessian because I had that same week flown to Memphis to identify Gloria's corpse after she was murdered under my own roof. PRIME management were fully aware of the situation, and against my better judgement I went ahead with the match. Grief-stricken and nowhere near my best I was beaten by a little punk who to this day holds the victory over my head like the sword of Damocles. This time would be different. This time, I'd be ready. ---- “How may I help you sir?” Jennifer asked with an illuminating smile, even though the man before her looked like mutton dressed as lamb; wearing a borrowed suit with scuffed shoes. “Uh, I was wondering if you could help me...I'm looking for someone. His name is Angelus Von Kelsig, he's about seven feet tall and has a-...” “Angelus Von Kelsig?” she cut in, muttering the name, “Yeah, he checked in earlier actually. I can send up a message if you like, who shall I say is calling?” “Well, I was wondering if you could let him know he has a visitor. I kinda want to surprise him.” the man replied meekly. He had travelled over 155 miles from Detroit to Cleveland after learning that ReVolution 209 would be broadcasting from Ohio. With what little money he had he jumped an overnight bus and started searching the hotels near the venue. First the Holiday Inn, then the Radisson. Next he tried the Marriot and struck third time lucky. “We still have to verify visitors with our guests I'm afraid. Company policy.” Jennifer insisted. “Oh...ok. Never mind.” He sighed, turning away and retreating a few steps before stopping suddenly and shaking his head sombrely. Looking up, he drew a breath and spun on his heels, walking purposefully back to the reception and planting his hands on the desk. He twiddled his fingers nervously as Jennifer cocked her head in anticipation. “I'm a friend of his, my name's Ryan Trent. I really need to see him...” ---- This is the life, I thought to myself as bubbles popped and fizzed all around me. The jacuzzi bath was as big as some of the hotel rooms I'd stayed in in the past. Designed for several occupants it fit me perfectly, and with half a bottle of bubble bath mixed in and soothing jets of water blasting against my tired body I was as happy as a pig in shit. It was a kind of peace I only knew previously by crushing skulls with steel chairs and destroying bodies in the squared circle. The bathroom was grand in all manners.. The walls were tiled with sandy porcelain and the wood ceiling was dotted with spotlights, while the floor was made up of heated stone tiles. The jacuzzi sat squarely in the middle of the space, and it had a console built onto the rim along the right side; a completely waterproof panel with a touchscreen and a phone nestled into a groove beside it. After fiddling about with it for a while I found a digital jukebox and set it to play every song in its list on shuffle. While I'd never been one to indulge in Indian pan pipes or hippy-dippy easy listening it set the mood nicely and I let it slide. To my right against the far wall sat the john, adjacent to a wicker cabinet full of towels and with a roll of toilet paper sitting on top of it on a fancy brass holder. A large porcelain sink bowl jutted out from the wall in front of me, next to a shower boxed in frosted glass. All the taps in the room were fashioned into golden lion heads; very exquisite. I took a sip of the Glenfiddich I had procured from the bar and breathed in the hot steamy air. The painful throbbing in my nose had dulled thanks to a combination of the whiskey and painkillers and already I felt brand new. Sitting the glass beside the console I took a deep breath and dipped under the water, going limp and listening to my heartbeat thud over the muffled music above. I didn't register when the phone first began to ring, but after a moment I cottoned on and sat back up, thumbing the soapy bubbles from my eyes and reaching for the receiver. “Hello?” “Mr. Von Kelsig?” a female voice asked. “This is Jennifer at reception...” “Oh, hello.” I smiled, “What can I do for ya?” “Sir I have a visitor waiting here to see you. It's our policy to check without guests before sending anyone up so I just need confirmation you're expecting someone.” she explained. “I'm not actually...” I mumbled confusedly, “...who is it?” “A man called Ryan Trent. He claims to be an acquaintance of yours?” “Oh, alright. Sure, yeah send him up, thanks.” I hung up and filled the room with booming curses. Grabbing my glass I necked the whiskey and let the raw heat swallow up some of my ire. As soon as I heard his name I made the snap decision to approve the visit. After our encounter in Detroit I was eager to get away from him after he dared to offend me by claiming to be my brother, of all things. Thinking he was trying to scam me somehow I took off asap. To then hear that he had followed me to Ohio caught me off guard. Why would he make the effort to travel hundreds of miles out of his own pocket when he knew I would just as readily get up and go? I got out of the tub and grabbed a towel from the basket, drying myself off and wrapping it around my waist. I had to cinch it with my fingers when I couldn't fold it into place. Heading out to the front door I opened it in time to see Ryan emerging from the elevator. When he saw me he shook with trepidation and visibly gulped before approaching the door. When I last saw him he was decked out in greasy overalls, his hair oily and his hands black as coal. Now he was dressed in an old blue suit, his hair still retaining its oily texture but combed into a smart side parting. His shoes were swish, though I soon noticed they were rough and scraped all over and poorly polished in a bid to hide the marks. “What do you want man?” I asked, puffing my chest out and making myself look even more intimidating, if such a thing was possible. “Please hear me out man...” he pleaded, holding his hands up and bowing his head slightly. I growled and stood aside, waving him into the room. He skulked past me into the room and I closed the door, following him into the living room. “Take a seat. I need to get dressed.” I groaned begrudgingly after he cut my initial R&R short. As I went back into the bathroom to turn off the jets and the music I caught him fumbling in his pockets for something. Thinking nothing of it I moved into the bedroom and threw on a t-shirt and jeans and headed back into the den where Ryan was watching the moving paintings on the wall above the drinks cabinet that was now one bottle short. “Ok,” I muttered, taking a seat on the chaise next to him, “let me hear it.” He composed himself, clearing his throat before he started. “Look...I want you to be clear on something,” he said matter-of-factly, “I'm not some scam artist or punk trying to do you out of money, alright? I'm from Detroit man, if I wanted cash I could think of much easier ways to get it than tailing some famous wrestler off the TV across the country looking for a handout.” I smiled, though it wasn't sincere. “I don't blame you for taking off like you did, but what I said was the truth. It took long enough to figure out who you were and where you were, so I wasn't about to let you just piss off without hearing me out. That's why I'm here now.” He continued. “With all due respect pal, I'm a world famous wrestler.” I reasoned, “Everywhere I go I see kids wearing my merchandise with my face plastered over it, and my matches broadcast on FX every Wednesday. You can't sit there and tell me it took you ages to find me man. This is the 21st century. Hell, the only reason we can't find bin Laden is because the Google Earth car doesn't have 4x4 to get into the caves he's hiding out in.” “Maybe so, but I don't have cable and I damn sure can't afford to waste my time trawling the streets asking children who the man on their shirt is. Only time I ever saw you was when somebody installed cable at the bar; I caught about ten minutes of your show before it went on the fritz. When I clocked you I just knew straight away, y'know? So I hit the library and did a bit of rooting around, then I found out one of the other wrestlers was a patron for some charity so I gave him a call-...” “...Which brings us here.” I interrupted. “Yeah. See, I know it's totally weird but you gotta believe me man. When you left we thought something really bad had happened, like you'd died or whatever...how the hell were we to know you were alive, never mind a big famous TV star?” He sank into the leather and I noticed he was rustling something in his hands. I lingered on them for a moment as a thought entered my head. “Wait...we? Who's we?” I asked, slowly digesting the information and coming to the conclusion Ryan wasn't in this for the money. “Well...see for yourself.” he replied, extending a hand with a glossy piece of card held firmly in his grasp. I took it from him and flattened it against my thigh, working out the creases before bringing it up for a look. My jaw dropped. “You recognize them?” Ryan asked, his eyes welling up. “I-...how the hell...” I couldn't find the words to describe what I was feeling. In my hand I held a photograph taken in 1980. The resolution wasn't anywhere as clear as that of today's pictures, but I knew immediately who I was looking at. A middle-aged man and woman stood hand in hand behind a small boy, the woman smiling and the man holding a stoic gaze over the photographer while the boy's attention was held by something off to the side. He was clad in a shirt, suspenders and slacks similar to his father's, his hair slicked to the side like Ryan's while the man's do was stuck up at the front in a similar style to Johnny Cash in his younger days. The man's face was hard and focused, while the woman had soft, gentle features. Her straight blonde hair was tied back in a bun and a red polkadot dress clad her trim figure. It was her lips that drew my attention most; bright and red and peeled back to reveal a pearl-white smile. They were standing in front of a wrought-iron fence, behind which stood a large redbrick house that looked recently built at the time the photo was taken. Saplings were rising up out of the ground, reaching for the sunny sky. I recognized everything; the family, the house. I just couldn't believe it. “Where did you-...?” I said almost in a whisper as I choked back the emotion. “Angelus...” Ryan uttered, “It's time to come home.”
