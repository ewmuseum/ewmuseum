Pentel R.S.V.P., Medium Line, Black Ink

27 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 27, 2007 I remember telling myself, asking myself really, what it was like to be just... nobody. It's weird. I've got a contract with fucking PRIME, I've got people hailing me down on the street, telling you how great I am everyday. Most people get that if, like... they're married, if they really... really love who they're with. But that's one person. Okay, well, maybe their parents, but then again, that's just... two people. You know? So few people get to feel that from everybody. Jeanne... I'm just saying I'm kinda lucky. No. No, of course I love you. I've just been away for a week. I haven't been touching anybody. I swear. I know you trust me. I love you. I'll talk to you later. *** This town was not Easton's sort of town. It was too small for his small-town aesthetic, too close-knit for his family values. He heaved himself out of his small rented car and toed the door closed, Timberland workboots crunching into the gravel and then clunking on the planked porch leading up to the general store. Everything around him was grey or red-grey: the sky, the buildings, the people. They looked at him with a sort of blankness. He pushed the door open and the signal bell rang cheerlessly. The general store was three aisles and brown tiles, shuffling old women, the back wall lined with freezers and stocked high with beer of every variety: Heineken, Molson, Labatt Blue, Busch, Busch Light, Natural Ice, Natural Light, Budweiser, and Bud Light. An overweight man under a half-working light pulled his fist back from the freezers, the rare Guinness six-pack he cluthed blessed by the flickering pale. An old man hunched over the counter, his hands unseen but his eye venomous on the back of the huge man. Easton started back towards the wall of beer, fixing his eye on a pack of Labatt Blue for lack of anything better. The Guinness man spied Easton with small eyes and made no move to get out of his way, his stained t-shirt-covered gut making an effective cowcatcher, black hair cut short but still messy under a hazard orange trucker hat. Easton fingered the pen in his pocket and wondered why he hadn't taken the precaution of shading his face before going in here. It would be quick, though, right? Just a little scribble and he'd be on his way. Easton's footsteps faltered for a second as he made to pull the pen out. The Guinness man lifted an eyebrow and twisted his body sideways. His lips squeezed as he grunted. Easton's foot fell too hard on his next step, but he kept walking. He let the pen lay still in his pocket. His arm scraped against the man's stomach as he made his way past. Easton straightened up as he stood before the glass wall, tucking back a few loose strands of hair and combing his fingers through his beard as he thought of what to get. He shook himself mentally and pulled open the door to retrieve his Labatt Blue. The overweight man was already gone by the time Easton had turned to head back to the counter. He glanced through the aisles briefly and saw the close-shouldered small women still fingering everything in the aisles as if they meant to buy it all. Easton blew clean air through his nostrils and set the six-pack upon the counter. The old man sized him up and, as a show of confidence, showed his hands atop the counter. “Six thirteen,” the old man said. Easton nodded and reached into his pocket for his wallet, watching the man's eyes, which were watching his. He pulled out a ten and handed it to the man. The old man grabbed the hanging end of the bill and slid it from Easton's grip. The register rang as it was opened and the old man made nimble change, his hands moving on tracks and then sliding the remainder back, coins atop bills. Easton scooped it up and, in a sub-fist ball, jammed it into his pocket. “Have a nice day.” Easton nodded, lifted his six-pack, and headed for the door. Not his sort of town at all. *** Three beers were left in the six-pack when Easton had had enough of his hotel room and driven his little red rental to a bar pointed out to him by the hotel's clerk. The other vehicles towered over his in black and camoflauge to help them blend into the drabness. Cleo's looked like it would topple at any moment, squat and grey-bricked, its roof just angled enough to let rainwater sluice off. A couple men stood outside smoking, leaning against the wall of the place and letting the thick haze float into the sky. Easton walked past them, pushing open the doors to get inside. The tavern was rather low-key but crowded. Stringy country music seemed to hover above the crowd and a tinny voice declaimed what could be any number of things, because no one could really hear it even if they had wanted to listen. The chatter was sparse and mostly involved catcalls to the youngish brunette behind the bar who busied herself with passing out drinks and giving the stink-eye to anyone who tried to paw at her barely-covered breasts. Another person was behind the bar as well, a scraggly-jawed man with a ten-gallon hat nursing a shotgun that twitched with every greedy tit-reach. It didn't stop anyone. “What do you want?” she was asking before Easton had even sat himself on a barstool. He settled himself atop it and gave her a little smile. Her eyes retreated for just a second, passing along a full glass, before returning to him. “You've got money?” “Labatt Blue,” Easton said, drumming his fingers on the table. She nodded and made a glass appear, filling it and pushing the glass towards him. He plunked down the amount he'd read from the chalkboard behind the bar and she slipped that away somewhere. Her eyes watched for his hands as she twisted to serve another man. He lifted his glass and let the froth coat over his mustache as he drank. The air was thick as the music grew in volume on its own accord. A conversation behind Easton swelled. He drank his beer in short gulps, shooting bright if close-lipped looks to every corner and side of the tavern and receiving little in return. He flicked away froth with his index finger. Easton felt the urge to swing his feet, but this was not the place to do that, so he didn't. The glass was half-empty when he sighed into it, staring at the dark liquid. A squeaky scrape behind him had every head turned, including his own. Two massive men, a tangle of biceps and flannel and grayed-through beards, were on their feet and their work boots were punishing the coarse wood floor. The skinny man behind the bar had risen to his feet and, pointing the gun, shouted “Outside,” but the two were already moving, shoving each other and snarling, stopping to grind noses and lock eyes before a shove would get them moving again. The tavern seemed to rise as one and follow. Easton was half a step behind. One of the two, in the red-and-black flannel and a dingy pair of cutoff jeans in this cold, stumbled away from his leather-vested trucker-hatted nemesis, nursing his jaw. Trucker Hat approached Cutoffs with his fist raised, only to catch a shoulder in the stomach that had him reeling, heels drumming on the ground one after the other, shoved quickly into a beam, shivering his spine and making him yell. Cutoffs rose and slugged him, making Trucker Hat spit, but Trucker Hat punched Cutoffs in the ribs and shook him thoroughly. “Hey! Hey!” climbed Easton's voice. He pushed aside the outer ring of spectators, edging through. Trucker Hat laid an unpracticed close right hook into Cutoffs's temple and Cutoffs turned around like a dog, trying to catch himself. “Stop this!” He pushed forward again, but found himself pushed back. His eyes flashed emerald anger as another set of hands gripped his shoulders and thrusted him backwards. “Get your hands off—!” Easton smacked the hands away, but a flash of silver stopped him quick. Two men bore down on him, neither very tall, about his height, and both skinner. They looked tough in a rangy, desperate way, but not strong. What drew Easton's eye was the knife the one on his left, a greasy black-haired man with a jackal's face and a Pantera t-shirt, held in his left hand. His eyes were deep set and close and brutal. His long nose flared. “You let Jimmy settle this,” the knifeman said. Cutoffs slammed his palm into Trucker Hat's face and Trucker Hat screamed as a fistful of bottle shards cut into him. The knifeman waved his weapon towards the building. “Get the fuck inside.” Easton locked eyes with the knifeman and straightened his shoulders. “I'm not gonna ask you twice.” Sizing up the situation, Easton was confident. The man was too scrawny to be much trouble and his friend was much the same. Neither had likely eaten a fist from someone who knew how to throw. Still, the man was looking him dead-on and would not budge. Easton decided it was better to not make a scene. He lifted his left hand and backed into Cleo's, the knifeman's eyes never leaving. Someone had given Cutoffs a bottle and he was circling Trucker Hat, taunting him, looking for the best spot to hit him. Easton shook his head as he tramped over towards the bar. It was nearly empty now, a stick of a man huddled over a glass at the end of the bar and no one else. Easton hopped up onto a seat near the center and rested his forearms on the counter. “What'll you have?” the bartender asked. Her hands rested on the counter and when Easton lifted his head, she looked tired through the strands of his brown hair. “Gimme a fuckin' Labatt,” he grunted. She lifted an eyebrow and lifted a glass. “Don't you fuckin' cuss at me,” she snapped, filling the glass with beer. She laid it on the table and pushed it towards him. “Who do you think you are?” He caught her eyes and she looked at him, but she barely saw him there. *** What am I talking about? I mean like... look, I'm in the Jewel in the Crown tournament, I just got back and I'm main eventing. Marquee, you know? Facing off against Chandler Tsonda, former 5-Star champ, got all this heat. It makes you feel good when you have everybody screaming for you, all these women who just want you... What? No, I'm not. I told you. Well, look, remember in HIW? Remember when we went to the Slammys? That guy who tried to grab your ass? Nothing? Jeanne, I punched that guy. He went to the hospital. Yeah, you didn't hear? But anyway, this isn't about me punching him. You were telling me about how you were just... See? See what I mean? It's the same thing. I mean, I love what I do, but not everybody has a job where when you walk in, fifty thousand people just react, y'know? Jeanne, I swear on the Bible that I have never touched anyone but you since we've been married. I know I don't read the fucking Bible. *** Easton was sluggish as he hauled himself out of the hotel, duffel bag on his back weighing him down. He looked down the row of cars and saw the red of his rental, the red of salvation, of getting-the-fuck-out-of-Dodge. His hair was dirty and ugly, hanging over his face like a beaded curtain. He edged between cars and popped the trunk. He sighed heavily as he heaved his bag over his shoulder and dropped it into the trunk. “Hey!” came a small voice from behind him. Easton looked over his shoulder and saw a brown-haired boy standing behind him. “You're... Easton Hall!” Easton smiled and slammed the trunk shut. He turned around and sunk his hand into his pocket. “I am.” The boy beamed. “I watch PRIME all the time, Easton!” Easton nodded. The end of the pen poked above the rim of his pocket. “Do you want an autograph?”
