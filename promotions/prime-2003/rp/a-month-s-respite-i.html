Date: The Not-So-Distant Past October 13, 2006 "You didn't land right on the Drop, did you?" Silence. "Or the suplex at the end?" "Grrmmph." The noise of discomfort from Troy's mouth was promptly followed by a sharp release of air through her nostrils. Stretched out on top of the medical trainer's table, she had her sweaty face buried in a towel while a pair of lightly calloused hands started to work wonders on her neck. One of the perks of being in a national federation: traveling massage therapists on the staff. "Are you going to say anything besides indistinguishable grunts or do I have to make all the small-talk myself?" the man, whose name was Tommy, inquired. Or was it Lonnie? At this point, Lindsay didn't care, as long as he kept working the pain out. "I could," came the asserted, muffled reply. "So why don't you?" Over her sports bra and down her spine. "Too much effort." Troy lifted her head to look back over her shoulder at Heavenly Hands. "And I don't like to talk about what's bothering me." "Anyone with a pulse could see that," he dead-panned. Her "Touche," was accompanied by a sigh of comfort when the knot in her lower back was broken up. "You'll forgive me if I'd much rather square with myself, in private, the extend of my injuries instead of announcing them to the entire world, right?" Tommy-slash-Lonnie shrugged nonchalantly. "I don't tell people how to deal with their health. I just try to make them feel better when they come to me." "Which I did," Lindsay pointed out. "So maybe there's hope for me yet." "Aye. You'll live, for now at least." The warmth of his skin lifted from her back, and she sat up. "You might want to do something about that shirt, though," he said, motioning to the balled-up cotton attire on the floor. "I don't know if all the Tide in the world will get Johnny and Eve's blood out." ~*~ ~*~ ~*~ ~*~ Each unique shirt of hers had a backup in cases like this. Then the backup had a backup and that backup had one as well. And so on. Post rub-down and shower to wash off the filth her defeat left on her, she sat on a chair in her Fortress of Solitude for the week to catch the remainder of the night's action. No one to bother her and nowhere else to be. Just her, a bottle of Dasani water, the tiny television monitor and the blaring music of "Livin' Lovin' Maid." ... One of these was not like the others. If only the other objects were the foreboding ones, the Zeppelin-singing cell would have been granted a reprieve. "Hey, Alaina." "Linny?" a tearful, sobbing voice called for her sister by a childhood nickname long forgotten. "Alaina? Alaina, what happened?" An audible sob was forcefully choked back. "Uncle Brent had a stroke."
