Who Am I is Who I Am - JitC Round Two

4 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/12/04 “That was a disgrace kid. An embarrassment.” Sweat beaded on the forehead of “The Mecca” Alex Creed while he jogged on the treadmill in the hotel’s five-star fitness center. His cell phone sat in the treadmill’s cup holder, his Bluetooth fastened to his ear. It was about four in the morning, and the manager had been kind enough to let Alex have access to the equipment so he could work out in private. “Thanks for the words of encouragement Mister Sensitive,” he told Ray Berg, owner of the MMA promotion Fury Pro. “I’ve got a sick puppy you can kick while you’re at it.” Berg wasn’t amused. “I thought you were supposed to be some kind of household name in pro rassling.” Despite being an entire country away, the disdain in Berg’s voice when he spoke of professional wrestling came through loud and clear. “Thought people were going to jump out of their skin when you walked through the curtains. Isn’t that why you used your old ring entrance music rather than the music you use in Fury Pro?” Alex pulled his drenched tank top away from his muscled chest. “I’ve been out of the business for about two years. Wrestling fans have short-term memories for the most part. They’re also very territorial. I’ve never wrestled in PRIME before. I was coming into this tournament with those two strikes against me, fan-wise. I wasn’t expecting them to roll out the red carpet for me.” He paused. “Although a dusty welcome mat would have been nice at least.” Berg was in Creed’s ear again. “Alex, the only reason I let you do this tournament is because you assured me it would be great exposure for my promotion. What I saw in the ring wasn’t what I would call a wrestling clinic. It was more like an emergency ward. You were lucky to get though that with a win.” Several seconds of dead air passed before Alex spoke again. “First, let’s go back to the part where you gave me permission. I thought that part was the funniest.” The humour was lost on Ray. “How’s this for a joke, smartass. I pay your contract. You are a world champion in mixed martial arts. More importantly, you are my champion. Mine!” “Better throttle back on the creepy possessive vibe there, Ray,” said the Mecca. “Yes, I know that wasn’t my best performance. For my first live match after a two-year layoff from professional wrestling, a win is a nice little feather in the cap. I’ve had time to settle for Round Two, and I’m working on getting some sparring time in so I can iron out a few things. Get my groove back, so to speak. So keep your wig cinched down, will you?” “I don’t wear a wig,” chirped the Bluetooth. “It’s never too late to consider it.” Alex turned and looked out the window. The long shadows of the Rocky Mountains were starting to step out of the night, signalling the start of another cold Canadian prairie morning, a bleak outlook for a kid who’d grown up on Venice Beach. “Now if you’ll excuse me, it’s a quarter to sunrise and I have a busy day ahead of me.” The click of the door latch caught Alex’s attention. He watched while a bulky bald man, clad in a black, nondescript suit, stepped through the doorway and surveyed the scene with a slow pan of the room. “All clear sir,” he said over his shoulder and stepped to the side, holding the door wide open. The hum of a small electrical motor preceded the wheel chair carrying a small, frail-looking man. His long grey hair was tied back into a ponytail, and Alex recognized the cut of his tailor-made suit. This was a monied individual, possibly someone very important, and despite his dead, twisted limbs he was a force to be reckoned with. His next thought was about breakfast. Maybe a nice blueberry muffin. Yes indeed, that would certainly hit the spot. Ponytail blew into his breather straw and propelled himself past the shaved gorilla in the suit. “Thank you Lars,” he said before puffing into the breather once again. Alex doubted this man was here to get a workout. Best to play it cool until he knew what the angle was. If there was anything he learned from his father, it was to be suspicious of everyone. The realization that he might have learned something from his father made Alex want to throw up the blueberry muffin he hadn’t even eaten yet. “Morning,” Alex said with a smile and a nod. Lars let the door swing closed and fell in line behind the wheelchair. “Did you hear that?” Ponytail asked him. “The Son of Sabretooth has acknowledged our presence.” Alex wasn’t amused. “That’s a good one.” He paused. “Are you waiting to use the treadmill? Cause I’ll be done in about fifteen minutes.” “My name is Ravenel McBain. I was a friend of your father’s.” “Well any friend of my father is a friend of m – oh wait, no they’re scumbags. Yeah, that’s what they are. So you can take your Steven Segal ponytail and your hot wheels, and roll right on out of here.” “You’ll note that I did say ‘was’ a friend, as in ‘he’s responsible for putting me in this wheelchair and sodomizing me with a baseball bat.’” “Sounds like you’re not tight anymore… in pretty much every sense of the word.” “I’m here because I want to help you.” “Okay, let’s assume that I really did just fall off the turnip truck. Why would I want your help?” “Because I know talent when I see it. I see a lot of your old man in you.” Alex pushed a button on the treadmill’s control panel and the front end of the machine began to incline upward. “Put that hose back in your mouth and blow. I’m not interested.” “Lex, listen to me. I used to run from city to city, state to state, country to country, guiding your father’s career. I’m not proud of much during that time. I’ve got plenty to answer for, and I think helping you might be a step in the right direction… so to speak.” “Yeah that’s great. Now try it again, only leave out the bullshit.” “Fine. Look at me. I’m a head. I had a life once. I had nice cars and beautiful women. Now I have to blow into this straw to get from Point A to Point B. I get pressure sores that weep puss. My ride is a pimped out van. Women? I can’t even pay for one now. Hell, I can’t even jerk off if I wanted to. All that, thanks to your father.” “By helping you become a huge star, it’ll drive him around the bend. That I can live with.” “I’m not asking you to sign papers with me. I’m asking for a chance. That’s all.” “I need to get some ring time in. I still have some ring rust I have to shake.” “Already taken care of. I talked to a local promoter and he’s agreed to set up his ring for you. He’s also lined up some of his workers for you to spar with.” ---- “Look out Nelly!” The body crashed to the mat, the impact echoing off the walls of the school gymnasium. Alex helped the young man, “Jumping” Jimmy Quaid, to his feet. Satisfied the rookie wrestler was intact after taking Creed’s snap powerslam, Alex crossed the ring and knelt down to pick up a bottle of water. Rave looked up from his wheeled seat at ringside and smiled. “You’re welcome Lex,” he said. Alex took a sip. “Okay so this isn’t an entirely bad idea. But don’t get ahead of yourself and have Lars pat you on the back just yet. I should be working with guys more like Ian Nackedy. After all, he’s the one I have to beat this round.” “I have someone better in mind.” Lars waved toward the Boy’s bathroom, and a young man stuck his head into the room. He shouted something and darted out of the way. The door slammed open and a tall, muscular man stepped out of the bathroom. His white hair was short-cropped, his sunglasses sitting in front of a frowning face. The wrestler slid into the ring and stared a hole in the Mecca. Alex wasn’t amused – the man’s resemblance to his father was disturbing. Alex snapped at Rave. “What the Hell is this?” he demanded. “This is you learning to fight with aggression. You have a lot of anger in you, and not just at your father. You need to tap into that and focus it. If you can do that, you won’t need a wheelbarrow of steroids to make you a destructive force of nature. You are far more athletically gifted than just about anyone else in the business. Time for you to show it.” Rave puffed and guided his chair away from ringside. “Besides, that’s his gimmick. He’s Vic “Grizzly” Reed. That’s grounds for a beating no matter how you slice it.” The bell sounded, and the two wrestlers locked up for their sparring session. After a second of jostling, Reed shoved Alex back several feet. The Mecca shook his head, refusing to get baited into lashing out. They locked up a second time and Vic shoved him back, only three feet this time. Rave’s eyes studied Alex’s reaction and saw something in the young man that struck him as familiar. “That’s it kid,” he said under his breath. “Let go.” The Mecca circled his larger foe and locked up for a third time. The Grizzly took one step, then another, forcing Alex back into the corner. He broke the lock and slowly backed away. Alex kept his arms up to protect his head. Vic smirked, shoved Alex in the chest, then slapped him across the face when his arms came down. “I’m the Main Man, bitch!” Reed shouted and flexed. That was the moment Rave had been waiting for. “Yes,” he whispered. The body kick sounded like Alex had swung a length of two by four and smacked Vic in the stomach. Alex strode out of the corner and grabbed Vic behind the head with both hands, locking his fingers together in a Thai clinch. He pulled Vic forward and repeatedly drove his knee, piston-like, into Reed’s stomach. Each knee was punctuated by a “Guh!” from the brutalized Grizzly. The tenth knee almost lifted the larger man off his feet. The eleventh was delivered to Vic’s face, which brought him up from his doubled-over position. One hand remained on the back of Vic’s head, but the other snapped like a boxer’s right hook, only with an elbow rather than a fist. The blow caught Vic above the left eye and instantly opened up a deep, bloody gash. Vic’s legs also buckled, but to his credit he managed to stay on his feet. The promoter, who had been speaking with two other men on the opposite side of the building, immediately stopped his conversation and ran, full sprint, to the ring shouting “What the fuck are you doing! You’re going to kill him!” Mercifully, Alex stopped his assault and let Vic’s bloody head rest on his chest. He then grabbed him in a front facelock and, showing that deceptive strength that he’d once been renowned for, lifted the two hundred and eighty-five pound man into the air with a vertical suplex. Vic’s blood ran down the side of the Mecca’s body, and Alex seemed content to let him drain out. “That’s enough Alex!” Rave’s powerful voice seemed out of place compared to the fragile body that contained it. But when Alex brought Vic down by transitioning the suplex into a sit-out piledriver, his voice softened. “The Kodiak Krippler,” he whispered, recognizing the move as one used by Alex’s father once upon a time. That made him smile. Alex, however, wasn’t smiling. In fact, once he’d realized what he’d done, he looked very embarrassed. “Oh shit...” he said and dropped to his knees just as the promoter and four men slid into the ring to check on their motionless compatriot. Alex stared at the mat, watching the blood from Vic’s head start to pool towards him. “God I’m so sorry,” he said to it, looking so shameful that he couldn’t bear to lift his head. “I’m so sorry.” Rave said to Lars, “Go throw some of my money at these people, see if we can’t smooth things over with them.” The big man nodded and walked over to the corner of the ring where a small crowd had formed. Someone called for a paramedic, called for anyone with a cell to call 911. One long puff on the breathing tube brought Rave’s chair to the opposite side of the ring. He watched Alex, buried in his shame, and smiled. “Your eyes are open now Lex,” Rave said softly. “Now let’s see what you do with your newfound truth.”
