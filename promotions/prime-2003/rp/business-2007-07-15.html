Business

15 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 15th, 2007 “Will there be anything else, sir?” The desk was chaos incarnate. A haphazard collection of crime scene photographs, family albums, police reports and newspaper clippings from the Charlotte Observer littered the reddish hue of the mahogany desk top. His right elbow rests heavy upon the final photograph of Linda Roberts, shot three times in the quiet comfort of her North Carolina home, crinkling the middle portions of the crime scene capture. However, after four intense hours of meticulously studying that picture along with a hundred other collected pieces of evidence, Tyler Rayne could just not find the will to care. He rubs at his forehead with the thick of his index and middle fingers, a futile attempt at relieving some of the frustration and exhaustion that seemed to be compounding as each hour painfully ticked by. A long sigh blew more frustration through his lips, dark, tired eyes turning up to address the thin little Englishman. “The usual suspects should be arriving shortly. I’ll be in the lower levels. Once they arrive, you’re free for the evening.” There was no verbal response. Just a short, curt nod, the edges of his pencil-thin mustache twitching above a servant’s smile. Alfred took two steps backward, ensuring there would be no further instructions, before turning his back to the master and taking leave. The sound of the closing door was like thunder to an otherwise silent room. Feet propped upon the edge of the desk, Tyler pushed back, reclining the leather office chair to dangerous proportions. Seconds pass on the precipice of toppling, but gravity fails to win the round, and Rayne eventually leaves his contemplation with an agitated slam of his fist. Pictures and notes flutter from the epicenter of impact, revealing the photograph of a handsome young man in his graduation gown. A beaming mother, proud smile spread wide across her aging face, rests safely in the long reach of her son’s arm. If only Linda Roberts had known what would await her just a few short weeks after. The boy’s smile, an innocent showcase of pearly white, is nearly large enough to rival his mother’s. A shaggy mop of blond hair, unable to be contained by the baby blue cap atop his head, combine with a set of striking ice blue eyes to provide the perfect picture of American stock. The quintessential properties of standard aesthetics. Simply put, he was a good-lookin’ kid. His father, bespectacled and balding, held himself in much more reserved tones. A brown suit and muted expression convey a sense of proper that his wife and son seem to have forgotten. The closeness with which he stands, however, an arm wrapped tightly around the graduate to bring him near, reveals William Roberts’ own sense of pride, despite the almost professional expression etched upon his face. William Roberts, found dead with pieces of skin and hair beneath his nails, his murderer’s blood splashed across his knuckles, had not gone quietly. Above all else, William had loved his family absolute. He had not let them go without a fight. Tyler Rayne’s focus, for what may well be the millionth time in just this one evening, narrows to the sickeningly joyful expression of Drake Roberts. Those icy eyes. That award winning smile. An exemplary capture of youth and its unending potential. “What the fuck did you see, kid?” With a hollow shake of his head, Tyler pushes himself away from the desk, standing for the first time in God knows how many hours. The stiffness makes it impossible to walk. The sharp blasts of pain coursing through his spine make it unbearable to stand. There are some days when Tyler Rayne finds it impossible to express just how happy he is to be back in the ring. It is far from an easy task, something as simple as walking to the bookcase, but these days, few things came without some sort of difficulty or pain. It’s just the nature of the business. Or so he keeps telling himself. The bookcase, a large but ultimately boring piece of furniture, is filled to capacity with dozens of volumes covering a vast litany of subjects, few of which Tyler Rayne has ever actually read. Or even opened. Various medical journals and dictionaries, books of law and doctrine from random states and countries throughout the world, a catalogue of military weapons and strategies, histories of world leaders and theories on politics. There are, of course, a handful of books he has read, located in a more centrally accessible row upon the shelf. Sun Tzu’s (no, not that one) The Art of War. Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas by Hunter S. Thompson. Both of Homer’s classic works The Iliad and The Odyssey. Dante’s Inferno. Milton’s Paradise Lost. The complete Michael Crichton collection. A smattering of Stephen King novels, (most prominently It, Insomnia, and Hearts in Atlantis). All six Harry Potter books. The Lord of the Rings trilogy and The Hobbit, as well as The Silmarillion by J.R.R. Tolkien. And every single Star Wars novel ever written, even the Young Jedi series. Above that, right at eye level, are the crown jewels of the library: his collection of trade paperbacks. Of course there are standards here, as well. Alan Moore’s Watchmen and V for Vendetta. The entire Sin City series. A couple other of Frank Miller’s finer works, including Welcome Back, Frank and The Dark Knight Returns. All three books of J. Michael Straczynski’s Rising Stars. And right in the middle, a handful of Batman stories written by (arguably) one of the most inspired writers to ever take hold of the series, Jeph Loeb. Hush, Dark Victory, and smack dab in the dead center of the entire bookshelf, The Long Halloween. Tyler reaches up, two fingers grasping at either side of Loeb’s classic. He pulls the book away, taking a second to admire the fantastic artwork of Tim Sale. There is a quiet metallic click followed by the low hum of a whirring motor. The bookcase vibrates, a loud thud announcing its short descent into the wall behind. With another heavy thud the bookcase comes to rest an inch behind the main wall. The ear-gnashing noise of grinding gears. This time the bookcase moves horizontally, rolling to the left. As the giant wooden encasement disappears behind the wall, it reveals the dull metallic grey of an elevator door. Tyler steps through the new opening, a finger pressing firmly against the only button on the elevator. Much more quietly than the bookcase made its retreat, the elevator doors slide open. Tyler takes a leaning position against the back of the elevator, still admiring the fine work of Loeb and Sale’s dynamic duo. The elevator descends. The doors open upon a wide staircase, each step flanked on either side by matching suits of medieval armor. The armors come in all different shapes and designs. Some are very plain, the generic type of English armor one finds in the movies. Then there are similar pieces with more intricate designs. Etchings in the breastplate. Bronze emblems bearing the forms of lions and eagles. A pair of Italian suits with pointed, and somewhat unwieldy looking, shoulder pads. The stairs open onto a foyer, a little red bridge that leads over the babbling brook of his own underground Japanese garden. It’s a rather small little enclosure, more of a relaxing way-between for the hallway on the other side. This hallway, much shorter than the last, is flanked on either side by Samurai armor of varying colors and makes. It opens into another hall, this again encompassed by a mass of armors at attention. Stormtrooper armor, to be precise, the final doorway guarded by two red-robed Imperial guards. The door, a single entry passageway, opens onto a vast, cavernous expanse of underground…well, cavern. The cave is enormous, narrow walkways of rock and stone winding around expanses and chasms that could well lead down for miles. Some of the stalactites above drip rivulets of water, creating serene puddles upon the rough rock. Tyler Rayne takes move to the left, a raised dais of rock providing the perfect platform for the command center he’s installed. He jogs up the embankment to a large command chair, an exact replica of Captain Picard’s seat on the Enterprise, and jumps inside. Quite comfortable, if we do say so ourselves. He tosses The Long Halloween onto a metal ledge extending from the main frame of the command center, pulling a keyboard toward him to resume the investigation. Before him glows one large, rectangular screen flashing through random images of Drake Roberts. To his right are two smaller screens, the lower one replaying Nitz Donnelly’s tag team endeavor with Devin Shakur, a higher one showcasing Rayne’s PRIME debut against Tom Walczak and Chainz. His focus, however, remains upon the central screen, fingers clacking command protocols, prompting the pictures of Drake Roberts to fade. The foreground is replaced by yet more crime scene photographs. Two Japanese men, one donned in the sort of generic suit that identifies him as a government agent. The other, much younger and clad in a more fashionable ensemble, is presumably Yakuza. The third photograph is a young woman, American, brunette with large brown eyes. The bullet hole in her chest depreciates the wholesome beauty she once portrayed, a deep crimson stain covering the largest portion of her white halter, pooling beneath the brown half-jacket and tight jeans she’d worn that evening. Details were skim, at best. The basic reports and initial investigations were easy enough to come by. Hacking deeper into the system, however, was proving to be as difficult as he had expected. Though he honestly doubted there would be little in the way of helpful information, at this point, any possible point in the right direction would be most welcome. There was no sure bet as to what happened here. A few theories, sure, but nothing seemed to fit. Deep down in his gut, somehow, Tyler knew that something was inherently wrong. There was something obvious he was missing. But it wasn’t clicking. As best he could figure, the story goes like this. After graduating near the top of his class at East Wake High School, Drake celebrated with a trip to Tokyo. Despite his starting position on the varsity basketball team and relative status of popularity within the unforgiving social structure of high school, Drake harbored a deep love and fascination for Japanese anime. Particularly anything that dealt with mechs. So as a graduation present, his parents provided two tickets to the city that spawned his favorite form of entertainment. Drake took his girlfriend, Summer Scott, and the two enjoyed a fantastic vacation for five wonderful days. Then, somehow, the two end up lost. The story, as they inevitably do, takes a turn for the worst. Suzuki Ichiro, the youngest Japanese corpse, was a low-level Yakuza lieutenant. The other Japanese man was Tanaka Nagano. Details of rank and affiliation have been buried deeper than Rayne’s been able to dig. Government of some sort, that much is certain. Ballistics reports draw out a very clear timetable of the scenario. Drake and Summer, presumably, stumble upon this chance encounter between the two opposing Japanese forces. Ichiro murders Summer. Nagano lands a lethal, but not instantly fatal, retaliation shot. Before he falls, Ichiro drills Nagano between the eyes. Leaving Drake Roberts as the only witness to a triple homicide. A tourist with a dead girlfriend and too much information. The question now becomes, of course, what happened to Drake Roberts? When local authorities arrived at the scene, there was no trace of the recent graduate. He did not wait for the police to arrive, and he did not go to them afterward. Why? Two obvious conclusions leap forward. It is possible that Ichiro was not alone. Drake may have been on the run from other gang members, and therefore unable to make contact with local authorities. Credit card records indicate that he purchased a one-way ticket back to the States that very evening. If the Yakuza were after him, it would have been prudent to leave the country immediately. Which leads to another question: why did he not go to the authorities once he arrived back home? Most likely because he could not trust them. As much as he doesn’t want to admit it, Tyler can’t help that the most likely conclusion is collusion. Ichiro and Nagano were in on something. Together. The two kids were unfortunate enough to stumble upon this meeting, which lead to Ichiro murdering Summer. There certainly can’t be witnesses to a thing like this. Nagano, betraying whatever criminal intentions he’d brought to the meeting, shot a criminal in direct response to witnessing the crime. Both men killed each other, leaving Drake to escape. The kid knew he couldn’t go to the police, as he’d apparently observed enough to realize that Nagano was an official of some sort. He also probably assumed that, as soon as the scene was discovered, both parties would be looking for him. So he hopped the first flight out of Tokyo, a direct route to Chicago, from whence it took him three days to carefully make his way back home. Unfortunately, it only took two days for an assassin to burst into Drake’s home and murder his parents. It is assumed that William Roberts attempted to protect his wife from the intruder, at which point he received a bullet to the abdomen. The murder weapon was traced back to Roger Philmore, a repeat offender based out of Raleigh. After shooting Mr. Roberts, Philmore put three rounds to the missus. He finished William with a final blast to the head before bolting. Philmore was found a few hours after the incident, smashed against the concrete seven blocks from the Roberts’ home. He allegedly “fell” from a nearby building while attempting his escape. The story fits nicely, but from here, the trail goes cold. Roger Philmore, obviously a hired gun, was presumably murdered by his employer(s), and thusly, cannot identify them. Suzuki Ichiro and Tanaka Nagano are unable to testify, if they would have been willing, as to what questionable activities they were consorting in. Summer Scott cannot bear witness to these activities either. And Drake Roberts, the only surviving member of this entire ordeal, has completely dropped off the grid. His last known electronic purchase was a bus ticket to Charlotte. Security surveillance proves he arrived in Charlotte as expected. After that, nothing. No credit card transactions. No bank withdrawals. Not even a possible sighting. Absolutely no trace. Drake Roberts has become a ghost. “Any luck?” The refreshingly optimistic query of Katterina Wylde turns him from the monitor. Tyler jumps to his feet, greeting the leather clad temptress with a warm hug. She responds in kind, a friendly embrace that ends quickly enough. Leaving them to other matters. Katterina turns her eyes, a jaunt of her head in Milena’s direction. The stealthy little ninja stands before the twenty foot visage of Abe Lincoln’s copper head, completely mesmerized by the enormity and utter uselessness of such a giant trinket. “So you finally got the giant penny, I see.” “You’ve no idea how much I love e-Bay.” “Did it ever occur to you that you might have too much money?” He laughs, the two of them watching Milena as she turns her attention to the life-sized replica of Tyrannosaurus Rex. “Not even once. If I didn’t have all this money, I wouldn’t be able to build my own Bat Cave, now would I?” Katterina just shakes her head. Sometimes even she is surprised by the level of absurdities Tyler will go to satiate his nerdom. Milena’s hand reaches out to the Tyrannosaur, shocked at the real-to-life feel of its reptile-like skin. Tyler’s smile widens as he watches the lithe Japanese beauty admire the collected wonders of his underground compound. “I just realized she’s never been down here before.” “Yes, well, we’re all not graced with the luxury of being your best friend.” “I think I sense a note of sarcasm there, Katt.” “Cool as this place is, Ty, I do wish you’d find someone else to share your inner geek with. You know, like a girlfriend.” At this he walks away, a shake of his head the only response, marching back toward the bevy of computer monitors he had previously been occupying. Katterina follows, taking place behind the captain’s chair as he works to shut the consoles down. Her wandering eyes flutter to the right, glimpses of Tom Walczak beating on Devin Shakur. Nitz Donnelly’s aerial assault on Diego Delgado. “A little homework, Ty? How professional of you.” “The more you know and all that.” “Right. So what’s new with the kid?” He sighs. “Not a god damn thing. I’ve gone back three generations with this kid. Him and the girl both. Nothing even remotely illegal. Squeaky fuckin’ clean. Whole god damn family. Whoever’s hidin’ ‘im now sure as shit isn’t blood.” “Great. Anything from Sota?” “Won’t return my calls. Which means he probably can’t return my calls. Someone’s got an eye on him.” “You don’t think he set you up, do you?” “Fuck no. Yoshi’s had my back for fifteen years. Good kid. I got a feeling he knew more about this than he let on, but he’s not leadin’ me. He probably expected me to figure all this out. Too many eyes where he’s at.” “So someone in the government is making deals with Yakuza. Not exactly new.” “No shit. But someone’s gone to a lot of trouble to cover this incident up. A lot of trouble to get to this kid. This, whatever it is, is bigger than usual. Fuckin’ worm can, and like usual, I’m swimmin’ in it. You get that info on Paris?” “Yeah. There’s a KFC right around the Forum des Halles. Made a few phone calls, and at least one of the frogs remembers seeing someone fits Blaze’s description. Frequently.” “Almost too easy. Best guesses with those satellite configs places most of those phone calls in the first arrondissement. Same area as the Forum. Not exactly residential, but at least we know Con’s spending a lot of time there. And Shakur didn’t fuck me over, which is nice for once.” “And all this time I thought you liked getting fucked over.” “Fucked, sweetheart. Fucked over I can generally do without.” “So what’s the next move?” “Now that we’re sure he’s there, I go to Paris and track down Con. I don’t know shit about NC, and I’d rather go in with whatever little info he’ll be willing to give. Depending on what I get from him, we’ll know where to start pounding the underground.” She smiles. “My favorite part.” Tyler stands, stretching some of the soreness from his arms. There’s nothing much for his back and shoulders, but he’ll deal. The two friends descend from the console bank, Milena awaiting their arrival with a barely contained enthusiasm. “So we goin’ out or what?” He laughs, turning an amused question toward Katterina. “Since when did she become the club rat?” “I think we’re starting to rub off on ‘er.” “Guess so. Lemme make a phone call real quick.” Milena blows an exasperated sigh, almost pouting as Tyler pulls the phone from his pocket. Yes, he does get reception down here. That’s Japanese technology at its finest. “Lavelle. Hey, it’s Rayne. Look, I got me two girls here who are very anxious to be out with the drinking. Wouldn’t be proper to do so without giving you a call. So let’s go. … C’mon, man. You know me better than that. I’m not trying to… What makes you think I can’t handle two girls by myself, anyway? … Look, they’re just a few friends… No, it’s not Brooks… Shut up for a second, will ya? Damn, kid. This isn’t a discussion. We’re going out. You’re going with us. … I don’t care. … Well she can come, too. I’d love to meet her. … Oh, so now you’re embarrassed to introduce me to your girlfriend? … That’s fine. I know a few places we can get in for free. … Yeah, well, I know some guys. … Drinks are on me. No excuses. You and Dawn get yourselves ready. We’ll be there in an hour. … Yes, I know where you live. … I Googled it. You’d be amazed how much that really works. … Alright, man. I’ll see you in an hour.” “Now we can go?” “Yes, Milena, we can go.” “Can we take that?” Her finger points across the cavern, an opening from above shining brilliant moonlight down upon the vehicle in question. The Batmobile, circa 1989. “I thought perhaps we’d take something slightly less…conspicuous.” The disappointed look that falls across her face is almost too cute for words. He can’t help but smile as she sulks by, a defeated retreat to the elevator. Katterina just shakes her head, the two following Milena back toward the upper levels. “So you and this Pierce guy seem to be getting along well.” “He’s a good kid. Well-intentioned. Besides, friends are hard to come by in this business. Better take ‘em while they come.” “And which business is it you’re referring to?” His expression fades, thoughts and conjectures of Drake Roberts once again floating through his mind. “I don’t even know anymore.”
