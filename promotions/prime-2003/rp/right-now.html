Note the above, by his side project Fort Minor, which basically conveys the same message as the movie Crash, except with less Ludacris - in more ways that one. To an extent, though, that song serves as a metaphor for our theme and our story. Witness one Dan Ferguson, a sixth grade teacher in Lexington who as once a simple audition away from becoming one the biggest stars of his lifetime. It was many, many years ago when Dan was just another kid in Berea, OH, whose mother took him to an open casting call for the new sitcom, 'Four Is A Crowd.' Upon realizing the cheap and malleable benefits of undiscovered child talent, producers went looking everywhere, including the talent-bare and simple-minded Midwest. Hence, Berea. Under no circumstances would the Fergusons normally go the mall on a Saturday, because it's always so busy and seven-year-old Dan got so tired after playing in his morning youth-league soccer game. That day, though, he had scored a goal in their last game of the season, so his father Lance promised that he could go get a new action figure from Toys 'R Us after the team's pizza party. It was there, just as they walked out of the store with the He-Man toy halfway out of the package, that his mother saw the sign. 'Hollywood Auditions' it beckoned. 'Children 5-12.' Susan Ferguson, who had always dreamed big for her little boy, pleaded quietly with Lance to get them to go check it out, and after young Dan learned of the prospect of being on TV just like He-Man, he was all for it. Little did he know that the chain of events acting and reacting from that decision would make him the hermit-like Very few times outside of class would Dan step outside, other than the trip to work and back. He gained a reputation around the PTA for his solitude, but because no kid had ever filed charges or claimed any malfeasance, what reason did they have to worry? He was a good teacher, he just didn't socialize. Plenty of people went through that time in their life. And it's not hard to believe that the third-best Science teacher at Lexington Central Middle School would want to be alone every once in a while, stuck in a dead-end job with no prospects for advancement, nowhere else to advance to and a romantic life that hadn't registed a blog entry since November of 1991. Dan had long since given up on RSVPng for events that he knew he wouldn't attend, a decision that was helped by the fact that no one would invite him anywhere anymore, since they knew the answer. Why would he do that? Why would he shut himself away? Xenophobia. Yes, I said xenophobia. Zebraphobia is a fear of striped horses, dummy. Actually, it's more like agorophobia, but when you're a kid, everyone's strange or unknown, so it may as well be xenophobia. Dan's failed audition sent him spiraling into a crippling fear of being around people. His parents just shrugged it off - a brief tease of fame, something fun to do on the weekend - but the impact shattered poor Dan's psyche. He didn't want to play little league soccer anymore, he didn't want to go to school, even though they made him. He wouldn't even go out in public with his parents without throwing tantrums so intense and distracting that they resigned to not going out themselves. They never could understand what the problem was, and since they could never get the boy to a doctor, it seemed like they would never find out. Assured that Dan was alive, and healthy, they stopped troubling themselves over his problem and just let the boy live his life in lonely fear. Bang-up job parenting. A-Plus. By the time Dan finished college, his parents had moved away from Berea, all but ensuring that he would never come visit them. Truthfully, it was that plan which led him to Kentucky to begin with. Since he had been taking correspondence courses to prevent actually going out to class, he still hadn't left Berea. He had taken up residence in the converted garage, and his parents simply sold the house on the condition that the new owners take Dan along with it. When he got word of this, he tried to track them down with the money they left behind for him. On the way to Florida, he only made it to Kentucky before his disease overtook him in such a fit that he was physically removed from the Greyhound bus. Left to find his own way at the Lexington station, he opted instead to find an apartment and update his address by mail, to prevent having to handle the fear he experienced on that bus. Clearly, Dan has become one of the worst cases of Hollywood jilting in recorded history, if there was someone to record it. He froze in that audition, and the producers were merciless. There was such a thin hair dividing the line between the next child star and some brat with a lisp. His mother had asked to come inside to calm his nerves, but right then, a phone call from her sister pulled her away. Who knows what would've happened if she stayed. Well, we know, but I'm pretending we don't for effect. Dan started his gig as a teacher because he hadn't been anywhere besides a school since he was eight. He couldn't get used to any more change. Every day, he staves off the soul-pinching loneliness by playing seven to ten hours of Halo 2 on the Xbox, and occasionally posting on Internet message boards about which wrestler is the greatest. He also joined an e-fed with a character loosely based on one of his own favorites, the late Matt Register. Occasionally, he updates a blog that he uses to follow e-fedding to he can keep his finger on the throbbing, frenetic pulse of wrestling-based internet writing competitions. From here on out, though, he wanted to make a stand. Sure, he said that every Wednesday, but this time would be different. He was going to stop ordering his groceries at home and he would actually go to the store. Groceries would be a harmless errand, something quick and painless, a baby step towards the eventual turnaround that his life had been waiting for. How much had be missed by being out here? It probably merits a list that I don't have time to mention. Just think of all you do in a single day. Kindly ignore that I'm stretching on these one-line paragraphs. Late RPs have become my habit, but it doesn't look like anyone else in my match is gonna post, so I'm in the clear. Hippopotamus. Fizzle. Tinntinnabulation. Man alive, I can't finish strong to save my fucking life. Next time, I swear I'll use my whole ass. Oh, you want to hear the rest of our boy's story? Would he turn his life around and put him back on the course to normalcy? Probably not. He immediately breaks down outside the Food Lion, nearly convulsing, before sprinting back to his car and driving (comfortably at the speed limit, mind you) all the way home. Quivering, Dan lets the door slam behind him as he curls into a ball on the couch. He wonders what it might be like to breath free of all this, to step outside and to enjoy the company of others, and even have them enjoy yours. He wonders what it would be like to be famous.
