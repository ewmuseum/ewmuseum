Date: ReVolution 199 “You’re going to do all right, I promise you.” Philadelphia’s sultry voice though didn’t endow David with much hope in his mind. His palms sweated worse and worse with each passing minute, making them resemble the forehead of Patrick Ewing late in the 4th Quarter. As he stood in Jackson High School, he wondered to himself what had possessed him to do such a crazy thing like speaking to a room filled with nearly fifteen hundred people. Philadelphia grabbed David’s hand and looked at him with such worried eyes. “What’s making you stress out so much? You’ve talked to people before, more people than this,” she told him, trying to give him the confidence he needed to go out there on that stage and say something meaningful as opposed to coming off as a blundering idiot. Considering that his sister, Sarah, sat out in that audience, the pressure only mounted. “Sure, I talk to millions of people every week, but I’m a different guy out there. I’m not me, you know what I’m saying? I hope you do because I surely don’t know,” David rambled until he finally just shut up and looked over towards the curtain where he saw the Principal of the high school approaching him. David’s hand immediately shot out and the man grabbed it eagerly. “I have to say, this is such an honor to have you here to speak to these kids. When Sarah came to our school last month, she added a layer and dimension to the class that can’t be described in words. She has told me so many times how you’re her inspiration, how she wants to grow up to be just like you,” the obviously proud Principal spoke in a high tone. David nodded his head as he pulled his hand away and turned away from both Philadelphia and this scary man. “Hopefully not too much like you,” David mumbled under his breath before he spun back around with a smile on his face. Philadelphia took that opportunity to slide her arm in with his. Surprisingly, he didn’t shy away from her. “Sounds like she’s very proud of her older brother,” Philadelphia continued and saw this glimmer of hope in David’s eyes. He was locked in and ready to do his thing. “Well, I’m going to go out there and give you an introduction, David, and then you take it away.” David nodded his head. “Let’s do this.” Philadelphia could only smile before she whipped David around. “So, I was thinking that after this you and I could go get some lunch. Have this weird craving for some Chili Fries. Think the baby wants some. What do you think about that?” David looked at Philadelphia while his lips pushed out in thought. “Hm…,” David moaned as he stood there with this pained look on his face. “I’d love to, but I already promised to take MacKenzie to lunch. Well, more or less, I promised Andrea who told me that I needed to spend more time with her. Apparently, she’s having a difficult time adjusting to California. Philadelphia’s smile turned into a deep frown. “Oh, poor thing.” “Yeah. So, I’m going to go check on her, talk to her, and hopefully settle her down a little bit.” This is why she had cared so much about David and wanted him in her child’s life. Deep down, he cared, cared more than he let on and more than most people would ever realize. “That sounds really good, David. Very much.” He smiled before he wrapped his arms around Philly’s body and brought her into him. They held each other for a moment before he began to hear the Principal begin to wrap up his introduction. “So, without further introduction, let me bring out the man of the hour. As some of you in this audience will tell you, he is the #1 Contender for the PRIME Intense Champion, and will compete for it on PRIME’s largest stage. Jackson High School, let’s give a very warm welcome to David Noble.” David rolled his eyes at the words before he turned away from Philadelphia. As he reached the first of three steps, he turned around and looked at her. “The limo is going to take you home so you can take a nap and then we’ll hang out after then. Sound good?” She simply nodded her head before she watched him disappear behind the curtain and heard the loud roar of the excited students in attendance. That smile on her face only grew as she heard his voice come on over the speaker system. David looked out at the kids and felt this nervousness take over him ever so slowly. “You know, when Sarah asked me to come out here and speak to the 2009 Class of Jackson High School and the incoming seniors as well, I really didn’t know what I was going to speak out. Sure, I stand in the middle of a ring and speak to the fans all of the time, but that’s normally about how I intend to beat somebody’s ass later on that night. I could easily do that tonight and call out the Star Quarterback sitting in the 4th row, but I know he’s got a commitment to the Miami Hurricanes, so I think he could actually take me.” Laughter from the crowd as David began to find his comfort in being on the wooden stage. “Instead, Sarah looked at me and told me how each week, I talked about my dreams and aspirations. She mentioned to me that I had gone through my own difficulties until I saw my dreams come true, stepping inside of the ring and competing week in and week out. From there, I knew that I could do this. I could speak to you guys and tell you about a kid, who was once in your very shoes. A kid who aspired to become more.” He paused as he looked down at the blueish carpet on the ground. “A man that became more.” *** “How’ve you been doing, MacKenzie?” The question was quite an easy one as David sat in the cafeteria with his youngest sister who looked to be rather sad on this particular day. Several of the other kids stood around the two as they watched in awe at the man that they’d become accustomed to seeing each and every week on ReVolution. David casually waved at them before a few teachers walked up to the children and ushered them away. David looked at MacKenzie and saw the sadness in her eyes. He put his hand on the side of her face and stroked her cheek softly. “Talk to me, ‘Kenzie. I hate to see you so sad, and just want you to tell me what’s wrong.” He then pulled out a box and opened it. “Plus, I brought pizza. The pizza needs you. Badly.” That created a smile on her face and broke the ice that had surrounded them. “I’m just not happy here,” MacKenzie confided in David as she took a slice of the simmering hot pizza. As she blew on it, David grabbed a slice for himself and placed it on his plate. “What’s not to like? I thought you liked it here? It’s beautiful, you’re with your family, and you always seem to be having fun,” he told her and she just shrugged her shoulders before taking a piece of the pizza. It took her exactly two seconds before she spat it out onto the plate and began to wave her hand in front of her mouth from the burning sensation left in her mouth. David laughed. “Not funny,” she mumbled and then took a drink of water to cool her off. “Anyways, sure, it’s great and all, but I just thought…,” and then her voice faded away as the sadness returned to her eyes. MacKenzie’s head dropped and David put his hand on her shoulder. “Come on, talk to me,” he pleaded with her. “I just thought that when we came out here, that we were going to be a family again. Even Andrea came to live with us. But, it’s just like it always has been. You’re never around, Andrea is too busy to actually hang out, Mom and you are fighting, and now you have a baby on the way. I just wanted it to be us, and now it’s not going to be.” David heard her words and felt his heart break in the process. “Oh, MacKenzie.” His voice was somber as he moved around the table and wrapped his little sister into his arms. “I know, it’s been rough. I’m sorry I’m not around as much, and Mom and I aren’t fighting.” She looked up and shot him an evil look which took David by surprise. “Don’t lie to me.” MacKenzie spoke with this anger to her tone that David never knew her capable of. He nodded his head in an attempt to apologize to her. “You were gone for so long, David. How come you can’t just stay at home for once? Why can’t you focus on us?” David sighed as he tried to find the best way to explain it all to her. Several thoughts raced through his mind before he took a bite of his pizza. “MacKenzie,” he started in between bites. “At the end of the day, I still need to be out there doing my thing. But, know this. I think about you all of the time. I’m sorry if I’ve been distant since I’ve come back. I promise you, I won’t always be like that.” He took another bite of his pizza and then shared his slice with MacKenzie. She gladly took a bite. “But, you’ve got a baby on the way.” He chuckled at the thought. “It’s more difficult than that, MacKenzie. We don’t know that for sure yet. Okay?” “Yeah, I guess.” “Look, MacKenzie,” David finished off the pizza with a large bite. “We’re going to be a family, I promise you that. All of the problems everyone is having, it’s going to resolve itself. I promise you that too. It just takes time. In a way, I guess we forgot what it means to be a family together. It’s not going to be easy for us, but being back here, with you, it makes it all worth it.” David looked at his little sister and a smile appeared on her face and he smiled back at her. The two hugged and David kissed her on the forehead. “This is some good pizza,” she told him and he agreed with her. The day continued to wear on as the two just continued to talk until a teacher approached the table. “Lunch is actually up now, Mr. Noble. She’s going to have to go back to class.” David looked over at his little sister and she got out of her chair. David slowly closed the now empty pizza box and nodded at the teacher who walked away. “Do you want to go with me to a few of my shows? School’s almost out,” David asked his sister, eager to bring her on the road with him. “Maybe. It could be cool. Could everyone come?” He laughed. “Maybe. We’ll have to see though. It’s not that easy all of the time to get everyone eager to go from town to town. I do it because I love it, but you know how Mom is. She might not be down for that.” “Good point,” she responded quickly and the two laughed together. Then, MacKenzie came up to David and wrapped her arms around David’s waist as hard as she possibly could. A moment passed between them before MacKenzie looked up at David with a sad look on her face. “I miss Daddy,” she told him and he felt this pain overcome him as he patted her on the back. “So, do I, MacKenzie, so do I.” The youngest member of the Noble family then ran off and left David all by himself. *** Day had turned into evening, and David sat inside of his small apartment all by himself. As he glanced over at the clock, he saw that it read a few minutes past one, and sighed. A large bottle of Tequila sat on the counter and he felt this pounding sensation in his head. Somehow, a day that had been so positive turned into one that he wanted to forget. Not for any particular reason. He just felt the pain, the loneliness, that had lived with him each and every single day of his life. The pressure had mounted on his shoulders over the past few weeks. He saw the look in people’s eyes and knew that they doubted him where it pertained to Colossus VI. There was no way that he would be able to take down Jonathan Rhine, a legend in his own right. He had lost to Hessian and Jason Natas, and some claimed that they were nowhere close to the level that Rhine was at. It wore on him each and every day, not wanting to let those close to him and those who believed in him down. The sourness hit him, the Tequila’s poison trying it’s best to take over him, but he didn’t let it. Instead, he just drank it and felt the pounding in his head only grow worse and worse. He wanted to leave this world, badly. David wanted a chance to rid himself of the pain of not being accepted. The drink finished itself without him ever realizing it and poured himself another one. Not remembering the last one, he thought it had never existed. It’s how he had come to this point. As another drink went down the throat, he felt his fingers tapping around on the hard counter. His life was screwed, or so he thought. Turned on its head, Philadelphia had entered with a child in womb, and now he had to figure out how to be a father. Yet, he knew he could never be a good one. In all honesty, how could he? His biological father left him. Shawn had been shitty. It wasn’t in his nature to do this the right way. Philadelphia had told him that she wanted David involved in the baby’s life. She didn’t know him, he thought. His hand groped for his phone and found it hiding behind the nearly empty bottle before him. David began hitting buttons at random before he placed the phone to his ear. “Come on, answer…” David spoke to no one in particular as he heard the phone ring once and then twice. Then it went to voicemail. She had ignored his call. “Fucking…” His temper only rose as he hung up his phone and called back again. It rang once and went to voicemail. She had ignored him again. As it went through the typical spiel, he felt compelled to leave a message. David waited patiently until he heard the beep in his ear. “Mary-Lynn, you bitch.”
