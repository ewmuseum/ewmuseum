The Call Of The Wild (Match RP - v Chainz)

8 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/01/07 The following events take place between GTT6 and King of Kings 2006. 0 You’re here because of two words, and a nice dream. Broken, on the side of the mountain, you’d made your peace with the world, ready to depart from it to the most spectacular of views. But, with the planet not yet willing to return you to its soil, its grip was stretched all the way to the emergency room, where a nurse’s final, desperate heart compression tucked you snugly back into the world’s palm for safe-keeping. Just as someone who rescued a stray couldn’t help but feel bound to its fate somehow, the nurse watched over you as you slept, working any extra shifts he could to make ends meet as a single father. His daughter, quickly tiring of reading or watching TV in the staff lounge, talked to you through the vast loneliness of your coma, planting the seed that blossomed. Flowers carried the sweetest, most heady scent, while roots settled deep in your subconscious. She told you how she wanted the perfect family, a new Mommy, and your unconscious mind scripted her words into a lavish screenplay. You lived it, you felt what it was to find true happiness, immune from your cynicism and insecurities. You’re stubbornness neutralised, selfishness diminished. A child’s untainted optimism mixed with regular doses of morphine can pen quite the fairytale. And then you woke up. The pieces were in play, the plan was in motion, and you latched onto the fantasy, squeezing it with all you had until you’d moulded it into reality. And then you tied it all in place with two words. “I do.” You’re here because of two words, and a nice dream. I Despite the toasting of a roaring, open fire, you shiver. Your face flushes red, for which you can pass the blame onto the warmth. Attention ricochets around the room, and although you couldn't say who or what any picture or photo is of, they'll supposedly be what's distracting your focus. You peel your outer-layer off, knowing you can only repeat the trick once more and still retain your modesty, explaining away the beads of cold sweat defying your muffled curses to escape through your brow. It's almost your 25th birthday, that's why you're downing drink after drink, only dehydrating your already arid mouth. You've a reason for everything, you always do. You've an excuse for anything, you always have. You're teflon to blame, responsibility, growing up, settling down. Any burden can be relieved with a nonchalant shrug of your dismissive shoulders. You ARE Karina Wolfenden. Right? You're tired, is why you can't look at him. "Just what I needed," you announce, swiping another arrival of vodka and lemonade from his hand, eyes guiding your voice down towards the floorboards. "Be are be." The bathroom door slams shut behind you with such force that it bounces back out of its frame. You’re pacing back and forth, looking up from the bottom of the well to the brief glimmer of light, of home, taunting you from above the slippery walls that your fingernails scrape at. You wonder how you got down there, how you let yourself get down there, how you couldn’t grab onto a rope of honesty as you plunged, saving yourself. Saving yourself the feeling as you hit the bottom. Saving you from having to drag someone down with you, to save your own skin. Your ears buzz, as if you’re trapped at the bottom of the ocean, the pressure of millions upon millions of gallons trying to reduce you to puree. You stare at yourself in the mirror, inquisitively, judgementally, angrily, damningly… pleadingly? You slap the cold tap around, beginning a cascade to try and ease the pressure, splashing water up onto your face. With auburn fringe wetted down onto your forehead, and droplets of diluted mascara abseiling over your cheekbones, your lips part. "I can't marry you," you confess to the only person you can talk to, and the only person you ever listen to. "I can't do this. We can't do this. YOU can't do this. Not with me. There's som-... you're not th-... I don't love you, not enough." You’re the only two in the cabin, probably the only two within a kilometre of one another, and still you hear the call of another. Whistling through the evergreens, rattling the windows in their frames, lashing again the glass. The call of the wild. "I'm leaving you," you can finally say, finally allowing you to breathe, exorcising all that's been nagging at you. "I belong with another." As you finally pop the cap off your shaken bottle of insecurity, you gasp, remembering what air feels like in your lungs. Your kidneys seem to kick back into life, your blood feels purer, you feel purer. Vibrancy comes back to your skin, eyes regain their sparkle, you almost feel the dark cloud escape from your pores. You exhale a relieved sigh, then another, and it becomes breathless laughter. Your tears mix with the water still running down your face. The threshold has been broken. Then you look over the shoulder of your reflection, where he loyally stands, broken, and you go cold. As if paddles were thumping 4,000 volts into your chest, you snap up, rigid yet trembling, pale, just as you have every morning for the past fortnight. Just as you’ve only confided in your dreamland confessional every time, just as that same withering look in his eyes met you every time. Your hear the rattle of mugs across the hallway, faint, as your senses are slow to reboot. Feet swing down into a pair of fluffy Garfield slippers, you tie your hair back, and the sides of your mouth are forced up into a smile. Your hear his daughter’s excited footsteps pitter-patter past your door, ajar, swept up early in the rush of her 8th birthday. You look up again, seeing the light at the top of the well dwindle, and wipe a tear from your eye. Your fingers clamp your engagement ring, sliding it up to the bitten nail, teasing it around the finger tip. “Kari!” a child’s voice screeches from outside your bedroom, before a sugar-charged little fist knocks at it like a woodpecker. You slide the ring back down your finger, as if its too heavy to remove. And you live the lie for another day. II Did you know that Dolphins often go deaf in captivity? Did you WANT to know that Dolphins go death in captivity? Well, now you know, regardless. “Studies of echo location on Dolphins both in the wild, and in captivity, have shown that in their natural habitat, the creatures are exposed to far softer sounds, covering a much wider bandwidth,” the nature program lectures. “In aquariums, where a Dolphin is subjected to a barrage of highly concentrated sounds, it in effect scrambles their hearing with white noise, deafening them.“ You don’t watch nature programs, but, it’s been established that this isn’t exactly you. Did you know there’s a point at which a captive or domesticated animal can no longer be introduced back into the wild? That it would be like filing down their teeth, cutting back their claws, and just feeding them to the wolves? Yes. That one you knew, since it’s a point you’ve been counting down to every day, afraid to throw yourself from the runaway train before it ploughs into its oncoming brethren. “In such intelligent creatures, boredom, lack of exercise, and inability to interact with other creatures from their own social organisations all cause vast variations from natural behaviour,” it continues, unrelenting. “No longer required to hunt, blunting their instincts, after a point the creatures are rendered incapable of ever being released back into the wild.” Every word punches you in the stomach, every syllable kicks you in the teeth, and every image thumbs you in the eye. What’s worse is that you can’t turn it off. ““In the wild, animals such as Tigers spend around 10 hours every day hunting and guarding their territory,” the voice-over announces, its volume seemingly increasing every time you focus harder on the last song you listened to. “This, combined with the limited amounts of energy in their territory, help prevent the animals turning on one another when natural instincts take over. Compare this to animals in captivity, where plentiful energy and little natural release for their aggression can cause members of the same group to attac-“ It’s then that you realise this isn’t just about you. You look down to the child, someone else’s child, sitting between your feet, avidly scribbling down the bits she understands in her class notebook, complete with crayon drawing of what looks like a Walrus crossed with a Tigger. Extra ‘g’ intended. You’re a wild creature, world-roaming, globe-trekking. Territorial. Your claws are sharp, demeanour fierce, instincts honed. You want to hit something, someone. Daily. You bite. And you’re locked in a cage of supposed family bliss, so far from your natural habitat that you have to decide every morning if you’ve really woken up, or if its simply a dream-trick. You’ve felt the frustrations building up, you found yourself just pacing back and forth for minutes, head in the clouds so that time would pass beneath you. But there’s only one problem with the sands of time tricking through the hourglass. You’re getting married in three days time. III He fiddles with his cufflink, a charade for yet another checking of the time. It’s 30 seconds since the last time he checked, which was 30 seconds since the time before that, which was 30 seconds after she was 20 minutes late. You toss the towel to the floor and look up to the mirror, where auburn hair dye has been bleached out to a golden blonde, and streaked with black and purple. Giving yourself the same look you offer a friend when you finally catch up after years apart, you return your eyebrow ring to its piercing, then drop your vivid green contacts back in. He breaks into a reassuring smile for his daughter, standing there dutifully with her bouquet, fidgeting, but patient. Running the back of your hand down your wedding dress, you step back to admire it as it hangs. A minute later you’ve slashed off the bodice and are wearing it under a leather jacket. He adjusts his tie for the 7th time, his buttonhole flower for the 5th time, his hair for the 8th time, his collar for the 3rd time, his cufflink for the 43rd time. He goes to scratch his hairline, using it as a chance to wipe his brow. You finally detach yourself from your engagement ring, leaving it beside a photo of the three of you, the happy family that was, in a world of dreams. You tuck a letter into the one place he’d know to look, and you do the same under her pillow. Telling yourself there isn’t a tear in your eye, you grab your bags and home in on the beacon of the Taxi’s horn. He hears the whispers from the small group his family’s side, which has spread across into the empty pews on yours. Great Aunts who’d never gotten along with his mother start to spin their rumours, while the other pessimistic relatives tut and sigh. You speak aloud, letting yourself know that, for once, this wasn’t you being selfish. “Sir,” a representative of the Chapel has to awkwardly relay. “We’re going to have to start preparing for the next wedding party.” “Flight 1602 for Columbus is now boarding at gate B5,” comes over the tannoy as you press your free hand over your left ear, so you can hear Chet Worth on the other end of the line. And then you give him your answer. “I do.” You’ve been released back into the wild, and your claws are sharp.
