A Reason to Stay

4 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/4/07 THE BOOK SIGNING As told by The Spectator Ian Nackedy wore white shoes with a black overcoat. It’s something that sort of stood out at the time, which is pretty much what Ian has tried to do his entire life. Ian made jokes, said random things, and avoided being serious for so many hours that the owners of the book store asked him politely to keep the outbursts to a minimum while he was signing his book, the aptly titled Ferret Wheels, Cosmic Discrepancies and Bad Toupees: The Ian Nackedy Story. Ian Nackedy politely showed the book store owners his rear end, and he was thusly politely booted in that rear end and out of the establishment. He received cheers for his actions, as there is and always has been a large audience for the type of silliness and randomness that follows Mr. Nackedy everywhere he goes like a bad mobster cliché follows Jason Snow. People were shouting his name, asking for an autograph, and one fan even banged on the windows of his car as he tried to pull away. Forever trying to avoid the typical and serious reaction of being scared at this, Ian smiled and gave the double thumbs up. Then he hit the highway by himself. The cigarette hit his lips. The lighter button was pressed down. Ian Nackedy sighed and closed his eyes, which although very helpful in determining character emotion is a very bad idea when driving down a long road. However, there was no car accident today because establishing Ian’s frustration takes precedent. Death can wait for a few years. Or maybe next week. Or whenever PRIME books him against Chainz. Ian Nackedy did the unthinkable out on the road, forty miles from home and eight hours of sleep away from true functionality. He broke character, and a tear dropped down his cheek. Grabbing his cell phone, he dialed some numbers and waited, then sighed into the phone. “I don’t know what to do, Gildenstern. Maybe we’re done. Maybe we always have been.” FAMILY MEETING As told by The Spectator Ian closed the door and found Gildenstern and his brother Foster waiting for him. Ian looked at them, sighed, and sat down. “All right, here’s the thing, guys. I don’t know if I’m just selfish, or that I am so desperate for victories that I’m being a sourpuss because we haven’t gotten many. I don’t have any clue what it is, but all I know is I passed a homeless man in the gas station on the way home. And you know what I did?” Gildenstern and Foster looked at each other uneasily, and Foster opened his mouth, but Ian continued. “I didn’t make fun of him, or pretend to be homeless too, or anything else like that. I just gave him twenty dollars and walked away.” The two shared glances again, and then stared blankly at Ian once more. Ian, in moderate reply, flapped his arms spastically. “Don’t you see it? I’M NOT IAN NACKEDY ANYMORE! I’m some…some…” “Human being?” came Gil’s weak reply. “Yeah,” said Ian. “And I don’t like it at all.” Ian sighed. “I just don’t see the point anymore, guys. We dress up like weirdos, make about fifteen people in the audience laugh, and then get the glory taken away from us. We’re not good, we’re not fan favorites, we’re just there. Is there anything worse than being just there?” Gildenstern and Foster shared one more glance, and that was enough to finally get Ian’s attention. “What is it?” he asked impatiently. “Well,” said Gildenstern, tracing a frown face on the table, “there is something. Foster and I discovered something that kind of makes our identity crisis in PRIME a little unimportant.” Foster, finally letting loose his anger, tossed down a piece of paper with a red circle around the “$-244” selection in the corner of the page and shouted, “A real fucking identity crisis.” POURING As told by Gildenstern There’s some stupid cliché that says “when it rains, it pours.” I hate clichés, and I hate admitting that this one is true even more. But that’s how it goes sometimes. Ian has been wrapped up in this fog of doubt since our loss at Dice, Dollars, and Tragedy. We keep getting fed to people who should eat us up, and when they do it just breaks his heart. Like I said before, I can let these things roll off of my back, but Ian takes everything to heart. You didn’t hear it from me, but honestly he gets a little maniacal about the whole situation. Foster and I have been dealing with a lot of stuff in the past week, not really being able to take advantage of our little vacation. There’s some stuff we really needed to hammer out with Ian, some stuff that he as one of the main bread winners of the household should know about. But he’s been writing a book. I read that crap, and every page is sillier than the last. I love good fun and all, but I am not unrealistic enough that I stop paying attention to the real world when it’s sending me cards I don’t like. To use one of the endless annoying analogies that Five Year Old Integer Chris has been spouting out, you either play your best in that hand or fold it and wait for the next. You don’t go all in just because you’re feeling desperate. Because when times get really desperate, you might not have the money to throw down. I told you I was bad at this analogy thing. IN A HOLE As told by The Spectator “You have no fucking money? Then what have we been living off of for the past few months?” Foster growled at his brother. “MY MONEY. That’s why it’s all gone. But that’s not the only reason why.” Gildenstern took the piece of paper and began to outline the box above the negative total for Ian. “You can see the withdrawals, all made at random cities in Arkansas. Look at the dates. They were when Foster was in the hospital because of the bad chicken Face-Eater gave him.” “Son of a bitch,” said Ian, his eyes going glassy as he thought about the events recently. “Those motherfuckers shouldn’t have gotten the win over us. We were just distracted, and you were still burned, and…” “Ian, pay a-FUCKING-ttention,” Foster said, grabbing his younger brother by the ear. “Someone’s fucking with my finances, and it’s going to negatively impact all of us. I called the bank about a fraud charge, but they have to review it through their system before I get any money back. Which means the checks I wrote last week will all bounce, including…” he trailed off, hoping for Ian to get the message. “Your Hustler subscription?” Ian did not. “No, you mothershit, the rent check. I’ve been paying for your apartment like I said I would, and I’ve made good on my promise. But with my main resources cut off, I can’t cover this, at least until we figure out what’s going on.” Ian shrugged. “Big deal, we’ll take money from our own account and cover it. We were only living off of you because we let you come into PRIME after us.” He waited for both Gil and Foster to realize his point, agree with him, and, maybe, cart him off into the sunset as hero and king. Amazingly, none of those things happened. “There’s only one small problem with that,” said Gildenstern, taking out another sheet of paper. “We won’t really be able to cover it either.” Ian leaned forward and looked at the total and nearly fell off of the chair. $-1742 THE SITUATION As told by Ian Nackedy Well, this really fucking sucks. SEMANTICS As told by The Spectator “That’s not how you’re supposed to fucking do it!” screamed Ian. “What do you mean?” asked a confused Foster, whose anger quickly dissipated in the face of said confusion. “Okay, look. There are two numbers, right? And the higher they are, the worse it is, like golf. If you’re breaking bad news to someone in the form of those numbers, wouldn’t you want to do the big one first to get the bad shock out of the way?” Gildenstern pondered that for a moment. “I don’t know. I kind of thought it was better to do it on an increasing pattern, like a line.” “Or like stairs,” offered Foster. “Yeah, like stairs,” agreed Gildenstern. “No, not like fucking stairs!” shouted Ian, leaving any sort of silly behavior he had behind. “You want to backtrack it; give me the bomb first so I can hit and recover, and then everything else doesn’t look so bad.” Gil shook his head. “I still think you should lead INTO the bomb, and that way…” “GUYS SHUT THE FUCK UP!” yelled Foster, and the tag team hastily obeyed his command. “Semantics are never worth fighting over.” “Not if you’re not an English major,” Ian quipped. Gildenstern nodded. “Exactly.” “Well, you’re not English majors. You’re wrestlers. And two very broke wrestlers at that, living in an apartment they can’t afford because someone is stealing their money. So the important thing to do is decide what you’re going to do and where you’re going to go.” Ian nodded. “Well, I guess there’s no way we can quit PRIME now. Better keep the old box of costumes out. Want to be 80s pop stars this week?” “Sure,” replied Gildenstern. “Still doing gimmicks even now? Incredible.” After a pause, Foster nodded. “As far as needing a place to stay, you can live with me and Magen in Baton Rouge, but it won’t be comfortable.” Ian sighed. “All right, that’s the best we’ll get, I’m sure. Well, let’s go pack up our stuff. I’ll get the replica NWC Tag belts.” He stood up and saw that Foster and Gildenstern have not moved. “What? Another bomb?” Gildenstern’s face turned red. “Well, we’re already in the process, it’s too late to change it.” “Yeah, there’s another thing,” Foster said. “Mr. Abbot called while you were gone at the book signing.” Ian had still not slept at this point, and thought to himself that one more thing to worry about would make him pass out then and there. “Dad’s neighbor? Why?” Foster’s face morphed, and suddenly he looked much older as he sighed for perhaps the fiftieth time that evening. “Because Dad’s house is empty, and he hasn’t been seen in three days.” EDIT: THE SITUATION As told by Ian Nackedy Yep, this really REALLY fucking sucks.
