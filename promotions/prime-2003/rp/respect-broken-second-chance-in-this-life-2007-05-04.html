Respect Broken (Second Chance In This Life)

4 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/4/07 Between the vast hills of Hollywood and the towering skyscrapers of the Financial District is a world that the glamor magazines conveniently forget to tell you about. A place named Skid Row, where most people work minimum wage jobs but can't keep the rent payment coming in. A place littered with cardboard boxes from the homeless, far and away the greatest number in the entire United States. The Nickle. For a good spell, home. I set BloodWorks here because of the contrast in gross income. You can see the law firms sitting there, Bunker Hill haughtily peering from the side. And the people that live here, they have nothing. Truly have nothing. It's one of the toughest places in the world to live. Like a west coast version of Hells Kitchen. Bars on the windows, housing projects to the side. What I was doing was community outreach. I see it that way. If you want into this business, you don't give a damn where you have to go. All you care about is getting your one chance. Because a wrestling promotion contract snares you at least sixty grand a year. You have to take a chunk out of that for travel expenses, but you're still going to keep the lights on and the eviction notice at bay while you're still earning dough. And if you're lucky, you get to live in the Hollywood Hills. You can make seven figures and have your travel paid for. But at the end of the day, no matter how much scratch you make, you still come into this with flush cheeks and high hopes. You're a fucking kid. They say it's hard to stay hungry in this world once you've made it. You become jaded, want to go back to being a normal guy with normal problems like normal people do. A little more cash in your account. I didn't piss away a college degree eating cans of tuna, working shit jobs for shit companies to come out west and get trained to fade off into obscurity. And it's a view point I've never understood. Keep your nose to the grind. Go to a fucking independent show. Shit...open your own place and train. I did. And every day I earn a high end paycheck working one day a week at a job I was put on this Earth to do? It slides into focus. You're either blessed or your not. And in a place like Skid Row, training wrestlers hoping to make something of themselves, that's apparent. I've seen lot of young, bright eyed kids walk through the doors into the training center, only to leave beaten up and broken. It's about respect though. *** “Don't you motherfuckin' even COME AT ME LIKE THAT! You KNOW why I did it. And I ain't payin' a fuckin' thing. Hear me? Feel me? You bring me some punk ass bitch who comes in here with his chest puffed out like he got something to prove. Well he GOT proved Al. Where the fuck you find that lazy ass nigger friend of yours?” Spit was already flying from my mouth, drenching the hate my words spoke. Alastair just stood there, not even able to look me in the eyes. “Brandon...you just called him a nigger. You don't know him. I could've--” I cut him off, seeing his confidence shaken up by his fidgeting legs kicking in their gray sweat pants. “I call 'em like I see 'em Al. And that guy was a no good piece of shit charity case.” Alastair's eyes shoot up, meeting me head on. “Had he not fallen into the bad crowd in school, he'd be in the NFL Brandon! He dropped outta school when we were--” “So that's how you know him...huh? Old running mate? Decided to bring back ties with someone like that? I don't care about circumstances. Ain't no no good corner huggin' nigger going through the place I built Al. I ain't a damn haven for filth like that.” *** But respect is earned, not handed out. It's seventy plus, which makes the Canadian in me want to get a pair of shorts and cut off t-shirt. But hot summers spent in San Antonio Texas, bailing hay... Yeah. It's not so bad. It's Thursday, which on Pay Per View weeks is day before fly out night. I used to drive to the venues, but having seen the country once, I already know what is coming; large open spaces and farm fields. And really, the edge from that wears off when you're needing to travel cross country from LA to St. Louis. With gas at over three a gallon, no fuckin' way I'm doing that drive. At BloodWorks, we work a regimented schedule, which is to teach the kids discipline. The cardio is meant to break them to begin with, to allow the cream to rise to the top. And from there, it's survival of the fittest. We do not have a lot of space in the Skid Row district. The money for the building was out of my own pocket, and I chose this because it actually seemed like it was in the best condition of all the damn fixer-upers in the neighborhood. It was an old corner store, where in the fifties kids would by candy and get milk shakes. By the time the nineties rolled around, bars were on the windows and the most asked for item was cigarettes, which you could smoke in the building at the time. We have tried to get the stench out of the place, even going so far as to rip the hideous green vinyl flooring out, then the nicotine stained white ceiling tiles from up above. No air freshener takes the stink out of years of chain smoking. I should know. That's how the apartment just down the street was. When I sold my desert oasis, I pocketed most of the money and put a lot of stuff in storage lockers in West Hollywood, so that my shit wouldn't be jacked by doped up punks trying to find the next high. It's not safe to own that sort of stuff in the Nickle...no matter how many bars you have on your windows. The place I got was in a refurbished deal from some government housing projects years previous. The thing with getting a place in this neighborhood, in this place, was to be as close to the ground level as I could be. Creaky wood and all. I'd fallen from grace and had the money to retire. But I stayed hungry, waiting for my second chance. And I built something with my own hands. And that is why I have such of level of malcontent for people like Killean Sirrajin, or any number of people in this industry. And rather than associate myself with them, with plush mansions and sprawling family circles...I pretty much have turned wrestling into my life's legacy. Which is why in twenty years, even if I never win the big one in PRIME, I will always be considered to be a legend. Titles do not make you all in all. It's about hunger. And while Killean, a man who is around my same age, seems to be winding down...I seem to be going just as strong as when I got here. It's about chances, opportunities in life. And when you forget your roots, you forget your place in this life. My abilities were my vehicle, but wrestling gave me what I am. I fear no man, because every day I come down here I stare a possible drive by in the face. Respect is earned. Al had just come back from another Japan tour. Being a Los Angeles native, he had a flat in the city that he called home, and whenever his runs stopped, he'd fly back home. He'd cash his check, spend a night out on the town with us, and then he'd be right back to wrestling. Not exactly like he had a choice in the matter. His decision making was questionable at best. Growing up with him in the business, I realized it pretty quick that Al was someone who needed a tight leash around him or else he was going to go wild shooting shit up his nose. I mean, I didn't help. I kicked his coke habit into high gear when it became about showmanship. But trying to be the fallen father figure, we worked it out that he would have something to do with his time. Sobriety is a one hour at a time thing. And for a creature of excess like him, it was something that would need to be fought off with every craving. Al was good to the guys that stayed, showing them nuances in how to really work as a striker. But today, he had a sort of smile on his face. I came in around nine in the morning, dressed in my usual black shirt and track pants ensemble. And everyone was just stretching out. Giving it was a Pay Per View week, and I had already given my booking assignments for the weekend out to a majority of the people days before, it was basically my beginners just doing stuff to be active. We had three. Make it four. Al was sitting in the far corner on a bar stool, right by my office door. He had a tall African American man at his side. I wish I could see his attire, but the guy seemed to be all blinged out, with plenty of chains around his neck and his smile showing some platinum caps. Why Alastair brought him, I didn't know. We usually had a rotating sign about being open to people who desired to join the school. And my May group was in. “Something shiny catch your eye on the way in today?” I joked, slapping Al on the shoulder. The guy was about my height, though he was mostly sinew. Lanky arms with a do rag. “He want in in June? Because we got no open slots right now. Guys just ended their preliminaries and I'm not about to make an exception for a guy who might not even stay around.” Eye contact was key. I didn't look at him as I stated my policy. Didn't want to make him think I was about to cave in and make an exception. But he interjected in anyway, slurring his works through his faux chompers. “Nah way man...dude its like dis man...I'm in man. I got to be in man. Tayral Brown ain't wait on nuttin man. Al says you get decent moneys just grabbin onto dudes and shit. I ain't gay or nothin' but--” Al grabbed his right wrist, giving it a tug. His counterpart just looked down towards him. “Dude man...I ain't got to stop man.” “Shut up for a minute Tyrell.” Al mumbled, then looked me in the eye. “He's a friend of mine Brandon. We go back a bit...like to school and stuff. Tyrell fell into some hard times and dropped out, but he's cool. Has his head on straight and all that.” “Now I do.” Tyrell poignantly put it. “I'm outta the game now, but I got to replace my income you know? Moms gots bills I gots to pay, so Al said this would be a good way to make cash. I played good at the sports...the books wasn't so good to me doh.” He laughed at it, playfully shoving Alastair's shoulder as he did so. All flashed a smirk and giggled to it, but didn't seem overtly amused by it. More like slightly ashamed. I hadn't looked him in the face to formally acknowledge his existence until that moment. But here I was, staring eye to eye with someone who was making an awfully bad first impression. “Wrestling pays well only if you make it dipshit. I don't know what yarn Al's been spinning you...but we had to eat cans of tuna and work full time jobs on top of traveling up and down this coast getting bookings that wouldn't even cover the cost of gas. Judging by your appearance, you're a project, and you won't even be ring ready for a good four months anyway. And I ain't taking you in. I don't give a fuck if you're Jimmy Jack Bollea--” He interrupted, butting in with a sort of practiced bravado. “Who da fuck is Jimmy Jack Bollea man? I'm Tayral Brown. Been haters all my life, you're just another one I got to overcome from the looks of things.” I stared blankly at him for a moment. Al was too busy staring at the hardwood floor to even react. I slumped to my side, just staring at the guy for a moment dumbfounded. “It was a situation where I inserted a name. Now you want to call me a hater...that you got to prove me wrong, fine. Here's your chance.” We had our wrestling ring set up to the left far corner of the place. It was a rickety old thing that stuck out like a bland eye sore. We put it there because the wall had be tapered off. When we remodeled it, we wanted there to be a safe place where a high flyer could try something without fear of hitting his head on the damn ceiling. Now a part of the room had ceiling tiles missing, actually revealing the wiring and air tunnels that piped in AC when it was hot and heat when it was cool. I walked over to it, rolling underneath the bottom rope, and stood in the middle of the ring. Something stuck in my head from earlier. Something about 'the game'. I hadn't noticed it before, but most of the young guys in the room couldn't look at Tyrell, going as far as they could to not make eye contact. Little puzzle pieces in my head started to come together. “I give you one chance at this. Old school sort of test. You last five minutes with me in the ring, you're in Tyrell. You're in. I'll train you. No questions asked. Fuck...I'll do it free of fuckin' charge. And given that I'm PRIME's 5-Star Champ...I'm worth some dough. So put it down like that Tyrell.” Brown seemed to shrug it off, looking at Alastair. “Dude...your homie is wack man.” Al got up from the bar stool and got into his face. “Tyrell...come on man. Trust me...it'll be worth it. He won't hurt you that bad.” Tyrell shot me a dirty look, then focused in on his friend. “You better keep yo end of the deal on this shit man.” *** “Look Brandon...it was a mistake. I know it was a mistake. But come on man--” “NO!” I wasn't going to let him finish. “You saw them people in there? They know a pusher when they see one. Fuck...I KNOW A PUSHER WHEN I SEE ONE! You think I'm dense Al? What the hell deal you make man? Tell him about how much money he could make selling shit behind the curtain up and down the territory? Tell him stories about the good old days? Man...thinking about that reminds me of how convenient it would have been. I wish I'd of had some fucker riding in my car packing dime bags like it was fuckin' candy Al.” My sarcasm was razor sharp, ripping into the man I once thought I knew. “Brandon...I didn't think we were going to sell...” He slipped. My eyes grow wide. “We?” I can see Al's face turn white. “Oh shit...” *** Tyrell Brown had a conspicuous coat on, gray and black fur a whirl wind around the hood. And as he took off his coat, he had all stand in front of him, to obscure my view. Digging into his pockets, he put various different ziploc bags full of God knows into that coat of his. Then the chains. “So you ready for some reverse racism, chump?” Tyrell grins towards me, following me into the ring. The students begin to gather around the ring. Al was still far away, not able to look. “You start a stop watch or something Al, alright?” I knew he didn't have one, nor did I even care if he did. What was going to happen here wasn't going to take five minutes. “I gots this shit. I'm a scrapper dude. And you fake fight.” He throws a solid punch my way, connecting across the side of my face. My response? A not so fake boot connects into his ribs with a kick, causing him to double over. I quickly pick the legs, dropping him to the canvas. Grabbing onto his right ankle, I get a grapevine around his leg, and instantly begin twisting the ankle. “WHAT THE FUCK?! WHAT THE FUCK?!” Tyrell screams, trying to escape as best as he can. But he's not trained. “TAP OUT TYRELL!” Alastair yells from across the room. His behavior was odd today. Usually he was getting up and down, doing various whatever's to get guys loose around him. But he seemed tight. Uncomfortable. I knew that behavior from my years spent living with him. He had something to hide, but he couldn't. Not before me. “He's right Tyrell. Tap out.” I said. “FUCK YOU CRACKER!” A twist and a snap. A blood curdling scream. I don't stop twisting until Al is in the ring, pulling me off. Without a second's hesitation, I snapped Tyrell Brown's ankle, and had a ready made excuse to do so. Tyrell just kept screaming, his ankle mutilated and flopping around. Sometimes I don't know my own strength. “Someone call 911!” Someone yells. And all I do is roll out of the ring, with Alastair following with me as I go to my office. “Dude...DUDE! What the fuck was that?! You just got yourself a law suit!” “Physical provocation. You sure they won't find a gun in that damn coat of his?” Al follows me into my office, and I merely sit at my desk. Nonchalantly, I grab the phone and begin dialing the appropriate number to get an EMT out here. My door quickly slams shut. “WHAT THE HELL WAS THAT BRANDON?!” I looked up at him, I feel like I'm staring down the barrel of a gun from this decision. “Hiro Matsuda broke Hulk Hogan's leg to instill respect. He didn't take the business seriously. Now if he comes back after it gets fixed, I'll train him free of charge and get him the best bookings I can.” That was lip service, but it was an out. “Besides...maybe then...maybe if he isn't slinging dope...maybe--” “How you know that Brandon? Bought from him lately? Need to cover you're own ass.” He transferred his lie onto me. I have a problem whenever I get angry. Political correctness flies out the door, and all I can see usually is red. *** “You were selling with him Al? What the fuck man?” I was shocked. Bewildered. Hurt. I'd taken him from a point where he had nothing and tried to build him up again. I helped get him work in Japan again after the bottom fell out. I was Alastair Nagatsuka's second chance in this life. And I had been shivved in the back by someone I had helped to foster into a brave new world. “Brandon...you didn't have to do what you did to him.” “YOU'RE CHANGING THE SUBJECT ON ME! WHAT I DID WAS JUSTIFIED! He won't be walking out there tonight, selling to kids on the playground--” Al cut me off. “No...he never did--” And I returned the favor. “IT DON'T MATTER IF YOU DID OR IF YOU DIDN'T! It doesn't. It doesn't matter. What matters to me is...how much does it hurt your damn pocket book? How much does it fuck you? How many dollars do you lose for every day he's in a hospital bed?” He started shaking. “Nothing. I got nothing. Brandon...I wasn't selling to kids...I wasn't selling to anyone or storing anything.” I sneered. “Then what the--” “I lose Brandon. He was my fuckin...” He caught himself, and began to sob like a frustrated baby, seemingly out of nowhere. “I'm usin' again man. I'm sorry. I'm so sorry.” “You lied to me.” Al just stood there, looking in the eyes, tears streaming down his face. “I'm not as strong as you...you know? I fucking got weak man. I NEED IT! He came to me with the idea...he knew what we did and I said the only way I could get him in is if he was a wrestler or a manager. So he was gonna be my stateside manager...we'd do business and all that and he'd do his.” “After all I did for you...you couldn't keep your nose clean? It's been over a year man...” “I've been on for five months Brandon. But it was a bad idea. You we're righ--” “Get out. Now.” He tried to speak, but couldn't. I wouldn't let him. “My respect for you? It's broken. And now...you're somebody else's problem.” Al tried to grab on to my shoulders, trying to calm me down again, but I forcefully shoved him away. Pushed against the wall, he tried to close the gap once again. “Brandon...I need help man. Come on man...gimmie some help. I'm fuckin' sorry man. I'm fucking so fucking sorry. I can't do this man...I can't.” His works were racked by his sobs, his once shattered life once held together by super glue once again broken hole, leaving nothing but a beaten down addict who could never hope to reform. I just stared at him coldly for a few seconds. He must have expected a warm greeting, a hug of reassurance. That everything was going to be okay. That everything was going to be fine. But two minutes passed by. And he got nothing. “Fuck you Brandon. You piece of hypocritical shit. FUCK YOU!” My punch connected strong, dropping him to the floor. “I see you again...I'm turning you in to the police.” He coughed blood onto my floor, and I stepped over him and out the door of his life.
