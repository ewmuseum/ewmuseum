The Waiting Game Jonathan Winters leant back in the altogether too comfortable waiting room leather chair and wearily rested his feet upon the delicate French glass table, not much caring whether it was sturdy enough to hold the weight. The elderly receptionist of the Stakiri Corp research department peered over the tiny reading spectacles that lay perched upon the tip of her nose, with a raised eyebrow and a look of deluded superiority. Winters did what he did best and what he’d been doing to all of the accusing stares ever since walking back into Cornelius Stakiri’s employment. He ignored her. Coming back to what Stakiri laughably referred to as ‘work’ was hard, harder than he’d ever thought it would be. He had no idea what his illustrious employer had done when Winters had first walked out upon the wealthy ‘business man’, but as soon as he stepped foot back in the building all eyes seemed to dart his way, some with in anger, but mostly there was fear. It strange, Winters didn’t mingle too often with all of Stakiri’s legitimate staff and often took pains in avoiding the mundane office workers with dreams of promotion and advancement, but they all recognised him nonetheless. Of course, he knew before Stakiri attempted to reintegrate him back into his inner circle for his less than legal affairs that it would be worse and he was right. That said, even before he parted ways Winters fully understood that any one of them would stab him in the back given Stakiri’s say-so, but at least then they would laugh and joke around with him. So Winters had been ostracised by his peers for reasons beyond his knowledge and control, but the thing was he didn’t much care. It was only even an issue with him because he didn’t particular care for the attention it brought him. He yawned and rested the back of his head upon the comforting embrace of the soothing leather cushioning. He’d been up for thirty-six hours straight and red bags surrounding his eyes only served to prove it. Stakiri’s researchers had been reluctant to supply any kind of timeframe for what they were attempting, but none of them had ever expected it’d take this long. “You should go get some sleep,” the familiar voice reassured. Winters opened his eyes to see stood looming over him wearing an expensive black pinstripe business suit and a half-smile. “Looks like you could use it.” Winters nodded, removing his feet from the glass table and sitting upright. “Can’t.” Stakiri developed a smug grin. “You can’t sleep? When did you get yourself a conscience?” “You know it’s more than that, Stakiri,” Winters snapped, his fatigue getting the better of him. Stakiri nodded, the smile fading some. “I’m sorry. Bad taste.” Winters was only even in Stakiri’s employment because he understood that the wealthy businessman was the only one in a position to help him. Two years ago, Winters woke up in a cornfield somewhere with his memories wiped and he’d always know Stakiri knew something about it. Winters also suspected that he and Aeryn were something of an item, but she had always been reluctant to confirm it. Three months ago, when Aeryn came back from a mission of industrial espionage, they had become an item, but that turned out to be a lie. During the mission, Stakiri’s less than legitimate business rival captured Aeryn and ‘programmed’ her to act as a counteragent, or so Stakiri’s best analysts had told him. Now, they were busy taking shifts ‘deprogramming’ her and Winters wanted to be here when she woke up. He wanted it to be his face she saw first and more importantly he had to know. He had to know whether what he and Aeryn had between them was real or just a way for the competition to get an edge. “So,” Stakiri began. Winters rubbed his eyes. He knew precisely what the next question was going to be, after all Stakiri had been asking it every day for the last two weeks. “When are you coming back to work for me? It’s getting pretty crazy out there. We need you.” Winters sighed. “You already know the answer to that question, Stakiri. What makes you think it’s going to be any different today?” A wide grin spread itself across his face. “Why do you think I’m here?” Winters struggled to his feet, his eyes widened. “You mean…?” Stakiri nodded. “They paged me half an hour ago. Jonathan, they’ve finished deprogramming Aeryn.” “Well…?” Winters growled, sick of the suspense. “The procedure was mostly a success,” Stakiri said, patting Winters reassuringly on the shoulder. Winters decided to let it go. “We’ve got the Aeryn back!” He wanted to laugh and cry at the same time, but instead focusing on specifics. “Mostly a success? What does that mean?” Stakiri visibly squirmed on the spot. Something was wrong and obviously Stakiri was having a difficult time explaining it. “What the hell happened?” “From what I was told there were a few…complications during the procedure.” Stakiri paused and sighed. “Let’s just go see her.” Winters nodded and followed Stakiri’s lead. The receptionists distasteful glare followed Winters around the room as they approached the thick security doors, no doubt wondering precisely why Stakiri was letting me of all people access to one of his most secure area’s. Stakiri swiped his access card through the port and then typed in a five digit code upon its stainless steel panel. The lock clunked a release and the heavy set doors groaned open. On the other side of it, the head of the research department was waiting and nodded a brief greeting before thrusting a progress chart into Stakiri’s waiting hands. The researcher nervously lead them along the corridor, Stakiri idly flicking through the colourful pages of the report and Winters desperately attempting to catch a glimpse over his shoulder. The three of them filtered through another doorway and into a medical room, whereupon Stakiri and Winters stopped in their tracks to see the beautiful woman with jet-black hair sat upon the edge of the operating table wearing a simple green medical gown. “Aeryn…” Winters muttered. “How are you?” Stakiri added, edging closer and handing the medical chart back to the researcher. Aeryn’s brow furrowed and glared at the two of them in turn. “Do I know you guys?” Winters heart sank. End
