'It's high time to pray for redemption and pity on your immortal soul, beast' (ReV 111, JiTC Round 1)

10 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/11/06 With a sneer, the hunter raises his hand high, ready to strike the palm down to the stake held right above the heart of the Vampire Lord, Count Sirrajin. The hunter had caused upset when he had fought and beaten Sirrajin in combat before, back when he was still a mortal, but this time it’d be different – how exactly do you stop the living dead? He hadn’t needed to kill him when he was mortal, but with the eternal thirst he now had to quench it looked like Sirrajin would stop at nothing to reach the summit again. Ever since Sirrajin had first got a sniff at the chance to drink from the chalice of immortality he’d abandoned the good life he’d lead before and devoted himself entirely to getting, by any means he saw fit, what he saw as rightfully his – ownership of the font the water flowed from. Only a few men had ever climbed the arduous path to the very top of the mountain the font was held at, but many had fallen from the very final ledge as they struggled to make the final jump from mortal to immortal. Some had been kicked down by the bitterest of rivals, the two climbing the mountain from opposite sides only to meet at the top and battle it out. Some had climbed side-by-side with a partner, swearing every step of the way to share the prize only to fall to vicious in-fighting as the fantasy loomed, ever-closer, as a reality. The hunter had heard tales of men kicking the feet out from under the closest of friends just to take the glory of the prize to themselves. But one thing none of the newly-crowned immortals ever mentioned was the curse that came from drinking of the font – with their name now etched forever in the annals of history the man could now never stop drinking lest he fall from grace and become one of the undead: vampires like Sirrajin had now developed into. All reasoned, logical thought had been wiped from his mind now – leaving him an unthinking beast with a bloodlust to sate. Men had heard of these undead beasts and these was speculation abound as to what caused previously mortal men to be turned into such ravenous, single-minded undead monsters – some even remembered the days when the beast-form of a previous owner of the font had managed to return to drink again from the chalice, becoming a man again for as long as he fended off all comers from the precarious peak. The hunter had his own thoughts on the matter, an inkling that the dethroning could cause the fall from man to undead. He didn’t believe it fully, though, for to have such a firm belief would undermine the awful truth that he too was in pursuit of the font’s ownership for his own selfish needs. He couldn’t admit it to himself; asserting and reasserting that his aim was merely to scourge the terror of the Vampire Lord. So here he waited; at the very base of the mountain, awaiting the inevitable arrival of Count Sirrajin. It was the biggest event of the year; men from the four corners of the globe made the journey to the base of the mountain, the thought being that if enough started the climb simultaneously they might be able to stage a coup and take the mountain top in the name of their kingdom, lordship or religion. Yet from year to year none of them learned; every time only one man would reach the final ledge, pushing aside friends, comrades or enemies to take the last step for the prize and accolades himself. And then there he would stand, face to face with the owner of the font of immortality. Only one would ever remain on the very top, the other resigned to be footnoted as now being a ‘previous owner’ or merely a failure at the moment of truth. There was no way the Count could stay away; with so many competitors vying for the title of ‘Owner Of The Font’ that he saw so rightfully as his own, jealousy and hatred would built up in his gut, his black heart and whatever remained of his mortal soul. It would eat at him from inside out and force him to return in an attempt to show his previous dominance was not a one-off. Giving up on his initial plan, consumed by the secret inner desire for the promised glory, the hunter had started his climb; telling himself he would meet and eliminate the Vampire Lord at some stage on the way up, even if it was the very last foothold before the mountain top was taken. By nothing but pure chance the hunter had picked the ledge that the Count chose to start from; their eyes locking as Sirrajin pulled himself up to begin the perilous climb. Immediately the beast’s teeth were bared, the hunter’s hand at his belt and the circling begun. The battle raged, the hunter nearly having his throat ripped out on more than one occasion, and the stake missing the Count’s chest by the luck of what could only be the powers of darkness under his command. But with a mind focussed on more than just the final prize, the hunter was able to trick the Count and take him tumbling to the ground, pinning him down with the stake held firm, hand raised high and ready to drive it through the beast’s chest. And so we return to where we began; the hunter poised and ready to make the kill. If he could find the shrivelled, blackened excuse for a heart that remained in the Vampire Lord’s chest with his aim true, he would triumph and then move on to the rest of the climb. He would deal with having deceived himself over his true intentions later, only once he’d truly started on the climb to immortality. But there was still the risk, with time frozen as it stands, that the sheer aching need for the return to the top in the Count would yet allow him to wrest the stake from the hunter and slay him where he stood. And there, as sheer greed meets sheer greed and where there is no difference between the mortal and the undead in their epic battle to take the first step to eternal life and notoriety, the tale ends – leaving the question lingering… If man triumphed over beast, would we even know?
