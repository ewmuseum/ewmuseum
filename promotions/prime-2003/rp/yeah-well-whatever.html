Yeah, Well, Whatever

Date: eh It looked like something blew up in there. We were stepping over deadweight after deadweight, grabbing what was unfinished and finishing it ourselves, leaned up against a dresser or a counter or some type of furniture that was convenient to us. "Drinks finished them, huh?" Lucius was all cool about it, like to him it didn't matter much whose they were. It wasn't my money that bought 'em, and I would've been wild about it if I hadn't caught -- out of the corner of my eye -- this commercial playing for some magic box you could sit in, and everything would be ok. You could literally go anywhere. "So which one of these boys is Deadweight?" "Wish I had me one of those." "Johnny, you listening, man?" Lucius shrugged up, pointing his hands out both ways with fingers apart. "They're all deadweight, man." "But which one of these deadweights is Deadweight," Lucius continued. "You said this was where he stay? One of them on the bed, right?" "One of them," I confirmed. There he was alright. Couldn't punch his way out of a paper bag awake, but I can't recall seeing a meaner set of knucks on an unconscious man before. "Don't get too close, now. He'll think we're fucking with him." Above his bed, the wall tilted at an angle, and there was a skylight big enough to get out of if you could open it that far. It was just a matter of breaking off some kind of safety contraption. What good's a roof, though if you can't sit on it and look up? Then it's just a ceiling. "Shit!" Lucius jumped back as Deadweight whiffed on a haymaker aimed nowhere particular. "Ain't nothing going to happen, just stuff that happened," I consoled. "Give me that pipe there." He wasn't shaken, or nothing. He just handed me the lead pipe I had propped there against the wall. I used said lead to smash at the device, some crank that operated some other metallic prop up or down. Deadweight made some nonsense, and I allowed sufficient time for the ruminations to settle to remove the covering, and I put the lead pipe on the roof only to hear it roll down and clank seconds later on the ground. "Shit," I planted both arms and pulled myself up, and rose like a mole's ghost. I pulled myself through and crawled onto the roof, swiveling pointed around my belly so that my face poked in, puncturing like I was pushing it through a painting with eyeholes cut trying to get a better view but tripping forward pressed up against into it stumbling over unsturdy footwork. "Come on up, Lu." Lucius Shazam cautiously made his way up onto the mattress with his too expensive boots, and I reached a hand down to help pull him up as he tried to traverse his way towards the opening without disturbing the slumbering juggernaut of a beanpole sleepmoving with all that wasn't yet paralyzed. We got ourselves situated on the tilt, and sat up looking at the sky, about how it was all dark and starless. Lucius reached into his pocket and procured the necessary supplies to roll a blunt, doing so accordingly, and I just looked up into the clear dark and couldn't tell which dark was closer or farther, just that there was more dark behind it. "I don't know if I can keep doing this, Lucius." I sighed. "Hmm?" He managed out as he licked across the wrap. "Doing what?" "I don't know," I scratched my head. "Anything, really." "Here," he said, pinching the blunt out to me. "Smoke this." I followed instruction, flicked my lighter unsuccessfully a few times, readjusted for wind direction and inhaled. It wasn't a magic box anymore. Not even a magic box was a magic box after a while. It used to be that it'd make it all go away for a while, but that part of it went away a while back. I passed it back and took out a cigarette to replace it, just to have something to hold when I wasn't holding something. "What do you mean by 'anything,' though?" Lucius inhaled and exhaled. "I don't even know what it is you do." "I'm not really sure myself," I reasoned, and then Lucius traded the blunt for the cigarette, and we each took drags. "I know I do something, though, and the point is that I do it, and I'm sick of doing it." "You ain't got menthols?" "No." By this point, I was bad at answering rhetorical questions, but I must've been good at posing rhetorical answers because neither of us said anything for a few exhanges. "So what are you going to do?" He quizzed, and I thought short and hard about it. "Nothing, I suppose," I supposed. "Not much I can do, is there?" "Don't you already do nothing?" "I thought we went over this. I do something, and by nothing, I mean I'm going to keep doing something half-assed until I'm doing it with no ass at all." "That's it, huh?" "Yeah, I guess so." "Why waste the time, though? Why not just stop now?" It was a good question. I had been doing the same thing worse and worse, noticeably declining. I had an answer, but it wasn't a particularly good one. In fact, it didn't answer either of his questions. "I got lucky." I stood up and walked towards the edge of the slanted roof as the pieces chipped beneath my shoes. I used to know what those pieces were called, at one point, but then when things started to degrade, it became unimportant. You can't get high on a roof in some ways. In most ways you can, but in this case, it was impossible, and in most ways, you didn't need to know what the pieces of roof were called. Lucius stood up and followed behind me as I looked around, over the edge. "What are you doing, man?" Lucius motioned with his arm for me to return to the relative safety of a more medial area of the rooftop. I didn't listen, or anything. I just kept on looking. "Fuck it," quit Lucius, with a damnable ease. "You do whatever you're going to do. I ain't going to stop you." "There it is." Lucius was already halfway lowered through the hole, but he pushed himself back out slightly to watch as I turned back towards him. "What you mean?" "Let's go down there and get that pipe," I surmised calmly. "I think I'm going to need it."
