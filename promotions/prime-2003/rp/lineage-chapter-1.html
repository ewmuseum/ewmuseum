Date: Multiple Troy Douglas wasn't going to be the one getting surprised this time around. He'd barely slept a wink anyway, so making sure he was up well before his future brother-in-law - who was currently camped out on a cot and looked no less ready to wake from his slumber than a hibernating bear - wasn't going to be an issue. Troy wasn't exactly paranoid, but whenever someone sneaks into your hotel room without a sound while you're in the shower, playing things safe is never a rash decision. So as Tommy Wade slept, Troy Douglas waited, catching up on e-mails and the latest news as he sat cross-legged on his bed, working his way through the last dregs of a cup of lukewarm coffee. Troy definitely hadn't been getting enough sleep as of late. Poring through the New York Times web site and trying furiously to clear his head, Troy forced himself to become oblivious to the goings-on of early-morning Saitama 20 stories below. It was an old mental trick of his, one he'd forced himself to learn five years ago during his extended sabbatical, and it had served him well on the all too frequent occasions when he became overwhelmed by the burden of his own thoughts. It had the regrettable side effect of making him almost completely oblivious to everything he wasn't staring at directly, including his fiance's erstwhile brother across the room. "Morning, roomie." Troy Douglas didn't want to be surprised again. He was having a lousy month. Avoiding slamming his right hand through his keyboard and splattering his coffee into an impressionist painting on his sheets, Troy perked his head up, shaking it back and forth in disbelief. The Wade family had an infuriating possibility of sneaking up on Troy at the strangest of moments. 7 a.m. in a Japanese hotel room was no exception. "You know, I should kick your ass," Troy stated matter-of-factly. Finally clean-shaven after his late-night arrival, Tommy smiled, utterly nonplussed. "You should," he said. "But, you won't." Well, Troy thought, it was a horribly idle threat to begin with. Still shaking his head, Troy closed and stowed his laptop, leaving nothing to provide a distraction. Like it or not, Tommy Wade was about to tell the truth, no matter how long it took. "Alright, Tommy. You promised me an explanation, and now you're going to give me one. Spill." Tommy rubbed his eyes, shaking away the last vestiges of the first solid night's sleep he'd had since before he'd spent the majority of his time running from angry loan sharks with loaded guns. He'd been running long enough, it was time for full disclosure. With just one caveat. "I'll tell you everything. Just need one thing first." "What's that?" "Gonna need a cup of that coffee." Troy smiled. That he could handle. And after that, story time. ***** No matter the time, Gate B at JFK International Airport is always crowded. The crushing mass of humanity constantly coming in and out of the City that Never Sleeps ensures that and the crowd, amongst a litany of other things, made Tommy Wade vastly uncomfortable. The little crushed velvet box stuffed hastily between socks and underwear into a duffel bag on the conveyor belt was one of the others. He hadn't planned to steal the rings when he saw them in Troy's sock drawer that morning. Tommy Wade had many flaws and vices - gambling, Scotch, cigarettes, women, Scotch - but petty larceny had never been one of them until just a few hours ago, when he received the text message. He didn't know who it was from, probably never would, but he wouldn't be able to forget those five screaming words. Five words that left him here, right behind his sister, a half-dozen people away from passing through a security checkpoint at JFK. THEY KNOW WHERE YOU ARE. He wasn't sure why, but after seeing that and suppressing the urge to fling himself out of Sam and Troy's seventh story fire escape, he was almost unnaturally compelled to reach into the sock drawer and procure the rings. He certainly didn't wish his sister or her fiance any harm, he just figured that, if he was going to get trapped, he was going to get trapped with something that could get him the hell out of Dodge. Of course, at that point, he still thought he was getting on a plane back to Boston. Tommy didn't know it yet, but that couldn't be further from the truth. Nervous to the point of nearly soaking his hooded sweatshirt through with sweat, Tommy shook wildly as he passed through the metal detector, hoping like hell that he wasn't being painfully obvious in his furtive looks towards the scanner and his duffel bag. The search took what seemed like seven eternities, but finally he was waved through and hurriedly snatched up his shoes and bag as he waited for his twin to pass through security. As Sam slipped her shoes back on, Tommy studied his ticket, flicking his head to and fro as he found his gate. Then came the tap on his shoulder. For a half-second, he froze, expecting to find a bullet in the back of his head or a blade in his back a moment later. In hindsight, the fact that the hand poking him was clearly reaching several inches up should have been a dead giveaway. "Jesus, Sam! Go ahead and scare the crap out of me, why don't ya?" Samantha colored deeply, the same way Tommy knew he did whenever gravely embarrassed. Sure, they might be fraternal twins, but there were still more than a few identical things about them. "Sorry," she said. "I need to go freshen up, you can find the gate?" "I'm running away, Sam. I'm not an idiot." "That remains to be seen. Just ... don't disappear on me this time, okay?" "You got it, sis." Sam veered left towards the rest room, Tommy forked right towards the waiting area at Gate B11 for US Airways Flight 422 to Logan Airport. A quick puddle jump and he'd be on his way to confronting the forces that had brought him to this moment, desperate, confused and half-crazed. Then he saw something, just for a moment, that threatened to take away that "half". It wasn't much. Just the sweeping of black leather against worn carpet, a brief glimpse of the steel toe of a boot, the smallest hint of the cuff of a a pair of black jeans, but it was enough to unsettle him. It wasn't the kind of outfit one would normally choose to fly in, and Tommy only knew one man who always wore those boots, those jeans, that same leather duster. Instinctively, he tucked his duffel bag closer to his body, until the vision disappeared into the crush of people swarming the gate. Heart still beating like a hummingbird's, Tommy tried to calm himself as he waited for his sister. It was, he supposed, just the manifestation of his own paranoia, the product of two weeks lived in a constant state of panic. Nothing to worry about. His breathing calmed, Tommy picked up a stray USA Today and tried to work his way through the sports section, until he felt a familiar vibration in his right jeans pocket. A text message, he assumed one from Troy or his sister. Then he looked, grabbed his bag and peeled himself out of the chair, striding back towards the entrance to the gate until he blended into the crowd enough to break into a dead sprint back to the dank December day outside. He hailed a cab without a word, giving the driver one simple instruction. "Go." Still palpitating, Tommy dug back into his pocket and grabbed his Blackberry once again, staring at this newest missive. It wasn't five words this time, just one. RUN ***** "That's one hell of a tale," Troy said, still nodding his head blankly. Tommy sipped his coffee, smiling wryly. "You'd be amazed, Troy." "Good. That's it for today, but I'm not letting you out of my sight until I've got the whole story." "Then where to, Captain?" Troy grinned cryptically. "Shanghai." "Goodie."
