M & W: You Don't Know Me At All

Date: April 2.1 the mirror: a little nuts It hasn’t been very long, but you think you’re tired of this whole life coach thing. At first the things that Aaron was putting you through were a little strange, but made sense on a fundamental level. He made you watch old videos of SCCW and FUSE TV to show you how your priorities weren’t exactly in order. He made you call your parents and interview them about how difficult it was to raise you; this was a little awkward because you hadn’t talked to them in long while and were truthfully sort of avoiding them. He explained to you, though, that hearing their voice and seeing that old part of you might help you get back on track. It hadn’t really worked, but at least you understood where he was coming from. It was about the time he told you to dress up like Aimz and Desade, though, that you started to think this guy was a little nuts. He told you that getting in the character of two of the most harmful people in your professional career would help you get a perspective of what it was like being on the other side, the side that isn’t obstructed by morals and ideals. You tried to be nice at first, telling him that you could probably get that perspective without wearing fishnets or women’s business suits. When he persisted, you firmed your stance by putting him in a wristlock. He backed down, but the damage was already done. How were you supposed to get your life back on track with a guy who thought that progress was dressing up as a woman? You had pretty much put your life in his hands, because you didn’t know what to do yourself. And you still don’t really know, but you do know that this isn’t it. All of the reservations that you had in the first place came rushing back, and you realized that no one could really help you until you helped yourself. But Aaron convinced you to go through one last night, one last trial, to see if you could right yourself, and you reluctantly agreed. But you’re starting to have second thoughts. Each task that you’ve been through has been explained, even though some of the explanations have been a little odd. So you’re wondering, at this moment, how Aaron is going to explain the benefits of going into one of New Orleans’ sketchiest bars in the middle of a Tuesday night. ---- 2.2 the window: entering “The Three-Legged Dog, Aaron? Really?” Jonathan eyes the rickety-looking corner bar with a bit of reservation as Aaron “Dith” Timble smiles at him. “What, you don’t like this bar?” “I’ve never been to this bar, but I think from appearances alone that’s sort of understandable.” Jonathan looks at the door creaking on its hinges with disgust. After a second, a rumbling of guitar emanates from the bar. Jonathan looks up in surprise as Aaron smiles. The song is “Piece by Piece” by Strata, which is significant because… “My theme music.” Aaron’s smile widens. “See? You can’t pass that sort of thing up.” Jonathan’s eyes narrow slightly. “Did you queue this up before I got here?” “Maybe. Come on, doesn’t it make entering the bar that much more fun? Pretend that you’re entering the arena. It’ll help your confidence, I promise.” Aaron says this not with the tone of a friend but with the tone of a director, a voice he had been using a lot more with Jonathan lately. The New Life had started to get a little aggravated by it, but this is a suggestion he can get behind. Rolling his neck slowly, Jonathan Rhine hops from foot to foot. The scream hits suddenly in the song, and without warning he pushes open the doors with overdramatic vehemence and enters the bar, his mind in a place where thousands of fans are waiting to hit their feet at the sight of him. The reality of the situation couldn’t be more different. There are maybe six people in the bar, all appearing as if they haven’t moved for years. No one spares a look at the two young men entering the bar, except for a dark-skinned man who looks anywhere between 21 and 45. He motions for Jonathan to follow him. “Excuse me?” Jonathan asks. He isn’t answered. The man points at a chair in front of The New Life, and he looks at Aaron hopelessly. The Deer in the Headlights shrugs and goes to the bar to get a drink. “Thanks,” Jonathan says as he sits down in the chair he has been offered. The dark-skinned man snaps his fingers and a short, blonde woman in pig-tails approaches the chair timidly. After a few hand gestures, the girl turns around and bends over in front of Jonathan, causing him to widen his eyes in shock. But the shock doesn’t end there. The man grabs the girl’s skirt as she is leaning in front of Rhine and, with the flourish of a matador, pulls her skirt up, revealing her bare ass to Jonathan. Covering his quickly reddening face, Jonathan offers a meek thumbs up to the matador, stands up, and moves to the other side of the bar, where Aaron is standing with an amused look on his face. Jonathan grabs the beer Aaron is holding, takes a long pull, and shoots his life coach a stare that is usually only reserved for the ring. “Are you fucking kidding me?” ---- 2.3 the window: keep swinging You’re done with this guy. There’s no way around it. He’s put you through myriad embarrassments and humiliations, all under the guise of helping you get your life back together. But this isn’t helping you pick up the pieces; it’s creating new fragments, forcing you to slip deeper into this black hole of depression you’ve created for yourself. You convince yourself that this has all been a trick; maybe Aaron was still mad for the way he was disrespected in wrestling academy. Maybe he wanted to show you what it was like to be messed with and looked down upon. Maybe he was just an asshole. Whatever the case, you are no longer playing these games, clearing these hoops, humoring this Deer. It’s time to realize what you have been ignoring all this time; you’re not going to get better by listening to someone else, following their tactics, adapting to their way of thinking. You’ll get out of your slump the same way a baseball player gets out of a hitting slump. You’ll keep hitting. Keep swinging. Never change your routine. Because your biggest problem in this downward spiral is that you’ve thought yourself into a corner. You had convinced yourself that you were hopeless, you needed help, and you would look anywhere to get it. And that got you dressing in women’s clothing and having random women expose themselves to you in bars. You realize it's because Aaron Timble doesn't know you at all, and he probably never will. Maybe it’s time to remember that you are Jonathan Rhine, you were on top of the world as recent as a year ago, and if anyone is going to get you out of your current funk, it’s you. ---- 2.4 the window: into the ground “This isn’t working, Dith.” It’s the first time since the academy days where Jonathan has called his life coach by his unflattering nickname, and the pain this causes Aaron is evident. But Jonathan is past caring. “What are you talking about?” “This whole life coach thing. All you’re doing is putting me in uncomfortable situations, and I don’t even see the point in all of them.” “The point is to make you stronger, more confident…” “That’s not what I need, Aaron. I don’t need strength, confidence…I’m a wrestler, these things come with the territory. What I need is direction, and I don’t think you’re any better at providing it than I am.” “Why would you say that?” Jonathan’s eyes pop wide in disbelief. “Why would I…why would I say that? I say that because we just ran into Ass-shower and the Mute Pimp! Listen, Aaron. You’re a good guy. And I’m sure you think you’re helping me somehow. But this…this isn’t working. You’re not coaching my life, you’re running it into the ground. And I’m not letting you any longer.” Aaron calls out, but Jonathan is already standing up and finishing his beer. He tosses it in the trashcan and makes a step for the door when he sees a woman in the doorway of the bar who makes him stop. Her blonde hair is hanging loosely, most of it trapped in a bun but some of the strands fighting to get out. Her glasses are perched low on her nose, as if they aree more for appearance than for sight correction. Her business suit has been slightly untucked, unironed, and unbuttoned to suggest casual formality. And her face is red, yet pointed upward in confidence. And her eyes? Her eyes are staring directly into Jonathan’s. The New Life stops and has to catch his breath, as he knows this woman very well. Her name is Sarah Riley, and she is the one who started it all. ---- 2.5 the mirror: sarah riley You haven’t seen her in months. She isn’t a part of PRIME. Most people who see you now wouldn’t see the significance of this. But you’ll never forget what she did to you. It was two-fold, double-edged, sugar and spice. The spice was that she joined with Desade in SCCW to make your life a living hell. By seducing and charming you, she tried – and succeeded – to break up your relationship with Katie. She was the first person to make you question your morals and wonder if you really were as good a guy as the fans thought you were. The sugar is that you enjoyed being seduced. Even as you stared across the battle lines at her, you felt the urge to be with her. To talk to her. Because she knew more about you than Aaron Timble ever will. But as you look at her, thinking of what question to ask her, whether to start with the obvious “what are you doing here” or the plea to help you figure out this mess you’re in, you turn around and look at Aaron. And he’s smiling. And you realize that maybe Aaron knows a little bit about you after all.
