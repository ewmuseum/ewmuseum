Deja Vu - Prologue

19 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 19, 2010 The last time PRIME fans had seen Bryan Dawkins in action, January was ending and anyone who knew anything about the Flyin’ Hawaiian knew that something wasn’t right. Something was just off, and anyone who knew a brief history on the young Hawaiian could have probably guessed what it was. Five months before, Dawkins had suffered one of most brutal head and neck injuries seen on PRIME programming in the company’s history at the hands of Devin Shakur’s outlawed Tombstone Piledriver. Following the move, Dawkins laid on the mat motionless for nearly a half hour before being maneuvered onto a stretcher and taken to the nearest hospital. Dawkins’ career nearly ended that day in Boston, and he took the next three months off. Dawkins returned in November to enter the Jewel in the Crown tournament, where he made it to the second round where he would eventually lose in the second round to close friend Tyler Rayne. Unfortunately, coming back so soon would come back to hurt him. * * * “Ah, just another day in rehab, bruh…” It had taken almost three months, but Bryan Dawkins was finally beginning to feel like everything was going back to normal. The twinges of pain were subsiding, the stiff neck wasn’t so stiff anymore, and the headaches were all but a memory. He sat on the examination table attached to more sensors and monitors than a heart attack patient, but was all smiles, as was usually the case with PRIME’s Resident Hawaiian. His pearly whites were glaring back at the cute therapist’s assistant attaching the various sensors and monitors to his well-toned body. “So, how are we feeling today, Mr. Dawkins?” He smiled at the girl, whose young pretty face indicated she could be no older than the legal drinking age. “It’s Bryan. And just fine. Could be worse, that’s for sure.” Again, nothing but smiles from the Hawaiian, this time receiving a smile in return from the therapist’s assistant. “Good to hear. You know the drill; gotta keep an eye on your brain and what the rest of your body’s doing. We need to see how you’re reacting to the treatments.” “Sounds good to me. Keep hookin’ me up, sweetie.” Should all go well, Dawkins had planned on being in therapy for only a few more days. His strength was returning, and he was eager to get back into the ring, and more importantly, back to work. “So what’s the doc think? I’m gonna be outta here soon, right?” She smiled at him, staying silent but still going about her business. “Ah, I see…holdin’ out on me so that doc can give me the good news, right? I understand, you don’t wanna take all of the credit.” After winking, Dawkins subtly flinched as a quick shot of pain burst up his spine. With a quick gasp, he continued. “We’ll just keep it all our little secret.” She looked at him, obviously aware of the small dose of pain Dawkins just exhibited. “Which part?” She winked, giving him the nod that she had indeed seen his flinch. “Both?” Dawkins laughed and felt the little bit of doubt start to creep in. Maybe he wasn’t going to get to go back to work as soon as he’d thought. Maybe the injury is a bit more serious than he or anyone else had originally thought. Maybe everyone was right; maybe he shouldn’t have come back so soon. Little by little, the various seeds of doubt were planted in Dawkins’ mind. “Yeah, we’ll see. The therapist, not doctor, will be in to see you in a minute.” After attaching the last sensor, the assistant left Dawkins half-naked in the exam table, sensors scattered randomly but precisely around his body. Within minutes, a man entered the room, carrying a clipboard and charts. Wearing a polo and dark khakis, the man sat down on a stool next to Dawkins and began to record his findings from the monitors and sensors down onto the clipboard. “Nice to see you too, bruh.” Unfazed, the therapist continued jotting down notes and comments at his leisure. “So uh…what’s up, bruh?” Finally, after a few moments, the therapist sat the clipboard and pen down on his lap and peered at the Flyin’ Hawaiian, who simply gazed back. “Well Bryan, we’re definitely making progress. Yeah, it’s taken a while, and it probably could’ve been prevented had you waited to resume activity after the first incident, but we’re getting closer to where we want you to be. I see here that you’re having no more headaches; is this correct?” Dawkins nodded his head. “Nothing concussion related, bruh.” Dawkins smiled, but his joke was unappreciated. “And you’re still getting the occasional jolt of pain in the neck?” Dawkins nodded again, this time silently. “Feel free to elaborate on that one, Mr. Dawkins.” “It’s Bryan. And yeah, every once in a while I’ll get that quick shot of pain up my spine and into my head. Seems to happen when I start to get a little more mobile and push my limits a little bit.” “Well then I think it might be in our best interest to hold off on the accelerated therapy sessions. At least for a couple weeks or so until we can see if we can get those neck pains to go away.” Dawkins sighed. “Don’t get too down on yourself, Bryan. This is all for your safety and well being. What good are you going to be if you’ve got to work through pain for the rest of your life? Yes, I realize that pain is a major part of your job, but I think it’s a little ridiculous to put your well being and performance on the line just to get back a couple weeks earlier. You realize that you’ll probably just end up back here anyway, right?” Nothing but silence from Dawkins. “I know that you know that this is the right thing to do. It’s not what you want to do, but we both know it’s the right thing. Now let’s get you off of a couple of these monitors and get you doing some work.” As the therapist began to unhook various cables and plug different ones in so that Bryan could become mobile, Bryan’s phone vibrated once, alerting him of a text. Opening his phone, he quickly scanned the screen: From: Mom Found dad having trouble breathing in bed. Going to the ER. Please come. Not a split-second went by before Dawkins began tearing the monitors and sensors off of his body while throwing his t-shirt on and bursting out of the room. Dawkins hastily made his way out of the building where he jumped into his car and quickly sped away, repeatedly muttering… “…not again. Not again. NOT AGAIN.”
