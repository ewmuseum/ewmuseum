Date: 8/3/08 "Sometimes the questions are complicated and the answers are simple." -Dr. Seuss August 4th, 2008 8:16 A.M. "What are you going to do?" The question of the hour, without a doubt. Was I gonna beat Devin Shakur, becoming another agent of the Colossus Curse? Was I gonna ever earn back Bryan Dawkins’s trust? Was I gonna act on the information that my father had gone missing? But Dr. DiPendo hadn’t been that specific. He had blurted out a simple question. The way his fingers tickled the pen on the table suggested that he was asking out of festering curiosity, rather than genuine concern. Maybe he didn’t even know all that all the shit I was supposed to be concerned about. "Do?" My voice carried insincere surprise. "What am I gonna do about what?" "Everything you’ve been working for is within your grasp," he ignorantly beamed. "Not only are you close enough to touch the Universal Title, but you seem genuinely happy for the first time since you sought me out. What’s the next move?" We were on neutral turf in the coffee shop, neither the forced faux-comfort of DiPendo’s office nor the security of my lair. We weren’t on normal doctor-patient terms anymore, something we both recognized. I’d entered the uncomfortable limbo between client and friend. And the reason for my "genuine happiness? Three crushed up Tylox pills in the glass of water I’d slugged seconds before stepping out of my apartment. "I’m trying to figure some stuff out before Colossus." "Like what?" DiPendo seemed content in our limbo; his questions stampeded into my privacy, but I couldn’t be mad at the guy. Especially not while chemical apathy paddled lazily through my bloodstream. "Well," I started, fighting through a lethally concerned look from the Doc, "my deadbeat fuckup father’s managed to sneak back into my life by up and vanishing. The government’s ten ways up my ass about it. They even came to my esteemed place of business to bother me." Since the visit with Agents Johnsen and Gibbons, several law enforcement agents, including Boston PD and the FBI, had made house-calls, just for little ol’ me. Apparently, whatever interagency task force was responsible for Calvino’s whereabouts had become quite stingy with information, even for its sister agencies. "It didn’t make TV." "It wouldn’t. The agents who questioned me seemed like the type of guys you hire when you need a big-time story to stay off the front page." Even with traffic jam in my mind, Gibbons and Johnsen stayed particularly fresh. I knew if the mob found my father before I did, they’d have him doing his best Luca Brasi impression. But if the two Agents found him? I wondered. "Well, I know it’s painful, but I’m glad to see you’re letting bygones be bygones." "I am?" "Yes, Chandler," he replied, with a schoolteacher’s soft, condescending voice. "In the past, you’ve let your father dictate your life from afar. By ignoring his most recent flare-up, you’re taking control of your own destiny. You’re your own agent of change." DiPendo’s lips curled in a small, neatly-kept smile. He was so damn proud of my monumental change that I barely had the heart to tell him it didn’t exist. Barely. "Well, I’ve actually thrown some pocket change over to my lawyer, Gil, to hire a private investigator." While I took a long sip of iced frappuccino, DiPendo waited for me, his narrow shoulders bunching up nervously. "Doc, it’s nothing." "Oh, Chandler," he replied, voice fading off with as the crush of lament and disappointment hit. DiPendo withdrew the glasses from his face. "This isn’t what you want. There’s so much ahead, why are you stuck looking behind you?" He scrunched up his bare eyes and shook his head slowly. The Tylox stopped my attempts to feel bad. I figured that, soon enough, I’d be able to regret showing this particular hand to DiPendo. That sad, diminished glow in his eyes. But now, a symphony of questions zipped through my head, the most pressing of which was DiPendo’s: why are you stuck looking behind you? And whether it was nonchalance brought on by Tylox, or actual human connection, I don’t recall. But I figured it was time to come clean about the other winds in my life the perfect storm. "Doc, can you keep a secret or two?" I had to tell someone. "Doctor-patient confidentiality dictates that–" "I need you in human mode, here, Doc. Not whether you’re legally mandated to, but whether you want to bear something that I can’t alone." He’d retreated from full-on scolding me with his eyes, and he slipped his glasses back on. "Are you sure I’m the ideal confidant? Isn’t there someone–" "No, it’s you." Rayne had pledged himself to indifference. Dawkins was behind enemy lines. Hadn’t been with a woman, outside of one-night antics, since before Culture Shock. Not a soul else to tell. "Alright, then." He relayed the words slowly, thinking as he went. "You can trust me." "Ever since Culture Shock, my back hasn’t been right. I told you that it kept me up, howling like a bitch in heat." Where DiPendo usually would’ve cringed and apologized to the couple behind us, he stayed completely silent. Dude was ready to listen. "And I wasn’t going to do it for long, just until I could schedule a surgery." It sounded pathetic to justify the orange bottle that followed me around, like someone making excuses. "It’s just that surgery and the path I was following…ended up mutually exclusive. And, ya know, the whole redemption bit, what I’m looking for. I knew I needed to put myself in the record books if I ever wanted to be happy. But it’s at a cost. And, don’t get me wrong, I’ll keep paying it. But I’m man enough to admit that I’m not in control anymore." The Doc nodded pensively. Behind those high-arched temples, a machine buzzed with activity. Well, I knew I didn’t want to go to rehab. Not unless you’re allowed shiny gold guests. Other than that, I wasn’t sure. Just telling someone, even clumsily confessing it with the vaguest of words, felt...different. It wasn’t the relief I’d expected, but my burden felt somewhat lifted, as though my fight to crawl out from underneath my addiction was half his and half mine. "Chan–" "Ma, you’re sick. Whatever he told you, it’s a lie. He’s taking advantage of you." "Chan," she chided, looking me square in the eye, "Sick, not helpless. I let him in to say goodbye." I turned in my seat, looking up at my father with the same judgmental eyes I reserved only for him. "Goodbye?’ "That’s right," he mumbled. "Didn’t Sal tell you we’d meet up again?" What I’d mistaken for Christian mumbo jumbo had been an announcement of this family reunion. "So he did," I returned, coldly. "And now you’re off to become an international fugitive? Congrats, you’re slimy to the nth degree." "No, son. I’m being relocated by the government. After all the information I shared, they have to hide me. Even from you. So, like your mother said, I’m here to say goodbye." "But…" I struggled to see how all the pieces came together. "The interagency task force. Why are they looking for you if you really did turn?" "The feds couldn’t find their asses with their hands. The guys I snitched to, they’re so top-secret that ninety-nine percent of the government doesn’t even know they have me. Not the end I’d expected, after all this." He pointed haphazardly, as if I could pluck his experiences out of the air. "The food is for you," my mother said, calmly. "Chan, I’ll be upstairs when you’re done." As my mother walked away from Ettore Calvino, for the last time, she didn’t show the slightest emotion. And all I could think about was checking off "lethargy" as a new symptom, a hallmark of the first year’s effects. We were alone now. "Where are they taking you?" I asked out of duty more than desire. "Can’t tell you," he frowned. "Funny, how I came to tell you everything I never could, but I can’t even tell you that." "Funny like a corpse." Just like always, his apologies were hollow, the stuff of someone who didn’t understand empathy. "Why’d you do it?" "Sal told you. We were outgunned and–" "Outgunned? You’re a mobster in a mobster’s city, that shouldn’t have stopped you. A sudden epiphany that crime doesn’t pay, maybe?" "Something like that," he shrugged. "Just ready not to be the bad guy anymore." "So you became a canary?’ I couldn’t accept that this decision was selfless. He’d saved dozens of his own people, and now was getting off without a slap on the wrist. The feds would write him up as some confidential informant, but his written testimony would start the indictments rolling. Agents Johnsen and Gibbons would eventually find nothing but dust of Ettore Calvino, and the investigation would stop. I would move on to Colossus V and win the Universal Title. The world would keep spinning without Ettore Calvino. "I don’t know what to say, Chandler, but if anything, I’d like to have a decent last memory with you. Agents are waiting outside when I’m ready to go." He didn’t deserve it. All my life, he’d been an obstacle between me and happiness. And now he wanted a stay of execution. I stared at the adjacent seat, the one that had always been empty. "Why don’t you…sit down and eat? I don’t wanna talk, but we can eat." He nodded politely, pulling out his chair. I’d expected a full, beaming grin, but it never showed up. I watched him shovel bi bim bop onto his plate, and it was strange to find my mind wandering elsewhere. Past the old questions, onto the new answers.
