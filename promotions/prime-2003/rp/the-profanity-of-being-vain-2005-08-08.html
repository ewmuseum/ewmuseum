The Profanity of Being Vain

8 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/8/05 scene: The ocean below him was a sickly gree, and the foam that roiled from it was brown and thick. Above him was the Brooklyn Bridge, and it offered him a shade that he was grateful for. A homeless man argued with a police officer, but in the end, Mayor Mike Bloomberg won. They were an eyesore, and humanity was reserved for the tax-payers. The Latin Assassin gave the man a passing glance, careful not to see anything in the man that reminded him of himself. Frankly, the risk was real. Only a few years before, and several lucky breaks back, he himself was sleeping in Las Vegas casino bathrooms, doing exhibition shows, and shopping a demonstration tape. But that was moot. It was history, damn near ancient. Today, he was standing in the shade of the Brooklyn Bridge, playing with the frayed ende of a plane ticket, trying to remember if he leftr the stove on. With a chuckle, he imagined coming back to New York as the last beam of his seven million-dollar loft turned to ashes. After a laugh that was extended by nervous energy, the man tucked the ticket into his back pocket, and returned to his waiting car. The black Mercedes-Benz SLK was one of the few truly excessive things he bought with his accumulated wealth, but the feel of the masterpiece on the open road more than justified the vanity. He dropped his luggage into the trunk, and set out toward the Greater Beyond. The camera sat on the dashboard. Like the mesh-covered eye of a priest in a confessional. And as it was intended to be, it was to be his video confessional. With a sigh and a grin, The Latin Assassin tossed the stick, and peeled down the Brooklyn-Queens Expressway toward the airport. And the video camera was his only company. So he began. D: ' I find it amazing...this feeling of nervousness. I mean, I've walked this road more times that any one outside of this business can believe. I've lived out of a suitcase, a knapsack, a brown paper bag, and even a hankerchief on a stick like a movie hobo. I've seen the gutters of every major city. I've also seen the palaces. In my time in this business, I've wrestled at Royal commencements, coronations, and for drunken businessmen in empty garages. In the world of 'Been THere-Done that', I can stand with the best of them. And yet, here I am, my mind racing, my stomach in knots, trying to remember just why I have decided to hit the road again. I think of the broken bones, broken promises, mishandled business relationships. I think of women with tears running down their faces, wondering why I would choose to be on the road then sharing my bed with them, and accepting them as my wife. And I can say, I would give anything to make these amazing women understand. I loved them, most of them. And I can say that they deserved a husband, not a post card from the road and an envelope of money to keep them faithful and happy.' Damien Cruz gows quiet, and the tape keeps filming. He is clearly deep in thought, but the words seem to have dried up. In the background, the Deftones have faded, and are replaced by Radiohead. After a stretch of road, he breaks the silence D:' But maybe I am doing this because I realize that I have spent too many noble years being the best among the mediocre. I mean, I'm in PRIME now. I'm not in some shit-stain indie fed that pays me with a free lunch buffet. And I'm facing a man who has crashed knuckles with the best of them. Maybe, in my twisted insecure head, nothing I have done, despite the accolades, seem to be worth a damn because I haven't done it in PRIME. Fuck, it would be so easy to turn around, nestle into retirement, and let my C-level celebrity pay the bills for me. And no one could say a goddam thing. I have over a decade of scar tissue and hard assets to be all the laurels I need. To the cynics, I can point at my history. To the fans, I can point to my legacy. And to those who have yet to see what I can do, I can easily allow the rumor mill inflate my worth into iconic proportions. And it would be fine. We have all seen it happen a million times. I could sign a deal and end up in some crappy Scorpion King knock-off, or show my comedic side and be hailed as a star of both the ring and the box office. I could sell my soul to Microsoft, like we all will have to anyway, and sell whatever blinking, beeping piece of shit they pay me to. I've earned that privelage, after all the blood and broken bones, it's a right I can easily claim wihtout batting an eye.' scene: He smiles. d: ' But what fun would that be? As long as there are guys like Kano out there, praying to whatever God will listen, I know that I have a place in this life. As long as I can see him boo-hoo-ing over the missed steps and broken toys in his past, I know that there are those waiting to validate me. For every moment that I question my purpose, there is a victim that exposes their soft underbelly and makes me salivate at the prospect..and that serves as the reminder. I know, for as long as my heart races at the idea of littering the canvas with the debris of broken bodies, souls, and spirits, that I am never going to be normal. I'm never going to be satisfied with the opiates that are supposed to satisfy me. Maybe I'm too misguided and brain-washed to know better, but I know that the sight of Kano kvetching about how lost he is makes me predatory. And that scares the shit out of me. It scares me because I know that there is a very ugly part of me that will not stop hitting him until he stops fighting back. I will, as I have, looked at my handiwork after the bell has rung, and wondered just what made me do what I had. And, of course, I try to rationalize my actions. I try to pomtificate and deify my behavior as a philosophy of personal evolution. But in the end, it's stell tables and 3-counts, not tribal sacrifices and catharsis. But...and I doubt I am the only person who has noticed this...the line between the two is blurry at best.' As the planes overhead get lower, and the signs begin to point toward the airport, Damien Cruz atempts to give a reasonably logical end note to his filmed ramblings. The video, he knows, that will end up on the PRIME broadcast somehow or other, so he tries to keep up appearances. D:' I think I may be over-thinking my motives. I mean, for the same reasons I think religion and faith are escapist and self-serving, I think my philosophy may be an intricate lie to justify my sociopathology. And maybe it isn't the wealth, the woman, the stability, or the social status that fails to keep my attention, maybe it's the lack of stimulation. Perhaps, and this may just be blind speculation talking, perhaps I am like those people who think pain is the truest form of self-awareness. It's trite, but it's convenient. And I guess convenient is the best we're going to get. Whatever the reasons, whatever the text-book may say about me, all I know is that I have a plane leaving in less than an hour, and I have the strong urge to put my fist through a window just to scare away the butterflies in my guts. And for Kano, that may be as good as it gets.But when he and I reach the ring, I hope that he has enough instinct to stop me from doing more damage than any logical reasoning would warrant. And I know that all I need is the 3-count. But dammit wouldn't it be great if I could REALLLY get the people talking? And nothing gets the hive buzzing like a senseless act of profane aggression. Damn, I feel like a virgin again.' Scene: a chuckle, and then The Latin Assassin grabs his bag from the trunk of his car. And as a red cap comes to help him with his luggage, he sees something about the man that reminds him of himself, ad he drops the car keys into the man's hands. Some things, Damien understands, are just anchors in a world that moves too fast to be weighed down in.
