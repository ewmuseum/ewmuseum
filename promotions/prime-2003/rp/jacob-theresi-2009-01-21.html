Jacob Theresi

21 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-21-09 The wind swirls around Fusenshoff’s body, imitating a crowded street on a warm workday lunch break. It nudges him unexpectedly one moment. The next it pushes him powerfully into the snow as it melts and bites at his face. Fusenshoff is spilling booze all over himself as a streetlamp is the only witness of his inebriated state. As snowflakes fall the wrestler wanders aimlessly without purpose or direction. He raises what’s left in his flask high above his head like a priest reciting his words before communion. He stumbles forward while spilling the alcohol on his chin and cheeks before it reaches its proper orifice. As Fusenshoff stupidly plunges in an awkward manner through the snow he is ambiguous to his surroundings. He doesn’t realize he’s walking above a lazy river that’s frozen over from the winter chill. One wrong step sends Fusenshoff slipping through a crack in the ice that quickly becomes a hole. Before he even realizes what happened he’s sucking in ice cold river water. Flailing like a helpless infant, Fuse reaches desperately for anything stable that can save his life. His boots, which have been so reliable and sturdy for years, quickly betray him as they masquerade as cinder blocks attached to his ankles. Hypothermia starts to set in and his body stiffens. All feeling in his arms and legs washes away. Fusenshoff gives up on his struggle just moments before his epiphany. He realizes that his body and mind reacted instinctually to help him survive. In truth, this is what he’s been asking for ever since he played a role in his sister’s death. He’s been yearning for an event like this to happen, to end his suffering. Fuse is dying now, but he didn’t take the coward’s way out. He didn’t commit suicide like he’s sworn he’ll never do. This is an accident brought on by fate to alleviate his suffering in this world. The tormented twenty-four year old stares up at the moon through blurred vision. He slips away, motionless and embracing this moment as a plea finally answered. The white moon soon fades and is replaced by a glowing green light, barely discernable. His sinking feeling is replaced with a warm inferno growing as if his soul is consuming his human form. Meanwhile the green light continues to bud and expand until it fills Fuse’s entire line of sight. Fusenshoff feels like he’s being pulled into a green sun as it flashes and explodes before his eyes. Then his vision clears for just a moment. He spits out a liter of river water as he blinks just once. It was long enough for him to see a large, hulking man standing erect and looking down from above him. The next sight Fusenshoff sees is the flame of a fire burning with an aggressive stress as it consumes the logs below. He realizes immediately that he’s nude, save a soaking wet pair of boxers and the comforting warmth of a blanket that’s enveloped his entire body. He looks around the room to see stacks upon stacks of books along a wall to his right. A chair sits in the corner that looks to have survived generations of history. It is worn, yet magnificent in some strange way that Fuse can’t quite put his finger on. A deer rifle, a twelve-gauge shotgun and several handguns sit in a case along the far wall. The only light surfacing within the room is from the soothing ambiance of the fireplace and a light bulb emanating from where this room meets the kitchen. A man in a forest green long-sleeve shirt steps through the incandescent doorframe carrying two mugs and a set of clothes. There is a presence that this man possesses which strikes Fusenshoff immediately. He walks with purpose and meaning, seemingly utterly comfortable in his own skin. One might confuse his body language as arrogant if it weren’t for the incredible humility and openness that radiates through his eyes and face. The man isn’t particularly large. He’s about the same height as Fusenshoff, yet has a more slender, normal build than Fuse’s wide frame. The man in green sets the mug down on the end table next to Fuse, who immediately recognizes the aroma as hot chocolate. He drops a pile of clothes in the alcoholic’s lap and pulls up the old chair from the corner. He sits casually as Fusenshoff continues to stare. This man just saved his life, yet it’s not that detail which has him so transfixed. He’s never met a man so at peace with himself. He admires his self-control as powerful yet welcoming, though the two of them haven’t even spoken a word. That’s when reality sets in and Fusenshoff remembers the gravity of the situation. Fuse’s expression changes immediately to one of distaste. He sits up in his chair and takes on an agitated posturing. This man sitting in front of him saved his life. Fusenshoff is pissed off. Neither man speaks for a moment as one is furious and the other totally serene. Fusenshoff loses his self-control and goes on the offensive. “What the fuck is your problem?!” he screams. The man in the green shirt looks at him, somewhat surprised by the outburst. “I beg your pardon?” Fusenshoff doesn’t know where to begin. How does he explain his reaction to having his life saved? He stumbles over his words a bit. “I mean… what the fuck were you doing out at that park?! Were you following me?! Who would be so fucking stupid to risk their life like that? I shouldn’t have survived even with your help! You should’ve been pulling a corpse out of that river!” The man waits until Fuse finishes before choosing to speak. He lets the gravity of the man’s words sink in for both of them. “You certainly have a tough time expressing gratitude, don’t you, Fusenshoff?” This startles the alcoholic sitting in his boxers underneath a blanket. Immediately his mind triggers an alarm. Fuse wonders how this man knows his name. He’s having a hard time even expressing anger now-confusion has overwhelmed that emotion. Fusenshoff looks around the room, distraught. He’s looking for something to explain this man’s motives. “Listen man, I don’t know how the hell you know my name or why you risked your life for mine. You better start explaining yourself. You’re acting like a psycho and freaking me the fuck out.” The man in green responds. “I’d argue that you’re the one who doesn’t know what to do with himself, but that’s not important. My name is Jacob Theresi. I was going for a jog when I saw you tripping all over yourself on a patch of ice in the middle of the Red Cedar River. You’re a young man with much to live for. I felt like my sacrifice would be worth it on the chance that we’d be sitting here now.” Fusenshoff glances up at the man who now seems focused on the luminance of the fireplace. Realizing the two of them have nothing more to talk about unless he chooses, he immediately starts thinking of an excuse to leave. There’s no chance Fuse is going to thank the man for saving his life. He feels like beating him half to death for the inconvenience. He realizes that he wouldn’t act on that impulse even if it drove him mad yearning to. “Where’s your bathroom?” Fuse asks. Jacob points down the hall. “Last door on your right. I’ll fix us some soup for dinner. You should eat something before you leave.” [i]You go ahead and do that,[/i] Fuse thinks to himself. As Jacob gets up and heads toward the kitchen Fusenshoff snatches the clothes and gets changed. He pauses a moment while changing to stare at himself in the mirror. He fights off an urge to break down right there in the bathroom. Sucking it up, he looks at the white sweatshirt and blue jeans Jacob gave him to wear. It’s completely different from his usual black attire. Fusenshoff feels very uncomfortable in the clothes, but he has no choice at this point. He leaves the bathroom and stops at the front door. Jacob is still in the kitchen making soup. Fuse throws on Jacob’s boots as quickly as he can before leaving the house. The wrestler is blocks away and searching frantically for a liquor store. That’s when he realizes he doesn’t have his wallet and… “Awww fuck!” He left his grandfather’s flask at Jacob’s house too. It’s a family heirloom. It’s the only thing Fusenshoff has left to remember his grandpa by. His grandpa was the man who raised him until he died when Fuse was sixteen. He expresses aggravation that he has to meet Jacob again, yet at the same time he feels contentment at the prospect somewhere deep inside himself.
