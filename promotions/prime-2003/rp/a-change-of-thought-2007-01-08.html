A Change of Thought...

8 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/8/07 Following Revolution 145, many of PRIME’s fans noticed the abrupt disappearance of one of their most hated superstars. The man known as the Polish Punisher quickly left the Air Canada Centre following his devastating loss to the Commie Emo, Devin Shakur. This was the second time that Walczak had lost to Shakur, but this time defeat proved too hard to swallow. No interviews, no confrontations, not a soul saw Walczak as he exited the arena and drove away in his white, 2007 Yukon Denali. Why would he have stayed? The first time he lost to Shakur, it could have been ring rust. Almost three years since his last fight, anyone would have understood that, but what would be his excuse this time? He could not chalk this one up to ring rust, nor was he sick. A momentary lapse or a lack of preparation, how many people could honestly believe this? They would think Walczak was just making excuses, not willing to take his defeat like a man. There was no reason to make excuses, because the truth was that Walczak lost. He lost to the better wrestler, and it was something that he now had to face. How had he been reduced to this? The man had once been a proud champion, hoisting titles above his head on a nightly basis. Now, he cannot even advance further that the opening round of a tournament that could have reinvented his career. He could have once again been feared by wrestlers across the world, and championed by the people who have supported him so strongly. Now, he would be reduced to a joke, an afterthought to people who followed the Jewel in the Crown Tournament. Who was that guy that Shakur beat? Oh yeah it was that Polish guy. Not even giving Walczak an identity, but instead using the generic ‘Polish Guy’, Walczak could see the joy on the American’s faces by cheapening the importance and heritage of Walczak. Why not just leave? It was one of the first thoughts that entered the mind of Walczak. Was it time to leave? Who would notice if he was gone? The American’s would be overjoyed once they had noticed the disappearance of Walczak. All he ever did was bash their culture, and blame them for the world’s problems. It would be a breath of fresh air to not have him in the ring taking up time, they would rather see someone praising their intelligence, or some two bit whore in the ring showing a little skin. The minds of the American’s were to underdeveloped to understand Walczak’s message. They had been corrupted by video games, and useless television programs. The fact that Walczak was trying to show them the correct way to lead their lives was lost on them, so why even bother trying to convince them. Maybe it would be better if he just left them to self destruct, instead of trying to convert them? He couldn’t. He did not care, what the American’s would think. As much as he tried to convince them that the way they treated people was wrong in the end he did not care whether or not they adhered to his message. It was the Polish people that prevented him from quitting. If he gave up preaching, then the people of Poland would have no one to voice their opinions. The United States would just go back to ignoring Poland, putting them in with the rest of the countries they deem useless and unimportant. There was no doubt that Walczak needed some time to think about what his future held, and there was only one place to do it. The only place Walczak has ever seen in a positive light, Poland. _______________________________________________________________________________________ For the past three weeks Walczak has been in Poland. Talking to the people he cares for most. Walczak has undergone a transformation of sorts due to this visit. When in the United States, Walczak does nothing but think of how to change the American people, he runs into those who spew hate on a daily basis. This could not be a greater difference from the time he spends in Poland. He is welcomed with open arms everywhere he goes, and thanked for the voice he provides the people of the country. He was even visited by newly appointed President, Donald Tusk, but in the United States, he was not even acknowledged by his local assemblyman. However, this visit has not produced the results that Walczak was hoping for. He thought that the visit would reinvigorate him, give him the strength that he needed to restart his crusade in the United States. If it had done anything it only made him question more as to why he was in the United States in the first place. In the United States he is chastised by even his closest neighbors. In Poland people have come from around the country to praise him. His hometown of Gdansk has even thrown a festival in his honor, and many of the countries wrestling promotions are willing to pay through the nose in order to get control of his services. _______________________________________________________________________________________ Walczak sits in his newly purchased home, high atop Pulaski Hill. Looking through any of the windows he can see the beauty inside the city of Gdansk, and the shores of the Baltic Sea. Ever since he was a boy in the orphanage he had dreamed of one day living atop this hill. It was one of the few things that drove him to leave the country in the first place, knowing that he would never amass the fortune he needed under communist rule. Now, he has achieved it, and there is no reason to go back to the United States. In fact Walczak searches through his cell phone for the number of Lisa Tyler, hoping to get his way out of his obligation to PRIME. RING…. RING… RING… What was this? Walczak had just installed his land line yesterday, and he was not listed, nor had he given the number out to anyone. A confused look remains plastered on Walczak’s face, as he makes his way towards the phone. “Hello…” “Where the hell have you been?” The shrill annoying voice on the other end of the line could only belong to one person, Blaine Blair. Not necessarily the person that Walczak wanted to talk to, but there was no reason to put it off any further. “I’m back in the homeland, and I gotta tell you I am having the time of my life.” “I don’t remember you having permission to leave, actually to be honest I don’t remember you even asking.” “Yeah, I didn’t. I just had to kind of get away from the everyday labors of being a PRIME employee. Traveling from city to city only to get booed, and shit tossed at me isn’t exactly my idea of fine living.” “Well, you better get back on the next plane because you have a match coming up this week.” “About that, I don’t think that I am going to be coming back anytime soon. In fact if I am being honest I don’t think that I will be coming back at all.” “WHAT!!!! YOU ARE UNDER CONTRACT AND WE WILL NOT STAND FOR THIS!!!” “You can bitch and moan all you want, but I am a Polish Citizen. A proud one, and I don’t think that the people here will be that willing to extradite me to the United States. You see Blair, here I am wanted, I may even be a hero. Why would I leave a place of peace and joy to return to one of ridicule?” “You’re right. Well, I guess that I will just have to announce to everyone that Nitz Donnelly has beaten you without even having to step into the ring.” “Nitz Donnelly…” Walczak had crossed paths with this so called wrestler before. His nickname the Ego, was a bit of an understatement. The man thought that he was God’s gift to everything, man, woman, animal it did not matter. He thought that anyone who crossed paths with him was lucky, and to Walczak he epitomized everything that was wrong with American culture. He was brash, cocky, all in all a huge douchebag. “I can only imagine the victory dance, speech or what have you Donnelly will put on if he takes down the Polish Punisher. The man who has gone on a rampage against the United States, the man who was afraid to step into the ring with The Ego of New England.” “I never said I was afraid. I would gladly step into the ring with that punk.” “Then do it, or you know just how you will be pictured in the years to come.” Walczak knew that Blair was right. Had it been anyone else Walczak may have stayed in Poland, but he could not allow a man like Nitz Donnelly to have the satisfaction of claiming to have defeated Walczak, and more importantly put an end to his cause. Now, Walczak was packing just as quickly as he unpacked, and knew what he had to do. Whether he wanted to or not.
