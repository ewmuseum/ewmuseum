Date: Mar. 1999/Aug. 2009 March 11, 1999 University of Pennsylvania Hospital Phiadelphia, PA "You know, this would be a lot easier if you just let me do this." Troy Douglas was as progressive and modern as the next guy, but even now, four months after suffering what the doctors had termed as "massive neck and spinal trauma", he wasn't exactly thrilled about being wheeled around the ground floor of this hospital by his 5'3" fiancee. It wasn't that he was such a staunch traditionalist, it was just that, when getting up to get a glass of water from the bathroom sink becomes a gargantuan task on the order of climbing to the summit of Everest barefoot with flippers on your hands, sometimes, a guy just feels he needs to do something that still makes him feel like The Man in the Relationship. And being carted around past orderlies like a sick grandmother wasn't really doing much to inspire Troy's inner manhood. "Actually," Lauren said, her eyes twinkling mischievously as Troy slowly craned his neck to look back at her, "this would be a lot easier if you weren't a 260-pound lump of goo." "Lump of goo, am I?" "Well ... an attractive lump of goo." Troy smiled against his own will. Even in the grimmest and most somber of situations, Lauren Bennett still had a way of getting a rise out of him. "Damn straight," Troy said. "Now onward, captain. Warp factor seven." "So that's what you do all day while I'm out at work, huh? Sit around and watch Star Trek re-runs?" "Star Trek: The Next Generation, actually." Lauren leaned down and batted Troy playfully on the side of the head, a gesture she'd perfected since they were squabbling seven-year-olds in Greensboro, steering his doctor-ordered chariot left into a familiar room. "I can stay if you want," she said. "Just say the word." Troy flicked his right hand in the air, waving her away nonchalantly. "Nah, you go. No need to watch me spend 40 minutes trying to remember 'left leg, right leg.'" "That's 'right leg, left leg,' Troy." That was the voice of Scott Cooper, the wiry, glasses-wearing physical therapist who'd been supervising Troy's rehab since he got out of surgery back in December. He didn't look like much, but Scott could switch from Drill Sergeant to Drinking Buddy at will, and his good humor was usually enough to get Troy to tolerate the thrice-weekly 40 minute therapy sessions. "Right, left ... whatever. As long as we minimize the number of times I end up on my ass today, I'm happy." Lauren grinned as she walked back towards he fiancee, her eyes glittering and dancing the same samba whose rhythm Troy had fallen victim to so many years before. "But," she said, holding her pause for dramatic attempt while trying to restrain chuckles, "you're so good at it!" "You see, Scotty? This is what I have to deal with. Now, off with you!" Troy shooed her away with exaggerated waves, but before she left, Lauren bent down to his face and caught him with a quick kiss. "See you soon. Take care of him, Scott." "Always do." With that, Lauren Bennett was gone down the hallway, ready to spend another 40 minutes reading a two-month old issue of Newsweek in the waiting room as she longed for the day where Troy finally emerged out of the room out of that damned chair and on his own two feet. Looking wistfully towards the space just vacated by his fiancee, Troy set himself grimly towards his task, determined to make that dream come true. Of course, first thing was first. He had to actually get out of the blasted chair. Luckily for Troy Douglas, Scott Cooper was more than accustomed to this sort of thing. "All right, Troy. What say we get this party started?" ***** New York, NY The present day "All right, Troy. What say we get this party started?" Funny thing for Samantha Wade to say, especially considering it was just the two of them in her uptown apartment. But that, as Troy Douglas quickly learned with each passing moment he spent in her company, that was just Sam Wade's demeanor. In 33 years of life, Troy had never found someone who could so easily spot the faintest silver lining on any cloud. Well, there was one other person, but as he felt Sam curl up against him on the couch and hand him a bottle of Blue Moon, he was thankful to avoid making any of those comparisons. Besides, a party for two was just the kind of thing both of them could do with right about now. It was a lazy August Saturday night, at least for the two of them. A dozen stories below, the City that Never Sleeps was in its state of perpetual caffeinated hysteria, but for the two of them, the symphony of taxi horns honking, wheels screeching, steam vents whistling and locals swearing was easily ignored by shutting the window. This was their night, one of their few remaining nights of peace before they returned to their equally hectic lives, him on yet another PRIME tour, her back to the Columbia classroom and her freshman American History class. This was the kind of break the new couple relished; a quiet night, a wide variety of Chinese takeout, a couple drinks and the complete second season of the West Wing on DVD. Although, as Sam pulled herself closer to him and let her hand trace lazily across the back of his neck, the exploits of Josh Lyman, Toby Ziegler, Leo McGarry, C.J. Cregg and the rest of the Bartlet administration were just about the furthest thing from his mind. Wordlessly, he reciprocated, pulling her head to his chest and aimlessly stroking her hair with his free hand. Two sips into his beer, he set it aside on the coffee table and devoted his full attention to the woman laying against him. The relationship was certainly physical by this point - a week alone in a Boston hotel room had seen to that - but both were committed to taking this relationship as slowly and carefully as possible. Sam, because she'd seen one to many of her own bad relationships spiral badly into unsalvageable chaos, Troy because, well, it had been a long time before something this good had come into his life, and he didn't want to risk it ending quickly by making any mistakes. Still, as their hands changed from playthings to instruments of possession, even the bells and whistles in the front of his brain telling him to slow down were getting tuned out by an old adage that sounded powerfully once again as he pulled Samantha close for a kiss, brushing a few loose strands of red hair away from her eyes so he could get a good look at her. Sometimes, you've gotta walk before you can crawl. ***** "C'mon. You've gotta walk before you can crawl, Troy." "You know, that seems a little counterintuitive, Scottie." Troy was straining against the wall, holding on desperately with both arms to a wooden bar that, for the moment, was the only thing keeping Troy and the cold linoleum floor from having a rather violent meeting with each other. The session had progressed brilliantly, in the mind of the therapist, but to look at Troy Douglas' face, one would scarcely know the leaps and bounds with which his condition was rapidly improving. Every facial muscle was twisted in a grimace, his skin swiftly turning varying shades of red and purple as he contorted himself, putting every bit of energy he had into making his brain remember that one foot goes in front of the other. Of all the training he'd endured, of all the marathon two and three-a-day practices is 100 degree-plus heat, dressed in full pads while 300-pound behemoths plowed into him rep after rep, of all the gassers he'd ever run while sweat poured off his like water flowing from a stream, re-teaching his body to do something it had done for nearly his whole life was the single hardest thing Troy Douglas ever had to do. But, damnit if he wasn't the stubborn type. He wasn't spending one second longer in that chair than he absolutely needed to. Across the room, Scott nodded up and down, surveying the situation as his patient struggled to maintain vertical balance. He waited until Troy finally looked as comfortable as he could be, then proffered the question. "You want to give this a go?" The question danced in Troy's mind for a moment. Part of him wanted to say no, sit back in that chair and wheel himself straight back to his apartment, dump himself onto his bed and sleep until his body could no longer take the rest. The debate lasted approximately 0.68 seconds. An eternity for Lt. Commander Data, no time at all for Troy Douglas. "All right. Let's do this." "Okay then. Let go." Troy hesitated a moment, sucking in his breath in anticipation. Slowly, tentatively, he let go of the bar, wobbling slightly as his feet steadied underneath him. Deliberately, he lifted his right foot, setting it down a couple inches in front of him. Then the left. Then the right again. Then the left again. Inches at a time, he started to close the gap between himself and Scott Cooper. Troy let a smile slip across his face, but only for a moment before he needed to grit his teeth and dig in once again. It was akin to walking in a vat of quicksand, or trying to swim against an incredibly powerful riptide, but he was moving. Slowly, surely, he was moving, increasing his stride by millimeters every time, determined to cross the room. And then, with no warning or cause, his knees started to buckle, his legs started to give, and Troy Douglas felt the ground rush towards him like a lightning bolt. Only, the crash never came. Two hands reached across the room and grabbed him underneath the arms, stopping him from falling. But, what intrigued Troy even more were the smaller pair of hands on his back, propping him up. The feel of the hands was enough to give away who they belonged to. The voice only confirmed his suspicions. "Biting off a little more than you can chew, champ?" came the ever-sarcastic tones of his fiancee. Troy tensed himself and groaned as he was guided back to his wheelchair, but still managed to get a simple point across as a smile finally returned to his pained visage. "Baby steps, Laur. Baby steps."
