Finding Peace

19 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19/09/05 He sat on the bonnet of his car, glancing over to the retirement home in which his past resided. His personal life was holding him back and everyone was saying it, ranging from the experts in PRIME to the casual fan who watched on his sofa. There were so many issues that had been unresolved from his past, some of which were gone and irretrievable whilst others still had the potential to be rectified. This particular situation needed to be dealt with, not for the benefit of PRIME or the chances of success over Kyle Lamen but for his sanity and for him to be able to live his life free from ghosts of arguments past. Paul looked down at the clothes he wore, a fine black suit with a red shirt, and wondered whether his grandmother would even recognise him. It had been a long time since he had been able to see her and their relationship had mainly been conducted by mail. Now though she was about to see the product of his hard work and dedication to his profession, the Armani suits and the designer sunglasses adorning a body created through equal persistence. He was surprised at himself for even though he was that man, The Serial Thrilla, he felt like a small child again. His grandmother had been a strong matriarchal figure who ruled their family with a firm but fair hand. When his parents abandoned him, she was there to pick up the pieces and get him through those teenage years which would determine his destiny. How could one so strong end up in a place like this? He slid off the bonnet and took the slow walk up the path towards the main entrance of the home. On either side of him were flowers or beautiful green grass, all very nice but he cynically mused that these things were only in place to make the residents final years, months and even moments a little bit more bearable. He despised these places and this irrational hatred was not helped by the fact that his own grandmother had resided here for the best part of a decade. As he reached forward and opened the front door of the home, he felt like he was entering a more presentable type of prison. “Good afternoon sir, how may I help you today?” The overly polite voice broke his thoughts as he stood in the reception area looking around him. To most people this would be a very nice start to the home with beautiful pictures hanging on the wall, plenty of windows to allow fresh daylight in and flowers dotted around to pretty the place up a bit. To Paul Cain however it was just a charade to cover up a place which was used to shunt the elderly away and leave them to rot. Even a man like him who had known feelings of anger, hatred, rage as well as having committed actions of violence against fellow men, could know feelings of sympathy and sadness. In his mind he realised it wasn’t the nurse’s fault he felt this way but he couldn’t help himself from snapping at her. “You can help me by telling me where my grandmother is.” The middle aged woman seemed somewhat taken aback by Paul’s short and sharp retort to her usual politeness. She slowly brushed some of her dark brown hair back behind her ear with her fingers and offered him a warm smile to attempt to break the ice. His expression remained just as cold though and she realised that he was not interested in tea and conversation. He turned his eyes away from her and slowly paced around the room as he waited for some kind of sensible answer. “Sir, if you would like to give me the name of your grandmother, then I might be able to help you?” Cain turned towards her and smiled but not in a pleasant way but more like a cynical and highly false manner. “Out of all the cattle that you have herded here, my grandmother is the one that goes by the name of Doris Jones. She’s eighty years old and was dumped here without my knowledge by a family that didn’t care a damn about her because she was no longer of any use to them. Does that give you enough information now, sweetheart?” This little outburst was followed by the darkest of his looks as he turned away from her. She sighed and walked over to him putting her hand on his shoulder but he quickly shrugged it off. Undeterred, she continued to attempt to make the best of this awkward situation. “I can understand your frustration and it’s very hard to come to terms with an elderly relative that you love dearly having to go into residential care but--.” Another intense glare from Paul Cain quickly encouraged her that discretion was the better part of valour. “Walk out of this nursing home, get in your car and drive all the way to Denver, get out of your car and walk the streets for about an hour then you might ACTUALLY find someone who gives a shit about your scripted sermon. I just want to see my grandmother.” She motions with her hand for Cain to follow her and without any further exchange of words, they walk down the passageways of the home in the direction of Doris Jones. He sees many fellow residents either sitting quietly in their chairs or shuffling along the corridors wearily. With each one that passes, he resolves that when he gets to this stage of life, a bottle of scotch and a loaded gun will be the only care that he needs, after all, the Serial Thrilla wouldn’t go out any other way. Eventually they come to a room where the nurse stops at and knocks softly on the door. “Who…is it?” The voice from the other side sounds tired and croaky which Paul Cain doesn’t recognise as being his grandmother. She had always been so powerful and strong when she spoke, it was enough to draw attention even in the most crowded room or as more likely, during the most intense arguments. The nurse placed her hand on the door handle and slowly opens it before replying in a tone that Cain felt was patronising towards this formerly dominant female. “You’ve got a visitor, dear. Isn’t that nice?” He followed her into the room and turned to the left where he saw his grandmother, Doris Jones, lying in a bed and propped up on pillows. Paul Cain had been through many bloody battles and fought many wars against some extremely tough individuals. The duel with Kyle Lamen that was yet to come was certain to be one such contest with Cain having to fight tooth and nail to survive and reach the podium of glory. However no amount of pain that Lamen, Imperium or anyone could put him through would match that which he felt now looking at his tired grandmother. She looked old and of course at eighty years old she was but this was different. Doris appeared as if she had grown weary of life with her face pale and tattooed in wrinkles. She would always colour her hair black to keep it to it’s natural shade but now it was brilliant white and aged. Life seemed to be leaving her slowly and she appeared to be growing weary of the fight. “Ohhh…My wonderful grandson…It’s been so long…Please do come and sit by my side.” He found it difficult to recognise her weak voice but he pulled up a chair and set it close to her bed. Before sitting, he turned to glare at the nurse and he needed to say nothing as she swiftly left the room, closing the door quietly behind her. Satisfied that she would not be disturbing them with her superficial niceties, Cain sat down and reached for his grandmother’s hand. Her movements were shaky and slow as she clasped her hand in his. “Hey Grandma, how are you? It’s been too long but you look well.” She smiled as she read the lie that he told. “I’m eighty years old but I feel one hundred, I know you’re just being polite my darling. I’m so glad to see you though and you’re right, it has been too long. You look so smart…and handsome…It looks to me as if your career is going well...Are you making your grandmother proud?” He found it hard to answer this question as he looked at this wonderful woman before him. From the moment he was born, he had been the apple of her eye just like it should be. When his parents left him it became clear that she was the one who loved him the most and had put all her faith and energy into him as a child. Yet as an adult he looked upon her as a man who had failed her countless times over having struck a woman, lied, cheated and robbed from so many people. She was a pillar of her community and a moralistic person, all the crimes he had committed against others would be crimes that she would be saddened by. Just as he felt like he was going to drown from his guilt, she squeezed his hand and her smile was soft and warm. “Life doesn’t always keep you on the right path, does it my love? I’ll always be proud of you, no matter what you do or what happens.” She had eased his conscience and it allowed him to relax as he spent the best part of an hour telling her all that he had experienced and seen. He left nothing out, even the things that he felt ashamed of, it just all poured out to his grandmothers listening ears and comforting smiles mixed with shining eyes of happiness. Doris was just pleased to see her only grandchild and no amount of sins could take that away from her. She conserved her energy but took time to reassure him and coax him along through the recounting of those difficult parts of his journey. At one point he showed her the scar that the Dark Age had carved into him. She reached her hand out and touched it softly and shook her head at him. “Don’t let that hold you back, my darling. Although the memory and reminder of it will always be there, it doesn’t have to stop you living your life. Learn from it my love, use it as something to make you stronger, not weaker. You’ve overcome that so you can overcome anything.” His heart fluttered slightly at her words as he realised how right she was. Doris Jones always knew how to make her grandson feel better and she gave him the strength that he needed when he was weak. She listened intently as he told her about Damien and the visions he had had in his dreams. He recounted the tale of his mother walking out on him as a child when he had made false accusations towards her. Her expression changed to one of sympathy as he struggled to tell the story without succumbing to emotion. “I still don’t know why she did it, Grandma.” Again she squeezed his hand then she wheezed heavily and coughed as she strained over to rest her other hand on the top of his. “Your mother simply didn’t know how to deal with her child being such an adult when he wasn’t supposed to be. It was through no fault of your own and she handled it badly but it didn’t mean she didn’t love you or care for you. Life was hard for her and she truly didn’t know how to raise a child when your father was away so often. He was her rock and when he left, she struggled to deal with anything including her loneliness and desperation. Raising a child doesn’t come with a manual, Paul. She just didn’t know what to do and she was at her wits end but it was never, ever, your fault, my sweetheart.” Paul looked down at the floor and swallowed hard. “I hated her for what she did to me, leaving me like that.” There was a cautious pause as his grandmother considered her words carefully.“I know you did my darling and that was why I took you into my home. You needed to be shown love and tenderness which is what I gave you. I had faith in you and I knew you would grow to be the success you are now. She was tired and beaten down which led her to make choices that she would later regret but it was too late by then. I took over the reins for her when it all became too much to bear and I’m glad I did because I’m so proud of you. However Paul, you have to repay me and do something for me…Would you do that for your grandma?” He nodded slowly in acknowledgement, his eyes slightly glassy now. “Don’t hate your mother for what she did, don’t blame her for it and don’t let it consume you. Go back and make right the wrongs you’ve made. Make your peace with Claudia and show her you’re sorry, granted the damage might be done but it is worth trying for. As for your mother, make peace with her in your mind too, because hatred like that will only serve to distract you further from your goals. Put it all behind you and focus on the future, Paul. By doing that, you can make me proud of you and I can look on from wherever I’ll be, knowing that my grandson has made it and that he’s going to be all right.” Paul Cain had resolved to be strong that day but it had been so difficult seeing his grandmother realising her mortality. He swallowed back the tears and stood up to enable him to embrace her tightly in his arms. Never before had he felt so free of the burdens of the past and it was all because of this inspiring woman. Her own embrace was gentle and weak but he made sure to squeeze her tightly so that she knew how loved she was. In the world of PRIME, there was little room for sentiment and emotion but to Paul Cain it didn’t matter. That one visit with his grandmother had prepared him for anything that Lamen or anyone else might throw at him. As he bid her farewell, he turned and smiled softly at her. “I love you, grandma Doris and…Thank you.” She smiled back to him and waved weakly at him. “I love you too Paul and thank YOU.” Neither of them realised this now but this would be the last time that they saw each other again. ******************************************************************************************************************************************** Paul Cain made his way back to the car and he saw the massive frame of Damien Kahn standing by the drivers’ side. Damien had already promised to leave Cain be whilst he attended to family matters and sure enough he had kept his word. “Did you get your shit solved, Paul?” Cain nodded his head solemnly as it had been a humbling experience to see his grandmother again and to be told the truths he had heard. He felt so very free and his mind had now turned to the pressing matters at hand. His attention had already turned to Imperium and how to burn down their church of hypocrisy. Hoyt Williams played on his mind as did the manipulation of Mark Blackwolf and how to stop that before it got out of hand. Then was the name that Damien shattered the peace with. “What about Lamen?” Paul shined up his sunglasses and slowly put them on with a smile. “Distractions were the only things that Lamen had in his favour. Look at this face, does this look like the face of a distracted man? Kyle Lamen doesn’t even that going for him now.” Cain reached for the door of the car and opened it up, unable to take the smile off his face. “You know what Damien, I think things are about to take a turn for the better.” Scene fades out to black as they both enter the car to leave the past behind.
