The Name Of The Father - Volume One - The Night The Stars Fell Down (Match RP - v Tsonda )

2 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/01/06 The Night The Stars Fell Down.Written: 2nd January, 2006. 'That's the doubt, the doubt,The trust in it.That's the sight and that's the sound of it.That's the gift and that's the trick in it. You're the truth, not I.' - Placebo. I 'No, don't be silly,' the more-than-slightly inebriated Max Wolfenden fobbed the offer of a lift off with. 'You two get off home, I'll get a cab.' The night was all stark contrasts. The deep, solid darkness that descended hours ago forced into shadowed corners by the glowing, almost molten amber streetlights. The night's breeze whistled a traveller's tune through the alleyways, accompanying those broken men who're soon to be nursing the most almighty of hangovers. 'Are you sure?' the sober tones of the designated driver enquired one last time for politeness' sake. 'It's not too far out of our way.' Max heard the last-chance offer, but deferred his reply until uncoordinated hands got into a buttoning rhythm, encasing him in a black winter coat. The chill that remained un-tamed by the clear sky gave the night a crisp substance. 'I'll be fine,' he assured them, in the overly-assertive manner that a few bottles of Claret will bring along. 'I could do with the walk to clear my head a bit.' 'Okay,' was conceded as the sounds of car door locks flicking open carried on the refreshing gusts. 'See you on next year.' Farewells and well-wishes were exchanged before Max's wave flung the remains of his cigar inadvertently down the street, a trail of embers skipping down to the pavement behind it's ashen crash-site. 'Bright and early,' he then grumbled, deciding against retrieving it from the sidewalk as his scarf formed the seal around his collar, trapping the heady, glowing warmth inside. A warmth he then had to sharply swallow to stop it creeping up his oesophagus. With hands plunged snugly down in his pockets, Max strode off, soles brushing off the sidewalk as a drunken stumble was incorporated into his stride. While his sister had recently quit her job, Max had just secured a promotion beyond even his ambitious dreams. His rise up the ladder of Adams, Falquero & King surpassed even his more free-spirited sibling's rapid ascent up the PRIME card just a little over a year ago. And, just as she'd flung her athletic frame up over PRIME's summit to capture the Universal Title after just 10 matches, in 10 months he'd secured his own client base, and was being groomed to make Partner by the following Christmas. As his nose and cheeks went past rosy and into glowing territory, Max's blurry eyes scanned around for a cab. 'Jesus Christ,' Max then winced with a sharp hiss as the breeze seemingly swept around to be funnelled into his path, almost pushing him back and toppling over mid-step. 'I should have taken the lift...' Despite the teeth-chattering chill that was ravaging his huddled body, the more traditional of the Wolfenden siblings burst into festive song... one best suited to his merrily intoxicated state. 'It was Christmas Eve, Babe...' Max half proclaimed/half slurred as his feet again missed a step, leaving him to tumble onto gloved palms. For a few seconds, he remained in the sprinter's start position, heaving in a few deep breaths after a 'phew!'. Then, as a hand peeled itself from it's chewing gum fixing to the sidewalk, it slapped down across his mouth. Under his scarf, Max's Adam's apple bobbed with preventative swallowing, but it was all to be in vain. 'Ooooh shit...' he let the rare bad language spew forth with, shortly before his dash to the nearby alley dished up his dinner to follow. Resting his forehead against the damp brickwork, Max again tried to breath through it, but the festering, mouldy stench of the alley only called insatiably for an encore. Further from the street he staggered, trying to find a drain to avoid pebble-dashing his shoes with partly-digested chickpeas and sun-blushed tomatoes. As wrenching coughs cleared the reluctant exit way, they began to expel laughter... the stuffier Wolfenden seemingly letting his neatly-cropped hair down. 'They've got cars big as bars,' he picked his slurred singing back up with, 'they've got rivers of go-' The rather exaggerated doses of Claret had made his synapses sleepy, and he didn't feel the pressure on the back of his head until the wall's brickwork rapidly grew in his hazy vision. Eyes slammed shut and teeth clashed harshly as his forehead smashed against the alley's wall, his brain rattling agonising in it's now possibly-flawed protective skull. Already unsure footing then collapsed under Max, and he felt a leading knuckle plough into his cheekbone as he dropped. 'Ugh, plea-' he began to plead, before a second swing swatted his head harshly to the side. Max's face collided with the grimy leaf litter on the alley's floor, which didn't offer enough of a cushion to save the tooth which was chipped on impact. Spitting out the dank, slimy carpet, a startled Max gasped for air, which was then forced right back out of him as heavy toe-caps crunched into his side. Then he felt the sickening feeling inside as bones lost the test of strength. 'Aghgh...' the blood gurgled deep in Max's throat as it congealed with the mucus sniffed down from his nose. The primal noise expelled was one that told of agonised desperation, but it earned him no mercy. Feet and fists commenced a heavy barrage upon Max's fallen body as he tried to cover up his head, despite the searing pain shooting up his forearm that told him there was a break. The rich, sweet taste of a split lip teased taste buds, helping combat the bitter chalkiness of a shattered tooth. As Max curled up even tighter in the desperate foetal position, his consciousness began to slip, a foot breaking though his splintered arm's barrier to stamp at his jaw. 'That'll te-... ende-... fl-...erse-...' Eyes, half screwed up as his expression told of the excruciating ache that overcame his body, saw nothing but a blur, until the foot burst through the cloudiness and into his near sight, stamping the last ounce of consciousness out through an enamel sharded mouth. II 'You're the sister?' Dr Reynolds confirmed as he hastily guided Karina Wolfenden through the bustling corridor. Never having liked hospitals, despite how frequently her lifestyle left her needing them, Karina looked ill at ease being navigated through the sterile white. Mixed in with panic for her sibling, and one half-hearted word was all the caring Doctor would receive. 'Yes.' 'We've tried to call his wife by the number on his cellphone, same with his parents, but we got no answers before we tried you,' Reynolds revealed, only to a short hum of understanding from Karina. 'Um, yeah,' she started to straighten herself out with. 'His wife is already in St. Lucia for Christmas, he was meant to fly out the day after tomorrow. Same with the parents... it's the family Christmas thing... and, um, they didn't take his phone?' 'He still had everything on him,' the Doctor sounded surprised himself in saying. 'Still had his wallet in his coat pocket too.' Doing her best to blinker vision against the reality of all around her, Kari followed Reynolds, not having had a straight thought since she'd received the call. 'So where is he now?' 'We sent him up for scans,' he tried his best to appease Karina's obvious fears with. 'And we're expecting him back any minute now.' As Dr Reynolds pulled open the curtain to show Max's expectant bed, the ever curious eye of the K-Wolf wandered. It was as if her sight itself itched, forcing her to squint, narrowing down her gaze, focussing it. Results of the tests they'd already ran were reeled off dutifully, but Wolfenden's attention had drifted, too far from shore to hurry back. 'Um, Miss Wolfenden,' Reynolds exclaimed as Kari looked right across him. Two beds down, in the gap between curtains, so slight it could have easily just been a shadow, she saw something. Such a fleeting glance, only recognition made it remarkable. Cold, fathoms deep, and instantly engaging. 'You...' she verbally jabbed, storming over to the curtain. 'Miss Wolfenden!' Reynolds repeated, rushing into her path, before gesturing to an alerted security guard that he could handle it. 'I can't have you disturbing other patients.' 'Why is he here?' Karina demanded to know, trying to force the Doctor back. 'WHY is HE HERE?' Grabbing the squirming Wolfenden by her shoulders, the Doctor tried to steady her, both mentally and physically. 'If you want to be here for your brother, you need to calm down, okay?' His stare rested upon her for a few seconds, until Karina finally gave him her attention. 'I know him,' she tried to tell Reynolds. 'Who?' he enquired, looking back to where Karina seemed drawn to. 'Mr Shepherd?' 'Who?' it was then Karina left asking, unsure if her erratic thoughts were even her own. 'Mr Shepherd,' Dr Reynolds stated once more. 'He was in a car accident this morning.' 'But he was just...' the panicking and confused Wolfenden tried to explain, getting halfway through a sentence before wondering where she began. 'I saw him... he was...' 'Look,' the concerned Doctor began, guiding the K-Wolf away from the beds. 'Your brother might not be down for a few more minutes, so why don't you go grab a coffee, call who you need to, have a smoke, whatever.' 'Yeah...' Karina pondered, finally finding herself in agreement with him in. 'There's someone I need to talk to...' III Returning home, despite the mileage, Angelo Deville looked every inch as stylised and pristine as when he left. Aircraft seats failed to crumple the delicate woven strands of his pinstripe suit, and not even two days of rain could remove the shine from his dress-shoes. With eyes as vivid as the morning he departed, he aimed the lavish key into the lock, contorting his shadowing body so that the lilac twilight could assist. With the heave, his bags were dumped over the threshold, before the Crimson Angel betrayed that not even he was above a homely, hearty sigh of relief. Pushing the bulky, protective door closed, Angelo flicked his residence's hallway lights on, bringing his extravagant decor and possessions to life in front of him. But despite the desire to relax and recover, his highly-tuned senses noticed the lack of a heavy door clunk. Instead, familiar footsteps clapped against the floorboards. 'Look, I know I should have sai-' the Deville went to greet the K-Wolf with, hands raised as if to halt her thundering strides. She looked as if the rain should be pouring back out of her drenched clothes as steam, such was the heated passion in her movement. Teeth ground together, and fingernails began to dig into palms. 'Why were you there?' furious words demanded to know, trying to storm in through Angelo's ears and force the answer from his mouth. 'I don't care about anything that's passed, just tell me why you were there.' As if his features were etched from Marble, he remained un-moved. 'Have you been drinking?' Angelo tried to dismiss the questioning with. 'I've only ju-' With a tear clearing the steep climb over each prominent cheekbone, Wolfenden jabbed a finger of accusation his way. 'I SAW you there,' she seethed, erratically pacing before him, before zeroing her focus in on his icy blue eyes. 'So don't lie to me, Deville.' 'Well, since we're no longer on first name terms...' he avoided being sucked into the frenzied interrogation by casually remarking. 'Where exactly was I meant to be, Wolfenden?' 'You don't want to joke around with m-' Knowing that arguing with Karina was akin to engaging in a headbutting contest with a solid wall, the Crimson Angel flung his arms out to his sides in an extravagant gesture. 'Where?' Deville again prompted, refusing to lose his cool. 'THE FUCKING HOSPITAL, AND YOU KNOW THAT!' Kari all but spat in his face. Despite the need of magnification best provided by an electron microscope to spot it, Angelo's head ever so slightly cocked to the side, accompanied by his right eye narrowing under the weight of its furrowing brow. 'Max got beaten unconscious last night, and left for dead in an alleyway,' stone-cold words stated through wavering, dry lips. 'They fractured his skull, broke his jaw, his arm, and three ribs.' Angelo lowered his arms, and relaxed back from his dominant stance. 'They didn't take his wallet, watch, anything,' she continued, burning emerald eyes making sure her words were drilled right into Angelo. 'If a paramedic on his way home didn't go by the alleyway, he'd of died of exposure.' 'I'm sorry,' was the instinctive response from the Deville. 'Really.' Words wavered as pained tears obstructed their delivery. 'No witnesses, no fucking chance of the police doing anything,' Kari managed to get out before sharply sniffing the tears back with. 'Even the boot print they left on him is way too common to help.' Remaining silent, Angelo began to foster a sneaking suspicion that he was soon to have something asked of him. Something that would start wheels in motion that he'd hoped would never have the brakes removed. 'They can't do anything,' she then looked back up to Deville with, steeling herself. 'But they're not you.' 'Hey,' Angelo used to halt her erratic, 100 mile and hour thoughts. 'I'm n-' 'If you won't do anything,' Kari then decreed, already losing her battle against the anger that rampaged inside. 'I'll do it myself.' 'What is it that you think I do, exactly?' the curious Deville demanded to know, adding an insulted inflection to his words. 'You're Angelo Deville,' she spat out with fury as eyes once again began to shimmer with tears. 'People don't look at you the same as everyone else. They smile that little more, but don't mean a bit of it. So don't try to pass yourself off as some ordinary guy in a snappy suit, because I've seen it.' 'You seriously think that I'm capable of all the things that are associated with Angelo Deville?' he pressed on, eyebrows gravitating towards the bridge of his nose in anger. 'I'm in the wrestling business, Kari. I'm on the road with PRIME most of the year, so where exactly do I find the time to run this supposed criminal empire, and now hit man agency?' 'Loo-' she tried to force herself back into the conversation with, but Deville was having none of it. 'Yeah, I talk it up when someone gets in my face,' he lowered his voice to explain, breathing deeply to calm himself. 'But out of everyone, I didn't expect you to believe I'm actually CAPABLE of those things.' 'Then why all the locked doors?' she hit back with. 'Why does everything in your life have a password, a code, a card-swiper?' 'What, because there are things I like to keep private, I have some deep, dark secret?' went the Deville's appeal, accompanied by a disappointed shake of the head. 'Seriously, Kari, if not letting somebody in is what constitutes something sinister, I think you've got everyone beaten. I barely know you more now, after everything, than I did those years ago when we first met.' 'And when did we first meet, exactly?' Karina hissed back as an accusation, wanting to know when the name of Deville began entwining with her own. 'How do you know my father? And how did someone from the wrestling promotion YOU worked for somehow, out of every gym in the north-east, pick the one I was in for all of 20 minutes? Was that discovery and an instant place on the Pro Wrestling Circuit all just chance?' Just as their butting heads were almost nose to nose, he broke away, thumping the heel of his fist against one of the doors that aroused Kari's suspicion. 'You want to know what's behind the door?' the Crimson Angel hurriedly flicked the subject of conversation back with, nodding to the slab of heavy oak. 'Do you think you can handle the horrors you think are hidden behind here?' 'Yeah,' she proclaimed. 'Go for it. I'm ready.' Striding over to him, she stood, hands behind her back, bracing herself for the sight. 'So what is it you're ready for then, Kari?' he enquired as the key turned the hefty lock. 'You think this is the way to my dungeon? Or are you expecting to see some guy hanging from his feet with his mouth taped and hands bound? Is that who... no, is that what you think I am? Because if it is, now is the time to say so.' Her silence told of no objections, prompting Deville to hurl the door open. Kari winced as the handle jabbed through the unfortunate wall, but as her curious eyes opened, they were met only with the sight of a study. Slow, cautious paces took her into the room, where no instruments of torture were to be found, only leather-bound volumes of classic literature. To emphasise his point, Deville flicked the light on, revealing all the hidden corners, whose only secret revealed were a handful of photos, and several colourful mineral-ore paperweights. 'It's... I...' escaped as pale bemusement swept down her previously assured face. 'No,' he cut her stumbled apology off with. 'Don't insult me with that shit.' With defeated steps backward, Wolfenden returned to the hallway, feeling the jab to her pride stirring deep in her stomach. 'The world isn't some fairytale, Kari,' began the Deville's harsh lecture. 'You can't just stamp your feet and have a tantrum to make things better. There are bad people, and because of that, bad things happen. I'm sorry about what happened to your brother, but grow the hell up. Go back to the hospital and be there for him, not here with me, asking for things that only happen in your little dream world.' 'I just... I tho-' Karina mumbled, head bowed as she grasped aimlessly for the right words. 'Now get out,' he ruthlessly ignored her submissive body-language to demand. Just as he knew she would, Karina stormed out, her usual reflex reaction to being caught in checkmate. Hearing the door slam, Angelo took laboured paces around the study's desk, before his body thumped down into the leather chair. To the ceiling his eyes gravitated, vessels dilated to make them blood-shot. Loosening his tie, the Crimson Angel took a deep breath, knowing that the K-Wolf would stand by her previous threat. That meant him doing nothing would lead to a whole lot of everything. 'Fuck.' And none of it good. IV Karina Wolfenden's pocket hung heavy. Paranoia made it feel as if the weight, and all the suspicion with it, would overpower her belt and sent her cargo pants dropping around her ankles. With every step it thumped against her thigh, and it was a raging battle against subconscious instinct not to pat it every 5 seconds to make sure nothing showed. The streets which she stalked were ominously empty, and even the stars had deserted the sky, leaving only a blanket of deep, bruised purple above. A pair of mirrored sunglasses sat atop the bridge of her freckled nose, and not for the purpose of protection against the harsh streetlights. Below them, a dark blue scarf hid the bottom portion of her face, only a few slithers of steam limply escaping from each breath. Through the blocks she snaked, sticking to shadow, looking up and around as if a vulnerable lamb who'd strayed from the flock. But behind the mirrors, under the loose clothing, she was anything but. 'C'mon,' she urged, dared. Her once frantic heartbeat had settled into a heavy, rhythmic beat, forming a tune with her heavy, attracting footsteps. Like a fly thumping into a web, or a struggling duckling's splashing on the surface, she wanted to draw out the predators, only for them to wish she'd displayed nature's danger signals of red and yellow. The feelers were out, tempting them in. The hairs on the back of her neck then stood up, triggering an icy shiver down her spine. She'd got a bite. The footsteps mirrored hers, settling into her pace. Resisting looking around, Karina simply slowed, again giving a dumbfounded look either side of her, before stamping a foot in lost, aimless frustration. Pursing her lips under the scarf, Wolfenden turned into an alley and slowed down even further... then let her shoulders subside in a sigh of disappointment as she heard the footsteps disappear off into the distance. Kicking an empty soda can down the alley in disappointment at not landing her catch, she couldn't react in time as thunderous footsteps rushed up behind her. Before Karina could plunge a desperate hand down into her concealing pocket, both her arms were restrained, high up by the shoulder to stop her squirming free. Instinctively, her feet thrashed around, pummelling the assailant's shins, before another darting figure was born of the alley's shadows, binding her ankles in his grasp. 'Get the fuck off me!' she roared, mustering tones far too rasping to be labelled a scream. Only able to struggle by tossing her head back and snapping her waist back and forth, Wolfenden was hurriedly carried to the mouth of the alley, where a black Mercedes, its windows tinted, skidded to a halt. With scorched types steaming in the bitter night, a rear door was flung open, and the reluctant K-Wolf was bundled into the back, sandwiched either side by her captors. Defiantly, a foot crashed through the corner of the back window, but as the throttle was slammed down, screeching types gained traction on the rubber they'd laid down, and the Merc sped off into the night. With the sounds of the erratic driving lost in the distant night, another pair of footsteps could be heard. Shiny toes almost displayed the reflection of the man above as they nudged the glass, pushing it down into the sewer. 'You sure you're doing this alone?' a gravely voice asked, sounding as if glass had to be crunched and swallowed to produce such a sound. 'Yes,' a lone word answered him, delivered with such assurance that you'd believe his vocal cords were akin to the pied-piper's pipe. 'I shouldn't have even involved you this much. This isn't your business, and if everyone goes all-in... this thing will blow up in everyone's faces.' 'Who was she anywa-... wait...' the gravely voice then paused, words dropping deeper into his echoing throat. 'This doesn't have anything to do wit-' 'Yes,' the silver tongue repeated. 'Okay,' replied a grumble, delivered snappily, as if the man in possession of the voice didn't want to hang around a second longer than he already had. 'I'm gone. I'd wish you good luck, but I ain't even involving myself that much.' No more words were exchanged between the pair, all that was heard being the metallic clunk of a mechanism readying itself, and two sets of footsteps going in very different directions.
