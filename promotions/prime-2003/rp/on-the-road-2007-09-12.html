On the road

12 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/12/07 Titan leaned back, pondering his next move. He had been in hiding for a few weeks now, trying to keep a step ahead of Yelemench, the last member of the Cult of the Poison Tree that wasn't in prison or dead. That fiasco at the safe house had been fairly wicked, ending with an iron lady setting the gas in the kitchen on fire to kill a whole mess of those deranged bastards. He reached forward and picked up his coffee cup, entirely too small for his big hand and took a sip. It was a hazelnut blend, not his favorite, but not awful. Yelemench was desperate to prove himself to a man serving two life sentences for conspiracy to commit murder, attempted murder, other charges involving the word 'murder' and drug trafficking. It had been made clear to the jury that this man could control a large group if he were free and so they decided to keep him locked up for a long, long time. But Yelemench had somehow slipped through the net. Best Titan could figure out, he simply went back to being his medical license and ducked the investigation. Up until recently. His psychological profile fit the the behavior; trying to please a mentor figure, even after such was pointless. He set the mug down, but as he did, he saw the red dot trace over the white plaster. He shoved away from the desk where he had strewn papers about and rolled onto the floor as a rifle shot cracked through the window and into the wall, not far from where Titan's chest had been. The giant moved away from the window and went for his pistol. He drew it from his duffel bag, knowing full well it wouldn't help against a man with a scoped rifle. He couldn't make for the door; this hotel room was cheap and the door was right in line with the window. He scanned the room for a moment and shoved the weapon back in the bag. He grabbed the mattress and heaved, throwing it in front of the window, then dove back to the floor. Just as he had expected, more shots blasted through. He had gambled that the shooter, it had to be Yelemench, wasn't experienced and would think Titan was immediately going for the door. When the shots stopped, Titan counted a total of 8 rounds fired including the first, he went for the door. So it was a magazine of eight rounds in an inexperienced man's hands. He put down his shoulder and barreled out, smashing the bullet-riddled door into the hallway. His duffel bag dangled from his fist, and he reached in and pulled out the panic button. The small device blinked when he pressed the button and he knew in about ninety seconds, he'd have FBI agents leading him out. But he didn't waste time. He hung a left, then took the stairwell to move down towards the exit to the building away from the shooter's vantage point. It was then that other men crowded around him and smashed through doors to a waiting Suburban. Titan took a long step and basically threw himself into the vehicle, knowing full well that the glass was bulletproof, but he had those precious ten seconds of vunerability between the building and the car. He lay down on the seat, though, trying to make himself as hard to see as possible as the SUV tore out of the parking lot and headed west. The contingency was that if things went south in North Carolina, he'd be heading towards Tennessee. Unfortunately, Titan didn't think that would make a whole lot of difference. He lay there in the seat for an hour before sitting up and probably belting himself in. The two agents in the front looked back at him, then resumed watching the road. * * * * “Where is she?” Titan asked angrily. His big hands were curled into fists. “I don't know,” the agent replied calmly. “I haven't been told.” “Look,” Titan replied, “I'm not trying to be difficult. I just want to know if she's alright.” “I know that, sir,” the agent replied, staying professional. “I don't know if she is or not.” “How is it that you don't know?” Titan growled. “What kind of communication is going on here?” “Sir,” the smaller man replied, sweat beading on his brow, “they're incommunicado at the base. They're trying to make sure that no one knows they're there.” Titan turned away and dropped unceremoniously onto the couch in the small apartment. This room had only one window, with the blinds shut, so Titan took a deep breath. They had taken his pistol and so now, he could only react to danger with his panic button or his fists. The smaller man took a quick survey of his protectorate and stepped out of the room. The couch cracked loudly in the middle and dropped to the floor. Titan's face painted with aggravation. “Typical.” “Surely not,” another voice answered. Titan threw himself forward and spun, coming up with his hands spread wide. Nothing to do... maybe... maybe... Profile 21 looked at him quizzically. “Did they take your weapon?” he asked in that infuriatingly dry voice. “Took you that long to figure it out?” “Indeed,” came the response. 21 strolled in calmly, attired in a black jumpsuit with pockets on the chest, legs, arms... just about anywhere there could be pockets. A thick tactical belt rested on his waist with a small pistol holstered in cross-draw fashion. Titan knew that 21 said it was the fastest way to draw a pistol kept on the belt. “Your family and Stephanie are fine.” Titan looked around nervously. “What did you do with the agents?” “Me? Nothing. They're all in the other room, looking over maps.” “How did you find me?” Titan asked, taking a deep breath. “We've been on the move for a few days now.” “Suburbans aren't the most common vehicles in the world. They really ought to try something else, even if they have to cram you in the trunk.” Titan chewed that thought for a moment. It had merit, even if he didn't look forward to it. “Where's Yelemench?” Titan said, switching gears. 21 looked at him for a long moment. “About a day behind you. I'm not entirely sure how he's keeping up, but it doesn't seem like he's actually sleeping.” * * * * Titan took as deep a breath as he could. It was cramped, the trunk on a Pontiac Grand Am, but it was just large enough to hide in if he curled up. Just. He'd been cramped up back there for three hours, but the fact that he used to sleep in a Humvee helped him a bit. He'd fade in and out, snap back to consciousness when the car slowed and then doze again when it maintained speed. Once, he heard an exchange that worried him. A police officer had apparently stopped them at a checkpoint. He held his breath for some reason, then realized what he was doing and carefully breathed through his nose. “Where ya' headin'?” the cop asked in a distinct Southern drawl. “North, heading towards Indiana,” the agent in the drivers' seat responded. “Federal tags. You boys IRS or somethin'?” Titan nearly bit out a curse. They were using a car with federal tags? Might as well stamp a sign on it, then, given that Yelemench had been able to hang with them. “Nope,” the man replied. “Got liberty off the base, goin' to visit the family for a few days.” “Have yourself a good time,” the officer replied. The car lurched back into motion and Titan let out a relieved sigh. The upright portion of the back seat in front of his face came down. “Worried about something?” the agent riding shotgun asked. “Us in a car with federal tags,” the big man replied. “I'm not used to being in dangerous situations where I don't have a measure of control, but that's just dumb.” “Not with this cover story.” “Your confidence fills me with relief. Speaking of which, when can I get out of here of and hit a bathroom, get something to eat, see my own feet, that kind of thing?” “Give us an hour,” the driver replied. “I want to put some distance between us and that cop.” Titan gave a small nod, all that he was capable of, and the seat went back up. All he could do was wait. His family and Stephanie were safe. Yelemench wasn't sure where they were. But he kept thinking about the fact that PRIME had a pay per view coming up. He was in the main event. This was no way to train for that sort of thing. But at this point, he just wanted to get there. * * * * Titan sat in the newest of the unremarkable apartments and pondered. It was two weeks into his flight from the man trying to kill him. Here he sat in a beat-up recliner, looking at the wall and letting the gears turn. Movement had gone from Georgia to the Carolinas and now into Tennessee. He was, as near as he could tell, an hour east of the Sevierville/Pigeon Forge area, a tourist hotspot. He was sitting in the mountains, in a small town where there was very little to distinguish it from other small, mountain towns. And he thought. Yelemench was obviously obsessed. 21 said the man hadn't been sleeping, which meant that gave him more pursuit time, but how was he following them? How was he able to find where the agents brought him consistently? There was some relief. His family and girlfriend were safe on the base in Quantico, Virgina. He'd finally managed to get that much, and he was relieved. Trying to get at anyone there with hostile intentions, especially while you were on the FBI Most Wanted list, was like trying to count sand with leather gloves on. There was no way to do it. Then, a thought hit him. The FBI might be trying to get Yelemench to follow him so that they could a definitive collar, especially in the act so he could be put away permanently. He knew it was viable; hell, he'd done it himself in the past. But doing it without telling the subject? The thought gave him chills. That and not having a pistol were not the most comforting thoughts to be having. A soft sound brought his head around. The door was to his left, up a short hallway. The hallway fed down into a living room and branched off to the kitchen connected both, just to the right of the hallway from where he sat. Carefully, he stood out of the chair and crept up the hallway as quietly as his bulk would allow. There was a piece of paper on the floor, folded in half. Titan took a step back into the kitchen and opened it. Yelemench on the way up, armed. Agents two minutes behind. Titan didn't need a name to know that precise hand. His cold blue eyes turned towards the door and he carefully positioned himself in the kitchen, just beyond the door and picked up one of the cheap glasses off the counter. The door was locked, but he knew that wouldn't stop this crazed little man. He heard footsteps, and the doorknob rattle. He then heard two steps and a crash as the cheap door fell inward. Titan gave it a half-second count and tossed the glass into the living room. It shattered with a brilliant sound and it drew the man's eyes like a magnet. He brought the shotgun in his hands around and pulled the trigger. Titan's eyes flashed to the living room where the blast hit the wall. It was small and put a whole through the exterior wall, so it had to be slugs. He moved quickly, diving at Yelemench. It seemed to happen in slow motion as his Krav Maga training kicked in. He controlled the barrel of the gun away from him, upward. Yelemench kept one hand on the trigger, trying to get another shot off, but his other hand couldn't get the pump action of the weapon to move. Titan's grip was right in place beneath the pump's grip, and his other hand reached for the man. He pulled the weapon towards him, still pointed away, and slammed a big fist into the small Indian man's body. He grunted, and his bloodshot eyes strained in their sockets. The look he gave Titan was pure fanaticism, and the big man almost took a step back in the face of that kind of hatred. He instead set his jaw and hit him again. Yelemench kicked at Titan's knee, smacking into his shin, and the big man grunted. That hurt, even with adrenaline flowing. He grabbed Yelemench's shirt and pivoted, flinging him away and into the kitchen, where he hit the counter and his head ricocheted off the fiberboard cabinents, smashing them in. Two more cheap glasses, probably the grand total of glassware in this rathole apartment, fell on him. Titan took the shotgun in his fist and flung it at the window in the living room. It shattered the glass and fell three stories to the parking lot. Titan's eyes found Yelemench again, only to find the man desperately clawing at a pistol tucked into his belt. Titan moved in again, putting one hand on top of the butt of the gun to keep it from being drawn, and smacked his head into Yelemench's nose. The little man desperately punched away at Titan's body, trying to drive him away. Titan knew he could take what the little man had, but he couldn't continuously control him as well as he'd like. This one would have no quit in him and would only stop when Titan was dead. Instead, Titan tried to wedge him against the counter and wait for the agents. He underestimated how fanatic the little man was. He bundled his legs and pushed away from the wall, stumbling Titan and him both back into the hallway. Again and again, he kicked off the walls, keeping Titan off-balance. He was also still working at the pistol, trying to get it free, but Titan had secured his grip on not only the gun, but the belt. He did lose his balance though. The wrestling ring actually gave him something there in that moment. As he began to teeter, he turned and popped his hips. His grip on the man's belt and the arm wrapped around his head kept the throw under control, but the odd version of a suplex threw Yelemench hard, and brought him down... right into the shattered glass of the cup and window. They hit and Titan felt it bite into his shoulder. Yelemench screamed as long shards dug into his back and legs. In that moment, he let go of the gun and Titan rolled away, taking the small Glock pistol with him. As calmly as he was able, he ejected the magazine and pulled the slide to eject the round still there. With that, he tossed the gun aside and placed the ammunition on the kitchen counter, careful to keep an eye on the cultist. Three agents came through the door, standard issue sidearms at the ready. The looked to see Yelemench on the floor and Titan standing in the kitchen, his shoulder bleeding and breathing hard. * * * * Titan finally got a decent hotel, a Holiday Inn, and enjoyed a hot shower. Well, as much as he could. Since it was a standard room, he basically had to sit in the tub to wash his shoulder after the glass had been extracted, but it still felt good. He stepped outside and toweled off. The thought ran in his mind over and over again. He though he was done with this. He'd seen it too many times where thinking the job was done got you killed. And actually seen it, not “saw it in a movie.” He pulled on a fresh shirt and a pair of fatigues and stepped out of the bathroom. The curtains were wide and 21 sat before the window. Titan's breath caught for a moment before he recognized the small man, and he sat down on the bed, unconcerned. “There are no more,” 21 said. “How do you know?” “There were all of four other members of the Poison Tree that weren't caught. They never will be.” Titan measured him with a long look. “Fine.” “You're going to this Roulette thing, aren't you?” Titan just nodded. “I understand.” Titan shot him another look. “You're not part of this life so much anymore. You can still do it, but you want to be the best in what your new life is.” His new life. Up until now, Titan hadn't thought about that. But he liked the idea. A new life. With that thought, he reached into a pocket of the fatigues for something he'd been keeping with him for a month now. The black felt box met his touch. His new life...
