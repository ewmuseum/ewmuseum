Chapter Zero, Prelude

4 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/04/07 ***June 10th, 2007*** Lindsay A cross country flight, with a three hour time change over, is rarely an easy task for someone to take. But she had packed up all her belongs, as minute as they were, and gotten on that one way ticket out of Hell's Kitchen. Onward, was she, over to the land of sunshine and Hollywood Boulevard. And it was of no consequence that when she packed what she owned that she had to carry with her shards of broken potential and promises left lingering from straying far from the womb. And so it was, that cursed blood that flowed through her veins, which made a genetic flaw spelling abject failure through her. Had it really been that long? She thought, shuffling slightly in her all too cramped space in coach. It had been. She was one of the middle children, and with that came a sense of despondency. Like many of the children under the Christian iron clad will of their parents, she took her place beside her other middle brother, Brandon...and went against the grain of what was expected. No ritzy college degree, just life as a hunger artist. And for years, the middle children of the Youngblood had a sort of relationship that kept them closely bonded. It was Brandon, after all, who had given her some weed after a some farm work out in the country in that matter of fact way. Not necessarily callous, she recognized. But at the same time, it was not conventional love. It was then, under the Texas skyline, that the pair spoke in frank terms about their stockade at the Youngblood estate. Crushed under tyranny, they believed. Candace turned her nose up at the both of them, ever mommy's little darling. And Tommy, well, he took Brandon's place as the prodigal son. The one who kept his mouth shut...the one who worked the grindstone like a slave, always fearful of letting his family down. Being middle children, Brandon and Lindsay were black sheep in a lineage of purported prototypical success. Even if his name was to be spoken by millions upon millions, his name would be but a harsh whisper amongst his former kin. And no matter how much Brandon made, or how much he helped, there was still a stain upon his house that he could never remove. Even in being peculiar, Lindsay saw the way they spoke of her brother...her best friend. And it caused a great deal of pain. At first. But the mob mentality that insofar had ostracized him, she dared not become as he. So she made her amends as best as she could, hoping with bated breath that she could escape that dubious fate. She reached into her jean pocket, pulling out a small oval shaped cellular phone. The obsidian coloration was marred by finger print all over, and the plastic display plate which showed the time had a small gash on its surface. Using both her hands, she flipped the phone open and began punching in numbers with faded black fingertips. Resting into her seat, she tilted her head to the side to allow herself a more cordial comfort. The volume had been turned to max, allowing the passenger sitting next to her (an oriental man with a beige duster that smelled like cabbage) to at least make out some of what was going on. He sharply pulled away, his eyes shooting at her in an annoyed fashion. "Is there a problem sir?" It came out a little too harsh, but she did not care. New Yorkers rarely gave two fifths of a shit what someone else thought anyway. She did it in many ways to see if he would shoot his mouth off. But he never let on his disgust in the verbal, rather he just groaned and looked at her through those slanted little fucking eyes she so despised. She hoped Los Angeles had a lot fewer chinks. She focused in on the phone in her hand, resting against her ear. It was at about it's fifth ring, and usually an answering machine would have gotten a hold of this at about this time. She roughly tongued the pair of silver lip rings from both lower ends of her mouth, sucking her lip in ever so slightly. It never dawned on her that her aloof behavior and her somewhat freakish (by modern standards, not Lindsay's) physical appearance. Not many carried that somewhat post-punk look very well, but she had plenty of practice with it. Her blond and red hair had been slightly feathered, grown slightly above her chin. After having gotten her head shaved, it took some getting used to with the sensation of hair tickling her ears. Another ring, and then the all familiar voice of her brother. "You know who this is...leave a message at the beep." So like him. She rolled her eyes, shuffling precariously towards Cabbage Patch, waiting for the beep. "You too close." It came out as if it was all one syllable. "Hello?" The voice was soft and reassuring, one she was ALSO familiar with. "You so rude. I call, get other seat." Multitasking was always a bitch for Lindsay Youngblood. "Hellooo! Kinda on the phone right now." He sneered towards her arrogance, violently shifting himself to look outside towards the runway from his small window. Rolling her eyes, she made sure to distance herself from him a little bit if only to solve this whole thing peaceably. Seven hours in a cramped space was too long no matter what the context. "Hello?" The voice from the other end came again, causing Lindsay to respond in an agitated manner. "You hear me?" "Hello?" Like a fuckin' parrot. "It's Lindsay. Lindsay, Missy. I'm on the plane, can you hear me?" She, unlike her older brother, had an off switch, or some way of cloaking her mood swings. "Oh...hey Lindz. Who was that you were just talking to...you sound kinda upset." Given who she had been married to (and been back together with), Melissa knew those old Youngblood anger trips when she heard them, even during their dawn. "It's the guy sitting next to me. I'm like...dude...we got such little space to begin with, you know? And it's like...eh...I dunno. I'm kinda sick of this already." She pulled the phone away from her ear, feeling a bit apologetic. Unlike Brandon, remorse was quick to follow an outburst, no matter how minute it really was. "Sorry." He deflected it, merely grumbling and saying something in his native tongue. Bringing the phone back up to her ear, she caught the tale end of what was being said to her. "-could be driving here." "OH I know...right? It's just...why does it have to be so far?" "Because we like warm weather here?" Melissa giggled sweetly on the other end. Lindsay always felt that this little girl was too good for an abrasive bastard like her brother. He's not that bad, she assured herself almost instantly after the negative thought crossed in her mind. "So...you still picking me up?" "You want to talk to your brother?" She retorted. And she winced at the notion. After all, Melissa knew. Brandon? All he thought was that this was going to be a week long visit. He had no idea that she had a more permanent idea in mind. Not that it would be all bad. But she knew that Brandon would not take kindly to not even as much as being asked to fiscally support someone when he was forced into a corner. She remained quiet. "It's not right Lindz. You know he'd be elated if you told him you got accepted into UCLA...you know...before you were coming over to get all the nuanced stuff taken care of." Of course, that guilt came through and hit Lindsay in the pit of her stomach. Even when she rebelled, she STILL wanted to be accepted by her family. "I dunno. I mean, he wasn't happy with how things went down years ago with Kyle. I don't know if he's going to like the fact that we broke off the engagement and went our separate ways." She always had a ready made excuse. I don't want him to gloat. She hated it when someone held it over their head that they made poor decisions. Brandon, being the person that vicariously approved her living arrangements and helped her make ends meet...would take the stand like a father, not like the close friend she once had. "Why you say that?" "Because of you--" She caught herself mid sentence, and an uncomfortable silence enveloped the conversation. Because you forgave HIM...why should I not forgive mine? Seconds crept by in agonizing fashion, almost broken by a sick sordid tedium that would make EVERYTHING uncomfortable. They had become good friends, but this...this was a touchy subject...and would be until they were both ash. "Look...I am not going to tell you how to live your life. Just...we're going to be happy to see you. That's all. Brandon hasn't seen you since the funeral." And THAT made matters even worse, for her. "You know, Melissa...the plane is about to take off. I'll call you when we make it to LAX, alright?" "Alright. HEY!" Lindsay thought the conversation was over, but apparently it wasn't. She sighed. "What?" "Don't fill up on that airplane food. Brandon's going to be cooking some manicotti tonight and he ALWAYS makes way too much." Lindsay laughed, easing off her trigger with a satisfied smirk. Always full of surprises, Brandon. One moment he's bland, the next he's cooking and reading classical literature. She knew this because even through it all, they still remained in decent contact over the phone, exchanging calls at least once a week. "It any good?" "Depends on how tired he is." "Alright." Lindsay couldn't help but suppress a giggle. "I'll see you soon. Love you." "Love you too. Good luck on your voyage." "Bye." And with a flip, the phone went dead. She rested her head against the cushion of the chair, her eyes fluttering in a haze as she allowed herself some sleep for the first time in what seemed like days. Al Goddammit...what the fuckin' shit. He was mere feet away from it, his hands still twitching, the pit in his stomach growing to epic proportions. It threatened to burst. This embarrassment, this CONTINUED EMBARRASSMENT! One punch, that was all he was worth. One punch and a man who he had bled and damn near died beside walked out of his life, not wanting to see him ever again. All because of the habit. Motherfuckin' shit...goddamn poison...ruined my life. And he was not far off. He no longer had the safety net that ensured his failures would not be as damaging. Brandon was a cruel bastard when he had to be as well, making sure to put a moratorium on Alastair Nagatsuka wrestling anywhere he had ties to. That pretty much made him persona non grata in the eyes of many who would book him for spectacle stateside. But it also extended onward to Japan. They'd listen to him before one of their own. Fuckin' rich. They had worked as a tag team, then as a solo act in the land of the Rising Sun. When Brandon's stock had plummeted, Al was the one there to pick him up. Now? When Al needed those dates to pay rent, they were being withheld over some petty grudge. When the fuck did he become the moralist?! Alastair was bullheaded about it. And it wasn't exactly as if the two hadn't supplemented their income in the past without a little bit of back door dealing on the road. For young kids in pain, the chronic has many benefits, and can catch a decent buck. But Al knew. Deep down, he knew. He knew he had gotten in way over his head, and this was his way out. He paid off his debt, and let the vultures do what they desired with his friend, not exactly caring what THAT was at this time. Al knew, as well, that this was a fatherly gesture from a man who had been thrust into the role within his own family. That severe actions called for stiff penalties. He reached into his pocket, grabbing a Marlboro menthol cigarette from a soft pack. Lighting it up, he waited outside the clinic KNOWING that this would be his second (...third...forth?) stint in rehab. And even though he hated Brandon at that moment, he still felt this tinge of guilt for having disappointed him. Throwing the cigarette down almost as soon as he lit it, he pushed through the twin glass doors, looking for the answers to a shattered existence. Brandon Boredom set in, the fan on his desk swaying to and fro. His desk was clean for once. Grabbing at his neck, he grunted. Even before he had gotten surgery, the amount of stress that was piling up into his tense muscles made him want to cut open his flesh...to relieve the pressure. Brandon would bleed like a hemophiliac, like he had a tendency of when he was inside a squared circle. The radio was on just loud enough to make out "Saint Of Me" by a band he despised; The Rolling Stones. Ain't Zeppelin, that's for damn sure. There was a small triangular block of parmesan over top of a piece of waxed paper. Brandon reached for the small knife he had left right by the cheese, grabbing on to the slice and cutting of a small section from the tip. He tossed it up into the air, catching it in his mouth. He did not savor it, like a man of his stature (fiscally) should. He was still very much a brute, a man who thought himself refined but in reality was as haggard as a cowboy on the plains of the old west. Not half bad. He thought to himself, chewing on it like it was a piece of beef jerky. On the desk was a glass of orange juice, which he downed in one gulp. For someone who had trouble breathing without some form of pain in the neck, he wanted to get his vitamin C in pure liquid form. Usually, rather than cheese, an orange would be sliced into sections that he would put into his mouth like he was still in grade school. It was going on midnight, and Melissa surely had picked up his sister by now. Wonder what she's gonna bleed me for now. It was not like Brandon was not completely justified in his thinking. Since his father had died, his mother had gone into a shell. Early retirement and a decent severance package with the life insurance made it so she could live the rest of her days comfortably. But her will to live...that made it seem like that would not last that long. And when that came, it would be up to Candace to slice up the inheritance from a modest family. Not because she deserved to, but because she was the oldest. It was Brandon who had to pay Lindsay's bills as she tried to be a painter in the Big Apple. Who was throwing two hundred dollar checks to his younger brother Tommy (who resented his brother, but still took the money) to make sure he could live well on campus at Texas Christian. Dunno why he transferred from Texas...he wanted to play there for ages. And it was Candace, being the mother's daughter, who had to constantly nag Brandon about giving the widow someplace to live. It wasn't Candace who would take it upon herself. She was going through a messy divorce, and just KNEW how much her mother despised giving up on the marriage. After all, they had plenty of laughs about it when Brandon's marriage dissolved. Now that the shoe was on the other foot, Candace couldn't face her own hypocrisy. Brandon had to keep the family unit that had pushed him away together, even though they themselves could not help but look down at him. A sick sort of symbiosis. Even with Lindsay, there was a sense that she had turned on him, which made things all the more complicated between him. But he was not about to say anything. He didn't need the headache with all the people out there in the wrestling world wanting to beat the holy hell out of him. Which was self warranted, of course. Brandon could not deny that. But acting like a tank in a world where you are growing increasingly older is not necessarily the best course of action. The beatings added up. They always do. Standing up, he turned off the light over top of his desk. He let the fan run, even with the air conditioner. Something about the hum always had a calming influence. Wrapping the parmesan with the wax paper, he put it in a small zip lock sandwich bag and deposited it in the small steel refrigerator that sat against the far wall. He left going out the back door to the alley. He reached into the pockets of his cotton shorts, fumbling with a box of cigarettes. After one, he would probably go back in for a swig of orange juice, just to clear his throat. He thought he heard footsteps. Multiple footsteps. TH-WACK He crumbled, his neck already throbbing in a searing pain he had never felt before. His forehead hit the pavement, this head almost instantly gashed. Laughter followed. "You remember what you did...you fuckin' shit bag?"
