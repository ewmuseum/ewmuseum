Stains: Frome The End to the Beginning (Match RP vs. Karina)

6 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/6/06 Thursday, July 06, 2006. Night. The hall seemed to shrink on him, scraping his elbow across its stucco finish. In a frantic rush, he slammed his shoulder into the door and practically fell into the room. Surprised, and perhaps partially petrified, several of the occupants of the location froze in place, eyes widening to cartoonish proportions. When they spotted his crimson fists, their surprise turned to terror and quickly, they jumped out of the way, pushing themselves against sinks and stall doors. Two elder gentlemen bolted out of the room completely, pushing past the disturbance with a strong effort to avoid contact. Matt stumbled, his vision was narrow, not even noticing the confused and concerned men who dodged his staggered steps. All he saw was the distorted glow of ceiling lights and hazed image of intricate tiled floor laying a path to a row of white porcelain basins. In a jolted few steps, Matt grabbed the edge of the sink in the far corner of the room. With his left hand, he reached to turn on the faucet, but he was unable to turn on the water, his hand sliding right off the handle. Again he tried, this time with the other hand, but the result was the same. His palm slid around the crystal piece, leaving only small streams of red to follow the contours of the crystal’s design. “God… damn… IT!” Frustrated, he cursed, saliva flying from his mouth as he did so, splattering the small circular mirror over the sink. Through the mist of his angered cries, he could see the panic and fear in his own bloodshot eyes. It had been years since the last time it had happened. He returned his gaze downward to the sink, sandwiching the hot water handle between his forearms. In an awkward movement, he twisted the crystal cap by sliding his right arm forward and pulling his left towards him. A rush of water signified his success and immediately he shoved his hands under its flow. Feverishly, he rubbed his hands across each other, weaving fingers around palms, watching as the clear water that fell from above rolled off his fists with a faint red tint. The blood spilled into the bottom of the sink, swirling around the drain, but it was no use… his hands were already stained. * * * He was With Another Woman Since he had debuted in PRIME nearly a year ago in September, he had crossed paths with a lot of very different personalities. The Dual Halo alone had brought him into contact with nearly forty other superstars. Among them was his boss, Tyler Nelson, who had been one of the larger thorns in his side. For Nelson, an opportunity to make Matt’s life a living hell was an opportunity that couldn’t be passed up. He had even gone so far as to recruit someone very close to Matt’s life as is own hand-picked weapon. The ‘Supreme Machine’. Killean was the definition of the word ‘nemesis’. The two had been friends and enemies and had come close to colliding in a squared circle on numerous occasions. But for nearly four years… ‘close’… was all it had been. Their lives had been intertwined for ages, both at work and at home… but they had never officially collided. Soon enough, however, they would, and Matthew salivated at the thought… that was why he had wanted to battle the best… to stay sharp. For the time being, though, Killean was the enemy who had never truly been dealt with. His greatest battles had come with another. Karina Wolfenden had been the most unique opponent he had ever faced. Aside from her sometimes unorthodox style… there was a rare and strange connection between the two that pulled at his insides in a manner he couldn’t understand. The similarities between the two were startling… other than being different genders anyways. Both had begun their careers in PRIME on lengthy winning streaks. Both had won major events in the federation. Both had captured the Universal title before even their tenth match. And both had lost that title in an instant, before they’d even had the chance to call themselves champions. His first defining win in PRIME had come against the K-Wolf at King of Kings in the JITC tournament semi-finals. That match had launched him to the top. That victory had made him a name people took notice of. It had been, without question, one of the most grueling and intense events of his life, and it was one of the few times in his career where he had been surprised at his own success. Defeating Kari was no simple task and few could claim to have accomplished the feat. From that match had come a unique appreciation, perhaps even a sort of attraction. Few, if any, had pushed him that far, that hard, and in his world, he wasn’t used to women who enjoyed the rush of the fight. His wife had walked out on him because of the battles that occurred in the squared circle. He had an undying gratitude towards Karina for not only accepting his choice to fight, but sharing in the experience with him. The truth was, Matt had a great amount of respect and admiration for Kari. More so than just about anyone he had ever crossed paths with in the course of his existence. It was why the possibility of her joining the ranks of Nelson and Killean bothered him so much. In many ways, it felt as if another woman in his life was walking out on him. Without ever truly realizing it, he had grown close to Kari, and it now seemed as if she was slipping away from the recent life she had been such a large part of. The kick to the back of the skull at ReVolution 99 had hurt worse in ways other than a headache. He had been one of the few people in PRIME who seemed to understand Kari and actually connect with her… or at least, that was what he had believed. Still, he wanted to be close to her, wanted to trust in her… to the point he nearly felt guilty, as if her were betraying Mary, and maybe in some way, he was. But the truth, as he saw it, was that he owed a lot to Karina. She had, after all, been his beginning. * * * With those words, every last piece of the puzzle he hadn’t even been trying to build came together in his mind, completing the picture. His ignorance had been the containment for his rage and with the chains broken and the prison lifted, anger began to flow from brain to fists. Then his eyes went cold. “Is there a problem, Matt?” No response came and the man just shrugged his shoulders in unsure fashion. “What?” Still silent, Matthew just cracked his neck in a violent jerk to the right, then without the warning offered by wasteful declarations or the slow boiling of emotions, he swung his right hand, clenching a fist in mid-flight. The attack found its destination successfully, striking against the left cheekbone of his new acquaintance. Never once stopping to give it a second thought, or even a first thought for that matter, Matt threw another punch… then another and another. Four times in as many seconds, his fist collided with the skull of another human being. For a moment, he ceased the assault… but the hesitation was only to change up the manner of the violent act. This time, he threw a left and the impact sent the man tumbling to the cool cement of the sidewalk. “Jesus, stop! Please… stop..stop stop!” The words were lost under the sound of traffic and typical city disturbances. People somewhere a block over laughed hysterically. From far down the street, a man could be heard cursing at his wife about wasting money on a new purse. Car horns sounded here and there. All of it was enough to silence the screams. The words barely reached Matt’s ears in audible fashion, to the rest of the world… they had never been shouted. Looking down at the fallen man, immune to his pleas, Matt began to drive his foot into the torso. Several times, he pushed the sole of his shoe into the chest and stomach, stepping on ribs as if they were twigs under his feet on a stroll through the park. The man tried to defend himself, crossing his arms over his chest, his wrists taking the brunt of a few of the stomps, but it was little consolation. With his efforts being blocked, Matt abandoned the kicks and once again blasted away at the face with bare knuckles. He dropped to one knee, hovering over the man and threw a punch right into the nose. Immediately, it broke, and let loose a river of snot and blood. “…god..help…he…stop. Shit! Shit!” His fist connected once again with the face, the red fluid smearing his right hand, then his left. Each punch drew forth more of the mess, as it began to freely spill into the mouth of Matt’s victim. This fact quickly prompted the man to muzzle his own screams simply to keep his mouth closed to the foul tasting crimson concoction. A car shot down the street, the sound of tired on wet asphault meeting Matt’s ears as he continued to rain down bloody fists onto a bloody face. And as soon as the noise had struck him, as the car had sped into the distant night life…he stopped. Somehow, it seemed, the sound of soaked gravel churning under tires had caused him to suddenly notice the broken man he kneeled over. Matt unclenched his fists and studied the movement of the blood as it rolled down his fingers into his palm. “Oh jesus…jesus… thank… y…you were gonna… kill me. I though you were…” As the man opted to pass on cursing him for the assault in favor of thanking him for not ending his life, Matt took his gaze away from his hands and jumped to his feet. In a strange, perhaps even disturbing gesture, Matthew extended his right hand in aide. The man accepted, locking grips, as Matt used his strength to help him to his feet. And then, the calm demeanor disappeared entirely. Matt’s serenity during the attack, his peacefulness of his offer of help afterwards… it all vanished. Perhaps it had taken this long for the realization of what he had done to sink in… or maybe it had just taken this long for him to care. Without saying a word… without any verbal threats or any sort of apology, Matthew bolted back into the building he had exited just minutes earlier, leaving his luggage sitting on the curb. He knocked open the glass door and sprinted across the lobby in some sort of panic, barely able to remember the location of the restroom or to even follow the signs that directed him to its location. Through the lobby, he rounded a corner heading down a corridor that, as best he could recall, led to the restrooms. Along the way, he watched the blood slipping across his hands, paying no attention to his surroundings. Just the blood. The hall seemed to shrink on him, scraping his elbow across its stucco finish. In a frantic rush, he slammed his shoulder into the door and practically fell into the room. * * * Sometimes, he wished he would pay more attention to Scotty. Sure, his younger brother could be a real goof and a pain in the neck… but he’d cleaned his life up. And recently, more often than not, the things he had to say were true and made a helluva lot of sense. Had he listened to Scotty, he’d have apologized to Mary a long time ago. Had he listened to Scotty, he’d have gone on that cruise… the four of them together. Scotty, Laura, himself… and Mary. And his little brother truly did know what he was talking about. Scott seemed to have all the inside scoops. Probably because he’d stayed in contact with Mary. He had known about the little birthday party Mary’s parents had thrown for Georgie. He had known about Mary’s new book deal, and he’d known about the new man in her life. All those things had been news to Matthew when he’d heard them from Scotty. He’d warned his older brother that he should apologize, that he should quit wrestling. He’d informed Matthew that he should pick up a phone and at least call, that the book deal was a great opportunity for Mary, that the new guy was nothing serious… just a few lunches together. But it didn’t matter how right Scotty might be, Matt had gotten out of the habit of listening to his little brother a long time ago. * * * “Nice to meet you. Name’s Matt” He shook the man’s hand and forced a polite smile, then turned his attention back to the rather empty street outside the hotel and watched the occasional car roll by. “So you’re heading to the airport?” “Yeah.” Matthew answered but didn’t bother to turn in the direction of his new company. “Got a late flight to catch. Someplace I need to be Saturday night. The midnight flight was the cheapest.” “So is this trip business or pleasure?” The man took a sip from his drink. “Both.” “You travel a lot?” “Yeah.” Matt paused before saying anything else. It seemed that this guy liked to talk a lot, even about pointless things to complete strangers. Quite the opposite of himself, and quite annoying. “Actually, I haven’t even been home in about ten days.” “I travel a lot too.” In an exaggerated pose of deep thought, the man tilted his head, raised his eyes up as if looking for God in the black clouds, and rubbed the barely-there goatee on his chin. “Lets see… in the last month I’ve been in Chicago, New York, Boston…” “I’m sorry.” The man went on, ignoring the joke. “… Baltimore, here in Detroit, in Cleveland and in Columbus. I’ve probably only spent a total of about ten days home in Dayton this past month.” “You live in Dayton?” Now Matt turned his attention back to the man, giving him a mildly surprised look. “Yup.” He took the final sips of his drink and nonchalantly threw the cup into a nearby trashcan. “Anyways, I know what it’s like to travel a lot. Sometimes I’m home for awhile, sometimes work has me on the road all the damn time.” “I know how that can be.” “But the job does have its perks. Too much travel can be a pain in the ass, but it is nice to see the world. And you do get to meet a lot of people, including a gorgeous blonde who I’ve really hit things off with.” “Can’t complain about finding a great lady.” “No doubt.” The man laughed to himself. “Best part of it is… despite all the traveling I’ve been doing, I was fortunate enough to find a lady who actually lives in the Dayton area as well. So there wouldn’t even be any ‘long distance’ problems. She’s got a baby girl, but I can deal with that. Otherwise, I think I hit the jackpot on this one.” “By the way” Matt dropped the gym bag he had been holding slung over his shoulder and let it fall next to his suitcase. “how’d you meet this girl? What is it that you said you did for a living?” “I’m a writer. I met her through work. We have the same publicist.” With those words, every last piece of the puzzle he hadn’t even been trying to build came together in his mind, completing the picture. His ignorance had been the containment for his rage and with the chains broken and the prison lifted, anger began to flow from brain to fists. Then his eyes went cold. * * * She was With Another Man A little while ago… “Hahaha” Mary reached across the table and shoved the man across from her in the shoulder playfully. “You’re a dork, Chuck. You know that?” “Are you kidding? Being a dork is how i got my first novel published. Apparenlty, Americans love to laugh at stories written about dorks by actual dorks.” “So you’re telling me I should try writing my next book in dork mode?” “It’s worth a shot. Although I think your first published piece did well enough with the public.” It was his way. Chuck liked to slip in compliments whenever he got the chance. Mary had been told the moment Chuck had smiled at her during a meeting with their publicist that he was the type of guy that would fill her head with wonderful words, but his actions sometimes failed to live up to the same standards. So far, she hadn’t run into that problem, though they’d only been to two lunches together. This was there first dinner date… if you would even call it a date. She didn’t… she just wasn’t comfortable with the idea of dating another man still hoping for a phone call from Matthew. Still, Chuck called it a date. Anne called it a date. Everyone called it a date. Everyone except her. “Thank you.” It was her only response to the compliment, as she turned her attention to the salad sitting in front of her and casually stabbed a couple pieces of lettuce with her fork. Italian dressing dripped off the soaked leaves as she pulled them to her mouth. “Oops” She tried to multitask, chewing, talking and cleaning up the small mess all at the same time. “Need a hand?” “No, I got it.” Mary took her cloth napkin and wiped up the dressing that had splashed onto the table in front of her. While she rubbed the napkin across the spot, she lost focus of her surrounding and bumped her drink with her elbow. Quickly, she recovered, dropping the cloth in favor of saving the drink. She grabbed the glass with two hands, just a couple drops of water splashing over the edge. “Ha. This dinner is proving to be quite a difficult experience for you!” “Yeah.” It was the truth, though he probably didn’t know just how right he was. “Just clumsy tonight, I guess.” “Must have been something in the water causing it. I refuse to believe a woman as graceful as you could ever be clumsy.” He grinned and winked at her, but she just looked down, avoiding eye contact and smiling uncomfortably. “So, we still heading to a movie after dinner?” “Sure. There’s a couple of movies playing now that look pretty good.” “Well,” Chuck reached across the table and took Mary’s hands in his own, “what do you want to see, sweetie?” “I…” She stopped, played back the last moment. “I…. don’t really care. Whatever you want to see. Maybe a comedy.” Had she heard what she thought she had heard? “Go ahead and think about it for a minute. I’m going to run to the restroom. Be right back.” She excused herself and walked across the restaurant floor, weaving in and out of tables. The uneasy feeling that had crept over her didn’t shake off as she made the trip. It continued to hang heavy on her shoulders. Once to the restroom door, she pushed it open and stepped inside making her way to the nearest sink. She looked at her hands… just looked at them, then in the mirror, she studied herself and thought about a lot of things, but not long enough to make sense of any of them. She wasn’t sure what to do or say, or if she even wanted to go back to the table and spend any more time with Chuck at all. Things felt awkward… wrong… and mostly, confusing. Mary turned on the faucet and ran her hands under the water, massaging them back and forth until they felt clean. * * * Matt closed his cell phone and stuck it back into the pocket of his jeans. One of the things he’d learned in his time as a professional wrestler was that it was, at the least, far less of a pain in the ass to just call a taxi company and have them send a cab. Flagging down a taxi on the street could be all kinds of frustrating, and sometimes, could even take longer than calling for one. Besides, he wasn’t the type who liked to stand out on the sidewalk and jump with a death wish into oncoming traffic every time a cab drove past, waving his arms like a maniac and begging for a ride as if his life depended on it. Content with the ten minute wait the company had estimated it would be, Matthew picked up gym bag, pushing the strap across his shoulder, and grabbed the handle to his suitcase. After making sure everything was situated, he started across the hotel lobby, pulling his suitcase behind him, its tiny wheels skipping in the grooves of the tile floor. He stopped at the front desk and dropped off his key card. “Checking out. 224” He said. The receptionist smiled, thanked him for staying and waved. Matt waited for a few seconds, then remembered that he had paid upon checking in and that everything had been squared away with the simple act of turning in his room key. He smiled in a bit of embarrassment and waved back, then continued on his way, pushing open the door to step into the cool summer night. An evening rain had killed the heat of the afternoon and left the air filled with the smell of wet city life. A few sprinkles continued to fall, but Matt decided not to wait under the sanctuary of the building and headed for the street. He came to a stop at the edge of the road, right under a medium sized tree that’s soaked leaves provided a steady drip of water onto his shoulders. It didn’t bother him. He rather liked the rain. “Crummy weather we’re having, huh?” Matt turned and looked over his shoulder as a man came to stand next to him… a couple feet over, just out of reach of the tree’s leaking leaves. “I dunno. It’s not that bad.” He was in no desire to get into an argument about enjoyable types of weather with some stranger who had appeared out of nowhere. To Matt, he was just some man who wanted to make small talk. Tomorrow, he wouldn’t even remember the guy. Why bother debating the weather with him. “I’m just waiting for a taxi to the airport.” “Ahhh, yeah… I’m gonna flag one down and hit this restaurant across town that’s supposed to be really good.” The man switched a plastic cup he was holding over to his left hand, turned and extended his right. “By the way, I’m Charles. Friends call me Chuck. Pleasure to meet you.” “Nice to meet you. Name’s Matt” He shook the man’s hand and forced a polite smile, then turned his attention back to the rather empty street outside the hotel and watched the occasional car roll by.
