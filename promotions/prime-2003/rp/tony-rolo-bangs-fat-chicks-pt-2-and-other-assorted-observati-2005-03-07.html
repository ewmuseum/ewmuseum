Tony Rolo Bangs Fat Chicks, Pt. 2, and Other Assorted Observations From the Heart

7 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/07/05 The Irony.A man walks into a juke joint. He’s gone there ever since he could remember. He shakes some hands, smiles at a few familiar faces, and takes a stool at the bar. He asks the bar tender for a drink. “Sorry,” says Jasper, “those men in the corner say they don’t like you. They say if I give you a drink, they’ll kill you.” So the man shrugs. He takes a seat at the bar and lights up a cigarette. A moment later, he lays a pile of coins on the bar. The bar tender pours him a shot, and the man drinks it. Later that night, the men kill him where he sits. But not before he gets drunk. That was Emilio’s favorite bedtime story as child. His father was a bit of a roughneck, but loving nonetheless. His mother would tuck him into bed, pulling up the sheets before kissing him on the cheek. A moment later, his father would walk in, the familiar tang of his nightly coke and rum permeating through his breath as he spoke. He would tell the story, and Emilio would begin to drift off to sleep. His father would look down and shake his head in satisfaction as his son closed his eyes, the radiating grin on his face a testament to their family creed: Live by the sword, die by the sword. And they did. Years later, however, things have changed. The very story which put him to sleep, now causes Emilio insomnia. Restless nights of wrestling with the sheets have haunted him as he’s grappled with himself over his most recent decisions. Still, the enigmatic dilemma insists on rearing its nasty head, weeks after the decisions were already finalized. A duel with both halves of himself pursues, trying to decide whether to fight a fight that is, well, un-fightable...or move on to a better place. So the man at the bar still haunts him, thirty years later. It has become his story. The story of his life. But would fate dare haul off and kick him in the balls? So it appeared. For the hand of time now tickled him with the one story that was only told when it needed telling least, and now, when it needs telling most, the tellers are dead. Indeed, we all know how his family was murdered. The bottom line is, Shakespeare would cum in his pantaloons. Trials, Tribulations, and alas, Goodbyes. Perspiration. Inspiration. Desperation. What do all these “-ations” have in common? Emilio Rage stepping into the squared circle. Walking down the tunnel to the Dual Halo. Perspiration, as he sweat his fears out, driving away the demons of exhaustion to survive an impressive 102 minutes in the Dual Halo. Inspiration, in the form of a promise: the winner gets whatever he or she wishes for. This kept him on the narrow path to enlightenment up until the last seconds. And lastly, desperation. Desperately fighting his way to the final ten, clawing and crawling his way to the final four, then using his second, third, and fourth winds to advance to one of only two competitors left in the most grueling match in the known wrestling world... Of course, all only to be felled by a fresher, quick-footed predator. The K-Wolf. But for the past month, Emilio’s only “-ation” has been that of recuperation. Bruised ribs, broken ribs, dislocated this, that, and the other thing, and a sprained ankle on top...the injured body sundae with the works. But three medical appointment, dozens of iron pills to replenish the waning blood supply, and a month later, comes a refreshed, quicker, and smarter Emilio Rage. And more focused, too. Aside for the physical recovery, the week off meant a safe-haven of mental recovery. No shocker at ReVolution 56, and Wolfenden is yet to make her announcement. Careful reflection and many calls to friends led down the path to his final, and controversial, decision: goodbye Enlightenment. Hello lone wolf. The collective Rage has cut his losses and gone solo upon the request of himself- someone he hasn’t consulted in months. Emilio reasoned that if he were skilled enough a competitor to vie for the Global Title, certainly the Intense or Universal titles were not only in the realm of possibility, but indeed, likely, and therefore worthwhile, causes. Rage may have had to share the limelight as the figurehead of the Enlightenment with several others, but behind the scenes, he was the glue. Hot on the tail, matching step for step PRIME’s alpha fan favorite in Boda, Rage learned to respect his companion’s ability in the ring. But closer inspection came with the revelation that he did not fear it. Indeed, the same goes for Killean Sirrajin, as unmistakable as his ring presence may be. But both men are mortal, and neither made the long haul in the Halo. For Emilio, dropping the Enlightenment banner meant picking up his own gun. And Emilio is aiming high. Both Boda and Sirrajin are ripe for the pickings. But that doesn’t mean he hasn’t had to dodge a few bullets himself. The press, some stray fans...even a less-than-impressed Ozric Mortimer, have haunted him for weeks. There’s nothing as heartbreaking as a sad clown, or worrisome as a psychotic sad clown. Indeed, Ozric Mortimer has been the centerpiece for Emilio’s mental circus of late, and seems Rage has stepped in the elephant shit. Regardless, his final decision stands, and he will not jump through flaming hoops just to please the crowd. Emilio spoke on the phone with false confidence, but it seeped through like a sponge. “No...well of course not! It was a very tough call, man, nail-biting decision.” Angered chatter from the other side. “You should have shadowed me all these nights. I made contemplation look like an Olympic spor-” He was cut short. A slender tear began to form in the corner of his eye, rolling down his cheek. “C’mon, bro, that’s not fair!” They say that the skin is the largest organ of the body, so to have every hair perk up is a sensation not soon forgotten. Emilio’s eyes were bulging. “Speaking of backstabbing, quite literally, I might add, remember when -excuse me?” Emilio shook his head as quick as a feline caught under the spray of a garden hose. “No, no it wouldn’t be smart.” In the end, as always, it turned into humor. You can’t be one in either of their positions without holding a few laughs under the ol’ sepulchral belt...ya know, just ‘n case. “Where do you get off on that, man?” Slight pause. “No...no...I mean figuratively.” Huffing and puffing. “So? Big words are funny with your accent, gringo.” The Nicaraguan briefly chuckled before -“alright then, goodbye”- hanging up the phone. He had an appointment to make at the gym later that day, and besides, this war couldn’t go on forever. He had made up his mind. And there it was. An autonomous Emilio Rage should have been leaping for joy. Instead, he let the phone fall, and he slid down the wall to the floor. He ran a dirty hand through his thick, greasy hair. His heart pumped acid. When the monkey you pry of your back is a clown, it’s not as joyous. And when the friendship you just soured is that with the Intense champion, it’s downright unbearable. Ozric Mortimer was a good friend. He made Emilio a believer. Although the psychotic clown was the original proselyte, untrustworthy for a great deal of time, Emilio soon learned to follow his step. Emilio’s conservative streak of oppressing his inner-animal was questioned, and finally washed away, in a bloody tide of truth: Ozric Mortimer showed the Alias champ that fighting fire with inferno was okay. That a bit of craziness may be just what the doctor ordered, the right pill, when gunning for a fellow crazy patient. He taught Emilio to face the Dark Age head on, no holds barred, no questions asked. So Rage released the rage on the Age. Tragically, though, it just wasn’t enough. Doting on last week’s ReVolution is like diggin’ up grandma’s bones in this case, though. Proof being, in the world of PRIME, no mountain stands tall for all that long. The tectonic plates of the promotion are in constant, unabashed motion. Take, for solemn example, Ozric’s state of being mere months ago. Ozric Mortimer and his rusty blade were the modus operandi of the up-and-coming Dark Age. Where was his kinship, people ask, when Emilio’s back was being carved into the pentagram, the Devil’s geometry written into his flesh? As the mark was instilled in Emilio’s very consciousness, Ozric looked on while the Nicaraguan’s sanguine eyes burned a hole through the arena ceiling. But one day, his revenge could come. Many a night were spent, long after the clown’s bond to the Devil had been broken, on bended knee. Emilio squeezed his balled fists, seething in anger. His boiling blood seemed to arouse his scar, the veins throbbing at the same rate as his heart, as if the animal had surfaced and was ready to escape the body of its host. But those days were over, and Emilio soon forgave his once-mortal enemy. And now, the days following have gone by as well. The questions regarding his controversial career decision still remains, but the answer has already been given. Goodbye. For now. Preparations. “Remember: tuck...and roll. Tuck...and roll. Jus’ tuck and roll, buddy.” Emilio pulled himself to his feet, wiping the imaginary dust off his knees. The thick sweat stuck to his aching hands as he rubbed the back of his tender neck. For the last hour he’d been training with an old friend, Mike Lornitzo, at the local gym. Thus far, Emilio has been choked out thrice. After a week of using “doctors orders” as an excuse to himself, sitting at home, watching cheesy horror flicks -he wanted to further familiarize himself with American culture- he finally decided to get off his ass. Hence, a few hours everyday had been spent with Mike, training at the gym. Mike was a good sparring partner and a key ally and friend in Emilio’s adjustment to American life. He was an avid PRIME-ate, and an avid amateur competitor himself. Generally, Mike usually stuck to his own guns of Ju-Jitsu and only stuck his neck in the wrestling ring when he either had an offering from an indy promotion, in need of some quick dough, or had one too many beers. Either way, he’d usually gone down for the three-count. He saw Emilio as his salvation. Currently, he was competing in a subsidiary of the UFC, hoping to eventually land a contract. With a champion from the greatest wrestling promotion on earth laid up a block away, nursing his pride, Mike took the opportunity to give Emilio a call. On more than one occasion had he saved Rage’s ass when the cocky pro. athlete decided to pick a bad bar fight or two. But most of the time, he just showed him around the neighborhood. Boston was small, but it was a rough city to get used to. So both men saw it is an opportunity to compare notes on their respective disciplines. Mike was still laid off from the plant, and a big-time contract seemed his only way out. Emilio, well, he just needed some new moves in his arsenal. Big matches were just around the corner. Needless to say, at first, it was a little awkward. Hand to hand encounters inevitably led to Mike being slammed to the mat. Conversely, quick shootouts led to fierce, although quick, grappling bouts with Emilio inevitably lying on the mat, gasping for air. Brazilian Ju-Jitsu practitioners have wrenches for arms, vices for legs. Emilio found himself tapping out each time. “Alright, man,” Mike said, cracking his neck as he flipped back to his feet, “from the top.” Emilio let out a sigh, preparing for another ass-kicking. “C’mon, man. I’ve just about enough of this. There’s not gonna be much left for Deville and Youngblood to pick apart when you’re through twisting me like a pretzel.” “Then don’t bend like a pretzel.” “And just how in the hell do I do that?,” asked Emilio, with his hands folded across his chest. “Don’t be a pretzel.” Emilio raised a brow as they backed away from each other, ready to spar off. A little annoyed at the day’s events, and giddy for his upcoming match on ReVolution, Emilio fell into another trap. He lunged first, finally deciding to go low. It seemed a safe bet at the time, and unexpected move on his part. But slick as grease, Mike hopped to the side before launching himself on Emilio’s neck, helping to drive his skull into the mat. One bent leg over the throat later, and Emilio was gasping for air. Tap. Tap. Tap. “If I didn’t know any better,” Emilio said as he wiped more sweat from his brow, “I’d say you’re half man, half boa constrictor.” “Precisely.” They shared a quick laugh before Emilio patted Mike on the back and headed toward the locker room. “Where you going?” his friend called after him. “Dinner. I’m too tired to fight.” He shook his head solemnly. “And think,” Emilio added. “Suite yourself,” Mike taunted, “it seemsyou’re a quitter nowadays anyway, man.” Ouch. He touched a hot spot. Even those this was just a tease, Emilio wasn’t taking too kindly. “I mean, if I want something bad enough,” said Mike, “I just reach up and take it, man. Just reach up and take it. It really is that simple, man. But you wouldn’t know, now would you?” At first, Emilio’s face went red. But moment by moment the flush of crimson through his cheeks began to subside as he wits came about him. “Well, rather I quit something than got laid off something, eh?” Score. After a mocking laugh, Mike launched himself at Rage, not about to let it end at that. But Emilio lowered his head and stood firm. Before the man could fully get a grip on Emilio’s sweaty neck, the big man fell to one knee. He whipped Mike’s legs out from underneath him and caught him on the way down, grabbing his arm. Armbar. It was over. Tap. Tap. Tap. “I win.” Mike shook his head scornfully. “No. I do.” “Huh?” “I told you so...You want something bad enough, you jus’ reach up an take it. No need to turn your back. You walk away all your life, how you ever gonna stay in one place?” Emilio shook his head. “Come on. Let’s go get some dinner you philosophical butcher, you” Mike grinned. And they did. The Test. As of late, PRIME has become a milieu of ignorance. Unfounded beliefs. Chaos. Whether or not Hin See is pandering to the network officials, makes no difference. Loading cards with such combustible talent is a fire hazard. You have the Dark Age still romping about like a buck in heat, clashing heads with whoever they see fit. This isn’t any more true than with the Universal Champ, Boda. The Dark Age’s franchise, the steroid-chugging neophyte thinks he owns the whole damn world. Then you have guys like Deville and Youngblood., two fairly new cats on the block who think the impressive displays early in their PRIME careers are noteworthy to the point where statues should be erected in their names. How foolish. Top it off with a shot of fresh, raw talent -Trashy, Kano, etc.- and you have one volatile potion. The whole roster is in disarray. And the network is living it up. While the wrestlers huddle in their respective locker room corners, too frightened to step out to the water cooler, feeling a nauseous spell of downward descent gripping their promotion, the ratings say otherwise. Up, up, and away! All this drama, as physically harming and emotionally alarming as it is, looks good for the front office. In Hin See’s best interest, however, he better not let out too much string on the balloon. They tend to fly off. That leads into our story... At this point in time, Emilio was also ready to fly off. If he didn’t get a cheeseburger, he’d shoot himself. Unfortunately, after a lawsuit by some ~Unstoppable~ force -who, for reasons of legality, we won’t name- the McDonald’s corporation was suffering. Some unfortunate tot got his giant torso stuck in a Play Palace tube, and the ensuing litigation resulted in a hefty financial fallout. Now, all Boston branches of the said corporation were filing for bankruptcy, replaced by lesser known chains. “I know a place,” said Emilio, giving Mike a look of confident assurance. “And...” started Mike, more a protest than a question. For he knew what was coming next. “It’s called McG’s.” Contrary to the Ju-Jitso master’s protest, they rolled into the parking lot a few minutes later. “Nice,” said Mike, “real...nice. We fit in like peas and jello.” “Precisely.” His Chevy S-10 just didn’t fit in well with the low-riders and occasional limousine. As the loud bass from a nearby ghetto blaster nearly tore his ear apart, Mike had to lean over and yell at Emilio, unwilling to be drowned out. “Are you sure, man?” “Yes. Of course I’m sure!” started Emilio, surprised. “It’s a new joint in town...up and coming, you see.” “I do see,” said Mike as he looked around for a parking spot. Unable to find a proper one -cars were parked sideways across the parking squares, some even lined the drive-thru- he swung the truck around and pulled into an empty lot toward the back. However, there was a little yellow sign hanging from a telephone pole: “This is fo’ niggas who ain’t got legs, bitch.” A handicap sign -with a “40' in one of the crippled cartoon pedestrian’s hands, and joint in the other, smoke emanating from his mouth- hung beneath it. “Shit.” Emilio laughed. “I wonder how they got this joint, anyway?” Mike asked, as they finally pulled into a spot in the “pimp-mobiles” section. “I heard some Rodney King shit went down in Southie a few months back. The result was this dude getting a nice wad of cash...we’re talkin’ triple my figures. He went into the restaurant business, and so, here we are!” “That’s some crazy shit, dude,” Mike said as they got out of the car. “Yep.” They quickly made their way inside the small brick building. A large man dressed in baggy jean attire patted them down as they went in. “You good to go, fellas,” the big man said with a toothless grin. They waited their turn in line as Mike uncomfortably twiddled his fingers. An old man shouted from some dark corner of the restaurant at a group of teen boys causing a ruckus across the way. Emilio grinned, looking down at his buddy’s hands. “Shut up,” said Mike, “at least I’m not runnin’ out like a baby.” Emilio snorted. A sign above the ordering menu read “All Lil’ Meals Come With Free Toy.” “Who next!?” yelled this crazy old woman with a cat perched on her shoulder. She was heavyset, dark skinned, and had long, graying hair. Emilio stepped up to place his order. “You two together?” she asked, eyeing them suspiciously. Emilio nodded his head. “Hi. Yes, I’ll take a bitchin’ burger, hold the pizz-ickles. A Coke...the soda. And maybe a chronic brownie for desert.” “Yeah, that all?” “Yes, ma’am,” answered Emilio as the woman typed in the order. “And remember, no pizz-ickles...I hate pickles.” Emilio gave her a stern look. “Yea, shu.” Mike stepped up next. The woman gave him a mean eye, rapping her fingers impatiently over the top of the register as he stood there looking up at the menu, dumbfounded. “Well, you gon’ take all day?” “Uh...Yeah, I’ll take an order of onio-“ Emilio gave him a quick nudge in the back. “Er...a ‘bling-bling onion ring,’ and shizzle shake...I guess.” “‘K. One moment,” the woman said, typing in a fury numbers on the register. “That come to seven bone, eighty cent’.” Mike reached into his pocket to go for his wallet. The woman jumped back in alarm. Emilio laughed before tossing a $20 bill on the counter. “Keep the change.” But the woman just looked down at the bill. “Nigga, please! You want me to get robbed?” Emilio sighed, taking back the bill. He replaced it with a $10. The woman smiled. “Thank you kindly.” Emilio gave her a nod. “Well Mike, it looks like we’re gonna survive” said Emilio, giving his buddy a quick nudge. “The fat lady ain’t even warmed up yet,” said Mike. Emilio chuckled. He grabbed their trey, and the two went to find themselves a spot at the tables. Unfortunately, they were all covered with graffiti. Emilio picked one that read “PRIME-Dat-Shizzle!” He took a seat, nodding his head in agreement. Mike let out a slight laugh. Emilio looked down at his drink. Little quotes from “P. Diddy” giving his band advice, from “Making the Band II,” line the cover: “You wanna make a record, y’all gotta think like a record....Y’all gotta spin ‘round and ‘round in circles, makin’ music....metaphorically.” Emilio sighed. He bit into his burger. “Bah!” A wave of resentment sent ripples of red across his angry face, as he spat the food out. A shriveled pickle fell into his trey. “Well, well,” started Mike, with a mischievous gleam in his eye. “What you gonna do about it big man?” Emilio sat there, shaking his head. “Yep,” what I thought,” taunted Mike, poking Emilio with a hot mental stick. “Best thing to do is turn the other cheek.” “It’s just a pickle...” Emilio said, as a frown slowly came over his wrinkled face. “But...but I hate pickles!” He clamored to his feet. He gave Mike a nod. Emilio began edging his way toward the counter. “Good luck,” said Mike, jokingly, just as... “Get your asses down on the ground! All of you, I says. Now!” Three angry men charged into the restaurant, holding pistols wildly above their heads. The leader, a redhead with a face full of pimples, began quickly barking out orders. People dove for cover, and children began to scream. The cooks all shuffled out from the back, another man with a gun forcing them out to the front and onto their knees. “I’m not playin’ games...don’t any of you move...any of you move an inch!” Emilio silently fell to his knees, peering intently into the eyes of the alpha gunmen. “You buddy, on the floor!” Emilio grudgingly obeyed, a grimaced look overtaking his intense face. One woman looked up at the man, and yelled, “hey, ain’t he the old McDonald’s manager?” “Naw,” said some old man from the back, “he run the grill.” “Naw, swept da floors,” another cried. Every minute, the man’s face turned to a deeper shade of red. Before long, he broke out in hives. “You...you just shut up,” he cried, “all of you...shut up!” Murmured complaints echoed throughout the room as he made his way to the front. “In the bag, now!” he demanded from the old woman behind the register. The cat hissed, swiping at the man with a paw. The man jumped back in alarm. A round of laughter echoed throughout the restaurant, even on the part of his three white, geeky followers. He gave the short fat one a glaring eye. The kid swallowed hard. “You think it’s funny? Huh? You think it’s funny...Well you know what,” started the kid, with a smug grin on his face, “the only thing that’s funny is that I’m holding this!” He raised the gun in the air- and fired it by accident. He ducked down as a piece of sheet rock fell on his head. Another round of laughter. “Enough!” He motioned toward the fat kid again, who held a brown bag, to begin packing money. Emilio lay incapacitated on the floor. He was unwilling to risk his neck again. After all he’d done for people, he thought, didn’t he deserve a day off? And besides, he reasoned with himself, trying to clear his stained sense of morality of all duty, it’s actually quite humorous. These geeky high school McDonald’s employees, back to extract revenge on the new chain that put them out of business. The poor kids probably didn’t even have a good retirement plan to fall back....you know, allowances were not good as they used to be these days, so the word on the street goes. “Yeah, that’s right,” said the boy. That kind of “pig snort” common in the laugh of unbearably annoying people came forth from his mouth. “Put it all in the bag, bitch! All of it. And remember, no pickles...Hehehe, get it?” Emilio got it. Suddenly, he snapped. Something came over him. A wire loosened in his head, short-circuiting his common sense. An aura of impregnable defiance lifted him to his feet. Even with guns trained on his heart, he walked silently toward the man. As the kid turned to face Emilio, the fierce determination in the wrestler’s eyes burned holes through the kid’s suspicious glare. The kid’s mouth hung open, his tongue like a welcome mat. Emilio wanted a bitchin’ burger...without the pizz-ickles. He wanted it bad. He wanted it now. Like a rhino on a rampage, Emilio flew through the boys as they blocked his path to their leader. They began dropping their...well, evidently quirt guns, one at a time. The audience, who continued to eat throughout the ordeal, let out a sigh of relief, coming to their feet. “Well,” a senile man called out, pushing himself up by the cane, “I gots to go take a piss.” Emilio cracked his knuckles, glaring intently at the kid. “You want a knee to the balls with that shake?” “Huh?” The boy didn’t get the joke. But it was of no concern anyway as he had bigger things to worry about, for the next thing he knew he was sprawled out on the tile floor, holding his spoiled family jewels, tenderly. “Oooh....oohh...ah...” Ignoring the commotion as a SWAT team plowed through the windows -even though the doors were very much accessible- Emilio stepped back up to the counter. He began to speak with a calm, John Wayne-like tone of authority. “I want a bitchin’ burger. Hold the pizz-ickle.” The woman smiled as her cat rubbed up against Emilio’s chest, purring. She made him his burger. Without the pickles. And he ate it. The Answer. Mike hollered obscenities at Emilio the entire drive home. “What the hell was that, man? You crazy?!” “Nah,” said Emilio why a sly grin, “I just really didn’t want them pickles.” Mike kept his eyes on the road ahead. He had learned so much from the PRIME superstar the past few weeks, and Emilio from him. “Fair enough,” he said, shifting gears. Emilio rehearsed a few of his new moves that night, a new sense of self-confidence overtaking him, suddenly. He slept good that night. ”Nigga’, please! Nigga’, please!” So where does that leave us? Culture Shock and the fallout. The pro-wrestling world has seemingly turned up-side-down. The press have had a field day of late. The headlines soar across the front of news sites all over the net: “Face of PRIME Forever Changed.” Has it? Many would argue yes, and for the most part, they’d be correct: A new guard has come to power, and the house crew are going to have to step it up a notch if they wished not to be bowled over by the tide of newcomers. They pack great skills and great power. But maybe the real question is what hasn’t changed? Well, Emilio Rage, for one thing. He is still the confident, no-holds-barred, heart-driven machine that he once was. Except now with an improved arsenal, refurbished system, and a growing level of confidence. And the heart of a warrior, if anything, shines most brilliantly when it’s “out of its element.” Many feel, coming into this match, that Emilio has the most to prove. A solid win can seal the door on his controversial choice to walk away from the Enlightenment, signaling it as a “smart” move. But a loss doesn’t necessarily mean it was the wrong choice. It simply opens many more doors. Emilio, however, will do his utmost not to open this can of worms. Youngblood may come in swinging hard, and Deville may come in with solid management and an apparent sense of entitlement. But the only thing either of them are entitled to is a solid, old fashion arse whooping. PRIME style. If anyone has something to prove, they do. They have to prove that not only was hiring them the smart move, but a pertinent one. They have to prove, beyond a reasonable doubt, that they are better than anything PRIME has to offer. That they are better than the best of the best. They feel this in their hearts, and to prove it to their minds, they must defeat Emilio Rage. They will not. Stack the odds as they may, but it’s not the way the odds are stacked, but the way the chips fall, that matters. Rain or shine, the PRIMEal Rage champion will not bow down to the bullying of two egocentric young bulls. Experienced in the realm of past promotions -some notable- or not, they are fresh meat in PRIME. If need be, they will be shown the food chain the hard way. Brandon Youngblood believes that he’s the hottest thing since pancakes. In reality, though, he’s a visceral, free-spirited hothead with, unfortunately, just enough skills to back that vanity up with. Jolt’s Triple-Crown Champion, near-PRIME tag team champion, and a supposed technical force to be reckoned with in his realm, the squared-circle. The lucid truth, however, indicates that deflating his ego entails nothing more the poke of a needle. The ROAR will suffice for this purpose. Brandon Hothead plays it up like he took on a myriad of immortal demons at Culture Shock, outshining all but two of which, therefore establishing himself as a warrior. But reputation is only skin-deep. Especially those based on exaggerations. Let him expect the rapture at ReVolution 57 though, that the angels will soar down from the heavens and carry him to bliss. Because reality will suffice as a wake-up call to his imploded state of arrogance. When he awakes from his posthypnotic state, compliments of a loving ROAR, looking up at the arena lights as his dilated pupils shrinks like his sense of self-worth back into his head, he’ll know who’s mama bitch-slapped him. Reality. Brandon, let Emilio be the antiseptic to your cancerous ego before it poisons the well of PRIME pride. Let not this promotion make the same folly as Jolt has: Someone vain enough to think they can meet their maker by simply wiping the fog off the shower room mirror every morning, doesn’t need a title shot. He needs psychiatric evaluation...And in this category, put Youngblood on the list with the English Luchadore for the number one contender spot this week . In the days when slavery was legal, masters would rub the lips of their slaves with bacon fat to make them appear healthy and well-fed. Maybe that’s why, up close, Youngblood smells like pig. But Youngblood, no one is buying you. You can’t sell yourself with such an inflated ego. Better keep rubbin’ the fat in, boy. And what of the “Crimson Devil?” What of the man that put Emilio in The Soprano, but couldn’t get him to tap out? He has a vendetta. He took the hurt to Los Diablos last week, drenching Baltasar in his vengefulness. But whether or not he feels slighted because of the Halo is of no concern to the Nicaraguan Nightmare. If Deville’s dreams come true, it will be in the form of Emilio Rage, in all his glory, transforming Deville’s head to pure bliss, as he drives it through the mat like a rusty nail through wood. ‘Less Deville de-rusts himself in time, fate awaits the one-time unstoppable squared-circle force. It ends here. Deville crudely and foolishly assumes that he’s the new omnipotent beast of PRIME. By reputation, he’s the de facto bully on the block. That is all fine and dandy, but when push comes to shove, Emilo won’t walk away from his playground. He’s built too many friendships and has played too many games to get one-upped by the newest kid in the school. Ask Rage for lunch money, and he’ll show you the ROAR. Mr. Deville, I bid you, walk down the streets. Breath the crisp air, saturated with the stink of anticipation. Feel the buzz of the people. The tacit truth, well known in hearts and minds of PRIME-ates around the globe, is that the Rage will not subside. Ellie Kannon’s ReVolution 58 lament is sure to follow her husband’s sound defeat, and such a fiasco will be the source for Emilio’s only regret. Coming out of ReVolution 57...his only regret. As for the Dark Age, win, lose, or draw, their presence is not welcome at the Joe Louis Arena. Emilio is not looking for a fight, but is history serves us any purpose it is to prevent us from making mistakes in the future that we’ve already made in the past. And that mistake was not finishing the Dark Age off for good. But maybe, if they just decided to get in his way... Gentlemen, Detroit is your Waterloo.
