Living With Dirty Laundry

4 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/04/07 VOID As Told by Ian Nackedy He’s been in the house for about two weeks, and I’ve probably said four or five words to him, at most. What am I supposed to say to a father who has only been in my life for a short period of time? One who, when he actually was around, stole money from me and my brother and used it to further our debt and his addiction? It’s not like I want to talk sports with the motherfucker. This is how I see all of it. My existence, to me anyway, started around 6 or 7. From that first time I looked around the orphanage and realized that I didn’t have a real family other than the kids who lived with us (and they didn’t count because they all ate their boogers), I felt like I needed something in my life. Something that would make me whole. And it never got better. A few years later me and Foster were picked up by this old wrestler training guy named Pat Gray. He became our foster father. He was the reason me and Foster started doing this shit. He was our friend, our provider, our trainer, but he was never close to being a father. I didn’t feel safe, protected, comfortable with him. I still had that void. Flash forward years and years. In comes Foster Nackedy III, also known as my dad. He gives us hugs, says all the things we wanted to hear, and just like that the void was filled. I was happy. Satisfied. I felt like part of a family again. You know what happened next. I feel like Devin Shakur going over all of these emotions all the time, but I felt more pain there than I ever had before. During that time I was a serious wrestler demanding respect, a lot like my brother is. After Dad hurt me like that, I blocked out the pain. I became silly. I became goofy. I became that way so I would never have to take the business seriously again, so I couldn’t get hurt again. Just recently I’ve been getting serious again. I work out before matches. I watch game tape. I’ve become professional. I even stopped peeing on my opponent’s locker room door handles before the match. And I felt like everything was coming together. Like I could finally feel again. And now he’s back. Ready to match my serious demeanor with another world-shattering blow. And I’m supposed to welcome him back with open arms and talk about college football? Fuck that. The worst part in all of this is shutting him out makes me the bad guy here. That’s the problem with society today. A man can abuse family ties, steal people’s money, and stick it in his nose but when a son punches his old weak father in the face after seeing him for the first time in years, HE’S the jackass. TWO WEEKS AGO: THE PUNCH As Told by The Spectator “Ian, what is wrong with you?” shouted Magen as she bent over Pops Nackedy, pushing his overgrown hair from out of his face so she could see if he had started bleeding. He had. It appeared Ian’s training was paying off. “I deserved that,” Pops said weakly, his hand covering his bloody mouth. “No, you didn’t,” replied Magen, going to the counter to get a washcloth. “That was a terrible thing for Ian to do and you didn’t deserve it at all.” “Yeah, he did,” Ian insisted. Lifting one hand now tinted in crimson, Pops points at his son. “See?” Magen finally arrived at Pops’ side again, fully armed with wet washcloth. As she wiped the blood from his mouth, she shook her head like only a mother can. “I don’t care what happened in the past, Ian, this is ridiculous and – oh!” she gasped in surprise as part of Pops’ mouth came with the washcloth. “You lost a tooth! Ian, that’s terrible. He really didn’t deserve that.” Gildenstern suddenly shoved open the door to the kitchen and was halfway towards the refrigerator when he noticed the scene around him. “What happened?” “Ian punched Pops in the face,” Magen sighed. Gil whipped his head around to stare at Ian. “Really?” Ian smiled. “Yeah. Knocked out a tooth.” Gil nodded. “Hm.” Then, he looked down on Pops Nackedy and shrugged. “You deserved it, though.” “Truth,” Pops said weakly. “Stop it!” Magen suddenly yelled. All three men turned to face her as she dropped the washcloth, standing up and backing up so she could see them all. “This is ridiculous, guys. Yes, your father walked out on you. Yes, when he came back he stole your money. Yes, he always got in the way of me and Foster’s sex time when he was here before. He’s made a lot of mistakes, and we know this. But you’ve got to let it go.” Pops couldn’t help but find a problem with one of the things she said. “Well why didn’t you just tell me to leave the room then? I can take a hint, you know.” Magen looked at Pops, deadpan. “We had Ian record a song called ‘This House is Only Big Enough For Two, Our Clothes are Gone And You Should Be Too.” “We’ve actually got a new chorus to that,” added Ian. “But that’s not the issue here,” Magen said, finally getting back on track. “For you, Pops, I know you did some terrible things, but you can’t just wallow in pity over it. You have to make an effort to change things. You can’t be in the same stage, never trying to grow as a person. You’re in your fifties, you should try growing out of this financially insecure lifestyle and get a job.” Pops nodded. “You’re right. I’m sorry, Magen. I’m sorry to both of you. I know it’s got to be annoying, having to deal with someone who, according to tradition, should be taking care of you. And it really pains me that I can’t. But right now, I’m in a tight spot. I can’t be sitting at home, and more importantly I can’t be alone. I won’t be here long, but I do need some help.” Ian and Gil looked at each other. Ian frowned, because he knew what was coming. Pops continued, proving Ian right. “I know I haven’t given you a reason to trust me, and I don’t have the right to ask you of anything, but…please let me stay here. Let me borrow some cash. Let me get myself right again. And then I’ll be out of your hair, I promise.” Magen looked over to Ian, who after a second threw his arms up in the air. “Why are you looking at me?” Ian shouted. “This isn’t my house. I don’t make the rules. If you want to raise two five year old kids and a fifty year old kid in the house, I don’t have the power to veto it. All I can do is work out in the back room and pretend that the worthless lump sitting on the couch is just Foster’s dirty laundry.” Ian walked past his still-fallen father and then sniffed the air dramatically. “The smell won’t be a stretch, at least.” Ian went into the weight room in the back of the house, slamming the door behind him. After a minute of staring after him, Gildenstern shrugged and continued his journey to the fridge while Magen and Pops could only think about Ian’s behavior and whether he would eventually calm down. SERIOUS As Told by Gildenstern I’ve never really seen him this angry. I’ve seen him pretend to get mad, and I’m pretty sure he was only mildly disturbed as I was rolling on the ground on fire. But his dad coming back really has hit a nerve. And I know exactly why that is. Like I said, I haven’t seen him this angry, but I also haven’t seen him this serious in a long time. It’s really weird to think about it, too, because when I first met him, he was always serious. He was an angry, troubled boy. Pat Gray was my foster father too, as I never really knew my parents. And until Ian and Foster came to the home, I really didn’t have a lot of friends. I kind of stayed in the corner and read. When the Nackedy kids arrived, I made quick friends with Ian, but I could tell that something was wrong. He wasn’t funny, he wasn’t goofy; if you saw the Ian I knew in our teen years compared to the Ian that floods the airwaves now, you’d have no idea they were the same person. Except for the crappy hair. But he grew up rarely smiling. We had fun, I guess. We talked about music, wrestling, and got along pretty well. But he just seemed to have something wrong with him. Like he was always thinking, brooding. Even when we started wrestling, he didn’t like my gimmick-of-the-week idea. It took him years to warm up to it. And now he doesn’t want to even do that anymore. We’re tag team champs, we’re doing well professionally, but I am really getting nervous about Pops showing up again. I’m not one to get superstitious, but last time Pops paid all of us a visit, saying he was clean and straight, our replica NWC World Tag Team Titles went missing. We’re not ready for another loss like that. I’ve come to realize people don’t change. Maybe Ian was goofy for a bit, but that’s not who he is. He’s just a hurting guy trying to mask the pain. And Pops is just a guy who will never be responsible. He’ll never be a good parent. And we’ll never be able to trust him. PRESENT DAY: SPOTTING As Told by The Spectator Ian exhaled loudly as he pushed the barbell up, his mind completely blank. This was how he trained, how he excelled: by not thinking, by just working. Foster’s gym in the back of the house was a godsend at this point, because it provided Ian with the burst he would need to get past Alex Creed at Revolution 147, and furthermore it distracted him from a certain annoyance that now lived in the same house he was occupying. An annoyance that walked up to him while he worked, looking down and smiling. “You’re so much stronger than I remember,” said Pops Nackedy proudly. “I remember when I first found you, and you were fighting in some alleyway brawl and you lost because a wall fell on you and kept your shoulders down for the three count. It was so funny, and I used to be so embarrassed telling my friends ‘I have a son who wrestles…but he lost to a wall.’” Pops chuckled to himself as Ian continued to lift, murmuring under his breath, “…what friends?” His disillusioned father didn’t hear him, though; he instead continued his trip into (good) Memory Land. “And then when you faced your brother…man, he really kicked your ass good. I remember watching it and thinking, ‘Well, at least one of my sons is a good wrestler.’” Ian grunted as Pops sat down at the end of the bench, looking at his son. “But look at you know. Champion, beating someone favored to win in this tournament…you’re doing so well. I can’t believe how much you’ve grown.” After a moment, Pops realized he wasn’t getting anywhere and shook his head. “I know you’re mad at me. I know I deserve it. But how can I prove that I’ve changed to you when you won’t let me? I want to be a father, like I wasn’t before…to help you…” “You want to help me?” Ian suddenly explodes, pushing the weight high and holding it there, straining. “Then spot me.” “Oh, okay,” said Pops, eager to get some sort of reaction, no matter how small. He stood up and walked behind the bench, holding his hands out under the bar. “This good?” “Yeah, thanks,” Ian said quickly before letting go of the bar. Pops, surprised, tried to use his arm strength to keep the bar up, but failed, and the bar fell towards Ian’s neck… But Ian stopped it, grabbing it right above his eyes. Lifting the bar back up on its rack, he sat up and shook his head. “You want me to trust you. I can’t even trust you to help me lift weights. How can I think you would help me when you can’t even help me in something so trivial?” Pops, still shocked at the events, looked to Ian with a pained look on his face. “I didn’t…” “Yeah. You didn’t. You didn’t do anything. You didn’t raise me, you didn’t help me. You’ve never helped. You’ve only hurt.” Standing up, Ian stared at his father in disgust. “If Magen wants you here so you can survive on her kindness, that’s fine. But don’t expect to survive on my kindness, because I don’t have any. Not when it comes to you.” He walked out of the weight room. Pops Nackedy sighed and put his hands under the bar, trying to lift it. After a second, he sighed and shook his head. “He’s gotten so strong…and angry…” Sitting down on the bench again, he looked down at his feet. “Just like I used to be.” EPILOGUE As Told by The Summarizer He sat in the gym, not moving, for hours, trying to find a way to reach his son. Not successful, Pops Nackedy fell asleep on the couch, hoping to never wake up. Like most things in his life, he was unsuccessful at this as well.
