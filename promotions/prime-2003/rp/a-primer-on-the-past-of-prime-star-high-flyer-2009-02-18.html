A Primer on the Past of PRIME star High Flyer

18 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/18/09 High Flyer is a joyous and quite boisterous individual, at essentially all times of day. No doubt he was either in the midst of bragging about some accomplishment, or stuck in the middle of an on going practical joke war with one of the many backstage individuals he dealt with on a day to day basis. The majority of his time is either spent playing a practical joke on someone, or relaying that practical joke to others in the form of storytelling. But not today. Jack Harmen sat alone in a dimly lit room, head slung low, cigarette dangling from his lips, unlit. His shoulders slumped even lower, looking almost Dali-esq as his knuckles scrapped the tile linoleum. Melting away into oblivion. His cell phone started to ring. A vibration at first, before the opening bells of Angel's theme song(by Darling Violetta) played. Flyer let the phone ring, careless as to who it was. Eventually, the tune cut out, signaling the call had gone to voice mail. With a sigh, Flyer put his hands in his warm winter coat, and closed his eyes. High Flyer reminisced fondly about his childhood before the age of six, like most children do, but as an adult could not remember much of it. Tattered pieces and flashes of memory were all the Lunatic had of his early childhood, until his adoption of a time machine in the late ninety's from former IWO wrestler and friend Rob Kestler allowed him to not only remember events long forgotten, but actually experience them. Bought for the single cost of a foot of snow, High Flyer wasn't able to just remember the past, he was able to smell it, taste it, be, and see, the past he always clung to. And why wouldn't someone cling to the time they were most carefree? Most overwhelmed with joy, friendly and kind to the world, and not quite so traumatically scarred as the psychopathic tendencies of Flyer would have you indicate he is. Memories of his deceased mother became vivid. Issues of his past weren't just recalled, they were clarified, defined, given context a six year old could neither understand nor retain. He thought about running up to his mother after she had just baked a fresh batch of cookies, wrapping his tiny hands around her and smelling just the hint of peppermint escape her lips as she lightly kissed his forehead. He thought about all the times she would take his side in a conflict with his older brother Jake, defending the runt of the litter of the Harmen household. And mostly, he just thought about that warm feeling he had whenever she looked into his eyes. That feeling that everything she could ever want in the world was wrapped up in a single package. Me. Every year High Flyer would travel back in time to see his mother in a distant memory. Whether it was tucking him in bed at night and scaring away the closet monster from Bulgeria, or taking him to see the Phillies play at Veteran's Stadium for his fifth birthday. This year, was different. The time machine had been dismantled after a trip to the early 80's just a year prior. There, High Flyer had unbegrudgingly caused the butterfly-tsunami chain of events that caused his family to fall apart. In a fit of rage, the Lunatic dismantled the time machine with a tire iron, his own bare hands, and six hours of leg work. Bloody knuckles, heaving lungs, scattered metal along the basement, the groans and screams of the Lunatic tearing at the hinges of the time machine still echo out in his basement. Linger in the air like ghosts of the past. And so, here he sat, in the midst of rubble, in the aftereffects of a tsunami caused by a Snow man, surrounded by ghosts of himself screaming in pain. He sat, trying to fight the urge to light up a cigarette. He'd quit smoking six weeks ago, and today, was worse than any other day before it. He sighed, surveying the destruction around him. A tear slowly slid down his cheek, slipping over the lips as he quietly whispered, "I'm sorry." Flyer looked up at a painting of his family, which contained his mother and was done before their untimely deaths. He stared specifically at his mother as he spoke, letting out a half smile as he continued to cry. "Happy birthday." Looking up at that picture, their smiling faces, he realized, for the first time, that he would never get that sort of family portrait with his former wife Kate Young. He scoffed. Cause, there was a time when High Flyer believed it was his destiny to die inside of a Professional Wrestling ring. And he believed that if he had Kate Young by his side, she would surely perish too. You see, High Flyer's brain is wired a little differently than the rest of ours. Maybe it's all the time traveling. Maybe it's all the fried foods. Maybe he's just crazy. Science will never know. But High Flyer believed that he was relieving the life of his father. Hear his delusions out for a second. As Jack Harmen, a little boy of six, attending his first professional wrestling event and seeing his father fly around the ring like an acrobat, shouted to his father "Go Dad!" at an inopportune moment. Shortly after, he was dead. For years, Jack Harmen shunned wrestling, refusing to participate in this sport, refusing to even watch it. Wait. It wasn't so much as a refusal. It was actually a fear. The little scamp would hide behind couches when it came on the ol' boob tube, no joke. And so, for years, Flyer grew up with a fear of professional wrestling. And while his brother was able to get Flyer to confront the fear and face it, it always remained somewhere under the surface, bubbling in the subconscious. And yet, now, it was all Flyer loved, and all Flyer knew. Wrestling was all High Flyer had left. High Flyer grew up, became a professional wrestler, and gained some success. Moderate, if anything, just like his father. In fact, his path went exactly like his father. Jobber to the stars for years. Win a low card title. Lose it quickly. Tag team for a bit. And then win the World title for five minutes. Yes. Five minutes. It happened to both men, and once it did, High Flyer knew, KNEW, he was repeating the steps of his father. That he was doomed to die at the same age, at the same time, from the same cause. He pushed Kate away in a misguided attempt to save her. Thinking, if she no longer has feelings for Jack, that she would be safe. And when nothing happened, when High Flyer continued to live beyond his fated, destiny of a time set by the Gods, he knew he had made a rather large mistake. He never really got her back. There was no reason for the Lunatic to move today, no reason to celebrate. And so he continued to sit there, stare focused, slack jawed at his old painted family portrait. He couldn't save his marriage. He couldn't save his parents. And this week? He wondered if he'd have the strength to save his 5-Star title reign. And he wondered. He wondered. He wondered ALLday long.: the future, the past, his wrongs and his rights, good and bad, ying and yang. The diversity of life. And he also wondered if he should eat. Ultimately, he decided against it.
