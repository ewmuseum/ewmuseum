Forgiveness (Match RP for ReV 79)

Date: 10/10/05 Her Memos ~October 10th 7:03 P.M.~ They were scattered around the house. Square-inch memories, stuck on the fridge, slid under the remote, wedged in the middle of chapter thirteen, to hold her place in that book she no longer read because none of it made sense. She wasn’t sure when exactly she had started to write down many of her thoughts on little cards and pieces of notebook paper, but the number of them she was finding all throughout each and every room made it clear that she had been doing so frequently. Nor was she entirely sure why she had started to record her thoughts and fragments of her life. There was no specific incident she could recall that had sparked the idea to jot things down on paper, but it was clear… she couldn’t remember. She didn’t remember why she was writing these things down… and that was likely her answer. She couldn’t remember these things. She had to write them down. If her memories weren’t on paper, then they weren’t anywhere. They didn’t exist. But why couldn’t she recall any of those little facts and thoughts? She hadn’t lost her entire memory. It wasn’t like amnesia. There was the wedding on the beach with Matthew, she clearly remembered. There was the birth of Georgie. Mary certainly hadn’t forgotten those hours in labor. It was only the little facts and events of the recent past that found their way on paper, and baffled her when she read them. Matthew asked me what Killean had called for a few minutes ago. I saw I left him a note, but I don’t remember taking the message from Killean. And I’m not sure why Killean would be seeing Matthew any time soon. Killean hasn’t been in for a session in quite awhile. Mary let the note fall to the floor, then noticed another that was lying on the bar in the kitchen, amongst some old newspapers and bills. ~September 26th~ Tonight, Matthew left the house, “for a bit” he said. It’s late, after six o’clock, but he said he’s going in to the office. It’s strange for Matthew to be going in for work this late at night. He usually tries to be done by five, but he said not to expect him home till about eight o’clock tonight. That night had been much like this one. She looked around at the empty house. Matthew was gone again. Everything was quiet, lonely. She had no one to talk to, perhaps that’s why she had turned her attention to the notes. Now they were tearing her to pieces, as if she were just a cryptic sheet of paper with haunting words. Mary wanted nothing more than to be with Matthew. It wasn’t that she expected him to be home at all times, but at this moment, she needed something to hold other than random notes. Without him there, she was alone. Georgie was with her grandparents, because Mary needed a night to rest… that’s what the note on the refrigerator told her anyways. And that left the house barren, like everything else. She picked up a sheet of paper by the cordless phone. ~October 10th~ Matthew went out to dinner with Scott. He said that Scotty wanted to talk about his work, but Matthew has told me on numerous occasions over the years that Scotty had never once shown an ounce of interest in psychology. Why now? In her fist, the paper smashed together, its wrinkles folding on top of one another, layer by layer… the memory deteriorating in her rage. Every one of them, something she had witnessed, thought, felt… in some way experienced, but they were all foreign to her. Enigmas of her own mind that drove her sanity to the brink of collapse. She wanted to throw the crumpled mess with every bit of energy that remained in her exhausted body, but instead she just dropped it on the counter top and used the hand to wipe away the tear that fought free from the corner of her eye. * * *The Dinner ~October 10th 7:24 P.M.~ Scotty was late, but Matt wasn’t surprised. They had made plans to meet at the Olive Garder for seven o’clock, and on a Monday night with business rather slow, Matt already found himself seated at a table, his first glass of coke halfway gone. It would likely be empty by the time Scott arrived… that was just his style. Despite deciding to meet at 7:00, Matt had expected it would be pushing 7:30 before Scotty strolled into the restaurant, acting as if he were right on time, no apology even remotely close to escaping from his lips. He also expected he’d be paying for dinner… it was the only thing more Scott’s style than showing up late. Matt reached into his pocket and pulled out his cell phone. He wanted to call home and check on Mary, make sure she was ok. The idea of telling her he’d be a little later than expected crossed his mind for a fleeting moment, but then the realization destroyed the thought. If he told her he’d be late, she’d forget ten minutes later and by the time nine o’clock rolled around, she’d be wondering where he was, when he’d left…wondering about everything. It was odd, how difficult it was for him to remember that she had the short term memory loss. Every day he would find himself wanting to tell her something, share something with her, to make things better or easier… and each time it was useless, but it never stopped his moments of forgetful hope wrecking the day. Perhaps he was the one truly losing his mind. The phone rang several times, before eventually Mary’s voice echoed in his right ear. A smile slipped across his face but it vanished as fast as the future had. It was their answering machine. Mary wasn’t home. “Bro! You started without me!” Scott’s words snapped Matt’s state of mind in half, caused him to jolt his head in the direction of the voice, as if he had never planned on company for dinner. “You’re impatient. Who you talkin to?” “No one.” Matt closed the cell phone and slid it back in his pocket. “And I’m not impatient. You’re half an hour late.” “Be honest, dude. How long did you wait for me. It’s not like you just got that coke. The glass is only half full, you’ve been sipping away at it for what… fifteen minutes?” “Yeah.” “See, you’re impatient.” Scotty pushed his chair forward, in close to the table, picked up his cloth napkin and let it unroll, the silverwear clanging to the table. Several people at a few other tables nearby turned to look towards the commotion… Scott smiled and waved. “So what are you going to get? Chicken Parm, right? It’s the best. Oh man… and the breadsticks.” “I know, Scotty. We’ve eaten here a hundred times before.” “So, what the hell was the deal with you the other night at ReVolution?” “Damn, Scott… you cut right to the chase, don’t you?” “Hey, bro, I tried to talk about the damn breadsticks, but you didn’t seem too interested. So I figured, what the hell… might as well try and figure out what’s going on in that crazy head of yours. I mean, at first I thought this was just some well deserved payback for Killean… for what he did in the Underground a few years ago. But uh… I think you’re actions at ReVolution Wednesday night make it pretty clear that something else is goin on here.” “What actions?” Matt stirred the ice in his half empty glass, then took another drink. “Don’t be stupid, Matt. End of the show… The Illustrious Face Eater… or whatever he called himself. The beat down. That wasn’t right, and it sure as hell didn’t have anything to do with paying back Killean for the Underground.” “This isn’t all about Killean and the Underground.” “I know. That’s what I’m saying… there’s something goin on here more than just that. So what the hell is it.” “Scotty,” Matt leaned back, closed his eyes and rubbed the back of the neck, as if he were in pain, “it’s just like I told you at the show Wednesday night. I needed an escape. I needed one night to get away from it all. One moment, one chance to be where there are no expectations, no commitments. One night in a place where, when everything repeats itself, when nothing moves forward… that’s a good thing. That means you’re standing your ground and breaking everyone who tries to tear you down. I need that moment, just once a week, where standing still isn’t so bad.” “Damn, Matt, you’re not standing still.” “Every day I wake up next to Mary… it’s just like the day before. Except I don’t even have the luxery of smiling and the two of us laughing about how similar, but comforting, each day is. She doesn’t remember that yesterday is just like today. She doesn’t remember when I tell her I love her anymore. I can sit there and say it till I can’t breathe, and a half an hour later, she can walk up to me and say ‘baby, how come you don’t tell me you love me anymore?’ I’m standing still, Scott.” “Matt” it was as serious as Scotty had probably sounded in ages, longer than anyone would remember, “you can’t handle this thing the way you are, you know it, bro. You’ve given up all hope a handful of weeks after the accident. You were a psychologist, you know amnesia can be something that lasts several weeks or even several months and clear up. The doctors have said there’s no physical damage to the brain. The memory loss is a result of the trauma, right?” “Yeah.” “So then there’s a good chance that this will clear up and she’ll be fine. And the worst you’re gonna be left with is a couple months of catching up to do. And everything will be alright.” “You’re right, that could very well be the case. I just… that escape the PRIME offers, feels like its draggin me through this.” “And it might be right now, but you better be careful, or it could drag your ass into trouble. What happens if, when Mary recovers… how’s she gonna feel about you going back to wrestling? And no offense bro, but at the rate you’re going, there’s gonna be a long list of enemies of yours, just waiting to get some payback of their own. You can’t let the thrill, or the serenity… or whatever it offers you… of the ‘escape’ to cause you to end up in a world of trouble.” “I know.” “And dude, Killean is a beast. And one you can’t even give your full concentration to, cuz you’ve got the whole JITC thing goin on and Oxios…” “You saying…” “I’m saying I just think you may have bitten off a little too much, and you should be careful.” The waiter stopped by, seeing that Scotty had arrived, and took the orders of both men, each got the same, Chicken Parm. “You know what you need, bro? You need to pray to God that everything’s gonna be cool and you don’t go crazy or somethin.” “When did you become the Mr. Religion?” “Hey, I go to church.. so…sometimes.” Scott’s serious nature was starting to erode, which was good, Matt needed at least a moment to laugh.” “And I say my prayers and I…” “Scott, yelling ‘hit me’ at the Blackjack table, then closing your eyes and saying ‘please God, don’t let me bust’ doesn’t count as saying your prayers.” “Dude, shuddup. Real prayers. For real, I’m not kiddin. And I’ve been baptized.” “What? When?” “Remember that time we went fishing on St. Mary’s Lake, and I fell off the boat…” “Jesus, that doesn’t count, Scotty.” Matt laughed, this time it was his explosion that directed the attention of the other customers towards their table. He didn’t’ laugh and smile like Scott had. “It involved saints and water. That’s good enough for me. The point is, a great girl and just a little bit of faith in God helped get me back on my feet. I’m not saying you’re in anywhere near the same position I was in… but maybe it’d help ya just a little. You’ve already got the great girl.” Matt had hoped the conversation would find itself to much more humorous and lighter tones, but hope seemed a rather weak concept as of late. “You’re right, Scotty. I’ve got the great girl, she’s just stuck somewhere about a month ago and can’t snap out of it.” Matt stood and reached into his pocket, pulling out a couple of twenty dollar bills. “Where are you going?” “Home, Scotty.” Matt through the two twenties on the table. “That ought to cover dinner, since I know you had no intentions of paying” “Bro…” Look, Scotty. I don’t need God or anything like that. I need Mary to come back to me, and until then, I need to keep doing what I’m doing in PRIME and keep working my way through the JITC, making the most of that escape. Where does God fit into that?” “Hey” Scotty looked frustrated, he wasn’t used to being in the position of trying to convince a stubborn fool to stop risking their life senselessly. “All I’m saying, is at least God’ll be forgiving. That may be more than you can say for Killean… or for Mary.” “I haven’t forgiven God. So what does that matter?” * * * Her Memos: II ~October 10th 7:29P.M.~ She had no choice. In the house, she was suffocating in the memories that were everywhere that he wasn’t. The cold fresh air stabbed at her lungs, yet gave her renewed life. With her, she brought just two small pieces of paper. As she walked through the night, the glow of street lamps occasionally lighting a way for her to read. In her left hand… Matthew was gone Wednesday night. I found another note that said he was gone the Wednesday before that. I don’t remember him being away, but apparently he’s been gone lately on Wednesday nights. The note she carried in her left hand was the one that told her all she needed to know. All the other memos fell into place as the words cut through her mind. Seph called just a few minuts ago. I didn’t even know he had come out of his coma. Matthew hadn’t told me a thing about it. He sounded upset on the phone, concerned for me. We only talked for a couple minutes, and he seemed reluctant to say much. He told me Matthew has ‘gone back’ and I should talk to him as soon as possible. And those words were enough to make it all come together. Matthew was disappearing on the same night, Wednesday, week after week. Old friends from his days as a professional wrestler were calling the house. And he had gone back… back to wrestling. It didn’t matter, that sinking feeling in her heart. By the time she got back home where she could write it all down, she’d forget. In fact, the breeze was already beginning to carry the thought away. * * * The Way Back ~October 10th 7:50 P.M.~ On the way home from the Olive Garden, Matthew had called Killean’s cell phone. As he had expected, there was no answer. That was fine with him, he didn’t have much to say anyway. After the beep, he left a simple message for his old friend… Please forgive me, Killean. It’s only gonna get worse It was the truth. There was no way for things to get better. Not any time soon. There was a part of him who dreamed of the possibility of a KoK finals match where he would step into the ring to do battle with Killean, the glorious prize at stake just an accent to the history which had led each man to that point. But a part of him knew that the longer the two men waited till they finally clashed, the more Killean’s hatred would grow, and the more the match would become everything Matt wanted it to bed. It was an interesting scenario, but one that didn’t matter. The current task at hand was that of each week of the previous month. At ReVolution, he had one more step before KoK. If his focus wasn’t there, there would be no semi-finals, no finals, no match with Killean… and no true escape for the week. He craved that escape. * * * Home ~October 10th 8:00 P.M.~ Matthew walked in the front door, smiled at Mary, who had returned home just a couple minutes before, nothing on her mind. He kissed her, lied to her, and they went to bed, just like every other night of the last month. And even though she couldn’t remember the last time he had told her how much she meant to him, even though the day was disappearing from her memory faster than her strength to stay awake… he was the one who cried himself to sleep.
