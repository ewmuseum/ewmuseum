Making Amends...and Sandwiches

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 167 Felix sat in the driver seat of his car, his gaze transfixed on the dust-filled dashboard. He may come in here and punch me right in the face, he thought to himself as he reached into his pocked and clutched his now vibrating cell phone. Quickly scanning the screen, he pressed the center button to allow the new text message to open. It was from Eli, and luckily, he didn’t seem too perturbed. “Well, that’s Eli for you. Never likes to wait but has no problem keeping you waiting.” Felix chuckled to himself in the empty car. Laughing like that felt good after the last twelve months. He and Eli may not have completely made amends, but they were surely on the road back to normalcy. The next five minutes seemed like an eternity until the slamming of Eli’s apartment door shattered the silence. Eli exploded through the narrow frame of his dilapidated apartment building as if propelled by cannon fire. Startled and now acting completely on instinct, Felix quickly unlocked the doors while simultaneously starting the car. His 1978 Buick Cutlass roared to life (more like groaned) as Eli tore open the door and dove inside. Closely in tow was his landlord, with his round, hairy belly protruding from the bottom of his stained white tank top. “Damn you kids!” was all that could be heard as the door slammed. Felix gave the gas a kick David Beckham would have gotten aroused by and sped off down the street, finally turning to face Eli when they were a safe distance away from the apartment. “Are you going to offer an explanation as to why we were being chased by the crypt keeper?” Eli returned Felix’s curious gaze with a confused look on his face. He’s obviously thinking up some sort of excuse, Felix thought uncomfortably as the awkward silence marinated the car. “It’s sort of a funny story, ya know” Eli began uneasily, “My landlord, or now crypt keeper as you’ve so affectionately dubbed him –“ “I didn’t call him that because he was ugly, I called him that because your apartment is gross. Thus the crypt. He is your landlord. Thus he is the crypt keeper.” “Thanks for breaking it down for me, douche.” Laughed Eli, “Do you write your own material?” With that simple exchange the tension seemed to dissolve. Eli relaxed into his chair, unfastening his seatbelt and reclining. Digging into his pocket, he removed a package of Newport cigarrettes. After a quick struggle with the lighter, two were lit and he placed one into Felix’s fingers. “I thought we quit these things man?” “So did I, but life is short. Where are we going anyway? I thought we were getting food?” “We are,” Felix replied, “but first tell me why we had to escape from Jabba-the-landlord before.” “Well it goes like this. You know I’ve been spending my money on what we’ll refer to in public as ‘flaxseed oil’. I’ve been buying a lot of flaxseed oil lately to help us achieve our goal of winning the tag titles. Therefore, I had very little fundage left to pay my rent on time exactly.” “Wait a minute, I’ve been injecting flaxseed oil into your asscheeks when we’ve been going to the gym? Dude injecting flaxseed oil does nothing for your muscles! You’re so dumb.” “I cannot believe my best friend is so dense.” Said Eli as he somewhat viciously palmed his face. “Easy does it, big guy. I know our match this week is huge and that the ‘flaxseed’ oil is an absolute necessity. I got that, I understand. You know advancement in the PRIME company isn’t high on my list of things to do before I die, but you also know that you are my best friend and that I will do anything to help you achieve your goals. Taking on Team V.I.A.G.R.A is big for both of us, and whether we win or lose I don’t want to come out of there with any regrets.” “That means a lot to me man, really.” “We had our falling out a year ago over in-ring miscommunication. Listen, we can lose as much as we want, it’s still a steady paycheck. Who knows, we may be PRIME’s most memorable tag duo one day.” “I don’t know if they keep a running record of most consecutive losses, Felix.” Came Eli’s sarcastic reply. “All I’m saying is that we need to be together on this. By the way, thank God we’ve arrived at our destination because I think we just had a moment.” “And I feel all warm and fuzzy too, jerk. Wait, why are we at your house?” “Welcome back to the Young residence. Decided to move back in with pops after my mom passed. I’m pinching pennies as it is. I gotta run around back, let yourself in.” Eli stepped out of the car and trudged towards the house in the same manner he had for the last 19 years. To Eli, it was a welcome breath of fresh air with Felix after their estrangement. Eli pushed open the front door and stepped inside. It had been almost two years since he’d been to Felix’s childhood house, and he found himself becoming choked with memories. As he wheeled around the corner into the living room he came face to face with what could only be described as a shrine to Felix’s late mother. Pictures littered the mantle from all points of Felix’s life. He became particularly choked up when his eyes caught the largest photo in the room. In it was himself, Felix, and Felix’s mother, all with their arms around each other on the day that the boys had graduated from high school. Eli instantly became inundated with remorse for not attending her burial. He had no mother of his own and had perpetrated the ultimate disrespect by not going to her funeral. Just then he heard the creak of the door as Felix let himself in. He instantly whirled around. “Listen, Felix, I –“ but Felix cut him off. “I know, bro. I know. Listen, I’m starving and I just bought 5 pounds of cold-cut turkey from the store. You have a lifetime to make amends, and you’ve already begun to do it, but right now we need to be making sandwiches.” So that’s just what they did.
