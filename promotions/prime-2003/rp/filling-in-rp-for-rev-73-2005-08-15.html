Filling In, RP for ReV 73

15 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/15/2005 “Alright, Bobby, good hustle!” Scott shouted. “Come on, Floyd, pick those feet up!” It’s not quite what you’d expect from Scott Matthews, also known as Rampage. It was really something else for him, but it really felt good. He had a rough and rowdy little crew that was ready to face the challenge that lay before them. Yes, Scott Matthews was coaching middle school football at the fifth and sixth grade level. He stood there in the attire, t-shirt with a whistle around the neck, a white t-shirt stretched over his massive chest and shoulders, a pair of running shoes he hadn’t worn since he started wrestling and yes, a pair of blue denim shorts. He clapped his team on as they went through their pre-game warmup, doing high-steps and jumping jacks. Yes, his little intrepid squad, the Fed’s Creek Pirates, had taken not only to the field but their new coach right away. Scott hadn’t expected much when he’d volunteered for the job, but the superintendent, site-based council and the parents were ecstatic that such a recognizable face with real football experience wanted to coach their middle school squad. Scott had intentionally asked for a middle school squad so that he could remind himself that the boys were young and he didn’t have to demand perfection out of them the way his coaches in high school had. Instead, he had worked simply to work hard with the boys, encouraging them in rather light conditioning and working on plays. They boys were eager, they wanted to give their all for the big man that they saw wrestle on TV and knew he was a champion of the game. He knew what he was doing and had almost given him an honorary apotheosis. Scott shook his head whenever he saw their eager faces regard him. He was Coach Rampage to them and they only wanted to really please him. So today they had their first game, a scrimmage with the Prestonsburg Middle School Warriors. Scott stood on what passed as his home field and surveyed the opposing team and their coach. He was a short, rotund man wearing a visor and had a clipboard clutched to his chest. The team had walked out to the field and quite blatantly half-assed their warmup. Scott’s Pirates had already finished theirs and were just hopping to get the game going. Scott smiled at his gang as they sat there on the bench. “Okay, guys,” Scott said to the lively little crowd. “Here’s the deal. You boys have worked really hard, you’ve worked at doing anything I’ve asked you to and you’ve done it well.” He paused, thinking. He was no good on the stick on a wrestling show and talking before a group of excited kids really didn’t feel all that different. “You boys are in great shape for your age. You don’t need to expect big muscles or to be as fast as guys on the high school squad, but you guys have worked hard already in a week’s time. Those guys over there don’t have the heart you boys do. Let’s have a great game.” With a whoop of delight, they hopped up, pulling helmets of dark green on over their shining faces and roared their way out onto the field. It brought a smile to his face. “Scotty!” a voice called out from behind him. He turned around to see his mother and his sister, Lacey, sitting there in the bleachers beaming at him. Scott bit his tongue and waved back. Lacey had a tendency to dress in a way that... well, bothered him. Even Alyssa thought that Lacey was entirely too conservative to be dressing that way. It wasn’t that she shouldn’t be proud of herself: she was a premiere member of the dance team and the track squad, but she enjoyed diaphanous necklines, miniskirts and short shorts. It wasn’t so much that there was anything wrong with it, but she had little intention of dating much and Scott was sure that it would garner her a reputation as a cocktease. Maybe it was the over-protective streak of a big brother that was away a lot, but there was little he could really say about it. Even Alyssa had tried to talk Lacey into changing her wardrobe choices, encouraging outfits that covered a bit more but could be just as alluring. Scott turned back to his team to see them readying for the kickoff. As the home team, they were able to receive at the start of the second half, so they stood ready, like pint-sized little legionnaires, to deliver a devastating charge to the lines of their opponents. The kick came and the ball sailed through the air with the wobbling grace that you can’t help but cheer for. Scott bellowed out encouragement as the ball fell into the arms of a kid that seemed a bit big to be in the sixth grade. His attitude once he took off conveyed it as well, tucking himself and charging forward like a maniac. But his boys surprised him. They had spread out and met the opposing blockers with their best attempts at a thunderous crash. The big kid outdistanced his blockers and came right upon a group of his Pirates, all charging right for him. He didn’t falter or change direction, though; he intended to knock them aside like bowling pins. That’s when Scott saw Lawrence break away from the pack. Lawrence was a little fifth grader on his team that had an incredible determination that impressed his coach. He was small, with shoulders that would broaden as he grew, but he was like watching Invincible Gabe on the field: he wouldn’t back down from any challenge because he thought he was just a few inches and a few pounds smaller than Scott himself. Scott cringed in spite himself. Poor Lawrence was in for the reality check of his young life. But instead, the jaws of everyone in the stands and even Coach Matthews fell open as little Lawrence exploded off his short legs and took the big kid squarely in the stomach with a miniature version of Scott’s own RAGE, hitting with a loud crack. The big kid’s legs flew skyward as he folded around the point of impact and landed flat on his back, felled by almighty determination of Lawrence. Scott threw back his head and cheered for the diminuitive tackler as he stood up and headed towards the huddle with his joyous teammates. They patted his back and knocked helmets before getting right down to business. Their discipline was incredible, Scott marvelled at the group. He’d have watched high school teams cheer and carry on after a hit like that, but these kids didn’t want to waste a second of the clock while they didn’t have the ball, just as he had coached them. But the clock was stopped as the big kid was rolled over on his stomach and puking there on the field. The referees, the opposing coach and various other people were gathered around the victim of what could be called the Middle School Spear. Scott jogged out on the field to help check on the condition of the kid. The opposing coach looked up at him, still in a state of shock. “Did you teach them to hit like that?” he asked. Scott shrugged. “I told them how to tackle, it looks like that little wildcat just took it to heart.” “You sonofabitch!” someone shouted. Scott took a half step and turned, coming face to face... well, face to sternum with a red-faced man of slightly heavy build that was pointing at him angrily. “We held him back a year so that we’d be able to have him grow and be an unstoppable runningback.” Scott caught a whiff of that oh-so-familiar scent of beer on the man. “Nice cop look,” Scott returned, trying to tamp down on the anger boiling in his chest. He had commented on the crew cut and thick mustache the man sported, reminding him slightly of Magnum Wolf. “But don’t get mad at me if one of my boys took down your secret weapon.” “Coach?” came a small voice. It was little Lawrence, his helmet off and his eyes worried. “Is he gonna be okay? I didn’t meant to hit him so hard, but I really wanted to stop him.” “You did fine,” Scott said, feeling quite protective and proud of the kid. “You’re the one!” the red-faced man shouted. “You better learn how to play football you little shit,” he said, smacking the kid on the side of the head. It was really a bit much. Scott shoved the man hard, knocking him into an unsteady stumble that ended with him landing on his ass. “You will not lay a hand on one of my boys,” he said with a snarl. “Just because you’re pissed that your boy has a soft gut is no excuse to...” The man hopped back to his feet. “Do you know who I am?” he screamed, standing on his toes and doing his best to scream in Scott’s face. “I’ll do damn well what I please and you’re not gonna stop me!” With that, he pulled back and swung at Scott. He took the blow full in the face... it was lighter than Tessa Windsor’s quick forearms. “Is that right?” Scott asked, smiling. The red-faced man grabbed him by the shirt front and threw... well, attempted to throw Scott. Instead, Scott’s t-shirt just ripped down the front. “You’re starting to piss me off,” Scott returned, his voice sinking to a bass rumble. The last punch took him right below the eye and really pissed Scott off. The referees were currently very unhappy that there had been such a poor display of sportsmanship after just the first play of the game. “Alright you,” one shouted, “off the field!” “No, no,” Scott waved them off. “I tell ya what, buddy, how about I give ya the same tackle that your boy took and if you can get up after I do it, then I’ll let someone else coach this game.” Now, to those of you reading, you’re thinking one of three things to yourself. First, this is really gonna suck for this guy. Second, you might be thinking this sounds like some bad cliche out of a Disney movie. Or third, you’re wondering where the wrestling ties in. To the first two, fine, that’s your opinion. To the last, well, this is a little bit of character development and we’re getting a peek inside the big man’s life away from the ring. So just hold your horses. “Okay, fine!” the man shouted back. “You do your best!” He turned on his heel and walked about ten yards (or near ten meters for our metric friends, give or take a cubit) and spun to face Scott. “I can’t believe this is happening,” Scott muttered, pulling off his whistle. “This is like something out of a bad geocities promo.” With a quick snort that flared his nostrils like an angry bull, Scott took off, picking up speed... and delivered a RAGE to a five foot, eleven inch former high school football player that was trying to live out success vicariously through his son. They landed with a thud to the cheers of his little team and the home crowd. Scott popped up immediately, just like hitting it in the ring. The man rolled over and proceeded to deposit everything he’d ingested in the past few hours into fertilizing the field. “Like father like son,” Scott muttered, wincing at even muttering the cliche. He’d never thought he’d see it in his life, but someone had been put away outside of a wrestling ring with someone’s honest-to-God finisher. ------------------------------------------------------------ The delighted laugh made his skin tingle. “Oh, that’s marvelous, Scotty,” Alyssa said to him. “You really did lay him out didn’t you?” Having just arrived a half an hour before, she had missed the entire debacle. Scott shook his great shaggy head. “It just kind of happened. I didn’t really consider any of it until it was over.” “Scott,” his mother said, setting down her teacup, “will this be bad for... PRIME, is it?” “Eh, I doubt it,” he said, settling back into the coach in his mother’s living room. “I mean, if need be, PRIME can play it off like I just blew my top like a gimmick or pay the guy off to shut up. Either way, it’s nothing big.” “Okay everyone, I’m going out,” Lacey said as she paraded her way through the living room. She shot him a look, almost accusingly. “It’d be easier if I didn’t have to borrow Mom’s car all the time,” she muttered. See, this was an old argument that had always caused an explosion. Apparently she didn’t remember that little fact. “You see?” Scott shouted, “do you see why I didn’t want to keep any of it?” “Lacey!” his mother’s voice cracked like a whip. “You sit down right now.” Her eyes wide, she silently took a seat down on in the wicker rocking chair that Scott had given his mother for Christmas. “What did I do?” “Young lady,” she said, her voice acid, “you and I have had this conversation before. Your brother killed himself for four years trying to provide for us. He slept in that Jeep and ate the cheapest excuses he could find and you’re pouting about having a car?” Alyssa’s eyes were wide and swivled back and forth from her spot next to Scott. Scott himself was sitting there with his head in his hands, shaking with barely controlled emotion. “The last thing your brother needs out of you is demands for more money and stuff,” Cathy continued on with a tone that would make hardened Marines flinch. “Did you know he didn’t buy new shoes for those four years? He only ever got his hair cut when he came home to visit? He told me once he went for two months sleeping in that Jeep, all because he sent as much money as possible home.” “I was just saying,” Lacey responded, her voice pitching upward, her shaking head making her blonde hair whip back and forth. “You need to not ‘just say’,” Alyssa cut in quietly. Both sets of eyes turned towards her, regarding this unexpected interjection. “This man has worked so hard to try and take care of you all and keep you happy it almost killed him. So sitting there trying to rationalize away why you made some whiny remark.” Lacey’s face flushed with anger. “Whiny?” she said. “And who are you to be...” Mission control, countdown has just reached zero. “Shut up,” came the roar from Scott, lifting his head form his hands. “You need to sit there and keep your mouth shut for just one damn second,” he told his sister. Her faced blanched white and slowly, she nodded. “Now you listen, and you listen good. I know that it’s nice to have something to drive that’s your own. But the fact of the matter is that you can not expect that a family that’s supported by a wrestler and a receptionist can afford that? I may have just been signed to PRIME, but I’m a low-card guy that doesn’t have any merchandise. So you tell me, where is this going to come from.” Scott took a deep breath. “And those clothes,” he sighed. “What is wrong with the way I dress?” Lacey shot back hotly. “Just because you make the most money doesn’t mean that you can...” “Y’know what, as your older brother, I’ve got some serious standing when it comes to garbage like this,” the big man growled. “You mean like the time you beat up that guy that came to pick me up?” she responded. “He was a date rapist!” Scott roared, slamming a fist down on the coffee table. Unfortunately, the oaken table just didn’t have the ability to hold up. His big, frustrated fist crashed right through the sturdy little table. “Son of a bitch!” he howled at the top of his lungs. “Scotty,” Alyssa said to him, “please calm down.” Scott looked at her and took a deep, shuddering breath. “I’m going outside.” As Scott came to his feet and stalked out of the house, Alyssa turned to Lacey. “You wouldn’t believe it out of him, but Scott is right. Lacey, honey, it’s not that I think there’s anything wrong with dressing that way, but I don’t think it’d be the best choice for you.” “You’re one to talk,” Lacey said, pouting visibly. Oh this was not going well. “Fine, young lady,” Cathy said. “You’re going nowhere tonight.” “But MOM!” she very nearly squealed. “You will not talk like that to a guest!” Cathy retorted sharply. “She thinks she has a point,” Alyssa replied, her attitude remarkably cool. “Yes, you can say that, Lacey, but the difference is, those were my choices and in making those choices, I know very well what will result from them. I know what they mean and what they can lead to.” “You’re so full of yourself you can’t see straight,” came Scott’s rough voice. He waved to his dripping shirt. “It’s raining out, so it didn’t seem all that bright to stay out.” “No, I would think not,” his mother said to him. “How much have you heard?” “Everything, basically. Lacey, I ought to be pissed as hell with you, talking to Aly like that. Matter of fact, I am.” She looked at her big, big brother with wide eyes. “Scott, I’m just...” “You’re just thinking that if you whine about it, then you’ll get what you want.” Scott shook his shaggy, sodden head. “You know, I’m thinking real seriously about ripping through your closet.” “I don’t think he’d be out of line,” Alyssa said, her attitude still very cool. “From what Scott told me, you’re not interested in dating much, you’re just trying to tantilize them. Guys don’t appreciate seeing the goods if they can’t handle them.” Alyssa shook her head. “Trust me, I’ve seen what can happen.” “W-what?” Lacey asked her. “Friend of hers in high school,” Scott said, taking Alyssa’s hand in his massive paw. “She did the same thing you’re doing. No one says she deserved it, but it drove a few drunken football boys over the edge at a party.” Lacey’s eyes were wide, but the question was clear in the air. “Yeah, exactly what you think,” Scott said. “She told me all this when they got backstage passes at an RPW show back then. I rounded up some of the other guys and had a long... talk with them, seeing as how she was too scared to ever press charges.” “You were heading to a party tonight, weren’t you?” Mrs. Matthews asked. Lacey’s downturned eyes and quick nod told all that they’d need to hear. “I tell you what,” Scott said softly, “you go change into something else, and I’ll help you crash a party tonight.” The wolfish grin in his eyes told a great deal. “These parties never go down without some kind of fight... and I think that I’m in the mood to pick on someone.” ----------------------------------------------------------- It wasn’t much fun, going to a high school party to watch out for his sister. Alyssa hadn’t even gone, and he’d been bored out of his mind. His sister had too, as he disocvered. But they had come home two hours ago and Scott was still lying on the couch, his right arm folded behind his head and his clear eyes gazing up at the ceiling. He was concerned. It was time to step up, to roll with some of hte best that PRIME had to offer and he couldn’t miss a step. Here he and Van stood, ready to take on Civil Disobedience. They were a team with quite a history to them. He and Van had a .500 record. He hadn’t talked to his partner for a few days now, and he really didn’t think it’d do much good. He knew Van lived in almost his own fantasy world and sometimes managed to come out long enough to win some matches. He’d been RPW Eclipse champion and they’d pulled a great trick last week. But now? “Scott?” came a soft voice. He lifted his head fractionally. It wasn’t Alyssa, he knew that... “Scott, are you awake?” “Yeah, sis, I’m in here,” he responded in a quiet rumble. Slowly he sat up and manuevered himself back into the corner of the couch. He hadn’t changed since they got home. Still in denim shorts and a black t-shirt, he had been laying there, not even thinking of going to sleep. Lacey made her way into the room, wearing a set of deep red full pajamas that almost made her look like his five year old sister again. She gently padded her way across the carpet and looked at him, her face scrubbed clean and masked with concern. “I’m really sorry about earlier.” She rubbed at her nose momentarily. “I shouldn’t have said that. I know it upsets you and... I’m just really sorry.” “Hey, sis,” he said, wrapping her in a big, thick-armed hug, “it just happens sometimes. You’re 18 and you know it all right now.” “I thought I did...” she trailed off. Her faced changed when she looked back up. “I’m sorry about all that, because I’m worried it might distract you from your match.” “Oh, don’t worry about that. I do enough for me and Van and a couple of other folks myself,” he said with a forced smile. “No, really,” she insisted, “I’m just worried. This is so big, you just joined up and already your team is in a number one contenders thing. It’s just really big.” “Bigger than you know,” he said, raking his hands through shaggy hair. “Just being in this crazy mix is enough. I mean, I’m in PRIME. I’m in their tag division with a crazy-ass, doofy partner and we’re going for the contendership to the straps. It’s wild.” “Civil... Disobedience, is it?” Lacey asked him. “What are they like?” “I dunno,” he replied honestly. “I have no clue. I mean, they’re known for their tag work, have been for a long time. I’m known for tagging with Gabe in RPW, a small-time place without this kind of competition. So here I am, some damn rookie with a clueless dingbat as a partner going up against known names.” He rubbed at his bleary eyes. “Yeah, I’m a little worried.” “Do you know anything about them?” she asked. “Style? How big they are? Anything?” The big man shook his head. “Not a damn thing,” he muttered. “So what do Van and I do? We’re gonna do the same thing we’ve done twice so far: go out there blind and hope for the best. They’ll probably have scouted the hell out of me and him both and be ready to take me out and pin him for an easy win or something.” “Why are you so sure?” she asked. “Scott, they could be afraid of you. They could be past their prime and hoping that a handful of old tricks and a big name would be enough to pull them through. You and Van might not be veterans, but you guys are younger and faster and stronger than them.” “Lacey,” he said, ruffling her sandy hair, “while I appeciate the sentiment, it didn’t do us much good with the Forsaken. Those geezers are so old they fart dust and they still managed to take a win over on us.” Scott stood up slowly and peeled his shirt off. “And with all that worrying done for the night, I’m going to bed.” “Is Alyssa waiting on you?” Lacey asked, chiding him. “Nah,” he replied, throwing his shirt at Lacey. “She’ll be asleep by now. I’ve got to get ready to head out tomorrow anyway, I’ll be needing my rest.” “G’nite, Scott,” Lacey muttered as her brother lumbered down the hall. She followed with a soft whisper: “Thank you.”
