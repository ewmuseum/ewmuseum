The Canterbury Tales (PRIME'd).

1 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 1, 2007 In the 14th Century, Author Geoffrey Chaucer pieced together a collection of stories, Contained inside of a “Frame Tale”. The purpose of these tales were to expose the Middle Age Church as the fraud it was. Every false minister...every conniving clerk...every adulterous figurehead...every rock shaking scandal...He exposed them all. The result? A change in Middle Age religion that would extend full lifetimes. In circa 2004-2009, esteemed champion, Vangelus Olsig, sought to do the same with Professional Wrestling. Expose it for all it was worth and in turn change it's very landscape. Contained below are passages that dive into secrets, scandals, and lies that wrestling promoters never desired to be released towards the public. Contained below are the 21st Century Canterbury Tales. Professional Wrestling: Exposed. ---- Originally, I'd pitched the idea of these memoirs to Chet Worth at the beginning of 2007, immediately after the conclusion of the Culture Shock pay-per-view. That following week, en route to the company's ReVolution event, we'd found ourselves in the presence of several various problems concerning advertising, revenue, and workers who'd seemingly assumed that showing up for work a few hours after deadline was a grand idea. To say it was a stressful day would be a blatant understatement in every sense of the word. In that one day alone, I'd witnessed more backstage politicking, childish bickering, and corporate shenanigans than in the entire month of January. So much drama occurred during that day, and there I was to witness it all first hand. Thankfully, we pulled through and managed to put forth a show that was somewhat decent, if not pretty good. The effects of such a day could still be felt on the plane ride home, however, resulting in a leery and somewhat boring aura of silence. So, out of infinite wisdom (Okay...boredom), I decided to express my feelings towards the events of that day through writing. Taking time to revisit every aching moment, I offered my opinions on how various situations were handled, could've been handled, and the aftermath that would no doubt proceed. I was writing a million dollar story - even if I failed to realize it at the time. So there I sat on the plane with eight hours to spare. Through hand cramps and curious glares alike, I continued to write. Before long, eight hours had turned into four, and four had been shed into a mere two; before I knew it, I'd arrived in the grand city of Atlanta with over thirty pages of grammatical errors, misspelled words, and a handwriting so hideous that it almost seemed criminal. Yet, between the lines of my poor attempt at literature stood the most honest, detailed account of one of the most controversial days in the history of Hin See's wrestling enterprise. A story that would inspire a series of many more for years to come. Throughout the midst of my savage crack at writing, I found myself constantly queried by a highly curious man whose name was Frank Paul. Yes, he had two first names. Frank played the role of referee Nick Hammond on ReVolution. "What're ya writing?" he'd ask, southern accent in full gear. I'd reply with something new every time. The first time, it was a letter to the Wife. The second time, it was a rough outline of a storyline I planned to pitch to Chet. By the time the fifth query rolled around, I found myself highly annoyed and desperate to get him off of my back. Out of infinite wisdom, I promised him the first read as soon as I completed it. It shut him up for the remainder of the plane trip, sure, but ultimately meant that I had to live up to that promise. Paul was quite clear of the position I held in the company and therefore knew the validity of my take on that day's events. Needless to say, after reading the story behind many of that day's firings and the cruel treatment that many of the performers were given by management, Paul was left disgusted. The next day, he quit. Though being the author of an entry that caused a man to quit his job didn't humor me in the slightest, it did reveal to me just how deep and intoxicating my story truly was. Even at thirty pages long, Paul found himself unable to stop reading - and even though it ultimately caused him to lose his job, he made sure to compliment me on what he described as a very "intriguing" and "exposing" piece of work. "That's money in the bank there, kid." he stated as he walked off. Throughout the remainder of the week, that stuck with me. The memoir I held in my hand along with the events I witnessed on a daily basis as a veteran of PRIME Entertainment was indeed money in the bank. It was the scandalous, controversial information that fans wanted to hear and promoters wanted to keep locked away. It was like a dirtsheet in a sense, except it contained the main factor that dirtsheets lacked... Authenticity. Though they have a great knack for snagging the 'inside scoop', those dirt sheet reporters were still nothing more than glorified fans. No matter how acclaimed, there'd always be that hint of 'speculation' in the back of their reader's mind. Is this really authentic? Are his sources truly reliable? But those questions would cease with my reports. They'd be full aware of my position and therefore of my validity. Simply put, I had millions waiting to be made. With grave confidence, I took the idea to Chet. He hated it. Without even taking the time to read the memoir I'd written, he shot down the idea. Now that I think of it, it's probably a good thing he didn't read the memoir - otherwise he'd probably have fired me on the spot. After telling me where I could shove the idea, he sent me on my way. With such humiliation still in tact, I threw the idea out of the window only to find myself picking it up once more a week later. From that moment on, I continued to write and write and write. Even after leaving PRIME in order to begin my own operations, I continued to write. Now at the ailing age of 56, I hold in my possession a collection that the wrestling world has been demanding for years. Many figures have tried to buy it off of me with either the motive of selling it off or keeping it hidden along with the avid secrets that it contains, but I've held steady to my dear collection, constantly reminding myself of Paul's concluding words. Money in the Bank. Now eight years later... I'm cashing in. ---- Scott McKannon. If you'd ask a random PRIME fan who he was, only 2% would more than likely have the slightest idea as to what you were talking about. And that's being generous. But ask an employee and you'll find that his name is as well known as Chet's himself. The man was an activist, able to make things happen. He loved his job and more importantly loved the company he worked for. He'd do any and everything in his power to make sure PRIME was operating at it's best. Apparently, having Neil Carter at the helm didn't qualify under that category. Neil Carter was set to be brought in as a Chief Consultant. In a nutshell, he wouldn't have “power” per se, but instead “influence”. It's only be sheer circumstance that his “influence” would be over those with the greatest amount of “power”. It all worked hand in hand. But Carter was also known for some pretty shady dealings in the past. Eastmont Championship Wrestling, for example, was once ran by respected promoter, Arnold Tucker. Arnold's promotion buzzed greatly. Talent was through the roof, audiences were always sold out...it was very successful indeed. Neil Carter wanted in on that success. Tucker boasted to any and everyone who would listen about the “gold” he'd stricken upon discovering Carter. The two formed a close bond together. Which is why the theory that Carter killed Tucker seemed so ludicrous at the time. Someone killed Tucker, however, and who was there to swoop up the fiery promotion? You guessed it. Ol' Neil Carter. McKannon, who was an understudy in ECW at the time, didn't take to that greatly AT ALL. So when Carter swaggered on in to PRIME, McKannon was furious. He wanted to get Carter out of there as soon as possible, and in order to do so, he devised a plan. Let's set the scene here. Basically, we have a very popular, very high-end media outlet in the form of NGN (New Gaming Network) who've been provided the opportunity to interview several PRIME Personnel concerning various topics ranging from the upcoming Pay Per View events to the release of the PRIME merchandise and everything in between. Granted, this wasn't their first, second, nor even third time engaging in something of this caliber with PRIME, so there was nothing to worry about, right? Not everything was as it seemed, though, as we'd come to learn. Peter Vetra, a staple in the PRIME community, was the first to be interviewed. Following him was Killean Sirrajin, Vince Howard, and Lisa Tyler. For some asinine reason, after only a month on the job, Carter served as the fifth in line. The interview went along the lines of: NGN: So I take it that you're familiar with the Core Wrestling product? Carter: Very familiar, actually. Many of my former proteges are employed by the company and I find it highly intriguing to watch where they are now as compared to where they were when I first took them in over at Eastmont Wrestling. Plus, I'm a mark for that Jason Hughes fellow. NGN: Wow, that was unexpected. I'm tempted to ask if Chet knows of this... with you still being employed by him and all. Carter: Of course Chet knows. There have been many times when I've informed him of some nice shifts going on over at Core and how it wouldn't hurt to expand on them. Of course, he wasn't willing to listen, but he's indeed well aware of my viewing them. What many don't know is that while still employed by PRIME, I'm basically a sitting duck. I'm incapable of being punished in the same manner as some of the other guys. If this was a Karina Wolfenden or a Nova, then yeah, maybe there'd be some form of repercussion, but I'm not aligned with them in any sense. What are they going to do? Take me off TV? Never been on TV. Job me? Gotta accomplish the first option before that can happen. I'm basically in a lame-duck session, if you will. NGN: We understand that we're totally taking a detour here, but given the chance after your contract expires with PRIME, would you be willing to jump over to Core or any other PTC Promotion? Carter: As of now, I see Core and several PTC promotions in the same position as Eastmont during it's early 90 days. Just as Core, XUW, and AWC are now, Eastmont was viewed as secondary, maybe even third, to PRIME. But even with that handicap, I took them and made them a viable competitor. Even in it's dying days, there was still that possibility of Eastmont regaining the glory it once held. If I could do it for them, who's to say I couldn't do it again for AWC? Or Core? Or XUW? It'd be a pretty exciting challenge, actually. Why would NGN ask those questions, you wonder? As I'd discover later myself, McKannon had been feeding all of them to the interviewers. He knew Carter's weakness was Diarrhea of the Mouth and sought to capitalize on that. He'd work for Core, AWC, and XUW if given the chance? Those statements were sure to get Carter fired. Right? ...Right? ............Right? Wrong. Not only was Carter kept on board, but get this... He was given a raise three days later. The madness that resulted from this feud would shake PRIME forever. Upcoming - Chapter Two: The Kyle Lamen Screwjob.
