Date: June 9, 2005 You were never the best student. You cared more about studying the proper execution of Ricky “The Dragon” Steamboat’s armbar than the proper chemical symbol for carbon or doing an essay on “A Separate Peace” in English class. But, out of all the subjects, you always loved history. You loved hearing about the kings of old and previous presidents, of political maneuverings, diplomatic jousting and the series of wars smeared across human history in the blood of the fallen. Maybe it was what your mother used to say. “Remember son, you got to know where you’ve been before you know where you’re gonna go.” Mom knows best, right? That’d explain why you’re here, 735 miles from your slightly-less-shitty apartment in Charlotte, North Carolina and 2,253 miles—kilometers be damned!—from the next PRIME show in Winnipeg. After all, you’re not just out for yourself any more. For better or worse, you’ve got a tag partner. While it resulted in a victory last week, it could just as easily be a 478-pound albatross dragging you down into bad habits… or, perhaps, a future blood feud. You know how that song goes. Heard it, hated it and bought the T-shirt at the concert. To be honest, you never wanted this. Never wanted a partner—at least in the ring. You wanted to make it on your own, prove that Dave Gibson could still get it done in the wake of a surgeon’s scalpel and a checkered past. It’s too late for all that now. Like it or not, you’re part of Team Zambo. But all you can think about is the betrayals of that checkered past. That’s why you’re here. That’s why you’re pulling into the driveway, walking up onto the marble patio and ringing the doorbell, making an unannounced intrusion into an old ally’s life. Like Mama always said, you figure it’s the only way you can figure out where you’re gonna go. The door opens. You find a pair of hard eyes looking up at you in a steely gaze from under a mop of blond hair. He’s let it grow since he walked away from the business, and that’s not the only change. You see less definition in his physique, a little more thickness around his middle. The silence makes you uneasy. Better break it. “Clay,” you say. “Give me one reason why I shouldn’t kick your ass off my property right now.” He doesn’t mince words. It was just one reason why you and Clay Vendetta made such a strong team. You haven’t seen “Classic” Clay Vendetta in almost two years—the last time just a momentary meeting a few months after he left OSW. “I’m not here to cause trouble,” you say. “So give me a reason not to start some.” “Look… I’m here to talk, alright? Just hear me out. You owe me that, don’t you? For old times’ sake?” You waded through a river of gore with him, fighting every fearsome opponent from the sadistic Satanic fury of Hessian to the utilitarian usefulness of Callisto Bane. Then, you and he battled the entire OSW tag division. Da Westsidaz. Civil Disobedience. Ronin. For one night you were better than all of them. For one night, you and Clay stood tall—well, Clay stood tall, you were tied up with Riptah in what was left in the mangled remnants of table—and held the OSW tag team titles high. But then Jane Reagan came calling. With New School Wrestling. Suddenly, four months of teaming with Clay Vendetta didn’t seem to mean quit so much. All those battles, all the blood spilled, all those nights together on the road, they all fell by the wayside as you accepted Reagan and her dollars and betrayed Vendetta. Now, you were here to pay penance. Asking forgiveness from those you offended. If you still went to AA, your sponsor would be proud. The cool, crisp taste of the Sprite over ice helps ease some of the sweltering Miami heat. “How you live in this heat?” ”Hey, this is home. Besides, you know how many people would give their eye teeth to live here 365 like me?” “They must be immune to humidity. I’m sweating like Steven Smith at a construction site right now.” No response from Clay. He sits there, never taking his eyes off you, completely stoic. The only sign he’s still conscious is the rise and fall of his chest as he breaths and the clenching and unclenching of his gnarled hands. You’ve felt the grip in those powerful hands. Felt them try to choke the life out of you, or rip an arm out of its socket, or tear knee ligaments asunder. But he’s felt your strength as well, hasn’t he? “I saw you made a comeback. Thought you were done when OSW closed.” “That’s what all the doctors told me back then. I found one with an experimental procedure, though. Turns out it did the trick. Between PRIME and the wrestling school, things are going pretty great right now.” “Well, how wonderful for you…” “Yeah, like you’re hurting.” You lean over in your leather recliner and can almost see your reflection in the gleaming parquet floor. Straight ahead, you catch a glimpse of the Sub-Zero in Clay’s spacious kitchen. Vendetta is sitting in front of a 40-inch HDTV. You already know what’s beyond the walls of the home. Even if you’d been blind, when you got out of the car you felt the warm, soothing breeze that’s only produced close to the ocean—and you smelled the salt in the air. The sights, smells and sounds help make your apartment seem even shitter than it actually is. “Selling out didn’t work as well as you thought, eh?” You dart your eyes toward Vendetta and see a satisfied smirk. You glance down at your slightly tattered blue jeans and faded red sport shirt and realize he’s probably enjoying seeing his old partner Dave Gibson living just slightly above hand to mouth. “You said you wanted to talk. So talk. And make it fast. Nikki is supposed to be home soon.” “How is married life?” “Fine. We’re expecting. That’s why I want you gone before she gets here.” Nikki Weathers was Clay Vendetta’s girlfriend back in the day. When you and Clay were the Throwbacks, she was your manager. Later, after the split, you dragged her into the squared circle by her hair and made Clay Vendetta LET you break his hand with a hammer. Then, you piledrived her anyway. Back then, the dirt sheets said the moment helped you “arrive” as a top-caliber heel on the national wrestling scene. But now, looking back, it just makes you feel like an asshole. “Look, I don’t know if you watch wrestling these days, but… I made a comeback.” “I saw. How is life teaming with Azala Zameer?” You can’t help the sigh, and inside you squirm at another smirk from Clay Vendetta. “I’m afraid. Not of him, but of me. Everyone who’s been my ally, everyone who’s gotten close to me in this business, I’ve betrayed. I don’t want to do that any more. I don’t want to be that person.” “But you ARE, Dave.” The words sting. You look up and there’s no smirk on Vendetta’s face. “Even when you mean well, even when you want to do right by someone, you still let them down. Maybe it’s money. Maybe it’s a relapse into your old boozing days. Either way, everyone you ally with or grow close to, you betray. You say you’re old school? You say you’re a team player? I say it’s bullshit. “You’re a taker, Dave. Plain and simple. And it’s just a matter of time before you betray Azala. And when that happens? I’ll be watching—and praying he breaks your neck AGAIN.” You force yourself to finish your glass of Sprite in one long swallow. Suddenly, sitting there and taking in Vendetta’s palatial home and vicious words and beautiful wife with child who will be home any minute, you realize you can’t take any more. Without another word, you set the glass on the end table all too aware of the shakiness in your hand. The look in your old partner’s eyes says it all. It’s time to go. You wander to the front door and step through, not waiting for Vendetta to “see you out” like a good host. As you back your rental car out of the driveway, sensory memories run through your brain. You remember the taste of your own blood during the no holds barred street flight with the Cult at By Any Means Necessary. You remember the roar of the crowd as you and Clay Vendetta held the OSW Tag Team Titles high. You remember the dull wet snaps of the bones in Clay’s hand as you struck again and again with the hammer. The shrill sound of his screams. And you remember the unmistakable thrill at maiming someone by your own hand. You try to tell yourself that was a different time. That it was an old Dave Gibson, not the man who wrestles in PRIME today, not the one who teams with Azala Zameer now—for better or worse—on a weekly basis. You’ve never been a godly man, but as you get back on the interstate you say a silent prayer that you do not betray Azala Zameer as you did Clay Vendetta, Shawn Stewart and so many others throughout your own life. Not for the sake of Azala… but to preserve your very soul.
