Shoot out the Steetlights

3 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/1/06 Shoot Out The Streetlights Black is the colour…of my true love’s hair. “Aye love ye Hannah…” “Then never leave me Paddy.” Paddy was surprised by the swiftness of her reply. It seemed stern and even a little pleading. Yet he smiled anyway and stroked her black hair gently, it coming alive in the mid Summer sun. “Aye won’t,” Paddy cooed into her ear. Her lips are like…some roses fair. Paddy leaned back on the makeshift picnic blanket they had brought and she did the same beside him, so that her head lay gently on his shoulder, the palm of her hand stroking his chest. Her presence was nice he decided, the way her body fitted his seamlessly was more than coincidental and so yes, excuse me if I’m wrong, but he really did love her. Paddy gazed at the sky and saw only a wift of cloud, a tiny blemish on a gorgeous open blue. In the middle of a field they lay, the grass long enough to hide them from onlookers. There wouldn’t be any of course, they were a half a mile from the village, but something about being hidden was reassuring. I wish the day, it soon would come… It was two years ago almost since when he’d first laid eyes on her, as she scuttled into early Sunday service in her emerald green dress. “Beautiful,” he had muttered. And she was. Hannah McElhinney, her Father being Henry. Just to the outskirts of the town they lived, he soon learned. For a year and a half, he’d sit in a pew every Sunday morning, waiting for her to walk in, waiting to dazzling him with her beauty. Made him a better Christian, so it did. Genuine innocence stole any prospect of him being able to talk to her. Then one Sunday afternoon, in a state of mingled daze and boldness, he rapped at her front door and asked what was almost a perfect stranger, if she’d like to go to the dance in McCallion’s barn next week. And she’d said yes. And the past moved into the present, and Paddy O’Shea was holding her in his arms. Goddamnit, he just couldn’t stop grinning. She had made him number one in her book. No one had ever done this for him - his mother and father had other favorite children, the few friends he ever had had better friends, he never had a girlfriend before, his teacher’s hated him. He was a population filler, just making up numbers. Until she devoted herself to him one hundred percent. He didn’t think he ever had said thank you for that. When her and I, can be as one. “Aye love ye,” Paddy cooed into her ear, making sure she knew he was genuine. “Then don’t leave me,” she murmured back, almost robotically. Two months later, in a blistering Autumn evening, Paddy O’Shea did just that. With his Grandfather demanding he disown his wayward Father (his Grandfather’s son), Paddy panicked with the predicament and fled, leaving no note, not hide nor hair. And he left his beloved Hannah, refusing any feelings he had had for the girl, that way he wouldn’t yearn for her. How could she forgive him? Someone is there in your life and then is suddenly gone. When there’s no word whatsoever. He could have been abducted for all she knew. And how could she forgive him? Hm? It was characteristic of the O’Shea’s, this running away business… ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ January 30th 1972. They had come that day to make a stand, to tell those that oppressed them, that there would be no more of their injustices they had had to put up with all their lives. They came to make calls for civil liberties and an end to tribal governing. And they came in their thousands. United in anger, Catholics and Protestants of Northern Ireland, marched peacefully through the hallowed streets of Derry. They held their banners high and made their protest without violence and with a hush. For the first time in five hundred years, this city wasn’t the battleground for the waging of war. This city was at peace with itself, so quiet it was, it seemed the whole settlement was holding its breath. The people wanted to see jobs. No more would they go without opportunities equal to their Belfast born comrades, no more would they be neglected “Shoot out the streetlights! Shoot the streetlights!” No more would they be thought of as the black spot of the United Kingdom. They were Irish, and proud they were of that, but this was more than what country you were born in, this was about looking directly into the eyes of those that wrong you and screaming, this is the end! “Shoot out the streetlights! Shoot out the streetlights!” A peaceful protest needed diffusing they said, claimed it could erupt into a full scale riot. In went the paratroopers, kitted out and ready to dance. Like animals, they bundled out of their wagons, almost shaking with the malicious intent on their minds. Their cheeks flushing in the cold Winter evening, they encircled the protesters. “Shoot out the streetlights! Shoot out the streetlights!” the Major screamed to his euphoric men. Soldier A obliged. Sixteen bullets from a tracer, flashed before his eyes. The crowd were caught unawares. They didn’t scream or run however, as the advancing troops approached, as they tried to decipher the motive behind their sudden appearance. Gripping the cusp of each other’s clothes, they looked on, their faces as solemn as those of the children they had put to bed not an hour ago. Sorry son, poppa won’t be coming back tonight I’m afraid. In a moment, they would all be screaming. Leaderless, they would dart in all directions, making it easier for the scattered paras to bring them down. But for this moment, amidst the calm, the look of almost holy solemnity was all that was on show. One man in the crowd began running a comforting a hand over the Short he carried in his jacket pocket. It was heavy, and seemed to drag his left shoulder down as such, but it was all he needed. He fingered the butt of the gun, then it’s trigger, and that him feel all the better. “Shoot out the streetlights! Shoot out the streetlights!” And Soldier B obliged. Something’s weren’t meant to be seen after all. The city held its breath, the peoples jaws began to retch upon in a silent scream then the first volley was fired. Gibsoned…gibsoned. All around people were screaming, darting in all directions as a second volley was fired. All around people were sent flaying through the air. Pleas for help, and that goddamn Major reiterating over and over again ”Shoot out the streetlights”. But Danny O’Shea didn’t hear a thing. With a hint of a smile, creeping over his young face, he removed the pistol from his pocket and raised it, cocking it as he did so. “Last orders Major…” he whispered as his eyes locked onto the mustached ring leader, already fantasizing how this act would accentuate his climb of the IRA hierarchy. Then he was in the guns sight and it was out of Danny’s hands. Once the gun found the target, it gained it’s own mentality, it became the judge. He slowly squeezed the trigger, the major still unawares, people still darting in his field of vision. BANG! Danny closed his eyes and when he reopened them…the major was still standing now with a young soldier beside him who had his tracer aimed at him. Danny’s face was warm though and wet. Casually, he looked at his wrist because for some reason, it was dancing with pain…and was met by a mass of blood. The bullet went right through, he would later discover when he began to think more clearly. But for now, he just stared. The gun wasn’t even in his hand anymore, it had went flailing. And his watch, the beautiful watch his mother gave him was covered in blood, its leather edges scorched black. “What…aye…” Run! And he did but he didn’t know why. All thoughts of guns completely eradicated as he lost all cool. Soon, he was joining in the screaming as bodies dropped everywhere. What time is it he thought stupidly as the image of his scorched watch flashed temporarily in his head. And that call was still being shouted from on high… “Shoot out the streetlights! Shoot out the streetlights!” And they called this day Bloody Sunday…thirteen lines were scratched into a wall somewhere as the crown claimed another handful of lifes. And all Danny would think about for the next twenty years was…it was the day he ran away… ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ It broke his pride, it shattered his spirit but he dug down and said what he should have said seven years ago… “Aye love ye…an‘ aye‘, sorry aye left ye the way aye did,” he muttered, prohibiting himself from staring into her uncompromising eyes. Then a silence followed, one in which she had no intention to breaking. And sustained it when he repeated again and then again. Paddy chanced a glance and seen little more than contempt in her narrowed eyes. She could have at least gave him the closure of the effortless ‘fuck off’, maybe even have laughed in his face. She gave him neither, he wasn’t even worth the effort to muster a scathing attack. He was entitled to nothing but silence, a piece of shit on her shoe that she didn’t want to look at it and get that shit away from me. So, rejected and belittled without the batting of an eyelash, Paddy decided to do what he, his Father, his Father’s Father, and countless other O’Shea’s had done umpteen times before. He tucked his tail between his legs and decided to head back the way he had came. He was a gypsy after all, that’s what they do, move place to place, footloose and fancy free. He didn’t care if the cold bitch didn’t reciprocate his feelings - hell he’d stay if he really wanted to. Yeah, he would! It’s the smell just…yes, the smell. Unbearable see, don’t want toxic fumes cutting his life short. In rural Ireland? Sure, why not.? So, his bags packed in his quest for…er, fresher air, he emerged from the porch of his family home, an old duffle bag stuffed with odds and ends, and draped over his shoulder casually. It was cold, his breathe condensing in front of his face was seamlessly blending in with the morning dew. He stomped his feet, rubbed his hands, raised the collar of his old khaki army jacket. He flicked his sleeve back and looked at his watch. It didn’t tick, never had since the day his father had gave it to him. Scorched black and stained a dark red, it was pointless, but it’s history his Father whispered, and aye deserve t’ be reminded of what aye didn‘t do. He could have stopped it all…he never said what all was. Paddy covered the watch and began to make his way down the winding path, to the train station. He didn’t want to think right now, quite akin to his general reaction of completely blanking out any form of humiliation in the hope that history would decide to cut the ol’ pykie a little slack. It never did but hell, he didn’t want to deal with that shit right now. The walk would serve it’s purpose of contemplation on the future. A pulse title around his waist. That would be nice…wouldn’t be the pulse title though, and it sure as feck wouldn’t be a Super Fucking Duper championship, pardon my language. Trust that attention seeking, wiener deprived prat to make a bigger dick of himself. As if he didn’t have enough to put up with over at the AWC, now he has to get involved with the childish games over here? Busting laptops, covering people in ants, storming into ladies locker rooms…come on, Adam, really, can’t you put yourself forward a little bit better. Okay, calm down now, you’re just a little flustered and- He’s undeserving. Paddy’s too damn modest to admit it but I will. A person like that, who steals and cheats and steals some more, shouldn’t be a face for a federation, he should be confined to mopping floors and opening doors. And keeping his mouth closed while he’s at it. Okay you can have your fun, fine, but don’t piss on somebody’s doorstep because they aren’t going to clean it up for you. Finished? Does it feel good to pass on your frustrations? Paddy breathed hard. It felt good to pass on his frustrations. He gazed out at the land. His ancestors had taught, he could not own this land, no matter how you fenced it and divided it. This wasn’t their land to claim. You can only respect it and hope it will accept your presence. It is a privilege not a right. And Paddy believed it. The earth had soul, it was corseting through him every time the sole of his foot touched soil. Chain of lakes in the horizon, a perfect sapphire piercing the hills on it’s journey to the sea; valleys like empty cups cut deep into the ground. It was truly beautiful. He could talk all day about the reasons why he was leaving, he would spent another deciphering the truth from it. But one thing he couldn’t lie about was the captivating beauty before him. In a world of smog and smoke, this was a haven, his haven, and if there were no faces to draw him back, the dipping valleys and the emerald hills, would always keep a grip on him. And for the first time that day, he sucked in the stimulating icy air, and smiled. “Goodbye…aye’ll see ye again someday…hopefully soon.” The fact he couldn’t even think of a person here who’d want to say farewell to him suddenly struck him and he sighed. He supposed the villagers never forgot the day the boy ran away, from his sick Grandparents, from his girlfriend. Maybe he didn’t deserve forgiveness from any of them, but if they only knew the predicament he was in- Writing a few short lines isn’t hard. -they might understand. It was beginning to amaze him that he was seeing no people. He was passing the occasional house, most of them so backwards they seemed only a step up from a thatched cottage, yet no one was in sight. They might be on the bogs cutting turf perhaps. After all, many houses didn’t stay along the path and few sat together. His wondering was cut short when he nearly tumbled over a little girl. He managed to balance himself. “Shit…feck.” He grabbed the girl’s arm before she fell over, and then dusted himself off. “Er…sorry about tha’ miss.” At this twist of the road, there was a mountain stream on his right that came battering its way down, making white lace in a ten foot fall before it was swallowed by a gullet under the road. On each side of the stream, the grass was very green and it was here the girl was kneeling and collecting daisies. Paddy stopped to look at her. She was…seven or eight perhaps, and dressed in her Sunday best of an emerald green dress. She remained still however and merely stared at him. “Er…hallo there!” Paddy said with a jolly voice. It seemed to take a little while for the voice to penetrate to her mind. Then she looked up at his face and Paddy thought her features to be hauntingly familiar. “Who are you, now?” Paddy went on. She put her head to one side as if to hear better. “Aye said, who are you?” he repeated. There was a pause. “Francis,” she whispered. “I am Francis.” Paddy coughed in the silence that followed then kicked a pebble away at his feet. Paddy took another glance at her face. She wasn’t all there, he supposed, her eyes were devoid of any true life, her emotionless expression all too different from the face of a child. But it was she that spoke first. “You would like some flowers?” she asked. She took a daisy chain she’d tried to make and held it out towards him. She was close, and Paddy could see those eyes just didn’t have the intelligence of someone her age. She was afflicted, and what a terrible shame he thought, for a girl as pretty as her. “Thank ye…aye’ll put it on.” And Paddy did. “I don’t hurt the flowers?” she said suddenly. “How?” “When I stick a pin in them?” she replied. She looked mildly concerned as she spoke. “No, ye don’t hurt them…” Paddy suddenly felt very sad. All his troubles and woes seemed nothing compared to the inner grief this girl will suffer when she gets older. Not having a normal life…not even coming close to one. He felt he had to leave suddenly. “Well Francis, aye must be off. It was nice talkin’ t’ ye.” Francis thought for a second then waved her hand at him. As Paddy backed away, he seen the waterfall was above her. The sun caught it and made a hazy rainbow above the water. He shook his head and blinked, the scene was just unreal for him. Then it got more unreal when he backed into someone else. He swung around and was looking into the face of- “Hannah?” She didn’t respond for a while, only looked at it with muted frustration. “Going again are ye?” This time, Paddy didn’t respond. “Well…aye figured as such. Aye couldn’t care less t’ be frank. But aye have t’ tell ye something’. Because if aye don’, aye doubt if the Lord in heaven would let me through the gates. An’ after aye tell ye, aye hope ye kno’ this changes nothing.” She was talking to him like a stranger. What happened to the dances Hannah? What about the picnics? The walks? The films?…feel something, please! “Wha’ is it?” Paddy whispered as if the secret was dirty. Hannah raised a slender finger and pointed to the girl. “That’s my daughter.” Paddy sucked in a breath. “And she’s yours too.” He thought of the implications and was surprised that there was only anger running through his mind. He seemed to answer all the questions himself though. Why didn’t you tell me you were pregnant? She didn’t know until you left. Why didn’t she come look for him? You had hidden yourself so you couldn’t be found. I’m really a father? Yes Paddy…yes you are. “Say tha’ again…” Paddy said, perhaps he heard her wrong. “Ye heard me.” She began to walk away form him. Paddy needed only a split second, he didn’t know how and he didn’t know why but he was running, and it felt good. Hannah was probably startled as hell to see the O’Shea darting past him, like a child running away from trouble and he felt pathetic for it. But there was no other way. He needed to get out of this place NOW! No thinking, no hesitation. NOW! Only her words screaming into his ears brought him round. “That’s right! Run away again! Go! Jus’ like ye, isn’t it?” Paddy’s run slowed and then stopped. A daughter…a daughter…a daughter… He turned and walked back up the path. “Someone shoot out the streetlights…” he murmured, and continued his walk to face the future. TO BE CONTINUED
