The Drought (.01)

24 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/24/07 “Yeah. Sure, kid. Go ahead and have another. That’ll shut me up.” The whiskey splashes against the back of his throat, barely tickling the taste buds on his tongue before burning and swirling its way down his throat. Fire courses through his veins for half a second. The moment is fleeting. Gone too fast. Tyler Rayne places the glass on the nightstand, fingers lingering for a moment as he contemplates the brown bottle next to the alarm clock. His hand relinquishes the glass, a heavy sigh heaving his scarred chest. The hand rises, a nervous reflex to pass fingers through hair that no longer exists. A slow growth of stubble has itched its way from the depths of his skull, covering his crudely shaven scalp. His fingers pass quickly over the stubble, tips dropping into divots and scratching over scars left by the straight razor. “Hell of a job they did on ya. Figures the thing you’d miss most is your hair. Hope to Christ I wasn’t such a fuckin’ fairy.” Tyler’s eyes dart to the bottle once more. A mocking laugh turns his attention forward, away from the floor he’d been staring blankly into. No one is there. Only a mirror, the reflection of a man he barely recognizes as himself staring just as blankly back. The lazy growth of stubble across his face is actually longer than that on his head. His eyes are bloodshot and heavy. His infamously scarred torso is spotted with bandages and wraps. The smell of roasting pork wafts in through the open window, reminding Tyler of the healing flesh beneath the bandages. He closes his eyes, and there, on the backs of his eyelids, he can see the blood-red strips sliced from his body with the same razor they’d used on his head. He remembers the sickening pop of his own fat as the flesh was cooked, right there in front of him, and then tossed on a plate at his feet. The only thing they’d feed him for an entire month. “How fucked up is that? I mean, seriously, who thinks of some sick ass shit like that? That is all kinds of fucked up, and you won’t even get up off your lame ass and kill these cocksuckers.” The lids of his eyes seem to lower of their own accord, his hands passing from the back of his head to the front. A long stretch of silence spreads across the room as he sits on the bed with head in hands, forehead sinking deeper and deeper into his palms. The seconds tick by in time with his ragged breaths, head slipping further down the slope of his cupped hands. Just before it seems he’ll lose grip all together, a sudden intake of breath heaves his entire body upwards, and Tyler Rayne finds himself on his feet, watching a scarred body so much like his own pass through his peripheral vision. He stands, head still hung low, eyes shifting amidst the pile of clothes on the floor. “You can’t ignore me forever, asshole. Sooner or later, you’ll have to face this. Face me.” At this his head snaps upward and Tyler finds himself staring into the dead, black eyes hidden beneath a shock of rotting flesh and ashen bone. He can’t help but notice, there in the corner of his eye, that it’s only his own reflection flashing back at him from the mirror. There are no words. No expressions. Just another long, silent pause as he stares into those emotionless, unforgiving orbs. Tyler bends, plucking an old Murderdolls t-shirt from the pile of rags on the floor. He pulls the t-shirt over his head quickly, boots thumping against the floor as he takes his first step toward that door in days. His fingers fall upon the cool brass of the door handle, but pause there, suspended in the moment before a final curl to grasp. The cold, piercing voice of his alter ego brings Tyler to a stop yet again. He doesn’t turn. Doesn’t face the accusing black eyes hidden behind that grotesque façade of violence and death. He may not have to look, but he cannot keep from listening. “When are you gonna stop running, huh? When are we gonna stop hiding and get out there and fight. Kill.” “I’m tired of fighting.” His throat burns with the strain of producing the sound. His voice comes out cracked and ragged. He hasn’t spoken in days. His hand tightens around the door knob, yet still, he cannot seem to make the twist. “I WANT MY REVENGE!” Now he does turn. The expression on his face is tired. Exhausted. His eyes hang low in the shadow of the past month’s tortures. One of the rare times he’s ever actually looked his age. Shown the years and the hardship and the heartache beneath that smile and stubble and insufferable charm. “I’m tired of fighting. Tired of bleeding and playing the tough guy. To be honest, I’m just fuckin’ tired. I’m done killing. Been done a long time now. I told you that. Go ahead and haunt me. Drive me crazy if you want. Not like it gets much crazier than talking to myself anyway. But I’m done with it. All of it.” For a handful of heartbeats, there is nothing but the steady rise and fall of his breath. He stands there, staring at the crazed figment of his broken mind, those dead, black eyes glaring maliciously back at him. “This isn’t over.” For the first time since waking, Tyler smiles. It’s an amused, condescending expression. He offers no words to accompany it. This time, when his fingers fall upon the cool knob, there is no pause. No hesitation. There’s the faint click of the door mechanism disengaging, the creak of hinges as he pulls the door open and steps through. Bright, beautiful sunlight illuminates the hallway, shining in through the kitchen windows and large patio doors of the dining room. Through the doors he looks out to the beach and the ocean beyond. He can see two figures, dark and unrecognizable, walking along the edge of the tide. Further up the beach, the tall silhouette of an old friend. And there, just outside the door, a beautiful woman with long dark hair and a bountiful chest poking at meat roasting upon the grill. There is a pang in his stomach that has nothing to do with the tortures of last month. His eyes linger upon the bright streaks of purple that highlight her hair. The sharp and striking features that intrigued him from the very moment he’d first seen her. A cheap, dingy apron stretched tight against those amazing breasts. The corners of his lips curl as he watches, remembering a time, long ago, when he would have wanted nothing more than this moment. Every day. A clatter from the kitchen brings him from his stupor, and Tyler trots through the last bit of hallway to find Katterina Wylde pulling plates from the cupboard. She turns, smiling as her eyes immediately fall upon her own image emblazoned across his chest. “Nice shirt.” “One of your better.” She takes one last plate from the cupboard before closing it and turning to face him, her small and elegant frame leaning back against the edge of the counter. “Who called?” “Huh?” “I thought I heard you talking to someone. Just wondering who called.” “Oh. No one. I was just…” “…talking to yourself?” She raises a suspicious eyebrow, though he can see the corners of her lips twitching with the suppression of a playful smile. “Something like that.” He cuts the connection, pulling his eyes from hers and drawing them to the floor. All hints of a smile fall from her face as she pushes off the counter, drawing to her full height. “What they did, Ty… I can’t even imagine what you’re going through.” “Can’t be worse than some of the things Jakob—“ “I always knew what I was getting into with him. Deep down, we all knew who he was. What he was capable of. We’ve all been through some shit. You and I the worst of it. I can’t bear to think about some of the things he did to me. Some of the things…what happened with the Fallen. All of it…it’s just so…wrong.” The fingers of her left hand ever so slightly graze against his. Her right caresses gently against his chin, lifting to bring his eyes back to hers. “We’re all so fucked up, Ty. What we are. What we do. I never thought…you…you’ve always got me. Always understood. And I can’t… You’re not there anymore, Ty. You’re here. With us. You’re not alone. You don’t have to do this—“ “Dinner’s ready.” His head snaps up immediately, eyes darting toward the patio door where Miss Dionne has pushed half her body through the smallest of openings there. She frowns, just slightly, at the sight of the two in such close proximity. Katt hastily backs away, turning to grab the dinnerware, but Dionne has already returned to the cool wind and sand outside before the Dark Angel can turn ‘round again. “Sorry. I…” “Forget it. She and I, we’re just…it’s always gonna be a little awkward. I figure she still thinks I should have asked her to marry.” “Most people do.” “And you?” “I know why you didn’t. I know how much you loved her. How hard it must’ve been to walk away.” “We’re…really fucked up, aren’t we? I mean, really fucked up.” She smiles. “If we weren’t, I imagine things would get pretty dull around here.” “Yeah. Yeah, I suppose they would.” “C’mon, let’s go eat. We still gotta figure out what to do with this kid. I imagine your friend would like to have his witness back.” “Prob’ly. Guess I oughta thank Duke for watchin’ ‘im while I was gone, eh.” “Yeah. Maybe you should take him to Japan with you. I think he misses this. The action. Might be good for him to have another little adventure. Make him feel important.” “And you’re tired of having to deal with his whining.” She laughs, and this time Tyler too cracks a smile. “For such a big guy, he is quite the little bitch sometimes.” Their eyes meet for another second, all the unsaids laid to bare in that fleeting glance. He nods, unable to fully express the weight of everything pressing upon him. She turns and leads their way from the kitchen, Tyler trying desperately hard to ignore the imagined breath of his own evil doppelganger on the back of his neck.
