Confidence is the Key

29 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06-29-10 Intro When Steve Starr officially announced that he was making a return to active competition in PRIME, many people yawned. There were no fireworks, people were not dancing in the streets. Honestly, Steve Starr was a guy who maybe wrestled a handful of matches in his first stint in PRIME, none of them memorable by the way. He somehow lucked into a title match for the Intense Championship, won that match only because the current champion was set to retire, and vanished from our conscience as quickly as he arrived. So technically, Steve Starr’s impact was minimal at best. He did shine nicely in the first ever “Jewel In The Crown” tournament, advancing all the way to the finals before coming up short against Ian English, but other than that he was merely a footnote in the extensive backdrop of PRIME’s history. To most casual wrestling fans, they could not point Steve Starr out of a crowd. He’s just not that important. That’s why Ultraviolence meant a hell of a lot more to Steve Starr than to his adversaries. It is not simply because it’s “The Return of Steve Starr”. Most of the new PRIME superstars have no recollection of a Steve Starr. Ultraviolence was important because you only get to make that first impression once. This was that one chance to show this new breed that any hype that may have surrounded this return is justified. It was also an important stepping stone for Steve Starr. This marked the first time in over two years that Steve Starr actively competed in a wrestling match. His last official contest came all the way back in March of 2008, at a show for the Killer Instinct Wrestling Federation. No the company was not based off the early 90’s arcade fighting game of the same name. And that show did not even take place on American soil, it was in Canada. It has been even longer since Steve Starr competed in front of American fans; you know the group considered the most intelligent on the planet. Or the most fickle depending on who you ask. There was a tremendous amount of pressure on Steve Starr to deliver. Getting things started off on the wrong foot could have spelled disaster. Picking up a victory at Ultraviolence, a PPV event no less confirms what he has believed all along. Not only does Steve Starr still have it… but he may just be better than ever now. End Intro Start Scene 1 Kansas City, MO Steve Starr is definitely looking fresh and clean. Opening the door to his room reveals Steve Starr dressed in black designer leather jacket, a matching black v-neck, black shades, a black Chicago White Sox New Era fitted cap(Steve’s a die-hard Royals fan, but the White Sox cap went better with the attire), basic black Levi Jeans, and a pair of fresh designer kicks to complete the black-out. Manager/confidante Manuel Rivera takes one look at Steve and utters: “Damn Steve, I might just wear black for a year straight.” Steve chuckles at his buddy’s classic Jay-Z reference. The color black had become synonymous with Steve Starr recently. Instead of vibrant colors and pretty pastels, Steve Starr preferred to keep things simple inside and out of the ring. Besides, it was not hard to find things to match with the color black. Everything goes with black. A lot about Steve Starr has changed since he last competed in PRIME. “I think a lot of people are foolish when they say that individuals cannot change. I’m living proof that a guy can make a change if he feels the need. I think back to some of the things I used to do when I was younger, and man I do not even recognize that guy anymore. For example, Steve Starr’s debut victory at the Ultraviolence PPV would have normally been cause for a celebration. The old Steve Starr would have rented out some lavish hotel suite, invited a bunch of strippers, druggies, and lord knows what other criminals. The Patron would have been flowing, weed, cocaine, and ecstacy would have no doubt been in abundance. Steve Starr used to party hard and it cost him. “You know what I did following this last match? I went home. I grabbed a copy of the tape. And I studied.” Sure does not sound all that exciting. One has to wonder, where has all the excitement in Steve Starr’s life disappeared to? “People say this all the time, but as you get older you start to realize what’s important. I am in the process of starting my own family now. I am a soon to be married man. I got my first kid on the way. I could never live the same kind of life that I used to portray in the past. That’s not what I am about, and I don’t want people to view me in that light. It was a dark and chilly night. The temperatures had dipped just below 50 degrees, and a few clouds started to form giving the impression of a light drizzle. The usual energy that filled the streets of downtown Kansas City, Missouri were absent as most had probably already headed indoors in fear of the weather. Steve and Manny were headed to a banquet for Steve’s Starrs, which was an athletic camp for young teenagers in the Kansas City area. Steve had been running the program for the past 7 years, but he had only taken an active interest in the program the past three years Before, you know when he was so busy worrying about making money, Steve Starr simply left the duties with some lucky assistant. He might show up to an event for 10 minutes, sign a few autographs and pose for a few pictures, and then on his way he went. The kids were usually so in awe just to be in Steve’s presence, they normally never complained that Steve Starr was actually absent from the camp. The event was being held at the Municipal Auditorium in downtown Kansas City. Steve Starr had suddenly taken a divine interest in giving back to his community. “I just don’t wanna be ‘that guy’,” Steve elaborates. “You know ‘that guy.’ He leaves his hometown, makes a ton of money, and then never ever comes back. He totally separates himself from his past. “Well I think it’s great what you’re doing Steve. I have always believed in maintaining a role within the community. Especially when you have the kind of clout that you posess.” Steve Starr may not have much of a following as he once did, but in Kansas City he is still revered for being their hometown hero. The people have watched him grow as a young, talented, high school kid into a professional wrestling money machine. While most of the mainstream world condemned Steve Starr for being a greedy, selfish, “money-whore”; he always could return home to Kansas City and still be adored by everyone. It is not an uncommon sight to visit downtown Kansas City, and catch Steve Starr walking through as if he were a normal citizen. No bodyguards, no entourage, just Steve. Municipal Auditorium, is an art deco wonder. Constructed in 1936 at a cost of $6.5 million, it was the highlight of the government’s 10-year public improvements plan that was approved in 1931. The building is a masterful blending of work that was designed to accommodate the broadest possible range of events; everything from conventions, athletic events, exhibitions, industrial shows, concerts, plays, or lectures, no matter the size. The building houses three large areas; a multi-purpose arena, the music hall, and a stylish ballroom properly named “The Little Theater.” Maybe a few years from now, depending on the success of Steve Starr’s current profession, he can provide to host his banquet in the actual arena. But for now, with an estimate of 200-300 people expected to arrive, “The Little Theater” will have to make due. The original idea for the event planned for the kids to help pay for the event with various fundraisers. You know the like, bake-sales, buying wholesale packs of candy from Sam’s Club and distributing at school, car-washes, you name it. When the kids exceed the initial projections, Steve Starr was so thrilled he decided he would up the ante. First, he decided that he was cover the expenses to rent “The Little Theater” for a night, complete with amenities such as catering, decorations, a DJ, the whole 9. “When Steve Starr throws a party, it has got to be done right. I’m not going to have my name associated with some bullshit.” Ah Steve… he used to be so tight with his money. He was the guy who would skip out on checks at restaurants, and if he did pay, you better not expect a tip. One of his favorite tips: “don’t walk down dark alleys at night.” But that was back when Steve was basically raising his two younger twin sisters by himself, and when he was trying to make it through the Royals farm league. Do you ever think about what life will be like ten years from now? Steve Starr always did and does… and even in his wildest dreams he could never imagine the turn his life has taken. At first he seemed destined for greatness, a hot-shot athlete who excelled in two sports. While he was certainly no dummy, Steve Starr never really tried in academics. That probably scared big time colleges away, as Steve Starr barely even attended class. The coaches were so enamored with him, they had no problem getting teachers to pass him through. Back then Steve Starr did not care about that type of stuff. He had a lot on his plate. That probably explains why he has taken such an active interest in giving back, and becoming a positive role model for the young up and comers in Killer City. In planning for the banquet, members on the committee thought it would be fitting to run a special tribute for Steve Starr. They had compiled a videotape of some of Steve’s signature moments; including the day he was drafted by the Royals, to the time he captured the PRIME Intense Championship, to the day he announced that he would be taking an overseas voyage across the country, challenging anyone in any promotion to a match to prove that he was truly the world’s best. Steve had no knowledge of this special tribute video, he normally allows a select few to actually book the activities for the banquet. He normally shows up, presents a few awards to some of the outstanding kids in the camp, issues the final word, and then the event turns into a party. There’s a DJ playing live music, the kids and adults kick back and enjoy themselves. During the presentation of the tribute video, it appeared that Steve Starr got a little teary-eyed. It is certainly possible that watching a video of all your great accomplishments can do that to a person. Or maybe those tears were related to something else. Perhaps Steve felt as if there was some unfinished business that needed to be taken care of. Either way, Steve Starr delivered a powerful message to the children at the end of the event. “I appreciate each and everyone of you for coming out and participating. Without the contributions from the kids, this is not possible. For a lot of the people who may not be familiar with me or how I run the camp, I am a huge believer in self-confidence. It is not about being cocky or arrogant, but as a young man growing up, no matter what I always believed in myself. I think confidence is an often overlooked trait in a person’s character. I think it is a critical part of being successful.”
