A Winding Road to Inevitability

Date: I can do this in three days July 26th, 2008 1 AM Hanging over an endless amount of water 25 miles off the coast of Myrtle Beach, South Carolina, Christian Daniels rose to his feet. Barely able to see any lights from land while staring out the airplane window, he checked the restraints on his parachute. In the process of tightening harnesses, Christian looked at the rest of his family strapped into their seats, ready to leap back into the fray of warfare. Outside of the wrestling ring, the art of death was perhaps their greatest collective sport. The plan was simple but innovative enough to avoid detection. Instead of donning disguises and forcing their way back into the States, or sneaking over on a cargo ship, they opted to be aided by the night and open sky. Renting a plane for the weekend, they flew overnight from Taiwan and slated to parachute onto the shore of Myrtle Beach. From there an associate would drive them the necessary four hours into Raleigh. While waiting on their pilot, Brandon Smith, to give an all clear signal, everybody had a moment to reflect on the past and future. Escaping via the grandiose smokescreens of a house explosion, foiling North Carolina law enforcement agencies, allegiances were made and all swore never to return to the United States again. Yet, in just over two years, all promises were being recanted. They had all given up a life of fame to extinguish their personal problems and acquire a clean slate. Now, Devin needed help and nobody could refuse him. He was the one person who had sixteen years of bond to his name with each family member. Even though seven years separated the eldest in age, everybody treated him like a son. Endless time and patience were invested into him, and at some point reimbursement for all the gifts given was expected. On the one hand nobody could be ashamed of his accomplishments. Taking Devin from a wide eyed doe who didn’t know anything other than Puritanism at the age of thirteen and turning him into a former CEO and global wrestling superstar before the age of thirty was nothing short of spectacular. He was the personification of their image, and they couldn’t be happier. Yet, they were now convinced that one can never fully escape their past. Devin’s father had recently run him through the mud, sullying his reputation to the point of submission. Picking up the pieces and super gluing Devin back together to get half of what he was today was strenuous unto itself. Now he was entrenched with figures from their past, one assumed never to be heard from again. Nobody would allow Devin, their child, to become a statistic of history. Without a doubt in their minds, this would be the last hurrah in North America forever. It didn’t matter what the cost was. It didn’t matter what the body count was. Whatever they had to do would be done and nobody would be left behind. They were coming to take their son back and wrap him in a protective bubble so nobody could ruin him again. Brandon Smith gave the thumbs up. Christian pulled down the latch and allowed powerful gusts of wind to sling open the door. In succession, Chris, Rachel, Michael, Jason, and Matthew all unfastened their seatbelts and assembled in a line. After doing a final check of their equipment, Christian gave the OK and then looked at Brandon. "Make sure to call Devin when you land and let him know what’s up." Brandon nodded his head and flashed a small cellular phone. Like members of the army doing a late night dive, everybody filed out of the plane one by one into the endless abyss. Their whole downward journey would last a total of about sixty seconds before they collided with the smooth sand of Myrtle Beach. ----------------- July 26th, 2008 8:30 PM Rubbing sleep from tired eyes, Larry Connors reached into his office drawer and retrieved a small canister. A dragon emblem grazed the front of a black bottle with the letters Gazuu Energy underneath the design. Contained within two ounces was enough energy to keep anybody up for five straight hours without the crash of a typical guarana enhanced drink. Larry had a drawer chock-full of the $2.99 shots. He unscrewed the cap and threw back his head, allowing the low calorie boost to go down easy. After letting out a refreshing "Ahhh", Larry used the tip of his forearm to wipe any remaining drops on his upper lip. His eyes gazed downward at the paperwork in front of him. The only new developments in any pertinent cases were in the death of a prostitute who, according to fellow streetwalkers, had an angry customer who stalked her when she was off the clock. Thirty eight days, twelve hours, forty seven minutes, and thirteen frustrating seconds had elapsed since Devin Shakur stormed away from their botched interrogation. Since then, nothing new had panned out. Their eyewitnesses had gotten foggier on crucial details, and their circumstantial case became even more incomplete. A massive sweep of Mike Grimwood’s office yielded nothing which indicated Devin was involved in committing the murder. None of the forensic analysis provided anything useful other than strands of Devin’s hair. Unfortunately, nobody was able to deduce at what time the follicles were distributed. They were at a dead end and losing traction by the day. Soon the investigation would be pushed to the backburner and true criminals like Devin Shakur and the Smith family would be allowed to roam free without repercussion. It sickened him to the very core and made him question the judicial design of the world. A knock at his door shifted his focus over toward his twin brother, Steven. The two exchanged a glance while Steven leaned against the wall. Stretching the elastic on his lapel with his right thumb, he looked uncomfortable and it wasn’t hard for Larry to read his brother. "Something on your mind?" Larry said, swinging around his office chair and discarding the latest Gazuu shot. Steven replied back, "You mind if I vent for a second?" Larry shook his head, "Shoot." "Do you believe in karma or superstition or things like that?" Scrunching his face, Larry gave quick thought to the question, "Honestly…No. I think believing in things like that diminishes one’s own free thoughts. Why?" Steven scratched the back of his head and put more weight on the left side, pushing harder into the wall, "It’s just…I’ve had the feeling something bad is gonna happen real soon." "Where did you get the feeling from?" "I really don’t know if there’s a where. Everything has just been a little off lately." "You can say that again." Steven shuffled his feet almost like a child who was scared of presenting his view, "And you don’t think that’s going to entail something bad happening in the future?" "Well, by that logic, something good could also happen." "Bro, we’re detectives, nothing good ever happens to us." Larry offered a smirk and stood up, experiencing the most superficial symptom of dehydration, thirst. At least the new energy drinks still held some characteristics of the older versions. "Just try not to worry about it." Larry passed by his brother out into the bedroom and quickly toward the kitchen. "Shit like that is gonna have you tossing and turning and we’ve only got a few more Tylenol 3’s on the old ‘script." Steven nodded and tailed his brother out into the kitchen. "Besides," Larry continued, "Like you said we’re not in a job that generates positive connotations most of the time." Larry closed the refrigerator door and handed a Budweiser to his brother. "It’s probably pretty commonplace, now that I think about it, to have that thought." Squeezing the caps off their beverages, the brothers clang bottles. "I suppose you are right, bro." Steven said, taking a big swig, "Chalk that one up to a boring couple days at the office?" "Yeah, you know something will come up soon and we won’t have time for chats like this." "Hope you are right." Steven said, walking over toward the window. "Course I’m right. Crime never takes a break and neither do we." Larry’s eye caught the movie rack next to the television. "You want to watch some TV, get your mind off this?" "Sure" "I’ll let you pick what we watch." Later that night, neither man fell asleep right away. For an hour, they separately wondered about the potential avenues Steven’s thought could produce. Much to their chagrin neither had enough depth perception to fully anticipate what awaited them when their eyes opened the next day. July 26th, 2008 9 PM Pullen Park was a quaint majestic piece of scenery locked deep in downtown Raleigh. Spanning seventy two acres, the sanctuary held something for all ages: Ample sidewalks with breathtaking nature, endless trees and flower gardens that never stopped flourishing, sport courts for the athletic, swing sets on multiple playgrounds for the children, and even a train that toured the entire park for the lazy who wanted nothing more than to soak in the atmosphere. Tommy Morrison and Frank Davis always enjoyed taking a leisurely stroll after leaving the Triangle Transit 301 bus. Following a well organized route, they could take in the finer aspects of the park and be home within 20 minutes. The walk also allowed any remaining frustration from work to dissipate. They were college friends who set out to conquer the world and capture their slices of the American dream before the age of thirty five. Having six figure salaries, two story homes, and the optimal wife with two kids combination indicated they were rather close to that objective. But now there was an opportunity to breathe rarified air. Stuck under the tutelage of Mike Grimwood and the Braintrust ever since 2000, Frank and Tommy were slated to take over the company thanks to an en masse administration exit after Grimwood’s untimely murder. They were also the individuals who identified Devin Shakur to Larry and Steven Connors. Frank was adamant about checking in with the pair on a consistent basis, but always received the company line of "Nothing new has developed. We will inform you should something manifest." Stepping down the four steps of the 301, Frank waved back toward the bus driver before watching the vessel ride off into the sea of traffic and darkness. Immediately they came upon their route, which started off with a fresh water pond full of frogs hopping around on lily pads. One night while intoxicated, the duo vigorously attempted to get the slimy creatures to replicate the Budweiser commercial. Needless to say it was an epic fail but still considered a fond memory. No ribbits interrupted their walk on this particular evening. Instead they walked past the pond toward the swing set and train tracks. Off in the distance they saw the faint outline of a carousel encased within a waist high level gate. Frank, who was the spitting image of Robert Downey Jr., started out the conversation, "I’m going to be glad when school starts in a couple weeks. Jake does nothing but sit in front of the TV all day and play video games. When he’s staring at that screen, I can’t get him to do shit. Homework will do him good." Tommy, who could pass off for the stereotypical car salesman, replied, "You know just because he goes to school that only means he’s going to go over to other house and play games there. I heard Jimmy just got his boys an X-Box 360 for Alan’s birthday." Rolling his eyes, Frank groaned, "Make a mental note for me not to allow either of them over to Jimmy’s house. I’m not particularly fond of him anyway." "What’s wrong with him? Seems like a nice guy." "I picture him being the alcoholic father who beats his wife when the kids aren’t home." "Wow, Frank, that’s pretty damn harsh." "Take a long glance at him next time instead of being mesmerized by his fancy toys and you’ll see things my way." "You gotta admit that plasma flat screen he’s got is kick ass." "I wouldn’t be surprised if he doesn’t pay taxes to keep the amount of toys flowing into that house." "Now that’s just cynical." The guitar intro from "Layla" screamed from Frank’s pocket and he wasn’t pleased. "Ugh, I’m ten minutes late for dinner and she’s already freaking out." Frank reached into his pocket and flipped open the phone. "Hello?" "Dad?" The voice on the other end wasn’t his wife, but of his son Jake. Frank slipped into the role of loving father, "Heyyy champ, how’s it going?" Tommy barely saw the incoming figure rushing toward them. He heard the footsteps but thought nothing of them until the sound increased progressively in his ears. When his head perked up, he saw the zigzagging outline of a human body clad in complete black. Tommy was confused when the person stopped twenty five feet from him. Tommy was even more confused when he spotted the small muzzle of a gun aimed at his head. Screaming out of the 9 millimeter at 1000 feet per second, the bullet easily found Tommy’s stagnant body. The actual bullet itself sliced through his epidermis and then went effortlessly through his prefrontal cortex like the five o clock express subway. He couldn’t have screamed if he wanted to because the bullet shattered his Broca’s area, voiding any further speech before continuing forward through the motor cortex and exiting behind the middle of the skull from the parietal lobe. He was dead before his body started to slump toward the concrete. Frank dropped his phone and looked at Tommy, eyes full of horror. His attacker didn’t anticipate this reaction and had to angle the weapon in order to successfully fire a shot. Lacking precision, the bullet starting by hitting the ethmodial bone right above the nose and losing speed by traveling upward toward the primary auditory cortex. Continuing upward, the bullet lodged comfortably into the top of his primary somatic sensory cortex, with not quite enough power to exit the body. Dropping much like Tommy, Frank at least had a moment to say goodbye to his life. With his final seconds on Earth, the soothing melody of his favorite song, Bob Dylan’s "Knockin on Heaven’s Door", played in his head. His attacker decided to assess the damage, with the full realization that there was an enormous amount of time before anybody noticed. Their route had been scouted for weeks in order to prepare for such a boisterous move. Removing a hood, the attacker looked down at both deceased victims. Tips of Rachel Smith’s long flowing black hair whispered against her pale cheeks thanks to an incoming gust of wind. Placing the gun into a custom made pocket inside her jacket, she crouched down and looked for any signs of life from either person. Frank’s lifeless eyes stared up toward the sky at a captivating dark blue scene full of stars while Tommy got a rather uncomfortable view of ants maneuvering their way in between sidewalk cracks. "Daddy?" A prepubescent voice from Frank’s phone said, turning Rachel’s head. The voice couldn’t be older than nine years and Rachel wasn’t particularly fond of children. The worry began to grow when a response didn’t ease the child’s inquiry, "Daddy?" "DAD!" Rachel stomped out the phone before sensing a presence somewhere in the park. Shooting off into the night, she pumped her legs furiously, attempting to avoid any identification by oblivious bystanders. What Rachel actually heard was nothing more than a rabbit making his way some bushes. It would be hours before anybody came upon their corpses. ----------------- July 27th, 2008 10 AM "This is bullshit." "You ain’t kidding." Larry and Steven Connors stood back and watched a lone photographer snap pictures of the crime scene. If weather were the only analysis for days, July 27th was nothing short of perfect: Sunny, 89 degrees, cloudless sky, and a gentle breeze blowing through the park. Now instead of enjoying the scenery for themselves, they glanced around and tried to ensure that the police officers kept the growing crowd at bay. Concerned mothers explained to their wailing kid that the park would be closed for a couple of days, while employees were being verbally admonished for missing something so enormous. Larry and Steve attempted to decipher the puzzle. Neither was well versed when it came to what the motive means to the scene or expert detective correlations, but in their opinion, none of those factors were going to play a part in this double homicide. Everything about the crime screamed randomness: Not many professional contract killers were going to risk eliminating two targets in the confines of a park, even at night. It goes against the ethics and harbors a very high potential for getting seen by someone. There are many more isolated spots for assassins to commit double homicide with far less risk. Only one piece of concrete evidence contradicted the randomness theory, Larry Connors thought, that being the precision of the bullets. Whoever committed the act needed pinpoint accuracy to develop the scene seen before Raleigh’s finest. Neither man had a chance to run. There should have been a longer period between deaths than ten seconds. Nearby civilians should have heard the shots and called 911 immediately. Yet, none of that happened. They were killed so quietly and quickly that an eternity elapsed before a straggling passerby’s dog noticed them lying on the sidewalk. Both detectives had only one suspect in mind and the elements were in his favor for innocence: Devin Shakur was seen in Charlotte on state wide television last night and emerged from his hotel two hours prior, dodging reporters on yet another Colossus promotional stop. However, that didn’t stop hope from lingering. These were their only eyewitnesses in what was still a respectably hot murder investigation. Devin's involvement had brought up all kinds of police corruption questions since his exit from the station was well televised on local news outlets. All of the conspiracy groups chimed in with their hateful e-mails, accusing the brothers of jealousy or trying to live through the shadow of their fallen fathers. The public was not too kind when dealing with homegrown celebrities being accused of outlandish crimes. "Found a casing," The CSI technician said, lifting Tommy Morrison's death bullet. Carefully placing the evidence into a small plastic bag, he passed it to Larry and looked at the detectives, giving his final analysis. "Looks like your standard 9 millimeter to me, doesn't appear that the second victim had an exit wound." Both detectives grunted while Steven took the plastic bag from his brother with the objective of moving it to a bigger evidence container. Just as he was about to drop the bag from his right hand, something out of the corner of his eye prevented the release. Noticing this strange incident, Larry quizzically looked over at Steven and leaned over his shoulder. "It does go in one of those-" Steven was not in the mood for humor, "Shut up and give me your magnifying glass." Confused, Larry shrugged his shoulders, "Uh...Alright, here." Larry handed the magnifying glass from his pocket to Steven. Removing the bullet from its plastic bag, Steven held it up to about neck level and put the magnifying glass as close as he could get. Looking down, Steven saw markings that sent him into a furious rage. Three round circular blue rings and the small letters of MC, Mike Connors, engraved along the casings compacted right side. "Dad-" Larry interrupted, "What?" Steven moved Larry's head closer to the magnifying glass so he too could see the spectacle. "...Oh my God!" "Those sons of bitches are going to wish they had never been born." --------------- July 27th, 2008 8 PM The external life of a Universal Champion is usually egregious and annoying. Ever since Devin stepped back into the hometown of Raleigh, a media circus always surrounded him, waiting to catch him out doing errands so they could snap a picture of their hometown hero. He almost felt like a highly decorated soldier coming back from battle to once again grace his family and friends. What was supposed to be a ten minute drive into town for dinner turned into a thirty five minute Q and A session with anybody who had a vehicle and knew about wrestling. Traffic stopped for miles and people angrily sought alternative routes to avoid the blitzkrieg of attention. With PRIME’s tenuous schedule, making a public appearance outside of his home amongst the people was nothing short of astounding. There was a particular reason for this conquest, something Devin longed for ever since UltraViolence concluded. Finally he was able to embrace the glorious heaven of spice that was his favorite restaurant ever, BoJangles. No place could pack more into a piece of spicy fried chicken. The fries were greasier than anything ever seen at the worst McDonalds or Burger King. Sweet tea sold for a measly 89 cent/32 ounce ratio. Indulging here was something even a training athlete preparing for the biggest match of his career couldn’t refuse. Forgoing his signature black dress wear, Devin opted for a drastically toned down casual feel: A white Carolina Panthers with the giant team logo covering the front of the shirt. It was commemorative of when they went to the Super Bowl and got defeated by John Kasay’s utter stupidity. His jeans were nothing special, worn out and Wrangler, something Devin had been known to flaunt a time or two. However, he went through the trouble of picking out new shoes, something he frowned upon if they weren’t produced by Armani. He indirectly taunted envious teenagers in two corner tables salivating like Pavlov dogs at his brand spanking new, yet to be released, Jordan JAQs. Devin figured the minute he stood up to leave, offers to buy them would fly from all directions. He chuckled and removed the orange protection sheet around his Chicken Filet biscuit. Next, he dumped the fries out on the same long protective sheet, a few going over onto the table and one even sliding to the floor. After the first bite hit his taste buds, nothing mattered anymore. To Devin, the food was just about as good as sex, and when Sunny wasn’t around, he openly declared the food better. Two people exiting the restaurant gave the Universal Champion a hearty wave, to which he responded verbatim. Devin would entertain waves but only those. He held only one rule about his time in restaurants. Since Devin was a connoisseur of food, much like many other Hollywood icons, nobody was to engage him in conversation while eating his meal. Perhaps that is why the two people spying on him were stationed across the street in a parking lot, keeping tabs via the assistance of binoculars. Larry and Steven Connors could barely contain themselves staring at Devin innocently chowing down on his favorite meal. "Look at that little shit in there, acting like nothing else in the world matters." Steven spat, adjusting the focus on his pair of binoculars. In an idealistic world, he would be able to run across the street, ignoring the fleet of car horns and angry drivers, slam through the door and put a bullet through Devin’s skull. Larry was equally offended, slipping down into a tone almost identical to Devin’s, "Oh I’m the big superstar. Everybody is out to get me because I’m soooo famous, fucking moron." "Yeah, we’re gonna show you who is out to get you, asshole. Wait until you come out of there." Larry said, his right hand slid down toward his belt buckle. He slowly caressed a 9 millimeter service revolver. Devin calmly continued to eat his food, ignorant to the scene soon to follow. ---------------- July 27th, 2008 8:45 PM After refusing endless amount of money for his sneakers, Devin exited from BoJangles and made his way out to his green Ford F-150 pick up truck. When in front of the cameras, Devin loved to show off by flaunting half million dollar Rolls Royce’s and 200 mile per hour Ferrari’s. However, out of the national limelight, he fancied a normal car that wouldn’t attract curious eyes looking to chop the vehicle for a quick buck. Climbing into the truck, he hastily backed out of the parking lot. Next on the agenda would be making a few phone calls regarding his Colossus entrance. The elaborate set up required notification long before his music blasted through the stadium. With all the anticipated costs, he probably wouldn’t be taking home any purse money from the event. Turning left onto a side road, Devin leaned down and started to fiddle with the radio. He was hopelessly addicted to music at all hours of the day. The ride home wasn’t more than ten minutes, but that didn’t mean he couldn’t crank up the tunes on the way. What Devin was completely unaware of was the car containing two emotionally unstable detectives trailing close behind, trying to avoid detection until the appropriate time. If Larry and Steven were going to do this, they had to do it right. Finally acquiring the desired radio station on his XM Satellite, Devin didn’t need any inspiration to shout out lyrics alongside Freddie Mercury to one of his favorite songs. "Are you gonna take me home tonight Ah down beside that red firelight Are you gonna let it all hang out Fat bottomed girls You make the rockin world go round" The beat overcame Devin, causing him to drum the steering wheel while pushing down the accelerator to narrowly avoid a red light. Larry managed to hold his own while keeping a safe distance of about five car lengths. From what Steven could gauge, Devin still had no idea that someone was tailing him. Both drivers increased their speed to 55 miles per hour while rounding numerous turns like IRL drivers. Larry quickly closed the distance on Devin, getting so close that he was drafting. With no other cars in between them on a hot Sunday night, Larry could use all facets of aggression at will. Steven nodded while Devin prepared to make a sharp right turn, "Do it" Flashing high beam headlights brought Devin back to life, causing him to turn the radio down and check a nearby speed sign. He was going ten miles over, still clocked at 55. Next, he elected to check the road lines on his left. They were single yellow, indicative of being capable to pass. Devin was perplexed, trying to see through the fading sunlight at whoever was behind him. Another flash of the high beams prevented him from being able to accomplish that. "What the fuck?" Not in the mood to make trouble, Devin searched for a side road to pull off on and allow the belligerent motorist to pass by. Their horn screamed into his ear. Now very annoyed, Devin stuck his head out the window while going up a steep hill. "GO AROUND! YOU HAVE THE ROOM!" But they weren’t going to go around. He was going to be the one who ultimately paid for the crime since Christian and company wasn’t readily available to shoot repeatedly in the head. Once again flashing the high beams, Devin wondered who would be following him toward his home, none of the kids seemed interested enough to actually rob him for the shoes considering he was a legitimate mixed martial artist. He wondered if he could pull out his cellular phone and make a phone call to- Before Devin could finish his thought process or pull out his phone, the vehicle slammed into his bumper at an uncomfortable 62 miles per hour. Devin was rocked, having to fight for the steering wheel just to regain control of his truck. In a panic, he frantically continued to wonder who could be targeting him and for what purpose. Noticing an upcoming turn and a free lane to his other side, Devin waited on the mysterious driver to get close enough to where he wasn’t capable of stopping. Then, he spun over into the other lane and slammed on the brakes momentarily to get a good view of the driver.
