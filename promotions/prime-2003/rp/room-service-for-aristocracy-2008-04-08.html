Room Service for Aristocracy

8 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/04/2008 The extravagant room of the Broadview Hotel is simply a sight to behold. With beautifully furnished art-deco interiors carved out of the finest beechwood and with black, red and yellow Aztec-styled linens covering the king-sized beds it’s well complimented by the matching curtains and various other furnishings. The walls are lined with placid, dull-coloured pictures of simple flower and foliage designs, which blends perfectly with the beige, painted walls. It would be well enough to even suit the overstated needs of a politician with an exaggerated opinion of himself, but apparently it isn’t enough to meet the requirements of alleged aristocracy… “This just isn’t good enough, Jeeves!” Union Jack declares, striding out of the white-tile pristine bathroom, a large fluffy brown towel wrapped around his waist, a smaller one draped over his shoulders and his standard union jack embossed mask still covering his face. “What isn’t good enough, sir?” Jeeves enquires. He’s stood by the bathroom door attentively, a well-rehearsed -- but entirely false -- look of concern smoothly spread across his face. Union Jack gestures all around himself wildly. “This! All of this! I‘m ari--stoc--racy, Jeeves! I can‘t been seen staying in--in hovels like this!” Jeeves sighs and perhaps justifiably so. After all, his master has been whining for the better part of twenty-four mostly non-stop regarding this very subject and Jeeves had hopes the issue had been dropped, but no such look for the spoilt aristocrat’s ever-faithful butler. “Sir, we’ve talked about this,” Jeeves tells him, desperately trying to hide his utter frustration. “This is the best hotel in the area, it comes highly rated by the mayor himself!” There’s a knock at the door and Jeeves immediately strides over to answer. “You should think yourself lucky, sir. The majority of the PRIME roster are staying at something called ‘the Lucky Seven Motel’.” Union Jack chuckles to himself and shakes his head. “Peasants.” “Indeed, sir,” Jeeves agrees, opening the door. On the other side is a member of the hotel staff wearing a broad smile and pushing a large sturdy room service trolley, placed upon it a number of exquisite-looking dishes, all resting upon silver platters. “Room service,” the man needlessly states. Union Jack’s grin broadens. “Excellent! I’m famished!” Union Jack bounds towards the food, open eyed and salivating, but is stopped short by Jeeves’ cautionary glance. “One moment, sir…” Union Jack pauses on the spot, visibly deflated. “What is the meaning of this, Jeeves?” “Apologies, sir,” Jeeves says. “This will take but a moment.” Union Jack nods in acceptance, but stomps away like a small child who can’t get his own way nevertheless. The hotel waiter glares at the both of them questioningly. “Is there a problem?” Jeeves’ face turns to thunder. “Is there a problem? You have the gall to ask me if there is a problem, with food presentation like this!” The hotel waiter glares down at the dishes on his trolley and then glances back up at Jeeves, with that same look of befuddlement plastered across his face. “I’m afraid I don’t follow, sir.” “No,” Jeeves tells him, “I didn’t think you would. Can you not see how poorly constructed the arrangement of this food is? You have the mashed potato next to the gravy, the meat next to the side orders and the vegetables…” He pauses, eyes darting around the trolley. “There aren’t even vegetables on the plate!” The hotel waiter stares at him blankly. “I’m not sure I understand.” “Well isn’t that the understatement of the year!” Jeeves angrily declares, briefly losing his British upper-class cool. “You’re dealing with aristocracy now, boy, not a bunch of bloody gypo’s!” The puzzled expression latched upon the waiter‘s face intensifies. “What are you talking about?” “Ha! I’d expect such ignorance from the likes of you, boy!” Jeeves angrily dismisses. “The mayor informed me that you once housed the President of the United States! Did you serve him up the same PAP as this?” “No.” The waiter shakes his head. “He had Pretzels and Oreo’s…” This time it’s Jeeves who glares at the waiter blankly. “I don’t even know what that is, but I bet it was FOWL!” The waiter rolls his eyes. “So, you want me to take this back and have Chef cook you up something else?” “Woah!” Union Jack burst back into the room, now wearing dull grey jogging bottoms, a plain black t-shirt and the same old Union Jack mask. “That won’t be necessary.” “But sir, I must insist that we make them take this food away and do it properly,” Jeeves says. “We aren’t of the usual class of customers they usually serve, we must teach them a little class and perhaps humility. I will go down with the boy and see to it, sir. I‘ll be back--” “No!” Union Jack shakes his head and wheels the trolley laden food further into the room. “This will do fine. I need to bulk up for my match with that bloody CHAV, Bonafide. I don’t care how the food is arranged. As my father used to say, it all goes to make a shit so why worry?” A wave of outrage washes over Jeeves’ face, whilst the waiter desperately tries to hide a chuckle and holds out his hand cheekily for tip. Union Jack eyes him for a second and then finally understands. “Ha!” Union Jack declares, “I’m an aristocrat, I don’t actually carry money around with me!” The waiter looks a little deflated and a little pissed off in the same heartbeat, but is too slow to get out of the doorway so Union Jack forces matters by grabbing the boy’s forehead and shunting him out, onto the corridor floor. Jeeves allows a victorious grin, before shutting the door behind him. “Regarding your forthcoming match at Revolution One-Five-Six, sir,” Jeeves begins, turning the lock on the door and immediately laying the hotel room dining table ready for his masters supper. “Yes?” Union Jack questions, sitting in his designated seat. “Are you sure it’s wise, sir?” Jeeves questions. Union Jack raises what reports to be, under his mask of course, a speculative eyebrow. “Is what wise?” “Fighting this ruffian, Jimmy Bonafide,” Jeeves responds. “I think we should start your career off a little…lighter, sir.” “What are you talking about, Jeeves?” Union Jack questions, ploughing into some chicken breast without a thought to etiquette. “I refuse to back down to these hooligan’s! Besides, the match has already been signed; there’s very little chance of getting out of it now.” “Perhaps if you bribe the head booker, sir…” “Doubtful,” Union Jack tells him. “I hear some peasant called Devin Shakur already tried it -- unfortunately, the head booker cannot be bought.” “Surprising, considering the state of the clothes he insists on wearing…” Union Jack nods in agreement, stuffing a fork-full of chicken breast into his mouth. Jeeves hands him a napkin. “Well if there’s no way out of this match, sir, then we should make the…um…appropriate arrangements.” Union Jack chuckles, spewing foodstuff across the thick hotel carpeting. “What do you have in mind?” Jeeves chuckles and pulls up a chair. “You’re going to like this…”
