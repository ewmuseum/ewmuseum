Not Gonna Change

11 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/11/2005 Begin. “Where are you going?” “Something needs to be done.” “Limo?” “Mustang. Get dressed if you want to come along.” Flash forward about ten minutes, and start visualizing. Picture a bright yellow 2005 Ford Mustang going down the highway at a speed that would best be described as fast. Sometimes while riding on a road like this, the young lady in the passenger seat of this vehicle would think about how trees look like broccoli when they are cluttered together on the mountainside. She thought that because of observations like these that she could be a writer someday. Her name is Passion, and she’s never going to be a writer. Inside the Ford Mustang now. Tyler Lopez is in the driver seat, and there is no music playing. Lopez usually always has music playing in the car. For characterization purposes, Lopez grits his teeth. In case you were wondering about his wardrobe he has changed into one of his standard shirts real quick like since we last saw him.Passion, now dressed in a short-sleeved t-shirt and loose fitting jeans, breaks the awful silence in this vehicle. She has a pretty good idea what the answer to this question is, but communication in a relationship is important, or so they tell you. Passion: What’s the matter, exactly? Lopez: Everything. Passion: Care to elaborate? Lopez takes a second to laugh in a way that shows that he’s not so amused. Passion: Can I ask you a question? What else is bugging you. Lopez: I have a “friend” that is always right, and I don’t want him to be. Passion: What are you talking about? She is ignored again. Lopez makes a right hand turn, and in moments parks in a large parking lot. This is their destination. Lopez: Here we are. Lopez gets out of the vehicle, and after a moment Passion does too. The image playing in Lopez’s mind as he walks through the big parking lot to our mystery location is this: first match he ever had in PRIME. The Dual Halo Lopez remembers this often.] Cut. Flash forward about a day. Video Feed. We are in the back yard again, and for the record the sky is blue in every direction. Clouds stain the previously sky here and there, but such is life. For a few moments the only sound that can be made out is the wind. With these few moments you notice that Tyler is in some very stereotypical chef gear. His chef hat is custom made and says “APPARATUS”. His apron that covers a shirt also has some lettering on it that we can’t quite make out because it folds as he sits. Lopez’s eyes are striking, and locked on us. Lopez: My name is Tyler Lopez and I’m underrated. Also, I went to college a long time ago had to write an essay at least twice a month. These essays were long and difficult if it wasn’t your niche. There were three types of people in that class, and I find that this is true in life in general. There were the kids that did well by doing a lot of studying of the pieces that we read each week. The second kind of student wasn’t as book smart as they were street smart. These are the people that BS’ed the essays and they usually did well too. The third kind of kid was just lazy. It was the little things that they did wrong. These are the students that would drop the class at midterms. So I suppose what I’m saying Cimmerian, my boy, is that maybe it’s time for you to stop kidding yourself. Lopez: I don’t buy that you’re a complete moron. I’m making a tremendous leap of faith here, because you are indeed, dumb like rocks. Lopez nods approvingly. Lopez: You see, I think you’re just lazy. You’re arrogant, even more arrogant than I. If you can’t respect what you’re going to be in the ring with, which is so blatantly obvious, then I don’t even know what to tell you. You’re a small time little shit in the grand scheme of things, and yet I know that one of your favorite moves is the inverted surfboard. Do you know what this does to my self-esteem? Lopez shakes his head, sadly. Lopez: Awful things, I can assure you. One thing that you said that I found particularly interesting was that whoever wins this match will be admired by those below him. Such a perfect midcarder world you must live in. You act as if I am an afterthought. I am like the defending Super Bowl champs. Everyone wants to hit me in the mouth. Every time I wrestle someone they call it the match of their life. You know, the one where you are third in command and I have already made the two above you my bitch. There are a few more points I will make, though. Lopez can only sigh. This is quite the endurance trial. Lopez: You’re not dealing with someone who gives a flying fuck about how they win a title belt, so I’d adjust my game plan accordingly. You still are dealing with a talented wrestler, something that you also seem to forget. I’ll be right back. For whatever reason, Lopez walks off camera for a moment. Interesting noises from off camera, sizzle noises. In a few moments he returns, still in chef gear, and sits down. Despite the clear sky, we hear thunder. Lopez: If this is a moral victory for you, getting in the ring with me, then I think that you just might deserve what you are getting into. You can believe that defeating me will do you some good the next time we face, but the facts are not with you on that one. It’s best to just lose to me and get it out of your system. The price later might be a little steeper. Flukes happen, I’m not in denial, crazy things can happen in these sorts of things. You just might pull the upset of the year and then everyone will throw you a parade, but I’m going to let you in on a little secret. Lopez stands up, and walks over a few paces. What is sitting there now is Lopez’s new grill. There is some kind of metaphor going on here. That was his errand with Passion earlier. With chef hat looking marvelous as ever, Lopez flips over a hamburger. It’s almost lunchtime. Lopez: It’s not going to change a thing. We end with Lopez whistling.
