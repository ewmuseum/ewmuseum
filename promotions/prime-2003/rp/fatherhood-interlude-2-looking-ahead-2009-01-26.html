Fatherhood - Interlude 2 - Looking Ahead

26 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 26, 2009 Monday January 26, 2009 Hilo Medical Center Room 3233 11:35 AM On weekends and some early weekdays, the hospital room had become home for Bryan Dawkins. Complete with a fairly comfy chair which could be extended into a bed, a flat screen television, and an amazing view of the beach, room 3233 was the place where Bryan Dawkins felt most comfortable anymore. With Nicholas being mere weeks away from being discharged to live in the free world for the first time, Bryan Dawkins was stocking up on supplies. Diapers, clothing, playpens, cradles, baby food, and most importantly, baby toys, were slowly filling up the room, not to mention his car and the Sivec’s house. Although Bryan probably wasn’t going to be the most knowledgeable father in the world, he was sure going to attempt to be the most well-prepared. Another item Dawkins had been buying in bulk lately were the various “what to expect,” “fatherhood,” “so, you’re going to be a daddy,” and other various expecting parenthood books. At times it, appeared as though Dawkins was more of a librarian than wrestler, often carrying four or five books around with him at a time. With all of the flying he had been doing over the past few months, he certainly had the time, and he had been telling himself that he would not let it go to waste. Sitting in the previously mentioned roll-out chair, Dawkins relaxed with Nicholas in his lap and a book in his hands, rocking the little guy to sleep and attempting to prepare himself a little more for the long journey ahead for the both of them. Not knowing exactly when Nick’s discharge date would be, Bryan was taking every and all opportunities to stockpile his information. Sadly, his impending venture into the world of fatherhood was not the only thing on Dawkins’ mind anymore. He was quickly rising up the ranks of the PRIME roster, with a shot at the 5 Star Championship forthcoming. Dawkins was becoming one of the more popular members of the roster, and his compelling story over the past six months had only attracted more fans to young man known as the Flyin’ Hawaiian. Unfortunately, lately his relationship with Chandler Tsonda had been fading. The man who Bryan chose to be his son’s godfather was a much smaller part of Dawkins’ life than he had been not even two months earlier. However, Dawkins understood this; Tsonda was the top dog in the company, and he had his own business to attend to. Being at Dawkins’ beck and call wasn’t at the top of that list of business matters which needed to be taken care of. With many members of the roster gunning for him, Tsonda had to watch his back. While his allegiances still laid with Dawkins, that teacher-student relationship had faded in recent months, and if you’d ask Bryan, it couldn’t have faded in a more inconvenient time. “Mister Dawkins, how are we doing today?” Enter Dr. Kevin Malone, pediatrician, and Nicholas’ personal physician. “Oh, hey doc. Not too bad, just doin’ a little reading.” Unexpected visits were the norm in Nicholas’ room. Whether it be a nurse taking an extra peek into the room to make sure everything was okay with the little bruh, or it be Dr. Malone making an unannounced visit like today, Bryan and the rest of his family had become accustomed to them. Dr. Malone smiled, noticing the picture-perfect father-son scene in front of him. “Doing a little bonding as well, I see.” Glancing down the Nicholas, Dawkins cracked that signature grin and rocked his son ever so slightly. “Yeah, now that he’s not hooked up to the machines and stuff anymore, it’s become kinda routine. Bruh, holding your son might be the best feelin’ in the world, ya know?” Taking a moment to check out the charts in his hands, Dr. Malone took a long deep breath. “Alright, let’s see. According to these charts, we’re lookin’ at Nicholas being discharged around the weekend of March fourteenth. That’s gonna give him about another month and a half in here, just to make sure he’s fully healthy and ready to go home and begin living a little bit more of a ‘normal’ lifestyle.” Dawkins nodded, following along with what the doctor had to say. “He’s in good health right now, but being a premature infant, there is always the possibility for there to be more complications after he leaves here. That being said, it’s gonna be a good idea for you guys to keep in touch with us, just in case something would happen. When we discharge Nicholas, we’re going to give you some literature about what to do if certain situations arise, along with some numbers for people who you can contact for help with different situations.” A slight look of concern covered Dawkins’ face following Dr. Malone’s instructions. “So doc, are all of these numbers of people and everything here in Hawaii, or what?” Looking rather confused, Dr. Malone tilted his head slightly. “What do you mean? Of course they’re all in Hawaii. In fact, most of these people are either in Hilo, or a short drive away from the city.” Dawkins sighed. “That’s what I was afraid of…” Again, Dr. Malone quizzically peered at Dawkins. “Nobody told ya, did they?” Dr. Malone shook his head and retorted. “Told me what?” With another sigh, Dawkins slowly rubbed his head in frustration. “Doc, I’m a professional wrestler, right?” Dr. Malone shook his head in agreement. “Well, when Nicholas gets discharged, he’s living with me, out on the road. Meaning that having steady care at all times with the same group of staff simply ain’t gonna be possible, bruh. We might be in New York City one week and Los Angeles the next, so if you’re gonna give me numbers, they’re gonna have to be for doctors and medical facilities all over the country, bruh. If I’m in Boston one night and Nicholas gets really sick, havin’ a number for a doc in Hilo ain’t gonna do me any good.” Dawkins stopped rubbing his forehead and focused his attention on Dr. Malone. “I mean, I appreciate the idea of what you’re tryin’ to do, doc, but I hope ya understand that we’re not gonna be livin’ what you’d call a ‘normal’ lifestyle, so normal measures aren’t really gonna do us any good.” Stonefaced, Dr. Malone exhaled deeply and shrugged. “So I guess I’ll get another set of numbers for ya. I’m thinkin’ they’ll be a lot of national help hotlines for different situations, but those people would definitely be able to point you in the right direction in your time of need.” Dawkins nodded. “Thanks doc.” Returning the nod, Dr. Malone exited the room, leaving Bryan Dawkins alone again with Nicholas, who still laid sleeping in his father’s arms, even after the short conversation between his father and doctor. Bryan went back to reading his fatherhood books, but in the back of his mind, one statement semi-comically was lingering. ”Too bad they don’t make a book for dealing with Chandler Tsondas.”
