Ghost Story: Part Three - Respect. (Jewel in the Crown Round Three vs. Tony 'The Grin' Gamble and Ian Nackedy)

13 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 13th, 2007 “Ahhh, there we go.” The patter of urine on snow ceased as PRIME’s Bad Dog, Wade Elliott zipped up his work-hewn blue jeans. He crouched low, taking a handful of the white powder and rubbing it through his fingers. “Good as any sink,” he muttered to himself, turning back toward the road, taking a moment to appreciate the brisk Canadian evening. He breathed in deep through his nostrils before exhaling a satisfied breath through his bushy brown goateed mouth. The sound of a dog barking caught his attention. In the passenger’s window of his rig was the exciteable face of the infamous bluetick hound, Angus. The ‘Bama Bruiser grinned, and trudged back to his shiny black F-150, smoke pissing out the tailpipe as it idled on the side of the highway. “I’m comin, I’m comin,’” said Wade, walking around to the driver’s side. “Don’t git yer jowels in a bunch.” The pup panted happily as Elliott hopped into his seat, taking not of the digital clock on his dashboard, reading “11:30 pm.” Wade took a moment to ruffle Angus’s floppy ears. “Can’t spend a damn minute without me, can ya?” said the Drifter, popping the clutch into first gear and pulling out onto the highway, what he understood was called “1A,” accelerating off into the night. “Best git comfortable,” said Wade to the dog, popping a Marlboro Red into his mouth and lighting it with a silver Zippo. Angus barked. “Suit yerself…” the Southern Sparkplug replied, exhaling smoke out the window. His blue eyes locked onto the open road as he cruised passed the red beacons of tail lights and the yellowish-orange hues of headlights. “…but we got a long ways to go.” * * * * * * * “’Vancouver...’” Elliott nodded to himself as he passed the road sign reading “Vancouver - 60 kilometers.” “Lookin’ to be on the right track, Angus,” he said, turning his head toward his traveling companion. Angus yawned, his long doggy tongue sticking out as his mouth opened wide. The bluetick stood from the passenger’s seat and gave a big stretch before hopping into the back seat, curling up in the corner where he could hide from the lights of the truck’s dashboard, as well as the speeding lights of cars on the road. Wade shook his head. “Sure, leave me to stay ‘wake. Not like I’m needin’ sleep ‘r nothin’,” The Bad Dog grumbled, adjusting his drifter’s hat on his head. Angus didn’t reply. “Shithead.” The white lines of the highway whipped by. Fresh snow was falling lightly onto the F-150’s windshield, only to be instantly obliterated thanks to the arc of a wiper blade. Wade pitched a cigarette butt into the night, sparks exploding on the pavement before he rolled the window up. He reached for his back pocket, squirming a bit to retrieve his trusty brown flask. He shook it, finding it to be about half full. He peered ahead. Coast was clear. He looked into his rear-view mirror. Again, clear. He twisted the cap, and brought the flask to his lips, tilting it back in a long swig, wiping away the residue in his goatee with a forearm. “What the fuck’s th’matter with you?” came a harsh, gravely voice to Wade’s right. “JESUS!” yelled Wade, dropping his flask. Elliott slammed on the breaks, gripping the steering wheel tight as the truck’s tires squealed on the asphalt. The ghost of Wild Bill Hickok had suddenly appeared in the passenger’s seat. Bill’s steely gaze sat on the terrified Wade as the truck eventually came to a hault, cockeyed in the right lane of the highway. Angus whimpered in the back, awoke in a less than optimal manner. “FER FUCK’S SAKE, BILL!” huffed Wade, catching his breath. “Ye can’t just…pop up all sneaky like!” “Shut yer fuckin’ mouth,” spat Wild Bill, eyes burning underneath his wide-brimmed hat. Wade offered a confused look, his breath finally coming back to him. “What?” “Don’t gimme that horse shit,” said Bill. “How much longer did you plan to drown yerself ‘fore ye actually wind up dead?” Wade rolled his eyes, turning back toward the road and reaching for the ignition. “I ain’t needin’ a fuckin’ intervention ‘bout my drinkin’, Bill,” said Wade. “That Dusk cunt already tried…” The hot sting of knuckle on jaw interrupted Wade’s sentence. The ‘Bama Bruiser sat still, wide eyed, surprised and in disbelief at the attack. He turned to see Hickok’s fist returning to his side, his terrible gaze ever stalwart. Wade spit, and swung the door open, stepping out onto the highway and slamming the door shut. Traffic had slowed, horns blaring as they passed Wade’s obstacle of a truck. In a moment Wade’s anger returned to shock as Wild Bill’s specter moved through the side of the rig as if it didn’t exist. “What the hell d’ya want from me?” asked Wade, snow collecting on his drifter’s hat. “Christ! Ya told me to quit my blubberin’, an’ I did! I put a whuppin’ on Kosidlo an’ hopefully shut Shakur’s cunt mouth fer good!” “An’ what makes ya think you could pull that bullshit off again?” hissed Wild Bill, taking steady steps toward the Blue Collar Brawler. “I don’t care if ye drink like a fuckin’ fish, Wade, but there’s a fuckin’ time to put the bottle down! Sooner’n fuckin’ later ye gotta open yer fuckin’ eyes!” “Fer what!!!” snapped Wade, teeth grinding. “What the fuck’m I s’posed to be ‘fraid of? Tony fuckin’ Gamble? I’ll grind ‘im into the fuckin’ dirt!” “An’re ye gonna be fuckin’ shit faced when ye do it?” “Been workin’ so fuckin’ far!!” Bill swung a heavy fist into the side of the Drifter’s head, spinning him to the side. Wade furled his brow, turned, and swung back at the dead lawman with fury, but find himself tumbling through Hickok’s ghost and running into his truck. Elliott, horrified, spun around, leaning against his truck as Bill advanced. Angus scratched at the window from within the F-150, barking violently at the endeavor outside. “Are you fuckin’ shittin’ me?” stammered Wade. “How in the hell…” “You have no fuckin’ idea, Wade…” The Bad Dog stepped forward, fists in the air. “Alright, Bill,” he started. “How’sabout you git the hell outta here…” “What, in that thick fuckin’ skull’ve yers, thinks you can beat Tony Gamble while yer trippin’ over yer own damn feet?” seethed Bill. “That fucker tricked me!” spat Wade. “Only reason he wo…” Bill swung a leather boot into Elliott’s gut, dropping him to the snow-covered pavement. He struck him twice more, leaving Wade on his hands and knees, heaving for air. “OF COURSE HE FUCKIN’ TRICKED YA! HALF THE FUCKIN’ SPORT IS FUCKIN’ MIND GAMES. WADE! AN’ YA LET ‘IM GIT THE BEST’VE YA!” Wade couldn’t respond, the air in his lungs depleted. Bill knelt next to him, tilting his hat up onto the top of his head, calming down. “Do ya know what this tournament fuckin’ means, Wade?” Wade rolled into a seated position, leaning against one of the tires, arm across his chest, making every effort to suck in the sweet oxygen. “The Jewel in the Crown is the tournament, Wade,” continued Bill, his stoney eyes narrowed. “It’s the chance fer the ones sittin’ on the bottom to prove they’ve got a set’ve nuts to tangle with the big dogs.” “So where does that leave me,” Wade forced angrily through grit, blood stained teeth. Another punch to the jaw. Wade’s eyes rolled from the strike. “Right to the god-damn bottom if ya don’t smarten up,” hissed Bill. “Fer christ’s sake, Wade, do ya even know who won the fuckin’ tournament last year?” Wade, seeing stars, spat blood into the snow, shaking his head “no.” “Tony fuckin’ Gamble.” The Bad Dog’s eyes bulged. “Yer shittin’ me,” said Wade. “Sure as fuckin’ horse shit it was,” replied Bill, standing up. “That fuckin’ four foot nothin’ cun…” Another swat to the mouth interrupted Wade’s insult. “Yer missin’ the fuckin’ point! Open up yer goddamn head!” Wade said nothing, rubbing his jaw. “As much’ve a cunt as he is, Tony Gamble ain’t no fuckin’ idiot,” said the lawman. “The little bastard wins matches, he’s smart as fuckin’ hell, an’ that’s why he won the Jewel in the Crown a year ago, and damn near got that Universal Title afterwards.” Hickok took the opportunity to extend a hand. Elliott stared at Bill’s rugged paw, wary, but took it, and was pulled to his feet. The ‘Bama Bruiser leaned against his truck, releasing a groan, holding his aching ribcage. Bill stepped up close. “Ye’ve spent yer whole life fightin’, Wade,” he said. “Yer street smart. Yer a survivor. But ye ain’t yet learned the most important fuckin’ lesson.” “Yeah,” said Wade before pushing a throaty cough. “An’ what’s that?” “Respect.” A bit of silence. The sounds of horns from disgruntled cars continued to fill the night sky. “You can run yer mouth,” continued Wild Bill. “Start all the fights in the world. But if ya don’t respect the cunt on the other side who’s lookin’ to tear yer head off, then ye’ve missed the fuckin’ picture.” Wade thought on Bill’s words, and after a moment, nodded in understanding. “Step into that ring on Sunday. Git angry. Look to end Gamble’s fuckin’ career. But ya better fuckin’ understand that that li’l fucker beat ya, he took a belt from ya, and he’s been at the top’ve that ladder. Hate his guts, but fuckin’ respect ‘im Wade. An’ you can do that by givin’ him a good fuckin’ fight. Not by stumblin’ ‘round the ring like god damn wounded animal.” Bill gave Wade a pat on the jaw before turning away, walking across toward the other side of the highway. “’Sides,” Hickok called back. “Nackedy’s in that ring too, and he’s pretty fuckin’ good.” Wild Bill disappeared into the darkness. Wade’s surroundings returned to him in a rush. Angus was now whimpering behind the glass, unable to help his master. The Blue Collar Brawler panned the area, spotting his drifter’s hat on the ground. He bent over to retrieve it, grimacing at the pain in his ribs, and returned it to his head. He swung open the door, taking a heavy seat, closing his eyes as he sat in pain. “Ya think you’d offer to take the wheel in a time like this,” said Wade to Angus a moment later, who had returned to his spot in the passenger’s seat. Wade reached for the ignition, turning the engine over with a throaty bellow. He cut the wheels, straightening the truck, and cruised off down the snowy highway. “It’s lookin’ like I’ve got a lot to learn, pup,” said Wade a few minutes later. Angus whined, flopping his head onto Elliott’s lap, eliciting the scratching of ears. “A lot to fuckin’ learn.”
