Date: 15th January Prologue: Two Months Ago Los Angeles, California The wind whistled through the centre as the leaflets, crumpled and ripped, fluttered along the emptied floor where feet of ecstatic children had once stood applauding, jeering and chanting for the show that was on for them. The remnants of the destroyed triangle remained on the ground as he stood on the sidelines observing the pieces of his history being removed slowly but surely. “Sad to see it all come to an end,” she said as she patted his back and gave a solemn sigh. Sarah Kennedy approached Pierce dressed in casual jeans and a Redskins t-shirt, her hair tied in a small ponytail and her luscious green eyes depleted of energy and happiness. “It’s weird, you know, one minute you’re on the top of the world and the next in the shit hole,” Pierce grumbled. Sarah nodded her head and listened to the workers as they set about cleaning the arena for a basketball game the next day. “Things have been hectic, got quite a scare when David was -” Pierce grunted and turned to Sarah Kennedy. “Harber got what he deserved,” Pierce spoke bluntly. “You’re serious?” Kennedy asked with her eyebrows raised. “Deathly, Harber was a jerk and took Prometheus and in the end it caught up with him.” “I know you’ve had your past with Harber, but he did almost die.” Pierce strolled past her and she followed, the two continuing through the sparse building that once held so much life. With his wounds patched up and the closure of the AWC, he wondered what was next for him. “Things happen; it’s time to move on…” Pierce said and turned toward the car park where a cab sat with the engine running. “What you going to do?” Sarah asked as she swung her bag over her shoulder. “Head to Washington DC and see where life takes me,” he said. The silence lingered a moment before either spoke. “And you?” he asked. “Back home I guess, take a break and then figure things out.” Sarah walked up toward Pierce Lavelle and wrapped her arms around his neck and pecked him gently on the lips. “Goodbye Pierce,” she said softly. “Goodbye Sarah.” ---- Chapter 1: Grave Young Men December 2006 Washington, D.C. Senator Ron Philips stood at the stoop of the Lincoln Memorial, the dark shadows of trees mere spectators at his demise as the approaching darkness of night drew in like a dark blanket. Ron Philips was a man of patronage, democracy and the toupee on his head belied the truth about his decreasing stamina and approaching old age and extracurricular activities, but as he stood next to Lincoln’s feet alone in the night he felt the chilling realisation that this phone call and meeting point wasn’t a friendly visit. The sound of a rumbling engine emerged in the faint distance, footsteps and chattering approached Philips and he felt his ticker kick it up a pace. Steady old boy, Ron muttered to his heart feeling the adrenaline and sweat course over his body. “Mr. Philips, glad to see you are, in fact, a man of your word.” A tall but stocky built man emerged behind a large column, two more men followed. “I keep a promise,” Ron found himself saying for no good reason. The other two men approached Ron Philips from two sides, the shiny butt of a gun on the inside of their coats glistened against the moons shadow. Oh Lord, I’m going to die, Ron thought, a clog rose in his throat and his palms became moist. The stocky built yet lean man moved forward, his face shadowed by the darkness. “I warned you Ron, but you kept going…always thinking with your pecker instead of your brain.” “What is this? Who are you?” Ron Philips demanded, feeling a hard point against his back as he realised he’d walked into Lincoln’s feet, the ghostly face overlooking the travesty beneath. “Any last words Senator?” The word’s rolled off the man’s tongue in a snake-like rasp. Philips froze, gripping his briefcase tightly in one hand. A sly grin, silence, the pitter-patter of his beating heart, regrets and then… ---- The news room rumbled with the chattering of people and the ringing of phones, an endless sound for those who worked on the 6th floor of the USA today headquarters in Massachusetts Avenue, overlooking the Judiciary Square. A sound Lavelle had become familiar with in the last few months as he replaced the wrestling boots with posh suits and a clean cut appearance. He was dressed in a posh black suit, white crisp shirt and royal blue tie and felt a little more enthused about going to work every morning. Lavelle and Rose rode inside a ‘06 black Lexus, listening to the CD player as her favourite band “Queen” played to their hearts content while she leafed through documents and scribbled notes, every so often cursing Lavelle for a quick stop and a jolt of the car. “We’re going to be late…again!” Lavelle mumbled to himself, drumming his fingers on the steering wheel while traffic dwindled. “Settle down Nancy Pants, we’re in good time.” Rose smiled, turned up the volume and watched the scenery pass by as Lavelle turned onto Constitution Avenue. A pandemonium of police cars came shooting past the black Lexus, sirens blazing as Lavelle turned off Capitol Hill and onto 3rd Street. “By jingo, Batman, looks like there’s trouble!” Rose said with a touch of sarcasm. “The President must be leaving his house again!” Pierce remarked. The news room was unusually alive as Rose and Lavelle entered, the floor was a bustling mess of paper and feet. Lavelle moved toward his small desk opposite Rose allowing them to work closer together on cases, he was her assistant on Crime writing until he found a better job. “Looks like something big is kicking off,” Rose said slipping her suit jacket onto the back of her chair. Laurie Pine waddled across from the hall dressed in a suit that accentuated her long legs and curved physique, calling Lavelle’s name as he slouched into his chair and feigned a smile. “Hey Pierce, that a new suit?” Laurie asked politely. “Eh…yeah,” he muttered and straightened himself up, attempting to look busy. “Anyway I won’t dawdle too much, just wanted to give you this message,” Laurie handed him a yellow post-it with a chicken scratched name and number. “Thanks Laurie,” Pierce said. Pine walked off toward her desk, his eyes watching her leave. Pine continued with her errands as Lavelle looked up to see Rose holding in a laugh that was about burst. “What are you, 16?” she asked. “I don’t know what you are talking about…” Lavelle retorted. Rose snorted and leant into her chair, fiddling with a blue biro. “Come on, you so like her.” Lavelle remained silent. “It’s the secretary thing, right?” Rose hinted. “Oh come on Hails,” Lavelle said with a higher pitched tone than normal and fingered the post-it in his hand. Rose leant forward. “Who is the message from, Sport?” Lavelle leant back, sighing. “Blaine Blair,” he said with confusion, the name sounded familiar. “You know this chap?” “Not sure, left his number though so I’ll get around to - ” “ROSE!!!” “LAVELLE!!!” Hailey Rose and Pierce Lavelle looked up from their desks toward the Ice Queen (Chief Editor Lindsay Fallon); her appearance was flustered as she stood with her arms folded outside her office. “My office…NOW,” she demanded and stormed into her office. “What’d you do now?” Lavelle said with a smile. “Maybe she finally caught her husband doing Mrs. Peacock in the cellar!” Rose nudged his arm. “You were playing strip Cluedo again!” Rose shrugged her shoulders. “Jess finds it addictive, me, I’d rather have the sex.” The two entered the well furnished and lavender smelling office and were greeted by a swamped Lindsay Fallon, her eyes a deep red. “Sit!” she ordered from behind her desk and with the immediate tone, they did. “We were a couple of minutes late not like - ” “Save me the crap Lavelle,” Fallon said with a quick roll of the eyes. Rose smirked and leant into her chair, deciding it best to deal with Fallon in the only way she knew – getting down to the woman’s level. “So what are we talking about?” Rose asked. “We hit the big time on this one,” Fallon paused with excitement. “The big cahona of murder stories.” “Bigger than the Black Dahlia?” Lavelle asked with a smile, a little less pleasing to his boss. “No more clever quips!” Lavelle nodded his head and twiddled his thumbs. “I want you both to cover the murder down at the Lincoln Memorial,” Fallon said with astute clarity. “Who died?” Rose asked. “Senator Philips,” Fallon responded without as much as a flinch or sign of sorrow for the man’s demise. The years of being in the reporting business had turned her cold and the scenes of death no longer pierced her frozen heart and therein lay the name “the Ice Queen”. Lavelle and Rose got out of their chairs and hastily walked toward the doors but before they could make bail, Fallon halted their escape. “It’s not everyday that we have a Senator found dead at an ex-Presidents foot so try to put a little more into it and get there before the Times.” Rose nodded and yanked the door open, the news room was silent, each to their own listening in on the conversation, their eyes green with envy as Lavelle and Rose gripped their suit coats and yellow notepads and sped for the door. Howard Marshall stepped forward to Lavelle and the two gazed at one another for a second. Marshall was the leading political writer, with a degree from Yale and his long blonde hair platted down his back brought a different appearance to his usually stoic personality. Lavelle let a cheeky smirk graze his lips before running after a quick and expedient Hailey Rose leaving an irate Marshall stood in the hall. ---- “So how does strip Cluedo work?” Pierce asked as they drove through Capitol Hill toward the Lincoln memorial. “Eyes on the road, Lavelle,” Rose humoured him. “I love it when you tell me what to do!” Lavelle smirked as she rolled her eyes and opened the window for some fresh air. “I thought we were going to have our head’s blown-off again,” Rose blurted and focused on her blank notepad as she scribbled down a few items: Senator Ron Philips – Democrat – Married “You usually do Hails,” Pierce said with a toothy smile and pulled the car onto Madison Drive. They immediately hit bottle-necked traffic, cars stuck in an endless loop of traffic as a young warden stood to direct some of the flow. Lavelle patted his fingers off the steering wheel and a thought crossed his mind, the name he saw earlier was all too familiar. “Looks like we could be awhile,” Hailey said with a dulcet tone. “The story will still be there and the Times truck is two cars in front.” Hailey peered through the window and low and behold there was a white Van with the word “Times” painted on the back with a dish splayed on the roof. Rose smiled and leant back. “They always go all out on these cases,” Hailey said with a smug grin. The traffic began to move at a snails pace, Lavelle turned to face Hailey. “What do you think about me wrestling again?” “What?” Lavelle pulled the small post-it out of his pocket and handed it to Hailey. “That man, Blaine Blair, he co-runs the company Prime.” “The wrestling company?” “Exactly,” Pierce said and drove the car further up the line of traffic and stopped once again. Rose twiddled the piece of paper for a moment and then places it back in Lavelle’s chest pocket. “It was the thing you were best at and you said it yourself, you loved it.” “See, that’s thing, I did … but I don’t know if I’ll - ” “TURN!” Hailey Rose bellowed and pointed to a small left turn, Lavelle took the turn and the two were suddenly tearing down the road toward the monument. ---- Matthew Collins wasn’t your usual Washington Police Officer, he was 35 years old and had been divorced twice and now lived with his sister in Chevy Chase but this morning was his turning point as he and Jessica Morelli stood by the yellow “Crime Scene” tape and looked toward the dead Senator Ron Philips and scratched their heads in a pondering manner. “What’d ya think sweet cheeks?” Collins questioned. The stagnant smell of whiskey was on his breath. Morelli remained quiet and handed him some gum, she was dressed in a casual cream suit and white blouse with her badge balanced tightly to her hip “Not your typical mugging gone wrong,” Morelli said as she bent under the tape and approached the body. Collins folded his arms and popped some cherry flavoured gum, his eyes wandering to the female coroner bent over a step collecting evidence. Morelli leant down and with the tip of her biro lifted the small plastic sheet covering Senator Philips. She examined the wound and turned to Collins. “Collins,” she said, rolling her eyes as she watched her partner leer over the female coroner. Collins turned to Morelli with a toothy grin. “Yes, cup cake!” “This was a professional hit,” she said blankly. “A .35 to the forehead.” “Meant business then, seems our Senator wasn’t really a ‘Good Samaritan’,” Collins said pointedly and spotted Lavelle and Rose emerge from a black Lexus. The scene was pandemonium… Pierce gazed toward what appeared to be chaos: So this is why we were almost trampled by squad cars earlier. He thought to himself and followed Hailey. The willows that grazed the lawns outside the monument had been sheltered by an array of vans and people, covering their natural beauty. A squadron of officers from the local police offices had formed a barricade around the monument and began the procedure of tedious questioning. Rose was content and smiling as she spotted Morelli in the distance waving. A hellacious roar came from a woman dressed in a posh blue suit accompanied with pearls and white shoes, her blonde hair flowing in the wind as a Detective tried to calm her, Rose turned to Lavelle as the two walked, whispering in his ear. “That’s Mrs. Philips,” she said. “Hailey!” Jessica proclaimed in a joyous giggle and kissed Hailey smack on the lips. Collins turned and bit his lips, steady, steady man! “Hey Lavelle, what’cha doing here?” he asked. Lavelle turned to him. “Got a murder to report,” he said with his head turned in the opposite direction. “You got anything on your end, Collins?” Collins wrapped an arm over Lavelle’s shoulders and pulled him away from the torment of bumbling journalists. “Here’s the deal, Sport,” Collins said with a pause. “It’s a professional hit, 35 slug to the forehead, clean and quick.” Lavelle nodded his head and was about to ask Collins another question but found Collins had begun to walk toward a small Latino reporter for the Times, her lips large and pursed in his direction. “An acquaintance Matt?” Collins winked toward Lavelle. “You can bet’cha yo bottom dollar.” Hailey smiled toward her girlfriend, she couldn’t be happier, her life was perfect and she’d found her soul mate. The onlookers didn’t bother her and Jessica seemed to be beaming. “Why you looking at me like that?” Hailey asked. “Just happy to see you,” Jessica replied as the two walked. “You saw me this morning,” Hailey said with a smirk and spread her arms out in a “what’s the deal” manner. Jessica leaned in with a piece of paper extended from her hand. “Professional hit, but I want you to check this out for me,” Jessica said and handed Hailey the piece of paper. “This woman has been ringing frantically since 12 midnight and the calls stopped around 4 this morning. I can’t take this because I’ve to question the wife.” “I’m on it,” Hailey said and kissed Jessica on the cheek. Hailey turned to Lavelle and waved her arms. “Lavelle, we’re moving.” ---- Cincinnati, Ohio Tony Rush drove the small Volks Wagen along Route 75 back from the office, in high hopes of making the Bengals game tonight. His night at the bureau, although expedient and tedious, kept troubling him as he played his favourite band “Creedence Clearwater Revival”. He hummed gently to the song “Heard it through the grape vine”, but his mind was on other things, miles from Route 75 and his day in work. Rush was a mild mannered man, originally from Detroit and at 38 he’d established a well paid career for himself in the FBI or as he now called them occasionally ‘the fumbling bureau of idiots’. He kept thinking of a certain night, three months ago when he laid eyes on a beautiful girl and at the time he wanted nothing more than to help raise her but as the days and nights past he kept thinking of one person…the real father. Lillian Connelly-Rush was seated next to him, mumbling in Italian to her Uncle on the other end as Rush swung the car left toward an exit. Lillian slammed her phone shut and turned to Rush. “You’ve been awful quiet sweetheart,” she said with a despondent tone, his mood swings and irritating discussions had begun to sway her patience. Rush pulled the car to the traffic lights and stopped. “Lilly, I think you should tell the father,” Rush said matter-of-factly. “I’ve been thinking about it a lot and I don’t think it’s far to keep it quiet.” “We’ve been through - ” “This isn’t a topic of debate, Lilly. We’ve got a three month old baby girl at home and you know the father should know about that beautiful baby.” His eyes gazed toward her as he said this, his lips pursed tight with determination and an unwavering blink said it all to Lillian as she gazed toward her husband. The thought had crossed her mind plenty of times, there was even a letter at home for the father of her child, she always wanted to return to him a year ago but found each day would bring another and they’d remain apart. “I don’t think he’ll be ready, I left suddenly,” she said softly. “That was 12 months ago, he loved you, Lilly,” he said bluntly, without an ounce of jealousy. “And he deserves to know…” “I think we can raise Calleigh ourselves,” Lillian said. Their arguing continued and in the distance two eyes grew brighter watching them and illuminating their faces. Lavelle couldn’t have been happier to leave the scene at the memorial, the smell of putrid flesh and decay had begun to make his Turkey sandwich rise up through his throat as he stood in the elevator and took breaths. Death wasn’t his beat, not really, although a year ago he’d become a close acquaintance to it, having had ties with a known mafia gang – the bullet wound would forever be a reminder. He loosened his blue tie and gazed toward Hailey Rose who seemed completely at ease and sipping a fresh Latte from Starbucks. “First dead person?” Hailey asked as the doors pinged open on the 6th floor. “Yes,” he lied; some thing’s were better left forgotten. “Nausea?” she asked him as they strolled through the 5th floor toward archives. Lavelle nodded his head with a frown. “Beginning to wish I hadn’t eaten the Turkey sandwich now.” Rose smiled and tossed her Latte into a nearby waste bin and approached Marianne Lynch, who was seated at her small cubicle, drumming her fingers tirelessly on the keyboard. A stack of documents lay beside her, obstructing his view of her face. “Hailey, long time no see,” Marianne spoke with a Southern accent and pulled her reading glasses down. Hailey smiled. “Same to you...” The two continued with meaningless chit-chat as Lavelle felt more like a spectator seated on the corner of a neighbouring desk which was empty. Marianne eyed up the tall and well built man gazing at a Furnishing magazine. “Who is your friend, Hailey?” Marianne asked curiously. Lavelle placed the magazine down and extended his hand. “Pierce Lavelle,” he said confidently and with astute etiquette. She obliged, shaking it, his touch gentle. “Marianne Lynch.” “Ok, with all that aside, I need your help with something Marianne,” Hailey said getting down to the nitty-gritty bits. “Can you run this name for me?” Hailey asked handing the piece of paper she’d received from Jessica earlier. Lavelle looked at the name, his eyebrows rising. “Alicia Rhodes?” “The last caller on the Senator’s cell, just routine check-up.” Marianne went to work, typing the woman’s name into the computer and ran it through DMV and other known police networks. Rose was chilled, but as the computer hummed it was never a good sign. “Alicia Rhodes has a background,” Marianne said as she turned her flat panelled screen toward Lavelle and Rose. Alicia Rhodes was a known exotic dancer and escort for the Flamingo gentlemen’s club in Downtown. She’d recently been picked up by the Virginia Police for trafficking illegal drugs through boarders but had received a minor time in jail. Lavelle folded his arms. “Why would a stripper be calling a well known and married Senator?” “Exactly what I was thinking, Lavelle,” Hailey said. “You got an address for this Alicia?” Marianne Lynch went to work again and low and behold, Alicia Rhodes was registered as having an apartment in Whitman Park. Hailey scribbled the address and name down and turned to Marianne with a smile. “Thanks Marianne, I owe you,” Hailey said. Rose nodded her head and opened her mobile. “Well I’m in the mood for a little relaxing myself; we’ll get to the flat have some lunch and back to business?” When Rose got going, her ambitions to snag the perfect story exceeded anything. Morelli was a few steps ahead of him, her arm poised with the gun toward the bedroom where a foot was blocking the door. Morelli pushed with her biro and steadied herself; an instinctive smell knocked her backwards – the smell of death, as she saw the body of a woman, lying face up on the floor in her underwear and a bullet in her forehead. “Now that’s some messed up shit!” Collins said, peering over Morelli’s shoulder. Their apartment had a spectacular view of the city, branching to the green fields of Langley High School – a prestigious and academic school – to the Washington Monument that skewered the sky. Lavelle was exhausted as he slumped through the door and hurled himself into the comforts of the leather sofa and kicked back, listening to Hailey muttering as she looked through bills. The weather had turned from a sunny clear day to a dreary wet one, and as Hailey said, it did fit the proceedings of today’s events. Lavelle turned his head sideways and smiled as he saw her approaching with two chilled Heinekens. “Thanks Hails,” he said as he took the glass and took a swig, savouring its bubbly and refreshing taste. “So what now?” Good question Pierce thought as he took another swig and placed the glass on one of many coasters. “I guess we let Jessica and Matt handle the murder investigation and we just sit tight.” Hailey furrowed her brows. “No, I meant about Blaine Blair.” Oh shit, Pierce thought scrambling out of his seat and fingering his pockets for the post-it. “Chest pocket Lavelle,” Hailey called from the living area. Wrestle again? Pierce asked as he found himself with his elbows rested on the table and his free hand balancing his forehead. The thought had crossed his mind when he first realised who Blaine Blair was and suddenly Pierce was smiling, a glint in his eyes. “You’ve got a deal, Mr. Blair.” Blair was pleasant on the phone. “Excellent. Welcome to Prime Pierce Lavelle.” And that was that, he was no longer AWC’s most established wrestler, he was now beginning a whole new life and a new beginning in… Prime
