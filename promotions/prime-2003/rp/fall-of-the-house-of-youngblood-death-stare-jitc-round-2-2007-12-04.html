Fall of the House of Youngblood: Death Stare [JitC Round 2]

4 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/4/07 Fall of the House of Youngblood Overview ***October 19, 2007*** Abigail Youngblood acted, during her finer days, as the diamond to Benjamin's blackened coal exterior. She was the gentle caress, the soothing embodiment of comfort for us Youngblood children. Ben and Abby had their pious ideals to uphold, and as time ambled on, they lacked the control necessary to indoctrinate us children. Brandon's rebellion acted as a catalyst towards the disintegration of all the things that our parents held dear, their shock of being tossed aside masked by their contempt of their first son. And such began the poisonous damage control that acted more like a political campaign than an actual paternal relationship. For them, Brandon was dead. And as those who still held their teachings as unalienable, we were to share their misgivings. Mom physically aged as if she had lived two decades within the compact time frame of three years. She held these three years with a bitter resentment towards both her husband and her oldest son. But she overlooked her own disdain, if only for the simple fact that Dad was dying an agonizing death and had survivors guilt for dropping the hammer that cast Brandon from our home and onto the street. It became her nature after a while to think that she actually cared about him, almost to the point where she actually believed it as reality. Her hair was in silver strands that were wiry and thin. Bags saddled under her eyes, her mouth trembling in a sneer. She was barely sixty. Her signature on the documentation to pull the plug on her husband had left a dry bitterness that could not be washed away. She became lost without her compass, and after three months of trying to mend the fence with her oldest son, she suddenly realized her true feelings and forever burned their relationship ash. Vile, disdainful things came from her mouth, with Candace and Thomas going in line with mother. They were all resolute in their hatred for Brandon to the point of irrationality. I sat on the sidelines for a while, my own wounded sense of self worth causing pangs that forced me to try and not alienate the people on both sides of the divided line that I loved. I could not deal with what they were saying about Brandon, that he was a junkie who made his bloody millions by circumventing those around him. But after hearing that he struck Melissa in anger, the feminist within me abhorrently rejected him. I would make my plane rides for family get togethers and verbally destroy the man who had taken on the father role within our financially, doing so without remorse. His money kept me afloat in New York, and I did not as much as send him one thank you card. Our correspondence dried up for a while there. Brandon's resentment grew, he told me, drawing to the point where he confronted Mom about the reality of her husband's demise. That the 'saintly' man had flushed away his life by overdosing on pain medication, doing so because the pain of his brain tumor was too much for him to bare. Her fingers snaked towards the corners of the drapes, drawing them open. Her body had fallen into disrepair, her mind now a decaying mess inside her skull. It was to the point where she was forgetful, often time delusional about reality. "God came down to me today." Brandon remembered the uncomfortable conversation that his mother had had with him nearly a year before. "He said it was okay." "What was okay?" "That I killed your father." Brandon had become desensitized towards the world given his vocation. But the creepingly cold matter of fact style in which her words found resonance shook him. His mind raced then, trying to understand how should could come to such a conclusion. "Why you say that?" "Because he was a sinner, son. Because he was a sinner." Candace put her foot down any time Brandon attempted to get psychological testing done on her, the venom from years of hatred finding their way into their discourse. Our eldest sister refused to allow Brandon to take hold of the family estate in rural Bexar County, which was nestled on it's own half acre, nearly a mile each way separating it from neighbors. Dad had bought well over an acre of land when he moved us from Canada all the way down here. In actuality, Brandon had paid off the rest of the loans. The Youngblood estate was as close to solitude as one could get. Candace received power of attorney, even though the household and everything in it came from Brandon's pocket. The second Brandon made bones about pulling his money from underneath Mom and Candace, she threatened to take him to court because he would be abandoning his mother. Rather than deal with even the headache, he just signed his monthly checks and posted them in the mail. "One cup sugar. Two cups milk. Do it right next time." Alcoholism seemed to run through our bloodline, Mom falling further into her own veiled mind with the help of whiskey. Her life was dedicated to cross stitching and prayer now. Brandon had eased onto the couch, the plastic wrapping crinkling under his weight. The sun was still shining bright outside, but all the shades had been drawn, giving the abode a tone more reserved for a tomb. Pools of sunlight collected on openings within the blinds, pouring onto the carpet. Whenever I walked across the floor in those spots, I could feel a warmth on the sole of my foot. It reminded me of the better times. Back then, the entire house would be filled with warmth. Or perhaps I am simply romanticizing my childhood. Brandon always said that if our childhood's had been as perfect as we remembered them, then we wouldn't be pushing away any idea of structure that they had forced upon us. He looked back and forth as Mom craned her head from the window towards him. The flask of Jack Daniels rested on its side atop the living room table, mere inches away from a wedding picture of herself and Benjamin. Those had to be better times, because her smile practically shimmered beams off the printed paper. It was puzzling how far she had fallen, but for whatever reason I was not shocked by her sickening transformation. She had become the physical embodiment of all those twisted things she had said, almost to the point where I could swear a new wrinkle would find residence on her face every time she made a nasty comment. "I didn't invite you in here." She looked towards Brandon, who would rather have been anyplace but this one. To him, the house that Youngblood hands had built was always a tower that stood for the very things he was against. I remember his railing against the almost picture perfect nuclear family we had. That the reality was, things were falling apart around us, and rather than confront them, we sat around reciting Bible verses and thinking of better things. In many ways, he was extremely spot on with his teenage angst. "I didn't invite you here Brandon. Get out." Her skin was like paper, her words more akin to a growl than human tones. He did not pay her any mind, rather, he simply sat in the spot he had always sat in as a child. Whenever Candace was feeling frisky, she would take his spot after dinner, and Brandon would whine and whine about it until Dad would shoot her a glare. She did that all the way until Brandon simply pulled her off the cushion by the hair, proceeding to slap the hell out of her in a display of frustration. In many ways, I find it ironic that that scene is very much the sort of thing that plays out on a much more reserved level whenever the two of them are in the same room. "I just think it would be nice to have Thanksgiving as a family this year Mom. Like old times." She stared at me as she eased into her easy chair, her steps coming in jagged breaks before she arrived there. I could not help but roll my eyes at the acting display Mom would put on. I knew she looked dated and whithered, but I also understood that she could walk without help because she had never had any real injuries to speak off. She still planted in her garden, which seemed to be the only lively place on the half acre save for whenever she would have company. And even then, she was not as active with fixing the place up as she used to be. Candace would come over and give her help most of the time. "I don't want him around me. He lied to me." Any time she was with him, it seemed as if Mom would go into a tirade about her husband and how Brandon had told him to keep his secret hidden from the rest of the family. Brandon had explained the realities of what happened after the fact, but said that it was Dad's wish not to have anyone know. He only said it once, and never repeated it again. He rose up from his seat, not even giving his mother a glance. He walked outside the front door and slammed it shut, which brought back a rush of memories for me as to when this whole thing started. And it was sad, to see two aging adults hold this grudge, completely unable to break down the walls even though they shared the same blood. I sighed, half wanting to follow him out the door and apologize, and half wanting to make sure Mom was okay. Her mood had completely shifted, tears cascading down her eyes and a moaning whine punctuating her cries of despair. I drew close, hugging her tight against my chest, giving her a kiss on the forehead as if she was MY child. "It's okay Ma. It's okay." "Why does he hate us?" She choked it out between wet hacks, her sudden mood swings something that had grown more erratic as time pressed forward. "All I've ever given him is love." She put herself in the role of victim constantly, almost to the point where it was a parody. Her mind was out of joint, sliding into a focus only she could understand at any given time. And because she couldn't understand her swift shifts in mood, her history became malleable. Rather than face the reasoning why SHE had pushed him away yet again, she put the onus on his shoulders. She was my mother. And as much as it would pain me to say it, some part of me loves her even to this day. I reset myself, putting my hands on her shoulders, looking into the pools that were her hazel eyes. We were matches in the eyes, and I shared a lot of traits with her appearance wise from seeing old pictures. But my creative side, I do not know where that came from. Both of them had rigid sets of rules that made expression an almost sacrilegious art. "I have a friend that is coming down with me on break. Her name is Aurora, Ma. We live in the same room up in college." "That's nice." She gave me a smile. "That's nice that you met a nice girl. What's her name?" "Her name is Aurora. She's quite a character." I gave an uneasy breathless chuckle, my head swimming in a sea of emotions at the mere utterance of her name. Aurora. I do not know why she wanted to come down with me, because she had her own family she had to see. But she wanted to come down, get closer. I do not know why I said yes. "Lindsay...you're not a sinner...are you?" The very question caught me off guard. "No ma. Why would you say that?" "Because of where you live...I worry sometimes Pumpkin. That's all sweetie. And with all those things in your face-" "Ma." I chastised her with a look of disapproval. We had had these discussions in the past, and I did not want to have a repeat of them. "I'm just saying...you could learn a lot from your brother." "How do you mean?" "He's a sinner. And look at how far he's fallen." I no sold the notion, giving her a hug and rising up. She brushed the tears off her cheek, and stood up with me. "Don't let him hurt you Lindsay." "I won't Ma." "It would break my heart if you let him influence you." "I'm my own woman Ma. Don't worry." Walking out the door, I looked over to the right side of the porch. Brandon was resting against the far side of the house, looking out towards the fields to the west. His bottom lip was protruding outward, his saliva a sickening black as spit onto the grass. "You done?" "I'm sorry Brandon." Brandon brushed past me, not even looking back as he made his way towards the rental car we had gotten at the airport. "I don't want you callin' me down here no fuckin' more Lindsay. I'm finished with her. Aight?" "Alright Brandon." He took in the landscape, looking onto it one last time. I remember that now because of what happened later. It seemed auspicious, as if he knew something bad was on the horizon, keeping the secret of it tight knit towards his chest. A gust of wind ripped through the air, and our eyes met. His were green, just like Father's. "I didn't want it to be like this." Getting into the car with him, I could only echo his sentiment. "I know Brandon. I know."
