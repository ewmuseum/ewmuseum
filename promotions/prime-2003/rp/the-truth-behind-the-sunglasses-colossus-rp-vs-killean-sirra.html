The Truth Behind the Sunglasses (Colossus RP vs Killean Sirrajin)

undated

Date: Pre-CVI Breakfast is the most important meal of the day. At least, that’s what people seem to think. Perhaps they’ve never had the continental breakfast at Motel 6. If they had, they would most certainly condone skipping the meal altogether. “Jesus,” moans Tyler Nelson as he looks over the spread layed out in front of him. “I can’t understand why I’m not dead yet.” The Greediest Player in the Game stands in line on this August morning, still trying to shake the effects of his miserable night’s sleep. In addition to the musty smell in his room, he might as well have just been sleeping on the springs inside the mattress. To top it all off, the hot water didn’t work when he tried to take a shower that morning. He thought about praying for death, but considering the fact he was already in hell he didn’t see the point. Sporting a couple days’ facial growth, the former business mogul stares blankly over the breakfast buffet. He’s still not entirely comfortable wearing denim jeans and t-shirts, but living on a slave wage doesn’t really allow for the finer things in life. It doesn’t even allow for the mediocre things. It does, however, allow for free meals. Nelson grabs a plate from the stack and hands it over to Mr. Simmons, who is in line just ahead of him. The bodyguard is dressed down as well, sporting a white t-shirt, khaki cargo shorts, and some flip-flops. “What the hell is that?” queries Tyler, pointing to an object on the plate. “What?” “That thing on the plate.” He points again. “Right there.” Simmons inspects the plate closely, then takes his tumbnail and scrapes the object a few times. A chunk of last nights dinner comes loose, and the bodyguard brushes it off to the floor. “There we go.” Simmons examines the plate with satisfaction. “What looks good this morning?” There was an eagerness in Simmons’ voice that disturbed Tyler. Simmons was the typical morning person. Tyler? Anything but. “Am I really supposed to answer that?” Simmons starts down the line, with a hesitant Tyler in tow. There are a few stainless steel pans full of various (alleged) breakfast foods. The first one contains some sort of yellow substance. Mr. Simmons grabs the handle of the spoon and plunges it into the dish. It makes a sort of sucking sound, like when you pull a wet t-shirt away from your body. “Scrambled eggs?” he asks his boss, moreso wondering if Tyler wanted some than asking his opinion. “I’ll take your word for it.” Tyler groans. “Did they even cook them? It looks like soup.” Simmons hesitates with the spoonful of runny scrambled eggs suspended over the plate, looking back at Tyler for approval. The King of Greed turns up his nose and looks away, but eventaully gives an affirmative nod. It was free, after all. Simmons pours the eggs onto the plate, careful not to let them splatter. The two men continue down the line, with Simmons piling on some burnt bacon and some sort of hashbrown concoction under the direction of Tyler. A couple slices of lightly toasted (as in, not toasted at all) bread rounds out the plate of food. Simmons carries the plate over toward their table in the back corner of the dining room. There were few people taking advantage of the free meal, and they were probably smart to avoid it. Tyler had made a mental note long ago to buy stock in GlaxoSmithKline and Proctor and Gamble, the companies that made Tums and Pepto Bismol, respectively. The former executive and multi-millionaire makes his way over to the juice cart and pours himself a glass of orange juice. Tyler makes his way to the table and sits down, frowning as he looks over his breakfast. Grabbing the salt and pepper, the former CEO of Greed, INC begins to douse the plate. A few moments later Simmons arrives with his own plate of food and slides into his chair, grabbing a fork and proceeding to eat. “At least we’ll know the cause of death,” says Tyler as he takes a deep breath, holding it as he takes a bite. He had heard that holding your breath while eating can help avoid actually tasting the food. Not the case. The urge to gag is strong, but Tyler forces the food down. Simmons has no problems, even going back for seconds. Tyler finishes and shoves the plate away, turning his head with a look of disgust. “There better be plenty of places to stop on the road today,” Tyler moans, clutching his stomach. “I have a feeling I’m gonna be on the shitter all day.” Simmons wipes his mouth and drops the napkin onto his empty plate. “Well, there’s a slight change of plans. We’re going to the airport today.” Tyler furrows his brow. “What the hell for? We can save a bunch of money if we just drive to Boston. It’s not that far. Unless you managed to get us some comped seats, of course.” “We need to go back to Iowa.” Simmons cringes as he waits for the coming reaction. “Like HELL we do!” Right on time. “I saw enough corn to last me the rest of my life. I’ve got no reason to go back, and that’s that!” “I have to go home,” Simmons says somberly. “Just for a day. Grammy fell and broke her hip.” Tyler smirks. “Serves her right for threatening me, the banshee.” The remark draws a scowl from Simmons. Tyler simply shrugs unapologetically. “If she’s laid up, what the hell are you supposed to do about it? I’ve got probably the biggest match of my career coming up and you will be there. There’s way too much riding on this for me to go out there alone.” “I told you it would just be for a day. I need to put Grammy in a nursing home. My sister won’t be able to take care of her while I’m gone.” Tyler stares across the table at his bodyguard. He probably owed him at least this much for dropping everything and coming to help him, working basically for free. Simmons was doing all the driving, and even took a beating for Tyler at the last Revolution. Just like old times. “One day!” Tyler thrusts his index finger into the air for effect. “But if anyone asks me to try Prairie Oysters again I’m leaving!” “They’re a delicasy, Tyler.” The bodyguard fights the urge to laugh. “Yeah, in undeveloped countries where there is no education system!” Tyler waves a stern finger at Simmons. “I didn’t see you eating them!” “I never got a chance to.” Simmons throws his hands up, proclaiming innocence. “The guy bet you twenty bucks you wouldn’t eat five of them, and they were gone before I could get the words out.” “You could have said, ‘Hey, boss. Those are goat balls.’” A glare from Tyler. “You thought they were pretty tasty until you found out what they were.” “You just shut up! Let’s go before I get sick.” Simmons chuckles as Tyler’s stomach begins to turn just thinking about eating goat testicles. ************************************************************************************* The flight from Rhode Island to Iowa was terrible! The pilot might as well have just crashed the damn plane and got it over with. He was flying through turbulence like he was looking for it….like some sort of adrenaline junkie with a death wish. There were times where it was like a parabolic flight instead of a domestic trip, which didn’t help after this morning’s breakfast. Have you ever tried having diarrhea in weightlessness? I feel bad for the poor schmuck who went in there after me. No I don’t. And since when are there male stewardesses? I never had one on any of my private jets. I don’t feel particularly comfortable with some dude coming down the aisle and offering me some nuts. There’s just something fundamentally wrong with that. Combine all this with the fat lady that just had to have the window seat, or should I say seats. She needed a Goddamn three foot extension for her seat belt, and she obviously had some sort of bladder infection because she was heading to the john every fifteen minutes. Of course, the flight was packed full so I couldn’t move to a different seat, and they said they couldn’t legally stow her below with the luggage. And I couldn’t shmooze the homo steward to put me in first class because, well, he was a man, and I’m not Jay Phoenix. I almost wished that I was getting Lisa Tyler a cup of coffee. Almost. If I ever get that bitch a cup of coffee you better believe that there’ll be some arsenic or cyanide in it. All this inconvenience and suffering, just to go and put a delirious old hag in a rest home. Why couldn’t the vet just put a bullet in her and put her out of her misery? Isn’t that what they do on the farm? ************************************************************************************* “We’re just gonna head straight to the nursing home. My sister is going to meet us there with Grammy.” “Oh darn,” says Tyler flatly. “You mean we don’t get to drive through all that corn again?” Simmons looks over at Tyler with half a smirk on his face. “We can if you like…” “No thanks.” The former chief executive officer of Greed, INC scowls as his bodyguard chuckles quietly to himself. Mr. Simmons navigates the streets of his small hometown in Iowa as he steers the rental car to a left turn, then a right. It doesn’t take long before they pull into the parking lot of Maple Manor Retirement Community. The town is only about two square blocks in size, after all. The single story, brick structure used to be a school, but the local kids are now bussed to a neighboring town in order to save money. The two men simultaneously open the doors of the Chevy Cobalt. “Now hold on a second,” says Tyler as he struggles to climb out of the compact car. “If your sister’s already here with Almira Gulch, then what the hell are we doing here? We should be on our way to Boston, preparing for my date with destiny!” Simmons gently shuts his car door as Tyler slams his. “I’m the one who has to sign the paperwork, Tyler.” The hint of sadness in Mr. Simmons’ voice doesn’t resonate with the Greediest Player in the Game. “Her insurance and everything else is in my name.” “Haven’t they heard of fax machines around here?” Tyler looks around. “Probably not.” Tyler moves around the front of the car in a huff. He’s met by Simmons, who blocks Tyler’s path with a rather stern look. “I’m responsible for her, Tyler. I’m not going to put her in a retirement home via fax machine. This is something that is most likely going to be permanent, and I’m not going to do it from across the country. I’m sorry that you don’t like it, but unfortunately in this instance you don’t have much to say about it!” The King of Greed is shocked. Simmons has never talked back to him…nothing even remotely coming close to it. Maybe it’s because Tyler is broke, and therefore vulnerable, giving Simmons a sense of power. Perhaps he feels that Tyler has nothing to hang over his head, since Simmons is helping him for free. Maybe there was something in that breakfast they ate that made Simmons lose his Goddamn mind. Or maybe it was just that important to him. “Fine!” Tyler pouts, frowning and shifting his eyes to avoid making contact with Simmons’. The two men enter the building, and immediately Tyler turns up his nose, pinching it with his fingers. “What is that stench? Is this a rest home or a morgue?” All eyes inside the retirement home turn to Tyler, who is too consumed by the odor to notice. Simmons shakes his head as he stands at the receptionists counter. As the bodyguard talks to the woman working the desk, Tyler decides to take a look around. He gazes upon a large commons area, littered with dining tables and chairs. The place is full of geriatrics, and the ones who aren’t vegetating at tables are either shoving around walkers, struggling with a cane, or roving around in some form of wheelchair. Over in the corner of the room is a television area, where several couches are situated in front of a large, old console t.v. with a picture tube that’s almost shot. The sound is turned up almost to maximum, since most of the residents are either deaf or just short of it. A few white haired residents are seated on the couches, staring blankly at the television screen. Tyler surveys the area, hands on his hips, his upper lip curled in disgust. Tyler catches a glimpse of an old man out of the corner of his eye. He’s seated at one of the tables on the far side of the room, gumming his dinner of mashed potatoes, applesauce, and pudding. His hair is long and grey, stringy and unkempt like it hadn’t been washed for weeks. Obviously suffering from kyphosis, his back is hunched over beneath a loose fitting flannel shirt covering the torso of his hundred pound frame. The man’s face is pruned from old age, with a bushy, scraggly silver beard. What really makes Tyler look twice, however, is the large, black pair of sunglasses the man is wearing. Inside. Tyler looks back at Simmons, who is still at the receptionist desk filling out volumes of paperwork, then back at the old man. The King of Greed’s curiosity is spiked, and he decides to make his way over to the old man’s table. Successfully dodging several runaway wheelchairs, Tyler arrives and takes a seat at the table. No sooner does his ass hit the thin padding on the chair, then a shrill voice screams from the other side of the room. “He can’t sit there!! That’s Howard’s seat!! He can’t sit there!!!” Tyler’s head whips around, eyes glaring at whomever is making all that ruckus. He spies the culprit, an old woman dressed in a floral patterned house coat. She’s seated at another table clear across the room. How she can even see that far is anyone’s guess. She waves her cane in the air as she continues to broadcast her complaint. “He doesn’t belong here! That’s not his seat!” she shouts. Finally a nurse comes over to calm the woman down, shoving some sort of pill in her mouth and making her swallow it. After a few moments her objections quiet and her eyes glaze over. Tyler smirks as he turns back to face the old man. Before Tyler can utter a syllable, the old man speaks. “What can I do for ya, son?” It’s more of a croak than anything, raspy and thin. Tyler crosses one leg over the other and leans back in his chair, resting an arm on the table. He points with his other hand at the man’s sunglasses. “What, uh…what’s the big deal about wearing the sunglasses inside?” He looks genuinely puzzled. “I’ve never been able to figure it out. I mean, I know Simmons does it, but that’s just what bodyguards do. It’s part of the uniform. Poker players wear them because they think that hiding their eyes makes it harder for opponents to get a tell on them.” The old man sets his spoon down and grabs his napkin. His shaky hand wipes his mouth and beard. “But why do you have them on? I take you for a bodyguard or a poker player.” Tyler furrows his brow as he awaits the response. “You’re a dense one, aren’t ya?” quips the old man. “As dense as they come, old timer.” Tyler smirks at first, but it quickly turns to an embarrassed frown after he realizes what he just said. “Just answer the damn question!” “What’s it to ya?” The old man seems a little perturbed by Tyler’s line of questioning. He’d much rather just be left alone. “You see, there’s a person I know who I’ll be retiring soon that likes to wear sunglasses inside. Although I’m sure sometimes fluorescent lighting can be a little bright, it’s nothing that would burn the retinas or cause blindness. There aren’t many lasers going off in the backstage area. So I’ve always wondered why the idiot wears sunglasses inside, and at night no less.” “Maybe he’s got cataracts, like I do,” explains the old man. “The sunglasses protect my eyes.” Tyler leans in closer to the old man. “So….can you see?” “Not very well. Everything’s kind of blurry and I can’t really tell colors apart anymore.” The old man raises a shaky middle finger to Tyler’s face. “But I can see good enough.” The Greediest Player in the Game taps his finger on the table a few times, ideas running through his head. A devious smirk begins to spread across visage. “It all makes sense, then. The son of a bitch is going blind.” Tyler concludes. “I’m going to murder him at Colossus!” The grin on Tyler’s face couldn’t get any bigger. The old man, on the other hand, almost has to catch his jaw in his hand to keep it from hitting the table. “Did you…just say you were gonna murder someone?” The old man looks around the room, motioning for a nurse to come over. Tyler confidently rises from his chair. “Don’t have a conniption, old timer. I’m not going to actually kill him. I was speaking more in the figurative sense, although if he happens to die in the course of me kicking his ass, so be it.” The Greediest Player in the Game turns to leave the table, but he doesn’t get very far before he’s rammed from the side by a motorized wheelchair. “What the…?” Tyler turns and is horrified at what he sees. “YOU?!?” Sitting there, hand on the joystick controller, a maniacal grin on her face, is Grammy. Dressed in pajamas and a pink robe, Simmons’ grandma’s leg juts straight out like a javelin. Her long white hair is wind blown and mussed, adding to the psychotic glint in her eye. “You’ve got a lot of nerve coming around here again, Nelson!” She jams the joystick forward and rams him again with the wheelchair. “Devil woman!” exclaims Tyler as he tries to get out of the way. He takes off through the maze of tables and chairs, dumping chairs on their side behind him to try and block his getaway. “SIMMONS!!!” The bodyguard turns around at the reception counter, a stack of documents in his hand. Standing next to him is his sister, Myrna. If her hair was cut short, they’d be twins. To say she’s manly would be an understatement. “Grammy!” Simmons dashes across the room, putting himself in the path between his grandmother and Tyler. “Stop that!” Tyler dives behind Simmons, his chest heaving heavily in and out. Myrna comes over as well, and Simmons looks back over his shoulder at his sister. “I told you not to leave her alone in the same room as him,” he says. Myrna looks at Tyler with disdain. “He’s got it comin’ to him as far as I’m concerned.” Tyler glares at Simmons’ sister. “What is that supposed to mean?” “You know exactly what I mean!” She points an angry finger at the King of Greed. “The things you’ve put my brother through….” “Enough already!” Simmons turns to Grammy, taking a deep breath and slowly letting it out. “Now, I’ve got you all registered and the insurance paperwork is all filled out. You shouldn’t have any troubles as long as you behave yourself.” The last two words come with a little more sternness. “Just give me one shot at him,” Grammy threatens with a wave of her fist. She tries to move the wheelchair forward, but Simmons puts his hand on the controls, pushing the joystick back into a neutral position. Tyler peers over Simmons’ shoulder. “You don’t want any of this lady. I’ve- Simmons turns his head, scowling at Tyler as a cue for him to shut up. He complies with a snarl, walking over toward the door as Simmons kneels down to speak with his grandmother. “Don’t worry about a thing, Grammy.” He places his hand on hers. “Everything is gonna be just fine. These people will take real good care of you, and you’ll be better in no time.” “And then I’ll go back to the farm? With you?” Her voice is hopeful. “I don’t know, Grammy.” He releases a long sigh as he looks in her brown eyes. “We’ll see how things go.” A nurse arrives to take Grammy to her room. “You’re room is ready, Mrs. Simmons.” Grammy nods softly, and Simmons leans in and kisses her on the cheek. Myrna does the same after him. “We love you, Grammy,” says Myrna. “I’ll be around to see you every day.” The nurse guides Grammy toward a hallway that extends from the right side of the common area. As they depart, Simmons turns to Myrna. “Make sure they take care of her,” he says, his voice quivering. “I will. You just make sure you watch out for that snake,” she says, pointing at Tyler who is standing by the door, tapping his foot anxiously. Simmons gives his sister a hug. “Don’t worry about me.” The brother and sister exchange pecks on the cheek. Myrna heads in the direction the nurse took Grammy as Simmons heads toward the door. “That was close,” says Tyler. “If she would have rammed me one more time….” Simmons just rolls his eyes as he pushes the door open. Tyler follows him out, but taps Simmons on the shoulder when they get outside. “We need to talk for a minute, Simmons.” “What is it?” Simmons turns around as he puts his sunglasses on. “So…the old bag-,” Tyler pauses as Simmons glares. “I mean, Grammy’s going to be in here for a while?” Simmons slowly nods his head. “Probably for good.” It’s not something he wants to imagine, but it’s probably for the best whether he was home or not. “So the farm is going to be empty then while you’re on the road with me, right?” The tone in Tyler’s voice is becoming more and more devious. Again, Simmons nods his head, this time cocked warily to the side. “Yeeeaaah…” Tyler steps up and puts his arm around Simmons, steering him toward the car. “I need you to do something for me.”
