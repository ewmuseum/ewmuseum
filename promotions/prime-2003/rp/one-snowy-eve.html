One Snowy Eve

2006
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/09/06 It was a hot and steamy night in the jungles of Guam, and Staff Sergeant Wilcox was forcing us to do pushups in the mud… Wait, that’s not how it goes…start over. It was late in the night Monday evening, and a lone leather clad superstar walked on a snow filled pathway. Some people who were casually walking by may ask themselves what in gods’ name he was doing there, but they were instead content to walk by and gawk at him. Even more curious was the fact that “Electric” Eric Evans was without his compatriot, “Jammin” Jason Bass. He strode up a long path, the soft snow crushing beneath his feet. Left behind were imprints of his boots, a reminder to passers by that somebody had been there. “The Electric One” reached a large steel gate, and stopped, raising a hand up to rest against the cold granite wall that held the gate in place. He silently mused to himself about all of the recent goings on, and what the arrival of the team meant to the world of professional wrestling. He flashes back to a time not so long ago. He was a young man in a garage with his Marshall Stack and beat up guitar. He hadn’t even met Jason yet, nor had he any idea that only ten years or so in the future they would embark upon a most triumphant endeavor across the world together. They had played small clubs, huge arenas, even a few bar mitzvahs. It didn’t matter because it was all for the common cause: Rock and Fuggin Roll. Occasionally there were high points and low points. Yet for the most part, it was one wild ride that not a lot of people can honestly say they had lived. Eric smiled a little as he thought of the places he had seen, and the names he couldn’t even remember anymore. Still, the weight of the business is always the downside of any great job. Nobody likes to talk about being on the road nearly every day of the year. Nor do they mention how a man is expected to pick up a guitar and play with cut and bleeding fingers. No one mentions what a great time was had by all if the band doesn’t sell the arena out. Eric hangs his head a little. He turns and begins to lean on the wall, with one hand in his pocket and the other fumbling around in his pocket for a cigarette. He finally pulls out the pack and taps one out. What a filthy habit really. Inhaling smoke, yeah that sounded like it would hit the spot right at the moment to Eric. He stuck the cig between his lips and went back in the pocket for a book of matches. “Heh, of course, other pocket Eric.” He pulls the matchbook from the other pocket and twists one off. As he is about to strike the match, he stops and looks at the book. More accurately he looks at the back of the book and the small number that is written across the bottom of the plain white matchbook. “[detail withheld]” He mouths out the numbers, the cigarette flipping up and down between his lips as he does so. Quick blurred visions, snippets of memory, if you will, come to him all at once. Eric sees a small club, and a good head count in the room. He sees the crowd bopping along with their tunes, eager to hear more from the band. He sees very clearly the brunette sitting by the jukebox giving him the eye the entire time he is on the stage. Oh yeah, he sees her all right. He leans his head back against the cold stone and smiles. The white cancer stick hangs low and he remembers suddenly the smell of her perfume, the taste of the whiskey he drank after the show, and the little white panties that barely worked their way up past the low cut jeans she wore. Oh yeah, he remembers those too. Life on the road is full of all kinds of ups and downs, this was a definite up. Long black leather boots and holes wore into the ass of her jeans told the story. This girl wasn’t at the club like some of the other little groupies that he knew. She was a fox, a vixen, an assassin…and Eric was the mark. A few snowflakes had started to fall now, and they began to bundle up into his long black and red hair. He turned his head to the left and the scruff of his two day old shave rubbed against the collar of the broken in leather jacket he wore everywhere. Steely blue eyes pierced through a few fallen strands of hair, and his breath turned to frost before his face. The Six String Sex Symbol struck the match and lit the cig, inhaling the deep smoky tobacco flavor, and exhaled it, remembering again that fine night. Jason was busy, as usual, playing his bass in the dressing rooms. He was a stickler for practice, and he almost never put the five string down. It’s really a damned shame the guy doesn’t get more respect, but then again, when you have a head of hair like he does, and a high pitched singing voice…Respect…well it aint exactly a seven letter word at that point. The rest of the band usually made busy drinking, playing to the crowd a bit, or packing up the instruments for the next gig. Eric himself usually took a nap after signing autographs for a while. Singing and shredding the axe are strenuous jobs, after all. He had prepared himself to go off on that route, drinking that shot of whiskey he could still taste, and pulling out that same match book he had in his hand at this very moment. Wouldn’t be long now, and the man would have a much deserved rest. Just as he made it to the door, however, and as anyone can probably guess, stood the woman from the show. Shit, she was even better looking up close. Eric stopped momentarily, then pulled a cigarette from his pocket, and stuck it between his lips. That cigarette hung through the hair which had fallen down over his eyes, not unlike Slash ala Guns N’ Roses. She reached up and ran her hand along the side of his face, pushing his hair back, and watching as he smiled and looked up to the door. Just outside the tour bus was parked, and inside there, Evans was the MAN. He grabbed her hand, not a word spoken, and they made for the door together. Jason stopped playing long enough to look up and see Eric leave. He rolled his eyes and went back to trying to solve the problem of world hunger through bass. Eric Evans took a long drag and paused before releasing the smoke back into the world. He tossed the cigarette on the ground and rubbed it out with the bottom of his boot, then blew the remnants of the journey into flavor country back into the crisp night air. His mind again raced with memories, most of which were too graphic to repeat. The last one however, he could remember clearly. She took his matches and wrote her number down, then handed it back. He pocketed the little book and stood in the doorway as she stepped backwards off the bus and smiled at him. “If you’re ever in town again, call me.” He watched her walk away from him, then walked back in and sat down on his bed. Another shot of whiskey, and Eric was ready for that nap, which at this point was far overdue. He woke up a few hours later. The bus was moving now, going to the next show no doubt. They were on their way out of town, and just on the outskirts he saw police cars lined up. The bus was stopped as part of the local police standards ops, and as it did, their driver asked the cops what the commotion was about. “Young woman got killed. She got shot on her way home from a rock and roll show down town. These damned neighborhoods. Every punk on the corner has a gun these days.” The cop waved the bus along, and as it picked up and moved past the crime scene, Eric stood up and peered out the window. Long leather boots…holes in her short cut blue jeans…little white panties barely poking out of the sides. Eric opened his eyes and looked down at the matchbook. A little snow had gathered on it, as well as his hand. That chill was hardly the one he was feeling at the moment though. With a final sigh, he tossed the matchbook on the ground and stood up, no longer leaning on the wall. “The Electric One” jammed his hands in his pockets and began walking away, only a rubbed out cigarette and an old beat up white matchbook ever proving he was there. Yeah, life on the road…it has it’s ups…and it’s downs.
