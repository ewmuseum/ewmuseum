Nami simply can't turn on the TV these days without wanting to throw the remote through the screen, at the image of whatever mouth-breathing ingrate is currently befouling her name. These program hosts, almost always comedians, are referred to in Japanese as tarento, the romanization of the English world "talent", though in reality there is nothing talented about them at all. In the late 1970's, the Japanese film industry was in the process of collapsing in upon itself, a period when even the great Kurosawa had trouble securing financing for his work. Toho, so far in the red it was struggling not to bleed to death, was forced to cut its payrolls. Nami's father was one of the unfortunates forced out. He tired to find work elsewhere, worked some menial jobs, but it wasn't meant to be. His body was found early one morning, hanging by a rope from underneath the Dotonbori bridge. He left no note. "Does the smell of their inferior blood make you sick to your stomach?! Is that it?!" "Oh shit," Trevor says, leaping from his chair to try and pull Azuma off the guy. He's already got the pharmacist pulled to his feet, arm twisted behind his back, whimpering and sputtering. "I think it's about time we leave, Mr. Tanaka," Trevor says, placing a hand on his partner's shoulder. Azuma glares back at him, hatred in his eyes, before shoving the man away and heading toward the door. Two men coming in clear out of his path as they see him coming. "Someone call the police," Trevor hears the pharmacist say, as he follows Azuma out the door. "Look, hey watch it," he says, as the other man wheels on him, almost expecting his partner to drive a fist into his face. "Can you calm down for a second? Can we talk about that?" "Forget it, I'm going home," Azuma growls, turning back in the direction of the subway. After he takes a few steps, he shouts out, not bothering to turn around. "I'll see you at the airport on Sunday." Watching his partner disappear around the corner, Trevor shakes his head, realizing just how little he understands the man after working with him for almost two years. Unseen currents are churning beneath the surface, a dangerous undertow. He's almost ready to head back into the bar to apologize for his friend when his phone rings. Seeing Kaz's name show up on his caller ID, he flicks the phone open and lifts it to his ear in the same motion. "Moshi moshi, motherfucker." "Yeah, yeah," Kaz says back, sounding impatient. "Hey, I need to talk to you. You want to get a cup of coffee tomorrow afternoon?" "Make it a scotch right now and you've got a deal." "Ok, sure. Meet at the usual place?" "Yep. You buying? What am I saying, of course you are. See you there." Trevor hangs up, not giving Kaz a chance to complain. Another crazy night in Osaka, he thinks to himself as he puts the phone back in his pocket. Somewhere in the heart of the city a fire was breaking out, and Trevor, feeling a faint tug at his chest, sirens in the background, begins to walk in the direction of the conflagration.
