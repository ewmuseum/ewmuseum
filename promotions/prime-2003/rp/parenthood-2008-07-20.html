Parenthood

20 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/20/08 The tenor of the ER was calm, but past the cold doors it was always frantic. Lives were on the line, every second blitzing by in a New York minute. But Brandon was stoic, limpidly resting against the tear away paper as a geyser of deep lilac bled through a patch of gauze resting on his forehead. Words buzzed by his ears, his teeth clenching a gob of skin inside his mouth. He sighed. "I don’t want to be here." But they weren’t listening to him. They never listened to him. "I said leave me alone!" he roared. But those people dressed in scrubs were more interested in getting him from point to point. Youngblood pushed them away with his hands, finding their arms to be spongy. They gave too easily. Before they could handle him, he sat up and punched the metal gate on the right of the gurney. He struggled to his side, dropping to the tile and walking away. They pawed at him, told him to calm down and get back aboard the trolley. But like them, he refused to listen, and tried to walk out of the ER. The wound on his skull wasn’t enough. He had to finish the job. His life was over anyways. It took fifteen hospital staffers to restrain Brandon, to lasso him where they needed, and that was only thanks to the help of the sedatives they stabbed into his arm. ---- Summer hadn’t been kind to Lindsay Youngblood, but then again, she wasn’t exactly sure what kind was anymore. Ever since the end of 07. Ever since the-- She dumped her purse on the glass display case, spreading out assorted junk like her iPod, prepaid cell phones, and old pairs of glasses. Sydney’s was a pawnshop that gave out cash quick, at least that’s what the sign in the window said. The guy behind the counter was busier trying to sneak a peek down her tank top, but she didn’t mind. Anything to work in my favor, she was quick to remind herself. Lindsay Youngblood blinked her eyes rapidly, a coy smile snaking across her face as she rapped the glass with her ring fingers. Two gold rings were pinned on them, the one on the left with a small amethyst stone which looked like a bubble while the right was plain. They were both family heirlooms. "Them music players aren’t worth much without USB," the guy countered as he ran his hand across the scuff marks of its screen. She wasn’t sure whether she could believe him. "Don’t know about the phones. Everyone and their sister has one of those big circle plans these days." It wasn’t as though she had a choice; bills were coming due, and ever since it had happened, there was nowhere else to turn. Brandon had been helping her. He’d paid for her college loans and supported her as best as he could. But the well had run dry. But to her, it wasn’t a well; she missed her brother more than anything. The money was what had been forced upon her. What she wanted was his guiding presence once again. They shared the same city, but both Youngblood children were transplants that were running away from what they had known for years. Running away from secrets that had to remain buried in the ash of Bexar County. She tongued the brass rings of her spiderbites, trailing her eyes at the coin collections and nickleplated pistols prominently displayed underneath her clutter. "What can you give me for all that?" The question was quick and to the point. She didn’t need to hear about what each piece would be worth or what would make it more valuable. All of that was just haggling, dancing around the matter. She pushed her bra up. "Well— let’s be honest here. You got a scratched up mp3 player…" "iPod." She cut him off, the streaks of red through her pin straight brown locks glowing as passing cars reflected sun in through the windows. "It’s what it is. The glasses might be good frame wise, but I don’t know about the phones." She’d done this song and dance before with him. "Oh come on," she said as she bit her lip, pressing the heels of her hands into the glass top and letting gravity showcase her natural endowment to curry his favor. "I know you can give me a deal. You’ve done it before." Her flirtatious nature had just developed as a street smart tactic, a necessity to survive. Lindsay had matured, her lithe form appearing soft to the touch and so well manicured. Faint traces of black remained on her nails, and had she been able to afford make up to doll her face up, she would have played her usual muted gothic veneer. He reached over, lightly tracing his fingers along her pale forearm. She forced a smirk, forced out a giggle, but inside felt tense and uneasy. She tried to steady herself for appearance’s sake. "One hundred and twenty for all of it?" It was an arbitrary number thrown up out of the blue, she figured. Money was money, and she wasn’t about to grow a consciousness as it pertained to dealing with snakes. "Sounds good to me." He brushed the clutter to the side, popping open the register and taking bills out. "I’d give you three hundred for the ring," he said as he looked down at the amethyst bulb. She put her hand in her jean pocket. "That used to belong to my mother," she somehow managed to choke out. The statement was like a head rush, an intense out of body experience. "It’s the only thing of hers I have left." "Just saying," he said perhaps a little too coolly. ---- When Brandon woke up, he realized he was still alive. He wasn’t exactly sure how to feel about that. Resting into the pillow underneath his head, he closed his eyes again, the swelling of his forehead around the stitches all too oddly familiar. Perhaps this sensation was just a byproduct of having beaten his body up so much over the years. At some point, the pain becomes rationalized, and for better or worse, he grew used to it. Maybe even accustomed to it. Brandon needed the pain to remind himself that he was still alive. Needed the headaches and the sour throbs as a focal point. Because without them, there was very little keeping him going. That was a partial lie, something macabre to cart around to support groups for welcoming hugs. But he had grown used to the soreness, if only because every single throb sounded off in such an intense manner that his thirst for Percocets was rational. His body had built up a tolerance to the chemical influence, but as he grew older, the slightest pain winched across the surface of his brain and left jagged glass synaptic trails imbedded along its breadth. This was agony. Not so much the cut, but what had brought the sudden schizophrenic pang which made it easy to saunter off into the underground void. His voice was a hoarse echo repeating the same diatribe. Family. Amends. Child. My child. My child. Melissa had sins in her closet too, Brandon assured himself. Maybe his sins trumped hers by leaps and bounds, but they were forever. This was just a rough patch, brought on by a sudden and unquenchable desire. Was it repressed? How out of sync was his mind? Where did the lies end and the truth begin? Brandon Youngblood didn’t know who he was. Even as he closed his eyes, he couldn’t lock his wandering mind, nor could he hold the almost psychedelic experience he was experiencing. Whatever they’d pumped into him to put him out was to blame. Had to be some freak toxin with horrible side effects which made lucid thought disappear. But who was he? What was he? A wrestler, surely. But he wasn’t that anymore. There had been a time where he was a major star, a twenty-something on the fast track to a vaunted legacy. But moments like this, like the one he found himself in now, caught him in a quandary where absolutely nothing made sense. Fresh gauze was taped across his wound. He wanted to tear it off. He heard this sobering whisper that taking off the bandage was necessary, that when the stitches were pulled out, what was being held back would rush forward, and all things would be clear in a singular moment of transcendence. The third eye. Brandon Youngblood’s third eye was struggling inside its bondage, and GODDAMMIT it needed to be free! Raw, rough, acerbic emotions flooded his mind like a torrent of venom which broke away wrinkled fragments in an acid lobotomy. This was all causality. Perhaps the chunks and hunks of his brain were already picked apart by sweeping crows and vultures, the mad cawing filling the empty spaces so his head never felt out of balance. There was madness in every direction and there was no compass to show him a steadying point. Was this blood loss? Was it a tumor? Brandon knew that brain tumors are usually removed with the patient still awake because the doctor needs them to be. If there is one issue during the process, the surgeon needs them to be awake so he is aware of what is being damaged. But the patient’s scalp is spread like bloody flower petals, and a bone saw cuts through the skull and opens up the brain pan. For whatever reason, the idea of being awake during the entire horrible process stopped him cold. But worst of all? The doctor was constantly asking the patient questions, making sure everything is functioning properly. He hoped to Christ what he felt wasn’t the result of a tumor. His father had had one. That’s how Brandon knew about the entire goddamn process of removal. Figured it was intelligent to understand just what would happen the second his father went into the operating room. Benjamin Youngblood never went into an operating room. He chugged vodka and pills and asphyxiated on his own tongue. He’d been surrounded with death. His family. His friends. Maybe being so up close to the random demises of others had done this to him. Maybe their death rattles had splintered his mind. How could he be thinking this way? His son was coming. For once, he was going to be something more than a destroyer. For once, there was something to nurture, to care for, and to support with the vast wealth he’d accrued at the cost of his health and sanity. The cycle was completed the moment he realized he was going to miss Cody’s birth. Melissa was in her third trimester, and he was about to spend ninety days in county lock up because he was a cocaine snorting faggot. If there were tears left in him, he would have shed them. But there was nothing left to weep over. Things were going to be fine. He’d made it clear to Melissa that he was going to make this up to her. Help was on the way. She just needed to wait for him to get out. Then he could be the parent his father and mother never had been. ---- Lindsay sat on the wooden bench, her purse strap lazily hanging off her shoulder. The sun was going down, and as she waited for the bus to pick her up, she lit a cigarette, taking a drag as she rested her arms across the frame. As smoke sputtered from the burning menthol, she kept her eyes trained on the streets. She had to be vigil; around the Skids, you had to have your head on a swivel. She had a few days off from work, but she was hoping to change that tomorrow. Belts were tightening, and if she was going to scrounge up enough money for rent, she was going to have to pick up some shifts. Maybe she would do what she had been doing; just showing up and punching in, waiting on tables and avoiding her manager for as long as possible. She’d been warned about the practice, but they didn’t understand. Or maybe they did and really didn’t care. Lindsay was a writer and had changed her major when she transferred to UCLA. She didn’t like that she had to read a bunch of stuff which was of little use or interest to her. She wanted to focus on modernism, on the more recent era, because those authors were speaking to her experience. What did Jane Austin know about living in poverty? What did Chaucer know about abandonment? What did Melville know about the undercurrent of the dying American dream? She’d wrestled with their texts, understanding them but at the same time loathing the pretty words which rolled off their pages. They said nothing. She wasn’t going to lie about faint passages having all encompassing meaning. No writer sits back with the notion of building a muddy road map just so a pompous asshole could take what they’d written and turn it into grant money. Academics were a necessity but she’d run that gauntlet for too long. In reality, she didn’t know what to make of anything. Until recently, she’d lived in a comfortable bubble with other bigots. She took no risks. She wasn’t alone in the vestibule. He wore a t-shirt and jeans and shivered heavily, his obsidian skin chalky and his eyes glazed over with a tepid yellow tint. She’d dropped out of UCLA. Nobody else knew because there wasn’t anyone to tell. Tommy was in some hole in Texas, probably in jail. Candace was a trophy wife in a suburb somewhere in the Midwest, surely fermenting the doctrine of white hate which had been hammered from when she was a child. Home was gone. "Excuse me-- miss", he stuttered between his cupped hands. "I’s wondering if you could hook a brother up with a light?" He rustled through his pockets, pulling out a pack of menthol cigarettes. They smoked the same brand. Lindsay rested her purse in her lap, unzipping the main pouch to pull her lighter back out. He stood up, looking down at her. She didn’t see the way his face dyspeptically transitioned as he coiled. Her head was jolted downward with a stomp to the side of the head. Her forehead cracked against the bench. There was no sound. His hands ripped at the strap of her purse. Yanking it free, he beelined away from the scene. Lindsay tried to collect herself, staggering to her feet. A gash had opened across her forehead, and her head was too heavy to hold. "Hey!" she yelled, but the entire process was filtered through a stuttering tremble. She was woozy, but tried to give chase. The people that dotted the street stood frozen, their eyes sympathetically glancing towards her. Those that weren’t frantically grabbed their cell phones, punching in the number for emergency services. Someone tried to comfort her. "You okay?" she was asked over and over. "Yeah." But she wasn’t. Who could be? They tried to steady her back onto the bench. She let them, if only because she was still so shaken by the suddenness of what had just happened. What she needed was her brother. She needed Brandon. She always needed Brandon. ---- Why Melissa decided to come to the hospital was unknown. The jut of her stomach was passively petted, the touch causing gooseflesh to break out across his skin. Brandon’s touch was foreign now, an alien push that hardened her insides and made her feel raw with anger. But she had to do this. It wasn’t love that brought her here though. Maybe it was the crystallized visual of what he was parting with that brought her here. "I’m never going to leave you." "I know, honey. I’ll always be there for you too." It was a lie. But then again, Brandon was a liar as well. Melissa figured she’d at least wise up to the game. "I had to take the plea deal. It was the only way I’d get to see Cody." She nodded her head. "We’ll survive this." But her manner was far to practiced. The idea of Brandon being anywhere near her son sent signal flares off inside her head. "It’ll take time to forgive you, but I’m there for you." ---- Lindsay was a bruised ashen ghost. She swallowed her pride, stepping back into the pawn shop. It was Los Angeles; they weren’t going to catch the mugger. She didn’t even give a thought that she was going to get her purse back. But she needed money so she could stay afloat. In the past, Brandon would have been there. But this wasn’t the past. In her time of greatest need, she was abandoned by the closest thing she ever truly had to a father. Slumping over, she expressionlessly stared at the man behind the counter. He had questions, wanted to see if she was okay. She didn’t want to be touched. Couldn’t stand being touched right now. With a fierce tug, she pulled her mother’s ring from her finger, setting it on the glass. This wasn’t the time for a beggar to be prideful.
