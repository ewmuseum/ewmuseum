Road Rage (The Great American Nightmare 2007, Dog Collar Match, Intense Title defense vs. Tony 'The Grin' Gamble)

1 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 1st, 2007 “Dude! Turn this shit up!” “Sure, man, if you pass that joint.” The needlessly flamboyant skater-punk in the passenger's seat handed off a smoking joint to his compadre to his left, who gladly brought it to his lips to inhale. He spewed sticky smoke through his lips while turning a dial on the console, the “fly beats” of Chamillionaire bumping louder through a mediocre sound system, causing all four of the young skateboarders to bob their heads with stoned grins on their faces. Each and every one of them sported over-sized, brightly colored t-shirts, tight jean pants, studded belts, and flat brimmed baseball hats in assorted colors and patterns, ranging from orange argyle to yellow plaid. “Bro!” said one in the back. “Get this one comin' up!” “Aww yeah!” The driver accelerated their modest blue Oldsmobile into the passing lane on Route Ninety, East, just outside of Grafton, Massachusetts. They pulled up alongside a green Honda CRV. The one in the passenger's seat, by name of Robbie, rolled down his window and stuck himself out of the car, a near-empty bottle of Mickey's malt liquor in his hand. “Hey lady!” he yelled, his long, straight brown hair whipping in the night's wind. The motherly-looking woman looked to him, to the road, and back again. “Catch!” Robbie chucked the bottle, sailing it through the dark, landing it square on the hood of the woman's car, shattering the green bottle to pieces and covering her windshield in liquor-residue. She was visibly screaming through the window as she slowed her car down, leaving the mischiefs to speed off ahead into the dark. “Good one, man!” came the voice of John, sitting in the back. “I know, dude!” Robbie replied. “Yo,” piped Willy, the shaggy-faced driver, pointing through the windshield. “Check this one.” In front of them cruised the frame of a large, black Ford F-150, a lone drifter on the seemingly empty highway. “Dude, we gotta fuck with this one!” said Tony, the second “bro” in the back. “Moon him, man!” “Hell yeah!” said Robbie, immediately un-doing his belt. The group laughed as they pulled up alongside the truck, Robbie pressing his bare-ass against the window, giggling like an idiot. They roared with laughter, but their joy faded as the truck maintained speed. “Dude,” said John. “What's he doin'?” The man in the front seat, face partially hidden by a low black drifter's hat, simply stared at the window, his face like that of a statue's. “What is he, gay?” chuckled Willy. “Ah, sick!” yelled Robbie, sitting down and pulling up his pants. The truck remained alongside. The man in front kept staring. “What the hell is he doing?” said John again, squinting through the darkness, trying to get a better look. “I'm gonna beep him...” said Willy. Willy pressed his hand on the horn, releasing an annoying beeeeeeeeeeeeep into the night. The truck hit the breaks, causing a quick, high-pitched squeal on the pavement. The boys and their Oldsmobile sped past. “What the...” Looking out the back window, the boys saw the truck accelerating, gaining on them quickly. “OH SHIT!” yelled Tony. “HE'S COMIN' AFTER US!” “SPEED UP, MAN!” “I CAN'T!” shouted Willy. “THIS THING'S SLOWER THAN SHIT!” The headlights of the Ford were blinding as it came within feet of the car. The boys, terrified, bustled around, trying to take some sort of cover inside their much smaller vehicle. The truck crept closer. Whack! “FUCK!” The sound of a chrome bumper-guard on the Oldsmobile's plastic bumper caused more pandemonium. The car lurched a tad forward. Willy screamed, wide eyed as his foot pressed against the floor-board as far as possible. “THIS GUY'S FUCKIN' NUTS, MAN!” “CALL 911 OR SOMETHIN'!” “ARE YOU OUTTA YOUR MIND?! WE HAVE ALL KINDS OF DRUGS ON US!” “AAAH, SHIT!” The truck released a guttural bellow, jumping into the next lane whilst speeding up alongside the boys once again. The skaters screamed in horror as the truck started moving ever so slowly into their lane, pushing them closer and closer to the edge of the road. “OH MY GOD, MAN! WE'RE GONNA DIE!” cried John in the back as their car passed over the yellow line. Their car hit the rumble strip, the uncomfortable bumping putting the skate-punks into even more of a fright. They started to slow down, and the truck slowed with them. There was still no sign of another car on the interstate as they decelerated to about forty miles an hour. The truck sped past, but quickly skidded around one-hundred and eighty degrees, its menacing grill facing the horrified group of punks. The four stopped their screaming, sitting still like deer in highlights, hands in the air, mouths wide open, as the F-150 seemingly stared them down, its high beams causing the adolescents to squint. The truck revved its engine. “Oh shit. Please, please pleasepleaseplease...” pleaded John, clasping his hands together, eyes watery. Another throaty growl from the black Ford. “What do we do, man?” whined Robbie in the front seat, turning to Willy for comfort. “I don't know, dude!” was all Willy could reply. A pause. The truck squealed it's tires. It flew backward, then quickly whirled around another one-hundred and eighty degrees. A smoke-show on the pavement sent the truck careening down the interstate once more, leaving the mortified group of skaters behind and in a state of shock. They sat for a while, frozen, until traffic started moving past them, the honking of horns and passing headlights bringing them back to reality. The four turned their heads to each other. In unison: “Holy shit!” * * * * * * * The wonderful southern sound of Lynyrd Skynyrd played softly within the interior of Wade Elliott's black F-150. His thick-goateed mouth was curled into an uncharacteristic grin as his blue eyes gazed through his windshield. The infamous Bluetick Hound, Angus, stared at his master from the passenger's seat, tilting his salt and pepper colored head. The pup barked a judgmental bark. Wade turned his head to his canine companion, offering an expression of innocence. “What?”
