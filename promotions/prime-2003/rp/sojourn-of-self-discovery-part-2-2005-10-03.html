Sojourn of Self-Discovery, Part 2

3 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/3/2005 The crunch of familiar gravel under Scott's boots brought back a barrage of imagery. The smell of turf, the biting cold of snow on the ground, the crack of hard pads and helmets. His old high school football field, the track framed in a strip of gravel. He looks around, the first chill of autumn air drifts around him, the smell of a crisp season filling his head. The clear blue sky above was an illusion of beautiful weather, but Scott's thick sweatshirt was evidence of otherwise. He paused at the door of the fieldhouse. It was a door he'd charged through many times, barreling out on the field to do battle, to fight on the field of pigskin pride. Heading back in without his pads, he felt unarmored, almost naked. He missed the field of combat, what could have been in a future that never was. But today, he was on a quest. It was a journey to find out who he was. Who else would have known him quite like his old coach from his championship team. With a shove, he swung the solid door open and stepped in. It still smelled of old sweat and work, of hormones and determination. Some folks would roll their eyes at calling football players a team of gladiators, but to Scott, it was as apt as could ever be. The lockers housed pads of all sizes, helmets marked with trophies of sacks, tackles and injuries. He looked at his old locker, same one he had used for his entire tenure, right at the front. He had always been first out the door, ready to go and take position as the center. He'd buckle down, hold back what felt like an onrushing tide of attackers, and played defensive end, ready to rip around the end of the offensive line and destroy the quaterback after wading through the hellish onslaught of obstacles. But to him, it still smelled like a second home. However, that home had been long replaced. Replaced by dingy lockerrooms, an old Jeep and the fours sides of the squared circle. By cold, hungry nights and the long drives to the middle of nowhere and crappy paychecks at the end of the night. Slowly that life had finally progressed into something new and more rewarding. First it was RPW, the newer, bigger crowd and the wins with Gabe. It had been great. Then had come PRIME. Tag champion, Jewel in the Crown tournament, the whole deal was just a mound of pressure weighing down on his shoulders. His problem was, he couldn't be confident if he didn't know who he was. That's why he was here. “Coach Rossman,” Scott said, rapping sharply on the plywood frame. “Scott!” he shouted from behind the desk. “Come on in!” Scott made his way in carefully into an office he remembered being stacked with videotapes and filing cabinets. His coach sat there behind that same old desk, the Wal-Mart special covered with scratches and scuffs that had been in his office apparently since God created the universe. The old coach waved to one of the folding chairs across the desk from him. Scott eased down into the chair, which creaked under his weight. It was a feeling he remembered quite well. “Scotty, my boy,” Rossman said with a jubilant laugh, “how ya been, son?” “Keeping busy, I guess,” Scott responded, resting his elbows on his knees. His shaggy hair threatened to tumble down over his eyes yet again, but he didn't bother looking. “Havin' to do a lot of traveling and all.” “Hell son, I figured a wrestler would'a been from one side a' the country to the other,” he responded, folding his hands on a stomach that was still flat after all these years. “Didn't know anyone had told you,” Scott muttered. “Sheeeit, son,” he said with a laugh. “You think I don't get that channel PRIME is on? 'Sides, I recognized you as soon as I saw you come trompin' down that stage. I gotta tell ya, that tackle of yours sure is a thing of beauty. Always did say you had one of the best tackles I'd ever seen.” “That's... uh, quite a compliment,” Scott said, grasping for the words. “Damn right it is,” came the reply. “This past week, you sure put out them folk with it. Gotta say, it's almost scary to sit in the room with ya, even with a desk 'tween us.” Old Rossman tipped his faded ball cap back on his head. “But that ain't why yer here, is it?” “No, it's not, sir,” Scott admitted. He sat there a moment, trying to get the words together to tell this man why he had come to visit. “Listen, I need to ask you about something,” Scott said with a deep breath. “I need to ask you, straight up, what you thought of me when I was on your team.” “What's this for, son?” the old coach asked, leaning on his desk. “Interview fodder,” Scott blurted out, immediately surprising himself. “Y'know, they call wrestlers up and ask what folks in their lives thought of them and stuff.” “Oh, okay,” he said, sitting back. “Scott, you were one of the most determined guys I'd ever seen play football. I could see it practice all damn day. You'd go tearing down that field at the end of the day just as hard as you did at the beginning. Might'a been slower, but just as hard. And you never, ever quit trying.” Scott just sat there and nodded. The determination was nothing new to him. He'd heard it before, many times, but he didn't say anything. Old Coach Rossman would get started telling stories and ramble on. That's exactly what Scott wanted. “I always wondered why you were the first locker, first to the door, all that. It finally hit me that night you put a dent in the Big Steel Gate,” he said, chuckling at the ceremonial name he'd given the heavy steel door of the fieldhouse. “You were so pumped, standing there. We were taking on Allen Central that night and you were convinced we were gonna beat 'em. You were so damn sure that we were winning that night, keeping that undefeated season. Then, without even your helmet on, you headbutted that door and put a huge dent in it. You didn't even blink. Just BAM, big dent and that team followed you right out.” He sat up and leaned forward, pointing a finger at Scott. “That night, I realized why you were so valuable. You were our murderer, our assassin, our destroyer. You had that killing instinct to go tearing around the end of the line and take that QB's head off. No one I ever saw was better than that.” “So is that it?” Scott asked quietly. “Just that you were the best damn leader for a team I ever saw. You never quit trying on that team, Scotty. Damned if I ain't surprised that you're a tag champ all over again.” He looked at the clock and stood up. “Practice starts in twenty, my boy. You take care.” Scott nodded and headed for the door. He paused, running his fingertips over the dent he'd blasted in the hard steel with his head. He remembered that night, running out on the field with tears in his eyes from the impact. It didn't hurt, but that reflexive action was what he'd covered with his helmet. That's what he told himself anyway. But as he kicked his Jeep into gear and turned down the street, he couldn't help but turn what his old coach had said over and over again. What was he to the team? He was the assassin, the murder, the monster. He was the guy that would lead the team like a screaming demon, that headbutted a dent in the steel door. “Is that all?” he asked himself aloud. “Is that all people see? A determined monster?” The thought itself almost broke his heart. He drove himself on home in silence. It was almost more than he could handle. If that was all he was, a heartless monster, he wondered why he cared. And he wondered how he had people in his life. Monsters were people like Stanislav that had followers, but no friends. They had anger, but no life. Success... but no one to share it with. He did not want to be like that. But apparently that's exactly what he was. Unheeded, tears gathered in his eyes. At that moment, he knew that he wasn't a heartless beast; they didn't feel fear or sadness. But that was him, alone, confronting some of his worst fears. Slowly, he took the turn to head towards his house. The winding road looked like a deathtrap to those that headed down it the first time, but Scott could've driven the winding pavement with his eyes shut. He just made his way along, trying to get home and hide himself away. But there was something wrong. It's a weekend... and his mother's car was out. He pulled in the drive and got out slowly, not sure what to expect. He pulled screen door open slowly and tried the knob... open... and stepped inside. He walked his way in, listening. No telltale TV for his mother watching the news, no stereo of his sister's... it was too quiet... He walked into what had been his room from high school. Now, it was just incidentally where he slept when he was at the house. But on his bed, there lay Alyssa Sanders dressed in his old football jersey... and that alone. A good time later, Scott lay on his bed, Alyssa snuggled on his shoulder and returning to that same train of thought: surely a monster wouldn't be feeling quite like this. Alyssa's legs wrapped around his right thigh, her hands gently tracing the lines of his defined chest. God, he felt alive. He felt like a real person. He didn't feel like a beast. “So when did you get here?” he asked her. “Your mom left not too long ago, and I let Lacey take my car for her drivers' test. They're going to be gone for a couple of hours, still. She said they'd be back at seven for dinner and such.” “Seven? Damn, what time is it?” “It's about eleven-thirty,” she said with a broad grin. “So what were you out for?” “Visiting my old football coach,” he said matter-of-factly. “Oh? Why?” she asked, curious. “I... I'm just...” he muttered. “Nothing.” “I know you better than that,” she said, kissing his chest. “You might want to just tell me, else I'd have to pump you for information.” “I think you just gave me incentive not to tell you.” “Mmmm,” she smiled, “but this way, I don't have to tie you up.” “Well, it's gonna sound really weird, baby,” he said, pushing his hair back out of his face. “I'm in the whole tournament... but I don't know that I can win it.” She sat up, gloriously nude, her face marked by confusion. “Why do you think you're not going to win?” “Honestly?” “Honestly.” “I'm not sure who I am.” There, he had said it. He didn't want to tell her, but no one knew her better, and only one as well. She deserved to know. “Scott,” she started, “I... don't know. It's just that... well, I don't understand, Scotty.” “Aly, what am I? Am I some big crazy monster, or am I something more? Is there really more to me?” She giggled and flopped down onto him, landing on her forearms and her raven hair cascading down around her face. “Let me make it clear to you, Scotty,” she said, planting a kiss on his lips. “You are so kind, careful and loving to me, to your mother, to your sister. You stood up for Gabe back when you were in RPW, you stood up for Van. Even in it all, you felt you failed when you and your teammate didn't win.” She knelt down and kissed him again. “You're only a beast two places. In the ring and right here.” Scott laughed. He was no closer. He still didn't know who he was. But one thing he did know: he wasn't a monster. He knew something else: monsters didn't feel this way or have this kind of fun.
