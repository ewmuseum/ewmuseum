The Lost Tapes: Chapter Two

2 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-2-06 Writers Note: The following story may not be very well comprehended without first reading the Lost Tapes: Prelude and Chapter One. Prelude Alfred Culpepper. As sweet of a man as you’d ever meet. Somewhere around his early 60's, he owned and operated a local mart properly named the Culpepper Bargain Mart in which he sold goods to the people of Bogota Columbia. His main concern was not to rob the citizens of their hard earned money, but instead to provide them with the necessary goods to carry out their daily lives. This made him a saint. This made him a target. Midnight...Bogota, Columbia. Closing time. He grabbed his hat and coat and made his way to the door in order to lock up the shop in preparation for the next day’s line of work. Suddenly, the bell hanging on the door sounded fiercely as a man decked out in a white ski mask, black shirt and pants as well as black boots kicked down the door, wielding a pistol in hand. Do as you’re told and you wont get shot old man he yelled, guiding Alfred over to the counter where he began to demand money. As the criminal gazed outside of the window in dodge of police or eye witnesses, he heard a gasp come from the old man who was now looking down at his feet. Whats the holdup!?! he shouted. This came a voice from behind the counter, but it was not the voice of Alfreds...instead it was a more familiar voice. Suddenly a lead pipe came flying from behind the counter catching the criminal in the wrist making him drop the gun. Then...a figure came shooting from behind the counter, grabbing the .22 magnum that the criminal dropped and holding it by its barrel as he swung it and caught the criminal in the face, tearing through the mask and right into his skull...a shot so fierce that it sent the criminal down. The figure retrieved a string of rope from his back pocket and tied the criminals hands behind his back. With his work done, he stood over the criminal and buried his foot into his back in triumph. For Vangelus Olsig... It was just another criminal... Another day. Present Time: Bogota, Columbia The blaring sounds of police sirens engulfed the atmosphere as several officials scattered the area, taking witness reports towards recent events as well as arresting the culprit of it all. It was nothing more than your usual police scenario, but the significance of it all was deeper than any scenario ever before seen. Shuffled in the swarm of police officers stood Vangelus Olsig, decked out in his trademark black trench coat, staring through the battered and torn door, reminiscing on the events that’d just taken place. Behind him came a shuddering Alfred Culpepper, obviously still a bit shaken up from the attack. “M-M-Mr. Olsig...I-I must express my deepest thanks for saving m-m...my life.” He took a deep swallow before proceeding. “I-I must ask, though...how did you know...and why!?!” he asked in deep expression. Without even bothering to turn around and look the elderly man in the eye, Olsig gave him his answer. “Easy. Men like Robert Cain, the man who tried to rob you, are often predictable. Add that in with the fact that I overheard him discussing the plan with some of his local goons. After which, it was simply hook, line, and sinker.” Olsig stated in his usual deep toned manner. “Right....but that still doesn’t answer the question of why you did it...” Culpepper expressed, deep concern ever so present on his old wrinkled face. Finally, as the question was presented, Olsig turned around in one swift motion and faced the man who’s life he’d just saved face to face. “You see, Culpepper...theres something I need from you that is of far greater importance than what I’ve done for you tonight.” Olsig responded. “A dead Alfred Culpepper could not help me in the way that you can, since you are indeed alive and breathing...thanks to me, might I add.” Culpepper shook his head in a negative response. “N-No...no, I can’t give you any money Vangelus. I’m an aging man and I need every cent I have. I’m sorry...while I appreciate what you’ve done for me tonight, I just ca-...” “Its not money.” Olsig abruptly stated. “What I need from you is far more valuable than any amount of money you could ever give me, Culpepper.” Stated Vangelus, drawing a blank and confused gaze upon the face of Alfred Culpepper. “Well, if its not money...then what is it?” Culpepper queried. And in one swift motion, Vangelus Olsig extended his hand revealing the tape with the white label that read Hidden: Confidential Hideout. Shift Scenario “He’s Alive?” Culpepper queried, turning around to face Vangelus Olsig as he did so. “According to that tape he is, and now I need to locate him, and I need your assistance in doing so.” Olsig stated. “How could I possibly help you in such a manner?” asked Culpepper, taking time to push himself out of the chair he sat on while watching the video. “Simple. You knew my father better than anyone in Bogota growing up.” Olsig stated, placing his hands deep in his trench coat pocket. “That’s true, Vangelus. Me and Czar go way back. Why, I remember the first time I met the young stud...he was just wondering around the streets with no real place to go. His father, your grandfather, was always busy with something which sort of left Czar lost somewhere in the fold. We established a mighty fine relationship if I’d were to say so myself...but it seems as if he’d grown stray of me as he increased in age.” Culpepper stated as the smile that was on his face during the beggining of the story slowly began to fade. A moment of silence took the room as the two recollected the memories of the life and times of the oh so influential Czar Olsig. Finally, the silence was broken. “But...I-I still don’t understand how any of that knowledge could help you in such a situation, Vangelus.” Culpepper expressed, still with that same look of confusion that had seemed to become so familiar on his face. Without uttering a word, Vangelus Olsig reached deep into the inside of his trench coat pocket and retrieved a brown envelope in which he tossed into the hands of a standing Alfred Culpepper whom caught the envelope and looked at Olsig sort of confused as to what to do with it. Olsig gave him a nod which he signaled as a sign to open the envelope, in which he did. With the envelope now opened, he began to search through its contents, withdrawing a white sheet of paper. As he unfolded the paper, he began to read the inscription that was laid out in big bold black letters. Hint Level Uno. Deep within the 5825, or so, issues of this landHidden beneath a valley of a placeYou’ll find my brook, which with-holds my round season-like locationIn such a worthy of wastes. Culpepper shook his head, re-reading the encrypted message over and over again in a desperate attempt to realize its meaning. Finally, he gave up, looking up to Vangelus for some sort of relevant information regarding the message he held in his hand. “Wh-What is this?” Culpepper asked, holding the paper out as a sign of what he was talking about. Olsig turned around looking outside of the window as he began to speak. “It was located in my father’s files that I acquired from the government. They were said to be short poems he wrote while he was supposedly alive...but I know better. My father was never a poet, nor claimed to be. Nobody except me and my mother could have known that, though...and she’s gone...I’m sure of that. He knew that one day I’d found out about this whole fiasco, and these are nothing more than hints as to where his location truly is.” Olsig stated, not moving an inch other than his lips as he continued to stare outside of the window at the night sky that blanketed the atmosphere. “I still don’t understand. If he knew that you would find out one way or another...why would he try and lead you to his location?” Culpepper asked. “That part I’m still trying to figure out. Perhaps it could be a setup...perhaps he wants my blood.” Olsig stated with deep concern expressed on the reflection of his face. “Why would he want you dead?” Culpepper asked once more. “Who knows...maybe if he lays waste to his only son, he’ll never have to worry about anyone finding his secret location...or maybe he just wants revenge for all the years we spent bickering and threatening each other. I really don’t know...but theres only one way to find out. We’ve got to decipher this message and follow its path.” Stated Olsig as he finally turned around to face Culpepper. Culpepper, meanwhile, picked up the paper once again and began to read over it yet more. “I-I just don’t get it. It says ”You’ll find my brook, which with-holds my round season-like location.”...What’s a season-like location?” Culpepper wondered out loud. As he repeatedly uttered the phrase, deep thought could be seen appearing on the face of Vangelus Olsig. “Season-like....season-like....se-“ “I’ve got it” stated Vangelus, interrupting Culpepper’s out loud thinking. “What’s the main thing people attribute the seasons with?...the number four. There are four seasons...even a kindergartner knows that. Let me see that paper.” Vangelus demanded. Culpepper handed him the paper as he began to survey the contents once again. “Ah ha...just as I thought.” He threw the paper back at Culpepper. “Now read every fourth word in each of the sentences.” Vangelus demanded. Culpepper did so, reviewing all of the words before repeating them out loud. “5825 in the first sentence...Valley in the second...Brook in the third...worthy in the fourth. I still don’t get it.” Culpepper stated as he peeled his eyes off of the paper and looked into those of Olsig’s. “I’m sure you’re familiar with the Worthy Mart, owned by Timothy D. Worthy.” Olsig stated. “Why yes...yes I am. He’s been one of my greatest competitors in the industry since the day I started.” Culpepper stated. “And what’s the address of the store?” “Let see...ah...its 5825 Valley Brook l-....oh my God....I get it. It’s the address of the Worthy Market.” Culpepper stated with a sudden glee on his face. “Right...and it says his brook is located Beneath a valley of places. Behind the Worthy Market is a pine tree forest...his location has to be hidden underneath that forest.” Olsig stated. “By golly, you’re a genius, Sir Olsig...lets go there now so we c-...” “NO.” Olsig yelled, extending his arm to stop Culpepper’s fast track to the scene. “Theres more to it than that. Going there now would be a federal offense, since most of that forest land is well operated and owned by Worthy himself. We can’t just bombard the place...first there must be something done....” The envious grin on the smile of Vangelus Olsig only meant an envious plan was brewing... ”Officials are saying that the fire occurred sometime early this morning around two or three o’clock. Officials have no means of what may have caused the fire that has disestablished the Worthy Market, but their narrowing it down to an inside cause. We’ll have more on this story as it develops.” “...you truly are a calculating individual, Sir Olsig.” Culpepper stated as he watched the news report from a portable television monitor in his hand as the two stood in the pine forest directly behind the black and tarnished Worthy Market. “Yeah, well if you were in my shoes you’d probably have done the same. Best thing about it is now this pine forest is free roaming land the police are blaming it on an inside miscalculation.” Olsig stated as the two brushed through the forest desperately. “Yeah, but obviously you were the culprit.” Culpepper stated in a joking manner. “Nah...I just accidently dropped my cigarette on the gas pump outside of the store...that’s all.” Stated Olsig through the sound of trees and branches being swiped away. “These tree’s are killer. My hand has been scraped so bad to the point where dry blood is starting to rush out. I’m too old for this, chuck.” expressed Culpepper. “Ah, suck it up old man. I think we’re getting warmer. Obviously it wouldn’t be located at the beggining of the forest, but at the end...and I think we’re nearing the end.” responded Vangelus. “Don’t you think that’s more a sign of failure? We’re nearly to the end and have found nothing? I mean, I would think tha-...” “THERE IT IS!” Olsig shouted, abruptly interrupting Culpepper as he pointed to a stone hill that peaked outside of the dirt ground. “How do you know that it?” asked Culpepper. “The message said that it’d hold his “Round” season-like location. That stone is round, so I’m figuring that it.” Olsig stated. “Is that your ingenious way of figuring it out?” asked Culpepper. “Just grab a shovel and start digging” demanded Olsig. An Hour Later The cutting sound of shovels echoed throughout the forest for the better part of an hour as Olsig dug desperately while his partner, Alfred Culpepper, had fallen down to a seated position in exhaustion. There work showed, though, as the stone was nearly unburied revealing a door. Olsig dug out the last few dirt piles and tossed the shovel. “Finished.” Olsig stated. “With no help from you, of course.” “I told you once...I’m too old for this. Looks like we’ve hit he jackpot, though.” stated Culpepper as he examined the mildly sized boulder. As Olsig walked around the boulder glancing at his contents, he began to quiry. “How could anyone live in such a hole, though?” He asked. “Guess theres only one way to find out.” Stated Culpepper as he reached into the bag and tossed a sledgehammer at Olsig who caught it with his hand and began to lay waste to the door. Finally when the stale door cracked open, the two walked inside of the boulder. The place was an average space, but resembled just about any hideout that a man like Czar Olsig would acquire. There was a miniature television that obviously was portable and had long since lost its way. There was a rotted out couch in the center of the room and an even more rotted bed at the far end of what wasn’t a very large room to begin with. Other than that, there was nothing much more of note. “I’m guessing by the shape of this place, he’s not here.” stated culpepper, as he brushed off a spiders web that hung from the ceiling. “You’re right...but I sort of expected this.” responded Olsig. “How so?” “He wouldn’t lead me to his hideout with one simple note which he knew I’d obviously figure out sooner or later...no...my father’s a smarter man than that. I’m afraid this is only a series of what’s to come.” Olsig stated as he glanced over the room. “A series” Culpepper asked in negative amusement. “I told you one too many times, Sir Olsig...I’m too old for this. Now this little trip was enough, but I simply refuse to make a career out of this. I still have a business to run, you know.” stated Culpepper. “So, what...you’re going to leave me for your business?” Asked Olsig in an uproar. “Its not for my business, Olsig...I can’t handle this. I’m sorry, but count me out.” stated Culpepper as he made his way towards the door of the hideout. “Fine...leave you son of a bitch...but you remember that I never forget those who betray me and my family.” Stated Olsig with red hot anger in his eyes. Culpepper simply turned around at the door and looked in the face of Olsig shaking his head with a displeased look on his face. “Stick it, you ungrateful brat.” He muttered just enough so that Olsig could hear him, and with that he made his way out of the door. As Olsig watched him depart, anger began to boil up all the while before he turned around and smashed the television unto the ground in sheer rage. As the Television splattered across the floor, though, something fell off of it that caught Olsig’s attention. Vangelus fell to his knees and retrieved the picture that sat on top of the television. The frame was now cracked, but Olsig could still see the picture inside. It was of he as a young boy hugging his father as the two shore a moment of glee that could not be described in words. Olsig stared at the picture for a matter of seconds before dropping it unto the ground once more and burying his face in his palms... Crying. Weeping. And for those who’d ever loved a father... you’d know why.
