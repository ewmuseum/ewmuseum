I'll See You At The Crossroads (PTC Unified Title RP vs. Johnathan Rhine)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 15 and 28, 2008 SATURDAY, MARCH 15, 2008. ARENA MONTERREY. MID-HALO "My fucking jaw." Parting the curtains with a flourish, Lindsay Troy staggered into the backstage area, holding her mouth and grimacing. Thanks to Devin Shakur, the Queen's hopes of a Halo victory to cap off her already highly successful weekend went up in smoke and the pain shooting through her was nearly unbearable. She tried to shake it off, but how does one shake off the aches of three matches over the course of two days? Besides, she had way more important things on her mind. "Hey," she said to a passing runner, extending her hand to clasp him on the shoulder. Her hand fell away from her face. "Where'd they take Danny?" "I, uhhh, I dunno. I was down in catering." "FUCK." She released him and lurched down the hall, eyes darting back and forth to see if any doors might be ajar that would have occupants with information. Naturally, now luck decided to not be on her side. She kept walking for a distance, eventually rounded a corner and, thankfully, saw Matt Mills and Angelica Brooks standing next to a monitor. The former interviewer and the current interviewer, respectively, stood there in silence with baited breath as the Halo pushed on without Troy. "Matt," Lindsay interjected. Both he and Angelica turned to look at her. "Do you know where they took Danny?" "They rolled him through here not too long ago." "I figured," came her worried reply. "Did you hear what hospital?" "Santa...shit. I don't-" "Please, Matt." Her eyes pleaded with him to remember, even just another syllable. She had made Danny a promise and her failure to follow through felt worse than all of her current maladies. "I think it began with an E." ---- SATURDAY, MARCH 15, 2008. SANTA ENGRACIA HOSPITAL. SOMETIME POST-HALO "Excuse me? I'm sorry...do you speak English? Habla ingles?" The nurse working reception glanced up from her computer monitor to regard a frazzled Lindsay with a weary expression. Night shifts were a real bitch. "Yes, I do," her accent was thick but her English fluent; a requirement to work in this private hospital. "How may I help you?" "Can you tell me if Danny Ferguson's been put in a room yet? And if he has, where it is?" "Oh, I'm very sorry. A rather large man came down and said that no one was to see the gentleman." Troy furrowed her brow, mind racing a mile a minute. "What'd he look like?" "Beg pard-" "The guy. Was he white or black?" "Ma'am, I don't see why that's important." "Just," the sharp intake of air through her nose signaled that Troy's renowned short fuse was getting shorter by the second, "Just please tell me." "He was black, if you must know." "Figures," she snorted. Her Blackberry was whipped out of her purse and her fingers started flying across the keys. "He never understood why I cared so much." It was the nurse's turn to furrow her brow, and the expression was complimented by a tilt of her head. "I beg your pardon?" "Look, you can't deny family access to seeing a patient, right?" "If the patient requests that family is denied visitation, yes..." "Except that black guy isn't family. I am." Lindsay could sense the growing puzzlement on the messenger's face, so she looked up from her phone and gave the nurse her most serious, I'm-totally-not-bullshitting-you-right-now look. "Do you know who I am?" "I don't believe so-" "Well, I'm Danny Ferguson's sister. And I demand to see him. Immediately." The puzzlement? Replaced by chuckling. "Ma'am, I saw them bring Mister Ferguson in and I don't believe there's any possible way that you could be his sister. You're too-" "Tall? Brunette? I know. Poor kid got picked on so much growing up. I'm the," she sighed for emphasis and replaced her phone, "adopted sister." She leaned in. "He doesn't like to talk about it much. Height issues, you know. Has to overcompensate with arrogance. But as we grew up, we learned to tolerate each other, and blood relative or not we're still family and I want to see him." "And you were conveniently in Monterrey." "I like to see my brother work as much as possible." "Wearing that." Troy looked down at her clothes, silently thanking herself for having the cognizance to at least remove the tape from her arms. "I'm his sparring partner too." The nurse's eyes narrowed, looking for a kink in the story. "Why'd it take you so long to get here?" "Traffic. And I'm ass with directions. Oh, and I couldn't find my car in the parking garage. Felt like I was in an episode of Seinfeld with that one." Before the interrogation could continue, the lumbering steps of Dametreyus Fuqueiawytas were heard approaching from down the hall. His expression was tight, concern clearly being masked in the face of keeping up appearances. Troy straightened up, stepped away from the desk, and lifted her chin in defiance. "Dam, you can't stop me from seeing Danny. I won't let you!" Dametreyus stopped in his tracks, pointing a finger to his chest in a 'are you talking to me' manner, and looked thoroughly confused. "Just because you can't understand the love between a sister and a brother, having grown up as an only child in the ghetto of a poor Chicago burrough where your only friends were rats and feral dogs, doesn't mean you can take it out on me! I want to see my brother. NOW!" "Uhhh, Lindz, yous ain't his sis-" "His REAL sister. Because we're ADOPTED." Lindsay gave him a very pointed look; the kind of look that instructs the recipient to play along, you big, stupid, lug, because you had to open your big, stupid mouth in the first place. "Oh..right. Tha's right. ADOPTED." "And I want to see my brother." The pointed look didn't go away. Dametreyus sighed and looked to the nurse. "She ain't gon' go away neither. She persistent." "I can see that." "She can come up. No one else." The nurse nodded and offered Troy a pen. "Please sign in here, ma'am." "Not going to ask for my ID?" "I'm afraid you'll bite my head off, ma'am." Troy took the pen and scribbled Lindsay TrFerguson onto the pad and placed the pen on top. "Thank you so much for understanding. I'm quite worried, you know." She strode over to Dametreyus and the two walked back toward the elevators. When she was sure the nurse wasn't looking anymore, Lindsay elbowed Dam hard in the ribs. "Thanks for nearly blowing that. You knew I'd be coming here as soon as I could." "Sorry Lindz. Not thinkin' real clear right now." They stopped at the lifts. "How is he, Dam? Don't sugar-coat it." "He's awake." The doors dinged open. "But he ain't lookin' so great..." ---- Dametreyus opened the door to room 546 slowly, so as not to shed too much light into the darkened hospital room. Lindsay ducked underneath his massive arm and walked inside, Dam following right behind her. Danny's other bodyguard, Jim Pibb, kept watch at the door. The room was a single, spacious enough for fifteen people to gather comfortably, with a decent sized window. It took a moment for Troy's eyes to adjust to the lack of lighting but there was no mistaking Danny Ferguson in the lone bed. The Superstar appeared to be sleeping at first glance, the illusion broken when his head lolled to the left to gaze with blurry eyes upon his guests. He looked at Troy, still in her wrestling attire, then looked at Dametreyus. "Who..." Danny started, cotton-mouthed and hazy. "Who is that? "Oh God," Lindsay exclaimed, hoping the darkness concealed the horrified expression on her face. "Oh God, he's got amnesia." A pregnant pause passed with Troy rooted to her spot. Danny moved his head slightly to look at her again and finally, he smirked. "Dam, you should have gotten a picture of her face with the Blackberry." "Boss," Dam protested, "you know I don't know how to work that thing." An indignant huff breaks up the pranksters' banter; Troy folded her arms over her chest and shook her head. "I guess I can be glad that fall isn't preventing you from still being a douchebag." "Would've had to kill me for that to have happened." Lindsay moved to the corner to retrieve a chair, then pulled it up alongside Danny's bed. Dametreyus retreated to the door and, with a nod, retreated back outside to give the two friends a bit of privacy. "Nice to see you cleaned yourself up before coming, Legz." His voice was wrought with fatigue, yet he somehow managed to keep the sarcasm coming. Troy chuckled. "I'm sorry Fergdor, have you had your sponge bath yet?" "No, and from what I was able to make out of the hospital staff through blurry vision I won't be getting one from any cute R.Ns either." "Poor you," she laughed. "Forgive my genuine concern in wanting to get here as fast as I could. I'll make sure to doll myself up next time." "Or at least change out of your gear." "Or that." Silence for a time. "So, you remember everything?" Danny sighed, the struggle to concentrate apparent on his face and no desire to relive what happened earlier on. "I remember having Chan on my shoulder. And the tunnel opening. And seeing Rayne." "Nothing else?" "Dam filled in the blanks for the doctor." A nod. "And what did the doctor say?" "Concussion. Out for about 20 minutes. I came to on the ride over here." "How bad is this one, Danny?" Lindsay softly inquired, preparing for the worst and not thinking about the best. "It's worse than the one from Shakur in November." This was the second concussion he'd had in six months. The first saw Ferguson out for two months. This one? This one... "I'm sorry," she started, chin drooping to her chest and shoulders sagging in defeat. "I should have been paying better attention. I told you that I'd help you conquer the Halo and instead I was too distracted to live up to my promise. You shouldn't be here again in this state." "No. I shouldn't be. But...this...isn't your fault." Danny fought to keep his mind focused and his comprehension from slipping. "I didn't expect – or want – you to rise up against Rayne for my sake. You stayed out of my business with Nova after FU imploded. You stayed out of my business with Chandler when I came back in January. I owe you that much in return." "You don't owe me anything, Danny." "I do. But right now," he groaned, sharp pains penetrated his skull and it was even an effort to grimace, "I don't want to think about it anymore. I just want to get out of here and go home." "Alright," she said. "I'll stop talking." Danny exhaled and closed his eyes. "If I knew getting knocked out was all it'd take for that to happen, I would have done it last year too." "Ass." ---- NOW It's no small comfort, but I know that this concussion of Danny's is going to signal the end of his PRIME career. Whether he knows this himself yet, I can't be sure. I'll bet that he doesn't. The signs and nuances are all there. I remember talking to him during his period of recuperation from the concussion Devin Shakur gave him and he said he was done then. Said he wanted to get back to making movies. But the bug came back to bite him. It invariably does for a lot of people. Danny Ferguson's one of the most stubborn people I know. He very rarely admits when he's wrong and lately, the only person he's admitted fault to is me. He's prideful, strong-willed and determined. I knew he'd be back after the New Year. I know he won't be back now. Wrestling's no picnic. It's no great lifestyle. If you're good at it, you'll be successful. If you're great, you'll be immortal. But there's so many young kids out there who see us and dream big. And for the vast majority, they'll never taste that dream. They'll never take the world by storm. They'll never even make it out of the frying pan. Johnathan Rhine is a very talented exception. I don't feign to know him. I won't boast that I know him like the back of my wrapped-up hand. He's not Matt [name withheld]; someone I stood and fought with. He's just a kid who has, apparently, made a poor decision and now faces an onslaught of opposition. He's just a kid who still has a lot of growing up to do. I hope to one day be a mentor to the next generation of kids who are looking to break in the ropes. After I finally hang up my boots for good. After I place the last title belt into its very own case. And when that day happens, I hope to instill the lessons I was taught and share the wisdom that I've learned through my experiences. But for now, I'll allow myself the opportunity to impart a lesson to young Mister Rhine as he gears up for the two biggest matches of his very young, very new, and hopefully long career: The Queen cannot be conquered. Not without a fight.
