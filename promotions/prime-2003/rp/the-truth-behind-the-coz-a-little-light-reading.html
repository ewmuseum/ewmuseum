The Truth Behind the Coz: A Little Light Reading

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: About a week ago. Cozen. What is that? Who calls themselves something like that? It's a verb, an archaic word, a lie about lying. Peel back the flesh, shred the misleading "facts" and crack the shiny outer shell and what lies beneath? Is there anything there at all? I never wanted to know before, never cared. I lie to Andy and Miss Siena about it all the time now -- I don't know why. They're so serious about everything -- comes from the top, I think. I've never met anyone more serious than Alexandra Pierce. But I want to know. No, I have to know. The not-knowing curls around my chest, squeezing my heart (yes, Lindsay, I have one). Nothing got past the blockade, no emotion was true. I am false empty nothing the Walrus curious as the cursed cat. They know, you know. I know. I can see it in their eyes, see the truth swinging, singing for freedom. It wants to be known -- "fact" only becomes "truth" when it's heard. But they won't tell, won't even whisper it, which hardly counts as telling. When I ask, they distract me. "It's not important," they say. "We have a job for you," they say. "Here's Tyler Rayne's home phone number," they say. (It wasn't really Rayne's number -- too bad for the guy on the other end of that line.) I don't know why they won't tell me. No, wait, that's not true. I don't know why they've been ordered not to tell me. I think it's supposed to be a carrot for later. "Become Michelle Obama and we'll tell you. (Not that they've ever asked for her -- it's almost certainly too high profile -- but I'm sure she has a great life. I mean, can you imagine how much fun she must have? Let's file that away for later.) Where was I? Oh, right. They think it's a carrot, that I won't be able to solve the riddle without the truth they keep under lock and key. But they don't realize my truth. I am the one with the stick. No. I am the stick, sharp and pointy. Everytime they deny me, it scoops out more of my insides, replaced with need. Craig -- stupid, silly, naïve, arrogant Craig. "Dusk" to my "Cozen. He knows. Knows me. Knew me. Wants to know me. As I write this, I'm driving a stolen Mazda to Washington DC and we're going to fight. Well, technically, I'm going to write this down later and am just speaking it aloud, so don't nitpick. Dandy Andy says I should be in another match, that I should be fighting for Chandler Tee-zonda's title. (Editor's note: the name is spelled that way on the page, hyphen and all.) He says Killer Serious (Yahoo's brother?) is in that match, but I choked him out. I say, "Pfft" in more words. I've been champion before and that life is not as great as advertised. That's not what I told Miss Siena; she gets kind of uppity about stuff like that. I told her I wanted to fight Craig, to shut him up, to break his jaw. And that's true. As far as it goes. Dusk knew me, but Dusk doesn't remember me. I knew Dusk, but I don't remember him. It's a quandary, like a riddle, like the Sphinx, but the answer is still a man. The Sphinx is an old man. The Sphinx... that name means something. That name MEANS something? That name means SOMETHING! It's on the tip of my tongue and it tastes like death, and it tastes like life chicken life. I'm going to Washington. I'll be Mr. Smith for one night. Dusk's brain has my truth in it, and I'm going to get it, even if I have to crack open his skull and scoop out the sweetbreads inside with a cracker. And heaven help anyone who gets in my way. I am very good with that stick, after all. * * * * * The room was lit by only a single, squat desk lamp, casting more shadows than light in the high-ceilinged study. The room was big enough, and it had enough books, it might as well have been called a library, but it'd more properly be called a yawning abyss. Creaking floorboards and fan blades spinning of their own accord brought gooseflesh out of the blonde seated behind the desk. Her job often required her to burn the midnight oil. It's just that this was one of those rare occasions that she did so and got to keep her shirt on. She read quietly from a yellow legal pad filled with mad scrawlings. In contrast, her own writing was neat, tiny, and compact, though the shorthand written there wouldn't be comprehensible to anyone else. Page after page of pad after pad kept the blonde's attention. She knew that, through the far doors lied the sanctuary of the Crazy, the theatre wardrobe room where Cozen changed who she was at her core. She wouldn't go in there; you wouldn't have, either. When her cellphone buzzed against her pocket, she nearly jumped out of the wheeled wooden chair, nearly put her through the ceiling. When she saw the name on the caller-ID, that fear only went down slightly. "Ms. Pierce," the blonde said smoothly as she answered. "I have found the lair, as you indicated I would." "Is there anything of use there?" She looked down at the stack of yellow legal pads, brows knitting softly. "Not a thing," she lied. "It's pretty barren, like we expected. Not a bit of anything that could trace it back to her." "Good. Burn it all down." "Are you sure that's -- " "I said to burn it down. I trust you can find a way to follow directions, yes?" "Yes, Ms. Pierce." The Spider in the Web didn't say anything else before disconnecting. The woman in that study sighed, somehow dissatisfied with the abruptness, but she knew that the same events that had the Director's attention allowed her this autonomy. A stack of books moved nearby and she knew that wasn't something that the ghosts of this building could manage. "Come out," she called quietly. "I know you're out there." It was his red eyes that she saw first as one of the shadows split off from the others, drifting towards the desk and the stack of yellow legal paper. Wrestling fans knew him as the Lost Soul, but after the reading the blonde had done in the last two hours, she knew him as something more. "What are you doing here, Dusk?" She knew he was the solution to unlocking the mystery of Cozen, and, more importantly, to keeping the weapon that the woman was all to herself. "I could ask you the same thing, Dr.... Riley, is it?" "That's right," she said with a brilliant smile, extending a well-manicured hand. "Dr. Sarah Riley. Pleased to meet you."
