As she had done every night, the ghost of his mother stalked towards him through the front door. Her decayed and burned hands caressing his tear soaked face. She embraced him, his emotional tidal wave giving way. His head rested in the crater enveloping the entire left side of her skull. ---- "You’ve got to leave." It was a moment he’d played back every single day since, his recollection a white wash of embarrassment followed swiftly by resentment. Mother and father were standing in their living room, cross armed as he walked back inside. It was Spring Break and he was a sophomore in college, right at the point where he was being forced into a major by his academic advisors. He’d only been home for three days. "What?" Wrestling season was over for him, cut short thanks to ankle and knee injuries. He’d gotten a scholarship to wrestle for the University of Iowa, and while he wasn’t the athlete the coaching staff had expected, he was at least trying to get better. But college was starting to wear thin; he didn’t know what he wanted to do, didn’t know where he wanted to go. Iowa City was just corn fields and ice and every-fucking-thing else middle America trumpeted as being beyond utopia. It was the Breadbasket. The Heartland. There was nothing out there. He’d been dressed up like always, wearing loafers and khakis along with a button up long sleeve shirt with a blue waffle pattern. He was living the experience through his own shaky footing, his brow furrowing as he looked at his folks. "What you mean? What’s this all about?" Whereas he’d built a construct of what had occurred, the reality was poignant and inescapable. He wasn’t cast aside because of his desire for wrestling. In reality, he never had the desire for the business. He was just going to college. "Bobby Doyle," his father wasn’t the brute he’d made him out to be. His shoulders slouched and his face was peeled to the carpet. The name made Brandon’s heart sink. "He’s lying." "I cracked open your bedroom door." His mother was adamant, her face trembling as she sneered from the living room. "You don’t think I didn’t see?" Brandon went completely numb. "You," his father started, "have no idea what this does to us." "What it does to you?" Brandon tried to respond. His mother was quick to cut him off. "Your kind aren’t welcome here." "Abigail--" "No!" she roared at her husband. "Do you understand what I saw?" "Your son was bent over the bed, and Bobby was--" Brandon tried to stop her. "It isn’t like--" "Shut up!" His mother yelled over him. "You bring that into my household. You’re not my son. No son of mine is a homosexual." "You’ve got to leave," his father croaked. "You’re not welcome here anymore." There was never a big fight for freedom; he’d soiled the sanctity of their home. He looked up to the second floor before he walked away from his security blanket, his life rend to shambles without a word of defiance. Nobody was there. ---- The sun burned his eyes as he woke up, his face flinching from side to side as he sat up from the floor. He was mere inches away from the gaping wound he’d made inside his house. As he struggled to pick himself up, he reached to brush his eyes, the dirt caked beneath his fingernails stinging as he fell back over onto the plush green chair. He whimpered as he pushed away, knowing he’d fall into the brain and skull fragments which had lined the chair. Panting, he looked for his pistol. I had it in my hands he thought as he tried to find it, but it wasn’t anywhere to be seen. Maybe it was in the hole. Maybe he’d buried it along with the body and he just didn’t know it. A sudden rush of fear shot through him; his fingerprints were all over that damn gun. He had to get it back. He dove into the hole, getting on his knees and digging into the fresh mound with his fingers, cupping dirt with his hands and dragging it away with pulls of his forearm. Heartburn and nausea raced through his stomach as he scratched the dirt. He wasn’t ready to see Faber’s body again. As he drew near, he noticed the tarp’s contents had changed; there was a black bag covering the body. He didn’t remember putting Faber in a body bag. All he’d done was wrap him in plastic. He yanked the tarp out of the ground, dirt filing into the space as he dragged it out. Scrambling with his hands, he ripped open the bag, his face numb to what he was about to see. There was no body in that hole. Inside the black garbage bag were accolades, title belts and accomplishments won over his years in the wrestling business. Brandon brushed the dirt away from his possessions, his fingers strumming across the sobering leather of his prized 5-Star Championship. The white strap had been pierced in spots, the gold plating fractured by the spade of his crowbar. He cradled it against his chest, rocking back and forth as fear raced through his entire body. There was no power when he draped the belt across his shoulder. He didn’t understand. Everything he’d done and heard had felt so real. Tears shrink wrapped his eyes.
