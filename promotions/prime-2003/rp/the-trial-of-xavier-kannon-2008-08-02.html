The Trial Of Xavier Kannon

2 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/08/08. “Out.” The millisecond that there’s the millimetre between the opening van doors, a cluster-bomb of camera flashes explodes in front of him. With handcuffs chafing his wrists, all demands for moisturiser denied, Kannon is led by the arms into the blinding light. Eyes squint to make out even the slightest detail. “Xavier, do you have any comments?” from the left. “Mr Kannon, how will you plead?” from the right. “I hope they fry you, motherfucker!” precedes a wet splash across the back of his neck. Senses playing catch-up, Xavier is bundled through a rabble of photographers, each climbing over the other to get a shot of the Scientologist. Their jabbing questions, hundreds of them, interweave into a queasy hum, while the scrum around him laves the air he heaves in tasting stale and salty. “Keep moving,” he’s urged by a guard who, despite dwarfing Kannon, has a heavy tone of urgency weighing on his words. The unquenched thirst for vengeance in the air makes his skin tingle. As more missiles are thrown, the Gold Patron Meritorious finds his head being slam-dunked down by a burly captor. Shadows engulf the dazzling camera flashes as his feet skid along the floor, now being dragged rather than guided. “Free Kannon!” a distant megaphone demands, a rallying call to the passionate roar that follows. “Free Kannon! Free Kannon!” Despite only being able to see grains of light through the weave of thick, black fabric, Kannon can sense the atmosphere around his brewing. The densely packed bodies spark with static as their ideals rub, and thunder follows. “How do you live with yourself?!” a manic wail drills into Xavier’s ear. “How do you sleep at night?!” A lunge is made for the controversial Gold Patron Meritorious. A baton is drawn. A surge bundles bodies into police. A baton splits an eyebrow. A barrier collapses. A policeman loses his footing. An overzealous cameraman has his lens smashed. A flying brick tears flesh from skull. A foot stamps down onto a protestor. “We’re getting you inside NOW!” is screamed through the fabric that dulls Xavier’s hearing. “The hell did you do?” - - - - - - - - - - XK - - - - - - - - - - “You. Did. Not. Do. Anything.” Encased within a gunmetal grey suit so sharp that it could gut a man, William S. Powell - the Master Debater - prods a defiant finger into Kannon’s face along with every word. “You. Did. NOT. Do. Anything.” - - - - - - - - - - XK - - - - - - - - - - “XAVIER KANNON…“ From above, a voice booms. The amplifying acoustics of the courthouse scoop up each authoritative word and pile them down upon the defendant. Head bowed, and with a neat braided ponytail restraining every dyed red hair from his face, Kannon is preoccupied with checking his Scientology ‘S’ insignia cuff-links. A navy, three-piece pin-stripe suit, every crease and pleat precise and a silver, Windsor knotted tie give the dastardly Scientologist a look of respectability. “…YOU ARE CHARGED WITH CRIMES AGAINST PROFESSIONAL WRESTLING, THE PERVERSION OF A ONCE NOBLE BUSINESS AND THE BREACH OF THE UNWRITTEN WORD BETWEEN PROFESSIONAL ATHLETES…” He raises his head to face the charge. Shoulders back, chest out, the Gold Patron Meritorious looks up in confrontation to the baying galleries. Emerald eyes shimmer, features dynamic with self-satisfaction ooze his disdain for his accusers, and lips naturally curl up at the right corner. “…HOW DO YOU PLEAD?“ A puff of air through his nostrils collapses Xavier’s rigid shoulders, head shaking as his drops from facing its accusers. A smirk of contempt is etched into every feature of his face. Lips are licked with gleeful anticipation. He raises his head to answer the charge. The words are delivered with enough disdain to curdle the milk in every cup of coffee that sits steaming in the courthouse. They’re as defiant as they are remorseless. “Not. Guilty.” Oh, and since he’d be spending the night in the cells anyway… “Peon.” - - - - - - - - - - XK - - - - - - - - - - “Um, like, objection!” From the benches behind her husband comes Eleanor’s ignorant yet impassioned intervention. While the other women crammed into the courthouse favour monochrome skirt suits as uniform, Ellie decided greys didn’t compliment her complexion. Amidst a dull sea of charcoals, a lone blonde and pink (and plenty… plenty of flesh) island reassures those keeping apace of the proceedings that their television sets do indeed still show colour. “MRS KANNON,” rumbles a disapproving voice from the bench. “YOU MAY NOT OBJECT ON BEHALF OF YOUR HUSBAND.” Body hugged tightly by a pale pink dress, she incites a sniping of wolf whistles and pro-Kannon cheers from the Gold Patron Meritorious’ fanatical supporters. “Then stop him being such a big meanie-head then!” Ellie counters, using the long-forgotten precedent of Prep vs. Meanie to substantiate her point. The aforementioned meanie-head would be one Mr Vincent Keller. A swept-back shock of silver hair entices the eye of everyone in the courthouse, contrasted by a pair of dense black eyebrows that sit straight across dark hazel eyes. Every feature is sharp enough to cut diamond, cheekbones, chin and nose creating a Bermuda Triangle for those ladies drawn to the older man to be lost in his confident, gamesman’s smile. As for his suit? Well, it’s a suit, making every other garment in the house appear to be patched-together, tatty rags. “It was a simple question, Mr Kannon,” Keller taints, a Matador stabbing verbal darts into his disorientated foe. “Have you ever instructed your wife to distract referees or opponents so that you could use illegal tactics to win matches?“ Silence. “If anything,” Keller tries to entice a reply from Kannon with as he strolls out from behind his desk, “this may be the tamest question I’ll be asking you during this trial.” Stony, contemptuous silence. Rigid on the stand, the Gold Patron Meritorious sneers down his nose at his prancing interrogator. A piece of gum is mauled between his teeth, the churning of his jaw intensifying disdainful features. “MR KANNON, YOU WILL ANSWER THE QUESTION…” is decreed from the bench, the lights almost shaking in their fittings. From humble beginnings wrestling academy shows in front of twenty drunk guys who only buy tickets to heckle the rookies, Xavier Kannon had not only risen through the wrestling business… he’d transcended it. His name was as hallowed as his ego was legendary. He answers to no man. “Your honour,” he dryly announces, not taking his shimmering green eyes off of the stalking Keller. “I fail to see the point in answering his questions when I ref-” And with that, the fragile truce that the courthouse walls were straining to hold shatters. “-USE TO RECOGNISE THOSE THAT ACCUSE ME!” he has to yell above the rapturous din to put on record. “ESPECIALLY THAT MAN WHO HAS NEVER SET FOOT IN A WRESTLING RING!” In the galleries, bodies are crushed shoulder-to-shoulder to witness justice being unleashed upon possibly the most villainous man to ever blight the wrestling industry. Their man, Vincent Keller, the most revered wrestling historian alive today, is the lightning rod that would harness their years of frustrations and finally strike him down. But even without distractions, without low-blows, without concealed weapons, without exploitation of small print, without an impressionable foe… he still refused to take the fall. “This is bullshit!” judges one of Kannon’s supporters. “This is religious persecution!” His words lit the fuse, and now Kannon gets to watch the fireworks, arms crossing in satisfaction of a job well done. Eyes remain fixed on Keller, letting him know the bull has horns. “I’ll be home for dinner, honey,” he then calls to Ellie, just as his arms are hooked by security and he’s hauled from his podium as anarchy descends upon the theatre of justice. - - - - - - - - - - XK - - - - - - - - - - “This entire trial is a sham!” Outside the courthouse, news crews take delight in giving camera time to the legions of partisans who’ve flocked to the trail. Running a war of chants against those who would see Kannon hang for his crimes, the Scientologist’s supporters vent their theories. “They tried to belittle Tom, they screwed over Isaac,” one woman pours into the camera, holding up her XK t-shirt. “Wrestling needed a scapegoat so they tossed Xavier Kannon to the lions!” “Xavier is a man of wisdom!” a fellow zealot reinforces. “He brings only enlightenment, but wrestling fears what it does not understand. He reminds them that he has transcended their remedial spiritual level!” “Wrestling is dying!” a young man crawls over to women to proclaim. “Xavier Kannon will be its saviour!” “Free the saviour!” then spreads through the crowd around the camera like fire through dry bush. “Free Kannon!” “KANNON IS THE SAVIOUR!” - - - - - - - - - - XK - - - - - - - - - - “Yeah, that’s right… this trial finally has a star!” Arms swing wildly back and forth, legs lunge for each step and head sways from side to side. A disability? Results of a tragic accident? No. That would be an Arm-Flapping Power Walk. Humming ‘No Chance’, Mr Silver, Witness In The Trial Of Xavier Kannon Of PRIME saunters into the jam-packed courthouse, clicking his fingers at the cameras in sequence at the precise second they should be cut to. With a curly mess of brown hair sitting atop a scarred forehead, the scruffily-bearded Mr Silver, WITTOXKOP makes up for his unkempt appearance above the collar by sporting a silver (yes, silver) suit. “Morning, Fuckwad,” he remarks to Kannon as his limb-flailing strides take him towards the stand. “Objection!” Ellie fires off. “My iPhone totally says it’s 12:01. I, like, way demand a retrial!” By now, nobody can really be bothered to instruct her about the law. Or tell her that she set her phone’s clock wrong. Or that she should have probably gone for a mistrial rather than a retrial. Or ask why she didn’t object to the ‘Fuckwad’ bit. Watching Silver with a conflicting mix of anticipation and horror, Vincent Keller takes a sip of iced water, tipping the glass to the Gold Patron Meritorious. Keller was going to enjoy this, and he had a feeling Silver would too. “Son-” “MR SILVER, WITNESS IN THE TRIAL OF XAVIER KAN-” “-ny Silver,” Keller begins, over the ruckus of security ejecting a dark-haired man in a PRIME logo’d polo shirt. “Do you know this man?” Stepping aside, the renowned wrestling historian gestures to the Gold Patron Meritorious, whose slit-narrow eyes betray the fact that he’s less than thrilled to see Silver having any say whatsoever in his fate. “Ummmm…“ Sonny muses, toying with Xavier. “I’m going to have to say no. No works for me.” The entire court freezes for a second. Keller and Kannon swap expressions, the former adopting the latter’s scowl. Lounging in his seat and kicking his feet up onto Powell‘s desk, Kannon rests his head back in his hands. Did Silver resent him? Yes. Would he rather drink the festering juices at the bottom or a garbage can than share a beer with Silver? Yes. Would Silver incriminate himself to spite Kannon? Heightened self-preservation senses say a resounding, NO.” Honour (or lack thereof) among heels. “Mr Silver,” Keller urges, sternly. “Are you really denying that you don’t recognise a fellow employee of PRIME? A man your current charge, Captain Justice even fought on pay-per-view? A man it has been suggested that you even conspired with to FIX the result of their match? A man that you’ve actually faced, and been defeated by, in a match on national television? A man that you greeted just then?” “Unsanctioned match,” Sonny is quick to correct. “Oh, and yes.” Throwing his arms up in the air, a despairing Keller turns to look up toward the judge. “Your Honour,” Keller appeals, “this landmark trial is being turned into a mockery. This is the first time that the wrestling business and its fans have been able to put one of the sports worst offenders on trial to account for his actions, and we are at risk of letting it turn into a pantomime. I want THAT man…” An accusing finger stabs right at Kannon. “…back in THAT stand and I want him to answer for his actions!” Those almost spilling over the railings of the gallery pour their scorn over proceedings, collectively urging the judge to grant Keller his wish. “Your Honour,” chuckles William S. Powell, his tone every bit as dismissive as Keller’s is urgent. “I think you see that the charges brought against my client are nothing more than dirt sheet rumours posted on internet forums by people who have no knowledge of the industry. An industry, may I add, that my client has devoted his lif-” “WE SHALL RECONVENE TOMORROW AT 9AM,,” decrees the judge, prompting Powell to toss his papers across his desk in disbelief. “WHERE, MR KANNON… YOU WILL TAKE THE STAND.“ “Um, like, hella objection?!” - - - - - - - - - - XK - - - - - - - - - - “If this trial doesn’t deliver justice, we’ll do it ourselves!” The grainy footage lets the viewer inside the ranks of anti-Kannon protestors laying siege to the courthouse. A podgy, stubbled face pushes itself into view, filling the screen. “No longer can he be allowed to spit in the face of the wrestling business,” a southern drawl declares. “No longer can he be allowed to degrade the foundations of the business that the Ivan Stanislavs, the Ignatius Lisieuxs, the Eli Flairs, the Boston Stranglers, the Vince Jacobs and the Scott Sluggers built. Not while we have breath in our bodies…” - - - - - - - - - - XK - - - - - - - - - - “Jeremy Howard?” Having been granted his wish, Keller paces in front of the stand to which Xavier Kannon has been confined. Doing his best to mask the indignity, the Gold Patron Meritorious maintains a strained, straight face, not wanting to give his accuser the satisfaction of seeing him squirm. “That name mean anything to you, Mr Kannon?” Keller probes, like he’s jabbing a stick between a cage’s bars to antagonise the captive animal. “You probably do, since you once tried to have his child taken into care by accusing his ‘Krayzie’ moniker of translating into real life. Or maybe a man named Titu Xansiv? You’d know him better as ‘The Pharaoh’? You once trashed a collection of rare Egyptian artefacts that had been traced back to his ancestors after you ambushed him at a Museum? Or what about DarkStar? You may remember fixing his drug test to have him fired? Trigger, perhaps? The rookie you granted the first shot at your PCW world title to, just to humiliate him? Or even BladeD? You didn‘t even give him the respect of being pinned by you, instead blind-siding him so that your wife could beat him!” Nudging the aghast man beside her, Ellie whispers, “I, like, so totally could have done it without him.” Kannon maintains his silence, only letting his facial features react, eyes searching in the top of their sockets for any recollection of the events. “Or if you’re bad with people, how about places?” Keller perseveres, having quite the dirt-list to work down. “Primetime Championship Wrestling? Once a globe-spanning wrestling empire, until you felt that it wasn’t lavishing enough praise on the man who’d backstabbed his way to power. So you set out to wreck it. Scorched Earth, you called it at the time. If you couldn’t have it, nobody could, wasn’t that the case? Or how about RUSH? A thriving, hungry young promotion… well, until you needed some pawns and swallowed up all they stood for.” Those in the gallery who had invested themselves in the RUSH philosophy restrain themselves to little more than a buzz, letting Keller relay their anger. “If your memory is still a little hazy, we can fast-forward to the present day,” offers Keller. “In a move that sickened the entire wrestling world, you shattered the bond of trust between professional wrestlers and assaulted Jonathan Rhine with a spiked heel, blinding him in one eye.” Turning to face his audience, the wrestling historian motions the stabbing to Rhine’s right eye, an unimpressed Kannon‘s glare burning into the back of his head. “It wasn’t just Rhine that lost something with that cowardly act,” Keller drops to a mournful tone in regretfully claiming. “The entire wrestling industry lost something. It lost some of it’s respect. It lost some of its credibility. It lost some of its honour. That day, wrestling died a little inside, and it was at the hand of THAT man!” Having heard enough, having had enough darts stuck in his flanks by the prancing matador, the red-headed bull rears up. “You think I’m killing wrestling?” a wounded Kannon fires back, disbelief drenching each word. “You think I’m KILLING wrestling? Wait for the Colossus buy-rate. You really, really have no idea, do you?” “Well, enlighten me…” Keller invites, even taking a step back to symbolically offer the stage to the Gold Patron Meritorious. “I’m all ears.” “Do you have any idea how many men and women are in the wrestling business today because of me?” venom-tipped barbed words fire at Keller. “Because of people like me? Because of the bad guy? Because of the villain people pay to see get his?” Wanting Kannon to vent enough rope to hang himself, Keller holds his arms open, face gesturing that he’s interested in the number. “All of them,” is boldly proclaimed. “The entire wrestling business exists today because some of us don’t play by the rules, some of us don’t always win the fair way, and some of us leap as far over the line as we can. You call yourself a wrestling historian, but how many years less do you think you’d have to worry about if every wrestler was the fair, clean-cut all American boy?” “Are you seriously trying to excus-” “WRESTLING. NEEDS. ME.“ Kannon erupts, burning emerald eyes channelling his passion into Keller. “You might not like it, and you might dream of a day when all matches are fought with handshakes before and after and not a single rule is even stretched. But the day you get your wish? You’ve just made thousands of men and women across America unemployed. What you want doesn’t sell, simple as. I don‘t get to be the hero that stands on the turnbuckles while fifty thousand people cheer me. I don‘t get to rake in the cash because parents buy their 10 year olds my t-shirts. But because I don‘t get any of that, this doesn‘t happen to other people…” He leans forward, raising a hand, then begins knocking digits down. “Development guys? Fired.” Kannon then reels off, his disdain for Keller having every word be spat right into his face. “The sound techs? Fired. Cameramen? Fired. Ring crew? Fired. Receptionists? The guy in the mail room? Website team? The woman that cleans the toilets? FIRED. The wrestling business, all of it… dead.” Yes, Xavier Kannon justified his acts against Rhine using the toilet cleaner. And what’s worse? For once, he’s being truthful. “Would anyone have gone to see The Dark Knight if the Joker patted Batman on the back and said they should talk things out before someone gets hurt?” he continues, wedging words into every crack he senses in Keller’s demeanour. “How about a Die Hard film where John McClain lives in a world with no terrorists, so sits there watching drivers all keep within the speed limits so not to be bad? 300, if it was ninety minutes of signing a peace treaty?” He leaves the floor open to a retort… but none comes. “I thought so,” the Gold Patron Meritorious sneers as Keller can’t scramble even a couple of stalling words. “Either people like me exist, or the rest of the wrestling world go back to stacking shelves and pumping gas. An eye for ten thousand jobs. Every event from my career you just dragged up? Yeah, I did them all. Done a few more than you’ll never know about, too. But does that make me guilty of killing wrestling?” “No,” Keller mouth, letting out a defeated sigh. “Then rest your case,” Xavier sneers. “I have a pay-per-view to make.” - - - - - - - - - - XK - - - - - - - - - - “My client will only make one short statement…” Despite William S. Powell’s declaration as he explodes out onto the courthouse steps, a rat-a-tat of quick-fire questions are fired the second an exonerated Xavier Kannon, a jubilant Eleanor on his arm, walks out through the doors. “Do you have any words for Vincent Keller?” blurs with “What do you have to say to those who pressed for a conviction?” battles against “Are you going to take legal action yourself?” Ignoring the din of intertwined question strains woven in front of him, Kannon steps forward. He straightens his tie. He clears his throat. “I don’t owe you peons a single word.”
