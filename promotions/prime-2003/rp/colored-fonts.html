Date: End of June People like to people watch. Some more than others, but everyone enjoys it to a certain extent. And why not? People can be as unique and varied as fonts… different colors, different styles… blue Times New Roman, red MS Sans Serif, black Arial (Narrow, Rounded, Unicode). And yet despite their many different styles, they all spell the same words. There’s the guy flagging down the waitress right now, plate of food pushed away from the edge of the table, as if he’s had his fill, even though it doesn’t appear that a single bit has been taken. The waitress walks over with a smile on her face, as he picks up a slice of his toasts. "Is there a problem with you toast, sir?" she asks… to which he replies, "Yeah, I think it was made yesterday" as he proceeds to drop it on his plate to showcase its petrified state. He doesn’t want to be prick about it… to be honest, he’s not really trying… but his nature says to be honest… and upset that he’s waited fifteen minutes for some toast and bacon. At the end of the day, he just wants a nice enjoyable dinner that’s worth its price. His six bucks worth. Impossible as that might be. The waitress smiles and apologizes as she picks up the plate, promising some fresh toast and bacon in just a moment. While she walks away, the smile disappears, and under her breath she mutters "serves you right for ordering breakfast for dinner." She doesn’t really mean that… in fact, pancakes are one of her favorite meals, anytime of the day. Nor is she actually angry at that man for being unhappy with his toast. Who wants to eat burnt, rock-hard bread? If anything, his display was actually amusing. It’s just that she has bills to pay and four bucks an hour isn’t going to cut it, and her tip is likely shrinking by the moment, even though she didn’t burn the goddamn toast. At the end of the day, she just wants a nice simple shift at work, making what she deserves. Not losing her tip thanks to a dipshit cook. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ 22 Minutes Later She slipped out of the booth like dress shoes on ice… gone in the blink of an eye. By the time Kaiser reacted, she was nearly across the restaurant and out the main entrance. Jumping up, he tried to head forward but ran into a small obstacle in the form of a table. Doing his best to maneuver his large frame from the booth, Kaiser thought about shouting her name, but she was already out the door and it would have just made a scene. An even bigger scene. Pouncing across the floor, his feet thudding with heavy steps, Kaiser made a rush to follow her, not knowing exactly what he would say, but figuring anything would be better than sitting at the table like some dumb oaf, mute with stupidity (or stubbornness). "Sir… excuse me, you can’t leave till you’ve paid. Sir.." "I’m not leaving. I just…" Kaiser paused only long enough to form the sentence, but didn’t bother wasting the time to finish it. He shove open the door, pushing past a couple waiting to be seated. Outside, as the rain pelted his flesh, Kaiser turned his head to the left, then the right, desperately looking for her or her car, anything that would let him know which way to run. Finally, he spotted her off to the left, saw the lights on her car blink, knew she was unlocking it as she darted across the asphalt. "Michelle!" He closed his eyes as he shouted her name, and though he told himself it was because of the raindrops smashing against his face, It was possible he just didn’t want to see if she’d stop. A moment passed without the echo of a car door slamming, and so slowly, he opened his eyes. Thirty feet in front of him, his baby sister stood at the side of her Accura… just stood there in the pouring rain. "I’m not doing this, Kaiser." "Whatever I said… I’m sorry." "Whatever you said?" Michelle looked up at the sky, tasting a bit of rain as she laughed briefly. "This wasn’t ever about me… or even us. I agreed to sit down over a meal and talk with you because you said you were worried about me. About us. But this whole thing… this was just to get your own head straight before you become a dad. And that’s fine, Kaiser. It really is. But don’t try to sell it like you’re concerned about me." "What do you want from me, Michelle?" "I want you to want to fix this because you miss talkin’ with your sister. Not because you need one less thing to stress about, or one less headache on your plate." "That’s not what this is about." "I believe that you think that’s the truth, Kaiser." She gave a small smile and shook her head, ponytail bobbing a bit. "It is the truth." Michelle reached down and opened up her car door, pausing before she got in. "I don’t blame you for what Anthony did. But you blame yourself, and it’s making you selfish." He watched, this time opting to play the role of the silent dumb oaf, as she slid into the car, closing the door gently. Everything she did was gentle. Even as he’d yelled at her across a parking lot, there’d been a softness to her words, making them barely audible over the rain. Michelle’s taillights ignited as she pulled from her spot and exited the lot. Kaiser turned and headed back towards the restaurant before they sent out a team of waiters to drag him back in to pay the bill. As he approached the front entrance, he clenched his teeth and slammed his right foot into a small bench seated near the canopy over the door. The bench rocked back, smacking against the stucco finish of the building, then slid down the wall, crashing into the gravel. Kaiser lacked that gentle nature. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ And then there’s the mother, smacking one child’s hand as he reaches across the table to pluck a french fry from his siblings plate. From there, its straight into to rubbing the back of the little one, trying to get her to stop crying. "Ssshhhh, stop it. We’re in public." She says to her daughter. It’s not that she resents her children. Only the first was even unplanned, and she couldn’t have been happier when he was born. But sometimes, it’s just almost too much. She just wants them to behave… to understand that raising three children is a major task. She wants them to know why she’s always so exhausted, why she can’t sleep at night. Of course it’s an unreasonable desire, a pointless hope, but it doesn’t stop her from dreaming. Its only those dreams that can get her through a dinner with three little ones, all under her watch while daddy works late. At the end of the day, that’s all she wants… just the chance to dream that the next time they go out for dinner, no one will steal anybody’s fries. "Hi" She slides into the booth opposite of him, shaking the rain from her hair… ponytail bobbing playfully behind her. "You and that ponytail. Some things will never change." He smiles, this the first time he’s talked to his sister face-to-face in far too long. "So… I’m glad you agreed to come." He’s not at all like the grumpy man with the day old toast, or the struggling waitress. Nor is he anything like the mother, holding together her sanity as she raises three kids without much help from dad. He’s a different font. A different color. And yet, he’s telling their story. All he wants is a peaceful dinner with his baby sister, a chance to say ‘sorry’, patch things up and get back to the laughter. His six bucks worth. Impossible as that might be.
