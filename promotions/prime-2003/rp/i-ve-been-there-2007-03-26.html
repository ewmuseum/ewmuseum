I've Been There

26 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/26/07 You wouldn't figure the guy was a reporter. Not really. At least film noir is good enough to disillusion us with the idea of the gaunt, haunted reporter that manages to have just the right contacts. Or is that the detective? To be honest, I'm not entirely sure. I took my seat across from the balding, rotund man in the black polo shirt. Odd, but think of a Bob Ryder wanna-be. Sad, isn't it? If you have nothing more in your life than wanting to be Bob Ryder... Anyway, I took my seat, pushing the basket of tortilla chips away. No need to take in anything right now except for the steak entree they have here. It's not a bad restaurant, but really, I don't come here much. Given that I did an exchange tour with one of the Mexican promotions, it seems like half the staff here knows who I am. “Mr. Stewart,” he says, trying to hide the fact that he's still crunching on a mouthful of chips, “glad you could make it.” I try not to shoot him an aggravated look. It's hard. But, fair enough, I'll play along. I wave the waiter over. “Yeah, could I get an unsweetened ice tea and a steak tampiqueno?” I ask him. With a smile and a nod, he heads off. Given the way this guy is going, he probably bypassed the courtesy of not ordering until the other person arrived. “So, Mr...?” I let the question hang in the air. He just stares back at me. Fine, let him. I never got a name of the guy I was supposed to interview, just where he'd be with a physical description. One that didn't mention he was a Bob Ryder wanna-be. “Mr. Danielson,” he replies, slightly indignantly. I ignored the not-so-subtle undertone. His job is to report about folks like me, and if they want the courtesy of me knowing their names, they need to tell me first. “Mr. Stewart, I wanted to interview you for a piece in the upcoming PRIME Material magazine.” Oh, wow. You mean to tell me that they actually publish something with that kind of name? Great. “Yeah, go on ahead,” I said, nodding the waiter that set the iced tea at my shoulder. “Well, first of all, you had a very impressive return in the Dual Halo.” He stopped, looking at me, setting his little tape recorder to the side. “You didn't ask a question.” Silence. I suppress a smirk. No one ever said I had to be nice or put up with amateur garbage. This isn't my first interview, and this guy has already aggravated me. “Well, you went far in the Halo before the elimination. Any thoughts you'd like to add?” Any thoughts? I tell myself to humor him. “Yeah, it proves to me a suspicion I've had for a long, long time; Ferguson is spineless. He hits me from behind while we get ready to group tackle someone because he's afraid to deal with me. As for going far in the Halo, I didn't go far enough.” He looked genuinely puzzled. “What would be far enough?” “Winning.” “Oh, um, well, yeah,” he sputtered, then took a moment to collect himself as the waiter set down the plates. I nodded and pulled the plate a little closer, nabbing my steak knife and cutting into the well-seasoned beef. Delicious and they always manage to get the vegetables on the plate cooked just right. There are few pleasures like a meal that's done really well. “So,” the man says, looking up from his pile of cheese and, from what I can tell, more cheese, “as of this interview, you're scheduled to take on Chainz on the next ReV. Does facing such a sick, depraved man concern you?” I stopped, mid-chew, looking up through his eyebrows in a way that makes the man look like he's about to commit a homicide. “Concern me? Do you know anything about me, really?” Okay, let's be honest; that's an insulting question. This guy has come to interview me about my return to PRIME and I asked if the guy knew anything about me. Of course, the guy is sputtering around his cheese with cheese because he doesn't have a clue what I'm talking about. “What, uh, what do you mean?” I took a deep breath and sat back. “Okay, get ready because I'm going to recount something that happened on television, so I really shouldn't have to tell you.” I sat up and pushed his plate away. “If you want that, eat it. I'm not going to be able to eat after this.” The man looked genuinely baffled. “I had a stint in fWo not too long back. Now, it was a miserable time of my life. I couldn't get a break, couldn't get people to take me seriously even as the Hardcore champion there. In fact, people forgot I was even on the roster. So, you can imagine what kind of mood I was in. Then, I saw Nick Brandish.” I took a small sip of my drink and chewed my lower lip. “I guess the best way to say it was that Brandish was the sickest son of a bitch I'd ever encountered. Remember, I was a state police officer, so I've met the scum of society. Brandish was the kind of guy that rumors flew about, and you tended to believe them all. The rumor that he killed his mother, I believed. That he raped a cousin or something to death, it wouldn't have surprised me. I'm talking about seven feet of tattooed psychosis. I saw him do some pretty sick things to people on the roster, and so I couldn't just stand aside.” The interviewer had actually stopped eating. He looked at me with a mix of interest and horror. “So, I confronted him. I told him outright that he needed to leave the fWo roster, because as long as I was there, I wasn't going to let him have a world stage for his disgusting behavior.” I rubbed at the bridge of my nose, feeling that injury from that bout that had broken it pretty clean. “You can imagine how well that went over. In fact, the very next week, he came up and started talking about my wife. If there's one thing you don't do, it's threaten my wife. So I broke his nose. Just a straight punch to his face and felt it snap. He didn't do anything, but I guess he took it as a challenge.” I raised my right index finger. “If there's one thing you don't do, it's threaten my wife.” I raised the middle finger. “If there are two things you don't do, it's threaten my family. And that's exactly what he did. I have a sister; she's a tiny little thing, about five foot two and can't be more than one-fifteen on her heaviest day. She's probably the sweetest little girl that exists, the girl that works with special education kids because that's just how she is. So what did Brandish do? He had pictures of my wife throughout her day. He had pictures stalking my parents. He had pictures of my sister, sometimes as close as ten feet from her.” I stopped. I could feel the heat growing in my chest, my hands were shaking and my eyes burned. I had to calm down first, before I hopped up and killed someone there in Casa Suerte. So, I took a breath in through my nose, blew it out between tight lips and rubbed my temples. I don't think that last one did any good, but I still thought it might help. I guess it was to give my hands something to do, other than rip a piece off the table or throttle the poor waiter when he showed up. I lifted my head to see Rob Byder looking back at me in a state of shock.. He sat there with a tortilla chip in his hand, cheese dripping off of it onto his shirt. I waved at it vaguely and he uttered a blue streak of curses trying to wipe it up. “So, what do you think I did? He and I had us a streetfight set up. I went in there with the intent that he could never hurt my family. He went in with the intent to kill me and then destroy my family, slowly. So it was do or die. I went out there with some surprises hidden away and he and I went at it. I broke a baseball bat over him, he threw me like a rag doll. We hammered on each other for what felt like forever. I don't know how long it was. I never went back and watched it. But I Finally managed to nab my guillotine on him and get him down. I used a... it's not a move, it's not a hold... It's a weapon I hadn't used since I cracked open a football helmet on Sonny Silver's head. I had him down on the mat with the choke still applied and just started throwing knees at his head.” I paused. “Now, let that process a moment. I started throwing knees into the top of this guy's skull. My legs firing in rapid impact with a human skull.” I stared at him while that sank in. His faced blanched a bit more and I nodded. “I threw fifty-eight knees into his head as hard and fast as I could. It should have killed him. No, it didn't. I didn't watch the match, but I saw a particular picture of me right after I got dogpiled by fWo officials wanting to stop the first death on their television shows. I was covered in blood; my body, my hands, but my right leg was what really stood out. It looked like I'd been shot, there was so much blood running down that leg. And the scariest part of that picture? I was still trying to get back at him.” It was true. They had pulled me off of Brandish before I could land any more shots. But I wanted to. They had pulled me up that aisle. They didn't show it, but I broke loose from them and almost got back to him. He was going to die. That, in my mind, was the only way I could be sure my family would be safe. But no, they stopped me. “Now, consider this: I picked a fight with one of the most depraved people on the planet. I almost killed him. Now, understand, I don't think Chainz is a pushover, but I've dealt with worse. I've dealt with the very worst. Am I worried? No. If Chainz knows what's good for him, his dealings with me will end the moment that bell rings.” With that, I really couldn't handle anymore. I stood up and pulled a twenty out of my back pocket and tossed it on the table. I stalked out, with a face that probably looked like black murder. I say probably because everyone nearly dove out of my way as I headed for the door. I looked for my Jeep, then bit the inside of my mouth in agitation. So, instead, I went to the rented Ford Escape until we decided on what we wanted. My drive home was a livid flashback of the story I had told the interviewer. I kept forcing down the intensity that made bile rise in my throat. I fought down the desire to find out if Brandish ever died and if not, going and finishing the job. It was a hard drive, even if it was only ten minutes. I got out at the gym, my shirt clinging to my body. I tapped the entry code on the door alarm and used my key to open it. Can't be too careful, not anymore. I walked the steel stairs and pushed my way into the apartment I shared with my wife. She was lying on the couch, watching Heroes. For some reason, she just latched right onto the show. Makes me love her even more. And as I stood there, all the emotion in me got cold and evened out. I knew why I did what I did to Brandish, and I knew why I'd do what I'd do to Chainz in a similar situation. It's an easy decision. I grabbed the cordless phone and the piece of paper off the endtable and make a call to the night desk of the PRIME magazine. “Yeah, this is Shawn Stewart. You're going to get text of an interview with me tonight. I want you to print it all. Yes, all of it and post it on your website. I know, just do it.” I sent a message to Chainz that night: Don't screw with me. You will lose and you will not survive to lose again.
