Addict

26 November 2005

Date: 11-26-05, 5:09 PM MST Killers never hurt feelingsKillers never hurt feelingsKillers never hurt feelingsGonorrhea, gorgonzolaGonorrhea, gorgonzolaSingle files for clean feedings... “This Cocaine Makes Me Feel Like I’m On This Song” by System of a Down Yeah, I was once an addict, and my drug of choice was the good ol’ nose candy, cocaine. I started dabbling with cocaine when I was about twelve, maybe thirteen years old. I mainly did coke to get away from the pain I went through everyday living in the Kreed household. There wasn’t enough of the glorious nose candy to keep me pain free; it actually added on more pain. Whenever I did coke, it would burn my nasal cavity, sometimes caused me nosebleeds. But I didn’t care; it put me in a realm where I felt I belonged. A realm where no one or nothing could hurt me. I felt complete when I was in that realm. Then my “Cocaine Realm” as I’d like to call it, clashed with reality. I would steal money from my foster parents, pilfer jewelry from my foster mother and sister to pawn and did dares for money. I’d usually put my body and life on the line for fifty dollars, one hundred dollars, anything to feed my addition. I was in pain after almost killing myself for money, but I was back in my Cocaine Realm. I snorted more choke, like Tony Montana from Scarface amounts, which would eventually lead me to my second death: cocaine overdose. Jason Brubaker, my childhood friend, found me in a dumpster in some sort of alley and took me to the hospital. I was asked to go to rehab, but refused with the utmost vehemence, claiming that I didn’t have a problem, that I could quit at any time. When I was fifteen, I killed my foster family, went to a mental institution and was rehabilitated completely, from my cocaine addiction, my psychotic and schizophrenic episodes. About eighteen months later, I was released, ready to reenter reality, ready to take on the world once again, but I had to be released under a doctor’s care just in case of any relapses, and lived with said doctor until I was ready to go out onto my own. I got an apartment when I was eighteen. I was ready to live out on my own. Now, I’m twenty-five years old, been sober for the better part of a decade, and I have a wife and a son. After all I went through ten years ago, I sometimes wonder if I’ll ever go back to that coked-up kid who stole money, pawned jewelry and destroyed myself for everyone else’s amusement, just to collect money from dares. I know for a fact that if I ever became that human being again, I wouldn’t be stopped. You’d have to kill me to stop me. My monster still haunts me, and going back on coke, the world as I know it now would come to an end. Everything would be lost. Let’s just hope that doesn’t happen anytime in the near future... ...Simon...Wake up Simon... ...Simon, please...Wake up Simon! ...Hello?...I have an emergency...My husband won’t wake up... ...Oh my God!...He’s seizing...Someone come down here and save my husband!... ...C’mon Simon...Don’t you die on me goddammit... ...We’re losing him!...Step on it Earl...Hang in there kid...For God sakes hang in there... “Welcome back to the living, Simon.” Those were the first words I hear when I was coming to. I felt disoriented, my head was throbbing and what was I doing in the hospital? The last place I remember being was in the basement of my childhood house. Or was that through meditation? I wasn’t sure. It didn’t matter anymore. All that mattered was that I was out of harm’s way. I let out a groan, rubbing my eyes with the heels of my palms. As my eyes slowly adjusted, I saw my lovely wife sitting next to me, smiling fondly. Her eyes had relief in them, speaking softly to me, though I couldn’t understand what she was saying in her inaudible voice. At the foot of the bed, there was Sylvia Madison, jotting down notes on a clipboard. She looked over at me and smiled. “It’s nice to see that you’re back with us,” I heard her say. I slowly sat up, looking at Yuriko, then to Sylvia. “It’s good be back,” I said, my eyes scanning the hospital room. “What happened? Why am I here?” “Well,” Sylvia began, her eyes locked on my own. “You had a brain sensation, then had a seizure.” I looked at Sylvia quizzically, then shot a glance over at my wife, who was nodding her head solemnly. I never heard of a “brain sensation” before; perhaps it’s a new phrase for a stroke. “So...I had a stroke?” I questioned the doctor. Sylvia shook her head. “Not quite,” she said, giggling slightly. “A stroke is a blood clot to the brain. What you had was a brain sensation. Whatever you saw while you were in meditation scared you so bad that your brain shut down immediately. Your brain couldn’t handle it. So when your brain shut down, your body when into convulsions, thus putting you in a seizure. Had to use a form of electroshock therapy to stimulate your brain and to stop your seizing. Wasn’t exactly pretty. In other words, you almost died.” “Wow,” as all I could say. I didn’t think going through all of that would kill me. The body can’t live without the mind, supposedly. “Since I’m still alive, when can I get out of here? I’d like to go home out. I can’t stand being in hospitals.” “You’re free to go, Simon,” Sylvia said. Then her expression turned serious. “But you have to take it easy just in case something like this happens again. Avoid meditation for awhile, avoid anything stress related.” Then she turned to Yuriko. “And Yuriko,” she began, “no sex for the both of you, so jumping on Simon’s bones.” Sylvia gave her a wink that I didn’t catch. Yuriko snapped her fingers with exaggerated disappointment. “Well, darn,” she said sarcastically. “I’ll try really hard not to my husband like my bitch.” She turned, smiling mischievously at me. I blushed a little bit. That promise won’t last long, I guarantee it. “Thanks, dear.” I got out of bed, got dressed and looked over at my wife. “Ready to go, my dear?” Yuriko nodded. “Hai, but I’m driving. You need to take it easy.” She held up my keys to the Land Rover, shaking them. “Okay, but if you wreck my baby---” “Don’t worry, Simon, I won’t wreck ‘your baby.’ I am a better driver than that.” “I sometimes wonder,” I answered, smiling ruefully at my wife, then giving her a kiss. “Let’s go. I don’t want to spend more time in this place if I don’t have to.” I took my wife’s hand, then nodded at Sylvia. “Thanks for saving my life...again,” I said, smiling. “Not a problem. Just doing my job is all.” Less than five minutes later, Yuriko and I were driving home. For the first time, neither one of us talked while on our way home. The silence felt uncomfortable, since Yuriko and I always talked about anything while we drove, anything at all, but there wasn’t much to talk about. Even after my near-death experience, there wasn’t much to talk about. Sylvia explained it all to us. I looked over at my wife, attempting to break the silence. “What do you want t---” My cell phone rang in mid sentence. I looked at the color I.D. to it see it was from my mother. “Excuse me for a moment, Yuriko.” I opened up my phone. “Hello? What can I do for you mother?” “This isn’t your mother, Simon.” It was Malachi, my mother’s husband. For five years he and I haven’t seen eye to eye. Lemme set the seen for ya. It was the fall of 2000, I was in London, England doing an International wrestling tour. I was sitting outside of a local fish and chips eatery, eating a five-piece chicken meal and a two-piece fish meal with chips in each order. I saw a family - who was Alexis (my mother), her husband Malachi and their daughter, Michelle, who was fifteen at the time - step into the restaurant, order their food and came back outside. I didn’t think anything of them and went back to my food. Of course, I didn’t recognize my mother since I haven’t met her since I was born. But she recognized me. She got up from her table, went over to mine and introduced herself. When she said that she was my mother, I lost my temper, lost my temper to a woman whom I never met in all my life. I said some unkind things to her, letting out my rage about her giving me up for adoption. Her husband got involved, we got into an altercation and things simply went to hell. When I got back to the United States, I looked up my mother’s phone number, hoping that she was still living in Springfield. Much to my surprise she did. I dialed her number, I talked to her for a long time, telling her I was sorry what had happened back in London, and we got to know each other. I forgave her for giving up for adoption, even though the Kreed family would’ve killed me before I killed them. I understood why she gave me away: she didn’t have much money, she had two dead end jobs and wasn’t ready for a child. She told me that I was a product of rape, which I already knew years prior when Steven had his field day with me. Even though my mother and I developed a mother/son relationship, Malachi would always hate me because of that day five years ago. With all of that said, let’s go back to reality, shall we? I got Malachi on the phone. “What can I do for you, Malachi?” I asked. The man wouldn’t give me the time of day if without a reason, and judging by the sound of his voice, it sounded important. “It’s Alexis---your mother.” He was struggling to tell me what he wanted to say. “She’s...she’s sick Simon. I don’t know how to say this, but she has cancer.” I almost dropped my cell phone. My mother? She has cancer? Is that possible? It didn’t make any sense. I’ve always known that she was healthy. She exercised, watched what she ate and she’d been looking good for forty-three years of her life. Now she has cancer. How did this happen? “But...but how?” was all I could say. “She’d been sick for awhile now. I took her to the doctor and he found cancer cells in her blood. I’m sorry, Simon. I’m really sorry.” The line went dead. I folded up my cell phone, my eyes grew wide. “This...this can’t be happening,” I said to myself. Yuriko looked over at me. “Simon? What’s wrong?” “It’s my mother...she has cancer. I want to see my mother. Take me over there.” “Simon, I don’t think that a good---” “TAKE ME TO SEE MY MOTHER, GODDAMMIT!” “Okay, I’ll take you over there.” Yuriko turned the Land Rover around and on our way to see my mother. You can’t avoid pain that’s for certain. With the announcement of my mother’s cancer still fresh in my mind, I slowly walk to the front door and rang the door bell. My wife was with me, her arm around mine as we wait for someone to open the door. A few seconds later, Malachi Armstrong answered the door, looking down at both Yuriko and myself. The six-foot-eight, two hundred and eighty-pound man nodded at us. “Come on in, guys,” he said, letting us in. He fixed his attention immediately on me. “I know you and I hadn’t seen eye to eye for many years, but I respect that you have a loving relationship with her. I may not like you, but I respect you, Simon, even after everything we’ve been through. I want to make my peace with you.” With that said, he held out his large right hand. I took his hand and shook. For the first time since knowing Malachi, he and I were actually being civil toward one another, finally making peace. For being a big time conglomerate businessman, he’s not a bad guy. “I appreciate it, Malachi.” That was simple. Now to the matter at hand. “Where’s my mother?” “She’s upstairs,” he said, and led me upstairs to meet her. Yuriko slowly followed suit. We stepped into my mother’s bedroom, where Doctor Nelson West was with her. Alexis was laying in bed and she looked the worst for wear. Her skin, usually clean and full of life, was ashen. Her red hair, usually vibrant and healthy, was limp and dull. Her hazel eyes, usually clear and strong, where weak and lifeless. This was the first time that I’ve seen my mother in such bad shape. I just talked to her a couple of weeks about after I gotten into my car accident. She sounded healthy as ever, now she’s the shell of her former self. Nelson West walked over to me, wrapping an arm around my shoulders. He and I had our encounters in the past. The first time when I shattered my ribs, he performed surgery to repair them, and when I overdosed on my cocaine, he flushed out my system. Doctor West was in his mid-fifties with short, brown hair, brown eyes and was clean shaven. He was dressed in black slacks, a white dress shirt, black dress shoes and a black tie. “You still using that nose candy, Simon?” he asked, trying to relieve the tension in the room. I let out a sigh. “You know that I’ve been clean for a decade. Let’s get down to business. How far along is her cancer?” “It’s in the early stages,” West replied. “But there is a problem.” “Which is?” “It’s inoperable, so the only solution is chemotherapy. Your mother refuses to do chemo.” I looked over at my mom. She needed chemo in order to survive. Judging my looking at her, she didn’t want to be saved. She wanted to die. “How long does she have to live?” I asked, my eyes remained on my mother. “I’m not sure, to be honest with you, Simon,” West replied. “It could be four to six months, perhaps a year. But it’s too early to know for sure.” “How did she get it?” “She claims family history, which could be a possibly. All girls from Alexis’ mother’s side of the family had cancer of some sort. Looks like she’s next on the list. I’m sorry, Simon, there isn’t anything I can do for now.” I nodded at the doctor. “Thank you, Nelson. You can go. Malachi will call if he needs your assistance.” Nelson took his arm off my shoulders, said some things to Malachi and left. I walked over towards my mother. She looked up at me, trying the best she could to smile. “Hey, Simon,” she said weakly, slowly placing a hand on my face. I took her hand into my own and kissed it. “Hi, Mom. I got here as soon as I could when I heard you were sick. I can’t believe this is happening.” “Things like this happen, and I was the unfortunate causality,” Alexis replied, tears brimming in her eyes. “I’m sorry that this has happened. Everyone on my mother’s side of the family---including my mother---had cancer. Looks like I have the worst of it.” “You need to get better, Mom. You need chemo.” She slowly shook her head. The very thought of chemo didn’t appeal to her. “I’ll do no such thing. I want to die, Simon. I’ve lived a long enough life. “But your family needs you, Mom. I need you!” Alexis smiled, chuckling a little bit. “You don’t need me, Simon. You’re all grown up, you have a wife and a family of your own. I’ve already talked to my family about my dying, and they are prepared for the worst. I thank you for...” She trailed off, slowly falling asleep. I got up and quietly left her bedroom. The pain was too unbearable for me to handle. My mother was dying, she refused to get chemo and there was nothing any one could do to change her mind. This wasn’t happening, this couldn’t have been happening. All that had happened earlier was a lie, it was fake. I want to believe it wasn’t real, but unfortunately it wasn’t. My mother had cancer and there’s I could do to stop it. My mother wanted to die and there’s nothing I could do to stop it. I wanted to get away from this reality, get away from the pain that I’m feeling. All this pain reminded of the pain I went through for eight years of my life, all the pain I experienced when I lived with the Kreeds. There was only one thing I could do. I turned into downtown Springfield, where all the bad things happen. All the murders, rapes and robberies happen here. All the drug dealers have their business here and all the pimps and hookers make their business here. I turned into the parking lot of a beat-up looking apartment complex. I got out of my car, went to apartment 3G and rang the doorbell. I never thought I’d be back here again. I thought about turning back, not associating myself in this bad part of the neighborhood. A few seconds later, a haggard looking man answered the door. The man was Nathan Killborne, a well-known drug dealer in Springfield. He was a balding man with green eyes, a beer belly and an artificial right leg. He was wearing a pair of tattered shorts and a white, greasy wife beater. His right eye was occupied with a black leather eye patch. He looked up at me and immediately recognized me. “Hello, Simon,” Nathan said. “I knew you’d come back to me. Come on in and I’ll get you fixed up.” I walked into his apartment, closing the door behind me.
