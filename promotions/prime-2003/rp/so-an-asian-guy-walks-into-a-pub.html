So An Asian Guy Walks Into A Pub...

undated

Date: <18 Hours Late There is a stone thereThat whoever kissesOh, he never misses-Francis Sylvester Mahoney As I step out into the Irish air, I’m less then thrilled. I was expecting public drunken orgies at every turn; considering how the Irish have physically evolved, that may be a plus. But now I’m here: where the biggest hero is their version of Austin Stevens: Snakemaster and the biggest villain is a pomme de terre. Shannon Airport here in Limerick is pretty unremarkable. I came in on a flight from Philadelphia to complete the given mission. And now I’m among the gingers, loads of them. Everywhere I look: brusque red hair and unabashed freckles rule the landscape. Sure, they’re covered up by business casual clothes or emo attire; there’s the same fashion backdrop as in America, but it’s all to cover up the blatant Irishness of all these people. They’re so…proud to be so homely. Pride is something, though; I know better then anyone else that sometimes it’s the only thing that keeps you popping back off the mat. I find myself a taxi; it’s one of those quaint little black ones that looks like it should have secret gun turrets that pop out in moments of extreme danger. The driver is a stout, little guy named Horace (or at least that’s what I decipher from his awful “I sound like I’m chewing on super glue and celery” accent). Horace fits him well since he’s wearing a funny hat and I think that men named Horace are almost contractually obligated in life to wear comical headwear. “Whar ye’ goin’, baddy?” Calling me “buddy” seems unnatural for him. He must cart Americans around a lot and he realizes that it’s one of those not-too-formal titles that he can use with just about anyone. I look up into the rearview mirror to find his dulled green eyes looking right back at me; I avert my own gaze to avoid an awkward moment. “I need to go visit the Blarney Stone.” Ah yes, the entire purpose of my trip here. I don’t even know why I’m going, but I’ve decided that the best way to find a way to beat Paddy O’Shea once and for all is to find myself to the heart of his people. After all, he seems to make a fairly big deal about them and all their traditions, so this stone must be something special. I’m going on the burning bush theory that once I get there, I’ll simply know what to do in order to either humiliate Paddy or laud myself. “I guess it’s sort of a personal pilgrimage. You ever been?’ Normally I wouldn’t want to know, but I guess there’s something slightly endearing about his fatherly air. “O’ course aye been! Once aye left me home, one o’ the first things aye did was grab a pint with my mates, then head on down to the stone. Didn’t even know why, just wanted to go see it. My parents never made too much fuss about it, but aye know there’s summat special about that ‘un.” It sounds like the typical runaround for this sort of mystic bullshit; they give you the old adage about how you don’t understand the power of the site or the object until you behold it yourself. I don’t fall for that…usually. But things are different in life now. Maybe it’s time to have a little faith? No, that’s not the word. It’s time to exhibit self-reliance in decisions and actions. That’s what I’m doing here in the homeland of Eoin O’Shea, Sinead O’Connor, and of course, the most famous Irishman of all: Shaquille O’Neal. Horace looks ahead for a couple minutes, concentrating intently on the road in front of him. It’s a balmy forty eight degrees outside and he opens the window a crack. Taking off his hat, he lets the wind smooth down what little white hair he has. There’s a poetic liberation to this whole scene; I just look the other way out the window at sprawling green fields all those Irish folk songs always talk about. It’s not a bad place, if you take out the people, the accent, the incessant amount of sheep, the Catholics, the Protestants, and the North. For instance, the beer’s second to none. “Is there a watering hole somewhere around here, old guy? I could use a genuine Irish pint before I head home tomorrow.” As he slows the car, Horace turns around with a massive grin on his face. Clearly, this weathered old man is one who knows his pubs. “But o’ course. We kin stop over at Dolan’s. T’aint yer Hard Rock Café, though. This here’s a real bar.” He pulls the car into a handy parking spot; it’s mid-afternoon so there aren’t very many people here. I follow Horace through the front door. He greets the classy-looking blond woman (6.5, could use a nose job), who refers us to the waitress (8, clothes not up to snuff). Horace sits down on the other side of the table. Apparently, he’s my bona fide Irish tour guide now. “Maester Tsonda, ye’ll be wantin’ to order some Guiness.” Well, that’s where the buck stops, guy. I’m not drinking that piss water. “It gets ye drunker ‘n anythin’ on earth.” Well, okay maybe one. The 8 comes back, looking at me cock-eyed. I wouldn’t say she’s had much experience in the Asian category. She’s a couple seconds away from asking me what Chairman Mao was like. “We’ll be havin’ two Guinesses.” She walks away and Horace looks at me. He still hasn’t really asked what I’m doing here and I don’t know what I’m going to tell him if he does. Well, sir, I’m fighting an Irish guy so it just makes logical storyline sense that I’m going to visit his home country, right? What’s more, I’ll probably make some profound discovery about my self and end up embracing Horace under the setting Irish sun. See? See how dumb that sounds? It’s five minutes before the drinks come out. Horace tips his glass to me, then cruises into the cream-colored foam. I look at mine, sizing it up like an opponent in the ring. Then I attack, spilling foam and pure beer into my throat. Here’s the part where Angello Deville makes a wicked funny gay joke; okay, now that’s done, so we’ll move on. It’s ecstasy. I hate to tell you, but Guinness is fucking superb. It’s as crisp as Heineken, cold as Coors, and got a better taste then…well…anything. I look at Horace and give him the masculine “nod of acknowledgement, respect, and props all in one.” There are some bad Irishmen and Horace is stupid as a can of turpentine, but I can’t fault his intentions. All he wants to do is get a foreigner good and drunk so that when he goes to look at a stone that’s a huge letdown, he’s not totally pissed. We drink in silence from here on out. He drops some Euros on the table and gives everyone a hearty smile before leaving. We’re back in the car and cruising within minutes. My throat is still wet with the last drops of Guinness and I swallow my spit to savor the taste. I close my eyes for two seconds…and I wake up to see Blarney Castle standing like a monolithic shadow on the hillside, interrupting the fields of green (I realize this is like the third time I mentioned them, but they’re everywhere; there’s a reason why the shamrock is green, dude. Horace leans back to me. “We’re here, Maester Tsonda. Ye want me come in with ye?” I guess some tourists need their taxi driver to come in and take cheesy pictures of them upside down kissing the Stone. “No need. I’ll be like five minutes.” He yells something about the admission of seven euros as I walk away. Yeah, five minutes and I’ll know the secret to winning at life and at the Nightmare. I almost call bullshit on myself and sit right back down in the car. But I’ve come a fucking far way, so I might as well join the hordes. There are dozens of people, but it’s not awful to get around. It’s the middle of the day, so it’s just other foreigners. The lack of blazing red hair is almost eerie (note: I think, at this point, you can just make your own Aimz joke here, laugh, and call it a day). “…may be the object referred to as ‘Jacob’s Pillow,’ brought to Ireland by Jeremiah the prophet.” I walk past several tours. One of the tours is led by an Asian guy and I do a double take. Yes, he has an accent. I’m shocked that any of my brothers and sisters would have chosen to settle here. Then again, Ireland hasn’t made a habit of forming internment camps, turning them into child prostitutes, or exploiting them for cheap labor, so they’re ahead of…oh, just about everyone in their treatment of Asians. “…real name is the Stone of Eloquence. ‘Blarney’ just means deception without intended malice. Interestingly enough…” I find myself to the upper level of the castle, pushing through several crowds of white people with fanny packs and full site maps. So there I am, among the battlements. And the Stone’s in front of me and I just sort of sit there. Some of the fanny pack tribe make their way past me; the man and his wife(?) go about the entire ordeal. He holds her by the waist as she leans over backwards (the only way to kiss the Stone now, another one of the points made by the tour guides). She kisses the stone and comes up with a real shit-eating grin. I mean, it makes me just want to palm strike her nose into her brain. Her husband doesn’t want to, so they leave. Another group walks up to me. “I’m sorry, are you going to kiss the Stone? You want one of us to hold you?” My reaction is just second nature; I don’t mean to, but I sneer at the lady who asks me and she recoils, but doesn’t fully retreat. She expects an apology. “No.” That’s right; I don’t need anyone to hold me down to kiss the Stone because I don’t need to kiss it all. I came, I saw, and I conquered. No reason to stick around and worry about the superstition. Maybe I understand Paddy better from all this, but is it going to give me a better way to knock him down? The secret recipe for the suplex that will take him out? C’mon, slapnuts, you know the answer to that. Maybe this is like one of those shitty indy movies where I made this entire trip because something’s wrong with me. Except I never like the ending in those movies, so some more stuff will happen here. Like I’ll walk out, pushing angrily through them all. I’ll go back to the car and the airport and PRIME. And I’ll step in the ring and this will all fade; the survival instinct will take over like it always does. I’ll make him bleed for that national pr…no, it doesn’t matter how or why. I’ll win. So that’s that.
