The Body

26 August 2005

Date: 26/08/05 Well I heard there was a secret chordThat David played and it pleased the LordBut you don’t really care for music, do ya? I can’t fully remember what happened to me, I felt numb all over. For weeks I had entered a state of denial, claiming that the child wasn’t mine. Too stubborn in my ways to simply turn to Louise and apologize for all that had been wrong in our relationship. The arguing, the lies and the distrust. Now it felt as though my world had collapsed and at that point I hated myself. Hated myself for only being able to think how hard this was for me, I guess I’ve always been like that, not good at the big conversations, on the other end of the phone was a woman, who against all odds I loved. A woman who had just lost our child, through no fault of her own and would be feeling like hell. So why couldn’t I say anything of comfort to her? All I could think about was that maybe this was my fault, without the stress I had been putting on her, if I had just accepted the child. Maybe it would still be here. “John say something,” her words were pleading; the tears may as well have been seeping through the phone. I stood in silence for a few more moments, my mouth agape, I searched for the words but none came. I wanted to hang up, hang up and hide in a world of solace and alcohol, but I couldn’t be that man. Not this time. She needed me. “I’ll be round as soon as I can.” Well it goes like this:The fourth, the fifth, the minor fall and the major liftThe baffled king composing Hallelujah I didn’t drive, I didn’t think it would be the best cause of action really; my driving was erratic at the best of times. Instead I simply walked, leaves crunched and crumpled underneath my feet, a lazy wind blew right through me, instead of simply going round. I allowed myself to glance at the other people who had ventured out into the cold. There seemed to be no smiles, no laughter. It was as though for just one day, the entire world was in mourning. I tried to work out what I was going to say when I saw her. We hadn’t spoken properly in weeks, now I needed to be strong for both of us, if just for the next few days. I remembered back to the death of my brother, how she had been there for me, how I had been inconsolable, yet she stayed there. She took my shouting, accepted that I was just venting my grief and she was unfortunate enough to be there. She had been strong for me, now I needed to return the favor, because after all. I loved her. I stepped out into the road, not bothering to check for traffic. I want to point out, this wasn’t intentional, I was just too overcome, a thousand thoughts rushing through my head, figuring that I would hear a car coming. Apparently my theory didn’t really work out, as I turned wide-eyed to the sound of a screech, as I bounced off a windshield and the world around me faded to black. Hallelujah Hallelujah Hallelujah Hallelujah I could have died right then, ended my story. But a deep down burning desire forced me back, in years gone past I would have embraced death like a lost father, now though, I had something to do. It was not my time I had to be strong, I HAD to be strong. The light flooded into my eyes, taking me by surprise. I coughed and gasped frantically as oxygen found my lungs once more, a crowd up people parted as I slowly pulled myself to my feet clutching my side. I could feel the cracks and the breaks, the burning agony with every draw of breath. “Are you ok, I’m sorry you came out of no-where, are you ok?” The drivers frantic voice asked, probably more concerned about his no-claims bonus than my welfare. I turned to him, aware of various shards of glass in my body, I’d felt it before but I’d never really been unprepared for it. “No, I’m not ok. I’m pretty far from ok.” Well your faith was strong but you needed proofYou saw her bathing on the roofHer beauty and the moonlight overthrew ya. I had cleaned myself up as best as possible, the aesthetics were as good as they could be with a face like mine. Internally I was aching, but that hadn’t prevented me from reaching my destination, the only problem now was passing the threshold, I had been stood outside of her house for half an hour, trying to draw up the courage to enter, knowing deep down I would not be able to cope with the outpouring of emotion. Mine, not hers. I had always maintained that strength was the key to everything, if you could somehow prevent others from seeing your human weakness, then everything would work out right in the end. Yet I knew that if I went in there I would cry, I would see her face and the tears would just stream uncontrollably down my face. I wanted to run and wanted to hide, denial is a funny thing; I was convinced that if I just went back home and slept, tomorrow would be a brighter day, everything would have sorted itself out and I could smile again. Wishing doesn’t work like that though, it rarely works at all. The door to her apartment opened slowly, she stood there arms crossed across her stomach, holding herself. In that instant I felt sick, she wanted comfort and I had spent all this time wondering how to run. And She tied you to her kitchen chairAnd She broke your throne and she cut your hairAnd from your lips she drew the Hallelujah I stepped forwards towards the door, as Louise stood there silent, she looked bad. The stress of it all had got to her, naturally. Without a word, without acknowledgement I wrapped my arms around her and cuddled tightly. In my head I’d told myself not to get too close, that maybe the hug should be withdrawn, but in real life it doesn’t always work that way. As we pulled apart and looked into each others eyes, we knew that there were no words that could possibly mean as much as the hug, so we didn’t waste them and returned to the hug, silent and that was the way it would remain for a week. Us not talking, not daring to say anything for fear of hurting the other. I was looking at the coffin of my unborn child; silent tears streamed down my face as I leant down and picked up the coffin, holding it out tenderly in front of me. Even though its true weight was next to nothing, it felt like a case of lead. I was scared that I would drop it, I could feel my arms burning, but I did not relent, I couldn’t. I had been a terrible father, this was my last act to redeem myself, even if no one else saw it, I had to do this. With each step the coffin grew heavier, I could hear the tears and sobs of friends and family around us, most of them hadn’t even known about the pregnancy, yet they still came. Through all the years I thought I was alone, I wasn’t, I’d just locked them out. And I've seen your flag on the marble archAnd Love is not a victory marchIt's a cold and it's a broken Hallelujah A hand was placed on my shoulder, I’d stopped walking, tears were splashing off of the coffin, which was quickly accompanied by rain, the clouds that had overshadowed the whole procedure deemed themselves fit to rain upon me, I tilted my head backwards and allowed the rain to seep over my skin, in the past it had cleansed me. Closing my eyes for a few brief seconds, the pain dispersed. The weight of the coffin on my arms and mind had gone, for a brief moment there was peace. I feeling of warmth and safeness and maybe just maybe, everything was going to be ok. Everything would work out… “John…” I inhaled deeply and carried on walking until I reached the open grave. I couldn’t help but wonder what happened when you die? The struggling acceptance, that one day everything would end. The thoughts that cause a persons stomach to knot and turn, this is all we know, to think of something beyond…well it can be painful sometimes. Hallelujah Hallelujah Hallelujah Hallelujah We were in the far corner of the cemetery, underneath a large oak tree, which shielded us from most of the rain. As I handed over the coffin it felt as though I was letting the child go. I wanted to do something, but was powerless to, as I always the case. I turned to Louise, she had passed the tears stage, she was now in disbelief, her eyes were lifeless, as they gazed at the coffin, at the hole in the ground. I stepped back silently to where she was and took her hand in mine. She squeezed it reassuringly, much in the same way she had done on our wedding day. She was trying to let me know that everything would be ok. That we would somehow get through this stronger people. I felt so empty, so bleak however that I found it difficult to believe her. I wasn’t really listening to the vicar, my own thoughts taking over, as they naturally would, funerals are simply a time to grieve a time to say goodbye.
