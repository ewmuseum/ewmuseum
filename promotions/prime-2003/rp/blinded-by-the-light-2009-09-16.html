Blinded by the light

16 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16 September 2009 The lights overhead flashed by like glimpses of lightning on a Spring evening; barely seen streaks of white that were mere memory before they even registered on the retinas of the person who seemed to glide by below them. One one thousand … two one thousand … three one thousand … why don’t I hear the thunder? A heavy set EMT, beads of sweat running down his ruddy forehead, pushed the stretcher in front of him, slamming it through the outer doors to the hospital as his colleague checked on the patient who lay prone below them. “His pulse is very thready again Tom,” the female EMT stated without taking her eyes of the man below her. “Definitely going into shock.” “Nearly there now Sue," Tom replied as he wiped his head with one forearm. “Just keep him stable a bit longer.” Automatic doors opened on silent hinges before them, an electronic herald that proclaimed their arrival like a modern day trumpet chorus for a celebrity. With even more haste Tom barrelled the stretcher through the open doors, barely glancing at the small sign beside them that read ‘Emergency room’, swerving to a stop as he avoided … barely … running over a doctor. Hey, Daddy, can you keep the jeep a little more steady please, my tummy feels really poorly. “Watch yourself, doc,” Tom barked out, a New York accent thick in his voice. “You nearly became a statistic there!” Without looking up from a clip-chart that seemed to contain her whole focus the doctor, a tired looking brunette woman, indicated over to a bay to one side with her free hand. “Well I am in the right place for it anyway,” she said, her eyes still locked to the page in front of her as she scribbled some notes on it. “What have you got for me tonight then Tom?” “Blunt force trauma to the face. Contusions and possible broken nose – looks like an older injury redone, though. Impact to the upper head with suspected concussion.” Tom rhymed off as his partner, Sue, put the locks on the gurney. “Oh yeah and his eye is … well, take a look.” “Jesus what happened …” the doctor asked as she brought her gaze up to peer over the top of the clipboard. Her eye widened as she took in the figure in front of her. “… car crash?” No … mummy … daddy … they can’t be Grandfather, they can’t be gone … please say that they aren’t gone … “Nope, believe it or not it was a so called ‘sporting’ event, wrestling to be exact.” Tom smiled at his own joke. “Doc Catherine Kelly meet Jay Phoenix - though you will have to excuse him if he doesn’t get up to meet you” “Wrestling? You are telling me that wrestling did this to him? I have seen people walk out of airplane crashes who looked better than this … Phoenix, was it?” Placing the clipboard down on top of a nearby counter surface, the doctor walked over to Phoenix and gazed down at him. Flames … why is my name like the flames Grandfather, does it mean something? “Yeah, believe it or not that is his real name, too,” Tom replied, staring down at the battered man. “He was fine, we were told, right up until a few minutes after his match finished then – WHAM – he hit the dirt.” Reaching into the pocket of her long white coat Cathy pulled out a small pen like spotlight and, after checking that it was on by waving it against the wall a couple of times … shadows playing over the prone form of Phoenix and making shapes from right out of a nightmare … she bent over him, shining the light into one of his eyes – the one with the most damage - as she forced his eyelid open. “That isn’t right …” she muttered to herself as she stared into his red and opaque iris, totally filled with blood. “What is it?” Tom asked as he looked over her shoulder. “This was from a wrestling match?” Catherine questioned, glancing up at the two EMTs, as she started to lean closer, peering directly into the eye. “The damage here is as bad as I have ever seen – I can just about see past the swelling to the …” Noooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooo! “Noooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooooo!” With a speed that was beyond Catherine’s capacity to even see she suddenly found her wrist in a vice like grip, as a wrenching scream filled the small room; it took her a few seconds more to realise that she could no longer move her hand and that she could no longer control her unconscious patient. It took her only a moment more to react herself. “Sedate him!” She ordered. “Now!” The needle slipped into Phoenix’s arm, easily, and he released the grip on the doctor, his arm falling to his side as he head gently lolled to the right – one eye half open, staring into nothing, the other swollen closed. Sightless. * * * The darkness was all consuming. It wasn’t just that light was absent, no it was more like in this time and place even the idea of light was lost; light wasn’t missing - it had never actually been. The darkness was everything. Like a soft down filled quilt it wrapped me in it’s soft embrace and whispered sweet nothings to me, promises that were just out of my hearing but teased me with their dulcet tones of pleasures unknown. The darkness was all encompassing. Its tendrils reached out and embraced me; it’s cold fingers caressing me gently with a lover’s touch. A living and breathing thing, a sentient entity the darkness made me it’s own. The darkness spoke to me. It told me secrets and I leant forwards to hear it. The words were soft, sibilant, and beautiful. Enlightening. At the core of my mind, at the centre of my being, it spoke to me. And I listened. The darkness taught me. At the periphery of knowledge and the edge of understanding I suddenly knew. Everything that I had wondered, everything that I had questioned – the answers were here. I just had to hear them. To take them. To make them my own. It was all there and, finally, I knew. The darkness … * * * “…………………………………………………………………jay………” “……jay…………………………………………………………………” “……………………………………jay…………………………………” “... Grandfather?” “Jay?” “Rick?” “It’s me. Wake up.” “….what?” “C’mon, Jay, it’s time to wake up now.” “Rick?” “Thank God, Jay. I was so worried about you!” “…. what time is it?” “Two o’clock. You’ve been out of it for hours.” “Turn the light on, Rick ...” “… what?” “C’mon, two in the morning or not, I want to see you.” “Doctor … Doctor!” “… Rick?” “Don’t worry, Jay, don’t worry – DOCTOR!!!!!!!!!!!!!” “Rick, what is it?” “It’s two in the afternoon, Jay – it’s daylight … it’s LIGHT!” “…………… Rick?” “ ……………………………………………………………… rick ……”
