A Third of What I Wanted

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 30ish This isn't something that's supposed to happen to you. You are smart. You travel with the crowd, in public places, and you're so unmistakable that it's hard to blend in and go unnoticed. You leave arenas in the company of others, never having to wonder about those who lurk in shadows thanks to security and your 'pack' mentality. You always have your keys in hand and you always check the backseat before getting into a car. Safety first. You are always careful about maintaining privacy; itineraries being leaked, personal information being compromised. You don't click strange links in emails. You can spot a spoof email or a phishing attempt from a mile away. Stupid chains and forwards get shipped right off to the virtual trash bin, then emptied into a vortex, never to be seen again. You don't get duped by scams. You always make sure to look over your shoulder. The public eye is always watching and it never, ever shuts. While paranoia doesn't rule your day, it doesn't hurt to think like a serial killer every once in awhile. But despite your best laid plans and all your good intentions, this is an unknown variable you could not have prepared yourself for. Your newly-won Unified title makes it necessary to travel to competing companies, where known friends and acquaintances are few and far between. The scenery may change from week to week, but the environment is generally the same until the outside world allows new species to enter, disrupting the natural balance. You remember your win against Jonathan Rhine. You remember not wanting to stick around, really, instead changing quickly, grabbing what little you brought with you and thinking about the nice shower you'll grab at the hotel. You remember that, for a Marine base, security was surprisingly not as prevalent on your way out as it was on your way in, but you know now that was due to your Unknown Variable with the lightning-quick kick and sharp-smelling rag that was forced against your nose and mouth. You remember the vortex, and how your world spun into it. And when you woke up, through the haze and the pain and the tightness around your limbs, you saw yourself staring back at you with a hollow grin and hungry eyes. The canary has been swallowed.
