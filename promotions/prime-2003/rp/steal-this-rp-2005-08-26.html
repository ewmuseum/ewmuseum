Which is why you’re about to sit through it, ya dumb bastard. Emptiness. Blackness. Nothingness. Coexisting alongside the world Farwell’s mind created for him, the emptiness faded in and out rhythmically, akin to the beating of a heart. One second there was nothing, and the entire world lay before him in the next. But the heart soon began to pump slower, and the two worlds fast became one. In between nothing and everything, he stood. He stood, because he was afraid to take a step in either direction, for fear of losing the other. He stood, because he didn’t know if he could walk, even if he wanted. He stood, because that was all he could do. And so James Farwell stood between light and dark, one foot in each world. But the decision was made for him, as he looked into the light and saw the only world he ever wanted to know. He took one step. Then two. And uncountable steps followed, until he didn’t know how far he had walked. His eyes scanned the lifeless world--everything he knew was there; streets, buildings, people; but they all seemed so meaningless to him now. This world wasn’t his. His heart began to pump faster as he turned to look for that place of comfort--the world between two worlds. As his heart raced in his chest, and his lungs stretched until they hurt, James ran faster and faster to try and escape the world that now meant nothing to him. Fall. The ground was pulled out from beneath him, but before he could brace for the inevitable impact of the descent, he found himself standing in a very familiar place, surrounded by screaming fans. But there was something wrong about this place, just like the world before it. Everyone was faceless. The lines in this world had been blurred, and all that remained was an out of focus picture of something that used to be. Something that never was. “And speaking of people we hate…hi, Angie.” The words surprised him, but what had to be even more surprising was that they were his own. His heart still pounded in his chest, and his lungs still felt ready to burst, but now it all made sense.
