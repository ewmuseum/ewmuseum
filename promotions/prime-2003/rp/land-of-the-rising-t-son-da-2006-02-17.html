Land Of The Rising (t)Son(da)

17 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/17/06 Things are falling apart in Tsondaland in this, the week before the Dual Halo. With every other wrestler and their dog preparing for a battle on a scale that Peter Jackson would spooge over, I’m mired in self-pity and inadequacy. I flip through the Rolodex, trying to figure out where there is left to look for a capable manager, but in my lukewarm heart, there is little more than apathy. In some honest corner of my mind, too, I am reduced to the fact that this charade may come to nothing. And if this plan is for naught, I’ll be a laughingstock again, among my friends and every fan who dares to raise their voice against me. Names of wrestlers long retired or broken in the knees fly past with every flick of my hand without even a glint of possibility. If I weren’t so melancholy, I might even laugh at these autobiographical snippets, tucked neatly onto small index cards. I look at the phone, which has been used and abused in my unproductive efforts to find a sidekick. Danny has the mouth of the South. Facey has…his own pseudo-schizophrenic regard. The time is ripe for me to rise up but I, admittedly, can’t do it myself. The story of my PRIME career thus far has been “almost there.” Almost beat Wolfenden. Almost won the Alias title. Almost beat Deville. Hell, I could beat the guy in a tournament a thousand miles away, what’s the jinx of the PRIME ring (or more specifically, an Irish bar, but under the PRIME banner, therefore we’ll allow it into the Chandler Tsonda Theory). It’s me. Pausing for a moment, I wonder if I’ve actually convinced myself out of winning; convinced myself that this other person will be the panacea. Then again, I’ve also never won against Facey and since we’re pals now, my record against him basically disappears, making me a sizzling 5-1. Oh, if only elevation were so easy. Back to flipping, I spy more ruined careers and questions of “what if he hadn’t fallen off after PWC?” After all, my wrestling knowledge goes no farther than PWC co-workers, who I notoriously slandered after the fact, and some of Danny’s Hollywood connections. And let’s be honest: no actor goes out of their way to go into wrestling. Look at what it did to David Arquette’s career…now laugh at the mere notion of Eight Legged Freaks or Ready to Rumble or Scream or…sorry. I pick up the phone, as if the penetrating dial tone will induce my brain into seizures of inspiration. Nothing comes, so I just sit there, inert. Why can’t you get it together before the Halo, Chandler? I’ve been afraid my whole career to truly let go in the ring. There has to be that phantom of control, even in moments without composure. Suddenly, the woman of my daydreams comes flying out of the cloudy fog of confusion in my head. Her blond hair caresses the tops of her shoulders, if she lets it lie unkempt. Her face is squarish and perfectly proportioned. In fact, she is relative perfection–I’ve never met her. Nonetheless, I pick up the phone to call the answer to all my problems. Before I finish inputting the numbers, I remember that the voice that will pick up won’t be hers. There will be obstacles to work around, but clearly, there is only one woman for the job. * * * * The hotel lobby is crowded with other Americans, speaking in their loud, elementary tones not to, but at the Japanese hotel workers. “We have a ROOM. ROOM?” I’m waiting downstairs for Facey, while Fergs has been available only by phone most of the week. The American guy is trying to explain the highly complex concept of “room” by making a box with his hands in the air, while the Japanese concierge is just clicking away at the computer. I pull out my Sidekick (the trustiest electronic known to man) and hit Facey’s number on the address book. First, I’m given an irritating busy message. I call back and get greeted by Sean Paul’s “We Be Burnin,” since he’s one of those awful people who pays 2.99 to have a song play instead of a regular old ring on my phone. With horror on my mind, I find myself singing along. Of course, Face picks up at this exact instant. “Not concernin’ what nobody wan…Face?” “Dude…gay,” he deadpans, from the comfort of his hotel room. I can take and give the constant ribbing, but Facey can also be genuinely irritating. I know, it’s hard to believe. “What do you want?” “We’re supposed to talk strategy, remember? Or at least that was the plan before you fucking ditched us at the airport,” I state with considerable venom. “Does your cannabis-riddled mind recall that?” “Oh right, yeah, tell Danny I’ll do it later. I just got to my hotel room and this was gonna be filler in my RP so I didn’t include it. In fact, you’re breaking the laws of time and physics as we speak by talking to me when I should be off killing a mouse violently.” There is a momentary, deadly pause. “Wait…what did I just say?” “Is getting high and listening to Popozao on infinite repeat really more important to you than the Dual Halo?” The subsequent click of his cell phone answers the question. Annoyed, I hit up Danny’s number on the Sidekick, cursing my actor friend for bringing aboard our own little CKY member. For the second time in four minutes, I get no answer from one of my teammates’ personal phone. I try again and get a big helping of answering machine. I consider giving up, but then decide one final attempt is in order. On the third try, Danny picks up exasperatedly. “What do you want, Chandler? I’m taking to the head honcho at Urethra Warfare; this isn’t some small-time joint like Sony. These guys are big dogs-” “I’ll leave to your precious fucking Hollywood life, then.” “Sorry,” he replies, considerably calmer. “I’m a little tightly wound from all this business.” “Have you give any thought to the Halo? I mean, I know Facey can’t understand logical cause-and-effect, but surely you can’t deny that an A-List victory in the Halo would cement us guys as the future of the fed.” I try to add a sense of urgency to my voice, wanting Fergs to help intervene with Facey and make the kid listen to some sort of logic about preparing for the match itself. “It’s on my list of priorities, but right now I’m desperately trying to get Gary Oldman to play the villain in Double Down. I mean, Gary FUCKING Oldman, dude. He’s one hundred percent pure insane with a kickass British accent.” I sigh as I hear the excitement in Ferguson’s voice over the prospect of getting a top-notch villain in his movie. “Fine, dude, if you want I’ll call Facey later about planning the match.” And with that, I’m the only A-List member who officially gives a damn. In a moment’s time, I’m furious with myself and both of them. Don’t they understand that we have the best odds of anyone of benefiting from this? It’s like Make-A-Wish has come to you and you don’t even have to be a cancerous Kyle Lamen to have your utmost desires fulfilled. Cross from my attempts at dealing with my allies, I step outside the hotel, light a trusty cigarette, and nobly inhale. There is a pronounced hustle and bustle to the street outside, despite the pervading Asian tranquility that is expected. I know better, though, than to expect a peaceful stay in Tokyo. * * * * I stepped through the door and forgot I was in Japan. The office was immaculate, almost symbolically undefiled by commercial knick-knacks, as I had heard Japanese wrestling was. The man behind the desk was unequivocally un-Japanese, but a plan Englishman with large, rounded glasses. Terence Fowler was the face of Ultimate Japan Wrestling, a medium-sized and well-received federation; I was learned of this thanks to several of their press releases that my agent Gil Lavenstein had given me to read on the plane ride over. “Welcome, Chandler. We’re very happy to have you in here for contract negotiations. We know that you’ll speak well to our audience.” In America, I would have taken that to mean that they need an Asian face for the other Asian faces, but I thought that perhaps there was an inherent difference to the way business was run in Japan. “Your in-ring potential is mind-blowing, if you don’t mind me saying.” Fuck, I thought, why the hell would I mind him showering me with compliments? Still, the moment was here where, quickly, the other shoe was going to drop. “However, we do have some slight concerns about the persona that you’ve played in the past.” Ooh goody, we were going to talk kayfabe. “How do you mean, Mr. Fowler?’ I was baiting him with my best polite, cooperative Asian impression. Of course, I knew what he was going to say: the same thing that American federations were sold on was going to be my downfall in trying to test the Japanese market. “Well, we’re not sure that your…on-air disrespect of other wrestlers and the whole sort of process will fly with the Japanese fans. Here, there’s a “holier than thou” approach to everything that takes place in the ring. I know, it’s silly.” Clearly, he didn’t know it was silly, but was, in fact, trying to draw attention away from the fact that he was looking to change my whole act. “With all due respect, sir, I’d love to wrestle here in Japan. However, that’s not some on-air personality. That’s me, through and through, and if you can’t give me a contract that won’t stifle that…I’m not certain how much farther this discussion can continue.” My voice was flat and uncaring, despite the fact that I desperately wanted to get back into a wrestling ring. PWC had been a good nine months ago and I was sick and tired of “training” in shitty gyms around the U.S. “Chandler, I’m not quite sure how to put this: the Japanese hold wrestling to be almost sacred. No, they’re not obedient little lapdogs who can’t imagine what a run-in looks like, but they treat it like a sport,” he calmly declared. His eyebrows jumped up and down every time he took a deep breath and I realized that he looked like the owl who asks “how many licks to the center of a Tootsie Pop”. He licked his lips and continued, “and you treat it like garbage.” I had no answer and he knew this, so he continued on, making his best play at getting me to join him and UJW. “You are backhanded and devilishly strategic. These are not values that the common Japanese fan is going to value highly,” he said matter-of-factly. I knew he was right, but still I snorted, pouting because I knew the ultimatum that was about to be shoved in my face. “Moreover, your in-ring style, at the moment, is crude and unpolished.” I wasn’t going to sit through any more of this. “That’s horseshit and you know it. If nothing else, I was one of the most exciting wrestlers that Pro Wrestling Circuit had to offer. I’m up there with the Devilles, Wolfendens, and Penances of the world.” Name-dropping wasn’t going to help me any, but saying the words convinced me that I actually was good enough to hang with those types. Fowler completely no-sold my offensive. “We both know that was almost a year ago, Chandler,” he spat sarcastically. Whether he knew that my in-ring skills had actually deteriorated some in the months off was yet to be seen. “All we’ve really seen is a bunch of high-flying, poorly executed crap and consistent cheating. If we wanted that, we could literally sign an ape.” Well, to say that I was getting pissed was an understatement. This ape was about to spring across the table and strangle the little fucker with his tacky red-and-white striped necktie. “So you’re telling me that Japanese wrestling is completely devoid of any aerial offense?” I was just testing him now, seeing if perhaps this had been a bluff to take me down a salary peg or two. “I don’t think even you believe that.” We would see if he had been full of hot air. “I couldn’t be as stupid as to think that.” I had won. “But what you do is Americanized high-flying. All flash and not a hint of style nor talent. Like I said, an ape could do the same.” Or not. It was no bluff. He was completely set on whitewashing my persona, rebuilding me in the ring, and probably passing me of as a son of Japan, instead of a proud Vietnamese-American. Even in my desperate state, I had decided that certain things were not negotiable. “I’m glad we had this little meeting, Mr. Fowler. If at any point in the future you wish to continue negotiations, you know where to contact me. I also invite you, very cordially, to go order the California cyanide wrap at the nearest Tofu stand, you pretentious fuckwad.” In retrospect, that was a mistake. Fowler’s guards were big and forceful and they dumped me behind the large UJW office. They sent fierce kicks into my arms and chest, but were mindful not to ignore my head. Cursing the fucks for messing up my fantastic clothes and hair, I spit blood at them as they walked back into the building. Perhaps Fowler had been right about my in-ring style. If it didn’t translate into even being able to defend myself against those slow-as-molasses oafs, what was it worth? Cheating wouldn’t do forever, although it would do nicely in a pinch. Without hesitance, I decided that, to show up the Fowlers of the world (and I had encountered many since PWC had closed, almost everyone trying to overhaul my in-ring idiosyncrasies), there was only one thing to do. I would get back into the shitty little gyms where I had been training and I would make myself an undeniable in-ring talent. I would be able to rely completely on an advanced aerial attack or on swift and hard-to-predict evasion. What’s more, I would add an element of countering, as any native Japanese wrestler would, so that even against two ogres like Fowler’s, I would never be outmatched. The first thing I showed the people at PRIME? My newly acquired in-ring skills. It’s rumored that after watching from afar just five minutes of my tryout, a set with Juan Santos, Karina Wolfenden had uttered, “well, at least he’s not a wreck in the ring anymore.” And it was true; I was a whole new entity inside the squared circle. * * * * Tired from walking around the crowded business district, I’m sitting in my room watching CNN. It’s mere ambient noise, but it’s a little touch of home (America, that is; my only touch of Vietnam is the pocket-sized flag that I’ve tucked into the pocket of my ring attire for Sunday night. What can I say? I’m just a tad superstitious and when it comes to the king of clusterfuck matches, it can’t hurt to have a little luck on my side). I’m over my previous anger with my A-List allies; in fact, despite what you see on TV, they know that I’m invaluable to the group. I glance to my right and see the Five-Star belt, an official perk of being in the A-List. It’s an unfamiliar security, this gleaming trophy, but I’m glad to be holding it for the day. It’s been a long time since I’ve tasted gold; too long, to sound like a clichéd action movie. Unlike my adventures in Japan over a year ago, this time I’m ready for everything that comes my way. I’m more ready, in-ring, for a match then I’ve ever been in my life. Not that I’m the guy who needs the ego boost, but I’m also shredding motherfuckers like nobody’s business in GTT5; in fact, I caused Angelo Deville to go into hiding…maybe. But, as you’ll here all too painfully put in sixty other RP’s, this weekend is only about the most masochistic match known to man: the Dual Halo. And as the lone Asian (when Xin Xin Xiong says or does something that makes me identify the letters X3 with him rather then the next installment of the X-Men movies, then maybe I’ll give him any semblance of credit), I’ve got to hold it down for my home continent. Plus, there’s also the fact of my new manager. From what she’s told me, she’ll officially be in my corner for the Dual Halo and even the most Neanderthal of wrestling fans (and thus, the Japanese) will recognize her face. But I don’t want to spoil the surprise. I have no doubt she’ll leave her “mark” on the Pay-Per-View, but first and foremost, it’s time to show Mr. Fowler how much things can change in a year. I’m talking about winning the Dual Halo. Didn’t wanna risk being subtle in the ol’ PPV RP, ya know. And I’m off.
