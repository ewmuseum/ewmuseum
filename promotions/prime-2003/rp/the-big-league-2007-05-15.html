The Big League

15 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15th May 2007 Introduction Memory Lane… Richard Leonard had always been driven, despite his lack of education. As a child, his parents died early and with the threat of being inducted into the country’s god-awful foster system hanging over his head, he decided to tough it out on the streets instead. Truth be told, it wasn’t much of a conscious decision, not at that age, it was more instinctive. He survived on the streets for years, before making the decision. Richard knew that living off scraps and sleeping in alleyways wasn’t much of a future and the polar notions of right and wrong meat nothing in the matter of survival. If he wanted out, if he wanted to live a little, then there was only one real option available to him. And that option was crime. A person could align himself with all of the right people if they don’t have a pesky little thing called a conscience to hold them back, and align himself he did. Within weeks of delivering good for a local gang-banger, he officially became hired muscle when one of Richard’s deliveries failed to weigh in. Richard got creative and hurt him just enough to deliver a message, but stopped just before it posed a serious problem to his employer. Things grew from there and seven years later he’d advance into what he considered the big leagues; he’d made all the way up to father of organised crime throughout all of the East Coast. He’d made it to the employment of Cornelius Stakiri and now only the sky was the limit. Or so he thought. On The Lamb Somewhere in New York “They told me I had to forget about you,” Aeryn told him, her face contorted with thoughtful bemusement, “they said that you weren’t important and you’d only distract me.” “I suspected as much,” Jonathan Winters replied with a sigh and shifted uncomfortably upon the top of the emptied upturned oil drum that currently doubled as a chair. “What kind of questions did they ask you? Was there anything specific?” “Not that I can remember,” she admitted, “but my memories of the interrogations are…distorted. They injected me with something and it made my head spin; I felt dizzy and nauseous and then things just seem to fade away into darkness. I remember asking them what they were injecting me with, but they wouldn’t tell me. They said it was none of my concern.” Winters shook his head. “Of course they did.” It had been three days since Winters had broken Aeryn free of their cruel employers grasp and the two of them were still trying to come to terms with their situation and each other. Winters couldn’t help but glare helplessly at Aeryn’s undeniable goddess-like features; her long black hair, with slightest of curl, her deep green eyes, her fair and spotless skin and her thick, full lips. More than once, she’d caught up mid-stare and Winters, a little embarrassed, was force to turn away. Six months ago, she’d agreed to work undercover for Stakiri, infiltrating a business rival with intentions of industrial sabotage and information ciphering. Turned out this business rival of Stakiri’s were more resourceful than he ever gave them credit for and also they liked to play even dirtier than he ever expected. Aeryn was captured early in her task and using their full scientific resources, they spent months reprogramming her mind. Since she came back, what Winters and Aeryn had was special. Except now, he wasn’t even sure it was real. Sure, he loved her, but then he always had. The question was, did Aeryn ever reciprocate his feelings at all or was that too just part of her programming? But at that moment in time, such a question was the last thing on his mind. “I don’t like this place,” Aeryn said, her eyes scanning the room the room intently. Winters nodded in agreement, following her gaze. “I’d been worried if you did.” She smiled and so did he. “It was the best I could do under the circumstances.” The room was all but bare and partially demolished. In lay at the heart of the suburban industrial area and was surrounded by old and decrepit warehouses filled with equally old and decrepit businesses, most of which were not legal. For as long as Winters could remember, which admittedly was only the span of the previous two years, the place had been empty and very people even knew of its existence. Still, that didn’t make the place safe, but then he never intended to stay there long. “Aeryn,” he began, pushing himself up from the oil drum and making his way over to her. He knelt beside and looked her deep in the eyes. “I’ll be going out of town for a few days on business…” “Wrestling?” Aeryn interrupted. Winters nodded, with a smile. “Yes…” “I don’t understand,” she interrupted again, “aren’t you making yourself an open target? They’re already looking for us, but you out in the public eye, on television of all things…” “I know, I know,” Winters told her, “but don’t worry, it’s all part of the plan.” Driven Stakiri Corp Building New York City “Do you know why you’re here?” Stakiri asked, nestling into his luxurious black leather chair. Richard Leonard stood at the door of Stakiri’s office, a perplexed look spread right across his face. He didn’t expect many formalities or small talk, but he had expected to be at least eased into it. “No, sir,” Richard lied. Stakiri leant forward and rested his elbows upon his desktop. “Come, come, now Mr. Leonard. I can tell you’re lying to me.” Richard broke out an uneasy smile, but did not allow it to linger. “I did hear something about having work to throw my way.” Stakiri smiled, but seemed to ignore the answer. “How long have you been working for me now, Mr. Leonard? About a year?” “Eight months, sir,” he answered. “Two months within the inner circle.” Stakiri nodded. He liked what he heard. “Your progress within my employment thus far has been impressive, Mr Leonard. You really should be proud of yourself. The question is, are you ready?” Richard raised a speculative eyebrow. “Ready for what, sir?” Stakiri produced a toothy grin and leant back in his chair. “The big time, Mr Leonard, the big time.” Satisfaction JFK Airport New York Winters waded through the throng of people desperately scurrying through the busy New York airport, with an idle smile wistfully spread across his face. It’d been a helluva few days, but he was glad there was at least some closure now. Ultraviolence had been and gone and although the encounter with Pierce Lavelle had taken it out of him, the taste of victory was very sweet indeed and it was much needed. So-called critics claimed that to be the end of the rivalry, but Winters saw it in Lavelle’s eyes soon after the refs hand pounded the mat for the third time -- it wasn’t over, not if Lavelle had anything to say about it. Winters pushed the notion from his head. The problem of Pierce Lavelle had been dealt with, albeit for the time being, now there were more pressing matters at hand and the problem of Brandon Youngblood just around the corner. As soon as he’d set foot off the plane, it felt to him as though all eyes were on him. It wasn’t an ego issue, far from it, but he was a much sought after man, at least to some circles. However, before he’d even set foot inside the terminal, he’d composed himself. He needed focus to spot what the average person would not. Entering the main lobby area, he already a glimpse of at least two poorly disguised trails hot on his heels and more waiting around at the various restaurants and concessionaries. It took all he had to hide a grin, but he managed to hold it together enough to make sure that he lost none of them and all the eyes who were there to see him, saw him. Approaching the exit, the heat surrounding him began to grow intense and he half expected a few of the impatient amongst the tracking detail to make a move before he stepped out onto the sidewalk. Things were about to get interesting. Observation Somewhere close to JFK Airport New York “What the fuck is he doing?” Richard muttered, glaring into the small rectangular monitor. Larry swivelled around in his chair next to him, with a half-eaten jelly doughnut sticking out of his mouth. “What is it?” Richard allowed him a brief sideward glance. He was already nervous and the smell of those damn things was making his nauseous. He ignored the compulsion hurl and tapped the monitor pointedly. “Clarkson. He’s following the target too closely. My grandmother could spot him a mile away.” “Has the target noticed him yet?” Larry questioned, leaning closer for a better look. “He’s shown no signs,” Richard admitted, “but that don’t mean much.” “Huh?” “Larry, Winters was Stakiri’s top guy for more than a year and he knows we’re looking for him,” Richard explained, fixing his eyes back on the monitor. “You can’t tell me he didn’t expect us to be waiting for him here.” Larry chuckled, spitting out doughnut crumbs all over his keyboard. “I think you’re giving him a little too much credit, don’t you think?” Richard raised a speculative eyebrow. “How do you mean?” “I mean, the guy was on Television staring on a wrestling Pay Per View event two days ago,” Larry began. “That was pretty dumb move for a smart guy don’t ya think?” “Perhaps,” Richard replied. “Get ready to move, he’s coming out.” Hideout Somewhere in New York Richard cut through the darkened hallway soundlessly, his night vision goggles allowing him to see all around. He pressed himself up against a decayed plasterboard wall and brought his assault rifle up close to his chest. The weapon was a little more powerful than those he used whilst coming up in the business, but he found them just as useful, if not more. Outside the airport, Winters had simply hailed a cab and taken them to a rather deserted location, just outside of the city. As Larry said at the time, he wasn’t the sharpest knife in the drawer, but Richard didn’t believe that for a second. There was something wrong, but Richard couldn’t quite put his finger on it. Richard’s men soundlessly encroached upon his position and then ventured forward just as soon as he’d given them the okay that it was safe. And that’s when he noticed there were only three of them. “Where are the other two?” He muttered to the man at the back of the pack. The soldier glared at him questioningly and then back down the stairs behind him. “They were right behind--” BOOM! Richard’s eyes widened. “A gunshot?” The solider moved off without thought, before Richard could even compose himself. “Wait,” he muttered, too late. He waited for a second, but things suddenly became eerily quite. This task had been a fuck up from the start and they’d been played. The biggest kick in the pants was, Richard had realised it from the get go. He sighed. He’d rather go in there and meet his maker, than go back and tell Stakiri of any of this. Setting off around the corner, he saw the first of the bodies. From his position he couldn’t tell whether he was alive or dead and didn’t even bother to check. He glided into the room and noted how it expanded out before him. The other two bodies were strewn across the floor, their weapons cast aside and their limbs disjointed. “Fuck…” Richard heard something stir behind him and did not hesitate to whirl around, his rifle outstretched almost ready for use. But it was already too late. The weapon was propelled out of his hands with the greatest of ease and Richard closed his eyes waiting for the pain to arrive. After a couple of seconds, he realised his assumption was wrong. He opened his eyes to see Jonathan Winters standing there, with a broad grin etched into his face. “So you’re the guy Stakiri sends after me?” His fact contorts. “After me?” Richard backed away a little. “Y-Yes.” Winters patted him on the shoulder and produced a broad grin. “Well, son. That means you’re in the big leagues now!” Richard found it hard to get himself worked up about it. “I need you to convey a message to your employer…” To Be Continued…
