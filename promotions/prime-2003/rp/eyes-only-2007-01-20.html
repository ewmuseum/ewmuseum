Date: 1/20/07 (repost) “Minister Shen? General Chang is here to see you.” “Ah. Please send him in.” Seconds later, the door to the office of the Minister of Propaganda opened and in walked the imposing figure of General Chang. Although in his sixties, Chang’s physique looked like that of a far younger man, solid, without an ounce of extra weight or weakness. However, the dark creases under his eyes betrayed the decades of service for his country, the People’s Republic of China. The minister smiled at him like an old friend, although the two men had never met. Shen wore a dark tailored suit and he wore it well. To some, the forty-something politician might appear soft, decadent. But even with that friendly smile and expensive clothing, the eyes told the truth. Chang noticed them. They were much like his own, revealing a soul willing to do whatever it takes to get the job done. One difference bothered the general. For Chang, the job was military service, direct and to the point. But what is the job of the Ministry of Propaganda except lies and half-truths? It worked in shadows far darker than that of even the most secretive black ops. The worst they could do is kill you. Propaganda made you kill yourself. “Please, have a seat, General. Care for a drink?” “No, thank you. I am on duty.” Chang watched as Shen went to a cabinet and took out a bottle of brandy and a short glass. “Ah, yes. Duty.” Shen said that word like it was some quaint bit of folklore, like ghosts or fairies. He filled the glass about halfway before returning the bottle to the cabinet. He sat down behind his desk and casually slid a manila folder toward the general. “Tell me about Project Terra Cotta, general.” Only a slight rise of one eyebrow betrayed Chang’s astonishment. Project Terra Cotta was more than just a state secret. For all intents and purposes, it did not exist. Documents weren’t hidden, there were none at all to begin with. Except… there was the history of the project in that folder. Any attempt at denial would be ludicrous. While Chang was surprised at Shen’s knowledge of the project, he realized if anyone was to discover it, he would be the one. “Thirty years ago, the military, with the full blessing of the party, began a project to create the perfect soldier.” “And you dubbed it Terra Cotta, after the clay soldiers found in ancient tombs. How poetic.” Shen looked at Chang like he was patronizing a child. The general resisted the urge to kill the man right there and instead continued. “The decision was made to begin with children as young as possible. Some were collected from orphanages. Most came from families with unwanted daughters, wishing for a son amongst their allotted children. As a result the majority of the subjects were female. All the children were given training.” “Military training?” “Far beyond that, minister. Every waking moment was spent in the instruction of martial arts, weaponry, espionage, languages, explosives, disguise… anything to create the ultimate operative. Defectors taught them about their own countries and cultures until the subjects knew them as well as second homes. Performers from the Peking Opera trained them in acrobatics until they had the balance of cats. Their fighting skills eclipsed those of Olympic gold medalists before they reached puberty. They became living weapons.” “Now, general,” said the minister. “I believe you are leaving something out. The… supplements, shall we say.” “From the beginning, the children were subjected to drugs to further enhance their capabilities. Regular dosing with chemicals designed for the increase in their physical and mental potential was given through their growth.” “And what chemicals were these?” “It varied. Some were created specifically for this project. The exact effects were unknown. This was as much an experiment to their effectiveness as it was anything.” “Didn’t you were about side effects?” “There were some. And even some deaths.” “And did this not deter you?” “We had more children waiting. And no one to miss the ones that were lost.” Shen smiled in dark approval. This did not please the general in the least. “Understand this, minister,” Chang stated sharply. “I do not view those subjects as mere lab rats to be discarded at will. They were soldiers who gave their lives just as a grown man that was felled by a bullet. My regret is not the sacrifice of their lives, but the fact no one can be made aware of that sacrifice.” “Oh, I understand, Chang,” chuckled Shen. “But let’s not dwell on the failures. I’m interested in a success.” The minister removed a photo from the file and slid it to the general. The face of a beautiful young woman looked up at the old soldier. “Chang, tell me about… Sun Tzu.” ~~~~~~~~~~ “Chang, tell me about… Sun Tzu.” Those words from Minister Shen in his office still echoed in the ears of General Chang as the state car traveled through the narrow roads of this industrial part of Beijing. The early morning mist gave square, blocky building and angular hulks of machinery an almost otherworldly appearance. Not unlike pictures in a very bleak children’s book. Chang allowed himself these moments of guarded whimsy. After all, were not some of China’s greatest warriors poets and philosophers as well? Warriors such as the legendary Sun Tzu. <i<“Sun Tzu was from one of the more successful classes.” “Ah… success after so many failures. Much like the breeding of the perfect dog to hunt weasels.” Chang ignored Shen’s analogy, but would not forget it. For the time, he continued with his statement. “Initially, the children were not given names. We wished to give them a sense of being a single unit. This would be the preferred way of thinking as both a soldier in a military squad as well as a member of our collective people. In time, we bestowed code-names upon them based on legendary figures throughout history and mythology. The idea behind the practice was to bestow a sense of pride and patriotism in the children while at the same time giving them an ideal to aspire to.” “And how did our little Sun Tzu take to this?” Shen ran his thumb along the rim of his glass as he spoke, seeming almost distracted. But Chang knew he was dissecting every word he spoke. “Quite well, minister. She has never lacked in drive or confidence. Her performance during her training was at the top of her class. Sun Tzu’s loyalty to the state can be described as no less than zealous.” “I believe there was an unspoken ‘however’ there, general,” said Shen with a wry smile. Chang was impressed. Either Shen could read even an old warhorse such as himself like a book or his intelligence went beyond simply knowledge of Terra Cotta’s existence and well into its inner workings. Of course, what else could be expected from the Minister of Propaganda? For not the first time in this meeting, Chang wondered what exactly his purpose was here. And not for the first time he felt he would not like that answer. “However,” Chang replied, “Sun Tzu displayed a definite loner streak throughout her training. In fact, after her adolescence, certain… liaisons she had with her classmates proved disruptive to team unity.” “Liaisons?” Shen’s eyes twinkled with a glimmer that Chang failed to find amusing. His own eyes grew small and cold. “My purpose here is as a commanding officer, not as a gossip or pornographer, minister.” “Of course, general. Do continue. Tell me of her skills” “Her combat skills are extraordinary. Sun Tzu has excelled in combining her acrobatic training with martial arts, creating a hybrid that even the most seasoned fighters find difficult to adapt to. A double agent from British intelligence instructed her in English from an early age. Her fluency is perfect. In spite of possessing a strong national pride, Sun Tzu has not deterred from studying the United States. One would almost think her a native, if not for her overt hatred for the Americans.” “Yes. Most excellent, Chang. And what about the supplements? Any side effects with our girl?” “Physically, the treatments worked to perfection. Her strength is that of a man far larger than she is. Sun Tzu’s recuperative abilities are also enhanced. I have seen her back on her feet in days after injuries that would out a normal person out of commission for weeks.” Shen gave him that cryptic look again. “However,” Chang continued, “Sun Tzu does seem to have certain psychological issues that can perhaps be trace to the enhancements.” “Fascinating. Do tell.” How Chang loathed this man. “On more than a few occasions, Sun Tzu has lost her temper and self-control, minister. Primarily in hand-to-hand combat. There have been times when she has become almost feral, causing serious harm to her classmates, including some she considered close comrades. Thought was given to terminating her, but then the incidents stop. Still, there remain times where her manner seems not like that of a disciplined soldier but that of a cat stalking its prey.” “So dramatic,” chuckled Shen. “And from an old soldier like you! Perhaps your chemicals were the cause of her… indiscretions? A cat in heat, to continue with your simile.” “That has been theorized, minister, as well as the cause for her preference for solitary duty. Now keep in mind, none of this behavior has been a factor in her performance since reaching adulthood. Sun Tzu’s missions have been carried out without flaw.” “Oh, I know, Chang. But it is for all her qualities that I find Sun Tzu perfect for what I have in mind. Her skill, her knowledge, her strength, her passion, her independence, her…” Shen’s voice trailed off as he slowly stroked the face on photograph of the beautiful young woman with a single finger. “Perfect… in so many ways…” ~~~~~~~~~ “Perfect… in so many ways…” General Chang still found himself disturbed by those words of Shen, the Minister of Propaganda. He thought of how desolate this part of the city was. Plenty of places to dispose of Shen’s body. The driver was a military man, he would follow the lead of the general. But no, the minister was too well connected. Questions would be asked, ones that would quickly lead back to the general. And his plan did hold some interest to Chang. They stopped in front of an old brick building with a battered sign declaring it a meat packing plant. Stepping out, Chang thought the cool, moist air felt like a relief after the time spent in the confined atmosphere of the car. Especially seated next to Shen and his decadent perfumes. As for Shen himself, he gave a slight shiver as he stood outside with his briefcase. But Chang believed it was just a show. It would take far more than a morning dampness to chill that bastard, he thought. The driver opened the door to the building, the rusty hinges creaking from long disuse. The interior reflected this neglect as well. Packaging machinery remained still, the large number of cobwebs bearing witness to the amount of time since they were last used. The entire enormous room resembled some sort of industrial mausoleum. General Chang ignored it all, leading Minister Shen across the floor as the driver waited outside. There was no sound except for their footsteps which seemed to violate the sanctity of the stillness. Chang opened a large metal door and entered, motioning for Shen to follow. The sudden chill revealed this next room to be a meat freezer. Chang noticed that Shen was unaffected by the much greater cold. In fact, the minister’s attention was focused on something other than the temperature. The hooks of the freezer were all empty… save one. And that one was occupied by something that was clearly not the carcass of a cow. A man hung from the hook, suspended by the chain of a pair of cuff on his wrists. He was shirtless, his torso bruised. There was a sheen of sweat on his flesh, even in the bitter chill of the freezer. His face was a mask of pain with blood pouring from a shattered nose, but his groans were muffled by the gag in his mouth. And he was not alone. His tormentor stood before him and just delivered a pair of kicks to ribs that appeared to be already broken. As soon as Chang and Shen entered, the torturer quickly spun around with a snarl, much like a wolf that has had its meal interrupted. The face was beautiful, feminine… and feral. As soon as she saw the two men, she snapped to attention and saluted. Chang returned the salute. “At ease, soldier. Minister Shen, this is Sun Tzu.” Although Sun Tzu’s stance relaxed somewhat, she still seemed poised to attack. Shen admired her taught physique, her arms and legs bare in the tank top and olive drab shorts she wore. Her skin was flushed from her exertions and a few strands of silky black hair had escaped from the confines her ponytail, lying rakishly along her lovely face. Shen noticed some of her prisoner’s blood stained her white top. He also keenly observed how her chest rose from her deep breaths a well as the effect the cold had on certain parts of her anatomy. Chang saw the wry smile on Shen’s face and guessed his thoughts. Then he cursed himself when those same thoughts briefly penetrated his defenses. Damn it, she’s a soldier, you old fool. You’ve known her since she was a child. But clearly she was a child no more. “And who might your guest be?” asked Shen pleasantly. “A spy for the bastard Taiwanese, sir,” answered Sun Tzu, not bothering to hide her disdain. “I am interrogating him.” “Ah, but would it not be easier for him to talk without the gag, my dear?” “If he talked, then I would have to stop interrogating him,” Sun Tzu replied coldly. Shen chuckled at the logic. “How delightful! Utterly delightful! And so very lovely as well!” Shen laughed. Sun Tzu gave a look to Chang that expressed that she shared his desire to kill the minister, but a quick shake of the head form the general reined her in. “And that is why you are perfect for this mission,” Shen continued. “So effective… and so marketable!” “Marketable?” sneered Sun Tzu. “That is the talk of the vile capitalists, sir.” “Soldier…” began Chang. Sun Tzu’s face immediately went blank. “My apologies, minister,” she stated. Shen merely chuckled again. “No worries, my dear,” he assured her. “I understand your concern. But this is a most unusual mission. Unusual, but rewarding to our nation. Have yiu heard of the sport of professional wrestling?” “Yes, sir. Idiots with stupid names and costumes that think they can fight.” “Accurate enough. For decades it has been dominated by the Americans. And how they have eaten it up! Millions of fans, not just there but all over the world! Canadians, Mexicans, Germans, Russians, and… Japanese. But there has yet to be a Chinese presence in the world of wrestling. We want you to be that presence!” “Me, sir?” Sun Tzu sounded skeptical. “But I am a soldier.” “Yes, yes,” said Shen with a wave of his hand. “But a soldier well versed in American society. One with extraordinary unarmed combat skills. One whose talent with acrobatics… and yes, physical beauty… would appeal to the wrestling world. And those sexist Americans would be crushed to see their brutes destroyed by a lovely lady as yourself!” “Destroyed?” Sun Tzu’s eyes flashed with the uttering of that single word. “Yes, destroyed!” replied Shen. “Unlike other sports, wrestling is quite lax in its rules and regulations. Drug testing is far less common, so your… supplements you have taken over the years will be less likely to be discovered. And more appealing to you, the brutality of the matches can reach almost barbaric lows.” Sun Tzu and Shen shared a disturbing smile. But then Sun Tzu's is replaced by a look of haughty disdain. "The whole affair seems like a waste of my training and ability.” “Soldier,” stated General Chang harshly. “That sound like vanity. And vanity has no place in the ranks of the army of the People’s Republic of China. You are being given the chance to be a representative of our nation and her citizens. This is not an assignment, this is an honor and you will conduct yourself accordingly. Are we clear, soldier?” Chang’s eyes stared into those of Sun Tzu. For a brief moment, he thinks he sees a bit of moisture there, but then it is gone. “Yes, sir!” Sun Tzu declared. “Excellent!” Shen interjected. “I will go over he details with you both in my office at noon tomorrow!” “I will be there, sir. Now if you’ll excuse me, I’ll just clean up here.” “Carry on, soldier,” said Chang. Sun Tzu saluted him, which the general returned. She gave a nod to the minister, who responded with another of his cryptic smiles. The two men turned and exited the freezer. As soon as the door closed. Sun Tzu jumped up and down like a girl asked to her first dance. She threw her arms around the spy on the hook, her embrace causing him to howl through his gag. “Yes! This is so wonderful!” Sun Tzu said, ignoring her victim’s agony. “A chance to show the world the superiority of China! The superiority of me! If I ever knew the date I was born, I would think this was a birthday present! I really should check my records for that. Never had many parties in the barracks, you see. Oh, I am so excited!” Sun Tzu stared up at the face of her tortured victim. A strange look came over her. A look of... desire. “So happy… and no one to share it with… but you.” She began running her fingers ever so softly across his bare chest. One hand slid down his torso as the other reached up and removed the gag. He lets out a hacking cough and a wad of blood lands on the chest of Sun Tzu. She simply smiled. “P-please!” he gasped. “I’ll tell you everything! Everything!” “We already know everything, silly!” she giggled. “We intercepted your contact in Hong Kong and he was most forthcoming!” There was pained bewilderment in the man’s eyes. “Th-then why… all this?” “Because I need the exercise, my love,” purred Sun Tzu. “And punching bags don’t feel anything. Anything…” As one hand teased the prisoner’s ear and cheek, the other undid the front of his pants and slipped inside. Sun Tzu raised one eyebrow, pleased with what she found and the reaction she received. “That’s sooo admirable,” she whispered huskily. “To be able to feel pleasure after all of that pain. That is the mark of a real man. Just think about that and let yourself go…” Her finger slid along his jawline as she gazed dreamily at his face. Then she jerked his head with one swift motion, a loud snap heralding the breaking of his neck. Sun Tzu quickly leaped onto him, locking her mouth over his to taste his last breath. Then she slid down his lifeless body, like a snake releasing its next meal for consumption, and sprawled on the cold floor, staring up at the dead eyes with a smile of pure contentment. “So delicious,” Sun Tzu groaned deeply. “Was it good for you, baby?”
