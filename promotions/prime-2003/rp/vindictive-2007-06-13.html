Date: 13.06.2007 Overkill Jonathan Winters’ Apartment New York City The Evening of Wednesday June 13th 2007 “So, I heard what happened,” Aeryn began, delicately peeling away the outer layers of her carton of Chinese food. “Wanna talk about it?” Winters glared at her for second, wondering precisely how she’d found out about it, all the way from the murky depth isolation lab where she’d spent the majority of the day. In the end, he gave up and slumped into his reliable -- and ridiculously comfortable -- leather recliner. These days, his actions within the Stakiri corps building were very rarely kept secret for very long. Not anymore, at least. “Not really.” Aeryn sat on the succinctly panelled wooden flooring, with her legs crossed and already wearing her crisp light blue pyjamas. As she arranged the chop sticks in her hands, she glanced over to him and rolled her eyes. “And why doesn’t that surprise me…” Winters allowed a chuckle and leant back in his chair, making full use of the chair’s natural reclining abilities. “Yeah, well. You knew what I was like coming into--” She remained silent, her attention solely focused upon her Chinese food and her shoulders heaved in addition to sad eyes. Winters cursed under his breath. The words had left his mouth long before his brain had ever had the chance to process them. Great, Winters, just great, he silently reprimanded himself. Nice going there, moron. She was going through a particularly rough patch at the moment, enhanced no doubt with the recent actions of their illustrious employer Cornelius Stakiri. As Winters already knows, amnesia is a tough ailment to overcome and that’s if you ever overcome it at all. It had been over three months ago now since Stakiri’s less than ethical business rivals, OmniTech, had sent Aeryn back to her employer’s set up, brainwashed and geared for the kill. Similar to Stakiri’s own set up, the organisation was backed, and ultimately implemented, by less than legal means and in combination with the business’ technological advances the sky was indeed the limit. Stakiri had originally sent Aeryn to the company with the intent of industrial sabotage, but his estimations of the organisations resolved and ingenuity were far below par. OmniTech quickly uncovered her true status and sent Aeryn back, supplementing her mind with a number of subliminal commands aimed at utilising Stakiri’s own industrial sabotage tactic against, mixing in just a hint of assassin for good measure. Luckily for Stakiri, and completely unlike the usual Aeryn Winters knew and loved, she failed her task spectacularly. However, despite Aeryn’s failure to murder Stakiri her mind was fracture, with OmniTech subliminal commands rooted too deep within her mind to counter. In the end, Stakiri’s leading technician’s surmised that the only option they had left was to essentially ‘reset’ her mind, wiping the majority of her memories away in one go, with minimal chance of retaining much anything at all. “Aeryn, I’m sorry,” Winters apologised, wrenching himself free from clutches of leather recliner’s too comfortable embrace to the edge of his seat. “I wasn’t thinking. I--” “It was a little Overkill, wasn’t it?” She questioned, capturing some noodles with her chopsticks and shepherding them into her mouth. “Even for you.” Winters sighed. This whole amnesia debacle that forced upon Aeryn was, understandably, a subject tough to broach with her and she still refused to talk about it. Or at least, not with him. Every time he even closed in on the subject, she deflected him and changed the conversation entirely within a blink of an eye. For now he decided to play along with it, even though his patience was wearing a little thin. “No,” Winters responded, hoping Aeryn wouldn‘t pursue this particular subject any further. “The way I heard it,” she said, “Bill Davies didn’t even do anything to you.” Winters rubbed the bridge of his nose and then extended the motion to his forehead. She was probing him for information and he knew it. There was nothing malicious about it, she was just trying to get to know him all over again, but nevertheless, he had to be wary. Too much of his craziness too soon could ostensibly send her packing in a hurry. “Trust me, Aeryn,” he told her, cagily, “he had it coming.” The Accord The Stakiri Corp Building New York City Friday 8th June 2007 All eyes directed towards Jonathan Winters as he strolled into the Stakiri Corp building with all the confidence of a man with nothing to lose. Very few of the onlookers had known him well when he and their employer, Cornelius Stakiri, were on speaking terms, a few more had known him only in passing and most had just heard about him. That was the way things went in the lower levels of the Stakiri Corp building. Speculations were endless and more or less all of Stakiri’s employee’s either knew or at least suspected their employers connections to the New York underworld. The security guards exploded onto the scene as Winters got within ten metres of the entrance way door. A little slow for Stakiri. After all, with his recent panache for paranoia, he had a monitor in his office patched directly into the security camera feed. He’d probably seen him coming quite a time ago -- Winters had half expected security to be on him as soon as he stepped foot through the door. The armed security force, all with crisp white shirts and hundred dollar pants, fanned out into something of a crescent shape before him, none of them daring to take their eyes, nor the barrels of their nine millimetres, from him. Winters cracked a smile. “Take me to your leader.” The security task force did not respond to his request, each of them remained perfectly still, their intentions to do harm thinly masked across each of their faces. Something was wrong and Winters knew it. From the inner depths of the other Stakiri Corp employee’s, just beyond the fan of security officers, somebody with a very expensive suite and a posture of superiority barged onto the scene and encroached the task force warily. Winters sighed as he instantly recognised the man to be Bill Davies, a former underling obviously elevated to a position of power. “Well,” Davies began, making sure to stand sufficiently out of reach, well beyond the security task force’s perimeter, “this is somewhat unexpected.” Winters rolled his eyes. “Really?” Davies chuckled to himself. “Well, yes. You did kidnap Aeryn Sun from the care of our great employer, did you not? You have been in hiding for the better part of three weeks, have you not?” Winters ignored the questions entirely, quickly becoming bored of Davies’ idiotic repartee. “I want to speak to the organ grinder, not the monkey. So why don’t you run back into the dragon’s den and summon the king of this castle before I get bored and everyone else gets hurt.” Davies’ grin faded and he stalked forward, beyond the security guards, but still made sure to maintain the expanse between them. “There’s nothing I would like to see more than these security guards put a thousand and one bullet holes in you, but sadly Mr. Stakiri has requested your presence.” This time it was Winters who forced a grin. “It must be painful to see, Davies.” Davies shook his head. “I’m afraid I don’t know what you’re talking about, Winters. Now shall we go?” Winters nodded and slowly lurched forward, eyeing up the security guards and their weapons carefully. They held their ground and looked towards Davies for reassurance and direction. Davies motioned with his hand for them to lower their weapons and let them both pass, which the security guards did without question or hesitance. Winters picked up his pace, making sure to keep in step with Davies. “You know what I’m talking about, Bill,” Winters continued, “You must be feeling a little…jealous about my impending return.” Davies stopped in his tracks and turned to face Winters, left eyebrow raised speculatively. “Impending return? You?” The words seem to mull over in his head and after a few seconds, he allows a brief chuckle. “You’re lucky if Stakiri doesn’t kill you, Winters. You’ve been a thorn in his side for too long now and with this war with OmniTech still at its peak, he doesn’t need your distraction clouding his judgement.” Winters shook his head. “You really think he would even dignify me with his presence, unless he needed me?” Davies turned in silence and continued on toward his employers office, grunting something inaudible under his breath. Winters smiled. “You really think he still needs you around, acting as his right hand man with me around?” Davies shot him a look of pure distain and Winters’ noticeably grin widened. *** As Winters entered Stakiri’s all too familiar office, he became more than aware that Bill Davies had pointedly dropped behind him and that his normally ever-present receptionist was nowhere to be seen. Stakiri whirled around in his extravagant chair, his thousand dollar business suit looking pristine as usual. He took his mug of coffee that rested upon his desk gratefully and drank, pushing aside his news paper and never taking his eyes away off Winters for a second. Davies slapped Winters on the shoulder and chuckled to himself once again. “Still feeling confident there, champ?” Winters ignored him, as Davies took a seat upon Stakiri’s elegant black leather sofa at the back of the office. Stakiri eyed Winters up warily still, but remained silent regardless. He took a last mouthful of his coffee and laid the mug to rest upon his desk, promptly leaning back in his chair and interlocking his fingers thoughtfully. “You always did have a certain panache for the dramatics, Stakiri,” Winters told him, slumping down in a simple stainless steel frame chair, opposite his former employer. “So, shall we begin negotiations, or do you intend to brood for a little while longer?” With that Davies’ eyes widened with anger and he lurched forward violently. “You dare speak to Mr. Stakiri like that, I oughta--” “You’d better control your pooch,” Winters interrupted, glancing at Stakiri’s new right hand man out of the corner of his eye. “Or I may have to neuter him.” Davies lunges further forward, aiming for Winters throat with both hands, but before he can even get close, Stakiri shoots him a stern look and shakes his head. Davies stop in his tracks, his eyes never leaving Winters for a second, his chest pounding, still dealing with the extra burst of adrenaline the best it can. “Why are you here, Winters?” Stakiri questioned. “Finally!” Winters exclaimed, directing his attention away from Davies and staring Stakiri right in the eye. “Down to business.” “Business?” Stakiri asked. “Whatever makes you think I’ll ever deal with you again?” Winters produced a toothy grin. “Because I have something you need and you damn well know it.” Stakiri’s eyes narrow. “Aeryn?” Winters simply nodded a reply. Stakiri shook his head and laughed at him. “I’m to believe that you’d actually bargain with Aeryn’s life? The woman you love? Come on, Winters. Do you take me for a fool?” “Her life?” Winters repeated, shaking his head. “No, you don’t understand. I’m here to trade you the knowledge she has acquired about your enemy, the knowledge you so desperately need if you are to in this little ‘industrial’ you have surrounded yourself in.” “You’re wasting my time, Winters,” Stakiri told him. “The process which will be required in order to obtain such information could very well destroy Aeryn’s mind and you know it!” “No,” Winters responded. “That’s not necessarily true now, is it?” Stakiri sighed. “You mean the staggered hypnotherapy process don’t you?” Winters nodded a silent response. “You know as well as I do that ill take too long,” Stakiri responded. “It could takes months or even years before her memories truly return.” “It’s already been a month, Stakiri,” Winters replied, “and you haven’t even come close to finding us. How much longer can you afford to waste resources trying to locate us?” Stakiri rested his elbow upon the arm of the chair and then cradled his chin within his hand, rubbing the stubble across his chin soothingly. Still looming overhead, Davies pipes up again. “Maybe it’s you who cannot last much longer. Perhaps you have nowhere else to go and in your desperation are trying to broker a deal before anything gets out of hand.” This time, Winters doesn‘t even acknowledge the fool. “Do you really believe that, Stakiri? Or is boy wonder here looking for a pay rise?” Davies wandered into view and perched himself upon the edge of Stakiri’s desk, promptly folding his arms and desperately attempted to appear menacing, which quite failed quite spectacularly. “We don’t need to make a deal with the likes of him, Mr. Stakiri. Let me take him down to the basement. I’ll have everything we’ll ever need from him by the afternoon.” “Pfft,” Winters allowed. “Like that’s ever going to happen.” “What do you want, Winters?” Stakiri finally questioned. Davies’ eyes widened and he turned to face his employer, with shock liberally spread across his face. “Sir! You can’t trust--” Stakiri shoots Davies with a stern glance and waves away his concerns with one simple hand gesture. “Why are you offering me this, Winters? What do you stand to benefit from such an agreement?” Winters smiled. “I want the same thing you do.” Stakiri developed a sceptical expression and Davies simply shook his head dismissively. “Oh, and what might that be?” “I want to see these bastards burn,” Winters replied. “You forget, Stakiri, they had Aeryn turn on us, turn on me, and in addition they pretty much destroyed any memories she’d ever had of me. I have a stake in this just as much as you.” Davies rolled his eyes. “Yeah, right. Sure you do. And we’re just to take you’re word for it. You don’t care about anybody but yourself, Winters and everybody knows it!” Winters lunged forward, grabbing Davies by the throat long before Stakiri’s new right hand man could even react. Davies eyes bulged out and after a brief saliva-enriched gargle, his face went a decidedly funny colour. “You’re estimations of my patience are greatly exaggerated, Davies.” Stakiri allowed a chuckle and his right hand man’s misfortune. “You can put him down now, Winters. Davies knows his place. He’ll fall back in line.” Winters shot Davies a shark-like grin and made sure he saw it, before releasing his grip. Davies rubbed his throat gingerly and stared at Winters for a minute, before flickering his vision to Stakiri and back again. “Sir, this is madness! Surely you’re not thinking of agreeing to this bargain…” Davies trailed off. Stakiri rolled his eyes. “I already have agreed to it, Davies. I need Aeryn’s memories and I could use Winters back on my side, so you’d better deal with it.” “But I have not agreed to any such accord yet,” Winters added. “What assurances do I have that you will not just deal with me once Aeryn is within your custody?” Stakiri produced a toothy grin. “You don’t.” The Interlude Part I Jonathan Winters’ Apartment New York City The Evening of Wednesday June 13th 2007 “And that’s why you did it?” Aeryn asked. “Because he insulted you?” The Chinese food was long gone now. Aeryn had climbed onto the recliner with Winters now and snuggled up close to him. She was mining for information and he was only too willing to oblige. Winters shook his head. “No. That was just when Stakiri gave me the nod to do what I had to do.” Aeryn’s face scrunched up, confused. “What? You never told me he said any such thing!” “You’re so naïve,” he told her, with a playful chuckle. “He didn’t say it out loud. Stakiri knew the history between me and Davies all too well and by dismissing him the way he did was the green light I was looking for.” “That’s so…clandestine,” Aeryn told him, shaking her head. “So, what was the real reason behind it?” Winters sighed. “I need a beer.” The Reason The Stakiri Corp Building New York City 3rd October 2006 “You see her earlier?” Bill Davies asked. The other members of his squad surrounding him within the company gym shook their heads, a little puzzled. They were also wondering precisely why the volume of moron’s voice had risen to ridiculous levels. “One of Stakiri’s freaks, the female one. That was the first time I saw her.” Winters had entered the gym a couple of minutes beforehand. They’d all seen him enter and each of them had shook him a look of distain. Like he could give a flying fuck. They were just Stakiri’s grunts; soldiers paid to fire guns and to take lives and from what he could tell, resented him every minute of every day. “Yeah,” One of his cronies agreed. “She’s got a fantastic ass that one.” Winters ignored the buffoons and continued to work the rowing machine, focusing his mind upon maintaining an established rhythm. “Hey freak!” It was obvious to Winters that Davies was addressing him, but he ignored the fucking clown-shoes purposefully, idly wondering what Davies’ face would look like crushed underneath his boot. “Hey freak, I’m talking to you!” Davies removed himself from the workout machine he’d chosen to use and wandered over to Winters. A few of his friends had tried to pull him back and talk him out of it, but Davies wouldn’t listen to them. He chuckled and then positioned himself directly in front of Winters. “Hey freak,” he began. Winters still did his best to ignore him, “me and my buddies as just talking about your freak girlfriend and I was just wondering how much she charged. I heard eighty-five, but Rico heard it was a full dollar…” Winters couldn’t restrain himself any longer. He stood and squared up to Davies, smiling as he his right hand began to shake nervously. “Did you say something? Because I just heard some whining bitch talk…” “I--” Then the building lights cut out and Bill Davies got the luckiest break of his damned life. The Interlude Part II Jonathan Winters’ Apartment New York City The Evening of Wednesday 13th June 2007 “So that was it?” Aeryn questioned. “That’s all he did to you?” Winters took a sip of his beer and then shook his head. “I didn’t think you would understand.” “Is this a male macho thing that I’m not privy to know about?” She asked. “No,” Winters said bluntly, “this was about me putting him in his place, before he got himself any ideas. The position I’m in, I can’t let anyone get the better of me…” “But it happened eight months ago,” Aeryn replied. “It’s a bit vindictive isn’t it?” “Definitely,” Winters told her. “Davies and all of his friends undoubtedly remembered it and therein lies the problem.” “Plus you just don’t like people manipulating you or just plain getting to you,” Aeryn observed. She smiled at the blank expression plastered across Winters face. “I saw what happened with that Devin Shakur character. I watch your wrestling show. This is the same damned thing!” Winters shook his head. “No it isn’t…” “Sure it is,” Aeryn told him. “He manipulated you into a match with Tony Gamble a couple of months back and that pissed you off.” “I--” “And you couldn’t deal with it right away because you had that would Charity/Lavelle situation to worry about…” Winters finished the last of his beer and got to his feet, somewhat defeated. “I need another beer.” The Incident The Stakiri Corp Building New York City The Morning of June 13th 2007 “Gha…” Davies screamed. Everyone around them stopped what they were doing and stared at them. Good. That was how Winters wanted it. They needed to see this nearly as much as he needed to do it. “Get the hell off me!!!” Winters grinned and focused more weight upon his right foot, forcing Davies head further into the thick carpeted floor. “What? I can’t hear you. All’s I hear is someone screaming like a girl.” “Gha…” Davies muttered. Winters enhanced the pressure and revelled in the snap of bones. Now, Bill Davies will be eating through a straw for a couple of months and Winters has a funny red stain upon his shoe, that doesn’t seem like it’s going to wash out…
