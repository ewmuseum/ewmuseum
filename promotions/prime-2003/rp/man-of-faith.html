Man of Faith

7 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/7/08 Jerry Holt is a simple, honest man. He lives a humble lifestyle with few if any luxuries. He doesn’t drink, and he doesn’t smoke. He reads from the Bible everyday and he prays for everyone – even the people he doesn’t like – every night before he goes to bed. He used to study at a seminary but he realized priesthood wasn’t his calling. Jerry, now sixty-five and 37 years married with two grown children, has two jobs. On weekdays, he works from nine-to-five as an accountant for Ernst and Young, one of the world’s largest accountancy firms. He’s going to retire soon – it’s been a long and fruitful career, and he’s finally got enough money to completely fund his “other job”, that “job” being Jerry’s true calling. Jerry owns and operates a non-profit permanent shelter for the homeless after years of renting it. At an enormous personal cost, Jerry funds it completely by himself so that those less fortunate than he can always have somewhere to stay when it’s cold out, somewhere to eat when they’re starved, and a place where they can find God – whatever God that may be. Jerry’s a Catholic, but he doesn’t push his beliefs on those who don’t want to listen to them. Anyone and everyone are welcome to stay in the “Safe Haven Motel”. He and his wife Helen now live upstairs. When Jerry works, Helen watches the “tenants”, who often come and go as they please. A few neighborhood kids help out for free, working in shifts. Jerry pays them in ice cream because they refuse to let him pay them a salary. He’s been caught a few times slipping twenty dollar bills into their jacket’s in their coatroom, kind soul that he is. Jerry wants nothing material; he wants only to give, and to love and be loved. He lives for his faith. Jerry has heard of Hoyt Williams. Jerry has also prayed for Hoyt Williams, and that’s all he’s ever had to say about him. He’s not much for blaspheming, but nothing Williams could ever say could sour him. When Andrew Rossi was growing up in Staten Island, he knew Mr. Holt. SB only knew him as the nice old man next door, but when you live your life as a modern day Saint Peter it’s difficult to keep a low profile no matter how hard you try. For a modest man like Jerry the last thing on his mind is being praised. SB was in town last week, and decided to pay a visit to the shelter. He wasn’t sure if Mr. Holt would recognize him, but as soon as he saw SB walk through the door he calls to him by his first name. Holt: Andrew! SB smiles at him, nodding gently. He’s not dressed up as flashily as normal, wearing a PRIME t-shirt and red gym shorts. SB: Mr. Holt! Holt: Oh please! Call me Jerry, my boy! How can I help you? It’s been so long I almost didn’t recognize you…but what smile gave it away. Mr. Holt’s face cracks into a smile of it’s own, and SB can’t help but grin from ear to ear in return. SB: Well, I was in town visiting my parents. I figured I’d drop. Mr. Holt: And how are they? Back together, correct? SB: Yeah, they decided not to go through with the divorce. Things are finally getting back to normal. Mr. Holt: Good, good! I’m glad to hear it. Great people, your parents. I tell your father all the time that he’s a good man, but I don’t think he really sees it. SB: He’s bitter. Mr. Holt: Bitter? Why? SB: Because I make more money than him and I’ve never had a steady job. Mr. Holt: You really believe that? SB: He told me that. Mr Holt: I see. Well, I’m sure he’s just having a rough patch. He loves you, Andrew. SB: He has a different way of showing it. Mr. Holt: We all have different ways of showing it, Andrew. SB: I suppose. Mr. Holt: Have a seat, I’ll get you a cup of coffee. SB pulls out a chair for himself and for Mr. Holt while he walks over a picks up a fresh pot. Sitting back down and pouring SB’s glass first. SB: So, do you ever watch me wrestle on TV? Mr. Holt: (taking a sip) I don’t have cable anymore, unfortunately. I bought your DVD, but the player is in a shop so I haven’t gotten a chance to check it out yet. SB: You don’t have cable? Mr. Holt: I prefer to read or go to the cinema. The television keeps you on the couch too much, I say. SB: Come to think of it, I only really watch LOST. Mr. Holt nearly chokes on his coffee, slamming down his fist and placing the cup on the table. Mr. Holt: So do I! I do allow myself a few guilty pleasures. SB and Mr. Holt share a laugh SB: Can I ask you a question? Mr. Holt: Ask away, son. SB: Why? Mr. Holt: Why what, Andrew? SB: I mean, everyone knows you’re loaded. You could be living in some mansion in a country where they don’t speak English, drinkin’ margaritas and soakin’ up the sun. What’re you doing here. Mr. Holt: I suppose I can ask you the same question – you’re as wealthy as I am, yet you risk your life wrestling for a living! I suppose it all comes down to passion, Andrew. SB: Passion? Mr. Holt: Humans are the most interesting of God’s creatures because we have passion. Lions don’t hunt for the thrill of it! They’re only trying to survive, just going through the motions. But that doesn’t satisfy humans, we’re always looking for something more. SB: Everybody’s got a hungry heart? Mr. Holt: (laughs) Exactly! Everyone needs to find what it is that they love to do, and do well. God put it into our hearts, Andrew. And that is “why” you and I do what we do in spite of the material possessions we could have. SB: Well, you know, I’m not exactly a Saint… Mr. Holt: Nor am I! You indulge, yes, but your heart is pure, Andrew. Don’t ever let anyone tell you different. You just need to learn how to settle down, but that comes with time. Patience is my favorite virtue, because without it you’ll never have all the rest! SB: I think I see your point…but it’s just so hard these days. If you’re a Catholic, everybody’s bashin’ ya to the point that you’re ashamed to say it. They took Jesus out of Christmas in Times Square! Mr. Holt: Who cares if they call it the Holiday Tree? We all know what it really is. SB: I guess, but what about all of these people doing their damned Jesus Christ pose, taking money right out of people’s hands so they can get to heaven? Didn’t we get rid of indulgences centuries ago? Mr. Holt: It’s the way of the world for people to manipulate God to fit their own needs. There’s always going to be people claiming they can cure your cancer with just a “little donation”, but their time will come. You and I can only hope and pray that they’ll see the error in their ways. SB: What about Hoyt Williams? Mr. Holt: The same goes for him. SB: But doesn’t it make you angry that he goes around the country claiming to be GOD?!? Mr. Holt: Somewhat. But more than anything, I hope that he changes his way. If not, he’ll get punishment that pales in comparison to anything we could do anyway. SB: How…how do you just take it in stride like that? I’m the worst Catholic in America and I’m furious! (laughs) Mr. Holt: (smiling) Andrew – love God and others as you love yourself. You can’t love God if you don’t love Hoyt Williams. SB: He digusts me. Mr. Holt: If you hold hate in your heart, it will turn into something worse. Why don’t you show him in the ring? SB: I have a match with him at the next episode of ReVolution. Mr. Holt: Good. Let your frustration out there. Don’t let hate cloud your judgment. Once you let that happened, you’re as doomed as he is. SB: Man, this faithfulness shit is hard. Oh, I’m sorry! Mr. Holt just laughs and shakes his head. Mr. Holt: I let them slip every now and then too. Just a habit. SB can’t help but smile. Jerry Holt really is a great man. ==================================================== “It’s amazing you don’t choke on your own hypocrisy, Hoyt. You DO lay it on so damn thick.” Simply Beautiful, shades pulled down to cover his eyes, is in a locker room in an unknown location. SB: Hoyt, Hoyt, Hoyt. Would you just look at yourself? For Christ’s sake, you make Mel Gibson look like the Holy Father by comparison. One week, you’re out blowin’ lines and bangin’ hookers, and then you’re bible thumpin’ and cursing my very name. For a second there I thought I was watching Taxi Driver! For real, if you’re planning on shootin’ the locker room up at 160, let a brotha know! You seem to think you have me pegged, but SB doesn’t get lumped into a group by anybody, Hoyt. You don’t know me so well. Because if you did, you might see that you and I aren’t so different after all. Except, I LITERALLY keep my nose clean, and I don’t think I’m the son of God. And I’m not a religious fanatic, but I do get a little fanboy-ish when it comes to Sega Genesis, so I can see where you’re coming from. If you were screaming about Gain Ground and Sonic II instead of Bible passages, we coulda been friends. But tell me, Jake Roberts…what makes you think you’re above all the corruption, greed, and rampant sinning that you’ve been sent to “carry out justice” for? I can see you’re not into reading the whole Bible, Hoyt. Just the parts that you can read aloud and try to fool the sheep that follow you into thinking you truly are sent from Heaven above. You read that “no man should make for himself an idol”, or “bow down and worship an idol” – I think you missed the point, Hoyt. When you called yourself the Son of God, you made everyone who believed the sinners you cry out against. You’re fucking sorry, man. I know I’m not the first to say it, but you’re so caught up in your own ego-feeding, God-complex lifestyle you lead that you’ve actually started to eat the bullshit sandwiches you’ve been feeding anyone who will listen all these years. Because the fact is, Hoyt, that no one can change the world we live in. There’s shit going down in every corner of the world that makes it hard to sleep at night, even for a junkie like you or a sexaholic like me. There’s times when I’m sitting in Church where I wonder if the Holy Water would burn my skin if I tried to bless myself, after all the rotten things I’ve done. But then I remember something that you either forgot or never cared to worry about at all; we’re all made in God’s image. Everything’s gonna be all right, because we’re inherently good, even if the flesh can be evil. All the sinning in the world can’t destroy our souls if we repent and we mean it…ah shit, I’m starting to sound like you know. But I’m not cuttin’ ice, jack. I see what you try to do to people, good people that are vulnerable and just looking for someone to save them. I bet you promised those women you got caught with a way out, didn’t you? Where are they now, Hoyt? Back out on the street while you’re out of town and out of sight. You prey on the weak for money, power, and a sense of worth because you’re so dead inside that you need to hurt to feel anything. But take another hit; we’re all doomed, aren’t we? You’re as evil as they come, Hoyt. You’re The Bakkers, Jerry Falwell, and Pat Robertson all rolled up into one big fat bloated ball of evil. You’re the Leper Messiah, taking donations to save our souls. You’re just a common crook, dressed up in what’s supposed to be good and Holy so that you can pull the wool over everyone’s eyes. But now you’ve got a problem, don’t you? People are starting to see the cracks in your armor, and they aren’t buying the Kool-Aid anymore. So now you’re spinning the “vengeful God” yarn to scare them back into servitude. If you cared, you’d help. All you want is to be praised. The last thing you wanted was to meet me this week, I can assure you. Oh, and to be clear, if you call me a dago again I’ll choke you with your own halo. You know, the one you use to cover your horns. The saddest thing about all of this is that a wrestling match isn’t going to help anything. It’s gonna feel great beating you, but you’ll still be the same cowardly son of a bitch you’ve always been. I can’t stop the cultural phenomenon that is Hoyt Williams outside the ring. I guess I can just wait until you destroy yourself, but how many lives will you destroy in the process? People say it’s the way of the world for the strong to dominate the poor, but that doesn’t make it right. There has to be more to it than that, because there’s still a lot of good and a lot of truly Holy men that practice what they preach. I ain’t a Saint, but I know right from wrong. And it doesn’t get more wrong than Hoyt Williams. I’m gonna beat you at ReVolution, but it’s just gonna be for me. It’s gonna be for everyone you’ve ever hurt. For all the lies you’ve told, and for every family you’ve destroyed…I’m gonna destroy you. FTB
