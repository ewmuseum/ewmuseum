Gettin' the Job Done (Revolution 131, singles match vs, Sun Tzu)

June 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June, 2007 “Ah, sheeit.” Wade Elliott grumbled to himself as his hot coffee splashed against his knee and dripped to the floor of his black Ford F-150. He reached around in the back seat, grabbing a blue work shirt and sopping up the mess. He tipped back what was left of his coffee and chucked the Styrofoam cup into the back, wiping droplets from his thick brown goatee with the back of his hand. He returned the gaze of his piercing blue eyes to the road ahead; interstate forty heading west across Oklahoma. Destination: Albuquerque, New Mexico, location of PRIME ReVolution. The sun had been up for only half an hour, and the coffee, whatever Wade was able to put into his system before he spilled it, was helping to keep the PRIME newcomer awake on the open road. You might ask, 'why don't you fly to Albuquerque, Wade?' To which he would reply, 'because I fuckin' hate flyin'.' Besides, PRIME had made Wade's paychecks a tad more sizable, and he had a new, shiny black, fully loaded Ford F-150 that needed breaking in. The twangy sounds of Toby Keith's “whiskey girl” played softly through the speakers as Wade cruised down the highway, his left arm dangling out the window. He had just passed Shawnee, Oklahoma. Albuquerque was quite a ways away, but that didn't seem to bother him. Of course, the feelings of freedom that the open road offered were soon interrupted. A silver Ford Excursion pulled up beside Wade, matching his speed. Elliott turned his head, and narrowed his eyes to see a grizzly fellow with black shades and a handlebar mustache staring back. After a quick stare-down, the man with the mustache put a piece of paper against the window, reading “Follow Us.” Wade courteously replied with an erect middle finger. The man seemed to chuckle, then bent low, reaching for something under his seat. He came back up, pressing the same note against the window with a pistol pressed next to it. Wade frowned and spat out the window. The Excursion drove ahead and pulled out in front of him. Wade followed. * * * * * * * Wade stared at the Excursion through his windshield. They sat in a parking lot behind a sketchy warehouse in Oklahoma City. After a moment of deliberation, Wade slid the drawer out from under his seat, moved a few car manuals, and retrieved a big shiny .357 magnum, which he tucked carefully behind him behind his belt and under his white t-shirt. With a frustrated sigh he stepped out of his truck and lumbered up to the huge SUV. He stepped next to the driver's side window, which was down, and greeting him with the barrel of a twelve-gage shotgun. “Why don't ya hand Alice on over there, Wade,” said the man wielding the weapon, a fellow with a blond goatee and a shaved bald head. Wade grit his teeth behind pursed lips, frustrated in the predicament. “At least you remembered 'er name,” he said, pulling his pistol out of his pants and reluctantly handing it over to the bald fellow. The man tucked it into his own belt, which bought a nasty glare from Wade, and stepped out of the Excursion. The man with the mustache stepped out of the other side and took place on Wade's left. “Good to see ya, Elliott!” said the bald man, with sarcastic joy. “Always a pleasure, Bill,” replied Wade, with defeated frustration. “Move it.” The two men flanked Wade as they walked to the Warehouse entrance. The mustached man opened the door, letting the other two inside, then followed, slamming the door shut. * * * * * * * “Wade fuckin' Elliott.” Wade sat in a steel chair across the desk of an older fellow with a salt and pepper mustache, black, combed back hair, and a blue three-piece suit. He grinned a tiny-toothed grin, a toothpick hanging out of his mouth. Wade sat stoic, eyebrows furled and boring a hole through the man's forehead. Bill and the handlebar mustached man stood behind him, arms folded. “Just like old times, ain't it Wade?” “Yeah, times I thought the bourbon had made me forget.” “Aww, that ain't no way to say hello to an old friend!” “How 'bout you stop runnin' yer mouth n' tell me what in the hell you want, Roy?” Roy frowned, his under-bite sitting below his mustache. “You ain't in the best place to get smart with me, Elliott. I haven't had Bill n' Jake kill nobody in a long while. ” Wade looked over his shoulder at Bill and Jake, both of which returning the look with a grin. Wade turned back to Roy. “Ain't a good idea to keep yer dogs hungry, Roy.” Roy chuckled. “I've got a job for ya, Wade.” “I don't work fer you no more.” “Oh really? Says who?” “Says me! I ain't worked fer you fer two n' a half years! I did yer dirty work n' moved on. I got a good gig goin' fer me, so don't fuckin' ask me to do anythin' fer ya.” “I wasn't askin', an' I sure as shit don't give a rat's ass about this wrestling bullshit you've jumped into. I've got a job, you're gonna do it, an' if ya don't, I'll fuckin' kill ya. How's that sound?” Wade breathed heavy through his nose, holding himself back from going berserk. “Gonna need my gun back.” “Ah, please, Wade. You ain't gotta kill nobody.” “N' when the cops start flingin' bullets at me?” “Don't let the cops catch on!” said Roy with a smile. Wade shook his head, annoyed but cornered. “There should be rules 'gainst hillbilly gangsters.” “But aren't you glad there ain't?” * * * * * * * The Excursion came to a stop in an empty parking lot. Wade sat in the back seat, an unhappy expression on his face. “We'll meet ya behind the warehouse at four-thirty. N' don't ferget, it's the one that says 'March.' Don't even think 'bout bein' late,” said Bill. “Yeah, else Roy says we get to 'ave a li'l fun,” said Jake. “Try not to git any stains on the seats,” replied Wade, stepping out of the SUV, leaving the two hired goons to stir that one over. Wade popped a Marlboro Red into his mouth, lighting it with his Zippo as he marched off into the streets of the city. * * * * * * * Wade shouldered his way through the Oklahoma City night life, his brown drifter's hat tipped low and an old army jacket hanging off his shoulders. It was around 3:30 in the morning, and things were dying down in this part of the city. He kept his eyes forward, stalwart and undeterred. He soon ducked into an ally-way, escaping the prying eyes of Oklahoma. After taking a few back streets he soon stepped into a small “plaza” of discount shops and odd-end stores. It was empty and dark; a good sign. He searched the signs for his destination, and nearly puked when he found it. The sign read Salvation Army. Wade slapped himself in the face and took off his army coat. He walked up to the store's glass door and made an attempt to open it. Naturally, it was locked. “Guess I couldn't expect it to be that easy...” Wade whispered to himself. He took off his white t-shirt and wrapped it around his right hand, which he quickly smashed his well muscled arm through the glass door, sprinkling thousands of shards all over the floor and street. No alarm. That was a good sign. He reached inside and opened the door, stepping into the secondary clothing store and returning his t-shirt to his solid body. He immediately lumbered up to a desk in the back and rifled through the drawers. It wasn't long before he found a lock-box, and shook his head to find it was unlocked. He flipped through a stack of envelopes, found one with 'March' written on it, and stuffed it into his back pocket. He quickly took off, grabbing his coat and heading up the street, back toward the warehouse. The sight of a couple cops made him tense. He tried to act natural as he walked by the two young officers, one a black fellow, the other white. Wade walked past, avoiding eye contact. “Hey, you there.” Wade stopped, eyes darting left and right. He breathed quietly through his nose and slowly turned around. “Yeah officer, what can I do fer ya?” “Where ya comin' from, pal?” asked the black cop. “Down the street a bit,” replied Wade, calm. “Yeah? Spendin' time in the alleys?” asked the other cop. “Naw, sir. I uh...” The cops waited, stepping forward slowly. “Ah...” continued Wade. “I was at the strip club, down a couple blocks.” The cops stopped, small smiles on their faces. “Much to drink t'night?” asked the black one. “Had a couple.” “Headin' straight home?” asked the white one. “'Straight as n' arrow.” “Alright, get yerself some sleep.” The cops turned and walked off. Wade breathed a sigh of relief, turned and quickly walked away. * * * * * * * Bill and Jake had grins on their face as Wade walked toward them. They were flanking the doorway to the warehouse, arms folded. “Got the envelope?” asked Bill. “Got my gun?” “Not before the envelope,” said Jake. Wade stared them down for a minute, then reached into his back pocket and tossed the envelope on the ground in front of them. Bill chuckled. Jake retrieved the envelope, placing it inside his jacket. Bill reached behind him, pulling out Alice, Wade's .357. “Still get the job done, don't ya Wade? “Gimme my gun, Bill.” Bill chuckled again, then handed Alice over, barrel first. Wade gripped the barrel, then smashed the handle across Bill's jaw, dropping him instantly. Jake fumbled for his weapon, but not before Wade flipped his magnum around and had the barrel pointed at Jake's face. “Go inside, asshole.” Jake complied, running indoors and slamming the door shut. Wade knelt down next to Bill, who stirred on the ground, moaning in pain. “Tell Roy that he should keep hirin' idiots like you, n' leave me to my own.” Wade stuffed Alice behind his belt and walked over to his truck, hopping inside, starting her up, and pulling out onto the road. He soon found himself back on interstate forty, once again en route toward Albuquerque, New Mexico and PRIME ReVolution. A cigarette dangled out of his mouth and Jason Aldean's “Johnny Cash” played through the speakers. “That's gon' come 'round n' bite me in the ass.”
