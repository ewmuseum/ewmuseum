Date: May 3, 2009 "Ugh..." A voice spoke from the other side of a door, "I'm really not cut out for this shit." Wrestling with the door, you can hear the obvious aggravation in her voice. The sound of keys jingling is shortly followed by the doorknob shaking violently. THUMP! A fist pound against the door in aggravation. "Arrrggh...." The woman vents before finally the doorknob gives way to a shove. Swinging open violently, the hot and humid Miami air swirls into the air conditioned low rent apartment. It was a far cry from the rich high life that she once lived on the beaches in Havana, Cuba, but it was home. For now, anyway. Walking in the door she was dressed with a low cut top and a pair of shorty shorts. Slamming it behind her, she shook her head in frustration before fixing her hair for some unexplainable reason. She almost acted like she was being watched. The name badge on her shirt read ELISE, like anyone would actually notice next to her rather revealing cleavage. With a brilliant smile and a beautiful face, she was everything a little girl wanted to grow up to look like... and she knew it. Being as beautiful as she was, she became used to getting anything she'd ever dreamed of. Clothes, cars, computers... you name it and she possesses. Very few of them from her own hard work or money, which she had plenty of. You'd never believe this apartment belonged to a woman who grew up as the daughter of the one of the wealthiest wrestlers outside the United States of America. Not like she'd ever admit it. It got hot in his native Vilnius in the summer, but this was June, and he never recalled there being a time when the air conditioner ran continuously, just to stifle the oppressive humidity outside. Kazys hated Miami. The well-over-six-foot giant sighed, running his fingers through his deep chestnut hair. It hung to his shoulders, and was board straight, hanging limp after his trip to the corner grocery at the end of the street – to get milk for his umpteenth bowl of Total. That reminded him of the fact that he'd been living out of a suitcase for the past eight and a half months. Said suitcase was sprawled open on his bed right now, as he'd rooted through it early this morning to find his one suit. It had been hopelessly rumpled and, feeling like he was playing with a doll's toy, he had plugged up the iron and pressed it as best he could, his giant fingers barely fitting through the handle. The crease had been off in the pants, he could see that now. He frowned. He remembered buying this suit, something that he thought he'd need when he applied for jobs as “security personnel” (their glorified name for bouncers) at the bars around this part of town. They'd practically laughed him out of the room at Il Duce when he'd showed up in the thing, and told him he looked “stiff as some old man's Viagra overdose.” It had taken a lot for him to resist the urge to snarl something rather unflattering about Americans and their lack of professionalism. It wouldn't have been particularly accurate, anyway, given that there were a lot more hyphen-Americans in this city than there were apple-pie eating patriots. He himself had been referred to as Lithuanian-American once, a term that almost made him sock the guy who said it. His boss was Cuban-American, the competing bar down the street was run by a Haitian-American, the cooks in the kitchen were all Mexican-American or Salvadoran-American, and the dancers were a varying bouquet of fetish niches – Russian, Latina, Asian, and “African-American” (a term that Kazys hadn't figured out the meaning of no matter how long he thought on it). So Il Duce hadn't worked out. His next interview had been at Hong Kong Phooey's, the strip club set up to mimic one in Bangkok (or so he was told). He'd never been, and he was pretty sure his boss hadn't either, given that the name of the club indicated that his geography was a little off. He'd gotten the job, but been told the first week that the suit was a no go. And so he'd been wearing slacks and godawful tropical tourist shirts since. That had been another thing. Tapping into his small amount of savings to change his wardrobe entirely for a new job. He was still recovering from that. Even though Miami's club scene meant that he worked almost sixty hours a week some weeks, he hadn't had a whole lot saved up from his ill-conceived run at a mixed martial arts career. First Vilnius, where his size and aggressiveness had quickly catapulted him into the bigtime. Then, his agent had convinced him that St. Petersburg was the place to be. Not so much. When he'd found himself kicked out of his third boardinghouse for not being able to pay the bills, Kaz had realized that it was time to make a change. And so for two months, he'd lived with his cousin Andrejs back in Vilnius, until he had enough money to pay for a visa. Then, off to the U.S., where so many Lithuanians had come to find only poverty in the early 1900's. He soon found himself among them, having timed his move rather awkwardly with the full onset of an economic depression. Kazys was bored, he had realized, in the past month. Bored, lonely, and without the money to change his circumstances. His American dream hadn't quite worked out like it was supposed to. It hadn't erased the stain of that ill-conceived move to Russia, whose fight scene had been about as energetic as a dying elephant, limping off to its graveyard. That was why, when he'd seen the ad in the Miami Herald for a private security officer, he'd taken a gamble and called the number. “Seeking In-House officer for personal security,” it said. “Hours and rate of pay negotiable. Guaranteed to be worth your time! The job you've been waiting for!” Thinking of that last part, Kaz's expression finally changed to one of outright amusement. In fact, he couldn't prevent himself from laughing at it. The job he'd been waiting for, he wasn't so sure. But it was definitely something. ---- Several Hours Earlier The restaurant that Kazys had been told to meet his potential client at was ritzy. A huge boardwalk near the beach for dining, well away from the 'rabble,' as Miami's elite would have seen it. It was one of those places where Kazys automatically knew he wouldn't belong, as he would be the person dipping his sleeve in the cocktail sauce, or drinking his wine when he should have only sipped. The maitre d' gave him a notably snotty look, as if to let Kaz know that he'd been found out. At moments like these, Kazys couldn't help but let a somewhat unsettling smirk curl over his features. He opened his mouth to say something, but found that nothing would come out. This woman was completely exasperating like no other he had ever met. But he needed this opportunity. She was right. What he also didn't know is that he was the only applicant in two weeks who had even made it to the interview stage. Most of them had decided against it after talking to Elise on the phone once. “I... usually don't do this kind of thing without some kind of trial period.” “Trial period?” she said, sitting up straighter. “Of course! Two weeks? A month? We can negotiate that.” Kazys only nodded, eyeing her intently. “And you'll be in contact to do that, I presume, since I don't see a lawyer.” “Oh, uh... of course!” Elise said, her eyes shifting to the side somewhat suspiciously. “Tell you what, I'll call you in the next couple of days about it when I have the contract all drawn up, and we'll get set.” “That's acceptable.” Elise was suddenly standing up, having rocketed to her feet unexpectedly. She was grabbing her pocketbook. “Listen, this has been great, and I'm glad that you got the chance to meet me, and that this worked out, alright? I've got your contact info and as soon as I have something worked up, I'll give you a call, but I gotta get out of here right now. Stuff to do, you know.. uh... important stuff.” I.e., she was going to be late for her shift if she didn't hurry home to shower and change. “Right,” Kaz responded, but before he could respond, she was on her way out the door of the terrace, and onto the Miami streets. He saw the hot wind ruffle her hair gently as she turned the corner, and that was the last of her. Maybe for good. It wasn't until a few moments later that he realized that she'd stuck him with the tip. A smirk melted over his features. All he could do was laugh, sliding his thick fingers around the stem of his wineglass. Oh well... one fancy lunch I can't afford in celebration of my getting work with the craziest rich bitch in Miami... ---- Back at the hotel By this point, having unwound the interview in his mind over lunch, a workout, and a shower, Kazys pretty much figured that he'd been had. There was something strange about Elise Ares that he couldn't quite put his finger on just yet, but that made her more curious than angering. If he didn't hear back from her, there was no harm in it at this point. Only he didn't figure that would be the case. It seemed that Elise Ares needed his services about as bad as he needed Elise Ares' money. So when she called back, he'd get the contract, and make damn sure it was legal with whatever lawyer he could get his hands on. Before he signed it. He didn't know exactly what Elise's deal was, but what he did know was that once he'd signed that contract, if she tried to screw him, he'd milk her for all she was worth. And if she didn't, then he'd have what he wanted: a way out of this goddamn hotel room. That was worth taking a few risks.
