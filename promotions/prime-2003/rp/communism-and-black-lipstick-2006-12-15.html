Communism and Black Lipstick.

15 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15/12/2006 Walking along one of the many marble-clad corridors of the luxurious Stakiri building, Jonathan Winters knew there was something troubling Aeryn; the top right corner of her mouth furled up slightly and her body language illuminated such trouble’s like a particularly colourful neon sign in the darkest of nights. He’d noticed just a little while back, just after lunch at the deli place a few blocks away, but had decided not to broach the subject. Aeryn had always been a difficult one and she packed one hell of a punch. Winters rubbed the underside of his jaw, his face contorting with pain that had long since passed; even the memories were pain laden. In end, he didn’t have to bring the subject up at all. “I don’t like it,” she told him, all of a sudden. “You don’t like what?” Winters questioned. His question was a particularly valid at present; there wasn’t a lot around to like. Recently, their illustrious employer’s hands had been burned in his dabbling within the world of organised crime and his tight grasp of the New York underworld had been slowly slipping away. Stakiri’s true enemy had yet to reveal himself, but had managed to elevate a number of small time crooks to become serious thorns in Stakiri’s side. He’d been brushing the vanguard of the opposition aside with relative ease over the past few months, but each fresh attack took its toll and the elaborate plans of this unseen, but undeniably devious, enemy had become clear. Nevertheless, the identity of precisely who he was up against was still shrouded and as far as Winters concerned would still be for the foreseeable future. Naturally, Stakiri wasn’t taking the news well and he damned sure let everyone know about it. “This thing you have with that girl...” she responded, trailing off at the end. Winters could tell she was trying to word it right, so that it wouldn’t sound like the accusation it really was. He sighed. “For the final time, I’m not fucking Charity Manale! She’s my manager and nothing more…” Aeryn glared at him. “So you keep telling me,” she said, “but that’s not the girl I was talking about.” Winters face scrunched up with confusion as his mind desperately sifted through all the pretty faces he’d ever known, efficiently discarding the ones Aeryn couldn‘t possibly know about. The search was not successful. “What girl?” “The Chinese one,” she abruptly answered. A wave of enlightenment visibly washed over Winters and suddenly, everything clicked into place. “Sun Tzu?” “Yeah, that Commie fuck,” Aeryn confirmed. “I never did like fucking Commie’s.” Winters shook his head and sighed, though he wasn‘t entirely shocked or stunned by her response. From what little she’d actually told him, she’d spent the majority of her life growing up with her uncle, who was a Vietnam vet and wasn’t exactly sympathetic toward the Red cause. He was also a damned drunk who liked to spout his opinion to anybody that’d listen and unfortunately, that was mostly Aeryn and the cat. “And she always carries around a gun,” Aeryn added. “Don’t you think that’s weird?” “No,” Winters replied, shaking his head. “This is America, the majority of American citizens are armed to the teeth. Besides, there are very few people in this world that I wouldn’t like to shoot in the face, given the opportunity.” Aeryn barged into him playfully and giggled. “Am I on that list?” “I don’t know,” he told her, “I haven’t decided yet...” “You bastard,” she replied, punching him in the arm. Winters was propelled into the wall at some speed and immediately grabbed at the wound, his face contorted with agony. “Besides, you‘re hardly one to talk,” Winters began, the hint of pain still clearly audible in his voice, “you don’t even shower without some kind of weapon in reachable proximity.” “Yeah,” Aeryn conceded, clearly building up to an air-tight defence, “but I have a dangerous job.” Winters nodded an agreement, but before he could verbalise it, Aeryn had already begun her next sentence, “And don’t get me started about that other guy.” “Who? Ember?” Winters asked, already knowing the answer. “I’m really playing with fire there…” Aeryn shot him a displeased glance. “Ha ha. I’m serious. The man’s a damned freak. I mean who goes around wearing that?” “Professional wrestlers,” Winters answered, his words dipped in only the finest sarcasm. This time, Aeryn simply ignored him. “And he’s already directed his attention toward you.” “I know,” he told her. “And it’s not in the least bit surprising.” “It isn’t?” She asked, left eyebrow raised speculatively. “Big mask-wearing freaks, who also wear black lipstick don’t surprise you?” “You’re putting words into my mouth,” Winters dismissed. “I’ve dealt with that big freak before. It’s nothing I can’t handle.” “Nothing you can’t handle?” Winters quickly worked out the question was a rhetorical one. “In his debut match against you, he blew a fireball into your face.” Her hands delicately the outline of the faded black burn marks. “He pretty much scarred you for life.” Winters dismissed her concerns with the wave of a hand. “Trust me, it’s nothing.” Aeryn brought them to a stop just before the lavish pine panelling of Stakiri’s elaborate office door and glared Winters in the eye. “Jonathan. I know that tone of voice. You’re worried about him and you’re trying to hide those fears from me, in some kind of primitive desire to spare my feelings. Don’t. I’m not a child and this isn’t eighteen forty-five. I don’t own a frilly dress and I certainly don’t faint at the sight of blood.” Winters had opened his mouth to voice a reassuring reply, when the office doors boomed open and their employer, Cornelius Stakiri, adopted a grand pose, not even trying to hind the nine millimetre in his right hand. This was his building. He could maim people in front of a dozen witness and nobody would say a damn thing. Hell, he had done that before. Many times. “What the hell are you two doing stood out here?” He questioned them, a stern expression plastered across his face. Before all this chaos had begun, it was an occasional thing that he only wore when he got really pissed off, but lately he’d been wearing it more and more. “The party’s in here?” Winters and Aeryn glared at each other questioningly, and made for the office. Stakiri turned in the doorway and held the door open for them, closing it behind them as they entered. Winters glared at the gun pensively. “Any particular reason for the gun, or is this a permanent security arrangement now?” Stakiri glared at the gun, absently, and then shook his head with a chuckle. “No, I intend to use it.” Aeryn squirmed on the spot and Winters grinned. Recently, she’d voiced certain opinions regarding Stakiri’s state of mind and today’s little show didn’t go far in dissuading such speculations. “This must be big,” Winters commented. “You’re as giddy as a school boy.” “It is,” Stakiri beamed. “It ain’t everyday you get to kill a traitor.” “Miguel?” Aeryn asked. Stakiri nodded in response and Winters grin widened. A couple of weeks ago, he and Aeryn had been sent to gather Intel on another minor power, who went by the name of The Mad Russian. Turned out he was just another puppet for whoever had been orchestrating the underworld attack on Stakiri’s territory. However, at the end of the little skirmish, they quickly discovered they’d been set up. Set up, by none other than Stakiri’s man on the streets. His name was Miguel and he’d been with Stakiri for years and even their illustrious employer couldn’t guess as to why he’d suddenly turned on them. If there was one thing the man couldn’t abide, it was a traitor and at that moment in time Stakiri didn’t much care anymore; questions and answers were irrelevant. Emerging from Stakiri’s more or less secret entrance, one of his most trust soldiers dragged the bloodied and battered Miguel out and unceremoniously dumped him upon the luxurious leather sofa, adjacent to Stakiri’s table. Miguel glared at them wide eyed, fear deeply etched into his features. “Mr. Stakiri, you must understand,” the pathetic specimen of human life pleaded. “They threatened my family, I had no--” Stakiri shook his head, remaining remarkably calm given the circumstances. “You did have a choice. You could’ve come to me. I would’ve helped you, Miguel.” Stakiri drew closer, gently swinging the nine millimetre back and forth, making sure his former employee got a good luck at the weapon that was going to be the death of him. “But you couldn’t even trust me enough to do that, could you? After how many years of service?” Miguel lowered his head. He had no good answer to give and Stakiri wouldn’t entertain one even if he did. “Don’t you see, Miguel?” Stakiri questioned. “You’re not leaving me a choice here!” Miguel raised his head, his eye’s now laden with fire. He’d had enough of Stakiri’s bullshit and he’d clearly accepted such a fate some time ago, when the deal was initially brokered. “Just do it.” Miguel’s defiance did not phase Stakiri one bit and with casual speed, pointed the gun at his temple and pulled the trigger. Miguel’s brains splattered across his nice beige office walls and probably ruined his leather sofa, but then he did have money to burn. “Nasty...” Aeryn commented, not even trying to hide her smile. After all, he had nearly got her killed, it was well within her right to take a snippet of pleasure in her actions. Stakiri tossed the gun to his solider and motioned toward the dead body. “Get someone to clean that up, will you?” The solider simply nodded and scurried away, as Winters leaned closer toward Aeryn. “Like I said, there aren’t many people I wouldn’t like to shoot in the face.” “Given the opportunity?” Aeryn replied, with sly smile. Winters echoed it. “Of course.”
