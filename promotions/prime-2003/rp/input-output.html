Date: early You know, your uncle’s life played out in a situation not too dissimilar from yours. He grew up in a wealthy house. His family smothered him with love and structure, tending to his every need and keeping him satisfied for the most part. He never had to work. It all sounds pretty swank, right? Just like how you used to have it? Well if it sounds so good, then you’d know that everything isn’t always as it seems. He grew up in Sandy, Utah, just like you did. Just like you, he went to Brighton High School in the fall. Just like you, he was told that all he had to do was stay in school until he was a post-grad, maybe head towards the medical track. Just like you, his parents cut him a deal that as long as he was en-route towards becoming a doctor, a lawyer, or an ordained minister; they’d keep supporting him. Your uncle had the same deal that your parents, his brother, gave you. It was the same deal that his brother, your father, took full advantage of; Dr. Reginald Dick. But you’re uncle wasn’t as content as everyone else. He came to the same realizations that you soon had. Mormon life can only be so fulfilling, unless you are a member of the population who seeks out solace in the form of a substance. It’s the better half of the American people who are hooked on some form of substance, whether it be coffee or nicotine or sex or meth or vodka or Jesus. The great thing about America is the freedom of choice; you don’t need to confine yourself to just one. Catholics tend to treat God as a chore, finding relief in the form of a beer after the Sunday Service and a cigarette to keep their nerves intact. Christians might actually be able to enjoy sitting through Church, but even they have their glass of wine with dinner every night. Then there’s the whole grey-area that comes with the medical front; Pain relievers, anti-depressants, sedatives, laxatives, muscle relaxers. Guaranteed that uncle of yours with the drinking problem is feeling just as good as Grandma at the Christmas party, who is more than likely loaded on a few hundred milligrams of Ambien. The problem with the Mormon community is that they prefer to restrict your vices to a bare minimum; Jesus and the Great American Trek (aka the Book of Mormon). This isn’t about to turn into a Mormon-bashing piece. You’re not going to clamor about details of how Mormonism believes in three tiers of heaven, or how Native Americans are original descendents of Hebrews, or that Joseph Smith was addicted to mind-altering hallucinogens that ignited the vision of the Golden Tablets. You’re just going to realize the same thing your uncle realized: ONE SIZE DOES NOT FIT ALL. Your uncle wasn’t exactly friendly with the concept of donating a fourth of his earnings to a church that works, in essence, similar to a pyramid scheme. Just like the greedy bastard that he is, and that you are, your uncle kept every penny to himself. He saved. He worked minimum wage jobs for a few years until he found that he’d had enough, and then he worked up the courage to leave. It took guts to pick up your ball and go home. Most people would shy away from acting selfish, because in Mormon communities, the objective is to better your community. The only reason why people don’t act in their own interests is because they’re afraid to be gossiped about in church. Your uncle didn’t give a shit; in any way, shape, or form. Not only did he discard any opinion his neighbors may have made of him, but in leaving he discarded any responsibility pertaining to the people around him. Were your grandparents terrible parents? Did your father always pick on his younger sibling? What could possibly be going on at the home that would drive a member of your family to leave such a humble and fulfilling utopia? These are all questions that the Dick family’s reputation has never really recovered from. These are all the same questions that were asked when you left. Mormon’s never consider the fact that God drives people away. The concept of a father-figure who is permanently shaking his figure at you from thousands of years ago, through the pages of a book, is not exactly appealing to everyone. He’s that sense of pride and regret and guilt that always bites at your impulses. He’s the conscious that makes you second-guess your actions right after you have an orgasm. God, or at least the concept of him, is what defines your moral fiber. For a person who thinks only of themselves, God wasn’t exactly a belief your uncle holds dear to. Not unlike yourself, eh? Your uncle couldn’t find peace in religious setting. He couldn’t find peace at med-school, where he would learn to heal wounds and cure ailments. He wasn’t comfortable in defending the rights of some scumbag who may-or-may-have-not (by the law’s definition) been on his third offense for the same crime. And he sure as hell was not about to spread the word of a cause he found shallow in the first place. There was no hope for this man living an acceptable, Utah life. So your uncle went to Nebraska, made a living in the capital of Omaha, and finally realized his calling in the way of murdering cattle. When word of your uncle working in a slaughterhouse struck the ears of our small Mormon community, the Dick family name was all but a joke. Your uncle made a mockery of the ideals your family held dear in such a morbid field of work, an act which drove your father to the success he has today. It seems like the Dick brothers, your father and your uncle, fueled each other’s flames much to each other’s chagrin. When daddy was achieving honors in his classes, unky was prepping the graze-cattle for an easy shipment. To them, it wasn’t a matter of status or opinion; how other people looked at their positions in society. Their rivalry was measured in money and how much they earned. It was a rivalry your uncle eventually won, that your father still regrets to this day. It’s a certain bitterness that can only be recycled when your father’s own son practically takes the same route. It’s almost the same bitterness your father gets when he realizes that you, his son, are lost. Granted, wrestling is not exactly killing cows, but when someone grows up in a place like you did, anything outside of the box is considered blasphemous. You may as well tattoo “Jesus was gay” on your forehead and you’ll get the same reaction. Everyone thinks once they’re famous that any ill-will once had towards them is gone; they’re too pretentious to consider how anyone can hate a person one hears about weekly on TV. You shouldn’t doubt that you’d offended a few people from the community when you left. You’d also better not doubt that everyone else without the name Dick is pissing on your family’s reputation now that you’ve left. The one thing you didn’t think of that you should have, was that your entire family would consider you a memory-turned-sour once you returned. You didn’t expect that once you left home, you wouldn’t have a home to return to. You can’t really see why, either. You can’t seem to recognize how joining a wrestling promotion is anything like cutting the meat from a living animal’s body. Your uncle was the same way, you know. While you may be able to see the atrocity that accompanies the death of any living being and why it can be looked down upon, your uncle wasn’t exactly able to look outside himself. Perhaps it takes another perspective to examine the rights and wrongs of both arguments. But when looking from the first-person perspective, its hard to consider anyone else’s opinions or rights when they are contrasting to your own. “I matter. Me me me. Everyone else can go fuck themselves as long as I’m taken care of.” My uncle couldn’t find the beauty in subtlety. He felt that if American’s were abusing this right called “the red-meat section” at the grocery store, they shouldn’t find any problem in the process of getting it. Anytime he’d be eating a burger or a steak with a stranger for the first time, he felt it necessary to explain the process of how your meal went from being a shit-machine that ate grass to a bleeding piece of flavor-heaven that sits between the two slices of bread on their dinner plate. He found no shame in mass-produced murder, which is probably why people found shame in him. “It all starts in the fields,” he’d say, as the process begins. “Cattle are herded towards one corner of the fence until it breaks-down in to three lines, forcing the cattle on to a conveyer belt one-by-one. Once the cows are lined up on the belt, their relevant actions ceased to be. They might squeal in pain every once in a while, but anything that happens here is outside of anyone’s control. It is in this method of controlled chaos that the magic truly begins. You see, it all starts when the conveyer belt takes the cows inside the warehouse. And then the belt begins to incline, because as soon as it hits the peak, the cows drop down head first. Before they fall, a worker on both sides of the belt drives a hook through each of the cattle’s rear ankles. Here the cows gather their final breaths as they continue down the paths, hanging upside down until they are put to sleep in a method that can best be described as ‘brain scrambling.’” Here’s the part your uncle doesn’t really like to go into detail about; the part where a worker’s specific job in the factory is to drive a heated rod from something comparable to a nail-gun into the forehead of the cow, killing it instantly. “From here, the process continues in a step-by-step basis. As the cow passes down the line, it enters stations that pertain to a single act to continue the process. First, a worker steps on to the line and drains the cattle of all its blood by slicing the jugular vein on its neck. Gravity then works its voodoo and washes all mass amounts of blood from the cow’s system. Then it passes by a set of booths. In each booth stands a man with two, razor-sharp blades, all for the purpose of executing a single cut on the cow’s body. In the first booth, the man steps out with each cow that passes and delivers one cut with both blades; one down the inside of each hind leg of the calf, starting from the hoof. The man then returns to his booth and is sprayed with sanitary chemicals while waiting for the next cow to pass, and he returns to his routine. These booths, like I said, are set in sequence down the line in accordance to each necessary cut. Then the cows pass through a robotic area on the line, where machine-opperated claws dig right under the seams of each cut that was inflicted, gripping tightly on both sides of the skin. The claws then proceed to rip the hide clean off the cattle’s body, leaving the skinless cow looking like a page from your anatomy textbook. The next part is comparable to a science experiment, in that we have stations that the cattle now passes through with the intention of removing every organ and every bone and every piece of flesh that isn’t considered ‘top sirloin,’ and we discard it. From there the cattle is cut to specification, fulfilling orders of tri-tips and rib-eyes and porterhouses and filets. Then we ship it out to your local grocery store, where our consumers search for all the necessary elements that we provide for you guys to have one kick-ass and tasty barbecue!” How enthusiastic, that such a man can emphasize a profession of murder with the aforementioned ‘kick-ass barbecue’ and not be disgusted with himself. You’re different, right? Because you’re a wrestler, you aren’t disgracing life. That isn’t very convincing, you’re thinking. That’s okay, you can doubt yourself. Self reflection is a necessary part of human growth, but it doesn’t exactly work well when that case of denial seems to be kicking you just as hard. But why is it so necessary that there is this contrast between you and your uncle? Is it really that painful for you to look at yourself, cast in the same light as he? Why can’t you just recognize that it took the same amount of hard-work your uncle exhibited to become the Chief Executive Officer of Angus Grade Sirloin™, for you to become the former Champion in a nation-wide wrestling organization? Your uncle worked every single job in that factory. At one point or another, your uncle has shoveled shit, fed cattle, shoved hooks in ankles, made every type of incision imaginable on a dead body, pulled off skin, taken out organs, cut cartilage from bones, and packaged dead cow in to nice, neat, saran-wrapped meals. He traveled down the line and climbed up the ladder. He’s dealt with so much shit over time, that now he’s shooting shit for other people to deal with while he sits in a very care-free state. Why wouldn’t you want to be like him? You may have skipped ahead of your own shit-shoveling process when you began to work for Core, but it didn’t take long for you to realize you weren’t in high school anymore. This was a way of life, not mere suggestions that you try to abide by. When you started so green, it was only a matter of time before the machine chewed you up and spit you out, so you got smart. You laid low for a hot minute, resituated and began training. THEN you started shoveling shit, working the arm-bars and the snap-mares and the cross body blocks and the body drops and the arm-drags and Irish whips and all that nonsense. It is all boring work that you’d think is common sense as you are doing it, but when the time comes for retrospect, then the realization of its’ necessity finally strikes. You’ve gathered so much shit that you can dish it out. So you start making moves that put you in a position above other people… you start making cuts. Core isn’t exactly precision in its’ purest form, but it isn’t exactly a playground for assholes who think they know everything. After making a fool out of yourself for the first time there, it isn’t hard to see that you wouldn’t want to go back after such a bad experience. And then there’s the ill-gotten reputation that accompanies your name because of what happened. Basically, it all adds up to you getting a fresh start in a new place. Some old ties are brought up, and you trace them to AWC. A petty grudge manifests itself over time, and you begin to hate a face from the past. You dwell on the accomplishments of others if only to benefit from it. You utilize the shadows that you hide within to scar your enemies. When you first came to AWC, as the Illustrious Face-Eater, no one had a clue as to who you or Adam Dick were. But the methods and the vague history that you described created room for everyone to remember something… From the shadows you hid within, you victimized Tim Shipley. You created a persona and an angle, utilizing the elements you had, to reflect yourself and your motives to be larger than life, AKA the Shadow Effect. When you were revealed, you once again were petty. Your justification was almost non-existent, and the attacks you made on Tim Shipley were rendered obsolete, but you still could benefit from it. You made a name for yourself. At the expense of another, the crimes of Adam Dick’s past were forgotten because you were brought to the fore-front as something else. You are now the Illustrious Face-Eater and you are not ignored. You traveled down the line in AWC, and the finished product is enough to be argued over at the water cooler for months on end. Only, now this isn’t the same line. This isn’t your uncle’s cattle-killing conveyer belt that is so easily traveled by some. This isn’t your uncle’s rise to the top of the food industry. This isn’t your ride from Core to the training camps that defined the skill you tend to exhibit. This isn’t even the same routine you pulled in AWC, gaining recognition at the expense of others. This is an entirely new process you must experience on your own. It can’t be relative to the experiences your father had in medical school, or that your uncle had stabbing cattle. This isn’t comparable to stalking a timid boy like Tim Shipley and expecting everyone to know your name because of the enemies you’ve made. You’ve got an entirely new task in front of you. But it is only a wrestling promotion. PRIME is the name, not the state. Appearance, observations, opinions; these are all facades of glory painted by the action contained within. It isn’t a matter of people knowing who you are because of your status at the top of PRIME. It isn’t a matter of being a well-known entertainer within the company for the sake of attaining a higher salary. This is an entirely different beast all together, and you’ll learn. You’ll learn this week, as you face a worthy number of competitors in the names of Psymon, Tyler O’Neill, and Paddy O’Shea. The addition of Paddy may throw you off, as you work with him in the AWC, but don’t be confused. THIS ISN’T THE SAME ROUTINE YOU’VE TRAVELED BEFORE. A unique beast all on its own, the path you travel in your time spent here will not be mulled over by your former friends and family in Sandy; examining each and every aspect of your career and how it has damaged their community. It will not be considered by your somewhat similar uncle; comparing his own travels to yours and finding sympathy for them. It will not be cast in the same light of your AWC career by wrestling enthusiasts; you may be at the top of the ladder there, but here… In PRIME, you ain’t shit. So once again, you start at the bottom, shoveling the very same shit you’ve come to recognize. And who knows for how long you do it? Certainly not yourself, so don’t try to anticipate anything. Don’t get your hopes up and think you’re better than the guy standing next to you, because he’s probably shoveled just as much shit as you if not more. Just keep your hopes up and your head high. Soon enough, you won’t notice you’ll have shoveled so much shit that when you look down, you’re standing on a mountain of shit, kicking down excess to those that crowd below you. Because when you get to that point, you start to have victims, AKA the people you start throwing shit at. You can live the carefree life that other people dread to interact with, for fear of having to deal with your shit. You’ll be the conveyer belt that living things travel down to meet their end. YOU will be the process everyone else loves to hate. But in the end, your success all works out to one simple equation. Input = Output And it all starts this week. Just remember, you got three other assholes all trying to shove their own shit in your way for their benefit, so watch out. These guys are all trying to get to the top at your expense. Maybe its time you should show them just who’s shit is the stinkiest…
