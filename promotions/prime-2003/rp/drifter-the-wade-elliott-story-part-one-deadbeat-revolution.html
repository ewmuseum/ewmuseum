"Drifter": The Wade Elliott Story - Part One: Deadbeat. (Revolution 138, Tag Team Match vs. Devin Shakur & Tony Gamble)

Date: February, 1975 “I'd best stop feedin' you, dog. Yer getting' too big fer yer britches.” Angus, the blue-tick hound, sat in the passenger's seat of a black Ford F-150, property of PRIME's Big Dog, Wade Elliott, panting happily as the two rolled down the highway on a beautiful sunny day, heading for Kentucky and PRIME ReVolution One-Hundred and Thirty-Eight. Wade patted Angus's belly with a work-worn hand, much to the hound's delight. “I'm spoilin' ya, I know I am. Yer parents spoil ya this bad?” asked Wade. Angus smacked his floppy jowls. “Yeah, mine neither. 'Course it was only me n' Ma growin' up. Only just met my Pa, an' nearly gutted 'im on the occasion.” Angus whined. “Ah, ain't yer fault, I ain't even met ya when it happened.” Wade paused, reaching over to scratch the dog behind its dangling ears. “You ever know yer Pa, boy?” Angus cocked his head to the side. “I hear ya. I still don't really know nothin' 'bout my Pop...” Wade picked a cigarette out of a box of Marlboro Reds, lighting it up with a silver Zippo and taking a deep drag, exhaling out the open window. “...but Ma tells me he was a real wild child...” * * * * * * * Pine Ridge Alabama, 1975 “GIT YER TROUBLE-MAKIN' ASS BACK HERE, BOY!” “YEEEEEEEEOOOOOOOOOOO!” Earl Davis leaped over a split rail fence, long blond hair flowing behind him as he darted toward a white Corvette, a smile on his face as a greasy, rotund fellow chased him, waving a twelve gage in the air. “C'mon, Earl!” hollered Karen Elliott, halfway out the window of the Corvette, sparkles in her blue eyes as the love of her life ran from her father's menacing weapon. “AIN'T NO DAUGHTER OF MINE'S GONNA RUN OFF WITH A PIECE'A SHIT LIKE YOU, DAVIS!” bellowed Karen's father, pulling up his shorts as he waddled after Earl. “THAT'S HER DECISION, MR. ELLIOTT!” Earl yelled back, digging into the pockets of his dirty overalls to find the key to his car. He slid to a stop, ratty Converse sneakers kicking up dust as he flung the door open, hopped inside, fired up the engine and hit the gas. “YEEEEEE HAAAAAAAA!” The tires of the Corvette tore up the dirt, kicking up a cloud as the tires spun. Earl's assailant stopped and let loose a round of buckshot into Earl's car, peppering his license plate as the hot-rod roared off. “YOU'D BETTER GIT THEM BROWN LOCKS BACK IN BED BEFORE MORNIN', KAREN!” roared Mr. Elliott. He could only hear the whoops and yells from his daughter and the boy sitting next to her. He threw down his straw hat in anger, kicking up more dirt. “Damnit all!” * * * * * * * “We're home free, Karen!” Earl and Karen sped off down the back roads of Pine Ridge, Alabama, holding each other's hand, all smiles as they left dust in their wake. “Oh, Daddy's gonna tan my hide 'soon as I get back, sure's shootin'!” said Karen with a chuckle. “Oh no he won't!” chimed Earl. “I'll be on 'im like a chicken on a june-bug!” “With them skinny arms?” toyed Karen, squeezing Earl's lanky bicep. Earl raised an eyebrow. “Roped you in with these skinny arms quick enough, didn't I?” Karen leaned in, planting a kiss on Earl's face, leaving the young buck with wide smile. She wrapped herself around his arm, leaning against his shoulder as they sped on. “The world's our oyster, Karen,” said Earl, eyes gleaming. “We're eighteen n' livin' free! Ain't nothin's gonna stop us!” “You promise?” asked Karen, looking up into his eyes. “Sure's shootin',” he replied. He planted a kiss on her forehead before turning his attention back to the road. Karen smiled, cuddling closer, closing her eyes, certain that life couldn't possibly get any better. * * * * * * * Night had fallen, yielding a starry Alabama sky. The white Corvette rolled to a halt atop a ridge, overlooking the lights of the tiny town of Pine Ridge. Earl cut the engine, and soon the lights, leaving the two lovers in darkness, hands intertwined as they peered over the town they called home for all of their young lives. “Ain't it beautiful, Earl?” said Karen, transfixed on the gorgeous view. “Ain't nearly as beautiful as you,” he replied. She smiled, pecking him on the cheek. “You aint' gonna leave me, are ya?” asked Karen, genuinely concerned. “Shit no!” recoiled Earl. “How'm I gonna leave the prettiest girl in Pine Ridge?” “We gonna git married? With kids?” “Ten kids!” “Ya promise?” “Course I do.” Earl leaned close, kissing Karen deeply. He pulled away, staring at her silhouette in the darkness. “Nothin's comin' between us, not no-one or no thing,” he said. She pulled him close, a deep kiss her only reply. He leaned her back, disappearing behind the dashboard. * * * * * * * Earl cruised his Corvette up a dirt driveway, Lynyrd Skynyrd's “Tuesday's Gone” blasting out the windows. He came to a stop in front of a ratty old cabin, dark wooden siding hanging onto a flimsy frame. Karen stood in the doorway, arms wrapped around herself as if trying to keep warm. Earl smiled, stepping out of the car and taking confident strides toward his girl. He turned his attention to a window, noticing the snarling visage of Mr. Elliott, shooting Earl a look that could kill before turning away. Earl turned his gaze back to Karen, a tad unsettled, but soon finding himself grinning ear to ear once more. He took a couple steps onto the porch, striding up to Karen, planting a big kiss on her cheek. Karen only looked at him, almost frozen, mouth slightly agape. Earl's face turned to that of concern. “What's wrong, baby?” he asked, putting a hand on her shoulder. Her lips moved, wanting to spill every feeling, but remained silent. Earl looked at her with loving eyes, drawing a thumb over her cheek. “You can tell me, darlin'. Sure's shootin'.” She offered a weak smile, mustering enough strength to release the weight from her conscience. “I'm pregnant.” The love in Earl's eyes turned to shock. His smile transformed into jaw hanging awe. His thumb fell from her face. He took a step back. “Earl...” Karen started, stepping forward. Another step backward. “Earl?” she pleaded, reaching out with her dainty hand. His eyes lay transfixed her blue orbs. He took another step back, then another, and soon turned around, sprinting down the dirt driveway, his Converse sneakers kicking up dirt. “EARL!” she yelled, stepping to the porch railing, gripping it tight as tears started to form in her eyes. He jumped into his car, slamming the door shut and flying out of the driveway in reverse. Karen could only watch, helpless, as her child's father tore down the road, accelerating RPMs echoing through Pine Ridge. Tears streamed down her face. She shook as her fingernails dug into the wooden railing. Her teeth were grit in furious anger, while her eyes rained in sadness. “Sonnuva bitch...” she shuttered. “You son of a bitch...”
