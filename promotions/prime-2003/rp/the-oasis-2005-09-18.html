The Oasis

18 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/18/05 The Oasis The restaurant was called Oasis, but Damien remembered it when it was a hole-in-the-wall café called The Spa Cafe. The jukebox was replaced by a jazz guitarist. The steel chairs and wobbly tables were now bamboo seats and marble tablets. The food went from Soul Food to Pan-Caribbean/Cajun fusion. And the owner who was once known as Mr. Frank now called himself Francis. They had changed everything in an attempt to get in on the hipster wave that was flooding into Brooklyn’s poorer neighborhoods. The math was simple; As the East Village, Greenwich Village, The Lower West Side, SoHo, and other artists’ havens became trendy, the real estate became more expensive. As the prices in these areas sky-rocketed, the trust fund babies and aging yuppies who fancied themselves Bohemians bought up the apartments, lofts, and studios that were once the only places the true starving artists could afford. So, the only place left for the population were the slums, the ghettos, the barrios, and all of the other areas that were not fit for anyone but the very poor. And so, the Artist Exodus began. As a result, The Spa Café became Oasis, the local thrift shop became a vintage boutique, and it was considered chic to live above a bodega, or in the basement under a brothel. With all that had changed, Damien understood that he had changed a great deal himself. When he first walked into The Spa Café, he had ordered a hamburger combo meal, and split it with his date because he could not afford two meals. He remembered having to leave his girlfriend’s house before twelve because the R-train stopped running into Manhattan after midnight and he couldn’t afford a cab. He would have to transfer three times, and wait half an hour between transfers just to get home. Damien remembered that he would often shower and leave directly from his girlfriend’s house to a show because the water and electricity in his shitty studio apartment was spotty, at best. Damien sat in that chair, now a millionaire, picking on a forty-dollar salad thinking about the grilled cheese sandwiches that he made using an iron because he was afraid of the stove that never stayed lit. Mostly, though, remembered the girl that would make a little extra food, or buy an extra bus pass, or keep a drawer in her dresser just for him, without being asked. Damien Cruz remembered Sylvia. It was a bad four-fifty from a broken ladder that did it. Because he could not get the right push, he lost a rotation, and fell on his right knee hard. It crushed his patella, and the fragments ripped through the underlying ligament. The pain was excruciating, and it took him almost seven months to recover after surgery. It was in the emergency room that Damien Cruz met Sylvia Castelles. She was working as the triage nurse when he was rushed through the doors by a pair of men in half-tights and sweatshirts. The men left Damien in her care, along with an address to send the bill. Two days after the surgery, when the drugs wore off, and Damien was being taught how to use his crutches, the first place he went was to her station, and he thanked her for giving him a private room, and making sure that he had a decent dinner the night he was wheeled in. Sylvia, enchanted by his quick wit, playfulness, and the shameless flirting, made a decision that went against her every instinct; she agreed to a cup of coffee with a man who earned a living by inflicting pain to others while avoiding or ignoring pain to himself. However, the romance was refreshing. It was a welcome change to her life. A change that included a new sense of feeling needed. While he was recovering, she often helped him hobble around and remain quasi-independent, all the while nurturing him when he needed help. She also offered him a warm meal and a comfortable couch to spend evenings with her on, never making an issue of the fact that he was unemployed, and unemployable while on crutches. And even as he got better, and began going to the gym, or taking meetings with new promotions, it was easy to be in denial about what came next because he always came home to her. So no matter what he did during the day, it was Damien and T.S. Elliot or Miles Davis or Kevin Smith that she spent the night with. For a long time, it was easy to be satisfied with that. And they were. When he proposed, she agreed. When he got on his feet, and began to make a good living, she planned. When he began to spend more time out of the house than at home with her, she waited. And when she woke up another morning to find his side of the bed cold at five in the morning, she mourned. That was a little more than a year ago when she came to terms with the life he was offering. They were engaged, but he was already married. She understood, as best as she could, and walked away. It was a week before he realized that her things were gone, and that the envelope on the kitchen table was not junk mail. Damien sipped the wine that he was served and waited. Three days earlier, he listened to his answering machine while packing for a promotional appearance with PRIME in Dallas. When he heard her voice, something in him felt utterly sad. When she asked to meet at the old Spa Café, he felt unbelievably relieved. He decided that PRIME would have to wait this time, and perhaps be put on hold for good. From where he sat, it was hard to see who came and went through the door, but when she walked in, his eyes found her instantly. They were fixed on her as she spoke to the head waiter, asking him the obvious question. In that moment, his head swam with superficial jealousy at the way she smiled as she spoke. He envied the man for being able to hear her soft voice, look into her amazing brown eyes, and be so close to her. Whatever the year had changed for Damien, it had undoubtedly done nothing to affect her incredible beauty. Damien, at the sight of her, was cast back through history at the times he would stroke her long black hair until she was asleep on his lap. He remembered the smell of her warm skin when she stood wrapped in his arms on the boardwalk. Damien was returned to the places they shared that would forever be with him. So even as she walked toward the table, and he rose to pull out her chair, he could not feel the realness of her presence. She was more beautiful than he remembered. But she was not smiling. God, he wished she would be smiling. “Thank you for meeting with me, Damien.” she said, keeping herself from making direct eye contact. “Sylvia, of course. I literally dropped everything to meet with you.” She finally looked at him, but her eyes were cold, “I’m glad that I demand such attention after all this time, that is so unlike you.” The words struck him like a blow, and he literally winced. Her gaze dropped, “That was not what I wanted to say. It was uncalled for. I’m sorry.” Damien tried to smile in acceptance of her apology, hoping that she had somehow gotten whatever that was meant to be off her chest. He did realize that it was a long-time coming. “It’s…I don’t know…That is not why I asked you to meet with me, I am sorry.” He nodded, and assured her that she did not need to apologize. He asked, “So, I am very happy to see you. You look amazing. Your hair, your eyes, everything. I can’t believe you are actually here” She seemed to take each compliment without accepting them, and simply opened her purse and placed a small white envelope on the table. Damien looked at it, and then at her. “Please open it. I did not want to take up any more of your time that I needed to. So please open it.” Damien lifted the envelope, and placed it into the inside pocket of his jacket. “I would like to enjoy a meal with you. Truthfully, I would like to share a dinner with you before I open anything. This way, if it is bad news, I will have had a fine dinner with great company and it will not be ruined by bad news.” “It is not bad news.” She said. “Then it will give me something other than dessert to look forward to.” He answered with a playful smile. There was something desperate in the way he felt, and was grasping at anything to keep her at the table with him. Even, if it was to be the last he ever would, to enjoy her company for the final time. “Damien, I don’t think that would be a good idea. Really, I just came to give you that, and to thank you in person for what you have done for me. It’s a check. Now that I can afford to, I am paying you back for the money you lent me to buy a car, and the money I borrowed when I had to fly back to Florida for my dad’s funeral.” Damien was stunned. He drew the check from his pocket, and placed it back on the table. “Sylvia, I did not ‘lend’ you the money for the car. I bought it for you. And you did not ‘borrow’ the money; I bought you the tickets so that you could be home where you needed to be. Please, this is your money. I am not taking it.” He was not sure if he felt embarrassed, insulted, or mortally depressed that she would treat those things like a business transaction. She, in return, looked at him with eyes that portrayed the same confusion. He continued with a slight smile. “Now if you would like, I will, as an act of genuine desire to share a meal with you, buy you dinner. We can talk about whatever you would like, and we can put that envelope back into your purse. In fact, I am almost insisting that we do just that.” Sylvia grew angry, and she stuffed the envelope back into her purse. “Then I will mail it to you, Damien. Now you can tear it up, burn it, or accept it; frankly, I don’t need to know what you do with it. So insist all you want, but you lost that privilege a long time ago. Now I apologize for wasting your time, but I think I am going to leave now. She rose, and Damien felt like everything he ever thought was important was leaving with her. He spoke without thinking. “Then say goodbye. Give me the goodbye that I missed out on the first time. Give me a chance to say goodbye in a way that you deserve. Please, Sylvia, I am not asking you for anything more than that. Let me say goodbye, and find the closure I probably don’t deserve.” Sylvia stopped, and looked at the man who she had spent a year falling in love with, and a year trying to fall out of love with. She looked at the way his eyes tried to reach out and say things that his ego and his arrogance made so hard. She looked at him, and could not deny that it felt familiar and right to be looked at the way he looked at her. She shook her head in her hand, and then retuned to her seat. “Damien, I spent a very long time building up the courage to ask to meet with you. I took a very long time to think of a reason why I had to meet you in person. To be honest, I spent a lot of time convincing myself that I could meet with you without anger, and regret, and bitterness. But I can’t do it, Damien. I cannot think back on the year we spent together, the promises we made to each other, or the reality of what I put on hold for you, and not feel very cheated.” “I don’t know how to apologize.” “I didn’t ask for one, Damien. I didn’t expect one from you, either. Just try to listen because I may not be able to finish saying what it is that I want to say if you interrupt me.” Damien agreed, and listened without speaking. She took a deep breath and continued. “I did not want to believe that you would have such a short memory about what we shared when you were not in that ring. I did not want to believe that my love for you would be a crap-shot, and that it would hurt me to invest so much of myself into you. I didn’t want to believe it, so I just didn’t. I played Sally Homemaker, I played your loving and devoted wife, and I did everything in my power to show you that choosing me was the right thing to do. I did it because I knew that I loved you more than I thought you had ever been loved before.’ ‘Maybe I was wrong, but I didn’t want to know if I was wrong. The point is, I tried with everything in my power to convince you that you had a choice. When you took me to Cielo Azul, and put a ring on my finger in the very same place that your father proposed to your mother, I took that as a sign that it had worked. I felt like I had truly been given Forever.’ ‘But in the end, Damien, once the offers started coming in, once you started getting the lucky shots, and hearing from the agents, you quickly forgot me. You quickly forgot the time you spent on my fucking couch, counting your fucking pennies because the company that put you there had sent you a letter of dismissal when they realized that you were no good to them. Damien, it hurt me more than you can imagine when I was able to walk out of your house, out of your life, and it took a week to even hear from you. And when I did, it was a ticket to Glendale Falls to watch you in a damn wrestling match.’ ‘Did you really think I would be so impressed by the gesture that I would run back to you and hope that ‘you’d take me someplace nice’? Did you think I would be naïve enough to follow you around like a groupie? Did it never occur to you that it was that stupid career of yours that made me leave in the first place? So please, give me a sense of closure. Tell me why chose to get back into that ring when you knew that it would drive me away.” Tears fell down her face in thin streams, and her lips quaked with more frustration, confusion, and pain than she thought she could ever feel for anyone or anything. Her hands trembled on her lap like dying birds, and the emotions that she had buried under layers of resentment and anger broke through, and made her feel like a discarded toy. Damien just watched, silent and ashamed, as she told him the things that he was too much of a coward to hear when she left. He slid his hands into hers, and tried to still her trembling. Her fingers were soft and delicate, and were hot with blood flow from a racing heart. He closed his eyes, and wished away all of her pain, all of her regret, all of the things that he had brought onto her. He wished them away, and apologized in a whisper. Her fingers rose to his lips, and he kissed them gently. She, composing herself, pulled her hands from his kiss, and wiped away her tears. “Who the hell do you think you are, Damien? Who do you think you are to apologize, and expect it all to be okay? What you did to me was unforgivable.” Damien, not daring to feel insulted, tried to explain. “I didn’t forget anything you did for me, Sylvia. I didn’t forget the nights sitting in my freezing apartment, trying to get warm enough to sleep, and hearing your voice in my head telling me that I was stronger than my circumstances. I never forget the days I would be in the gym, bringing my leg back to where it needed to be, making deals with God or The Devil, offering my soul to whomever gave me the chance to repay your generosity with my success. I never forgot the nights I would watch the sun set and rise in a strange city, trying to re-capture the feeling of your head on my chest. Sylvia, I never forgot you, or anything you think I did. Everything I did, every deal I made, and every chance I took I did because I felt like I was doing it for us.’ ‘And you are right, when the money came in, and the companies started returning my phone calls, I did leave the house more often, for longer periods of time. I needed to do that in order to pay for the house we lived in. I did it to buy the car you used so that you never had to be on the goddamned A-Train at eleven at night. For every show I did, I was guaranteeing that if you needed to be on a red eye to Florida to say goodbye to your father and to be there for your mother, you were there. You may have seen it as a choice between you and my career, but I saw it as a choice between the life I could offer you and the life you deserved. So you are right, when I proposed to you in Cielo Azul, you were being promised Forever. But I guess I should have better explained that Forever had a price. Before I knew it, I come home to find a Dear John letter and the engagement ring sitting on the kitchen counter.” “So what did that tell you, Damien?” “It told me that I had to work harder.” “No, Damien, it should have told you that you were doing what you thought I wanted. I didn’t care about the money, or the house, or taking the train at eleven at night. I did not leave my father’s house to move into my husband’s house and be treated the same way. I didn’t bust my ass in nursing school so that I could marry rich and sit at home watching my husband on TV from a thousand miles away. Damien, I told you every time you asked, and in every way I knew how; I wanted you. You, Damien. Not The Latin Assassin. Not the Superstar. I did not want to share you with a nation of screaming fans. I wanted you home with me.” “Doing what? Home doing what exactly? Punching a clock and selling insurance? Maybe going to college and becoming a teacher? Did you think I would leave the only thing I’ve ever been good at to become the assistant director of accounts payable at some shitty firm downtown? When you first met me, I was a wrestler. When I was in the gym, I was preparing my body to wrestle again. When I left, I left to continue my career in the ring. I never told you that I wanted to do anything else. So what made you think that I wanted to give it up?” Sylvia was quiet, and she wiped away the tears that returned as they came. “I made a mistake. Like every little girl hoping for the fairy tale, I let my heart lead my head into believing something that was never true.” Damien Cruz, who had grown wealthy, famous, and decorated in the only life he ever knew, felt empty. “I love you, Sylvia. I love you and I’m sorry.” Sylvia looked at him, and smiled. “I know you loved me. Your way. And that is the best you could do. I may not have been in the way that I wanted, but in this life, we don’t always get what we want. But you loved me in the only way you knew how. Thank you for that. Thank you for everything.” Sylvia Castelles said goodbye. And it may not have been the way Damien wanted her to, but in this life we don’t always get what we want. As she rose, she chuckled to herself, and made a confession. She showed Damien the empty envelope. With a slight shrug, and an embarrassed smile, she admitted that she was in no position to pay him back anytime soon. He, in turn, showed her the engagement ring he had hoped to return to her. He slipped the ring into the envelope, and dropped it onto the table, and they left with an understanding. Then went their seperate ways.
