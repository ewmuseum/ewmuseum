Give thanks

22 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/22/06 “And so, let us give thanks here, dear Lord, for all you’ve given to us and all that you’ve blessed us with. In Your Name we do pray, amen.” “Amen.” You wouldn’t think that the quiet Stewart apartment above the Panther’s Den gym would be a suitable place for Thanksgiving, at least not for more than Shawn and Nicole. But her parents had been adamant about coming to visit from New York on Thanksgiving. So there they sat, all around the oak table Shawn had inherited from his grandfather. Shawn and Nicole sat at the north and east sides, her father across from Shawn, her mother across from Nicole. They were nice people, Roderick and Janine Ericson. Janine had been a homemaker since they had been married, and Roderick had been a bigwig in the Ford Motor Corporation until he retired about ten years ago. Despite the stereotypes you might apply, they were nice people, if still a little worried about their daughter marrying a professional fighter and living above a gymnasium. “So, I understand things are going well for you, Shawn,” Roderick said. He insisted Shawn call him Rod, but the Panther was having a hard time breaking the ‘Mr. Ericson’ habit. “I actually watched your main event showing this past week.” Shawn paused to not choke on the piece of turkey he had just swallowed. The man had actually watched the match? Then again, he thought, taking a sip from his glass, it shouldn’t be surprising he wanted to make conversation at Thanksgiving. Shawn nodded as he got his throat clear. “Well enough,” he managed to get out. “Silver has always been a big obstacle, but now things are just going to get tougher. The next bracket has me facing PRIME’s longest-reigning Universal champion.” “Would that be the Willaims heathen?” Janine asked. Shawn just about knocked his plate over as he turned towards her. He fought to keep shock off her face. How did they know so much? Sure, Hoyt fits the definition of an antichrist, but he certainly didn’t expect to hear that out of Nicole’s mother. “Um, yeah,” Shawn said, straightening his place setting. Nicole looked at him with a small smile, the one she wants to make look like she’s trying to hide, but really isn’t. He looked at her, doing the wide-eyed ‘WTF’ signal she caught from him some times. “Oh, well, Shawn, given how much you work, I would think you’ll do quite well,” Roderick said, carefully placing another helping of green beans on his plate. Shawn fumbled the carving knife as he cut some pieces aside off the good-sized bird carcass sitting in the middle of the table. “Well, thank you,” Shawn said, setting the tools back down a little more carefully. “Honey, I think we left the gravy in the kitchen,” he said, scooting his chair back. “Oh, I think we did,” she replied, setting her napkin next to the plate. “Excuse us.” Now, there is a reason there’s a door separating the living room from the dining room in this apartment. Shawn put it in himself before he even met Nicole for a reason: to keep people in the dining room from seeing what was going on in the kitchen, whether it was his dirty kitchen, or more recently, so that Shawn and Nicole could talk discreetly. “Honey, what is going on?” he asked when the door swung shut. “Did your dad actually watch ReVolution or did he decide just to read about it or something. And your mom! How does she know anything about Hoyt?” Nicole burst into giggles. “Sweetie, they were so worried about talking to you tonight they asked me about your work, so I briefed them a little bit. They went to it immediately, and, Shawn baby, it was so funny.” “Okay, okay,” he said, taking a deep breath. “Let’s go sit down, take it easy, and now I know what exactly is going on. Where is the gravy boat?” Nicole smiled at him and held it up, crinkling her nose in that way he found so cute. He kissed her on the forehead and the two walked back out. “Sorry about that,” Shawn said, smiling and pulling out Nicole’s chair. “I hadn’t put it in the gravy boat and Nicole likes to hide it from me.” “It keeps him on his toes,” she smiled putting a hand on his arm as he sat down. “So, Shawn, you couldn’t have started out as a fighter,” Roderick managed to chew out. It sounded like the man was still trying to get used to the idea that it was a respectable profession. “What did you do before that?” “Actually,” Shawn said, stealing a sip from his glass of apple cider, “I was a Kentucky state police officer.” “Really?” Janine said, surprised. “I didn’t know about that. How hard was that to change over from?” “In a way, I never really did,” Shawn said. “The mindset still…” That’s when a scream shattered the air. This wasn’t normal for this neighborhood, just outside of downtown Lexington. There were some apartments nearby, though. Shawn was already on his feet and heading towards the door, and pulling a small safe from underneath the old redwood hutch next to the door. He tapped in a quick code and pulled out a collapsible nightstick. Nicole was right behind him as he opened the door. “Shawn? Be careful, honey, please.” “Call downtown, sweetheart, ask for Denny, he’s working today. Tell him what’s going on.” Shawn kissed her gently. “Your .22 is in your nightstand.” With that, Shawn was out the door, down into the gym and headed for the side door. He kept the front door locked and chained, but the side door was designed to set off the alarm if it was armed. He shoved through the door and headed towards the back of the building, where he had heard the scream come from. The old instincts came back, his feet falling heel-toe to minimize sound, and the nightstick held upright next to his head. Very carefully, he slid around the corner of the building, his eyes scanning in the fading light of the early evening. There hadn’t been another scream. It might have happened from an accident… “Bitch! Get back in here!” Oh, now that wasn’t an accident. Shawn’s head turned to see the front door to an apartment building swing open. A thin, blond woman ran out of the apartment with tears in her eyes and blood running from her mouth. Behind her came a man, in his early 20’s and a wifebeater, came out with a beer bottle in his hand. The interesting thing is that Shawn knew the man. Jimmy Kenton had been coming to the gym for about a year now, claiming to be working up to get into a boxing league. Now, up to this point, legally there was nothing to do unless the women pressed charges. That was, until he winged that bottle and it took her in the back of the skull. She crumpled if he’d shot her and collapsed to the sidewalk. Shawn’s eyes blazed. This was one of the main reasons he’d ever become a cop in the first place. Just down the street from him, when he was in the eighth grade, a mother and daughter had been beaten to death by a neglectful father and husband. Now, here he was, face-to-face with something that set his blood afire. Three options came to mind. One, he could face the man down, wait for the police to arrive and give his statement. Two, he could just beat the man to a bloody pulp. Three, he could drop the weapon, charge the man and dismantle him easily. The two seconds it took him to resist the urge to destroy the man utterly let him get to the woman, roll her over and strike her across the face. “Freeze, asshole!” Shawn shouted, leveling his old service weapon at the man. “What the fuck do you want?” came the response. The man stood up, facing him, but obviously unsteady. “I want you to back away from the woman and keep your hands up until the cops arrive,” Shawn responded taking a step towards the downed woman. “Ain’t nobody called the cops!” the drunk man shouted as he bent over and picked up the bottle. “And you ain’t gonna do nuthin’ to me!” To punctuate that, he smashed the glass against the ground, the bottle shattering. “You don’t know who you’re talking to, do you Jimmy?” “What difference does that make, shithead?” Shawn couldn’t help but smile. Oh, now this was rich. If he realized who he was talking to, he might very well piss himself, but he was too drunk to see or care. Shawn kept walking towards the downed woman. “Back off! I’ll cut you up!” Shawn kept walking. “I’ll do it!” Shawn didn’t stop. What he couldn’t see was Nicole and her parents crowded around the window off of the living room at Shawn advancing very coolly on a drunk man armed with a broken bottle to defend a woman he never met. Shawn stepped up, facing him, standing between him and the downed woman. “You aren’t going to hurt her,” he said coldly. “This ain’t got a thing to do with you!” Shawn stood his ground, armed with his wits, his body and a collapsible nightstick. “Freeze! Police!” came the shout. “Don’t move!” Shawn dropped the nightstick and put his hands up, facing the other man. “He beat her up! He beat Debbie up! I’m gonna kill him!” Jimmy shouted. It was an unexpected turn in drunken logic, but Shawn had been watching that bottle, knowing that he was putting all the stock he had into it. The broken Budweiser bottle made an arc that would have cut Shawn across the stomach, had his fist not intercepted it. The knuckles of his right hand made a right hook that punched the bottle just above the man’s grip. With a shatter and a shower of glass, the weapon lost about four inches and most of its sharp edge. “Shawn, that you?” came the shout. “Yeah, Denny,” Shawn shouted back. “Nicole tell you what happened?” “She told the dispatcher you went to check on a disturbance. Good to see you didn’t bring your old sidearm.” “Not on the force anymore, Denny,” Shawn replied, shaking his fist. The punch had eliminated the weapon, but he had some cuts on his knuckles. “Don’t wanna get taken in on charges of assault with a deadly weapon.” “Okay, well, did you see him actually hit her?” “Twice. Once he winged her in the head with that beer bottle, second time he rolled her over and punched her.” Jimmy was still trying to recover from the shock of the impact of Shawn’s counter-punch. Two officers walked up and one pulled his hands behind his back, cuffing him forcefully. “He hit her!” the man screamed. “He done it!” “Shawn, you got any other witnesses?” the other officer asked. “Depends if my wife and her parents watched at the window or not.” The ambulance siren came wailing down the street. What good comes out of such a Thanksgiving? Protection for a woman that didn’t feel like she had a choice. Nicole being proud of her husband. And two people that were so worried about the man their daughter had married suddenly became happier than they had been since the wedding. “Nicole, is Shawn always this brave?” Nicole’s thoughts drifted back to Shawn sleeping next to the hanging bag in the gym and his fears about fatherhood. “Yes,” she said, smiling through tears of pride, “yes he is.”
