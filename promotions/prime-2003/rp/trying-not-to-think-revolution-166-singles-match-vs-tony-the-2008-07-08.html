Trying Not to Think (ReVolution 166, singles match vs. Tony 'The Grin' Gamble)

8 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/8/08 "I don't sleep too good." The wide, blue eyes of Wade Allen Elliott stare at the ceiling of his black Chevy Silverado, gazing past the upholstery and into the heavens. His scarred, work-hewn arms splay to the sides, his big paws acting as a pillow for his heavy head. He lies shirtless, the Louisiana heat too much for his usual t-shirt. He lies very still, only the subtle heaving of his barrel-chest provides any proof of life. He is silent, and his deep, gravely voice comes from somewhere off-screen, like the narrator to his own movie. "Ya'll wonder why I drink so god-damn much. It sure as shit ain't cuz I'm enjoyin' myself. A feller like me needs to suck down booze like water just to fall asleep at night. Sometimes that don't even do the trick." Wade's head rolls slightly to the right, his eyes falling on the curled up ball of salt and pepper colored fur that is Angus the Bluetick Hound, snout tucked in toward his chest, fast asleep. "Ain't an issue fer him. Good thing, too. Life on the road ain't healthy fer a dog. He's a tough ol' shit, though. Tougher'n me, prob'ly. But he deserves better'n what I give 'im. Part've me wants to give 'im to Lindsay, give 'im a home, 'stead've a fuckin' truck. The 'Bama Bruiser rolls his head back to the ceiling, breathing deep through his nostrils. "An' Lindsay. That's a whole 'nother bag've chips." Wade sits up suddenly, snatching a wrinkled pack of Marlboro Red cigarettes from the dashboard. He pops the driver's side door open, pushing it outward quietly so not to wake his canine companion, and steps out onto the concrete, leaving the door open. He digs into his pockets for his silver Zippo, snaps it open, bursting the wick into flame and lights the end of his cigarette. "Wish I could tell ya what the situation was with her, but I cain't even fuckin' begin. All I'm hearin' is 'Wade's real hung up on Troy,' or 'Elliott's jealous've Rayne,' or 'Wade's lookin' fer something he can't git.' Trouble is, I can't even tell ya if it's true 'r if it ain't. It's got me all sorts've fuckin' mixed up, so I try not to think 'bout it much. Come to think've it, there's another reason why I'm a fuckin' drunk. The Bad Dog heaves a cloud of smoke into the air, watching it float away and dissipate into the night. "She deserves better anyways." Wade heaves another breath through his nose, crossing his arms across his bare chest, flicking ash onto the pavement. The details of his frame are barely visible in the darkness, leaning against nothing more than the silhouette of his truck. The outline of a rest area information building looms across the empty parking lot. He takes another drag. "I've been slippin'. Started with lettin' Dusk git the best've me. Been on a bit've a downhill since. It ain't much've an issue, ain't like I'm in this business fer the glory. I mean, shit, the fans cheer my fuckin' name every time I walk toward the ring like I'm some sort've hero. They don't know that I could give a fuckin' shit less if they live're die. An' fer the most part, I don't give a fuckin' shit to what happens in the ring, long as I get paid at the end've every week. At least that's what I used to think..." A single vehicle on the nearby interstate swiftly approaches, headlights flickering against the trees. The beams illuminate Wade's poker face for a moment as it passes by and quickly escapes in the other direction. "But lately...it's startin' to git to me. I ain't a wrestler, that's fer certain, but I ain't met too many fellers than can put me on the ground an' make me shut my eyes. Now all've a sudden I'm a 'wrestler' fer PRIME, an' I let cunts like Dusk, like Tyler god-damn Rayne, put me down fer three seconds..." The Blue Collar Brawler lets his cigarette smolder, dangling from his lip, eyes empty in the night. "Tyler Rayne..." Another drag. "There's a fucker who's everythin' I fuckin' ain't: cocky, mouthy, short...but at the same fuckin' time he ain't so fuckin' different. He's a tough li'l shit, I cain't take that away from 'im. Put me down at the Pay-Per-View, lucky or not, so if I have to give 'im anythin', I can give 'im that. I don't fuckin' like it, but I can give 'im that. But I can't let 'im get to Lindsay again. I couldn't handle it. I'd do somethin' fuckin' stupid an' end up behind bars. But I don't try to think 'bout that neither. An' again, that's where the bottle comes in handy." The Country Boy kicks a pebble across the pavement, little click clacks echoing into the distance. He takes one last drag of his Marlboro Red before flicking it to his feet, a miniature firework exploding against the asphalt. "I ain't adjustin' well. It ain't easy when ya make an instant fuckin' change from a life by yerself to a life in a travelin' god-damn circus. They call me crazy fer drivin' all over the god-damn country. They don't fuckin' get it. The time I spend in this truck is the only time where there ain't some cunt tryin' to git in my face, or some backstage prick tellin' me I gotta git to the ring in five minutes. It's the only time I can be left alone." Wade crushes the cigarette butt with the heel of his boot. "And even then, sometimes I git the feelin' I'm bein' followed..." Far away, on an interstate highway, a green van, almost pitch black in the dead of night, steadily closes the distance. Three sets of eyes pierce through the dark, ever focused forward. "But like I said, the booze usually does the trick, an' I try not to think 'bout it." He steps into his truck, slowly and quietly closing the door shut. "I'll just try n' git some sleep." He lies back, placing the back of his head into the palms of his hands, and returning his gaze to the roof of his truck. "But I prob'ly won't."
