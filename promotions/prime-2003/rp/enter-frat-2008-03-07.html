Enter Frat

7 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 7th, 2008 (As you may or may not know, I was once a student at Wake Forest University in Winston-Salem, North Carolina. When I first designed Hank Cobb’s biography, I wrote that he once attended Wake on a football scholarship, figuring that it would give me a back story to help write future RP’s with great detail and realism. Never, however, did I imagine that it would be put to use as it is today. Hopefully you will find this RP as enjoyable to read as it was therapeutic to write. This one’s for you, Coach Prosser.) On the edge of his bed, with his feet set firmly on the ground and his reddened face almost completely covered by his hands, lies Hank Cobb. Often the very essence of good natured, southern ease, the picture before us paints a very different version of the native North Carolinian. For Cobb’s reddened face is not due to an overindulgence in his brew of choice, Budweiser. And neither is it the remnants of a hearty belly laugh in response to his partner Colby Korver’s irreverent, and often offensive, comedy. But he sits instead the victim of an emotion completely contrary to his natural demeanor—unadulterated grief. For it is not sweat that pours through the cracks between Cobb’s gigantic fingers, but tears. Tears not of joy and adulation, but wrought from the crippling pangs of painful and utter sorrow. Only days before his first appearance with Prime in the legendary Dual Halo match, when he should be operating at his absolute physical peak, ‘Country Strong’ Hank Cobb sits before us a battered and broken man. Although a laundry list missed opportunities both in and out of the squared circle may very well have left behind a very palpable and distasteful memory in the mind of Cobb, it is not the true origin of this raw display of his misery. But the true reason is indeed at hand, in the form of three tear-soaked papers now lying crumpled on the floor. In the world of professional sports, the tragedy of untimely death is always magnified by the intense scrutiny of the public eye. And on the heels of this examination often comes both unwanted and unseemly details, thanks largely in part to an around-the-clock media culture that often neglects the existing facts in favor of a created story. But this story is a little different. This story is as simple as it is saddening. This is the story of Skip Prosser. They first met during Cobb’s freshman year at Wake Forest, which had just introduced Prosser as its new head basketball coach. Only seventeen years old, Cobb was finding it increasingly difficult to adjust to the rigors of college academics, while at the same time balancing his duties as an offensive lineman on the football team. After each grueling practice, Cobb would shuffle slowly back across the freshman quad, from the football facilities to his dorm room in the Collins Residence Hall. Thanks to Wake Forest’s rigorous admission standards, Cobb’s gargantuan frame dwarfed his fellow classmates, although he was always quick to exchange pleasantries with his welcoming Southern drawl. On this day, however, he had just endured an abortion of a practice, during which he consistently wound up on the losing end of his match up with the starting right defensive end. Completely unfamiliar with such athletic failings, Cobb found himself wandering aimlessly across campus. So preoccupied was he with worry and misgivings that he even went so far as to impolitely ignore the greetings of several passersby, an oversight whose mere publication would have sent his poor mother to an early grave. There was one particular individual, however, who did not pass by him so covertly. For as Cobb began to descend the stone steps that separate the upper quad from its lower counterpart, he ran smack dab into a man of far lesser size, but far greater stature. And as the native Pennsylvanian dusted himself off, Cobb struggled to stammer out an apology. “I…I…I’m really sorry Coach Prosser. We just finished up practice and I’m fixin’ to grab some supper and then get to my studyin’. I can’t tell you how embarrassed I am. I really should have…” “Son, just because I wasn’t smart enough to get in here doesn’t mean I can’t tell when an athlete’s had a bad practice. So do me and everyone else around here a favor, for the rest of today get football out of your mind and just worry about watching where you’re going. You have only have 24 hours left to wait for something that you’ll have 24 years to remember. Take it from me, if you continue to improve, you’ll have a chance to get better.” As Cobb responded to the coach’s renowned wit with a huge grin, Prosser continued on his way, having done what he had always done best—positively impact the lives of the students around him. Although it was not the last meeting between Coach ‘Skip’ Prosser and Hank Cobb, as they would enjoy many more during his freshman year at Wake, it was this first encounter that always remained most poignant to Cobb. And then, on July 26th, 2007, Coach Prosser collapsed in his office around noon after jogging on the track adjacent to his office on Wake Forest's campus. A staff member found him unresponsive around 12:45pm and medical personnel performed CPR and used a defibrillator in efforts to revive Prosser. Although he was rushed to Wake Forest University Baptist Medical Center, it was for naught, as he was pronounced dead about an hour later from an apparent ‘sudden massive heart attack.’ The man that once quelled a young Hank Cobb’s fears with but a few simple words was no more. But his memory will live on forever. So as Cobb mourns, wholly stricken with grief at the passing of a man who meant more to those he touched than he ever truly knew, he turns his gaze back to the papers at his feet. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ [detail withheld] ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ As he reaches the open letter’s end, Cobb finds himself once again sobbing uncontrollably for this great man. This was a man who taught him, though not his teacher, and coached him, though never his coach. A man who had such a strong influence on Cobb, that he now sits on the eve of the biggest singles match of his career, without even so much as a single thought directed toward wrestling. For this is not about the Dual Halo. It is not about Prime. It is not about Delta Upsilon Iota. And it is not about Hank Cobb. This is about only one man…Coach ‘Skip’ Prosser. May his life and legacy be remembered forever. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Sometime the next day… ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ The camera opens in the living room of the Delta Upsilon Iota fraternity house.
