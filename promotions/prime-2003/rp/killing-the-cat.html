Killing the Cat

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July MRI machines are loud. Pretty damn loud at that. So how is it that a guy can fall asleep during the process? Thing must produce some tryptophan along with those magnetic waves. Kaiser yawned as he stared at some images up on a board, the smokey grey of his insides standing in stark contrast to the black background. "Doesn't seem to be any tear of the muscle or ligaments. Joint looks good. I'd say you've just got a pretty sore shoulder there." The Doctor tapped his pen on Kaiser's arm, as if the patient were unaware of which body part it was that had brought him in for an MRI in the first place. "If I tell you to take it easy, I suppose you'll just say... 'no can do'?" "More than likely." "Ok. Then how about this... next time, try not to get so powerbombed." Kaiser looked up at his doctor and raised an eyebrow in annoyance. "Take that up with the other guy." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Curiosity killed the cat, or so they say. And despite the widespread use and popularity of such a saying, no one ever took the advice. Kaiser sat in his car, parked across the street from the Columbus office of Dr. Matthew Ward. Peeling away the wrapper of his Burger King Cheeseburger, he lifted the sandwich to his mouth and took a large bite, his eyes remaining locked on the main entrance of the building that housed Matt's practice. He'd been sitting there for maybe fifteen minutes... long enough to start on his second burger after polishing off the first (not too mention a few fries). All Kaiser needed was the Aviator shades, maybe a gun in the glove box, and he'd be the perfect Private Investigator, or a young cop who drew the short straw on a boring stake-out. And this was boring. But he had to know if he'd really seen what he thought he'd seen after his last session with the doctor. It hadn't really been intentional... sort of an accident of curiousity. He hadn't meant to look... to see the name sitting in the planner. To be honest, the fault lied with Nancy, Dr. Ward's secretary. If she wouldn't have gotten that phone call, wouldn't have bolted so quickly away from her desk in search of some privacy, he never would have had the opportunity to wander around the edge of the desk and catch a glimpse of the computer monitor, scheduler still displayed while the screen saver waited to kick to life. He never would have seen that name. And he wouldn't have to be sitting in his sweltering car, eating a lousy burger, playing the rookie detective, trying to catch a glimpse of the original villain. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ "Alright. Let's go." Kaiser nodded his head towards the main entrance as he crossed the lobby, barely even looking at his brother. David Vashaun tossed a four year old issue of Sports Illustrated back on the side table and pushed himself out of his chair. "How is it that you're supposed to wrestle next week when your shoulder is so sore you can't even drive? Leaving me to chauffeur your ass around, Miss Daisy?" "I haven't figured that part out yet." "Any specific time you plan on doing that?" Dave fumbled with his car keys as he pushed open the door and stepped into the excrutiating heat of early July. "I've got a couple days. If I just kinda leave my arm hanging here at my side, I'm hoping it'll heal itself in time for the show. At least enough to be able to move it without screaming like a sissy." "That probably wouldn't help ya win over any fans." "What's that supposed to mean?" "Nothin." As the brothers approached the car, Dave unlocked the doors, walking around to the drivers side. "Just sayin'." "Just sayin' what? I need to win over fans?" "Do you ever watch the shows back, Kaiser? Are ya oblivious to everything when you're out there live? You're not exactly anyone's knight in shining armor." "Funny." "I try." David opened his door and slid into the car, then looked out and up, through the passengers window at the hulking frame of his kid brother. "Now get in the car. Or do I need to come open the door for ya too?" "I got it." Dave started the engine and backed out of his parking spot, then rolled out into the street without really looking to see if things were clear. "So how did things go with Michelle?" "Are you serious? You haven't talked to her?" "Not in the last week. Been crazy busy at work." "It went... what's worse than 'awful'?" "Horrendous?" "Yeah. That would probably be an accurate description." Kaiser bit his bottom lip and turned his head, staring off at the various blurred images that rushed by the passenger window. "She pretty much ripped me a new asshole in the middle of the parking lot. Told me I was being selfish and wasn't really ready to talk about things." "Maybe she was right." "I don't think so." Kaiser said it plainly... just continuing to stare off for moments that ticked by without his knowing it. Finally, he turned away from the window, staring at his brother."Ya know, it's a bit of an act out there. The wrestling, I mean... all the ego and stuff. I can change all that. I'm actually a pretty good guy." David laughed. "Just because the other dude is a bigger dick than you are, doesn't make you a good guy."
