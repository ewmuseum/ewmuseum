Fortuna

2 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 2, 2009 \"Listen to me, goddamnit, I\'m telling you that I\'m being followed!\" It was a fruitless endeavour, trying to talk to these detectives. For 90 minutes, Troy Douglas had been going around in circles with two of Des Moines\' Finest, and all it had done was exacerbate his already driving headache. After all, it hadn\'t exactly been the best week of Troy Douglas\' life. An unscheduled trip to the Centers for Disease Control in Atlanta for six hours of relentless poking and prodding before it was finally determined that he did not, in fact, have swine flu or SARS or the damn Bubonic Plague didn\'t help. Nor did the confirmation of the thing that had been haunting him for weeks now. There was someone following him. He\'d felt something for a while now, but was never able to get a clean look at who or what was after him. Every sideways glance, every furtive look gleaned little more than what was obvious to the naked eye. Maybe it was the headache - or, more likely, the years of head trauma - talking, but Troy was unable to shake the doubts away, and for the first time in five years, he was afraid. Of course, his two questioners weren\'t helping matters. \"Mister Douglas, we\'d like to believe you, but you\'re slinging us a pretty farfetched story here.\" That was the senior detective, a slight, balding man in horn-rimmed glasses named Miller. He didn\'t fit the TV cop archetype, but Douglas could tell he was good at his job. Would\'ve been a little more helpful if he actually believed him, though. \"So please, go over this again,\" Miller said. \"Where did you first see this man, and what does he look like?\" Troy slumped down, nearly slamming his head against the linoleum covered table in the process. He hadn\'t slept in two days, his brain was dancing the conga against his forehead and this was the fourth time he had to go through with this story. \"Like I told you before, it was in Atlanta...\" ***** Two days earlier... Six hours in a clean room environment being probed like an alien abductee with medieval instruments made Troy Douglas appreciate the trappings of a warm spring day in the South even more than usual. Still, he was in quite the prickly mood after being yanked out of Kansas City by men in facemasks, stuffed on a private plane to Atlanta and examined like a side of beef before finally being let out into the wild. He had to get out of Atlanta, and fast. He strode down the sidewalk, not even giving a passing glance to anyone or anything, walking with a purpose to get to the nearest corner where he could hail a cab or grab a bus to the airport. That\'s when he got that feeling again. The feeling that here, on this bustling Atlanta street, there was someone watching him, following him, tracking his every step. He\'d felt it before, but was never able to confirm any of his suspicions. Not this time. He wheeled around on the spot, trying to catch his pursuer off guard. Out of the corner of his eye, Troy thought he saw the tails of a long, black coat disappear into a corner. Other than that, nothing but a bunch of confused Atlantans staring at him blankly. \"Uhh, there was a bee. Almost stung me.\" It was a horrible explanation, but it got any questions off his back. Thankfully, a taxi soon pulled into view, and a quick wave got Troy on his way to the airport and a flight to Iowa. ***** \"That\'s all you\'re working on? You think you saw some guy\'s coat?\" Miller\'s junior partner, a burly, athletic-looking farmer type named Haskins, wasn\'t buying it. Considering this guy\'s idea of investigation looked to be squeezing things until they oozed, Troy didn\'t care much about what conclusions he jumped to, no matter how frustrating the roundabout conversation was. \"Of course not.\" Troy leaned forward, tapping his fingers on the table in an incoherent, syncopated rhythm. He\'d been trying to explain this for almost two hours now. \"It was at the airport, here in Des Moines. Finally got a look at him...\" ***** The ghost was waiting. He\'d been patient, but now he was fed up. There was a lot of money scheduled to come his way, and far too many unusual circumstances had kept him from collecting. So he sat at Gate 4E, waiting among this band of in-bred corn farmers and simpletons for the plane to land.\' The plan was simple. Acquire the target, nonchalantly bump into him, and in the ensuing confusion, administer a special injection of his own design. Clean, untraceable kill, like so many others. Damn plane was already 45 minutes late, and he was getting anxious like he hadn\'t in years. He had barely entered the business the last time it took him so long to complete a job. Still, with the money about to slip into his bank account, patience was most certainly a virtue. Wouldn\'t kill the guy to be on time for his own death, though. Well, it probably would, but no difference there. ... ... After circling over a fog-bound airport for 45 minutes, Troy Douglas was glad just to be off that damn tube and on solid ground once again. Carrying what luggage he was able to bring from K.C. to Atlanta to Des Moines, he stumbled through the jetway, dead tired and not just a little drunk. He\'d tried to drown his disconcerting premonitions in a few too many shots of Jack Daniels between the airport bar and the plane, and he was hoping for nothing more than a quick cab ride followed by a soft bed. But, as he staggered to the gate, his eyes adjusting to the sunlight glaring through the windows, Troy saw him. It was only for a moment, but Troy recognized the long, black coat. The man in the coat didn\'t look all that intimidating, maybe 5-foot-10, dark hair, slender, but there was a look in his eyes of stoic determination. Troy blinked for a moment, his eyes still struggling to stay open. When he looked back at the spot, it was empty. No man, no coat, nothing. Not caring to explain the phenomenon, Troy gathered his things and picked up his pace, hustling for the nearest phone. He wasn\'t taking any chances this time. ***** \"That\'s your story? You saw something for two seconds while your were drunk?\" If Haskins was convinced of anything right now, it\'s that the guy in the chair in front of him was more deserving of a straitjacket than police assistance. \"You sure we don\'t need the nice men in white coats to come and help you?\" \"Goddamnit, I know what I SAW! I don\'t know why, but this guy\'s following me, and if you would just do your fucking job instead of heading straight for goddamn conclusions, maybe you could actually help!\" \"That true, huh? Tell me this, pal. Why the hell should we believe you?\" \"Because it\'s the truth! Check the airport\'s surveillance tapes if you don\'t believe me!\" This was Miller\'s turn to return to the conversation as he slid between Douglas and his partner. \"As a matter of fact, Mr. Douglas, we just got those tapes.\" Miller grabbed a remote control from the far side of the room and pointed it at the small TV perched in the right corner. Soon enough, it sprang to life with grainy security footage of Gate 4E, 10 minutes before Troy arrived. The tape played, and for 10 minutes, Troy watched in bewilderment until he saw himself step through the jetway and freeze as he looked directly at ... Nothing. No man in a coat, no secret stalker. There was nothing there. Nothing on the tape for 10 whole minutes other than a drunk Troy Douglas wobbling towards a bank of pay phones. Thankfully, it was Miller who broke the stunned silence. \"That enough to convince you, Mr. Douglas? Now, go back to your hotel, get some sleep and get this fantasy out of your mind.\" Troy rose in a daze. He was so sure he saw something. He\'d been sure since he first got the feeling at the hospital in Denver. But now, here it was, plain for the eye to see on video. He wasn\'t being followed, that was for sure. But, what was happening in Troy Douglas\' head?
