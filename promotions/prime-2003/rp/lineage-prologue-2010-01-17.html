Lineage (Prologue)

17 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/17/10 Troy Douglas sat on his bed, waiting desperately for the effects of his most recent dose of Tylenol to set in. His brain was echoing thunderclaps against the front of his skull, just as it had for the better part of a month. Not that the month he was having didn't justify it. As one of the increasingly rare breed of professional wrestlers who actually liked to keep his life focused around his work in the ring, the last month had kept him thinking about anything but. Mobster, cops, psychopaths, sociopaths, social elites and a pair of twins who would leave an indelible imprint on the 34th year of his life and beyond, such were the faces that dotted the tapestry of the last four-plus weeks in Troy's life. Four weeks that had taken him from New York to Texas to South America and now, to Japan. His head should be buzzing from jet lag alone, but he'd dealt with the symptoms of that for near a decade. These headaches were the byproduct of a completely overloaded mind struggling against the strain of its own capacity. Most wouldn't be able to pinpoint such a cause so quickly, but twice before in his life, Troy had felt the same pain, the same unrest that was causing his current bout of sleepless nights all over the globe. The first was seven years ago, as he tried desperately to glue himself together in the wake of the murders of Lauren and his father. That time, the pain had overwhelmed him, drove him into a completely insular state that took him the better part of a year to scrape himself out of. The second was just months ago, as he battled the manifestations of his own fragility and insecurity. His own efforts weren't good enough to close the book on that occasion, it took the first of those twins to step in and rescue Troy from himself. She was his fiance now, and even through his pounding headache, Troy allowed his face to crinkle into a smile as he envisioned Sam as she likely was right now, wrapped in her favorite light blue Columbia sweatshirt, working her way through the sesame chicken from their favorite Chinese takeout place as she graded assignments and prepared lesson plans. Troy scowled, however, as his thoughts turned to the other Wade twin, who had caused Troy and Sam so much strife and grief in the past month. Tommy had shown up on their doorstep asking for help and had caused them nothing but trouble in the following month. A slip at the airport started a slide towards utter chaos, and for all their work, neither Troy Douglas nor Samantha Wade had been able to track the erstwhile Wade twin down at all the past two weeks. And the headaches still weren't going away. He'd made a promise to Sam, that no matter what, he'd find a way to keep her brother safe. Fat lot of good he'd done at that, even with his considerable resources and a series of friends with high places, he was no closer to tracking Tommy Wade down and getting him out of harm's way once and for all. Not to mention, Troy never found out what the little sneak did with his wedding rings, something he hadn't been able to bring himself to tell his fiance about. Anyway, it wasn't like he was just going to turn up here in the middle of goddamn Japan. Might as well try to deal with these damned headaches the conventional way and try to work through yet another leg of PRIME's globetrotting expedition. Yawning, Troy forced himself out of the bed and flicked his socks across the room onto the top of his open suitcase. He staggered to the bathroom, quickly turning on the shower and letting the hot water build up the requisite steam in short order. He stepped into the shower, letting the beads of hot water splash over him in a feverish attempt to relieve the stress that had twisted not just his head, but his entire body, into a tense, twisted coil that only needed the slightest provocation to snap completely out of control. The rushing waters of the shower prevented Troy from hearing the source of all that tension sneak his way into the room. Finally done, Troy wrapped himself in a towel, not even waiting a moment to dry himself off before he walked back into the bedroom, beads of water casually shedding off his dripping hair and spattering on the carpet as he kept his head down. That's why he saw the pair of muddy black boots dangling off his bed first. Impatient as always, Troy's neck snapped up immediately, seeing a face that nearly caused him to jump out of his towel. "JESUS CHRIST!" Sitting on the edge of his bed, filthy, unshaven, nearly zombified, was Thomas Gabriel Wade. He looked, to be frank, even worse than when he'd turned up at Troy and Sam's apartment in December, worse than when he ran from them in Boston three days later, or in Greensboro at Christmas. Running had taken it's toll with a vengeance. "Glad I found you, Troy." Troy struggled to keep his composure, grabbed a t-shirt out of his suitcase and sat in the armchair across from the bed, still unable to wrap his mind around exactly how his future brother-in-law made his way across the planet and into his hotel room in Japan. "Mind if I ask how the hell you got here?" Amazingly, through his matted beard and black eyes, Tommy managed to crack a smile at the question. "My fish guy's brother is the concierge here. He got me a key card when I found out you were staying here." "Your fish guy?" "Yeah, small world." Troy shook his head as Tommy nodded his, neither one entirely sure of his position. Half of Troy wanted to take Tommy, pitch him out the door and send him back to whatever cave he'd evidently crawled out of. The other half wanted to drag him to the airport, get on a plane and deliver him straight to his sister in New York. Before he had a chance to even think about doing either, Tommy dug into the pocket of his tattered grey hooded sweatshirt and tossed the contents directly at Troy, who barely had a second to react and snatch it out of the air. The "it" in question was a small, velvet-lined box. A very familiar box. Unable to even comprehend what he was holding in his right hand, Troy flipped open the lid on the box. He could feel his jaw fall agape as he saw them, two glittering rings, unspoiled from the last time he saw them, when he safely tucked them away into his sock drawer. Three days before the man sitting across from him stole them. "What the hell?" Tommy grinned sheepishly, or as much as one could when they've been through the wringer that was his life in the past two months. "Consider it my wedding present to you and Sam," he said. "Listen, I've got a story, you've got a story, but right now, it's pretty obvious we both need some sleep. We'll start fresh in the morning." It had been a long month. Troy Douglas was about to find out just how long.
