The Threshold

18 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/18/06 The man’s grin was packed with ultra white teeth, too large for his mouth. The cologne he wore was stinging. His suit, white by all accounts was impeccable and very expensive, hung on the man like a shadow. It fit him perfectly, and that was the problem. There was nothing genuine about the man. He was the epitome of the corporate mouthpiece. He stood in Damien’s way as The Latin Assassin tried to walk into the arena where he was to have his first PRIME show after too-long an absence. It was where Damien was to re-introduce himself to PRIME, and change everything. Damien smiled politely, assuming that the man was simply uncoordinated and unable to let Damien maneuver past him. When that didn’t work, Damien Cruz greeted him directly, and asked him to please stand aside. When the man reached into his pocket and met Damien’s request with an offered business card, Damien took it absently, and again tried to push past the man. Growing more bold, and ignoring every clue that Damien gave that he was uninterested in anything he was selling, the man stretched out his arm, and pulled Damien’s hand into a handshake. Damien was caught off guard, but shook the man’s hand instinctively, excusing himself. When the man put his hand on Damien’s shoulder, Damien spun around, and glared at the man. “What the hell do you want?” The man dramatically held his hands up, apologizing theatrically. “I meant no harm, Mr. Cruz.; my bad.” Damien instantly annoyed by the man’s quasi-charisma, nodded, and again began to enter the building. “Aren’t you going to read the card?” Damien looked at it, then at the man. If not for his sense of Greater Purpose, he would have lost his temper a great deal sooner. However, some battles invite themselves without any fanfare. This, as it was quickly presenting itself to be, was one of those. “It says that you are a sports agent. And frankly, I have never needed one. I think I’m going to pass up your services. But I do appreciate your interest. Thank you.” The man laughed, and Damien paused and looked at him. The sports agent was shaking his head and the laughter was trailing off. “That is a rather diplomatic brush-off. I know when I’m not being taken at face value. I get it. What you just said to me, that whole spiel about not needing an agent…it’s typical. It’s an average excuse because you are used to dealing with average people. Well, Mr. Cruz, I’m far from average. In fact, if given the chance, I would be among the most successful agents in this country.” The Latin Assassin felt a hard pull in the back of his skull, and the rational side of his mind, the aspect of himself that would hear the man out, smile and nod, and generally give the man the benefit of the doubt, was slowly being overtaken by the very irrational part of himself that would not afford the man such liberties. However, he was not without some civility, so he once again thanked the man for his time, and turned to the door. “Damien, if you walk away from this opportunity, you will regret it. The choice is yours; take the hand that being extended to you, or continue on the path to decline, insignificance, and frankly, disgrace. You know, if I were in your position, I’d think about the connections I have and the ones I need to start making.” For the second time, Damien paused. “Exactly what are you saying?” The man’s lips curled up like a jagged wound, and his words were spat if not spoken. “Your peak, your best, your better years are becoming a blur in the rear-view mirror, Damien, and I think it’s time you start opening new doors for yourself. I mean, there is no shame in using your former glory to fuel your future opportunities.” Then the world stood still. Before he realized how it happened, the best agent in the world was staring into the eyes of The Latin Assassin, and the wind in his throat was whistling through Damien’s fist. Damien pinned the man against the wall, and the sound of The Latin Assassin’s words hissed in his ears, which thumped with the sound of his heartbeat, desperate to get blood back into his brain. “I have a little advice for you, before you insult a man, be sure that he is not prone to fits of severity. As of right now, I have no reason not to smear the pavement with your shit-eating grin. You are hereby warned; if I ever see you again, I will not hesitate to show you how little you know about me, my capabilities, or the extents I will go to silence those who question me. So now, as the blood begins to pool in your neck, and the whole world starts to fade at the edges, I want you to remember this; you are nothing. You are insignificant, you are parasitic, and there is nothing you can offer me that I cannot wrench from the grips of this world myself.” In a gesture of absolute disregard, Damien released the man, who slumped down on the cold ground. The copper-red taste rose in the back of his throat, and he huffed in cold air that stung his airways. As he squeezed the pain from his vision, he watched as The Last Son of Lucha Libre straightened his collar, and walked into the arena without looking back. In the world of The Latin Assassin, some things did not require much effort.
