CUT. 52112 furrows his brow at the comment, but that's as long of a reaction as he can get. Before 76933 advances one more step, a shot rings out, blowing a hole in the pocket of Luis' jacket, and consequently, in the large agent's face. The man drops to the ground and everyone in the room instinctively reacts. The other agent dashes for him, and the bodyguards both dive for Danny, but he's already gone. CUT. DANNY FERGUSON I know you. MATT REGISTER (not even looking back, sighing) No, you don't. CUT. MATT REGISTER Well, they won't be happy about my disappearance, but I've got a new...business arrangement that will avoid any trouble. He holds up a small credit-card-like object in front of him. The back is plain white with a magnetic strip, but as he turns it over, we see the front as far more interesting. A large, gothic-looking 'C' adorns the front of the card, along with the label 'Cronus Enterprises.' After observing the card, presumably for the 23rd time, he tosses it back onto a dresser. CUT. DANNY FERGUSON Sorry, kid. It's a great story. Somebody should write a script about it. CUT. LUIS ALAZAR El Spiffy makes us into stars...then it makes us into pawns. CUT. LUIS ALAZAR Now it's time to for me to listen. CUT. FADE IN. INT. SPORTS BAR - NIGHT There are still a few more people dotting the tables around the place, but nobody notices the booth at the corner where our main characters sit. Of course nobody notices the booth. If there were supporting-character schmucks walking up and interrupting the scene every five minutes, then we wouldn't have much of a story going on, now would we? Danny Ferguson sits, eyes squinty, staring at the table just below Luis Alazar. Luis ducks his head a little, like he's trying to coax a look out of a shy girl, but he isn't so gentle with his words. LUIS ALAZAR What's the matter? Forget your line? Any vulnerability on the part of Ferguson is quickly done away with, or at least covered up by the kind of venemous defense mechanism he's become known for. DANNY FERGUSON Fuck you. LUIS ALAZAR Clever. It doesn't get us anywhere, though. DANNY FERGUSON What are you looking for? LUIS ALAZAR Let's try an ANSWER. All I know is that nobody sees or hears Danny Ferguson for the two years that you were El Spiffy!, and then afterward you vanish for two more. Then, all of a sudden, you're in the #1 movie in the box office. I don't know Hollywood, but I'm not retarded. That doesn't just happen. There's something missing there. DANNY FERGUSON Look, did you even know where I WAS before this fucking mask came along? Do you even know WHO I was? Don't talk down to me because I looked out for number one. LUIS ALAZAR Is that what you call it? DANNY FERGUSON Yeah, it is. I didn't kill anybody and I didn't shoot anybody - which is more than you can say. All I did was sign a contract, a sponsorship agreement. LUIS ALAZAR You're sponsoring Cronus? DANNY FERGUSON I have an agreement to eventually sponsor them. I'm in holding for a future agreement, and I - (mid-thought) You know what? You don't want to hear about my contract, so let's stow it, hey? Fact is, you've never been on top, so you don't know what it's like to NOT be up there, to not have anything you want at your fingertips. When Four Is A Crowd ended its run, the work didn't come in, and everyone said my 15 minutes ran up. FUCK THAT. A couple of people in the bar turn their heads at the outburst, but Danny drops his voice to prevent further attention. DANNY FERGUSON Someone approached me. Said that I sign a deal and Cronus Enterprises handles everything else. They get me a new manager, a new agent, they pull strings and sell me around Hollywood. Suddenly I'm not a former child star and a failed wrestler. Hard Money happened because I put my name on a dotted line, and I would do it again in a heartbeat. LUIS ALAZAR I'll be willing to give you some leeway here since you obviously are pretty uninformed about what you got involved in. But this can't keep going, Danny. DANNY FERGUSON Why? Because you showed up, jumped one of my people and shot some guy in the face? I didn't have a problem until you got here, kid. LUIS ALAZAR Yet. DANNY FERGUSON Excuse me? LUIS ALAZAR I was correcting you. You didn't have a problem YET. You will. DANNY FERGUSON Well, I appreciated the heads-up, Nostradamus, but I don't think we're here to talk about my choices. Alright, fine, I got set up by the mask and now Cronus has me. Fuck if I won't enjoy myself before that relationship ends. Are we worried about my future or our past? He stares at Luis with a fire in his eyes. Maybe it's fear, or maybe it's an agenda. Luis meets his gaze, and we know this is going to take a while. CUT TO: INT. LOCKER ROOM - NIGHT The latest edition of RUSH Wrestling's 'Adrenaline' program is still being set up in the arena, so there's virtually no one around. From a corner of the room, a familiar voice echoes. DANNY FERGUSON We gotta tone it down tonight. (pause) Are you kidding me? Do you know how my body feels tonight? (pause) Well, you can't feel, so maybe you'll have to take my word for it. (pause) No, no. I won't do it again tonight. I'm sick of sleeping in the hospital... The camera creeps around the corner of the lockers to show El Spiffy!, alone, picking through a bag. The bag is practically empty, though, as Spiffy! doesn't have any other clothes - he's already wearing his tights. He continues. DANNY FERGUSON You don't understand. My body HURTS. I can't keep this up. This time, when he pauses, he steps back from the bag and looks as though he's listening to something. You know the way you react when you're listening to a message on the answering machine, or having a conversation on speakerphone? That's how he looks. Except no one's talking. And it's kinda hard to fit a hands-free set inside that mask. DANNY FERGUSON I'm sorry...We can't keep this up. I- We jumped off of the RUSH-Tron at the Pay-Per-View two weeks ago! I ache, man. Why can't we take a week off? There's another pause. He puts his hands on his hips. I know what you're thinking - What the F is this Gollum/Green Goblin shit? I'm as creeped out as you are. Well, maybe not AS much. You've always been kind of a pansy. DANNY FERGUSON Well it's great that you feel like having another show-stealer, but what are you going to do when I'm too broken to continue? You just gonna sit and wait until I'm healthy again? This time, he doesn't move. He's not getting an answer. Under the mask, he smiles smugly. Getting one over on your imaginary companion is just as satisfying as doing it to a real one. Not that I...um...speak from experience or anything. Shut up. DANNY FERGUSON Yeah, that's what I thought- A door closes at the other end of the locker room. Almost immediately, El Spiffy! twitches slightly, like signs of a nervous tick, then turns toward the sound. Juan Carlos Smith, who we've met countless times already, steps into the open. JUAN CARLOS You thought what, Spiffy!? There's a fleeting second where both men look at each other. Just short enough for neither to give it a second thought. EL SPIFFY! The Spiffy! does not the think! The Spiffy! only the knows! JUAN CARLOS (sarcastically) Riiight. Well, they've almost got the ring set up if you want to run the ropes a little and get that soreness out. EL SPIFFY! Spiffy! knew that. JUAN CARLOS Just, uh...just making sure. EL SPIFFY! Sure. JUAN CARLOS Sure. EL SPIFFY! Sure. JUAN CARLOS Sure. EL SPIFFY! Sure. JUAN CARLOS Su- (realizing he's the only one who can stop it) Nevermind. See you out there. He turns for the door, and Spiffy!, with another twitch, turns back to his bag. He gathers his nonexistent belongings one more time, then sits down on the adjacent bench, burying his head in his hands. His shoulders are low and weak, and his breathing is harsh, a result of the wear and tear of the job. As the camera begins to pull away, he remains unmoving and quiet. VOICE The mask takes advantage of its hosts' desire for success and their willingness to sacrifice. It pushes the limits of the human body as well as the human mind. El Spiffy! stands up abruptly and heads for the door, pushing out of the locker room as the voice continues. VOICE But it is the fourth quality of the hosts, the shameless self-interest, that does not allow them to pull away. Success and fame are too great a drug to walk away from, no matter the price, and no one knows that more than the chosen. CUT TO: INT. SPORTS BAR - NIGHT The bar is all but empty at this point, save for that booth. The bartender is busy cleaning glasses and getting ready to close up. It's not even close to last call, but if the place empties out, he can go home early and miss the angry Angels fans coming to drown their sorrows before the World Series game. At the booth, tempers have calmed, but neither man is terribly comfortable, as you could guess. LUIS ALAZAR I've been following all the previous wearers, trying to catch up on where this has gone. Information gathering. DANNY FERGUSON Uh-huh... LUIS ALAZAR I was staying back, doing what I could without directly engaging anyone. Active contact - ie, what we're having right now - is a green light for the Watchers to step in, which is what happened back at your guy's office. DANNY FERGUSON Right. When you kidnapped me. Luis sighs, exasperated (big word!). Danny's been following along with a suspect tone in his voice. He sensed the momentum of their conversation shifting to his side, and took advantage of it to put Luis back on the spot. LUIS ALAZAR Listen. Two former Spiffys are dead. The other has a Cronus ring and a Watcher tattoo. I THOUGHT you were the one I could talk to before I saw that ring. DANNY FERGUSON Hey, the ring is just a passport to the bucks, kid. I'm no patsy. LUIS ALAZAR Well, as much as I value your word... DANNY FERGUSON Oh, and I should be valuing yours? Luis hasn't been challenged too much over the course of this entire story, so excuse him if he's a little flustered by being called out. They say the innocent people are the ones who look the most guilty during police questioning. This kid would be proof positive. LUIS ALAZAR Um...Excuse me? DANNY FERGUSON You heard it. Why do I trust you? (cutting a response off) When did this little 'fact-finding' mission of yours START, Luis? Luis starts to answer, then sits back a little. He looks around the place, which is now empty. The bartender must have gone to the back to clean up the stock. LUIS ALAZAR What are you getting at? Danny's smile is cringe-inducing, smug to the point that you choke a little. He moves in for what he thinks is the kill. DANNY FERGUSON Well here's the thing...you said Matt could take it off, right? The mask? (Luis nods) Do you ever remember having that luxury? I mean, if what you say about the mask moving is right, then I was ALLOWED to take it off eventually, but before then...nothing. What about you? Luis furrows his brow. LUIS ALAZAR No...I think the mask learned its lesson with Matt. It needed constant control, and starting with you, it clamped down. It wouldn't let go. DANNY FERGUSON Right. Sure. LUIS ALAZAR I mean, you felt what it was like. I'm sure you tried to pull it off just like I did. DANNY FERGUSON ...But it wouldn't come off. It was like a layer of skin. It burned to even try and loosen it. LUIS ALAZAR Not to mention the voice. DANNY FERGUSON Of course. The screaming, the arguing. Always in your ear. Always needling. LUIS ALAZAR Right. DANNY FERGUSON Right. There's a pause. Danny's had a very intense and interested look for this entire exchange, a look that Luis hasn't seen on him for the entirety of their company, even after detailing a rather hefty story about ethereal forces and prospective Mexican hoodoo. If the man could've slept through that, what's piquing his interest all of a sudden? LUIS ALAZAR I'm sorry, I don't think I follow your point. Danny leans forward a little, his voice harsh and accusatory. The way your voice gets when it sounds like every sentence could be punctuated with 'motherfucker.' DANNY FERGUSON I don't know if you've checked out a mirror as of late, but you're not wearing the mask anymore. LUIS ALAZAR (confused) Of course not. DANNY FERGUSON Well you don't exactly look like a broken man, like you've been tossed aside along with the rest of us, and yet we're face-to-face like any other two crazy people in Los Angeles. So I'm thinking maybe you've bypassed that process, and maybe you've got something you want to share about YOUR employers. LUIS ALAZAR (begging off) And I'm thinking you need to back up a little. I think the running like a dog and the shooting a Watcher in the face is enough proof thst I'm not on that team. DANNY FERGUSON Then where's the mask, kid? Why aren't you stuck under it anymore? Luis swallows hard. Truth be told, once he was free of it, he never thought to ask that question, just that he ought to get away. He never thought about how different his experience seemed to be from the rest. Unfortunately, though, the answer to Danny's question was about to be postponed for a series of others. JUAN CARLOS / AGENT 52112 (O.S.) Because I turned it off. Both men look over at the agent, who stands by himself at the head of their booth. Just beyond him, though, are two men with rather large guns. The bartender is nowhere to be seen. Luis' shoulders sag, and Danny, as has become the norm lately, looks confused. JUAN CARLOS / AGENT 52112 I think you two should come with me. CUT TO: EXT. CITY STREET - NIGHT A very familiar-looking 'LUCHAS SABADOS' sign sits outside on a street corner, but as we pan up beyond just the sign, the skyline is most definitely NOT Mexico. The Searss Tower looms amongst the cityscape, so bold and dark amidst the night sky that you expect a pack of cavement to be throwing bones at it while 'Thus Sprach Zarathustra' bellows. Chicago is just beginning to rise at 7:30, but here on the West Side, everyone is headed to one place. The warehouse that sports the aforementioned poster has a line stretching down the block and around the corner. And among that crowd, wearing a very familiar black hoody, is Luis Alazar. CUT TO: INT. WAREHOUSE - NIGHT The ceilings are low enough that a crazy indy star could try a twisting somersault something-or-other off the rafters and bust their head. However, that hasn't stopped the Olympia Mundial de Lucha Libre (OMLL) organization from running shows at this venue week after week. It's not the biggest show in town - that title belongs to Old School Wrestling, which has confined itself to downtown Chicago thanks to a 'business arrangement' with the DaVatta family. But the four-match OMLL cards here on the corner of Pulaski and Armitage are the closest that the immigrant section of West Chicago can come to traditional lucha libre, thanks to a small crop of dedicated workers and fans. Tonight, though? Tonight's different. Scattered among the usual fans are a few more caucasian faces, more than just the locals and the local smarks. It's a very mixed crowd, thanks to tonight's special guest. I'd keep talking, but all these paragraphs are wearing me out. So I'll defer to the two pock-marked and gross-looking gentlemen who are eager to discuss the big story of the evening. LOSER #1 And they didn't sign him? LOSER #2 Not even a temporary deal. The best they'd offer him is a CDC contract for 'development.' LOSER #1 Seriously? LOSER #2 Seriously! What the fuck were they thinking? LOSER #1 Hey, doesn't bother me. OSW would just tell El Spiffy! to cut his act down and start doing comedy matches. LOSER #2 No kidding. If they picked him up, I wouldn't have had a chance to see him for $10 tonight. It's OSW's loss - the guy gets a huge reaction for a whole weekend of house shows, and then you show him the door for another Stanislav main event? Please. LOSER #1 Well, you know how Jane Reagan loves those oversized guys. You can't get ahead in OSW unless you're over 6'3. Thank God Matt Register puts those boot lifts in. LOSER #2 Dude, is that proven? They continue repeating all the things they read on the message boards that morning as the camera pans away. We catch sight of Luis Alazar sitting in the front row as the warehouse fills up. CUT TO: INT. WAREHOUSE - LATER The place is rocking as the familiar sight of El Spiffy! in the ring brings people to their feet. He bounds to the top rope and prepares for one of his trademark airborne maneuvers. He pauses, though, hindered by the low rafters, and eventually jumps off, only to be caught by his opponent and slammed to the mat. A few seconds later, there's a three count, and the crowd explodes. The Spanish-speaking ring announcer chimes in with the victory announcement, but not everyone is paying attention. In the ring, El Spiffy! is back on his feet cursing and spitting. A career (or 1/4th of a career, in the hosts case) of shortcomings and deferments is only so much for the human spirit to take, and we have already established that our main characters are not pinnacles of human character. At some point, it becomes too much for the pride to bear. VOICE It is the avarice of the host that permits the mask the opportunity to move forward, to transition itself. Truly, the mask controls the change from host to host. El Spiffy! paces the ring, tearing at the back of the mask, and a stir begins to rattle the Chicago crowd. VOICE It makes its move by convincing the host that they control it, that they have the option to move on and leave the mask in their dust, when that circumstance is very much to the contrary. Spiffy yanks at the mask, pulling it from his head with one final heave, letting a gasp through the crowd. Matted red hair puffs out a little, freed from the prison that had constricted it for so long. Certainly not the hair of a future action movie star. With a final f-bomb and a baseball wind-up, Danny Ferguson hurls the mask into the crowd, where it lands in the lap of Luis Alazar - the only fan left seated in the front row. A few of the white fans scramble for the souvenir, but they're held back by the regulars, who spit their venomous Spanish slurs at the actor-turned-wrestler-turned-has-been who stands in the ring. The crowd closes in as Ferguson bails for an exit, eager to escape before anyone can really make a positive ID. As some of them push for the exit and some push for the locker room, El Spiffy stands at his seat. He looks down at the prison he holds in his hand, not aware of the role reversal that awaits. CUT TO: INT. VAN - NIGHT Danny and Luis are cramped into seated positions just behind the front seats of a large conversion van. The vehicle bumps along the nightlit highways of Los Angeles. Somewhere, a taxi cab piloted by Jamie Foxx is carting a gray-haired Tom Cruise around. But that's another story. There are two other men in the back. One is a heavily-armed man from the bar - the other is behind the wheel. The fourth man is Juan Carlos, or Agent 52112. Whichever you prefer. I prefer Juan Carlos, even though it's not his real name. JUAN CARLOS You boys gave us a little scare back there. Neither man responds. Luis stares daggers through the 'manager.' Danny just looks at the roof. JUAN CARLOS It's going to be a bit of a drive, and I get the feeling you won't be talking much once we get there, so now's the time for questions. Danny looks down, then at Juan Carlos. Then at Luis. Luis gives a quick 'no' shake to him, trying to establish some solidarity. Danny looks back at Juan Carlos. DANNY FERGUSON What the fuck do you mean you turned it off? Juan Carlos smiles, and if you were in Danny or Luis' position, you would instantly want to kick his face off, it's one of THOSE smiles. But neither man reacts as such, probably because of the assault rifle aimed at them, but who knows for sure? Finally, JC opens the jacket he'd been wearing since his reappearance in the bar. He pulls something from a large pocket and drops it on the floor of the van in front of both captives. THE MASK. The bright and grinning El Spiffy mask looks up at the two men with hollow eyes. As it hits the floor, and recognition sinks in, both men cringe and turn away. It may as well have been a neutron bomb. Content that he has their attention, Juan picks it back up again and holds it on his left hand like a grotesque puppet. JUAN CARLOS It's a good thing you two are sitting down. He thinks for a second about how to begin, lowering the mask and staring at it as he passes it back and forth between his hands. JUAN CARLOS It was somewhat providential that you American hosts were poor masters of the Spanish language. Luis scowls, since that was an indirect jab at his counterculture views of his Mexican upbringing. JUAN CARLOS The Watchers. It was an accidental term, a mistake. Probably someone who'd watched Highlander too much. (pause) The real term is Los Vigilantes. The Guardians. We weren't just around to keep you guys out of trouble. We were steering. We were handlers, pulling on the leash instead of letting the animal run rampant. Four eyes are locked on Juan Carlos and the cursed mask that passes between his hands. JUAN CARLOS You see, Cronus Enterprises has its hand in a lot of things. It's an umbrella corporation for a family of different outfits, ranging from international weaponry distribution to washers & dryers. Whatever pays the bills. He pauses again. There's a certain way to handle revelations like these so as to not sound like a supervillain, but Juan Carlos is working without a net. He stops passing the mask around and lets it rest at his feet. JUAN CARLOS Several years ago, one of the companies under the Cronus banner was commisioned with a new project: an experiment on the manipulation of human emotions through electromagnetic waves. (pause) It took several years to perfect, and there were several successes and failures before it could even get off the ground. Eventually, the receptor was developed as a very small microchip, easily hidden but fatally powerful. He wrings his hands as sweat beads on his forehead. Hearts are racing, and Danny starts to squirm with a hard sinking feeling in his gut. Surely, nobody has a choice over how their life is laid out in front of them, because if they did, no one would have to face a reckoning in the back of an unmarked conversion van going just over the speed limit down a Los Angeles highway. JUAN CARLOS Problem was... (pause) ...was that they couldn't implant the chip without first knowing how effective it was. No test animals had the brain capacity nor the size of humans, so the only option was a true human test. (pause) But how do you get a microchip that close to the human brain without directly inserting it into the body? He lets his actions speak for themselves, and picks up the mask again. He turns it inside out, and gently moves his thumb across a section just at the forehead, between the eyes. A slight squarish bulge is evident, not big enough to feel, but easily seen when you know where to look. The cloth around the area is double stitched. JUAN CARLOS You two were part of a five-man experiment on the effectiveness and limits of the NetBIOS chip, which has since made advancements beyond exterior contact. To that extent, I guess I owe you a thank you on behalf of the Cronus family, and particularly, on behalf of our American partner who developed the experiment... The final pause, and easily the biggest. If this was an episode of Lost, the music would have built and the camera would zoom in on Juan Carlos' face as he delivered the last word: JUAN CARLOS ...GlobeTECH. FADE OUT. TO BE CONTINUED.
