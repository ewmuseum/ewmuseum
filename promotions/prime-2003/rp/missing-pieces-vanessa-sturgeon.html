Missing Pieces: Vanessa Sturgeon

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: ReV 226 Tokyo, Japan- 9:17 p.m. 09/03/09 “You don’t look happy.” He was not. In fact, that might have been one of the furthest emotions from Tyler Rayne’s current state that she could mention. He sat in the office of The Katana’s Point, feet planted to the newly installed glass floor. The soles of his boots pressed flat against the reflective surface, he was conscious enough not to put too much of his weight down at once. Kairi’s idea to replace the old (and quite functional) carpeted flooring with the same viewing panes as the office walls had been… inspired. Aesthetically, it was an ingenious idea. The office hung suspended over the middle of the dance floor, surrounded by a wall of two-way mirrors that allowed them full view of the club. Anyone in the office could look out the mirrored glass and see damn near the whole building. Those looking up from the dance floor would see their own reflections smiling back at them. Unless, of course, the curious club goer were standing directly beneath the office. That was before. Now, with the freshly installed tempered (and supposedly quite safe) glass plate, anyone directly beneath the office could see their reflection as well. Anyone sitting in the office, as Tyler was now, could look down and observe the patrons below. It was… a bit discombobulating at first. So he sat, partially reclined, in the firm (but amazingly comfortable) tan seat in Kairi’s office. The chair provided incredible lumbar support as he leaned back, arms relaxed and hands clasped in his lap… legs cautious not to displace too much of his own weight upon the glass. Not that there was even the slightest chance he would go crashing through. The immense desk behind which he sat weighed twice what he did. Kairi had been bustling in and out of the office as per her usual nature for weeks now. Still, he couldn’t quite shake the paranoia of falling through. Even if he knew better. Tyler popped his right eye open and took a sideways glance across the desk, noting the blurred image of his personal assistant there on the other side. He closed his mouth, ebbing at least some of the exasperation from his expression, and sat up. The usual growth of dark stubble around his jaw was starting to fill in. Much more of a beard and a clear indication of his distraction the last couple days. “Seriously, you look like hell.” Juli Lee handed him a mug of steaming tea and sat down in the chair across from him. It was a large, plush leather seat that nearly engulfed the small woman. She took a sip from her own mug and nodded toward the computer monitor. “Who’s she?” The images of a forty-something woman had been queued up in various windows of differing size. She was a very plain woman. Big, dirt brown hair. Teased. Kind smile, but a bit crooked. Her teeth were uneven. She had no obviously distinguishable features. Soft eyes. Paler than usual skin. In a few of the pictures she was wearing some gods awful jumpsuit dated from the early nineties. “Vanessa Sturgeon. Her brother Matt was a friend of mine.” The Chinese-American (former) paralegal might not have been working for him long, but she had learned to read him well enough. She noticed the small wince at the mention of his name. Tyler spoke with as little emotion as possible. He was obviously tired and the woman’s name rolled from his tongue in a very matter-of-fact tone. His name, however, came out with just a tinge more emphasis. Tyler’s lips curled ever so in the corner when he said it. As if mentioning it caused him some bit of pain. Knowing him, it most likely did. She knew better than to press more than the obvious issue. “What happened to her?” “Suicide. Back in ninety-four.” “So this is what you’ve been doing for the past thirty-two hours? Looking up suicide victims from the mid-nineties?” He leaned back in the chair again and took a sip of tea. Eyes drifted toward the monitor. The woman. The answer to all his questions. “I killed her.” “You just said she committed suicide.” “Yes.” A second of silence. “Awesome. You need some sleep. You sound crazier than usual.” “I’m fine.” “Beg the differ.” “Jules, this is it. This is the key I’ve been missing. Everything makes fucking sense now. Milena’s been searching non-stop for Drake since he got snatched. We can’t find him anywhere. It’s been months. In theory he should be dead. These fuckers took him, though. No ransom. No body. He’s out there. Somewhere. Hidden. I’ve got two dozen properties stateside still smoldering from some serial arsonist who somehow found every single apartment I owned across the country. A serial killer now stalking and taunting me. One that I was hired to track down myself, mind you.” “OK. What does any of that have to do with some soccer mom who died fifteen years ago?” “She’s the epicenter. It all just… ripples out from her.” “Sleep. You seriously need to get some – ” The door to the office burst open. Both of them turned to stare at the unexpected intruder. She was standing at the corner of the desk, having covered almost fifteen feet in the literal blink of an eye. Milena Takahashi. An absolute goddess. Japanese perfection. Beautiful. Sensual. Assassin. “I found him.” She threw a stack of surveillance photographs on the desk. The same four, maybe five block radius from different angles. Different viewpoints. A warehouse district, somewhere on the far side of Tokyo. Mostly abandoned over the past few years. In each of the pictures she had circled the same building in red marker. “There.” Tyler took half a glance at the photographs and attempted to control the sudden explosion in his heart rate. It couldn’t be. It just fucking couldn’t be. “He’s there.” “You’re sure this is the place?” The question was rhetorical. He knew with absolute conviction that this was the place. The words just sort of tumbled from his mouth before he could stop them. Barely even realized he’d said them, to be honest. A thousand different thoughts blitzed across neuron tendrils, distracting him from the conversation at hand. The puzzle pieces collected in his mind… blurring in the edges of his brain …forming a more coherent picture in the center now. One he should have seen long ago. He thought the discovery of Vanessa Sturgeon’s fate was the answer to his questions. This, though… now. Unbelievable. Milena paused before answering. The sheer size of the desk before her was imposing. Tyler had insisted on turning off all the lights inside the club, so the office was surrounded by dark glass and strange shadows. His sudden withdrawal shocked her. Made her more aware of the situation. Made the office feel like it was closing in, though it was very apparent he was a thousand miles away. Milena’s eyes flickered from the distant gaze of Tyler Rayne’s own brown oculars to the black and white photograph she had just supplied him. The buildings had not been well cared for. The Japanese assassin had expected a reaction from her revelation… though Milena now realized she had not known what to expect. “I watched them take him in.” “How’d he look?” The edges of her mouth turned downward. The observations she had made were… unsettling. The answer to his question something that had troubled her since the sighting. Milena shifted her weight from one foot to the other. She did not answer. He raised an eyebrow and turned his gaze toward her. She could feel the weight of his concern, but could not bring herself to voice the accusation. He knew. Somehow he had concluded the very thing she feared. The evidence presented a plausible scenario, though she still lacked an idea for motive… Of course he knew something she didn’t. Some long forgotten string that tied this all together. Milena was not used to these conflicting emotions. The world she inhabited was made of strict and defined roles. Good and evil. Right and wrong. Drake had been a clear cut example of the former, but now… “We have to rescue him.” “We’ll find him.” He spoke with complete confidence. There was an edge to the words, though. A harshness she had not expected. It did not sit well. He had not used the same term and she noticed. Her gut twisted at the lack of an assurance of Drake’s rescue. She turned from the desk. Took a step toward the door. No intentions of leaving, but it was easier if she wasn’t facing him. Juli watched over the whole scene in silence. She weighed the words and subtle body movements of two and knew better than to interject. Something profound was happening here. “Was he walking?” Milena hesitated. “… Yes.” “Bound?” “Hands. Not the legs.” “Still in one piece?” “From what I could see. It… this situation is odd. I have searched for months without a sign or trace. He disappeared. Along with the ones that took him. Now, suddenly, he reappears. I watched Drake walk into that warehouse. Walk. No more than a handful of guards to escort him. None with the same training as those who kidnapped him.” “Clan Tagashi.” “The Ninth Clan?” She could not hide the surprise from her voice. “That does not make sense. Yamato Tagashi is dead. He had no heirs. You…” “…killed them. Yeah. I thought so, too. Yamato was the ninth member of the Yakuza Council.” “The Son of Nine. How?” “Fuck if I know. Honestly, I wasn’t even convinced until… this. I figured someone had heard the story or something and was just fucking with me. Could’ve been coincidence. Now… well, now I’m sure someone is fucking with me. Someone who knows their history. This warehouse used to belong to Tagashi.” Milena began pacing. Her mind flashed with thoughts and images. Pieces of the puzzle he himself had just put together. She watched them fall in place and wondered if he knew what she had found. “Seeing Drake… disturbed me. I knew there was something odd. I did some research.” “Christ, kid. You just found him last night.” “So you’re not the only one who’s been up way too long.” They both ignored the remark. “I had to know. I traced the lineage of the buildings in the area. After the death of Tagashi, the warehouse and the surrounding buildings were bought and sold four times. There was a pharmaceutical company, some sort of technological industries… a robotics developer… the buildings were traded and sold to various owners up until just a few years ago. Then this upstart business appears. Fusion Aeronautics. Specializes in computer chips used on satellites and space stations. Fusion starts purchasing all the warehouses that used to be owned by Tagashi… including this one. Their work is legit. Fusion Aeronautics does develop hardware and software for use in space programs, but…” “It’s also a front for some of the Jade Scorpion’s less than legal dealings. Fuck. I should have… FUCK! How did I not see this?” “See what?” Juli had not been around for all of these dealings. She hadn’t come on until after Tyler’s death at UltraViolence. Though she knew the names and had heard the stories… there was obviously still something missing, because she certainly wasn’t putting the pieces together. He stood. Took a step toward the corner of the desk. Paused. Turned back to the computer. Away. His eyes adopted that far off glaze. Lost in thought. Milena watched him quietly. The implications of these discoveries were chilling. “What now?” “I have to talk to Kai. She needs to know about this.” He waved one of the photographs in the air. “Jules, can you call Katt? We need her here yesterday.” “Sure thing, Ty.” The young woman quietly removed herself from the chair and shuffled out of the office. Silence filled the room in her wake. The two remaining friends stood mere inches from each other. Neither could speak for what seemed like an hour. “Milena… I’m sorry. I really am. I was hoping it wasn’t true, but…” “It’s OK. I had feared the worst when we didn’t find him. I just did not realize what the worst could be.” “There’s something on the computer you should read. Things will make more sense after that. Then, you need to get some sleep. We both do. We’ll be leaving as soon as Katt drags her ass in.” “Tell me we’re going to get these fuckers.” “Every last one. I promise.” She nodded. He left the room. Milena continued to stand for some time before she could actually convince herself to move to the chair. She pulled up next to the computer monitor. Something cold and all together vicious began to settle in the pit of her abdomen. The assassin could not wait to start killing.
