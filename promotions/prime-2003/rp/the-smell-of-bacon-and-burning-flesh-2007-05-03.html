The Smell of Bacon and Burning Flesh

3 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/3/07 This is Heaven… with the most soothing sounds that seem to come from nowhere in particular. With clouds and laughter, plenty of sunshine, the occasional warm rain, and people. Lots of people. This is Heaven. The voice bellowed from the sky above. Like the first clap of thunder from a summer storm that popped up out of nowhere, it made him jump a bit, though he noticed his fear instantly vanish. Around him, he saw children running up and down a hillside composed of the greenest grass he had ever seen. The slope ran down to the edge of a colossal lake, whose surface was so bright rolling under the intense sun, that the flickering pattern of light from the cresting waves should have given him a seizure… but he just stared, not even blinking. And that was when he noticed the kids racing across the surface of the water… on foot… like a rat pack of teenage Jesuses. “What the fiddlesticks?” As soon as he finished the sentence, Matt had another thought. “Wait a minute, I said ‘fiddlesticks’ not ‘fiddlesticks’.” “In Heaven, you can’t say the ‘F’ word, unless you really mean it… and no one ever does.” A young boy, not even ten, sat smothered in the shade of a large evergreen, playing a Gameboy of some sort. He looked up at Matt, who was nothing but a silhouette against the burning sun, then immediately returned his attention to his video game. “Who are you?” “I’m George.” “Like my daughter?” “Very much like her.” The boy tilted his head to his left. “You can sit down if you want.” “No thanks, I’ll stand.” Matt once again surveyed the scene, still partially in a state of shock at the images that he found. The boys on the lake laughed uncontrollably, splashing water at one another, shoving each other around. And each time one fell, they landed on top of the water, as if it were literally a sheet of solid glass. Then they’d thrust their hands into the lake, reaching under its surface, and pull out fistfuls of water that soaked the others. It made no sense. Then again, neither did the snow covered mountains in the horizon that hadn’t been there only a minute before. “How did I get to Heaven?” “Probably by dying. Seems to be how most people get here, anyway.” “So,” Matt went on with his next question, somehow completely satisfied with the child’s simplistic response, “how long have you been here?” “Long enough to make it to Level 73.” George pushed a few buttons and his hand-held game played a triumphant sounding tune. “74” “What do people do around here?” “Pretty much anything they want. Go swimming, fishing, rock climbing, watch movies.” “People sit around and watch movies in Heaven?” “All the time.” George nodded his head, but didn’t take his eyes off the game. “God has an awesome DVD collection.” “Bigger than the rap stars from MTV Cribs?” “Not that awesome.” Matt looked back over his shoulder to the lake. There were no more kids splashing in the water, just some adults strolling peacefully across its surface. Then he noticed that a man had on the same red and blue shirt one of the children had been wearing. As it all started to come together in his head, the sound of George’s game snapped him back around. “So… do I get to meet him?” “God?” “Yeah.” “Not standing here. Go look around; there’s a lot to see.” “Ok. Well,” Matt leaned forward a bit and shook the boy’s hand. “It was nice to meet you, George.” “It was nice to finally meet you, too.” In a flash, George jumped up and sprinted down the hill. By the time Matt spun around, the child was already cannonballing into the water. A splash five times too big for a kid his size erupted skyward… and he was gone. Matt found himself smiling as he headed in the opposite direction of George, moving past the evergreen. Beyond the tree, a gorgeous city sprawled across the landscape, and he made his way for the entrance of the first building he came to. Inside, a crowd of people moved back and forth across the black marble floor of what appeared to be the lobby of a five-star hotel. Light-hearted conversation and effortless laughs swarmed around him. “You look a little lost, sir.” A man in a Hawaiian shirt strolled up to Matt and put his hand on his shoulder. “Can I help ya?” “What is this?” “Only the finest hotel in all of Heaven.” “Heaven has hotels?” Matt gave a puzzled look as he watched the movement of the room. “Of course. People in Heaven love vacation too. Only here, it’s even better! There’s no job to have to get away from or go back to, you can go just about anywhere you want, and everyone can stay in a top-notch place like this. Absolutely no worrying about nasty bed sheets in Heaven!” “That’s comforting to know.” Matt turned, this time, to look at the man as he spoke. He was the stereotypical ‘nerdy-guy-on-vacation’. Palm trees on his shirt, khaki shorts that were a little too short, sandals complete with socks, and a floppy hat. The only thing he lacked was the glop of sunscreen smeared across the nose. Probably, Matt decided, because people didn’t sunburn in Heaven. “So, you guys just vacation whenever you want?” “Yup. Why not? Heaven isn’t too restricting, ya know.” “Apparently, it has a word-filter.” “Oh come on, that’s funny.” “I don’t think so.” “That’s because it’s happened to you.” The man chuckled. “Just wait till your walking along the beach and you see some new guy lean over to his girlfriend, kiss her on the neck and say… ‘Baby, lets go back to my place, and we can fiddlesticks all night long’. You’ll laugh then.” The man adjusted his hat, and on cue, Matt laughed. “See.” There was a moment of awkward silence… well, it would have been so on Earth… awkward, it seemed, didn’t exist in Heaven, and Matt carried on casually with the conversation. “I still can’t believe I’m here.” “Most people can’t. What is it that has you so surprised?” “I haven’t exactly lead a pristine life. I’ve goofed a few times.” Matt shoved his left hand in his jeans pocket and rubbed the back of his neck with his right. “And I’d probably be lying if I said my faith, or whatever you wanna call it, hasn’t wavered a time or two. “How so?” The vacation guy pulled up a seat out of thin air and ordered a milkshake from the bar, where a sign hung above that read… ‘Welcome to The Heavenly Hotel. Please help yourself to some complementary anything you want.’ “Ya know… start doubting when stuff goes wrong. Like when your career looks to be heading down the toilet, when the girl of your dreams walks out on you, or when your wife has a miscarriage. Those sorts of things.” “The miscarriage… it was a boy, wasn’t it? The baby?” “You guys up here must know everything.” Matt wanted to feel uneasy about the question, but couldn’t. “It’s one of the fringe benefits.” “Yeah, a boy. We were always gonna name our first child after Mary’s father. When we had a baby girl a few years later… we just went with it. But I was supposed to have a son named George.” The final words escaped his lips and hung in a cloudy image right in front of his face. A son named George, not even ten. “No way…” “Yeah. Your boy’s a real wiz with the video games. And funny as all-get-out. Probably gets his sense of humor from his father.” “So I… I just met him.” “That’s what Heaven’s all about. Getting to experience the great things ya never got the chance to on Earth.” The man’s milkshake arrived and he took a sip. “You’re a good guy Matt. You may doubt it, but I think you belong here. Ya just look to the wrong places for the wrong things sometimes. But I think you’re catchin’ on to that. And you’ve done some good things lately.” “Like what?” “Well… you offered Tony Gamble a chance to get that Gold Ticket he wants so badly. That was a nice gesture by you.” “You’re not serious? It was manipulative, heelish…” Matt trailed off, not thinking the point needed to be made any further. “Well when you put it that way… guess we’ll have to move that one over to the ‘sins’ side of the board.” “It wasn’t already there?” “It was a bit of a stretch, but we’d managed to work it onto the ‘good deeds’ list. Until you went and said it was manipulative. Anyways, you’ve made some improvements in your life. You’re coming around, but you’ve got work to do yet.” “Yet? I’m here, aren’t I?” “Haha… you’re not dead, Matt!” “I’m not?” “No.” “So” Matt scratched his head, “this is a dream?” “It’s an experience. You’ll see and do and learn things you never have before. It’s a bit of a ride, just go with it.” “Go where?” “To the next stop. This is kinda like an afterlife version of Dickens’ A Christmas Carol, only you get to go to Hell next, not ‘The Present’. Sorry.” The man took another drink of his milkshake and waved goodbye. Matt waved back. Around him, the laughter was suffocated by a heavy heat that crept against his flesh. The lobby bar collapsed, the smiling faces of a hundred happy vacationers faded away, and The Heavenly Hotel was no more. * * * Thursday, May 3rd… 8:17A.M. He could feel every vertebra in his spine separate as he pushed his hands high towards the heavens, stretching away the last bits of slumber. There had been no dreams. None. For eight hours, his mind had shut down, not allowing a single thought or sight or sound to enter his head. At 12:08A.M. the night before, the red numeric glow of the clock had drowned in darkness, and only now had that black canvas been interrupted. Next to him on the bed, a crumpled mess of covers occupied the spot where he had expected to find Mary sound asleep, hopefully as peaceful in unconsciousness as he had been. Taking a moment to allow all his senses to come to him, Matt noticed the smell of breakfast cooking as it waltzed through the air around him. Stretching one last time, he shoved the sheets over the edge of the bed and swung his feet to the floor, dragging them across the carpet as he headed for the shower. Lazily, he reached and turned on the hot water, holding his hand under to gauge the temperature. While he trudged back and forth across the bathroom, readying things for the shower, he let his mind wander to thoughts of the huge match he had in front of him in just a few days, the one last ride he was about to take, and breakfast. Man, he was craving that breakfast. His mind was so lost in thoughts of main events and toast, that he didn’t notice his life about to change, right there in the trashcan. Ten minutes later, he shook his head like a dog, flinging the last drops of water in his hair across the bathroom mirror, pulled on a pair of gym pants and strolled into the kitchen. Mary was just setting two plates onto the table, each covered with bacon, scrambled eggs and a slice of toast. “God bless you.” Matthew slid out a chair and let himself fall into his seat, then his meal, in one fluid motion. “Thanks, sweetie.” She took her seat across from him and started stabbing at the eggs with her fork. “What’s wrong?” “What do you mean?” “Just the way you said ‘thanks’… I dunno… just, makes me think something’s wrong.” He stopped the conveyer belt of bacon running into his mouth, good manners and all, and waited for her response. “Nothing’s wrong.” “You sure?” “Yeah.” “Ok.” Satisfied, perhaps too quickly, Matthew went back to work on breakfast. “So whatta we got planned for today? “I dunno, I think I’m gonna take it easy.” “Still not feeling well?” “No, not really.” “Well,” Matt finished off the bacon and spread some butter across his toast, taking a large bite before it’d even had a chance to melt into the bread. “maybe you should go see the doctor. We can go this morning if ya want.” “Naw, that’s not necessary.” Mary took a bite of the eggs and chewed them slowly, every bit as lost in thought as Matt had been when he’d hopped in the shower. Her thoughts, however, were slightly less superficial than ‘mmmm…bacon’. “So remember when I told you that I wanted you to retire from wrestling when you were ready…” “Yeah, what about it?” “How soon do you think that’ll be.” She still didn’t have his full attention, the orange juice had that. “Soon.” He downed half his glass of OJ. “I figure this is my last run in the business. Why?” “I’m glad to hear that?” “Why’s that?” “I took a pregnancy test this morning, Matthew.” He stopped his fork halfway to his mouth and a large clump of scrambled egg splashed onto the plate below. Now, she had his full attention. “You…” “Congratulations. You’re gonna be a daddy again.” * * * This is Hell… with the most unsettling sounds that seem to come from everywhere around you. With fire and screams, plenty of darkness, the frequent smell of decay, and people. Lots of people. This is Hell. As the stench of burning flesh worked its way into his nostrils, his lunch rose through his stomach and into his throat, and he had to choke back the vomit. Already, he was missing Heaven. The scene in front of him looked every bit like he’d always imagined Hell. It was very much like the core of a volcano, at least, he assumed… he’d never really seen the inside of a volcano. Everywhere there were rocks; the ground beneath him, the walls around him, hanging over his head… grey and brown stone as far as he could see in any direction. A large lake of magma bubbled and steamed as its overflow caressed the crevices in the ground, forming tiny orange streams here and there. Matt began to walk forward towards the lake, but made it no further than two steps when he turned his ankle in one of the countless cracks beneath him. “God Dammit!” “No wonder you’re down here.” A monstrous looking hand reached down and helped Matt off the ground. “Gotta be careful around this place. You’ll feel plenty enough pain without going and tripping over your own two feet.” “Thanks.” As Matt regained his composure, he saw the ‘being’ that had lent him a hand. Its flesh wasn’t that unlike the rocks around them, both in color and texture. Even at over six feet in height, Matt felt small next to its stature. “Who are you?” “I’m the guard.” It stopped to point to its right. “That’s my dog. Together, we patrol Hell and stop all the runners.” “Where” Matt glanced at his surroundings, “do they think they’re running to?” “Don’t know. Our jobs just to stop them.” “So,” Matt reached down and rubbed his ankle. For a simple miss-step, it was absolutely killing him. “this is Hell?” “The outer layer. Here, only your body is at harm.” “And the inside… uh… inner layer?” “There, your mind takes control.” “Beg pardon?” A large bubble of fire burst from the ground, causing Matt to stumble back. This time, his fear didn’t dissipate so quickly. “In Hell, you get to torture yourself. Its really not anything you aren’t already used to, only more intense. Much more intense.” “Fantastic.” Matt started to walk forward, but as he did so, the guard put out his hand to stop his momentum. “No.” Matt tried to step around, but the decaying dog at the guard’s side growled, flesh falling from its quivering jaws. “Besides, where exactly would you run to, Matt?” “Good point.” “You go that way.” The monster pointed a finger over Matt’s head. For some reason, he decided arguing wouldn’t do him much good, and hesitantly, he turned inward. It was just a split second later that things began to morph. The general ‘volcano’ theme remained, but a series of ‘stations’ sprawled out in front of him. Each was open to the environment, set atop a their own small plateau. Their specifics seemed to vary, but every one held a common feature… a large jagged rock that seemed to act as some sort of horrific chair. “Jesus.” Matt paced passed several of the plateaus, looking for some sort of end to them all. What he found, instead, was an image that stopped him dead where he stood. “Mary!” He climbed the side of the rock, digging his fingernails into the gravel. Waiting at the top, Mary sat alone on the ground, tears streaming down her face. “What’s wrong?” There was, not surprisingly, no response. “What the hell is going on?!” Matt turned to his left, to the next station over, and screamed to his tortured neighbor. “What is all this?” “That, is your Hell.” In front of the man, a gorgeous brunette pulled on her hair and screamed with delight while a gentleman ‘entertained’ her. “This is my Hell.” “My Hell…” “Yeah.” The look on the man’s face showed centuries of misery and anguish. “Hell is what hurts you the most. In your case, it’s your wife’s pain… you wouldn’t know what that looks like, cuz you’re hardly ever around.” “So my Hell, for all eternity, is to watch the pain I caused my wife for the decision I made?” “Cruel, isn’t it?” The man turned back towards the image in front of him just briefly. “I get to watch my cheating wife bang my cousin over and over and over. That’s what she’s doing up on Earth now that I’m not around… come to think of it… that’s what she was doing up on Earth when I was around” He turned back to Matt. “That’s every man’s Hell. A nice little room, a jagged, burning rock as your throne, and the scene that pains you the most playing on and endless loop right in front of your eyes. And all the while you sit and watch, you get the distinct pleasure of listening to those around you suffer as miserably as you are. Their cries are your background noise, like someone left the radio on some fucked up station while you were trying to watch TV, and you’re too lazy to get up and turn it off.” “Oh man…” Matt collapsed backwards onto his rock. Its rough surface burned his flesh and he winced, but didn’t move. “Oh shit.” “Yeah.” “How do I end up here? What did I do?” “Turns out… lying, deceit… not things that make the decision makers happy.” As the man pushed himself across his boulder, repositioning himself so that he was facing Matt’s room, the very real image of his wife and cousin followed him, shifting itself in the line of his sight. “So… its dishonesty that gets me all this?!” “You can’t just keep getting away with lying and hurting people. Sooner or later, it’s too many lies. Too many broken promises. Too many hollow words. And then, there’s a price to pay. You’re about to cross that line where it’s too much to go unpunished.” “I’m trying…” Matt pulled at his hair, in a very different way and for very different reasons than the man’s wife was doing. “I’m ready to retire. This can be my last run. I can make things right by my family. I can be a father and a husband I won’t hurt them anymore.” “I know, but it’s more than that, Matt. It’s not just the lies and meaningless words. You’re a bad friend. A violent man with nasty intentions that you like to hide inside and pretend aren’t there, but you’re about to give yourself away. He’s actually a good guy… you’re the ugly one. All that bullshit talk of ‘nothing but respect’ and ‘buddies simply in an unfortunate situation.’ You acted like you were his friend, and the truth is you hated him… resented him… but you sat back and fed everyone a load of crap about ‘honor’.” “What are you talking about?” Matt pushed himself off the rock. “It’s not just the mistakes you’ve made with your family. It’s not just the lies to Mary.” “Then why THE FUCK am I here?!” “You’re here… after all that talk of respect… for what you go and do to Nova Sunday night.” The man turned back, his wife following him. Matt let his head sink, cursed through tears, wondered why he hadn’t stayed retired, and somewhere in the distance, lava spilled around a large rock, burning its core, causing it to glow an almost delightful orange, just waiting for the next in line to take their seat.
