Date: This Week "You're killing me, Danny. Do you know that? You're killing me. I'm dying right in front of your eyes." "You might be blowing th-" "How can you come in here and tell me you won't be at the premiere?" "Prior engagement." "No no no, THIS is your prior fucking engagement! When we signed that contract, More Than Hardcore became priority number one." "Something came up." "Push it back down." "The star doesn't need to be there." "For the debut of a movie? You don't need to be there to sell the picture?" "Sells itself." "Not without an ambassador to smile for the newsmagazines." "Mann won't be there?" "No one knows what he looks like. Hell, I couldn't pick him out of a lineup. Fans want to see YOU." "Come on, Al. You know that the lead interview in all the coverage is going to be Terry Funk anyway. He's quotable and he's done the Hollywood thing himself, so they'll put him everywhere." "No, they won't." "I think I've watched enough E! to know tha-" "Terry's not going." "He's what?!" "He called the studio on Monday and said he won't come to the premiere. They showed him a final reel last week and he didn't like the way it was cut." "I told them to keep his scene." "It was hokey bullshit." "It was a peace offering." "Anyway, he refuses to attend." "That's terrible!" "It's not great." "It's going to kill us in the media!" "I'd consider it less of a fatal blow and more of a papercut." "Allan, if the wrestling fans hear that Funk doesn't like it, our opening weekend will suck." "So we should re-cut and pander to the NASCAR demo?" "It's the core audience." "Wrestling fans aren't the core anything right now, D. Maybe if this was 1997 and every movie-ticket-buying yokel had an Austin 3:16 shirt in their closet, we'd be concerned. Do you know how many REAL wrestling fans are out there anymore? I'll give you a hint - not enough to affect our box office. What DOES affect it, though, is if the fucking star won't show up at the premiere!" "Overkill is that Sunday. I need to be in Houston." "Nevermind the easily-recognized joke that you're allowing this little 'side project' to interfere with your day job just one week too long, and it's an event called Overkill. Why this needs to take precedent over one of your finest hours as an actor, I'll never understand." "It's complicated." "No shit." "When you want to be a crossover star you have to make tough choices." "You're kidding me, right?" "Do I look like I'm kidding?" "Do you know what a crossover star is, Danny? It's someone who has no business in Hollywood forcing their way in by being popular somewhere else. Nobody who's already here tries to 'cross over' anywhere else." "I'm a boundary-breaker." "You break a number of things - movie contracts, studio heads' hearts, my patience - but let's not mistake this vanity project as something noble or history making, unless we're speaking in terms of catastrophe." "Wrestler-actors have a tougher road." "But you're supposed to be an actor-wrestler." "I move back and forth." "Which, in and of itself, can be written off as a loss." "I don't understand why it's such a big deal. People like The Rock. They like Hulk Hogan!" "Oh, COME ON! Those guys are meatheads who learned to read a cue card! People are impressed that they made it through a sentence without using the word 'ass'. You are an ACTOR, or at least you're supposed to be." "Do I pay you to talk to me like that?" "You pay me to keep you on Best Week Ever instead of I Love The 80s. Lately, I've had a rough go of it." "What's that supposed to mean?" "It means that I let you get into this PRIME bullshit because it was a marketing move. It kept you in the public eye when we were between projects. Then when you didn't want to move on to the next project, I found something for you to double-up on, so you got to stay in the ball pit a little longer. Now it's gone on too long. I'm starting to feel like a babysitter." "That's funny, because I feel like I'm getting treated like a kid because I'm doing a little TV work?" "...a little TV work..." "Yeah, that's what it is, Allan." "A little TV work is a cameo on Entourage - which they offered and retracted - or a three-episode arc on 24 - which Kiefer suddenly reneged on in February." "We had a little thing." "Is that like the thing you had with Irakoy Kincaid three weeks ago?" "That's nothing. My legal team applied some pressure. The linebacker and his boys didn't want to take it to court and have to talk about what else they were doing at the party." "Yeah, well, you're lucky that the NFL didn't get involved. We wouldn't have studio lawyers to back us up." "We wouldn't need them. Cali judges would side with the actor over the athlete every time. "What about the professional wrestler vs. the athlete?" "Since when did you become Phil Mushnick?" "Since you came here and told me you're not attending the premiere of the movie that could skyrocket you to the top shelf in this business!" "Did you read that line straight off a promo poster?" "Something's wrong with you, Danny. I don't know if it was the jail time or something else, but I can't believe you reached the point where this became optional. You tell me you want to be Dwayne fucking Johnson when everyone I talk to says you can be the next Arnold?" "I have somewhere to be. I don't see why you're so pissed." "Why am I mad? Are you kidding me?" "No, why are you pissed?" "Is there a difference?" "Being pissed means you have some personal attachment. You can be angry, man, perturbed, even a little ticked. But pissed? What does this matter to you other than a slight delay in a commission check?" "I..." "..." "..." "What is it?" "The only thing this town ever wants to know is how big my dick is." "Uh, excuse me?" "I move here and try to come up in the world as a legitimate businessman in the talent representation business. I play all the right cards. I never backstab anyone who didn't deserve the knife. I do everything as fucking close to the right way as one can do to stay alive in Hollywood. But do you know the first thing people ask me in a client interview or an after-hours buisness meeting over drinks? They ask me if my cock is as big as my porn star twin brother's." "I don't get it." "No kidding." ""You know what? Fuck this, I-" "Obviously I've learned to deal with the dick questions, because that's what business in Hollywood comes down to - everyone taking it out and seeing who is bigger. Now everyone wants to see how big my Hollywood cock is. How many three-picture deals have I negotiated? How many franchises have my clients scored? When it comes down to reppin' actors, am I packing heat or am I just some needledick who came here to ride a famous name to an easy payday?" "Allan, believe me, we're all impressed with your story of triumph - rising over male inadequacy issues to become a moderately successful Hollywood representative - but unless you're pitching me a script, I don't see the poi-" "Don't pretend to be surprised when I say you're my highest profile client, Danny. You knew that when you hired me. You wanted a fucking putz who'd work his balls off for you and not offer his own opinion. And I love you for it, because you put me on the map and gave me something to sell to other clients. But don't do what I did and mistake gratitude for loyalty." "..." "A year ago I had America's next great action star on my hands. We had a surprise summer hit, a change-of-pace dramatic role on deck and franchise offers falling in our lap. NOW, I'm repping a fucking World Tag Team Champion whose greatest hits have included a three-month jail term and a public brawl with an NFL player." "He wasn't an NFL player yet." "What the fuck ever. If you gave up your wrestling fantasy in February and signed on Green Lantern, I'm the golden boy, a guy who took a two-bit child-star-turned-wannabe-hero and turned him into a bankable megastar. But instead, you pass and get labeled a flash in the pan headcase, and I'm the enabler who went out and found a wrestling movie for the boy who wanted to be Hulk Hogan." "Like I said before, Allan, you're blowing this way out of proportion." "Maybe, but how many careers get wrecked by being overly concerned? You brush this off like you can weather the storm, like it's just a hiccup. It's not a hiccup for me, Danny. If this seems personal to me, it's because I'm seeing myself get dragged down with the Danny Ferguson ship." "I'm telling you, it's not that ba-" "And I'm telling you IT IS. Just show up at the premiere, Danny. Skip a day of training and press stuff for your stupid wrestling show, we'll right this ship and get it through the opening weekend, and the phone will start ringing and I will work my balls off for you like I always have." "Yeah?" "You have my guarantee that the next project will be signed by the following Monday, right after MegaKill." "Overkill." "Sure, whatever." "I'm just not sure if-" Some early screeners went out this week, and feedback has been good." "Al, I-" "There's no discussion. You NEED to be there." "Are you giving me an ultimatum?" "If I have to, yeah. If you're not here for the screener on Thursday, I'm going to need to do something about it." "Like what?" "If you want to throw your future in this industry away to be a wrestler, then you don't need me." "Hold on a second, Al..." "Well, you've made it pretty clear that-" "You can't just drop me!" "Actually, there's a stipulation on the contract..." "You know I'd get hung out to dry in the trades!" "...My own lawyers are pretty good, and..." "If you drop me, I'd crucify you in the 'what happened?' tabloid feature." "...might have to do some damage control on my end, but..." "You wouldn't get another client in this town." "On the contrary, I'll bet my portfolio triples. Think about it, Danny - will Hollywood blame the guy who put YOU in a Michael Mann film? The guy who had the Green Lantern people beating your door down?" I can't believe you're threatening me with this." "I never thought I would have to." "That's fucked up, Allan." "That's Hollywood, Danny."
