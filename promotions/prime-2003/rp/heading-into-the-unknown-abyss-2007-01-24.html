Craig didn’t have the slightest clue what to expect from South Africa in terms of the weather, yet he liked how it felt. He could breathe in comparison to the normal Florida humidity and it helped ease his soul slightly. As he sat outside on a patio that was attached to his hotel room, he had a perfect view of the sun setting before his very eyes. The hue of the sun was majestic here in Johannesburg as it lowered itself beneath the trees to allow this section of the world to sleep a few hours before moving on with their lives and taking one step closer to death. As PRIME embarked upon it’s World Tour leading up to Culture Shock in March, Craig had started to see the world around him a little bit more each day. Last week, they had been in Rio De Janeiro, Brazil and now they were in South Africa. Seeing the people made it all so much more real to Craig. Tonight, thirteen people would die from murder, natural causes, or a disease. That thought continued to run through Craig’s mind as he had been more in tune with death over the past few weeks. He felt sorrow for all of them as he knew what death felt like. He knew how the soul vanquished into thousands of pieces as it raced out of the host’s body. His biorhythms had changed over the past few weeks into a purely emotional cycle. He perceived things in a much different light then he had over the past few months. Everything around him had slowed down enough for him to analyze things in a way he hadn’t done before. Before, everything was physical. Things raced past him without him even realizing that. With the betrayal and deceit that he had experienced, he had to start thinking before acting and making decisions or else he would spiral even faster into a darkness that even he couldn’t prevent. He couldn’t risk losing sight of everything until he had his daughter back in hand. He owed her that much. With the light around him dimming by the second, street lights as well as the hotel lights started to flicker on as if they knew that the light was fading so fast. He sat in the wicker chair dressed in a pair of dark blue jeans and a black knit sweater. His bare feet rested upon the stone balcony that surrounded his patio. Around him there was nobody. There was nobody except on the streets below, yet he didn’t feel connected to them. Instead, he felt like he was in his own world without anyone to answer to. He liked that feeling and wanted to immerse himself into it a little bit more, allow his thoughts to flow, and see things that he might not have seen before. He had taken the time to analyze the past few months to see if it all made sense to him. Unfortunately, they hadn’t. Instead, he found himself hitting a dead end at every corner inside his mind. Specifically, last week. His right hand could still feel the imprint of her neck. That sensation made him shudder with fear as to what he was capable of and what he was starting to become. Yet, he had to do these things if he wanted a chance to get his daughter back. He would go to no end to get his daughter back. He needed her as much as she needed him.
