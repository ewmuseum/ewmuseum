Let's Play Two (w/ Lindsay Troy, ft. Wade Elliot)

20 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/20/07 "You know, I honestly have to say I'm surprised that you didn't spring for the suite. Who would have thought Danny Ferguson would want to be amongst the commoners." Danny's met with a smarmy grin as he snapped his head to the right to look at Lindsay Troy, sitting next to him with a half-empty cup of beer in hand. The roar of the Wrigley Field crowd momentarily drowns out his response, and when their rowdy bleacher-mates take it down a half-decible he replied with a know-it-all smirk. "Occasionally, I like to step down from the clouds and walk amongst my people. Besides, the boxes in this place aren't much better than the cheap seats." "No need fer that fancy shit anyways," piped in Wade, lounging in his seat to Danny's left. "I'd guess ya git a nice view from up there..." Wade stopped mid-sentence as a pair of tight-clothed, giggly college girls strode past the three lounging PRIME superstars. "...but the view down here's just fine." "Someone hit the eye-roll just now," Danny remarked to Wade, who chuckled. "Gee, what was your first guess?" Lindsay replied, "And...whoops, there goes another one." She gulped down some more of her beer, then looked over her shoulder to see if a concessions guy was coming remotely near them. No such luck. "'Always come prepared,'" that's my motto," replied Wade after finishing his own beer, only to retrieve hip-flask from his back pocket and pour a brown liquid into the cup, taking a strong swig after capping his flask. "At least it would be, if I started havin' a motto." "If anything, your motto should be 'Bring a Map.'" "Falls in the 'be prepared' category." Lindsay reached across Danny and extended her index finger. "Except when you get lost in the woods and have to call me to tell me you don't know where you are." "Yeah, and a whole lotta help ya were, weren't ya?" replied Wade, sarcastically. "Hey, my cars have GPS," she threw her hands up defensively. "You're the bullheaded 'Bama boy who's barely surviving in the technology age." "I'm gettin' along just fine," Wade retorted, sipping his bourbon. "I ain't movin' too slow, it's the world that's movin' too fast." He offered Danny the flask, but Ferg waved it off with little hesitation. "No thanks...I don't make a habit of accepting a drink that's been in someone's back pocket." "Funny," Wade replied, putting it away. "I don't make a habit of offering my stash to prissy boys, but I did anyway on account of the occasion." Troy shot Wade a look, but Danny feigned offense, showing remarkably thick skin. "Just remember who got you the tickets, mountain man." "Right. 'Cause I woulda hated spendin' the afternoon in one the fine local drinking establishments." "Oh please, we're at Wrigley Field," came the scoff from the Queen. "Like you would have passed up free, hard-to-get tickets to go sit in a bar with Angus." She kills her beer off and puts the cup on the cement. "Who the hell do I have to scream at to get another fucking beer?" "WE'LL GET IT FOR YA, QUEENIE!" The three turn in the direction of the shout. Two rows up, a group of six mid-to-late-twenty-something guys sit, double-fisting beers while hooting and hollering along with everybody else. Having finally gotten the PRIMEates attention, they start bashing their cups together. Lindsay smirks, then lowers her shades down her nose and gives them all a 'what's up' nod. "You guys go find me a beer man, and I may have to give ya a kiss." "FUCKIN' RIGHT!" And, trying not to trip over the feet of the people sitting next to them, all six up and book it out of the row. Troy turns back around and shrugs her shoulders. "Hey, I'm not married anymore." Elliott nudged Ferguson, mumbling to keep himself out of Lindsay's earshot. "Someone's been takin' tips from Faith." "I HEARD that, Drifter. And the next time you compare me to one of the Microphone Sluts, I'm putting your balls in a jar." Wade tips his drifter's hat low, stifling his chuckle with a big swig of bourbon. "Hey, if you can do it, I can do it," Wade drawls, then continues checking out the scenery as more girls walk by. "What, make out with some Northside yuppie men?" Troy laughs at Danny's smarmy comeback. Wade no-sells it and cracks a smile of his own. "Strong words from a man who was playin' Youngblood's bitch last week." "Ah, Brandon just gave me a few love taps, no pun intended." Danny gives Troy a weak elbow. "I'll be back up and moving in time for the main event this week." "Oh, I have no doubt of that," Lindsay smiles, then hears the thundering of feet and the whooping of success. "Must be the calvary with the beer." She stands up to find the six guys from before, plus others and, most importantly, the beer guy. "Don't get jealous now, boys," she says to Danny and Wade, then steps over them and out onto the steps. "Heyyy, my savior with the Sam Adams!" "Come on, LT!" Danny moans. "This ain't Fenway! You get Old Style in the bleachers or you get NOTHING!" The twentysomethings raise their beers at Ferguson's rallying cry. All six managed to buy one for the Queen, so she graciously passes two each to Wade and Danny. One of the young men cocks his head at Danny. "Hey, you're the actor!" "You could say that!" "I loved you in-" he turns to the others. "He's in that movie I told you about! When he throws a poker chip through the dude's neck and shit!" Troy looks at Danny. "Through a guy's neck?" "Special effects." "I know that, assface, I meant...nevermind." The dudes all quote Hard Money poorly, and the one who noticed Danny notices Wade sitting there, too. "And you're...uh...you're that guy..." "Never been one for big-time recognition," the Blue Collar Brawler remarks, then raises his glass. "But I thank ya anyways." "Hey Danny, you're gonna be, like, so perfect in that Roulette match." Ferguson's grin fades pretty quickly at the Cub fan's attempted compliment. "Yeah, thanks." "No seriously. You're like, you're gonna go in there throwing poker chips and shit, and motherfuckers are gonna be like, 'aw damn' and just jump outta that cage." "No way, dude!" shouts another one of the guys, slapping his friend's arm. "Queenie's got the belt, she's gonna kick everyone's ass, fuckin, uh, fucking Warrior Princess style." Troy smiles off the praise, turning to her allies. "Been a while since I heard a Xena reference. Yikes." She notices Ferguson's suddenly-sullen demeanor and gives him a nudge. "Something wrong." "Nah, I just...nothing, it's nothing." Behind them the discussion wages on. "Dude, Danny's been tearing shit UP in PRIME! You see what he did at Colossus to Nova!" "Fuck that, man! Nova was cool! Besides, Troy won the belt off Nova to begin with, and she's kicked plenty of man-ass!" "Yeah, well, she probably couldn't hang with Danny in a one-on-on-" "AAAALL right, boys," Troy says, interrupting before things grew any worse. "First of all, um, yes I could. Secondly, we'll cross that bridge when I come to it. Thirdly, are you all gonna be at the United Center Wednesday?" "Shit yeah, Lindz, floor seats!" "Good. So you'll get to see me and Phil Helmuth over here take on the world's most mismatched tag team in the main event. THEN we can talk about who's better." She silently points to herself as she finishes up the statement, and the guys all laugh emphatically in the hopes that she might perceive them as "not like the other boys." No luck. Troy's keeping an eye on Danny, who has taken to staring off in the distance as the conversation went further. "Something you want to talk about, champ?" "Nah. It's nothing, really." "Is it..." Wade leans in to avoid having to say this very loud, "...man problems?" "Ugh. No." "Thank God," Elliot sighs, sitting back. "'Cause I wadn't gonna hit the restroom with ya but I figured I could lend an ear if you were having some sort of cowboy moment." "Real sweet of you, Wade," Troy laughs. "I think Danny's just having to-" "Can we just not talk about it?" Danny snaps. "Please?" "Okay." Troy sits back a little. Wade shoots her a "what the hell?" look and she mouths "I don't know" back to him. Nobody quite knows what to say for a few seconds. Suddenly, a light bulb goes off over Ferguson's head and he perks up. "Shit!" He checks his watch, then swings around to look at the beer delivery boys. "Hey drunks, what inning is it?" Wade looks at Danny sideways. "Haven't you been paying attention?" "Come on. No one comes to Wrigley to watch the games!" Up above them, the six men are embroiled in deep discussion trying to figure out how far along the game has gone. He gestures to Troy. "See? Hey, dumbasses, check the scoreboard?" With a couple of f-bombs and other expletives, the men turn around to the massive scoreboard overlooking the bleacher section. "It's, uh...it's the sixth. NO! Fifth. It's the fifth." "Shit," Danny repeats, standing up. He shuffles over to the aisle. "We gotta go." "Go where," Troy asks, standing up to follow him as he bounds up the steps to the exit. She looks back at Wade, who is slowly but surely rising to meet her. "Probably needs to change his tampon after the griping he's doing." "G-ross." Troy says, walking past her beer boys. They all pipe up, asking for some digits or the kiss they were promised. "I'll see you boys at the show, right?" They all nod in the affirmative, like lapdogs. "Great. I'll catch up with you then." If it was possible for men to swoon, they all would have just swooned. But maybe it was the beer. - DF - LT - WE - "So you DID spring for the suite?" The three walk along the catwalks just above the main crowd of fans, just outside Wrigley's small collection of luxury boxes. Since the walkway is in open air, quite a few fans below recognize them. To be fair, how many Amazon women travel with undersized red-headed actors and low-key country gentlemen? Troy and Wade wave to a few, but Danny stays slightly ahead, checking all the doors and not answering the question above. "If I'd known we were goin' hiking, I woulda worn my boots." "You're always wearing boots, Wade," Troy scolds, knowing full well that she's pitching him a softball (pun intended). "Well then I woulda bought another beer." She shakes her head and the two of them catch up to Danny. "Not this row," he grunts. "Where are we going, Danny?" Troy asks, growing irritated. Ferguson slows to a stop and opts to answer the other, unspoken question rather than the direct one. "I'm sorry about getting short back there. I've just...had a lot of things on my mind. And then the Roulette thing kinda caught me off guard." He breathes deep, not wanting to delve further there. "And then Youngblood kinda caught me off guard." Lindsay nods and gives him a faint smile. "I gotcha." "I'm sorry," Danny repeats for emphasis. Emphasis that is then interrupted by Wade. "Well that's real sweet of you, Danny. Apology accepted." "Not you," Danny says, the smirk returning to his face. "I don't apologize for anything I said to you." "Wouldn't have it any other way," Wade drawls. "'Sides, you shouldn't apologize to no one for bein' short. Can't fix the way God made you." "Funny," Danny deadpans, finally finding the door he wanted. "It's a real wonder you don't have more friends." "I've got Jack, I've got Jim, I've got the lady here, what else do I need?" Responding with just a wave of his hand, Danny pauses in front of the door. "So I didn't get us the suite, but I did one better. I figure, if we're gonna get the Wrigley Field experience, there's one thing we gotta do." "Are you gonna tell us or are you gonna keep standing in front of that door until someone pulls a Sonny Silver on you?" Troy teases, prompting Danny to swing the door wide open. Wade and Lindsay peer inside. "The PA booth?" A man in a headset spins around to see them. "Are you my stretch singers?" "That's us," Danny says. "Or rather, her." "Wait, what?" "You remember the words to 'Take Me Out To The Ballgame,' LT?" Danny says, as realization sinks in for Troy and Elliot. Meanwhile, a flurry of motion pulls Troy away from them and gets her prepped, Cub jersey and all, for one of baseball's most well-known traditions. If there was ever a "deer in the headlights" moment for Lindsay Troy, this would be it. "Oh no." The man in the headset perks up. "She doesn't know the words?" "She knows the words." "I know the words. I just don't want to do it." "Don't worry about it, ma'am, everyone gets butterflies. I mean, the people give you a little leeway if you're tone deaf, but go too far off tune and they'll crucify you." "I'm not tone...ARGH. I'm not doing this. No dice." "Aw, c'mon, Troy!" "Then YOU do it, it was your brilliant idea in the first place!" she hisses as a production monkey holds out the Cubs jersey for her to put on. The Red Sox fan in her wretches. "Me? I'm just some wash-up actor." "And I'm just 'that guy' according to Jimmy Cubfan out there." "You, on the other hand, are PRIME's Universal Champion." "Among other accolades, thankyouverymuch." "Well, right. But that's all that matters as long as we're here." "There's only one way I'm going to do this." "What's that?" Moments later, the last out is called in the top of the seventh and the crowd rises to their feet. High atop Wrigley, Lindsay Troy tries hard to avoid looking embarassed as she's introduced to the crowd...and then as Danny Ferguson and Wade Elliot, each wearing bright white Cub jerseys of their own, are introduced alongside her. The two men, trying hard to keep from laughing, give her a nod and the Queen of the Ring turns out towards the crowd. "Everybody ready? And a one, and a two...TAKE ME OUT TO THE BALL GAME..."
