Date: June 7th, 2008 Thursday, June 7, 2008, 12:30AM CDT Hilton Garden Inn Detroit, MI "This is a big promotion, Dam. What do you mean you're not sure about it?" Dametreyus Fuqueiawytas shifts uncomfortably on the comfortable king-sized bed in Lindsay Troy's hotel room, eyes cast down to the off-white carpet and hands resting, clasped, over his knees. He'd been in this same position since walking into her room and taking a seat at her request. Troy, meanwhile, sits in an executive-style office chair, feet up on the mattress and her laptop in her lap. "Ain't like I ain't grateful for it, or that I couldn't do it," he mumbles, marble-mouthed. "Well, then what is it?" Her fingers click furiously over the keyboard and her brow furrows in thought. "I'm still paying attention, don't worry." "It's just--I ain't done much else but bodyguard work lately an' I figgered after you's was done with the Crazy--" All clicking stops. Lindsay pulls the computer screen down toward the keyboard and looks Dametreyus square in his chocolate-brown eyes. "Is this about Danny?" Silence. "Dammmmm..." "He ain't gon' be too happy 'bout this..." In return for his confession, she grants him a sympathetic look. "Dam, listen, Cantrell may not have a finger on the pulse about much in this company, but he's right about this Director of Security job. You love it here. Everyone here loves you, and it's not like Danny doesn't have Jim." "Ain't the same, Boss. You know that." "Yeah..." she trails off as the gears turn in her head. "I'll talk to Danny." "What?" "Look, there's nobody who can talk to Danny Ferguson like I can talk to Danny Ferguson. You've worked your ass off for him, you've worked your ass off for me because he told you to, and you need to take this job because you want to. So don't even stress." "But-" "No 'but's," she smirks, lifting the laptop screen again. "And I'm throwing you a party to celebrate. Next week, since I doubt I'll get anything on this short of notice." Dametreyus opens his mouth, but her right index finger springing into the air silences him. "No. No more protesting. It's about time we all showed you some appreciation. I'll make the arrangements. Heavy thuds echoing down the wall, Wade Elliott swings his head left and right, counting under his breath as the golden numbers of so many shiny white doors passed him by. If the Southern Sparkplug wasn't contrast enough in this pristine hotel environment, the pants of his companion, Angus the Bluetick Hound, finished the job. "There we go. 823" Wade rapped his work-worn knuckles against the door a few times before stepping back and shoving his hands into his jean pockets. He looked around from under his old drifter's cap, weary of the watchful gaze of security cameras. He waited some more. "Maybe she ain't in," he said to himself. Angus whined. "Ah, quit yer blubberin'..." After a moment, the sound of flip-flops clopping against heels filters underneath the door before it swings open. Lindsay Troy stands there wearing a black tank top and shorts with her hair up in a towel. She throws a half-grin at Wade, whose eyebrows hitch upwards slightly. "What'd you do, take the Pony Express here?" she quips, petting Angus on the head. Wade takes a moment to tip his hat and clear his throat. "You um, ya need a minute?" he asks, looking her up and down to refer to her "shower look." "Nope, I'm dressed. Well, not in what I'm going to wear later, but I'm fine. Come on in." Angus doesn't need to be told twice, as he bounds into the room and immediately takes up shop on the king sized bed, tail thumping happily against the mattress. Wade lumbers in, but doesn't get too far inside before he's halted in his tracks by Troy pulling at the waistband of his jeans. "What're you...?" he turns his head around to see her inspecting the tag on the inside "OK, good, keep going," she orders. When he doesn't move, she gives him a gentle shove. Wade steps forward, confusion spread across his face, a tad flustered. "What in the hell's gotten into you?" he asks as his hands work to adjust his jeans. "Don't ask so many questions, just go sit." Wade grunts and trudges over to the bed. As he sits down on the edge, Angus crawls over to lie next to him. Lindsay, meanwhile, is now tugging at the collar of his shirt. He tries to pull away, grabbing her wrists to resist her snooping. "What the hell'samatter with you? Quit pokin' 'round!" "Oh, don't be such a baby. I'm done." When Wade doesn't let go of her wrists right away, she wiggles her fingers in front of his face. "Can I have my hands back now?" "You gonna stop snoopin'?" "For the moment." Wade squints his eyes, then reluctantly releases his grip. Lindsay rolls her wrists in mock hurt, which elicits an eye roll from him. She walks over to the closet, slides one of the doors aside and produces two Nordstroms bags, which she carries over to where Wade's sitting. "Scootch, Angus," Lindsay instructs, waiting until the Bluetick stands on the bed and moves further back before setting the bags where he was just laying. Wade lifts a cautious eyebrow to the bags, and almost visibly shies away from them. "What're those?" "Shh," she says, a shrewd smile forming as she rummages. A moment later, a dutch blue Tommy Bahama button-down shirt is produced along with a pair of Diesel bootcut jeans. Lindsay places the jeans on the bed, then holds the shirt up against Wade's chest. "Matches your eyes. It's your size, like I guessed, but I've got a size smaller and larger in case it runs funny." "Aw no, nononono, you ain't prettyin' me up fer some bar! It ain't gonna happen!" The shrewd smile grows wider. "But it is." "Good fuckin' luck! I ain't never worn no collar, and I ain't 'bout to start! You wear the damn thing, I'm goin' as yer seein' me!" "You are absolutely NOT going to Dam's Promotion Party wearing that hanging-by-a-thread T-shirt, faded-out, perma-dirt jeans and ten year old boots. Look," Lindsay reaches into the other bag and pulls out three boxes, "I even got you new, somewhat-respectable-in-public, boots." "My own shit-kickers'll do just god-damn fine!" "Can we talk about these for a second?" She bends down, grabs Wade by the ankle and lifts his foot up against her thigh. "What are you holding these together with? Super glue?" He pulls it away quickly. "An' what if I am?!" She grabs his foot again as the towel slips off her head. "Look, these? Not conducive to our partying schedule. Those over there? Same style, same color and the only difference is the brand name and the fact they're polished." "Non've which means a good god-damn to yer's truly!" An exasperated sigh escapes Lindsay's lips as she lets his foot slip down to the floor. "Look, this place isn't a dive, alright? Most of the roster is going, I invited his volunteer firefighter friends and some of his poetry group members are flying out here for it. If you want to go like that then...fine. Wade clears his throat, readjusting himself to the awe that is the dolled-up Lindsay Troy in a rich, one-strap magenta dress that fits snugly and hugs hazardous curves. Perfectly matching with the tight sheath are peep-toed pumps that put her inches above Wade in height. "Weren't no picnic, but I'll live," he says as calmly as he can. The alcohol hits her harder than she expects once on her own two feet, but probably isn't too surprising considering how much she's had since Rayne's unexpected and uninvited arrival. She silently thanks herself for having the presence of mind to be as inconspicuous as possible in her intake. Her slightly-glazed eyes travel down to the dance floor, to the hundreds of people jumping and grinding and screaming off-key, and she doesn't allow her orbs to linger any more than a fleeting moment. A thought of her stopping to tell him she's leaving too pops into her brain, but the haze filtering through instructs her that he'll be alright and to not interrupt him. "I was just bein' polite. Would've rather been drinkin' with you, but it looked like you were wantin' to be left alone." "I just—I don't know," she shakes her head, getting her bearings after the sudden stop. "After the interruption of Rayne being there, I didn't want to interrupt you, so I kept off to the side with a stupid amount of rum and coke." Wade offers a small, sincere smile. "I would've enjoyed the interruption.." His smile is reciprocated. "Maybe next time then." The cab finally pulls onto the street, driving off into the night. Behind it a pair of headlights belonging to a green, 1995 Dodge Caravan come to life, pulling out behind them and giving quiet chase.
