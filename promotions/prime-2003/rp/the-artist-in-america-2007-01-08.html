The Artist in America

8 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/08/07 Some months ago... To say Georgie Clevelander was nervous would be a gross understatement. Whenever a young go-getter is given his first real opportunity in his career, there is a chance to rise to the occasion or to go down in flames. But in Georgie’s case, failure didn’t mean losing a big client, it meant an international incident. See, Georgie worked for the US State Department. To a lot of people, diplomacy conjures up images of the movers and shakers of the world emerging from sleek black limos or ominous helicopters to exchange firm handshakes and steely expressions with their equally firm and steely contemporaries. Or perhaps dreams of lavish dinner parties at the palatial homes of ambassadors, dozens of beautiful and exotic people from beautiful and exotic lands waltzing until midnight. But when people looked at Georgie Clevelander, they thought he was a substitute teacher for a junior high. It wasn’t that Georgie didn’t try. He wore the best suits he could afford. Unfortunately, a low level civil servant didn’t make very much and his off-the-rack attire fit him about as well as an old bed sheet worn as a ghost costume. And the baby face didn’t help. Just the other day an old lady working behind the counter at a gas station actually pinched his cheeks! Georgie could feel his testicles falling off and rolling down his pant leg to the ground. Then there was the hair… a mess of curls with a mind of its own. When people saw Georgie’s hair, they always assumed he was running late for something. His boss always checked a clock when he entered a room she was in and Georgie could tell she was mentally making a note, even if he was early. But what really made people disregard Georgie Clevelander was his complete and utter lack of backbone. The man was a milquetoast, to use dated terminology. Georgie always preferred to reason his way out of difficult situations instead of stooping to violence. To his dismay, he discovered those difficult situations most often chose to ignore reason and just stick with the violence. If it ain’t broke… So Georgie thought the State Department was his way to change the world. Make it listen to reason more. But he soon realized just how many parts of the world were simply geo-political versions of the bullies at beat him up as a child. And everyone could see that Georgie was still afraid of bullies. As a result, Georgie got stuck with research a lot. Need to know the names of the children of the prime minister of Belgium’s brother-in-law? Go ask Georgie. Have to learn to sing "Happy Birthday" in Swahili in less than fifteen minutes? Georgie’s the man. He was good at research. He excelled at research. But the idealistic, and bloody-nosed, child inside him still wished to make an impact on the world. Which bring us to Georgie standing in a terminal at JFK holding a sign written in Chinese. "Babysitting" isn’t exactly a plum assignment, but it’s a way out of the office. None of the other agents wanted it. They looked down on the idea of escorting a foreign national who wanted to be, of all things, a pro wrestler. No helicopters or dinner parties there. Not Georgie though… he was a fan. From the days of the territories to the nights of Pay-Per-View, Georgie was hooked. He never got along too well with his old man. George Sr. was a military man, he didn’t take a shine to his son’s insistence on diplomatic solutions to the world’s problems. And Georgie was just plain scared of the old bastard. But put the pair of them in front the TV when wrestling was on and they were two peas in a pod. Those were some of Georgie’s fondest memories of childhood. And maybe that’s why he’s so excited about this assignment. And maybe why he was sweating so much. Damn, why is it so hot in here? Actually, the terminal has the AC cranked way up. Some people are chilled, but not Georgie. Of course, those people aren’t waiting for someone they’ve never met from a country their government isn’t especially fond of. While they put on jackets and sweatshirts, he’s got puddles forming under his armpits. A drop of sweat rolls off of his nose and hits his loafers. Finally, after an eternity, Georgie notices people exiting from the gate. Unfortunately, it looks like there are quite a few Asians arriving in New York that day. Jesus… which one is she? That file photo was awful, like she hated to get her picture taken. So many of them… maybe I should get closer… Just then, Georgie feels the sign being taken out of his hand. He quickly looks over and sees a stunning woman in a dark suit and sunglasses holding it, he straight dark hair prefect down to each and every strand. With zero expression on her face, she turns the card upside-down and hands it back to Georgie. Or rather, she turns it right side up. Georgie finds himself sweating even more and wishing he could just melt away. "Oh… uh… sorry. Uhhhhh… Sun Tzu?" The woman gives him a small nod. "Uh… ummmm… Ni hao. Errr… Wo… uh… Wo-duh ming… ming… ming-d’zih George Clevelander." Georgie sighs in relief and holds out his hand, smiling at the woman and hoping she ignores the dampness of his palm. She ignores the offered gesture and instead removes her sunglasses, staring at Georgie with a look that sends his heart to his throat and his stomach to his shoes. "You are with the State Department, correct?" she asks with a trace of a British accent. Sun Tzu reminded Georgie of a photo of a bullet passing through an apple. So still, so beautiful… but the violence was clearly there. "Uh, yeah…" "Then the next time you butcher my language I shall rip your tongue out and send it in a diplomatic pouch to Condoleezza Rice." Her rosebud lips move into what could almost be a smile, but Georgie wasn’t sure if it was at him or at the thought of mutilating him. "S-sorry. Soooo… how was the flight? I bet being cooped up on the plane for so long was torture!" He laughed nervously. His shirt was bonding itself to his skin. "Torture?" echoed Sun Tzu with a raise of an eyebrow. "When I was eleven, I spent seventy-two hours locked in a metal drum by my superior officers. Do you why?" "Uh, no." "Because if they had kept me in there any longer I would’ve missed my cello lesson. Now let us go collect my luggage." Sun Tzu walked off with a confident stride. The stunned Geogie took a moment to recover, then scampered to catch up. As they walked to the baggage claim, Georgie again attempted conversation, although he felt like he was trying to take a bone away from a Rottweiler. "So it wasn’t too bad, huh?" "Other than the three hour layover in Chicago. So many of you damn Americans with screaming brats and complaining about food most of the world would give up a limb to have. I wanted to kill someone…" "Yeah, I’ve been there." "…so I did." "Ye-what?!?" "One of those bag ladies your capitalist society churns out like clockwork. Just the two of us in an otherwise deserted lavatory. Strangulation was the cause of death. Granted, that meant touching her filthy throat with my own hands, but at least there was soap and water close by. Didn’t take long, I’m guessing she had emphysema or something of that nature. Most likely did the wheezing old biddy a favor. I left her corpse in a stall, and then went to go buy a magazine." Georgie comes to a complete halt. He tries to swallow with a throat that is completely dry. "Y-y-y-you must be k-kidding!" "Not hardly," Sun Tzu stops and turns towards him, her hair moving like a curtain of black silk. "I have it right here in my carry-on. Us Weekly, see? I do admire Angelina for adopting a child from Cambodia since you Americans are responsible for so much of the misery there." "Wait… y-you murdered someone because you were stuck at an airport?" Georgie’s voice squeaks like an adolescent’s. Sun Tzu stares at him with a face as cold and inscrutable as a porcelain mask. "No. I jest. Obviously." "Heh. Yeah… obviously…." Georgie wonders how soon he would be able to make a call to Dulles International Airport. Then he decides he was better off not making that call. The odd couple reaches the baggage claim without any further small talk while Georgie decides to stay more than arm’s length from Sun Tzu. But the wait for the luggage to begin appearing on the conveyor belt breathes an uncomfortable air into the silence. Georgie has to do something to break the tension before he screams his lungs out, most likely sounding just like a little girl. "Soooo… is this your fist time in America?" Sun Tzu’s only response is to take out her sunglasses and slowly put them back on her face as she stares out a distant window at the New York skyline. It looks so much like any other American city. But then, America did always just throw buildings up like children playing with blocks. She thinks of Georgie Clevelander and the question he asked with that nervous, wheezing voice of his. I wonder if I cut this doughy fool open if he would bleed like a sheep being sliced for mutton? Or simply deflate like a balloon, that quivering double chin becoming even softer and more rubbery as he turns into a pile of pale, pink flesh? Alas for scientific research, that would have consequences even diplomatic immunity wouldn’t be exempt from. But even as she tries to present an air of remorseless cruelty even to herself, Sun Tzu finds her memories reaching back through the years. Against her will, she returns to a poor neighborhood in Los Angeles on a hot August day… ... ... ... "Dayum! It’s hotter than hell out here!" James wiped the sweat from his brow with the hem of his Sacramento Kings jersey. Some folks considered his choice in teams treason in the land of the Lakers. But James thought the Kings were more of a team, not a pair of egos and some other guys like the Lakers. Still, his friends gave him hell for it, even calling him "King James." Well, except for around Mama. Mama didn’t tolerate no blasphemy. And the fastest backhand in Southern California never had to repeat itself. King James looked down at Marcus, sitting on a basketball with his headphones on. Not a drop of sweat of sweat on that boy, even with that big ass crazy afro. Marcus was bobbing his head, no doubt to some Marley or Tosh. He didn’t hear a word King James had said, boy was in a world of his own. Most folks thought Marcus was on the chronic, but those that knew best realized that was just how he was… cool as ice. If he got any more kick back, brother might just forget to breathe. Nothing bothered Marcus, not even when James kicked the basketball out from underneath him. As his ass hit the asphalt, Marcus just looked up at the grinning face of his friend. He raised his eyebrows in a silent question as he lowered his headphones. "Bitch!" said King James. "I said it’s hotter than hell out here!" Marcus appeared to be pondering this like it was quantum theory. Then he gazed down the street, his eyes watching the eldritch forces of the universe converge into one point.. "No," he replied slowly with great deliberation, like Moses coming down from the mountain. "THAT is hotter than hell." King James looked down the sidewalk. Holy shit, Marcus was dead on! This totally slammin’ Asian honey was walking towards the teens. Actually, "walking" didn’t cut it. Neither did "strolling," "strutting," or "sashaying." King James couldn’t find the right words, but she was definitely a body in motion. Body like a…. dayum, good thing Mama couldn’t read minds or James would get the ass whoopin’ of the century. As the honey walked past, she smiled down at Marcus. He replied with a nod. She gave the same smile to King James. But when James saw that long black hair shimmering with her steps, and caught the scent of sandlewood on her, his mouth fell open. She smiled wider at his reaction, her pearly white teeth sparkling like her dark, almond eyes. King James never said a word as she walked on by. "Smooth, my brother, smooth," observed Marcus. King James looked down on his friend with an expression of pure exasperation. "Bitch! My throat was dry! I told you its hot! That’s it" "Uh huh." "I’m serious! I could’a talked that sweet thang into drivin’ to Vegas wit’ me to get hitched by Elvis!" "I believe you, James." Marcus just smirked as he put his headphones back on. "Kiss my black ass, Marcus! I’m gonna go to Yun’s and get me a crème soda. If you want summin,’ you can get your own ass up and get it!" King James headed down to Yun’s, the local corner store. James knew some brothers and sisters hated Koreans moving into the ‘hood, saying they disrespected the people and the community. Man, but they didn’t know Mr. Yun. He had been at that spot since at least back when James’s grand-daddy was his age. And if Mr. Yun ever treated a decent customer like anything less than an old friend, then ain’t nobody around here in the last fifty years ever done seen it. Which was why King James was shocked to see the metal gate down over the door this time of the day. For him, that was like the sun deciding not to rise or Beyonce’ deciding to stop being bootylicious. Just ain’t natural. "Hey!" yelled James as he rattled the gate. "Mr. Yun! Wassup!" He rattled the gate again and called out some more. Finally an old Korean man came to the door. He was short, thin, and with many, many years behind him, yet there still seemed to be a quiet strength about him. Mr. Yun’s brown and wrinkled fingers hung up a handwritten sign on the door… "SHOP IS TEMPORARILY CLOSED DUE FAMILY EMERGENCY. SORRY FOR THE INCONVENIENCE AND THANK YOU FOR YOUR PATRONAGE." Mr. Yun gave James a small wave. There was a sadness in the old man’s eyes that had James forgetting about the heat. He even forgot about trying to look slick. Instead, he tried to project as much care and understanding as he could through the metal and glass. James just wanted Mr. Yun to know that he wasn’t just a place for snacks and soda, he was a neighbor and neighbors care about each other. It’s what Mr. Yun did when he cleaned up a six year old James after he fell off his bike in front of his store. Or when he organized all those donations for the Wilsons after that pipe burst in their apartment. James tried to communicate all that with a look. And the nod Mr. Yun gave James provided hope that he had succeeded. Then the old man was gone into the shadows of his shop and James was left feeling like he just said good-bye. As the youth walked back towards his friend, Mr. Yun sighed. He would miss seeing the children grow, seeing the new families flower. But as they say in this country, all good things must come to an end. And Mr. Yun’s life in America had been very good. "Nice sign." He turned and looked at the young Chinese woman in his shop. Her black eyes, her black hair, her black suit, her black gun. She gave him a smile that was both angelic and diabolic. "Although I think that ‘Closed Due To a Death in the Family’ would have been more appropriate, don’t you… colonel?" Mr. Yun looked at the smiling face of the young woman pointing a gun and sighed. "I haven’t been called "colonel" in a very long time," he said "Perhaps it’s because you are far from worthy of the rank and title," snapped the young woman, her smile gone. "Now let’s take this to the back. I don’t want chance any of the juvenile delinquents and underage single mothers you cater to seeing us." Mr. Yun reluctantly headed to the small office he has in the back of his store. He couldn’t really say that he’s surprised this day had come, but that’s didn’t make it anymore pleasant. Still, he was an old man. His wife passed away a few years ago. His children had grown and were successful, their children were on a similar path. At his age, the time Mr. Yun had left with his family and friends was a luxury. Now, it seemed that is a luxury he can not afford. "You’re not Korean, "stated Mr. Yun as they enter the office. "No, Chinese," replied the young woman coldly, her dark eyes not hiding her contempt for this old man. "When you chose to defect to the damned Americans during the war, you decided to sweeten the pot with intelligence on troop movements. Except you couldn’t stand the thought of betraying your own people, so you gave them a battalion of the People’s Revolutionary Army. So many Chinese killed, maimed, or captured… and its all blood on your hands, you backstabbing traitor." "I needed something better for my family," Yun explained, even though he knew it was futile. "We thought we were fighting for the people, but really we were only allowing a new group of thugs to take power." "I don’t care," snapped the young woman. "I do my duty. That duty exceeds any politics or selfish wants. That’s something any soldier understands." "Yes," replied the old man sadly. "I do understand. And I pray that someday you understand that duty isn’t blind obedience, but to truly stand up for what you believe in. Now, do what you must." The young woman snickered at Mr. Yun. "You are just going to stand there?" she asked icily. "Not bother to fight for your life?" "No," he answered. "I must answer for my actions." "You disgust me," she growled. "Were you ever really a soldier, you old bas- " Just then, the young girl ran into the office. Pink ribbons in her hair match the color of her Minnie Mouse jumper "Grandpa! Grandpa! I made straight…" The girl, eight at most, stopped when she sees the woman leveling the gun at her grandfather. She knew what the lump of metal meant and froze as the color drained from her face. "How the hell did she get in?" barked the woman. "There… there’s a back door… for deliveries," said the old man, his voice showing fear for the first time. How could he have forgotten about that door? "A pity," replied the woman almost pleasantly. "But as any soldier knows, civilian casualties are a necessary evil of war." The woman pointed the gun at the girl while smiling at the old man, her soulless eyes chilling him to his core. The click of the safety being turned off was the most horrific sound he had every heard in his long and bloody life. "No witnesses." Something lit afire in the old man. He was Colonel Yun once more. The soldier screamed in rage and grabbed the arm of this demon that would attack his grandchild, a power in his arms that hasn’t been there in decades. But all his emotion-fueled strength was nothing. Still, he strained with all that he had until he felt a sharp pain in this chest. A heart attack? Now? Then he felt the sticky warmth on his skin. Looking down, Yun saw the knife handle protruding from his chest. He fell to his knees and his assassin cupped his face almost affectionately, feeling flesh that felt like newspaper left out in the sun. "You die a soldier," she whispered into his aged ear. "And you saved your grandchild." And with that, there was one last glimmer of light in the old man’s eyes that quickly faded. Colonel Yun died a happy man. The woman pulled her knife out of the corpse. She looked over at the ashen young girl. Tears streamed down her soft and innocent face even has no sound escaped from her mouth agape in horror at the shattering of her world. "Your grandfather died like a man, even if he did not live like one. You owe your continued existence to that fact. Feel free to call the police. After you give them my description, those uniformed baboons will end up putting out an APB on Lucy Lui." As she was about to leave the office, the assassin turned back and stared at the girl now cradling the lifeless body of her grandfather. "And remember… the one thing none of us can escape is the past." And without looking back, she walked out of the shop and into the hot August air... … … … "Uh… ma’am?" Sun Tzu turns her head towards Georgie. Her sunglasses mask eyes that look not at him, but through him. "Excuse me?" "Just… asked if you’ve ever been here before or is this your first trip?" Georgie gives her an awkward grin, and to his surprise it is returned with a smile of unexpected warmth from that cold marble face. "Yes. This is my first time in America."
