Or Maybe It Was the Oriental Food

3 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/3/05 The chill of the winter air is enforced by the image of the ground crew of this private helipad hustling about in light brown and fluorescent orange full body coveralls, making preparations for a pending arrival. While one of the crew members pulls a fuel hose toward the pad, another flips the switch on the reddish orange beacon lights that square off the area like a blazing target in the night sky. It’s an extremely secluded area on the outskirts of Dallas, Texas, with only the faint outline of a single, but quite large, estate within sight. The lights inside the helicopter hangar are dim, allowing the helipad to be plainly seen from the sky. A hand-held two-way radio manned by one of the crew members squawks to life. “Eagle 1 to helipad….Eagle 1 to helipad, over” The crew member unclips the radio microphone from his shoulder, bringing it close to his mouth. “Helipad to Eagle 1…go ahead, over” “Eagle 1 is approximately ten miles from your location, over” “Roger that. Ground crew is standing by, over” “Copy that. Eagle 1 out” “Ground crew out” As he clips the mic back to his shoulder, the crew member shouts to the rest of his team. “ETA for Eagle 1, ten minutes!” He holds both hands up in the air, all fingers raised to give the crew a visual of his audio announcement. “Let’s make it happen, people!” He turns to the hangar and hollers to someone inside. “Make sure the car is on the way down from the house.” A generic male voice calls back from inside the structure. “Just talked to them,” he says. “They’re pulling up now.” Sure enough, a set of headlights is seen coming from the direction of the mansion. The headlights belong to the long, sleek, black H2 limousine that has become somewhat of a trademark of Tyler Nelson since his Media days. It’s somewhat of a tank with its Kevlar reinforced body and bulletproof glass, but no expense was spared to make the inside of the vehicle as luxurious as possible. The limo pulls up next to the hangar and parks, dimming the headlights in favor of the parking lights. The rear door opens and spits out Mrs. Beasley, bundled up tightly in a long, charcoal colored winter coat with a pair of stylish boots of the same color peeking out from underneath. A scarf wraps around her neck loosely as she waits for the helicopters arrival. Off in the distance a dull whirring sound can start to be made out, followed closely behind by the sight of the helicopters red flight beacons, some flashing, some not. The black helicopter can barely be seen until it’s almost upon the helipad, but the wind caused from the bird’s rotors rudely announces its arrival well ahead of time. A small cloud of dust is blown off the helipad as the chopper gently sets down. Mrs. Beasley wraps herself up in her own arms and braces herself against the circulating air, her hair whipping about angrily. As soon as the rubber of the tires hits the concrete pad, the crew springs into action, chocking the wheels as the helicopter engines begin to power down. After a few moments Mrs. Beasley approaches and opens the back door of the helicopter. “Good evening, Mr. Nelson,” says the assistant as she moves to the side, allowing the CEO of Nelty Investments, and more importantly Greed, INC, to exit the craft. “I trust things went well in North Carolina?” Tyler Nelson, dressed in a black wool trench with a grey Armani suit and black shoes underneath, disembarks the helicopter and heads straight for his limo, barely giving his assistant a passing glance. “Could have been better, I suppose.” Even though he got the last word in over Emilio Rage yet again, the victory was bittersweet for the CEO. Yes, he had ripped the Nicaraguan’s heart out for the world to see. Yes, he had taken legal custody of his adversary’s daughter in a shocking revelation. Yes, he had left North Carolina one step ahead. He even had a lavish office built for his personal use, but for Tyler Nelson the still unfinished business of completely destroying Emilio Rage left an awkward feeling in his gut. Or maybe it was the Oriental food. It just didn’t sit right with TN that Rage had any sort of leg to stand on….a bargaining chip, if you will. Granted, Tyler still had total control of the situation, but the one ray of light that Rage had…the one last straw that he could grab at…well, it kind of pissed the CEO off. Mrs. Beasley follows her boss to the limousine and climbs in after him, happy to escape the night air. She nestles into her comfortable leather seat across from Tyler and lets out an exaggerated shiver. “Brrrr,” she says as her body shakes in a minor seizure. The middle aged woman tries to fix her shoulder length brown hair, now a tangled mess from the helicopter, but finally resigns herself to pulling it back into a tight pony tail. Meanwhile, Tyler stares out the window with his chin resting gently on his fist, either stewing or in deep contemplation. Perhaps it’s a bit of both. “The son of a bitch pulled a magic rabbit out of his ass, didn’t he?” he asks of no one in particular. Mrs. Beasley doesn’t even look up as she digs through her attaché case. “It’s not that big of an issue, sir.” Her reassurance falls upon deaf ears. “I had him,” bemoans Tyler. “I had the death blow ready for him. His head was on the chopping block, the axe was sharpened, and I was bringing that fucker down on his neck.” Nelson clenches his fist and grits his teeth. “Then he pulls that paper out of his ass.” The clenched fist is pounded into the door panel with a loud thud. Mrs. Beasley peers up from her paperwork, just barely peeking over the reading glasses perched on the end of her nose. “Sir, what does ten percent of PRIME really give him?” she asks innocently, still trying to soothe Tyler. The CEO looks over at his assistant, a bit of annoyance on his face. “It gives him hope.” Mrs. Beasley maintains eye contact with Nelson for a moment before turning back to her paperwork. The ride is quiet for a while as she continues to shuffle paperwork back and forth. Finally she passes a stack of papers over to Tyler and he starts to look them over. “Those are the papers for the Dual Halo reconstruction,” she says while passing over a pen. “Gotta keep those two happy I suppose,” he sighs as he scribbles his name on the papers. “I think they were jealous of my office…I could see it in their eyes.” That’s more like it. Knowing…or at least believing…that people envied him gave Tyler that warm, fuzzy feeling. There weren’t too many things better than a good ego stroking, something that every greedy bastard in the world loved. But then it was back to the issue at hand. Like a slap in the face that brought him back down from his gluttonous high. Rage wasn’t finished yet. He was only hanging by a thread, but hanging none the less. After a shot across his bow, a glancing blow, and a massive direct hit, Rage was listing like the Bismark but the son of a bitch wouldn’t go down. “Where’s the girl?” Nelson queried. “Mr. Poteet is keeping an eye on her at the house.” Mrs. Beasley pauses and removes the glasses from her nose, a serious look on her face. “He kind of scares me, you know.” Tyler smirks out of the side of his mouth. “Heh…good.” Mrs. Beasley frowns and sets the glasses back on her face. “He’s the real deal, this Clint Poteet,” Tyler says satisfactorily. “A Goddamn tank, if you ask me. The guy hates everyone and everything. You have to admire that quality in a person.” “As you wish,” Mrs. Beasley says under her breath. “What do you want us to do with her?” A moment of pause as Tyler furrows his brow in contemplation, tapping his finger against his chin. Finally he waves his hands dismissively. “You and Clint just take her and keep her out of sight somewhere, I don’t care where, just not at the house. The last thing I need is to have that little piece of him staying under my roof.” Just then his face lights up with an idea, the evil smirk he wears so well spreading across his face. “Hey…she’s Mexican, right?” “Umm...something like that,” Mrs. Beasley says, unsure exactly where this is leading. “How about we get her a uniform and put her to work with all the other housekeepers? Wouldn’t that be grand? Rage’s daughter cleaning my toilets? And the real kicker is that I’ll be getting all that labor for free!!” The excitement in Tyler’s voice is thick and his shoulders begin to perk up from their slouched position. A wry smile slips across Mrs. Beasley’s mouth. “Always thinking, aren’t you? Nelson is almost giddy. “I knew I could find something good to brighten up my day. Never miss out on the chance to twist the dagger a bit so that the wound won’t heal. Make it happen, Mrs. Beasley!” Nelson’s assistant pulls out her PDA and jots down a few notes, then tucks it neatly back in her attaché. As they pull up to the front of Tyler’s palatial mansion, she gathers the paperwork she had been working on, placing it in the attaché case as well. The driver comes to the back and opens the door, allowing Tyler to exit the limo. Mrs. Beasley follows and the two make their way up the steps to the house. “Have you reached my sister yet?” “Not yet, sir. We are still trying, though.” “We’re running out of time,” Tyler growls impatiently. “I need to talk to her before the match I have against Rage and her retarded offspring. I have a plan.” He rubs his hands together sinisterly as he enters the house. “You always do, sir.” Mrs. Beasley follows him in and starts off in the opposite direction. Nelson heads up the stairs in the magnificent foyer, a crystal chandelier lighting his way up the plush red carpeted steps. Various pieces of artwork, undoubtedly priceless in value, cover the bright white wall along the winding staircase. A hand carved molding, about waist high, follows the slope of the stairs as they climb up to the second floor. Tyler unbuttons his trench, allowing it to flail in the wind as he walks down the hallway. Sitting outside one of the rooms is Clint Poteet, a four hundred-plus pound mound of mean. Standing about six feet, four inches tall, he would be imposing even without the added girth he carries. He’s not a bodybuilder by any stretch of the imagination, but he’s got power to burn. His bald head and red, pointed goatee and ever present scowl give more reasons to take notice of the man. Wearing a Harley Davidson t-shirt, blue jeans, and a pair of steel-toed work boots, Clint sits in front of the door in a metal folding chair with his arms folded across his chest and resting on his belly. “What’s the status?” Nelson asks, motioning toward the room with his head. Clint looks over his shoulder with a glare at the door, then gets to his feet and moves the chair. “The little bitch pounded on the door for over an hour when we brought her here,” he reports in a rough voice. “I damn near went in there and choked her to death. She finally stopped and it’s been pretty quiet for a while now.” Tyler walks up and knocks on the door. “Sylvia, my dear? Are you awake? It’s daddy!” The insincerity drips over each word Nelson speaks. “Go away you jerk! You’re not my father!” “Oh, that’s not what the government says,” chides Tyler. The sound of something smashing against the other side of the door is heard, causing Nelson to take a slight step back from the door. “You can’t keep me locked in here forever!” screams Sylvia. “As soon as you let me out I’m going to run away!” The CEO chuckles. “Don’t worry, we’ll be letting you out of your room tomorrow. I’ve come up with something to keep your mind occupied while you adjust to your new life. Don’t get any crazy ideas of running off, though. Mr. Poteet will see that you don’t. Get plenty of rest tonight, tomorrow might be a long day for you.” Nelson turns to Clint, pointing a stern finger at him. “Don’t kill her. I need her alive…for now.” “I ain’t makin’ any promises, man.” Tyler spins on his heels and heads toward his room, comfortable in the fact that he’s turned his day around. He’ll now be able to sleep well on this night.
