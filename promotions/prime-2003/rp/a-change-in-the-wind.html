A Change in the Wind

undated

Date: Post-ReV 184 To expect the unexpected shows a thoroughly modern intellect.--Oscar Wilde ---- Nobody could have predicted this. Not even Lindsay Troy, a woman who prided herself on having the solution to every problem, and a contingency plan for every short-coming. She would have expected Fusenshoff to come to her aide should something go awry in her reckless run to the ring to assist Rayne, Tsonda and Dawkins. Or Mary-Lynn Mayweather, apprentice to greatness and fearless in times of strife, as proven once before. Or even the DUI boys since, well, if there's a ballyhoo going down they'd probably want in on the fun. Provided they weren't too sloshed to hold their own. But Wade Elliott, who had disappeared without a trace? She thought he was gone for good. And those thoughts slowly became reality with every unanswered phone call and every voicemail that would be unreturned. Eventually, the voicemail box filled up, so no more could be left. She waited for months for some sign of life, but none was afforded her. Unable to travel much in the early weeks after her surgery, she had requested others try to find him, to no avail. All hope seemed lost. Until Skynyrd hearkened the return of the Country Boy. "Where've you been?" Lindsay muttered, the cobwebs finally falling to pieces inside her head and allowing her some clarity. Beside her, Wade offered no reply; not even a grunt or a snort to acknowledge the question. They had finally made their way through the gorilla position, careful not to trip over any cords or wires as they emerged from the darkness into the bright backstage light. He helped her down a hallway, and she couldn't tell if he was ignoring her deliberately or if he just didn't hear her. "I asked you something, Wade," she said, a little louder. Still nothing. Lindsay could feel Wade's muscles contract. His grip around her tightened, and she could feel her legs not moving of their own free will. The Bad Dog was dragging her, destination unknown, and silent as a tomb. Vashaun might have caught her off guard with a blind-sided attack, but she wasn't going to allow another person to exert control over her. Not tonight. She put on the brakes and twisted out of his grasp. The sudden movement caught Wade off-guard, and he turned to look back at her. Before he could protest, or make a move to further assist her, Lindsay's hand was flying towards the side of his face and her fingers were snatching his earlobe. "OW! Whatta ya think yer-!" Wade's feet fumbled underneath his bent-over upper body, and he was unable to right himself before Lindsay tugged him into a locker room. The door opened with a bang, its metal handle reverberating against the concrete wall. She managed to swing him around to face her, phasers set to Death Glare, and let go of his ear. "What the shit, Wade?!" she screamed, never one for non-committal answers. "You fell off the face of the Earth for months. No phone calls, no anything. Didn't bother to return mine, either. Didn't bother to check in so I knew you weren't dead in a fucking ditch somewhere in Hicktown, USA!" "It ain't-" She cut him off. Once Lindsay Troy gets on a tirade, well, not even the President with his finger on the Button could stop her. "And now, you want to crash back in here all White Knight on your Black Horse and act like I don't have a right to know what happened? Fuck that. I want an explanation, Wade. And you're not leaving until I-" "Ahh, this is a pleasant surprise, yes?" Troy's head snapped over her shoulder, already narrowed eyes widening slightly at the person standing in the threshold behind her. Dr. Fihlguud smiled professionally at the Queen and gave Wade a slight nod. "I should thank you for delivering Ms. Troy to the infirmary, Mister Elliott. She has had quite the eventful evening, all things considered, hm?" For the first time since barging into this space, Lindsay took a good look around. Two medical tables were set up alongside the walls, and along the back counter were various wraps, ointments, bandages and gauze packets. So consumed with anger was she, that she hadn't even thought to see if the room was labeled for any special purpose. Realizing her carelessness, she grimaced and swallowed her pride the best she could. It was never an easy thing to do. "Wade didn't bring me here, Victor, I just pulled him into the room without looking." "Nonsense! I have never known you to be so modest, Ms. Troy. Now, if Mister Elliott will kindly leave, I can make sure that brute Vashaun didn't put you on the shelf ag-" "Wade's not leaving." Victor Fihlguud sighed, and folded his hands in front of his body. "If you could please just have a seat." "I'm not sitting anywhere! I'm fine! Damnit. Wade, tell him." With her body half-turned to Fihlguud, and her attention being diverted, Wade took the opportunity to slink behind her towards the door. "Wade?" He almost made it the whole way out too, before Troy turned back to face him and saw him...not there. "Wade!" The 'Bama Bruiser looked at her, his face set to stone. He opened his mouth, but no words came out. With a sigh, he shook his head and forced himself to speak to her. Not in protest, but in resignation. "Maybe later." "Goddamnit, Wade, don't you walk away!" But he was gone before the threat could fully reach his ears. Lindsay moved to storm after him, but a gentle hand on the crook of her arm gave her pause. "Please, just sit," Fihlguud nudged again. "I told you, I don't want to!" ---- The cool night wind lifted the hair from her neck, air licking her skin like a lover's tongue. Outside on her hotel room's spacious balcony, Lindsay sat in an oversized chaise lounge and looked out to the twinkling city lights. It was late, well past the Witching Hour, but like most nights she couldn't sleep. Or, couldn't sleep long enough. A bottle of water rested on the concrete beside her, half empty, with the cap somewhere on the ground below. She had been playing with it, twirling it around her finger, when it slipped away and tumbled through the black and out of sight. Too many things ran through her mind on this night. Wade's return to save her from another potential injury. The complications that she faced before his disappearance, between her friendship with him and her friendship and suppressed feelings for Tyler Rayne, were sure to rise up again with his return. "He disappeared. Left me and the friendship. Why should I give a damn about his feelings..." the words came forth in a murmur, were instantly regretted, then quickly carried away by the breeze. She did care, deep down. Always trying to please everyone. But with things between Tyler and her slowly progressing to something more than flirtatious friendship, it would be a lie to say that their "relationship", whatever it could be classified as now, would even be at this point had Wade been in the picture. It was a reality she didn't want, and hated, to admit. "Can't sleep?" Lindsay turned her body and found Tyler standing in the opening between the deck and the hotel room. The baggy sweatpants he wore caught with the wind and rippled against his athletic frame. He was shirtless, but his chest showed no signs of being affected by the wind's temperature. "That's a silly question." "Ha," he said with a smirk, then disappeared into the room for a moment. When he returned, he carried with him the comforter from the bed and two pillows. "Got room for one more over there?" "Maybe," she said, scooting over on the chair as far as the sides would allow her. Tyler handed her a pillow and laid down next to her, draping the comforter over both their bodies. There was, surprisingly, enough room. He lifted his arm, and she nestled into the space created for her, head resting on his shoulder. "I'm pleasantly surprised, I didn't think there'd be enough space for two." "You might have just had to lay down on top of me, if that were the case." She smirked. "Bet you would have liked that." "I have been known to think about such things, on occasion," he quipped with a smile, and adjusted his pillow. "So what, needed to see more of the city that you'll be leaving behind in the morning?" "No...I don't know. I just needed some air I guess. A lot happened tonight." Her right arm draped casually over his chest, fingers running along the scars. He loved it when she did that, although admitting it to her has not come so easy. "Yes, because Snow disguising himself and waylaying Hawaii, Tink and myself with fuckin' chairs is exactly what I was hoping for. Not to mention Dildo Baggins popping by for a spot of Shire Tea." "Wait a minute...Snow?" She looked to Tyler, mildly amused. "I would have thought you'd have had a clever nickname for him by now." "Still working on it." "Tell me something, and this may be a silly question...but you knew nothing about Wade coming back, right? No executive power privilege or whatever?" "Why would I have wanted Country back?" he asked, his turn to be mildly amused. "Things between you and I were beginning to get less complicated." Lindsay sighed, the Truth coming back with the wind. Only, she knew Tyler didn't regret saying so. A quiet settled over them, adding another layer on top of what covered them already. This blanket was much heavier. "You're thinking things are going to go back to the way they were now that he's waltzed back in." "I don't know," came the reluctant admittance. "He disappeared. I don't know where he went or what happened, if anything. But Wade doesn't get to dictate anything to me after that. If it gets brought up, he'll have to find a way to cope." "Kid, you and I both know the only way Country knows how to cope is with his fists." "If he wants to stay my friend, he'll have to learn another way." "Easier said than done," Tyler replied, calloused hand running along the sleeve of her shirt, bringing its long length up to her elbow. Fingertips trailed over her smooth skin. "You've been sleeping worse since Japan, Lindz. Did you think I didn't notice?" She shrugged against him, not replying, but knowing that the growing silence couldn't be ignored for long. He would bring it out of her, eventually, and she didn't want to let him in on her secret. It was better that he didn't know, for the candor would undoubtedly hurt him. He did deserve her honesty. "I haven't been able to get the severed head out of my mind, Tyler." He sighed. Could do nothing but draw her tighter against him. He craned his neck and let his lips touch her hair, his nose take in the light scent of coconut. "I'm sorry," he whispered. More sorry than she'd know, or that he could ever express. "The image will pass, Tyler. I-I don't blame you. You were protecting your home. But when you've never seen something like that before..." "It's a shock." "Yeah." "Listen I-" They both stopped short, the phrase shooting from their lips at the exact same time. Eerie in its timing. An awkward chuckle followed, and Lindsay lifted her head to look at Tyler. "You go." "No, ladies first." "Alright. I was thinking...since most of your homes have been destroyed by whoever it is that's doing it, and the club is compromised, or, at least I think it is, would you want to stay with me?" "What, like live together?" "I'm not suggesting you get all domestic," she rolled her eyes. "I'm sure you have other places you could go, but if you wanted to stay somewhere familiar for an extended period of time, I'm offering you my place. It's just something to think about." She watched his eyes lift away from hers, off into the distance, past the city lights and towards the West. He was glad he acquiesced and let her go first, for his suggestion was far less pleasing and a lot more painful. The last thing Tyler Rayne wanted to do was to cause her harm, or put her in harm's way. He had done that, unwittingly. Or, maybe, a part of him knew that she would be in danger just by following him across the US and to Japan. He wanted her by his side, for selfish reasons. He needed the reassurance of her presence. But once the ninjas attacked, and once she admitted that his actions caused her distress, he didn't want to be the reason for more of her sleepless nights. He didn't want to be the cause of any undue pain, and he didn't want her to become a target for the violence of others. He would have let her go. As agonizing as it would have been, he would have given her an out, and he would have dealt with the pain of her leaving, like he had dealt with the pain of so many others before her. But Lindsay Troy wanted to stay. And, in these troubled times, he was more grateful for that then she knew, and would know for quite some time. "Alright. I will."
