Escaped Science Experiments and Men Who Wear Cowboy Boots and Who Aren't Even Cowboys...

17 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17.08.08 The midmorning sunlight spread itself across the New York skyline with delicate ease, weaving past and beyond the tower skyscrapers with minimal effort and maximum finesse. Union Jack glanced upward and squinted at the sun’s gentle ferocity, reminiscing of better days spent in a much better country, eating better food and getting squiffy on much better liquor. He sighed. Union Jack hadn’t had a decent cup of tea for almost six months already and it was beginning to get to him. The stuff Jeeves acquired in this godforsaken country tasted like piss and what’s more the knuckle-dragging Neanderthals actually drank it cold, iced no less. He shook his head and wondered precisely when the philistines were going to drag their stupid, obese carcasses into the twenty-first century and embrace the ways of their true forefathers (and betters). However, out amongst the locals, Union Jack didn’t figure that was going to happen anytime soon. Union Jack wandered further along the street, observing some more of the painfully ugly locals go about their awfully unfulfilling lives and turned to his ever faithful butler, Jeeves, who was still keeping pace at his side. “Why the bloody hell do we have to do this again, Jeeves?” Jeeves walked with his hands held behind his back, desperately trying to ignore the wayward glances he received for wearing such audacious attire; a full blown suit with large coattails and a rather spectacular bowtie. “Because that idiot PR man you hired suggested it might be good publicity, sir.” Union Jack contorted his face confused. “Publicity? Walking through the streets desperately trying not to contract polio? When did I agree to this?” “This morning, sir,” Jeeves sighed. “I should have realised you were not listening to the man by the glazed expression in your eye. However, at one point you did mention how it might be interesting to ‘see the colonists in their natural environment’, but it seems that has slipped masters mind.” Jeeves paused, waiting for his master to respond, but Union Jack glared at him blankly. “Rest assured, I will not let this happen again.” Union Jack nodded. “See that you don’t.” “Hey you’re that British guy ain’t you?” A voice questioned, from seemingly nowhere. Union Jack didn’t all that hard to search for it’s origin, the guy in his late twenties made himself known quick enough anyhow. He approached from the rear, jumping directly in front of him and forcing Union Jack to stop dead in the street. “Jack, right?” Jeeves cut in between the obvious wrestling fan and his master in one fluidic movement. “That’s ‘Sovereign’ to you, young scamp.” The fan developed a confused expression, then sidestepped the butler and shoved a pen and paper at Union Jack with swiftness that would make a ninja cry. “Can I get your autograph?” Union Jack glared at the pen and paper and then at the man, with distain spread clearly across his face. “God you’re ugly.” “Um, what my master is trying to say,” Jeeves interjected, in a very diplomatic way, “is that he hasn’t the time for autographs today.” Union Jack ignored him. “Are you some sort of science experiment gone wrong? Have you escaped the laboratory with the intent of reigning down destruction and mayhem upon the human race for subjugating you to numerous torturous and entirely humiliating battery of test the likes of which man has never seen before or since?” Union Jack turned to Jeeves, eyes wide with fear and determination. “Jeeves, call the Ghostbusters! They live around here somewhere, I’ve seen it on the TV! Egon will know what to do!” Jeeves glared back at him blankly and sighed. “Sir, that won’t be necessary.” “Are you sure, Jeeves?” Union Jack questioned. “Surely it’s our moral obligation to report this atrocity to the police or at least the A-Team!” “He isn’t a science experiment, sir,” Jeeves explained. “It’s just a local.” Union Jack took a step backwards. “You mean they all look like this?” By now the fan had started to cry shamelessly right there in the street; his head lowered and his autograph book and pen rested by his feet. “No, it’s just acne!” Union Jack’s face contorted in disgust. “You’re crying openly....in the street? What are you, some kind of homosexual who likes to take it up the wrong ‘un?” The fan didn’t reply, instead he shuffled off to get on with his meagre life leaving Union Jack somewhat perplexed. “Was it something I said?” Jeeves rolled his eyes. “Reminds one of the time you volunteered for the Samaritans, sir.” Union Jack nodded, then paused. “Why?” “That was something of a disaster too if you recall,” Jeeves replied. “You took three calls and all three of them committed suicide. It wouldn’t have been all that bad, but one of them was a wrong number.” “Yes, yes, yes,” Union Jack waved him away. “They came on the phone moaning and whinging about their parents being dead and how their girlfriends have left them! The nerve of it!” “And you told them to ‘stop being girls and do what everyone else does in your situation: audition for X-Factor of Pop Idol or one of those god awful reality TV programmes...you’re sure to get through.’” “Pfft,” was Union Jack’s initial response - then, “Like any of those peasants even know what real problems are!” He paused to wipe a solitary tear from his eye. “I haven’t had a decent cup of tea for six months, Jeeves. SIX months.” “Hey fruitloop!” Came an unfamiliar voice, in a dialect of one of the locals. Union Jack ignored it. “I demand that you find me some Yorkshire Tea! None of this Typhoo shite! The stuff takes like piss!” Some leather clad individual approached from the distance, with a murderous look in his eye and a ridiculous moustache. He pointed at Union Jack accusingly. “I’m talking to you, limey!” “Yes?” Union Jack replied, glaring incredulously at the man‘s cowboy boots. “What do you want, peasant?” “I just saw what you did to that other guy,” the leather clad buffoon declared. “You think you can come over to our country and do whatever the hell you like?” Jeeves developed a confused look. “But isn’t that what you’re doing in Iraq and Afghanistan, sir?” The local conveniently ignored him. “This is America. A-M-E-R-I-C-A and we won’t take any of your shit!” Union Jack rolled his eyes. “First of all, I know how to spell America because a) we invented the English language, b) we’re superior to you in every way imaginable and, c) you won’t catch us wearing cowboy boots when we’re not actually cowboys.” The local glanced down at his boots and then at Union Jack, angry. “I’m gonna fuck you up!” The man struggled out of his leather jacket and Union Jack simply sighed. “Jeeves!” Jeeves nods. “Right you are, sir!” With that, Our Sovereign’s fearless butler lunged forwards and managed to land a vicious kick to local’s testicles, whilst his arms are still tied up in jacket. The local thug slumps to the floor clutching his nether regions painfully. “Remember this, you bloody colonial!” Union Jack told him. “We didn’t build an empire as great as ours by fighting fair! Go ask the Argies!” “*cough**cough* Man, I hope *cough* Troy Douglas kicks *cough* your ass this week!” The local moron spluttered. “So does he,” Union Jack replied, continuing along the street, “but the truth of the matter is; I’ve seen paraplegics with more fight in them!” Jeeves nodded at the man as he passed. “Good day, sir.” “Bloody colonials...”
