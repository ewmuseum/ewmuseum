Karma is a @#$%!

undated

Date: Here, there, and everywhere “One should perform Karma with nonchalance without expecting the benefits because sooner or later one shall definitely get the fruits.” -Rig Veda Ramada Inn Chicago, Illinois, August 19th, 2:17 am The low irradiance of an outdated Zenith kept Sonny Silver half-awake. With his inability to find the land of nod, he decided as long as he was wide awake he’d partake in studying the events of ReVolution 136. Half the Roulette competitors – himself included -- had a hand in making this recent ReVolution something special to watch. Special to the winners, anyhow. It meant some much-needed momentum as far as Dice, Dollars and Tragedy was concerned. It meant Sonny was able to collect data necessary to calculate the lengths some of these men would go when crunch time arrived and As for our fair Loose Cannon… "One!" Richard: WHAT THE- "Two!" Nick: He broke free of the Saito Suplex! "Three!" Richard: NOOOOOO!!! Nick: HE DID IT! TYLER RAYNE JUST BEAT SONNY SILVER! Richard: This has to be the worst officiating since the Spurs-Suns game where Horry cheap shotted Nash! Vince Howard: HERE IS YOUR WINNER... TYYYYLEEEEERRRR RAAAAYYYNNNNNEEEE!!! FAST-FORWARD… Hoyt Williams: Honey this is an arena not a chapel. I know the world can be a confusing place it even baffles me sometimes. Like how could Sonny Silver not win against a little girl?? You know. FAST-FORWARD SOME MORE… Two chances. Two chances for The self-proclaimed Chairman of PRIME, Sonny Silver, to down the challenger in Danny Ferguson. The result? He failed...both times. YOU GET THE IDEA… …The night hadn’t gone exactly to fruition. He reminisced about walking into the building with a chip on his shoulder big enough to weigh him down. Sonny Silver was to march directly into that ring, methodically pick apart whichever poor soul happened to draw against him and grind his bones into dust beneath his wrestling boots. He would unquestionably be THE force to be reckoned with when whispers of the Roulette monstrosity began. But as time proved, the only certainly in life was absolute uncertainty. Things don’t pan out just because you say they’re going to. Colossus IV was evidence of that. Even moreso was this Mystery Opponents night. Perhaps the aforementioned chip was what rendered him unable to kick out of a shock pinfall attempt by Tyler Rayne, weighing him down in the process. The night saw him with more egg on his face than ever as the trash talk piled on and his name was smeared through the mud like a member of the former Spears-Federline domicile. In between silent cursing as he observed PRIME’s inaugural show of its fifth year in existence, one idea crossed his head to market the recent show as “Take a shot for every time you TAKE a shot at Sonny! Who will survive?!” If anything, he’d make some merchandising profits off such a plan, but knowing how much deep shit he was already in with the PRIME brass, he’d never see penny number one. Once his natural Sardonic Quip-o-Meter wore out its welcome among his frantic train of thought, he went back to sulking. Emo-ish, even. It was quite scary. Giving up on his endless quest to learn the tricks of the trade employed by his future adversaries for PRIME’s ultimate prize, he shut off the idiot box before throwing himself back into the comfort of his bed. His thoughts drifted to his new companion on the road occupying the next bed over; his mother. It’d been a week or so since she asked her to come along with him on his adventures and they gave each other the companionship and time with family they desperately sought. Quiet suburban life was becoming a bout with tedium for his mother who’d barely even left the confines of her Seattle home in some time. She’d be able to find out what makes her oldest child tick as he rode the proverbial rails, chasing the dream he’d carried with him as a young child. For Maria Silver, it was one step closer to figuring out the mystery of why her son’s life was unraveling. Perhaps this bonding experience would put him at ease or at least give his life meaning. Now that reconciliation with the wife and daughter he abandoned for fame seemed to be an unattainable pipe dream, she’d do anything else within her power to help him. For Sonny, however, it was somebody he trusted to keep him from firing shots from the nearest available clock tower. The road was almost too painful to bear now that he was truly alone amongst a sea of piranha that wished nothing but to feast upon his flesh at the first drop of blood. His sanity needed some bolstering and nobody else he knew would be willing to undertake the task. Sonny Silver was persona non grata in basically any PRIME clique and allies were few and far between. Alliances he formed himself were privy to crumbling under the test of time. Fife Posse? Cast away at his hand. Fuck You? Crumbled to pieces. Kenjiro ITO? Who fucking cares? Probably dead in a ditch or something. Caesar Vega? A casualty of the business. They’d been something close to genuine friends post-FU, but even Nova himself couldn’t overcome the tremendous pressure the job put upon him in the wake of his wife and child’s untimely departure. PRIME was poison to him now and he’d made a quasi-ceremonious exit with some crazy guy atop a horse from New Frontier Wrestling. From this mutually beneficial companionship, Sonny would hopefully be able to find whatever it is he lost on this road when he peeled back the layers of Mr. Silver, Chairman of PRIME. But until that moment of clarity began, all he could try to do was fill that gaping void with whatever may fit. Allowing sleep to finally gang up on him, he let his body become enshrouded within the comfort of his cotton cocoon. By dawn, with any luck, the metamorphosis would produce something other than a miserable wreck. ~~~~~ The Next Day Ford City Mall, 11:56 AM Maria’s plastic spoon dug into the next bite of Chocolate Chip Cookie Dough as she turned up to look at the hustle and bustle of Chicago life. She watched people blaze by at all different speeds. Some may be late for work, others on their way to run errands, probably a few playing hooky. It was all quite fascinating, but her attention became fixated on her oldest. She couldn’t help but smile at her son, who was in the process of wolfing down an oversized pizza from Sbarro’s and getting the cheese remnants stuck in his beard. The day was still young as their walk amongst the other hoi polloi proved to be just what the doctor ordered for Maria Silver. As much fun as camping out in a not-too-shabby hotel room could be, it was the adventures they had seeing the sights of PRIME’s touring cities. He surmised that getting out and traveling at his side for a while would be cathartic for her. Getting away from the rules and regulations she regularly followed from that “stupid gay-ass Neighborhood Tenant Committee” as he so eloquently put it would do her a world of good and allow her to unwind. It was true, she decided in that moment. She valued any time she could get with Sonny like heirlooms she couldn’t bear to part with. Times like these made her almost forget that Sonny seemed to be falling into some rut form which escape was nigh impossible. She let the smile cross her face as she lightly chuckled at Sonny finishing the last remnants of his pizza and haphazardly tossing the crust behind his shoulder. “You know, my son, there’s a trash can right next to that bench.” She gestured over to said bench where a young couple were making out like animals in heat. “True, but that involves walking out of my current trajectory. I’m perfectly in sync with the escalator and for me to walk over there would waste time. Whereas, casually tossing my garbage away from me saves time. See? I’m getting rid of garbage and moving us along. Efficient, huh?” “I see you can’t turn off the snide remarks?” “I could. But let the world miss out on my joyous wit? I’d be depriving my fans.” “You’re a bad guy. Any fans you got left are malcontent punks that look up to people that fight authority.” “Touché. But said malcontent punks won’t be cutting their wrists if they weren’t fixated on me, thus I’ve effectively saved the world, yes?” Shaking her head with a mix of disbelief and slight amusement, they continue up the escalator. As they traveled upward to the next floor, Maria caught sight of one of her favorite shopping hangouts when she and her friends had a spare Saturday back home. Lots of things for the kids, you see. “Oh! I wanna go there when we get a chance!” She pointed a finger out to Claire’s, which is met with a sigh of disgust from her oldest. “Huh. My body-switching device appears to have worked. You are now the child.” After rubbing a tuft of her straight black hair out of her eyes, she elbowed Sonny in the stomach. “Sheila’s daughter’s been going ga-ga for some of that High School Musical merchandise and I heard they carry that stuff. We ought to get her something.” Sonny shuddered, remembering the Espinosa family all too well. As a kid, Sheila and her husband, Rodrigo, would babysit him. He loathed the stench of that cat box they kept locked in the bathroom that never seemed to get emptied. The only fun had at that house involved playing with open paint cans of Turpentine. Otherwise, they were one of those families that lived vicariously through their kids’ achievements by working them to the bone with no room for anything “fun.” Their son, Bobby, engrossed himself in football and recently got a full scholarship ride to U-Dub. The daughter his mother spoke of – Jo -- was one of those people in the class of “fell out of the ugly tree and hit every branch on the way down.” Nobody had the heart to tell her she was very homely growing up, but she had a brain like Stephen Hawking which would mean big things for her future. Already a Sophomore in high school and a virtual prodigy in Science. “That woman’s ugly,” Sonny bluntly stated, to which he received a second and a third elbow for his troubles. He kept up the lip, though. “And she’s obsessed with High School Musical? She double-fails at life.” “Well, not everybody can grow up to land a cute girl and dump her to the curb.” He rolled his eyes. “What do you mean…?” A mock slap landed on Maria’s shoulder accompanied by fake laughter. “Oh, you! Anyhow, we’ll go there later if you really must satisfy your estrogen-induced impulses. Right now, Foot Locker’s the object of my desire! They got these awesome shoes with the computer chip you can place in the soles that’ll work in sync with my Ipod so when I go for runs, it’ll tell me my distance.” She laughed a little bit. Just enough to break Sonny out of his giddy mood long enough to pique his curiosity. “What’s so funny?” “You’re awfully chipper for somebody that had a rotten night on that wrestling program. I’m glad you can look at the sunny side of things… pun intended.” Giddy mood? It stepped out of the building to deal with a personal matter. Feel free to meet his replacement, brooding mood. Maria? Brooding mood. Nice to meet you. He folded his arms and kicked at the ground half-heartedly like a child that couldn’t get his way. “Mom, not a good subject right now.” “I’m just saying you handled it very well, which is very unlike you. Not to call your father a lesser man – rest his soul – but when he had off nights, his first instinct was to punch something and ask questions later. You take after him like that, but I’m glad you can at least hear this out. Shows you’re growing as a person, my son.” “Okay.” And that was that. Sonny let himself breathe out every last huff of hot air before returning to the pleasing task of picking up kick-ass sneakers. That was until… “But… I gotta know… what’s gotten into you lately?” A lone foot halted just as he reached the front entrance to Foot Locker. He barely glanced over his shoulder when he looked at her. “Mom, do you really want to do this here?” One of the nagging traits of Silvers was their inability to let matters go, regardless of importance. When somebody wronged a Silver clan member, their bodies were not unlike mindless zombies whose only instinct was to do something to avenge the wrong and they were willing to travel vast distances to do so. Luckily, they all handled these urges differently than Sonny whose first tendency was “KICK. YOU. TEETH.” Maria wished to talk all these little matters out to avoid repressed feelings cropping up at inopportune times. “Inviting me out here wasn’t just for my benefit, Sonny.” She crossed her arms over her chest and stared down her boy in that stern way she knew he loathed with every fiber of his being. “You came to me with a problem and you worked hard to talk me into coming out with you. Obviously, this thing has you rattled if you wanted me at your side, so tell me. What’s your malfunction?” Rather than answer her inquiry outright, the thoughts danced around in his head. He knew this moment would come out sooner or later, but he’d hoped that her insane moral compass wouldn’t appear before him unless they were away from public eyes. “Can’t talk. Sneakers aren’t gonna buy themselves.” “Answer me.” “Not here…” “YES, HERE!” Maria’s shrill voice got the attention of a few passers-by, but the intent to get her son to voice his concerns overpowered her rationality. Once she composed herself a little bit, she sat down on the nearest bench, motioning for Sonny to join her. He signaled with a solemn nod and joined her on the uncomfortable metal construct. “I’m sorry, but son, we can’t do this if we’ve got this elephant in the room. You wanted me to help you and damn it, this is me helping you.” The look on his face as he focused on the tacky brown tiles at his feet tells Maria nothing. She put a hand on his shoulder and surprisingly, found herself met with little resistance. “Then you want to know what I think?” Sonny turned his head, readying himself for a motherly pearl of wisdom he knew she’d blast him with eventually. “I think that you’re regretting ever putting your job ahead of Eme and Gaby, and that’s been reflecting on your work. You lost the only people who you ever gave the time of day in order to further your own career. I think that somehow, you have this horribly misguided notion that friendships and love are signs of weakness and by severing these ties you think it’ll make you stronger because your focus is turned exclusively to this job…” “STOP calling it a job. This is my life, Mom…” “It’s KARMA.” At the utterance of the last syllable, a guttural laugh escapes from Sonny’s lips, incensing Maria in the process. “You believe in God. You don’t seriously buy any of that…” “Look at the facts.” She counts on a finger. “You ignore your wife and daughter, so they flee the country and you don’t even know where they are now. You malign people every week on that stupid show, then they do the same to you and you get pissy about it and act shocked. You severed ties with that Lindsay Troy woman that you’ve called a friend to me, she becomes your enemy and she shatters your hand. You kick her around a few times, she beats you badly in front of your family in Seattle…” “I HAD HER…!” “Fine. But my point is you lost her and you lost TO her all because you wanted to restart a career that you thought was in flux. You’ve severed every last tie at human kindness and you’re spiraling down. Into what, I don’t even know. You’re suffering now for every bad thing you’ve ever done, Son. You know it and it’s killing you inside, but you’re too bull-headed to admit it!” “Let me tell you something. I…” “AAAAAAAAAAAAHHHHHHHHHHHHHH!” The argument between mother and son is halted when they both look on at an adolescent kid in a tattered Slayer T-shirt, decorated with several tattoos on both arms, racing for his life. At a frantic pace, the kid flees the scene of a sobbing young woman, shoved to the ground. Before his brain has a chance to process the goings-on, the boy raced by Sonny and Marian with something in his grasp that doesn’t quite belong to him. “He’s got a purse!” Sonny screamed. He bolted up to his feet and with every last bit of energy in him, he raced after the thief as he shoves down people near an elevator. “WAIT!” But it was too late. Sonny was already too far gone to hear the pleas of his mother’s cries. Sweat trickled down the boy’s forehead as he looked behind him to make sure nobody was following him, but panicked quickly when a large man barreled down the escalaor, affirming his worst fear. Crack wasn’t going to buy itself and he didn’t even bother to check the inside of this woman’s purse. All he knew was that he needed to get in and get out NOW. “GET THE FUCK BACK HERE, YOU STUPID PRICK!” The kid ignored his pursuer’s screams as he raced through a crowd of people in a last-ditch attempt to flee the scene. He didn’t’ know or care who any of these people were. He knocked down man, woman, and child alike to keep from being caught. Freedom, he thought to himself as he neared one of the front entrances. All he had to do was make it to that street and he’d get away from this madman trying to catch up with him an… AHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHH! Using every last muscle to fuel one last-ditch tackle, Sonny leapt through the air and brought down his entire body weight around the kid’s waist, slamming him face-first into the ground worse than any televised Quarterback Sack. He rabidly took two handfuls of the kid’s shaggy blonde-brown mane and pinned the obvious junkie to the ground to prevent escape. He didn’t care where the purse landed, all that mattered was this kid got what he deserved. “Think it’s funny to rob a woman, you stupid fuck?” Sonny contemplated turning this child on his back and leveling him with such a right hook that his skull’s features would become unrecognizable to even forensics detectives. He would certainly think twice about attempting this stunt again. Luckily, God appeared to smile on the poor soul as two members of mall security arrived just in time to keep the kid from a much worse fate at Silver’s hands. When it registered with people what happened this boy and why this enormous man tackled him to the ground, sparse applause erupted from several rubbernecks that just happened upon this odd scenario. Silver gestured over to the tan purse he tried to swindle off the sobbing lady he’d left behind upstairs as one of the men went to retrieve it. “HEY!” As the PRIMEate stood to his feet, Maria huffed frenetically and bent over, clasping her knees in an attempt to catch her breath. Sonny merely stood upright and took in the situation at large. He just stopped some would-be mugger from making it into the outside world with somebody else’s belongings. People were clapping around him. It was… eerie and foreign to him all the same. “I…” As soon as she regained her breath, she smiled at her son. “You… I can’t believe you did that…” “Bullshit.” Maria was taken aback. “Wait, what?” “Karma.” He hadn’t forgotten the harsh things his mom accused him of doing. His mind had gone back to the last conversation they had prior to this entire mess. “Karma’s a load of bullshit. Get it through your head, Mom. There’s no outside force trying to guide the hands of these motions. It’s all jealously. Eme took Gaby because she was jealous that I got to live my dream while she got stuck in an office job wondering what could’ve been. The people I wrestle with WISH they could have half the talent I possess and Lindsay couldn’t stop living in the past while I bettered myself. I had no use for her, so I cut her out and she’s been trying to get back at me for it ever since. There’s your grand explanations. In shock, Maria just stood there, taking this all in. “I’ll meet you in the car. I’ve had enough of this place.” Maria could do nothing but watch her son leave through the doors. The rent-a-cops would take care of the rest, but in his eyes, he was merely doing their job for them when he tackled that boy to the ground; a feeling he’d had many times before with simpletons like them. It was what made most of the human race weak. Dependability and the hopes that somebody would come along and fix everything for you was not something Sonny Gabriel Silver believed in. Maybe he was wrong to bring Maria out on the road with him. She was better off back on the other side. ~~~~~ Later That Night Ramada Hotel, 11:18 PM He’d been incredibly hard on his mom and the fact bothered him immensely. He knew all she was trying to do was help and he knew full well that was why he invited her out here aside from the fact he didn’t want to be alone. Tomorrow would be a new day and hopefully he could make some kind of peace offering. But he still didn’t understand why he snapped on her. Maybe he was hoping for some more rational answer than “Karma hates your guts.” Maybe he wanted something logical and meaningful as to why things seemed to be going south with no sign of stopping, despite the fact everything he was doing he felt was for the better. A simple answer for a complex problem. Sonny didn’t buy into any of that God nonsense after the loss of his father and the idea of a mystical force predetermining every single action was just some crazy guy’s idea. Seemed like a great slogan on a t-shirt or to sell a movie to Michael Bay, but not as a belief system that you put blind faith in. Putting your faith in something that doesn’t exist was a foreign experience to him and not one he’d undertake any time soon. There was no magic see-saw that measured good and evil. No black and white. Just horrible, messy splotches of gray. But for right now, he couldn’t worry about all that. He had a match this week and a second chance at the Universal Title on the horizon. Matters like this couldn’t get to him. It was not long after he passed out when his cell phone sprang to life, vibrating across the wooden surface of the nightstand. Upended from his groggy state, he carefully peered one eye out from underneath the blanket, feeling around the table until his cell ended up in his grip. “What the fu…” The name on the phone stunned him right out of his groggy state.
