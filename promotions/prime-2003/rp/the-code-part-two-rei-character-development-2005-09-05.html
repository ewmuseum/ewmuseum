The Code (Part Two: Rei) [CHARACTER DEVELOPMENT]

5 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9-5-05 Bushido For the uninitiated, a code of ethics that prevailed among the warrior class in feudal Japan...the code of the samurai. This is the story of Kyle Lamen, the story of a man who unintentionally lives by this code. Not because he read it in a book, or saw it in a movie. But because inherently, it is his way. The Six-String Samurai. The seven virtues of Bushido: Jin (Benevolence)Rei (Respect)Chuugi (Loyalty)Gi (Rectitude)Yu (Courage)Meiyo (Honor)Makoto (Honesty) * * * * * THE CODE: REI 08.24.05. Tallahassee, Florida. “Mom, it's not necessary!” His trademark bright blue hair was more vibrant than ever, as Kyle Lamen hoisted a large suitcase into the back of a black Chevy Tahoe. Unfortunately, perspiration was beading up on the forehead of PRIME's Prince of Punk Rock (now with 100% more alliteration), and it had a distinctly blue tinge to it, a sure sign of recent dyeing. “I swear, if you pack one more effing suitcase, I'm going to-” His words were swiftly cut off as a middle-aged woman, came out of the house, and waved a finger at him. “You just never can tell, Kyle. If that hurricane passes through here, we'll have trouble catching a flight home.” Amanda Cameron, the middle-aged woman, was Kyle's mother. She peered out from behind tortosie-shell glasses, and hefted another sizable duffle into the back of the Tahoe. She wore a simple pair of jeans and white blouse, but wore a pair of white gloves on her hands, and her hair was very much askance. “Right, ma. Whatever. It doesn't matter, though...Jonathan is going to watch over the place while we're gone. Sarah should be here soon, and Bekah's done packing. We need to get up and out if we're going to make it to Hawaii in time, y'know!” Ms. Cameron just shook her head, and with her perm, her hair was quite resistant to the force of the shake. “I know, I know...let me just go grab Bekah, and see if she's got everything she needs...” She cracked a very small part of a smile, as if to say 'you know what I mean,' and went inside the garage. Such is the way of mothers. Kyle sat outside, on the back of the Tahoe, and waited. The order of his thoughts went as follows. Man, I'm hungry...I don't remember the Tally airport as having a very good food court...Maybe we could hit Crispers on the way out...If we've still got time...The flight's at four?...Man, I really need to get a traditional iPod, the Shuffle thing just isn't doing it for me...I wonder how Bekah's holding up?...She could use some time in Hawaii...Grrrrr, Blackwolf and Walczak... Finally, Bekah Cameron, a very attractive, leggy brunette around the age of twenty-five, steps out of the door from the inside of the house, to the garage. She's wearing a simple pair of track pants (white) and a black tube top, with her thin-rimmed glasses obscuring her green eyes that match Kyle's, and her hair pulled back in a short ponytail. She can't but smile at seeing her little brother perched on the back of the Tahoe, obviously thinking. “Little brother, you look like those gears are grinding in your head. What's going on?” Kyle shook off his internal dialogue, and jumped off the back of the SUV, lifting his hands in a shrugging manner. “Nothing important, sis. Wrestling and food stuff, basically.” She winked at him. “Some things never change, huh?” Kyle shook his head, and Bekah leaned back against the riding lawnmower parked off to the side in the garage, which admittedly hadn't seen too much use recently. “By the way, Bekah...I've got a list drawn up of wrestlers you can and cannot socialize with backstage at the show.” Bekah blushed a little, and stood up, putting a hand to her sternum, faux shocked. “LITTLE BROTHER! You've got NO right to tell me who I can and cannot socialize with! I'll speak with whichever hunky wrestler I wish...” As Bekah ranted and raved, Kyle simply dug around in the pocket of his cargo pants, and produced a folded-up sheet of paper. Bekah walked over and snatched it out of his hand, before he could manage most anything, and began to read from it... “Angelo Deville...man, you even underlined that one! Hoyt Williams, Blackwolf and Walczak...seriously, Kyle! I DO have better taste than that! Nova? Come on, bro...is it about the drug thing? And what's this? You have Dave Gibson crossed out?! Is he the only person on the roster I'm allowed to socialize with?” Kyle, voice monotone, shakes his head. “Skip to the end, Bekah.” Bekah does fairly quickl, for such a long note, and gets to the end with a sour face. “You put Gibson back on the list!!” Kyle nods. “Why!? Isn't he your tag team partner!?” Kyle nods again, but this time more slowly. “Yeah. I trust him with my future in the ring, but it's not like I'm gonna trust him around my sister, you know?” Bekah simply groans a little, and procures a pen from her track pants, and begins editing Kyle's list. “Well, this is simply ridiculous. Gibson's coming off the list, because he's a pretty good-looking guy, and he actually IS one of the most trustworthy of this bunch. I'm also overruling you on Rampage. It's not like I'd have anything to worry about with him, from what you've told me, and what I saw when I used to watch RPW...” Kyle shook his head at both his sister's fascination with wrestlers (inherited from his mother, unfortunately) and her fascination with well-defined abs. “...Oxios HAS to be harmless, as goofy as he is. YOU ACTUALLY PUT AIMZ ON HERE!?!” She walked over to Kyle, and swatted him on the arm. Kyle simply responded the best he could. “Never know about her. I still haven't met her, and she seems to hate every woman in the company.” Bekah simply clicks her tongue in disapproval. “And there's no way I'm letting you keep me away from Ellis Easton, or definitely not Danny Ferguson! I can't believe that you actually wrestle these people!” Kyle can do nothing but shake his head at his sister's zeal, as she looked at him with her green eyes glinting in the Tallahassee sun. “Oh for god's sake. I'll pull Gibbo and Aimz from the list. Sound fair? And Ferguson, because he and those bodyguards would never let you get close enough to begin with. Alright?” Bekah Cameron, sister to Kyle Lamen, smiled and hugged her brother warmly. Kyle couldn't help but to roll his eyes. “That's a nice enough concession, brother, especially considering the fact that you'll be far too concerned with your match to keep watch on me anyways!” Bekah skipped off, back into the house to gather the rest of her belongings, and glided right past their mother, who was coming back out, struggling under a heavy suitcase. Kyle darted over and lifted it for her. “Mom, you're kidding me. There's no way that you should be trying to carry stuff like this!” Amanda Cameron shook her head, her perm only slightly waving in the air. “Are you kidding me, Kyle? The day when I can't handle my own luggage is the day when you put me out to pasture with the other old women who play bingo, got it? I used to haul egg crates at the Meadowlands during wrestling events and rock concerts, and a few pounds of-” Kyle just waved her off with his free hand, and laughed a little. “Alright, alright. You want to carry your own suitcase, you can.” Kyle's mother shrugged. “You've already got it. It'll be fine.” Kyle could do nothing more than shake his head, and throw the suitcase into the back of the Tahoe. It's silly to me, how Walczak and Blackwolf talk about respect. It's the only thing in the world that HAS to be given, and can never be taken. My sister shows me respect, even though like she said, she doesn't have to. I show my mother respect, for all the things she's done for me. Gibbo and I respect each other, based on our prior accomplishments, and the work we did together, in the ring at ReV 71. Bekah comes back out of the house, with a suitcase of her own, and Kyle nnchalantly tosses it in the back of the Tahoe. No matter how that match finishes in Hawaii, unless the two of you guys can beat Dave and I cleanly, for a three count...there's nothing you can do to earn our respect. And that's not about being 'Old School' or 'New School.' If you can come through when it matters, it's deserving of respect. Amanda and Bekah Cameron smile, and Kyle returns the smile. 08.24.05. Honolulu, Hawaii. Inside the 'Will Call' booth of the ticket window at the Stan Sherriff Center in Hawaii, there's a young intern putting tickets to white envelopes, to be kept for call-ahead orders and special persons. He puts two into a white envelope, and then marks it with a pre-made label. Respect is what you make of it. It's what you show friends and loved ones. It's giving more than it's taking. You show respect sometimes to people that don't deserve it... He affixes the label, and pulls his hand away, so we can read it. Johnny Lamen ...and at this pay-per-view, that's exactly what we're doing. Because THAT is what respect is about.
