Date: ReV 195 I'm talking to mirrors now. Worse, I'm seeing things in them that I know aren't really there, and there's not a thing I can do to stop it. I thought I was just paranoid, that a decade of restless nights and a little too much head trauma were taking there toll on me as I approached the big 33. Now? Who the hell knows. It started out with dreams - nightmares, really - not that those were any strange new development in my life. You go through the shit I've gone through, then the insides of your eyelids can become pretty damn frightening movie screens on a nightly basis. Still, imagining a creepy guy in black was hunting me ranked in the upper levels of disturbing nighttime visions. Still, a dream's a dream, right? That's what I thought, until I started seeing things while I was awake. It was just glimpses at first - a faint figure in a crowded airport, the flash of a black trenchcoat sweeping against the gound - but it was enough to send me off the walls. I didn't know what was happening, or why, but I was sure that my dreams were the herald of something awful. Then I saw the video. Clear, absolute proof that this man, this ghost, was nothing but a figment of my imagination gone wild. Just a creation of my subconscious, my mind creating its own sense of irrational paranoia. An hallucination. That was the good news. If it was in my mind, it was something I could take control of, cope with. Turns out my mind ... had a mind of its own. You normally don't expect to be disturbed in the middle of a sleepless night by an imaginary man in the mirror. If you have been, I'd like to extend an invitation to our very exclusive club. Of course, I'm pretty sure you're alternate personality isn't a sarcastic contract killer, so I've probably got the trump card on that one. From that first night i saw him, it ... whatever, I haven't been able to rid myself of this affliction. I see things, day and night, things that aren't there, things that never would or should be there, and all the therapy, all the medication, all the meditation I've tried has accomplished a damn thing. The only product of all my frustration is a man in my head who won't stop gnawing away at my psyche. Which leads me to one conclusion. I'm crazy, or, I'm well on the way. And I'm scared. There were times that I had people to lean on in times of trouble. My Dad, Lauren, they were always there to pull me through my darkest hours. There not here anymore. Now, the only one left to fight the battle is me, and I'm terrified of being overwhelmed. But, if my mind is to be a victim of an all-out assault, I will not go down without a fight. I'm going to stop this, or I'm going to lose myself trying. God help me. -Troy Douglas Columbus, Ohio June 2, 2009
