The Sickness (A Nova/Ferguson Joint)

17 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/17/07 The Celebrity Tavern in Marina Del Ray, CA made up for a lack of creativity with an abundance of what people euphemize as "atmosphere." The place has probably seen as many famous faces as it has cans of Lysol - it's a small number in both cases. Longtime patrons - all five - will cite that the Celeb has had shit stapled to the walls long before T.G.I. fuckin' Fridays came along and made that cool. The Cali license plates, the framed Raiders jersey, the faded 8x10 of Johnny Carson that employees pointed to as justification for the name - everything has a story, but none of them were worth telling. A record July crowd of six is scattered around the Tavern, a third of them saddled up to the bar proper. The television propped on the far side is showing a static-plagued version of the Angels game but at this point everyone has grown accustomed to listening instead of watching. It's the Thursday after ReVolution 133, and despite the ever-present push through Colossus, Blaine Blair had opted to give the cast and crew an extra day in Los Angeles before turning the wagons north and continuing The Road To... This was great news to more than a few of the traveling party. There was at least one man, though, who was NOT happy with the vacation time, and that is the same guy for whom extra time in Hollywood was anything but a vacation. So skip past the joke about irony as Danny Ferguson parts the doors of the Celeb - he didn't look at the sign atop the building so much as the neon "Budweiser" one in the window. He'd driven past the place - or rather, been driven past it - plenty of times, but had never given it much thought. In the middle of the biggest crisis of his career, he needed a haven from the cameras and the slumming tabloid reporters, and he found it in the graying standalone watering hole a quarter-mile from the beach. With his aviators still on and his jacket pulled close to discourage recognition, he limp-walks up to the bar and sits down, pretty obviously trying to look cool rather than actually BE cool. Story of his life. Fact is, he had little to worry about around the place. Most of the capacity crowd fell outside of the coveted 18-49 demographic in which Danny had been considered a minor deity. Better still, they hadn't watched wrestling since the heyday of "Iron" Mike Sharpe - and we'll leave it up to you to decide which two months would have been his heyday. Ducking into the Celeb was nearly the perfect decision for Danny - it was the kind of place where he could get away from everything that had been chasing him since June. The key word there, though, was "nearly." Unfortunately for the Superstar, one of the other nonregular Celeb patrons knew him well enough to not be thrown by a high collar or big shades. “Hiding from the world you wanted so much to endorse and saturate, Ferguson?” Danny’s eyes widen in shock as he cranes his head slowly – he doesn’t want his eyes to confirm the source of the voice – around to see former Universal Champion and current bane of his existence Caesar “Nova” Vega sitting at barstool not six feet away from him, nursing a bourbon & coke. "I don't-" "I don't, either," Nova jabs, "so keep your blood pressure down. I'm here looking for the same thing you are - peace and quiet." "And you really think you can show up in my bar and claim you don't want trouble?" Ferguson snaps back. "I think that I came in here first and you seem about as familiar with this place as you are with romantic comedies, therefore it's my bar. I also think you can't afford to get another story in Us Weekly about being a public nuisance, so how 'bout you stay in your seat and your agent doesn't drop another press release on your behalf." "Cute," Danny growls. "Please, continue to make my existence as miserable as your own. It's especially fun to hear you get cheered for it." The Risen Star lights a cigarette and takes a long swallow from his drink. “Oh, right…because you weren’t loving it or anything when you charged the ring at 122 and waylaid me with a chair after my match with Worth, and had the fans chanting your name over it. Fuck you. Do you, even now, have any idea of the impact of your actions that night? I was ready to throw off the shackles, and after you played ‘hero’ – pathetically, I might add – the last thing I heard before passing out was your name chanted by the people who used to love me.” Nova ashes his cigarette and brings the tumbler to his mouth again. He drinks until ice hits his teeth with a clink-clink-clink, and then holds it up for the barkeep to notice. “So you won’t mind me inviting you to piss off over the whole ‘they love me again’ thing, Danny…because you never knew what it was like to have them really love you. They just went along with you, the fans and yourself both playing at it like drunken college kids on a one-night stand, pretending to love each other and ignoring the lie they can see in front of their own faces. But I did, Danny. I worked my ass off before you ever got to PRIME and I earned their admiration; losing that was excruciating, no matter how arrogantly I brushed it aside at the time.” "I had my reasons for doing what I did," Danny says, his voice a harsh whisper as he tries not to draw attention. "Did you?" Nova scoffs and looks straight ahead at the television as the keep refills his drink. "Right, that. Somehow it's stupid for me to accuse you of blowing someone up, but it's perfectly normal for you to plan and exact a half-year quest for revenge." "You thi-" "I don't know what I think, thanks. But what I know is that giving you an accidental dose of the world outside of Hollywood is NOT among the long list of regrets I have in this lifetime. If that qualifies as ruining a life, then you sure as shit had a life worth ruining." "And that was your choice to make, I guess." "Stop being a fucking crybaby," The Risen Star spits, his eyes downcast. Down the bar, one of the regulars, the kind of grisled and perpetually lubricated guy who would swear to his grave that he could knock out Chuck Liddell with the right punch, looked up from his current dosage to cast an eye towards the two of them. Danny angles himself away and pulls his jacket tighter. Nova, meanwhile, doesn't pay the guy any mind. He continues. "Shit happens in this world. Is everyone proud of the decisions they’ve made? Would some people do it over? Maybe. Probably." "So I should back off because you're sorry, right?" "I never said I was sorry." Nova pauses and turns his head enough to look Danny in the eye. "But if this is how you reacted to the first curveball the world threw you, then you're going to have a rough few years in the bigs. It doesn't get easier." On the set across the bar, the crack of wood on leather jerks Danny's attention. The keep spins to see who just got the base hit in the game. "Besides," Nova says, calm as ever, "you and I both know that you can't just back off now." "I don't want to back off now." "Whatever." Danny shakes his head. "I had plenty of time to think and rethink my intentions during my stay in the quote-unquote 'real world.' You're right. It WAS good for me. It gave me plenty of time to meditate and second guess. It gave me confidence that if I decided I needed to destroy you to close that chapter in my life, I fucking meant it. It's cathartic - I exorcise the demons and then I get on with my life." "But it's not that easy." "The hell it isn't." "If all it took was a little beating on yours truly, then why are you still around?" Nova asks. "Why not leave after Culture Shock, or after the sneak attack at 122, or the massacre at Overkill?" He pauses for effect and takes drink. "Because you can't. Because it's not...that...easy." "There's a big difference between all those and Colossus. No bullshit, no one else, just me and you." "And your Asian ass-pirate friend." Silence from Ferguson. He stares at his drink and ponders taking the first sip. Nova chuckles. "Exactly. Because you talk a big game about making peace with yourself and righting the wrongs, but at the end of the day you're just a prick who'll do whatever he can to get ahead. Maybe Troy didn't see it, or maybe she and Tchu and Killean and the whole damn company were willing to overlook it, but I am and always have been the one to call bullshit on this whole act. Don't pretend you're being noble, Ferguson. You're a fucking coward." This time Danny laughs. "Funny that you use relationships as a metaphor for the fans. That's where you and I part company; you're looking for love and I'm just trying to get what I can and move on. You spend your life pandering to these people and they'll eat you alive." He receives his own drink, a cheap bottle that he bought for effect more than taste, and shakes his head. "I spend a few months giving them what they want, and not only do they get me a step closer to putting my hands on you, but they absolve me of blame when I do it. I tried to cripple you and they cheered. That was icing on the cake - if I was the one-night stand, you're the deadbeat pothead boyfriend who got dumped after the hook-up rocked her world. But you're still hung up on getting them back in your corner?" Ferguson almost jumps back when Nova’s response is loud, almost barking laughter. “You lie to yourself more than you lie to the rest of the world, Danny. They’re what it’s all about. This isn’t a single-player game we’re playing. There’s always a ‘them,’ be it the fans, or the roster, or management, or your alcoholic abusive dad, or whatever…and it’s always ‘their’ approval you seek when you climb in that ring. You can’t tell me that everything you’ve put yourself through since joining PRIME, nights where you bled from every pore in your skin, nights you practically cried screaming bloody murder into a microphone…you just can’t tell me that was all for yourself, or that you were ‘giving them what they wanted,’ Danny. That’s bullshit.” Nova knocks back half the glass’s contents (and probably about four dollars) before snuffing his cigarette and lighting a fresh one. Danny waves smoke away from his face. "Tell me, then, if you're doing everything for this mysterious 'them', why are you all alone in a fucking dive bar miles from anything you could remotely call home?" The Risen Star bristles at Danny's words, his grip tightening on the glass. After momentarily tensing up in anticipation of the evening turning physical, Ferg notices he picked the right nerve to hit. "When you do it for anyone other than yourself, sooner or later you end up as a disappointment. Believe me, I know. You may not get it yet, but when I see to your forced retirement at Colossus, you'll wonder why you didn't spend enough time doing it for yourself.” “Is that why you’re pulling this whole stunt?” Nova fires back, redirecting the heat, “this whole ‘fuck Hollywood, it’s my party and I’ll wrestle if I want to’ thing? Is it because underneath that glitzy condescending ‘shit-don’t-stink’ exterior, Danny Ferguson…(loud gasp)…loves the industry?” Nova puts his hands together, bats his eyelashes, and grins a toothless, sappy, mocking grin. “That’s so…cute.” "Go to hell." "I'll get there eventually." "I don't even know why I stick around the 'industry' anyway. Maybe when I bury you I can start looking for a new project." "Please. I did a little reading while I was in town. Picked up a stack of rags that the teenage cashier recommended. They all say the same thing about Danny Ferguson: Your wrestling is interfering with your acting career, but your acting is interfering with your wrestling career. Anyone who could just walk away, like you claim you can, would have been gone already." "Sorry, I didn't know you were the authority on walking away. How's the family?" A deep breath and the most extreme display of patience in Nova's personal history keeps the quiet scene from becoming something more. "Take all the cheap shots you need to feel better about yourself. It won't get you any closer to what you want, which is to be gone." "Excuse me?" "You can bullshit yourself, but I know someone else with The Sickness when I see them. You can't just get up and walk away. You probably wish you could, but you know that even if you managed to get out for a few months, even a few years, you'd always come back. We always do." "Don't you dare put me in your company." "A little late for that. Truth is, Danny, you WANT me to win. Maybe if I turn back your lame-ass Darkman act at Colossus, the sheer shame would be so overwhelming that it could cancel out your need for the game. Then you could have an excuse, a reason to leave, an alibi. You want me to force you out of PRIME because you're too goddamned scared to leave on your own power." He puts his drink down and pushes it towards the keep, waving his hand to signal that he's done. "So you can go out in front of the fans you don't care about in the ring you can leave whenever you want, and you can run me down because I respect the game in a way you wish you could. But don't you dare accuse me of not being able to leave. Whether it's true or not, you, of all people, have no room to lecture me about The Sickness, you fucking hypocrite." Nova stands up from his seat. He lays a heavy bill down on the bar and turns for the door without another word. Danny hangs his head, eyes closed and breathing sharp, unable to say anything else. The crowd at the Angel's game lets out a roar for a home run shot, the cheering crackling into static.
