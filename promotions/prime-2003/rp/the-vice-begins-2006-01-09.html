The Vice Begins

9 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01.09.2006. Scattered ThoughtsEl Paso, Texas 'In El Paso, things are different,' explains the voice-over of El Vice, 'here you've always got to watch your back. There are damn Mexicans crossing the border every day. Man I hate those Mexicans. And then there are cowboys that like to shoot at them. And sometimes they get confused between illegal immigrants crossing the border, and illegal immigrants that have been here a very long time, like El Vice. You see, in El Paso, we live in el vice. At least that is what I understood from my very few Sunday school sessions.' The voice of El Vice continues. 'I learned in my early days that life is not a walk in the park,' he continues, 'no one ever knew where my father was, and my mother was sick. The kids down the street were older than I was and they picked on me. Sometimes they would steal the groceries that I bought for my mother, because she was having trouble walking to the grocery store. We barely had enough money to get by. But soon my mother was dead and I was on my own. Can't say I didn't enjoy that, for the short time I could get by on the small portion of money I had been left. It didn't last me long. I blew it on women, cigarettes, and an old, ratty acoustic guitar.' Several chords are struck in quick succession. 'So I went out to try to find a job at age eighteen,' he said, 'and let me tell you, it's hard to find a job when you're of Mexican descent, a child of illegal immigrants, and eighteen years old with ratty clothes. For weeks I went on with the search in vain. Soon I gave up, and I sat on benches in El Paso with my guitar, playing the chords of old country songs and singing along. I didn't speak very good English back in those days, in fact I substituted many of the English words for Spanish. I got some money that way, at least enough to get by. I could buy new guitar strings when they broke. I could keep that small roof over my head and occasionally get a coffee at the local McDonald's.' Again, those same distinct chords. 'Three years later I was approached by a middle-aged man, who listened to me play,' he continues, 'my popularity had grown by then. I got plenty of tips at the park. People came around to listen to me. They said I had a pure country voice, a smooth tone. They told me I had achieved mastery of the guitar. Anyway, this man, he told me after I played that there might be a position for a character like me in a wrestling promotion in El Paso. Of course, I knew nothing about wrestling. El Vice did not have television. The man had to explain everything to me. Truth be told, I was brought into the wrestling industry by my talent, I wasn't just a talent out looking for a wrestling promotion. So I learned the art, from the ground up, the way it was meant to be learned.' There are seven chords, each as pronounced as the last. 'And as soon as I stepped out from behind that curtain, I was addicted. The reaction of the crowd spurred me on. My completely original spanglish song rocked the house. They cut my hair a certain way. They made me wear certain clothes, but as soon as I got out there I was a star. They called me 'El Rey', that's Spanish for 'The King'. I don't know where they got that, exactly, but when I was out there, I certainly did feel like a king. I was a legend in my own mind. And soon, when I got a bit more wrestling under my belt, I was tearing up the league. I won enough matches that they sent me off to a bigger, better, and a slightly more legal wrestling school to learn some new moves and gain some more entertainment insight.' The expected chords did not come at the expected time. It was the same tune, but now it's melodic, high-pitched notes. 'That place was far from home, in a place called Phoenix, Arizona. I was trained by one of the best men in the business, Mr. Salideus Stone. He taught me how to wrestle even better. He showed me some of the best grappling techniques. He taught me how to entertain better. He even taught me how to be an even better guitar player. Now I'm the complete package. The scouts came raining down on me as soon as I graduated from Stone's Academy. I chose to come to PRIME, because I knew that I could make the biggest impact there. I knew that PRIME was the place for me.' The resolution of the song is a simple chord, held in vibrato. Walk in the ParkEl Paso, Texas El Vice is walking in the park in El Paso, Texas. There's a slight breeze in his hair. He's wearing a pair of blue jean cut-off shorts and a t-shirt. His sideburns are neatly trimmed and his sunglasses are wide-brimmed. He does not wear a hat on this occasion. He finds a bench and takes a seat, stretching out his legs and leaning back, cracking his back again the wood. The stiffness looks to be relieved. 'I've heard the names,' he softly says, 'who hasn't? I'm in a league with the big boys now. I'm no longer worried about the local legends of El Paso, as impressive as some of them were. It's now all about the real big names. It's about the combatants in PRIME. There's no one better.' El Vice takes a deep breath of the fresh air. 'Chet Worth,' El Vice mutters, 'the name means something to the fans of PRIME. He's impressive for some reason. Is he some kind of up-and-comer? Should he have my attention?' El Vice shakes his head. 'I can't say that I'm particularly intimidated. Then again I rarely am. I'm just here to have some fun. I'm here to play some music and win some matches. This is one of those in which I'd really like to make a very good impression. I'd like to get off on the right foot. And at the moment, Chet Worth seems to be one of those standing directly in my path to success.' El Vice frowns briefly. 'Chris Collins,' El Vice whispers, 'I've heard that name many times before. He's been around, this one has. He's gained a reputation. I don't know how well he has done in PRIME, but I know that he's got potential. I know that the man can unleash hell, and I know that I'm going to have to be careful around him. He's a menace in the ring, a real captivator. He's one that can sort of stand in the shadows, reach out and play Dark Horse for a bit. Chris Collins is another that can take this match if he has the passion. Unfortunately it is I with that passion, it is my debut, and Chris Collins has no special reason to go out and to win this one. There's no motivation.' El Vice strokes his chin for a moment. 'Damien Cruz,' El Vice says, 'there's a name I've heard some of. He's another with a reputation. I like the name. It's full of ... ' El Vice pauses, 'heritage. I wonder how true he's been to that heritage. I often wonder if he deserves a name like that, if he's served it well. Damien Cruz is a wrestler in PRIME, and that is a signal of what he is capable of. He faces the same problem that Chris Collins does on this occasion. His issue is that this coming ReVolution is my in-ring debut, and I intend to make it special. What motivation does he have to take that from me? It's all about the drive.' El Vice continues on his list. 'Peter Vetra,' he begins, 'he's been around PRIME for a while. I've watched before, and that name is always a constant. I'm not sure why he's being placed in a match like this. Maybe he wants to stamp his dominance over us, bring us under his umbrella of control. I'm not here to put up with that. I'm going to be sure to make a point in this match to Peter Vetra. I'm going to get in his face. I'm going to show him that I'm la nueva kind of force. It's possible that he won't even know what has hit him. El Vice, the doctors will tell him in the back, while giving him the smelling salts.' El Vice pauses to think for a moment. 'And we come to Brian Arson,' he says with a smile, 'Brian Arson is a name that I am very familiar with. I must admit that I have been very impressed with Brian Arson thus far in my career. Arson is someone who has persevered. He made it into PRIME. He's done decently for himself. He's overcome the odds. And someone like that makes for a very difficult opponent, especially when you're the favorite. Unfortunately for Mr. Arson, I'm not the favorite. I'm the underdog on this occasion. And yet I believe that I will prove the most potent threat.' El Vice takes another deep breath. 'And last but not least, Tyler O'Neill,' he concludes, 'he's the last one in the match, and one of the ones I know least about. I will have to do some studying on him. He seems to be quite the wrestler. I also have heard some talk about him. His reputation precedes him, they might say. But yet that's not important. All I need is one weakness. All I need is one thing to bring him down. All of these men have them. All of these men are arrogant. They don't see what's about to grip the company. They can't comprehend it. They don't sense it. It's El Vice. It's the man with the acoustic guitar. It's the man that can tear a man apart ten different ways. It's the newcomer. All of these men are completely unaware of the force that is upcoming.' El Vice chuckles to himself. 'Blissfully unaware,' he continues, 'but that's okay. That's what learning is all about. You must first be ignorant to be enlightened. My six opponents are about to be enlightened. I am about to show them the way. But they won't be the only ones. It'll be the entirety of PRIME, from the administration to the fans, to all the wrestlers backstage. I'm about to make my first impact. It will be at Revolution 86. You saw the beginning of El Vice on 85. You'll see the excellence of El Vice on 86, but it will only be the tip of the iceberg on my domination. How long can it last?' El Vice smiles. 'PRIME's walk in the park is over.' El Vice comes to his guitar and picks it up. He sets it on his lap and begins strumming some chords lightly. It's a sad tune. El Vice's voice is absent, but it's not required. It's a voiceless muse in a peaceful meadow. Fade.
