Gone (Great American Nightmare - Tyler O'Neill)

Date: Timeless I’ve been sitting here Can’t get you off my mind I’m trying my best to be a man and be strong I’ve drove myself insane wishing I could touch your face But the truth Re- Mains You’re Gone - Justin Timberlake, “Gone” ~><>…<><~ The way she moved was mesmerizing. Hypnotic. As if the music was being conjured in time to her swaying magic. Every flounce of hip brought a thud of bass, a pulsing aftershock that rippled from her to consume her greyscale imitators. Every whip of mane inspired a dulcet string, delicate and refined, followed by a sharp lash of electronic cymbal. Whenever her tongue flitted between trembling lips, a heart-stuttering pause, as if allure had caught the breath of the world. Deville had been watching her for nearly an hour, wondering if her spine was a slinky in disguise, crooking a half-smile when her pelvis thrashed violently at the bottom of each rhythmic roll of torso, embracing the impure thoughts that piqued. Other observers dressed up as dancers occasionally obscured his view, but their pretence bade them move on before irritation could grip him. The Deville made no pretence whatsoever; he sat swamped in strobe, leaning back in his chair, one arm dangling over the back, the other cradling his drink, and watched, blatantly. It had been a long time since he’d suffered the obnoxious clamour of a nightclub, but after three days of being cooped up on a train, with only the sound of the rail and idle chatter for company, he craved something loud. Something explosive. He needed a release, a discharge that had been building since well before he resolved to flee. It’ll be easy, he assured himself, ignoring the shadows of doubt that loomed. A piece of cake. You’ve done this how many times? He couldn’t quote a figure, but there was no doubt he’d hogged more than his fair share. He started to reflect that it was like riding a bike, but cut himself off with scolding words about thinking in clichés. Shifting, stifling a sigh, he drowned the thoughts with a draught, lost again in mental stalking. Her hair really was like a mane of fairytales, thick and lustrous yet obviously soft and forgiving to touch, perfectly curved to brush her shoulders. It shone blonde, but the roots were anyone’s guess. The authenticity of her bosom was also in question; the sly black supporter peeking through the mesh shirt was undoubtedly a WonderBra. Despite noticing it, Deville couldn’t convince himself that it mattered. What mattered was that he’d selected her. He couldn’t bring himself to define the impetus, but it tickled the back of his mind, and his subconscious laughed at the attempted denial. It’s been too long. Far too long. Performance anxiety wasn’t his worry; it never was – the doubt and hesitation were wrought of guilt. For misdeeds done and yet to come. It should never happen again. You’re a monster. All you sow is pain and fear. No amount of spied shooting stars would change that fact for a wish, and no amount of candy-coating or white-washing with pretension could offer redemption. She doesn’t deserve it. Bear your burden alone. Leave her be. He wished he could. While he bore no illusions of her innocence, she was young, and her eyes belied her beguiling manner. She knew she was being watched, and she likely had an idea of the thoughts curdling the minds of her observers, but the depth of her darkest suspicions couldn’t have scraped the roof of The Deville’s depraved reality. When she spotted him and smiled invitingly, almost impishly, she was blind to what she was really inciting. Spare her. She didn’t have to be like the others, tormented for eternity for the sole misfortune of lighting a spark in his eyes. Let her remember you as an attractive what-if, not an unending nightmare. He didn’t have to ruin her. He didn’t, yet he did. It was a part of him. It had always been, and he knew it would always be. Trying to deny it was refusing to accept who he was, and he was tired of playing that game. It had led to more pain and frustration than anything the former fashioned. You’re a monster, he repeated as he stood, drawing her attention and arching an eyebrow, cocking a suggestive smile. Never missing a gyrating beat, she smiled in spite of herself, winked, then smiled shyly as if appalled at her forwardness. The Deville cut his glance toward one of the backdoors, wincing inside as hers followed. She flashed a petulant look, but gave it the lie by snaking her way through the crowd. He followed. He had to do it. He needed to recover a part of himself, the power he’d stripped away. He needed to prove to himself that he was still the man he used to be. A chill washed over him as she pushed through the door to the outside, but it wasn’t from the nippy autumn air. She doesn’t deserve this. She didn’t, but he did. She was backing away as he pushed into the alley, hands clasped behind her back and chest thrust forward, chin raised. A wry smile graced her lips and danced in her eyes, underwritten by nervousness. Her mouth opened, curling to form flirtatious words, but at once her eyes widened, and all that came was a throaty squeak. The Deville strode toward her like imperturbable Death, arm ramrod straight at shoulder height, the slim metal of his pistol gleaming damningly as it measured her for the next life. Horror painted her face as she gave her head an almost imperceptible shake, raising her arms before her as if she believed they were Kevlar. She couldn’t summon the question, but it radiated from her shaking body as it crumpled in on itself. He had to. It was that simple. He couldn’t explain it to her, couldn’t make her understand. His killer instinct had fled him, his ruthlessness chased away by what he’d believed was noble intent, and now he had to get it back. It wasn’t her fault. She was innocent. But it was the innocence that damned her, faced with a devil who had decided personal redemption lay in killing on a whim. The Deville snarled as he pressed the barrel to her forehead, relaxing his finger for the inevitable squeeze. Tears overcame her shock as a high-pitched whine sounded in her throat, but he blocked out both. I’m sorry, he whispered in his own mind, then closed his eyes and averted his head, as if to shelter himself from the shot. She screamed. He tensed. His body roared to life. The scream followed him as he ran for the mouth of the alley at a dead sprint, and he knew it would haunt him for months to come, echoing in his perfect memory. His mind was an erratic jumble of irrational thought, impossible to decipher. The gun remained in his hand as he continued running and running. He was oblivious to everything except what he’d done. He didn’t stop until he was deep in another alley several blocks away. A sheen distorted his vision as he glared accusingly at the gun, blaming it for his inability to pull the trigger. He opened his mouth to curse it, to curse himself, but as he did the bile revolted, forcing itself from his system, doubling him over to empty his insides on the concrete. He was supposed to be a killer, a heartless monster. He knew his merciless cruelty was the only reason he was as successful as he was, let alone alive. It was what had sustained him, what gave him his notoriety, his pride, his comfort. Now, no matter how he tried to recapture it and rebel, it was gone.
