Album (Prologue)

Date: 4/18/10 The attic was musty and moth-ridden, as most untended attics have a habit of being. But, even by attic standards, the one in Troy Douglas' hose was a doozy. Spending upwards of 200 days a year scrambling across the better known parts of the globe had the general side effect of not allowing one much time for spring cleaning. Then he met Samantha Wade. And while Sam's apartment wasn't exactly a candidate for the centerfold ofGood Housekeeping, but it was still a damn sight more organized than the veritable demilitarized zone that was Troy's attic. He clambered up the ladder, slipped his 6-foot-5 frame under the wayward beam that had clipped his head nearly every time his dad sent him up here to fetch something, pulled down on the old chain to flick on the attic's lone light bulb and was engulfed in a cloud of dust that would make an Icelandic volcano proud. "Holy shit!" Well, at least, that's what Troy attempted to say. Truth be told, he got about halfway through "Holy" when the dust invaded his throat and turned his exclamation into an awkward series of coughs and hacks. Staggering under the newly unleashed dust cloud, Troy waved his right hand helter-skelter in front of his face, trying to clear a breathable path. "You okay up there?" Apparently, Sam had heard Troy's coughing fit all the way from the downstairs bathroom she was currently attacking with enough OxiClean to sanitize a football stadium. "Yeah," Troy shouted back, his words still fighting through inhalations of age-old dust. "Just, uh, just a little dusty up here." Wiping yet more dust away, Troy reached back to the top step of the ladder to the attic and grabbed his weapons of choice for this particular mission: a full, industrial strength bottle of Windex and a heavy duty roll of paper towels. Very quickly, he gave both an extensive workout, storming a quick path through the middle of the attic, giving himself a much-awaited view at the rest of the attic. Even clear of the dust, it was clear that navigating the crawlspace was going to be an Olympian task. Boxes were piled on boxes, teetering to and fro like a hundred miniature cardboard replicas of the Leaning Tower of Pisa. Loose items were crammed into every available nook, the removal of any single one of them threatening to trigger an avalanche of old jackets and tangled audio cassettes from the mid 80s. His eyes, adjusting, Troy scanned the attic as a whole, surveying the litany of his childhood memories, dusting as he went. An pair of long, thin boxes he knew from first sight. He thought his dad had thrown out Troy's once brimming baseball card collection a year ago, but Jack Douglas, accomplished pack rat that he was, had apparently stowed them here in this dusty Cave of Wonders. Over in one corner, a dowel jabbed awkwardly out from the wall, overstuffed with hangers that sagged from weight and age, looking like the sheer load of it was ready to yank the entire wall off the house's foundation. Most of them were covered in plastic, dust, or both, but in the pack, Troy picked out two garments he would've recognized from a hundred miles away. The first, trying desperately to fight off the onset of mothballs, was his old varsity jacket, riddled with patches, the bright red "E" finally starting to fray away from the fabric after 15 years of neglect. Troy remembered the first time he'd slipped the jacket on, the feeling that he'd finally "made it" that coursed through him. Almost 20 years later, he still wasn't exactly sure he'd ever made it. The second jacket, well, half of Greensboro would recognize that. At least the ones who played high school basketball from the early 70s up until 2003. His father's navy blue tweed blazer, the same jacket Jack Douglas stubbornly insisted on wearing for every single game of his coaching career. Each one of his seven state titles. Each one of his North Carolina record 587 wins. Each one of his son's varsity appearances. Torn, stained, frayed, falling apart, Jack always got that jacket cleaned, stitched, repaired and rescued from the depths to get ready for the next season. Hell, he was wearing it in the portrait that hung over the gym that bears his name. For a moment, Troy thought about grabbing it, not even sure what he'd do with it. Honestly, he was just glad to know it was still there. Besides, as he moved the jacket aside to pick it up, he saw that it was blocking his real quarry. It was large, plain-looking cardboard box, much like every one of the hundred or so other boxes piled up in the attic. But this one, this one was what Sam had sent him up here to look for. Scrawled on the top, in black marker, in his father's barely legible handwriting, was the word "PHOTOS." Every photo Jack Douglas had ever developed was crammed into this box, and Troy's fiancee wanted her hands on it. Something about an idea the wedding planner had concocted or some such nonsense. All Troy knew was that he'd let Sam look at those photos on her own. He didn't need to see them, he already knew the story each one told. Now it was her turn to learn.
