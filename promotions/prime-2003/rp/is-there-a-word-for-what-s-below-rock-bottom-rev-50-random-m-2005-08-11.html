The [name withheld] Series Presents: Is There A Word For What's Below Rock Bottom? (ReV 50, Random Match Assignment)

11 August 2005

Date: 08/11/05 The scene fades into a shot of a roaring fireplace before panning back to reveal an elderly gentleman seated in a leather armchair, a red terry cloth robe wrapped around his person as he gently puffs on a dark cherry wood tobacco pipe. He smiles without baring teeth into the camera. 'Hello there! I'm Michael Gough, and welcome back to the [name withheld] Series. Having explained the intention of the series itself in the first installment, I'll move on ahead directly to the proverbial meat and potatoes of this week's show.' He puffs on the pipe for effect as the fire is mysteriously dimmed behind him. 'The timeframe was just before PRIME's Christmas show of 2004, ReVolution 50, and it was to be the company's most ambitious show to date. Every match on the card would be determined by a random drawing of names from a hat. None of the competitors had any idea beforehand who they would be paired off with. We now know, in hindsight, that Nova drew a Tables, Ladders, & Chairs Match for the 5-Star Title against Joshua Taro Freedom, Ozric Mortimer, and Tyler Lopez, a match that he would go on to win for the first time (but not the last) and immortalize himself by becoming the only superstar to win a title in his second match with the company, setting a record that will probably never be eclipsed.' Gough takes a breath, and puffs again on his pipe. 'But Nova knew none of these things in the two-week period leading up to the show. No, all he knew was that he hadn't debuted in quite the fashion he had originally hoped, and now the darkness of the unknown awaited him. It is with this in mind that I take you back in time...' The fire behind him flares up again, flooding the room in yellowish-orange light. The lenses of Gough's glasses shine. '...to the second installment...of the [name withheld] Series. Enjoy.' Gough fades out, and is replaced by a date. December 1, 2004… As soon as Nova came to, he wished for another chair-shot to put him back out. “Oh…oh, God…” Every blink of the eye set off an atomic bomb inside his head. With a loud, sticky sluuurp! Nova peeled his face off of the cold concrete floor. Blood had crusted his face and matted his now strawberry-blonde hair. How many hours it had been since Sean took his block off with that chair Nova did not know. He was, however, more than a little miffed that no help had arrived. “Don’t worry about me!” Nova screamed down the empty hallway despite his head-ache, or perhaps in spite of it, “I’ll be fine! JUST AN UNCONSCIOUS MAN IN A POOL OF BLOOD! NO CAUSE FOR ALARM!” Slowly, Nova attempted to sit up… “Ah! AHH!” …and collapsed quickly against a stack of equipment boxes, an arm covering his right shoulder protectively. Fireworks explode in Nova’s head and his vision swims as Sean brings the chair down onto his shoulder. A second later, the back of the chair connects with Nova’s cheek. “Don’t ever think you’re above me, Nova! I’ll always be the puppet-master, and you’ll always be dancing beneath me.” Sean’s words sound far away and muted to Nova. He can taste copper. The effort to sit up left Nova out of breath, but still he tried to breathe in tiny gasps so as to not aggravate what might be a concussion. Maybe I’d know whether or not it was a concussion if the goddamned EMTs had taken the time to find me. Bet they wouldn’t have left Ian-Bloody-Fucking-English stranded in a hallway to bleed to death… A gleam caught Nova’s eye, causing him to squint and turn his head away, which caused a small brass orchestra to begin playing football fight music within the acoustically beneficial confines of his skull. When he saw what was reflecting the light into his eyes, he couldn’t help but chuckle in spite of himself. The Tag Titles! Sean must’ve dropped them before way-laying me with the chair. There is a God. Time it took for Nova to crawl over to the Tag Titles, retrieve them, use the bathroom, and pull himself upright…11 minutes, 31 seconds. “Hey! There’s no bathroom around here!” I didn’t say there was. “Oh, my.” Nova pushed open the side EXIT door, and stepped out into the cool Sacramento air. He sat down on the steps leading down and away from the door, set the Tag Titles next to him, and lit up a cigarette. “Hey!” Nova turned his head to see a young boy pedaling up to him on a bicycle. The boy came to a screeching stop in front of Nova and gave him a once-over with his eyes. “What happened to you?” “I got hit with a chair,” Nova explained, “several times.” The boy nodded as though this were something that happened on a fairly regular basis. “Whoa!” the boy exclaimed, his eyes training on the Tag Titles sitting next to Nova, “what are those?” “The PRIME Tag Team Titles,” Nova answered. “Are you a wrestler?” the boy asked. “Yup,” Nova replied, “recently signed to PRIME.” “Oh, man!” the boy gasped excitedly, “Are you one of the Tag Team champions?” “Well, actua-,” Nova began before stopping himself, “Why, ONE of the Tag Team champions, my boy? I’m THE Tag Team Champion!” “All by yourself?” the boy replied quizzically. “Hell yeah,” Nova affirmed confidently, “you know why I look so beaten up right now? I had to defend my belts against two men in the Main Event tonight.” “I thought you said you got hit by a chair,” the boy said, obviously confused. “Well, I was lying then,” Nova replied with a shrug, “I’m telling the truth now. I defended my Tag Titles against Tony Rolo and Ignatius Lisieux in the Main Event.” “Holy cow!” the boy murmured in awe, “and you’re STILL the Champion?” “That’s right,” Nova continued, “I hit Lisieux with the Dying Star Drop-that’s my finisher-and then, I raised my arm, pointed my finger at Rolo, and glared at him so intensely that he started to cry.” “Wow! You made Tony Rolo cry just by looking at him?” the boy asked, his eyes growing wide as he gazed upon this man, this wrestling legend. “That’s right,” Nova concurred, “Rolo got down on his knees and begged me not to destroy him, but I told him it was nothing personal, but no one was taking my belts. I think he understood. I think he was a champion or something, too, at some point.” “Maybe,” the boy said with a shrug, one of his PRIME heroes already forgotten as he stood in front of Nova, beaming with admiration. “C-can I have your autograph?” “Five bucks,” Nova said before catching himself, “I mean, sure.” The boy held out a piece of paper, and Nova quickly scribbled his John Hancock on it before pulling his hand away…then quickly adding “PRIME Tag Team Champion”…then beneath that, “Defeater of Tony Rolo and Ignatius Lisieux at the same time”…then beneath that, “Winner of the 2004 Biggest Peni-“ “That’s enough,” the boy said, wrenching the autograph away from Nova, “well, I gotta go home…see ya later, Champ!” “See ya!” Nova replied before grumbling through his teeth, “God, I’m such a loser.” Ten minutes after making the phone call, a cab arrived in the deserted parking lot of the Arco Arena to take Nova back to his hotel room. The door creaked loudly as Nova opened it. He tossed the Tag Team Titles on one of the beds and made a bee-line directly for the shower. After he emerged from the bathroom (which looked by this point like a set prop for Psycho), he sat on the edge of the bed and began channel-surfing. “Nothing on,” Nova said to himself, “holy fuck, I’m talking to myself. Gah! I did it again! GAH!” Determined not to make a peep, Nova rolled over on the bed and stared out the window… …and it was then that it really hit him. He was lonely. His life had gone to complete shit in less than a month. It had felt as though he were riding a rollercoaster down into Hell, and somewhere hidden in all that turbulence of first being betrayed by a friend, then being kicked out of his house by his wife, then being subpoenaed for a divorce and losing most of his money, it hadn’t really hit him how lonely he would be. Even on the stormiest, nastiest night, a zillion miles from friends and family, Nova could always at least call his wife on the phone and talk. Sometimes Ariel would put Lulu on the phone, and Nova would listen to her gurgle and coo into the receiver, her beautiful voice given no justice by the static of the phone line. Never again. And if he were home, he’d probably be sitting in front of the fireplace on the couch, reading the newspaper and occasionally stealing a glance over at his wife, his fucking gorgeous wife, who would most likely be surrounded by stacks of paperwork from the office, while Lulu rolled around happily on the carpet. He always had friends before, too. Wherever he was, there was someone. He had no friends here in PRIME, however. One of his boys had gone AWOL, disappearing shortly after joining the company, and the other had tried to cave Nova’s goddamn face in with a chair last week before quitting the company. You could choke on the friggin’ irony. The only other person Nova knew in PRIME was Clayborn, and Clayborn was a douche-bag, his head crammed so far up his own ass that if Nova wanted to have an intelligent conversation with him, Craig would probably have to scrape the shit out of his ears first. Yep, it was just Nova tonight...just Nova… …and the PRIME Tag Team Titles. 12:00 AM, December 2nd… Squueeeeeaaaaaakkk… The Sharpie makes a hideous noise as Nova draws a smiley face first on the belt with “Daniel ‘First Blood’ Pierce” engraved in gold on it, and then on the one labeled “Ignatius Lisieux.” Nova takes a step back, admiring his handiwork, before adding a tongue poking out of the mouth of Lisieux’s belt. 1:15 AM… “Look, Daddy! Every time a bell rings, an angel gets its wings!” “That’s right! That’s right! Attaboy, Clarence!” As the credits to It’s A Wonderful Life roll down the screen, Nova wipes a tear from his eye, swigs the rest of his beer, and stands up. “Hoo, boy,” Nova says, stretching, “I’ve gotta piss. Say, pal, you sure are chillin’ on that beer.” Nova stares down at one of the Tag Titles, positioned in a chair next to Nova’s with a full, untouched beer leaning up against it. “Well, I guess that’s fine,” Nova says with a shrug, “no use makin’ yourself feel bad.” 1:38 AM… With a loud clattering noise, the wooden blocks scatter across the table. “HA!” Nova screams triumphantly, pointing a finger at the Tag Title positioned across the table, “nice work, Butterfingers! JENGA!” 2:20 AM… “Okay, let’s see here,” Nova says, staring down at the playing board before spinning the dial with his finger, “right hand red!” The Tag Titles, placed in leaning position against each other in the middle of the mat, shudder before toppling over. “No, no!” Nova protests, “that’s right foot yellow! Haven’t either of you ever played Twister before?!” 3:00 AM… Nova lies on the bed, with one of the Tag Titles placed in a chair next to him. A Beer-a-mid shakily remains upright on the night-table. “Man, I don’t even know,” Nova says with a sigh, choking back the tears, “some fucked up shit, isn’t it?” He looks over at the Tag Title belt, and when he gets no response, he continues on. “I mean, I can’t BELIEVE Arby’s cancelled the French Dip ‘N’ Swiss!” Nova shouts, no longer able to control his emotions, “I fucking LOVED that sandwich!” Nova’s composure crumbles. “Hold me…” 4:20 AM… “Is it overwhelmingTo use a crane to crush a fly?It’s a good time for SupermanTo lift the sun into the sky.” As “Waitin’ For Superman” by the Flaming Lips softly plays on the bedside CD player, Nova roasts the end of the coner hanging out of his mouth and immediately pulls it away, coughing hard as smoke floats up to the ceiling. “Hot damn! That shit is cha-ronic!” Nova holds the joint up to the “mouth” of one of the Tag Titles. “Oh, don’t look at me like I started smoking yesterday,” Nova scolds, “You know how I roll. If you ain’t chokin’, you ain’t smokin’.” Just as the stoner motto escapes his lips, the Beer-A-Mid collapses on the night-table, and beer splashes up onto Nova’s shirt. “Awww!” Nova exclaims, staring down at his soiled Zeppelin shirt, “who didn’t finish their beer?” 4:45 AM… Nova is right. The weed was indeed chronic…too chronic, in fact, because Nova now lays face-down on the bed, a healthy puddle of drool forming beneath his lips. But where his body lies dormant, his mind ticks away, synapses firing feverishly in his brain… ReVolution 50…t minus one day and counting. I can’t say I’m exactly teary-eyed with memories from the other forty-nine, but I do recognize the importance of this occasion, if only because it has the potential to completely change the face of the company. No card. Three of the five titles on the line, though we don’t know which ones. Completely random drawing of matches. Popping a boner over the possibilities yet? So am I. Or I was, anyhoo…was until I came to my goddamn senses. I didn’t get my start in this business yesterday. I’m not naïve enough to think that I have a chance to win the Universal, the Intense, or even the 5-Star Title. “But Nova,” you might be saying, “Motoki established that it would be random draw. Anyone has a chance to get title shots!” Right. And what I’m saying is this: No, they don’t. Let me hit up a culinary analogy for those of you who still need a dab of clarity: There’s this place…and in this place, people compete for food. There isn’t a lot of food, so it’s a real honor to have the opportunity to compete for some. Some people have been in this place for a while, and some have only been here a short. Now, are you gonna give the opportunity to compete to the person who’s been starving for almost a year, or to the guy who’s only been hungry for a couple of weeks. That’s rhetorical. I already know your answer, and if I don’t know your answer, you’re lying. I’m not gonna get the Universal Title shot. I know. I’m not gonna get the Intense Title shot. I know this as well. I’m certainly not banking on a 5-Star or Alias Title shot, either. And, like every other fed in the history of goddamn Creation, the Tag Titles don’t mean anything, so I’m not really taking them into account. Besides, I probably wouldn’t get a shot at them, either. Call me jaded or what-have-you, but in the end, you’ll know I speak. All this isn’t to say, however, that ReVolution 50 is not an important show. That’s not true at all. It is. ReV 50, as I said before, is a landmark show. Everyone is gonna be watching. That’s a pretty decent setup to make a splash if one so chooses. It’s an opportunity to turn some heads. I fucked up pretty hard on ReV 49. I haven’t lost a debut match since I can remember, but I managed somehow to do it last week. Some might say that I didn’t, really. I mean, I didn’t get pinned, and truth be told, I spent most of the match whompin’ criminal amounts of ass (shooting star press onto both of my opponents = totally sweet)… …but still, in my book, if you wrestle a match, and aren’t the winner, you’re the loser…so by that logic, I lost a match last week. Debut matches are important. They let people know right off the bat who to watch out for, and who is gonna be relegated to the mid-card. Last week, Karina Wolfenden sent the message that she’s the rookie to beat, and I sent the message that I’m really good at being tossed over the top rope and looking like an ass-hat, two skills that, in all honesty, probably aren’t gonna get me anywhere here in PRIME. So, here’s my second chance. Everyone who’s everyone is gonna be watching ReVolution 50. It’s time to make a statement…because in the PRIME world of extended “pay your dues” periods, every win is a step forward, but every loss is two steps back. Every win brings you a week closer to a title shot, but every loss is another month of opening matches. It’s time to put my name on everyone’s lips. It’s time to jostle my way into contention. It’s time.
