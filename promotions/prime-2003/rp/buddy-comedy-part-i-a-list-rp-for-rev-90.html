Buddy Comedy, Part I (A-List RP for Rev 90)

Date: Post-90 THURSDAY 'DANNY!' 'Danny, over here!' 'FERGUSON!' 'DAAAAAAAAAAAANNY!' Holding up a hand to shield his eyes from the sun, Danny Ferguson walks away from the H2, leaving the 'shut the door' part of exiting a car to someone else. His aviators aren't doing the trick against the California sun; then again, they ARE doing the trick against the California papparazzi. When they can't see your eyes, you don't have to stop. Slowly shifting the hand-shield into a regal wave, he signals to the dime store 'journalists' that line the curb at a careful 10-foot distance from PRIME's favorite actor. He hangs back a little, pretending to pause for photos when he's actually just waiting for his two bodyguards to flank him. When they do, Team Ferguson quickly leaves the press row in the dust as they enter Mann's Chinese Theater, the high-profile site of today's 'More Than Just Hardcore' media event. The interior of the theater lobby is decked out thematically. The crown jewel is a regulation-size wrestling ring, donated from one of the independent Los Angeles feds, with the ropes replaced by fake barbed wire. A pair of steel folding chairs sit in the ring, and a reasonable facsimile of one of those old-school lighting rig hangs overhead to set the tone for the afternoon interview sessions. As Danny strolls in, he waits until everyone notices him before coolly removing the sunglasses. 'Sorry I'm late,' he drawls, without a hint of sorrow. 'Traffic.' Studio personnel flock to Danny before any media can. He gives the same cheap acknowledgement to the writers that he did to the photogs, and allows himself to be whisked to a staging area in preparation for the first volley of questions. There, his agent, Allan North, is already waiting. “Danny,” he beams, ever the best friend when his clients are in the room, “as prompt as ever.” “When someone gives me a call time, I’ll be there. Hopefully I won’t be flying back from Japan at the time.” “You just got in?” “Yesterday. Or this afternoon. I dunno, time zones, whatever. I just slept for eight hours, that’s all I know.” “And here I thought the disheveled look was part of the image.” Danny runs a hand through his hair. He’s already missed his weekly trim and it’s grown at least a quarter-inch longer than his consultants suggested he let it. Blasphemy. Instead of rushing to the prep chair, though, he shrugs it off. “It’s called ‘making the transformation,’ Al. I’m trying to get into character.” North tries to smirk a response, but D-Ferg quickly changes the subject. “Who and what do I need to know here?” “Big news, as I texted you last week, is no director. Astin was going to do it himself-“ “Dickwad.” “Ahem. Anyway, the studio wants to spin it in their favor, obviously, so the story we need you to drop is that the search is on for a top-quality captain to helm this ship. Really push everyone’s commitment to making this project a success.” “Are we really going to get a top-quality guy?” “Depends on your definition, but sure.” Allan walks Danny over to a smaller group of people. One man, a blonde with a crew cut and those metrosexual Lisa Loeb-esque glasses, steps forward and extends his hand. “Danny, nice to meet you, I’m Steffen.” We should pause at this moment to tell you that he spells it Steven, but he pronounces it Steffen. You know where this relationship is going to go. Danny half-heartedly finishes the handshake, all the while looking at Allan instead of his company. The power agent eventually obliges. “Steve-“ “Steffen.” “Steffen is the writer. He’ll be working with Scott Williams, the co-author of the book.” “Co-author?” Steven pipes in: “Usually when one or more people write a book-“ “I’m familiar with the prefix, Stefan.” “Ste-“ “Why aren’t you working with Terry?” Danny looks at Allan and rewords. “Why isn’t he working with Terry?” Allan flushes and doesn’t answer, apparently hoping the topic wouldn’t come up. Steffen smiles a smug little smile, and tips his glasses. “Oh, nobody told you?” He looks to Allan, who leans in before Danny responds and turns this scene ugly...well, uglier. “Terry hasn’t signed on,” the agent whispers. “We’ll talk about it later.” Danny looks at the floor, biting his tongue. It’s his first day officially on the film, and despite the kind of star status he enjoys in PRIME, he can’t afford to throw his weight around here. He needs this role. So he swallows, closes his eyes for a second, then looks back up at the biz nerd before him. “Sorry about that, Steff. So what kind of stuff have you worked on?” “Well, I spent five years on ‘Whose Line Is It, Anyway?’” “Excuse me?” “You know, with Drew Carey?” Allan’s Agent-Sense tingles again and this time he pulls the plug. Gesturing to someone across the room, he excuses himself and Danny, while Fergie mulls over the ramifications of being a writer on an improv show. When they get to the door of the green room, Allan pushes Danny towards a man in a steel gray suit. “Danny Ferguson, this is Vernon Wallace, head of production at “ “It’s nice to meet you, Danny. I was just hearing all about you from your friend out there?” In retrospect, Danny should’ve seen trouble right there, because he has no friends. Still, he pressed forward. “What, is one of those reporters saying he knows all about me?” He turns to Allan. “Probably some wrestling news site. Fucking losers.” “No,” Mr. Wallace continues. “He says he’s your...tag team partner, I believe? See for yourself?” He gestures back out towards the lobby, and Danny steps out into the open, just behind the for-show wrestling ring. He pauses there, and just stares. In the ring, applying a front facelock to some lucky journalist, is The Illustrious Face-Eater. “So Danny’s like, ‘please let me go, Adam, my neck hurts and you’re so strong!’ and I thought about snapping him like a twig, but- HEEEEEEY BUUUUUUUUDDY!” He releases the facelock and spins toward Danny. The reporter drops to the mat and rolls out of the ring, holding his neck but otherwise smiling. A few of the reporters jump up to ask questions out of turn, but Danny doesn’t answer any of them right away. He just stares. 'What are you doing here?' Facey removes the cape around his neck with a magicians ease and places it over some of the barbed wire. He signals for Danny to join him in the ring. 'Don't worry too much - it's fake. Well, semi-fake. How you doin' over there, Scotty?' Danny looks over at the crowd of reporters. One is pressing a slightly-bloody towel against his arm. 'It still stings pretty bad.' 'Grow a pair,' Facey snorts, gesturing again for Danny to get in the ring. Since repetition is the thing to do around this bitch, Danny goes with the same question: 'What are you doing here?' The SFD champeen quickly jumps out of the ring, showing a rare discretion in not shouting his next words at his stablemate. 'We've got a tag team match on the show this week.' 'So?' 'Well how are we supposed to operate as a team when we don't spend any time together. You don't call me, Danny. WHY DON'T YOU CALL ME?!?' Ok, I lied. He did shout. Like you, Danny just sighs.' 'Do you remember that week before Culture Shock? You know, when I kept calling you and you kept hanging up? And that was BEFORE you bought that kelp from the street vendor and tried to smoke it.' 'I thought 'seaweed' was just a clever name.' 'Right. Anyway, I think we'll manage without me spending the week-' 'Fuck that. This plan worked for me and Tchu-ing Gum that one time. And we carried the carcass of Chris Collins to the win, too.' 'I'm pretty sure he was - and is - still alive.' 'Oh, you can tell? Listen, I've already moved my stuff into your living room and made a duplicate key. There's no turning back.' 'Actually, I think there IS-' 'THERE'S. NO. TURNING. BACK.' Danny just stares at him for a second, then looks over his shoulder towards the media, who are growing restless. Behind him, a similar buzz is growing in the green room. The next decision he will make is going to have a very large impact on his first week on this movie, which will likewise affect the entire scope of the movie, which will likewise affect the rest of his movie career. So it's especially helpful to the decision-making process when Facey starts snapping his fingers in Danny's face. 'NO. TUR-' 'I heard you.' He sighs. 'Fine, you can stay. But if you blow this for me, I pull the plug.' 'Whatevs, Fergie. I can do this Hollywood shit.' Not moments later, Danny's first exposure to the press has begun. Though the original setup called for he and one reporter sitting in the folding chairs in the ring, but that was audibled to Facey and Danny sitting in the chairs and the reporters standing outside the ring, asking their questions all at once. Imagine the joy being felt by the event planners and publicists hired for 'More Than Just Hardcore' at this moment. One reporter raises his pen to signal a question. 'Danny, how did you spend this past week in Japan?' 'Well, I was doing my best to scout some locations and really get a feel for the country. Ideally, a good portion of the film will be shot in Japan, given Terry's success there early in his career.' 'Totally,' Facey chimes in. 'At first, when we'd try to get dinner, it was like, 'HEY YOU FUCKING BACKSLASH-EYED MANGA COCK, WHERE'S MY RAW FISH?!' but by the end of the second week, it was more like, 'Excuse me you Tokyo rice monkey, but my food has not yet arrived at my table.' That was pretty sweet.' A dead silence fills the room, and Danny freezes, not wanting to follow up on Facey's quote and become guilty by association. Finally, the same reporter breaks the silence with a follow-up. 'So you're already prepared to shoot? Does that mean a new director is going to be chosen?' 'Come on now, guys, I didn't say that. We're really trying to find the right guy for the job, so production won't start until we get who we want. But with the script we've got, it's pretty likely that I'll be back in Japan within a few months, no matter who's behind the camera.' 'Pfft,' Facey snorted, leaning back in his chair, 'probably that chinaman Ang Lee. He speaks the language, after all, and he probably doesn't want to be the Cowboy Buttsex guy for the rest of his life.' 'Isn't Ang Lee from Taiwan?' 'Who cares what city it is? He's on the island somewhere and he likes man ass. It'll probably be him or Tarantino, that Jap-loving film dweeb.' Danny hangs his head, unable to put on the show any more. He pretty much resigns himself to the fact that this press event is about to become a debacle, and that the film itself could be in jeopardy and his career will suck and he'll have to sell the mansion and give up the car and fire the bodyguards and and and... ...and then one of the reporters laughs. Then another. Then another. Soon the whole room is bursting into laughter at Facey's cutting remarks. Danny looks up, a little astounded at what's going on. He looks at Facey. His tag partner gives him a thumbs up. FRIDAY Inside the Ferguson mansion - so identified by the giant 'Hard Money' poster on the wall - Adam Dick is snoring loudly, clearly irritating the small Hispanic maid who dusts furiously around him. The Face-Eater is spread-eagle on the forest-green leather couch in Danny's living room, clad only in his mask, cape, a pair of tube socks and some old boxer shorts with a noticeable hole where his balls are. Some dust kicked up by the maid flutters into the air and settles right within his line of breathing, resulting in a snort/cough/sneeze/dry heave thing that sends him sitting up in a flash. 'JESUS CHRIST CONSUELA, why don't you fucking flip the vacuum in reverse and blow this shit straight up my fucking nostrils!' The maid says something naughty in Spanish. 'Isn't this Southern California? Go take a motherfucking night class at Chico State and come back when English is your FIRST language, you stupid bitch.' He rubs his face through the mask and swings his feet onto the floor. Snorting and hacking, he coughs up something disgusting into the day-old Big Gulp cup that was sitting next to the bed. After swishing it around a little, he whistles to the maid and holds it up, presumably expecting her to take it away. Naturally, he gets another naughty Spanish word. Facey stands up and rubs his chest the way most men do when they stand up, then lets the hand wander down south to check on the troops. He stumbles over to the matching leather loveseat, which is decorated by a wrinkled pile of clothes and acts as a makeshift dresser. Looking through his belongings, he throws unneeded items over his shoulder and onto the couch. 'I'm surprised you're even up at this hour.' Danny's voice pulls Adam away from his laundry. The actor, dressed in street clothes as opposed to a tailored suit, is standing at the staircase that winds down to the living room from the upstairs den. He's standing near a window, and Facey squints against the sunlight creeping in. 'What is it, like 10:30?' 'Try 3:00.' Pause. 'P.M.?' Danny doesn't answer, and instead strides over from the staircase to flip on the television in front of the couch. 'You wouldn't have this problem if you opted to take the guest bedroom like I insisted. It has an alarm clock. You know, in addition to the bed.' 'Nah, I'm cool here, man. Don't want to be an inconvenience.' 'I don't think you've told a worse lie in your life.' Danny watches a few seconds of the TV - permanently pre-set on E! - and becomes frustrated when he doesn't see his own face. He flips it off...and suddenly begins sniffing the air. 'What the hell is that?' 'What?' 'That smell.' 'Talk to your housekeeper, Stinky Gonzalez. That's what happens when you bathe in that Mexican water, I hear.' 'No. It's not that...' Danny walks over to the couch, still sniffing in the air periodically. 'It's...Christ, have you been smoking in my house?' 'I dunno, do you still consider it 'inside' if I crack a couple windows?' He gestures towards some fancy cathedral-style windows at the other side of the room. One of them is hanging open just wide enough to accomodate the small electric fan perched precariously on the sill. Danny lets out an exasperated growl. 'Goddammit, Facey, that smell is going to stick in the leather! This couch is ruined?' 'That mean you're gonna trash it? Any chance you could ship it to Arizona instead?' Pulling out a move that has become a trademark of his since the FergFace combination came together, Danny drops the subject and moves on. He calls it 'cutting his losses.' Grabbing a set of keys, he starts to head for the door. 'I've got another press thing today, so would you mind not burning my place down while I'm gone?' 'Actually, I need a lift.' Danny turns around to see Facey stumbling towards him, haphazardly dressing himself along the way. He's not buying. 'We're not driving through that part of the city again. I told you-' 'No, I've got a media deal, too.' 'You what?' 'After yesterday's showing, one of your d-bag movie pricks said I had 'it' and so I got your agent to hook me up with some people. I've got a couple interviews around town.' 'For what?' 'You know, stuff. Who gives a shit, some dumb-ass Hollywood thing, I dunno. I'm just spraying the Facey seed anywhere I can.' 'First, that's a disgusting metaphor. Second, I thought you were all about sticking together for the good of the team?' 'Meh.' SATURDAY Danny is sitting on the couch, surrounded by Facey's clothes, watching E! with the kind of dead-eyed expression reserved for men who just watched their ex-wife win the lottery. The TV tells the story. 'Tinseltown is has gone crazy for The Illustrious Face Eater. In a short span of time, the Liliputian luchador has sprung into Hollywood and taken it by storm. Earlier today, he was spotted having lunch at Wendy's with James Cameron. And of course, everyone remembers his grand entrance at last night's 'Lucky Number Slevin' premiere, when he repeatedly punched Josh Hartnett in the testicles.' 'That's right, Suzy. Facey, as he's affectionately known, is having the kind of impact on this town that hasn't been seen since the Johnathan Lipnicki era.' 'In other news, there is still no director for 'More Than Just Hardcore,' the upcoming wrestling film starring former child star Danny Fergus-' Danny shuts the TV off and throws the remote. He checks his phone, but has no messages. Then he sits forward and lets his head sink in his hands. Frankenstein jokes were so passe. TO BE CONTINUED
