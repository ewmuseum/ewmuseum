Down on the Upside

30 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30th October Now. “Tell me, Jason,” Jim, the balding, middle-aged businessman, spoke in a voice straight out of Goodfellas. “This whole ‘wrestling’ thing, it ain’t real, is it? I mean, you guys don’t actually hurt each other, do ya?” Jason leaned back, the wooden chair creaking as he transferred the bulk of his weight onto his shoulder blades. “Don’t hurt each other?” he said, tightening his lips around the droopy Marlboro Red hanging from the corner of his mouth, curling the other corner into a half-grin. “Ask Troy Douglas if we fuckin’ hurt each other.” The proclamation draws stifled laughter from a group that either actually found it funny or is collectively too scared not to laugh at the jokes of a man of Jason Natas’ reputation. The dealer – Jason couldn’t remember his name – shuffled the cards across the table, sliding them across the worn mahogany surface ‘til each man had a full hand. Carefully sliding his cards towards him, Jason pulled them up close to his chest, away from prying eyes as the dealer placed five cards for the flop. “You sure you don’t want one of these Cuban’s, Jase?” enquired the cigar-chomping Brendan Bradley. The man had a head of brown hair so perfect that it had to be a toupee. He too sat inspecting his cards, not even glancing up to make eye contact with the PRIME wrestler. “Just got ‘em shipped in the other day. Classy, elegant. Not like that shit you got dangling from your maw.” Finally Bradley looked up, offering Jason a cheeky wink. “Ain’t for me, boss.” The Anti-Superstar leaned forward again, placing his cards face down on the table. His eyes passed by each one of his compatriots individually. Jim was playing a pretty solid game; he’d definitely won more than he’d lost, and he wasn’t taking any unnecessary risks. Not bad for a guy who looked – and sounded – like a poor man’s Tony Soprano. Brendan? A cocky, slimey fucker no doubt. He’d probably be heading home with less money than he’d came with, but Jason figured that such poker meets were more about the camaraderie more than the dough, for Mr. Bradley at least. The older gent whose turn it was to deal seemed like a slight cut above the rest. He wore the finest suit, drank the most expensive alcohol, and played the game like a real professional. Wrinkled and craggy his exterior may have been, but this was one old boy that was far from long in the tooth. And then, finally, there was the reason that Jason had made it into the poker game in the first place. Najera and Loftman sat side-by-side, Loftman looking slightly edgier than his Hispanic business partner. His managers weren’t doing too well; Najera had won a couple of small hands and lost a couple of larger ones, while Loftman was haemorrhaging money like it was going out of style. “Alright boys, starting at ten. S’go.” One by one they each tossed a little blue chip into the pot, going around the table in a clockwise direction. Warily, Jason pulled his cards towards the edge of the table, pulling them up by the corner for a quick glance. Out of the corner of his eye he caught Loftman glaring it him. Immediately Jason swung his head around. “That’s right, Suit. This is what a winner looks like.” Digging his index finger into his chest, Jason flashed Loftman a cocky smirk before returning his attention back towards the game. The Potemkin partner was sweating. The room was stuffy and a musky funk was beginning to settle in the air; that’s what happens when you lock six guys in a poorly-ventilated room with enough booze and narcotics to sink the Titanic. As agitated as he was by the stance his client was taking, Loftman decided against a verbal retort; he wanted to leave the place with all his teeth intact, after all. “Okay guys, here we go.” The dealer leaned across the table, flipping three cards over. Mr. Loftman shook his head. “Fuck.” * * * * * Then. He took a deep breath, sucking in a great quantity of the clean LaGuardia air, prepping himself for the plunge. Standing just a few feet from perhaps the biggest set of revolving glass doors he’d seen in his life, he was in no hurry at all to return to the outside world. Clad in a brand new black leather jacket, blue jeans and a black beanie, topped off with a pair of mirrored Aviator shades to further conceal his identity, a quick glance through the glass confirmed Jason’s suspicion. There they were, lined up either side like a pack of vultures waiting for something to die. Cameras held close to their chests, they hovered around the airport’s exit restlessly. Jason sighed and shook his head as he threw his holdall over his shoulder. His managers – Potemkin – had told him that flying to LaGuardia would be perfect. All the paparazzi would be at JFK, they said. Hitting New York’s second airport was supposed to throw them off… It didn’t. There wasn’t a massive amount of them – five, maybe six – but there was certainly a lot more than Jason would have liked. Dealing with the unrelenting jackal press seemed to become a bigger and bigger priority for Jason Natas as every week went by. He was nowhere near the levels of hysteria that greeted a public appearance by, say, Tyler Rayne, but he was still notorious enough to attract at least a small amount of fanfare, and with good reason. They used to stalk him for his mistakes, his discrepancies; he was the talk of the internet wrestling community for weeks following his suspension from PRIME a few weeks back. He was vilified, public enemy number one. What a difference a win makes, eh? Sighing, Jason finally walked into the revolving doors. He raised his hand to his brow, trying his best to shield his identity from the vultures, but they recognised the bearded PRIME wrestler immediately. They swarmed on him, like flies to dogshit, their flashbulbs like strobes and their questions incessant. Mr. Loftman noticed the commotion and pushed himself away from the Mercedes. As Jason forced his way through the photographers, Loftman turned and pulled the car door wide open, ushering Jason inside a vehicle so luxurious that it was almost Utopian. Clambering in after the PRIME wrestler, Loftman pulled the door closed hard. Still the photographers refused to give up. They continued to flash their dimmed bulbs through the tinted windows, despite the fact that they were clearly photographer the side of Mr. Loftman’s face and not a whole lot else. The driver made a hasty retreat, pulling the car away from the front of the airport. “Shit, Suit. Ain’t exactly the welcome party I was expectin’,” mumbled Jason as he pawed at the buckle of his seatbelt. “Ignore them. Fucking parasites, every one of them.” Mr. Loftman’s sharp, city-slicker tones were rife with venom and vitriol. He spoke like a man who knew what he was taking about. Jason nodded to himself as he stared out of the window aimlessly. His rocky relationship with his team of agents meant he was still unwilling to spend any more time than necessary with Loftman, Najera or Jewell, but truth be told he was quite grateful that Loftman had taken the time to help him evade the bastard paparazzi. “Anyway, Jase. Big plans for you these next few weeks; beating Troy Douglas last week turned a lot of heads, it seemed,” Loftman began, his voice completely devoid of all sincerity; he didn’t want to be sharing a care with Jason any more than the wrestler himself did. “Jewel in the Crown’s coming up, and that’s a big ‘un. But we’ll get to that later; tonight we celebrate…” “Tonight we celebrate?” Jason turned his head, raising an inquisitive eyebrow. “Makes you think I want to hit the town with a bunch of Suits?” Biting his lip and swallowing his pride, Loftman did his best to ignore the insult. He reached inside his long, black coat and pulled out a handful of tickets. “Black Crowes at the Hammerstein, tonight. VIP, drinks all inclusive.” “Black Crowes, eh?” Jason paused, stroking his chin through the moderate layer of hair that had accumulated over recent weeks. He wasn’t a massive fan of the Altanta-based classic rockers and a night on the tiles with Potemkin was about as appealing as jamming a rusty fork up his nose. Nope, spending time with those goons wasn’t particularly enticing; but being siphoned an unending supply of free alcohol all night certainly was. “Fuck it. I’m in.” “Alright. We’ll have a car pick you up about six-ish,” Mr. Loftman said as if he were talking to a lowly subordinate. “Done deal. Let’s make it clear though; I ain’t comin’ to hang ‘round with you slick bastards all night, okay?” “Of course, Jason.” Mr. Loftman turned his head and rolled his eyes. It was going to be a long day. * * * * * Now. “MotherFUCKER!” Violent and unrestrained, Loftman clenched a fist and thumped it straight down onto the table, the shockwaves displacing cards and rattling glasses. He pulled his hands up to his face and ran his palms down cheeks that were flushed with red fury. “Whoa, whoa, whoa!” Jim blurted, scowling. “Take it easy hotshot. Just a game. Just a game…” Unlike his cohort, Brendan Bradley seemed to find some amusement in the plight of Mr. Loftman. He chuckled under his breath, swallowing a mouthful of warm, smoky bourbon. “Well, I guess somebody’s gotta take the rebound…” As happy as a pig marinating in its own excrement, Jason stood up, leaned across the table and pulled a sizeable mound of chips towards him. He began to pick them up, stacking them in colour-coordinated columns with a smug grin etched on his rugged features. “Aye, what’s wrong, Suit? Ain’t got what it takes to hang with the big boys?” Immediately Loftman sprung to his feet, inadvertently standing his chair crashing into the floorboards. “Shut the fuck up you rancid fucking urchin, or I’ll-“ “You’ll what, Suit?” Jason cut him off, calm as calm can be. He glanced upwards, locking eyes with Loftman. “Si’down, Lofty, ‘fore you make even more a fool outta yerself…” Breathing heavily, Mr. Loftman had heard just about all he could take from Jason Natas. Again he balled a fist, this time tapping it against his thigh as he stared at Jason with a look that would have turned Medusa herself to stone. An awkward, unwelcome silence hung over the room like a grave spirit. The poker buddies had seen a few games get pretty intense every now and then, but nothing like this. They were treading on eggshells, plotting their words carefully; they knew well the reputation of Jason Natas and, despite the fact that he was their visitor, didn’t want to incite his ire. Mr. Najera, a man who himself had lost a good chunk of money on the last hand, was the first to act. He rose from his chair and picked up Loftman’s discarded seat, correcting its position. “Alright Loft. Just calm down, will you? We’re just trying to unwind…” “Unwind?” barked Loft. “How can I fucking unwind? This guy’s been breaking my balls all day.” He swung his arm round towards Jason, pointing at him accusingly. “Even now, this is ‘sposed to be a nice, friendly game, a chance to relax. But no, this fucker’s gotta come along and piss on everything as per fucking usual. This is our game - our fucking game - and he rolls in like King Shit of Fuck Mountain!” A sharp snap of anger suddenly twanged in the back of Jason’s head, and despite his unending urge to punch Mr. Loftman’s teeth halfway down his throat, Jason refrained. Besides, taking the guy’s money would be give him much more satisfaction than any brawl ever could. “Are we going to have to ask you to leave, Nathan?” the wise old sage of the group enquired. His voice was deep, assured and authoritative. “We don’t want anybody making a scene.” Loftman started to argue, and then he stopped. Just stopped. He might have been a motormouth moron, but he wasn’t blind, and he saw something in the man’s eyes that made him immediately clamp his lips together. Embarrassed, his nose firmly out of joint, Mr. Loftman reluctantly returned to his chair and stared mournfully at the severely diminished stack of chips that sat before him. He didn’t even want to consider the amount of money that he’d blown tonight, let alone check out Jason’s burgeoning mountain. Jason, meanwhile, felt fantastic. Ever since he’d signed up with the Potemkin Group they had caused him nothing but distress, cutting his paycheques in half and flying him away to Seattle to keep him out of trouble. They’d been pulling on his leash for almost half a year, but now he had one of them in the palm of his hand. Not only had he taken a sizeable chunk of Mr. Loftman’s money, but he’d also embarrassed him in front of his friends. But it wasn’t enough. For Jason Natas, it never was. “Alright, Suit,” Jason said casually as he leaned back and pulled a fresh cigarette from his breast pocket. “Got a proposition for ya.” Pulling out a scratched, well-used silver Zippo, Jason flipped the lid and lit the end of the cancer stick. “Ya mightn’t need to go home ‘n explain to that pretty little wife of yours how ya managed to piss away this month’s paycheque after all.” “What the hell are you talking about?” enquired an aggravated but interested Loftman. “I’ll give ya the chance to claim all this back right now,” Jason pointed towards his chips, now neatly arranged into individual piles. “Just you and me, one hand.” Mr. Loftman shook his head and flashed Jason his best “are you an idiot!?” look. “Oh yeah? And how the fuck do you propose that’s going to work? I haven’t even got a fraction of what you have! Now way I can match that bet.” “Ain’t yer money I’m wantin’, Suit.” A mutual air of confusion descended upon the group as Jason folded his arms across his chest, exhaling noxious cigarette fumes through his nostrils. None were more confused than Mr. Loftman, who extended his arms out to his sides to emphasis this. “What are you talking about then!?!” He raised his eyebrows, then noticed that The Anti-Superstar’s gaze was firmly focused on his left wrist. “Oh, this?” he quizzed, beginning to unfasten the silver Rolex. “This ain’t shit, take it.” Loftman pulled the watch over his hand and extended it towards Jason, but the New Yorker blocked him, holding the palm of his hand up and shaking his head. “Keep the timepiece, Lofty. More interested in that fine piece ‘a American engineering you got sittin’ outside.” Eyes popped as Jason made his demands known. He smiled devilishly at Loftman. “Whaddaya say. Pussy?” Brendan puffed out his cheeks and shook his head while Jim looked on in complete and utter astonishment. This was supposed to be a friendly game, you’d put a few bucks on the line and if you were lucky you might just leave with more notes in your wallet than when you’d entered. The ante had just been upped in a spectacular fashion. “Loft, think abou-“ began Najera. “Fine,” Loftman said firmly, not breaking his steely glare. He reached into the pocket of his dress pants and pulled out a set of keys, dangling the silver Lexus keyring for all to see. Pulling his fingers apart, he dropped the keys beside his remaining chips and pushed them towards the centre of the table. “Deal.” “Holy fuck, Loft, you’re out of your mind,” proclaimed Jim, but it bounced right off Loftman. Natas shook his head, knowing the risk that his agent was taking seemed to humour him. He pushed his own chips into the put as Brendan took his turn to deal the cards. “Alright guys, I think you’re both batshit crazy, but here goes,” he sighed as he slid the last of the cards towards Jason. “Tell ya somethin’, Suit. Never had you down as the kinda guy to take a risk like that,” Jason said as he anchored his elbows into the table, checking his hand. “’Spose I should give ya some credit…” But Loftman wouldn’t take the bait. He offered no words, nothing. Just started straight ahead, awaiting the flop. “Sure you boys want to go ahead with this?” Brendan enquired pensively. “Just flip the fucking cards.” “Alright,” Brendan sighed, “your funeral…” Mr. Loftman seemed to breath a sigh of relief as the cards were turned. Immediately he placed two of his own down. “Two pair, sixes and eights. Fuck you very much,” he breathed easy, wiping the sweat from his brow. “Well now. Ain’t that some shit.” Jason smirked, placing two of his cards down. “Sixes and eights.” “What are the chances of that…” Brendan pondered while Jim leaned in close, not unlike a hyena. Najera, meanwhile, turned his head away, unable to watch. “What’s the kicker, gents?” “King,” Loftman leaned back, slapping the card gleefully down on the varnished mahogany. “How d’you like that one, huh?!” “Interesting.” Jason took his time, dragging on the cancer stick. Eventually he placed the card beside the others, slowly peeling his palm away from the surface. ”Ace.” * * * * * Then. He tightened his grip on the guardrail, looking out from the balcony across a sea of bobbing heads and outstretched arms. The Hammerstein Ballroom was packed to the very brim, and the floor below was but a sweaty, heaving mass of bodies doing their best to sway along to the raw, rootsy rock and roll emanating from the stage. The Black Crowes had hardly made it into the first verse of “Hard to Handle” and already the place was in pandemonium. Aging rockers surged to the front of the pit while plaid-clad hippies stood at the sides, shaking their hips to the loose, slippery grooves of Rich Robinson’s guitar. Towards the centre of the room a swirling mass of bodies circled and smashed into each other, a deliberate display of youthful exuberance from audience members who may not even have been born when the band first unleashed their “Shake Your Money Maker” debut onto the world. Jason was enjoying the proceedings a lot more than he’d thought. The band weren’t that bad and he’d done a pretty good job of ignoring the two Potemkin partners in attendance thus far. Oh, and the free alcohol probably had a thing or two to do with it. Behind him, just out of earshot, stood Mr. Najera, tapping his toe and nodding his head to the rhythm as Loftman necked a final mouthful of Cristal. His stance was rigid and uneasy, as if he were distracted. “What the hell’s wrong with you, Loft?” the well-dressed Hispanic agent enquired. “Hang loose, man. This is supposed to be fun!” “Fun for you maybe,” Loftman shook his head causing a stray lock of blonde hair to fall loose. “Spending time with that ungrateful swine isn’t my idea of fun.” “Come on, cut him some slack. He beat Troy Douglas; that’s no small feat, you know. Should be a nice little earner for us. Besides, it’s not like you’ve actually had to talk to him much…” “Prick’s got no respect,” Loftman began. “None whatsoever. I can’t wait ‘til we get rid of him… looking forward to playing some Hold ‘Em.” Suddenly Najera’s facial expression soured, his features sagging, as if he was hiding something. He glanced up at his taller business partner. “Ahhhhh, yes. Well, about that…” Loftman could tell straight away. He shook his head. “You’re kidding me…” “So what, I asked Jason to come along? Is that such a big deal?” “Are you out of your fucking mind?!” Loftman almost screamed. “The guy’s a lunatic! How do you think he’s going to react when, drunk off his face and in the middle of a game, he loses a big hand, huh?” “A ‘lunatic’ he may be, Loft, but he’s our client! We have to at least make him feel like he’s wanted…” “What’s the matter, boys?” Jason asked, his voice buzzing with faux interest. He glanced down at Najera who responded with an awkward smile, and then Loftman, who wore a dissatisfied grimace. “Awwwwww, what the hell’s wrong wit’chu, Suit? Somethin’ crawl up yer ass and die?” Refusing to even dignify Jason with a response, Loftman turned on his heel and disappeared down the stairs towards the bar. Jason tapped Najera on the shoulder. “Still good for that game afterwards?” “Ahhh, yeah. Sure.” “Good. Been lookin’ forward to dippin’ into that fucker’s pockets.” * * * * * Now. “FUUUUUUUUUUUCK!” Loftman roared like a silverback and mashed his knuckles into the table. He slumped forward, elbows on the mahogany, holding his head into his hands. “Fuckfuckfuckfuckfuck. FUCK!” “Well now. Guess ol’ Jase got himself a new set ‘a wheels,” the words seemed to trickle from Jason’s dry, cracked lips, torturing Mr. Loftman. Natas pulled the keys from the pile of chips and held them up to the light, dangling them in front of his eyes. “Not bad for a fuckin’ ‘urchin’ from the Bronx, huh?” “Go to hell you piece of shit,” sobbed Mr. Loftman. This would prove to be a big blow for the businessman. Financially he’d lost four or five weeks earnings, not to mention his stylish new Lexus. But his pride had been knocked more than his bank account could ever be. He’d been completely annihilated by a man he holds nothing but resentment for, and it sickened him to the pit of his stomach to know that he’d been one-upped by a classless ruffian from the streets of NYC. “Awww, chin up, Suit,” Jason stuffed the keys into his pocket. “Maybe if yer lucky, ol’ Jimbo here’ll help wipe them tears away.” The trio of strangers could do nothing but glare back and forth at Jason Natas and Mr. Loftman, too awed to even consider piping up. They glances were sporadic but their feelings were mutual, and as they noticed Jason’s delight at taking a giant chunk out of another man’s pride they could only draw one conclusion. Jason Natas was a no-good piece of shit. “Be seein’ you boys later.” Jason rose from his chair, chest puffed out like a lion. Slowly he turned and paced towards the door, his heavy boots clunking down against the floorboards with all the grace of a baby elephant. “Oh, and Suit,” he paused. Cocked his head back. “I’ll be in to collect my winnings in the mornin’. Best not fuck with my chips, now.” Muffled, loud voices reverberated around the confines of the room as soon as Jason closed the door behind him. Shouts and screams, curses and insults. It was chaos, and he was the architect. As he swaggered down the hallway, Jason was helpless to resist the smile that crept across his lips. First Troy Douglas and now “Nathan” Loftman. The Anti-Superstar was back in business.
