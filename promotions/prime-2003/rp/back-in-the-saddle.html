Date: 10:03 AM - Whoops. Back in The Saddleby Danny Ferguson Ok, so we open on a movie set...wait a second...where's our usual font? And why is the spacing not all fucked up? We had an incident with the screenwriter, sir. Some complications during a hot oil massage. He was unable to make it here today. A hot oil massage? Well, boiling, technically, as per the 'incident.' But truthfully, sir, I think he could have made it here and thumped something out with his bandaged and hairless nubs. But he isn't. Uh-uh. I think our own author just got lazy and didn't have the time to open up his Final Draft software. That's just great. How the hell are we supposed to do a script? We're on a deadline! Sir, I hired this typist. The what now? A typist. The small woman sitting to your left. The one typing. HELLO. She just said hello. No, she didn't just say hello, she typed hello. She didn't even look up at me, she just typed hello. Is that a greeting? What the fuck is wrong with this woman? I said she was a typist, sir. Well this is going to be a fun experiment. Should we start then? Fine, yes, let's just get it over with. ---- Ok, so we start on a movie set. You know, one of those big swooping shots that they use in the beginning of feel-good flicks to trick the audience into thinking they're watching something important. We pan down through the backlots and the soundstages, then come down to eye level with one of the studio sets from the outside. Somebody will pass by the camera, so we can transition to a second shot that goes all the way into the studio, weaving through the crowd of passersby outside. We could even have them talking some Hollywood bullshit, the way everyone talks when they walk around this place. We settle inside, on a makeshift production set, with the crew from 'Late Night With Conan O'Brien' running around. This isn't his set, though - the only way we recognize the staff is from the logo on their shirts and the director's chair. In the middle of all this is Danny Ferguson, dressed as a leprechaun with the sleeves cut off his jacket - so I guess it's just a vest - and spandex green shorts instead of pants and stockings. His wrestling boots have those big gold buckles on them, though. He shouldn't look happy to be here. And as the camera settles, he looks around the room and says, 'Can somebody please explain this to me again?' So the director, some skinny shithead with Lisa Loeb glasses - I don't know what the fuck they're really called, but Loeb's the one who made 'em popular - walks up with a clipboard and says, 'It's real easy Danny. You're a new character for Late Night - Patches McHogan, the Performance-Enhanced Leprechaun.' 'Is that supposed to be funny?' Danny says and the guy backs away a little, not ready to start something. 'Well, Conan thought so,' he'll say. And Danny responds with 'What's he trying to imply about me?' 'No no, nothing about you, sir, it's just that you were so cooperative that we hoped you could have a sense of humor about-“ “About the climate we live in where every muscular man is juiced up?” he’ll scream that, interrupting. Everything will get real quiet on the set, and then the director-guy, this four-eyed jerkoff, he’ll say “Why don’t we take a quick break, everyone?” except it’s not a question, it’s a statement, a statement that he’s frightened to death. So Danny will take a break and walk over to this other set piece in the back, the full interior of a house or some shit like that, and all of his people will be standing there. You know, the bodyguards, the chatty Asian manager, the angry Southern white wrestling manager, and so on and so forth. They mostly look bored, except the Asian girl, who’s chatting on the phone, and the wrestling manager, who might be ready to blow a gasket. Danny will ignore pretty much all of them except his agent, Allan North, who’s furiously typing away at a Sidekick. “Allan,” he’ll say, “We gotta find me a real role again. I can’t wait around for Double Down to be ready for filming – I mean, you know how shaky screenwriters are.” We’ll toss that in there as an F-U to our script guy, since he can’t show up for fuckin’ meetings. No, I don’t mean I want to give him a Death Valley Bodyslam. I don’t even know what that means. No, thank you, I don’t want some. Well, I’m not going to get some if I don’t want some, so- Yes, I can see you. Yes, I can. I can. Yes. Can we get on with this? ANYWAY, Allan, his agent, will look up and nod in agreement. “I’m working on a few things right now, actually. We’ll get you another project within the month. I’ve got a few things on the table.” “Oh yeah, like what?” Danny will ask. “Well, it turns out you’re a shoe-in for a major celebrity whose biopic is almost good to go, so we’ve got the inside track on that.” “What celebrity?” Danny will be getting pretty frustrated, hence all the questions. “Bobby Flay.” “Who?” “Bobb-“ “Yeah, I heard you say his name, Dipshit, I mean who is he?” And Jim, one of the bodyguards, will chime in. “American chef, big time on the Food Channel. Specializes in Southwestern cuisine.” Danny won’t be happy to hear this, obviously, so he’ll turn back to Allan with eyes afire. “You want me to star in the life story of Iron Chef Taco?” “Technically he’s not an Iron Chef?” “Technically that changes NOTHING!” Danny will snap. “Did you really think this is the kind of role I was looking for?” Allan will put down the PDA for a second and focus on his client (shock!). “I thought it would be a nice break from the norm.” “I’ve done one major film, Allan. There is no norm. I need to establish myself before I play a fag or a ‘tard and win an Oscar.” Allan will furrow his brow, nodding along. “You’re right, you’re right…maybe I can get in the ear of those people and change it up a little. Get Flay to be saving the world from demons or uncover a Nigerian drug ring or something.” “Yeah, get on that,” Danny says, starting to walk away before Reggie Delray, his wrestling manager, stops him. “Are we done now?” he’ll demand, and Danny will shake his head and say “We haven’t even started filming yet.” “You’ve got the biggest match of your career this week!” the guy will sputter, and Danny will do that apathetic scoff thing that he pulls out a lot. “Take it easy, Mike Tenay. The next match is always the biggest with you. I already forgot who I’m facing.” “You made the challenge for this match!” the manager is all uppity now. So Danny’s like, “Yeah, and then I forgot about it. I say and do a lot of things, and the reason I pay people like you is so you will REMEMBER them for me! So…who’m I facing?” And Reggie, his manager, will sigh and take a handkerchief out to wipe his brow, then he’ll say, “You’re facing a PRIME forefather, Emilio Rage.” And one bodyguard goes, “The Mighty Ducks guy?” And the other chimes in with “Emelio Estevez?” So Reggie’s all like, “No, he spells it with an I.” And Danny says “Istevez? Is that Rage in Spanish?” “Something like that.” Danny will take a bottle of water from one of his guards and walk a few feet away to lean against the set. He should tug uncomfortably at these tight green shorts he’s got on. “So he’s like some tiny Spanish luchador, then?” “Actually,” the manager will say, “he’s like 6’7 and two and a half bills.” “From Mexico? And people think I’m juicing?” “Nicaragua.” “Gesundheit.” “No, I didn’t sneeze, I said Nicaragua. Rage is from Nicaragua.” “Isn’t that a city in Mexico?” “Not quite.” So Danny will sit there silently for a second, trying to wrap his mind around the geography lesson, and then a staff worker will wander up. “Excuse me, sir,” he’ll say, “I’m going to have to ask you to not lean against the fourth wall here. It’s already taken a lot of abuse, and it might not hold up any longer.” Danny will step away from the wall and look back at it. Then we’ll have James Farwell be standing there, taking a leak a yard or so away from where he was just standing. He’ll look back at the staff guy and Danny as he finishes up, then keep watching them as he zips up and turns around. Then he’ll spit on the ground and exit through a Clyde-Walkins-shaped hole in that wall, and we’ll never see him again. The staff kid and Danny will look at the hole for a second, then the kid will pipe in with “Also, I think we’re going to start filming again.” “Fantastic,” Danny will curse, walking back towards the Late Night cameras. “This ought to be a barrel of laughs…” As he approaches, somebody from wardrobe will put an oversized green derby on his head, and he’ll reel back like he’s going to smack them. They’ll fall to the ground and crawl away as Danny walks back onto the set, and we slowly pan away right before we hear “Action!” --- That’s not bad, right? I mean, given the circumstances, we can work with that. Have this bitch type this stuff up and we’ll send it in. Then get on the phones and get us a new screenwriter. It might take another week or so, sir. Fine, whatever. Just handle this. Yes, sir.
