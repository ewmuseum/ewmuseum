Date: ReVolution 197 David stepped into the kitchen with a solemn look on his face. Those blue eyes of his looked solemn, sad. Andrea and Samantha were already in the kitchen, talking amongst themselves. Neither of them looked over at David. "So, what do we do now?" Samantha asked Andrea who shrugged her shoulders. "Not sure. Get a test, make sure everything adds up." Andrea felt the pressure in between her eyes. She tried her best to rub the pain out, but it was no use. Everything had been building up to this point for the last few weeks. David opened one of the cabinets and pulled out a glass. "You know," he began, in a soft, hushed tone. "I'm right here." There was no response though as he reached towards the top and pulled down a large bottle of whiskey that he poured into his glass. "Do you believe her?" Samantha asked. Yet, it wasn't directed at David. A beat passed as Andrea wrestled with that issue in her own mind. "No. Yes. Maybe, hell I don't know. Is it possible? Yeah. Knowing David and his pension for beautiful girls, well, she fits that mold especially well," her words were even if not stressed. "Still here," David injected, but still nothing. He rolled his eyes as he felt the liquor hit his lips, the one comfort he had in this world. Samantha's fingers tapped against the wooden table that they sat at. "What if she's running game? You know what I'm saying? A money grab. David's starting to hit it big, they slept with each other once, and now she's trying to claim he's the father. I could see that story fitting." "That could fit. That's why we need a test to see if he's really the father." "Can you even do that during the pregnancy?" "In the first few weeks, yeah. But, she's gotta be almost five months pregnant. Not sure any doctor would do it at this point." David leaned up against the refrigerator and watched them continue to discuss this issue even though neither of them had a real claim to this. He opened his mouth, but thought twice about saying anything as he took another drink of his whiskey. Warm, it didn't taste nearly as good as he wished it did. "Amniocentesis?" Samantha queried. Andrea shook her head quickly though. "Too dangerous. Baby could die." "Fuck." His ears perked up at that as Samantha was not one to normally use such profane language. Deep down, he wished they would just look at him, and ask for his input. But, he knew they were disappointed in him. David could add them to the list; it was growing rather large. "You know what?" Andrea rhetorically asked. Samantha's eyes rose slightly. "Send her away. What's the worst that she can do? Go to the media? We paint her as a gold digger. Said that she showed up to the house without any kind of proof and that we'll be more than happy to do a paternity test after the baby is born." Samantha smiled slightly. "I like it. Keeps the drama out of the house for a few more months. Keeps her away." "No." They both turned around at this point. David's voice was large and powerful. The look in his normally calm eyes revealed to them of how angry he was now. "I'm tired of hearing you two discuss my life as if you have any control over it. I'm twenty-three. I make millions of dollars. You," his finger pointed at Andrea. "Are my manager, not my God. And you..." Samantha looked a little ashamed from the look David shot her. "I can't believe you." The glass, rich with condensation, sat perfectly in the crook of his hand. His eyes closed for a moment, but neither Samantha nor Andrea thought about speaking. There was something elderly in the way that he commanded the room, even from the time that he was thirteen. It was as if someone sage and wise was speaking to them. "This is my decision to make," David told them as his eyes opened up. "I'm not about to label her as an outcast. She doesn't deserve that." Andrea cleared her throat. "When did you sleep with her?" "Back in February. I had just signed my contract with fWo. I was... lonely. She stayed with me for a day or two in Seattle." Samantha looked a little sick. David showed no concern though. "From what I know of her, she wouldn't come to me unless she was certain. I'm also certain that the two of you looked at her and beat it out of her. So, I'm going to take my drink out to the balcony and just sit there. Maybe even stand. And you know what I'm going to do? I'm going to think about everything. Clear my head. Because, not only do I have to deal with Philadelphia, but I have to think about Mary-Lynn. Lest I forget though, my family. But, it's my decision, not yours." David had looked at both of them, in the eyes, as he spoke. Samantha and Andrea were a little too frightened to say anything and just watched as David walked through the back door to the balcony. Slowly, the two looked at one another and let out a heavy sight. There was one thought that kept persisting with Andrea. "What kind of name is Philadelphia?" *** A nice, peaceful breeze filled the air as David leaned against the wooden barrier attached to the dock. The house overlooked an elegant lake that looked absolutely majestic as the moon hit it at the right angle. “So, no profound thoughts for me tonight, Dad?” David took a sip from his drink of whiskey. “Well, I guess I’d have to look into a mirror for that. So you could berate me some more for being such a horrible human being. Knocked some chick up, I guess.” His words were to no one in particular; though somewhere deep in his mind, David actually thought his father could hear him. “How,” he began, but stopped. His throat became extremely dry. After another splash of alcohol against his tongue, all felt right. “How could this happen to me? I don’t know this girl. Hell, I don’t even really care about this girl. Nice body, nice face, perfect attitude, but I didn’t want this. Not now. Not fucking now.” Anger began to set in as he slammed his right hand into the platform. He did it again, more force this time, and he felt a twinge of pain. David’s face scrunched up for a moment as he reacted to the pain. It lingered for a moment before he had another thought. “Neither did she though, isn’t that right? You use protection and you think none of this would happen. You go through life, thinking these things, and then one day it just isn’t true. I found out about this like thirty minutes ago, but she’s lived with this for months. That fear, being so alone, it must’ve been something else. Not easy, that’s for sure.” His blue eyes looked up at the sky. “You better be listening. Putting me through the shit you’ve put me through, the things that I know, you better be listening. If not, I’m so kicking your ass.” A smile appeared on his face. “So, what do I do now? Bring her into my life? Care for her? Keep her at an arm’s length? There’s no manual on what to do now. No Wikipedia page.” He sighed as he took another sip from his drink. “There is life though.” The voice surprised him. Part of him had expected to hear something, but more from a manly voice. This was very much a female voice and as he turned around, he found Sarah standing just a few feet away from him. She wore a pair of jeans and a revealing black shirt. Her eyes told him that she had just arrived home. “What’re you doing out here?” David asked her and she point her thumb back towards the house. “Andrea and Samantha, don’t know where they’re at. Walked in though and found Mary-Lynn sitting by the door with a worried look on her face. Then saw some pregnant chick sitting on the couch. So, what’s Juno doing here?” Sarah asked him before she moved closer to him. This wasn’t the kind of conversation he wanted to have with his little sister. Thus, another sip of alcohol was in order. “She’s here, because she’s pregnant.” Sara gave him the oddest look. It basically said that David was a fucking idiot. “No shit, Sherlock. Let me just take a guess at this. You slept with her. Didn’t call her back. She got pregnant. Came to see you. The thing I don’t get,” she began before she grabbed the glass from David’s hand and took a sip of it herself. “How did you manage to get her? She’s way out of your range.” David laughed at the suggestion. He yanked his drink back when he stopped laughing. “I’ve got skills, little sister that you could only dream of. Though, I’d prefer if my younger sister didn’t know so much about life or drink my alcohol. Get your own. Oh, and wear some clothes next time you go out.” “Screw you.” She joked. At least, David thought it was a joke. “So, what’re you going to do? Because, I’ve got some advice if you want some. David just groaned. “Go ahead and let me have it.” “First, I’m certain Andrea was fine with you just pushing her away until the child’s born. Don’t do that, you’ll look like a douche. Trust me. You need to be a man, something that Dad used to badger you about. It might suck, it might cramp your style, but you need to be there for this girl. It might not be your child, but you’ll show commitment to her and to everyone else in your family that when shit gets tough, you’re willing to do the right thing. If it is your kid, then you’re going to get involved.” It made sense. Perfect sense. He hated her for it. “When did you become so smart?” “The day I was able to first talk to you. You just forgot how to be you.” “What about Mary-Lynn?” She pondered that thought for a minute. Then, Sarah pointed at the nearly empty glass and David looked at it. A second passed before he decided to give it back to her and she finished it off. “Be straight with her. She’ll understand. Maybe not at first, but she will understand. If you care about her, then be with her, but give her some space and time. This isn’t going to be easy for her. Sure, you might be a ladies’ man, and she might know that. But, this thing with Juno and you, it’s not going to be easy with her. Most importantly though, you need to get inside and talk to these people. Stop standing outside and sulking for yourself. It’s emo. So not cool.” Sarah then handed the empty glass back and turned on her heels before she walked back into the house. David looked at the empty glass and shook his head. Things had been so close to good. So close. He could feel his life starting to come together, but this had to happen. It had to just mess with everything he was working so hard on. “I blame you for this,” David spoke towards the sky and pointed his finger up there in an angry fashion. He looked towards the door again and shook his head before he threw the glass with all of his might up against the wall and listened as the shards of glass fell without purpose on to the ground. Much like how he felt about his life in this very moment. *** Philadelphia Montgomery was a simple person. She had lived most of her life in Seattle and loved every minute of it. The only flaw about her was the wasted potential she showed throughout her life. In High School, people were certain that she would be the valedictorian at her graduation. The only problem was that she earned a 3.5GPA that semester and ruined her chances. Captain of the cheerleading squad, the week before their largest competition, she got sick. Deep down, she had this innate ability to screw things up. In her mind, she added this to her list of failures. An hour had passed since the room emptied out. She was tempted to leave, and come back another day. Never had she intended for everything to go this way. Her true intent was to come and talk to David about what had happened, not cause a scene. The girl with the red hair had looked so sad and shook up; it only made Philadelphia feel worse about the situation. “Having fun by yourself?” The voice came from the kitchen and the man revealed himself to be David. He looked somber and uneasy. However, even with those feelings, he sat down next to her and grabbed her hand. It took her by surprise. “Can’t say I am,” she responded in this quiet tone, a frightened tone. “I’m sorry about everything. Can’t say we have too many people walking into this house claiming to be pregnant with my child. I promise we’ll do better next time.” She laughed. Her smile lit up the entire room and eased any fears that David had. “Look, if you want, I’ll come back tomor—“ “Nonsense. Look, I’ve done some thinking. You know this is never easy.” David took a look around and sighed. Philly squeezed his hand and looked into David’s blue eyes. “Let me speak for a minute. I came here to let you know that I’m pregnant and it’s your child. I know it’s your child because there’s no one I’ve been with for a while. I’m not looking for you to give me money so I can go away. I really don’t know what I expect, but I don’t expect you to change your life for me. But, it’s not fair for you to not know this.” A moment passed as David took all of that in. His palms continued to be sweaty as he felt this oddness circle around him. “I appreciate that. I want to offer you a chance to stay here, at the house, for a few nights. We can talk about our options, what you want to do, and then go from there. I want to be part of this though. Every step from here on out. I don’t make any assumptions at what’s going to happen, but I know that I need to be here for you, for this,” David spoke in an uneasy tone, his words awkward in his mouth, but his meaning sincere. Philadelphia nodded her head in agreement; tears welled up into her eyes for the briefest of moments. David thought for a moment as his eyes looked towards the ground. “Philly, why didn’t you come to me sooner?” “I didn’t want to ruin your life.” He looked at her. “You haven’t.” Philly smiled back. “That girl who was in here, the one with the red hair, was that your girlfriend?” Mary-Lynn. He had forgotten all about her. Quickly, David held up his finger as he sprang up from the couch and rushed towards the front door. His eyes scanned the room all around, but didn’t see her. He opened the door and looked around, but still nothing. The palms of his hand collided into his covered legs in frustration. “She called a taxi, about an hour ago,” Samantha offered from behind. David turned around and bit his bottom lip. “Said to call her tomorrow or the day after, whenever you’re ready.” David began to walk towards the front door. Samantha looked at her brother and kissed him on the forehead. “For what it’s worth, she didn’t seem upset with you. I think she’s very understanding. Don’t let her go quite yet.” David offered nothing as he stepped back into the house and back towards the living room where Philly sat. “Any luck?” David shook his head. “I’m sorry, I didn’t know, or want to screw anything up. I don’t even know her name, but she seemed like a sweet girl. You two weren’t dating when we--,” she stopped short as David shook his head. Somehow, that made everything just a little bit easier for Philadelphia. “Let me show you to your room, Philly. I imagine you need some sleep. We can have breakfast in the morning if you’d like.” She flashed her smile at him. David helped her up and she nodded her head. “I’d like that very much.” Philadelphia then took David’s arm as he led her up the stairs. The day had started strongly for David and had ended with everything being turned on its head. Somehow, he would have to make sense of everything, he knew that much. What he didn’t know was how he would manage to do that.
