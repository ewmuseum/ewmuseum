Fate (ReVolution 68 - Million Dollar Match v. Xavier Kannon)

Date: Timeless “When you see the little red light on the camera flash on,” went the scratchy, muppetish voice of the strangest creature Angelo Deville had ever seen, a child-sized beast with purple skin and yellow fangs named Minion the Imp, “you speak into the fuzzy end.” Smiling condescendingly, Minion tapped the bottom of the microphone in Deville’s hand so there could be no confusion. “Thanks,” Deville responded wryly, giving his head a faint shake. He hoped the little fiend was joking, but given his treatment by Primetime Championship Wrestling and their Academy thus far, it wouldn’t surprise him if Minion was being dead serious. It had been a long time since Deville had been regarded as a curtain jerking nobody, but as PCW had made painfully clear with their referral to this glorified training centre, his past accomplishments meant exactly nothing in their world. “We didn’t have microphones and cameras back in WEEF when I won every championship they had,” he continued in his sarcastic way, sneering at the imp, “nor in WIW, where—“ “Oh for shit’s sake,” Minion interrupted with heaved impatience. “Don’t talk about your past. No one cares. Everyone and their slutty mother was a champion in XYZ fed at one time or another.” Deville pursed his lips. He hated this attitude, the idea that what happened before was of no consequence, that mentioning one’s past and acknowledging the effects of it was somehow disrespectful to the current organization. History and experience were what moulded people into who they were. To ignore it was to ignore oneself. “Look,” the purple monster went on as if lecturing an obstinate child, “just be modest and talk a bit of smack about your opponents tonight. That’s all we expect, that’s all we want, and that’s probably all you’re good at.” Narrowing his eyes to see if Deville was sufficiently chastised, he nodded when there was no argument, as if that was the end of it. It was, as far as Deville was concerned. He wasn’t going to argue with a three-foot-tall manager slash interviewer smeared with purple paint. That’s not why he signed up with the school. He also didn’t sign up to be modest and talk empty smack to a couple of guys who didn’t know a rest-hold from a restroom, guys he knew would never amount to anything in the business. He’d make them look retarded in the ring, but that was only a means to an end. “You need to prove yourself,” he’d been informed through the informality of a letter stamped with PCW letterhead. That was their only response to the multimedia curriculum vitae he’d sent them, complete with match film, magazine articles, audio tapes of promos and radio interviews, title histories, and everything else he could think to include. He’d won his first World Title by powerbombing a near-500-pound behemoth off a roof in one of the largest federations in the world, then defended the title nearly every week for three months before incarceration killed the reign prematurely. He’d only been in WIW a week before he won their Television Title, and would’ve won their World Title the following week if not for outside interference. After nearly fifty matches he could count the number of defeats he’d suffered on the fingers of one hand; he could count the number of clean defeats on his thumbs. Apparently, that wasn’t the sort of proof they needed. Apparently, in order to prove he was ready for PCW, he had to make mockeries of men who would never make it there. He had been told by his headmaster, Hessian, to hide that attitude, to keep his mouth shut, and with time the right people would take notice. Once he’d proven himself and been hired he could say whatever he wanted, but until that time came he needed to think low and walk lower. Unfortunately, that wasn’t The Deville’s style. He wasn’t there to prove himself or tow the line of humility. Deville was there for one reason and one reason only. It was a gateway to what he needed, to what had driven him his entire life. He’d left WIW because in one tape sent to him by an old friend he’d seen something he hadn’t seen in years, something that gave him goosebumps, something that would make him put up with any amount of pompous nonsense to reach it. He’d seen someone that could beat him. For most people, red meant stop, but whenever Deville was confronted by it he kicked it into another gear. He hated being told that he couldn’t, shouldn’t, and wouldn’t. He relished proving those people wrong, revelled in letting them know it was coming before it happened. So when the red light came on and Minion introduced him, he laid it out in plain black and white. He didn’t feign humility or suck up, he issued a warning to the entire Primetime Central world, telling them he was on his way, letting them know point blank that there was nothing and no one who could keep him from his goal. “I’m never going to need to introduce myself again,” he sneered, stabbing a lordly finger into the camera, ignoring the apoplectic imp at his side, “but I’m going to at every opportunity, to mock this slight. My name is Angelo Deville, and in time you’re going to beg me for what you initially refused. “I’m not here for fame or money. I’m not here to learn to be a better wrestler. I’m here because you have something I want, and when I want something, I do whatever it takes to get it. I’m here to fight one man, because he’s the best I’ve ever seen. Xavier Kannon, mark my words: I’m coming for you, and I’m not going to rest until I find out which of us is the better man.” ~><>…<><~ Pacing. On the air, The Deville employed it as a tool to pull the audience in, to instill a sense of unpredictability and indecision, a feeling of entropy. It was a part of the show, a part of the act, a facetious chaos designed to undermine suspicions of his calculative character. The irony of it often made him smile, but regardless of how frequently the result contravened the offered intention, people rarely caught on. In his personal life, pacing had as much place as pacifism. An unwavering affinity with his schemes, his perpetual planning, prevented it. There were questions to answer and forks to choose, but uncertainty never weighed him down long enough to warrant wracking his brain with worry. The turmoil he faced was always when, not if or why or what. Three days after he’d chased Karina away from his estate – interminable days like living, taunting beasts, finding sustenance devouring his soul – he paced the length of his library like he was caged inside. His sleek mobile phone had been clutched in his hand like a microphone the entire time, occasionally flipping open and up to his mouth. This wasn’t like when he was in the ring; here his only spectators were the volumes of lore, the marble walls, and the great cathedral windows magnifying the sunlight to make him sweat; this was real. Isn’t this what you wanted? his own voice demanded scornfully, mockingly, for all the world as if it were another person. Cursing himself a fool, he knew that it was. Yet at the same time, it wasn’t. It had been at first, but now . . . now he abhorred his very nature for forcing him to plough forward when all he’d truly wanted was to call the whole thing off. For the first time in his life, his beguilement was having a worse effect on him than his victim; he’d deceived himself as cruelly as his prey. ~><>…<><~ I made good on my promise, dominating the Academy as I warned that I would, but still PCW ignored me. They called up others around me, men I’d already defeated in the ring, but for me they had only a wry smile and a shrug. Some people told me I was being punished for demanding promotion and speaking my mind, which was somehow anathema to being a good student. Others, superstars from PCW who worked for the Academy as trainers, had no explanation whatsoever. They told me to hang in there and my day would come. It came during my fifth week. I’d be lying if I said I wasn’t excited when the notorious Phantom Booker approached me about being called up. Everyone knew he called the shots, and if he was coming to see me, it could only mean one thing. My hopes were being realized. That idea was dashed with the first words out of his mouth. He told me he was re-opening an old promotion by the name of Five Star Wrestling, told me I’d be perfect for it. I told him I was holding out for PCW, for Kannon, that I was honoured by the offer but would have to decline. He was silent for a long moment, then shrugged and told me it was up to me. What lay unspoken in his words but flared angrily in his eyes was that if I did turn him down I wasn’t going to go anywhere. This was my chance, take it or leave it. I took it, telling myself that PCW would pick me up when they saw how huge of a draw I would be. The problem with that, as I soon discovered, was that FSW was completely choreographed. Everything was planned out months in advance, and it didn’t matter that I could manhandle everyone on the roster; if someone had a greater marquee than I did, I was supposed to lose to them. Everyone had greater marquee than I did. I was a lowly PWA graduate, and my initial booking was to feud with a man named Jason O’Neil, a shallow undercard dispute over which of us was the better aspiring student. Luckily, O’Neil was a smart man, and he saw the angle for the same limiting bullshit that I did. Rather than feud over nothing, we concocted an angle that put the others in the promotion to shame. Talentless hacks like Timo Bolamba, who was in the world title scene by the simple virtue that he was a big wheel in the previous incarnation of the league, became so jealous over our instant overness that they tried to undermine us at every turn. They’d moan and bitch at the fans, arguing that the praise and accolades should be theirs. I loved FSW because of O’Neil, who was always stimulating and challenging me, pushing me to push him. I loathed it because I couldn’t just pull those other guys into the ring and choke out their words with the sweet aria of The Soprano. Week in and week out I had to sit back and watch them pretend they were better than I was. All I wanted was competition; all they wanted was to reap the unwarranted benefits of pretend glory. The final straw came by the name of Kyle Velocity. He was a hippy-haired monkey with a guitar that was a big wheel in some league I’d never heard of, and when the owners hired him, they thought it would be good marketing for me to put him over. Against my better judgement, I agreed to it, and the week after I let the schmuck pin me he disappeared without a word. That was how it was in the perverted, angled version of the sport I loved. It almost turned my adoration to hate, but luckily, I decided it was time to take matters back into my own hands. With the Golden Turnbuckle Tournament already heading into the third round, I made a few calls and forced my way into it as the only round three play-in. I’d originally signed up for it a month in advance, but when sign-ups were re-held a week before it kicked off, I was on vacation, and by the time I returned it was already underway. Fate, however, wasn’t to be denied. I won that tournament as I knew and boasted that I would. In response to my dominance, Primetime Central introduced a championship designed to put the Global Title to shame, a title that would be defended anywhere, against anyone. The Universal Championship. I was the inaugural champion, and I lorded the title as only I could. My first real challenge came less than a week later, over the phone, by none other than Xavier Kannon. He knew I was coming to PCW to take part in their Alliances extravaganza as part of Team FSW, and he proposed that we shock the world by having an impromptu Universal Title match beforehand. I was too stunned by euphoria to say much more than how honoured I was. The dream was finally coming true. Xavier Kannon versus Angelo Deville. Fate indeed. ~><>…<><~ Call her, the voice urged, and again the cold plastic of The Deville’s phone found his cheek. His mouth opened to engage the voice-activated dialling, but he couldn’t bring himself to utter her name. Over and over like a bad dream he saw the blood-soaked shirt sailing down to where Karina waited unsuspecting in his labyrinth of hot-tubs, blissfully unaware and unsuspecting. He could still feel the tightness in the arm that had thrown it, the agony in his mind wishing he could have it back. He’d known what the result would be. He’d been planning for it longer than he could bring himself to admit. ~><>…<><~ The Deville felt good meandering the halls at Alliances, swaggering around backstage with his title, feeling the anticipative buzz of competition, which was never in attendance at FSW shows. It had taken him nearly six months, but he was finally getting what he’d originally come for, a match with the only man he respected as much as himself, Xavier Kannon. He hadn’t encountered the phenom yet, but he felt like the clichéd kid on Christmas morning, tingling with excitement from head to toe, every hair on his body upright and electrified. Destiny was finally drawing nigh. When he rounded a corner and saw his stable-mate Tyler Nelson being choked against a wall by an oversized Russian, Deville was in a good enough mood to pick another fight. Ivan Stanislav wasn’t on the level of Kannon, but he was close to it; he was another man The Deville wanted to meet in the ring. Stanislav thought he was too good for Deville, however. Deville had suggested they meet weeks before, but Stanislav wanted it to happen in OSW, where he was assured a false victory. This was the only instance in the longest time he’d poked his head out of the safe, angled confines of OSW, and Deville wasn’t going to let it pass by. Here in PCW he didn’t have the safety net or glass ceiling that made him a terrifying household name. Here he was as subject to talent as everyone else. Pistol-whipping him probably wasn’t the classiest way to issue the challenge, but it was effective. Nelson was in hysterics as the irate monster backed down the hallway, snarling, grabbing the back of his head, telling the gun-brandishing Deville this wasn’t the end. Joining Nelson, Deville told Ivan he looked forward to the continuation, that he hoped Stanislav wasn’t just boasting to maintain his fierce image. “Subtle,” came a very dry voice from behind them, and when Angelo spun, he found himself face to face for the very first time with Xavier Kannon. The pistol disappeared into his jacket as a sheepish grin crept over his face, then Deville was extending a hand for Kannon to shake. The cameras were off now, so they were free to talk and act candidly. “It’s an honour to meet you, Mr. Kannon,” Deville beamed, smiling. “I’ve been looking forward to this for longer than you know.” “The honour is mine,” Kannon responded as if by rote, giving Nelson a grudging nod. “Now, here’s how I figure it should go down.” Deville blinked, uncomprehending. “You and I will trade moves for a while, bump the ref, then Nelson here will come out and blindside me with a chair, leading to the pin.” The plan unravelled quickly and matter-of-factly, as if it was the most natural thing in the world. To Deville, it was the antithesis of natural, an incestuous replication of everything he held dear. “Hold on a second,” Deville choked, holding out his hands. “What are you talking about? I came here to wrestle you, not to dance around and put on a show.” “Oh.” Kannon stole a look at Nelson. “I suppose I should have been more clear. This isn’t a genuine contest; I don’t want your title. This is just to give you some added steam.” He favoured Deville with a generous, altruistic smile. “You’re the next big thing around here, Deville, and I’m going to make sure people know it.” Deville felt like he’d been hit low. With a sledgehammer. He opened his mouth to protest, but Kannon beat him to the punch. “Don’t worry about it. I’ll make you look good. You’re going to be the biggest star the world has ever seen.” Proffering another of those charitable smiles, Kannon patted Deville’s shoulder. He felt like a kid at Christmas again, but this time his older brother had just told him about Santa Claus. “Now if you’ll excuse me,” Kannon went on, checking his watch-less wrist, “I have to prepare for the main event. See you in the ring.” Deville watched him go, paralysed and disenchanted. His dream had done a complete 180, and there was nothing he could do about it. “You must feel on top of the world,” Nelson observed in his blind way, seeing only what he thought he should see. “I want to beat him clean,” Deville muttered, still unable to put any gumption into his voice. “I want to beat him honestly.” “What?” Nelson demanded as if that was the most absurd thing in the world. “You’re a heel, Deville. You don’t win clean. Just be thankful Kannon’s so generous.” Deville couldn’t even seethe. He was too numb. He wasn’t defined by being a cheat. He was a wrestler; he was a champion. How could they expect him to be grateful for being robbed of that? ~><>…<><~ He knew he should shelve his pride and call her, but again the phone snapped closed. Why couldn’t she call him? Did she expect him to lie about who he was, to pretend a life other than he knew? She’d known coming into it what he was about. She knew who he was. But did you have to throw it in her face? You knew what it would do, and you know why you did it. He never intended to genuinely fall in love, and when it happened, it seared him to his core with fear. He tried to tell himself it wasn’t true, that he was merely falling victim to his own deception, but the more time he spent with Karina, the more attached and infatuated he became. She was unique, priceless, a marvel of pride and spirit and sincerity. And she’d fallen in love with him. That had been his manipulative intention from the onset, but actually being blessed by it, experiencing something so rare and precious and beautiful, was something he hadn’t been prepared for. Again he felt himself throwing it off the edge of the cliff in the form of a bloodied shirt, all for the sake of his damnable pride and planning. His heart broke when he did it, but he swallowed the feeling down, telling himself the fallout was inevitable, that it was best to get it over with before the true pain could gain a foothold. It wasn’t until afterward that he could bring himself to admit it was already embedded in his soul. He wished it wasn’t true, but it was. He loved her, but sooner or later she would discover his deception. When she did, he knew it would shatter her. She donned aloofness like a suit of armour, but she cared and felt more deeply than anyone he’d ever met. She submitted to him, gave herself to him unabashedly, and still he couldn’t bring himself to return the favour, to abandon his planning and surrender to the love that made him feel unparalleled completeness. All because of five insincere minutes two and a half years ago. Because of a Whirlwind Romance and a deserved pin. Because he was too corrupted to appreciate anything more than the competition people could provide him and his perverted sense of pride. Was he really doomed to the lonely peak of unparalleled success? He never would have considered it a possibility before, but now he knew as surely as he’d ever known anything that he wanted more. He just had to hope he hadn’t already killed his opportunity at happiness. He hoped his caste wasn’t set in stone, that it wasn’t too late to undo the damage he’d done, the villainous shadow he’d lost himself in. He was sick of cheating, of deceiving, of lacing falsehood in his every word and deed. “Call Kari,” he ordered the phone, plans dissipating, love scoring its first-ever pinfall over avaricious pride. ~><>…<><~ I hated you for that match, Christian. Despite your intentions, I felt nothing but robbed, like you didn’t think I was good enough to face you, like I needed your charity. You did to me what only I’ve ever been able to: you turned my dream into a mockery. When you retired mere months later, citing your back injury, I lost my desire to compete. I quit PWC, and my brief surfacing in OSE was nothing but a vain attempt to recapture the thrill of the chase I’d experienced hunting you. Instead of finding those who loved the sport as we did, champions willing to put it all on the line just for the sake of doing so, I found gluttonous pretenders like Stanislav and Bolamba, guys that wanted it all for the sake of having it all, without putting anything on the line. I despised them for it, Christian, but I despised you more for abandoning me to them. I retired, officially, and disappeared from the world. And you . . . as soon as I left, you resurfaced. You took the only title in OSE that meant anything, then you went on and won my Universal Title. It might surprise you, but I was jubilant. I’d always suspected your back was better than you let on, that your sense of adventure wouldn’t allow a niggling injury to keep you away from the stage, and your return confirmed it for me. With your renewed vigour, it also bloomed anew within me. I returned to the life and re-embarked on my quest to face you. Then, just as it was all coming together, you retired again, taking my title with you, harshly twisting the knife you planted in my heart that first night we met. Why, Christian? What made you so bitter? What made you hate? Was it me? Guys like me, gunning for you, aspiring to be like you and face you in the ring? I wasn’t trying to take you down or make you less. I wanted to make us both more, to inspire you as you inspired me. People think I’m doing this to prove some shallow point, to overcome you once and for all, that I value my greed over your health. I’m not going to lie about it; there’s a part of me that craves the satisfaction of defeating you, the sense of triumph that toppling you would ignite. But there’s more to it than that. So much more. I don’t hate you anymore, but I hate what you’ve become. I hate the way you look at the ring and your peers, the emptiness in your eyes when you accepted my challenge. I hate that it took a million dollars to make you face me, that if you didn’t need the money I wouldn’t have the honour of meeting you in the ring. I hate that you see this as a chore instead of an adventure. I hate that all your memories are acidic. My primary prerogative now is changing that. I offered the million because I knew you’d take it. I know that you need it. I have it to spare, and after all you’ve given, you certainly deserve it. But you mistake me if you think I’m trying to buy a cheap sense of victory over a broken man. That’s not my goal; it never was. My goal is to un-break the man, to rediscover the legend, even if only for one night. I want to rekindle the fire in your eyes, the inferno in your being that motivated me and countless others to be more. Win or lose, I’m going to reacquaint you with the thrill of competition, the sheer bliss of being lost in a world of poetic violence, a world you and I know better than anyone else. I’m going to sweep away the bitterness with the sweet lullaby that precedes the beautiful dream. I’m going to make you respect me in the ring first-hand. Not through word of mouth, not through watching me on film, but by engaging and embracing me as the warrior you used to see in the mirror. More importantly, I’m going to make you respect yourself, Xavier Kannon. I’m going to guide you back to your love of the sport, to the unbridled affection you emanated when I first saw you and knew we had to fight. I’m going to make you remember Xavier Kannon as the paragon that he was, as the champion we all strived to be. I’m going to inspire you to inspire me. To me, that’s worth an entire forest of dirty paper. To me, that’s what this is all about. Our coming together is fate, Christian, and fate isn’t to be denied. Thank you for daring to dance this final dream with me; whatever happens, it will be my honour to shake your hand on the other side.
