Upheaval

17 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17th June He swayed into the office with all the swagger of a chihuahua with a twelve inch penis. Detective Harry Stone; the precinct's top dog, the man with a case clearance rate that most cops dream of, sashaying through the open-plan room as if he owned the place. With an Egg McMuffin in one hand and a teipd, coffee-filled Styrofoam cup in the other, the portly lawman had soon made his way through the cubicles, via a quick rendez-vous at reception to check for mail. Stone made it to his desk and let the envelopes drop from the warmth of his armpit. The gas-lift chair hissed as he plopped his body down and took a hefty bite out of the unsatisfying breakfast sandwich. Harry wasn't one to make a habit of sampling the “cuisine” dished out by those fast food joints, but what's a man to do when he's in a hurry and his belly's a-rumblin'? Swallowing a mouthful of watered-down filter coffee, Harry slipped his beige jacket from his shoulders and let it hang over the back of his chair, worrying not for folds and creases. Casting his mail to the side for a moment, he pulled a manilla folder from his desk's plastic filing system and smiled, remembering his reason for coming into work today. Harry slid the paper-clip from the top corner and flicked it into the waste paper basket. From the folder he pulled sheets of profiles, statistics, and reports; typical case file bullshit, along with a single black and white Polaroid that caught his attention. The boy was young, yes; fresh-faced and nubile, with a flawless complexion and baby-blue eyes that were so striking they'd make Zac Efron look like Joseph Merrick. His impeccable, pearly-white smile shone brightly even through the blurred image; his happiness infectious. Tangible. From just looking at the picture, Harry felt as though he'd know the kid for years, when in reality they'd never crossed paths. Leaning back in his chair, Harry balled a fist and cracked his knuckles against the palm of his other hand. It had been over a month since he'd sunk his teeth into a good, solid case and he'd been relishing this for weeks. When you're a homicide detective in one of the city's most dangerous areas, thirty days of street patrol and hanging out misdemeanours was never going to create the same buzz as cracking open a sturdy, tightly-wound case, and he was glad to be back in the saddle. His spine curved forward as he propped his forearms up on the desk and gave the documents another scan. Aidan Chase. Twenty-three. Missing presumed dead. A wannabe pro-wrestling superstar with no prior convictions and no ties to the organised crime families had risen to boss the district in recent years. Called-in sick to his Wal-Mart day-job over a month ago, and that was the last anyone had heard of him. Father lives upstate, Mother's six feet under. No brothers, no sisters. No significant other. Harry grinned. Perfect, he thought. The case was a twenty-four ounce porterhouse, and he was a carnivore that hadn't had steak in a long fucking time. Just glad to be doing some honest-to-God police work, Stone pulled a sharp pencil from the draw and set to work. “Mornin', Harry,” a friendly but deep voice boomed from a couple of feet away. Harry looked up; the pencil's point snapping and rolling across the desk as a ripple of surprise shot through his body. Stan Callaghan approached with all the grace of an elephant on a unicycle. Big and bulbous with a shiny bald head and a heavyset brow, the man had the appearance of a bear wrestler and a warm, almost childlike demeanour that bellied his intimidating visage. “Stan,” Harry said curtly, nodding subtly as his huge colleague leant one of his tree-trunk arms against the top of his cubicle. “Still grinding that double homicide?” Immediately Stan's facial expression sagged; his case obviously wasn't a preferred topic of conversation. “I ain't got a fucking thing, man. Can't get a damn word out of the families, the two 'eyeball witnesses' Hughes got me? Might as well be blind for all they're givin' up...” “Guess that's just the way things are right now,” Harry shrugged. “Everybody in the Bronx seems to be connected these days. An officer flashes a badge and they're too terrified to talk 'case somebody dangerous finds out...” A heavy, displeased sigh escaped Detective Callaghan's sizeable lungs. “Yeah, well, what are you gonna do?” He shook away his annoyance and quickly changed the subject. “But hey, ain't all bad news around this joint. Know that case file you picked-up the other day? The missing wrestling kid, or whatever it was?” “Chase, yeah.” “Right,” nodded the gentle giant. “Guy here to see you. Left him in Room Two. Says he's got some knowledge to share...” “Uhhhh, sure,” Harry began. Bringing the case's first informant into the fold would be an all-together more exciting prospect if it weren't for the matter of the Egg McMuffin and half-full cup of coffee waiting patyently on his desk. “Let him know I'll be through when I've wolfed this sandwich, will ya?” “Could do that,” Stan said over the ringing of telephones and the faint pitter-patter of fingers hitting keyboards, “but he seems pretty tetchy, man. Guy's getting himself in a bit of a mess...” Stone huffed. “What's the name?” “Loftman. Nathaniel Loftman.” “Hmmmm,” mused Harry, raising a hand to scratch his stubbly chin. “Doesn't ring a bell...” “Well, it should,” Stan Callghan said carefully. He paused. “He's Jason Natas' agent.” * * * * * See the man. Jason Natas; twenty-six years dying and not yet dead. His skin tougher than leather and his features worn and beaten like the edges of an old tin. A man plagued by a sullen mien and a tumultuous attitude. A fighter. A selfish brute concerned not with the troubles of others, and attentive only to his own charges. Scuffed, well-worn black leather jacket. Ragged blue jeans and old Dr. Martens. A shaven-head and a seven-day beard. Face tattoos. A great juxtaposition between a man and his surroundings there could not be. The house was nice, yes; pleasant, quaint. Two storeys, three bedrooms. Built up in a quiet neighbourhood away from the hustle and bustle of the city, surrounded by families of two-point-four children and tremendous, artificial greenery. The road outside was calm with barely a vehicle passing, the sidewalks were generously wide, and there was an elementary school just round the corner. If all you wanted from life was a peaceful spot on which to raise a family, it was perfect. It made Jason Natas want to hurl. The community spirit that others found “warming” and “encouraging” and the legions of ankle-biting demon spawn scuttling around the streets on colourful tricycles and bicycles paid for by their parent's riches. The whole family waving Pop off to work as he pulled out of the driveway in his Mercedes, only to stop short upon the realisation that he'd left his peanut butter and jelly sandwiches sitting on the worktop. Yuck. He couldn't have felt more out of place as his heavy footsteps echoed throughout the bare walls of the empty home. Jason was a man used to slumming it; even during childhood he'd never lived in a place that had more than one bedroom, and even then he'd remained in the same block on The Bronx's notorious south-side for longer than he could remember. The Anti-Superstar was built for the streets, not suburbia; but the fact that his apartment had burned to the ground certainly made moving back to squalor rather difficult... Stepping through the tastefully-decorated kitchen slowly, Jason walked through the fully-glazed back door and out onto a meticulously manicured lawn. The sun shone brightly above him. The smell of barbecues wafted over the fence, and in the distance he could hear birds chirp and children play. This would not do at all. He felt very much like a wolf in the flock, but this was his fate. He couldn't return to his old neighbourhood; not after everything he'd been through the passed couple of months. Cordell Abraham and his squad of goons and mooks had been trailing him ever since the day he turned his back on the mobster's underground pit-fighting ring almost two years prior, but recent events had seen the heat turned-up way beyond boiling point. It was only going to be a matter of time before Abraham's goombas tracked him down, but at least here, surrounded by joy and innocence, he was safer than in the city. A man gets killed in The Bronx and nobody bats an eyelid; it's such a regular occurrence that nobody really cares anymore. But a man getting killed in such a middle-class suburban utopia? Boom. Instant front-page story. “So, whaddaya reckon of the place, then?” a sickeningly-polished New York accent quipped. Jason turned around. Saw the filthy sleaze-merchant of a realtor bounding towards him with the enthusiasm of a Labrador. Clutching a clipboard close to his suit-clad chest, the estate agent masked his contempt for The Anti-Superstar with a well-rehearsed plastic smile. “Not bad, eh?” Jason nodded. “Love it,” he said with all the trustworthiness of a chocolate coffee pot. “Where do I sign?” The pompadour-wearing realtor beamed from ear-to-ear as Jason Natas swallowed hard and prepared to imprison himself in a world he could never grow to love. * * * * * Harry Stone closed the interrogation room's heavy door as he and his partner, Callaghan, stepped over the threshold. “So...” began Detective Stone, “how does the agent of one of the country's most notorious personalities wind-up sitting in my place of business, hmmm?” Stone slid himself down into the uncomfortable plastic chair and leaned forward on the oak table. Behind him, Stan folded his chunky arms across his barrel-chest and carefully observed the wealthy superagent, who twisted and squirmed like a fish out of water. “I understand you've been looking into the Aidan Chase incident,” Nathaniel Loftman said in a frail wafer of a voice. He brushed a wayward strand of straightened blonde hair from his eye and tried to make himself agreeable in his dismal surroundings. The wall was grey, the floor was grey, the door was grey. Hell, the only things in the room that weren't half-way between black and white were Callaghan's “zany” Mickey Mouse tye and Stone's cup of Joe. “Jeez... world travel's fast these days, don't it?” Stone said, before taking a slow sip of the quickly-cooling brown liquid. Loftman only responded with a nod and a glare. The procedure was not one that he was overly familiar with, and was definitely one that he wished to complete with as little time and effort as possible. It wasn't going to be easy, though, and the hefty brute standing at the other side of the room wasn't helping matters either, despite his Technicolor Disney tye... “Well at this stage in the investigation we ain't exactly got much,” said Detective Stone, “so basically, anything that you can offer up to us is gonna go straight to the top of the pile.” He leaned forward further, examining Nathaniel Loftman. Beads of perspiration had already began to build on his brow, and he clearly hadn't shaved in a few days. He was jittery; unable to sit in the same position for longer than a couple of seconds. Fiddling and fidgeting like he had ants in his pants. Clearly, a man ill-at-ease with himself. “So, Mr. Loftman. What you got for us? You seen our little stray? Caught wind of someone who might have?” “No. No, not at all.” The Potemkin Group executive gulped hard and slackened his black tye. He felt red heat begin to boil behind his scalp. “I haven't seen Aidan Chase, and I don't think anybody else has, because...” His muscles tensed. Throat tightened. “Because...” He remembered what Cordell Abraham said he'd do if he ever found he'd been talking to the police and dismissed it. Shot it down. How would Abraham ever find out? “Jason killed him.”
