Exploring The Cause (ReVolution 65 - Justin Williamson)

Date: Timeless Manifest Destiny. As a historical movement, the ideal was to spread freedom and success, knowledge and liberty, to equip the unarmed with the weaponry required to determine their own fate. America believed they were the perfect model of civilization and prosperity, and as such felt it their duty to educate and liberate those suffering under barbaric government. One-hundred fifty-nine years ago America tried to force their conviction on the revolt-torn new republic of Mexico, accepting the seceded Texas into the Union and supplying it with troops, precipitating the American-Mexican war and coining the phrase. Mexico tried to re-conquer what they believed was theirs, but the sophisticated and infinitely more powerful Americans quickly crushed the rebellion, infiltrating California and New Mexico and invading the heart of Mexico herself. After mercilessly crushing the homeland army of Santa Anna, America moved its occupation into Mexico City, marking the first-ever subjugation of the capitol. A treaty was eventually signed out of America’s mercy, whereby Mexico forfeited huge chunks of land in exchange for peace and a nominal gift. ~><>…<><~ “People often see what they want to see and believe what is most convenient. It's so much easier than looking deeper, than taking the time to explore and discover.” The Deville had told that to his would-be Disciple on the night they met, when Williamson wasn’t even a vague notion and the clown-haired HIT was still calling the shots. The entire conversation had been a pissing contest of subtle twists and metaphor, with Deville trying to divine The HIT’s motivation for helping him in the ring and The HIT trying to obscure the path. When Deville finally tired of jokes about sides and angles and asked him straight a second time for his motivation, the answer was issued in the most oblique way possible. “That, Mr. Deville,” The HIT had begun smugly, brushing past him to the door, “is a question that will be best answered at a later time, in a different place than this. Not here, not now. Instead, I shall leave you with this. Consider the fact that I'm here, talking to you. When was the last time anyone ever spoke to you by choice?” The implication was obvious. Deville needed a friend, someone to watch his back, and the Trapezoid was willing and able to fill that role. A more convenient situation, a more shallow subterfuge, The Deville couldn’t begin to imagine. “What a shame you were never receptive to the one honest piece of advice I chose to impart on you, Justin.” Resplendent in a three-piece suit of pinstriped black and white silk, Deville kept his eyes glued on his Disciple a few feet away, panting and tired, in his wrestling gear, looking very angry. Williamson couldn’t hear the words, but this time it wasn’t due to closed ears or mind. The Disciple bent down to pick up the prone form of Amy Campbell, but her arm snaked up between his legs, going shoulder-deep with the vicious low-blow. Eyes bulging, then clamping shut to keep from popping out, Williamson collapsed onto his side as Deville shook his head, mirrored by another likeness of himself. “Pause!” The Deville ordered suddenly, sharply, bringing Williamson, Aimz, the K-Wolf, and the other Deville to statuesque stillness. He loved digital technology, all the wonders it could be used for with a little imagination and money enough to indulge it. Computers mounted in the ceiling projected the digital forms into the room like spotlights, recreating them in splendid 3-D. While Deville couldn’t interact with them – if he touched one, the reflection would merely be displaced around his hand – for every other practical purpose it was as if he was right there. It was the next level in match-study, and though Deville still watched tapes from time to time, if there was a digital recording available, watching it on a screen just wasn’t good enough. He saw so much more with this, and when he wanted to, he could examine things from angles no camera had actually caught. There was no ring structure or crowd or anything of that sort; the competitors had been isolated and extracted so they moved across the marble blue floor as if it was the ring, on a level with the projected Deville at ringside. The fake Deville flickered as the real Deville swept through him, eyes no longer locked on his fallen, nut-cupping Disciple. With a hand wrapped around an invisible rope, Karina was halfway to her feet, the barest hint of a grimace staining her porcelain face. “Slow-motion rewind,” Deville commanded as he came up beside her, stopping a foot away and watching her legs intently. Rather than sink slowly back to the floor, the K-Wolf began inching up, her hand loosening from the rope. “Pause!” he shouted again just as her right knee buckled, her eyes flaring wide to precede the wince. He hadn’t noticed it when he was actually at ringside because he was following the action, but here in his flawless re-creation it was impossible to miss. Shaking his head with frustrated affection, he bent down to stare into her gaping eyes. “Why must you lie to me, Kari?” She’d assured him the pain in her knee was nothing serious, that with her brace it never bothered her in the ring, but the proof was in the footage. He knew he shouldn’t have listened to her, knew the doctors had missed something, either as a result of her stubbornness to admit any pain or their own incompetence. Seeing it buckle like that, much as he had seen twice in her match with Aimz at Overkill, Deville knew it wasn’t just a passing pain. If a ligament in her knee wasn’t torn or at least sprained, he was a pauper. Telling his multi-million dollar equipment to play once more, Deville took a step back and watched Kari catch herself on the ropes, wondering what he could do with her. Pleading with her to take time off, pointing out that she wrestled too often for her own good, had resulted in petulant glaring and a bitten off comment about hypocrisy. “You’ve been booked and in action on every show for over two months,” she’d accused, stabbing him with a finger. “How dare you come down on me for wrestling injured when you almost pinned yourself fighting Magnum Wolf.” It wasn’t a question, but even had it been, Deville wouldn’t have answered. Telling Karina he’d been distracted instead of injured, that his body seemed to be beyond injury, wouldn’t have gone over well. Not that Deville actually believed himself invulnerable to injury and wear, but throughout his entire life the only time his bones broke were when bullets shattered them, and of those he had not even the scars for proof. His capacity for convalescence contravened common sense and medical knowledge. He wasn’t impervious to hurt, but he’d never suffered a sports-related injury, and inflicted pain rarely remained to hinder him longer than a day. Pulled out of his reverie by a grin as his reflection buried its hands in the flames of Aimz’s mane, Deville watched gleefully as she was whipped to the floor and the stomping ensued. His attention didn’t remain there, however, and wincing was hard to avoid as Karina all but broke Williamson’s neck for the second time with her dreaded and then-new manoeuvre, the C-4. Williamson looked dead. Karina, wonderfully suspicious as she was, took a moment to eye the reflected Deville before hooking the legs for the cover. After a moment, that Deville looked up and pretended to just notice what was happening, then feigned an internal war as he placed a knee on the apron and poised to enter the ring. He’d never had any intention of helping Williamson, of harming Karina, but for the sake of his scheming, he’d had to pretend. Rubbing his chin, he admired his performance. It was good, after all. The entire world had bought it, many of them deluding themselves into thinking they’d seen one of the first signs of weakness in The Deville they’d come to fear as much as they loathed and loved. “What a shame,” The Deville repeated, grinning to himself as Karina flopped off of his Disciple, victorious. ~><>…<><~ “So,” The HIT had begun, dragging out the word thoughtfully, eyeing The Deville beside him in the full-length mirror of his locker room. “I’ll be like your apprentice?” The Deville grinned, nodding. “The Anakin to my Obi Wan.” “Perfect,” The HIT had slipped without thinking, but Deville let on that he didn’t notice. “That’s exactly what I had in mind.” “Good.” More sage nodding was followed by a slap upside the afro. “But if you’re going to start to emulate a champion, you’re going to have to start looking and acting like one.” Sighing, HIT ran a hand through the mop of red tangles and looked shamefully to the floor. “It’s pathetic, isn’t it?” From a fashion standpoint, Deville agreed it certainly was. With regard to The HIT’s future success, however, it was his only hope. It wasn’t just a gimmick, it was his personality, his strength. “It certainly is. How is anyone supposed to take you seriously when they’re waiting for you to pull out a balloon and shape it into an animal? Right now you’re fit to be a clown, not wear a crown.” There was some truth to the statement, that The HIT was regarded as a sideshow, and Deville exploited it, knowing that the second best way to sabotage an enemy was to destroy their confidence, to take their insecurities and blow them out of proportion. The only better method of attack was to build up their reverence for what they’d have to fight, to make them want to emulate it, to respect it more than they did themselves. “Unless you’re a rooster trying to attract chickens,” The Deville went on disgustedly, eyeing the afro again, “that hair really needs to go. No more shopping at K-Mart for clothing, either. I’ll loan you money if you need it, but no matter what, we need to revamp your appearance. Looking like a moron impresses and intimidates no one.” The HIT frowned, but nodded his agreement. “Most importantly, we need to change your attitude and in-ring style. Your barbed-wire matches might have gone over well in the sticks, but if you ever want to be taken seriously, you’ll need to learn to wrestle. And when you’re talking down to someone, damnit, talk down to them. Don’t just lob careless insults with a wry grin; lord your superiority, make the mark feel his inferiority to his core.” Narrowed eyes flickered up to regard Deville sceptically for a moment, then whipped back to the floor. “I thought I had been,” he whispered sheepishly, a touch irritably. The Deville blinked, then took a deep breath and shrugged as if he couldn’t help the man’s ignorance. “If you don’t want my assistance . . .” “No!” A hand clamped down on Deville’s forearm, pre-empting any attempt to depart. “I do.” Their eyes locked again. “Please . . . Help me be like you.” A chuckle escaped The Deville. "I'll try . . . but let's not get carried away here. Baby steps, Mr. Trapezoid. Baby steps. I can mould you into more than you are, but it’s not going to be a quick fix. It’s going to take time. And trust." “I trust you,” The Trapezoid lied. “Good. Now pay attention.” Sauntering across the room, Deville turned on his heel and returned. “From now on, you don’t just walk anywhere. Whatever you’re doing, wherever you’re going, it’s important, and it’s going to be made infinitely more important by your presence. From now on, you swagger.” It took half an hour of strutting back and forth, interspersed with cutting laughter and further demonstrations from Deville, before The Deville would even acknowledge that The HIT knew how to walk. From the beginning of their relationship to the end, that was the sort of help the devious Disciple received from his incomparably insidious mentor. Nothing truly helpful at all, but advice meant to demean and destroy, to rebuild a once-strong sideshow as a worshipful shell of a man. ~><>…<><~ Despite his reputation, people always approached Deville offering friendship or a flattering word. The best thing about having a reputation like his was that it precluded honest people from initiating conversation. No one with pure motives wanted his attention, not to mention his allegiance, so when people came seeking it, they may as well have been slithering on their stomachs and darting their tongues experimentally. “How long are you planning on stringing Williamson along like this?” Trim, bare arms crossed over the chest of Eleanor made Angelo sigh, but the tone of her voice, so challenging and in his face, made him follow it with a grin. “I didn’t know we came to PRIME so you could carry someone through the tag ranks.” “Neither did I,” Deville allowed off-handedly, checking his blindspot before moving into the left lane to pass one of those lemmings who obeyed the speed limit. “But then Los Diablos struck and I had to strike back. Williamson injecting himself into my life, trying to deceive me . . . that was just icing on the cake.” Eleanor hummed disapprovingly. “You didn’t answer my question. How long before you actually take the cake and indulge?” Eyeing her sideways, Deville shrugged under his seatbelt. “If it bothers you that much, we can do it tonight. I had plans to have Los Diablos artificially deported, but I could easily axe that and get the backstab over with instead. I just wanted to build it up a little more, to teach Williamson his lesson on payperview.” “Do you really think he’s going to wait that l—“ Eleanor cut off with a blink, then glared at Deville as if she hadn’t heard him properly. “Wait. You’re having Los Diablos deported?” The Deville laughed, pleased with himself. “Oh yeah, it’s gonna be great. When they announce us for the match, a pair of associates of mine are going to approach Los Diablos with false papers and remove them by force when they resist.” Deville nodded into the gaping silence. “Then I’ll announce our tag team name, Manifest Destiny.” “Cute,” Eleanor murmured with a roll of her eyes, shaking her head. Grinning wryly, Deville pulled into the arena parking garage, waved through immediately by the attendant. “You have no idea.” Eleanor glared, knowing it was probably true. “Does Justin know about this?” More laughter from The Deville was a good enough answer, but he elaborated anyway. “Of course not. I’ve been building it up as our first defence, running him in circles with idiotic training and advice that a three-year-old would know was pointless.” “Isn’t he going to be pissed off?” “That’s the point.” “I can’t say I like this, Angelo,” Eleanor bit off warily, not sure how to phrase doubt in his plans. “If you push him too far, he might drop his charade sooner than you expect.” The Deville backed into a parking spot before answering, and when he did, it was with a wink. “That’s why I have you, Eleanor. To watch my back.” “Me?” Eleanor demanded incredulously. “What am I supposed to do if he attacks you?” “If he attacks me?” Deville made it sound as plausible as Williamson sprouting wings and flying to the moon. “If he does that, believe me, I’ll handle it. But here, just to ease your concerns . . . Next week, you’ll ride to the show with him and find out how close to the edge he is. Tell him I refused to give you a ride or something, that’ll get him thinking you two are on the same side.” Deville laughed again with rich amusement; relative to his former business partners, manipulating most wrestlers was as easy as a hit in a trunk. “If he’s ready to snap, I’ll flatter him and pull him back on board . . . If he’s still just biding his time, the knife falls at Overkill.” Eleanor shook her head as she unbuckled her seatbelt. “And if you’re wrong? If he isn’t planning to betray you?” The Deville laughed as he pushed his door open, sounding the bell. “I’m rarely wrong, Eleanor, and never in matters of betrayal. How do you think I’ve survived on the top this long?” ~><>…<><~ Historically, Manifest Destiny wasn’t solely an ideal. It was an excuse – a justification – for America to take what she had no business taking. Many Americans saw it as the expansion of an empire, as capturing not only land, but people, people who would become slaves. They wanted to tear down other nations and rebuild them as subservient states feeding into their whole. The parallels here are too rich to ignore and much too convenient for coincidence. If you look deeper, my Disciple, if you explore all that’s transpired, you’ll discover it isn’t coincidence at all, but cunning strategy. Our Manifest Destiny was an exercise in the bestowment of education and freedom, but it was also a battle of individual greed. Not for money or titles, or for the celebrity of being champions, but for power and influence. When people think back on our reign as champions, when they remember PRIME’s Manifest Destiny, they’re not going to remember the cohesive unit of Angelo Deville and Justin Williamson taking the world by storm. A lot of them won’t even be able to recall your name. What they’re going to remember is Angelo Deville teaching Los Diablos a lesson, with that HIT guy tagging along for the glory. They’re going to remember how you facetiously befriended me, hoping to make yourself more through my reputation and eventual downfall, hoping to make a mockery of The Deville. Most importantly, they’re going to remember how that backfired, how after destroying your personality and image and rebuilding you in a hollow re-creation of my own, I broke you in half and left you for dead. It was never your intention to genuinely be my apprentice, so instead of taking you under my wing and instructing you, I decided to teach you a lesson, much like with Los Diablos. A master cannot hope to inculcate a disingenuous apprentice, one who believes he’s already surpassed him and has no will to understand. All the master can do is demonstrate how wrong the student is in that belief. You’ve been biding your time as my partner, waiting for me to betray you so you’d have the excuse and motivation to try to dismantle me. The problem there is that I never gave you my trust or friendship in the first place. My loyalty remained where it always has, the only place it’s consistently reciprocated: with myself. I used you, Justin, to extend my dominion not only over the tag team world of PRIME, but over Los Diablos and yourself as well, and I feel the same remorse for it as the gunfighter who’s still standing at 12:01. It’s been your plan all along, your dream, to take me out, and you’ve been plotting it since before I ever laid eyes on you. In a way that’s flattering, but in another more real way, it’s disgusting. I re-made you in my image as a mockery, but you genuinely believed it would make your career to battle me, to come up through my shadow. I wasn’t lying completely when I told you your career was pathetic, that you’d been spiralling into the gutter, but my intention was to make it worse, not better. Now you don’t even know who you are, or what you stand for. You’ve been doing nothing but fetch my slippers for months now, taking my insincere advice to heart as if it meant something. You were a joke before, but at least then you had a punchline, a raison d’etre grounded in your own life. Now you’re just a marionette with cut strings. You probably think I’m upset that you finally worked up the courage to publicly do what you’ve privately designed all along. Giving me the finger when I gave you the chance to do something to prove you’re a champion probably makes you proud. The smart money says that you, in your inestimable ignorance, feel that vindicates you. Allow me to burst your bubble by telling you I don’t care, and to remind you yet again that I knew it was coming all along, that I did it to you first on a much grander scale. I cared about having those titles, but only in the sense of what their ownership implied, and whom it denied. I cared about Manifest Destiny, but it was never about us working together, or making you into more than you could be on your own. It was about the manifestation of deceit and the price that must be paid. It was about my Machiaveilian destiny. I hope you’re rested up from the pleasant ride on my coattails, Justin, because Overkill was the last stop. It’s time for you to climb off my back and fall on your own.
