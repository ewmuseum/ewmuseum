Date: ReV 215 Minneapolis, Minnesota- 12:02 a.m. 12/14/09 Johnson Carver stood at a towering average of five feet, six inches tall. On evenings like this, when he was bolstered by the thick soles of his riding boots… he might be lucky enough to claim five-nine. Hardly the tallest patron seeking refuge from this cold and blustery night. It was more likely that he was the shortest man to pony up to the grease slick bar at Jake’s in quite some time. He was not the heaviest man in the room, either. No stick by any means, but with such a height disadvantage, well, the pounds weren’t necessarily packing themselves on. Johnson was fortunate enough not to have been scarred or disfigured in what was a relatively rough life, compared to most. There were plenty of people sitting around the creaking, lopsided tables that wore more obvious badges of dishonor. One chap there in the corner with a whole quarter of his face shriveled and burned around the left side of his mouth. A half dozen or so that he could see with knife wounds of various lengths and grotesqueness. This sort of thing might have endeared him to the few leather-clad women that made Jake’s their prowling ground. Under different circumstances, of course. He was far from the most hideous of the denizens. Nor was he the most attractive. He kept his dirty brown hair just a little over shoulder length. A vain attempt to hide the heredity thinning with as much volume as possible. His facial hair grew in scraggly patches that barely managed to form a full (if not proper) beard. As a true traveler of the roads, hygiene was not the utmost of his priorities. Decades of coffee and cigarettes stained both his fingertips and teeth, though again, not nearly as bad as some of the more offensive characters watching his plight. There was nothing particularly special or appealing about Johnson Carver. He was average in most every sense of the word. Neither handsome nor hideous, he had done nothing to deserve the rapt attention of all that took an uncomfortable seat in Jake’s dimly lit and smoke-filled pub. Nothing save for the unbelievable and rather awe-inspiring ass kicking he now received. His lip was split just a hair left of the middle. There was a large knot forming just under his left eye, and while he could feel the bruise pulse with each beat of his heart… the sensation seemed distant. The left half of his head was more or less numb (which was positive in regards to the split lip, as the pain had been quite excruciating). He was disturbed by the unfamiliar leakage from his left ear. Blood more likely than not, but how it was he was bleeding from inside his ear was quite the mystery. Though, to be fair, his memory of the last few minutes was a bit fuzzy. His ribs ached, just under the lung. Every breath was like swallowing shards of glass. There was a sharp pain in his neck. Intense and constant. Made it impossible to turn his head at all. For all that, though, Johnson Carver could concentrate on just one thing. The most bizarre and strangely fascinating development of the evening. His knee. More specifically, his patella. He could feel it sort of… floating there inside his leg. Completely unattached. He did not feel the pain as he thought he should. The injury itself was such a rare oddity that whatever anguish such a malady would cause had been replaced almost entirely by a morbid curiosity. He had no idea such a thing was even possible. It should be noted that Johnson’s face was not quite as numb as might have been inferred. True enough, he did not feel pain on a general basis. When, however, the point of an elbow came sweeping across his jaw, well… he could feel that. It struck first just in front of his ear. Right around the spot where the jawbone attached to the rest of his skull. There was a screeching sound in his ear. The sound of bone grinding against bone. He felt his jaw drop maybe half a centimeter or so. Not much, but the pain… Oh the pain. The elbow continued through its original trajectory, undeterred by the unfortunate placement of his face within its path. It stuck true and hard to his flesh. Scraping the bone beneath and dragging the skin across his cheek. When the flesh could stretch no more, it split, opening a wide gash just a half inch from the corner of his mouth. The point of the elbow was stuck hard against his jaw, pulling and tearing as it swooped downward. His head had no real choice but to follow. The momentum. The power. It just pulled him along for the ride. Down and to the right. Though the elbow followed completely through, now beyond his torn face and swiping through the smoggy air, the vicious energy of the blow still persisted. It was this inertia that continued to pull Johnson downward. Threw him off balance. His head and shoulders tilted in one direction. The shifting weight pulled him off his left leg for just a second. His natural reaction was to plan the foot firmly and reestablish balance. This would have worked… had it not been for that whole floating patella thing. The instinctive movement did keep him from falling forward. As soon as he planted the sole of his boot to the dust-covered floor, his body righted itself for just long enough to cease the momentum. Without the support of his kneecap, however, he almost literally had no leg to stand on. His leg buckled and now Johnson Carver found himself stumbling backward instead of falling forward. He staggered for a handful of steps. His arms spun windmills in valiant but vain attempts to regain control. The last thing he saw was the darkened silhouette of his assailant as it floated in his direction. ********** There are moments in life for which there is no compare. This moment, for example. The two or three seconds it takes to cross the distance between himself and the target. The weightless feeling as he sails through the air. The tension and anticipation of inevitable collision. The fear and confusion that hangs like a horrid shadow on the face of his victim. It is a moment that Tyler Rayne relishes every single time he experiences it. Like all moments, though, this one too must end. There is a hesitation in the moment itself. As if the entire world took pause to watch the proceedings. He moved through the air in slow motion. Able to appreciate each of this moment’s peculiar and satisfying factors. Then his knees make contact with the victim’s sternum. The world comes rushing back to full speed in violent and excessive fashion. He can feel the snap beneath his knees. The collapse of the sternum. The force of impact is so great that it actually repels Tyler just a bit. No more than an inch or two. It is enough for him to land on sure footing, though. The momentum of the human missile that was Tyler Rayne is transferred to the diminutive stranger. From the knees to the chest… which snaps in two under the pressure of such concentrated power. The energy continues to dissipate in a wave that ripples through the man’s body. These waves lift him from the floor. He is thrown back by the explosion. All five feet and six inches of him crash against the old jukebox. His back bends at an odd angle. Entire body contorted into an unnatural bow. Glass shatters and showers about the wood planks upon which he is soon to fall. Machinery groans from within the jukebox. Something clicks. The man slumps. His head lulls and he slides down the front of the music machine. Unconscious. A whining sound emits from behind him. A drum beat is soon to follow. Tyler waits for just a moment. He looks down on his victim and watches. The man responds with a single twitch of his finger. Involuntary. Tyler leaves his eyes locked on the crumpled mass of man, but his attention is turned on everything but. There is no sense of tension permeating the air. Only the stale stench of too many cigarettes. He can feel the stares of the various ruffians gathered here… but there is no concern. Perhaps a bit of curiosity, but nothing more. This was unfortunate. He had picked the wrong mark. He turns, running a hand through the mess of his dark hair. His usual shadow of stubble was growing dangerously close to a full-on beard. There were blood stains on his knuckles. Spatters up the backs of his hand. A thin line of crimson across the bridge of his nose. None of it his. Tyler stalks up to the bar. The men there are kind enough to give him a bit of room. The bartender keeps a watchful eye on, without a doubt, the most handsome man in the room. “Shot a Jack.” Look around. Everywhere you turn is heartache. The bartender is a tall, sinewy fellow. Six and a half feet. Lucky if he pushes one hundred and eighty pounds. Stringy hair and a big, misshapen nose. His dark eyes sunk deep into his skull. Goatee about five inches long. Braided. It’s everywhere that you go. “That’s a helluva show you put on there. So what’d he do? Fuck your girl?” Now Tyler Rayne has known exactly what he was going to do since before he took his first step toward the bar. The fact that the bartender would mention “his girl” however, well, that would just make things a whole lot bloodier. His fist flexed. Once. Ball close. Palm open. He quietly took a deep breath and suppressed the urge to lunge at the bartender immediately. Instead he concentrates very hard on holding the shot glass without breaking it. You try, everything you can to escape, the pain of life that you know He downs the shot and asks for another. “Didn’t know ‘im from Adam. You talk about my girl again… it’ll be you ends up in a puddle next to the jukebox.” He downs the second shot. He does not ask for a third. He does, however, keep a very firm grip on the glass. The bartender scoffs. His palms press down against the grime of the countertop. His arms stiffen. Tyler does not look directly at the bartender. He does not look directly at anyone. He watches the mirror behind the bar through the corner of his eye. He takes note of the three men with rapt attention. I know a place where you can get away “Listen here you little cock sucker. I could give a rat’s ass you wanna beat up on the midget there. Don’t think that intimidates anyone here, pretty boy. This is my bar, and I’ll talk about whoever the fuck I want. Including your whore of a girl.” Two shots in less than a minute. It could be enough to put a little buzz in almost any man’s head. It could even be reason enough for Tyler Rayne to fall off his barstool at this very moment. Though it is far more likely he only wants it to look that way. It is certainly more of a slide than a fall, but you’d have to be paying very close attention to notice that. Tyler reaches up and snatches a handful of the bartender’s stringy hair. He jerks down. Hard. His own strength added to the pull of gravity brings the bartender’s bulbous nose into quick and direct contact with his grease-covered bar top. The nose is flattened. Smashed and broken. Let your body go with the flow The three men closest to Tyler were the ones who tensed along with the bartender. They are the most likely to retaliate. Beating one man, regardless of how small or large, would not be enough to impress such a sordid clientele. Tyler had hoped that bloodying the smallest man in the room would bring some peers to his aid. Unfortunately for both of them, the little midget didn’t have a single friend in the bar. The bartender, though… a place like this was bound to build up a large base of regulars. Men who would share a loyalty, perhaps even a friendship, with the owner of such an establishment. Or at the least, the man who provided them with liquor. You. Know. You. Can do it. Tyler drops to a crouch on the floor. He kicks his leg out in a sweep that knocks the barstool out from under the closest possible retaliator. He rises as the unfortunate drunkard falls, planting a knee squarely against the man’s temple. The poor fool is out on the floor before he even knows he’s in a fight. The other two, previously sitting to Tyler’s left at the bar, are more aware of the circumstances. They have placed about three or four feet between each other. Both of them are large men. Over six feet. Well around two-fifty, maybe even three hundred pounds. All you need, is your own imagination. So use it. That’s what it’s for. The closest of the two grabs Tyler from behind. Big meaty paws dropping down on both shoulders. He is strong. Very strong. He throws Tyler in the direction of his compatriot. Tyler tightens his fist, crushing a shot glass within his grasp. Painful? Yes. The tiny shards dig deep into his skin. The dust and glass powder infiltrates his blood stream through the smallest of lacerations. Stinging. Burning. The fist the size of a bowling ball buries itself deep within his abdomen. The air is blown from his lungs. This, too, is pain. Blunt force trauma at its finest. You know what’s more painful, though? Having a man with a palm full of glass slap you right in the eye. There is no proper description for the kind of agony that goes along with having your eye sliced in half. There is no proper description for the banshee-like wail a grown man can loose when inflicted with such tragedy. You’re a superstar. Yes. That’s what you are. Tyler presses the advantage. The sole of his boot plants firm and square against the man’s knee. The larger man collapses forward. Tyler’s own knee rises to meet him. The point connects flush and clean with the forehead. Unconscious before he hits the floor. The remaining combatant announces his impending onslaught with an outraged roar. He charges like an unstoppable juggernaut. Tyler waits a full second before falling yet again. Down to his knees. He tucks a shoulder at just the right moment, undercutting the bruiser at the thigh. Tyler shrugs, shifting his balance from one shoulder to the next. The motion gives the juggernaut’s momentum a direction, and the man topples feet over head across Tyler’s shoulders. He crashes to the planked floor. Tyler spins out what would normally be a sweep kick. Instead, his heel connects flush with the man’s jaw. The crack can be heard even over the music. Tyler stands, looks down at the stunned and scarred grimace looking up at him. He puts the lights down with a single stomp of his military issue. “Now that I have your attention.” He speaks to no one in particular. He looks at no one in particular. Much like before, he stares down at his victims… attention everywhere but. Now he feels it. Awe. Fear. Confusion. There is a tension in the air. This is a moment in and of itself. Two seconds pass while every person in the room debates between the flight or fight response. Not just where you bump and grind it “Back in May, there was a wrestling show took place here. Now there were a couple dozen bikers had a hand in the proceedings. Helped break my legs. Killed me, even. I figure at least a few of them were local boys. Might’ve been drafted from a place just like this.” Tyler reaches into his jacket and pulls out a manila envelope. He tosses it on the nearest table. There is a woman there. Forties. Short red hair. Face more worn than her leather jacket. He gives her a single nod and, reluctantly, she opens the envelope. Inside are twenty-four different photographs. Still images taken from the UltraViolence archives. Each one of them a member of the biker entourage that helped Devin Shakur and Christian Daniels on that oh so infamous evening. “One of you knows one of them. Of this I am absolutely positive. Now that you have seen what I am capable of… let my intentions be made clear. Someone has three minutes to speak up. Give me a name. A real name. Give me an address. Give me something I can use to find these mother fuckers… and I’ll go on about my evening. For every three minutes that passes without what I want… I will randomly pick one of you sorry sacks of shit to leave in a pile next to the others.” To her credit, she is fast. She’s up from the table and after him before the last word is out of his lips. Her wrist snaps open the switchblade and she is on him quicker than anyone in this room has moved yet. Fast is just not fast enough. He takes a single step to the side, dodging the initial knife lunge. His hand finds the back of her head and he shoves her forward. Her own momentum does most of the work. She hits the edge of the bar chest first. It takes a second for her to catch her breath. By then he has already bounced her head off the greasy top. He pulls the switchblade from her hand. Takes a moment to admire the blade… and then slams it down on the bar top. Through the back of her palm. She screams. He shrugs and turns back to the captive audience. Pun intended. “The cunt just bought you another three minutes. Make ‘em count.” Don’t just stand there, let’s get to it.
