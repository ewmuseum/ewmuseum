The Road to Redemption: Decisions in Life

7 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 7, 2008 Marquis Peeples needed to make a deal, bad. He was supposed to meet up with the Sandman in the morning, expecting his money in full. Marquis knew full well what would happen if he didn’t have the money, and even if it took him all night he was going to make sure that did not happen. It was now two in the morning, and he only needed one more deal. A couple hundred more and he would have the money he needed. He could stop selling drugs, and focus on school in order to be the first in his family to graduate. His mother would be so proud. The only problem was, there was no one to be found. Almost all the other dealers were done for the night. Hell, even the hookers had turned in for the evening. There wasn’t a trick, a mark, or any generalized forms of lowlife to be seen. It was close to three-thirty in the morning when Marquis noticed someone approaching him. The guy's hair was frazzled, and his skin was pale and blotchy. His eyes shot rapidly from side to side, as if he was watching a game of Olympic Ping Pong. This young man was so paranoid that Marquis could practically see the monkey hanging on his back. “Yo man, what’s up,” Marquis asked with a nod. “What you need?” “I...I need,” the hesitation in the guy's voice, and the way his hands shook when he talked, brought a smile to Marq's face. “I'm hurting, man.” “I got what you need, bro,” Marq replied with a smirk. “It’s gonna cost you twenty though.” “Yeah, man, whatever,” fidgeting like an epileptic, the addict nods really fast. “I… I just need it.” The man was twitching nervously, impeding his every movement. He reached into his left front pocket with great effort, removing a crumpled up wad of cash. He handed them to Marquis, who counted them twice rapidly. Marq grinned wide as he stuffed the handful of ones into his pocket. “Tight, dog. This what you need?” Marquis produced a tiny bag of white powder from his pant pocket. The junkie’s eyes stopped shifting as soon as he pulled it out. At first, Marquis thought the man was just happy to have his fix. “That’s just what I wanted,” It was the sudden change in his demeanor and the way his posture straightened that wiped the smile off of Marq's face. The man's hands stopped shaking, and he began to smile maliciously. Marquis realized that he was in serious trouble, and when the man pulled out a wallet with a badge in it; he nearly messed his pants. “Detective Thomas, narcotics.” A siren suddenly sounded around the corner. “You have the right to remain...” Marquis was running before the detective could even start. “Freeze!” detective Thomas screamed as he broke into a sprint behind Marquis. “Son of a bitch.” Marquis had always been pretty street smart, but that was not what he needed to be right now. Right now, he needed to be an athlete. There was a high chance Marq could have escaped, but the officers coming around the corner in the cruiser caught him off guard. His hands braced against the hood, his eyes wide as the two police officers stared out at him from their seats. A hand gripped Marquis’ shoulder before the detective pulled his arm behind his back, locking it in the small of his back as he took him to the ground. Marquis felt his shoulder get knocked out of place as soon as it made contact with the concrete curb. His skull bounced off the pavement with a sickening thud, sending a sensation of nausea surging through his nervous system. He was drowning in an ocean of pain, and eventually passed out. ---- Marquis woke up in a holding cell at the downtown precinct. His meeting with the Sandman had probably passed, and news on his arrest had surely reached him by now. Someone always had their ear to the ground, and something like this would not be missed. He sat up quickly, but quickly dropped back to the cot in a spiral of dizziness and confusion. Marquis crawled to the toilet and began to throw up. Despite his pain, all he could think of is how the Sandman was going to react to this. He had lost his drugs, lost his money, and those were two things you did not do if you valued your life. That was one thing the Sandman made sure Marquis knew, and now…now he was going to have to figure out how to survive. The same detective from the night before approached the cell and banged his coffee cup across the bars. The dull, metallic thud echoed in Marquis’ ears, sending him back to the toilet for another round of stainless steel tongue kissing. “Get up, Peeples,” the detective said with a smile. “You still have to be printed and photographed.” “Don't I get a phone call?” Marquis asked, his fingers dabbing at the sore spot on the side of his head. “Later.” Marquis dragged himself from the cot, stumbling like a drunkard from last call. He was fingerprinted, the ink staining his fingers and marking him like a biblical sinner. He could feel the ink run from his fingertips into his bloodstream, poisoning him forever. He was a criminal, despite his best efforts to convince himself otherwise. Marquis was allowed to call his mother after his mug shot had been taken. He simply told his mother where he was, what had happened, and that he would see her soon. Marquis didn’t bother to ask for bail money. He knew they had none. For twenty hours Marquis lay in his bed staring at the ceiling. He couldn’t sleep, because he had to use the bathroom. He couldn’t bring himself to use the toilet, fearing a latent venereal disease still lay dormant on it’s seat. They still hadn’t bothered to clean the vomit off of the side and the smell filled his lungs to the point he could still taste it in his throat. He began to cry. “Peeples!” someone yelled loud enough to wake him from his self pity. “Get up, your bails been posted. Marquis sprung up, equally shocked and relieved. “What? By who?” “You can see for yourself,” the cop said with a smile. “He’s waiting down the hall.” Marquis began to panic. At first, he thought his brother DeAngelo went and did something stupid to get the money, and then he realized that not even Dee would be that stupid. Right now, Marquis was the only criminal in the family. He stood up and left the cell, heading toward the waiting room. Once there, he saw his mysterious benefactor. The man stood at about six and a half feet, his white tee hanging low to about mid thigh. Surely this had to be one of the Sandman’s men, here to pick him up and drop him into the Gulf with a pair of cement shoes. “Marquis Peeples?” the man asked, sizing him up as he walked into the room. “Yeah,” Marq replied hesitantly. “I know you?” “You the boy that plays linebacker for Lincoln High?” “What is this about?” Marq was getting even more nervous by the second. “Your mother came and talked to me,” the man said. “I don't have the time to explain everything to you right now, but I want you to believe me when I tell you that I have an offer that you would be stupid to pass up.” Marquis joins the man at the table. “I'm listening.” “The man extended his hand across the table. “My name is Tyrell Dawkins.”
