Date: 8/23/06 The plane ride back was long and arduous. It had taken not only a lot out of Titan's PRIME schedule, but a great deal out of him personally. It just didn't seem right. But there it was. The man that had taken a personal interest in Titan while he was in boot camp was gone. Sgt. Pummel, ace with an M-16, able to outrun men half his age and go twice as far. And there he was, lying in a casket with the flag of Israel draped over him because an anonymous rocket flew out of a contested zone and hit the window of his living room. It wasn't just the shock of his death. It was more, in fact, in that the request that Titan act as one of the pallbearers for the funeral. Inside, he'd been numb. Hoisting the steel casket wasn't hard, not sharing the burden with five others, but it still weighed on him, now, days later. He sat on the plane in the first class seat, the only ones he could fit in, his mind churning. Next to him, Stephanie dozed, her head leaned on his arm and her small, slim arms wrapped around his elbow. She had insisted on coming, to help him deal with it, she said. He didn't tell her that he hadn't needed help before; it would start the same almost-fight over again. She said he had incredible psychological problems. He outright denied it. Sure, the problems from seeing so much happen would weigh heavily on someone's mind, but he wasn't developing problems because of it. She sat up, blinking away sleep behind her glasses. She didn't wear them much, but since this was a redeye flight, she didn't want to fool with her contacts. He gently patted her hands with a massive paw and leaned his head back. “Hey, how are you?” she asked him quietly. “I'm fine,” he said, closing his eyes. “Are you sure?” “Yes.” “I think you're not telling me the truth.” He lifted his head and turned to look at her. It was the same thing again. His face openly betrayed his annoyance, he was too tired to hide it. She looked back at him, her face expectant but not angry or irritated. “What makes you think that?” he asked her softly. “Because no one can be fine after having someone like that die,” she replied matter-of-factly. “You can't be fine because you haven't been yourself since you got the news.” “I'll be fine,” he replied shortly. “I want you to be better than that.” “What's that supposed to mean?” “You carry around so much in your heart,” she said, running a soft hand down his hairy forearm. “Things from your time in the service where you had to deal with so much and just keep going. You don't have to do that anymore, you can get that all out of you. I want you to be happier and I think that would go a long way.” “Stephanie,” he began. Her scoff and turn of her head stopped him short. “What was that for?” he asked quickly. “It's the same thing. I care about you, but you can be so stubborn sometimes.” She unwrapped her arms and turned herself to face him. “Listen to me. I saw the episode at your parents' house. They've told me about more.” “They have?” Then it hit him. “They?” His eyes widened. “Yes, I went to visit once by myself. Your father was there, too. You've had attacks in the middle of the night and destroyed the room you were in. They told me about it.” The big man immediately clammed up. He wouldn't say anything, he wouldn't acknowledge the problem. She looked at him, evenly, and it was a wonder to him that he could be matched by this 5'2, 100-odd pound redhead. He looked away, taking a deep breath. The odd thing about this exchange was that the in-flight movie was a lesser-known one called Finding Forester. On the screen played the scenes, but the old lady in front of them had her headphones turned up entirely too much, and a particular song drifted back. It's a Hawaiian medley of Somewhere Over the Rainbow and It's a Wonderful World. I hear babies cry and I watch them grow,They'll learn much moreThan we'll knowAnd I think to myselfWhat a wonderful world (w)oohoorld Someday I'll wish upon a star,Wake up where the clouds are far behind meWhere trouble melts like lemon dropsHigh above the chimney top that's where you'll find meOh, Somewhere over the rainbow way up highAnd the dream that you dare to, why, oh why can't I? The big man shakes his head, fighting the tears that form in his eyes. “Damn it all,” he mutters, trying to quickly wipe them away. Stephanie's gentle touch turns his head. Her eyes are filled with concern, totally devoid of the look from the discussion just moments past. “You hear that song?” he asks quietly. She nods, allowing him to continue. “I hate those songs.” He swallows against the lump in his throat before going on. “They're wishes for a place that should exist, but it doesn't. They have to wish for a place that's so far away, or it only looks at certain small things to make the world beautiful. The world isn't like that. It's hard, cold, callous and uncaring. It's why I was trained, honey. I was made to be one of the people that tries to hold the line against all the hell in the world against those beautiful things, so that people can still hope for that perfect world. And I hate it, hate it all. I hate the bad that is and the good that won't ever be.” “Oh, Hunter,” she says softly. She used his name in such a voice that it made him fight even harder against threatening tears. “No, no, baby. You don't anymore. You aren't part of that anymore.” “I am,” he says quietly. “I am,” he insists against the shake of her head. “It didn't stop when I left the service, babygirl. It still goes on. That's the whole reason that I got in Ivan's way. I couldn't stop myself. I had to do something to stop him from hurting people that didn't do anything.” He takes a deep breath. “I beat him once, but now he's totally focused on me. And I have to again, or no one will be able to stop him.”
