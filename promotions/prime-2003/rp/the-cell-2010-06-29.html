The Cell

29 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/29/10 The cell was cold, dank and small. A narrow shaft of moonlight crept through the high, barred window and served as the only source of illumination. The stench of raw sewage from the overflowing toilet lingered in the air along with alcohol, must, and the unpleasant smell of stale cigarettes. The graffiti amongst the chipped blue paint on the walls created a portrait of hopelessness and despair. Although there is despair, a leaky faucet produces a pleasant melody in the silence. While coughing, Adam Garcia opened his brown eyes and blinked a few times trying to adjust his sight to the darkness around him. A sharp pain shot through his head and partnered with a dull ache that was already present from way too much alcohol. He realized that he was using the wall for a pillow amongst the hard floor and struggled to sit up. He gently stroked the knot on the back of his head comfortingly, and blinked once more to clear his vision. “Ugh,” he moaned as he rubbed his slender tan digits through his dark extensions. “Not again.” He rose to his feet and peering into the darkness, he saw a shadow move. “Hello,” he cried as he motioned toward the figure. “Is anyone there?” There was no response. He felt his knees beginning to buckle and stumbled on to a nearby bench. The room was spinning out of control… wait corkscrewing, and Adam had a headache the size of Nebraska. He dropped his head into his hands and sank into a stupor of self-pity and dejection. Unpleasant memories began to bubble up to the surface of his mind, dark memories he knew he would be better off without. “What is going on,” he asked of himself. “How could I allow this to happen again? I’m better than this.” Panic surged through him as he noticed the room’s fourth wall; a row of iron bars. Nervously, he began to rock back and forth on the worn rickety metal bench biting his fingernails down to the skin. He chuckled briefly and then grimaced. “This can’t be happening,” he thought to himself. “There is just no way. I’ve got to be dreaming.” He mustered his eyes open again and glared at his surroundings once more. “What happened last night,” he questioned himself. He could not recall the events that led him there and began to panic slightly. Adam could feel the stone walls closing in on him and it made it hard for him to breathe. His palms dripped with sweat and his teeth chattered in terror. He was so anxious that he wanted to pull the locks right out his head. He pinched himself but he felt the pain and there was no waking up. This was no dream, and so he decided to scream. “HELP!” “Shh,” someone immediately hissed. “Go to bed and quit making all that NOISE,” snapped the voice again which came from the other side of the room. Startled, Adam spun around and focuses his eyes on the larger figure that approached before him. He planted his back against the wall and clasped the bottom of the bench in fright. “Don’t cha’ see me tryna’ sleep here?” Adam cleared his throat and swallowed a huge lump. “I-I didn’t mean to disturb you,” he stuttered. “I’m sorry, really. I’m just trying to find out where I am. Please don’t hurt me.” The man came into the moonlight and stopped dead in his tracks. Giggling slightly, he scratched his bald brown head in bewilderment and asked, “hurt you? Child, wake up and smell the roses.” Clad in black business slacks and a white dress shirt which had been unbuttoned at the collar and rolled at the sleeves, the larger black man stood over Garcia and pointed to the other end of the cell. “Don’t cha’ see the huge bars over there,” he asked. “We’re in jail. I damn sure ain’t gonna try and hurt you in here.” The man chuckled once more and loosened his tie a few more inches. At least they were only in holding, that was a good sign. Meanwhile, Adam squeezed his eyes to keep his head from exploding and released his anxiety with a big breath of relief. His intoxication was beginning to wear off but he knew that he would soon be in for a rude awakening. “I understand that,” Adam mustered up a reply. “But what am I doing here?” “Beats the hell out of me,” the man shrugged and backed off without causing a fuss. “Fuck I look like? Cleo the psychic? In all honesty, I thought you were dead. You’ve been laid out like that for some time now. Musta’ been some kinda’ wild party, huh?” His eyebrows rose up and down as his fingers traced quotation marks in the air. Adam’s cheeks turned beet red amongst his hairy face in embarrassment. Not knowing what else to do, he stroked his long, thick mustache in deep thought. “Haha,” Garcia chuckled nervously. “I honestly couldn’t tell you,” he finally replied. “I don’t remember too much of anything at the moment. But since I’m drunk, it had to be a very special occasion. I haven’t had a drink in over five years.” Adam shook his head in disappointment, signed and looked down at the black jeans that fit loosely over his waist. His snug white t-shirt clung to his body as if he’d taken a shower with his clothes on. No obviously clues as to where he’d been moments, hours, or even days before. “I’m Adam by the way,” Garcia introduced himself after finally regaining some sort of composure. The bear of a man extended his hand and smiled. “Nice ta meet cha’,” he replied. “I’m Jamal but my friends call me ‘Blue.’ Haha… don’t ask why.” “Look Jamal, I’m terribly sorry,” Adam began to explain. “This is all new to me and I’m still trying to take it all in. I’ve never been in trouble with the law before and I just don’t understand where I went wrong.” Although Blue found it hard to believe he nodded his head and sat down next to Adam. He draped one of his massive arms around Adam’s shoulders and said, “No need to apologize. I get it.” Blue wiped a bead of sweat from his thick brow and continued. “Besides, I’m much more worried about you, knowing your situation and all.” Adam tried to piece the events from the night together but nothing came to mind. He also wondered why he was highly intoxicated. Being a recovering alcoholic and working so hard to stay sober for years, he felt disappointed in himself. “So Blue,” Adam quipped through his pale lips trying to spark up a conversation. “What’re you in for?” “Funny story actually,” came Blue’s reply. “I violated a restraining order,” he replied whilst shaking his head. “Being a lawyer I should really know better, huh?” Blue could feel Adam looking down upon him and hung his head in shame. “I know. Sounds stupid, right?” Adam immediately interrupted, “No, no not at all. Stupid is not knowing why you’re in here to begin with.” The two laughed as heartily as their bodies would allow. Meanwhile, trouble reared its ugly head next door. “Will you two ingrates knock it off and let us get some sleep,” an inmate from the opposing cell muttered in a raspy voice, half-sleep, half-awake, and all-angry. “Jesus fucking Christ, it’s the middle of the night assholes. What on earth could you be chit-chatting about at a time like this?” Adam’s glazed over drunken eyes glared out at an old man who rammed his forehead into the bars of the cell. His short, gray hair was plaited in tiny braids and his wrinkled porcelain skin which hung helplessly over fragile bones clued him to be at least sixty. It looked like he hadn’t eaten in months. Someone throw that man a biscuit! Adam humbly apologized. “We’ll keep it down. Sorry sir.” “Fuck that,” Blue immediately chimed in. “Don’t mind “Big” Tom. He’s usually cranky this time of night.” Upon rising to his feet, Blue walked over to Tom’s cell. “Tom, why don’t cha’ take yo old crabby ass back to sleep!” “…Big,” Adam questioned himself. Tom rose a blue veined fist and shook it violently in the air. “How’s about I come over there and stick my foot up your ass you big oaf!” “I dare you,” Blue shouted and lunged forward grabbing Tom by the throat. Tom’s slender body rammed forward into the iron bars of the cell. Adam jumped up from the bench in haste and pulled Blue away from Tom. Upon release, Tom slid to the ground and coughed for breath. “Blue, get a HOLD of yourself!” “No,” Blue refused. “Get off me. Lemme at him.” Blue punched Adam square in the face, and pushed him back hard sending him crashing to the cell floor before leaping toward Tom and the other cell. Immediately as if God himself were responding, “CUT,” came from the director’s weary voice. “That’s a wrap people.” Everyone in the room clapped and cheered causing Adam to blush slightly. The other two actors bowed themselves as the bright lights cued and brought some illumination to the room. Jamal extended his hand to Adam and pulled him to his feet dusting him off a bit. “You alright?” Adam nodded as Jamal walked off. “Well I’ll be damned Adam. Didn’t know you had that in you,” the director praised. “You really got into that last scene. Dixie Normas was right about you after all,” he chuckled and playfully punched Adam in the shoulder. “You have a bright future in this industry. We’ll pick up from here tomorrow. Pack it up people!” As the camera crew began to pack up the equipment Adam headed for the dressing rooms. Waiting for him was Dixie Normas former manager of PRIME’s the Trust Fund. Without letting her get a word out Adam said, “I quit. I can’t take playing these pussy characters anymore. This Hollywood thing isn’t for me… I got a call from PRIME’s headquarters this afternoon and I’m going to take the job offered there.” “But,” Dixie tried to interrupt but Adam intervened with a kiss to the lips. “I’ll see you at home, hun.” He said before walking off into the darkness. His career in show business may had been over, but his second coming in PRIME was just about to begin.
