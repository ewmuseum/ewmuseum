To The Place I Call Home

23 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1 - 23 - 06 In the corner beside my window There hangs a lonely photograph There is no reason I'd never notice A memory that could hold me back This is interesting. My whole life turning into a transition from the good, to the bad, into the nostrils of the ugly, and back towards euphoric. I'm finding unquestionable meaning and light. It appears the fog may have cleared from my windshield and the roads are no longer icey and uncontrollable. I'm within my own utopia and at the tip of my ship, sailing the wastelands and wreckage that scatter around me. I can envision the war inside of my head but it seems to be halting more and more with every day that goes by. I can't rest inside this coffin anymore. I have to break out and be free and be my own person. Don't surround yourself with yourself,Move on back two squares. My objective of existance doesn't include the humorless slump; that which clings to me like a fungus who serves an attraction towards me. It's an attraction I can no longer hide from anymore, an attraction that burns like fire and has a dangerous scent of moldy sickness. So, in an attempt to seek out the glory hole of escape, I'm doing things the way I personally see fit. This isn't about buckling down and being an innocent man, no, I wasn't brought into this world to fucking be innocent. I was brought here to cause an uproar of controversy. I was here to commit unholy acts of carelessness, if only to bring people to their knees in excitement and suspense. That's me. That wavering well is, but was never meant to be, dry. It's time for everyone to experience the pure nucleus that is Ozric Mortimer; Nicholas motherfucking Brocken. Stark determinationPoisoning the soulUnfettered beast insideClaiming sovereign control The enemies are there. I can sense them surrounding me and feasting on the fear that seaps out of me. The darkness chants lullabies, calming me for the set up of the century to destroy whatever I have left within, and thrashing into me like leather clad pillows slapping repeatedly against my kneecaps. I refuse to let out a sigh of resignation, afterall, this is merely a test of resistance. How long can I continue to walk the path of immortality until I finally lose my tracking and slip into oblivion? When will this congestion finally take its fateful toll on me? The fact is, I don't know and I'm not sure if I want to know. There's plenty of reasons why I've gotten by as long as I have; reasons I'm a little unsure of, but determined to fully understand. If you think about it, I'm a skin-covered, oxygen-breathing capsule stuck on this blue speck inside a vast universe of creation; why have I been stricken with this madness? Looking through my windowI seem to recognizeAll the people passing byBut I am aloneAnd far from homeAnd nobody knows me Strangers stare at me like I'm somekind of misunderstood victim who's had his mouth duct-taped shut. I hide inside these shady motels and befriend old television sets and mattresses that support my wiery head. I don't have unlimited resources of friends to turn to when I've lost all focus of sanity. I don't have a family to visit and have dinner with and women? They're so fucking disconnected from me now that I barely know what they even look like nowadays. Those who I grew close to either killed themselves, were comitted, or are behind prison bars. I even got to know a female palestinian inside a Bickford's while on tour with PRIME, but she became very intoxicated and announced she had a belt of dynamite strapped on her underneath her coat. I through a punch, knocking her out, and the bartender called the police. Needless to say, I was a heroic stranger and she was deported. So, needless to say, it never ends and the dementia grows no matter what good I try to do at times. I mean, I did punch a girl -- But for all the right reasons, though, I think. Speak to me now and the world will crumble Open a door and the moon will fall All of your life, all your memories Go to your dreams, forget it all When I think back to the days where I lived an ordinary life, I grow nauseous and curbed. The people who live their lives like some bland infantry routine. You wake up to the buzzing of an alarm clock, bust the alarm clock, run into the shower drowsy and disconnected, get dressed, go to work, come home, eat, clean up, go to bed, repeat. We live like repetitive drones in a world where there's no such thing as the 'obsolete'. Everything has its very own public eye. You've got homeless people boxing eachother on the Internet for a sack of KFC drumsticks. You've got lawyers flaunting their theory of law all over the television. You've got idiots jumping on professional soccer and football fields just so they can get on T.V. Everyone WANTS to have this public effect of appreciation, shock value, and disgust. Whether I'm a clown, dysfunctional, insane, irate, or continuously ambiguous -- it's never a tool to push me farther into the queue of the public eye. Fuck the public eye. It's nothing but an excuse to unearned fame, like Anna Kornikova. White faced, haggard grinThis serenity painted deathWith a halo of bitter diseaseBlack paragon in lingering breath To cap everything, the quintessential moment in my life will be when I can see past the heap of shit I've been climbing through; where I can get to the top of this rugged mountain and see the tops of trees, the intricate landscape of highways, parks, and bodies of water. The quintessential moment in my life is when I can raise my arms above my head, close my eyes, breath in a morsel of fresh air, and scream at the apex of my lungs: 'I did it.' When I opened the door to my very first locker room, my life's clock re-set itself and started from square one. As if I moved in circles for twenty years, I was thrown back to where it all began. This time, however, wasn't hazy or coagulated. The pieces of my incredibly complex puzzle were directly in front of me, prepared to be put together. From this moment onward, I would face the test of time, head to head, in the biggest war I've ever been put through. So, point the gun and pull the trigger because the shadow of my former self, I refuse to let it linger. I. Home Bitter Home In most ways, this was an effort to bring back the family I've lost on so many occasions. The drive to the broken home of a recluse mother, a father who blew himself away with a shotgun, and a brother who disapeared years ago. My mother, just recently, found another bloke at some shady bar down the street, or so she said on her monthly message on my answer machine. For what I've heard, he's a construction worker with a couple of teenaged kids living in Florida. This pleases my mother. I repeat, this pleases my mother. The thing with her is that she thinks that I've also disapeared. It appears to her that I've decided to take a couple steps away from one of her welcoming hugs. I've 'abandoned' her after seeing my father take his own life and my brother wander off into a forrest marked as private property while rolling on ectasy. Now, I was the one trying, and emotionally losing myself in the process, to get him away from the woods, which are in the back of the old house, but he insisted that there was something in there of interest, dire interest. I remember hearing someone chuckle within the darkness of the primevil and that got my brother into such a tangle of joy. Kyle: 'We gotta go in there! Johnny's calling for me..' Nick: 'Who's Johnny? I'm not going in there, Ky. Just come and hang out with me inside the house.' Kyle: 'The house? Nick, man, I ain't going back in that house ever. I've got plans.. I've got plans, all right?' I could remember bear-hugging him, in an attempt to keep him from running into the mysterious jungle of trees. This incredibly euphoric smile painted across his face, Kyle wanted nothing more than to get away from a father who teased him because he didn't turn out the way he wanted him to. I was there for him every time he was heckled by Dad because I knew he would've done the same for me had I taken his shit. My knuckles had turn bare and white and the cold from the Winter season had me an uproar of shivers. My brother, who was running around in the nine inches of snow, was wearing nothing but a tank-top, beige cargo shorts, and a pair of slippers without socks protecting his feet from the cold. At the time, I couldn't relate to what Kyle was dealing with because he was five years older than me and experiencing things I barely even understood at the time. I started to get nervous that he was going to snap at my refusal to let him trot into the unknown, afterall, he was on a severe drug unless he was lying to me. Kyle was a pathological fabricator, and he loved doing it. This time, however, was much different than most of his 'Boy who cried wolf' episodes. He was certainly on ectasy, and I could tell because of his pupils, and the fact that he was severely grinding his teeth -- I mean, you could hear it from a hundred feet away if you listened hard enough. He would suddenly stop what he was doing and focus on one object for a couple of minutes and then suddenly lose all sense of concentration by collapsing into the snow and rolling around. Our father found him like this in the basement and he slammed him against the wall a couple of times before throwing him out of the house and shouting to 'never come back again'. Kyle: 'The answers are in there, man, donch' you know this, man?' Nick: 'Sure.. Sure, I do, Kyle. Why don't you come inside with me and have some water and we'll talk about it?' Kyle: 'I can't do that.. you know I can't do that. Dad's pretty dedicated to letting me loose, so, I'm gonna head out before I start turning into a bad example for you.' I started to panick, simple as that. I couldn't believe what my brother was saying to me. He was never a pensive type of guy and he hated people that lived by the book. Very spiritually within himself and responsible with his future, the words that were coming out of his mouth were formidable and apparational at the same time. I could see that this wasn't any of his banter and that it was making so much more sense to him than it was to me. So, on impulse, I swung a haymaker right into his jaw, sending him to the ground. To this day, I'm not quite sure why I did this. Kyle: 'What the fuck?' Nick: '..Oh my God. I was trying to knock you out..' Kyle: ..You were what?' Nick: '..Wait that didn't sound right. What I meant to say was..' Before I could mouth an explanation, he charged at me and connected his shoulder into my midsection, sending me to the ground with such force that my head snapped backwards and clashed against it. He tugged at my shirt and lifted me towards his face and wounded up his fist. I closed my eyes and waited for the unsurmountable pain. It never came, though. Instead, there was a sniffle and whimpering. I opened my eyes and gazed in amazement as my brother started to cry .. no wait, he started to sob. I didn't know what to do. I wanted to hug him, but didn't know how 'off the edge' he was and didn't want to put myself inside danger's embrace. I wanted to apologize for punching him, but figured that he was as childish for returning the light punch with a Jerome Bettis-charge into my stomach that I can STILL feel to this day. Kyle: 'DON'T YOU UNDERSTAND?! GOD DAMNIT!' I shook my head and kept my guard up, just in case. Kyle: 'Everything that I want to be is inside those woods.' I looked towards the wood and could hear the faint howl of an unknown animal; kind of sounded like a wolve, now that I think about. Nick: 'What's that? Animal food? A wanderer without anything but.. but...' Kyle: '..Without anything but myself.' I stared into his eyes, and strangely, they were no longer dilated, but sincere. God, had I been that ignorant? All he wanted was to be with himself. He was a wanderer by heart and by soul and grew into that on his own. These were his dreams and his aspirations, no matter how much I was against them, they were all he had left. His family, me being the only exception, had turned him into a scapegoat and made him out to be nothing but a zero. We said nothing for almost five minutes and our thoughts became transparent with reality. He knew that I knew that he was going to do what he felt was right, and I knew that he knew that I knew that. Kyle: 'Hopefully we'll see eachother again..' And with that, I started to cry. It was the first time I had done that since I was a wee toddler. My brother and I had become as tight as a nun's asshole in those last few years and I couldn't help but pay respect to that with tears of inspiration. I admired this guy because he could catch every angle life threw at him blindfolded and with one hand. I always wondered how he did this and until today, while driving in my car en route to my family's house, I was never able to do the same. He got off of me, dusted his shorts off, and exhaled a sigh of preparation. Without turning around, he started to walk off into the distance as I stood there, an emotional mess. Nick: '..What if I don't ever see you again?' Kyle continued to walk into the forrest. I could barely make him out now through the darkness now. Kyle: 'Just look to the sky, I'll be there. That's what's so good about brotherhood..' He did one of his hilarious 'Jack Nicholson' laughs and faded into the murkiness of the forrest. I sat there in the snow for another forty minutes, praying he'd come out and sulk that he didn't fool me. Like I said, my brother was a pathological fabricator, he gets off on doing theatrical stunts like this. But, forty minutes came and went, and I was still there by myself staring into the dark confines of the woods. He was gone. It was amazing to me that a human being could have the mental strength within him to take off without anything but the clothes on his back. It just surprised me, ya' know? Really took me off-guard. I could remember waking up to the sound of a gun going off near my house, or inside my house. I ran towards the house, which is where I found my lifeless father slung over the hood of his light-blue pickup truck. I didn't have a reaction, but I figured he found some kind of note that Kyle had written shortly before he ventured outside, which is exactly what happened. My mother had jumped into her car and drove off into the sunset for the rest of the day, so I guess she discovered my father's body and then noticed my brother was gone and had a complete breakdown. I ran back down to where I was before and, because of the daylight, the woods visibly opened up more. I could see something white hanging off of one of the trees twenty or thirty feet into them. I could make out a couple letters, which were bolded and outlined with a marker of some kind. Nick: ...'N.. I.. my name?' I started to run down the small hill that leads into the woods and tripped on a rock right before I entered, nailing my chin on a pile of them, as well. Wait, it wasn't a pile, it was a.. fire pit? My brother must've stayed there and made a fire somehow before going any father in his journey, but I don't remember smelling any smoke and our house wasn't drastically far from these woods, either. There was pleasent gash along the side of my right jaw, but I pressed on and got to my feet, only to notice that that white something was a piece of paper lodged between two pieces of bark on a tree. I pulled it off and couldn't believe what it said: Begin here. The rest of the journey is easy. I'll see you soon, brother. Make the effort. ..And until this day, I never did. I figured he had died from the cold and was so far off into the distance within the forrest that it'd be life-threatening for me to go out there and find him. That's the way I was brought up to think. Death wasn't all that dramatic in my family. My mother got over my father's suicide in less than two weeks and they both set my brother's runaway aside completely to handle matters that they truly believed were much more important. Years later, I find myself going back to the place I called home. Years later, I find myself taking part in the journey to find my brother, afterall, this part was the easiest.. What would soon follow would serve as the true test of endurance and dedication. This was my admiration, this was my inspiration, this was... My brother, and I was going to find him.
