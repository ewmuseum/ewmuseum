Hallelujah

27 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/27/05 Recommended listening:'Hallelujah' by Jeff Buckley Well I heard there was a secret chordthat David played and it pleased the LordBut you don't really care for music, do you?Well it goes like this:The fourth, the fifth, the minor fall and the major liftThe baffled king composing Hallelujah --Hallelujah by Jeff Buckley Fat, white snowflakes drift downward in a gentle descent, tumbling gracefully to make their soft landing on the Appalachian Mountains. The gentle backdrop creates a beautiful display for a Christmas Eve, white ground with bare tree branches reaching out, creating a criss-cross of calm and quiet. But the shouts from below ring out, shattering the soft silence that had eased over the landscape. It's a scene that's never happened before. There, outside of Scott's childhood home, where his mother and sister live now, stand the Manbeast, his fiancée Alyssa Sanders and his partner, Vangellus Oxios. And something that has never happened before is raging: they're all arguing. Scott's arms are wide, his face red from the shouting. Alyssa is standing just to his left; her face is red and tears still streaming downward. Van's facing Scott, his hands up with palms outward in the universal nonthreatening gesture. Scott snarls at him and barks a comment to which Van vigorously shakes his head 'no'. It's an outright terrifying sight. Scott has completely lost it, his anger held back only by the thinnest strands of self-control and his eyes burning with a fury that no one has ever seen him display in the ring. But in his anger, he's hiding from something; both Van and Alyssa know he's angry at himself. H knows it, too, but it's deeper. He's ashamed. All the garbage that's been pumped into the mainstream lately about him being a remorseless monster has gotten to him again. The shame deepens when he's realized that his mother and sister haven't come out of the house, not since he flipped over the couch in the middle of a screaming match that came out of nowhere. Van is doing his best to try and stand as a wall between Scott and a total loss of self control. He knows better than to try and stop Scott when he's in one of his rages. Instead, Van is trying to just help the big man cool, trying to get him to see that there's no threat and that no one is going to yell back at him. The problem he's having is that Alyssa is so distraught that she won't calm down either. Van is desperately trying to keep Scott's attention and soothe his unrelenting rage. Alyssa is terrified and hurting for Scott at the same time. In her heart, the man she loves has pulled away from her and retreated into a castle of rage and fury. Scott's heart is walled away from her in, and despite every attempt to reach out to him, she comes away with nothing. Her eyes burn with tears, trying to find a way to pierce through and reach him, but each statement, each pleading request that he calm down, is met with hateful shouts. She knows Van wants her to calm down, but she quite simply can't. Scott turns towards her, his face reddening and his arms quivering. He raises his hands and screams back, causing her to shy back before responding. Her words make his eyes widen, his jaw to work soundlessly and his face to blanche. Vangellus Oxios is rarely afraid, but right at this moment, he is definitely afraid. Be that as it may, he steps between the two of them, still trying to defuse a situation before it should get any worse. Scott doesn't seem to see him. He mouths the comment back to Alyssa. 'What's wrong with me?' and takes one very deliberate step. Van has a split second, now or never decision to make. He can't try to defuse Scott now; he's too far gone. Van very directly changes Scott's focus of attention and puts his hands on Scott's chest as to stop him from going any further forward. The unblinking eyes lower to look at the smaller man and Van's fears are validated. Scott is very nearly gone in the convoluted pathways of his rage complex. Scott's hand pulls back and a massive fist streaks forward. Van doesn't even get a chance to consider how the impact might affect him. The solid THWACK of fist to extra-thick skull cuts all sound off in the valley. Van couldn't roll, he couldn't dodge, and so he took it full-force. The thrust of the punch propelled him past Alyssa and onto the hood of Scott's Jeep. It was then that the rage seems to wash from Scott's face. He blinks. He swallows hard. And he takes in what is in front of him... Van is rolling off the Jeep, still feeling rattled even with his clinically thick skull and a busted eyebrow dripping blood down onto his cheek. Alyssa, with her hands at her mouth, her face deathly pale and her eyes as wide as saucers. Even from the picture window of the house, he can see the eyes of his mother and sister, stunned. He had never hit someone close to him in one of his rages. He'd never lost control so completely as to totally unload on someone he considered a friend. And yet, Van had just taken an all-out shot to the head. Panic grips Scott's throat with an iron clinch and he moved. Van has slipped off the hood of the Jeep and taken a wobbly step up next to Alyssa. Scott bolts for the door of the Jeep, digging his keys out of his pocket. In just seconds, he's inside, pushing down the lock and starting the engine. It roars to life as he spins his tires in the snow for a moment, then takes off up the winding mountain road towards Pikeville. There weren't many places to go, but he wasn't going to need to hide for long.... Well your faith was strong but you needed proofYou saw her bathing on the roofHer beauty and the moonlight overthrew youShe tied you to her kitchen chairShe broke your throne and she cut your hairAnd from your lips she drew the Hallelujah Scott sits there, his eyes awash in tears of his own that he never lets another living soul see. All around him are empty bottles of cheap liquor: whiskey, scotch, beer, whatever he could grab in the store and carry out. Considering his size, it's quite a bit. And it's all gone. His gym bag is sitting over by the bathroom door of this rattrap of a hotel room, picked up out of sheer habit from climbing out of the Jeep. People didn't check into the Mountainside Hotel if they could help it. But Scott's there, his eye almost floating in alcohol and he's still awake. The same thoughts rush through his head over and over again. It's true, it's true, damn it all, it's true. He was the monster they said he was. He didn't have control. If Van hadn't been right there, would he have hit Alyssa? Alyssa, the love of his life? He couldn't answer that. And again, the man called Rampage, the Manbeast and sometimes just Scott Matthews, put his head in his hands and wept. The big man ceased and lowered his hands, wiping them absently on the legs of his jeans. Monster. Beast. Psychopath. Those words flash in his head. What he'd done tonight had really been brought on by all this, but he saw tonight as validation and not being pushed over the edge. The cheap mattress squeals in protest as Scott looks around dimly. His eyes fall on the mirror. He looks at the shaggy, red-faced man sitting on the bed gazing back at him. The eyes are dull and lifeless, but in Scott's mind, he sees a demon looking back at him, not a man of broken will. Scott stands unsteadily, facing the broad-chested man in the mirror he hates so much. With a roar, he charges and crashes the same fist that had struck Van into himself. The glass shatters as the impact travels through to put a hole in the cheap drywall behind the wallpaper. The shower of glass seems to move in slow motion, all around him and everywhere, glittering in ambient light. Scott watches them fall to the floor in the cheap shag carpeting. More images come rushing back to him: Him hitting Van. Spearing the football dad at the game so many months ago. His blowups backstage. They're right, all of them.... and he shouldn't endanger anyone. Never again. He takes his steps to the window carefully. Outside, the snow falls in almost a continuous sheet now, blanketing everything around him in a quilt of white. Scott shoves the window open and looks down three stories. The concrete parking lot yawns back up to him, its maw made up of broken concrete and bits of trash. Scott places one boot on the ledge and looks out into the sky. The laughter of children stops him. Located right next to the parking lot are three kids, young but of varying ages, all bundled up in worn coats and gloves, playing in the Christmas Eve snow. They laugh and frolic, some of them getting to enjoy the powdery whiteness for the first time in their memory. Scott's heart breaks. He cannot let his death be a mar on the Christmas Eve that these kids are enjoying. He simply can't. So he turns and steps back. He doesn't want to ruin anyone's Christmas on his sorry account. His feet shuffle as he looks about the room. There, on the floor, shining in the light of the lamp on the dresser, sits a long sliver of glass. It's half the length of his forearm and only two inches across at one end while it tapers into a long point. He kneels down and carefully picks it up. It will do. But he isn't exactly happy with the idea of only half-doing this. So he reaches into his gym bag and draws out a large, brown bottle. It's something that has been beaten into his head by everyone that worries about his drinking. He knows what it'll do. It's the backup plan. Prepared to finish this horrible experience, Scott makes his way into the small bathroom. The light is harsh on the once-white surfaces, but he isn't going to be worried about clean for very long. Scott sets his glass and bottle down on the floor and gently lowers himself into the bathtub. Well baby I've been here beforeI've seen this room, and I've walked this floor, I used to live alone before I knew youAnd I've seen your flag on the marble archBut love is not a victory marchIt's a cold and it's a broken Hallelujah It wasn't much, but it was something. Van's 1999 corvette wove through the empty streets of Pikeville in search of the Mountainside Hotel. Scott's mom had told them that it was a place he'd mentioned as a “hole someone would crawl into.” The yellow sports car carved its way along, tearing up the road in a desperate attempt to find this place.... while the occupants desperately hoped that they were right. Alyssa is crying silently. In her heart, she never wanted to hurt Scott, only get him to talk to her. He wouldn't, and it frustrated her, which frustrated him. Back and forth, back and forth until they had reached a fever pitch and she had shot off the one question that shouldn't have been asked. 'What is wrong with you?' Her frustration had led to Van getting hit, and she feels the guilt weighing on her. It was the utter fear that she might lose Scott that had really taken over. There had been one case, at least two years past, where a fight between herself and Gabriel Brown, Scott's partner at the time, had led to him getting hospitalized for alcohol poisoning. It was the only way he knew to deal with anything. When he was with her and Van, he didn't need to deal, but tonight... her sobs begin anew. Van's mind is racing. Scott had never even come close to hitting him, not even back in the early days of their partnership when he found Van to be unremittingly irritating. But as time passed, their team formed into a friendship and camaraderie that rivaled friendships that either of them ever had. But tonight, tonight something had snapped. Van knew what it was: it was the hateful message presented by the media and the loss of their tag championships to a team they’d beaten time and time again. They shared the same fears. Scott had crossed a line with each of them that he’d never even approached before. He’d never yelled at Alyssa, he’d never even attempted to swing at Van. But tonight, he’d screamed at her and clobbered him. And combined with the continuous hatred brought to bear by an ignorant public, they were afraid he felt he had nothing to live for. Van pressed the accelerator down further. Well there was a time when you let me knowWhat's really going on belowBut now you never show that to me do youBut remember when I moved in youAnd the holy dove was moving tooAnd every breath we drew was Hallelujah Scott sits there, the pain in his arms subsiding as he dropped the now-streaked piece of glass onto the tile floor. His arms ebb and he can feel the rivulets run down his arms, to his hands and drip into the white ceramic of the tub. He leans his head back, waiting. He would miss some things. He’d miss, most of all, Alyssa. The beautiful girl… No, the woman that he’d met back in RPW. They’d started dating in a questionable manner, but as time had gone by, they had come closer and closer. Scott had never been happier than when he was with her. Even with the trouble she and Gabe had gone through, even with the concerns his family had expressed, it had all worked out in the end. Until tonight. In fact, he was looking forward to marrying her. She was his little angel, the girl that made his life brighter and burdens lighter. She was exactly the kind of girl he never knew he’d be so happy to have, but it was such a blessing that he had. He hoped, he prayed, that when he was gone, she’d find someone else that would love her the way he did…. Someone that was better to her than him. In that same instant, Alyssa is thinking to herself about how no man had ever come close to treating her the way Scott did. She felt like a princess with him, almost something like a loving dragon or a courageous knight. But none of those were right. He was just her man, and she his woman. That’s really all that could say it: he was her Scott. The Corvette swings around the last curve to the Mountainside Hotel and into the lot, leaving more rubber on the pavement than anyone would have thought healthy. The children out playing in the snow mutter to each other about how cool that was and hope that the car will do it again. But it screams to a stop and two people, a slender woman and a lean man hop out and run into the building. It’s a strange sight to the desk clerk. He looks up to see two figures he knew burst through the door and into the lobby. Vangellus Oxios and Alyssa Sanders; he knew the former from recent PRIME broadcasts he watched, but both of them from when RPW was still on the air. It was a strange night. Scott Matthews, the hometown hero, had come in earlier with a big bag and thrown down cash for a room. The clerk didn’t ask questions; it would be a great story to tell his buddies. But this? This was something else entirely. Van leans across the desk and asks where Scott is. The clerk is still a bit dumbfounded and is slow to respond. Van repeats the question, his face growing grave and his eyes boring holes into the man sitting there. Alyssa stands behind Van, her face tearstained. The man’s eyes go back and forth between the two of them before he tells them which room that Matthews was in. But the problem arises: there isn’t a spare key to the room. Van snorts loudly and takes off at a sprint. Scott moves in the tub, thinking that this is taking entirely too long. His arms are tingling from the holes he stabbed into them. But he’s not gone yet and it’s too slow. He reaches over, gingerly, picking up the brown bottle. Inside it, he knows, will be the end to this entire wretched existence. It slips from his grip once, twice. It falls to the floor again before he gets a firm grip and sets it up on the edge of the tub. Van rounds the corner, Alyssa hot on his heels, and counting the room numbers. There, at the end of the hall, the number reported beckons to him, almost as if Scott’s begging for help through the door. Van’s sprint slows and he tries to open the door. Locked. Locked…. Van sets his jaw. Hell with it. His right fist slams into the cheap pressboard, rattling it against the frame with a thunderous crack. His left follows immediately, again making a thunderous racket. His arms begin to pound, back and forth, back and forth, almost like a pair of hydraulic pistons. Alyssa looks around, waiting for people to come out and complain about the noise. It hits her that on Christmas Eve, anyone that would be in a ratty hotel isn’t going to bother complaining. So Van continues, uninterrupted. His fists ricochet off the door, rapid-fire, without a pause. Inside, Scott is dimly aware of an incredible thudding at the door to his room. But his focus is on the bottle in his hands. They’re streaked with his own blood and, combined with the numbness tingling down his arms, he can’t seem to move the once-white cap. His fingers fumble against it. His hands can’t keep the grip…. He sets his jaw and continues his efforts. Van’s knuckles have split all over the door, his blood splattering on the locks of the door. Van ignores the burning in his hands and continues pounding with everything he’s got. In his life, he thought that he’d always be working towards an event that depending on his combative prowess. He never imagined that it would be for him to save the life of his friend by punching through the door. With a scream, Van doubles his efforts and his right cross finally does the deed. A loud crack answers his last punch, blasting the latch through the doorframe and swinging open. He has to stop and breathe, but Alyssa doesn’t. She almost dives past him, her eyes desperately taking in everything in the room. Empty liquor bottles, a broken mirror, they tell a great deal to her. But finally her eyes fall to the bathroom. Her panicked steps find her gazing at her fiancée. He’s covered from the stomach down with blood from two puncture wounds in the middle of his forearms. She swallows at the amount of pain he must be in from trying to cut himself that way and sees the rubbing alcohol in his hands. She reaches for it, screaming that he stop, but he pulls it to his chest and screams back to forget him, leave him. Van follows, pausing only briefly to take in the horrifying scene of desperation and grief. He can’t afford to wait and steps around Alyssa, grabbing Scott’s elbow and pulling. His entire body against his partner’s desperately strong arm…. And it gives. Alyssa’s hands, now slick with Scott’s blood, have incredible difficulty in pulling it from his grip. He won’t let go, but she’s determined to hold on. In her mind, it’s a struggle for life, the one she’s desperate to save and the one he’s desperate to lose. Van’s free hand makes the difference, slipping in and pulling at the top of the bottle, causing the massive man’s slick fingers to almost peel off of it. Van takes the bottle to the sink immediately. He flips on the faucet to rinse his hands and the cap and, with the blood off, unscrews the top and leaves the bottle to drain. Alyssa, weeping, crawls in atop her man, wrapping her arms around his neck and sobbing into his chest. Her clothes are a total loss; the red hooded sweatshirt and the faded jeans streaked with the life of the man she holds so closely. Van hesitates, then walks to the nightstand in the room and dials 9-1-1. His guts turn. His best friend was nearly dead. He probably would have been had they not arrived when they did. With an assurance from the dispatcher that help is on the way, he turns and picks up the garbage can. Just in time, it turns out, as he regurgitates everything he’s eaten in the past few hours into it. He sets it down and walks back into the bathroom. Alyssa is carefully wrapping one of the room’s towels around Scott’s right arm and muttering assurances to him. Van sits on the floor and grabs the other towel off the rack. He presses the white cloth to the big man’s arm and looks up. He turns his head away. His powerful friend, always there for him to help in any way he could looks so weak. Maybe there is a God aboveBut all I've ever learned from loveWas how to shoot somebody who outdrew youAnd it's not a cry that you hear at nightIt's not somebody who's seen the lightIt's a cold and it's a broken Hallelujah Van and Alyssa sit in the back of the ambulance, both looking at their massive friend. He’s pale from the loss of blood and is barely conscious. On the wall across from them hangs a bag of O Negative blood flowing into his arm. Scott looks at them blearily. “You guys found me. You really… really do care,” he whispers. “Hallelujah.”
