Prelude in C Minor

26 June 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/26/2007 [Editor's Note: This piece is not just a random break in the action, but one made necessary by the story. This portion of the story will be tied up at the end of the overall storyline. Thanks for reading.] June 16th, 2007 “Lindz, I need you to pass me the damn paperwork!” a man yells out across the room. On the other side of the room sits a young woman, doe-eyed as she has been reading stacks of paperwork, reports, and other miscellaneous pieces of work for weeks now. Her soft brown eyes look up at the man yelling at her as she sighs inside of her mind. She pushes her glasses up onto her face after they had fallen past the bridge of her nose. The man had grown rather irritable over the past few weeks, and it had grown worse over the past few days. She pulls her chair out and picks up a stack of reports and paperwork, and walks across the room before dropping it on his desk. He doesn’t even look up as she drops the stack of paperwork as he’s focused on the task at hand. “There you go Daniel,” she says to him slyly and he just simply waves his hand at her. His eyes continue to read through the words at a frantic pace, trying to find something, but what that is he isn’t even sure of yet. He’s a detective for the FBI and has spent the past seven months trying to find something that ties all of this together; something that would give him enough to indict the man he had grown to hate. The two had first met nearly a year ago and now it had come down to this. After the man had saved his ass, it was time for Daniel to put him away behind bars for good. For far too long, this man had been doing whatever the hell he felt like he had come to find out, and it was time to put it to an end. The words on the paper just floated up towards him, but at every step along the way he had been thwarted. Every person he had talked to had nothing new, nothing incriminating on him, and it was beginning to frustrate him. He no longer talked to his wife as they had separated three months prior after she realized that he had become obsessed with this case. The case was starting to wear on his mind so much that he was starting to sleep at his desk and probably hadn’t showered in a good four days. However, that was nothing compared to the hell he had endured over the past three days. He had been put on a timetable by his superiors. The words were fresh in his mind. “Daniel, if you don’t solve this, if you don’t find a piece of evidence that finally gives you something worthy of indicting him, then we’re closing the case, and removing you from active duty,” his superior Simon Rice had told him. Those at the FBI had grown worried that Daniel was starting to lose it after having being viewed as one of their top detectives. Daniel had taken it to heart as he had used so many of the FBI resources in the preceding months, but he was far from ready to refuse defeat. So many times he had come close to being able to pin the case upon him, but then it fell apart at the very seams. He wasn’t sure how this was possible, such a common man and yet he had the resources to thwart Daniel without even knowing about it. He was certain that the man had no knowledge of Daniel’s investigation as he had even been given a court order to place taps on his phones and inside of his house. Not once was there a mention of the crime, the one that they were following the hardest, that he had committed, nor any of the other crimes Daniel suspected of him committing. Even with no witnesses, no clues, or any DNA tying him to the murder, he knew he had done it. Daniel had promised this woman that he would save her and he had failed. Now, he had to do something to honor her memory or he would never be able to live with himself. He had sacrificed too much to let this go. As his eyes scans over some more material, hoping to find the piece of evidence he desperately needs, he finally finds something that gave him an idea. “Lindz, bring me the statement from the European guy!” he yells out once again, startling Lindsay from her intense reading. She once again looks up at him, angered by all of this, before pulling out her chair and walking over to the filing cabinet. She had been the fourth assistant on this case in the past seven months. The other three had quit out of sheer frustration and she was starting to see why. As she flipped through the cases she was beginning to feel that this case was more than just that to Daniel; this was personal and she couldn’t figure out a single reason why. Finally, she found the document she was looking for and brought it over to him. He eagerly took it out of her hands before he started to flip through the pages looking for a specific paragraph. As he found it, he started to mouth it to himself, knowing that this was starting to make sense in his head. “Eureka!” he yelled out as he finished reading the paragraph. Lindsay could only look at him at disbelief, not because he had found something, but because he had said the word Eureka. Seriously, who uses that word anymore? Then, he picks up his cell phone and starts dialing a phone number. She is puzzled by this, but decides to sit in the chair across from him, obviously intrigued as to what he had just found out. After a few moments of silence, Daniel places the phone to his ear and waits for the person on the other end to answer. It rings. It rings again. Then there is a click. Someone has answered the phone. “Hello?” the person on the other end asks. Lindsay could barely make out the voice, much less the sex of the person. “Yes, my name is Daniel. I’m a detective for the FBI. I need to ask you a few questions about a certain individual that you’re familiar with,” he responds and then there is silence once again. The person on the other end is obviously startled by the phone call. “Pertaining to what and to who?” the person asks. “This is pertaining to the murder of Jocelyn White. And who? That person is Craig Maloof,” he says. To Be Continued.
