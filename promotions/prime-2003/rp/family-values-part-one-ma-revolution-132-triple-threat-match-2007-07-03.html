Family Values, Part One - Ma (Revolution 132, Triple Threat Match vs. Lindsay Troy and Tony 'The Grin' Gamble)

3 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 3rd, 2007 A small yellow light in the shape of a gas pump came to life on the dashboard of Wade Elliott's Black Ford F-150. Wade frowned at the “runnin'-dry” signal. He turned his head back to the paved path of interstate forty and the red dirt of Arizona, catching a sign reading “Holbrook – 1 Mile.” “Good timin',” he said to himself, turning up “Swamp Music” by Lynyrd Skynyrd, blasting it inside the cab. Holbrook was where he had intended to get off of the interstate anyway, planning to take a scenic route toward Payson and further south to Phoenix, the location of PRIME ReVolution One-Hundred and Thirty-Two, and coincidentally, the second round of the Chris Williams Memorial Cup. He soon came to the Holbrook exit, took a left off the interstate and rolled into the mid-sized western town, discovered a small Mobile station and parked next to a pump, hopping out and lumbering into the store, a bell ringing as he pushed the door open. “Good morning!” said a plump, cheery woman behind the counter, blond curls bouncing of he shoulders and glasses sitting on her nose. “Mornin',” said Wade, grabbing a Slim Jim as he stepped up to the counter. “Forty-nine in gas, if ya will.” “Sure!” she replied, tapping the keys on her cash register. “Forty-nine ninety-nine in total please!” Wade rummaged through his wallet and tossed a fifty-dollar bill onto the counter, pocketing the Slim Jim and turning to exit the store. “Have a great day!” she called after him. Wade answered with the wave of a hand, the bell ringing once more as he left. Of course, he stopped dead in his tracks to see someone leaning against his truck. He squinted for a moment, ready to hoot and holler at the mysterious person, until he realized who it was. “Aunt Julie?” “Hey Wade,” said Julie, a middle aged, homely woman with glasses and mid length, curly brown hair. “Good to see ya.” Wade walked up to her, confused. “I knew you was livin' in Arizona, but I gotta wonder why yer leanin' against my truck, waitin' fer me to step out of that store...” “You know where yer Mama's livin'?” Wade frowned. “No, an' I don't care to.” “Oh for Christ's sake, Wade!” retorted Julie. “She ain't dead yet!” “I don't give a shit! I ain't seein' 'er like that!” “She ain't got much time, Wade! She wants to see her son before she's gone!” “I told ya, I ain't seein' 'er like that!” “She's yer mother!” “Don't fuckin' tell me that, Julie!” roared Wade, pointing a finger. “I don't need to hear it from you!” “Oh fuck you, Wade! When're you gonna grow up n' grow some god-damn balls!” Wade shut up. He dropped his hand to his side. He looked around, noticing the prying eyes of the curious, and somewhat terrified, gas station tenants. Wade made them turn around with the piercing gaze of his blue eyes and furled brow. He turned back to Julie, who had taken a step closer. “Look, Wade, honey, you've gotta see her. It's all she wants. 'Sides, she's got somethin' to tell ya.” “Yeah? 'Bout what?” said Wade, anger still apparent in his voice. “It's about yer father...” she answered in a hushed tone. Wade stared at her, rage burning within his eyes. “I don't know what about 'im,” she continued, noticing Wade's building hatred. “She just said she had some information about 'im. That's all.” Wade curled his lips in, suppressing his anger. He looked away, breathing heavily out of his nostrils, then turned back. “You stayin' with 'er?,” he asked, slowly. Julie nodded. “Lead the fuckin' way.” Wade filled his tank, and was soon following his Aunt's blue Carola towards Winslow, further west on interstate forty. * * * * * * * Wade sat in his truck outside a run-down house. Light yellow paint peeled off the rotting siding. Some windows were broken and all sorts of junk littered the front yard; old cars, scrap metal, an old rocking horse, none of which was of Wade's concern. He stared at the dirty, white front door. He leaned his head back, eyes closed, breathing in deeply through his nose. “RRRRRRRR SON OF A BITCH!” roared Wade suddenly, smashing the back of his head into the headrest repeatedly. He stopped after a moment, breathing heavily. He collected himself, fumbling for his Marlboro Reds and popping one into his mouth, lighting it with his Zippo and taking a long, relaxing drag. Once calm, he popped his door open, punching his steering wheel for good measure before slamming his door shut and walking up to the house. He stepped inside, surveying the small home. It was a pig-sty; beer cans littered on a torn-up sofa, cigarette butts everywhere, the smell of tobacco and rot lingering in the air. Julie had gone inside while Wade was in his truck. According to her instructions he climbed a rickety stairway, stepped down a short hallway and pushed the last door on the left open. The sight stopped him dead in his tracks. In a small bed lay his mother, or at least what was left of her. She was almost skeletal; wrinkled skin clinging tight to brittle bone. Her wispy brown hair rested against a pillow. She was covered by a thin white blanket, riddled with cigarette burns. Strings of tobacco smoke lingered from a cigarette sitting carelessly between her thin, pale fingers. Julie sat next to her, turning her gaze to Wade upon his entry. Wade's mother rolled her bright blue eyes, the only part of her body that remained vibrant. “Take that fuckin' thing outta yer mouth,” she said with disgust in a raspy, dying voice. Wade stared. “I SAID GET RID OF IT!” She snatched a small lamp and chucked it across the room, smashing it against the wall. The outburst caused the cigarette to fall out of Wade's mouth, which he failed to notice. The two stared at each other, laying eyes on one-another for the first time in thirteen years. After the stench of rage within the room died down, Wade's mother spoke through cracked lips. “It's good to see you, Wade.” She brought her cigarette to her mouth, inhaling deep. Julie shook her head. “What the hell happened to you, Ma...” said Wade, finally, voice filled with disgust and despair. “I don't wanna see you smokin' them cigarettes again,” she said. “You ain't got room to talk!” he said, stepping forward and nabbing her smoke, tossing it away. “Oh Jesus...” she sighed, reaching for her pack of Marlboro Menthols. “I'm old n' rotting, Wade. Ain't makin' no difference now.” “You ain't even fifty...” he replied, stunned and mad. “Yeah yeah, heard it from everyone else an' heard it from you. It don't change anythin',” she replied, lighting up a smoke. A pause. “Ya need help, Ma.” “Ah, fuck off.” “Karen...” interjected Julie. “You too, Julie! I've heard eno...EAAACK!” Karen went into a violent coughing fit, hacking up blood and phlegm into a plastic bowl. Wade winced at the spectacle. She was soon over the episode, leaning back against her pillow and taking a drag of her cigarette. “Alright, enough about me n' my dyin'...” “I'm leavin', I don't wanna hear th...” started Wade. “Ah, shut yer yap n' listen to me, ya pansy!” Karen interrupted. He stopped. “I'm on my way out, Wade. Pretty obvious. But before I'm in the ground you gotta hear this...” “Hear what?” he asked, frustrated and curious. “I know where yer pa is...” Wade narrowed his eyes. His eyes boiled over. He paused for a long while, holding back his hatred before replying. “How long have ya known?” he asked, each word spoken carefully and deliberately. Karen paused, lowering her eyes. “Since ya left. 'Bout thirteen years.” Wade stared at his mother, teeth grit behind pursed, curled in lips. He shook, then whirled a violent fist into the nearby wall, throwing hole after hole after hole into the sheet-rock. “RRRRRRRRRR WHY DIDN'T YA FUCKIN' TELL ME!” he bellowed, face red, veins popping out of his head and neck. “Because of that!” she tried to yell back. “Because you woulda looked fer 'im an' probably killed 'im!” “YER GOD DAMN RIGHT I WOULD!” “I couldn't let ya git thrown in jail, Wade! Or worse! I ain't much of a mother, but I love ya too much to let ya throw yer life away like that!” “DON'T FEED ME THAT HORSE-SHIT, MA! DON'T YOU FUCKIN...” “HE'S IN NEVADA!” she screamed as loud as her rotten lungs could. Wade stopped and stared at his mother, who lowered her eyes, regretting the decision to tell him. “What?” “He's runnin' some dive in Vegas...” she answered. He stood frozen, eyes on his mother for a good while. Then, without a word, he turned and stomped out of the room and out of the house. He jumped into his truck, turning it on and roaring out of the driveway, kicking up dust and dirt behind him, squealing tires as he turned onto the main road. He grabbed his pack of Marlboro Reds, crushed them in his hand and tossed them out the window. * * * * * * * An hour later he was on route eighty-seven, on his way to Phoenix. His father would have to wait. Wade had work to do. He muttered under his breath. “Gonna have to pick up some smokes...”
