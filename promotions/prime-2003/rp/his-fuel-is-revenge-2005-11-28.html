His Fuel is Revenge

28 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 28-11-05 As much as Ignatius Lisieux hated the winter, he was finding it extremely difficult to hate a winter where the days were up in the sixties and the nights at the high-end of the forties. Despite the fact that he was struggling to get one full meal a day and that he had been wearing the same white tee-shirt and charcoal sweatpants for over a week now, he was actually feeling pretty decent. Besides: the carnal hunger of revenge was enough to keep him warm every minute of every hour of every day. The pain in his legs was reminder enough. All he had to do was remember the flames at ReVolution 50 in Sacramento, almost exactly one year ago. All he had to do was remember the intense pain he felt throughout his carcass as he was burned like a martyr on a cross, the look on the faces of the Dark Age members and the lack of support he received from the likes of Boda, Emilio Rage and Paul Cain. He could also look down at his chest and see the pentagram that had been sadistically carved into his now pale and lifeless skin. All of this was enough to keep this shell of a man warm through even the coldest of nights, though it was quite clear that San Diego would struggle to provide such a litmus test. This was not the Ignatius Lisieux that people had known before. The handsome poster boy of one of PRIME’s former eras. The man who had waged a war with the likes of Tony Rolo, Joey Troy, Ian English and Black Angel and usually had come out on top. Gone was the brush-like highlighted hair, gone was the heavy tan and the cocksure grin, the chiselled physique and gone was almost everything he and you had known previous to this. All that was left now was a cold, grey, lifeless carcass, fuelled by designs of revenge and unfinished business. The never ending desire to be proven as the best that PRIME had ever seen. No more room for emotion, no more room for friendships or trust, no money, no prospects… nothing… “Could you please spare some change, Sir?” Ignatius was on his knees in a doorway of a vacated business unit on one of San Diego’s most hectic sidewalks. He was begging for change from passers by with hands outstretched and the most piteous look he had ever worn across his travelled face. This is what a former Global Champion had been reduced to. “Please, Madam, could you spare a quarter for a cup of coffee?” A man who used to sell out arenas across the land and used to bask in the adulation of thousands upon thousands of PRIMEates was on his knees on a dirty sidewalk, stinking like a hard day’s work and looking like something akin to death, begging mere mortals so that he might get some food and drink. “Sir, Sir…” They didn’t listen, well the vast majority of them at least. They walked on by with things of greater importance in their own lives to tend to. A homeless and directionless man on a street was of no coincidence to the general public. Ignatius bowed gratefully as a dime was passed his way by a young man in board shorts and a hooded-jacket, the youth stopped and looked at Lisieux, seemingly thinking “Nah, couldn’t possibly be”, and then he kept on moving. Lisieux stayed on his burned out knees. He could not vanquish the blood lust that haunted him every waking and sleeping moment of his life, the painted face of Ozric Mortimer danced around in his mind, laughing at him, goading him. It would not be removed. The gigantic sadistic bastard that had set him alight as an example to the world and then returned almost a year later to Ignatius’ television set in his room at the hospital to BOAST about what he had done to the MVP. “Thank you, thank you…” A dollar was placed warmly into his hands, accompanied by the warm smile of an elderly lady, but still the anger did not subside. Ignatius could not rest until he had wreaked revenge on the people that had reduced him to this. He was now a man without dignity or pride, a man who had quite simply lost everything except his name and his reputation. Ignatius could do nothing but ponder. For some reason, Ozric Mortimer had been brought back into PRIME on the exact same night that a fire had broken out in very close vicinity of Ignatius’ quarters – perhaps some kind of sickeningly ironic reminder as to what he had to do. Luckily, Lisieux was not mentally battered enough to have lost his nouse or his intuition and he had seen the following ReVolution on the screens of a hundred television sets in a late-night superstore. He had heard Motoki talk and then his suspicions had been confirmed, the puppet master was back in PRIME and back at work. It had taken a massive amount of discipline to stay away for this long, let alone for much longer. Later that day, after Lisieux had stocked up with three square meals and a dozen cups of coffee he simply began running… literally running. Think Forest Gump with a harder edge and more direction, the wheels of his plan had now been set in motion, the hate and the bloodlust was his fuel, revenge was his final goal. Ignatius Lisieux would not stop, could not stop, until he had laid these demons to rest, he ached for normality above everything else. So he was running and he knew exactly where he was going, he knew exactly what he had to do and how he would do it. The Eternal Sunrise would cast itself over PRIME once more, but it would be on his terms and by his rules and with a new face. The cool night air whistled through his ears and crashed into his cheekbones, but he could feel nothing but the damning heat of injustice and pain. His wrecked legs ached like a thousand layers of hell, but they kept motoring onwards, driving him towards retribution, driving him towards PRIME.
