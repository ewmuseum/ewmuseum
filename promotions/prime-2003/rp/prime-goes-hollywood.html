PRIME Goes Hollywood

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: The day after Rev 81 The offices of PRIME wrestling are tucked in to the Albany cityscape, away from most of the bustle that a third-tier city like that has to offer. In fact, you probably would’ve driven right past them if you didn’t know any better. Hell, I didn’t even know where they were until Hin See dropped it in there on Revolution 81. But I’m nothing if not a man of opportunity, so we’re in muh’ fuckin’ Albany, NY. Hin See, ex-owner-but-still-the-guy-running-the-show-sort-of-until-Nelson-pulls-that-string-and-collects-on-his-interest, is at his desk, working diligently on some building agreements for future shows. There’s something nice, as an authority figure, about being at your own desk. Not the makeshift one that the arena gave you, the one that you hang your picture behind or put a suit of armor next to so it feels like home – YOUR desk. Bought and paid for. Or bought and placed on layaway for a while until that check came in, then paid for incrementally. Whatever. In the periphery, he hears the front door open, but doesn’t look up. It’s around the time Taun shows up, and as of late he’s been bringing Motoki with him. This is not the time to deal with Toshiaki, so he keeps working, eyes down towards the desk. He doesn’t hear that the door doesn’t shut right away. Nor does he hear the sudden abundance of voices in the main area of the office, behind his glass wall. In fact, it’s not until a girlish scream from Blaine Blair shakes that wall that he even bothers to look up. And boy, did he wish he hadn’t. Without a second thought, he’s up from his chair and at the door, going over the sentence in his head to filter out the instinctive profanities. It’s not professional for a man in his position to let f-bombs slip out. “Can I ask you people what you’re doing?” The lobby and cubicle area of the office is teeming with unnamed men and women, all talking over each other, all looking out of place. At See’s question, they all pause and look at him…then go back to their conversations. Who are they to ignore him like that? Zombies? But they all seem unnaturally healthy, the fake-tanner kind of healthy. And besides, they’re too poorly dressed to be zombies. Before he can utter another sentence, the front section of the crowd parts – not gracefully like the Red Sea, but more like a group of 13-year-old posers getting shoved aside by a 300 pound maniac at a metal concert. The source of their upending pushes forward towards See and emerges from their crowd. I’m sure you know who it is by now. “Hey there, Rhinestone! Thought I heard your perennially confounded voice around here…” Danny finally takes of his sunglasses, despite the fact that he’s been inside for a while now. He feels out some of the wrinkles in his charcoal-with-green-pinstripes suit as he looks around the place. “Decent digs, old man. It looks…what’s a euphemism for shitty…quaint? I mean, with the production values you put into Revolution, I figured you’d have a TON of cash left over to blow on the H-Q, but I guess you’re thrifty all the way. Must have a pretty nice house sitting at home, eh?” “Danny,” See sighs, ignoring for a moment the mob that the Superstar has brought into his office, “this isn’t about personal glory for me, it’s about keeping our organization in the black at the end of the month.” “Yeah, I hear that’s been working out swimmingly. How much interest are you paying to Nelson?” Hin See pulls back and retreats to his office. Danny follows, obviously, but lets the door shut behind him to give the two a semblance of privacy. “By the way,” Ferguson continues, “you need any help with that loan? The guy I pay to care about my money says I need to find a place to invest. Of course, the guy I pay to care about my money is Alan Thicke, so we’re not exactly running on MSNBC here…What were we talking about?” “You were just about to explain to me why you and the small army of people foolish enough to follow you have opted for a field trip to Albany this afternoon.” Danny acts wounded as he paces the room slowly. “Guy, you sell me short. Those people aren’t just kiss-asses and leeches. I mean, they ARE, but they also do other things…” “And I am hanging on your every syllable to find out exactly what those other things are, but at the same time, I should remind you that my job consists of slightly more than sitting at a desk and waiting for the next ‘heel with an inflated self image’ to barge in and talk down to me. I do have work.” “Ah, but that’s the beauty of why I’m here. That’s why you’ll be thanking me in a minute or two.” He pauses, waiting for See to bite on the setup and say “Oh yeah” or “Why’s that?” or any other hook, line and sinker question that will allow him to continue. See doesn’t follow through there, and instead just sits there, eyebrows raised, expecting the answer to come anyway. Danny waits a few more awkward seconds, then caves to the anticipation. “I’m here to take over for the week!” The excitement in the room could put a hyperactive kid into a coma. At first, it sounded like there was some fanfare, but that was just the “Final Countdown” ringtone of one of the hordes in the lobby. “Danny,” See begins, still wearing his ‘cool, collected boss’ hat, which looks a lot like his cowboy hat, “if you’ll remember the terms and conditions of your reward, it said ‘General Manager for a Day.’ Actually, there are no terms and conditions; it’s right there in the name. For a Day. Is that day today, or is that day Revolution 82?” “Both.” “Do I need to get out my desk calendar and give you a math lesson?” And finally, Danny caught a bite. He sits down in a chair opposite his boss and explains how he, temporarily, is no longer the boss. “You see, See, wrestling doesn’t work in ‘weeks.’ Every show is like the next day. That’s why it takes people a week to confront someone about the last show, or to see video from last week. It’s only been a day in wrestling time.” Hin follows along, but skeptically. “So in order to be GM for a Day…” “I need to take over for a full week. I knew we were on the same page.” He does the little point-to-my-eyes-then-point-to-your-eyes thing to punctuate that. “Great minds. Well, maybe one great mind and one Asian guy who keeps screwing up his company. But that’s ok, because I’m here to help. And so are they.” He gestures grandly behind himself, towards the crowd…but no one notices. So he puts his fingers in his mouth and whistles. Still no response. He sits there for a bit, looking at Hin See, then pops out of his chair and runs to the door. As he pokes his head out, we can hear a significant amount of swearing and threats, but it’s mostly muffled. Anticipating that this is not a battle he should fight, Hn See begins gathering some of the paperwork on his desk into the “Stuff Danny Can Be Allowed To Play With” pile and the “Stuff I Need To Take With Me So He Doesn’t Ruin PRIME” pile. Finally, a small group of the many in the lobby move in to the head office with Ferguson. “See, I’d like you to meet some people. They will be crewing the show this week.” “We have a crew.” “Hmm, yeah, about that…I had my secretary give everyone a mandatory vacation.” “Your secretary?” Outside, surrounded by strangers, Blaine Blair whines his way through another phone call. “Anyway, let’s meet the crew. First, our director this week, you might remember him from the Oscar-winning ‘Ruler of the Bracelets’ series, this is Lars Thompson.” The man looks like he hasn’t shaved or showered in months. But apparently that’s what it takes to breed genius. So you should always listen to the homeless, because all that shit they babble about fantasy worlds and ‘Central Earth’ is the next big thing. Thompson offers his hand to Hin See, who politely declined. “Lars and I have been wanting to work together for a while, and I think he’s got a great vision for a wrestling show. Big changes. Of course, that’s going to require a couple other creative minds, so I hired a handful of writers to help with direction.” He gestures towards a weasely-looking crew to his left. The first face has round glasses and a square head, and looks unenthused. “Darrick Kincaid is the master of modern action films, and really knows how to capture the grit and realism of street brawling. He turned down several offers from Tarantino because he thinks Q is a ‘pussy.’” The second is a huge man, three bills plus some extra, and most of it in the gut. Jeff Garlin but unhealthily obese. “Billy Flaherty and his brother are famous for the sophomoric comedies ‘Stupid Guys’ and ‘Penis: The Musical.’” “Lovely.” See declines another handshake. “Let’s see, there’s a big team here, so I’ll just hit the highlights. Susie is a 25-year-veteran of afternoon soap operas, Gary was the brains behind the last incarnation of the Muppet Show, which I think we all can agree was the most brilliant and socially conscious version, and Steve wrote for the WWE. So, as you can tell, none of them have any sort of experience in wrestling, so this will be quite a treat.” Before we go any further, let’s give Hin See a hand, because he’s taken all of this like a fucking champ. A lesser man would’ve broken down into cursing and screaming and tear-inducing anger, but he’s holding his shit together like superglue. Straight up and down, like six o’clock. “Danny,” he says, “I’m not sure if my staff will sit well with an entire week off.” He looks beyond Danny, and the rest of the group turns around. Although it’s hard to tell because of all of Danny’s Hollywood people, every single regular PRIME staff member has vanished. The front door is swinging shut as they all look. Danny turns back, content with himself. “We’re going to take this week and do what I’d like to call a creative overhaul. Hold a few meetings with some characters and discuss direction, give them some new ideas to go forth with, you know. I’m also thinking about some personnel changes.” “Excuse me?” “Well, you know, beyond announcers and all that. I gotta say, I never liked the guy who plays Scott Matthews. Doesn’t seem convincing enough for me. I might fly somebody in to try to breathe life into that role, if not others. But hey, that’s a decision to make over the course of this week, right?” See lets his head sink a little, and Danny moves in to cheer him up…while also signaling his crew to begin setting up for the first day of work. “It’ll be fine, buddy. I already bought a luxury box at the Allstate, so the tickets will be waiting for you at Will Call. You can just sit back and enjoy the show.” The man in charge looks up at the temporary man in charge and sighs. On one hand, he needed to be concerned that the damage done by Ferguson – and their would be damage, oh yes – could but PRIME back in the kind of financial jeopardy he had faced not two weeks ago. On the other hand, the kind of contacts Ferguson had in the entertainment industry were invaluable, and he did drive in some “casual fans.” So what if he does his own thing for one week? Could it be that bad? He rises from his seat and Danny beams, showing him towards the door. He doesn’t walk with him, though, assuming See knows his way out. The owner takes a look at the headquarters…then decides that it’s best for him to get as far away from this as possible. He could use a vacation, even if it were only for six days. The writers and director set up shop as the film crew begins sketching out some sets for the new Revolution. Casting agents begin calling for extras, for bit actors, and the roster itself, setting up auditions and meetings. There’s a lot to be done and little time in which to do it. Amidst all that, Danny Ferguson, PRIME’s General Manager, sits down at the desk and kicks his feet up. This ought to be fun.
