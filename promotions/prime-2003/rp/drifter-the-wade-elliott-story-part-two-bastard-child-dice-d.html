"Drifter": The Wade Elliott Story - Part Two: Bastard Child. (Dice, Dollars & Tragedy, Fans Bring the Weapons Match vs. Devin Shakur for the Intense Title)

Date: Spring, 1975 Pine Ridge, Alabama March, 1975 "Fucking bastard. Fucking. Bastard…" Her whispers were barely audible, drifting down a valley toward the small town of Pine Ridge, Alabama. Her bare feet dangled over the ledge, her head hung low, tears dripping onto her dirt stained yellow dress. Her lips trembled behind walls of beatufiul brown hair as a dainty right hand rested on her stomach. The wind swept by, racing down the hill, the sun on its way over the horizon, leaving streaks of pink upon the sky, but her eyes gazed over her home. She grit her teeth. She narrowed her eyes. "Fucking bastard…" She leaned forward, releasing everything. "FUCKING… * * * * * * * …BASTARD!" Wade Elliott’s black F-150 swerved out of the way of a small white Honda Civic attempting to cut into his lane. He layed on his horn, causing the smaller vehicle to whip back onto the other side of the road. Harsh barking sounds came from the black truck’s passenger side that could only belong to the infamous traveling companion of The Blue Collar Brawler, Angus the Bluetick Hound. "Fuckin’ cunt best watch where he’s steerin’ that ricer ‘fore he gits run the fuck over!" Somewhere Outside New Jersey Present Day A cloud of smoke streamed through the thick goateed mouth of Wade Elliott, disappearing out the open window of his truck as he and his dog cruised down the highway toward Atlantic City, New Jersey, where Dice, Dollars and Tragedy, and hopefully the Intense Title, awaited. He dug into the worn pockets of his blue-jeans, retrieving a small dog treat, which Angus was quick to vacuum into his floppy jowls. "Easy now," said Wade, eyeing his dog with mock concern. "Eat any faster’n you’ll choke yerself, an’ I don’t know how to give CPR to a dog." Angus chomped away, oblivious. Wade grinned, scratching the back of the pup’s neck. "Guess I ain’t gotta worry ‘bout you." Angus replied with a gulp, then turned a happy, panting face to his owner. Wade released an amused grunt before taking another drag of his cigarette. "I shoulda left ya back with the bear, boy. I can tell yer goin’ to cause me all manner of trouble…" Angus whined. Wade turned his blue eyes back to the road, a solemn tone taking over his voice. "…but not as much trouble as I caused my Ma…" * * * * * * * Pine Ridge, Alabama April, 1975 "As far as I can tell, yer plenty healthy to have a baby, Karen. Eighteen's real young to be havin' a child, but plenty old enough fer yer body to handle it." A large, mustachioed man in a white doctor’s robe returned a stethoscope to its drawer. Karen Elliott sat on a hospital bench, clad in a light blue patient’s garb, a contrast to the dull green of the room. "Thank you, Doctor Evans." "Yer welcome," he replied with a southern drawl, removing his rubber gloves and tossing them into a trash can. "Keep away from the liquor an’ keep yerself clean. I want this baby comin’ out with ten toes n’ ten fingers." Karen forced a giggle, an opposition to the fright in her eyes. Doctor Evans grinned, retrieving a clipboard and pen before taking a seat across from her, jotting down a few last minute notes. "Anythin’ else need testin’?" asked Karen, her hand running over her belly. "Nope, that’ll do," replied the Doctor, setting down the clipboard. He leaned forward in his chair, locking into Karen’s shiny brown orbs. "What?" asked Karen, after a long pause. "Are you gonna keep this child?" asked Evans, genuinely concerned. Karen froze. "I…" she started, her mouth hanging open. "Karen?" asked the Doctor after another pause. "I don’t…" * * * * * * * An old screen door creaked as Karen slowly pushed it into her home. She poked her head inside, peering into the darkness of the worn down cabin before taking a cautious first step. She closed the door behind her as quietly as she could, then started across her living room. "What did that doctor tell you?" Karen stiffened, the questioning, angered voice of her father cutting into her ears. She swallowed hard and turned, seeing the large silhouette of her dad in the dark. As terrified as she was, she stood tall and lifted her chin. "I’m havin’ the baby, daddy," she answered. "THE HELL YOU ARE!" He smashed his heavy hand into a nearby table. The crack of fist against wood made her jump. Her lips quivered, but her feet remained planted. "You don’t lissin’ to that son-of-a-bitch doctor. You ain’t raisin’ no child," he growled. She could smell the stench of whiskey on his breath from across the room. She wanted to run, to flee into her bedroom and lock the door, but she held her ground. "No daddy," she objected. "I’m raisin’ the little…" "I AIN’T GON’ BE THE GRANDFATHER TO NO BASTARD CHILD, GIRL! DO YOU HEAR ME!?" He jolted to his feet, knocking over a coffee table, the sound of beer cans rattling in the dark. "Daddy," she pleaded. "Listen to m…" "SHUT UP!" he bellowed, kicking the table out of the way, taking heavy steps toward his frantic daughter. "Please daddy," she trembled, bending at her knees. "Please, don’t…" "I said shut UP!" he roared, his heavy-set frame coming into light. His eyes burned with rage. A bottle hung from his fingertips. He breathed heavy through yellow-stained grit teeth. "Now c’mere…" "No daddy," she begged, taking a step away. "No…" He swung his massive left hand, sending her to the floor with a vicious blow. She yelped, clutching at her cheek. He loomed over her, like a predator ready to finish its kill. "We’re gonna take care of this ourselves," he seethed, taking a long swig from his bottle. She lifted her stinging head, tears in her eyes. She spotted an empty beer bottle in front of her… "We ain’t gonna let no fatherless shit spoil the Elliott name!" he rumbled. She took the bottle… "I’m goin’ to git rid of yer burden myself!" He produced the coat hanger. She smashed the bottle. Karen jumped to her feet, the jagged green glass of the bottle held menacingly in front of her. "STAY AWAY FROM ME, DADDY!" she screamed. "STAY AWAY!"" "You li’l fuckin’ cunt…" he muttered, face contorted. He stepped forward. "STAY BACK!" she threatened. "You li’l fuckin’ WHORE…" he yelled, taking another step. "I SAID STAY AWAY!" She swiped upward, the glass slicing across her father’s right eye. "AAAAAAHHHHHHHH!!" he bellowed in pain, clutching his eye as fresh blood streamed down his face. She dropped the bottle and sprinted out the door, tears flowing as she ran into the night. "WHAT’VE YA DONE TO ME!?" screamed her father. But she didn’t answer. She didn’t look back. Her father’s roars faded away in the dark. She ran. "WHAT THE FUCK'VE YA DONE TO ME!?!!" * * * * * * * Thud Thud Thud Melinda Rivers lifted her head from her book at the sound of knocking. She set down her novel and slid along in her nightgown to her front door, pushing her bright blond hair behind her ears before answering the knocks. She slowly opened the door, curious as to who would bother her family at this hour. On her porch stood Karen Elliott, face stained with tears, lips quivering with terror. "C-can I s-stay here fer a while?" Karen was finally able to ask, body shaking. "Oh, darlin’, c’mere," said Melinda in a motherly tone to her best friend, placing a hand on her back and ushering her inside. "It’s alright. Shhh, come on in n’ tell me what happened…" * * * * * * * "What a fucking waste…" Whisps of steam rose from their tea-cups. Karen sat on Melinda’s couch, clad in fresh clothes, lifting the comforting beverage to her lips. "What was a waste?" asked Melinda, sipping from her own cup, curled in a recliner. "Everythin’," answered Karen. "Just…everythin’." "Earl?" Karen nodded. "Oh, sweetheart…" Karen set her tea on a coffee table, wiping at the tears forming in her eyes. "It’s just…why?" Karen asked. "One minute everythin’s fine, n’ the next he’s off runnin’!" "That baby sure turned ‘im into a coward," said Melinda. "I can see where it’d be scary, but to up n’ leave? That’s pretty much the nastiest thing you can do to someone, sure’s shootin’." "I just wish I’da known," Karen remorsed, shaking her head. "We was steadies since third grade. If I’da known he was goin’ to spook this easy I wouldn’ta wasted all this time. Just brought on all manner of trouble…" "Are you keepin’ the baby?" asked Melinda, changing the subject. Karen paused. "Yeah. Yeah, I am." "What’re you goin’ to do? Earl’s gone, n’ the bridge with yer pop went up in flame…" "I’ll raise this li’l one my god-damn self," claimed Karen, fighting the tears with fierce determination. "Yer a brave one," said Melinda. "Tough as nails." She sipped her tea. "Still," Melinda continued. "Sure’d be easier if Earl was still around…" Karen stared into her tea-cup. "It’s funny how things change so fast," she started. "One day everythin’s so perfect…" She lifted her head, offering a weak smile. "…n’ the next it’s gone. Like a fart in the wind."
