March to the Scaffold ( REV RP, against Aimz and the K-Wolf)

11 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/11/2005 When Justin Williamson closed his eyes, he could see the scene perfectly clearly. His hands were tied behind his back, as two burly looking men escorted him from the cart he and four others were crammed in. He had been charged with treason. For what action of his he knew not, but one of his fellow citizens had betrayed him. In the cart with him were three others. He recognized one as Chris Sloboda, his head in his hands, weeping bitterly in a manner unbecoming of the giant man. Another was Emilio Rage, the PTC Global Champion, stoically looking on as he and the others were borne to their fates. It was not Justin Williamson who was being dragging bodily from the cart, but the Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid. His giant red afro waved slightly in the breeze, as the crowd shouted mocking obscenities at him. “Vive le ReVolucion!” one woman shouted, before returning to her knitting. She chatted cordially with a friend of hers, also knitting away with her needles. They had both been there to watch as the Guillotine’s victims mounted. There had been precious few at first, one, two, perhaps three…but the number had grown. Officially, the HIT had been branded with a “9” on his neck. He was to be the ninth victim of the deadly device. He endured the crowd’s mocking as best he could, until finally he could take no more. The loud cries of “LIBERTE! EGALITE! FRATERNITE!” had changed into something else entirely. “KA-RI-NA!”“KA-RI-NA!” Damn it all! Did he have to be REMINDED of his failure by these ungrateful peons, men and women that could have given a damn about the effort he made? The damnable chanting increased in volume and scope, until soon, the entire crowd was chanting the name. “KA-RI-NA!”“KA-RI-NA!”“KA-RI-NA!” Fine then! So be it! “Let me die,” he thought. At least that way, it would all go away. The chanting, the mocking, all of it. It was ludicrous, really, the cult that had developed around the Guillotine. It was considered the perfect weapon, the most humane device of execution in the world. It had its own fans, the bloodthirsty citizenry who would come out to watch it be employed day after day, no matter how many people it killed, careers it ruined, or lives it shattered. The Guillotine was The Guillotine. None could triumph over it. Its word was final, the omega. Slowly, the Trapezoid felt himself being led up the steps to the scaffold where She lay in wait, ready for him. The two guards guided him to the top of the scaffold, one of them asking him gruffly, “Any last words?” How could he possibly limit himself to a few words, or even a few minutes? There was so much that he had to say. He was about to die. Everything that he had worked for his entire career would be for naught. With time running out, however, he said the one thing that he thought would sum up his life. “Hardcore in life, hardcore in death.” He hadn’t expected to go out this way. In all honesty, he had expected to die in the squared circle, attempting some ludicrous move, or being driven by an opponent through glass and fire. This was just cruel and unusual. He was to be executed like a dog in front of the eyes of the world. As he fell silent, the guards forced him to his knees. He lowered his head to the chopping block, as one of the guards clasped the top piece of the block over his head, securing him in place. There was, however, no blade above his head. But the Guillotine most certainly was here. She took a few steps forward, stretching out a powerful leg, preparing to do what she was meant to. In his wooden prison, the Trapezoid stirred restlessly. “Come on,” he muttered, “just get this over with.” The last thing he heard was the voice of Karina Wolfenden. “I’m sorry,” she said, leg high in the air, about to peak. And then, the Guillotine fell. For a few moments, there was a searing pain, and then, nothing. The crowd cheered as the Trapezoid’s oddly shaped head bounced once, then rolled to a final resting place. Karina picked it up, holding it high so the crowd could see. One thing was perfectly clear. The Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid was dead. ----------------------------------------------------------- With that, Williamson’s eyes finally snapped open. “I’ve been thinking about that damned dream too much,” he said, looking around to remind himself of where he was. Namely, in his car, about thirty miles from the Key Arena. Williamson’s vehicle lay parked in a spot about fifty yards from a rest stop Burger King. Evidence of Justin’s foray into the building lay on his passenger’s seat in the form of a bag and cup. He had been eating quite calmly before his mind so rudely took over his day. The ‘damned dream’ had been on his mind ever since he’d had it the night after Ultra Violence. To Justin, the symbolism was deadly clear. “Karina Wolfenden killed the Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid,” he said to himself, coldly. “The HIT is dead, yet the powers that be insist that I face Wolfenden once more. It was, despite what he said in public, entirely accurate. Justin Williamson didn’t purge the violent, sadistic Trapezoid from the depths of his soul just because he decided that he didn’t want to get stitched up every night. He could handle that. But what he truly could not handle was the haunting voice in his head. “Deal with it.” What had begun as Karina’s mantra to the HIT, and evolved into a crowd chant meant to throw him off of his game, had finally ended its existence as advice that Williamson took to heart. He couldn’t beat Karina Wolfenden. “Deal with it.” He couldn’t win the Dual Halo. Hell, he barely even lasted a half hour in it. “Deal with it.” And so, he did just that. With the help of the dapper Angelo Deville and hours of training, the Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid had reinvented himself as the man that PRIME now knew as Justin Williamson, the Deville’s Disciple. It was Williamson who sat in the car, a 2004 Lexus, clad in a suit that cost more then most of the furniture in his Salt Lake City apartment did. No one asked any questions about where he’d gotten the money. There was no need.All of the money in the world, however, couldn’t have bought Williamson what he truly craved, and that was success. Beginning his sixth year in the industry, Justin still had never been a World Champion. Being a tag team champion was nice, certainly, but Justin always craved more. ReVolution 60 was his first chance to potentially get more. It would be Williamson and Wolfenden. One more time. Except this time, there was a third party involved. “Aimz.” Justin said the name bemusedly. He’d never spoken to her before Ultra Violence, and yet she had come to him for advice on how to tick off Karina Wolfenden. “Girl’s going to get herself into a lot of trouble tonight,” he mused, figuring that she would make a beeline for Karina and try to re-arrange the Negasonic Lupine’s face. “She forgets the first rule of being an arrogant asshole: you’ve got to be able to back it up.” Clearly, in Williamson’s mind, she couldn’t. Forget a win in the fWo, that company was a gigantic soap opera anyway. As far as Justin Williamson was concerned, the Red Raver was a blight on the sport he loved so much. A woman that had slept her way to the top, always latching onto the next big thing. Rent-A-Hero. Reno Davis. Brand Frontier. And thus far, it had gotten her modest success. Of course, SHE didn’t see her success as quite so modest. “I can’t believe she actually believes her own hype,” he laughed, shaking his head sadly back and forth. “She wins a couple titles, gets voted female wrestler of the year for her work in fWo and all of a sudden thinks she’s hot shit. At least Karina knows what it’s like to get in the ring and go at it. Aimz being a hardcore champion of anything BUT pornography is disgraceful to the man I used to be.” The Deville’s Disciple stretched out, arms extended in his seat. His limbs were exhausted from the drive, but he still had about a half an hour to go. In the back of the car was his bag, with his ring gear. As soon as he arrived, he would change into the new, less elaborate attire of Justin Williamson. That made him smile. This would be his first match by himself since the transformation. He had a new style to utilize, and a more calculating ring psychology to use to defeat his two female opponents. He was confident enough that, if it came down to it, he could take out the Red Raver with relative ease. As always, it was Karina that concerned him. “Leave it to my luck to get the damned K-Wolf the week after she loses the World Title and gets her record win streak snapped. With the mood she’s likely to be in, the only thing snapped in Seattle could be my neck.” He was worried, of course, about the C4, the ludicrous maneuver that Wolfenden had intended to be used in Killean in their Ultra Violence match. Somehow, the Intense Champion had escaped the move, and managed to do what Williamson never could: pin Karina Marie Wolfenden’s shoulders to the canvas for the one, two, three. Justin had given the C4 its name. He’d really hoped he’d never be in a position where she’d be able to use it against him again, but wrestling, such as life, was full of surprises. “The most ironic part of all of this,” he told himself, perhaps the only one of the three both acutely aware of and admitting the fact, “is that the two of them are so damn alike. Both are stubborn to a fault. I’d have to pry an apology out of Amy Campbell the same way I tried to Karina, namely with barbed wire. Karina’s better in the ring, but personality wise, those two could be sisters…” It was a thought so horrible that Williamson couldn’t help but shudder. “Ah well,” he told himself, trying to put the horror and worry out of his mind. “Nothing I can do about it until I get in the ring. No use worrying.” Of course, as he fastened his seatbelt, he knew that he was worrying very much indeed. Losing was a no-win situation. Kind of obvious, wasn’t that? Not really. If he lost to Aimz, he would be BEYOND embarrassed. Losing to a woman who had probably whored herself from one coast of North America to the other would jeopardize his career as a credible wrestler. But someone, losing to Karina AGAIN would be worse. He put the car into drive, pulling out of his parking spot. A minute or two later, he was merging back on the highway, headed for the Key Arena. In the back of his mind, however, he could not escape the horrible thought that he was merely marching towards the scaffold once again. That would mean all of his effort, the transformation…all of it was for naught. But as Justin retreated back into his mind, letting his body drive him the 30 miles to the arena, he saw a very different vision of the scaffold. One that made him smile. ------------------------------------------------------------ The guillotine, for all of its strengths, was an indiscriminate killer. It had no control over its victims. It simply killed whoever the man at the controls told it to. On this day, the Lord High Executioner was Justin Williamson. Contemptuously, he looked down at the headless body of the Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid, which lay no more than a few feet from his finely polished dress shoes. Despite the expensive shoes he war, he gave the body a hard kick, knocking it farther away. It was with great delight that he welcomed the two victims. The redheaded girl kicked and screamed violently, while the other woman bore her situation with seeming grace. He gestured for the redhead first. Despite her slight size, she gave the guards all that they could handle, hissing and clawing wildly at their eyes, their throats, anything she could reach. Disgusted, Williamson stepped forward and drove the air from her with a sharp boot to the midsection. For the moment at least, that stopped the fighting. “Put her head on the chopping block,” he sneered. “Let’s get this over with. Perhaps then she will stop her ceaseless prattling.” The two guards forcefully shoved her head into position, clapping the top part of the block over her head. Her struggles picked up once again, but all was in vain. This time, Karina was not the Guillotine. The guillotine was the guillotine. And Williamson simply let the blade fall. It fell with frightening speed, yet the redhead’s struggles had caused the execution to fail on this first attempt. Her head lay half detached from her body, blood-curdling screams passing from her lips. All things considered, Justin thought, it was somehow appropriate that the loudmouth would die painfully. The guillotine blade was raised slowly back into position. This time, the sobbing, bloodied redhead could barely move. This time, the blade fell true. Bouncing once, the head rolled several feet, before Williamson stooped to pick it up. Thrusting it skyward, the bloodthirsty masses cheered, for the guillotine had claimed another victim. It was with almost reluctance that Williamson bade the next victim forward. This woman was calmer, more serene. She knew that death would come one day, and so she lived life to the fullest. In her twenty-three years of life, she had accomplished more than most people would in eighty. And that was why she could accept going now. She didn’t have to like it, of course, but damned if she’d put on a spectacle like the one the redhead had. She kneeled, placing her head on the block, whispering her final words to herself. For slightly over two decades, Karina Marie Wolfenden had lived life on her own terms. Now, despite the circumstances, she would die on them. Williamson himself placed the top piece over her neck, stepping back into position. Before he let the blade drop, however, he lowered his head, and said two words, from the heart. “I’m sorry.” And for the final time, the guillotine fell. The former Universal Champion’s head came off of her shoulders neatly. It bounced once, before the Deville’s Disciple caught it, gazing into the eyes of a woman that he would, for better or worse, never forget. What he saw shocked him to his very core. Wolfenden was blinking. Justin counted at least ten blinks, before finally, the last signs of life left the Negasonic Lupine. To the end, Karina LIVED. His right arm dangled at his side, still holding Wolfenden’s head by the hair. He looked around him at all the carnage that had been wrought. The bodies of Boda, Emilio Rage, the HIT, Aimz, and Karina Wolfenden all lay in a heap. Now, however, the killing could end. The Reign of Terror was over. An Empire could now begin, led by an emperor with acumen and force. For Justin Williamson, conquest was destiny. The king is dead.The queen is dead.Long live your Emperor.
