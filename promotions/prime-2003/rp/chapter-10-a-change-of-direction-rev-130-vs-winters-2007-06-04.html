Chapter 10: A change of direction (Rev 130 vs. Winters)

4 June 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4th June Introduction: Part 1 Harrisburg, Pennsylvania At 8:00 p.m., Rebecca Lavelle-Mayer approached the Royal Diner on Strasseberg Street, her eyes scanning the road around her as her heels clipped off the concrete sidewalk. Inside the smell of coffee and dinner tickled her taste buds; she watched as the butter melted on the soft white baked potato and licked her lips, her stomach rumbling with delight. She took a seat by the window and gazed at the door, looking up each time the small bell chimed. “Hello Butterfly, it’s great to see you.” Rebecca looked up at the withered old man in front of her, her father. She tensed immediately, she was in shock. The image of him being a strong, blonde drunk still laced her dreams like a spider spinning a web, but in front of her, he was nothing more than a decrepit man with wrinkles and a slight limp. “I haven’t been ‘Butterfly’ in over sixteen years,” she stated between clenched teeth. He slid into the chair opposite her. “It’s a shame, you always loved butterflies.” Rebecca looked at the menu, out of habit, than necessity. “Why after all this time did you want to meet? Why now?” “You have your mothers blue eyes and her sweet smile,” he said, gazing lovingly toward her. Rebecca inched closer, the hard shell from being a Lawyer, edging out of her, “I didn’t come here to discuss old times with you. You walked out of our lives a long time ago, no turning back. So, why did you want to meet me?” “This is why,” he said, “you’ve always been the rational thinker and I need your help.” This caused Rebecca to snicker and stand out of her seat, his hand gripped hers, but his strength was feeble. “Let go of me,” she ordered. He did as told and straightened into the red leatherette seat, “Just hear me out first, it’s about your brother.” A sense of worry and care fluttered through her, she sat back down, intrigued as to what the old man had to say. Ordering a coffee from the kind waitress, Rebecca sipped it slowly, and listened. “His curiosity always got the better of him, that boy, but what he’s digging up now is very dangerous,” her father said, his head dipped low and his callous grey eyes hidden from the unkind light. “My past life is filled with regret, danger and stupid mistakes. Mistakes which if brought up, could cost myself and Pierce dearly.” “I don’t get what you’re talking about, what past life?” He stiffened; the room was stifling causing beads of sweat to line his Trilby hat. Picking up a small white cloth, he dabbed his grey balding scalp and sighed, “I was a con-artist for six years after leaving home. I grew a new persona, actually they were more personas. I went around the country as many different men, pillaged as much money as possible, and sent half to my father.” “You’re telling me that grandfather’s multi-million dollar pharmaceutical company is built on the foundation of debauchery and laundering?” she asked with widened eyes, “and that you are a con?” He nodded his head; shame is etched in his worn face. “My worst memory of my escapades was May 1978,” he said, “I was under the alias Allan Cooper, one of my favourites. I was getting cocky and familiar with the skill of ripping people off. I’d never had a challenge quite as big as the Hume family fortune, but when I saw it, I wanted it. My greed, ignorance and immaturity, cost me everything. I got a woman killed that night in May, a woman I loved,” he whispered, as though the entire world was listening in. A mournful sob emerged from his once hardened façade. Rebecca couldn’t fathom what she’d been told; her father wasn’t even the man she believed. “And us, mom, our family name … was that a forgery?” He looked at her, a large knot in his throat, unable to answer. He was a stranger. Standing up she began to walk off, in a daze. He followed, leaving a tip and money for the waitress. It was raining now as the night had crept above them. Rebecca stood under the unpleasant glow of the street lamp, the cold droplets streaking her make-up, hiding the watery tears as they cascaded down her white cheeks. He gripped her arm and turned her, “I know this is hard for you to hear, but Pierce is digging it all up and it’s dangerous. You have to think rationally, Butterfly. You have to stop him, if not for my sake, then for his.” She stared blankly toward him, lost in the void where his soul should be, “I can’t promise you anything. Pierce isn’t just a good brother, he’s brilliant at his job, and he will find you and put you away for a long time.” “I won’t object, I’ve lived in fear and isolation for a long time, but you have to warn him. The Hume family are dangerous, please Butterfly.” Rebecca shook her arm from his grip, “I will see to it that he knows.” He watched her storm off toward her Lexus, swaying from side to side, shock and terror evident. She looked at him like he didn’t exist to her, no more than a stranger in the street. Flapping his jacket across his chest he moved off into the dead of night. ---- Observations: Part 2 Hume Private Jet Somewhere between Kansas and Washington The sound of the power jets echoed around them, enclosed in the lavish suit of the Hume jet, Dante sat into the leatherette seat and picked up a phone. “Are the votes coming in, Shirley?” Dante asks flicking his biro against a jotter. “Excellent. Keep up the good work; I’ll be back in Kansas in a week.” Placing the phone back into its stand, a smirk graced along his lips. His thoughts filled with the idyllic possibility of being Mayor of Kansas, an accomplishment he’d desired for a long time. Running his hand through his slicked back strawberry blonde hair, he looked over to Roger Dumont, stoic and bemused. “Something tells me, old friend, you aren’t as pleased as I am right now,” Dante announced as he pours himself a glass of Cognac, swirling it around he licks his lips and takes a taste, revelling in the luxury of his life. “I believe we have a problem, Sir.” Dante sat up, folding his arms and drilling his fingers on the plastic table, “Do tell, Roger. We land in Washington very soon.” Roger Dumont slowly rose up from his chair, straightening the lapels on his tweed coat he approached, reluctance in his voice. “A man named Pierce Lavelle has been digging up your families past. More specifically the death of your mother and the con-artist involved, Allan Cooper. He is becoming adamant on solving the case,” Roger announced but furrowed his eyebrows as Dante was laughing as he sat back into his chair. “I seem to have trouble finding the humour in this, Sir.” Dante inched closer, opening a folder, “I know of this old friend. Why do you think we’re going to Washington?” Dante questioned, flicking open a satchel of photographs. “I will not let anybody ruin what I have created. My families skeletons won’t come back to haunt me, Roger. I found out about this man because he’s my half-brother.” Roger nodded his head and approached Dante. Gripping one of the photos with a furrowed brow he looked toward a small black and white image of a man. Various photos lined the table, but Roger was more enthused by his apprentices initiative, “Then how would like to proceed, Sir?” “He’ll figure it all out quickly and when he does …” Dante gazed at a photo with a crooked smile, “…I’ll be waiting in the dark.” ---- The Black Goose Lounge: Part 3 Washington, D.C. He took a seat by the bar, the chipped wood felt rough under his palms as he sat into the bar stool. A slow and quiet tone played in the background as the jukebox rolled to the next song. He recognised it as the opening entered with a cheer from the crowd around him. The clipping of pool balls rang in his ear. Turning to the bar keep, Pierce Lavelle took a beer mat. “What’ll it be?” “I’ll have a whiskey straight and a cola, thanks.” The bar tender nodded his head. Dawn approached from the bathroom; exhaustion bearing down on her as her trip to Washington D.C. had been spent touring the city with her boyfriend. Sliding into the bar stool beside him she kissed his cheek. ‘Mmmm’ by the Crash Test Dummies played is the background, getting the odd cheer from a drunken customer. He turned to her with a tired look. “A small break from the sight seeing, I didn’t realise how many steps are in those monuments.” Dawn laughed and took the cola from the bar tender with a smile, “I didn’t have a chance to tell you earlier, but I found that file you asked for.” His eyebrows rose as he sipped his whiskey, “And?” “Allan Cooper attempted a robbery six years prior to the incident in ’78. He tried to rob a small pharmacy in Utah in ’72. It didn’t go according to plan and the wife of the Chemist hit Cooper over the head. Cooper used a sanitary towel to mop up his wound and fled, leaving the evidence behind. Thanks to modern technology, I pulled in a few favours, and got a DNA comparison to the blood found at the ’78 crime scene. It was a positive match,” Dawn said taking a breath, having regurgitated everything she’d remembered. Pierce nodded his head and looked at the CNN news report on the television in the corner. Apparently the New York Stock Exchange was experiencing its best month this year. “What was the blood type?” “AB negative,” she replied. His eyes widened, finishing his whiskey he ordered a second. Dawn watched him curiously. “OK, you want to tell me what’s going on. I know that look by now, and it’s not everyday I’m asked to look at a file from ’78.” He sighed and fiddled with a beer mat, nerves spread through him. “What if I told you that Allan Cooper was my father?” “I’m all ears. Off the books, I promise” “I came across the case because of a box I found in my mother’s basement,” he replied, pausing for effect, he continued, “in it contained passports and a newspaper clipping. The box belonged to my father. He never came back for it after he served time in prison. My father was Allan Cooper, Bradley Lavelle and Jeffery Donovan. His real name is Andrew Conklin.” “That’s quite a stretch. He’s a wanted felon.” “Strange part of it all is that the man I knew as my father was nothing more than a character, a con. No birth cert. He didn’t even exist.” Dawn sipped her cola; pondering the information she’d been given. “What are you going to do?” Pierce leant forward onto his elbows, swirling the whiskey in its glass. “I have no idea.” She placed a hand on his and smiled tenderly. “I’m here for you and will help in any way I can, I promise.” “Thanks- ” Pierce was broken mid sentence as his mobile erupted in his chest pocket. He answered without hesitation as the caller ID read, ‘Rebecca’. “Hey, Becca, what’s up?” Dawn watched as a small frown merged onto his lips and he shut the phone. “We need to go back to the apartment for a while. My sister arrived and she sounded pissed off.” “OK, but you still have that club to show me.” Pierce smiled. “This evening, I promise,” he said and kissed her on the lips. They exited the bar and toward their car, Dawn being the designated driver sat into his Jeep comfortably and turned on the engine. ---- Perilous Decisions: Part 4 Washington, D.C. Rebecca Lavelle-Mayer rested on the hood of her rental car, hugging herself against the chilling winds. The afternoon steeped in a humid sweat as the rain filled streets rose with fumes, dancing in the breaking sunshine. In the distance she watched as he approached, followed by a young woman, holding hands. Pierce and Dawn strolled out of the street from their parked car; he turned to her and kissed her. Watching till she crossed the street toward the apartment, he turned and looked toward his sister. Flicking the collar of his jacket up against the biting cold, he walked with a sturdy posture. “What’s so urgent that required you to drive all the way out here?” he asked. “You might want to tell me what you’re up to, Pierce,” Rebecca said. Pierce looked at her, the keys to his car dangling from his finger, “This why you came all this way, a lecture?” Rebecca approached now, venom and steam flaring. “God damn right it is. You’ve always got yourself into problems, and this time you’ve gone too deep. He came to me you know, told me to help you. I didn’t even know what you were doing until he told me. What the hell, Pierce.” He stood silent with his shoulders slumped as he moved back toward his apartment. “I’m sorry you had to get involved, Becca, but this isn’t something you need to worry about.” “Worry? I’m beyond worried, a little pissed off maybe. It’s obviously bad enough that he had to walk back into my life. I know everything, Pierce. I know about Mary, about his profession and I know what you’re doing.” Pierce turned sharply as she gripped his arm, stopping him. “Then you understand the conflictions I’m feeling.” Rebecca gave him a stern and unforgiving look, determination creased into her brow. “You think you’re a bad son to write this … this farce of a truth that is our past. Don’t you?” “I keep telling myself that he’s a murderer and a con, but then I sit down and the deeper I get into this case the more I feel like I’m betraying him.” Rebecca looked him deep in his eyes. “You still have nightmares about that night, and I know that. You hate the man and despise him and now you grow a single caring feeling toward him. I can’t believe you both dragged me into this.” “Becca, it’s not that dangerous,” he retorted. Rebecca bit her lip, she answered with a dulcet tone. “I believe he said dangerous people would stop you, which is why he’s been hiding.” Pierce laughed, “He’s a brilliant liar, Becca. It’s what made him a good con.” Rolling her eyes she let out a frustrated moan and pulled out her car keys. “You’re just as stubborn. I can’t talk to you when you’re like this, never could. I’m staying at the Hilton with Josh. When you want to discuss this, I’ll be waiting. Got it?” Pierce looked at her and nodded his head, “I’m not going to change my mind, Rebecca.” “No matter what the cost is, right? Always the same Pierce.” That was the last she said to him, storming off past the humming traffic and toward her car. Turning around he sighed and placed his hands into his jean pockets. He began to think about her warning. She’d never been one for a sudden burst of worry or angst, but the impending misfortune that was their life had stirred her to the bone. Shaking the pessimistic thoughts from his mind he turned the corner. “You should listen to your sister, boy. She’s quite a knowledgeable young woman.” Pierce stood frozen as the voice flooded around him; he turned slowly to be greeted by the shadowed figure of his father. “You look good son,” he stated, approaching Pierce with a smile. Pierce backed off instantly raising his arms, anger welling up inside him. “No. No. Stay back. Just stay back.” His father stopped, his long cream coat fluttered in the wind. His grey hair shone against the peeking sunshine, glinting as the raindrops trickled from his face, “OK, I’ll stay here. Just hear me out.” “No. I don’t have to stand here and accept you back in my life. Not now. Not ever. Don’t ever visit Rebecca again. Stay away from us.” “Just promise me you’ll stop,” his father said with a sincere and soft tone. Pierce began to move away in a daze, unable to control his emotions he turned away from his father. His father followed and grabbed his arm, Pierce turned and eyed him. He remained silent and slipped a small card into his pocket, “Promise me,” he whispered. Pierce stumbled up his steps, wonder curved into his brow. Yawning he grabbed his spare set of keys and opened the door, before he closed it, the cogs of his mind working in overtime, a knock came. Pierce turned to see a small delivery man dressed in a UPS uniform stood in front of him. “Are you Pierce Lavelle?” “Yeah,” he replied. “Got a package here for you,” the UPS man declared and handed Pierce a thick brown manila folder. Having signed for the package Pierce shut the door. In the distance he could hear the shower running in the guest room, Dawn was making herself at home. Stepping into the kitchen he slit the opening, a small recorder scuttled along the marble counter-top. Confused he pressed play. “Hello, Pierce. I hope you are keeping well. By now I’m sure you’ve understood the real truth behind Mary Hume’s death. If that’s the case, you’ll see enclosed in the envelope six photos. Please take them out.” The message paused. He ripped the parcel and looked at six photos, flipping them over his eyes widened, his stomach churned and his mind raced. In front he saw a photo of his mother in her bedroom, a red X on her face. The next photo his sister in court, a red X on her forehead, and then him at the market, Hailey at a recent news conference. He flipped over the last two and felt a thud of worry flood through him as he gazed at Dawn and Detective Matt Collins. He flipped the tape over, his heart racing. “Each photo I’m sure you’ll recognise, Pierce. I have men watching each person. I hate to do this, I truly do, but what you’re uncovering should’ve remained buried a long time ago. So, here’s the deal. You end this case otherwise the people in those photos, starting with your beloved mother, will disappear. Have a good day, Mr. Lavelle …” A loud static followed and he chucked the recorder into the bin, the photos lay in shambles on the counter-top. Yelling, he ran his hand along it and knocked them to the floor. His breath had quickened. “Damnit, he was right, why does he always have to be right!” Dawn emerged from the guest bedroom, a white bath robe wrapped around her, “Pierce …” she said with a pause, looking toward his face, “what happened?” “Somebody doesn’t want me digging deeper,” he announced and grabbed his mobile phone; slowly he pulled out the small white piece of paper he’d been slipped only moments ago. Dawn plodded over to the kitchen, her bare feet pattering along the wooden floor. She grasped the recorder in the thrash and hit rewind. Her ears picked up the familiar noise as it stopped. Pressing play her eyes widened. She heard a partial comment from Pierce as he made a call. “ … Now, we are going to talk. You will tell me everything … including who is scared of the truth being revealed. I’ll call with a place.” Pierce shut his phone and heaved a sigh, his chest rising and falling rapidly. ---- Crossroads: Part 5 Lincoln Memorial He stood beneath the Lincoln memorial, his hands delved into his suit pockets, shielding them from the bitter cold. A splash of rain trickled against his skin; he shivered in the nightly gaze. Looking on carefully, Dante spotted a slender man approach from the road, hidden in the murky abyss. “I thought we had a deal,” the figure announced, walking up the steps of the monument he came face-to-face with Dante. “We did, but I expected you to keep up your end of the bargain, dad.” Bradley Lavelle snorted and turned to the statue of their fallen president, his eyes cast onto the Potomac as it flowed like a blanket through the city, dark and mysterious, the moon’s glow lit by its shimmering waters. “I told you I’d take care of it. You went too far, you promised you would not hurt him.” Dante sighed and stood back, sheltered by the great speeches of Lincoln. “Actually, I said I wouldn’t kill him. You just chose to read between the lines. I had to step up, father. You’ve become weak in your old age. I gave him warning. It’s up to him how he continues this charade. If he doesn’t comply, I’ll do more than hurt him.” “How could you do this? He’s your brother.” “And your son, but you treated him no differently,” he replied, cynicism evident in his voice. “How dare you. You’ve lost sight of the bigger picture. Your obsession with being Mayor has hindered your good nature, Dante. You wouldn’t harm a living soul.” Dante burst in a menacing laugh, “You don’t know me at all, do you father? How do you think that petulant old derisive step-father of mine died?” Dante looked at his father with steely eyes. “I killed him, dad. He deserved to die. He had to be judged.” Bradley Lavelle shook his head in disbelief. “You’ve completely lost the plot. This is madness, Dante. You call me up with worry of being exposed. You threaten my son and my entire family and now you admit to killing Joseph Hume. What’s happened?” “I didn’t choose this life, god damnit!” yelled Dante with flaming eyes. “You think I asked to be born into a family with debauchery, murder, fame and incest? You think I asked to have my mother die because of an adulterous relationship? No. I did not. Like Pierce, I lived with a man that beat his wives and abused his children. We did not ask for our lives, but here we are. At a crossroads and it’s your entire fault!” “God damn you. Do not make me out to the bad guy. You’re threatening everybody my son loves and cares about; you’re as bad your step-father. He did the same thing and it ended up killing Mary, is that what you want, more death?” Dante inched closer. “No. I’m worse. This conversation is over. I’m giving him ample warning, whether he chooses to follow and save his loved ones, that’s up to him.” Bradley Lavelle began to follow, anger running through his old veins. “Do not walk away from me, son. Do not.” “Go home, dad. Go back to the shadows. Save us all from our own humiliation, just go.” He watched as Dante slipped into the rental car. He stood alone in the dark and for the first time in his life he felt remorse. Flicking the collar of his jacket and wrapping it around his withered frame he walked off into the night with a heart filled with guilt and regret. Opening his mobile he saw a voice message and held it to his ear. “I know you know about the warning I’ve been given. You went out of your way to stop me and now somebody else is doing the same. I think you know whom, father. I won’t sit by as my family and friends are threatened. I won’t let harm come to any of them. Now, we are going to talk. You will tell me everything … including who is scared of the truth being revealed. I’ll call with a place soon …” He closed his phone, having listened to Pierce’s message. A feeling of angst rumbled in his stomach, he dreaded the truth. To be continued …
