A Brother In Arms Is A Brother In Hands

Date: 15/32/2008 A Brother In Arms Is A Brother In Hands A Garbage Bag Production I had been celebrating before there had been anything to celebrate about, and when the big check cleared, it had accelerated the celebration to garish proportions. I spared no expense. I had hired escorts and servants and even a small militia of armed mountaineers to stand guard. I rented the executive suite -- overpaid for it in cash -- and the hotel manager said, "right this way," and stormed right in on the fat cat while he was in there nailing some other broad that was a generational gap or two younger than his wife. "What the fuck?" he barked, too steamed to cover up his loose, elephantine hide. "I paid for this fuckin' room!" "Yes, Mr. Condottiera, and this gentleman has agreed to pay more." I stood there and watched, and I couldn't help but stare in uncomfortable situations such as this one. Mr. Condottiera walked towards the hotel manager and me, in full exposure. He tilted his head at me, peering up at me sideways, and formed his hand into an obscene gesture. "Fuck you," he spat spitefully, and then he walked right on out of the room, buck naked. "And what about her?" the hotel manager gestured at the frozen mistress, her blanket stuck in front of her. "She can stay if she wants," I surveyed, taking accounts of the room's many appointments as I measured its threshold for housing apocalypse. *** "You have to leave," the hotel manager pounded through the heavily barricaded door. It couldn't have been more than a week, and most of my personnel had left, lured away by more auspicious offers. "Come in and get me!" I taunted back, drunk off a bottle of gin we'd made up in the bathtub. Three of the mountaineers stuck there with me, and we kicked the bottle around for swigs, each hand armed, waiting for them to call in the battalions to ram down the door. I had promised them a firefight, and for these backwoods rent-a-militias, there was no greater payment that could be tendered. "You cannot stay here if you've run out of money!" I couldn't tell them apart, because they all looked pretty much like each other, except that one of them, more so than the other two, could have been my splitting image. One of the other two aimed his rifle at the door, and the other one of the other two followed suit, but before the one other from the other two could raise his barrel, I lowered his weapon and ushered him with me to the back of the suite. "Excuse me, but, well, have you . . ." I sought the right phrasing and readjusted. "Well, first, what's your name?" "Captain Major Duff Columbus, at your service, sir!" reported the foot soldier. "At ease, Duff," I studied his facial features, and it was just uncanny; ghoulish, even. "The reason I called you back here is that I was wondering if you noticed kind of a resemblance between us." "Correct me if I'm wrong, sir, but you commissioned us to fight a war, not to notice kind of some resemblances." "Well, what if I commanded you to notice?" I countered. "I'd say I was still waiting on that first firefight you promised us." "Well," I tongued my gums over my next move. "I command you to notice." "I'm still waiting on that first firefight you promised us." I had expected that he would play that card. You see, I had a clever intuition for war games. While he was remembering what he said he would say so he could say it to me, I pulled him by the rifle, pointed it up at the ceiling and pulled the trigger. The the one and the other one of the other two turned as crumbles of wall and the mysteriously powdery stuff of ceilings floated around me. "I've got your attention?" I declared behind a dusty mask. "I'm willing to up all of your payments from a firefight to a skirmish." "How do you suppose you're going to pull that off when I haven't even seen a firefight in miles?" one said. "There's no way this guy's going to show us a skirmish," supported the other one, but the joke was on them, as in a seconds, I had already engaged them in quite the skirmish. "Hear me out!" I reined. "I'm going to have to use Duff as a decoy to buy some time." "They'll never buy it," one of them doubted, and I suppose it didn't matter which one it was at this point. "You two look nothing alike," another one chimed in, or maybe it was the same one continuing. "You just got to keep them busy for a while, so I can get the funds together to pay you a proper skirmish." I thought on nimbly. "Just make sure Duff stays far enough away that they think he's me. Hell, have a firefight to keep them occupied. It'll be on the house." The four of them embraced my new way of thinking, but there were still questions looming. "How long you need us to keep them occupied?" "I don't know," I roughed. "It shouldn't take longer than a week or two to rustle up a good skirmish." "A week or two?" "Yeah." "Well which is it?" one asked, and I did not know which one it was. "Which is what?" I would have asked had someone not beaten me to it. "How long is it going to take for you to rustle up a good skirmish?" "I said it shouldn't take longer than a week or two," I said. It shouldn't take longer than a week or two. "Two's worth more than a skirmish," one said. "Keep in mind about the firefight!" another said, and by logic since I wasn't one, I must of been an other, but I wasn't another. Not necessarily, anyway. "Let's just say it takes two," but two didn't take, and the other two seemed just as well with that, and I was one of the latter. None of us were sure who had said it. The fifth was still pounding at the door, and we were all sure that we were not him and he not us, but not that we were not us. We weren't getting anywhere this way, and it didn't help that there were so many weapons, with a number of interesting names, rifling about. "Two for a firefight and a skirmish?" "Think about this," I reasoned. "You get paid a two week firefight to have a two week firefight. You're being paid to receive that payment, and then you get a skirmish on top of that." In the new light, it looked different to one of the ones that didn't take and to one of two that had taken, and while the former one of the ones of the ones and the twos expected there to be three and then one, the latter saw that the ones and the twos were even, and then there were three. None of them remembered how it had began, and now they were all three convinced that another one of them had vanished into thin air. He had shifted into another person, and they had all shifted into other people by some occult mischief. "Did you see that?" he said. "He disappeared." "Who disappeared?" "Weren't there just four of us in here a second ago?" "That's what I thought," he said. "But I only count three of us," he replied to him, not himself. "There's me, and there's you, and then there's you." "You counted me twice, and you didn't count him." "I counted him, but I only counted me once, and I counted you twice." He -- all three of hims -- counted and recounted and compared numbers, no longer aware of sides. Among them, there were no sides. There were just numbers, and numbers had to be supervised to keep from pairing off and multiplying or splitting across divisions. They had no idea where I had gone, and neither did he. *** Sometime later, I had returned, but when I had made it back, they had all left. I didn't realize how long I had been gone. I'd guessed it had been a week, maybe two, and truth be told, I couldn't tell you what day it was anymore. I liked to think that they had held off as long as they could, and maybe there were none of them left, but I figured as much as figuring was worth that they'd all gone off to serve other purposes. Far be it for me to question what those purposes may be. All I knew was that they were always adding and subtracting numbers no matter what else they were doing. You couldn't much trust a man while he was counting numbers. You could just hope that if they fuck up one or the other, it ain't the numbers, because either way you're fucked, just so long as you don't get fucked twice.
