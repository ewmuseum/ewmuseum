Holiday

4 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 4, 2005 You’ve never been a big people person. Crowds? Lines? Never been your thing. And yet here you are, walking down the boardwalk in the midst of a teeming, sweaty mass of humanity on Independence Day. Oh, goody. But truth be told? It’s not all that bad. Because all the heat and all the people, not to mention the overload of sensory information—the sights of so many faces and bodies of different shapes and sizes passing by, the sounds of excited chatter with some top-40 music piped in as the background, the smells of various fried foods wafting through the air—you’ve completely forgotten about other things. About PRIME. About your first title match in more than two years. About Killean Sirrajin and his constant attempts to meddle. And about her. Yes, you’re not thinking about her at all. Nope. Not a bit. You make your way through the holiday crowds at Coney Island, feeling the summer heat baking the top of your head should’ve grabbed a hat, dammit and the soles of your feet even through your shoes. The sweat runs in frigid tickling streams down your back as you try to get around a morbirdly obese man whose impressive growth of backhair makes it seem as if he’s wearing a sweater underneath his powder-blue tank top, who is moving at a painfully slow pace. You see an opening in the crowd and scoot past the man. “GATHER AROUND, FOLKS! THE COMPETITION BEGINS IN JUST UNDER AN HOUR!” Cursing, you look at your watch and continue forward. There, straight ahead, you see the street that runs alongside the boardwalk. A large white bus continues to creep forward at a snail’s pace. You break into a run, darting through the rapidly opening and closing holes in the mass of humanity like a running back navigating his way through zone blitz coverage—and considering the few times you have to stop, apparently it’d be coverage stolen from the St. Louis Rams’ playbook. The bus comes to a halt with a squeal of tires and the hiss as the door opens. You see an obvious security guard—black sportcoat and white wire trailing up to the left ear, way to blend in—get off first and do a quick scan before some douchenozzle in a salmon-colored polo shirt follows and goes into the usual spiel. One by one they get off the bus—and it’s a motley crew. One guy in a muscle shirt, a little old lady and a couple of skinny Asian guys (how the hell can they eat so much?!) along with your usual assortment of stereotypical big fat guys. You see the intense looks on their faces as they eyeball the stage before passing into the area commonly referred to as the bullpen. Competitive eaters… And people make fun of what YOU do for a living. Then, you see him. “AZALA!” you yell out, waving. A massive head turns. Blue eyes light up. “GIBBOOOOOOO!” Before the security guys can react, he’s bulling his way up against the guardrail. Large arms reach over and pick you up in a massive bearhug, thrashing you back and forth in a rib-crunching display of affection. “Mr. Gibbo came! Mr. Gibbo came to cheer on Azala!” A head presses your face against Azala’s chest. Your nose is placed perilously close to Zameer’s left armpit. The smell of the Snack Daddy, some indescribably odiferous combination of freskly baked cookies and salami with a hint of shame, penetrates your nostrils. Weakly, you start tapping Azala on the arm, and he releases a little bit of the pressure. “AIIIIIIIR!” you gasp weakly. “Oops!” You feel yourself being lifted over the barrier. “It’s OK, this is Azala’s cheering section! Mr. Gibbo is Azala’s partner!” You feel the wave of security personnel closing in start to part like the Red Sea. Hey, would YOU want to step in the way of a 400-pound African warrior—before he prepares to chow down on hot dogs, no less—and get him in a bad mood? Didn’t think so. You follow the big man, who—Jesus Christ, he’s starting to fucking SKIP, he’s so happy—is making his way to the bullpen. “How you find out where Azala going to be, Mr. Gibbo?” “Text message,” you say. “I figured you sent it like all the others.” Azala turns to face you, scratching his head. “What text message, Mr. Gibbo? Dusty take care of all that for Azala! Give Azala more time to train for matches.” Zameer leans in as if to conspire with his partner, and whispers, “That mean Azala have more time for snacks.” You catch the whiff of Twinkies on Azala’s breath. You heard most of these human stomachs fast before a competition—if you can call trying to consume the most chow without booting it back up a competition. Azala starts to do jumping jacks, no doubt his idea of a warmup. You have no idea when the big guy found time to qualify for the Nathan’s Famous Hot Dog Eating Contest—but you were hardly surprised when you learned your tag partner’d be up there battling it out with the other gastrointestinally gifted competitors. You were here… well… why WERE you here, any way? Because of your love of competitive eating? Negative. To get your mind off a certain Firesnatch? True it was a factor… but no. Maybe… just maybe… you were here to support your partner. Your friend? Azala? A friend? The same man who made a habit of spearing you as violently as possible in OSW? Was it fathomable he’d become not just an ally—but the only one you could trust? Maybe so. “Listen, Azala?” The big man turns, licking his lips in anticipation of the coming feast. “Good luck.” Azala grins as the announcer starts his spiel. “AND NOW, TO INTRODUCE OUR COMPETITORS!” “That Azala’s cue!” He waves and follows the other eaters out onto the stage. TWENTY-SEVEN MINUTES AND NINETEEN SECONDS LATER HOOOOOOOOOOOOOOAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAARRRRRRRRRK!!!!!!!! You give the African Nightmare a few comforting pats on the back. “Twenty-three… twenty-four… and a half. There. That should be the last of ’em.” Azala coughs a couple of times, dry heaves once and turns to face you, wiping his mouth. Groaning, he sits on the floor, leaning back against the commode. “Azala sorry, Mr. Gibbo… especially about the shoes.” You wiggle your bare toes against the tile of the bathroom, remembering the initial splash from Zameer’s first projectile regurgitation. “Hey, I hate shoes any way. And you were doing pretty good till you barfed. I TOLD you chasing those dogs with peanut butter milkshakes was a bad idea.” “But Azala love hot dogs and shakes!” “Even when you’re trying to eat 50 in 12 minutes?” “Hmmmm… true. Azala shouldn’t have to eat that many to win. How does little Ming eat that many? He so tiny!” You shrug at the remembrance of watching the slightly-built Asian slurp down wieners like Steven Smith on Fire Island. “Beats me, partner.” “Azala know what to do next year.” “Oh?” “Yes… Azala eat little Ming! That way all the hot dogs little Ming eat count for Azala!” “Was that a joke?” “Yup! Hahaha!” Azala claps his hands delightedly. You’re given a brief reminder of a certain fannypack-wearing plane enthusiast who is probably still trying to wrap his dim intellect around the notion of you snapping limbs for a living. “Listen, Azala… thanks for today.” “Thanks for what? For puking on Mr. Gibbo’s feet?” Zameer’s massive brow furrows. “Azala not know Mr. Gibbo into things like that…” “No no. Just for putting up with me. I needed a break from… well, from everything. And this really fit the bill. Thanks, pal.” You give Azala a comforting clap on the shoulder, and see his blue eyes getting a little watery. “So… so Azala and Mr. Gibbo are… are friends now?” “I… I guess we are, partner. And no more of this Mr. Gibbo stuff. Makes me feel like I’m in a movie about hobbits or something. Gibbo the Swollen-Livered or something, heh. It’s Dave, big man. From now on. Got it?” Azala nods, his double chin undulating hypnotically. “Now what do you say we go win us some tag titles?” Azala’s face lights up. “Azala love that idea Mr. Gi—Dave.” Zameer smiles shyly and lumbers to his feet. “Can we stop at Sizzler on way? Azala hungry!” You’re not sure where this road will lead. You’re not sure if you’ll ever reconcile with Amy. But it seems you’ve gained a friend for the one you lost, someone who’ll stick by you till the bitter end and—who knows?—you might become a superstar again along the way. For now, that’s enough.
