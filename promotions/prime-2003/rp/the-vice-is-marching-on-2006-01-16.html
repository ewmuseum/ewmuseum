The Vice Is Marching On

16 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01.16.2006. Encounter with Prime-Ates (ala Drunks)Sunday, January 15, 2006Albany, New York There is a stubborn, pouting look in the youth of the latino face of El Vice, even as the cab driver opens the door for him. He gets in without saying a word. The driver knows exactly where to take him. El Vice is grumbling to himself, obviously in a horrible, terrible mood. The cab driver turns on the radio as he enters the car. 'Hey buddy,' he says, 'I figured this might lighten your mood, sorry about your luck.' The driver is young, obviously naive. He doesn't understand that El Vice needs to be left well enough alone. Nevertheless, El Vice listens to the music he has just turned on. There is something very familiar about it, but El Vice cannot immediately recognize it. 'HEY!' he exclaims, upon recognition, suddenly angry, 'THAT'S MY SONG!' The cab driver. 'Yeah,' he acknowledges, 'thought you'd like it.' 'I HAVE A WORD IN MY HEAD! WHAT IS IT?!' El Vice is struggling with his English. 'PLAGYRIST! PLAY ... GA ...' 'Plagiarist?' 'SI! CLARO QUE SI!' 'Open your eyes then, dumbass,' the cab driver says with a grin and a laugh. This leaves El Vice quite confused. 'But yes, I thought you would like to hear some of this ol' classic country.' 'THAT MAN STOLE MY SONG!' El Vice angrily shouts. 'How dare you play this ... dishonesty?!' 'What are you talking about?' the driver asks. 'This song has been out since well before ... you and I were born, mate.' 'What are you now, Australian?' El Vice inquires. 'I am not your mate, despite rumors to the contrary ... that haven't been spread yet ... but are sure to start now ... ' 'You're crazy, man,' the driver rationalizes. 'I can't believe you take yourself this seriously. It's just a gimm - ' El Vice cuts him off. 'ERES NADA BUT EL HOUND DOG, YOU IDIOT! HOW DARE YOU TRANSLATE MY SONGS INTO ENGLISH?! THEY'RE JUST PERFECT IN SPANGLISH!' The cab driver has had enough. 'Alright,' he says. 'Out. Go. Get out of the cab. No, don't even pay me.' 'I wasn't going to, hombre,' El Vice snarls. 'You should be ashamed of yourself. I am a professional wrestler. I am an ICON of this business. And a damn good singer. So good, even, that people plagiarize me. No, don't give me that look. I'm not coming back in. I know you're sorry. Hey, wa - ' 'Asshole,' the driver yells as he drives away, flipping El Vice the bird. El Vice flips his ring finger up at the driver, completely dumbfounded by the expression. El Vice makes his way onto the sidewalk, his figure stepping into and out of the illumination of distant headlights. On the sidewalk there is a sponsored bench. El Vice takes a seat on the bench, his arms extended along the back. 'I was SO CLOSE!' he justifies. 'There it was, the pin, before my eyes. I was only inches, inches away from breaking it up. Chet Worth gets the victory. I get the loss, but I have still not been pinned. I proved my worth in that match, certainly. These fans know that I am a potent young force, not to be messed with in PRIME. They now understand that while I do have quite the bark, I also have quite the bite, as these filthy Americans might say. Stupid idiom, of course.' 'Huh huh,' someone guffaws, 'stupid idiom, that's repetitive.' A drunk man makes his way over toward the bench, with two other, semi-drunk, friends keeping him up. 'Sorry buddy,' they offer as an apology, 'he just ... tact is lost on him at the moment.' El Vice does not accept this apology. 'I've had an awfully poor day,' El Vice grumbles. 'I don't want to put up with a bunch of drunks.' That doesn't stop the middle drunk, who plops his weight onto the bench beside El Vice. El Vice finds his arm smashed between the man's obesity and the bench. El Vice attempts to tactfully remove it, but fails to remove it, although he does fail with tact. 'So we were just at this rasslin' show, weren't we boys?' the drunk slurs. Finally El Vice wrenches his arm free, but it is tingling, asleep. 'Yes we were, Doug,' they answered, hoarsely. 'So the drunk has a name?' asks El Vice, with a whip of an edge to his voice. 'Well of course I do,' he answers, 'ever'body gots a name, donay? Speakin' of, whats yers meesta?' 'El Vice,' El Vice answers. 'Now that there is an int'restin' one,' Doug mutters, 'that sounds like ... Mongolian. Are you a Mongol?' 'No,' El Vice answers, annoyed. 'I bet you are!' Doug says with a laugh. He slaps El Vice on the back. 'They built a big goddamn wall to keep you out of China! Ain't that sad? I mean ... there's billions of people in China, and they built a wall just to make sure that you couldn't get in.' And he guffaws. The two semi-drunks shake their heads. 'So anyway, we just came from this rasslin' show. And there was some guy there, that thought he was Elvis!' 'Elvis is English for El Vice,' El Vice says. 'Yeah, whatever,' Doug says, with a wave. 'And he got his ass whooped by some guy named Chet Worth. I mean, that's got to be a Papau New Guinish name. Don't you think so, Bart?' 'Definitely,' Bart says. 'I said Bart, not Andrew!' Doug mutters. 'I am Bart,' Bart says. 'Absolutely!' answers Andrew. 'That's what I thought,' Doug says, 'don't ever let me forget that you're the good one, Bart.' 'Anyway,' Doug says, 'he gots his ass whooped. And that really was great. I enjoyed it. Because he thinks he's Elvis. But he's not. He's just a guy dressed like Elvis. What's the word ... he's a ... a, uhh ... ' 'Thief?' 'No, no,' Doug says, 'close though. It rhymes. Fief! He's a fief! Hey, speakin' of, you dress an awful lot like him. Who was you talkin' to earlier, anyhow?' 'Myself,' El Vice says. 'Now that's just weird, I had a grandma once that - ' El Vice gets up and leaves. The drunk never notices. After about a minute of walking down the sidewalk, El Vice hears an exclamation. 'HEY!' ... 'WHERE'D HE GO? WHICH WAY DID HE GO? Oh hell, forget it. You got another Killean's, Bart?' 'Andrew.' 'Yeah, whatever,' ... 'well do ya?' El Vice shakes his head. It has not been a good day. He sticks his hands in his pockets and slows down. He looks up into the starlit sky. 'I cannot believe my luck,' El Vice mutters, 'I come all the way from El Paso, I put in time and effort, and I can't beat some American bastard named Chet Worth. I can't break up the three-count. I can't do what I was trained to do. Oh my. I have a situation here. And now I've got some kind of tag match next week, with some guy I don't even know against two guys ... I don't even know. This is just ridiculous. I don't know anyone. And I don't like my odds either. I don't like putting my winning percentage partially in the hands of someone else. This is not good news, not good news at all ... ' ' ... I'm going to have to focus. I'm going to have to be at the top of my game. I showed people something, but I didn't show them enough. They laugh at me when they are drunk. They compare me to that plagiarist. Elvis is English for El Vice. Don't they understand? He's a thief. A plagiarist. A fraud. And one day I'm going to kill him! Kill him like drugs! Made the song before I was born. Pfft.' El Vice shakes his fist. 'I'll show that cab driver. I'll show all of you. I am the original. ERES NADA BUT EL HOUND DOG, POR CRYIN' ALL THE TIME! No one can match my Spanglish vernacular!' El Vice holds his face in his hands. 'One day I will show everyone,' he growls. 'It may not be today. It may not be tomorrow. It may not be next week when I tag with someone I don't know against people I don't know in a federation in which I know no one amongst a group of people among which I know cero, which is spanish for zero. Zero, that's important, remember.' He's instructing everyone, including himself. 'But one day I will be at the top of my game. One day I will work out the rust in the ring, and I will completely dominate this industry. And then the primates will quiver in their branches and their homes. El Vice has entered the building! But until then, I have one question that I will focus on totally, one question upon which I must brood furiously, one question that I must answer.' And then, at the top of his lungs, he screams a question for his faith: 'POR QUE?!' Fade.
