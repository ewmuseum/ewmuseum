Every Cloud...

19 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 19th A sharp, unpleasant pong that was equal parts body odour and antibacterial cleaning fluid lingered in the air, diving up Jason’s nostrils every time he inhaled. His intense travelling schedule since joining the PRIME ranks had ensured that Terminal 8 of the John F. Kennedy International Airport was almost like a second home to him, yet no matter how many times he stepped into the departure lounge he just couldn’t get used to that God-damn smell. Dressed in an outfit of denim and leather befitting a man of his relative notoriety, Jason reclined in the uncomfortable plastic chair, hoping to rest his tired eyes before the gate opened. Any notions of relaxation were immediately shattered, however, as an ear-piercing feminine shriek cut through the background noise at a completely unnecessary decibel. Another fuckin’ argument, Jason thought. A quick glance to his right confirmed this; a young couple engaged in a full-on domestic, shamelessly bawling obscenities at one another while the baby in the pram between them ran its tear ducts dry without relent. It must’ve been the fifth family dispute he’d seen boil over since he’d arrived, and Jason had only been in the airport for fifteen minutes. Meanwhile, a suit-clad businessman strolled by Jason, furiously yelling into a cell phone at a deafening volume. Natas offered him a threatening glare as he strolled passed. S’right Clean Shirt, keep on walkin’, Jason thought, b’fore I jam that damned thing halfway down yer windpipe. “Ex-excuse me?” a faltering, high-pitched unexpectedly chimed, catching the New Yorker off-guard. For a moment he kept his gaze focused on Clean Shirt, who was still shouting down the phone, but his addressor became considerably harder to ignore the second time he spoke. “Sir?” With nought but absolute indifference lingering in every crevice on his rugged face, Jason finally turned to face the voice. A young boy of no more than eleven or twelve years of age stood before him, much to Jason’s surprise. “Are you Jason Natas from PRIME?” the kid asked, unable to hide the excitement in his voice. He had a couple of milk teeth missing on the top of his mouth and his medium-length blonde hair was sideswiped in a way that his mother probably thought looked “cute”. Clearly energized by the sight of a real-life professional wrestler, he beamed from ear to ear. “Sure. What is it, kid?” Jason croaked in the friendliest voice he could mutter. The child produced an open copy of this month’s edition of Primetime Central Magazine and a ballpoint pen, practically thrusting them into Jason’s chest. “Can you pweeeaaassseee sign this? You’re one of my favourite wrestlers in the world! Well, after Lindsay Troy. And Tony Rolo. And Bryan Dawkins.” Jason found that hard to believe, and snorted as the words trailed from the child’s mouth. How he could be anyone’s favourite wrestler, let alone a twelve year old boy’s, was completely beyond his comprehension. Even Jason Natas wasn’t cold enough to shoot him down though; he was just a harmless, timid kid looking for an autograph after all. Natas parted his lips to respond, but before he could utter a single word the little blighter was at it again. “Are you going to ReVolution? I am too! Mom and Dad are taking me ‘cos I got good grades this term!” “Well, not exactl-“ “We got front row seats and everything!” the energized little runt was practically bouncing off the walls as Jason gave up trying to communicate with him and began to scrawl his signature across the page. “Xavier Kannon’s going to kick your ass,” the boy suddenly stated bluntly. Jason groaned. Just when it looked like his headache was finally starting to vanish… * * * * * ”Not fucking good enough, Jason,” said Mr. Najera as he violently slammed the taxi door behind him. “You know we’re going to have to do something about this, right?” Jason barely had a chance to fasten his seatbelt as the inevitable verbal siege from one of his agents began. The cab sped away from PRIME’s Chicago headquarters as Jason began to conjure a suitable response. “Fuck d’ya exact? Really. They knew I wasn’t some fuckin’ ‘sports entertaining’ fairy when they signed me up, and know they wanna fuckin’ complain?” “That’s not the point!” snapped Najera. “You can’t just go out and beat the hell out of people every single night, you know. This isn’t some street-fighting bullshit, Jason; this is the biggest wrestling company in the world!” “Why not? S’a combat sport, isn’t it?” The Hispanic tycoon slammed the palm of his hand into his forehead, frustrated by his client’s bullheadedness. “That was your secondary disciplinary meeting in four months, you know what that means?” He continued without giving Jason a chance to respond; “One more and you’re out. Finished. Done.” “Least then I wouldn’t have ta sit he-“ “Save your breath, Jason,” interrupted Najera as beads of perspiration began to build up on his brow. The meeting with Lisa Tyler had taken a lot out of him; he knew that Jason wasn’t exactly the easiest guy in the world to work with, but he had no idea that so many complaints had been lodged against his client. He knew what had to be done, though, so he reached into the breast pocket of his fine Italian suit and pulled out his cell. “Loft? Yeah, it’s me,” he began after a couple of rings. “Listen, you know what thing we talked about earlier? Yeah, the ‘training’. Put the wheels in motion, meeting didn’t go so well. Okay. Catch you later.” “Hell was that all about?” Jason asked as his agent snapped his phone shut. “We’re going to fix you before you get yourself fucking fired, Jason,” Najera nodded. “Got it all sorted out...” * * * * * “Boo!” The immediacy of the pre-pubescent voice took Jason by surprise, and not for the first time today. He shuddered instinctively, accidently bashing his knee against the chair in front of him. With a sigh Jason slowly turned his head; the kid was really starting to push the envelope. “Wot’cha doin’?” said the familiar, twelve year old face that was pressed in the gap between Jason’s chair and the one next to him. He grinned playfully; clearly the novelty of being sat behind a professional wrestler hadn’t quite worn off yet. “Dyin’ on the inside,” Jason huffed as he turned away from his blonde-haired tormentor. “Billy, sit down!” a sharp female voice barked behind Jason. “How many times do I have to tell you?!” “But mom-“ “No buts, Billy! Now sit down, or there’ll be no wrestling for you this Wednesday night!” “Hmph!” “Next time I’m flyin’ business class,” Jason mumbled under his breath. “I’m sorry sir, is he distracting you?” Billy’s mother asked through the gap between Jason’s seat and the window. “He gets a little excited sometimes…” “Ya know you can see a doctor for that,” he sneered in response. Even through there was no verbal retort, Jason could hear the boy’s parents whispering various disparaging remarks. He wasn’t about to confront them though; an airborne fracas was the last thing that he needed given his recent run-in with PRIME officials. Jason reached into his pocket and pulled out his MP3 player, pressing his thumb down on the power button. He wasn’t so sure if he was allowed to use it on the flight, but fuck it, it sure beat listening to Billy and his mother arguing again. Mudhoney began to pound through his headphones as he gently pushed them into his earlobes. An appropriate choice of music, he thought, given his destination. To say that Jason Natas wasn’t looking forward to relocating to Seattle would have been a massive understatement. He’d never actually visited the cold, grey city himself, but he’d heard all about its coffee-obsessed, flannel-wearing half hippy half yuppie populace, and the reasons for his sudden West Coast relocation didn’t exactly fill Jason with joy either; if he ever found out who it was that had complained about him, he swore that he’d wring their necks. He knew absolutely nothing about the guy – he presumed it was a guy – that was going to mould him into a “better athlete” or the gym he ran; heck, he didn’t even know the guy’s name. In fact, the very notion that he needed extra training filled him with scorn; alright, so he wasn’t the most graceful grappler inside the ring, but he got the job done… right? “WEEEEE! BOOSH! KABLAM!” Even over the top of his tunes Jason could hear Billy. The kid was slamming a couple of action figures, accompanying this act with some over-the-top sound effects. His parents didn’t even bother to stop him this time. Jason clenched his fist and gritted his teeth. Only another thirty minutes of trying to resist the urge to bury his fist in Billy’s face. * * * * * ”Uh-huh. Yeah. Alright, that’s fine, but seeing as we’re booking this thing for almost three months, any chance of a little discount?” Mr. Loftman asked his Nokia as he paced back and forth in the office. “That works.” He smiled; obviously his haggling had paid off. Across the room from him the fax machine fed a single sheet of warm, white paper into the grateful hands of Mr. Jewell. Amazing how these things can send a document all the way across the country in just a couple of seconds, thought Jewell as he gently tore the paper away. “Fantastic. He’ll be arriving on Monday, I’ll make sure he knows the arrangements. It’s Jason Natas, yeah. What time can he pick the keys up at? Excellent. Great. Pleasure doing business with you.” Loftman couldn’t help but adopt a Cheshire Cat grin as the telephone exchange came to an end. Jewell noticed this; “what are you so happy about, then?” he asked. “We’re finally getting rid of the scumbag, what do you think I’m happy about!?” “Settle down; it’s only for a couple of months.” “I know, but at least this way I’m not gonna have to deal with his bullshit for a little while! No more disciplinary meetings, no more complaints… hopefully.” He glanced down at the sheet in his partner’s clutches, “that from the gym?” Jewell nodded. “It’s a hefty bill, but I guess you get what you pay for.” “Of course. It might seem quite extravagant sending Jason all the way over to Seattle, but this guy’s apparently one of the best in the business; retired less than a year ago, years worth of training experience… I tell you, Jason Natas is going to go from over-hyped street thug to sleek pro-wrestling machine in no-time!” “I don’t know, Loft. I mean, sure, I know this guy was a decorated grappler and all, but I’ve heard all about his reputation. Apparently he was one of the biggest jerk-offs in the business…” “Yeah? Well, he should get along with Jason pretty well then,” laughed Loftman. “Shall I call Naj, tell him the good news?” Jewell nodded. “You do that. I’ll get to sorting out Jason’s plane tickets.” * * * * * The elements bombarded Jason from the very moment he stepped off the yellow and green Metro Transit bus. Immediately he pulled the hood of his black Rwake band sweatshirt over his head and tightened the cords, restricting his vision to a small porthole of cloth. Walking against the howling gale and the droplets of rain it seemed to take great pleasure in throwing at him; Jason followed the directions given to him by the bus driver. If the advice was sound it shouldn’t take him any longer than a couple of minutes to reach the gym. The Washington evening was dark and grim with the lashings of rain pouring down from the heavens being the icing on the cake. At this time of night, in Jason’s Bronx neighbourhood, a man would think twice about walking the streets alone, but this Seattle district had none of the lingering threat and menace of New York City. The sidewalks were barren and the traffic was sparse to say the least; the dearth of life in this part of town reminded Jason of a Specials song that he hated. As Mother Nature battled against him Jason questioned his decision to come out here at this time of night. He wasn’t due to report in at the gym until tomorrow morning and it was just gone ten ‘o clock so there was absolutely zero chance of there being any hangers-on around. Still, he had nothing better to do. He’d already settled into his swank yet somewhat bare apartment and had run out of things to do, so he thought he’d track down the gym to save himself getting lost in the morning. Jason crossed a street and turned right, before noticing a small, wooden sign swinging wildly in the feral weather conditions. The dim streetlight illuminated just enough of the sign to show an image of a small wrestling ring, but unfortunately the paint where the text once was had almost completely flaked away. He stood dead in his tracks and glanced once at the name on the door, before reaching into the depths of his hoody and pulling out the small, crumpled piece of paper he’d written the address on. It was the right place, sure enough. “Sure don’t look like much,” Jason shrugged, as he caught sight of Sonny Silver’s Silver Lining Academy for the very first time.
