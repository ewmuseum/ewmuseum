The Second State

26 August 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26/08/09 Why do people fear death? Death is the one certainty in our finite time in this world, it is something that we should prepare for and embrace when the time comes. It is simply a case of moving on to a new state of existence. However that fear is created by the unknown. A compound terror compels us all as we struggle to comprehend anything, other than the world that we see around us. The ground, the sky and people all around us, we take it all for granted. What is there in the next state? Is it better than the short existence that we live? No-one knows. And that's the scary thing. For years religion has used the next state as a method of controlling the masses, if you sin you will go to hell, if you live a righteous life you will be rewarded with eternal joy and happiness in heaven. This of course is just the Christian perspective and as a result cannot be one hundred percent correct as there is no, one true faith. Sikhism, Hinduism, Judaism, Buddhism I could go on, all have their own opinions on the next state. For centuries 'mediums' have used cold reading as a profit making technique. Preying on the distressed and grieving people, who have lost someone dear to them, pretending to communicate with them and deliver messages from beyond the grave. The real victims are the one's being used, who are so caught up in their grief that they are looking for answers, or some acknowledgement of the next state. And not because of their lost loved ones. But because they fear for their own existence and the thought that is normally in the back of their minds, has suddenly been thrust to the forefront making their face up to their own mortality. A church bell echoed ominously across the graveyard, as a light summer shower formed a mist which obscured view for more than a few meters. The graveyard itself was kept in good condition, the graves well tendered and cared for, both by ground staff and loved ones, numerous gravestones decorated with floral wreaths and photos of the family and friends. Towards the centre of the cemetery was an open grave with seven people stood around it, four of them were pallbearers dressed in dour black suits, each clutching a velvet rope tightly intended for the lowering of the coffin into the earth and the bodies final resting place. Stood silently at the head of the grave was an aged vicar, who if truth be told was wondering how many funerals he would conduct before someone presided over his own. His robes were as tired and worn as his face, but he wore them with a dignified pride, like a soldier clutching to his medals earned years ago. As he clutched the bible tightly in his coarse and jagged hands he looked up at the two mourners who were stood quietly looking at the ornate coffin. The vicar could not help but wonder, why there was no more than the two people present. He had always seen funerals, not as a sorrowful time and of distress, but as a celebration of life, sharing in memories and a chance to say goodbye as they moved onto something far greater. There was always a pang of worry in the vicars stomach when he saw a low turnout, generally it meant that the deceased had not led a life to be celebrated and as such, in the views of God,would not have much to look forwards to in the next life. "Shall we begin?" The vicar asked with a raspy voice, trying to hide his inner beliefs and be as sympathetic to the couple as possible. The two other people were Brandon Pierce and Claire. The two were stood side by side, Brandon clasping onto Claire's hand as a sign of support. Both were sombrely dressed as per the occasion dictated and was a far cry from the razzle dazzle of their work a day world. Following the death of Claire's father, she had been living he life in a daze, she had continued working with Brandon as best as possible, but neither of them had their mind in the game. A sub par showing at Colossus VI against Troy Douglas had shown that quite emphatically. However Brandon had been a rock in her time of need, the man she had once seen as nothing more than a piece of meat had grown into someone, so much more. He had been kind and compassionate towards her and yet demanded nothing in return. It was as though he had changed entirely and was a new person, simply occupying the old body. She felt her hand squeezed tightly by Brandon as she turned her head towards him, she saw his sympathetic eyes gazing back down at her, as he nodded that it was time to start and time to say goodbye. Secretly Brandon was not all that emotionally distraught at the death of Steve, the man had been the bane of his existence for years, every major event in his life could be linked back to the man. It was as though his destiny had been predetermined and he was little more than a puppet with Steve pulling the strings. With his passing though, the strings had been cut and an overwhelming sense of freedom flowed through Brandon. Now was not the time for joy though, now was a time for Claire to say goodbye and for her to exorcise her own demons. "Certainly." Claire's voice broke as she spoke, however she maintained her composure and allowed herself a slight cough to clear her throat. The light mist of rain, took form into something much heavier, large droplets fell from the sky and immediately began to seep through clothing, soaking everyone to the bone. The noise of water bouncing off the sleek wooden coffin began to ring in all their ears as the vicar began to speak. "Our time on this earth is a short one, its what we do in that short time that counts. Steven Lester Green was at heart a family man and in his stead leaves behind his daughter Claire Green, who will carry forth memories of him until passing. It is in this passing that we can truly appreciate what sort of man Steven was, for when something is in front of you, you cannot truly see who they are." As the vicar spoke, Claire nestled herself into Brandon who moved his arm to hold her tighter as the rain continued to rattle off the coffin. Her eyes had not left it and whilst she was sad, the rain would not conceal her tears for there were none, it was as though she was devoid of all emotion and just numb. "At the the centre of Steven's life was work, but only ever to provide for his family and his family knew this. Yet Steven always regretted the lack of time that he had with his family, but could never quite get the balance correct, something that was true to his final days." The vicar paused and looked over to the couple obviously filled with grief, but stood as rigid as a statue. It was a common reaction, you had the hysterical one's who would fling themselves on the coffin, so full of denial of the situation. You had those that cried uncontrollably, but ultimately knew that nothing could be done and it was more an out pouring of grief. Then you had the third stage, silence and acceptance. It was the third stage that always worried the vicar the most. To see someone that calm and collected, without even a slight out pouring of grief worried him. "Claire, would you like to say anything?" The vicar asked in muted tones as Claire nodded and stepped forwards towards the coffin, the rain continuing to hammer down on the casket. Placing a hand on the cold wet wood, as though trying to maintain some sort of connection with her father. "I never gave you the sort of respect that you deserved as a father and a man. Only in your death do I realise what I truly had and I have lost. I will never forget you and I will avenge you. I will find out what happened, I promise. For now though I have to say goodbye." She leans down and plants a soft kiss against the casket, before stepping backwards to Pierce and hugging him once again. "You did fine there hun." Whispered Brandon into Claire's ear. "I'm going to find out what happened to him, I swear." The vicar signals to the pallbearers who begin to lower the coffin into the earth very slowly and for the first time Claire shows a sign of emotion as she swallows heavily. "Lord we ask you to take Steven into your salvation from this mortal coil and grant unto him eternal life. We ask you to grant Steven peace forever more." "Earth to Earth" The vicar tosses a handful of dirt as the casket is lowered into the ground. "Ashes to Ashes" Brandon Pierce is the next to take a handful and throws the it silently onto the casket which was now far lower into the ground. "Dust to Dust" Claire takes the final handful and stands on the edge of the grave. The casket has now reached its final resting place as the Pallbearers step backwards and allow Claire her last moment with her father. "I will never forget you." And with those final words, she tosses the dirt onto the casket and turns away back to Brandon, but continues to walk past him towards the exit of the cemetery, leaving Brandon and the vicar in the falling rain. "What do I do for her vicar?" I've never been through anything like this before, she has just been so quiet and withdrawn and intent on finding out what happened to her father." Brandon had moved to stand next to the vicar, shaking his hand and thanking him for his service. "It's important at a time like this to be there for her, but to also keep her grounded, you can't get her get so wrapped up in her guilt and grief that she does something ridiculous. The world is a dark enough place without delving into the depths of your own mind. You just have to be there for her Brandon, that's all you can do." Brandon nods and turns to where Claire had left and begins to follow her, leaving the vicar stood silently in the rain. Who spends a few moment over the grave as he watches Brandon walk away and cannot help but think of the two he had seen today. A couple that was clearly in love, but he feared that ultimately the grief that was flowing through Claire would be the end of them ultimately, however life is a fickle thing and you cannot know what it possibly holds, all he could do was pray for them. ~ ~ ~ The journey home was mostly uneventful and experienced in silence. As the miles passed the tension in the car began to grow, but not a level where it was going to combust. It was more a situation where neither knew exactly how to handle things. It did however offer an opportunity to mull on the events of the last few weeks. For Brandon, it had been all change. Very much a case of all change not just his name but within himself. Had it not been for the personal problems as Colossus, the outcome could very much have been different. He felt more confident than he had done in an age. As he strode out to face Troy Douglas a new found sense of direction flowed through him. He was teeming with confidence and there was a new bite in his persona which had appeared from nowhere. With this bite he was going to tear through PRIME, the previous incarnation was now nothing but a distant memory. Anyone that was to bring it up again would soon know about it. The day that he had changed his name officially, he had signed a brand new contract in PRIME. As far as he was concerned he was just a brand new wrestler, looking to make a point and that would begin as of now, anyone who dared to bring up the Meat persona would be met with a fist. The fact that numbers were thinning out in PRIME meant it was all the more important to cement himself as a star right now, as new and hungry wrestlers would be bought in, he needed to put himself ahead of the pack and nothing would do that more than a strong showing, maybe even a victory against Chainz. A man who himself was now tearing his way through PRIME and had the Intense Title as a scalp as a result. "Are you coming with me to California? I was thinking maybe the sun would do you some good, a chance to relax before and after the show? Maybe make a little rest of it?" Brandon asked as he placed his hand lightly on Claire's leg as she changed gear abruptly, as though to signal her lack of interest or more aptly reflect her mood. "Yeah, I'll be there don't worry. I want to discuss some finer points of your contract with Tyler, there are some issues that I need to iron out." Claire's eyes remained firmly fixed on the road ahead of her, her voice was cold and distant, the events of the morning clearly playing on her mind. "And what about the trip, the rest and relax? I mean come on, we'll be in California a perfect place to relax and get away from it all for a while." Brandon was extremely worried about Claire, she needed time to grieve, she hadn't allowed herself any up till this moment in time. "Brandon, I know you are trying to help but if I stop for even the slightest moment, I think. If I begin to think I get upset and I can't allow that to happen. When I get upset all I can think about are my last words to my father." Claire still didn't turn to even glance at Brandon. "What were they?" It was a question that was lingering in the air before it was even asked. "Claire?" "I told him, he was a bad parent and that I didn't love him. Those were the last words he heard me say Brandon, so if I stop and think about that it makes me sick to my stomach." And with those words there it was, the very first outbreak of emotion that Claire had allowed herself to show, a single solitary tear began to flow down her cheek, her chest was heaving up and down as weeks of pent up emotion was beginning to show. "Claire, you know he didn't believe that right? He knew you loved him, he also knew who you are. A fiery tempered woman, who is a lot more susceptible to emotional hurt than she would have others believe. I knew your father and everything that he did was for you and he knew that you loved him. Regardless of whatever you said in the heat of the moment." Although it was just a few words it was much more than that. The words that flowed from Brandon's mouth were almost like a healing elixir, as Claire listened to the words and tears began to stream down her eyes. "I know it hurts, I truly do. There is nothing wrong in crying Claire. There is nothing wrong in grieving for your father. It doesn't make you any less of a person. In fact I would be worried if you didn't. Claire it's a perfectly natural thing. Now if you want to throw yourself into work, then that's fine. But if you want to take time out to grieve and remember your father that is fine too. I love you and will do whatever I can to help you, but you have to realise." "This wasn't your fault." The tears continue to stream down Claire's face, but a small smile forms slightly on the side of her mouth. She is not happy, far from it but more content at her man understanding her, for being given the choice to do what she chose. "Thank you."
