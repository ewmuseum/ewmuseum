Ensemble - Episode 2

8 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 8th, 2007 Dust filled the air as the yellow Cadillac Cab pulled into the diner parking lot somewhere in the middle of the Arizona desert. The diner was metal on the outside, old and weathered in its age. As the car came to a stop, the windows were down and their conversation could be heard. “It’s not that bad, Big Bear. You’re only one win back. So what, ya lost a few matches. It won’t be tha-“ Before Cabby could finish, Big Bear had opened the door and already stepped outside. He squinted as the sun beat down on his face, still sore from the match at ReVolution 132. He threw on a pair of black sunglasses and his hair back, placing his black cap on his head. Before he took a step, he stopped in his tracks and looked over his shoulder. “After this, you’re taking me to the airport. This isn’t worth the humiliation. You got that?” Cabby slumped back in his chair in disbelief. Big Bear looked ahead, marching into the diner, not caring whather or not Cabby had anything else to say. The bell dinged as he came through the front door, catching eye of a beautiful brunette in a waitress outfit dead ahead. She stopped and kind of smirked at him, but not enough to laugh. He scanned the rest of the counter. “Perfect.” It was lined with truckers and kids, some old folks too. He spotted an open seat and made himself comfortable by letting his hair down and removing his sunglasses. The waitress walked by him as he flipped the coffee cup over and picked up a menu from the stack. “Cream?” She said as he read over the menu, not responding initially. She chomped on her gum, seemingly demanding a reply. Munson lifted his eyes, making contact and smiling politely. He shook his head. “I take it black.” She nodded, walking off to refill the other customers’ mugs. He watched her as she hustled away, admiring her figure and assets. Perhaps a bit too much, as he found his head turned 90 degrees. Sitting back, he grinned at his stereotypical male behavior. He drank his coffee, wincing at the initial shock. She made her way back to him. “Having anything to eat?” “I’ll have the ham and cheese omelet with a side of hash browns and bacon, plea-“ Big Bear stopped as a loud racket came at the end of the counter. A blonde, greasy looking sleezeball was sitting with his boots perched up on the counter, pounding his empty coffee mug. “Come on, sweetheart. If Daddy don’t get his coffee, little lady don’t get paid. Remember that, darlin?” Big Bear watched as the man bellowed through the narrow train-car sized restaurant. She finished writing down Monsoon’s order, but he was more interested in the racket down yonder. Leaning back, the greasy sleazebag caught Big Bear’s eye and paid no mind to him. As the woman approached the man, a few of his buddies joined him on each side. They were the prototypical bald, bad looking guys. They were much more intimidating than the skinny runt with the big mouth, that’s for sure. Big Bear, however, just sipped on his coffee and waited for his breakfast. While waiting for the food, Big Bear noticed the chef in the back. He had turned around at one point, looking down at the end of the restaurant at the trio of jerks and shook his head. Big Bear watched him kind of cower into the shadows of the diner kitchen, handing all the cash in his pockets to the waitress. He didn’t see him after that, though. Interesting… “Ham and cheese, right?” Big Bear stopped day dreaming and looked up at the eloquent and striking woman. He smiles, nodding and pulling away his silverware. “Oh, hey! Thanks. By the way, you holdin’ up alright?” The waitress was busy filling more coffee into the pot, and was sort of taken aback by the question. She looked away, then smiled and let out a sigh through her nose – shaking her head in refusal. She walked into the back quickly, avoiding anymore interrogation from the mustached stranger. “It ain’t easy bein’ pushed around. Trust me, I know. Sometimes you have to fight back, no matter how big the other guy is.” Soon, more pounding was coming from the same vicinity of the counter. She hustled her way out and scooped up the fresh coffee pot on her way, but was also wiping her eyes dry as she hurried down to the other end of the diner. Big Bear watched as she got down there, and was met with some strong opinions. “What’s wrong, baby? Getting’ fat? Cause you sure as hell ain’t movin’ as fast as you should be!” She shook her head and apologized in body language, filing their cups up pronto. As she did though, one of the bald fellas began to rub her arm. “What’s the matter darlin’, not this month? Don’t want the second benefit of doing business with us?” She jerked away, staggering backward behind the counter out of their reach. Except the big man stood up and grabbed her with his big mitts, yanking her close. The greaseball blonde jumped up on a stool and wrapped his slimy paw around her neck, inches away from her face. “Did you forget the agreement, sweetie? You do anything we want.” She shut her eyes and whimpered a bit as his breath was nasty and stale of booze and cigarettes. The other big man hopped the counter and popped open the register, laughing as the other two began to have their way with the young woman. A large thud was made as a stoll hit the hard floor. “Is there a problem, gentleman?” Big Bear stood up, slowly taking a few steps down the restaurant aisle. The big man turned his head, locking onto the smaller, but not by much, Big Bear. The blonde popped his ugly mug over his shoulder, peering his ratty little eyes into Munson’s. Big Bear held his palms out. The blonde turned to the woman, getting closer to her face and smiling. “No, it’s okay. No problem. Just, uhh… nothing, it’s okay. Go ahead, don't worry about it.” The waitress had a slight hiccup in her voice as her throat was still in the grip of the smaller scumbag. Big Bear took a few more steps closer. “You guys need money or somethin’? If that’s so, just let her-“ As Big Bear made his way closer, he was cut off by the other big man, whipping out a switchblade and waving it in Munson’s face. “How about you get lost, hillbilly? This ain’t no pretend time, this is real. I will cut you’re a-“ and just like that, Big Bear had sent his fist into the nose of this giant goon, and then followed with a knee to the gut, gripping onto the back of his shirt and belt. Big Bear swung him around and heaved him through the front door of the diner and crashing into the hot summer sun. Big Bear tuned around as pieces of the doorframe fell in the aftermath. Monsoon dusts off his hands, looking up in a ‘Who’s next?’ expression into the direction of the other two. “Like I said… is there a problem?” Suckers. Thought Big Bear as they took the bait. They gave each other a look, dropping the girl behind the counter and charging toward the burly man. He lowered his shoulder tackled the large man, sprawling out on the floor. As he did so, the smaller man was knocked down as well. Big Bear made it to his feet as the patrons in the area all scattered to the back of the diner, watching on in hope that these heathens get what’s coming to them. The big man wrapped his arm around Big Bear’s leg, dragging him down to the linoleum floor and out of reach to grab the big mouth little bastard from scrambling away. The man then clubbed Big Bear over his back with his large arms, knocking him onto his hands and knees. He then proceeded to lay the licks to Big Bear, kicking him in the sides. Big Bear fought to get up and exchange a punch with the man before collapsing to his knees. The smaller man had smashed a wooden chair over Big Bear back, ending his threat to their operation. The smaller man wiped the blood off his lip, taking in deep breaths. The runt looked down at Munson, who was still breathing, but barely as he was sprawled out across the floor in a pile of wood splinters. The two cohorts locked eyes as the shorter one pointed toward Big Bear. “Hold that piece of shit still.” The big man wrapped his arms around Big bear, holding him down as the smaller man got in Big Bear face. “This is why you don’t fuck with us, hillbilly.” He then reached around and picked up the woman with ease. For a small man, he was not muscular, but strong enough to fight and pick up 120 lb. women, it appeared. She whimpered some more and looked back toward the kitchen, reaching out for help, but saw nobody. Hoping to find a miracle, she looked down at the battered Big Bear, except his eyes were rolled into the back of his head. Then, she was an inch away from the little dirtball that had started this whole thing. “Oh, shit! Big Bear, this dude was carryin’ some serious cash. Check this… What in the hell-“ Cabby stood in the mangled doorway and looked down to way at the situation in front of him. The big man locked onto Cabby, as did the short man. Before they could do anything though, Big Bear stood straight up, smashing his head into the chin of the second bald goon. He dove backward, gripping his face, letting out a muffled groan of agony. His mouth was bleeding horridly, and he was missing pieces of his teeth, for certain. “I don’t think I caught that.” Big Bear, in the mean time, had stood up and grabbed the smaller man by the throat, holding him into the sky. The man squirmed as the oxygen line to his brain was slowly being cut off, and then somehow gave Munson a shot in the nuts with his black boot. The Big Bear dropped to one knee, wincing as he watched the little runt scramble toward the door. With everything he had inside him, Big Bear stood up, lunging forward far enough to smash his forearm over the back of the blonde man’s head and directly into the hard floor. He was lights out before he hit. Big Bar, on the other hand knelt on one knee, trying to catch his breath. He shirt was torn when he stood up, revealing the furry chest hair but muscular build to his burly body. He held out his hand for the waitress, who looked up at him with her beautiful green eyes. “Tha- thank you. Who are you?” She looked deep into his eyes as he looked into hers, almost now knowing how to answer the question. This was not part of the job, this was just one of the many thing he felt the need to do. To protect the people who make this world a wonderful place. To protect the good from the bad. One town at a time. One diner at a time. One match at a time. “Are you the Big Bear, Munson Monsoon, mister?” Big Bear broke the eye contact with the brunette and looked down to see a young boy, about 7 or 8 years old. “Sure am, buddy.” He smirked, looking back at the woman, who wore a puzzled look on her face. “Oh man! This is awesome! You’re my favorite to win the 5-star challenge, mister Big Bear! I just want you to know, me and all my friends at school’s favorite wrestler is you – the BIG BEAR!” The boy threw his arms in the air as Big Bear looked at him, not in embarrassment. Not in self-reflection, even. Big Bear looked down at that boy and saw the one thing he had been missing all along… hope. He broke the stare at the boy, kind of spacing out here and there thinking about the ReVolution triple-threat match against the world class Johnathon Winters and a mystery opponent. He was also thinking about his performance to this point in PRIME, posting a winless streak of 5 matches. This was it. If there was a shot at the stardom he sought and desired so bad, this was it. “Cabby,” barked out Big Bear, looking down at the woman, smiling and nodding as he walked away. He threw on his sunglasses, walking swiftly past his accomplice, who was still trying to pick his jaw up off the floor. “Get me to Hollywood. I've got a 5-star Title shot to win.” As Big Bear walked out of the diner, all the patrons focused on the blonde afro on top of Cabby’s head, and he was barely coherent. Looking around, he smirked and set the cash on the counter. “There ya go, folks.” He bent over, picking up the black ball cap Big Bear wore in. “Don’t forget your hat, brotha!”
