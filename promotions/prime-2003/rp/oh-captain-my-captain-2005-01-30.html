'Oh Captain, My Captain'

30 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/30/05 ..Free at last, Free at last. God almighty, I was free at last... I was no longer being held in an entrapment with question succumbing cocksuckers numbing my brain. I was no longer trapped inside a barricade of padded walls, nor was I the man under the microscope. Am I still addicted to drugs? Maybe so, but I can't feel anymore. I can eat full meals without that sudden drug withdrawal where that'd be all I'd want for weeks on. I'm sure I still crave it and if it was still in presence I wouldn't hesitate swallowing a pill or two. One man. I'm entering this Halo-Hype match with two dead eyes targeted on one man. One man. This man has threatened me and kept me on my toes. This man introduced me to the path of violence, this man showed me the very road to hell. I am in debt to this man for befriending me, but I am equally disapointed at him for brainwashing me and putting me in position that I wouldn've otherwise backed away from. Nevertheless, I made my exit from Dark Age at the right time when I realized that I was hurting an opposition. The 'humans' of PRIME. I was hurting the people who were trying to protect this federation and for that I am deeply sorry. One man. Vampir, it won't be long until the infection slowly rapes you of your talents and antics. It won't be long until I pay you back for what you did to me. There's a wonderful feeling behind this match. I'm meeting people for the first time and enjoying it to boot. I am becoming my own man and doing things the RIGHT way this time. With the likes of Aimz, Killean, Emilio and Nova backing me up, the force that includes First Blood, Vampir, HIT, Hoyt and Lopez will simply disintigrate. The opposition is a team that unites with another. A team that assists eachother through troubled times. We are the symbols of the now, and the 'now' is the only thing that matters now. We are here to extinguish the funk that's turning PRIME into the scab of an inner city. Through the expense of the late Dr. Klingman, Ozric Mortimer saw a green blinking light just a few feet in front of him. He passed 'GO' and collected a new sense of independance. Something of which he never came to terms with in his tenure of being heavily sedated by the psychiatric doom he was being driven through. A path moreso distinguished by the skeleton figures that lay on the grassy moss that sidelined it rather than by the people who've walked through it. That may be due to the fact that only a few have made it through what Ozric has partially defeated. By partially, I mean he hasn't seen the last of the stigma that put him through so much hell, changed him forever and forced him to down pills that would somehow force the pain to wither away just for that moment until the high was depleted. The pain would once again return, along with his depressive, near suicidal state. For obvious reasons, a sheet of guilt covered him. How could he possibly be happy when his doctor, his friend and sole companion died in his sleep? It just seemed way too ironic to him. Dreaming that Klingman and him were being held up by New Jersey authorities. Dreaming that Klingman committed suicide by jumping off an apartment complex? Dreaming about Klingman's call of distress where he was telling Ozric how they were going to [b]kill[/b] them both. Either overly uncanny or Ozric was in the right direction of pointing fingers and throwing out assumptions. Nonetheless, he was dead and the funeral was already planned. Jack's relatives located Ozric and made sure he was notified of the tragic news, and was invited to the wake and the funeral. Mortimer was sporting an enhanced look as the facepaint wore off the day he was taken back into Jersey's main mental ward. Through black strings of hair, his face was now somewhat exposed. Ozric rested his head on a stained white pillow on a flower printed twin-sized bed in a run down motel that had the scent of an ashtray and was shady enough to compliment any street whore or low-budget escort service touring the bitter half of Jersey. Ozric was now at halt, as he knew he had to watch his back now since turning on Dark Age. Where could he go from here? What could empower him enough to the point where he could forget about the doctors death? What could stop from believing he was the man behind the doctor's death? Ozric stood up and walked over towards a cheaply made coat-hanger which was in the offset shadows of the corner to the right of the entrance way into the room. A gray jacket was hung up and in the left pocket was a partially torn open evelope. Ozric pulled the envelope out of the pocket and emptied its contents; a letter and a small pin that noted a certain battlion in the Vietnam War. He walked over towards the windowsill that guided the only natural light into the room and placed the pin on it. Ozric then opened up the tri-folded letter and read the blue-inked writing. Dear Son, If you are reading this now you've reached an imperative learning age. Where you've heard things and realized that there were more questions than there were answers at the time. I know you've heard that I was murdered by vicious assailants who believed I stole something precious of theres. I also know that you've heard that your mother was also murdered by those very assailants after locating her through a letter in my wallet that they found in one of my pockets. This letter was given to you by your uncle, Gilbert, when you were much too young to understand the problems evolving around you. Now, you must be in your twenties and it's time for you to know the truth, Nicholas. Gilbert, your uncle, was the high chairmen of this group of assailants and hired a few of them to a job. This job was to kill me and leave not a trace of evidence behind. Unfortunate for Gilbert, I was much too clever for their cheap ploys of attack therefore I turned the table over on the assailants coming after me and ended up killing them in the process. I made a fake note detailing out the situation that I was killed and that my body was removed from the vicinity. Gilbert recieved this letter and was convinced that I was killed. I then hired one of these assailants, at gun point mind you, to send a letter to Gilbert to give to Ozric. A final 'farewell' letter from 'his father'. Gilbert gave it to you years ago and hopefully you followed the guidelines by not reading it until you reached the age of which you were old enough to understand. Thus leading to the point that I am still alive and in hiding from the latter individuals who haven't rested in their journey to find me ... and kill me. Gilbert has discovered that I'm still alive as well. Your mother was never murdered. She's in a prison hospital and has been there for quite a while, to be honest with you. She attacked a group of men in the middle of a busy commuter's block who were gloating about how you were born with many defficienies and a mental disfiguation to boot. Thus, she choked two of the men out and end up turning the third man into a straw-slurping vegetable. She's now on suicide watch at Glen Harbor Prison in the care facility. As for me? I can't tell you. You'll find out in due time and we'll meet once again. As for now, I send my love in this letter. Keep your head up and be brave .. no matter how troubled times may get. Your father, Galvin S. Mortimer Ozric held back tears as he folded the letter back up and intentionally dropped it on the ground. Out of the corner of his eye, he noticed a white light that jolted him backwards as the room got incredibly small. He lowered his head and noticed that the palms of both his hands were etched in circular embers of fire. For some odd reason, they showed no reponse of pain as he shrugged off the confusion and her a loud 'BOOM' come from outside. He ran over to the window and looked outside. Ozric swallowed hard and slapped the side of his head twice before slowly believing what he was seeing. A present-time Vietnam war had erupted in front of his eyes as the inner city turned into a massive grassland with thousands of men marching towards one another. Guns in hand and a look of pure concentration, the soldiers clashed and knocked down the opposition with the handle of their gun before shooting led into their bodies while they were down. Ozric continued to look on... (In his head) - This isn't happening. I'm dreaming... Just .... dreaming.[/FONT] Ozic could hear both forces screaming at one another, shouting commands to their squads and ordering air attacks. 'Another fleet of Congs are coming down the hill to our right. We need an air strike as soon as possible!' '...We apologize sir, but we haven't gotten a go-fly for an immediate air strike.' 'I don't give a damn! Send two bombers over here to take out the two squads charging towards us east bound! We're being bombarded!' His father was in the middle of it all, holding a rifle in one hand and a two-way radio in his left; struggling to move onward with a bullet wound in his right shin. Shreads of flesh could be seen protruding outward from his uniform as he limped forward towards a Cong coming toward him with a bayonnet. His father semi-juked the attempt and grabbed the Cong's bayonnet, stabbing the foreign soldier in the midsection. The midi ring of the phone inside his motel room gave Oz a shudder as he turned around and grabbed the red-colored phone.. 'Hello?' 'Ozric?' '..Yes? I'm a bit busy at the moment...' 'It's Jim Emerson, an ex-coworker of your father's.' 'My father's?' Ozric turned toward the window and realized that this fantisized war was continuing. '...Yes, your father's. I've got some news to tell you. I know where your father is...' 'Oh yeah?' In a bliss of carelessness, Ozric hung up the phone. The conversation didn't hit him as he was mesmorized by the enactment going on outside. The phone started to ring again as Ozric turned his head. He shook his head and slammed the reciever with his left palm, smashing it into pieces. He walked over to the window and looked down at a gentlemen who looked surprisingly just like Ozric. He shook his head in disbelief as he closed his eyes. Holding a rifle and suited in camoflauged armor, he walked over to his father and grabbed the canteen hanging off of his utlity belt. He noticed that there were enemy forces marching towards the two and tried to scream towards them. Nearly the whole motel was alarmed to this incident has the lights inside their room came on. The opening of doors and hollers of drug addicts and whores alike suddenly muffled the war story going on in front of his very eyes. There was a sudden knock at Ozric's door. 'Either open up this door or stop yer' screamin'!' 'Don't you see what's going on outside? Warfare! Warfare I tell you! 'What fuckin' warfare? All we can hear is your dramatic, insane cries for help.' 'Out there .. my father's out there with a gun in his hand and there's Vietnamese soldiers coming to attack him. I need a gun!' The pressure slamming against Oz's ears suddenly ceased as Ozric stumbled backwards and heard one last barotone knock at the door before collapsing into a heap of blankets on the floor. --- It wasn't the same any longer. Mortimer had started his drinking habits without hesitation. Reasoning solely on the fact that he was without his support, his guide, his friend. He was without Dr. Klingman and it suddenly felt incredibly lonely. The tank which held the everlasting tonic of inspiration was depleted and the wondrous world he felt apart of had now become a collapsed star in his eyes. This week's revolution was the most important of them all and he couldn't slip or slide down the pathway now. He had to grip on to each rock and take it step by step. He knew this week's challenge would send him over the edge if he won it. He knew that this was one of the most important matches of his career and he knew that letting down his team-mates was almost as bad as returning to what he struggled in walking away from. One error and suddenly Oz was hanging on the side of the boat by his fingernails; just moments from plummeting into the storm bowels of the ocean waves. Dressing like one just wasn't enough to justify how much of clown Ozric was, and he knew it. Holding a bottle of Jack Daniels in his right hand he tried to stand up, but was too dizzy to even think about doing it. He thought to himself about what he could remember of last night? A phone call and an abundance of rude people trying to knock his door down. Was their some kind of connection? The cell phone that was give to Ozric when he entered PRIME start to chirp. Ozric looked over and reached for it, flipping the flap over and answering it. '...Hello?' 'Ozric?!' 'Hello.' 'Hi, Ozric!' 'Hello.' 'It's Jim Emerson, I called you last night but you seemed to occupied with something else. I need to meet with you as soon as possible .. I think I may know where your father is.' 'My father's dead...' He cut himself off as he noticed the notice the tri-folded note hanging from the windowsill. His eyes were wide and through his drunken state was this slight awareness. '..NO .. NO! My father's alive! I got a letter!' 'Yes! Where are you?' 'The Stop-N-Go Motel on Research Parkway.' 'Ah! Not far from where I am. I'm going to swing by and pick you up. We've got a ton to talk about..' 'Yes ... yes, we do.' Ozric pushed the 'End' button and closed the cellphone up as he felt slighty rejuvenated. His father was alive and he was about to partake on a search to find him. He wasn't too sure about this Jim Emerson character as the name never caught on and the voice was familiar at all. He had a niche that this may be a trap in some way, but Ozric willing to take the chance at this point. He slowly got to his feet and walked over to the table that sat to the right of the bed at the motel. He noticed two white tablets in a plastic bag. He grabbed the plastic bag and opened it, pulling out one of the tablets and staring at it. 'Oh captain, My captain.' He swallowed the pill and exhaled slowly as he grabbed his jacket and quickly left the motel room with the light slam of the door. The bottle of Jack Daniels, tipped over and spilt on the rug. The ticking of the clock hanging above the headboard of the bed. The sudden exit of a wrestler ... of an addict.
