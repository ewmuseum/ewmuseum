The Other Colors of Lies

7 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/7/07 So this is how the world ends. Not with a whimper. Not with a bang. But with a "he’ll be with you in just a moment." Sun Tzu decided that if she was ever dosed with a fatal poison that she would sit in the lobby because time slowed to a crawl within its confines. It wasn’t so much the wait or even the boredom. She had spent entire weeks in solitary confinement during her training. No, it was the fact that she had to appear pleasant during this whole ordeal. Sun Tzu glanced at the pop star pretty receptionist, who gave her a smile worthy of a toothpaste commercial. Sun Tzu returned the smile while alphabetizing the ways she wanted to kill the girl. Asphyxiation… bludgeoning… cutting her throat… decapitation… no, that’s too much like cutting her throat… damn, I can’t believe I’m stuck already… "The assistant secretary will see you now." The receptionist’s melodic voice broke Sun Tzu from her reverie. She realized she hadn’t heard the phone ring or the intercom buzz. Odd, had her senses gotten that dulled living in the media-soaked landscape of America? Perhaps the girl had been e-mailed. Or maybe she was told to make Sun Tzu wait until now even though her appointment was for nearly two hours ago. Sun Tzu definitely wanted to kill the receptionist. Ah, but she was government property and Sun Tzu was nothing if not a good soldier. So she simply walked past her and entered the office past the large wooden doors. The office seemed luxurious, especially for a lowly assistant secretary to the Minister of Agriculture. But then that wasn’t the real title for the well-dressed man behind the desk. Shen was the head of the Ministry of Propaganda. Sun Tzu thought his cover was quite apropos. No one could spread fertilizer quite like Minister Shen. "Please… have a seat." Shen motioned to a leather chair, the cost of which was more than most Chinese made in a month. "I’m fine with standing, sir." The feeling was just starting to return the Sun Tzu’s glutes. Shen shrugged nonchalantly. "As you wish." Shen looked Sun Tzu over in a way she was none too pleased with. "I notice you are not carrying you gold belt with you, my dear." "No, sir. I did not think it was appropriate with my uniform." That and the thing was nauseatingly gaudy. "Ah, yes. I must say you look quite sharp in your official army attire." "Thank you, sir. I thought it would be only proper while back here in China." "Not that I don’t find what you wear wrestling quite fetching as well. I must say General Chang was quite resistant to it. I had to explain the whole concept of marketing to him and trying to maximize your appeal to the masses." Shen chuckled like it was the most absurd idea ever not to see things his way. Was the man completely ignorant of the fact that Sun Tzu shared most if not all of her mentor’s views? Or was he just trying to push her buttons? Regardless, Shen’s talk of "marketing" reeked of capitalism and Sun Tzu hide to fight to keep her disgust from showing on her face. "And your entrance music," Shen continued. "I told him that a more modern song that the Americans enjoyed would be beneficial in our mission. If the song made them cheer upon your arrival, that would be quite the coup. Alas, that has not been the case." Sun Tzu didn’t care if the Americans cheered her or not. She was glad that they hadn’t gone along with the general’s idea of using China’s anthem as her theme. There would be no way she could tolerate the jeering and mockery of those disrespectful American pigs as "March of the Volunteers" played. Plus she loved hip hop, not that any of her superiors really needed to know that. "I’m sorry if my lack of acceptance by the Americans is a disappointment." Sun Tzu mentally applauded herself for being able to say that with a straight face. "That’s all right, my dear. What is important is your in-ring success. And that 5 Star championship of yours is quite the coup. I especially enjoyed how you tied in it with the flag of our great nation. We just might make a propagandist out of you yet." "Just doing my duty, sir." It was a good thing General Chang was not there. Sun Tzu would want to crawl into a hole after being dubbed a potential propagandist. Of course, Chang would most likely have pistol-whipped Shen after the second time he called one of his soldiers "my dear." "There is one concern, my dear. Your relationship with this…" Shen glanced down at a piece of paper on his desk. "Angelo… Deville…" Sun Tzu didn’t know why Shen acted like he was unaware of Deville’s name. Information was Shen’s bread and butter. He probably knew the maiden name of the mother of the vet that fixed Angelo’s childhood pet. Another of his damned decadent affectations. "It’s nothing, sir. He’s nothing. Just a ploy to get under the skin of another competitor." Why did it sound like she was trying to convince herself more than Shen? "You must admit that your behave has been rather… erratic. Like those pictures you sent him that one of his associates posted online." Shen raised one eyebrow. The slight smirk he had made Sun Tzu’s skin crawl. "It’s nothing I’m ashamed of." "And you shouldn’t be from how you looked, my dear." "I meant that any sort of edge my actions might gain me outweigh any potential embarrassment on my part," she practically barked at him. She paused for second before continuing. "The photos were done in a tasteful manner that appreciated the female form. Our culture appreciates that, unlike the Americans’ hypocritical Puritanism." "I think you overestimate the Party’s… sophistication. Nonetheless, there has been little complaint. I believe that has much to do with the quality of the subject." Sun Tzu was tempted to tell him to just go ahead and masturbate right then and there just to get it over with. "I’m glad that I did not bring any embarrassment to the Party. That was not my intent." "No, you were simply using all the weapons at your disposal, my dear. Yes… there could definitely be a future for you here in Propaganda!" For a moment, Sun Tzu felt bile rise up into her throat. She managed to choke it back without any overt reaction. "Thank you, sir. Now if you’ll excuse me, I have an interview with CCTV." That was a lie. It wasn’t until much later, but Sun Tzu just had to get away from Shen. "Of course, my dear," he purred. She realized he was probably aware of her schedule with the media, but figured the bastard would enjoy knowing he could make her uncomfortable. The smirk he had seemed to confirm it. Sun Tzu gave the Minister a curt nod, then turned to leave. As she opened the door, Shen cleared his throat. "Are you positive that you do not have any genuine affection for the American, my dear?" "I am in control, sir." "Of course you are, my dear." Sun Tzu didn’t bother to look back at him as she left the office. She was perfectly aware that he would be wallowing in his amusement at her discomfort. The receptionist smiled at Sun Tzu. She knew the girl had heard the last exchange with Shen. "Bye-bye!" the girl chimed. "I want to shove my fist up your cunt, carry you like a puppet, and fling you out that window," she told the receptionist in English. The girl simply looked confused. "I’m afraid I don’t understand you." "I said that I want to shove my fist up your cunt, carry you like a puppet, and fling you out that window," Sun Tzu repeated, this time in Cantonese. Watching the blood disappear from the girl’s face was the only pleasure Sun Tzu had this entire meeting. As she left the building, she had an epiphany. Defenestration! That’s’ it! Then electrocution… flaying… gutshot… Sun Tzu continued with her homicidal alphabet as she walked the streets of Beijing. It was great to be back home and a beautiful day in spite of a chill in the air. The sun was bright, causing her to take out a pair of sunglasses from her uniform and out them on. She continued down the familiar streets, thinking not about what Shen had said to her, but of her own words. Was she really in control? Was it all a ploy? Sun Tzu had found herself thinking less of one-upping Karina Wolfenden and more about simply having Angelo Deville. Her whole interest in him was a sham, just a psychological tool. But now… was she lying to herself about how she felt? And did this me the lie this whole fiasco was based on was now the truth? "Watch out!" A near collision with another pedestrian ended her self-contained debate. She looked at a young man, about her age, with longish hair and dressed scruffily. To her amazement, Sun Tzu realized that she knew him. But it had been years go… With as much stealth as possible, Sun Tzu got out of bed without waking Sang. None the less, he stirred as she was pulling on her panties. "Nnnnnnn… do you have to?" he mumbled. "Yes," she replied as she fastened her bra. "Going to work naked would cause quite a stir." Sang sat up in bed and rubbed the sleep from his eyes. "I meant leave." "You know I have a job to do. An executive assistant is pretty much on call." "Yeah, but you take off for days." Sun Tzu sighed as she kept dressing. They had this conversation before. "I work for a bank president. He travels a lot, so that means so do I. And it’s not like you keep regular hours yourself." Sang work as a lighting director for a studio. It wasn’t uncommon for him to work fourteen hour days on the set. "Yeah, but at least I get time off between projects. You just work and work." "Sorry, but that’s just the nature of the beast." After slipping on her shoes, Sun Tzu stepped closer to the bed and leaned over to kiss him good-bye. As their lips and then tongues intertwined, she felt him pull her closer to the bed. As tempted as she was, which was very much so, Sun Tzu managed to escape him. She gave Sang a playful slap. "You’re incorrigible." "And that’s why you love me." "You think I do, anyways." "Well, do you love me?" "Of course I do." "Say it." "No. You’ll just have to accept that the fact I tolerate you as proof." "Well, close enough for me. When are you coming back?" "Thursday night, for the thousandth time." "OK, catch ya then, baby." Sang fell back onto the pillows. "Whatever," sighed Sun Tzu. She was smiling like a schoolgirl as she left the apartment. Well, her school had taught demolitions and poison use, but the metaphor still worked. Sang could never really give her a proper good-bye, but he always pestered her about when she was supposed to return. Always… interesting word choice, like they had been together that long. Only a couple moths and that time was broken up by Sun Tzu’s missions. Still, she felt a real connection to the carefree rascal. She had moved much of her stuff over to his place a couple weeks back to maximize what time they did have together. Things were great between them. The only fly in the ointment was the fact that the person she was around him was a complete fabrication. But at least her feelings were real. Sang was the first man that made her wish, just for a moment, that maybe her life had taken a different path. Of course, that was usually after having mind-blowing sex with him, so she thought all kinds of random things then. A car picked Sun Tzu up and drove her to a hospital only a few miles away. She quickly walked in and took an elevator to a floor that was marked "Administration" on the directory. But when the doors opened, it revealed several men in military uniform, including General Chang. "Good. You’re here. Get ready." Sun Tzu began changing out of her clothes, not caring that there were soldiers as well as doctors around to see her. Someone brought her an outfit that looked decades out of date. She put it on and checked her hair in the glass of a window. It was cut in an old-fashioned style, like something from the 50’. Sang liked it, he was a classic movie buff. Once she was satisfied with her appearance, Sun Tzu turned to the general. "How is he today, sir?" "He has been fairly awake," replied Chang. "And he’s been asking for you." "For me, sir… or for his wife that’s been dead the last twenty years?" "In this mission, it’s all one and the same, soldier." Sun Tzu nodded, then General Chang escorted her down the hall to a patient’s room with the door closed. Chang stopped a few feet away from it. "Ready?" "Ready, sir." Chang motioned to the door with his head. Sun Tzu opened it and entered the room. Instead of the sterile whiteness of a typical hospital room, this one was made to look like a homey bedroom. In the bed was a very old, very sick looking man. Sun Tzu smiled at him warmly and approached the bed. "And how are you feeling, my sweet?" The rheumy eyes of General Fong looked at. There was a spark of recognition in them. "Better," he rasped. Better now that you are here." Sun Tzu took his bony hand into her soft one. This was a good start. Fong was dying. But that wasn’t his only trouble, his mind had left him quite sometime ago. It was also trouble for Beijing as well. In the early stages of his dementia, the old soldier was heard murmuring about Tibet and his "only regret." Fong had been pivotal in the invasion of Tibet, the only flaw was the escape of the Dalai Lama. The nurse assisting him assumed that was what he meant. But then he muttered about "if only he had gotten to see it… if only he had used the map…" The nurse reported it to the military, who could no decipher what Fong meant. They hated loose ends, but he never mentioned it again. Over time, Fong slipped further from reality. Sometimes he was incoherent. But other times he thought t was nearly fifty years ago when he was a young man and he had a young wife. Sun Tzu would play that wife. They took advantage of his delusional state to get Fong to trust her. He had been long work. His dementia was not predictable, so Sun Tzu would have to spend days at a time at the hospital just hoping for a chance. Her reprieves came when it was obvious he was too sick to speak to anyone. But as soon his energy returned, so did she. The only question was she would be able to get the information before the inevitable. The race against time had been going on for weeks now. Fong seemed different this time. His eyes were sharper and he seemed to "recognize" her quicker. His other hand reached out and touched the tube leading to a nostril. "What…what is this…" he rasped. Sun Tzu was taken back briefly. He had paid no attention to the equipment keeping him alive before. He always thought he was at home in bed "You’re ill, love." "I feel so… week…" "It’s a serious illness. But the doctors are sure you’ll recover completely." Sun Tzu prayed that this change wouldn’t compromise the mission. Fong looked very confused, agitated even. "Where are my sons?" Sun Tzu figured that they were with their own grandchildren. The military did not let them in on anything that was happening here. "They are in school," she told him. "But they will visit as possible." The sad, old eyes looked at her. "You… are my wife." "And you are my husband," Sun Tzu replied softly as he stroked his head. "You love me." "Of course." "Say it." "I love you." Tears filled those sad, old eyes. "You lie," rasped General Fong. Sun Tzu was speechless. She mumbled a few words, but could find nothing to actually say. Fong let go of her hand. "I know I am old. I know my mind… isn’t right. But sometimes it is. And in those times, I know what you all are trying to do. But those times are becoming fewer and fewer. My life is coming to and end. And I don’t want to leave this world treated like a fool. Tell me what you want so I can end this charade." As stunned as she was by the prior event, the way she felt when she heard his word shocked Sun Tzu the most. She felt… shame. This was a soldier just like her. And he was right, she was lying to him. But not anymore. "What is your greatest regret?" Fong took a few wheezing breaths, then slowly pointed to a framed photo on a nearby bedstand. It was himself with Mao, so many years ago. "Behind the photograph…" Sun Tzu picked up the frame and slid the photo out. She discovered a folded piece of paper hidden there, one that was revealed to be an old map. "What is this?" she asked. "Shamballah…" whispered Fong. "as a young officer, I helped the Dalai Lama escape Tibet. In return, he gave me that map to a kingdom of peace. I yearned to travel there, but my heart belonged with China and with my family. Now it is far too late for me. But perhaps not for you…" "Me? But why? As you said, I’ve been deceiving you." "You were doing your duty, just as I did when I led our soldiers to conquer Tibet. When I look at you, I see a soul in need of peace. I found that with my family. But you need to find your own path. Perhaps on that map…" "Don’t you fear I will turn it over to my superiors? Invade Shamballah just as you invaded Tibet?" "Shamballah has a way of keeping the wrong sort out," Fong gave a dry, gravelly laugh. "Whatever you choose to do, I ask a favor in return." "What can I do for you?" General Fong took Sun Tzu’s hand in his. "Put me back on my own path. Let me see my wife again. You made a lovely proxy, but she is my soul. I want to go to her and leave here at least a semblance of the man I was." Sun Tzu felt something wet on her cheek. Tears. She brushed them away with her free hand. "You are every inch the man you were. It would be an honor, General Fong." The old man smiled and released her hand. Sun Tzu pulled the tube from his nose, then pinched his nostrils shut. Her other hand covered his mouth. It only took a few moments for it to be over. Sun Tzu looked down at the body f the soldier and saluted. She was about to leave when she thought of the map. There was Shamballah at the top. She thought to herself for a little while, then tore a strip from the paper. She tucked it away in her shoe and left. General Chang was in the hall. She handed him the map. "His greatest regret," she told him. "He aided in the escape of the Dalai Lama. These are secret pathways through the Himalayas." Chang looked the map over. "These could be of strategic importance if we veer need to quickly cross the mountains. Far more important than one religious figure. Excellent work, soldier. How is General Fong now?" "Resting peacefully." Sun Tzu and Chang saluted each other. She walked down the hall and got her own clothes back. Sun Tzu didn’t bother to change. She headed to the elevator with them bundled under one arm. She needed to find her own path, and that meant letting Sang know just who she was. She needed to tell him in truth the words she told an old man in deception… "I love you." The trip back to Sang’s apartment seemed to be twice as long as the journey from there. Sun Tzu ran up the steps two at a time. She felt weightless. Sun tzu threw opened the door to Sang’s small apartment and saw him still in bed. With another woman. Sun Tzu recognized her, a minor actress. She screamed as Sang pulled the sheet over himself, like Sun Tzu had never seen him naked before. "You weren’t supposed to be back yet…" Sang’s words sounded like an accusation, like Sun Tzu had wronged him somehow. She didn’t have a retort, she didn’t even think about laying a finger on either of them. She just ran down the stairs and out the building. When she got outside, Sun Tzu cried once more. Sun Tzu heard Sang’s words echoing in her head. His tone acted like she had lied to him. That’s when she realized it… Sun Tzu had always lied to him. She deserved no better than how he had treated. She definitely didn’t deserve his love because she was never the person she said she was. And yet it was Sun Tzu the liar that couldn’t tell him she loved him. As the tears fell from her eyes, she felt something scraping the skin of her foot. She took it out of her shoe. It was the piece of the map with Shamballah on it. Sun Tzu stared at it, then rolled it up into a ball and threw it into the gutter. That was her path to peace…. Sang looked pretty much the same after all the years, still a scruffy rascal. Sun Tzu knew she looked different… the uniform, the longer hair, the sunglasses, her whole demeanor was changed... harder, colder. In fact, Sang just kept looking at her. Finally, he spoke. "Excuse me… but do I know you?" Sun Tzu’s face remained a mask of porcelain. "No. You don’t." And that was the honest truth.
