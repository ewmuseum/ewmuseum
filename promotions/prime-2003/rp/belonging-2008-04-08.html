Belonging

8 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 8, 2008 There was something missing from his punch. He could feel it. In the past hour, David Cross had thrown more punches, kicks, and knees than he could count. The heavy canvas punching bag continued to sway even after he’d stopped his brutal assault. Sweat covered his body and ran into his eyes, wiped away with the back of his thumb. He pulled in a breath through his nose and watched the bag sway in front of him. Something was definitely missing. It wasn’t motivation; that he had in ample supply. He defeated eleven men in Dual Halo to earn a spot on the PRIME roster. He fought another four to prove it wasn’t a fluke. In a few days he would step into the ring with the man who’d actually won the Dual Halo match, the reigning Five-Star Champion, Tyler Rayne. He knew of Rayne, and had encountered once before back in Japan a number of years ago. That situation, such as it was, ended in a stalemate. This time both men were different, and the Five-Star title was on the line. David picked up his towel and pressed it against his face. So what was the problem? Why was his head not in this fight? "Kaiju!" "Wang," Cross said through the towel. "I’m sure we had this talk about interrupting me during my private time." Wang stopped in the doorway. "I thought that was only when you masturbated." David dropped the towel back on the stool. "Well look who’s got jokes today," he muttered. He threw a high roundhouse kick at the bag and felt it grunt under the impact. Wang stepped into the room and held up the wireless phone receiver. "Telephone for you." Somewhere inside him, a plug was pulled and all the color drained out of the remnants of David’s face. There were only two calls he could be getting – either PRIME, or the hospital. Neither would be good news. He took the phone in his hand and took in a breath, focusing himself to be calm. "Hello?" "Davie? It’s Marilyn." There was something in her voice that immediately sent up red flags. "Can you come - it’s Alex. There’s a problem." At "Alex", David was walking to the door, phone pressed to his ear, money clip wrapped in a roll of gauze in his hand. By "problem" his legs were pumping, sprinting down the hallway and taking the three flights of stairs in almost as many strides. He didn’t recall dropping the phone as he crossed the canvas training mat on the main floor. The next sensation he was aware of was the cold night air swirling around him as he stood in the middle of the street, flagging down a taxi. -==- The gauze he’d wrapped around his head sagged with the sweat of a stressed man. Marilyn Creed was Alex’s aunt, and the only remaining member of the Creed family that didn’t want Alex hurt or maimed in any way. She knew how close David and Alex had become, like an uncle and a nephew in her eyes, and right now David could see that she could use some family to lean on. Marilyn looked a little apprehensive to approach him when David ran down the hallway, the front desk nurses chasing after him with security in tow. "It’s okay," she told the medical staff when they’d caught up to them. "He’s with me. He’s family." Family. He knew she was just saying that to keep the hospital cops from dragging him away, but he still found himself smiling inside. David gave her a hug and could feel her tense in the embrace. It could have been from the slipping medical tape that covered his facial injuries, or maybe from the guilt that it had been her brother who had inflicted those injuries. But when he pulled back, he was a little relieved to see that she was genuinely glad to see him. "I’m... I’m sorry to call you out at this hour. I just didn’t know what else to do." Cross squeezed her shoulders. "You don’t have to apologize to me. Now what’s wrong with our boy?" Marilyn slumped down in a nearby chair, exhaustion covering her like a blanket. She’d been at the hospital for a very long time, and it was wearing her down. "He’s got some kind of infection," she said wearily. "They had to hook him back up to the machines. They’re helping to keep everything," she struggled to find the word before settling with "going." She rested her elbows on her knees and her face dropped into her hands. David looked down at her. "Do they know what caused the infection?" "No idea," she said through her fingers. "They’re running some tests." He knelt down on one knee and gently pulled he hands away from her face. "Mar, what else." Her eyes searched the ceiling for answers, but none fell into her lap. "It’s his insurance. His money. It’s all gone." Something fell on David’s head that almost knocked him over, and it wasn’t answers from Heaven. "What?" Marilyn stood up and started pacing. David remained on his knee, staring at the seat she’d been in while taking in what she was saying. "The doctor showed me the paperwork. He’s covered for the next week, maybe two. It could last longer if they didn’t need the machines, but... it’s all so expensive!" "How can he be out of money?" David stood up and walked up behind Marilyn. "He’s got great long term critical insurance to cover things like this, and he’s got plenty of cash in the bank." There was a tremble in her voice. Frustration and stress was breaking her down. "No, he used to have all that. His manager took it all." "Manager? You mean Bill, his business agent?" "I mean his manager. He took over all of Alex’s affairs." Marilyn threw her hands into the air and spun around. David thought she was going to slap the gauze off him. "He gave the guy Power of Attorney for Crissake! What was he thinking!" "There’s no way Alex would do that." Fatigue and frustration gave way to anger. It seemed to sharpen her up. David decided it was a genetic thing. "I saw the paperwork," she told him. "I saw Alex’s signature." Impossible. "It was faked." "There was a witness." "The witness lied." It was doing no one any good by continuing this line of conjecture. "Okay, well what happens if the money runs out?" The anger fizzled away, and the tired came back in a big way. "All the doctor would say is that he hopes it doesn’t come to that." It was David’s turn to feel angry. "What kind of answer is that?! Who is this doctor?" Marilyn had been trying so hard to be strong, but she was just too worn down to hold back any longer and started to cry. David held her again. She was used to pain, her being a Creed and all, but this was all too much for her. "I’m sorry Mar. Don’t worry. Everything’s going to be okay." She stood tall and wiped the tears away. "Look at me," she sniffed, "blubbering away like a little girl." David smiled. "I was right there with you sister. See how puffy my head looks now? The gauze is super-absorbent." She snickered and shook her head. "I’m going to get a coffee. Can I get you anything?" "Tea if they have it." She gave him a look. "Okay, get me anything that you deem to be butch enough to drink." The nurse didn’t bother to look up when Marilyn passed by on her way to the elevator. She did shoot a quick glance in David’s direction, but a smile and a wave from the man in the gauze mask snapped her attention back to whatever paperwork had held her attention previously. Marilyn stepped into the elevator and gave David a tired smile before the doors closed. The door to Alex’s room started to open, which startled Cross. He hadn’t been expecting anyone to be coming out of the room, given the late hour. It was another nurse, a beautiful raven-haired nurse, who gave David a friendly smile as she passed. "Even his nurses are hot," he said in disbelief. "Unbelievable." Once she had continued past the nurse’s station, David opened the door and let himself into the room. It was always dark in the room, no matter what time David came to see his friend. He guessed the hospital didn’t see the need to waste electricity and light bulbs on someone who wasn’t going to be awake for quite some time. That was a depressing thought, which seemed suitable given the current situation. David felt a bit alarmed when he saw Alex Creed buried under tubes and wires. "Part man, part machine," Cross commented as he sat down on the edge of the bed. He snatched up the clipboard and began flipping through the pages. It had become much thicker since the first time he saw it, so he skimmed through a few pages to look for the highlights. "Let’s see what kind of treatments they’ve got you on, shall we?" The more he read, the worse it got. Words like "unknown virus" and "rapid deterioration" jumped off the page and kicked David in the stomach, hard and often. His blood chilled. This was bad. Really bad. If the infection, whatever the Hell it was, didn’t kill Alex, the reduction in treatment could. "No," he croaked through his tightening throat. He looked up from the clipboard and stared at the young man’s face. He’d lost so much weight already, his face seemed to age every day. "I lost your mother," he told the shell of his friend. "I’m not losing you too." Right now, to save Alex Creed’s life, David needed money. The machines would help him fight the infection, but they needed money to keep them running. They needed money for better medication. Marilyn didn’t have that kind of cash, and even though David was earning a paycheck in PRIME it wasn’t the kind of cash that could keep things afloat for long. He paused, no longer looking at anything as a new thought emerged from the dark clouds of his mind.. Championship money... that could do the trick. -==- Wang knocked on the door. David had converted the entire top floor of the dojo into a rather large living area, with plenty of space but not much for comfort. The roof leaked, and the water ended up dripping through the ceiling. Before David had claimed it, the drafty expanse had been little more than storage space with large, cast-iron tubs to collect water. It was musty, cold, with boarded up windows and no electricity except for a single outlet by the door. It was amazing what some candles and a few ornamental blankets could do. "Kaiju?" Wang tested the door handle to see if it was locked. The last thing he needed to see was a mass of scars pumping his fist inside his trousers while staring at the dance studio across the street. The door was unlocked, so he turned the knob and let himself in. Privacy be damned; this was Wang’s building. It had been willed to him by Master Hiroshi before he died, along with the school. Who did David think he was to simply show up and take over everything? If Wang wanted to enter an unlocked room in his own building, that’s exactly what he would – Wang stood in the doorway. Seconds later, he threw up in his mouth a little. There was a circle of green candles, about eight feet wide, with flames flickering and dancing from the draft that always seemed to be present in the room. The air was cold, always cold, and normally smelled of wet fungus. It was the new smell that first caught Wang’s attention. Sharp, almost acidic. It left a coppery taste in his mouth. The light from the candles wasn’t even enough to outline David Cross, shirtless and cross-legged in the center of the circle. The large white candle in front of him, though, illuminated him from the chest down. His right arm was bent and leaning to face the burning light. His left hand hovered near the edge of the candle’s tall flame. No, it wasn’t his hand that was near the flame. It was a scalpel. That’s when Wang noticed the bloody rags beside David. "W-what are you doing?" he asked. He heard Cross suck a breath through his nose as the scalpel’s blade disappeared into the darkness high up on his right arm. Wang wasn’t sure if it was the wind or the draft, but he knew he could hear a faint hissing sound. A thin stream of blood began to trickle out of the shadows and down to David’s right elbow. "Have you ever been moved by something greater than yourself?" David asked. "I am... not sure I follow." Wang remained in the doorway and watched Cross bleeding in the dark. "A few days ago..." David stopped and sucked in a sharp breath. It was painful, Wang thought, whatever he was doing to himself. "I felt like something was missing. A piece of myself had d-dried up and fallen off." "Like your penis?" There was a pause in the movements. Wang could feel David’s stare almost physically pressing against him. "Oh dear boy, you really have no idea what I’m talking about." The movement continued. "But the day will come that I will show you. On that day, you will thank me for opening your eyes." The scalpel fell to the floor and David picked up one of the least bloody rags. It disappeared out of the light. "I’d forgotten what it was like to feel human. To be part of something meaningful: society, family, anything." The bloody rag was set down, completely crimson. The trickle of blood along the arm had stopped. "There’s one part of my heart that still beats, and it beats for the life of the boy I almost got to call my son. Now Alex Creed might die, again, but I can do something about that." Bloody fingers wrapped around the white candle and David lifted it up to his shoulder. Wang struggled to keep his lunch down when he saw what Cross had been doing with the scalpel. He’d cut thin slices out of his shoulder to form the letters "AC". The removed meat lay just underneath the freshly cleaned wounds. Wang’s stomach lurched when Cross poured some of the wax over the bloody letters. He set the candle back down on the floor, and Wang’s hand moved to the door frame to steady himself. A moment passed. Wang felt his strength return. He watched David take some of the skin he’d cut away and held it over the candle’s flame. The sizzling and the smoke was almost more than he could stand. Cross, however, didn’t seem to notice or care. "I plan to win a silly piece of gold and leather from a very capable wrestler. When I do, I’ll be able to save Alex’s life... and maybe redeem some of my own humanity." Things were getting dizzy again. "But what if you fail?" In the shadows, David started to chuckle. "Then someone will die, and I'm not talking about Alex."
