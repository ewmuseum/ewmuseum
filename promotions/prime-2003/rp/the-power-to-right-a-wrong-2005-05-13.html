The Power to Right a Wrong

13 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 13, 2005 ~o~ Dream Sequence ~o~ The moon was reflecting down through the glass pane that made up the window into the face of a small boy. The boy was sitting on his bed, twirling the blanket in his hands and using his pillow to prop his head up against the wall. Boredom was setting in with the young boy as he looked right back out the window, staring back into the moon. The bedroom really did not have much in it. The few toys that belonged to the boy were scattered all over the room and the dresser had a few items of clothing hanging out of the front as the drawers struggled to keep closed. The closet door was open with plenty of clothes hanging on the hangers and some clothes on the floor in the closet mixed with a couple pairs of shoes. The door leading to the hallway was open just enough to have a small bit of light creep into the room through the crack. The boy continued to wrap the blanket around his hands, release it to only start over again. Finally in a little bit of frustration the boy tossed the blanket onto the bed. He walked over to the door and opened it to continue out into the hallway. He left the door open as he headed for the stairs. He peaked around the corner of the rail leading down the stairs to peer into the living room. The light from the TV danced off the walls in the room and reflected back towards the boy. Sitting on the brown couch was a man of about 40. He had short brown hair with some grey showing and a bald spot in the front. He was wearing a ragged old t-shirt and a pair of ripped blue jeans. He had his bare foot propped up on the table and had a beer in his right hand. He was distracted watching television and didn’t notice the boy coming down the stairs towards him. “Daddy, I want to watch TV too.” The boy squeaked as he stepped down from the last stair. But he was stopped quickly by the explosive voice of his father as he spun around on the couch to sink his drunken filled gaze directly into the eyes of his son. “What did I tell you boy?” The man spat, half spilling his beer onto his shirt. “You are grounded. That means no TV, no games, no nothin’! You got that?” “But Dad I’m bored.” The boy managed to step back onto the top stair as he was fidgeting on his feet, trying to deal with his alcoholic father. “If you finished your Sunday School homework, it’s time for bed. Then you won’t be bored.” The older man coughed as the boy ran up a couple more steps, not wanting his father to further advance on him. “Thomas, it’s far too early for little Killean to go to bed.” A younger woman spun into the room from the kitchen area. “He finished the homework a half an hour ago.” She pointed to the clock that sat on the wall behind the television. The small hand was resting comfortably over the ‘7’ and the larger hand was just moving between the ‘3’ and ‘4’. “Damnit Loraine, the boy is only 8 years old! He should be in bed right now. So get to bed!” He turned his attention from his wife to Killean and his little legs carried him up the stairs as fast as they could. Turning back to his wife he scowled. “And you get back into the kitchen and finish those damn dishes! I’m watching TV!” The woman sighed has she turned to move back towards the kitchen area. Clearly satisfied with himself he turned back towards the TV and took a sip from his beer. However the can was empty because he spilled it all over himself when he was yelling. “Bring me another beer woman!” He yelled towards the kitchen, throwing the beer can in the same direction. The boy ran back into his room and shut his door completely. He always hated his father for being an alcoholic. It always made him moody and that caused him to yell a lot. It upset little Killean and he rolled onto his bed and began to sob ‘I always struggled in Sunday School and I had plenty of homework because of it. For being an alcoholic, my father was quite the bible thumper. He took our religion very seriously when it involved me. What a hypocrite! He drank enough booze to sink a ship some days and that is not quite religious. But once I screwed up, look out! Silly drunk bastard on the loose! He would ground me or spank me or sometimes both. He would do whatever he saw fit to try and teach me a lesson. My mother didn’t have any say in anything that involved my punishment because my father was the typical old time husband. It was his home and he wore the pants, or sometimes just sat around in his underwear watching television. Either way, I had always hoped that one day I would be big enough to defend myself against my dad. He would never regret pulling out the big leather belt. Like that one time…’ Killean, now a boy of about 10 was lying on his bed sobbing. Beside him was the tear soaked Sunday School study manual that had a grade of 42% inked in red marker. There were balled up Kleenex tissues on the bed and floor, which blew around when the door to his room whipped open fiercely. The tall figure of Thomas Edward Sirrajin was casting a shadow on the light blue floor and wall of his son’s room. In his hands, he snapped a leather belt perhaps attempting to scare the young boy. Killean had moved to cower in the corner of his bed as his father inched closer, his lips moving as if it was all slow motion. The belt end was waving around, void of the regular buckle which usually adorned it. Instead, the end was rather tattered and worn from what seemed to be quite a few uses. Killean’s mother left the room to avoid witnessing the savage act that she believed was uncalled for. But what was she to say? Thomas wore the pants and that was all there was to it. “Failed on the test again did ya?” Mr. Sirrajin yelled, perhaps waking half the neighborhood in the process. “I told ya I’d learn ya!” He wound up and cracked the boy Sirrajin over the back with the leather. Repeated smacks could be heard and Mrs. Sirrajin was sobbing in the hallway listening to her son be ravaged by the man of the house. In her arms was the 8 year old Summer, Killean’s little sister. She always looked up to her big brother and she continued to cry as she listened to the beating. Finally, Thomas Sirrajin emerged from the room and just as soon as he stepped outside the door frame, Lorraine was storming in with Summer to check on Killean. The boy was tanned and sore all over as he was bunched up against the wall in the corner. Crying was not an option as the pain was actually too much shock to the body. Instead, he was shivering and shaking from the excruciating pain.‘The belt always hurt. When I did something wrong or failed in my religious studies, Dad would break out that leather damnation. He would continue to strike me until the pain was so overwhelming, I could no longer cry or scream. I went into shock and sat there, receiving the punishment without as much as a whimper. My mother and sister came to be by my side to comfort me. Ahhh yes… Summer. Dear Summer Sirrajin… my little sister. She always stuck beside me and loved me no matter what. I loved her more than anything in the world at the time. It was pretty hard for a 10 year old boy to understand that his sister was diagnosed with cancer only a year earlier. Never mind, she was only 8 years old and she had no idea what she was going to go through. The suffering was something that was misunderstood by the both of us but we ended up fighting it together. We also promised to end up fighting dad together too. But I always hoped to grow up and stand up to my father. Unfortunately that time never came and the whole experience left me rather empty inside…’The two Sirrajin children had continued to bounce from foster home to foster home after the disappearance of their parents Thomas and Lorraine. Killean continued to be rather rebellious and was always the star of the wrestling team at whatever high school he attended. He was somewhat of a bully, taking out the aggressions from his earlier home life on the people he couldn’t care less about at school. His sister was feeling rather lonely at school and she never made friends with too many people. Her brother was not always there and they really only saw each other when they were home, where ever that was at the time. The boy Sirrajin never even got to return the favor to his abusive father. It always bothered him that his parents disappeared and abandoned Summer and him. The little girl was devastated and it took quite sometime for her to deal with the foster families. But the children got used to it. They enjoyed each others company and Summer looked up to her big brother more than ever now. Killean never continued on with the religious studies and he was rather proud that he became independently strong on his own without his bastard father to try and point him down the religious primrose path. It was always a path he didn’t believe in, now he was happy that he was beginning to head down the path that he wanted.‘I always tried my best in wrestling. After high school, I had a few part time jobs but nothing ever made me really happy. Then I met an older man named Jin Daikusa. He had his own wrestling school in San Diego and remembered seeing me in one high school tournament that I happened to win. Summer and I both moved to San Diego not just because of the wrestling, but because Summer would also get better treatment for her cancer.’ ~o~ End Dream Sequence ~o~ “What is it about a dream that always makes you compare it to the things that you are going through in your life at that exact moment?” Killean was calm with a determined look in his eye, filming his first promo for PRIME since he tossed Boda over the bridge into the ice cold water in Lewiston, Maine. “I was watching Boda for a few seconds when he hit the water. He was squirming and suffering like a child. It reminded me of my times as a child when my father used to abuse me. The belt hurt like hell and I am sure that the water shocked Boda into hypothermia. At one moment, I looked down into Boda’s eyes as he was falling and I saw my father. I used Boda as an outlet to get back at what my father did to me. What with that son of a bitch ditching me and my sister, I really didn’t have the opportunity to deliver the package of justice.” Killean rose up. He was different; it was like he was reveling in his evil plot to toss Boda over the bridge. This was not like the Killean of old. He always looked down on those kinds of things but this time he had his chin held high, proud of being able to equal Boda and the Dark Age at their own games. He recalled watching the video of the end of ReVolution and he was smiling at the reaction of the Dark Age. It was enjoyable to see them speechless for once. The number one contender for the Universal Title was dead at the bottom of a river in Lewiston and he couldn’t be more ecstatic. “I enjoyed myself immensely watching Boda suffer. It was almost like the time Summer...” Killean looked to the floor, remembering his sister lying motionless in the bed with their friend Seph by her side as well. “It was a long time coming, but she finally succumbed to the infernal disease that was ravaging her body. I was finally happy to see another suffering like I knew my sister did. She was the most special person in the world and I had to watch her die in her bed. When Seph called the ambulance, he called me and I left wrestling school to join her. I would have never forgiven myself if I would not have said goodbye. But I did say goodbye, just as I said goodbye to you Christopher Sloboda.” ~o~ Dream Sequence ~o~ The cold damp atmosphere was bone chilling on it’s own, but the constant beeping and fluctuating of the machines in the background was enough to remind any man, woman or child that they were indeed mortal. The idea was not lost on Killean Sirrajin as he sat silent beside the hospital bed watching mortality set in with his sister Summer. The doctor was in just a few minutes previously and had said that it wouldn’t be a matter of a half an hour and his sister would be leaving him and this world behind.‘I remember it all like it was yesterday. My sister was lying there, not moving a muscle. She couldn’t even say two words to me. But as I remember, the last thing that she said to me was “I am proud of you big brother.” She had seen me wrestling when she brought Seph by. I was excelling in the school and I was ready for a new challenge. But I always did well and she always said how proud of me she was. So as I sat there beside her bed and I watched her float away from the world that she knew, I knew that I would never be able to back away from something that she was proud of me for. I was doing something I loved and my sister continued to look up to me till the day she died. I always knew that I would make her proud even after her death. But I always wondered how much pain she was really in as she was lying on the bed. The cancer was devouring her entire body and I am sure it had to hurt. Not a type of pain like breaking your leg or not a type of pain like a paper cut, but there definitely had to be some pain. I only wished at that time I could take all of the pain for her so she wouldn’t suffer. If only…’Seph had walked into the room and joined Killean at Summer’s bed side. But all he basically did was hug the limp body of Summer and give Killean a knowing handshake. Even he couldn’t take watching Summer pass on. But Killean stayed because he wanted to be with his sister until the end, just as he was since the day she was born. He remembered everything about her when she was very young. From the day she got out of diapers to the day their mother brought her home her first pair of feety jammies, he was there. He may have been young and had plenty of difficulty remembering his parents, he always remembered Summer. She looked up to him.‘I smiled down into the crib when she was just a baby. It’s like she always knew I was her big brother. There was a connection ever since I laid eyes on her. I looked at her eyes and she smiled back to me, giggling with joy. I remember it and I will never forget it. That was the beginning of the relationship with my sister. I would have done anything for her.’ The machines started to beep a little slower and her body moved. IT MOVED! Killean’s eyes lit up as he watched his sister making her final movements. As the beeping continued to slow, she opened her eyes for a moment, but it was long enough for Killean to see the lovely brown that shone back to him. He had seen her smile for the last time and he smiled back with tears in his eyes. As the beeping stopped, replaced by a long constant beep, the sound was enough to knock anyone for a loop; Killean began to openly sob for his sister as her eyes closed for the last time. The puppy dog brown eyes would never shine back at him again and the smile that lit up an entire room would never again illuminate. It was something that he was not sure he could live without. But through the constant crying and the feelings that were drowning his soul, he managed to squeeze a few words out for Summer.‘To this day I know she heard me. When I said it I felt a huge weight lift off my shoulders and I knew she would always love me. It was the most meaningful and honest words that I have ever said to this day. Just the tone in my voice filled me with joy for the fact that I had the best sister anyone could ask for.’ ‘Goodbye… dear sister.’
