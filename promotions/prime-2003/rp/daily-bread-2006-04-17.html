Daily Bread

17 April 2006

Date: 4/17 They must have thought it was a miracle when the first rain fell. Water falling from the sky...sounds like some bullshit, something we'd roll our eyes at if we'd comprehended enough to understand how passe that is. It must have blown every ape-man's pea-sized mind. But they obviously didn't understand how commonplace it would become, how a week in places like Seattle or Florida would churn out this sign from God like they had it in surplus. And they certainly didn't get how fucking annoying it would be when it's pouring cats and dogs and you're trying to see out the side window of your Lexus. The mirrored tint keeps anyone in the funeral party from checking me out, but it also casts an annoying blue tint over the whole scene, joining with the gray sky to give this whole graveside scene a black-and-white feel, like a noir movie on a cheap idiot box that the old timers used to watch Milton Berle on. Since I barely watch any TV that doesn't feature my face all over it, this is a new experience: being on the other side of the tube, taking in the scene and not actively controlling it. Appropriate, because my attachment to the ceremony is far less than what is expected me. I'm distant...passive. I'm not even there. I did two things in the funeral home...or rather, my representative did two things. One was to remove 'and Husband' from the 'Loving Father and Husband' mantra under Dad's name. Regardless of that relationship, and who I sided with, even Lance Ferguson could agree that he was never 'loving' and 'a husband' at the same time. The second thing I did by proxy in the home was sign the credit card slip to pay for the arrangements. Of course, there's no press release on who paid for what, and the undertaker won't field any inquiries in a post-ceremony Q&A session. What will come out tomorrow, and make its way through the rumor mills and the message boards with all the Hollywood buzz, is that Danny Ferguson didn't attend his father's funeral. He'd been taking naps in his office, his assistant said. Work was coming few and far between, and he hadn't been taking care of himself or eating well because he didn't have any nice new clients. Usually when a new kid hit Hollywood, Dad could work the magic and land them enough gigs to keep the confidence up, but not enough to pay the rent. So single mom and the Mouseketeer would move in and Dad got home-cooked meals instead of the stuff he could but on the way home with a Power Rewards credit card and an open window. But all his clients were now pre-teens, all his meals were fried and all of his workdays included at least one nap. Four days ago that nap stretched through an appointment - one of only three that week. By the time the phone girl called EMTs, he was gone. Heart, stroke, something. I dunno. Couldn't make it out through the sobs. It killed him, whatever it was. I'd mark down cause of death as 'bacon' in all future paperwork. There's a voicemail on my Blackberry from that day, a few hours before they found him. Haven't listened to it yet. I click past it whenever I check my messages. Pretty soon that'll become force of habit, and then one day I'll upgrade to a new model and his last electronic words will get lost into the abyss. So it goes. I've never read Vonnegut, but I like to toss that phrase around anyway. So it goes. One day you're fast forwarding through the Entertainment Tonight episode that you Tivo'd and pause when you see what looks like another damn paparrazzi shot of your face...only its not a bad quality photo of you, it's a timestamped headshot of your father, Hollywood agent and 'beloved' somethingorother. They always said you looked alike, but you never saw it until the noise lines on the TV concealed his wrinkles. So you keep watching and you find out from the bimbo in the blue halter that your dad died in his office earlier today. You have that flash of anger, that moment when you demand to know why nobody told you...but slowly come to accept that there was only one person left in this world who would care about you at that level, who would think to notify you of a life change of that magnitude, and that person is the subject of the forgotten news. He certainly can't phone in his own death. Or maybe that's what he tried to do with that unanswered voicemail. It's a shame I'll never find out. He thought that once I grew up that he could move with me, transitioning from child actors to the real, live thing. Dad, like many other 'wannabes' in the industry, thought that any jackass could be an agent if they cut through the bullshit. Problem was that being an agent is all about bullshit - it's the parties, the fake-lunches that are really meetings, the fake-meetings that are really lunches, and above all, the Rolodex. I jumped to Allan because he knew how to get me in touch with people. If I stuck with dad, I'd be filming some doofus sitcom with Todd Bridges right now. No thanks. It's not like I left him alone. He knew where to find me. I paid his rent. We even had Thanksgiving dinner together...assuming I wasn't on set or on the road. There are plenty of people who don't even give their parents that dignity, who think of family out of obligation rather than, at the very least, respect. I respected my father. We just didn't work together, and business is my life these days, so I didn't see him. Around the grave, a few clients - some former, and some former-as-of-four-days-ago, choke back their emotions and huddle under umbrellas. Occasionally one will cast an accusing eye towards the black Lexus that idles in the road just 20 yards away. At those moments I think about bursting out of the door and show those motherfuckers that I am here, that I did care. But I know that it would accomplish nothing. If I'm not here, then I'm just a bad son, an ingrate who rode his father until he found a stronger horse. They'll make a number of Cat's in the Cradle references, pointing out the father who brought his kid to LA to leech on his fame, then watched the boy run amok well after he had he came to his senses. I was never a Harry Chapin fan anyway. But if I AM here, if I do step out into this downpour and listen to the painfully underdetailed eulogy, I'm just trying to make peace. I'm not burying my father, I'm looking for sympathy and a face in the headlines to go with my new movie. I certainly can'tbe celebrating the life of a man who I professionally disowned, right? I must be an asshole who cares about nothing but himself, and naturally, if that's the case, I would be willing to answer a few questions after it's all over with, right here next to his headstone. After all, I'm famous, and that makes it my obligation to be as accessible and honest with the media as humanly possible. If I'm not, if I stop for one second to ask for my privacy or respect for my father, then I'm dodging, trying to spin the story in my favor by not giving an interview. I don't care about him, right? I ought to spare a moment to talk to everyone if I want any part of this farce of a ceremony to be taken seriously, huh? Dad spent most of his career and mine deflecting questions and situations like that. On this, his last day in the surface world, he ought to have a moment of true serenity, one without the cacophony of Hollywood. If that means that his son doesn't stand by his grave when they lower the casket...so be it. I hope he will understand. For once in our collective life, he gets all the attention. I'll just watch it on television.
