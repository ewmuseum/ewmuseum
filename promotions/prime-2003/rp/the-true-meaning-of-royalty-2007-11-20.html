The True Meaning of Royalty...

20 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/20/07 Thousands of people make their way around a bustling city. This isn’t a city that is filled with skyscrapers, and taxis, but is centered around a wide river leading towards a castle, easily the largest structure in the city. The castle is one of the most identifiable in the world, Wawel, and its home is Krakow, Poland. As people make their way in an out of the castle’s gate, one person stands out. He towers over those around him, and he is nearly as wide as the entrance through which he is walking. Tom Walczak methodically makes his way into the courtyard, stopping to look at all the culture that currently surrounds him. Tourists walk around the courtyard, many of their eyes opened wide as they look at the grandeur of this national landmark, but for Walczak it is different. Walczak has come here to get a look at just how powerful his ancestors once were. Wawel was the home for Polish royalty for centuries, and the sight of it would instill fear in all outsiders who passed by. It is this type of fear that Walczak wants people to have when they think of Poland, and King of Kings will be his first chance. “Welcome guests, guided tours of the palace are available inside, languages include, Polish, English, Spanish, Portuguese, Italian, French, and German.” Walczak is caught off-guard as the message blasts over the loudspeakers. It repeats itself in a number of different languages as Walczak makes his way inside the castle. A number of tour guides are standing around the main lobby trying to get tourists to join them. Walczak approaches a young woman. She has her dirty blonde hair, tied back in a ponytail, and wears glasses to compliment her blue sweater and jeans. “Excuse me.” The woman turns around, looking taken aback by the size of the massive pole. “Hello, how can I help you?” “I was wondering when the next tour in Polish was leaving.” “There was a tour that just left, so unfortunately it will be about forty five minutes before the next tour leaves. However, I will be conducting a guided tour in English in about five minutes.” The woman’s answer does little to appease Walczak, as he was hoping to get away from anything English related, but how could he pass up a chance to delve further into his ancestry. “Do I just wait here?” “You can go over to the information desk to buy a ticket, and meet me back here. We will wait a couple of more minutes before leaving.” Walczak makes his way over to the information desk, slapping down fifteen zloty to get a ticket for the tour. As he makes his way back to the guide, Walczak notices that a family is there waiting. A mother and a father are surrounded by their daughter and son. The father is disheveled to put it lightly. Sweat drips down his balding head, and down onto his soup stained multicolored sweater. A fanny pack wraps around his grey sweatpants, and seems to be almost bursting. His wife is more cleaned up, but looks nearly as pathetic. She wears a large brimmed hat to compliment her dark brown sweat-suit, and jumbo sunglasses. The two children could not be more disinterested as the boy plays his Nintendo DS, and the daughter looks through a High School Musical Magazine. Walczak pulls the tour guide aside, trying to distance himself from the family. “Are these people going to be coming with us?” “Yes, they are a family from New York, and are touring a number of countries in Europe for their winter holiday.” “Could, I possibly get you to give me a personally guided tour, I just do not want to be bothered by these people while discovering more about our rich culture.” “I am sorry, but I am the only English speaking guide in today, and I think you are wrong to assume that these people will do anything to hinder any knowledge you will gain today.” The tour guide heads back towards the family, leaving Walczak a little disappointed with the predicament he is currently in. “Welcome to Wawel, the most well known castle in Poland, and the home of Polish royalty dating back to the fourth century.” The tour guide turns around leading them into the heart of the castle. Every couple of feet they move the father snaps pictures, only adding to the disappointment on Walczak’s face. “Throughout the tour you will notice rich tapestries adorning the walls. Most of these were gifts given to the kings and queens of Poland by visiting nobility. They depict…” “MOM!!! Why do we have to be here? Can’t we just go back to the hotel?” “JIMMY BE QUIET, AND LISTEN TO THE LADY SPEAK!!!!” The shouting gains the attention of nearly everyone in room. Their eyes now fixed on the family, and Walczak by association. He tries to shield his face, but feels hundreds of eyes gazing at him. “Okay, well if we continue forward, we will be entering one of the dining halls of the castle.” “Will there be food there for sampling. HAHA HAHA.” The fathers joke lands on deaf ears, as he is the only one who laughs, not even getting a sympathy chuckle from his family members. He wipes the sweat off of his forehead, and continues to snap pictures at everything in sight, from tapestries to signs for the restrooms. “Throughout this dining hall you will see various wooden busts lining the ceiling. The busts are of various royal family members, and you can purchase replicas in the gift shop at the end of the tour.” ”Yeah mom, that’s just what I want, a freaky head in my bedroom. Do you have any pictures of Zac Efron for sale?” The girl’s eyes light up as the teen stars name leaves her lips, but all it does is cause an awkward smile to appear on the tour guides face. “Unfortunately, we do not sell any modern day memorabilia, but I am sure that you will find something that you like to add to your room.” The tour continues to delve further into the castle, with Walczak learning little about the culture, but his hatred for that of America’s deepens. The next room they enter immediately brings a look of awe to the face of Walczak. The walls are covered in gold, and the columns are marble. The room contains the most exquisite tapestries they have yet seen, and to Walczak this epitomizes what Poland and its people should be. “This room is one of the most extravagant in the castle. The gold decoration was put in by Casimir I, after he had erected the Wawel Cathedral. It was done in order to show the wealth and power the Polish King had.” “This is the definition of power and nobility is it not.” “It was certainly a sign of just how powerful Poland was at the time. In fact the majority of Eastern Europe was controlled by the Poles. The surrounding countries feared their presence, and many of the gifts in this castle were offered as compensation for protection.” “That is the way that it should be, and god willing will be once again.” Walczak’s smile covers his face as he stares around the room looking at all of the splendor that adorns the room. “Ha, come on, you mean Poland was more than just the butt of my hilarious jokes once.” Walczak looks at the smug look of the man, and his smile disappears. “Once, no, not once. For centuries they ruled this land. They ruled over this area longer than America has been known. It is so much more than just the butt of some your misguided quips, but I would not expect you to understand that.” “Take it easy there buddy. You cannot honestly think that Poland was ever half of that what America is now.” “Of course they aren’t. They were never loathed by the people of the world, or having drones of people hoping for the day of their demise. Poland ruled fairly, not by preying on peoples that were weaker than them.” The man once again wipes the sweat off of his forehead, and attempts to take a strong position next to Walczak, looking up at him. “I don’t know how you do things here in the old country, but back in the states we don’t take to kindly to being disrespected.” “Disrespected? What do you consider those jokes that you think are so hilarious. You think that we like being mocked? That is disrespect. I am just stating the facts.” Walczak moves his face closer and closer to his American counterpart, who now moves backwards, sweat quickly building up, as he takes deeper and deeper breaths. The tour guide gets in between the two men, trying to squash any chance of a physical altercation breaking out. “Alright let’s just get back to the tour.” “No, I have had enough of this. I am going to go off on my own. I can’t deal with being disrespected as this gentleman so eloquently put it.” Walczak leaves the group as the man pulls up his sweatpants, and has a look of victory on his face. The last thing that Walczak is concerned with is the fact that this man thinks that he has won this battle of wits, but Walczak has come to expect this from his previous encounters with the American people. How pathetic is it that the American people have no concept of how ordinary they really are. Their country is made up of people that other countries did not want anymore, but they are quick to kick out those they do not want. What hypocrisy. They built up their riches on the discoveries of others, and now they want the world to bow to them. I for one will not get on my knees to give respect to a country that has not even begun to deserve it. Walczak makes his way down a hallway containing portraits of past Polish rulers. Their faces beam with pride, and they show them to be true leaders, not like the Presidents ruling the country that Walczak despises. This is what it means to be a king. Look at the people covering these walls, do any of them look Wade Elliot…no…Do they look like Hoyt Williams…no…Perhaps they look like Devin Shakur…the thought makes me laugh. I am the only one who exudes royalty, and I will prove that in the King of Kings…
