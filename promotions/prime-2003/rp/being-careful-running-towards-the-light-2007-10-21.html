Being Careful; Running Towards the Light

21 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/21/07 WHERE WE LEFT OFF As told by The Spectator Ian Nackedy looked up at his older brother Foster suddenly, his ears hoping he didn’t hear what he just thought he heard. “Say that again?” Foster shook his head, placing his hand on Ian’s shoulder. “Dad’s been missing, Ian. No one’s seen or heard from him in three days.” Ian looked back down sourly. “Yeah, that’s what I thought you said.” FOSTER “POPS” NACKEDY III As told by The Historian Foster Nackedy III, Ian’s father and progenitor of the original “Worst name in wrestling history,” is a man mired by mystery and hard times. And also cocaine. Lots and lots of cocaine. Pops Nackedy spent most of Ian and Foster’s childhood in a coma after a particularly bad car accident that ended the life of Kate, their mother. Pops miraculously came out of the coma after fourteen years; it was a story the New Orleans papers couldn’t help but cover for its strangeness. He found both of his sons who were training at wrestling school, and he studiously undertook the position of their trainer and personal assistant (although being a personal assistant to two people kind of negates the term, doesn’t it? Think about it. ‘Personal’ implies that you are serving this one person completely, getting whatever they want: toothbrush, ice cream cake, fourteen Vietnamese hookers with cerebral palsy, you name it. But they can’t get all that for you if they’re doing it for someone else, can they? I mean…fuck it). After a few years, though, Ian and Foster noticed a disturbing trend; when they each received their paychecks and handed them to their father to deposit into a friendly neighborhood bank, portions of the checks never reached their accounts. When the Nackedy brothers confronted Pops on this, he tearfully confessed he was taking their money to support his ongoing drug addiction. See? Told you there was cocaine. Foster and Ian were upset, but they couldn’t simply fire him; this was their father, the root of their DNA, the founder of the Nackedies, THE KING OF THE JEWS…okay, that took it too far. But he was still their father, and thus they couldn’t get rid of him entirely. So, fearful of his safety, they brought him to a rehabilitation center in New York and bought a house for him there to be monitored by his next-door neighbor, Mr. Abbot. It may have been a little cruel, but Ian and Foster were in the middle of their rising careers at that time. They didn’t have time to watch over their slowly-deteriorating father. So they just gave him money every few weeks and hoped for the best. JUSTIFICATION As told by Ian Nackedy What we did to our dad makes us sound cruel. I’m aware of that. But I really don’t know what else we were supposed to do in that situation. I had just gotten my first real NWC contract; not some appearance contract, not a few paychecks for going with Foster to shows. I had a real, wrestler contract, and I was making bank for the first time ever. Me and Fos weren’t the silver spoon boys like a lot of guys you see around here. We weren’t Shakurs with millions of dollars. We were basically poor orphans, living in a foster home for years. It’s a good thing in a way, because that’s how I met Gil. But it definitely wasn’t easy. So I’m making money for the first time in my life, and this guy – no matter what DNA connection he has to me – is taking more than half of it to facilitate a coke habit? Fuck that. I earned a spot on a roster thanks to minimal talent and my brother’s recognition, and damned if I was going to lose it to some father of mine. That sounded bad, didn’t it? Yeah, thought so. But seriously. Part of me didn’t like what we had to do to dad, but I don’t think we had a choice. Dad had a problem, and if we didn’t stop it there, something even worse would have happened. Something like him turning up missing. Hm. TWO WEEKS LATER As told by The Spectator Two weeks after the announcement, Ian Nackedy was still ruminating over the situation. He walked into the living room for another of what seemed to be weekly family meetings. “What’s up?” he asked, tossing his gym bag onto the floor by the couch. Gildenstern shrugged. “Any word from dad yet?” Ian continued, deciding to go on a different vein. “Nothing,” said Foster, who was getting a glass of milk from the fridge. “Mr. Abbot told the police, they put out a small search detail, but there’s no progress being made.” “Hm.” Ian looked down at the counter and began to wonder how deep this situation was going to get for them. “What’s our money deal?” “National Bank won’t comp the money for another two weeks, and I took your paychecks and cashed them so we have about five grand here. I’m going to be getting us some more cash throughout the next few weeks, but even though we have enough money I don’t want us clearing the debt with the bank, because for one that’s evidence, and for another someone has our pin numbers and can just take out money again.” Foster’s eyes were dull, but Ian saw that being in charge of something important again was giving his older brother the adrenaline rush he was missing from wrestling. Gildenstern sighed obnoxiously. “So we have to use cash now? Maaan! Only hookers and junkies carry cash.” “Then you’re going to have to be a hooker,” said Foster gruffly. Ian suggested, “You can always be a junkie if you want.” “Do you want to be junkies this week?” asked Gildenstern. “GUYS!” shouted Foster, bringing Ian and Gil back into the real world. “We’re going to have to be careful now, because things are getting weird and I’m not sure what’s going to happen next. So all I’ll suggest is you stay here with me and Magen, keep wrestling, and keep cashing your checks instead of depositing them.” “What about dad?” asked Gil, his face completely serious for once. “Just keep your ears open. If he’s in trouble he might try contacting us. Since all of us have full time schedules, we can’t go out to New York and search for him ourselves, so that’s all we can do.” Foster sipped his milk, sighed, and shook his head. Ian put his hand on his older brother’s shoulder and nodded. “It’s okay, man. We’re going to get through this. Dad’s probably fine, he just wanted to get out for a bit. We know how much he hates being all by himself. He probably visited an old friend or something.” RUN As told by Pops Nackedy I hope they forgive me. I hope they aren’t mad. I don’t know what I’m doing anymore. I don’t know anything at all. All I know is I can barely walk, and there’s a faint light ahead. I want to reach it, but I don’t know if I’m strong enough. But I have to be. I have to get there. This won’t be useless. This won’t be in vain. I can do this. I’m a strong man. I raised two strong men. I can get there. All I have to do is push. See? Light’s a little bit bigger now. Everything’s gonna be all right.
