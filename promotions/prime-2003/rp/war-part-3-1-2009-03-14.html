War (Part 3.1)

14 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 14th March 14th, 2009 10 PM Losing the love of a lifetime to murder is gut wrenching. Walking through the house, instinctively anticipating her glowing face staring back with a friendly smile and "Good morning dear," becomes more poignant as the days progress. Sitting down for a cup of coffee and reading the morning paper turns the stomach inside out, leaving emptiness hanging like a constant and individualized rain cloud on a bright sunny day. No more lunches, trips to the park, or long walks with inseparable hands locked together. Her silky skin would never again brush against his arm. Her lips would never rest against his in a passionate kiss. He would never get to tell her that he loved her again. Supporters told Larry Connors time would heal all wounds. He never believed the ridiculous mantra. In his line of work, when old ones healed, new and more devastating ones manifested. His daughter was taken next, her lily white throat slashed by Christian Daniels. The offspring Larry raised into the world was made another statistic in the massive collection amassed by the Smith crew. While local authorities were befuddled at the professionalism of the break in, Larry knew the truth all too well. Every time his eyes shut at night, the images flooded his dreams. The seven roamed the area of the home. After manipulating the guardians, Christian got into her room. Devin stood guard. Christian talked to Cynthia like he knew her. Christian slithered his right hand into torn denim jeans like a snake, deceiving his unsuspecting pray. Every time this portion of the dream manifested, Larry screams "CYNTHIA! CYNTHIA!" but nobody hears his voice except those in the apartment. Christian didn't hear him. Devin didn't. Cynthia didn't. The knife swung out so quick Cynthia never saw or felt the blade touch her throat. The dream was his inescapable prison. His body twitched uncontrollably, tossing and turning recklessly in the night. When Steven or another friend shook him awake, tears formed in only a few seconds. They all knew what he was suffering from and the only decent consolation available was an embrace. They wouldn't dare speak the phrase "Everything will be alright" because none of it would be. Christian, Devin, and fellow brothers vowed in August of 2008 to destroy the Connors' lives for interfering in affairs they had no business interjecting into. If they were willing to put down a four year old girl, despite Christian's troubled past with children, their bounds were limitless. They would go to the ends of the Earth for one another and to achieve their goals. Pure and unadulterated evil Larry couldn't bring himself to rise up from bed. The prospect of facing such a cruel and sadistic world which harbors such animals is overbearing. Everywhere he goes would remind him of the abhorrent incidents that have captivated his once happy life. He didn't even yearn to look over at his nightstand where pictures of Maria and Cynthia still remained. Maria and Cynthia were his life, the only two things he lived for. To enrich their lives with a stable roof, put food on the table, and provide himself as an accessible father and husband were his only purposes on the planet. The police job and following in tradition's footsteps were noteworthy, but not to the extent of his girls. He had nothing left to live for. His happiness had been shattered by men who would walk around with facetious grins plastered, almost portraying life to be a joke. For the first time in his life, Larry looked at the service revolver on the end of the bed in a new fashion. It would provide a cynical form of closure, even if it meant he lost the war. Fighting through the migraine headache to a seated position, Larry reached down and unhooked the gun from its holster, staring into the barrel. He couldn't do it here, but he knew of a place that he could. March 14th, 2009 11 PM A red towel covered the barrel of Devin Shakur's own service revolver, one of many guns stationed inside the family's artillery room. He held the weapon up to the mirror and nodded in approval, placing it back inside the gun shelf and gingerly exiting the room, locking the door behind him. Devin just came off one of the most stellar performances of his wrestling career, defeating Tyler Rayne in an absolute car wreck of a wrestling match on Night 1 of Culture Shock. Fighting through the developing pain in his legs and back, Devin managed to make the top 6 in the Dual Halo and eliminate Rayne in the process. It was impossible not to think of the benefits promised by Lisa Tyler for performing the task. Christian Daniels sat on the couch with his feet propped atop the glass coffee table, flipping channels on the plasma television. The Biker could be proud of his brother if more relevant matters weren't plaguing his mind. Only forty-eight hours ago, he committed the most heinous act ever in his forty years, and the repercussions were still lingering. It was never his intention to involve the child, but he needed a statement that wouldn't be ignored. The media circus surrounding Cynthia's death was monstrous. Christian couldn't find a local news outlet that wasn't presenting new developments at every available newscast. A police detective's daughter with the respect of the Connors name held an incredible amount of weight throughout the state. No suspects were being released. Pretty much everybody in the department knew who was behind the killing, but proving the matter would be difficult, plus a sense of vigilante justice ran through their blood. None of the people on the force wanted Christian, Devin, and the rest of the crew to run through the judicial system. The group had mountains of money to assemble the best legal team and make the experience even more poignant for every officer. Everybody who held an ounce of respect for the Connors wanted to paint the town with Smith blood. Christian was indifferent about their opinion and welcomed their hatred. He had a massive gang of individuals at his disposal willing to give their lives for the cause of 'Death'. Not to mention every target would expose themselves to Christian and the tacticians that stood behind him. He would ruin the fellow officers just like he was currently ruining the Connors. But an opinion that held weight was Caleb Daniels, a face he hadn't seen in seventeen years. Christian couldn't shake the image of his baby son in his arms, the same age as Cynthia was when he cut her throat. If he was watching these events unfold and knew that his father was behind the killings, how would Caleb perceive his dad? The possibilities made Christian uneasy for the past three days. Devin observed his elder brother on the couch, still mentally checked out since he came back from the murder. The only thing Christian did was drink beer and watch news coverage, coping mechanism that wouldn't bode well in the long run. Keeping things inside would only systematically tear away and build upon the already cemented mental anguish. Devin knew Christian wouldn't allow him to play psychologist and nitpick his brain, never in their relationship had that happened and he wasn't expecting that miracle to manifest. Devin tried to ruminate on possible outlets that would cause Christian to forget about Caleb, Cynthia, and the Connors, but everything led back to one form or another of violence. Nothing Devin could conjure would eliminate those thoughts from swirling. On the kitchen island, a loud vibration broke the uneasy silence between the brothers. Christian dismissed the sound with a hand wave and left Devin to walk over and pick up the phone, putting the device up to his ear. "Christian's phone, what's good?" Devin said. Devin put a finger into his other ear to block out the voice of Linda Loveland reciting the top news stories on WRAL, the latest station Christian stopped on. "Kid, slow down and stop speaking What, nobody's gonna put a bullet in your head." Devin snapped The mumbled voice continued to speak and Devin's facial expression changed. A break in the news brought Christian's eyes over to Devin and he noticed the difference. He paused the DVR, leaving a picture of Steven Connors in the top left corner of the television. March 14th, 2009 11:10 PM Steven Connors slammed the shot glass with a vengeance, startling the last patron that was foolish enough to sit near him at Mikey's Bar. Business wasn't usually fantastic on Sunday at 11 PM, and even less so after Raleigh had caught the crime epidemic once again. Even before the untimely deaths of Cynthia and Maria, street gangs were resurfacing and making their presence felt. Women who chose to go out alone or in small groups were the intended victims and police were on the verge of having to sweep the new cells out. To see the debauchery around him, tacked on with his personal problems, made Steven sick. He was working through a plethora of professional grief and incapable of taking his frustrations out. Unable to get near any paramount case due to the botched interrogation of Devin Shakur almost a year ago, he resorted to other methods in order to clear his head. Most of which consisted of drinking and having casual sex with the first good looking blonde he saw. However, each day he woke up feeling the burden of his brother's pain. No amount of comfort could ease Larry's anger at his mistakes. If only he had accompanied Maria out to the jazz concert then she probably wouldn't be dead. If he only took Cynthia back just a few days earlier, she wouldn't be dead. Steven garnered a sense of fear from the Smith crew on killing off the brothers, almost as if they were plotting a grand scheme especially for them. No loved ones would be compromised in their presence because then witnesses would have to be extinguished as well. The bartender was a young man who didn't want any problems, fixated on the pistol in Steven's holster and decided to leave the bottle of Jack Daniels for the angered man. He'd never seen a gun before and didn't desire seeing one in his face now. Steven unscrewed the cap and downed the remainder of the bottle, sliding it back down the bar and standing up. Nobody was going to tell Steven, his temper and troubles well televised, that he couldn't drive home. That gun wasn't just for show and most people in the bar could vouch for that. A buzz occurred in Steven's pocket causing him to emit a guttural groan and whip out the phone. In the process of moving it up to his ear, he almost flipped the phone twenty feet across the room. "Fuck is it?" Steven asked. The voice on the other line was brisk and to the point, one of heavy concern, "Steven, man, I saw Larry a few minutes ago and the kid wasn't looking too good." "No shit, Frank, man just lost his wife and kid, ain't nobody gonna look good after that.." Frank leaned up against the brick wall, hands shaking. "Steven, I asked him where he was going and..." "And he what?" Frank sighed before uttering the next sentence, "He said he was gonna end it all, showed me a gun. I tried stopping me and he waved it in my face." "You better not be fucking joking with me, Frank! Where is he?" "I...I don't know." Steven's temper shot up, "THIS ISN'T A GAME, GODDAMMIT! WHERE. THE. FUCK. IS. HE?" "I'm on James and he was heading down Woodlawn, near the apartments." Steven shut the phone and stumbled out of the bar, rummaging in his pocket for the keys. He needed to sober up quickly and come to rational senses, otherwise he would feel the emotional trauma of making a giant mistake of his own. March 14th, 2009 11:20 PM Christian closed the distance between Devin and extended his hand for the phone. Devin moved away, trying to grasp the information being told over the phone. "You still aren't making sense, junior, now spit it out or I'm hanging up." Devin said. "Give me the phone." Christian replied, moving his hand out again. Pushing out his hand to create space between the two, Devin still couldn't understand what the kid was saying. Christian didn't like being kept in the dark and used his ten inch reach advantage to snake his arm around Devin and snatch the phone from his grip. "What's it son, and don't give me no bullshit." Christian said. Hearing the familiar voice of his boss, the kid's voice unnerves and lucid words flow from his mouth. Devin only heard jumbled hooplah while Christian got the full version. "That so? Alright Doc, thanks for the info." Christian pocketed the phone and ran a hand through his hair. "One of my informants running around, claims he saw Larry climbing up to the roof of an apartment building with a gun in his hand." Devin's eyebrows raised, not so much at the information but at Christian's demeanor. His voice has purpose. "So what are you gonna do?" With no hesitation in his voice, Christian responded, "Make sure he does the job because I know how much of a pansy he is." Devin smirked and shook his head in sarcastic disbelief, "Your a cruel bastard." Christian cranked his neck from side to side after throwing on his denim trenchcoat, "Doc also said Larry was waving the gun at someone and that he thinks the kid called Steven." "How does that involve me?" Grabbing the keys off the rack, Christian flicked his wrist and put them in Devin's hands. "I'm going to need a reckless fucker behind the wheel, beat him there and get me out." The two brothers exited the home in a dash, seeking Woodlawn Road. The race was on to find Larry Connors. If Steven got to him first, his life would be saved. If Christian got to him first, the results would be devastating.
