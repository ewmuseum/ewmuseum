Lydia turned to him halfway down the hall and laced her arms around his neck, tipping onto her toes she reached up and kissed him gently on the lips, a smile lay across his face as she slid back down. “It was nice meeting you, Pierce Lavelle,” Lydia said handing him back his jacket. “Yeah, you too,” he said and watched her walk away. Matthew Collins appeared from the shadows, a glass of scotch in his hand. “See, I told you she was good.” ---- Hailey Rose greeted Pierce seated on the edge of the sofa in a white dressing gown and silk pyjamas, her arms were folded and the lights were all out – except for the small table lamp next to her. He rested his keys in the bowl as he flung his jacket onto the back of the kitchen chair. “Not now, Hailey, I’m really tired,” he said as he opened a bottle of water. “Matt says you got a big lead,” she mumbled, arms still folded and he couldn’t help get a sense that she was about to tell him something. “Yeah, took all night but I got it.” His bedroom door was shut, as was Hailey’s, he looked toward her and furrowed his eyebrows. “Is something wrong?” he wondered. “We have a guest,” she retorted and pointed to Pierce’s bedroom. He rested the bottle on the kitchen counter and turned to his bedroom door. “In my room?” Hailey just nodded her head and walked into her room. “You did good Pierce, I’m proud of you,” she said shutting the door behind her. Pierce stood a moment alone in the dark apartment hall, eyes cast onto the door handle, his hands shaking a moment: Who could be here to see me? He thought as his hand gripped the door handle. The sound of a baby crying emanated through the thin oak door, ruffling all of his nerves. He paused. The door opened and there stood before him was Lillian Connelly, dressed in a pair of his grey sweatpants and Hailey’s ‘Seahawks’ t-shirt. In her arms was a small baby, pawing at her mother’s hair, her lungs letting out a wailing cry. They both froze, his look said it all to her - he was in shock. “OK, I know this looks bad, but if we could just sit down and talk …” Lillian managed to say through the cries of her daughter. Pierce just stood frozen in one spot, looking at Lillian’s battered frame. “What happened to you?” he asked, looking at her broken arm, bruised face and hovelled step, in a worried tone. Lillian turned away, slightly, and slid past Pierce. “I don’t really feel like talking about.” “Well you can’t exactly waltz back into my life without explaining anything, Lilly, we did that once and I almost ended up dead.” He looked toward her, arms folded and leant against the wall. She turned around, holding the baby against her chest with one arm. He sighed heavily and moved forward. “Here, let me take her,” he said calmly and took the small child from Lillian, his eyes resting on her delicate face and pale blue eyes. “She’s cute, she’s got your nose …” he said with a smile, looking toward the small child. “Who’s the dad?” Lillian froze, her heart pounding in her chest. She looked toward him and he looked toward the baby’s eyes and features, her eyes a crystal blue - his eyes staring back at him as he kept her at arms length. Lillian moved forward, noticing the look of realisation creep on him. “I was going to tell you sooner, but every time was a bad time and we left on such bad terms … I didn’t know what else to do,” she said, approaching him with her arm folded across her broken one. “You could’ve told me anytime Lilly, I would’ve been there to help you,” he said looking at his daughter, her small frame nestled against his chest. “I know that now,” she mumbled. “Are you all right?” he asked. Lillian looked up, eyebrows raised. “I just dropped a bombshell on you and you’re worried about me.” “Yes, you and our daughter are the most important things in my life now. I am worried, because if you and this little baby have nowhere to go, I want you to stay here.” He said with a smile, and she couldn’t help but smile. “Just how do you feel with sharing an apartment with two other women?” he asked. “That’s fine,” she said with a smile. He lifted his daughter up and rocked her gently, the crying had stopped. “I suppose I’ve got a lot to tell you,” she said, running her hand gently along her daughter’s head. “You could start by telling me her name.” Lillian smiled and looked at her daughter. “Her name is Calleigh Lavelle.” Lavelle couldn’t believe everything that had happened to him, he stood in front of Lillian, holding his daughter Calleigh close to his heart and a smile lined his lips, things were looking up for him. Now he just needed a crib, clothes and a college fund started. How hard could all that be?
