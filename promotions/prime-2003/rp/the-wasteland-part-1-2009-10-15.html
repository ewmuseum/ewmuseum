The Wasteland: Part 1

15 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 15, 2009 Welcome to the Show April 18th April is the cruelest month, some people say, and though there are no lilacs being bred from the dead land, cruelty surely abounds at the DFW Arena this cool spring night in Santa Fe, New Mexico.[1] Mike Valentine looks like he just finished a ten hour shift at a butcher shop, his fatigues drenched in a bloody sweat from a botched blade job. It occurs to him, as he staggers across the floor to lift up the ring apron and pull out a bag of thumbtacks, that the US Marines had worked him hard, taken a lot from him during his brief tour fifteen years ago, but they had never asked him to bleed himself to death in front of a tepid crowd of 157 wrestling fans. The epiphany is short lived, as a double ax handle to the back of the head sends him stumbling forward, one, two, three steps and down head first to the ground, where he barely manages to get an arm up in time to prevent the side of his face from turning to hamburger against the concrete. Diego Foster soaks up the scattered applause from the fans at ringside, moving in slowly to follow up his assault, only to eat a kendo stick to the face from the recovering Valentine. Yes, ladies and gentlemen, wrestlers come and go, blood is lost and replenished, but Death-Matchpalooza only comes once a year. A one night tournament of weapons and gore typical of such marginal organizations, fun will be had by all on hand, save the participants of course. The smart money is on DFW's resident hardcore specialist and last year's winner, Mike Valentine to easily march to finals of the tournament again this year. Or it would be, if anyone cared enough to wager money on a DFW event. In the first round, however, Diego Foster is giving him more trouble than anyone thought possible. Diego Foster: young, athletic, bright-eyed and with big dreams, just winding up his rookie year in DFW. At the age of 25 certainly no longer a child, yet even standing 6'4, 264 pounds, there is still something undeveloped about him, a latency carried within him.[2] Judging by looks alone, he should be the clear favorite in comparison to his middle-aged, out of shape opponent. The crowd is appreciative as he catches the next swat of the kendo stick with one hand, brushing it aside in a spin that finishes with a roaring elbow into the gash on Mike's forehead, sandwiching him into the ring post in the same motion. A few minutes later, they're in the ring, surrounded by thumbtacks and florescent light tubes, and Diego has been making a show of it, rallying, the crowd actually getting into it for once. Mike can hardly stand at this point, the canvas stained a bland mauve with his blood. Diego is motioning at his opponent to get to his feet, looking dismayed when he stumbles, waiting, five seconds, ten seconds, until finally Mike is up. With a subtle nod he back-steps into the ropes, then comes running back with a high looping lariat, which Valentine drunkenly ducks under. He tries to lift Diego, but fails. Tries again, and drops him. Finally Diego jumps in the air, allowing Mike on shaky legs to somersault forward in a rolling Samoan drop onto the thumbtacks. He lays back on Diego and seconds later his generic military march music is being played throughout the arena, mere background noise to the catcalls of an angry crowd that knows a terrible job when it sees one. Mike, unable to walk under his own power, needs the assistance of two ring crew members to make it to the back. Diego returns to the locker room by himself, head hanging, hand cupped to his forehead in dismay. * * * “That shit was not my fault,” Diego says, upset. He's standing in the main backstage area, where the other wrestlers are sitting around, most of them uninterested in the distraught rookie. Looking over briefly at Mike Valentine in the corner, getting treated by the single medical personnel on hand for the evening, he turns back and lowers his voice into a hiss. “He's the one that fucked it up. Old man should know by now how to cut himself without opening up a fucking geyser.” “Whoa, fella,” says Wally, getting up from his seat to place a hand on Diego's shoulder, a wry smile on his face. “Just relax. We've all had stinkers out there. Well....not me, but most of the guys in here have. It's just part of the game, they can't all be highlight reel material. No biggie.” “That's easy for you to say, but somehow I'm gonna be the one who catches hell for it. Just wait,” Diego says, shaking his head. “These fixes, I'm goddamn sick of them.” “Just gotta pay your dues, that's all it is,” Jimbo says, looking up from a rapidly disappearing plate of nachos. “McKenzie had me pulling jobs my first year, too. You'll be seeing some real competition shortly.” Diego mutters, taking a seat at the table next to Jim. “I'm starting to think I pissed him off somehow.” Wally and Jimbo exchange glances over the head of Diego, who's staring at the table, still shaking his head. Wally reaches down into the cooler and pops the tab on a cold one, placing it down in front of him. “Ah, forget about it. You're done for the night. Just relax and have a beer.” The thing was, Diego Foster simply did not like to lose, at any time really, and especially not now that rumors were floating about regarding a GTT spot up for grabs. Wrestling was one of the few things in life that he was both good at and liked doing, and he didn't sign on to lay down for aging wrestlers at the request of management. There were all kinds of backstage politics he hadn't taken into account in the beginning, having since been expounded on at length by DFW owner Jack McKenzie on more than one occasion. It couldn't always be a straight competition. No one would last into their 30's if that was the case. Sometimes you had to give a hand to an old-timer, out of respect for their contribution to the business, let them get a few more years in, a few more paychecks before the sun set on their careers. Or else protect a top draw, someone that brought in the customers or was making a run for the title, preserve that mystique for the fans. It was just good business, he was told. Diego thought it was all a load of shit. Though nothing in his life up until this point had provided him with any evidence of its existence, he nevertheless held some faith in the fundamental justice of the universe. In other words, that the cream always rises to the top. It was this belief that sustained him over the course of a rough childhood after his father abandoned him at the age of nine. The mother of his youth, sweet and loving, was ravaged by time at a rate far beyond the norm, each year her face more weathered, her eyes more weary, the smell of alcohol heavier on her breath Though the universe conspired against him, he kept the faith, that would make something of himself, be able to get ahead on his own merit. He thought wrestling was the answer. These days he wasn't so sure. Wally thought that Diego was naïve. If you were looking for fair and honest competition, you were in the wrong profession. Wrestling was all about getting attention, that moment in the spotlight. That's what brought Wally Simmonds to the game, and if ever there was a man born to play a wrestling heel, it was him. Slightly shorter and less muscular than Diego, his dark hair cropped short and spiky, usually wearing an unbuttoned dress shirt over a wifebeater, he was known for telling whoever would listen how his character was basically an extension of himself. Which was true enough. Coming up from a poor family, through a series of savvy investments he found himself owning a number of Santa Fe properties at a rather young age, giving him the financial cushion needed to pursue his interest in professional wrestling. This all would be impressive and praiseworthy enough, if only he didn't insist on constantly bringing it up in conversation. His ego kept most people in the company at arm's-length, and a recent unpopular stint as the company's TV champion hadn't helped matters much.[3] Which is why he condescended to hang out with Jimbo and Diego occasionally, even if he did consider them below him. The real genius of Wally's character, though, was that he was completely oblivious to the fact that he was coming off as a heel in the ring. He legitimately thought that he was portraying an inspirational character, a modern day rags-to-riches story that could serve as a model for the fans in attendance. For some reason, they didn't see things the same way, though his constant cheating and occasional cowardice in the ring couldn't have helped matters much. Thanks to the recession, he's been blowing off the negative crowd reactions as nothing more than the jealousy of people hit hard by the economy, but Diego and Jimbo can only speculate how he'll attempt to account for it once things start to recover. “Probably attribute it to the growing socialist movement,” Jimbo imagines. “Or illegal immigration. Better watch out, D.” Your first glance at James Hutchison, better known as Jimbo in the ring, will immediately bring to mind George “The Animal” Steele. Though he still has a layer of short curly hair clinging to his head, he's carrying around an extra 80 pounds around that he doesn't need. If you combine this with an excess of body hair and a face that droops like a man twice his age, it's surprising that the man's a dedicated kindergarten teacher during the day. You'd think the kids would be terrified, but if you go by what they tell their parents, they absolutely love the guy. Which is fair enough, as he's a really funny guy. If you're a 5 year old. Though he tries, his humor doesn't typically translate to the adult set. His character, will usually unleash a string of cringe-worthy rhymes, puns, and malapropisms at his opponent before the match, which will either be cheered or groaned at, depending on how nice the crowd is feeling. He doesn't mind which, as long as he gets a reaction. Where Diego wrestles to compete and Wally wrestles for attention, Jimbo wrestles to entertain, a god awful comedian in tights and elbow pads. Though they all had their own reasons for coming to the sport, one thing was for sure: no one did it for the money. DFW called itself a professional wrestling federation, but it was sort of a misnomer, as no one on the roster considered it their main source of income. They got paid, all right, but what little they got was funneled into their hobbies: booze, drugs, and other forms of escapism. Diego's entire DFW paycheck, for example, was used to maintain his stock of marijuana. For most, DFW was no more than a limbo, a brief stopping point on the way to bigger and better things. This point of view lent itself to their escapism, and it in turn made sure a permanent escape was seldom realized. Yet even in Limbo, life goes on. “You're not going to cry in front of your girlfriend, are you?” Wally asks, shoving the still despondent Diego in the side and pointing across the room at Mercedes, tall and raven-haired, who at the moment is going through her stretches in revealing ring gear. “What? Girlfriend? No, that's not happening anymore. I haven't even talked to her recently,” Diego answers, waving it off. “Whaaaaaat?” Jimbo asks in disbelief. “I thought that was a done deal. You were seeing her three times a week.” “I don't want to talk about it,” Diego says, taking a deep swig of his beer. He nearly spits it out as a big meaty hand claps down on his shoulder from behind. “Great match out there, D” Ben says with a genuine smile. “You're really coming along. Thought you nearly had it at the end.” For a few seconds an awkward silence hangs in the air before finally: “It was a fuckin' job, Ben!” Wally shouts, glaring at the six foot nine Australian. “Now get the fuck out of here cause he doesn't want to talk about it.” Ben Harris was the pariah of DFW. Born in Adelaide, he had no friends in primary school, no friends in high school, no friends in uni. There was just something about him that put people off, whether it be his unnatural size or his penchant to say exactly the wrong thing at the wrong time. He moved to the States after graduation, thinking it would afford him the chance to start anew. He was soon disappointed. And though he was working by day at the local Toyota car dealership, due to his preternatural ability to make everyone around him uncomfortable, his sales weren't high enough for his commission to pay the bills. One day it was suggested to him to try out for a DFW show, get a few bucks to make ends meet, and three years later here he is. The promoter gave him the gimmick of Big Ben, an English soccer hooligan with a short fuse.[4] Possessing an Australian's natural hatred of the English, he was not happy with it at first, but when pressed, had to admit that the name certainly fit. He was Big. And he was Ben. What more was there to say? But you couldn't have an Australian going around calling himself after one of London's biggest tourist attractions. That just wouldn't do. So he was forced to swallow his hatred of the whinging poms in order to masquerade as one of them. Naturally this all did little to help his already shaky self-esteem issues. Ben's smile nearly falters, then he lets out a forced guffaw. “Ha! Nice one, Wally. You kidder,” taking a seat at their table. “So what do you fellas think about the big announcement McKenzie's set to make? About the GTT, they're saying.” Wally and Jimbo sigh at the prospect of having to engage Ben in conversation. Diego sighs at the prospect of having to continue living out this life he's found himself in the middle of. McKenzie April 30th A 37-year veteran of the sport, “Cowboy” Jack McKenzie had begun wrestling at the 16. He'd been a mainstay of the circuit during the time where the regional feds were really booming. He'd held the Mid-South title, the South-Midwest title, the Northeast-Southwest title, and the South Mid-Mid-Southwest-South title, among others. Using the money he'd saved during the years, he decided upon his retirement to invest in a federation of his own. Thus, the world witnessed the birth of DFW. He soon regretted his decision, finding himself at the helm of a ship caught in a slow whirlpool. He would have preferred a quick, devastating bankruptcy, but the slow week by week hemorrhaging was just too painful to endure. It was in this period that he turned to drink, and it was during these late night solo sessions that he decided a change was necessary. The regional fed was behind the times, the fans just didn't appreciate the pace of the matches anymore. DFW slowly metamorphosed into a hardcore federation, as McKenzie gradually let the old timers go, with their revenue sucking contracts, in favor of hiring unqualified, masochistic idiots he was able to pay peanuts. Though actually having succeeded in turning a profit, infinitesimal as it was, McKenzie was less successful in kicking the bottle. His wrestling physique long since turned into wave upon wave of repulsive fat, the heavier he got, the meaner he got, and he ruled over his federation like Caligula, with a tyrant's disdain for those underneath him. The people doing the hiring at PRIME wouldn't even waste their time giving half these guys a try-out. They'd be rejected on sight: mostly skinny kids and fatasses who don't mind getting light bulbs smashed over their heads. McKenzie was faced now with the task of selecting one of these rejects to send as DFW's representative for the GTT. Just the thought of it irritated him. It had already been causing him headaches, as various wrestlers had been clamoring for face time with him the past week to plead their cases, Diego Foster foremost among them. He hated to admit it, but Diego was actually one of the better choices to fill out the spot. He had that clean cut babyface look that would play well against the villainous Snow, and was athletic enough on top of that to ensure the match would be more than a 2-minute squash. Oh he'd lose for sure, definitely, but he might be one of the few guys on the roster able to do it in a way that'd make DFW look good. The problem was, McKenzie just couldn't stand the sight of the boy. Though as a pure wrestler, little fault could be found with him, it was his behavior outside of the ring that really stuck in Jack's craw. Lazy, showing up at shows reeking of marijuana, always chasing tail. The boy simply came from trash, and McKenzie knew better than to give a push to someone like that.[5] Taking his bottle of Wild Turkey from the top drawer, he takes a long draw, as if to cleanse his mouth of some foul taste. He didn't know what Mercedes ever saw in the boy. Obviously no future there. A camera crew was coming to film some hype interviews for the GTT in a few weeks. He'd weed down the contenders by then, let the last few send their tapes in. The GTT brass could pick whoever they wanted, he could care less. He had more important things to focus on, he thought, taking another swig of bourbon. More About Diego's Marijuana Use Some people manage to work their way through copious amounts of pot every week, every day, and carry on with their lives with little to no outward sign of the effects normally contingent on such maverick consumption of cannabis. Whether this can be attributed to some evolved physiological characteristic, the development a tolerance over time, or just the uncanny ability to act like they're not stoned when in fact they are really, really stoned is a matter of debate. But regardless of where they weigh in on the issue, everyone who's smoked with him will agree to the following: Diego Foster is certainly not one of them. As his friends sit around the room packing the bowl, Diego usually walks into the room literally giving off energy, his body fueled by a near-nuclear combination of espresso and Red Bull. Talking a mile a minute about some book he's read or movie he's seen, the latest absurdity in the news, or else complaining about the most recent wrench thrown in his plans, he's very lucid and impassioned, a nascent, undeveloped charisma hanging about him. The appearance of the lighter heralds the great shift in the evening's discourse, however. If you filmed the entire session and edited out everything except Diego's dialog, the viewer would likely get the impression of a slow motion effect gradually being applied by some mischievous sound technician. Unfortunately, this rascally troublemaker would be wholly the product of your imagination. The tempo isn't the only thing that changes, as sooner or later Diego will be sure to turn the subject matter to a rambling back and forth about how great it would be to write a show to pitch to Cartoon Network[6], that's the ticket, and they fucking deserve it, they're clever enough, and funny, REALLY funny, funnier than a lot of the people doing that kind of shit right now, you know those guys are just phoning it in half the time, and if only they could get off their asses and put something together, man, that's the ticket...and so on and so forth. This is usually followed by a weak attempt to formulate a pilot of the aforementioned program, but more often than not this results in some ridiculous one-off story more suited to a flash video than a long-running animation series. And even aiming their sights at a flash video is wishful thinking for this group. [7] And this is just after the first bowl! You should see him after the second. Or better yet, talk to him. You'll be longing for a return to the time when talk of a poorly plotted animation pilot was a possibility. After two bowls Diego is like that drunken baby mouse from the Tom and Jerry cartoon, doing a back and forth hiccuping stagger near the precipice of incoherency.[8] Yammering disjointed sentences leap from topic to topic, making a Samuel Beckett short story seem palatable in comparison. And after the third? Well that's lights out for the evening, ladies and gentlemen, ring the bell. We haven't even touched upon how dirty his apartment is, not having been cleaned in months due to his, um, preoccupations. This is the guy whose friend found a box of cereal in his cabinet swarming with fruit flies. Whose living room was invaded by a noxious odor for over two months before the mystery was a solved – a dirty, sweaty sock sandwiched underneath his futon. Dishes piled up in the sink for weeks on end. A pubic hair forest on the floor surrounding his toilet. Is it any wonder the man is single? Brief Interviews: #1 Q. 'No, it's my pleasure. Reunion July 29th “Shoot.” His breathing labored, Diego measures the man standing across from him, contempt tinged with awe in his gaze. He takes a step forward but then stops, unsure of himself. His foe, impatient, turns his head and spits to the side. “I said go ahead and shoot, you pansy-ass faggot.” Diego swallows audibly, but doesn't make a move. The other man is older, his face weathered, in his late forties at least. Yet despite his age, or perhaps because of it, he has a hardness to him, a warning to the world implicit in his bearing. Given a poncho and a cowboy hat, he would look for all the world like a modern day gunslinger. Yet Diego is the one being told to shoot. And ,with a deep breath, he does. The man's fist cracks across the bridge of Diego's nose as he stuffs his double-leg take down attempt. For a second the world turns white, but Diego quickly blinks it back into being, doggedly pressing forward. Yet each step he takes is negated by a matching step back from his opponent before a final deft twist into a cross-face sends him crumpling to the mat. “You think this is all fun and games?” the man asks, a dangerous smile on his face now that he's firmly in control. It broadens a bit more as he smashes a hammer fist into the side of Diego's head. “You learn a few moves, some basic chain wrestling, and you think you're a wrestler? You drive over to the gym after work three times a week in your shitty car, log a few hours in the ring, and you think you're a wrestler? It wouldn't matter if it was a few hundred hours, boy, you'd still reek of bush-league.” His eyes closed, a pained expression on his face, Diego tries to explode out. He barely gets halfway before his feet are pulled out from underneath him, and he eats a few more hammer fists for his efforts. “This world has made you soft,” the man says, a hint of sadness in his voice. His smile falters, his expression reforming into a glare as he pounds away suddenly, viciously at the back and sides of Diego's head. “You're soft, and Jason Snow is the hardest motherfucker on the planet! How are you going to beat him? How are you even gonna put a scratch on him? He's gonna tear to you to shreds!” The accumulated punches are well into the double digits by the time his tirade culminates, and Diego slackens in a panic, allowing the man to hooks his legs on the inside of his thighs. He straightens him out into a rear naked choke with little effort, and fear in his eyes, Diego taps. The man releases the hold and gets to his feet, shaking his head as he regards the gasping, shuddering boy on the mat. “No, we need to harden you up, and that's why I'm here. You can't form a diamond without pressure.” Spitting blood, Diego manages a half laugh full of self-pity, looking up at the old man from his knees. “Can't form a diamond overnight, either. It takes long years. So where the fuck were you this whole time?” Long pause. “Dad?” The old man's lips firm into a grim smile of acknowledgement, but he says nothing. * * * As most children growing up with a delinquent father are likely to do, during his childhood Diego spent a not inconsiderable amount of time imagining what he'd say if he ever met the man again. In the beginning, young adolescent that he was, profanity seemed to be the most attractive option, an attempt to wound the man with a barrage of insults, spit in his face at the end. But the more he thought about it, the more he came to realize that he could say nothing the man had never heard before, and anyway, a man who could so easily walk out on his family was not likely to be hurt by a little foul language. In down periods, he wrote dramatic monologues, trying to form the perfect composition to tug at the man's heartstrings. He figured the best thing to do would be to come off strong and arrogant, show him that he turned out fine without the man, that his presence wasn't needed, now or ever. The real point of attack would be to make him feel guilty about what happened to Diego's mother after he left. If there was ever a real life sob story, it was that, and if that didn't affect the man, well, nothing probably would. All those plans went out the window, though, the morning he walked out of his bedroom to find his father sitting at his kitchen table, eating a bowl of corn flakes and drinking coffee. An unbuttoned flannel shirt over a white tank top, black jeans, a mustache and beard. These are the things that Diego focused on, trying to place his uninvited guest. He was clueless until he focused on the man's face. Fernando De La Torre's face was similar to his son's, but where Diego's seemed made of clay, his father's seemed chiseled from granite. More than any other detail of him, it was that hardness that Diego remembered. “Well, just look at you,” the old man said, smiling as he took a sip of coffee. “All grown up.” Diego stood there speechless, all his hours of rehearsal worthless after all. “Your coffee really tastes like shit,” his father said, making a bitter face. “It's the cheapest kind,” he replied, the first words spoken to his dad in sixteen years. “Well I suppose you're asking yourself what your old man is doing sitting in your kitchen at 8 am on a Saturday morning?” his father asked, getting up from the table to walk over to Diego. “But isn't it obvious? I have come to grant you a boon.” “What?” “A boon. A blessing. You're in need of one, no?” His father managed to encompass the apartment, Santa Fe, Diego's life with one sweep of the arm. “Are you happy with this? Where's your desire gone?” Diego didn't answer, staring at his father, past his father at the wall behind him. “Is it there?” his father asked, pointing at the resin-caked bowl laying next to Diego's futon. “Is that where your desire went? Smoke it all up?” Diego said nothing. “You're just going through the motions. You need to realize that action without desire will forever remain rooted, a puppet show in the shadows while reality unfolds in the distance,” his father said, eyes closed. “Though I will admit some of the blame is mine. A failure to set an example.” “Yeah,” is all Diego said. Though he wants to say more, nothing comes to him. His tongue felt dry in his mouth. “Though I don't have much time, I'm here to rectify that. To make a beginning, at least,” he said, opening his eyes. “What do you want more than anything?” “I...” Diego pauses, unsure of how to answer. The words come out on their own. “I want to beat Jason Snow. See, I've been doing this wrestling thing and....” “Yes, I know all about what you've been doing, don't worry. Well if that's what you want, then there's no time to waste. Let's get started,” his father said, already walking to the door. “Get moving, if you're serious. And you better be serious, because this isn't going to be easy.” As his father left the apartment, Diego found himself following sheepishly, without question, without complaint, his will surrendered to something greater than himself. * * * The mythico-archetypical implications of it all are not lost on Diego. Father returning after a decades-long absence. He'd read the Odyssey in high school. He wonders if Telemachus felt this way upon the return of Odysseus.[9] No, that was completely different, he decides. For one, Penelope wasn't an alcoholic, or if she was Homer didn't feel it was anything worth dragging her name in the mud over. And Odysseus, though he did run astray of the gods now and then, caught up in the convoluted clauses and fine print of Olympian divine law, the reader can hardly fault him for his transgressions. Diego's father, on the other hand....Diego can only imagine to what extremes his life has taken him. They were sitting together now, talking strategy. Diego notices that even from a few feet away he could smell the odor of cigars, though he couldn't remember ever seeing the old man smoke one. It was a built-in stink, a deep down stink, emanating from the very core of the man. “The easiest thing to do would be to break his neck, make it look like you fucked-up piledriver on accident, a rookie mistake. But I don't think you have the balls for that kind of thing yet,” the old man says, pausing for thought. “No you're going to have to grind this one out. It's going to have to be a dirty, nasty match, no place for any of those pretty moves you've been practicing. You're not going to out-wrestle him, but make it a street fight and you might have a chance.” Diego nods his head, though as the weeks have progressed he's begun to have doubts.
