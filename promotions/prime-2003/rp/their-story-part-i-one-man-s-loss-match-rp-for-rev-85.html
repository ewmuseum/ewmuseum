Date: 1//2/05 There are old sayings that go something along the lines of… every exit is an entrance somewhere else… and… one man’s loss is another’s gain. They are old sayings. Baby Girls ~Tuesday December 27th , 2005.~ The Headlines could have read just about anything. An Inhuman Beginning , Hoyt’s Reign Haulted , The End of an Era , Faith is Dead… but whatever they read, he didn’t see them, and the only thing dead in his life recently, was himself. It had been a White a Christmas, but a miserable and rather lonely one. The ringing in the ears was back… had been since the match with Hoyt. Coupled with that was the newly acquired moments where the ringing stopped, but so did everything else. The other things hadn’t bothered him so much, but going deaf temporarily on random occasions had even himself concerned. On top of the health isses, he hadn’t seen Mary since he’d come home from Scotty’s a couple days before The Great American Nightmare and found the house void of the comforting presence he found in her. What he had once referred to as ‘home’ was now more accurately a barren wasteland. The pictures of her smile, framed in scattered places throughout the house, were only reminders of the life that had once occupied the home. Her lingering scent, held in the threads of what few articles of clothing she hadn’t taken with her, did little to dissuade his growing disdain for the roof he lived under. It was that guttural feeling which burned his insides that had led him to find any number of reasons to stay out of the house. He had chosen the “bare necessities” approach to grocery shopping, buying only what he absolutely needed for that particular day… simply so he had an excuse to leave at least once a day. Despite his general lack of excitement for all things Hollywood, Matt had seen three movies since Mary had moved in with her parents. King Kong , Jarhead , and… something else… he couldn’t even remember. Those three films were exactly three times as many as he had seen in the theaters the entire rest of the year. And on this particular day, he’d gone to lunch with Seph. It had actually been his old friends idea, but it was Matt who recommended ditching the plan of grabbing fast food, in favor of traveling out of town to a nice little Italian restaurant. Anything to keep him out of the house that much longer. He’d even ordered dessert out of sheer desire to prolong the lunch an additional fifteen or twenty minutes. Every single second he could find a way to stay out of the suffocating confines of home was a second he considered well spent. Lunch had proved a far more intriguing encounter than Matt would have ever fathomed possible. The congratulations Seph offered were at the least a bit unexpected. After the verbal warfared the two gentlemen had exchanged just a few weeks earlier, praise and pats on the back weren’t something he had anticipated. Instead, he had assumed Seph would start things off with simple salutation, then let his small talk morph into a moral lecture on the heartahces and tribulations he was causing Mary. Still, despite the unexpected nature of Seph’s commending words, it wasn’t what had caused Matt to choke on a bite of Chicken Parmesian halfway through the main course. That had been reserved for the news of Killean’s new plight. As it turned out, Seph had stumbled across the knowledge… though in all his ramblings, he never explained how… that Killean, it seemed, was the father of a beautiful young baby girl. After regaining his ability to breathe, he had commented on the irony of his baby girl being taken away from him with her mother, while Killean was finding daughters on his doorstep thanks to a mother who apparently wasn’t up for the task. Then Seph told him about the mother. Stacey. At no point had Matt even heard of any… encounter between Stacey and Killean. In fact, the last he had heard or seen of Stacey had been the look of shock and disappointment on her face when her attempted seductions one night in a hotel room had been interrupted by Mary. Apparently, Killean had seen and heard from Stacey more recently. She was an intriguing character. Matt had first met her through Seph, who had dated Stacey at one time. A little less than three years earlier, things between Seph and Stacey had fallen apart, much like they had between himself and Mary not long before. A few months after their break up, and the closing of the Underground, Matt… knowing well enough thanks to his education in psychology… checked himself into a mental health facility of his own free will. After a handful of weeks and some serious cleansing of the mind, he had checked right back out of the facility, but not before bumping into a familiar patient in the building. Stacey. After checking out, Matt had informed Seph of Stacey’s current troubled status, and the two decided to help her out of the hospital and offer her a place to stay and old friends to keep her company. Stacey was elated about a change of scenery and jumped at the opportunity presented to her. With things between Seph and Stacey officially over and left in the past, she found herself taking a strong liking to someone else. And one night, while staying in a hotel between R4W shows, she had made a move for Matt. It had started with a little sweet talking, but her flirtations evolved quickly and he soon found himself staring at a beautiful young lady who was biting her bottom lip gently as she unbuttoned her shirt in what seemed like slow motion. What exactly he would have done had flirtations escelated any further… he wasn’t quite sure. As it turned out, he didn’t get any real chance to react. In a twist of fate so convienent it had to have been handed down by the Hollywood gods, Mary showed up at the hotel room, ready to patch things up with Matthew and get their own relationship back on the right foot. Needless to say, Mary hadn’t been at all happy with the cleavage-exposed Stacey she found sitting on the edge of the bed. It had taken him quite awhile to successfully explain that one away. He was only lucky that Mary was far more understanding and forgiving then than he seemed to be now. * * * Her Story: Beef N’ Noodles ~Tuesday Dec 27th, 2005. 2:08P.M.~ “I can’t believe you didn’t eat hardly any of your lunch. I thought beef n’ noodles was your favorite dish.” “It is, Mom, but I haven’t been all that hungry lately.” Mary helped her mother clear the table, grabbing the glasses and some of the silverware why her mother gathered up the plates. “I know, but you’ve been saying that since you packed up your bags and came here a week and a half ago. You can’t just keep stirring your food around your plate, picking at it aimlessly, and just claim you don’t have much of an appetite.” “Mom, I’m not seven. I’m not playing with my food or picking at it. It’s just that… I haven’t really been hungry the past few days. And I’m not going to force myself to eat when my stomache says it doesn’t want to.” “It’s not your stomache that’s telling you that, darling. It’s your heart… playin tricks with you. How long are you going to let Matthew starve you?” “He’s not starving me.” “It’s the same thing.” Mary’s mother loaded the the plates into the dishwasher, first pausing to rinse off any remaining hunks of beef or stray noodles. “Same thing.” “No its not. And I’ll get my appetite back. Just need a few more days.” “Ya know, if being away from Matthew has you so distraught…” “Mom…” “…then I don’t even know why you came here in the first place.” “Do you want me to pack my bags and move back home?” Mary sat the glasses down, the one in her left hand landing forcefully and awkwardly, rolling on its rim before coming to a rest in the safe and appropriate position. “Absolutely not! I want my child right here where she wants to be.” “Thank you. Now, if you could just let me eat when I want to.” Mary headed out of the kitchen, choosing not to hear her mother’s response. She headed into the living room and crashed onto the couch, grabbing the remote from off the oak side table. As she pulled it towards her, the clicker fell out of her hand, landing with a disturbing sound, its corner scratching against the glass insert of the small table. “You ok?” Her father, already retired to the recliner after finishing his lunch, folded down the top half of his newspaper, looking over its ink to check on his daughter. “Yeah, remote just slipped out of my hands.” She held it up for no real reason, as if ‘remote’ was too ambiguous. “Sorry about the table, Papa.” “Don’t worry about the table.” He turned his gaze back towards the paper, flipping up its sagging top. “I never really liked it that much anyways. You’re mother picked it out.” “Should have figured. Seems like Mom’s style.” “Yeah, she made me drop a fortune on it. Could have probably got one for a fraction of the price if she’d been willing to hit a few garage sales in the county.” “Mom never did like second hand things.” “She was the fourth of four girls growing up. She never owned anything but a hand-me-down. So, as aggrivating, and expensive, as it can be… I try not to give her too hard of a time about it.” “Awww, Papa, always the sweetheart.” Mary laughed playfully. “Yes, ma’am.” “And that’s just your style.” “I try.” Sensing that his daughter was in the mood to for conversation, he folded the paper, then folded it in half again, dropping the nice square on the floor by his chair. “By the way, thanks for going light on the beef n’ noodles. Left more for me, and I sure do enjoy that stuff.” “Haha, you thank me for not really having much of an appetite, and mom lectures me about it.” “Well, you’re mother is very defensive about her cooking. She considers everything but cleaning your plate and begging for seconds to be an insult.” “She is a good cook.” “Well, then you should probably go tell her that. She might be less angry with you.” “You think?” Mary shot her father a quizzical look. “No, you hardly finished a third of your plate. You better devour your dinner if you want to get back in her good graces.” “Hahaha, so true.” For a moment, she paused, then her joking demeanor gave way to a more serious tone. “Thank you, Papa.” “For what?” “Making me laugh. You’ve always been able to make me laugh when I most need it.” “Have you heard from him yet?” It may have seemed like an odd response, but he knew when she needed to laugh, it meant something was weighing heavy on her mind. It was a trait she shared with her husband. “No, Matthew still hasn’t called, emailed… nothing.” “Well, he better not show up around here. I’ll go get my old rifle from off the gunrack and shoot him right in the ass.” “Papa!” “Well… I don’t like it when anyone hurts my baby girl. You know that.” “I know. But I’d kinda like to see him show up here out of the blue. That’s been the hardest part.” Mary pulled her knees up to her chest as she curled up on the end of the couch. “I hate what’s happened… but it’s tearing me to pieces that he hasn’t made any effort to get ahold of me since I moved out.” It was entirely unlike Matthew not to try and patch things up immediately. When they had first dated while in college, he would always rush to fix any problem he found in their relationship. It didn’t matter who was at fault, he always apologized and did so as soon as he possibly could. When she had studied abroad, and their relationship had turned to that of ‘long distance’… his refusal to let problems fester continued strong. There had been a few instances of arguments on the phone that had led to her hanging up on him in disgust. Every time, without fail, within five minutes, he would call back ten times more subdued than he had been just moments earlier. He would apologize, and just like that… they’d both forget they’d ever been at each others throats. So his week and a half of silence puzzled and terrified her. She was sure he would have called the evening he got home and found her and Georgie gone. When that didn’t happen, she just knew she’d hear from him after The Great American Nightmare. She had even called and left a message on the answering machine that night, congratulating him on the victory. But the Pay Per View had come and gone and the days following past with no word from Matthew. By the fifth day, she found herself exhausted and drained of basically every last drop of positive she possessed. “I just want Matthew to make some sort of move to let me know he actually cares I’m gone.” “I’m sure he cares very much, Mary. I think Matthew is rather confused right now and he’s in a pretty difficult situation.” He saw the look his daughter cast his way. “Hey, I’m not defending him, not his actions. I’m just saying that the fact that he hasn’t called you yet doesn’t mean he doesn’t care. You know he cares.” “Then I wish he’d just show up. I’d sleep so much easier if he just dropped by, even for just a few minutes. Just so I could know… something.” “Well, then, he better get his butt over here real soon and cheer up my baby girl.” “Papa, he can’t get his butt over here to see me if you’re gonna shoot him when he shows up at the front door.” “He doesn’t know I’m going to shoot him.” “Papa…” “You know I only want what’s best for you, Mary. And if you want him to stop by here and talk to you, then I want him to stop by here and see you.” He stood from his recliner and walked over to the couch. “He’ll show up soon.” “Thank you, Papa.” “Welcome, young lady.” He leaned down and kissed her on the forehead. “Ok, I’m gonna head to the store to pick up a few things. Don’t drive your mother cr… don’t let your mother drive you crazy.” * * * His Story: Things to Gain ~Tuesday Dec 27th, 2005. 2:08P.M.~ Matt pushed open the front door and stepped into the relatively quiet house. The only sound he heard was the rapid fire clacking of fingers at a keyboard. He headed through the living room and into the small office Matthew and Mary had set up in one of the two spare bedrooms. Scotty was typing away, not even aware of the presence of his own flesh and blood. “Don’t you have your own computer?” “Yeah,” he didn’t even bother to look away from the screen, “but the fiancee is out of town on business and she took the laptop with her… and I need me my porn!” “Right.” Matt shook his head and turned out of the room, heading back into the living room to hand up his jacket. As he made his way back towards the office, he stopped in the kitchen at the refrigerator and pulled out a carton of orange juice. A quick shake and he popped the top open, then downed the remaining few ounces. Mary would kill him if she found him drinking from the carton. It was ‘just rude’ she would tell him when he’d jokingly say that maybe they shouldn’t kiss either if she was worried about catching something. “So, anyone call while I was gone?” “Yeah, PRIME called.” “They release the card for the next ReVolution?” Matt made his way back towards to the office, stopping in the doorway to lean against the frame. “Yup. You’re booked in the next to last match of the night. Putting your newly won Universal Championship on the line against Clyde Walkins.” “What?” “Clyde Walkins… that one dude from that one team… Clyder-Man, Clyde The Glide…” “That was a basketball player.” “Oh yeah, I know that. The sonofabitch cost me a grand one time. All he had to do was hit two lousy free throws. God damn…” He let his words trail off in bitter fashion. “Anyways, you know who I mean. Clyde… he’s a champ too, been winning a lot lately. A lot. Pretty impressive stuff actu…” “I know who he is, Scotty… the ‘what’ was in response to the fact that I have to defend the Universal Championship already. I just won the damn thing less than two weeks ago. And not the main event spot?” “Oh man! You should see the main event, it’s stacked, dude! Only like four of the biggest names in PRIME history all in one ring at the same time. It’s gonna be huge! Hoyt and Nelson and Nova and Ang…” “I’m like your least favorite wrestler, huh?” “Sorry, bro.” Scotty shrugged his shoulders. “But I mean… c’mon…you gotta admit, Deville is pretty badass.” “What’s a guy got to do to get a little bit of respect?” “Earn it. And that takes time, man. Champ or not… you’re one of the new guys. You can’t expect to be one of the names most associated with a great organization like PRIME after just four months. Some of these guys have been with PRIME for a helluva lot longer than that. Besides, what do you care about other people respecting you?” It was a good question.. From the start of his days in the industry of professional wrestling, Matt hadn’t paid much attention to the amounts of praise and respect that were, or weren’t, being thrown his way. His concern had always been turned inward, towards his own satisfaction. The man who’s respect meant the most to Matt, was his own. He had always demanded immense amounts of success from himself. Failure to achieve the lofty goals he set was considered wholey unacceptable and no amount of comfort or condolences could change the lack of respect he would hold for himself in the wake of his failures. To be more caught up with the thoughts and praise of others was bizarre for his nature. “I dunno… I just…” He didn’t know how to answer his brother exactly. “Damn, you’re in bad shape, bro.” “Why is that?” “Cuz you’re physically beat up, alone in this house… at least when I’m not bumming your internet… and all the success you’ve had in PRIME so far has done nothing but make you even more uneasy.” “Scotty, what the hell are you talking about?” “I mean, everything outside of PRIME is kinda fallin apart. You see that, and just wonder if and when it’s going to happen in the ring too. The quicker you gain great success, the quicker you run the risk of suffering great collapses. Think about it… how many guys in history do you think have had so much to lose in just their tenth match with a federation. You’ve got an undefeated streak on the line, a win makes PRIME history. You’ve got your newly won Universal Championship up for grabs.” “That’s deep, Scotty. Real deep.” “Hey, you know its true.” Scotty leaned back in the chair, lifting its front two legs off the ground. “And on top of all that, you’re not even satisfied with your career. You can’t be happy with what you’ve done. And you’re never gonna be able to retire from wrestling until you’re satisfied. For Mary’s sake… I hope you achieve whatever goals are still eating away at you… cuz if you don’t… you guys are never gonna get back on the same page.” Everything his brother was saying, despites its rather heavy nature, had a simple truth to it. Matt couldn’t walk away from something he didn’t feel he had completed. His career in wrestling was an unfinished project. Despite all his past successes, as well as his current ones, there were pieces that still needed to be secured in place. One of those missing pieces was obvious. “And one of those goals is obvious.” Scotty shook his head, as if agreeing, causing his brother to wonder if he’d thought out loud. “How many championships have you won in your career. Four? Five? You’ve been the Immortality Champion, the R4W Wold Champion, and of course…” Scotty pointed to the shelf above the computer where the Universal title belt rested. “But how many successful title defenses do you have to your credit?” “C’mon, Scotty… lay off. I don’t need a hard time.” “I’m not giving you a hard time. I’m just pointing out a fact. N4O closed before you and Seph ever had to defend your tag titles. The Underground closed before you ever got the chance to put it up for grabs. You retired after winning the R4W Heavyweight title… as far as you know, you’re just a repeat one hit wonder. That’s what’s killing you inside. You’ve just got to get that successful title defense in your hands. You’re finally in a federation that you know isn’t going to be collapsing and closing its doors anytime soon. So now, just take care of your end of things.” “Fair enough.” “Maybe if you take care of that, get over one hurdle, you can focus on fixing your screw up with Mary. Cuz you look pathetic without her, and you know that’s the truth.” “You’re right.” He closed his eyes as he muttered the words and her image filled his mind. Slowly, Matt let his head slump, the weight of their problems too much to hold. “You’re absolutely right.” “I know.” “So humble.” Matt opened his eyes at his brothers comical arrogance. He stepped forward and slapped Scotty on the shoulder as if in appreciation. “You know, Scotty, for an goof… you’re pretty damn insightful.” “Well, you know, I’ve always thought of myself as the ‘Intellectual Idiot’.” “Haha, if you ever get back in wrestling, you’ll have to use that as your nickname.” “No way. Some of us know how to stay retired. You and Terry Funk can go grab a cup of coffee and discuss all the great gimmicks to use for a comeback.” “Don’t think I have Terry’s phone number.” Matt turned to head out of the office, but stopped himself abruptly. “Speaking of which, anyone else call while I was gone.” “Yeah, the NBC executives called. They want you to do Leno.” “I’m not even attracted to him.” “It’s the chin, isn’t it?” “Naw, the voice.” “Oh man!” Scotty scrunched his face in disgust, “I know! Dude sounds like a mouse having its balls squished.” “Scotty, I’m not even gonna ask how you know what a mouse having its testicles squished sounds like.” “Last year, we had a temporary problem with mice at the house. So, we bought a few traps and set them up around the basement. Well, one of the damn rats decided that instead of eating the cheese…” Thankfully, a ring echoed throughout the house, haulting Scotty’s disturbing story. The ring was very much unlike the one Matt frequently had been hearing in his ears. This one sounded more like a doorbell. “Dude, I think someone’s at the door.” “No shit, Scotty. You’re on fire today.” Matt shook his head and laughed as he headed into the living room. He made his way over to the door, pulling it open just as the bell rang again. One open door killed the stupid humor that had filled the house just moments before. One ringing doorbell welcomed in a new world of headaches. “Hi, Matt.” She smiled.
