Dual Halo, Indeed! (CS RP)

12 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/12/05 “Touch what I never touched befo', seen what I never seen befo',Woke up and seen the sun sky high, sky high.” - Goodie Mob, “Black Ice,” Still Standing Lisieux pulls Rolo towards the edge of the upper level in Halo 1. He motions for a German suplex, but Rolo counters, spinning around the Global Champion, and Lisieux is allowed a brief moment of panic before… “Good God, NO!!” …being unceremoniously dumped off of the upper rail and landing on the mat below. “OHHH! HOLY SHIT! HOLY SHIT! HOLY SHIT!” “Ignatius Lisieux was just german-suplexed from the top of the Halo to the ring! He's got to be paralyzed!” Nova turns off the TV. Dual Halo. Dual Halo. Dual Halo. What is it? Okay, physically I know what it is…I watched the matched. I studied the diagram. Two entrances. Two rings. Two ladders. Four platforms. One tunnel… …a monument for the macabre. But we have to get beyond the steel and mesh for a moment. The Dual Halo is PRIME’s most important match. But why? It’s not because of the halos themselves, or the high risk of injury, or even that the entire roster is involved in the match. Those are all physical things. I guarantee you no one in PRIME is thinking about the possibility of falling from the upper tier into the ring. It would suck ass, no doubt, but shit like that is what pro wrestlers deal with inside their heads every day. No, the Dual Halo means something…something different to each of the competitors involved… …something intangible. And since you’re here, I assume you’ve chosen to find out what it means to me. I’m flattered. Well, I’ll tell you…right after I take a dump. So cue music for a sec… “I am vindicated, I am selfish, I am wrong, I am right, I swear I'm right, I swear I knew…” Different music. I can’t take a dump to that. “Darkness…imprisoning me! All that I see! Absolute horror! I cannot live! I cannot die! Trapped in myself! Body my holding cell!” Much better. “Landmine has taken my sight! Taken my speech! Taken my hearing! Taken my arms!Taken my…” Whew. That was sinister. I almost douched my asshole just to make sure the demon was completely exorcised. But I digress. You wanna know the Dual Halo means to me? I don’t think about the two halos themselves, but rather, what they symbolize… …and what they symbolize is the Dual Halo of opportunities at Culture Shock. The Halo match-up and the 5-Star Title defense…both are absolute turning points for me in my career in PRIME. With both of them, something is on the line. The Halo match is the battle for a reward…anything you want. It’s hard to wrap my mind around it…anything I want? I mean, this ain’t like deciding between a Big Mac or the McRib here, guys. This is some genie in a bottle-type shit. Ian English got 10% of the company…for a while, anyway. Who knows the ramifications of this stipulation? I mean, people have been talking…what if Vampir wins? We all go to the 8th circle and PRIME is plunged into darkness so thick and overwhelming that only the great-great-great-great-great grandson of Sebastian Starr, sent back in time, can pull it out of Nosferatu’s ridiculously powerful hands? Because…um…if that doesn’t happen, I’m copyrighting the idea for a movie starring Bruce Campbell. $$$$!!!1 Okay, gotta get back on track here. The Halo match is for the reward. The 5-Star Title is the battle for respect. People think I didn’t deserve it. I didn’t earn my shot. It was a fluke. Who cares? I didn’t earn the shot. But let me make a distinction for everyone…it was a fluke that I got the shot, but it wasn’t a fluke that I won. I know not everyone is scrambling to line up for a shot at the 5-Star Title. It isn’t the be-all, end-all of respect or power in PRIME, title matches are rarely booked, and sometimes contenders are lackluster. All these things were going through my head when Hoyt starting mouthing off last week. I’m not stupid, or extraordinarily gullible… Nova stares at the ceiling. “I swear to God, I don’t see it written up there…are you pointing me to the right place?” Big Poppa collapses on the floor, laughing. “What? Show me…where is it?” Well, that incident notwithstanding, I’m not a gullible guy. I’ve been around for a while, and I’ve seen the “let’s get the champ pissed off so he’ll challenge me to a match and I’ll get a free title shot” swerve in action… “Hey, hey, Barry,” Johnny Nitro says, poking the Xtreme Champ in the side with a finger, “Barry, I froze your underwear. You like frozen underwear? Do you? I hear your mom does.” “Nitro, I’m not giving you a title shot,” Barry replies, his head still buried in a book. “DAMMIT!” It wasn’t Hoyt’s shit-talking that prompted the challenge. If that was the case, I’d have to give a shot to every goober that’s ever fired off the same damn hair and gay jokes I’ve been listening to for years. No, like I said, I was thinking about the 5-Star Title. Hoyt’s an arrogant, self-righteous asshole with a God complex… …but he’s an arrogant, self-righteous asshole with a God complex who knows how to wrestle, is respected in the profession, and brings a lot of viewers to the table…and that’s what the 5-Star Title needs. It needs people like Hoyt Williams competing for it, to show that it IS something of value to be honored, with a history that will blow your mind. For a time, the 5-Star Title was more exciting than the Global Title. Now, I’m not declaring that I will return the title to its former glory and herald in a new Age of prosperity where I am rowed to the ring in a gondola amidst a sea of gold for every match…and fellated constantly. … I might be fantasizing about it, but I’m not declaring it. But just chew on this for a minute: There’s a reason it’s called the 5-Star Title. Five stars is the highest ranking anything can receive, and nobody doles out the distinguished rating frivolously, trust me. Nova stands up in front of a Pink Floyd The Wall poster and tosses his copy of Rolling Stone across the room in disgust. “London Calling gets five stars?! Get ready 1980, Rolling Stone is GIVIN’ em out!” Well, almost nobody. The point is, when the 5-Star Title was conceived, the idea was that it would be held to a special standard. As the holder, I’m being held to the same standard. All I want is to know, whenever I lose the title, that I was a memorable and worthy champion. Admittedly, Hoyt is above me on the food chain. That’s why beating him will be so fuckin’ sa-weeeeet…for me and for the 5-Star Title. The bottom line is this: No one will be able to point their finger at me and say I didn’t do everything in my fucking power to make the 5-Star Title everything I possibly could. Somewhere in the Pokonos… Headlights light up the snow-covered trees in the darkness. A jeep weaves its way along the path, finally breaking out into a clearing. It stops and the lights cut off. The driver’s side door opens and Nova steps out into the snow. Tyler Robinson climbs out of the passenger side door. Tyler was the play-by-play announcer for the original AW in 2002. He saw Nova come in with his thumb in his ass and a grin on his face, and watched him leave as the #1 overall draft pick for Triad Wrestling. He saw Nova come into his own in FBW, and was at Nova’s house the night before the PPV where Nova finally realized his dream of being AW World Champion. Nova climbs up onto the hood of the jeep and sets a six-pack of Coronas next to him. Tyler takes a seat on the front bumper as Nova pulls a joint from behind his ear and lights it. Lightning flashes in the distance. Nova hits the joint and extends it to Tyler, who dismissively waves it off. “You know I don’t smoke.” “I know,” Nova says with a grin before taking one of the beers out and popping the top off with a lighter. “Now I will have one of those,” Tyler says as Nova hands him a bottle. Nova exhales from his nose, his eyes following the smoke cloud up into the sky. “Thanks for coming out here.” “It’s been a while, huh?” Tyler replies as he motions for the lighter. Nova smiles. “Yeah, it has.” “If you don’t mind my asking,” Tyler begins, pausing to sip the bottle, “why the fuckin’ Pokonos?” “Meh, I dunno,” Nova says with a shrug, “its…peaceful out here. The last peaceful I’ll have before Culture Shock, plane ride aside. But I always have shitty luck with seats.” ”So, like, I don’t even know, ya know?” the woman says as the stewardess walks by, picking up garbage, “it’s really his fault, ya know? God, he’s such a jerk…I don’t know WHY I still go out with him…stealing money, and no job! NO JOB! Can you believe it? And his mother, GEEZ! His whole family can’t stand me, and, hehe, oh boy, the feeling is mutual, believe you me! Hahaha! So what about you, huh?” Next to her, Nova is fumbling furiously with the emergency exit hatch. “Fuck this!” “Shit, Nova, my back yard is peaceful,” Tyler says, “why are we REALLY in Pennsylvania?” Nova takes another drag off the spliff before continuing. “It’s where the last show took place, and I didn’t have anywhere else to go.” Tyler is silent for a second before he pulls out a cigarette. “You try calling her?” “Almost every fucking day,” Nova replies, pinching the joint between the tips of his fingers and sharply inhaling before flicking it into the woods. “She just doesn’t answer?” Tyler asks, offering Nova a smoke. Nova takes one and shrugs. “She answers every now and then, just to tell me not to call or that she can’t talk. But no, most of the time, she doesn’t answer.” They remain silent for a minute, smoke drifting above the trees, lightning still occasionally flashing off in another set of mountains. “So,” Tyler starts, breaking the silence, “not long before the big event, huh?” Nova stares at him for a moment before accepting the change of topic. “Not long at all.” “You nervous?” Tyler asks. Nova doesn’t answer, but instead points out at the mountains in the distance. “You see that lightning out there?” “Yup,” Tyler replies before trying to blow smoke rings. “All I can think about is whether or not lightning can strike twice in the same place,” Nova says, his eyes trained on the flashes. “That’s scientifically…,” Tyler begins, but Nova holds up a hand. “I meant metaphorically, you ass,” he explains. “Right,” Tyler corrects himself, “I guess you’d be referring to your 5-Star title win?” “I would, indeed,” Nova says, turning to look at his friend, “still reading up, I see…” “Of course,” Tyler replies, a proud and nerdy grin on his face. “I dunno, man…,” Nova says, tossing his filter into the snow, “I know no one thinks I have what it takes to win the Halo match.” “That’s not…,” Tyler starts. “Oh, gimme a break, Tyler,” Nova interrupts, “in AW I was hot shit, but this ain’t AW, is it?” “No, it’s not,” Tyler concedes. “No, it’s not,” Nova echoes, “when people think of PRIME, they think of Karina Wolfenden, Emilio Rage, Tony Rolo…not me.” “What’s the point?” Tyler asks. “The point is,” Nova replies, “what if they’re right? What if I’m destined to spend the rest of my time in PRIME in a comfortable upper-card spot right below the big guns?” “I don’t think that’s going to be the case, and I’m not the only one,” Tyler says. “Man,” Nova says, lighting another cigarette, “that’s my biggest fear, that…” “That you’ll never make it to the top?” Tyler finishes. “Jesus, Tyler, can I finish a sentence?” Nova asks. Tyler mouths “sorry” and motions for Nova to continue. “I’m not afraid of ladders, or chairs, or cages,” Nova explains, “I’m not afraid of roided-up goons, and I’m not afraid of the Dual Halo. The only thing in my profession that scares me is that one day I’ll meet someone I can’t beat. One day I’ll go into a match knowing I can’t win. Because I don’t know how I’ll handle…maybe just quit and disappear.” “Do you think you can win the Halo match?” Tyler asks, popping another beer open. Nova stares at the lightning as it flashes over the mountains. “No, I don’t. And I hate that feeling.” “Why don’t you think you can win?” Tyler inquires. “I don’t know,” Nova replies, “there’s just something…overwhelming about all of it. The…” “What’s so overwhelming about it?” Tyler interrupts. “Tyler, I swear to God, just stick that bottle in your mouth and keep it there for, like, three minutes,” Nova growls softly. Tyler grins at his own impetuousness and obliges his friend, taking a swig from the bottle. “I don’t know exactly what it is,” Nova continues, “it’s…I think it’s all of it. The match, the competition, the prize…all of it is just overwhelming. I mean, shit…who am I, Tyler? I’ve won a few matches and paraded around with some gold, but who am I here? I’m really starting to feel like a small fish in a big-ass pond.” “Well, I think you’re downplaying the significance of your own career,” Tyler interjects. “Well, thanks, but I don’t,” Nova responds, “I mean, damn…how do I stack up to Karina Wolfenden, or Boda, or Tony fuckin’ Rolo?” Tyler opens his mouth, but Nova cuts him off. “It’s a trick question, because I don’t. And I don’t know how to deal with it.” “Nova, I think you’re just worried because this is a new situation for you,” Tyler offers. “In every other big game situation you’ve been in, from the Universal Title match in Triad to the AW World Title, you’ve had at least strong support, and sometimes overwhelming support, and you don’t have that here.” Nova pops the top off another beer, and Tyler continues. “Yeah, no one’s hyping you for this match, and that’s probably wounded your ego a little bit, and I understand that,” Tyler says matter-of-factly, “but you’ve gotta get past that. You remember when Jake Keeton shat on you when you first joined AW at the bottom of the ranks?” Nova grins for the first time in a while. “Yeah.” “What did you do?” Tyler asks. “I ripped him a new one in my promo,” Nova says with a chuckle. “And what happened?” Tyler asks again, working toward his point. “People noticed me,” Nova admits. “Exactly,” Tyler says, “they noticed you. You think if you had gotten all weepy-eyed over a big name like Keeton making fun of you that you would have been successful after that?” Nova shakes his head, lighting another cigarette. “Hell no you wouldn’t have,” Tyler says, “you would’ve stayed at the bottom and Keeton would be right. So what’s the point, man? Seriously, what’s the point? Fuck ‘em, man. If you go into that match believing that even one person you’re competing against is unbeatable, then you’re gonna be staring up at the goddamn ceiling lights before you know it.” Nova and Tyler stare out at the lightning. “You know you’re smoking this next joint with me.” “Yeah, I know.” “It’s good to see you, man.” “Good to see you, too.” Nova’s hotel room, somewhere in Pennsylvania… Nova sits by the phone, staring at it intently. “Dammit, this is stupid.” He picks up the phone from the receiver, and pauses a moment before setting it back down. He stares at it again. “Okay, this is retarded.” He lifts the phone to his ear and dials a number. He lights a cigarette as it rings, and waits…and waits…and waits. “Hi, you’ve reached Ariel. I can’t come to the phone right now, but if you’ll leave your name and number, I’ll be sure to call you back as soon as I can. Thanks. Bye.” “At the tone, please leave a message. When you are done, hang up the phone.” BEEP! Nova pauses a second, unsure of what to say. “Ariel, it’s Chris…I know you’ve been avoiding my calls, and I just wanted…I dunno. I don’t know why I’m talking into this goddamn machine.” He pauses a moment, calming himself down. Pissed off voicemails aren’t going to help him, and he knows it. “I sent a package for Lulu. It’s got a teddy bear and some pictures of me in it…nothing too fancy. Please give it to her, Ariel. Please. I…I’m afraid she’s gonna forget who I am. And that would…if that happened, I’d…I’d wanna die. Please give her the bear, and show her my pictures. I need to know that she still remembers me. I need to.” Nova is about to take the phone away from his ear when he hears the click of a phone being taken off the hook. His heart begins pounding. “Ariel? Ariel, is that you?” Silence…Nova can make out breathing on the other end. “Ariel? Say something, please. I wanna hear your voice.” Silence still… “Okay, don’t say anything,” Nova says, thinking to himself, you’re saying enough for the two of us, dumbass. Nova listens to her breathing for a moment, before the distinctive click tells him she’s hung up the phone. Nova runs a hand through his hair, and drops the phone onto the receiver before putting out his cigarette in a coffee cup. He lights another one and turns on the TV.
