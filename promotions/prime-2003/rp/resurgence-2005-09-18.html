Resurgence

18 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 18, 2005 There is a certain trend conjured up to attempt to describe the next big thing. It is said that if you do something great once it is just luck. Do it twice and it is a trend. Do it a third time and then it is evidence. Such a foolish axiom doesn’t apply to me… …that first victory in the first round of the JITC tournament is more than luck, a flute, or any blind excuse you can ever think of. I’ve been down this road before. I’ve traveled through the various businesses, worlds, and competitors, each time overcoming the hand. You don’t walk through the valleys of adversity in this business and not learn something. You do not walk through the swamps of sacrifice and not come out on top - especially not at this level…especially not in PRIME. So save your foolish attempts to explain what happened the last time I was in action. Save your envious lies to try and salvage something. There is nothing to explain. There is nothing to be in awe of. This isn’t my first time impressing the world, nor will it be my last. You would think being away from the business for more than a year would have hurt me. Hell, it could have hurt anyone. But it didn’t happen. That’s what separates the truly great ones from the also-rans, weaklings, and one night wonders. Everyone comes into the business being the next big thing. They get a good moment of victory, or some momentum and suddenly they seem legit. But then time marches on and they can’t stand the test of time. That’s where you start to separate the men from the boys. You separate those who deserve it from those who don’t… ...and take notice in who the great ones are. More than a year out of the business and I am picking up where I left off. What happened two weeks ago is merely just an example of things to come. There is no bigger stage than PRIME in this business and you better be ready to play when you come here. I’m ready to play. I’m ready to work. I’m ready to prove it. Chris Collins found out first hand what it is meant to go one on one with Juan Santos. No…not Syko Arsonist. That ship has since long sailed and gone. It was a part of my life - a very important part of my personality and success. Without it, I may not have had an identity for which to associate and differentiate myself. But it was nothing more than just an identity. I am much more than that. I am much more than another random worker. Last time I was in the ring, I was only there to attempt to get on the right foot. I did it. I beat Collins and advanced to the next round of the JITC – a tournament I intend on winning. There was no expectation there. There was no awe. This time, however, I know things are differently. People aren’t going to look at me and wonder. They are going to look at me and expect. They want to see if I can hang with their favorites, or if I am just another lucky bastard. But this week won’t be about proving them right. It’s going to be about evidence. # Juan Santos sat in the living room drinking from his favorite beer, Pilsener. The usually flamboyant and elegant interior design was now surpassed by a collage of scattered boxes and personal belongings. The inside of the house was dimly lit by the sun’s rays seeping in through cracks in the mahogany-colored curtains and gave the room a bit of an old Western look. The temperature inside was just as hot as the outside as the sweat on Juan’s head foretold. Overhead, a ceiling fan slowly rotated giving off a slight breeze, but nowhere near enough to accommodate. For being a man who had just gotten off to a good start in PRIME, Juan surely did not emit the momentum or design of a victorious man. It was not that he was indifferent towards the victory – or its important – but he knew, as a veteran, that there such things for not to be complacent about. Winning a match was a feat cherish by some, but for those with much more to gain and lose, it was viewed as nothing more than another day at work. For Juan Santos, it was merely the assurance that he could still wrestle despite his recent family troubles and back injuries. That’s why Juan Santos was not a strip club, his training gym, a press conference, or any place you could be at to cherish in a small, weak moment. He was here: at home. Juan knew that he needed to get things back on track. To reminiscence in the past is to invite disaster. Juan sat lazily on his leather couch flicking between two sports games. He constantly switched between the Eagles vs 49ers game and the Patriots vs Panthers game, watching the annihilation and surprise bought forth by both games. Perhaps they were a sign of things to come: everyone expects the greats to be great, the upstarts to surprise, and the broken to rebound. Juan looked at the phone from the corner of his eye. Any minute now, that phone was going to ring. # Three months ago. “What’s wrong?” Juan looked up from his crouched position towards his mother sitting on the other side of the truck’s load. They had just arrived an hour ago to the city of Guayaquil, Ecuador, and had resorted to hitching a ride to the city of Cuenca. There were no flights available for the night, or any buses making any trips to the city. Thusly, Juan and his mother had to travel through the dirt road to wait for someone to give them a ride. After about an hour’s worth of work, they finally managed to get a beat up old truck, a Suzuki, to stop and hitch them a ride to the next town. Juan sighed and looked at the beautiful mountainous scenery they were passing. “I never knew how beautiful Ecuador was. Are these the Andes?” “No,” Marissa replied, “these are the Rocas. You have to go a lot further south and to the east for the Andes.” “Oh...” ”You still haven’t told me what’s wrong?” Juan glanced quickly at the other passengers who hitched a ride on the truck. It was a very common thing in Ecuador---a country with cities separated only by wild jungles or treacherous mountain ranges. The other passengers either offered quizzical looks or looked away. It was a common, yet undesired, occurrence to see a foreigner in their country. “I just feel so weird,” Juan replied, looking down. “I mean I still can’t get over that this is happening. I didn’t expect you to show up in my life, and now this? What am I supposed to say to them?” “Nothing,” Marissa replied. “You’re my son. They know that and accept that. Whatever happened before was not your fault. You do not have to try to make amends for anything. All I want you to do is to meet the family that you should have met twenty-eight years ago.” Juan nodded and turned around to see the mountain range next to them. “Damn it…did we bring a camera?” # Juan stood at his front door watching the white Tacoma SUV pull up into his driveway. It belonged to his aunt, Jacqueline, from his mother’s side of the family. Jacqueline was a proud woman going through the finishing stages of her divorce. The courts had given her custody of the children, but after constant debts and harassment, she seemed less and less able to support them. Marissa, Juan’s mother, had offered her younger sister room and board at her house. Juan would have none of it. He immediately offered up his home in Long Island. It was both a generous and worthwhile offer. The house was big enough to support her and the children and anyone else she might need to shelter. In addition, it was relatively close to the city and her work. The commute was merely forty minutes. Jacqueline stepped out of the car carrying an overstuffed brown purse. She greeted Juan with a kiss on the cheek, and Juan saw that the children had not arrived with her. In the pack of the truck was a seemingly endless pile of luggage. “I still cannot believe you are doing this,” Jacqueline said, smiling. “Think nothing of it,” Juan replied, walking around the back to help unload. “We’re family now.” “That’s right,” Jacqueline replied, opening the back door. “So, where are the kids?” “They are with your mother. I wanted to move all our stuff here first. But they should be here later.” Juan slung one of the duffel bags over his shoulder and started to walk back towards the house. “Later? Who’s bringing them?” “You’re mother. She’s going to stay with us, too.” “Oh that’s great.” Juan continued to unload the heavier luggage from the car and set it inside his study den, just outside the living room. Jacqueline offered her help by carrying the smaller items and making way for Juan. It was a hot, humid afternoon, and the labor wasn’t making it any cooler. After a few minutes of traveling back and forth between the house and the car, Juan had finally unloaded the luggage and set it inside the house. In the kitchen area, Jacqueline was making juice for themselves and also made another gallon for the children whom would arrive later. She handed Juan a tall glass, which he promptly gulped down. She, too, gulped down her glass. “So tell me what it’s like?” Juan shrugged. “It’s okay, I guess. It does get overwhelming sometimes living here alone.” “I’m not talking about that,” Jacqueline said, laughing. “I mean the whole wrestling thing.” “Oh, that’s great. You know to just go out there and compete against the best. It’s not like football or basketball, where you compete on skill and talent. Here, you compete on your own survival and will. It’s just great. It’s overwhelming. It’s painful.” “Painful?” “Well yeah, and it’s real, too. At least that’s what the surgeries on my back tell me.” Their conversation was cut short by a series of loud honks. “Is that your mother?” Jacqueline asked. Juan looked out the window of the kitchen. “No. I don’t know who that is.” # Marissa leaned over and tapped Juan on the shoulder. “Wake up.” Juan slothfully sat straight up in the truck. “What’s the matter?” “We’re arriving into San Pedro.” “Do we have any family here?” “No, but we still have to get off here.” “Why?” Juan asked, turning around to get a good look at the town they were entering. “Because the driver says this is where he goes. Everyone’s going to get off once we get into the town square.” “All right,” Juan replied, accommodating his bag. “I think we should get something to eat.” “Yeah, that seems like a good idea. Hopefully this town has some transport so we don’t have to hitch hike again.” The town of San Pedro was a small. The streets were mostly all still dirt roads with the exception of the main street and all venues leading out of the city. The houses were all made of wood, none more than two floors high, and the citizens shared their town with various farm life. Juan looked at the town and the people dressed in the simplest of clothes. San Pedro was one of those towns still stuck in time. Everywhere he looked, he saw shades of a proud people who refused to move into western civilization. Most of the restaurants he saw were outdoor restaurants. But the town was not without its share of evolution. As they approached the town square, Juan saw signs of western architecture. Within a decade or so, San Pedro was going to be a city like Quito, or Guayaquil. Until then, it was just another small town. The truck stopped just outside of what appeared to be a bazaar. The driver gout out and told everyone that this was the last top. A few of the passengers pleaded, offering money, but the driver held firm. Juan and Marissa got down from the truck, taking their luggage with them. Juan looked ahead and spotted an indoor restaurant – perhaps the only one in the entire town. “El Asador…” Juan said, looking ahead. “Is that any good?” “It’s good. They got everything from fried food to sea food.” “That settles it.” The inside of the El Asador restaurant rivaled that of the most successful restaurants in the United States minus the reservations. The restaurant was half empty, but the mood was quickly enlightened with Ecuadorian music playing over the PA system. Juan found a table to sit at and was quickly attended to by a waitress wearing a white blouse and a red mini-skirt. She appeared to be youthful, perhaps not any older than twenty. Juan Santos and Marissa were awaiting their food when they heard a serious of loud arguments outside. “I hope everything is all right,” Marissa said, looking towards the exit. Juan shrugged and kept of fiddling with his silverware. “I’m sure it’s nothing. It’s just another drunken fight.” “Hey, I think that’s our food.” Juan Santos turned around to see their waitress carrying a tray. He looked at the tray and saw that it was indeed the dish that he and his mother offered. However, his sweet tooth was going to have to wait a while. Suddenly, without warning, there were gunshots heard outside and one of the victims was sent crashing into the restaurant from a window on the other side. People everywhere panicked, as did Marissa, and Juan turned to the exit to see a stampede of people trying to escape. # Juan walked outside with Jacqueline to see who their visitor was. Juan’s place wasn’t necessarily out of reach, so a visit wasn’t unusual. However it was unusual for him to receive uninvited guests. As they approached the black car, Juan couldn’t help but notice the feeling of familiarity he had with the car. Where had he seen it before? “Are you okay?” Jacqueline asked. “You look like you know him.” “Maybe. It just seems like I know that car from somewhere.” The visitor stepped out of the car. He was a tall man, the same height as Juan but a little bit more muscular. He was bald and wore faded blue jeans. Juan could not believe his eyes. He knew he had seen this car before and now he remembered. This was not a car, nor a person, he had seen in over three years. “You…” The visitor laughed. “I bet you weren’t expecting to see me.” Juan laughed. “You crazy son of a bitch!” Jacqueline looked at them quizzically. “Is everything all right?” “I’m sorry,” Juan said, “this is my best friend Alex Reynolds. Alex, this is my aunt, Jacqueline.” “Wow…so you do have a family.” “Yeah, go figure.” Jacqueline dismissed herself and walked back into the house. Alex looked around. To him, this house was just the same way he saw it last time. “So what are you doing nowadays?” Juan asked. Alex Reynolds was more than just a friend to him. He was also a business partner. In his previous employer, Alex Reynolds was Juan’s tag team partner and stable mate in a group known as the TITans. No, that’s not a spelling mistake. Alex Reynolds was just as successful, if not more, than Juan Santos was when he wrestled under the moniker Syko Arsonist. When political crisis caused Syko to find new employment, and later retirement, Alex Reynolds had remained in the business. Over time, he too began to find comfort in retirement. “Just sitting back at home living off my retirement fund and the funds from “Roommates Gone Wild” videos,” Alex said. “Sarah and Stephanie aren’t still making those are they?” Juan asked, wanting to know the whereabouts of his other longtime friends. “Oh no,” Alex replied, “Sarah gave that up when she had this weird idea of wanting a baby after Stephanie gave birth to um…that messiah.” “Oh, you mean the one she had after she claimed to be pure?” “That’s the one. Anyway, Sarah suddenly started getting these weird ideas of getting a job, making a family. I swear the girl’s gone insane!” “No more videos?” “Well, not of Sarah Reynolds and Stephanie Arsonist-Ali-Reynolds. Sarah’s been making new videos with a new girl, Chastity.” “Chastity?” “She showed up one day at my place. It was like a miracle. She said she was just going around getting signatures for her cause, or something, but I knew it was destiny.” “What was her cause?” Juan said, walking back to the house. “Something about passing a law for lesbian marriages, I don’t really know or care.” The two of them walked into the living room and Juan handed Alex a bottle of Pilsener which he downed like water. “Now I got a question for you, “Alex said. “What the hell are you doing wrestling again?” “I don’t know. I guess I got bored one day decided to send in an application and got accepted.” Alex shook his head. “I still can’t believe you went back to the business. I admit, sometimes I think of going back.” “It’s all right,” Juan replied, “I won my first match.” “I saw. Once a TITan, always a TITan. And now you have to face some gender confused vampire, a sexually frustrated high flying, multi-colored daredevil, and some guy I don’t remember.” “Um…” Juan said, fetching another two bottles of beer, “those are actually really good wrestlers.” “If you say so.” “Yeah, and now it’s back to the road for me.” “You know if you needed some money you could have asked me. Or you could do what Ali does: He goes back to our former employee for special guest appearance and collects the bonus.” “No…I actually wanted to wrestle.” Juan looked outside towards the sound of a car approaching his house. “Who is that?” Alex replied. Jacqueline walked in from the kitchen heading for the front door. “Your mother’s here.” # After four tiresome hours on the road, Juan and Marissa finally arrived in Cuenca. They hitched a ride on a taxi and went for about ten minutes through Ecuador’s third largest city. Juan rolled down his window, trying to take as many pictures of the city landscape he could. Moments later he noticed the taxi pull into a dead end street and at the far end so two houses, two floors high, side by side. “That’s where your family is,” Marissa said, holding back the urge to cry. They stepped out of the taxi with their luggage. Juan paid the fair, a mere two dollars. “Wow, this place looks great. How much is it?” “I don’t remember. You’re uncle is an architect, so we got a discount on this…considering he built it from scratch.” Juan looked at the gorgeous architecture of not only the house his family lived in, but of all the houses in the area. To his surprise, this wasn’t considered a rich part of the town. Houses like these were common in Ecuador. “It’s amazing,” Juan replied, setting the luggage just outside the front door. “Back in the states, a house like this costs a fortune.” Marissa nodded. She knocked on the door and in a moment or so a woman in her forties answered the door. She nearly broke down into tears of you, overly happy to see her sister. “What are you doing here?” she asked, in Spanish. Marissa stepped back and allowed for her sister to see Juan Santos. Her sister looked at her with eyes wide open. “This is my son, Juan.” She said. “Oh my God…” her sister replied, “come in! Come in!” Juan Santos politely introduced himself and walked into the house. There he saw all the cousins, aunts, and uncles that were his family. It took a moment for all of it to sink in, but Juan understood the meaning of it all. After twenty-eight years, he was finally meeting the family he should have always known. # Marissa arrived in a truck, with Jacqueline’s children and the rest of the luggage. Alex and Jacqueline offered to unload the luggage and the children while Juan spoke with his mother. “Are you going to be okay?” Marissa asked. Juan nodded. “I’ll be fine.” “Who’s that man? He’s not with Jacqueline is he?” Juan laughed. “No, he’s just an old friend. Trust me he wouldn’t be interested in her.” “Why?” “He’s married, mom.” The afternoon continued on with the relocation of Jacqueline’s family and the unloading of Juan’s belongings into his car. There was no more need for him to remain at this house. As a member of PRIME, he was going to be on the road quite frequently and would leave his house in very capable hands. The moment finally came to say his goodbyes. He said his goodbyes to his aunt, his nephews and nieces, and his mother. Marissa was still unsure about him returning to the wrestling business, but Juan assured her that he was more than capable of surviving. He told her of his victory and of his upcoming match. No matter how dire things seem, he said, it was always going to end up the right way. He was going to win. “Well, thanks for coming by.” Juan said, hugging Alex. “Yeah, I was in the neighborhood you know…” “I know.” “Remember when we first started out?” ”Yeah…” Juan said, thinking back on memory lane. “Remember how we had that skinny dork complaining about using his name?” “So we changed the spelling of TITANS to TITans,” Juan said, laughing.” “And remember the time Stephanie got pregnant and no one knew who the father was?” “I remember being forced to feed her!” “It’s not my fault you were the father of her child!” “She slept with the entire city! How the hell do you even know it was me?” The two of them remained quiet for a moment, and then broke out laughing again. “Those were some good times,” Alex said. “Especially the part where Sarah walked in on you and Stephanie…” “Yeah that was…hey wait a minute…” “I’m kidding, Alex.” Juan said. “But it has been some good memories. I want to thank you again for coming by.” Juan was about to get back into his car when Alex stopped him. “You remember how great it was being a TITan?” “Yes.” “Remember how much fun we had with all those girls, making the Roommates Gone Wild videos, teasing Stephanie, becoming champions.” “Yeah.” “Remember how much fun it was to invent TITism and get away from paying taxes?” “That was great. The Church was mad at us though.” “Well…I actually came by to tell you that once a TITan, always a TITan.” Alex said. Juan blinked. “The TITans? Again? Are you serious?” Alex nodded. “You’re back in the business. It’s only natural.” Juan pretended to think about the offer, but it was no use. “You’re fucking right I’m back in. Now I have a place to stay at when I’m on the road: the TITan Mansion. Is everyone going to be there?” Alex shook his head. “Well, who’s not?” “Stephanie.” “Why?” Juan asked, “What happened to her?” “She died.” I know I am just a newcomer to this business, but that does not make me just another case. I know that all of you, Nerezza, Karina, and Tchu are the best that this federation has to offer. But I didn’t get this far in my career, this good, by just laying down. I didn’t come to this fed to sit idly by hoping that someone might notice me someday. I came to PRIME because I knew this is where the best competition is, and that is what I wanted. I wanted to do battle with the Youngbloods, Williams, Wolfendens, and Eastons of the world. At Revolution in Missouri, I’m going to get that chance. I got that chance by proving once again that it’s not going to be easy to defeat me. This week in Missouri I’m going to give the world evidence of the greatness I have and can possess. This match isn’t going to be about just another tag team match. This match isn’t just about a group of people who won their first round matches. For me…it’s a match to solidify my place in PRIME. It’s a match about evidence…evidence that I am for real.
