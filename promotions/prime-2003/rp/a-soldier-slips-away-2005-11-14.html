A Soldier Slips Away

14 November 2005

Date: 14/11/05 A Soldier Slips Away Lonely Soldier, Go Home…Lonely…but never alone.Good eyes, see nothing to shoot…Good feet feel good getting off good boots. Night time was descending through the air when Paddy O’Shea sailed home into the land of his family. Paddy O’Shea was leaning over the guardrail of the HMS Stena now, the throb of the diesel engines under his boots. The sea was calm and was reflecting the great red orb of the sun as far as the wake of the boat, just as it touched the peak of the mountains in the East. The windows on the great carrier ship itself were now winking redly at the sun too as it, like Paddy, came to the end of its journey. Although unusually sunny for a November day, the temperature was dropping rapidly and already O’Shea could feel the chill in the air, nipping at his neck. Paddy felt the presence of a man beside him. It was Julio, a man that had proven an admirable companion in his few days travel with Paddy. “This is yours, eh?” he asked, a Spanish accent that would deceive the words he spoke. But they didn’t deceive Paddy. He had spent too much time with him now to not understand him. “Not here…jus’ o’er them mountains there,” Paddy replied, pointing a finger towards the hills the boat was sailing to. “It is good to be home, yes? Always, I like to go home” Paddy didn’t answer. Julio had told Paddy all about his home on the Atlantic coast of Spain. It is situated in a small village that consisted of a number of houses, cluttered right beside the harbour. There are two bars there and a littered garage, a few shops and apparently ‘all the time in the world’. Paddy had smiled when Julio said this and the more so when he had described his three children and his wife to him. Deep down though, he was jealous. For everything this man had, was everything Paddy had ever wanted. “So you say you come to see your grandparents?” Julio asked as he rubbed his bare arms of the cold emitting from the sea below. “Me grandfather. Aye’m here t’ see him. He’s sick ye see.” “Ah yes. Long time since you see him, no?” Julio muttered. He glanced over at O’Shea’s now troubled face and immediately regretted letting his curiosity creep into the conversation. Paddy himself, thought for a while. He remembered the wild bicycle ride down the wet road and the hate that consumed him at the time and then the worry as he sat in the train waiting for it to move. Worry that his grandfather would come running down after him, stopping him from leaving. He never did. He thought the bitterness that his grandpa had abandoned him in much the same way he did him had disappeared with time. But now, watching the water shimmer glorious colours of blue and purple, he realised the taste was still as strong and full in his mouth as it had been all those years ago. “It must be five years,” Paddy finally replied. “Tsk-tsk. That is too long my friend,” Julio commented. “Life is too short, no?” “Sometimes it’s too long,” Paddy murmured underneath his breath as he pushed himself away from the rail. Suddenly the horn blew overhead heralding the inevitable – the boat would pull into the harbour and within an hour he would be face to face with the man he thought he would never have the courage to face again. The thought of those accusing eyes suddenly made Paddy’s legs become fluid and he had to grab the rail again for support. “Almost home Senor. You will be happy to be back. You’ll see,” Julio chipped in then clasped a hand on Paddy’s shoulder, offering him a comforting smile that Paddy had to force himself to mirror. Paddy looked down at his hands. Since he could stand, these were the only things he could trust, the only things. But now, even that faith was waning. Two battles and two defeats for Paddy O’Shea. Sure he was worried about seeing his grandfather but his career was his life now. Never before had he been taught such a harsh lesson in battling than in his time in PRIME so far. In PCW and AWC among others, people fell at his hands. PTC events, where the biggest names in wrestling went head on, was a passion for him and even there, he had pummelled men twice his size. He was the little engine that could, the hardened terrier that never says die. And now, these hands were failing him. He could put it down to a blip or to a poor fitness regime but he knew better than anyone else, all those bumps and bruises were totalling up, and age was creeping in. Doubt. It was ripping him apart. An emotion he could associate with other fields in his life but not here, not doing what he was born to do. Paddy curled his fingers into fists, forming solid balls. They had been trained in ways no other fighter in the business had seen. Bare knuckle boxing. It had shaped his whole persona because when a gypsy fights, he fights for honour. Nobody listens to what a gypsy has to say so he is forced to make people hear his message in other ways. This was his way. He had brought men down in unique ways, convincing them he was an inferior opponent then shattering their jaw with a single hook. Now those hooks were becoming weaker, he would grow weary in matches quicker. Yes, doubt had set in. And once it did, there was only one way of getting rid of it. Beating a bigger opponent. Beating Juan Santos. He’d already proven himself, hell, he’d scored a pinfall over Danny Ferguson. It was all plain sailing as far as his career went, because no matter what else, he could always sit back and watch Ferguson succeed and say ‘I beat him, I have nothing to prove”. Paddy did. He had a contract but they could easily be tore up especially when you’re surplus to requirements. I mean, who the hell needs a deadwood 0-3 star? Furthermore, who wants a deadwood GYPSY 0-3 star? Aye can beat him You have to beat him. Aye can beat him Because if you don’t… Aye already said aye could beat him It’s the end of the road for Paddy O’Shea. “AYE SAID AYE CAN BEAT HIM!” Julio, who had been strolling along the length of deck ran over when he heard the shout to see his friend Paddy shaking like a leaf, his eyes narrowed, his teeth gritted in resilience; and as far as he could see, at nothing. Julio cautiously walked over to Paddy his arm outstretched, making sure he knew he was there. “Patrick…are you okay my friend?” Julio asked cautiously. Paddy didn’t turn around. Instead he closed his eyes and nodded his head. Julio now walked up beside Paddy and put an arm around him. “You know in my country, they say that only when you’ve lost everything can you appreciate what you had. Senor, be thankful…everything is not lost, whatever your trouble. And everything can be regained, that is for sure.” The engine was slowing now and soon the boat was simply gliding as it approached the harbour. Paddy looked around. Apparently, he would be the only one getting off here, as the decks were completely empty now in light of the strangling cold. Already, Paddy could see his breath thickening before his eyes. It was in that moment that Paddy asked himself why in the name of God he was coming home. He was feeling his muscles tightening and his stomach was doing somersaults, life had been stressful enough, why was he burdening himself further with this? Instinct. Instinct? What bloody instinct? He’d been walking down a path and he’d seen seen an old man and the smell of the hills came into his head; the thought of his true home circulating in his mind. It was like no other place in the world for that reason alone. He had tried to argue against it feverishly but all the time something was pushing his forwards. It was time to go home. It had been too long. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Ask your Father, how d’you fall.Ask your Father, how d’you fall.Like a leaf…sitting on the ground.Good for nothing except kicking around. The boat was turning into the dock as Paddy stood, his duffle bag that was housing a few loose contents tucked firmly under his arm. Sighing solemnly, he began making his way to the raised prow of the ship whilst noticing how the feeble lights on the dock were fighting against the afterglow. Julio joined him now and this was the part Paddy didn’t like…the goodbyes. With a heavy heart, Paddy turned to Julio and raised his hand to shake. “Ye were very kind t’ me…thank ye…aye hope we will meet again.” Julip smiled at Paddy, his teeth looking dazzlingly bright as it contrasted with his olive skin colour. He placed his hand into Paddy and shook it firmly. “Oh yes my friend. We are one in the same. We will see each other again, that I have no doubt. But in the meantime, enjoy your time with your family and be warm in their embrace.” Paddy smiled at Julio, always simple, always straight up – what you got was what you see with dear Julio. But now, this honest companion would part ways. Paddy hugged Julio briefly and walked away, not looking back – he was too afraid that comforting smile would tempt him away from what he had to do. He had to be a man more than ever. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Hate. Blind fucking hate. I’m standing, shaking, watching my father tear my brother apart. Bang. There goes two teeth. Bang. There goes a straight nose. And all the while, my father is screaming at me “move and I’ll kill you”. He’s not shouting or screaming, he’s saying it like he was warning me about bad weather. Bang. Split lip Bang. “Move and I’ll kill you” Bang. Then he falls. Mounting my brother, he swings left and right, the air almost whistling as his blood stained knuckles cut the air, digging further, widening the hole. “Move and I’ll kill you.” On my knees I fall, my legs too weak now to support my frame. Eoin is screaming now, only interrupted as a fist is lodged into his mouth. Let it be over. Please, I’ll do anything; ANYTHING, just let it be over. For a minute I think he is actually getting up. He actually hesitates as a thin trickle of blood begins to flow from the corner of Eoin’s mouth. Then he lifts his head up and I know it’s going to come down like a hammer. “Help.” I’m shaking violently, on the verge of throwing up. Fifteen years old, not knowing where to go with my life but all that’s gone now. That word is ringing in my head, over and over and over again. Help. Help me help you Eoin because I can’t do anything. Please don’t ask me again. Please. I don’t want to be lying beside you. I look into my brother’s eyes; only thin slits now as the bulbous lumps continue to swell further, restricting his vision to darkness. It’s a blessing anyway. It means he won’t see what I’m seeing now – really it’s me getting the worst battering watching you. Only now my words sound so cheap in my ears. So damn cheap. You’re a coward and a bastard. Just like your father who took his frustrations out on his sons. BANG The head comes down swiftly and when Danny O’Shea brings it up again, there’s a thick patch of blood, plastered to his forehead, a steady flow of the crimson liquid now dripping onto his son’s motionless face. My brother’s motionless face. Finally he pulls himself to his feet and walks towards me, mad but a precedent bitterness in his eyes. Without looking at me, he walks past. “Clean him up boy before yer ma gets home.” You’re a bastard Paddy O’Shea. Just like the rest of your goddamn family. That night, Paddy left for his Grandpa, too disgusted with himself to look at his brother, too hateful to look at his father. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ He died with his hand stretched out.He died with a hungry mouth.He died with a mind full of doubt.And thoughts of weakening. Paddy was looking down the lane at the house he grew up in when he paused suddenly, feeling repugnance about going any further. Why the hell had he come this far just to turn around and go away again? Paddy raised a hand and wiped a brow, feeling warm sweat moisten the back of his hand. Surprising of course for such a cold night. Paddy took a step. Then another. Suddenly, he was walking again, slowly mind you but he was moving. As he drew closer, he first noticed the sheen on the water of the lake, a small lake yes but tidy all the same; he could hear this year’s rushes rustling in the light breeze. It was peaking out from all sides of the house bar the bottom, like a halo, highlighting his path. There were a large number of bicycles and cars outside the old Irish house which was unusual because to Paddy’s knowledge, it wasn’t a special day. All the same, Paddy continued on, eventually weaving in between the cars to get to the doorway. There was a veranda here, overlooking a small porch, and beyond that was the two glass doors guarded by brass rails which would open at a push. All the time, memories were flowing back to Paddy, reminding him of why he’d enjoyed this place so much. Paddy pushed the door open and entered into the kitchen. The room was crowded, filled with many men and women he recognised from years ago, all modified by the decay of time. They hadn’t been talking loudly though, Paddy would have known before he entered and this was very surprising because he knew, more than anybody, these people weren’t shy. All eyes were on him now but Paddy refused to look back. Instead, he waded through the fug of tobacco smoke, across the kitchen, and through another door, feeling the silence burning his ears. Now he was in an even quieter room. It wasn’t as crowded but there were still an unusual number of people there, mostly old ladies with rosary beads, wrapped through their fingers. Now they too were looking at him in amazement. The Prodigal Son is back in town! He walked past them to the door on the left that led into a hall. He closed the door behind him, shutting off the sudden chatter that had erupted, and then he looked at the door on the immediate left. The bedroom of his grandparents. Hesitating once again, he opened the door and walked in. There wasn’t much light but he could see clear enough the figure lying on the bed in front of him. His grandfather was lying on his back, his mouth wide open, the breathing come hard and from his throat. There were two candles burning in brass candlesticks on a small table at the side of the bed, a large crucifix erected on the wall above the head it. On the other side of the bed, a woman was kneeling and praying. His abrupt entrance had startled her and now she was on her feet, her face suddenly full of recognition of who he was. They stared at each other. She spoke first. “Yer grandfather is dying.” “Aye know.” ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ He knew his grandfather was dying. He had seen a lot of dying men in his time. He noticed that his hair was still thick right down to his broad forehead. There was a few streaks of fading brown in its whiteness. The sunken cheeks emphasised the Asiatic-looking flat cheekbones and his big jaw. “He has cancer,” he heard her say. “He is in a coma. He won’t come out of it.” He wondered why he had come home at this time, the compulsion on him every time he had resisted. But what good was it now? He couldn’t talk to him after all; even if he could they’d probably fight. Yes, if they did talk it would most likely be ‘Why did ye try and make me leave me father?’ or ‘was aye jus’ leverage fer ye in some stupid, pointless fight?’ “He always said ye’d come back…before he died.” Her voice wasn’t full of enthusiasm. She was just passing on a message that she thought he should know. He could tell. Her voice was full of resentfulness. “And me grandma? Where is she?” “She died six years ago,” replied the girl regretfully. “Why didn’t aye come back before she died?” She didn’t answer. The two stood in silence another while longer then the girl, sensing she should leave, exited, and for the first time in years, Paddy was alone with his grandfather. He walked over to him and fell on his knees, not so much miserable but more frustrated. There was silence in the room so that the laboured breathing of the dying man was very audible. “So Grandpa…aye’m home then…” Silence. Paddy sighed for a moment. This was really stupid, talking to someone that wasn’t listening. Anyway, it wasn’t natural; there weren’t many pleasantries in his relationship with his Grandfather. There was love and respect and perhaps a civilised debate now and again. But there weren’t pleasantries. Paddy realised this may be his last chance get things off his chance though and pressed on regardless. “Ye told me t’ forget about me Da…said tha’ it was him or you…” Paddy mumbled, his hands trembling now though he hadn’t noticed it yet. “Ye said ye’d give me everything ye had, all the O’Shea land, all the property, if aye jus’ let go of him…Why did ye hate him? Huh? Why? He was yer son fer God’s sake, aye had t’ live with him, takin’ his blows, absorbin’ his insults, aye should’ve been angry, not you! Were ye afraid he’d sell the land? Did ye think he’d forget about bein’ an O’Shea?” Paddy looked once again at his Grandfather, the breathing all the time becoming weaker. “Look...ye can’t answer me so aye suppose aye should make this brief…aye loved ye, aye loved ye like ye were me father. But ye should have knew, ye should have KNEW, aye would never forsake him, no matter wha’ he did, jus’ like aye’d never forsake ye until ye pulled a stunt like tha’. Ye gave me no choice!…aye loved ye alright, ye were me everything…but ye crossed the line…aye guess ye never really knew me at all…” Paddy wiped a tear from his cheek. It’d been a while since he’d felt the sensation of warm tears staining his face. One tear…that was it. He patted his eye with the palm of his hand then got to his feet. Bending over his grandfather now, he kissed his brow, his skin felt decrepit to the touch. Backing away he headed for the door. He wouldn’t sit with him while he slipped through his fingers. His eyes wouldn’t open again to light on him. This wasn’t a fairytale. This was real life. This was facing the past a minute too soon. And of nothing else, it was time to start acting like a man again, that warrior in that bed was calling him to do so. Juan Santos. All for you Grandpa. So may your river, never dry,May your mouth, never lie.And may you be satisfied, to never know why,Sometimes, someone just wants to die. TO BE CONTINUED
