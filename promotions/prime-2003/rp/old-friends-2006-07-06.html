Old Friends

6 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/6/06 The door creaked open. Titan had been out on a date with Stephanie that night, and he'd had a nice time. The food had been good, an excellent t-bone steak cooked medium rare, but there had just been too little of it. Of course, he wasn't going to tell her that and ruin her evening worrying over whether he'd be okay. He had some stuff in his fridge, and that would suit well enough. He reached over to the light switch. Click. Nothing happened, exactly the way it was supposed to. I had unscrewed the bulbs just enough so that the connections weren't there. I wanted to see how he'd react. This house wasn't terribly large, but the interior was spacious, meaning that the big man hadn't spent a whole lot, but changed the living space to suit his needs. That's an acceptable choice. He's just standing there, though. He's waiting for his vision to adjust. Good, he's not lost his edge. I taught him better than to head into an environment where he can't assess the surroundings haphazardly. In fact, I can see his hand moving right now. I wonder, has he secreted away a little surprise? That hand reaches behind the hutch and there's a faint, but audible click. It comes back out with a fifty caliber Desert Eagle. He most certainly has not lost his touch. With a careful step, heel first followed by a roll to toe, he makes the first move in. His eyes track side to side steadily and he moves with a stealth belying his size. He brings his next foot up, then to the right, setting it down carefully and taking him around the couch positioned with its arm towards the door. He's going behind it instead of in front to avoid the coffee table there. There's nothing between the couch and the wall except for the bookshelf, a full two and a half feet to his right. The careful steps continue, firearm held upward and his body turned, empty hand fore. He stops, holds his breath; he's listening. Interesting. Is there something subtle here that I might have missed? Instead, he continues forward, reaches around the wall that reaches out to form the arched doorway and snaps on another light. Immediately, he takes a knee and turns, leveling that hand cannon into the room. “Going for the lights? Titan, you're getting soft.” That dry, passive voice hit the big man's ears like a hammer. He looked straight ahead, shocked. There, seated in that easy chair that Titan liked so well, sat none other than his old mentor, Profile 21. He was dressed in a pair of black slacks and a gray, button-up shirt. The long sleeves came to the backs of a pair of supple black leather gloves. Titan knew that underneath those was a pair of latex gloves. The man was almost a fanatic about not leaving prints. His dark hair was left somewhat long, long enough to comb back and seemed to have every strand in place. His dark eyes were unamused and unreflective, it seemed. Titan knew that looking at them was like staring at two pieces of coal. For someone so skilled, his codename always bothered Titan. A profile 21 in the IDF meant that someone was unfit for service for some reason or another. But he'd taken that name on in order to act as Titan's instructor for the MP training he received. “Then, I shouldn't be so hard on you,” the man's dry, unremarkable voice went on. “I didn't expect you to have a firearm secreted away where you did. Either I haven't been around you for too long or you had some tricks you never showed me.” “What are you doing here, 21?” Titan asked, getting to his feet. Unconsciously, he jerked the rolled-up sleeves of his navy blue button-up shirt straight on his forearms. “There not a Big and Tall around here?” the smaller man asked. “Answer the question,” replied the giant. “First of all, there's no Big and Tall around here and second, I'm here to help you. Pick whichever question you wanted the answer to.” The small man seemed on the verge of exploding out of the chair, sitting with his hands on the arms and his feet on the floor. Nothing obstructed movement, nothing to slow him down should he decide to move. “Help me?” the big man said skeptically. He reflexively reached behind himself and, not finding the holster for his weapon, he tucked it through his belt instead. “Old habits die hard, St. James,” said 21, “and you still remember how to carry a firearm, even if you don't have the holster. You ought to, though.” “It's something of a problem when I'm backstage,” Titan said dryly. “You're here... to help me?' “Yes, with an upcoming event of some import, as I understand.” Profile 21 deliberately betrayed his thoughts with a slight twist of distaste on his lips. “Yes,” Titan said with a sigh. He almost assumed a parade ground rest, but the thought struck him: this was his house. So, instead, he took a seat on the couch and purposefully put his feet on the coffee table. “I have to be honest, I haven't ever figured out why you went into wrestling. Obviously you're good at it. Your Krav Maga influences are apparent and your grappling training with the FBI are very clear. But I still don't know why you did it.” Titan sighed. He might need something to get through this. And some time to organize his thoughts. “Would you like something to drink, 21?” he asked. This time, 21 unconsciously raised an eyebrow. “I've heard of the famed hospitality of the southern United States. I wasn't aware that it was so quickly infectious.” “That's not an answer,” Titan countered evenly, setting his feet on the floor. “Point taken,” he said. “Fine, do you have any decent whiskey?” Titan nodded and pushed himself up off the couch of deep red leather. “Actually, if you're interested, I have bourbon.” “Really?” came the reply. “That's unexpected. I'll definitely have some, then.” Titan lumbered into his kitchen and pulled a pair of tumblers from the cabinet. One was large enough for his hands to hold comfortably, the other sized to the normal scale. He then pulled the bottle of bourbon from the top shelf. He poured his glass about half-full and then what he guessed would be two or three fingers in the smaller glass. What was he going to tell 21? With a sigh, he remembered who this was. May as well have a sign saying 'liar' on his shoulder. He walked back into the living room, where Profile 21 was standing in the middle of the room, with the overhead light on. Titan just shook his head and handed over the smaller glass. “You unscrewed the bulbs, didn't you?” he asked. The other man's even gaze was all the answer he needed. Titan sipped at his bourbon and took a moment to savor it. 21 simply sat down on the couch and touched the glass to his lips. “So, why did you leave the FBI?” the unremarkable man said, cutting straight to the quick. “Duty,” came the simple response. “Your duty was to the FBI.” “It was until I sent a pair of their former tag champions to Japan.” “That was the Matthews Incident, wasn't it?” That brought Titan's head up sharply. “How did you find out about that?” “Find out about it?” 21 said with amusement in his voice. “Titan, I watched it.” The shock was apparent in the giant's cold blue eyes. 21 could almost hear him running questions in his mind. “No, I wasn't a member of that ridiculous cult, I wasn't in the house. I watched the whole affair through a scope, Titan. You surely had notice of the cliffs off the east of the house, right? I was in those cliffs with a multi-level scope. I saw it all. You didn't kill the largest member of theirs, but it was a good move on your part to wound him.” Titan glared back at the other man and tossed back the rest of his drink. “Fine.” “So I know what happened,” 21 said, barely touching the glass to his lips again. “In fact, I even checked in on your charges in Japan. No, don't concern yourself. They're just fine. But, you say you joined up with this PRIME organization in order to take their place. By yourself? I know you're strong, but to replace two men?” The implication was clear. “I very nearly did. I came very close to winning their tag titles by myself.” This time 21 showed something of a surprise, a slight flicker in his eyes, but it was there if you were looking for it. “But that's in the past.” “I know it is,” 21 said. “In fact, you'll be interested to know why I'm here.” “Stanislav.” That one word said a great deal. “Stanislav,” the smaller man agreed. “It's not even so much that you need to worry that he's as strong as you are. It's because I've done work for the man.” Titan's face betrayed a great deal. He knew Profile 21 could tell and might even call him down for it, but the shock was massive, to say the least. “Now, let me explain. I haven't worked for him directly, but I worked for people he's associated with to further his goals. I'm going to have to tell you something you never knew. I was a contract hitman for years.” That line hung in the air, quietly. Titan looked at 21, his gaze even. “It's possible that you've guessed that. You're intelligent, deductive and I trained you to be even more so. In my time, I did work for a man named Alexei Ruslan, an associate of Stanislav's.” “More than an associate,” Titan replied, setting his glass aside. “Probably the closest thing that Ivan has to a friend. When he was assaulted, it sent the Russian into a murderous rage.” “No surprise. Stanislav has been encouraged to be a rather bestial, unrestrained creature by his government for a long time. Even now that the Soviet Union has collapsed, he still clings to the communist ideology like a security blanket. Ideologists are a frightening thing. But then, you're about the last person I need to tell that.” Titan didn't move. He knew that very, very well. The fact was that ideologists were more than dangerous. They were self-righteous, unwavering and had no regard for anyone aside from themselves. “Beyond that,” 21 said, just barely taking another sip, “there are some things that might help.” “Stanislav is old, but that isn't so much of an issue. The man keeps himself in such shape that it would be hard to exploit. But its his mindset.” “Exactly,” 21 nodded. “He relies on being stronger than everyone else. He can't do that with you.” Usually, when old friends get together, they catch up on friends, on family. They talk about people they knew, they worked with, that sort of thing. I don't have any friends. The closest I have is this giant that I trained, tempered by the hard sun of Israel and the burning duty to defend and protect. So when I caught up with him, he and I sat around and did what we used to do: we talked about weaknesses and how to exploit them.
