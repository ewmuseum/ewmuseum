The Dance

7 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/07/05 “It’s funny how things turn out. I always imagined my next showdown with Razor would be at the top of a card, surrounded by hype, fans packed in to watch the kid try and best the legend for the umpteenth time. I imagined gold on the line, the cherry on top of the bragging rights and immense pride that the victor would receive upon the demise of his enemy.” “That’s not how it worked out, though. We’re lined up in the mid-card, and most of the fans in attendance, truthfully, will be hurrying to buy their snacks before Youngblood, Deville, or Tony Rolo come out.” “It’s oddly appropriate, though. No one here knows about our history, or in all honesty probably cares. That doesn’t bother me. If every fan in the arena decides to go grab a pretzel with cheese sauce and a medium Pepsi while Razor and I tangle, I won’t blink an eye…because as soon as Razor meets me in the ring, everything else will disappear.” “The fight is our own. The beef is our own. The history is our own. Come ReVolution, Razor and I will go to war for ourselves and no one else. It’s an odd climax, really…the climax to my struggle, more than anything else…my struggle to prove that I could emerge victorious over the one person I’ve tried and tried and tried to defeat, and failed to, time and time again.” “It was late spring of 2001 when we met in the ring for the first time. It was during our stint in Championship Wrestling Federation. Razor was the star, of course, and I was just along for the ride, trying my best to learn from a seasoned veteran who, for whatever reason, saw something in me worth cultivating. I was ecstatic…and blind in my confidence that I could put him away and instantly make a name for myself, emerging into the pantheon of the precious few who could claim victories over Razor F’n Blade.” “I took it to him hard, and caught him by surprise, and before I really even knew what was going on, I had the hardcore legend splayed out over a table. I had to end the match with a bang. I wanted my victory to resonate in the fans’ minds forever…the day a greenhorn called Supernova put RFB through a table and added what then would have been the very first loss Razor had ever received in his distinguished career.” “As you can probably guess, it didn’t work out like I hoped. I went through the table all right…there was just no Razor Blade on it by the time I made impact. One rollover and three count later, and I was just another statistic on his impressive win-loss record.” “Fast forward to the end of 2003…Razor resurfaced in Empire Wrestling to make ‘one last run,’ which we all know in the world of professional wrestling is complete horseshit. I was running hot by then, seriously on top of my game and turning heads after World Title victories and a few match-ups that are still being talked about today in some dark corner of the world. I knew then that it was time to assert myself over the back-stabbing Blade and make him understand once and for all that, for all of his World Title reigns and impressive victories, I had surpassed him…to shamelessly quote from Star Wars, the student, in my eyes, had very much become the teacher.” “Again, it wasn’t to be, as Razor managed to humiliate me first in a tag match, where after his partner had been eliminated, RFB managed to single-handedly defeat myself and my tag partner…quite soundly, might I add. A week later, we met in singles competition and I again had to choke down another loss to his scarred mother-fuckin’ antique ass. I was blind with rage. How? How had he done it? It was the perfect time, the perfect place for me to forcibly pry the torch from his pudgy little fingers and run with that shit, but I had failed somehow.” “But I dealt with the humiliation, and like a viewing of a Hoyt Williams promo, I choked it down and did my best not to vomit. Empire closed, much to no one’s surprise, but I still had my career in NEW that was starting to really take off…yes sir, I could breathe easy and enjoy the strange kind of freedom that only comes when you’re completely on your own…” “…oh, wait. Did I say on my own? What I meant was ‘When EW closed, Razor followed in my footsteps to NEW and resumed being a total pain in the dick.’ Yep, RFB~! decided he just couldn’t get enough of my bottomless charm and subtle-yet-side-splittingly hysterical wit, and upon his arrival, we began all over again, first in a tag match where my partner, Craig Clayborn (you remember him, right? No? No one? He was here for at least five or six days) got splattered in the ring by Synn while I brought a steel chair upside Razor’s dome. Yep, another loss to the hArDcOrE lEgEnD, but at least I felt good this time…but not as good as I was about to feel.” “A win? A win, you ask? Did I finally do it?” … “No, no I didn’t. That would be TOO sweet. I did, however, hand Dr. Fat Fuck, pHD his first ever draw. And that, friends, was the last time Razor and I saw each other in a ring. It’s not the most epic story I’m sure any of you have ever heard, but that’s fine, because it means something to me. It means something to me that I have another chance. Razor, I’m not gonna tell you that you’re getting your ass kicked on ReV 57. I’ve said that before, and I’ve been wrong. But I will tell you this…when it comes to PRIME, you’re out-classed, soldier. You’re a throw-back to an era where the competition just wasn’t as fierce as it is today.” “People talk all the time about the good old days, about how wrestling was a different sport, a sport filled with legends and competitors who were more than men but less than gods…and every time I hear it, I smile. I smile because I don’t believe it’s true at all. The sport today is more competitive and more entertaining than it ever was before, and that’s the sad, unfortunate realization waiting for you at the end of the tunnel, Razor. Your day in the sun is over, man. Look at your debut…the mighty Razor Blade takes the big ‘L’…something that used to be completely unheard of. Now it actually seems normal. You can’t hang…your fish is about the same size, but the pond is a lot bigger now than it used to be…and in the long run, you’re gonna drown.” “And you know what? Maybe I will, too. But I’m willing to bet that I can tread water longer than you can.” “So let’s go, Razor. Let’s go out there and dance for an audience of two and pretend that no one is even watching. Because it’s not about that, is it? Maybe it never was.” … “You hear me, fat boy? Let’s dance!”
