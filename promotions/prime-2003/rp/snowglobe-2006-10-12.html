Snowglobe

12 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/10/06 “Get that shite oot yer nose!” With the back of his hand Adam takes a swipe at his wife, deftly knocking the rolled up bill from between her fingers – narrowly, but deliberately, avoiding her face. “What? And you’re the perfect role-model of a father? You were drinking a bottle of Jack a day; a bottle a day! “Aye; an’ did ah still bring home the bacon? Lookit the fuckin’ cupboards, woman – there’s nothin’ to eat in the whole hoose! Ah’m gonna hafta take the boys oot again t’get somethin’ in their bellies!” Katy sniffs and wipes a hand across under her nose, dislodging a few white grains which slowly float down to the floor. She’s buzzing, talking rapidly without so much as a pause for breath, but she lowers her voice after a quick glance at the living-room door. “Keep your voice down, I don’t want my kids hearing you shouting and swearing in this house.” “Our kids, Katy.” She smiles sweetly, wiping her hand under her nose again quickly, and strolls across to Adam, wrapping her arms round his neck and leaning in to rest her head on his shoulder. Adam snarls, his teeth gritted and fists clenched – he knows exactly what game she’s playing, and exactly what she’s about to say. It doesn’t make the words any easier to hear though. In her sweetest, most venomous whisper she breathes huskily into his ear: “That’s only what you think.” He closes his eyes, he digs his nails into his palms, he takes deep breaths, he counts to ten. But you can do that all you want and it still won’t block the thoughts that run though your head when poison like this is pouring in your ear. Adam shoves her off, sending her stumbling back to fall back on the sofa. She grins and flicks some hair from her eyes as she stands up and tilts her head toward her ex-husband, offering her open cheek to him. “C’mon – slug me, you know you want to. Think of it as target practice for this week’s work. You’ll do it sooner or later, might as well just start now, you shitbag.” He starts to shove her away, but she stands firm and starts talking faster and faster, trying to push him to his very limit. “Yeah; add another bitch to your tally – the only difference here is that you won’t be stamping your foot when your fist cracks my cheek. How many black eyes have you ever seen Troy or Karina with? They roll with the big boys and look pretty after every match. But – and here begs the question – after tonight… how many black eyes will I have?” She stands back a little, shaking herself down and standing defiantly before him; head held high and leant slightly back. When she speaks her voice is slow, deliberate and full of pith and spite. “Let rip – you spineless piece of shit.” With that Adam rears up, lifting Katy clean off the floor before he tosses her to the couch. As she’s about to sit up and continue her verbal assault on him, Adam neatly kicks out his left foot and shatters the glass table from underneath – sending bills, coke, credit cards and magazines tumbling to the floor with shards of glass. As Katy scrambles to try and register what’s happened Adam coolly steps across to her and grabs her wrists. “Ah’ve never seen a black eye oan her. Right here an’ now ah guaran-fuckin’-tee ah’ll never see one oan you.” As calmly as he can he mounts her, with her kicking, screaming and struggling every inch of the way, but his massive bodyweight and strength advantages show as he sits on her thighs and holds her arms off to either side of her body. Keeping his voice as level as possible, letting his accent fade away with his anger, he tries to reason with her. “You don’t mean what you just said, I know that, but it’s very, very fucking difficult not to let it hurt.” His voice wavers slightly, but he’s trying to be as diplomatic and composed as he can. A globule of spit hits his just under his right eye as he starts to try to speak again. “You kn-” He transfers one of her hands across her body and, after nearly losing grip of her right hand, takes hold of both her wrists in one of his hands. With his newly free hand he wipes the spit away then clamps his palm over her mouth, silencing her hysterical (by now near incomprehensible) roll of insults and screams. “I’m not gonna be goaded into hitting you so you can carry on like this under the protection of my guilt and regret. You’re just high, maybe not high enough – so you’re craving more – whatever, I don’t care.” He stifles a yelp as teeth sink into the flesh of his hand, but carries on even as blood is drawn. He grips her face with his hand and turns her head so she’s looking him eye-to-eye. “You know I love you, and I’m not gonna let some white powdery shite steal you away from me like I let the Jack do before. I’m gonna to help you clean up and get back to being the fucking excellent wife and mother you know you can be.” He stares at her, scarcely even blinking, breathing heavily as he tries to hold back the lump in his throat that’s arisen from seeing her in this state. After an endless silence her body starts convulsing, tears breaking the through levees to pour down her cheeks. He gingerly takes his hand out from her newly unclenched teeth, allowing her sobs to fill the otherwise still and quiet room. Cautiously, and ready to grab hold again at any moment, he releases her wrists – immediately she throws her arms round his neck again, burying her shoulder where it was not a minute before. Similar as it seems, things couldn’t be more different between the two situations. Seeing she means it, Adam relaxes his guard and holds her back, gently whispering into her ear. “Why’s Mum in Hospital? I thought she was taking her medicine to make her feel better.” “It was bad medicine, y’know, like that old song I played you?” There’s a silence from the backseat for a while, as Adam pulls out the grounds of the rehab clinic and turns onto the road leading back down into town. A different voice cries out form the back as Adam changes up through the gears. “How long are we gonna be at Auntie Sarah’s?” “Not long, your mum’ll be fine in a little while.” The first voice, the older of the two, pipes up again. “That’s not a real answer – how long?” “I don’t know, honestly. However long it takes for her to feel better.” The pout on the younger one’s face in almost visible in his tone of voice as he pleads; “Why can’t we stay with you?” “You know why – I’m on the road, you need someone to take you to school and make your dinner and put you to bed. I can’t do that if I’m Wyoming.” “But you’ll come back for us?” “Hrmm… it’s tempting not to.” The two voices in unison cry out from the backseat in protest to this, “Daaaad!” He chuckles slightly, but can’t keep a smile on his face for long – the chortle reminding him of his inability to laugh at his wife’s last joking comment as he checked her in, just before she kissed him goodbye; “Hey; think of it like this – at least after dealing with me, that Sun girl’ll be a doddle, right?” “Course I’ll be back for you. The hospital will make your mum all better, and I’ll give you guys a wave from TV when I win this week, okay?”
