Two Who Knew

4 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/4/09 "Learn everything you can, anytime you can, from anyone you can-there will always come a time when you will be grateful you did." -Sarah Caldwell August 18th, 2008 11:03 P.M. I knocked on the door with only a vague idea of what I might say. "Hold on a second," came the voice from inside Room 14F. I thought about turning around, but before I could make any sort of serious move to hightail it, the door swung open to reveal a posh suite. I still couldn’t see who I'd come for. "Hello?" Getting no response, but hearing a voice, I stepped inside. Not without a column of flame shooting up my spine; I was still operating at about 30% after Colossus. Even Tylox wasn't fending off the pain. As a result, I was puttering around, carrying what seemed like an extra twenty years. Stepping out of the bathroom, phone in hand, my host finally sized me up, a half-impressed grin on his face. "Sorry, was on the phone. Welcome to the digs." He wore the look of someone completely unsurprised to see me. "Were you expecting me or something?" "Yeah....or something. Everybody in your position wants some elder wisdom." "I just wanted to talk to you about-" "What it's like now that you can call yourself, with very little argument, the motherfuckin' cat's pajamas of the wrasslin' world?" "Yeah," I sputtered, nervously cracking my knuckles. "Horrible habit," he quipped, moving towards the mini-bar. "Give ya arthritis up the ass. You wanna drink? I've got your favorite, Zima." He noticed my quizzical look and shrugged his shoulders. "Little joke there, broheim. You gonna make me drink by myself like a creepy alcoholic?" "Gin and tonic?" "G and T comin' fresh off the presses," he replied, staying over by the well-stocked mini-bar as he mixed our drinks. It was going to be a pain in the ass to have this conversation if he wasn't going to sit down. /~/~/~/~/ February 2nd, 2009 8:17 P.M. I knocked on the door sure of what questions to ask. "Hold on a second," came the voice from just inside. It was a well-to-do, quintessentially American home, down to the gardens straddling its perimeter. Not as palatial as I might've expected, but thinking about the man who'd bought it, the home fit perfectly. The door swung open as I was admiring some particularly elegeant bluebells. "Hewwo?" A small voice, unmistakably from a small person, crept out from behind the door before the more dominant, familiar voice got things moving. "Honey, can you take her upstairs?" I heard no response, only the mish-mash of sounds that come from a parent carrying a child up the stairs: giggles from the child, lovingly frustrated scolding from the parent, and resonating thuds from footsteps on the stairs. My host opened the door wider. I barely recognized him in the trappings of his civilian alter ego. "Can I help yo...ah, you made it, Tsonda." "That I did. Wasn't easy to get myself here. West Coast tour and all." "You say that like I was the one who asked for this." The full smile on his face was something I could barely understand, the true sign of a man unburdened by petty (or are they?) grievances of Universal Titles and non-stop touring. It was the sign of a man who wasn't tired. "Yeah, well, there are some answers that I'm pretty sure no one else has." "So be it," he nodded. "Come inside. You want something to drink?" "Gin and tonic?" "I'm not a gin guy, but maybe my better half has something saved for company. Step inside, I'll check." /~/~/~/~/ "So let's have at it," he said, handing my drink to me. "Siddown, let papa bear ease your mind." I complied, but sitting didn't do anything for my wobbly nerves. "What was the first thing you did when you won the strap?" I figured our one piece of common ground was a good place to start. "Well," he thought out loud, "I probably bought myself an assload of Christmas presents, and got too drunk to remember where my face was. The hell did you do? OOH OOH tell me you've got a kickass entrance for ReV. Mannequins of yourself? An arena-wide singalong? The original cast of Hairspray?" "To be honest, I hurt so fuckin' much that I'm thinking about just skipping ReV." "Sphinctersaywhat?" "Yeah, my back's fucked to the moon." "No, you're supposed to say what and then I..." He paused, chuckling to himself. "...figure out that you're trying to do the whole serious conversation thing, huh?" "If it's not too much trouble." He leaned back on the bed, careful not to spill his drink, nodding silently. "You just spend so much time trying to get the goddamn thing, and now that I've got it...I dunno what to do." "Well, they're gonna come gunnin' for ya. They always fuckin' do," my host assured me. The distant look in his eyes told me that he was summoning up those old memories. /~/~/~/~/ "So," he started, handing me my drink and taking the other seat on the patio, "what did you want to talk about?" "What..." Forcing out the words was harder than I imagined, so I stalled with a stiff sip of gin and tonic. "...do you do when it's all over?" "The career?" His tone was entirely carefree. "Holding the Uni." "I'm afraid I don't get it, Tsonda." "Rayne finally got his shot at me. Well..." Explaining everything would take too long. Even if he'd kept up with PRIME since his departure, there was something more personal to it. Did I resent Ty's tactics? Sure. But I wasn't going to tell Mr. PRIME Everything that he didn't deserve a mano y mano shot at the belt. I'd been Cantrell's hand-picked champion, and he was always the redheaded stepchild. It was only fair. "Anyway, he's got his shot. Me and him. This Wednesday. What happens to me if I don't walk out with the belt? If there's anybody on the roster who can stop me, it's him." "Some of that hairspray get in your brain? You don't have the same swagger." His voice was still calm, though. None of that pent-up frustration that he'd parlayed into so many wins. Stepping away from the ring, it seemed, had given him peace. "Whoa there, cowboy. There've been two presidents and a Summer Olympics since I won this belt. I don't have to craft much of an argument to be called the greatest Uni Champion of all-time, present company very much included. I'm just...being reasonable about what's what at the end of this fucked up rodeo." "And, if you're the champion of champions that you claim to be, what good am I?" But if hanging up the boots had made him a better man, it hadn't killed his spirit for the squared circle. Something inside his eyes danced as we went back and forth. "Two heads are better than one." "So why didn't you ask someone el-" "'Cuz I can still learn from the best. There's only three champions I've ever considered worth a goddamn. And the one without a Y chromosome...I think she's betting on the other horse." "And the other one?" "I talked to him about it. A long time ago. But he ran into some bad times in another fed. Fucked up, how they did him like that. Either way...he doesn't have the answers anymore." "Which leaves me?" "Which leaves you." We paused to drink. His neighborhood was quiet, the place a guy could really get some sleep. "What do you think happens if you win?" "They keep gunning for me. It's a fuckin' neverending cycle," I sighed. I didn't expect his sympathy; it's a damn privilege to walk out there with five polished pounds of gold. But heavy lies the crown and all that. "True enough," he chuckled. /~/~/~/~/ "And when they do, the great ones respond. They post up on the top of the fuckin' Agro Crag, punch Mike O'Malley in the balls, and say 'This is my goddamn rock, and it's gonna stay that way until one of you weiners pulls Excalibur out of a fucking stone. Short of that, you better have some secret weapon finisher that's illegal in ten states, cuz you're sure as fuckin' sunshine in July not knocking me off.'" It was inane and yet...well, mostly inane. But I understood the sentiment that a champion had to have: the ultra-swagger, the knowledge that, as far the official record goes, you're God's gift to wrestling. And no one can dispute it without three handslaps against the mat in their favor. "Thanks for the fiery rhetoric." "Don't ask me to stizep to the pulpit if you're not ready to be redeemed, old boy." "So let's say I make it past the Nightmare-" "Easy on the reins, Tsondle. Only two of your Colossus Champion comrades have ever made it past the Nightmare with that strap." "I'm walking you down a windy hypothetical road here, champ." I felt it was necessary to set up my context. You don't get the answers you want unless you're asking the right questions at the right time. People forget timing. "Then by all means, whisk me away." "Past the Nightmare. Past King of Kings and onto-" "Never been done, but okay, I'm trying to bear with you." "February 21st, 2009. That's the day I'm thinking of." "Been planning a romantic tropical getaway with the mister?" "Not exactly. Cock." /~/~/~/~/ "And if you lose?" "That's the part I can't figure out, old bean." I looked out the window, feeling the gin and tonic mix around my teeth. "I've thought about hanging 'em up. I've thought about taking a rematch. I've thought about buying out the rest of my contract. I s'pose you'll tell me about the good life that's waiting beyond the mat." "If you want to hear about it, I'll talk all night. But I have a feeling you came here for some other answer, and my wife wouldn’t like you very much.” "Is there more than one? Answer, I mean.” "Absolutely." He took a deep breath. "I fought friends." Danny Ferguson. "I fought enemies." Devin Shakur. "I fought wild cards who didn't know better than to come at me." Dani Fuhrer. "And when it came time, I fought whoever they threw at me." The Roulette. This is what happens when Universal Champions sit down and talk. They see parallels, and even when they're trying to set themselves apart from one another, they find similar roads. "And at the end of it all, it's gonna be about whether you need to climb the mountain again. For too many of us," he grinned, "the answer's yes. But maybe you're different." /~/~/~/~/ "Two twenty one, oh nine," he recited. He stood up and paced, admiring the room's view. "Okay, uncle." "That," I replied, finally sure of what I would say, "is the day that I break your record, Nov." The Risen Star gave me a wily eye, and the type of grin a father gives a son when he says he's going to beat him. A grin that says, "let's see if your great is as great as my great." "Good luck, amigo." /~/~/~/~/ "You never answered my questions.” "You didn't seem to need answers. You came here to talk. We talked." "He...I think he might be my best friend, Ward." "That's usually one of those things you're sure about." "Was Killer yours?" "Touche. Sometimes, brothers fight.” I stood up. “If you could live with the thought of losing," Tchu reminded me, his eyes drifting away from me and into a memory, "you wouldn't be where you are." "Thanks for the drink." "My pleasure." I walked to the patio door, but something nagged at me. "You know, if I beat Rayne, I'm got Nova’s Uni Record in the bag.” "Then I guess I'll wish you good luck out there on Wednesday."
