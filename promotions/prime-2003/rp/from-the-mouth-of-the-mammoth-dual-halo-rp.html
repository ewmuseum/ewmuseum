From the Mouth of the Mammoth (Dual Halo RP)

Date: Early March JONAH He stepped from the mouth of the fish and for the first time in three days, his lungs ripped the taste of fresh air from the sky. The sun hurt his eyes, but he didn’t care, casting his gaze towards the heavens anyway. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Tuesday, March 10th Stepping through the opening, he entered the dimly lit core. Dr. Ward leaned against the edge of his desk, aimlessly thumping a pen against his notepad. The repetition pulsed without fail, its sound like that of a healthy heart, pumping life. “Aren’t you gonna sit in the chair?” Kaiser eased himself into the overstuffed leather couch, getting lost in its folds, swallowed by its mass. “Maybe.” “Ya make me nervous just standin’ there like that.” “To be honest, Kaiser, I want to know that you’re serious about this before I kick back and settle in for a full session. I feel like I took you under my wing, trusted you, and you disregarded my words. Disrespected me.” “I know.” He looked around nervously, understanding that this singular space, with its heavy air and poor lighting, had saved him. “I screwed up. Sit down and let me tell you about it.” “Ok” The doctor was hesitant, but pushed himself away from his desk and took a seat in the chair across from the sofa. “What brought you back?” “Felt like I was finally gettin’ up on my feet. Then I went and sorta pissed it all away.” Kaiser could hear his voice echo, though he thought it might have just been his imagination. “I don’t wanna do that. I’ve got too much going on, too many problems to solve. If I was headed down the right track, I need to stay there.” “Where do you want to begin?” Kaiser thought briefly, more for appearance sake. “I miss my father.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Saturday, March 7th “I think the doctor wants to kick my ass.” “You’re joking, right?” “Serious as I’ve ever been.” “Hehe, well…” Ellie took her eyes off the road just long enough to cast a sly grin at Kaiser. “that’s not saying much from one of the biggest goofballs I’ve ever known.” “Yeah… yeah.” Kaiser’s large frame rumbled in the passenger’s seat as Ellie’s car battled the endless stretch of cracks and potholes in the turnpike’s asphalt. The road’s surface was awful, more accurately resembling a barren desert of dry earth that had split and shifted without the nourishment of water. It lacked only the occasional cactus, or maybe a scorpion crawling in and out of the crevices, otherwise perfectly mimicking a western wasteland. “Are they actually charging us to drive on this thing? It’s like a crumbled mess of dirt and blacktop.” “If they fixed the turnpike, the toll would triple to cover the expense. Suck it up and be glad it only cost you a handful of quarters.” “How far to your sister’s house?” “Probably another two hours. More like two and a half, if I actually do the speed limit.” “Ok. Think I’m gonna go ahead and try to sleep the rest of the way.” “Thanks, tough guy. I’ll just keep drivin’ and try not to nod off.” “Sounds good.” Kaiser winked, then leaned his head against the passenger’s side window. It was startling, almost amusing, how quickly the moment changed. The senses could be so striking, flooding the mind with a memory so thick in viscosity that it stuck to the sides of the brain. The simple act of hearing, perhaps a single sight or smell… it was like some sort of time-travel, transporting a person from one date or place to another. In an instance, Kaiser Vashaun felt his father, heard him, saw him, could smell him there in the car. The sunlight stung his eyes and showered his shoulders, baking his leather jacket. It was a combination of the things ambushing his senses that did it. The sound of the jacket, which had been his father’s, the smell of the Kaiser’s cologne, the same brand his old man had worn, and the light that filtered in through the window… the three were enough to move him. There, with his head shrugged in his shoulders, propped against the window of Ellie’s car, he would have sworn up and down that he was sitting right next to his father, tucked away in the back corner of the last pew in church on Sunday mornings. Every weekend, it was the same thing, the type of routine that was such a part of their lives, it seemed to become as necessary as food and shelter. The family would crawl out of bed early to head to church, and each time, Raymond Vashaun would take his seat in the corner of the room, in the very last row. There, at the edge of the pew, he would sit in his leather jacket, never bothering to take it off, even as sunlight would shatter through the narrow window to his left, and heat the flesh of his neck, sending the smell of his Stetson cologne floating down the row. And as the sermon would go along, Kaiser would always tug at his father’s sleeve and ask him where they were going for lunch when church was over. “I dunno,” Ellie turned and looked at Kaiser, “you wanna swing past the Wendy’s Drive-thru?” “What?” “Didn’t you just ask what we were gonna do for lunch?” “Oh… yeah, I guess. Wendy’s is good.” “You ok, babe?” “Just thinking about my dad.” “I can imagine.” Ellie took her right hand off the steering wheel and placed it on Kaiser’s thigh, squeezing just a bit with her thumb. “Dave says you two were really close.” “When I was a kid, in the summer, I’d go out and help him with the yard work. Just the stupid little stuff. Hold a shovel, clean up the trimmings from the shrubs. Every once in awhile he’d let me hammer at a nail if he was fixing the deck or building planters for the patio. Made Mom nervous as hell to see her seven year old swinging away. But whatever we were doing, I had to do it just like him. If he took his shirt off in the summer heat, so did I. When he stopped to get a drink of water, I was right behind him. And I had to have my water from the big green cups he always drank from. Geez… those cups were huge. I could hardly get my hands around ‘em. It was like sipping from a barrel, but god dammit, that’s what he drank from, so it’s what I drank from.” “Where is all this comin’ from?” “Just kinda hit me. This used to be his jacket” Kaiser gave a tug on the leather sleeve, much as he had done every Sunday twenty years ago, “and I wear the same cologne… I dunno. I thought about him a lot when he first passed, but lately… until just now…” “Just now?” She tried to catch his sentence where it had fallen. “Realizin’ how much a father can shape who his son is.” “Kaiser, there’s something I need to tell you.” “Yeah?” He leaned forward and adjusted the heat, but didn’t bother to turn off the radio, though something told him he should. “I tried to tell ya the other week, but I…” “You’re not unhappy with us. Are you?” “No.” “Good, cuz I’m kinda likin’ this little thing we got g...” “I’m pregnant, Kaiser.” Now he turned off the radio, silencing Seger. “You’re joking, right?” “Serious as I’ve ever been.” “…oh.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Tuesday, March 10th “My father was the one person I could have never stomached letting down. And a part of me feels like the only reason I didn’t was because he died before I got the chance to blow it.” “You feel he would have had the same issues with your choice that your brother had?” “No. Probably not. Dad was always supportive, even if he didn’t necessarily love what was going on.” “So then why do you feel like you would have let him down?” “I dunno.” Kaiser tapped his foot against the floor, sounding similar to the doctor’s pen thumping against the notepad, only this time the heartbeat was more rapid. “I’ve just got so much on my plate. The problems with Dave, Ellie says she’s pregnant. I’ve got the match with Troy, and then the Dual Halo.” “I like how you just gloss over your girlfriend being pregnant… as if you were reading the grocery list and that was just a box of pasta.” “Its just one thing among many.” “Really?” “I’ve got the two biggest matches of my career coming up, on back to back nights, and I’m supposed to be worried about Ellie… or how I’m supposed to mend fences with Dave?” Kaiser’s foot stopped tapping, and the room fell silent. “Troy. Halo. I can’t ignore those like they’re nothin’. I mean… the Dual Halo for Christ’s sake. It’s the biggest match of the year.” “The biggest mistake you could make, Kaiser…” “Sound like you mean the biggest mistake I have made.” “… is to think that there’s no room, or not enough time, to deal with wrestling and family. If you focus entirely on your profession, and neglect your personal life… it won’t benefit either. You are way too caught up in succeeding in PRIME. I’ve been trying to tell you that for months.” “So I should focus on fixing things with my family and forget about my career?” “Balance them. People might say otherwise, they might say ‘you can’t juggle two things at once’. They’re wrong. Until you pull some attention away from your professional life, and put it towards your life at home, things aren’t likely to get better on either front.” “Juggle, huh?” Kaiser gripped the flesh of the couch in his fingertips. He pulled and stretched, trying to tear something from its surface, a child wiping the foul remains of unpleasant tastes from the tongue. “What if I drop something?” “Then pick it up.” The doctor looked at his watch. “Ok, might be hard to believe, but our time’s up. It’s been an hour.” “Feels like three days.” “I’ll meet with ya again when you get back from Russia. Schedule something with Nancy before you go.” “How do you tackle it, Doc? How can you possibly prepare yourself for something so big?” “Kaiser,” the doctor sighed and folded his arms across his chest, leaving his notepad balancing on his knee. “the Dual Halo is an extraordinary event. It takes physical strength, mental…” “No. The other thing.” “What?” Kaiser leaned forward, narrowing the distance between himself and the doctor, minimizing the risk that the answer would get lost in the space between them. “How do you go about being a father?” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ KAISER Lowering his eyes from the heavens, he smiled, while the sting of the salt in his wounds subsided. As he walked away, leaving the beast behind, he stopped and wiped the drench from his clothes, ringing the mess from his fabric. And when he finished, he stepped out of the putrid and murky puddle beneath his feet.
