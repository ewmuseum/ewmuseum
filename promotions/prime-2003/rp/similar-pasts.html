Date: Various A blank stare covers the ghostly white face of Walczak. His hands tremble as the rest of his body remains stiff, and his breathing is abnormal as he only manages to breathe in about every five seconds. “Thomas…Thomas…are you okay.” “Ummm…yeah, I’m fine.” Walczak’s breathing slowly returns to normal, and color slowly starts to filter back into his face. A couple of weeks ago Walczak did not even know that his parents had been killed, he had thought that they had just put him up for adoption, not wanting him, but now that had all changed. Not only did he know that someone had murdered his parents in cold blood, but he could actually be working for him. “I told you that finding out this information could put you in a bad position. Your mindset could change, and that would make you dangerous not only to yourself, but those around you.” “No…no…it’s good that you told me.” Walczak takes a drink from his mug of house beer, and takes another long breath finally gaining back his composure. “Are you going to be able to go forward?” “I don’t know.” Dmitry looks at Walczak, not sure what to say next. It is not as if he could relate to what Walczak is going through, how many people could? Working for the man who murdered your parents would put anyone on edge and while Walczak looks calm right now the smallest thing may just set him off. “You could go home now. Nickolaj would be upset, but I am sure that I would be able to think of something to tell him and get you in the clear.” “I just don’t know if I could live with myself knowing that I am working for the man that killed my parents.” “No, no, no, we are not here to help him. In fact the reason that you were brought him was to take him out.” “Why couldn’t you do this yourself? I was happy just living my life thinking that my parents didn’t want me.” Walczak looks towards Dmitry waiting for his response, but he gets none. All Dmitry does is take a drink from his mug of beer. “Are you going to tell me, or am I going to just sit here as you stare at me like a retard sipping your beer?” “I asked him to send you over here.” The eyes of Walczak once again widen nearly bulging out of his head. This man sitting in front of him, a man who treated him like dog shit for the better part of two days is the reason that he is in this situation for the first place. “WHAT THE FUCK IS THE MATTER WITH YOU?” Dmitry just takes another drink from his mug. Not being able to take it anymore Walczak knocks the mug away from the man sitting across from him, but Dmitry does nothing but take a napkin to wipe the beer off of his shirt. “Calm down and I will explain.” “I’m sorry; it’s just too much information for one day.” “You wanted to know, I told you that it would be better if you just waited until after everything was taken care of.” “I know, but you just don’t know what I am going through right now.” “I do.” “Ha, yeah I’m sure you do.” “The reason that I am working for Nickolaj in the first place is because this guy ruined my life.” “How?” “About twenty years ago I tried to open up a restaurant in St. Petersburg. Everything seemed to be going fine until Janek decided that I was in his territory. He asked me to comply, and I just didn’t feel it was necessary. I was warned about him, he was some former KGB hotshot, and now he was running an organized crime family out of St. Petersburg.” “So you went to Nickolaj for protection?” “No, I continued to run the restaurant, even managed to hire an acclaimed chef out of France, but about six months into business the place burned down. Everything that I had worked for was taken away from me.” “So why didn’t you take him out, go after him?” “I couldn’t prove that it was him, and he had much more power. I went to Nickolaj asking for help, and I have been working for him ever since, my end goal being taking out Janek.” Walczak takes a deep breath, wiping the sweat that had slowly built up on his forehead. He looks over at Dmitry who looks somewhat disturbed over the story that he has just shared. “I really do feel for you, I don’t care much about other people, but I do feel for you. The thing is I don’t know where I fit in?” “About two months ago I was in a meeting with Janek. I have been slowly trying to get information out him, but for years nearly twenty I have gotten nothing. Then the topic came to how he got started in his new life, and he told me a story that if I had not heard it from his mouth I would not have believed it. His best friend, one whom he had known since birth, was working to take down the communist regime in Poland.” “Poland? I thought that this guy was part of the Russian KGB, or something like that?” “It confused me at first as well. He didn’t feel that his friend was taking his opinions to heart. Thinking that this was disrespectful, Janek turned on his friend in exchange for protection, and eventually worked his way up the Russian hierarchy in Poland. When Poland eventually did come out of the Communist regime, he decided to take his training and influence over here.” “What happened to the friend?” “I’m sure that you know this by now, his friend was your father. He could have saved him, and he could have saved your mother, but he let them die.” Walczak’s eyes close, and for the first time that he could remember the behemoth shed a tear. “I felt that you were somewhat of a kindred spirit, and I asked Nickolaj to find you.” “Thank you.” Walczak takes an already used napkin to wipe away the tears slowly streaming down his cheek. “What do you want me to do?” “There is nothing more than can be done now. Janek disappears for weeks at a time, but trust me I will call you when I find out when he will be coming back. You should go back home now; there is no reason for you to be here. I’ll take you back to your hotel.” Walczak just nods his head getting up from the table. “Tom, you should take this.” Turning around Walczak sees Dmitry holding out a rather beaten down black and white photo. The photo contains a couple smiling as a small baby rests in the hand of the mother. “Are these my parents?” All Dmitry does is nod as he gets up putting an arm around the shoulder of Walczak and leading him out to the car. The trip to Russia has been a whirlwind of emotion for Walczak from being disrespected to now for the first time finding out the truth about his parents, and now being able to actually lay his eyes upon them. _________________________________________________________________________________________ ”Tomek….Tomek, where are you?” A lady walks around in a long dress, her hands and outfit covered in what appears to be flour, looks around a grassy field. A young boy hides behind a tree, peeking out to look at the woman. She does nothing but smile as she clearly sees the boys somewhat mediocre hiding spot. “I guess that I just have to call your father, and tell him that you are missing.” “NO….NO….Here I am.” The boy runs out from behind the tree screaming as he makes his way towards his mother. He grabs her leg showing her that he is in fact not gone. The mother giggles as she runs her hand through the hair of her son. “Don’t tell daddy, I didn’t run away.” “I know….” “I love….” The scene shifts immediately as Walczak jumps forward from his state of sleep. Sweat covers the giant’s face, and upper body. This has been an almost daily occurrence for Walczak over the past two weeks. Leaving Russia was supposed to take his mind away from this, but all he has done is dwell on it. His apartment has become dirty, and he has become a recluse missing two of PRIME’s last three shows. Not only that, but this dream would be a joyous one for most people on the earth, but for Walczak it is a nightmare. These should be memories, but they are nonexistent in reality, only dreams, and the worst part is Walczak knows the man who had taken them away from him. Walczak gets up from his bed and makes his way towards the kitchen. He grabs an already used cup filling it with water from the sink, and takes a paper towel wiping the sweat off his body, and disposing of it on the floor. “When are they going to call?” This has been the singular thought running through the mind of Walczak, his upcoming match with Big Bear being put on the back burner. Walczak isn’t a person to ever underestimate his opponent, but all his effort has been put into his quest to avenge his parents, people who less than a month ago he could care less about. With Walczak heading back to bed, the phone rings, Walczak running towards it. “Hello.” “Pack your bags…”
