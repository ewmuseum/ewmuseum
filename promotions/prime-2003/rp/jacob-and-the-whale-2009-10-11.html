Jacob and the Whale

11 October 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11th October 2009 Lucky 8 Motel The Bronx 3 Weeks Ago “Jacob! I need your help!” Sitting on the end of the bed wearing only his underwear and a perplexed frown, McKail placed the quarter full bottle of Jack Daniels on the floor and desperately tried to put a name to the voice. However, in his sufficiently drunken state just being able to answer his damned mobile phone was a monumental feat all in itself. Time passed and the silence grew a little uncomfortable; too uncomfortable for whoever the hell it was on the other end of the line. “Jacob?” He allowed the name to hang for a time. “You there, buddy?” Buddy? What the fuck…? It was getting too weird and McKail’s patience was wearing thin. His mind just wasn’t cooperating, but after sousing it with three quarters of a bottle of Jack Daniels he could hardly blame it. “Yeah,” he tentatively replied. “Who’s this?” “It’s Marty!” He declared. The name was familiar, but McKail still couldn’t attach a face to it. “You know, from AA?” Like lightening, the answer struck him. Marty was his sponsor, the guy he was supposed to call if ever felt the temptation to drink again. Couple of hours too late for that… “I’m sorry to bother you at this time,” Marty continued, “but I feel kinda weak right now.” He paused, obviously deliberating whether or not to say anymore. Much to McKail’s chagrin, he did. “I’ve got the urge to give into temptation! I really think I‘ll do it this time!” So? Why the fuck are you calling me? It was only then McKail realised that he was Marty’s sponsor too. He was supposed to be helping him through this, talking him through what he was feeling. Good goddamn, this poor fool has been dealt a rough hand… “I need your help!” McKail thought it over for a minute or so, before reaching over the side of his bed for his pants and his cigarettes. “I’m on my way.” “Hurry!” “Fuck you,” McKail absently muttered, hitting the disconnect button on his mobile and began the eternal hunt for yesterday’s t-shirt. How the fuck did I get into this? *** The Unity Church Hall The Bronx One Month Ago “Hello everybody,” the spectacled guy, wearing the bad jumper began, “welcome to another meeting of Alcoholics Anonymous. I see we have a few new faces in the crowd.” McKail felt his spectacled gaze burn right into him as he leant back in his chair glaring at the ceiling, already unreasonably bored. What the fuck am I doing here? He asked himself for the hundredth time that hour. Of course, he already knew the answer. Hell, it was his idea. Before the major wrestling organisation in the circuit would even consider employing somebody of McKail’s troubled background, they required some kind of show of commitment. The promoters needed a guarantee - or something close to it - that he wouldn’t fall off the radar and leave any of the prospective organisations in the lurch. Nobody came out and said that it they were concerned about McKail’s alcoholism because they couldn’t prove, but lack of proof didn’t matter a whole lot to them. So, McKail decided to make a grand gesture to show them he was willing to reform. How were they to know that it was bullshit? He sure as hell wasn’t going to tell them he had no real intention of giving up the booze and he very much doubted anybody else in the meeting could hold themselves together enough to school them otherwise. Still publically, he to attend the meetings and play along with his alleged quest for sobriety, but sure as hell didn’t mean he had to like it. “So,” the spectacled ringleader continued, “who would like to go first?” Across the way from McKail a balding man in his mid-forties raised his hand meekly. He was overweight by a good three hundred pounds and every time he moved the chair underneath his bulbous ass groaned painfully, threatening to give up and crumble away at any second. The group leader nodded at the fat man, who stood and nervously cupped his hands together, resting them on the hump of his gut. “Hello,” he began, his eyes shifting around the room, “my name is Marty and I have a problem. This is my first time--” After that, McKail just zoned out. Once he’d heard one confession, he’d heard them all and it was getting boring. Instead he gave thought to the Jewel in the Crown tournament, idly wondering who the mystery competitors might be and carefully considering the chances of him winning the whole thing. The odds were against him, but weren’t they always? *** Marty’s Place The Bronx 3 Weeks Ago An hour later and entirely pissed off, McKail pulled up outside the apartment block in which he suspected Marty lived. He’d been driving for what seemed like an age before he’d found the damned place, which was something of an accomplishment considering the state hew was in. He parked up just outside, took deep breaths and calmed himself. You can do this, McKail, he told himself. You ain’t drunk and he can’t say otherwise. Fooling Marty was important to him. One wrong word from him and the glowing report the AA group leader had promised would evaporate almost as quickly as his wrestling career. He only hoped Marty would be too drunk to smell the liquor on his breath or notice the way he couldn’t stand up straight. Stepping out his car, the music hit him like a bat up side the head and as he fumbled with around with his car keys, he figured finding Marty’s apartment from here on out wouldn’t be all that hard. McKail followed the music to an apartment around the back of the complex, resisting the urge to hurl all the while. The crisp night air had gotten to him more than he thought it would and his stomach didn‘t like it one bit. He just thanked the gods the apartment was on the ground floor. He propped himself up against the sharp stone walling hand-first, rapping the door with the other hand hard and loud. He heard some banging around inside the apartment before Marty opened the door with force, almost pulling the damned thing off it’s hinges in the process. His face was red and he was trying to hide a bottle of Vodka behind. “Jacob!” He exclaimed. “ So glad you could make it!” McKail glared at him confused. Make it…? “I’m having something of a soiree!” He declared, seemingly picking up on McKail’s confusion. He wondered whether Marty would pick up on his anger too. “Come on in!” McKail shook his head. “No, I--” But before McKail could say any more, Marty grabbed him with his fat sausage fingers and dragged him inside, shunting him into a chair in the corner. Fuck… Trying not to hurl and ruin the bastards carpet, he glanced around the room to see that he wasn’t alone; he also saw that it wasn’t much of a party. Some guy sat on the couch to the right of him, all thing and freakish-looking. McKail could see the outline of the man’s skull up to his curly hairline and it cast the impression that his damned skeleton was trying to bust right out of his skin. He wore glasses which were delicately balanced on the edge of his pelican-like nose and seemed entirely too big for his face. On the other side of him, stood a rather sizeable Goth girl who wore a striking resemblance to a panda. She wore a dozen or so piercing across her face and a low cut black top with a tattoo of a bandaged mummy’s hand groping her left breast. McKail felt like he was in the Jerry Springer green room, waiting to be called out and affronted by a seven foot black transvestite he could’ve worn he’d never seen before. Marty lurched forward and shoved a plastic container in his hand, winking and taking a generous mouthful of vodka straight from the bottle. “Let’s have an orgy!” He yelled, falling back over the corner of his bed. “Fuck off!” McKail found himself shouting, not really caring who he offended. *** The Unity Church Hall The Bronx One Month Ago “Okay gang,” the group leader began, rubbing his hands together and glancing around the room, “this is about the time we buddy each of you up with someone else in the group. You will be each others ‘sponsor’ and help them should they require. If you lend each other your strength, you will get through this. So each of you find somebody and get to know each other.” Fuck! McKail exclaimed, considering each of the freaks in the room and decided he would hate all of them anyway. What the fuck is this about? Do I really need to do this? The others filtered around the room, shaking hands and making friends, whilst McKail stayed put and checked his messages on his mobile. There were none, but he pretended there were anyway and went about hitting buttons randomly, hoping nobody would notice. After a time, he felt a presence loom over him. It was Marty, the fat man who’d told them all his problems first. It didn’t look like anybody had teamed up with him and after telling the group the story about how he got so drunk he shat himself, McKail was hardly surprised. “Hi,” Marty greeted. He waited for a response, but he sure as hell didn’t get one. “Are you buddied up yet?” McKail slid his mobile back into the pocket of jeans and calmly tried to conjure some sort of lie that would send this whale packing. In the end, he drew a blank. “No.” “Great!” Marty exclaimed, a wide spread lighting up his face. “My name’s Marty, what’s yours?” “McKail,” he replied, gently massaging his eyes. “Jacob McKail.” Marty set himself down in the seat next to him, almost nudging McKail off his chair with his flabby shoulder. “I’m sure we’ll get on just fine!” McKail glared at him. “I hate you beyond belief.” Marty’s smile disintegrated and was promptly replaced by a state of confusion. “What was that? I don‘t think I caught it.” “Sure you did,” McKail told him. “Sure you did.” *** Marty’s Place The Bronx 3 Weeks Ago “What the hell are you doing, man?” Marty questioned, as McKail used all his strength to shunt the fat bastard out of his apartment door - it was a tight squeeze too. Once outside, Marty drunkenly pushed McKail away, trying to claw back some modicum of dignity and failing miserably. “Get the fuck off me…” It came to something when Mckail was forced into being the sensible one, though he knew sensible wasn’t exactly the right term for it considering he drove all the way here from his motel heavily soused on Jack Daniels. Still, he’d sobered up some now and somehow responsible for this whole debacle. “Okay,” Marty began, raising his open palms defensively, “I fucked up. I know this. But I’ll try and quit tomorrow, after the party…” McKail shook his head. “That ain’t a party, that’s two people standing in your apartment.” He watched as Marty looked back at his open, scratching his head. “An’ watchin’ you get loaded ain’t anyone’s idea of fun.” Marty swayed from side to side as he desperately tried to prod McKail in the chest, missing most of the time. “Fuck you! You don’t know what you’re talkin’ ‘bout! They’re havin’ fun alright!” McKail shook his head and so Marty promptly turned and marched back into his apartment. “Is everybody having a good ti--” But before he could finish, he tripped over his porch step and sent himself sprawling halfway through the doorway of his apartment. McKail watched on from a distance, just glad he didn’t land on the skinny kid he’d met earlier. If he had, that guy would be dead. No question. “I’m okay!” he declared. They paused and developed a somewhat concerned expression. “But I think I’ve shat myself again. Could somebody please help me up?” There ain’t enough people in this apartment block, friend,McKail thought, shaking his head and heading for his car. “Fuck this…”
