Date: 29/08/07 Manhattan, New York Miller Pharmaceuticals Miller looked out of his large penthouse window to the flickering lights of the metropolis beneath him, his breath leaving a haze on the crystal clear glass. Miller’s eyes transfixed on the misty glow as the rain drops danced off its sleek frame, catching the lights beneath him. In his hand he held a small tumbler of whiskey, his eyes sore and tired, his throat aching with thirst, but his mind lay fixed on one important thing, the news channel in the background. ‘This morning residents in Harlem awoke to the crackle of gun fire as Columbia University became the site of a fire works display between New York police and a man believed to be involved in a series of murders that have plagued the delicate streets of New York. Police have yet to make a statement about this, but one can only wonder how this could damage our Congressman’s campaign for senate.’ In the corner of his eye he watched Pontius, seated in the black leather chair, the glint of the television bouncing off his tumbler, his eyes glued to the screen with scrunched lips. “Surely it can’t be him, Sir,” Pontius deduced with little evidence. Congressman Miller exhaled deeply and finished his whiskey. Without turning around, he said, “Of course it was him, Pontius. We sent him after Braxton to finish a mess I had started.” Pontius remained passive, like a closed book, he sat in silence. His mind was working and his eyelids blinked, but he had no answer to the difficult situation that had now been thrust into their laps. Congress was only a few weeks away and now the very dark secret they had kept so vitally hidden was unravelling before them in the media. Congressman Miller hated the long lingering silences, but tonight he found it relaxing as his thoughts dwindled on possible plans. Ronnie Barnes stood in the shadows, brooding into the night. In a cold and dark manner, Miller turned to his staff. “I want to know everything that my son blabbed to that reporter. My son was a weak and cowardly man, he will have told his last confession before he marched out to his death.” Pontius inched forward on his leather chair; the glass tumbler gripped with both of his hands. “Surely you don’t mean he gave a confession about everything!” Pontius declared. Sipping some of the whiskey, the liquor hot against the back of his throat, he said, “I know George was easily uncorked in tight situations, but he couldn’t have put you in the slammer!” “We have to assume the worst, Pontius. This is my career, damnit! My son was a stupid man and if he confessed to the reason why those three girls died then I will be pinned along with him...” he paused, a sickly grin lacing his lips, “… well just me considering he’s a corpse.” Barnes grunted. “I should’ve taken care of this entire mess, boss.” “And you will,” Miller assured Barnes with a stiff upper lip. “I want you to sneak into the forensics lab using another staff’s identity and steal the evidence. If they don’t have proof, it can’t go to court.” “If that’s what you wish. I will remove the pieces of the puzzle,” Ronnie replied standing up. Miller stopped him before he got across the floor. “Oh and Ronnie, a few more bodies under the rug won’t hurt our cause. Remove all evidence.” His tall and brute like frame leaving a monstrous shadow behind him as he strutted to the door, Ronnie turned to Pontius with a snickering laugh. “Aren’t you going to give me the rules?” Pontius sighed with a heavy moan and swirled the thick brown liquid around, he said, “No, Barnes. You have full control over this one.” As Barnes left, Pontius looked up to see Miller stewing in his chair, irritation flowing through his black heart. “I have a bad feeling about this one, boss.” Miller looked at him, an empty gaze. “Why are you still sitting here? Go and figure this out … Find out everything on that reporter … everything … NOW!” Pontius rose with little objection and fled the room. Miller hissed and listened to the howling of the night around him, the company was empty and in his chair he saw a picture of his sons face. Anger boiled within the dept of his bowels and in an unprovoked rage threw his whiskey glass at the television. ---- Manhattan, New York Dawn’s apartment, Harlem ‘Paint it black’ by the Rolling Stones echoed around the living room as the sun rose with an orange tint in the distance.
