M & W: Deer in the Headlights

undated

Date: then and now 1.1 the mirror: never forgot When did it stop being worth it? You’re not sure, actually. In fact, last time you won a wrestling match, your personal life was in as much shambles as it is now. You were single, alone, and half-blind. You were surrounded by people you didn’t know if you could trust. Yet when you won that match, when your music played and the fans cheered, you were able to forget anything else going on in the world except for that one moment. That didn’t really happen at Culture Shock. Now, you could make the argument that you didn’t actually win the Dual Halo, but that’s bullshit. Everyone knows that no one wins the Dual Halo. Except the winner, of course, but the point is that when you get into a match with thirty people, there isn’t one winner and twenty-nine losers. There are ten winners, or rather ten survivors. So you don’t consider your Halo performance a failure, especially since you took down the monster Hessian and won a title shot. But even through the congratulations, even though all of the publicity you received for your strong showing, you never forgot. You were never distracted. You never stopped thinking about Katie. It wasn’t always this way. ---- 1.2 the window: tag February 17, 2007 “She’s calling again,” rang the voice from outside the ring. Jonathan Rhine doesn’t bother looking over, or else he’d get a boot to the head for his troubles. “Ignore it,” he huffs, ducking under the boot and coming back with a quick lariat to get his sparring partner Walter to the ground. “This is the third time she’s called in two minutes,” the kid outside said again. Rhine spares one glance over the kid and shakes his head before hitting a standing moonsault that keeps Walter down for the three count. “No fair,” says Walter, getting to his feet. “The kid kept shouting, it was distracting. We need to go again.” Rhine dusts of his hands and shoots a skeptical look at Walter. “Distracting? He was yelling to me. That shouldn’t have affected you. And I’m tired of beating on you, Walter. It’s been what, three, four times in a row now?” Walter scoffs. “Man, don’t get cocky with me. Just cuz you’re the trainer’s golden boy doesn’t mean that you can talk shit all you want.” “Maybe not,” Rhine laughs, “but me beating you all the time does.” “Mr. Rhine,” the kid outside the ring walks to the apron, holding his phone. “This makes five times. Match is over. You wanna take it?” “Shut up, kid, grown folks are talking.” Walter grabs Rhine’s shoulder and turns him around. “How about a tag match? I’ll take your buddy Jack.” Walter nods to Jack, and he bounds over the ring and gives a pound to Walter. “Mr. Rhine?” Rhine shrugs. “Yeah, I’m game. Who you giving me?” “Mr. Rhine, Katie won’t stop calling. Will you just take the – ” Walter smiles, then points at the young man holding Rhine’s phone. “There you go. You and Dith.” Rhine looks at the kid, who is now smiling wide. “Me? Really?” says the kid, dropping the phone. He nearly springs to the ring, rolling under the ropes and flopping on the canvas. Jonathan looks at his new partner, looks at Walter, then smiles meekly. “Fine. Me and Dith.” ---- 1.3 the mirror: dith Aaron Timble. The gym assistant who your trainer took a gamble on. No one ever called him Aaron, though. He was either “the kid” or Dith, which sort of confused you initially until you faced him in the ring. Foster put him against you because you were his first big gamble, and it had seemed like you would face up well against him. And you did. For about five to six minutes you sparred pretty evenly, and you were actually impressed with the way he handled himself despite his smaller frame. But then you charged him once, and he locked up. You hit him with a lariat, and as you got him up he just seemed out of it. You didn’t know what to do, so you slammed him down with your finisher and ended it, but even after the recovery he was still a little slow. That’s when your friend Jack chuckled and explained it to you. Aaron would fight well for awhile, but then the gravity of a match would get too big for him, and he’d freeze up like a deer in the headlights. Deer in the headlights. Dith. He was a good kid, but his propensity to lock up in matches garnered him a lot of dislike and disrespect in the Academy. Even Foster started to lose faith in him, mostly relegating him to his previously held assistant duties. And you looked on, not really wanting to give up on him, but also not knowing if he was right for the business. And then Walter made him your tag partner. Fantastic. It occurs to you now that this was all you thought about during that time, and not at all about the fact that Katie had called almost ten times in the minutes prior. ---- 1.4 the window: get in the way “Tag me in, Aaron!” Rhine shouts, watching with dread as his friend Jack takes the young man nicknamed Dith down with an armbar. Jack looks up at Rhine as Dith struggles in the hold, winking. “Not doing a whole lot of good on the turnbuckle, are you Jonny?” Jack’s grin seems forced, and it appears to Jonathan that his friend may have been enjoying the pain a little too much. “Come on, Aaron, you can do it,” shouts Rhine, but he knows that he can’t. It’s already been a few minutes, and Walter had accurately pinpointed when Dith would live up to his nickname. Rhine had only tagged him in for a couple of moments before the tide turned. “Shouldn’t have even given the kid a chance, golden boy,” rings Walter from across the ring. Rhine just sneers, and they watch in relative silence as Jack keeps Dith in the hold. For a moment the only noise is Dith’s frantic grunts as he tries to escape the hold. Then the sounds are joined by a phone ringing. Rhine looks over at the bench outside and rolls his eyes. “Gotta tell that bitch to leave you alone, Jonny,” says Walter. “All she’ll do is get in the way. Tag me in, Jack.” Jack keeps his arm around Dith’s and tags in Walter, who takes a quick look at Rhine before unleashing an incredibly loud clothesline on the young man. He drops to his knees and gets Dith down for the three count, then snickers. “Yeah, that’s more like it.” Rhine scoffs. “What, claiming dominance on me when you never touched me?” “That’s how tag matches go sometimes, Rhine. Sometimes you just get paired with a lemon.” “Or a deer,” says Jack, and both of the victors laugh. Rhine shoots a betrayed look at Jack, but he merely shrugs. “I’m out.” As the two leave the ring together, Aaron slowly gets up and shakes his head, skulking towards Rhine. “I’m sorry, Mr. Rhine. I don’t know what happened, I just sorta…” “Froze up,” says Rhine bitterly. “Yeah. Oh well, it’s just a wrestling match.” “Right. Just a match.” Rhine can’t keep the bitterness out of his voice as he stares at the canvas. After a moment, Dith looks at the phone that is once again ringing outside the ring. “Are you gonna get that?” Shaking his head, Rhine rolls out of the ring and grabs his phone, silencing it. “Not in the mood.” Without another word, he walks out of the gym, muttering. ---- 1.5 the mirror: second place It was so easy to ignore her back then. That wasn’t even a match. There was nothing on the line. FUSE wouldn’t contact you for another few months, so you hadn’t been in your first real fed yet. You hadn’t even been to a point in your career where a match actually mattered for something other than pride. And yet you still let it become bigger than her. You didn’t even answer your phone until you got home because in some sick, sad way, you blamed her for losing the match. Not the circumstances, not even Aaron’s catatonic fit. You blamed the one person who loved you, because it was the easiest thing to do. It’s easy now to look back on it all and think how stupid you were, but it doesn’t change the fact that for over half of your relationship, Katie was firmly in second place to the excitement of wrestling. And now all of that has changed. You couldn’t keep your mind off of her as you stormed through the Halo, you couldn’t keep your mind off of her when you went to PRIME the next day, and you sure couldn’t keep your mind off of her when you checked yourself into the hospital after the glass attack on ReVolution. That coupled with the fact she wasn’t there to visit you made this hospital visit even more excruciating. ---- 1.6 the window: life coach March 30, 2009 “I guess I’ll play the cliché and say I figured I’d see you more after you decided to unretire.” Rhine’s doctor chuckles, putting his chart under his arm. “I’m fine, though, right?” “Yeah, just some scratches and such. Nothing a little more violence won’t help, I’m sure. Rest up, and we’ll let in your usual cavalcade of visitors.” Rhine frowns, looking down at the linoleum. “Yeah, I don’t think that’s going to happen this time around. A little bit has changed since last time.” Still smiling, the doctor heads for the door. “Oh really? And that’s why someone is waiting by the door?” Rhine looks up, his face filling with hope. She said she still loved him, so maybe the thought of him hurting would… “Hi, Jonathan.” Rhine tries to hide his disappointment as he looks up at the man who is decidedly not his ex-girlfriend, but it is a faulty attempt. “Dit – I mean Aaron. What are you doing here?” Aaron “Dith” Timble walks in the room timidly. Not much has changed in two years – he’s still small, still meek. He pulls up a chair next to Rhine and stammers, “I just caught the show, and I saw you might need a little help.” This time, Rhine makes no effort to conceal his disbelief. “What could you help me with?” “I can help you stop thinking about Katie.” Jonathan’s eyes go wide. “How did you…?” Dith shrugs. “It’s obvious, man. It was on your face all show. And right now you might be doing all right, but eventually you won’t be able to focus on anything else, and you’ll just…” He lets the sentence die, but Rhine knows exactly what he means. “Freeze up, yeah.” Rhine nods. “Did you ever get over that?” Dith shrugs. “It’s a work in progress. Finally beat Walter, though.” Rhine smiles. “Good to know. How do you plan on helping me?” “You just need a sounding board, man. You’re all by yourself, and you keep yourself all wrapped up tight with your trials and your emotions, and one day you’ll burst from the pressure. And that…that won’t be really good for your profession.” “So you’re here to be a friend?” “Sort of,” says Dith, shrugging again. “I’m also here because I just got my degree in psychology and sociology, and I needed a job. So I’ve decided to make you my first project.” Rhine raises an eyebrow. “Project?” “Yeah. I’m going to take it upon myself to be your life coach, so to speak. I’ll help you reorganize your priorities, discover what really makes you happy, and maybe one day help shape you into a person that Katie wouldn’t be terrified to be with.” Rhine stares at Dith for a few moments, not sure exactly how to react. Then, after a long stare, The New Life nods. “That sounds like something I’d be interested in. I have a question, though. We gave you no respect at the Academy. Why help me now?” Dith smiles. “That’ll be something you’ll find out along the way.” ---- 1.7 the mirror: just You still don’t really know why you said okay. The skeptical side of you was thinking that it made no sense at all. How does this guy who couldn’t even finish a single wrestling match think he could help you with your life? Is it wise to trust someone who doesn’t even look like he had it figured out himself? Is your life so bad that you are looking for help from him? The answer to that last question is why you ended up agreeing with him. Yes, your life is bad enough that you need help. You’re not sure what you can get from it, but you also know that another cliché you’re familiar with is that beggars cannot be choosers. And despite your high-profile re-emergence in PRIME, you’ve been begging pretty solidly for quite a few months now. You’re tired of it. And maybe this kid can make it stop. He might not seem like the best idea for a life coach, but he was the one who always tried to remind you of your priorities. He was the one who used the word “just” when referring to wrestling. Just a match. Just a sport. To you, wrestling has never been just anything. But maybe that’s part of the problem. It was only a few weeks ago that you essentially chose wrestling above everything else because you assumed that you didn’t have any other options. But maybe through this strange guy you’ll find out that you don’t have to choose anything. Maybe you’ll be able to get everything together. You don’t know if any of it is true, but it sure beats thinking about Katie all the time. And sometimes that’s enough. Sometimes that makes it worth it.
