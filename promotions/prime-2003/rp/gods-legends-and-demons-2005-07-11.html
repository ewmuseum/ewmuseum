Gods, Legends and Demons

11 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 11, 2005 You sit in the darkness and contemplate the demon in front of you. Clasped between your hands, you roll it back and forth over your fingers, feeling its smooth surface and contemplating its contents. A steady stream of tears pours down your cheeks. One drops into the corner of your mouth. How long has it been since you cried? REALLY cried? The kind of body-wracking sobs you see in a movie or on a TV show? You can’t remember. For all you know, your reservoir of emotions that deep dried up. Evaporated in the hazy days of big dollars and bigger bumps back when you were a “superstar.” A lot of good all that does you now. But your demon is still here. It never left. Only now, it’s no longer inside you. It’s right in front of you. You’re holding it. There, in your hands. It’d be so easy to let him out. You can almost hear him talking. Inside your head, you assign it a voice. A cute, piping sort of sound. Like one of those elves in the trees who make cookies. That voice speaks to you in soothing tones that only you can hear. It offers you relief. A hiatus from the numbing physical pains shooting down your right arm from your reconstructed neck. A sabbatical from the mental and emotional anguish. You can hear it whispering. let me out let me out let me out Saturday, 1:15 p.m. The gym is empty. All the students--those too young, too skinny, too old, too fat and those with true promise—have departed. School… is no longer in session. At least for today. Currently, the gym you call home—your shitty apartment is still too shitty to really qualify for the title—is nearly vacant. Usually a torrent of noise and bustle of activity, it sits dormant, awaiting the next crop of would-be grapplers to pass through the doors the next morning. The only sounds are the shuffling of feet on canvas, and the grunts of exertion. You’re responsible for the last of those, as your sparring partner cinches in a tight side headlock. You feel him step on the back of your left knee and chain wrestle into a chinlock. He’s increasing the pressure. Quick, get out of it! Preceded by a sharp elbow to the ribs, you roll to your left and bring your sparring partner down with a drop toehold. You stand, grabbing an arm, and roll into a Majistral cradle. “ONE!’ you say. “TWO!’ He kicks out, grabbing at your leg as he stands. You awkwardly stand on one leg, until he Dragon whips you over to the canvas. Uh oh, the Sharpshooter. FIGHT, damn it! Frantic kicks with your free leg find their mark. You roll to your feet and nail him with a chop. The meaty impact of flesh on flesh echoes through the empty building, and for once, you revel in the absence of the ubiquitious “Whooo” chant. You chop him again. You kick him in the gut. You hear the grunt of pain. Now… finish it! You hook one arm, then the other… when suddenly he’s loose! You feel a sudden fire in your shoulder joint as you’re driven to the canvas by your right arm. Sun-browned fingers on strong hands link right in front of your face, pushing back into your visage, then up. You refuse to groan or cry out in pain. You do it anyway when a knuckle starts to dig into your cheek. Shit… You tap out. It is, after all, just a sparring session. Your partner releases the grip, but you still lay there for a few seconds, wondering why your mat has the slight aroma of piss. Shrugging—and making a mental note to stock up on antibactierial soap or find a new canvas—you roll over to face your sparring partner, taking a little satisfaction in seeing him labor for breath. 'You've still got the moves, man. Though I never thought I'd see you winded... Too many cookouts and bottles of good Scotch in paradise?' “The Legend” Jeffrey Wayne responds with a warm smile. He’s different thah you remember him. His hair now is as much blond as brown—bleached by the sun. His skin is well-tanned, and he has the look of a man who has been busy exacting the best revenge, living well. The rivalry between you in the ring developed into grudging respect, then friendship, outside of it. After… the incident at OSW End Game, you still kept in touch. And that’s led him here, to Gibson’s Wrestling Academy. 'I'm a man of leisure now Dave. I do this for fun. Besides… I still made you tap. And I’m the one who’s retired.” starts to lean back against the ropes until you kick your legs out and kip up to your feet in one smooth motion. “Point taken. Let’s go again.” Thirty-two minutes later… After another three restarts, you successfully broke out of the Legend Lock. You lead Jeff into your office and toss him a bottle of Gatorade. “Phew! That’s what I call a workout… Thanks.” “Thank you,” Jeff said. “Good to see I still have the old skills. And you’ve improved since the OSW days. Must be quite the training regimen…” “I was drunk for about a year. Then I broke my neck. I wouldn’t recommend it.” Jeff takes a little stroll around the office—which doesn’t take long. He pauses at the wall of photos, lingering on one of Gibson and Matt Register locked in combat and linked by leather in their vicious Strap Match. Meanwhile, you turn and cut on the TV/VCR in your office and slide in a tape of Youngblood’s match with Angelo Deville. “All work and no play…” you hear Jeff say. “You know, I’ve never seen you so focused.” “Yeah well I want to win, don’t I?” You hear your voice trailing off, and hit the power button on the TV/VCR. “I say something wrong?” “No… Just reminds me of something.” A conversation now months old. In a different ring in a different city. You, doing a favor for a friend—much like Jeff was now. “You OK, Dave?” You realize your hands have clenched into fists so tight that the knuckles have turned white. You open your hands and briefly examine the indentations of your fingernails into the palms. Already, the marks begin to fade. But some marks, some scars, they won’t ever heal, will they? You start to run a hand over the incision just below your Adam’s Apple, in what’s become an unconscious gesture of nervousness, then look up at your friend. You see his brow furrowed with worry, and maybe it should be. You took last week’s loss hard—not because of the result, but because of what She said even before you walked the aisle. “Do you miss it, Jeff?” “What, wrestling?” You just nod. Jeff pauses for a moment, glancing at the picture of you and Register again. “Yeah, I miss it. But the it fades a little more every day. I’ve found something better to take its place. Being a father is a bigger high than stepping through those curtains.” You nod as if you have any idea what he’s talking about. “What about you Dave? Why’d you come back?” Now it’s your turn to pause, to reflect. “It was all I thought about for almost two years,” you say. “I was going to make my comeback in Rush Pro Wrestling. Did you know that? … Hell, you probably never knew RPW existed.” “I caught a few matches shows,” he says. “I’d already had my surgery and been rehabbing,” you explain. “I had an angle going with Shawn Stewart—better and more emotional than anything I did in OSW… barring anything I did with Ronin, that is. We planned on building to a match where, when Shawn won, he’d get 5 minutes alone with me. Then he’d get in one shot, take pity on me and start to leave. That’s when I was gonna kip up and put him in The Silencer.” “Sounds good,” he says. “Reminds me of the old wheelchair gimmick.” “Yeah, I was disappointed when they folded. Then again, that’s all my career has ever been. A series of disappointments. Sure, people in OSW respected me—but that was behind the scenes. On camera, all I did was help get others over.” “And got paid more for doing it.” “Which I spent on booze and pills. I don’t want to be that guy any more, Jeff.” “A drunk?” You shake your head. “A… a midcarder.” “There are worse things you could be Dave.” He hooks a thumb over his shoulder toward that picture again. “You could be a statistic. A martyr.” “At least then they’d remember me.” “Yeah, but not in the way you think.” He grabs a piece of paper and a pen off the desk and scibbles something down. He pushes the paper toward you. “You chose this life Dave. Now you have to decide how you want to live it. If you’re stuck in the mid-card you have to go out there and really blow their socks off. If that means you turn on a friend, then you do it. If that means you become the next super-patriot, you do it. Find your spot and take your shot. It’s on you.” You nod, knowing that, despite the cliches, he’s right. And also knowing that you just can’t resist… “HOOOOOOOOOOO!” you shout to no one. You both laugh and head back for the ring. But what’s that noise? “That yours?” “Nah… I had the good grace to put my phone on silent.” “Nice.” You turn back and head into the office, pushing the remnants of a Quizno’s sub wrapper out of the way to find your phone doing a happy vibrating jig underneath. “Hello?” “Yes, am I speaking with Dave Gibson?” “Yup, who’s this?” “Are you the son of Marsha Gibson?” You feel an unseen hand start to squeeze your stomach. Monday, 3:57 p.m. How long have you been awake? How long since you got out of this chair? Took a walk, got something to eat, checked your voicemail, did ANYTHING beside sit here and stare at your mother? Truthfully, you can’t remember. But you can’t get up. You have to sit here. You have to be with her. You have to watch as the nurses come in to check vital signs on some fancy digital readout. You know next to nothing about medical science, only that all the numbers seem to be decreasing bit by bit every time a nurse walks in. And you know that can’t be good. You stare at your mother’s face, hidden partially by an oxygen mask. You watch as her chest rises and falls in a series of shallow breaths. And on occasion, you listen to her moans. “Your mother had a combination of ailments strike her all at once,” the ER doctor said after an agonizing four-hour stint in the waiting room. “It appears as if she had a rather significant stroke.” Is there such a thing as an insignificant stroke? You don’t think so. “That, combined with the emphysema from her smoking, appeared to exacerbate the existing arrhythmia in her heart.” You should have made her quit. You should have tried harder. “And, as her heart pumps with greater force, it causes her blood sugar to drop. As far as we can estimate, she’s having four to five sugar drops an hour, as far as we can tell.” That’s where the moans come from. Even though she can’t speak, can’t respond, can’t acknowledge your presence in the room… she can still react as her blood sugar drops to dangerously low levels. The first time she started, you were the only one in the room. Her body occasionally jerked, followed by a long, low, sustained moan. You leaned in closer and saw one eye nearly entirely shut—from the stroke, you figure. The other? Bulging open, but staring at nothing. Fixed at some unseen point in some other realm of being. Between the eye and the moans, you can’t figure out which terrifies you more. She hasn’t had one of those episodes in a while now, though. She appears to be resting… HA! Resting… from what the neurologist who came in told you, she’ll be doing plenty of “resting” from now on whether she wants to or not. At least when she had the sugar drops, she was responding to SOMETHING. Now, she’s just lying there. Breathing. Which either means the insulin and other treatments are doing their work… or… Or… “Mr. Gibson?” You turn to see the ER doctor walking in. You see the worry on his face, and do a little math in your head. How many hours have you been here? How many DAYS? But, you know there’s only one question to ask. The same one you’ve been avoiding until now. “How long does she have, Doc?” “She’s not responding to the medicine as we hoped. She has so many ailments… and it appears as if they’ve finally caught up to her. I’m sorry, but you need to prepare yourself for the fact that you’re going to lose your mother.” “I lost Mom a long time ago…” “The Alzheimer’s.” The doctor nods. “Yes. Well, all the same, there’s nothing more you can do here.” “I’m not leaving her. I know she can’t respond or recognize me, but I’ve gotten used to that in the past few months, believe me. I’m staying… until the end.” “Of course, sir. But… it appears she has stabilized for now. All we can do is wait. Perhaps you could find some solace elsewhere. We DO have a chapel service here…” Nodding meekly, you leave the room. You don’t want to, but you do any way. After all, he’s a doctor. He deals with death every day. Shouldn’t he know the best way to prepare for it? Except you don’t know HOW to prepare. Even though her lungs are raped by smoke and her mind’s been turned to mush… she’s still your Mom. And she’s all you have. But there was something else you had, wasn’t there? Yes, that’s right… There was THE BOTTLE. Present Day You’ve lingered for two more days, waiting for your mother to die. After the doctor’s final judgment, you lingered for another hour before going home, having a shower, picking at a tuna fish sandwich… And then heading for your travel bag. At first, you thought you’d lost it. You rummaged through it, tossing mini toothpastes and balled-up socks hither and yon. And then, there it was. You’d held onto it for the past several weeks. Since your run-in with Nova. Just to prove that you could do it. A change of clothes, and back to the hospital you went—shuffling aimlessly from her room to the hall to the cafeteria… and always, for whatever reason, ending up back here at the chapel. Not much, really. If this is the House of God, you’d hate to see the Shack. A few benches, some Bibles, a lectern at the front that you doubted people used too often. And there, at the back, you leaned forward, clasping your own Personal Demon between your hands in the Laundry Room of the Lord. Oral Demerol is one of the most addictive substances there is. Cures what ails ya, as Mom would say. You give Nova credit. He has good taste in painkillers. Part of you wonders if he’s missed the bottle yet. Part of you knows it’s time to toss them. The rest of you is listening to the demon. And He’s getting louder. Let me out! Let me out! STOP BEING SUCH A FAGGOT, FUCKING TWIST OFF THAT LID AND LET ME OUUUUUUT! Tears are streaming down your face. You’re losing it. Your mind, your mother, your will to stay sober, everything. Does a match matter at this point? Does a career? Does anything? You duck your head when you hear the doorknob click as someone else enters the room. An old black man teeters in, swaying unsteadily as he grips a cane. Bone thin, he hobbles forward to the lectern. With exaggerated yet very determined movements, he sets the cane against the front pew and slowly bends to his knees. Watching from behind, you can almost hear his grimace as he bends—as evident as the pop from his joints as he sinks into the position of prayer. I can help him. I can help him and you. Just let me out, Dave. Please? It’d be like old times. The old man starts to speak out loud with a surprisingly resonant voice. “Our father, who art in heaven, hallowed be thy name…” You know you want to, it’s been so long, and it’s SOOO easy to open… “Thy kingdom come, thy will be done, on earth as it is in Heaven.” Why won’t you listen to reason? I can sense your pain, it’s worse than anything from your neck, let me ease your suffering… “Give us this day our daily bread. And forgive us our trespasses, as we forgive those who trespass against us.” How can you deal with this on your own? With being alone, with keeping your business afloat, with stepping in that ring night after night? You can’t beat Brandon Youngblood without me. You can’t even beat Los Fucking Diablos without me. Or land a girl like Amy Campbell. Or survive without your mommy. So do what we’ve both been waiting for and let me out! NOW! “And lead us not into temptation, But deliver us from evil.” You get up. You know the rest. Hands in your pockets, you head for the elevator and ride to your mother’s floor. You stroll past the duty station like you’ve been here a hundred times, step into the room and shut the door. You pause for a second, lingering over your mother’s form, watching her twitch, hearing her moan. Another sugar drop. They’ll be in soon to check her. You step into the bathroom, shutting the door behind you, and stare into the mirror. What do you see there? Is it the man who scrapped his way to stardom? Or the man who pissed it all away? Is it the man who rediscovered his passion for the business, who now teaches others in an attempt to perpetuate the industry he loves so dearly? Or a man too broken by personal and professional disappointment to go on alone? For thine is the kingdom, and the power, and the glory, for ever and ever. Amen. You twist off the top.
