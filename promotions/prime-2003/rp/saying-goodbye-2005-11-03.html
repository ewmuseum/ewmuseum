Saying Goodbye

3 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/11/05 “People live their lives through numbers every single day. The clock strikes a certain time to tell them to get up, their bank account tells them how well they can live and their age tells them how long they have left to make an impression on the world. Every wrestler in PRIME judges their career on numbers, the win and loss record, that’s all they think about, not the love of the business or what it means to be who we are. I’ve lost two matches and won none, to many that’s a disaster. So whilst the boys and girls in the back count their statistics and worry over whether they’ve won or lost, some of us realise that there are far bigger battles going on.” Paul Cain: on Winning and Losing. *************************************************************************************************************** Everything seemed so long ago, the battle against K-Wolf, the war with Olsig and the personal feud with his own mother as well as his former girlfriend. The immediate past had been covered in a dark and murky haze that he had not seen coming. When he had left The Staples Centre all he could think of was drinking the night away and making sure that people paid for their mistakes as swiftly as possible. Damien Kahn had talked to him about resolving the Blackwolf project and the dangers of wielding a bladed object in the face of those who can fire you without hesitation. There had been so much discussion and so many plans being made for the future. Then the phone rang and Paul Cain became enveloped in that choking haze. He didn’t hear Damien’s voice again afterwards and from that point on, PRIME meant little to nothing. There was no more Olsig, no more Blackwolf and no more Imperium. All that he could hear were those words reverberating in his head and all he could feel was cold hands on Death resting themselves down on his shoulders. That overly polite voice he had heard before had this time sent the most sickening of chills shooting through his body. “Mr Cain? This is Nurse Wilson, I’m sorry to disturb you but it’s about your grandmother, Doris. She is seriously ill and we think you should be here.” The phone had crashed to the floor and even Damien, a normally unflappable man had glanced up from his drink. Paul turned slowly to him and he saw Kahn’s lips moving but he couldn’t hear a single word coming from them. His eyes were glassy like a boxer about to fall and maybe the life and spirit of Paul Cain was indeed about to come crashing down. Commiserations Olsig and Imperium, looks like you didn’t get the final blow in after all. ******************************************************************************************************************************************** That was hours ago but it felt like time had stood still. Damien Kahn had been ordered to stay away and now Paul sat alone in the hospital waiting room with his fingers wrapped tightly in his hair. He didn’t feel the physical pain of Olsig’s best efforts because the emotional turmoil he was in now was enough to see that forgotten. Occasionally he would glance up from gazing at his shoes vacantly and then notice the doctors and nurses rushing about their business but most of the time all that existed was his thoughts. His foot tapped repetitively against the vinyl flooring with anxiety and tension as he fought to control his emotions. Two nurses laughed with each other as they recounted tales of last nights exploits and he felt the rage well up inside. Why weren’t they helping him? Why wasn’t anyone helping him? “Mr Cain? Paul Cain?” The doctors’ arrival brought a temporary moment of comfort as he stood in front of Cain like a knight in shining armour. This was the man that was going to make everything alright and save his grandmothers life. After all, that was what they were paid to do wasn’t it, to save lives right? He couldn’t contain the relief that this man brought with him and Cain felt himself throw a weak but heartfelt smile in the doctors’ direction. All he could think of was that this would be good news and that they had cured his grandmother, that she would be fighting fit and there to spur him on to the next level of life. Then the first words from the doctor reminded Paul Cain that life doesn’t work like that, miracles don’t exist and hope can be snuffed out like a candle’s weak flame. “Paul…I think you should sit down…” Like a robot, Cain obeyed the man’s orders and slowly guided himself back down onto his seat. From the moment you are told to sit down, that is the moment you know that God isn’t going to come and save his child that is suffering. He swallowed hard in an attempt to push his emotions down as far as he could. He was Paul Cain, professional wrestler and a man’s man; he couldn’t be seen to be showing signs of weakness. However sometimes, some things are just too overpowering and sometimes your emotions are an opponent that can’t be beaten. The sick and twisted feeling that clenched his stomach worked its way through his body causing his eyes to shine with the water of his tears. “We have been working around the clock to help your grandmother, Paul. She is a very strong woman and she has been battling hard against this but she has grown tired, her body is weary. Our first priority was to give her the medication she needed to alleviate the infection but she doesn’t have the strength to fight anymore…” Paul shot up out of his chair again, his eyes wide in both shock and disbelief. For a moment he stared vacantly into space unable to fully comprehend what had been said to him. It wasn’t possible that the strongest woman he had never known had just given up like that. She had been through so much and had been there for him when others hadn’t, to him she had held the world on her shoulders but never once given in. Now some doctor was telling him that she was throwing in the towel and giving up on life. She would never do that, not while she had an ounce of strength in her body. “No…You don’t know her…She WOULDN’T’…Give up…[his breathing became heavier as his anger built up]….You’ve got it WRONG...That’s NOT her…You DON’T FUCKING…KNOW HER! The doctor rose to his feet and approached Cain all too familiar with the anger of relatives facing their greatest fears. He reached out a hand and touched it to the arm of Paul who swatted it away viciously. The commotion alerted the nearby nurses who stopped in their tracks and watched the reality of how death affects the living. Cain’s reaction was not uncommon and the doctor had seen it all before, he would not be fazed by this. “I know how hard this is for you Paul, believe me I do. However your grandmother is suffering and she cannot go on fighting this. You have to be prepared to let her go, because there is really nothing else we can do to help her. I am so sorry, I truly am.” The tears poured from Paul’s eyes freely and he felt his body shaking uncontrollable as the emotion gripped him entirely. He flew around and grabbed at the collars of the doctor before throwing him against the wall like a rag doll. His face was merely a sliver of air away from the man who was condemning his grandmother to death. The doctor had been through this before but that did not make it any less disconcerting and his eyes showed their own brand of fear yet it didn’t grip him in the way that it had a hold on Paul. “YOU PEOPLE THINK YOU’RE FUCKING GOD DON’T YA?! WHO THE HELL ARE YA TO SAY SHE WON’T SURVIVE?! YA DON’T KNOW HER! YA DON’T KNOW WHAT SHE HAS FUCKING SURVIVED! GO PLAY GOD WITH SOMEONE ELSE BECAUSE SHE DOESN’T FUCKING DESERVE TO BE WRITTEN OFF! I WON’T LET YOU WRITE MY GRANDMOTHER OFF YOU FUCKING BASTARD!” Now the nurses came rushing over concerned that shortly they would have to be caring for one of their own. Their expressions were anxious ones as they witnessed Cain screaming into the face of their colleague as his hands tightened their grip. Rage against someone who has done you wrong is one thing, but this rage, a rage born out of helplessness, premature grief and frustration was a whole different beast. Unable to watch this anymore, the nurses called out to the doctor. “We’ll get security, doctor!” He looked into the grief stricken eyes of Paul Cain and saw the pain immediately. Quickly the doctor waved the nurses off and immediately returned his attention to the man before him. He had to get break through past this rage so that this relative wouldn’t join the long list of others he had seen with regret that would last an eternity. “I KNOW how you feel, Paul…I do…But…This isn’t the time…She is still hanging on…GO and SEE her, say your goodbyes…Don’t waste the time you have left on anger and hatred…You won’t get this time back…” Cain took his eyes off the doctor and looked to the right taking in his words slowly. He remembered everything that she had ever done for him, the times she had fought his corner and given him the family life his parents couldn’t manage to. Memories of when he first moved in with her and how good it felt to come home and someone would be there. He recalled that first night sleeping in her home and the safety he felt as he closed his eyes. The sands of time were running down, he didn’t have long left to pay her this final show of respect and gratitude. ******************************************************************************************************************************************** Maybe it was the time of year but the room felt incredibly cold. Or perhaps that was simply the feeling he had as he sat with his grandmother. Her eyes were closed as the respirator breathed for her and machines around the room bleeped showing the only true sign that she was still alive. He squeezed her frail and lifeless hand tightly in an attempt to show her he was there with her. There was no response and he knew there wouldn’t be but he was simply hoping against hope. This was not the woman that he remembered; she looked so different verging on ghostly. Her strength was gone and he could no longer recall her booming happy voice that would light up the room she would be in. There he was, a man who spent his life fighting other men and displaying strength that many could only dream of. Yet now here he sat powerless to do anything to stop what was inevitable and to save the woman that had saved him. He looked pleadingly at her in the hopes that maybe she would feel his presence and know there was something to fight for. He had sat talking to her over and over again, telling her his latest personal and professional goings on yet there was no response. No matter what he said or what he did, there was nothing he could do to bring her back from clutches of darkness. He felt himself tiring mentally and emotionally however he was determined not to walk away from this battle for life. Leaning forward, Paul stroked his hand across her forehead and kissed it gently before sitting back down once again. Finally, the weight of his looming grief became too much as he broke down in tears. Then a voice called to him softly and weakly, one that he had never thought he would hear again. “Paul…?” He didn’t hear it at first and she had to repeat his name to break through his emotions. “Paul…” Cain lowered his hands from his face and blinked away the tears as he turned to look in her direction. Her eyes were open but she looked tired as she struggled to smile at him. His heart leapt for joy and he suddenly felt a rush of happiness shoot through him as he saw this sign of life. Climbing to his feet and kicking back the chair, he moved to her side with a big beaming smile for her and tears of pleasure rather than pain. “I KNEW they were wrong about you, Gran! I just KNEW they were!” The eagerness in his voice was like a child such is the level that emotions take us to. One hand rested on her forehead whilst the other lay upon on her shoulder as he stood by the top of the bed. Despite what the experts said, he had kept hope and now someone, somewhere had repaid the faith he had held in her. She opened her mouth slowly and spoke the softest of words in his direction. “I’m…going to be fine, Paul…Don’t you worry about me anymore…Everything is going to be alright now…I promise…” He nodded his head eagerly as he felt his relief rising even further with every word she uttered. “And you know what, I’ll take a leave of absence from PRIME…I will! We’ll go away somewhere and get you out in the sun! Would you like that…? After all, I think you’ll need a break away and fresh air into your lungs…These places are horrible stuffy prisons aren’t they? Where would you like to go, Gran..? Anywhere you want, I can afford it, we’ll go to the Caribbean…What do you think…?” Paul had already got in mind places where they could go. He knew of a beautiful place called Allanha Villa in Holetown, Barbados which would be an ideal location for rest and relaxation. In his head, he raced forward imagining pampering his grandmother and giving back to her what she gave to him. He could hardly contain the happiness and he was glowing as the smile stayed fixed on his face. “Mr Cain…?” He turned around to hear the voice of the nurse he had met once before at the home. She walked slowly over to him and touched him gently on the back. Her smile was sympathetic which he couldn’t understand but then her words chillingly brought him to reality. “We have to go Paul…There’s nothing more that we can do here…” Suddenly the chills returned as Cain turned to his grandmother whose eyes were closed tightly and her mouth was open. He frowned at this sight that he couldn’t take in before turning back to Nurse Wilson. “Wait…Wait a minute…The machines…They were bleeping…She just…She…She just talked to me…I heard her! She said she was going to be fine! She said…She told me not to worry about her anymore! She was alive! SHE WAS ALIVE!” His voice grew to a distraught yell as he wheeled around to his grandmother and the tears returned as he pleaded for her to wake up. Nurse Wilson bowed her head for a moment allowing him this moment until she edged forward and took hold of his shoulders tightly. “She’s gone, Paul…She’s been gone for an hour now…” His scream was like that of a wounded animal and all that the nurse could do, was watch. So do tell us Imperium, where is your God now? Fin.
