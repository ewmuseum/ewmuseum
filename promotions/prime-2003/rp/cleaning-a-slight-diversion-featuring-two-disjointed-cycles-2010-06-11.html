Cleaning: A Slight Diversion Featuring Two Disjointed Cycles, The Carnival Hallucinations of Their Alignment, and The Revelation of All The Empty Space Behind Coincidental Refractions of Light upon Dust and Other Such Materials with Common Properties

11 June 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/11/10 Scene One *********** I was beside myself in what I perceived to be hell, and I was someone else disguised as myself, and there I really was or someone disguised as me. I couldn't tell. The signs were all there, but they acted as distractions, obstacles to confirmation. I could tell that it was smoky and tinted red, and that's what hell was supposed to be like. And the devil was there. I didn't see him, but I heard him laughing his slow motion laugh every now and again. They sounded like echoes, though, bouncing off cavernous walls so much that they were only echoes. At first, it had started because I thought about the possibility of a tiny fissure in one of my cortices, and the more I thought of it, the larger it grew, first into a crack, but then a hatching burst of color and light, the particles of thoughts. How clear it had seemed then, with all that light and color bursting from my head--and how certain I was of it--to have ended up there, apart from me, in that, the most desolate of lobbies. Reconstituted, there wasn't any way to make sense of it, because it wasn't like how it was in books with lakes of fire. It wasn't anything, really, but a memory of how I'd guessed it would be pressing me down into what it was, which was a blur of absolute black just rushing past, and on into the wind I pushed towards the pinhole, faintly straining a ray from all the light in the world on the other side. Mathematically, it was possible to split the distribution of the body's molecules in even parts and rearrange those parts two form two identical instances of oneself, each instance half the size of the whole and each part half the size of the whole's part, so that oneself becomes two, and so it was. I spoke my thoughts from one's mind to the other one's mouth, and I spoke my thoughts from one's mind to the other one's mouth, too, so that I was talking to myself, but really it was two of me, each talking to himself simultaneously, as confusing as that may seem to a casual observer. "What the hell am I doing over there?" I tapped at the air between us, but it wasn't air as air is air. The air was numb was the best way to put it. "I asked first." He was a formidable foe, was I, and a standoff ensued. I figured I'd crack first and make up some excuse about how heaven and hell are all on the inside, and when you die, your spirit turns inside-out, but at that point, I was already telling myself something completely different. "How the fuck do we get out of here?" I started feeling around, trying to make a shape of the situation. Dark as it was, a light shone out in front of me, and I could see it on the other side of the covering. I had no idea how to answer my question either, and for a while, both of me stood there feeling around, the other one closer to the light than me. "It's the other way, you fool." I tried to walk towards it, but I got in my own way. "Turn!" I found it strange that part of me wanted to stay there, in hell, but I wasn't one to argue with my own motives, as oftentimes testing as they were to try and riddle out. As I fell forward towards myself, I watched the environment shatter to bits and rain into sounds. The light dissolved. It was a false light after all. Scene Two *********** The knock was practiced, and I could hear the callouses on her knuckles as they struck the door. They were like piano hammers. I was too dumbfounded and curious to answer. As it were, for the first few moments of consciousness, I didn't recall having ever been alive before, and thought that perhaps I was being born, as foreign a presumption as it would've been had my assessment been accurate. There was little else to presume, though. Perhaps it was death, a preoccupation of man so infested in himself that he considers no other alternatives. The maid made her way in slowly, at first, calling out in some jarring accent, and I groaned and felt my head, which felt dry as if it had been wet--a warm, blood wet--and indeed, I tasted the tinted spot that had flaked onto my fingers and felt the aftertaste of nickel slick down my throat. "Dios mio!" She clutched the stack of fresh towels. "Mister...you need I call ambulance?" "No, I think I'll be alright." I laid there in place, and I let her do her work around me, sweeping the years of luck from around my spot. She played it all as professionally as she knew how, so I was sure to be polite about it, so I willed myself to act no differently than a rug would act. Though she wasn't very thorough, she worked quickly, and time felt like it was of the essence, somehow, like every moment as a rug was a moment wasted. However, it wasn't until she left and I stood, that I realized how pleasant it had been to be a rug, ignorant to the overwhelming responsibilities coupled with existence. Every movement was a struggle, and the further I went, the less of an idea I had where I was going. I made it to the chair in front of the room, where I had left my shoes. The curtain was nearly translucent, but its patterns imprisoned the sunshine. I held the tongue out and slid in my foot, wondering where a rug would go if a rug could get up and walk away, certainly not there, to be still somewhere else; why it was still there and could have just as well been in a theater or a banquet hall foyer or a photograph of a distillery--that is to say, in a still still, still still. "The fuck?" I withdrew my foot and overturned the shoe as the dusty grains poured out onto the floor, where a pool of sand had already founded itself into the carpeting. I had completely forgotten where I was going, but I figured it didn't really matter much so long as I went; that it'd come to me somewhere along the way. But it didn't. Scene Three ************* As I unlatched myself from the darkness, the light had grown, and where it once seemed, from a distance, that it was a constant stream of light, it was an army of flashing lights instead. The whole thing created a dizzying experience, and I swayed back and forth, leaving trails, and there before me ran a concrete path with flashing faces on bodies roped off at the sides. This was it, I thought, to be paraded around the cosmos, bound as spectacle; to be mired by the laughing heads and their shutters, flapping open and shut at me. I stumbled, and an arm caught me and dragged me reluctantly forward, past the gathering buzzards, and I was calling them so because they were buzzing, and towards the gate of the complex. "I don't want to be here!" My protests went ignored. There were four or five of them, carbon copies of each other, pulling me apart together. None seemed all too swayed, or perhaps none had heard me. "I said, 'I don't want to be here!'" I had been lured there by the light, drawn like a fly. It was all very phototropic; phototheistic, even. There was a light there, and it was a false light, and I found myself sliding down and slipping through. I heard a door shut behind me, and it became dim, but not dark. "Do you know who I am?" I nodded at it; that featureless it which could neither see nor hear me. "Good." It snarled. "Then I shall make this simple." "What the fuck are you talking about?" "I'm here to make you a deal. You can wipe your slate clean, proverbially speaking, of course." "What's in it for you?" "I need someone to do some dirty work, someone good. And you're good." "You want to be a little more specific?" "I don't think that's required. By the time you fulfill your end of the deal, you won't remember what I asked you to do." "Then there's no harm in telling me something I won't remember anyway, is there?" "I suppose not." He took a draught from his chalice and rested his hand on hairy knuckles. "But why risk it?" Scene Four ************** The words echoed off, as if from a dream, as I laid there staring up at the ceiling. They sounded distant and foreign, and I wouldn't have been surprised if I had never heard them put together like that. "Just lose, Johnny." I didn't recognize the voice, the one nestled like a thorn, burrowing its way through the fissure. "Lose 'em all." I pulled out from the lot, and the automobile clanked its way out onto the road. An old man watched as I merged back into the obscurity of traffic. He wore a long coat and a plain hat, and though I had never seen him before, it looked like he knew who I was and what I was about to do. I saw the disapproval in his eyes, behind heavy frames. "Lose 'em all, and you can walk away a free man." There was a leak in the roof where sand sifted through, filling the interior up past my ankles. For no man is completely free once he has a name, and I knew what mine was, and worse yet, what more it meant. I rolled along at a constant rate, the sand up to my knees and my foot stuck in place on the gas pedal. "Don't fight it, Johnny. The less you fight it, the easier it will be, and the sooner it will all be over." The sand poured down from a hole in the sky, and try as I may to relax, I found it difficult to purge myself of the desire to struggle. Soon, I was buried deep enough that I could no longer turn the wheel. Out over the dashboard, the sun hung lynched in the sky, and the world spun beneath me that it was always there, swaying like a dim bulb on a loose wire. Before too long, I was completely submerged, and as time went by, I figured the sand kept piling on up above me, above everything, until sometime later, it all shifted, and it all turned over, and in a nauseous rush, the world spat me out, propelling me through the sand and dumping me back where I had started. Scene Five *************** The gash in my forehead was still so apparent that I could fit a couple of fingers in the divot, and underneath the patchwork of scabs, a stinging sensation lingered. Had I no idea how the would had manifested, the trinkets, delivered as promised, would have been utterly worthless. I imagined I had something to show for my efforts, the ones that defied all logic. There must have been something rooted within that had compelled me to go on, some divine architect who would derive the slightest of advantages for having me toil around in the dark. There I was, clutching onto an artificial warmth, when out there somewhere, those invisible to the invisible were theoretically reaping the real rewards. I needed badly to escape from that hellish nightmare, but I clung to it like a rock, never certain of what else was out there. Each minor victory knotted me there further, and as I untangled rope and cast it out to lasso whatever pike or precipice I could ensnare. I needed to pull myself from the pile of dumped sand, from the dry hills, the dead terrain. I should have stayed there, and maybe next time I will, with my eyes closed and my shoulders pinned and my toes pointed skyward, but I failed to see the point in it--victorious or defeated, I don't know which--knowing there was a next time at all. I woke up in the desert and walked back to town.
