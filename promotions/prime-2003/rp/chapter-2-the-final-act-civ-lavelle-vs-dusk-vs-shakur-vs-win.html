Date: 2 - AUG - 2007 Manhattan, New York Thursday 5:00 p.m. Chelsea Pier The chilled evening left a hue in the car, steaming up the windows as he took deep breaths. The car sat idle as he listened to the radio and kept a weather eye on the pier, a sickly smile draped across his clean shaven cheeks. His callous fingers gripped the steering wheel, reassuring the nerves the bellowed out from inside him. His mobile phone rang; sliding one hand off the wheel he grabbed the vibrating phone with a tight and rough grip. ‘Are you in place Ronnie?’ asked the caller. Ronnie Barnes heaved a displeased breath, sitting with patience wasn’t a virtue he held in high regard. Slipping out his cigarettes and Harley Davidson platinum lighter he lit up and smiled, savouring the sweet taste and scent as it swerved around him. ‘I’m awaiting final confirmation,’ Ronnie said in a hushed tone. Blowing a steam of smoke from his mouth, he watched as it slowly trickled up to the ceiling of the car, joining the large yellow stain above. Shaking his head he reminded himself to get a cleaning service done when the campaign was over. ‘He wants you to move now. He doesn’t know how long it’ll be before the plan fails,’ the man directed. Ronnie nodded his head and wound down the window, the cool air billowed into the car. The chill shook him but he ignored it as he slipped his cigarette into the ash tray he had mounted on the dash board and gripped his silencing pistol with a pleasurable laugh. ‘I will call when it’s done,’ Barnes said. ‘Remember. No evidence. If you mess this-’ Barnes shook his head, and said, ‘I know the drill.’ Hanging up the phone, Barnes headed toward the pier gate with a slow and careful pace, keeping his eyes on the road around him and the small office at the gate. The television glow still flickered and the sound of laughter erupted into the eerily quiet pier. Somebody’s home … He thought and clicked off the safety. ~ Marcus Tyler remained in the comfort of the small office, sipping on a hot chocolate – cheap in its taste. He enjoyed being in the familiar surroundings of the pier. The sound of the crashing waves against the pier calmed him, but it held a foreboding memory of the young woman. He felt saddened at his inability to help the police with further details, he knew little of what happened or why, he just remembered slipping off the steps and landing in front of his crew member and a friendly fisherman. Seated comfortably he noticed a shadow in one of the monitors from the corner of his eye and turned to see the monitor, there was nothing there. It’s your nerves, old man. Relax. Marcus calmed himself, continuing to sip the coffee. As he began to relax once more he saw the shadow again, this time in camera three. Sighing he picked up his two-way radio and opened the network. “Chris, you messing about at the pier?” “…” “Chris, you there?” Releasing the button he received nothing but static. Chris Schomburg always responded. He was diligent and shy, but he kept his radio on his person at all times. Relax. It’s probably the lights on the pier shimmering in the wind. Marcus reassured himself but inside he felt an uneasiness he couldn’t explain. He rose onto his wobbly knees and grabbed his torch and engineer belt. Switching off the television he attempted contact with Chris once more, but again no answer. Checking the monitors he saw what looked like a foot in camera six. Sweet Jesus … Marcus thought as he flung the door open and felt his body being pushed backwards. A burning sensation erupted in his chest. He felt himself slipping. Colliding with the small chair and monitors he fell to the floor and fumbled with his coat until he felt two hot holes in the coat. Heaving a wheezing cough he felt the warm metallic taste of his own blood as it bubbled up his throat. Footsteps approached. Looking up with his fading green eyes he saw the ghostly face looking down at him. He shivered as a chilling cold swept through him.
