Dawn of a Dead Day

16 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 16th October Four knuckles smashed into Jason’s sandpaper rough jaw, propelling his bulky frame through the door with all the grace of an African elephant. A sharp burst of autumn wind almost knocked him from his vertical base as he shambled out of the bar in an alcoholically enhanced haze. It was a cold, cold night in Seattle, evident by the thin layer of cracked frost lining the pavement and the bellows of steam that puffed from the PRIME wrestler’s nostrils with every exhalation. Dazed and confused, Jason grabbed hold of a parking meter for balance. Within seconds the cold had fused his skin to the metal, so he suddenly pulled his hand away leaving a couple of layers of flaked skin on the metallic coin collector. Smarting, Jason glanced at his outstretched hand as pinprick sized beads of blood began to form on the palm. “Shi-“ he began, but before he could utter a single full word a seemingly gargantuan pair of hands clasped him by the shoulder, forcefully shoving him forward. Jason let out a whelp as his back was rammed into the side of the sports utility vehicle behind him, but that was nothing compared to the pain he felt as his aggressor threw another punch, catching Jason just under the ribs. This was something that Jason hadn’t experienced in a long time; a good old-fashioned bar-room beating. It looked like the guy stood at least 6’5” and he was built like a silverback. Not only that but he swung like a pro, as if he’d had some kind of formal fighting training. If Jason’s mind hadn’t been drowned under several litres of alcohol he might’ve been able to give this guy a fight. Hopeless, Jason Natas slumped forward onto the curb, the cold frost on the pavement stinging his wounded hand. Breathing heavily, wheezing, he pulled his palm away from the curb instinctively and scuttled sideways, much like a crab. “Fuckin’ pussy-ass wrestler,” the man huffed in a gruff Southern drawl that defied the Washington location. Jason was at a loss to explain to himself what was happening. He’d been a bare-knuckle boxer, a street-fighter for crying out loud, and here he was having his ass handed to him by some blue collar Joe Lunchbox in the city he’d relocated to in order to work at Sonny Silver’s gym. And it’s not like the place he’d been drinking in was a dive either. No, place was nice. A good selection of fine wines and imported ales, a tasteful if somewhat limited tapas menu and a trendy art deco interior. It all seemed far too classy a place to brew up the kind of shitstorm he found engulfing him. He didn’t have long to ponder his predicament. The brown-haired bastard stepped forward, pulled back his right leg and threw it into Jason’s gut. Almost instantaneously the pro-wrestler wretched, summoning a torrent of bile, half-digested potato chips and the single malt whiskey he’d been sipping on a few minutes prior. The disgusting concoction splattered against the pavement as Natas fell backwards, sitting upright against the side of the car. “Well shit, Nancyboy, big ‘tough’ wrestler like you, figured you’d be puttin’ up more of a fight,” huffed the t-shirt-clad bruiser. “Fightin’ ain’t much fun when yer the only one puttin’ in an effort.” “Thhh-at all you got?,” Jason mumbled as a warm, rusty tasty began to fill his mouth. He pushed his tongue against his right incisor with almost enough pressure to snap the loosened tooth from its root. “Hmm. Have it your way.” Joe Lunchbox lunged forward, driving his knuckles into Jason’s forehead, sandwiching his head between the car and his fist in the process. As Jason’s eyes rolled back in his skull, his aggressor took a step back. Eyeing up the bloodied mess of a professional wrestler that sat slumped before him, admiring his handiwork. He stood there for a great deal longer than he needed to, his ominous presence lingering like the angel of death. Joe Lunchbox soon bored, however, and wiped his grazed knuckles against his dusty jeans. “I were you,” he began, “I’d be thinkin’ twice before showin’ up here again.” The man was perspiring lightly despite the bitingly cold temperature. Taking a moment to adjust the one stand of hair that had fallen across his forehead, Joe Lunchbox soon turned his giant frame back towards the bar with all the precision of an aircraft carrier. He’d soon walked back inside the bar, Ent-like in his lumbering, leaving Jason out in the cold. Natas blinked, his eyelids heavy with exhaustion. For a moment he considered heading back into the bar and teaching Joe Lunchbox a thing or two. Maybe he could catch him from behind, drive his penknife through the back of his windpipe, pulled forward… No. That was a stupid idea. Jason dismissed it as soon as he tried to haul himself to his feet, his legs quivering under his two hundred and fifty pounds like a newborn foal. His head spinning, Jason fell back against the SUV as he tried to get his bearings. Easier said than done when you’ve got a stomach full of booze and a head covered with bruises. It took him at least half a minute before he could “walk” on his own accord. He set off down the pavement slowly and awkwardly, barely able to maintain a straight line. Fortunately for him he didn’t have to travel far to his rental car, and he fumbled around blindly in his pocket as he approached the white Hyundai. One arm on the roof of the car for balance, Jason finally located the keys and slid them in, unlocking the door. It goes without saying that he was in no fit state whatsoever to drive, but that didn’t seem to stop in. He winced as he dropped his beaten body down into the driver’s seat and hit the ignition, before closing the door and pulling away without buckling up. The streets were quiet, but that didn’t make his precarious journey any easier. He was driving blind, without his headlights, without a seatbelt, and in the midst of heavy intoxication. So naturally, it didn’t take long for things to go wrong. Jason barely saw the car coming towards him, and even the overwhelmingly loud screech of the Chrysler’s horn wasn’t enough to sober him up. He veered over to the wrong side of the road, colliding with the hapless family head on. He couldn’t have been doing more than thirty but it was enough to send his body flying from the seat. Headfirst he sailed through the windshield, tumbling over the hood. Eventually he came to rest amongst road salt and shards of shattered glass. Face down in the cold. Lifeless. Jason Natas drifted away. * * * * * ”Uhhhhhh.” Jason allowed the soft groan to escape his dry, cracked lips. He lay face-first, breathing heavily from his mouth as he allowed his sense to drift back to him. Touch came first, and he rolled over onto his back as soon as he felt the cool asphalt against the side of his face. Next, hearing. It was muffled at first, as if he was underwater, but soon enough the faint whir of an afternoon’s breeze sifting through the city centre filtered through his earlobes. Taste. His mouth dry and barren, complimenting a throat that was rougher than a camel’s backside. Then vision. It came to him slower than the rest and as he lay there all he could see was a pale gray mass directly above him, flanked at either side by massive, grim oblongs shooting skywards. He couldn’t figure out of his sense of small hadn’t came back to him or there just wasn’t anything around giving off a strong enough scent for his nostrils to pick up. Either way, it wasn’t exactly top of his priority list. Overcome with confusion, Jason sat upright, balled his hands into fists and rubbed his eyes. As his vision slowly returned to him he found himself in a familiar location. Downtown Seattle, a place he’d become more and more accustomed to since his arrival in the west coast city. But something wasn’t right. No, this wasn’t the downtown hustle and bustle he was used to. There were no pedestrians, no activity behind the shop windows. No cars, no buses, no cabs. He was alone, there in the centre of the road. Slowly he heaved himself to his feet, immediately recoiling as an abrupt barb of pain shot upwards, penetrating his cranium. Raising rough, calloused fingers to his scalp, Jason shook his head slowly with the deluded notion of the relieving the pain. Predictably, it didn’t work. He returned his gaze to the seemingly deserted city, the bleak, grey sky only compounding the overwhelming sense of isolation. “What the hell? Am I…” “Dead? No. But you might as well be.” The ghost, almost ethereal voice called from behind him. Innately, Jason spun his body round towards the source, almost jumping out of his skin as soon as he realised how close the figure was to him. “Fuck!” he barked, panicky. “What did you expect? Mary Poppins?” Taking a step backwards, Jason watched as the figure pulled a damp cigarette butt from between his lips and crushed it under a fine, Italian leather loafer. Clad in a finely-tailored pinstripe suit with a penguin jacket, white gloves and a top hat, the figure held a small black cane in his right hand. The most startling thing about this man was not his fine choice of attire, however. No, it was the reams of white bandages wrapped around his skull, completely obscuring his face save for a small mouth slit. “Take it easy, tough guy,” oozed the ghostly figure as Jason raised his dukes. “Fuck off. Tell me who you fuckin’ are b’fore I go about knockin’ the shit outta ya.” A soft, almost patronising laugh came from beneath the layers of bandages as the tall, gaunt figure shook his head slowly. “What, like you did to that guy at the bar?” Jason sneered in response. “All right, fine. Consider me your Guide, Jason.” Jason furrowed his brows, confused. “Guide? Fuck do I need a guide for?” “There are some things you need to see, Jason. Come.” The guide spoke softly but authoritatively, completely ignoring Jason’s question. “Oh yeah? What if I don’t want to see ‘em?” Behind the bandages, The Guide rolled his eyes. “You haven’t got a choice in the matter, I’m afraid.” * * * * * “Jesus Christ!? What the hell happened to this guy?” The young male nurse choked at the sight of Jason Natas’ limp, lifeless form as it was wheeled hastily through the Accidents & Emergencies entrance of the hospital. His clothes down and splattered with deep crimson, the professional wrestler was in a terrible shape. His face was peppered with cuts and bruises, a couple of small chunks of windshield glass clearly visible on his scalp. “Car wreck on Beacon Avenue,” retorted a jumpsuit-clad paramedic. “Retard didn’t have his belt on, went right through the ‘shield.” “Hey wait, I think I recognise him,” another nurse chimed in, her short legs struggling to keep up with the pace at which the paramedics powered through the hospital. “That’s that wre-“ “Nevermind who it fucking is!” barked one of the paramedics. “This man needs help, NOW!” The nurse bit her lip, preventing herself from barking back a response, for the good of the patient. The entourage of medical staff burst through a swinging set of double doors, into a room populated only by a few men and women in short, white coats. “Doctor! Surgery. He might not make it.” * * * * * His head hung low, Jason shuffled reluctantly behind the Guide in a similar manner to a school kid being lead to the principal’s office. He didn’t want to tag along with the suit-wearing mummy, not by a long shot, but the way he spoke to him… it compelled him to follow. He didn’t have any choice in the matter. Something in that voice… “You know what put you here, don’t you?” The Guide asked lightly. He strolled slowly, elegantly, hands clasped together behind his back. “Enlighten me,” Jason growled sarcastically. “Well, I’ll be blunt, Mr. Natas; you’ve been an imbecile. You find yourself in a position that many would die to be in, and yet you continue to behave much like an unhinged lunatic.” The Guide sighed, as if he - it - were disappointed in PRIME’s Anti-Superstar. “Consider this penance for acting like a spoiled little brat.” Natas increased the pace, now walking side by side with the apparition. “Hell d’you mean?” “Oh come on, Jason, it isn’t that hard to fathom, even for a man of your intelligence.” “I swear, you say one more fuckin’ thing an I’ll-“ “You’ll what, Jason?” quizzed the eloquent, well-spoken guide. He stopped his skinny figure suddenly, glaring at Jase from behind his thick bandages. If this were any other scenario, Jason’s fist and the Guide’s face would be very well acquainted at this point, but not this time. Something in Jason’s head, compelling him to push his anger, his frustrations to the side… “You are under the employ of one of the biggest sports franchises on the planet,” began the Guide. “You earn a sizeable paycheque, you’re face is on national television on a weekly basis, and you get to do the one thing you’ve always been good at – fighting – without any legal repercussions. You’ve got it made, Jason, and you don’t even realise it.” Jason opened his mouth with the intention of responded, but again something triggered inside his head, compelling him to stop. “You claim you hate the many overblown egos lurking within in the PRIME enclave; you made it your mantra, in fact – ‘The Anti-Superstar’. And yet you, Jason, are no better than any of them. You are worse, in fact.” The Guide paused, shaking his head. “So they may preen and pose, they make act like divas and take the odd strop every now and then, but at least they do their job, Jason. When was the last time you even showed up for a show, in fact?” “None of your concern,” growled Jason, refusing to abandon his headstrong, confrontational manner. “Here, in the window.” The Guide turned towards a large shop window blurred by a mix of dirt and dust. “What do you see?” Stepping forward pensively, Jason squinted his eyes and raised his hand above his brow to get a better look. There, in the window, the lightly toned tights and the tanned skin. The shaved head, the goofy glasses. “Dawkins…” “That’s right, Bryan Dawkins,” confirmed The Guide. “A man who entered PRIME only a week before you did, who finds himself in the main event at the Great American Nightmare.” “What’s yer point? I don’t care ‘bout no stinkin’ belt…” Again The Guide chose to completely blank Jason’s concerns. “You know how he got there, Jason? By doing exactly what you haven’t done. He’s worked hard, he’s conducted himself properly, and he may very well be walking out with Chandler Tsonda’s belt on Sunday evening. Could’ve been you, Jase. Could’ve been you.” Exhaling heavily through his nostrils, Jason huffed. He turned to face The Guide. “Ask me if I give a fuck.” “Hmmm. Okay.” The Guide’s wraithlike, eerie tones were again completely devoid of all emotion and sympathy. “In that case, look back in the window.” Again Jason turned to find no sign of The Flyin’ Hawaiian’s visage. Instead his visage was replaced by somebody else. An African-American decked out in full wrestling attire, his black hair pulled back under a red bandanna. “How does Jimmy Bonafide make you feel?” “Like runnin’ a rusty cheesegrater down my face.” “You bettered this man, Jason. You beat him in the ring, at Colossus. By all accounts, you should be flying much higher than this illiterate schmuck. But now even HE enjoys more air-time than you do! Even Jimmy Bonafide has started to overtake you, Jason. I know that can’t be easy to deal with.” “For the last fuckin’ time,” snarled Jason, “I don’t care.” He turned to face The Guide. “I don’t give a fuckin’ shit about this ‘sport,’ all right? I’m done with it. Finished. Fuckin’ leave me be.” The New Yorker pivoted, using his heel as a fulcrum to turn around. He began to march away, when the unworldly voice called out once again. “That’s no mindset to be in, what with Troy Douglas-“ “Fuck. Troy. Douglas.” Jason stopped dead in his tracks, swiftly. A scowl twisted across his granite-rough facial features as the red, unpleasant heat of anger rose up through his body. “Oh. So you do care after all?” “Did I say that?” asked Jason, turning to face The Guide. “You didn’t need to, Jason. I can tell from the way you act. Now come; perhaps there is hope for you after all.” * * * * * “Ohhh boy, this isn’t going to be pretty,” the Dr. Phillips said as he quickly pulled his wait mask over his mouth. “One, two, HEAVE!” the nurses collectively called as they underwent the task of transferring Jason Natas’ bloodied body from the stretcher to the operating table. A mass of doctors swarmed around the wounded wrestler like flies to dog’s dirt, picking away at the body, removing the intricately-placed pieces of glass from his skin and clothes. Phillips pulled his scalpel from the trolley and turned to face the group. “Okay guys, we need to act quickly on this one.” He strode forward, parting two of the doctors. “We might not have a lot of time.” He watched as a young member of the team pulled the tiniest speck of glass from Jason’s mangled forehead with a pair of tweezers. “Never mind that!” Phillips almost shouted. “There are more important things to worry about than the pizza face right now.” His head suddenly shot upwards. “Carson, Walker. Get rid of what’s left of his shirt.” Two young doctors clad in identical outfits nodded simultaneously as another grabbed Jason’s cold, pale wrist, feeling for a pulse. “It’s still, there, boss.” “Good,” nodded Phillips. “Let’s save this one, people…” * * * * * ”You aren’t particularly enjoying this, are you Jason?” “Gee, Sherlock,” began the Anti-Superstar, “whatever gave you that idea?” The Guide sighed with disapproval. “Well, fear not, you shan’t be here much longer. Our business here is almost done.” “Thank God for that,” Jason said, breathing a sigh of relief. He gazed down at The Guide, who now sat cross-legged on a bench at the edge of the curb, cane resting across his thighs. “Never thought I’d ever be lookin’ forward to goin’ back to Sonny’s cesspit, but anythin’ beats this shit.” He kept up the cold front, despite his wavering belief in it. “Hmmm, well. You’d better be hoping that Dr. Phillips is up to the task tonight, otherwise the place you’re heading to might be somewhat less… pleasant, than the Silver Lining Academy…” Genuinely confused, Jason screwed up his face. “Eh?” “You thirsty, Jase? Hungry?” asked The Guide. “I know I am.” The Guide unfolded his long, thing legs and pushed upwards with the palm of his hands, returning to his vertical base. Without his hat the man would still have been a few inches taller than Jason, but with it he seemed like a giant. He drifted past Jason and clasped his gloved-hand on the door handle behind him, twisting. The door glided open gracefully, the as the scent of fresh bread crept up Jason’s nostrils. “This’ll do,” said The Guide. The ghostly figure shrugged as he slid inside the bakery, becoming for Jason to follow with a flick of an impossibly long middle finger. Jason couldn’t quite believe what was happening; here he was being led around a seemingly deserted city by a being not of this world into a seemingly operational bakery. It was the kind of stuff that kid’s nightmares were made of, and yet he was apparently living it. But something wasn’t right. He knew this wasn’t happening. How could it be? How could the city of Seattle be so barren, so empty at this time in the afternoon? How could The Guide see from behind all those bandages, and how on Earth did he know so much about Jason’s career? The thoughts were wiped from his psyche almost as soon as they’d entered. Jason followed his Guide into the bakery, trying to wrap his head around the situation. In seemingly record time The Guide had managed to procure a couple of iced buns and two steaming cups of coffee from behind the counter. He leaned against the glass unit displaying all the bakery products. “How did you…?” Jason began. “Never mind.” “Coffee?” Jason took the cardboard cup from The Guide. Tilting his head backwards, he knocked back a long mouthful of the hot, black liquid. The coffee was too small and too full of caffeine to get rid of the razorwire wrapped around his windpipe, but maybe it would help clear his head a little. “So have you thought about it?” asked The Guide. He picked a small flack of pastry from the bun and slid it through the slit in the bandages. “Thought about what?” “You know what I’m talking about.” That he did. “Aye. An’ I’m sorry, but I ain’t goin’ back. When I’m done with Troy Douglas, I’m finished.” It was in Jason’s nature to maintain the headstrong, thick-skinned approach; he didn’t want to show weakness to The Guide by giving him the satisfaction of showing him that his words had been taking effect. “You don’t honestly believe that, do you?” The Guide paused to take a sip of coffee, leaving a small trail of black fluid on the bandages around his mouth slit. “Come on, Jason. Kill the bravado. Just this once.” Natas sighed heavily, his chest heaving. The coffee wasn’t doing his headache any favours, but that didn’t matter. On-air, PRIME fans knew him as the ultra-confrontational shoot fighter, the guy who’d sooner throw his fist against your jaw than laugh at your jokes. And here he was, Jason Natas, New York native, nastiest man on the planet, completely lost for words. “Fact is, Jason, you need PRIME. You need this sport, don’t you? Without it, you’d only fall back on hard times, back into the old habits of yore. And street fighting is no way to get by; you’d only end up dead or in jail. No, Jason. We both know that, really, you want to make this work. You’re just too proud and headstrong to admit it. But that’s the only way this is going to work, you see…” “Wha…” Jason muttered. “Come on Jason. Admit it. Say it. ‘I need this’. It isn’t so hard…” “I nee-“ Poof. The Guide jumped, almost spilling his coffee as Jason Natas suddenly disappeared. Vanished into thin air. Gone. The Guide shrugged, as if what had just happened was not alien to him. “Oh well,” he said, pushing another flake of pastry into his mouth. * * * * * …beep! …beep! …beeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeeee- “We’re gonna lose him!” a nurse suddenly cackled, waving her arms frantically, genuinely concerned for the health of the man on the operating table. Dr. Phillips gritted his teeth hard. This wasn’t going to happen, no. Not on his watch, not tonight. He hadn’t lost a patient in a long, long time and he was going to do his damnedest to make sure that wasn’t going to change. The doctor pushed the defibrillator irons together, warming them up. “CLEAR!” he shouted as he planted them down upon Jason Natas’ exposed chest, rocking the wrestler’s body with a series of spasmising electrodes, jerking like a human popcorn kernel. A crowd had gathered around the outside of the operating theatre, but Phillips went out of his way to kick the door closed violently with the heel of his foot. If he was going to lose Jason Natas he didn’t want any of these people to see it. He spun his frame around the bed one more time, placed the irons down on Jason’s chest… Thump. Nothing. “Shit shit shit. Fuck.” He began to panic. Beads of sweat building up on his forehead and the palms of his hands. “CLEAR!” One more time. THUMP! Jason Natas’ body jerked violently, the group held its collective breath. … beep! The sound of six sighs filled the air as Dr. Phillips took a step back, wiping the sweat from his brow. He did not allow himself the time to become too absorbed by the situation though; “okay, we’ve got him! Keep him stable now…” One of the female doctors walked forward as the IV monitor flickered back into life. She almost lost her hair, such was her fright when Jason Natas suddenly sat bolt upright on the table, panting. “Wha-… the…?” he exhaled, eyes glazed. “Okay, easy sir,” Dr. Phillips said calmly, helping Jason to lie back down. “We’ve got you now. Thought we’d lost you for a second.” Jason breathed a sight of relief through his hurried, frantic breaths. From the corner of his eye he thought he could see a suit-clad man with a cane and a top hat lurking in the corner of the room. “Do yourself proud, Jason,” The Guide whispered in his subconscious.
