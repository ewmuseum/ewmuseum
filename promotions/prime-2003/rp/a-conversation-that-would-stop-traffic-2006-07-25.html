A Conversation That Would Stop Traffic

25 July 2006
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 25, 2006 “Would you take a bullet for me?” “What, now?” “No, Joey…just…would you die for me?” Ahh, Rhode Island. NeverNeverLand to some with the last name of [name withheld]. It's in the smallest state in the Union where the lovely, intelligent and somewhat beleaguered couple named Lindsay Troy and Joey Melton were stuck in traffic on 95 North during rush hour coming from TF Green Airport and going to the Westin hotel in Providence. Now one might be wondering "why would they be stuck in traffic? TF Green Airport is in Providence, right?" WRONG! WRONG!WRONG!WRONG! TF Green is in Warwick which is fifteen minutes SOUTH of Providence but since the airlines are ninnies and use the capital city of Providence as the "point of reference" you only THINK you're landing there. But YOU'RE NOT. “What kind of question is that?” “One I would like you to answer.” Their Xenon Blue Cadillac XLR Roadster crawled closer to the Thurburs Avenue Curve, while Joey, sitting in the passenger seat tried to fiddle with the radio dial and hoped that he could find some Rod Stewart to smooth over the potential storm that was brewing inside the near $80,000 automobile. Troy swatted his hand away and the tuner remained on 94 HJY, where "Hemorrhage" by Fuel started playing. “No. I wouldn’t. Why is your life more important than mine?” Joey demanded to know, sinking deeper into the cushy leather seat and pouting. “I didn’t say that!” “Well you implied it by wanting me to sign off on being a human shield. It’s bad enough I’m an organ donor.” “Hey, you could have said 'no' when you renewed your drivers license this year." A quick turn of her head and Troy maneuvered the car out of the high speed lane and into the lane next to it, which was somehow moving faster amidst the construction. "Just last week you were convinced the entire Red Army was out to get me and that one of your kidneys could save me from a gruesome death. I can’t believe you, Joey. Seriously.” “Oh yeah. I’m the one with the problem. Look, I’m a man. You know we don’t talk about this kind of stuff, or admit our feelings.” “You’re one of the twenty percent of men who told their girlfriends ‘I love you’ first!” “I was moved by the moment, fuck…must I always be reminded of that?” “And now, you’d just watch me die. You wouldn’t even put a hand up to deflect the shot. What will it be this week? Yakuza hitmen looking to gun down CIA Operative Adam?” “Yeah catching a bullet is akin to Patrick Ewing goal tending during his Georgetown days. Get real. No. I’m just saying, it’s not like you’re going to cure cancer or anything. I don’t see kids in Sudan being treated for athlete’s foot because of you. I’m sorry.” Lindsay slammed on the brakes as some irritant in a blue Corolla, not even paying attention to where he was going, veered into her lane. She blasted the horn while Melton prayed for God's forgiveness should he die before making it to their King-sized suite. “That’s the worst thing you’ve ever said to me, Joey. Hands down, meaner than when the flu was wracking my body last year and you said I looked like a zombie from Shaun of the Dead.” “You want a testament to our love? Okay. How can I love you when I’m dead? I’ll live each day as a shrine to our time together and the bond we shared.” “Oh please, like you’ll even do that…” “Yeah, because God Forbid I move on and live a guilt free life. Meet someone new.” Exit 22 loomed ahead, and Troy carefully slid the car two lanes over to jet off the freeway and take the "A" exit to the Westin. “Meet someone new…you’re older and statistically will die first. So let’s be clear. Chances are I’ll have opportunities.” “Right." Melton flicked his gum outside the car, watching it fall down into the gully below. "Wishing your mate dead to get into his trust fund won’t spread. Nice.” “I'm serious. Would you want me dating again? Could you stand it?” “I’ll be dead. I reason I’ll be able to make my peace with it. But…dating again. How old are you?" "I'm twenty-nine still," Troy deadpanned. "Well, what are we talking about here?" The car slowed to a stop at the light. No turn on red. "My grandfather got remarried at sixty-three and he knew the woman for a week. Technically they had dinner and car pooled to bingo and the routine stuck. I don’t know if that’s dating.” “I’m talking…still in my sexual prime. Thirties. Could you stand it?” “What’s the right answer here? 'No, Lindsay, please don’t make any flush-faced trips to a Medium with the kids and ask for my blessing'? Who cares. I’ll already be in the 5th dimension viewing a life where I was never pussy whipped or coloring the gray out of my hair.” Light change. Troy swung the car around two right corners and pulled into the semi-circular Valet drive of the hotel. Two bellboys ran down the steps to greet the two, pulling their doors open and waiting for the trunk to be popped. “You’re whipped in any dimension, baby,” came her reply to Melton's speech as the staff members pulled their luggage out of the car. Joey walked over to where she was standing and offered his arm. “Then kill me now.”
