The Things They Call Him

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: DDT Fifteen years ago, four bartenders from Tokyo raged a war against the Japanese underground. Four men in black masks, adopting the names and likenesses of the Biblical Horsemen of the Apocalypse, stood against a criminal empire of vast resources, limitless wealth, and unimaginable power. The Horsemen, as they would come to be known in Yakuza lore, targeted the twelve heads of the ruling Yakuza council. Businesses were burned to ash. Stockholders and investors were threatened, or worse, until all but the most loyal were willing to back The Dozen in even legitimate business endeavors. Friends and family were woken from their sleep by savage beatings. Some were kidnapped from their homes, tortured to the brink of death and thrown at the blooded doorstep of whatever of the twelve they were unfortunate enough to call kin. The Dozen would regularly receive packages containing the dismembered limbs of a son or daughter. On one very special occasion, Number Nine received the severed head of his wife of forty-seven years. The bloodshed was beyond comprehension. The ruling council retaliated with all the might of a vicious criminal empire. The devastating fist of the Yakuza struck back at The Horsemen with unrelenting force. Three of The Horsemen fell before sating their revenge. The Horseman of Death, however, would not be denied. The whole of the Yakuza empire could not stop him. Through overwhelming, insurmountable odds, Death himself waded waist deep in the blood of his enemies. Toiled in death and destruction, he cut his way to the heart of the Yakuza. One after the other, Death came for The Dozen, torturing and maiming each one before beating them mercilessly into a coma. Then, he disappeared. The Yakuza were forced to restructure. The story became a legend within their ranks. Years passed and the legend became a myth, a ghost story told to scare raw recruits. “So, we gonna do this thing, or you fucks just gonna stand there and waste my time?” He’s standing in the basement of a Japanese teahouse in Oahu. The teahouse is run by three cousins of a long-standing Yakuza family. The teahouse itself is a legitimate and successful business venture for the cousins. It also serves as a front for their slightly less than legitimate business ventures as well. There’s a room in the back of the teahouse where the cousins do business. There’s also a holding cell for prisoners, as well as a room reserved strictly for the “interrogation” of said prisoners. There are a few other, smaller rooms as well, though none of much consequence. All the rooms are very well guarded, and it is impossible to get to the basement without at least passing through half of them. The basement itself, or more specifically, the door at the far end of the room, is guarded by a contingent of seven young Yakuza, all of whom are caught off-guard by his sudden and unexpected entrance. He stands at the foot of the stairs, the black mask more frightening than any scowl or menacing glare he could have thrown their way. He is shirtless, exposing a scarred and bloodied torso. None of it appears to be his blood. Crimson droplets slip from the shining edge of a katana in his right hand. The chromed metal of a .45 flashes at them from his left. As if in a trance, the seven men slowly rise from their game of cards, unable to peel their eyes from his form. One of them looks to a corner in the back of the room, at least five or six meters from where they stand. Their weapons have been stacked there, tossed aside to make more room at the table. His left hand raises, the handgun blasting out three quick shots before any of them can react. The man with the wandering eyes goes down, a bullet in each knee cap and one through the right hand. “I believe I asked you a fuckin’ question, kids. It’s not nice to keep your elders waiting.” Three of them come in a rush. The other three make for the guns. He fires two more shots, taking out one the knees of one of his attackers. The second takes a sword through the shoulder and a bullet to the thigh. He pulls the sword from his victim, spinning in a three-sixty that nearly takes the third’s head. Instead the third ducks, where he takes a boot to the face as He steps forward and into the throw. The sword flies straight as an arrow, impaling one of the others through the shoulder. The blade stops after slicing a chip from his collar bone. He steps over his third victim, not even looking back to shoot him in the abdomen, and rolls forward, avoiding the first round of hastily fired shots from his two remaining opponents. He comes out of the roll to shoot the first of the two in the testicles, then rolls to the right to pop up and throw his gun at the second. The .45 cracks against the man’s forehead, providing the extra seconds needed for Him to close the distance. He snaps the man’s wrist, wrenching the hand downward and pulling the trigger on the man’s own gun, forcing him to literally shoot himself in the foot. He steps down on the wounded foot with His boot, then shoves the man backward, making sure to take the gun in the process and snapping his ankle. The man with no testicles receives a bullet to each shoulder. He bends to pull his sword from the man with the impaled shoulder, slicing him across both thighs once the weapon is free. “You’re…you’re him, aren’t you? The Horseman of Death! You’re Death!” He responds with a kick to the teeth. The door is locked, but there’s nothing quite like a bullet for dealing with such things. He kicks the door open and steps inside, an ill-looking American boy rising from a pile of hay on the ground. “Who…who are…” “I’m Luke Skywalker. I’m here to rescue you.” “Wha…” “Sorry, kid. Always wanted to say that.” He tosses the weapons to the side and removes the mask, revealing the ruggedly handsome face of… “You’re…wait…the wrestler guy?” “Name’s Rayne. Tyler Rayne. Now, Drake, if you don’t mind, we’d best be along our merry.” “But...how…” “Long story. Let’s go.” Drake Roberts was exhausted, half-starved and entirely confused as to just what the hell was going on. Might even be drugged, for all Tyler knew. Not that it mattered. Tyler took him by the hand and began to lead him out of the room. “But the guards…” “Are taken care of.” “All of them?” “Yes. All of them.” “How…” “It’s what I do, kid. It’s what I do.” Drake weaves his way around the seven moaning bodies in the basement, led up the stairs and into the secret rooms of the teahouse. Tyler quietly thanks the gods that the kid was still here. It’d been a gamble, that’s for sure. After a lot of “persuasion,” Tyler had managed to glean enough information from “reliable” sources to pin the kidnapping on the three cousins. He’d been waiting it out, trying to gather a little intel on whether or not the kid was still in Hawaii. But after the ninja attack, waiting just seemed like a bad idea. Too much shit was going on to waste any more time. So he’d taken a chance and raided the cousins’ main hub, hoping against hope that they’d be stupid enough to have left the kid there. Turns out, people can be pretty fucking stupid sometimes. Tyler coaxes Drake out of a window in the back office before following him out, squinting at the brightness of the sun. Sirens wail in the distance. “Car.” He points at a shiny silver BMW, top down and keys in the ignition. Drake stutters for a moment, but rushes to the passenger seat. Rayne hops the door and slides down into the driver’s seat, starting the car up and immediately pulling away from the teahouse. “There’s some food in the back. Should be a bottle of water or two.” Drake’s eyes widen and he turns to start digging through a brown sack of goodies. Tyler smiles, reaching down to pull a cell phone from the cup holder. Speed dial. She answers in two rings. “Get ‘er prepped. We’re comin’ in fast.” That is the extent of the conversation. “So…you are rescuing me, right?” “Yeah. I’m rescuing you.” “Cool.” “So I hear.” “Where we goin’?” “Away, kid. Gotta disappear ‘fore more Yakuza crawl out of their holes.” “How are we—” “Got a plane. Once we’re in the air, we’ll be damn near impossible to track.” “Then we just…disappear?” “Like a fart in the fucking wind.”
