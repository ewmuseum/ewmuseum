Back with the Past (match RP vs STD)

12 September 2005

Date: 9/12/05 ~IN THE PAST~ Three years after he had crawled from the depths of the Underground, and an eternity since he had fallen right back to oblivion, he somehow had managed to find himself right where he didn’t belong. His life was good, and that was everything he had no right to. For the crimes he had committed against her alone, any purity or bliss that came his way was entirely undeserved. Yet, it was with immense gratitude he accepted the serenity that had come his way by the grace of some unknown entity. It had been two years since he had last lost a battle with the addiction. Through her, he had found a reason and the will to step away from the violence, and instead… accept her embrace. There, locked away in the depths of her soul, was the cure to his disease. For two years she had kept him clean, and the warm embrace of the violence manifested between the ropes had lost its grip on his mind. It may have still called to him, but it was lost in the breeze of contentment he shared with her. Amongst the hell he had become accustomed to, a peace had broken through the storm. Matt had let the business of professional wrestling go, leaving it miles behind him in favor of marriage and a newborn baby girl. His psychology practice had been going well. Mary had found herself on the New York Times Best seller list. In a way, it was perplexing for him. How things could turn around so quickly escaped his comprehension, but to see such an improvement in everything he believed in was a welcomed mystery. Then he experienced the flip side of the coin. The thought that had never graced the edges of his brain amidst the maelstrom of his mind was whether things could fall apart as rapidly as they could come together. The answer was yes. A simple, sickening yes. Everything had been beautiful, then there was the accident… no… the grotesque example of typical human nature. In one moment, sitting on the couch in his living room on what was little more than a Thursday evening to him, three short knocks at the door let him know that nothing moved as swiftly as hell, and it had found him, born in new form, once again. He had responded without second thought. There on the other side had stood a single uniformed police officer, showcasing one of those looks, the kind that said “I’m sorry, but nothing is right anymore”. Takes a crazy fucking thing to make a man wish he were being arrested. Then Matt was given that basic thing… enough to break him and let tears form, rolling down his cheeks, falling to the floor, the last of his human remnants puddled on the landing at his feet. * * * ~Sunday, September 11th 3:55 PM~ At a delicate pace, he traveled the sterile halls of the hospital. The news from the doctor had been just as he had expected. His years in the field of psychology had given him a solid base understanding of a variety of different problems associated with Trauma and their potential affects on the brain. It was generally the case with amnesia, as the doctor had reiterated, that the symptoms could last a few days, a few weeks, a few months… or for as long as anything could possibly exist. Permanence was something he had never known, now that possibility was laid in front of him. As he reached the end of the hall, approaching the lobby, he passed the room that his old friend, and Killean’s former mentor, had been lying motionless in for weeks. There watching the television in the corner of the room sat a conscious Sephiroth. For a moment, Matt thought about just walking past, not sure he had the energy to delve into stories about old friends and classic antics in past federations. Still, there was some curiosity that consumed him, and though he didn’t know how coherent Sephiroth might be, he knew he would have to get used to incoherent conversations. “You feelin up to some company ol’ timer?” Matt stepped into the room, his presence pulling Seph’s attention away from the glow of the TV, which danced across his tired face. “God damn… there’s a guy I haven’t seen in ages.” “That’s because you just came out of a coma, Seph.” He pulled up a chair next to the side of the hospital bed, watching to avoid the mass of medical equipment that enveloped the space around Sephiroth. “You haven’t seen much of anyone in ages.” “That’s a fair point. Although I wouldn’t say I ‘just’ came out of a coma.” “How long have you been back amongst the living?” “I dunno. I think it’s at about the forty eight hour mark. What is today? Monday? According to the nurse, I came to at about five o’clock on Saturday evening.” “So how are you feelin?” As Matt asked the question, he leaned forward in the chair, refusing to allow himself to grow comfortable. It wasn’t the hospital. He had never been the type of weakened spirit who was broken by the sickness and lack of hope that most seemed to associate with hospitals. Instead, his denial of comfort came from the simple nature of his character that had seeped back into his blood over the past three days. As he leaned forward, shifting his weight in the chair, he quickly glanced at the face of his old friend, studying it only for a moment to find any semblance of life. He knew enough to know that Seph was likely doing the same, though he imagined they would come to very different conclusions. The two had been friends for years, both making a name for themselves in the business while teaming together. They had spent time teaming together, even winning tag titles in several feds, and had spent time at each other’s throats, decimating one another inside the squared circle. Each could claim that they had achieved their greatest successes teaming with one another, yet each could lay claim to the fact that they had ascended to the top of the mountain by disposing of the other. As allies and enemies, however, there had existed a bond between the two that was stronger than any blood they shed. But time was a powerful weapon, able to deal vicious blows to the most powerful of warriors, and capable of shattering the strongest bonds. And it was a great amount of time that had found its way between the two old allies, though, the full extent of its damage was yet to be discovered. Sephiroth’s face showed no signs of an answer, only the expected fatigue of an injured man, lucky to still be breathing. Still, his own was likely less impressive. “I suppose I’m feelin ok for a guy who just woke up from a rather lengthy coma. Actually, considering it’s nothing more than a colossal miracle I’m still alive, I’d say I’m feeling pretty damn good.” Sephiroth shifted slightly, using all his strength to push himself into more of a seated position. “Maybe the better question is, how are you feelin?” “What makes ya say that’s a better question, Seph?” “Cuz you’re in a hospital. It’s not usually good news when you have to make a trip to the hospital. Not exactly a hot vacation spot. And don’t even give me that ‘just came to see how an old friend was doin’ line. I may have just woken from my slumber, and I may still be somewhat disoriented, but I saw you walk past the room and do a double take, slam on the brakes when you saw I was awake. You didn’t stop in to check on me.” “Gotta admit, looks like your still pretty sharp. Haven’t lost a step, I guess.” “So, what’s brought you to this wonderful establishment.” Seph tried to force a sarcastic smile, but it came across instead more as a wincing pain. “You having some health issues.” “That’d be nice.” “You wanna explain that one to me?” “I’m fine.” As the words escaped his lips, he lowered his head, casting his gaze towards glistening, white hospital floor. “I mean, physically… I’m fine. I’m healthy. It’s not me I was here about.” “Mary…” “Yeah, Mary.” “What happened? Is she ok?” “C’mon, Seph, you just said so yourself. You don’t come to a hospital because its fun. You don’t swing by because everything is alright.” His words echoed with some sort of resentment. It wasn’t that Sephiroth had done anything to earn the aggression with which the words had been fired, but three days hadn’t been nearly enough time to fully come to grips with the reality of the situation. The fact that he had let the conversation come this far somewhat surprised himself. Seph wasn’t the type of person not to be trusted by others, Matt just wasn’t the type to trust, at least, that seemed to the road he was set to travel again. “What happened, Matt? You can be straight with me. We busted our asses every night in a wrestling ring for two years. I always had your back then, and I always told you exactly how it was. Nothing’s changed about that. If you can’t tell me what’s goin on, you’re a long ways from being ‘just fine’.” “Thursday afternoon, Mary was out for lunch. She’d been meeting with her publisher, about working a possible collection of short stories. The meeting ran a little long, she decided to go out for a late lunch, and headed to that café downtown on 3rd street. Two guys, I dunno exactly what happened, they wouldn’t tell me much… but two assholes got into a shouting match. Things got out of hand between the two, the yelling turned to fighting and they spilled over towards Mary’s table. At that point, she tried to jump up and get out of the way, but she had a table against the window and got pinned down by one of the guys who was being shoved her way by the other. The guy who was pushing the other into Mary pulled out a knife, shoved it forward… other guy moved…” “Shit.” “It cut her across the shoulder, didn’t stab her, but she was knocked to the ground pretty hard. Anyways, the cut isn’t too bad… I mean… at least given what it could have been. It’ll heal over a couple weeks, doctors don’t even think it’ll scar. The problem isn’t anything physical. She’s not even here at the hospital, I came by to talk to a couple of the doctors.” “I’m confused. She was cut, but not bad? Physically, she’s fine… resting at home and everything? What’s wrong? Was it the fall?” “Yeah. Maybe. The trauma too.” “Trauma?” “From the event. She’s suffering from Anterograde Amnesia. It can occur following a traumatic event, or from damage to one of several areas of the brain. Damage to the hippocampus is one of the main findings in people with Anterograde Amnesia…” “Wait,” Seph cut him off. Matt was speaking in a very scientific, cold manner, though he didn’t get the impression it was simply because of Matt’s background in psychology. This wasn’t a man slipping unintentionally into a speech about field he had experience in… it wasn’t the scientific nature of his words that was striking Sephiroth unfavorably… it was their coldness that chilled the room. “Anterograde Amnesia?” “Have you seen the movie ‘Memento’? The movie that’s told backwards in short segments?” “Yeah, I think I remember that one.” “That’s basically what this is, minus the hollywood glitz and glamour. It’s short-term memory loss. She’ll be able to remember everything that happened up till the afternoon at the café, all that will be fine. Her memory of the past won’t be affected. The problem comes with everything that happens after the accident. From this point on, she can’t really form new memories. She can only remember something for a few minutes at a time, then she loses it. Mary could be talking on the phone with her mother about her writing, and if they got disconnected and her mother would call back a couple minutes later, Mary would have no idea that her mother was calling her back. She’d think it was the first time she would have talked with her mom that day. And when her mother would say ‘so, back to what we were talking about…’ Mary would laugh and say how they hadn’t talked in over a week.” “Jesus. She can’t hold anything in her memory anymore?” “Some things. She’ll likely be able to learn new skills. The amnesia seems to damage the ability to remember facts and events and things of that nature. If you teach someone with Anterograde Amnesia how to perform a new task or trick, the next day, if you gave them the same tools and asked them to perform the same task, they’d probably be able to do it… but they’d look at you with a puzzled look on their face and tell you they have no idea where or when they learned how to do it. They can’t store the memory of the actual event.” “Is this permanent?” The concern in Seph seemed to be giving him a strength he had been lacking when his friend had entered the room. Now, it was the man sitting across from him that looked to be in a comatose state. “I dunno. The doctors can’t tell me much. They say there doesn’t seem to be any damage to those areas of the brain, they think it’s just a combination of the shock of the fall and the basic trauma from the event. But with amnesia, you never know. Anterograde is basically like the other forms of amnesia. It could last a few days. It could last a few weeks, a few months, or it could last forever. Sometimes it’s a temporary state, sometimes it’s very permanent. Typically, if the amnesia remains after about the five or six month mark… its not going away.” “So, basically… it’s a waiting game? Mary might get better tomorrow or next month, but if she isn’t better in about half a year from now… she probably won’t get better at all?” “Yeah, I guess that pretty much sums it up.” “Matt… God, I’m sorry man. How are you holdin up? I mean… I suppose that might be a stupid ques…” “I’m fine.” Matt didn’t let Seph finish his thought. He stood from his chair, pushing it back with the force of his sudden movement. The chair slid backwards, its wooden legs making a pitiful noise as they rubbed against the slick tile of the floor, before ending abruptly as the seat collided with the wall. “There’s not much I can do about it. So I’m fine, cuz it doesn’t really matter a damn if I’m not.” “C’mon, Matt. You can’t treat this situation that way.” “Seph, it’s the only way to treat it. It’s the only way to handle it. There’s nothing I can do for this. I can’t help cure amnesia. I can’t be happy and optimistic and all of a sudden, Mary will regain her short-term memory. Nor is throwing a fit and becoming supremely depressed and curling up in bed and crying myself to sleep every night going to guilt her short-term memory to return. So I’m just gonna be fine with it. Because that’s the least hassle.” “So where do you go from here? It doesn’t sound like you’d be much in favor of continuing your psychology practice. What now? Other than being ‘just fine’?” “I’m getting back into the ring.” Matt turned his back to the lights and headed out of the room for the dimmed hallway. “It’s been a couple years, I figure… what the hell.” “I thought you retired. Weren’t you finished with it? For more than one reason?” “It never really goes away, Seph. You know that. For some of us, it’s a sickness. “So when are you gonna get back in the ring?” “Soon, Seph?” “How soon?” he shot Matt a puzzled look “Real soon. In a couple days. I joined PRIME. I’ve got my debut match at ReVolution 75 against a guy by the name of Smitty T Duluth.” “Are you even ready to step back into the ring? Do you know anything about your opponent? Can you win a match with two years of ring rust?” “One question at a time, Seph. No, don’t know a damn thing about Duluth. We’re victims of equal ignorance in that respect. It’s the debut for us both, first round of the JITC tournament.” “So there’s no real preparation there, fine. But two years? Guys get ring rust after two months out of the ring. You’ve been out of the game both physically and mentally for a long time.” “And now I’m back. It was never about being some peek physical specimen for me, Seph. You know that. It was all about that shit that was going on in my head. It’s going on again.” “You’re thirty years old, Matt. That’s getting up there for the business of wrestling. You haven’t wrestled in two years…” “What's the point, Seph?” “I just don’t want to see you getting yourself killed, or wrecking your life.” “Wrecking my life? No shit…” he let his words trail off and turned back to look at Seph, lying in the hospital bed. “You’re not a kid anymore.” “I get that. I know I’m not 25. I know I’ve been ‘out of the ring’ for two years, but I was never gone, Seph. Just medicated. And I may not be a young gun anymore… but that’s fine. I know I don’t have forever left…” Once again he turned with a seemingly effortless motion and headed out of the room. Each step was placed with an insidious lack of concern, and somehow, vaguely, Seph picked up on it. “I know I don’t have the rest of my life in front of me… I don’t have anything left in front of me, Seph. That’s just it. It’s all there behind me now.” He stepped away from the suffocating white of the room and turned down the darkened hall.
