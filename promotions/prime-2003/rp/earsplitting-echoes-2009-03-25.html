Date: 3/25/09 "It's cool to love to win, but it's better to hate to lose." -Nasir Jones March 23rd, 2009 12:57 P.M. If it ain’t broke, don’t fix it. For the two hundred and sixteen days leading up to Culture Shock, my half-year affair with the Universal Title by another name, that had been my attitude towards Tsuperstar Enterprises. Just as PRIME had left its bottom line to the faceless Prometheus Board, I’d left Tsuperstar Ent. in the capable hands of a dozen stewards, my own Board of Directors. Never send a Board to do a man’s job. "Chan, our public relations is a nightmare," Allan North groaned. From within the Tsuperstar Enterprises Board Room (all caps, all business), I could take in the entire skyline of Providence. Our modest little operation occupied the top five floors of a relative skyscraper, looking out over the city. To the east, the river peeked out, only to disappear under oppressive concrete. "Actually, let me recant. Our public relations could only be upgraded to an nightmare if I won the lottery, gave all the money to charity, and devoted myself to asceticism and the miraculous healing of terminally ill children." And for those of you wondering: yes, that Allan North. Ironic, I guess, how Danny coming back to wrestling, coming back to put an end to things between us, was the final straw for his lawyer. Allan cried uncle and dropped him like a bad habit. The shrewd business move, personal bullshit aside, was to hire Allan as an upper-level legal adviser, so that’s exactly what I did. Now, four months later, Allan was the only one with the balls to shoot me straight with the gloom and doom. "Allan, slow down the Panic Train for two seconds here. Last time I was here, we had just finished these sweet digs. We were trading at our highest level since going public. You had a sexy Hispanic secretary. Where’s Carmen at these days?" A dismayed glance from the former lawyer took some wind out of my sail. "You gonna tell me that we even fired the tail? Christ…" "Chan." "Ya?" "Did anyone ever fill you in on…" With the next words, Allan cringed, unsightly wrinkles rippling across his forehead. "…Dollar Mania?" "I…" Eyes seizing the skyline again, I could finally feel just how detached from my company I’d become. "…erm, did you know I was the Universal Champion for the last six months?" The irritated sigh from North answered the question, but I felt justified enough to plead my case. "Well, Christ, Allan, was it not enough for me to be the face of PRIME since last summer? I thought my people here had things under control enough that me stepping away for a minute to carve my Hall of Fame bust would be okay." "When the master’s away…" Allan mumbled the proverb to himself as he headed over to his lacquered mahogany desk. "Well, I’m sure you’re aware that the global economy imploded worse than Michael Richards’ standup career." Since my eyes were still glued to the massive windows–the hot, bright gleam off the adjacent building’s windows was intoxicating–it must’ve seem to Allan like I was avoiding the question. "Chan, you do know that, right?" "Fuckin’ A, Allan. Recession, recession, recession. Fearmongers everywhere are eating it up. It’s Y2K all over again." "Except that the recession isn’t just for nuts with bunkers in their basements. This is kicking average Americans right in their red, white, and blue nuts." While his wit was never dulled, even in the most somber situation, Allan North did have a hint of sobriety to his tone. "And you would know this how? I wasn’t aware that the private sector legal analyst demographic was suffering horribly from Recessionitis." I got a glare in return, as Allan pulled an old newspaper from the middle drawer of his meticulously kept desk. "While you were off…wrestling," he spat, giving the word the same treatment that one might offer "sewage," "…this company saw its profits dwindle. We’ve taken a loss for three straight quarters, and we’re expecting our worst quarter of all-time. Plus, there was Dollar Mania. It’s enough to permanently sear the financial problems of John Q. Public into my brain forever." Muttering to himself again, Allan handed me the fragment of newspaper, pointing to a story circled in angry red ink. It was dated February 14th, 2009. RECESSION SALES GIMMICK TRAGICALLY BACKFIRES DES MOINES, ID.–A day that was intended to distract consumers from the country’s crippling financial crisis ended with eight civilians in the hospital Friday. Shoppers looking to take advantage of Target’s Dollar Mania promotion caused a stampede within the store, as well as at a car accident in the store’s parking lot. The injuries occurred at a bottleneck near the store’s entrance, as frenzied shoppers piled in to take part in Dollar Mania, a store gimmick aimed specifically at the average American consumer, a figure retailers have been desperate to reel back in. Dollar Mania, co-sponsored by Outer Tshell and radio station WLRN, had been advertised for weeks as a recession remedy. Buyers were promised a half hour inside Target, during which retail items would be offered at huge discount prices. Sponsors stressed that stock would be limited, with special Outer Tshell winter items priced at only a dollar. Certain other items, like a 17-inch TV/DVD combo, were also being offered for a dollar. The caveat to all this was that WLRN would only announce Dollar Mania once, and would do so without warning. When the station made the call on Friday at 12:46 to conclude its signature Hot 9 at Noon, residents rushed to the store in downtown Des Moines. "Well, I heard the announcement, and I’d been waiting all week by the radio, so I wasn’t going to miss it," said Connie Larsen. Larsen, 44, had her left arm broken when she was pinned against a metal detector. "People were acting like animals, though…soon, it was real clear that the whole Dollar Mania deal was a mistake." Larsen’s was not the most serious injury. 66 year-old Boyd Dorsey suffered a mild heart attack during the panic and is in critical condition at St. Lucy’s Hospital. When asked about Outer Tshell’s role in drumming up Friday’s frenzied atmosphere, Theodore Pendergraft, Vice President of Accounting at Tsuperstar Enterprises, parent company of Outer Tshell, was aloof about his company’s role. "All we did was offer people a great product at a great price. I don’t understand how a conglomerate can be held responsible for the actions of a few irresponsible people. I hope that some people were able to benefit from the fantastic opportunity offered at Dollar Mania." Executives of Target and WLRN were not available for comment. Looking up, I felt my blood surge with heat, as I flexed a fist around what used to be the newspaper story. "That…" I growled, teeth grinding, "…is why motherfucking guys like Ted The Cunt-Lipped Douchebag get stuck away in Accounting. Because accountants have a knack for being the most sociopathic fucking…" I took a breath. Too many F-bombs were starting to give me a migraine. "Allan, could you please explain to me how we dealt with this situation?" "The story spiraled out of control before we could do anything," Allan shrugged, meeting my eyes with his. "Too bad we had to fire Ted Pendergraft when the old man died. Boy, that didn’t make things any better." "WHAT?!" "Jesus, Chandler, I’m kidding. That old man’s alive and doing as well as you can do when you live in Des Moines." He gave a concerned shake of his head, probably wary of my…ahem…short fuse. "But, no, seriously, I fired the living hell out of Ted Pendergraft. The only way he’ll ever a job in this industry is if I need to hire a rickshaw driver." "I’d motherfucking hope so," I scolded, as if any of this was Allan’s fault. "When I told you to keep the Board in line, told you that you were to act as my proxy…?" "It’s one thing for you to say that. It’s entirely another for me to try and sit in front of the Board and explain that. As you probably know, they’re a bunch of yes-men and patsies; the chance to actually make their own decisions was too tempting." "Of course they’re a bunch of yes-men and patsies! They’re the Board! By definition, a bunch of rich yes-men and patsies who’ll throw money recklessly at whatever I say!" I took a deep breath, realizing that there was no more reason to get worked up. "So the Dollar Mania thing blew up in our face? How come it never came back on me? Why didn’t PRIME lose sponsorships or something? Isn’t that what companies do when anything vaguely related to pro wrestling causes moral outrage" And how had I not heard of this before right now? "You told me when you offered me this position that you didn’t want the two aspects of your career to affect each other." "And?" "And," Allan replied, pausing just long enough to show his penchant for dramatic flair, "I’m very damn good at my job." "Touché." He was. "Well, now I’m back to take the reins." "You know it’s not that easy, Chandler." "Allan, we all know that Michael Jordan was a co-owner again the second he pulled off the Wizards jersey. Pull whatever magic he pulled." "From your tone," he started, letting the words come out slowly and deliberately, "I take it you’re no longer the…Unified Champion or whatever you called it?" "Universal Champion." "Right. You said that you were. As in not anymore." "Correct." It was strange, but Allan’s complete ambivalence towards PRIME made me emptier. My golden idol was in the hands of a rat bastard thief and Allan couldn’t have cared less. I guess I admired his stoic refusal to be bothered by anything out of his insulated world. "Do you think that you might be in some kind of vulnerable state? As someone who’s devoted a significant amount of time to this company, I think it’s only fair that you consider whether you’re coming back to help Tsuperstar Enterprises or to help Chandler Tsonda." Losing things is part of life. Girls, pets, friends, money. But sometimes, when we lose things, we try and replace them as quickly as possible. In recesses of our mind so deep that they’d make Freud’s prick stand at attention, we’re doing this so that we forget about losing that thing. We do it so that we can keep on living in exactly the same way. We do it while adamantly refusing to look change square in the face. We do it until something shakes us from that comfortable state of denial, and if nothing ever shakes us hard enough, we live our lives. Deny, deny, deny. We all do this. I’ve been doing this all my life. "You think that, by emotional osmosis, I’m looking to replace my totally shiningly awesome Title belt with a corner office and a hot secretary?" "Could be." "Could be. Last time I tried that, I turned this company from a tiny limited liability corporation representing my personal finances into a nationally-recognized industrial player on the fringe of the Fortune 500. Why I waltzed into the East Coast Headquarters today doesn’t make a difference." But that wasn’t enough to stop the wily eye of Allan North from questioning me. Standing up in front of twenty stuffy old white guys didn’t even make me flinch. Less than two weeks ago, I’d been standing up in front of thousands in PRIME. But now…that was over. Had to get that through my head. There was no place for Chandler Tsonda as a solo act. Without the complementary pieces, Ferguson and Facey, respectively, I felt like a relic. 5-Star Title absconded with by that jerkoff Winters, Jewel in the Crown hopes quashed by Gamble. I didn’t see much of a future for myself in that world. "My name is Chandler Tsonda. As of this moment, I’m the chief executive officer of Tsuperstar Enterprises, LLC. What you fabulously successful men want to know, though, is why Tsuperstar Enterprises is the place to invest." Several nods from the table. Without a permanent office, I’d resorted to renting out a section of a very fine restaurant, Un Poco de Suerte. A Little Bit of Luck. "Please, feel free to take full advantage of the complimentary food and drink. It’s here for you, whether you choose to invest with us or not." First rule of business: see if you can’t get your clients tipsy before getting down to details. Several of the heftier would-be-investors waddled towards the buffet table as I launched into my schpiel. No PowerPoint, no laser pointer, no fancy shit. Just my voice, and a absurdly expensive suit, to advertise my product. "I know that people usually start these things off with a quick biographical sketch. But let’s not mince words. It doesn’t matter where I was born, where I may or may not have studied, or what, in my life’s twisting path, has led me to this room. What you need to know is that I, Chandler Tsonda, am a born winner." Several eyebrows rose as I peered around the room. Real estate moguls, pharmaceutical impresarios, and industry barons sat at the table, and here I was, calling myself the winner amongst them. But with a quick grin, and enough confidence to power a nuclear reactor, I continued. "I already see doubt on some faces in here. That’s great. I wouldn’t want a bunch of patsies and yes-men investing in my product. But when I say that I’m a winner, amongst the presence of some of the most powerful financial players in the country, no less…I mean it with absolute certainty. Tsuperstar Enterprises will be on the national radar within two years." "Is that a guarantee?" Surprised it had taken this long for one of these needy fuckers to interrupt me, I barely even needed to go through my rehearsed answers. The questioner, a scrawny, coiffed W.A.S.P., folded his arms. "Guarantees, Mr. McDonald, are for the faithless. But here are the concrete facts: I’ve restored several war-era factories in Vietnam, and they’ll be ready to start production within two months. I’ve signed a tentative agreement with the San Diego Stevedores Union, and the group has agreed to give me somewhat of a family discount." "Hold on," McDonald yelped, "a family discount? What happens if I invest with you, and then in a year’s time, there are FBI agents knocking down my door to investigate me for racketeering charges? This sounds fishy." "Mr. McDonald, if there’s nothing keeping you here, I can ask the kitchen staff to prepare a doggy bag for you." He glowered in my direction before he found his tongue. "I’m just trying to get the facts and–" "And I’m trying to give them to you. May I continue?" Nothing like a condescending rhetorical question to shut up an asshole. With that taken care of, I wouldn’t have to mention Ettore Calvino, mobster-cum-father of mine. Better for everyone. "Right. I’ve got Sports Authority, Dick’s Sporting Goods, and Target ready to distribute our first large product, Outer Tshell attire, by the first of the year. Under Armour got the jump on us, but having been a professional athlete, and having trained in every imaginable environment, I’ve got insight that can help us compete. Engineers are working as we speak to perfect the basic items for the product launch. You know, shirts, jackets, track pants, gloves, all that. The average consumer in our target demographic, males 18-24, will receive at least fifty dollars in gift cards to one of those three stores at Christmas. And when those 18-24 year olds arrive, they’ll be greeted by brand, spanking new Outer Tshell gear, ready to be swept off the shelves." I cleared my throat, sensing that the room had become much quieter. Having spent my entire adult life making a job out of keeping peoples’ eyes on me, I had a sixth sense for knowing when my audience was eating out of my hand. These dudes were gobbling it up. "After Outer Tshell takes off, I have plans to expand with men’s fragrance, a line of designer sunglasses, and advertising. I have myriad connections in the professional world, from Milan to Hollywood. With capital invested by you, you can literally sit back and watch your bankroll multiply." The stunned silence lasted a few more seconds before a second doubter identified himself. "You recently left the world of professional wrestling," the grey-suited man with a French collar remarked. "I did. Before my time in that industry, I spent almost a decade as a male model." I heard one or two quiet snickers. "You a fan of PRIME, Mr. Anderton?" "Can’t bear the stuff, but my youngest watches it. He said you were some kind of champion until recently." The 5-Star Title loss I’d been running away from had caught up to me . But this was no time to act shook. Business is all about the mask of complete and utter confidence. "Right again. I was representing the company’s second most prestigious title belt." "Is it all fake?" "Isn’t there rampant steroid use?" "Why’d you leave?" The questions flew at me, but this too, had been rehearsed dozens of times in my head. "Gentlemen, if you want to talk about the past, you’ll have to get me liquored up first. I’ve re-entered the business world to leave all that nonsense behind. This, this is the real world of competition. And, along with those of you smart enough to invest, I’m about to start a major winning streak. Thank you for your time" My pitch finished, some of the men left. Anderton and McDonald were among them, too ridden with doubt to make the commitment. But those thirteen that stayed couldn’t throw their money at Tsuperstar Enterprises quickly enough. /~/~/~/~/ March 23rd, 2009 1:17 P.M. "So you have occupational ADD. You see something shiny, play with it long enough that you lose it or it becomes dull, and then you find something more exciting." "Allan, I had a shrink once before, and it didn’t really work out. You asked what evidence I have that this can work, and I told you." "Be that as it may, we’re talking about the worst global economy in sixty years. It’s no longer a matter of rolling out a spiffy new product or selling our line by putting naked girls in our ads." "So your advice is that I do nothing, thus letting the Board members breathe down your neck about the money that’s disappearing magically fast?" Allan cocked his head to the side, at least giving off the impression that he was thinking long and hard. I returned my eyes to the skyline. "What can you conceivably do for this company if it’s not your first priority?" He saw it. Allan recognized the difference between this time and last. Two and a half years ago, I’d been ready to hang up the boots, do something different with my life. But after entirely too much digging, I’d found the truth, though it was muddy. PRIME was the competition I craved. The world of business only ever gave you more money. But in that ring, where I’d left years of my life, there was something primitive and glorious. So for all the goosebumps I’d felt when that Russian crowd embraced me at the end of Culture Shock, I knew this wasn’t the right time to say goodbye. I couldn’t just let my golden goddess slip away without putting up one more good fight. "You’re…you’re right. I needed a distraction, and I thought that coming back here would feel right, feel normal. But the only thing I can think about, whether I’m here or at my apartment or at the gym, is getting back what was taken from me." "I’m not completely sure what that means." "Wrestling babble, don’t sweat it." I finally pulled my eyes away from the skyline and met Allan’s gaze. His eyes were unsympathetically focused dead ahead, on me. I couldn’t begrudge the man for not caring; to men in the Serious World of Business, it was a game. Maybe, at best, a sport. But to those people, if it’s not about the dollars, it doesn’t make sense. "I do have an idea, though. A way that you can help this company regain some of its mojo." "Shoot." /~/~/~/~/ March 24th, 2009 10:16 A.M. The flowers were radiant, yellow and blue petals folding outwards to show themselves to the sun. Long-stemmed purple tulips acted as the visual centerpiece, looming over the other buds as if supervising their blooms. The garden was as beautiful as I’d ever seen it. Probably because she could feel the Alzheimer’s more and more every day, because she didn’t know how much longer she would wake up and be herself. She found solace in these brilliantly-colored creations of hers. "Chan!" My mother’s delighted shriek made me jump. As I tore my eyes away from the flowers, I saw her hurriedly shuffle down the driveway. "Ma, just wait there." She didn’t, and I scolded her as she neared the garden. "Ma, you’re supposed to be taking it easy." Her rebuttal was a bearhug. It felt like being hugged by a child, which in itself was a sad reminder of her deteriorating health. All those years spent in Bumfuck, Vietnam, under a thatched roof and miles from the nearest doctor had left her with a slightly curved spine. Now, old age was acting as an accomplice, and she moved with a slight hunch. But she stood up completely straight to wrap both arms around my chest. I kept quiet about the bandages on my chest that made this hug particularly painful. "What are you doing here?" "Had some business on the East Coast, and more business in Hawai’i on Thursday, but I’ve been away for too long." "Chan…" When she started with that low, authoritative tone, odds were that I’d be catching flack. "…who did that awful thing to you?" I stifled a laugh, as I realized that wasn’t quite specific enough. It’d been over a month since I’d last seen her, and I’d stockpiled enough awful to depress a small town. I hedged my bets. "I’m not really sure." "Someone hurts you–puts my Chan in the hospital!–and gets away with it? That place is no good to you, Chan. No good, no good." Of course. She was referring to that little incident where someone (Snow? Shakur?) had been nice enough to give me the full body black-and-blue treatment. I still felt that beating, compounded by Culture Shock, every morning when I woke up. A Tylox here, a Tylox there. That particular deal with the devil no longer gave me pause. To not use painkillers meant surgery, which meant surgery and rehab. No longer the spring chicken I once was, that would be the end of things. "I’ll find out who did it, Ma. But I don’t want you watching that stuff if it’s gonna get you worked up. Nobody in PRIME can give me a beating that I can’t get up from." Except for sneaky fucks who think it’s kosher to blindside me. "I don’t watch. Your friend Bryan telephoned me. Said you were in the hospital, but…" She smiled as she put on her impression of Dawkins’s voice. "‘…don’t worry, Mrs. Tsonda. Tyler and I won’t let anything happen to him.’ Called you a funny name." "Tink. Yeah…it’s a guy thing." Her smile lingered; she liked Dawkins. "So how do you feel, Ma?" "Happy to see you." She knelt down and began to massage the dirt around her garden. She was readying herself to dodge my questions about her health. "Physically, I mean." "Fine. Sleepy sometimes." Usually, this was when we’d butt heads. I’d tell her that she couldn’t get help if she didn’t tell anyone what was wrong, she’d tell me that she’d speak up when the disease got bad enough. I’d offer to enroll her in experimental trials, and she’d tell me to go live my life. The silences at the end of those conversations were heartbreaking. Today, I didn’t need that, so I selfishly didn’t bother her any further. "So one of the guys over at the company had an idea," I started. A mother, after all, is one we run to when we feel troubled. "He’s got a pal who works at this juvenile detention center. It’s a jail for kids." "I know," she snapped. "Anyway, the company needs to boost its image for the public. Ya know, in case the recession ever ends and people start buying stuff again. Anyway, he thinks that I could do a hell of a lot of good by mentoring some of those kids, and help the company, to boot." She hadn’t looked up from her flowers. "Just the ones who’ve been locked up for stupid stuff, ya know? Kids who get in fights, the same type of stuff I was doing before I went into modeling. This guy, Allan, thinks I should talk to this guy at the jail about helping the kids. And…this isn’t exactly something I can go to Ty or Dawkins for. I’m just wondering, you think I’d be any good at something like that?" "Do you want to?" "I dunno. If it’d be good for the company and the junior inmates, I think I should." "Why?" "Isn’t it the duty of people in my position to give back?" She turned around, giving me a curious look. "Do you want to go to Heaven?" "I don’t know that I believe there is such a place. I’d love to be on the guest list, I guess, if it exists. But what does that have to do with this?" "You can go to Heaven. You have a good soul." My mother’s stilted English often produced surprisingly enlightened statements. That was as explicit an answer as I was going to get from a woman who prided herself on never telling me what to do. "But I dunno the first thing about kids." "You were one. Now, Chan is a big man who fights his own fights. A good man. Tell them how to be a good man." That would imply that I know how to be a good man, whatever that means. But on some subjects, you just defer to a mother. That’s why you asked her in the first place. I kissed her on the forehead, and she turned back to her flowers.
