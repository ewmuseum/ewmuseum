Date: July 31, 2007 The mellow, soothing drawl of Johnny Cash greets him as he steps through the swinging saloon doors of Shooter McGee’s. Familiar lyrics part his lips in silent sing-a-long, Tyler Rayne marching in time with the thrumming bass of Ring of Fire. The music adds to the ambiance of the bar, obviously influenced in both architecture and décor by the rowdier days of the Wild West. Shooter’s, as the kids call it, is one of the more recent and successful proprietaries to sprout up along the college block, located only a drunk’s stumble away from Eastern Oregon University. Tyler sidles up to the bar, taking a moment to appreciate the bartender’s dedication to the cause. “Nice ‘stache.” The bartender chuckles, turning the curls of his handlebar mustache with the smile. “Pa always told me, ya gonna do suntin’, best do it right.” “Sounds like a wise man. I’ll have a bottle of Jack.” The bartender nods, warm smile still etched beneath the mustache. His hand moves to grab the whisky, but stops halfway when the words sink in. It’s easy to get in a habit of things. Same students come in on the same day every week, order the same drinks, sit at the same table, tell all the same stories. It’s not difficult, in any vocation, to find yourself stuck in a rut. Thus the bartender, rumored to be ol’ Shooter McGee himself, had heard a request for Jack without actually listening to the words that accompanied. Now, though, the entirety of the order sinks in. Shooter returns to the patron, a curious expression now painted across his thematic façade. The patron’s smile is one of amused confidence, the twinkle in his eye one Shooter has come to recognize as mischievous intent. The dirt brown orbs, glint and all, move downward, bringing Shooter’s gaze along with. There, beneath the tapping fingers of Rayne’s right hand, is Benjamin Franklin in all his monetary glory. The smile returns to Shooter’s face. “Bottle of Jack. Three shot glasses.” “You got ‘er, partner.” Even in the mids of summer heat, Shooter’s was still primarily a college bar. As he turned from the lines of liquor, Tyler gave himself leave to peruse the other patrons of the establishment. The company was largely composed of the college scene, twenty-somethings pretending their way through pseudo-intellectual conversations with inebriated professors. A group of raucous fraternity brothers had squeezed themselves tightly into a corner booth, each taking turns gulping straight from a pitcher of beer. A handful of giggling girls were dragging each other toward what passed for the dance floor, the more modern beats of J-Kwon appealing to their musical palettes. Then there’s the dark haired woman at the end of the bar, long black fingernails tracing circles around the rim of an empty glass. Her skin is pale; lips full. Lonely eyes staring dull and unnoticed into the bottom of the glass. A sigh, the heave of her chest blossoming a barely contained bosom through the top of her two-sizes too tight spaghetti strap. He looked a bit older than most of the patrons, if only by a few years, or perhaps just carried himself with an air that suggested something slightly more mature. Worldly.
