Cleaning, Part One: The Fool And The Scholar

Date: May 02. 2010 Cleaning A Garbage Bag Johnny Production Part One: The Fool And The Scholar *** One Last Score *** It was just him and me there under the shallow canopy, and there might as well not have been one at all. These days, you couldn't smoke in bars, and it'd been that way for a while, but there really wasn't such a thing as freedom, and I was slowly becoming alright with it. I'd been drinking, but I wasn't drunk. I didn't see much of a point to it all, drinking without getting drunk, but old habits die hard, I guess, and all I really needed was one sturdy foot inside the wagon after all. "Some weather tonight, ain't there?" His name was Mike, Mike Wilson. At least that's how he'd introduced himself to me, every now and then always Mike first and then a pause and then Mike Wilson. I didn't introduce myself as anyone. "There's always some weather." I took a drag from my cigarette and looked off at the road. I could still see Mike's eyes trained on me in the periphery. His eyes made me uncomfortable, they were sad and tinted sickly yellow, and they pointed away from each other most of the time. "What you doing out here in this drizzling shit?" "It's Friday night, brother." He held his cup steady and silent. "Still people out. Ain't a little rain gonna stop folks." "You even make your bus fare?" "I got a card on Wednesday--no--Tuesday. Takes me right to the hotel where I'm staying, and it's good the whole week." From the edge of my sights, I could tell he was proud of himself. "Little late on the rent, but they know I'm good for it. Might just see if I can earn myself a little sip tonight." "God damn." I laughed out of the corners of my mouth, biting down on the filter of my cigarette to keep it in place. "Even beggars got better credit than me." "I ain't no street beggar." He raised his shoulders, puffing like a rooster, and tapped his chest. "I don't ask nobody for nothing. They see me. They know why I got a cup." "Didn't mean you no offense by it." "How you doing, darling?" The lady walking by wore a black peacoat that went to the knee. It was the kind of coat that was in fashion, and in the humid night, everything looked so drab I almost wouldn't have seen her. Mike, Mike Wilson, saw her with his tinted sickly yellow eyes, yellow where the whites should've been. "Okay. Keep on shining!" She continued down the sidewalk, ignoring sad, old Mike, Mike Wilson, and sad as he was, he grinned through it, used to it by now. I couldn't tell if he knew where he was. Didn't seem much like he did, but I didn't mention it. I couldn't tell him where the fuck we were if he'd asked. I was drunker than I let show. I fought to remain so constituted while I finished my cigarette, focusing my posture ironclad so the wet winds would not persuade me. The whiskey pumped its soul through my veins, and I could feel it rattling in me. "I gotta get back in there." I flicked the tan, speckled butt off into the road and lost it under the steady, hydroplaning traffic. "Got someone waiting on me." "I got you, man." His grunting resembled laughter. "Hot date tonight?" "Nah. Nothing like that." "I'll let you get back to it." He put his fist out, insisting a reluctant dap from me. "Say man, you got any change? Fitting to go get my drink on, too, you know." "Thought you didn't ask nobody for nothing." "Nah, man. I'll hit you back." He stood there expectantly, and a part of me pitied his impatience. "You know I'm good for it." "Christ." I reached in my wallet and counted out a few singles for him. "You're good for nothing. You know that?" The veteran street beggar, Mike, Mike Wilson, took the notes from me with a toothy grin. I had to believe that his gratefulness was a mask with what little he had to be grateful for. It kept me from feeling guilty. As thankful as I reminded myself to be that I had plenty, I still wanted for all the things I'd barely missed along the way. Wasn't too much I could do to stop myself from going back for them neither, no matter how incongruous I'd have to be to rationalize any particular need to go back for them. "Spare a smoke for me, too?" "Man." I looked down and shook my head and figured maybe he wouldn't see me choking back a laugh. "Go fuck yourself." *** The Fool And The Scholar *** Steve still wasn't back from the bathroom, but he was the kind of guy who shat where he wanted and took as long as he liked, so Johnny stuck a finger up in the air to call over the waitress, and when she came by, he lifted a second finger into the air, and he should've felt like an asshole about it. Maybe he did, if he wasn't drunk enough yet, but he was always a little drunker than he let on. "One Jameson, rocks, and one Maker's, neat?" She smiled. She didn't have to. Steve opened the tab. Johnny just nodded. He still wasn't completely comfortable with the density of the atmosphere, but in spots like this, the clientele were too ambitious to have heroes, and none of them recognized Johnny, or for that matter, Steve. Otherwise, Steve never would have brought Johnny here. Johnny was reluctant to go, but Steve insisted that they celebrated. Besides, Steve didn't care for discussing business in an office, as often as his duties called for it. Nobody cared much for offices anyway, Johnny included. "I feel like I just gave birth." Steve smiled at his joke as he slid back into the booth. Johnny tried to laugh, but nothing came out. He stirred his straw in circles around the bottom of his glass and sipped at the melted ice in his last drink. Steve took notice of Johnny toying at the diluted remnants of his once whiskey and raised a couple of fingers in the air. "I got a couple more coming." Steve lowered his arm and smiled, waving off another waitress who was on her way to scope out the situation. "Good deal." The suit didn't look as good on Johnny as it did on Steve. It hung off of Johnny, probably due to the few inches Steve had on him. Steve examined Johnny's lack of luster, a stark contrast to his presence before the cameras just hours prior. "Something bothering you?" "Just tired." Johnny chuckled out of goodwill or some other imaginary feeling. "Tuckered myself out worrying over how it'd go." "I can imagine. It was great, though. Just great." Steve smiled genuinely. "I can't wait to see the numbers. I heard they're going to be high." "Andy and Roberto put on a hell of a show." What came off as humility might have been better classified as nostalgia. "Roberto coming out tonight? I figure if anyone has cause to celebrate, it'd be him." "Nah. Roberto's a family man." The waitress returned with our drinks on a platter. She set them down on the table. "He's celebrating with his son." "Anything else I can get for you?" Steve looked up and met the server's smile before dismissing her. "You know, that's part of the reason I felt you'd be the right man for the job." "What is?" Johnny looked up, for the first time since before he had a fresh drink. "You're a dedicated man with nothing else to dedicate yourself to." Steve looked around the bar, and only half of his mouth grinned. "There's not much glory in steering the ship, Johnny, at least not the kind of glory there is in gold. Whatever there is to enjoy, it's a very internal thing." "I'm a very internal man." Steve knew Johnny well enough to appreciate his honest assessment. They both knew what they were. They were actors, and only slightly more decent ones at that than their peers. "So what is happening with the gold after Roberto hangs the boots? Are we still considering announcing it as the prize for Rampage this year?" "I don't think so." Steve shook his head, feeling foolish for even, at one point, having considered it. It was Steve's idea in the first place. "Putting the top belt up for grabs in a tournament where guys like Bishop Steele have a shot? That's lunacy...desperation." "I suppose." Johnny took a sip of his drink. "Might be what the country needs right now, though. Ain't everyone having an equal shot what's good about America, even if it ain't what America's about?" "I just don't want someone to get a shot if they haven't worked long and hard enough for it." "Plenty of folks work plenty long and hard and turn up with nothing to show for it." Johnny oftentimes asked the doctor about the conditions of anonymous friends. "I think people would really get behind something like that, especially now, the world mostly gone to shit and all." "The worst thing for a world gone to shit is a pipe dream." Johnny knew it, too, and the words echoed painfully through him. He drank more quickly, until his thin straw slurped spare drops through. He'd made a history of chasing his tail, catching it every now and again, but more often than not, coming noticeably close. Johnny came close enough often enough that people began to think he was good at it, whatever it was. "Mind if I step outside for a minute?" Johnny stood up to excuse himself. "I need a cigarette to get my head straight." "By all means." *** The View From The Top *** I never really saw the magic of New York City in the same light that most of the industry did, but being there in Madison Square Garden, time had slowed down enough for me to appreciate the gravity of the event. In preparation for the tournament's final round, I had never felt more focused as I had there in the dressing room, ignoring whatever brutal metal bled through the walls of Jack's room. He'd been there before, and I figured he was pumping himself up, but being that it was my first time to the dance, I didn't want to do anything to distract from what I was feeling. The moment would be fleeting, and through victory or defeat, it'd be lost beneath an impending stampede. Someone knocked at the door. I wasn't expecting visitors, and my heart jumped. For a second, I thought it was time. "It's open!" I turned and stared at the door as the handle twisted. Jason Snow stood in the frame. He was wearing the PRIME Universal Championship. He'd been wearing it since the last time I'd seen him, which was when we wrestled for it in a cage almost a year prior. He didn't step into the room. "Jason?" "I just thought I'd stop by and wish you luck." I stood up and slowly walked over to the door frame to meet him. "Thanks," I said. "I appreciate it." "I heard you've been looking impressive, but I haven't really been watching. I've got a hell of a schedule in PRIME and all." Jason had been eliminated a few rounds back. He seemed to be the only one not surprised by it. "Figured I'd stop by and see who takes it this year. Gotta keep your appearances, right?" "Right." "Speaking of which, you make up your mind on the Halo this year?" Jason compulsively tapped his fingers on the door frame. "I haven't really put much thought into it, you know?" I shrugged and scratched at my hair. "I'm not really looking past tonight." "I get you." He was one of the few that could understand, but in truth, I had spent a lot of time wondering what was next, and I'd made up my mind, even though I hadn't. "Well, it'd mean a lot to me to see you there." "Oh yeah?" At first, I thought little of the compliment. "It might be my last match." I couldn't look him in the eye, so I looked at his title. I couldn't say I was surprised. There weren't many wrestlers of his caliber left, and he'd been at it for a while. I thought it'd be a strange way to go. I never saw the validity in going out on top, but I never really had the balance to stay there for very long, neither. It was one of the only notches Jason was missing on his belt. "Come back carrying my shield or carried on it." "I hear you. I'd be lying if I wasn't thinking about hanging them up after this one, but what the fuck else do I got to do?" Jason smiled, and it was so rare that I started smiling from it and laughed something slightly uncomfortable. "I'll think about the Halo." "Hope you do," he said. "Anyway, good luck out there tonight." I nodded, and he retreated slowly, the natural wariness of balancing on top for so long showing in his muscles. "Hey, Jason. How does it feel?" "Huh?" His neck swiveled back, but only halfway. His body kept moving forward. "How's what feel?" I hoped I'd know soon enough, but I figured if I didn't, it'd be just as well. "Oh, this?" He looked around the hallways and realized where we were and why we were here. Then he thought about it. "It feels like it should." *** You Can Never Go Home Again *** With his shirt untucked, and the bottom trim poking out askew where his unbuttoned jacket ended, Johnny stumbled down the block, drunk past other drunks. His subconscious recognized the door, and he saw Meghan inside, wiping off the tables and stacking the chairs upside down on them. He knocked on the glass, and it didn't make much more than a dull thudding noise, and he knew Meghan couldn't hear it through the second door with Bowie playing as loud as it was. He could hear it through the half-open windows. She saw him, though, and he wasn't going away, so she let him in. "It's three, Johnny." She squeezed the cloth in one hand and leaned herself against the wall with the other, tired after working the double. "We're closed. I can't serve you." "I know." Johnny stumbled clumsy steps towards the bar, and he felt his way into a chair before pulling out a half-pint of sour mash whiskey. "I just need somewhere to drink is all." "Why don't you drink it at home?" "Cause I'm fucked up." It was an answer he oftentimes resorted to giving, but Johnny felt it was as good a reason as any to be there at Zero's, that fucking dive, after hours. "Have a shot with me, huh?" Begrudgingly, Meghan slipped back behind the bar and pulled out two clean shot glasses from the drying rack. He poured out the shots, clumsily as the rest of his movements, and lifted his in the air. "Bottoms up." Johnny threw his whiskey back quick, and Meghan joined in, lacking the same enthusiasm. "What's with the suit?" The whiskey didn't do much for her late night candor. As unkempt as he was with the suit drooping off him, this was the most primmed the bartender had seen Johnny over a consistent myriad of visits on slow weeknights. He played the free pool on Tuesdays. He wasn't any good unless he was a certain amount of drunk, so long as he wasn't too drunk. "Work," he said. Meghan didn't know what he did outside of drinking like a champion. She'd asked once, but Johnny was vague about it. He shrugged it off and responded with this and that. "You haven't been around here in a while." "I've been traveling." "Oh yeah? Where?" "Here and there." The redhead rested her chin in her palm, and Johnny suspected she was wondering what it was exactly that he did, but she didn't press him about it. She was just a bartender. Johnny felt it was best she didn't know if she didn't know already. "Listen, I've been thinking about some things, and I need you to talk some sense into me." "Thinking about what." "I don't know." Johnny was about mumbling, he used his arms to illustrate the points he could no longer coherently make. "I knew this guy one time. His name was One Arm Larry. You know why they called him that?" "Cause he only had one arm?" Johnny slapped the counter and hooted before pouring out another round of shots, mostly depleting the half-pint. He choked his down. "Motherfucker got trashed one night, absolutely shitfaced, and decided to robbed a liquor store one night back a few years ago and got away with it." "So you're thinking of robbing a liquor store?" "Nah, lemme finish." Johnny steadied himself on the bar to shift in his stool as Meghan lazily followed his story. "This is back when he had both of his arms, mind you. Guy hadn't done a thing of his life until he got all sauced up and hit that liquor store, but the next week--the next fuckin' week--Larry goes back and does it again. Guy's got some balls on him now, just bursts right through the front door of the same place with the same dude at the register. Dude unloads a fucking shotgun, catches Larry in the arm and really messes it up. The cops get there, and they send him to the hospital en route to prison. The doctors couldn't do anything about his arm but cut it off." "Okay." Meghan blinked herself awake and rubbed her tired eyes. "So One Arm Larry does a couple of years, gets a few months shaved off for good behavior, and decides that he's going to get some payback on the bastard that blew off his arm." Johnny pauses and quiets down, noticing the jukebox has played through. "Now, Larry ain't the brightest guy, so he heads straight back to the liquor store, just bursts right through the front door with his pistol, same way as he always did, and son of a bitch behind the register sees him coming this time, too--sees him coming before Larry can maneuver his way through the door holding a gun with his one arm and all. Larry doesn't even get a chance to lift his gun before he gets shot through his good arm, and wouldn't you know, they had to cut off his other arm, too. You know what he told the cops when they were bringing him back to the pen?" "What?" "He told them, 'Can't book me for armed robbery now.'" Meghan forced out a laugh she wouldn't have had to force if she wasn't so tired, but she just wanted to go home, and she wanted Johnny to go home. She was worried he wouldn't. "You like that one?" Johnny grinned, too goddamn drunk not to. "I've heard worse." She brushed a strand of hair away and rubbed her eyes again. "But what's this got to do with you?" "Ain't got nothing to do with me." Johnny paused longer and rubbed his heavy jaw. "Dumb motherfucker just doesn't learn." *** One More Last Score *** We were standing on a bridge, there, me and Mike, Mike Wilson, holding tall boys in paper bags. It was where he usually stood, and I'd stop sometimes if I'd see him there and have a smoke with him. He'd show me his old, swollen ankles, and he'd talk about the hotel room he had and how on Friday and Saturday, that's when business picked up, and if it was a good weekend, he'd pay off his whole week, and then he'd just have to make enough for a bus pass and a little something extra during the week when more drunk and charitable pedestrian traffic slowed down to a near halt. "Weekday folks ain't like weekend folks, man." Mike chewed and shook his head as vacationers looked out at the architecture on the horizon over the river. We looked the other way. "Weekday folks always acting like they got some place important to be." "They have jobs." "Jobs ain't important, man." Mike swatted at the air. "I worked at Ward's down the way every single day of my life until it closed few years back. Every single day! And I tell you what, never a day since that I've not been thankful to be alive." I was back in town again, taking a week off after the match with Snow and Tsonda. Anyone who'd been watching knew that I had no shot. I was in no form to be going toe to toe with either man, and Snow, especially, looked untouchable. He damn near was, but I pinned him once. I thought I had a shot. I might have been the only one. "I envy you, Mike." He grunted a laugh out and I watched him for a bit, trying to comprehend him. "You're out here living the dream, smoking free fuckin' cigarettes." "I'm making it by." He chomped down on the cigarette I gave him and nodded his head towards me in some kind of affirmation. "And you don't want to do anything else?" It all seemed so unusual to me. "You'd be fine just living out the rest of your days standing out here holding a cup?" "As long as I can pay for my room and my bus pass and maybe a bit of drink from time to time." The sickly yellow glowed from his eyes as he smacked his tongue against the roof of his mouth. "And 'sides, what the fuck else I got to do? Livin' the dream, baby." "To the dream, then." "To the dream." We clicked our cans together, through the brown paper, and finished off the drinks I'd bought. Then, we threw them over the railing and into the river. "Another round?" "You buying?" He laughed, the fuck of a menace did, and he hobbled behind me towards the liquor store. Beats the fuck out of me what the place was called. It just had a big yellow sign above it that said "LIQUOR" in bold black. "Got a story about this shop." Mike's voice bounced with each step, limping on both swollen ankles. "I ever tell you about One Arm Larry?" He had, about every other time I'd bumped into Mike, Mike Wilson, but it wasn't much of a story at the time because Larry was still in prison, then, and hadn't gone and gotten his other arm blown off yet. Except, he had a pretty good ending for it, too. He'd ask, "You know how I know they blew off his right arm?" And I'd shrug and play along if he even gave me time to respond, and he'd say, "Cause it wasn't left."
