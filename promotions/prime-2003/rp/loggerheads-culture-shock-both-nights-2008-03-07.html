Loggerheads (Culture Shock, Both Nights)

7 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3-7-08 The knocking would not stop. Easton stood behind the door, his back against it, tilting his head back to let orange juice flow down his throat. He had planned on burning down the blunt tucked neatly behind his ear and having a nice, relaxing day away from the hurts of constant training. Things never worked his way. He could feel the thumping against the back of his head. His heel pushed into the carpet and he finished the juice. He expected to see a big, bald man in a black trenchcoat when he opened the door and that's what he saw. Stan Hepton had a few inches on Easton and he used those to their fullest. His white button-down shirt revealed a bit of a paunch, something he had gained in the years since he had wrestled. He had always had the evil that sloped his eyebrows inward, that gouged forth those restless ridges above his broad nose. They had called him the Bringer of Bad News back then. Easton had, fortunately, never stepped in the ring with this particular man. "Easton," was Hepton's greeting – Detective Hepton of the Baltimore Police, though Easton didn't really need to remind himself – as his eyes swept past Easton and into the hotel room. It was plain behind him, his king-sized bed pushed up against the north-side wall and the television beside it, his dufflebag out of sight behind the bed, nothing disturbed. "Expecting company?" "Not really," Easton said. He made no move to occupy Hepton's attention. "You said you needed to talk to me." "Yeah," Hepton said, lowering his eyes and making sure Easton knew it. "I do. You gonna make me stand out here?" "As long as I can." "This is sensitive information, Easton." Easton sniffed. "I'm sorry if I don't care," Easton snarled. "If you want to talk to me, then talk." Hepton made his brown eyes snare Easton's greens and Easton did not flinch. Hepton leaned forward, brancing his forearm in the doorjamb, bringing his whole huge body forth to shadow over Easton. "It's about Makhpyia Sapa," Hepton hissed. Easton's forefinger tightened around the neck of the juice bottle. "Again, I'm sorry if I don't care..." Hepton slashed a glance over his shoulder. "Let me in, goddammit," Hepton snapped at Easton. His eyes flickered upwards and then his hand came forward, but Easton's was quicker, slapping Hepton away at the wrist. Hepton's eyes were trained on Easton's ear. "You're goddamn lucky we're in Mexico." He trained himself on Easton's eyes. "Let me in." Easton could have turned Stan Hepton back and both men knew it, but he didn't. He stepped back and he pressed his back against the wall and let Hepton step inside, slide past him into the room. Easton closed the door behind Hepton and watched his bare scalp. "What did you want to tell me?" "You been in Baltimore recently?" Hepton asked, stalking through the room. He couldn't shake his detective's instincts, not for a second. "Not since PRIME was last there," Easton said. "And when was that?" "Can't remember." Hepton made a quizzical little sound and stopped at the far edge of Easton's bed, glancing down at the dufflebag. Easton watched Hepton's fingers curl and uncurl. He pushed himself away from the wall and started towards Hepton. Stan inhaled deeply. "There've been murders in Baltimore. Heard?" "There are always murders in Baltimore." Hepton snorted. "Funny. You know what I'm getting at." "I guess so." Hepton took a few steps towards the window, his fingers grazing the heavy carmine curtains. "It's all very weird, all very... ritualized. Limbs spread to make pentagrams, incensed burned, inscribed daggers left at the scene. Kinda haphazard the way they did things." Hepton looked over his shoulder and made Easton stop his slow approach. "Makhpyia Sapa was your mentor, yeah?" "Yeah." There was more, of course, but Hepton didn't want that. That betrayal was not something Easton liked to remember. That period was isolation. Stan Hepton turned around fully, his pale hands hidden inside the pockets of his trenchcoat, reducing him to a livid head atop a business-like shadow. "See, what I'm curious about is how much contact you still have with him," Hepton explained. "Very tricky guy, you know." Easton's lips thinned against each other. "You think I'm tricky?" "I think maybe he taught you more than you're letting on." Easton folded his arms to hide the fact that he was balling his fists, to hide the fact that he would have liked to shatter Hepton's jaw. Not that he was doing such a good job of disguising his face. "I'm gonna have to ask you to drop this." Hepton stepped forward, keeping a tight rein on his swagger but letting it show nonetheless. "Maybe he had to reduce his visibility, so he gets his student to stay on the outside for him and manages to get himself tossed in jail." "I'm not gonna ask you again." They snared each other's eyes. Easton's bitterness was easy to read. Stan Hepton's frustration was easier. Hepton pulled his hands free of his pockets and adjusted the collar of his coat. "We suspect he's behind them," Hepton said. "They've got his mark. You're the only guy we know that has it." Hepton's eyes touched Easton's tattooed forearms before he met those green eyes again. "We came to you. What do you know?" Easton felt like wrapping his forearms up – Hepton would not stop taking note of the Wheels and Dragons – but he didn't. "Nothing," Easton said. "I didn't kill anyone for him, he didn't tell me he was killing anyone. He taught me to meditate. That's all." "Tomahawk Buddhist, huh?" Hepton cracked a smile. "I guess." "Listen, Easton," Hepton said, taking a step forward, trying to come close so he could tower over Easton. "The man is dying, but something's happening. Somehow his hand is involved in this. He pretty much said that to me. To my face, Easton. He has agents out there. What I need to know is if you're one of them." Hepton's eyebrows furrowed. "When you get back into the States, I'm gonna do everything in my power to get phone records, to watch you. If I find out you're working for Makhpyia Sapa, I'm going to ruin your life. Understand me?" Easton drew himself up, tilting his head back slightly so he could look Hepton full in the face. "I understand you," he said. "As long as you leave my wife out of this." Hepton chuckled with closed lips. "Now you know I have to go after her, don't you?" Easton pressed his teeth together and showed them to Hepton. "You're walking a line you don't want to walk, man." Hepton's big hand clapped on Easton's shoulder. "I got a job to do. Why don't you concentrate on nirvana, chief?" Easton's eyes swam like lava. When Hepton moved to get past him, Easton let him go. He'd have to call Jeanne. He'd have to try and get someone up to New York and look out for her. Easton's forearms had been hurting him recently. He rubbed his fingers along the dragons inscribed along his skin. He, of course, wouldn't know about the full extent of those killings until he got back to the States. To say that he was ignorant, though, would be hyperbole. Easton hated his dreams.
