Venice Lifestyles - S01E05 - Home Again Too Soon

May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 2009 May 23, 2009 – Boston The sounds of jet engines worked well to drown out the sounds of the many complaining people standing at the Continental Airlines counter at Logan International Airport. Just a few minutes before, he took his first steps on home soil in over a year. In mere moments, he would be taking his first deep breaths of New England air. He wasn't exactly ecstatic to be back home especially since he was living the life most would kill for in Venice beach, but somehow just being back covered him with a sense of comfort. He was concerned all it would be though was a cover and if the shit hit the fan when he got to the house, he'd be left back out in the cold. He couldn't let that concern him though as he lifted his big bag off the conveyor and extended the handle dropping it to the floor where the rubber wheels made a faint clacking sound. He turned to nod politely to the woman standing beside him as he began his trek out the door. It was only ten feet away that he was stopped by the sight of Olivia. She was the young girl on the plane who was flying alone to see her mother. Her Dad sent her back to the East Coast from Los Angeles. "My Mom is running late." Nitz stopped and sat down beside her, leaving his bag at his side. "You just got here on a plane and on time meanwhile she can't get here on time in a car from a few miles away?" "Tell me about it." The young girl replied, using the sarcasm to bring a smile to Nitz's face. "And here I wanted to have a few words with her about sending a young kid on a plane alone. No offense or anything..." "None taken, but as I said before the flight, it's like talking to a wall with her." "Really, have you ever been... I dunno... tested? I mean, it's like I'm talking to someone older than me." "Never bothered. I begged to just be left alone, so I can enjoy myself." Nitz nodded, thinking about the many kids who don't have the chance to be kids. He saw it as a shame because a proper childhood is valuable. "You've got a gift kid. Never forget it." He messed up her hair in the front a little bit. She giggled as he rose to his feet. "Thanks for treating me like a kid. It's refreshing for a change." "You be careful." "I will and I'll watch you on TV if you ever come back to it." "Think that's wise? I didn't exactly talk like a kid on there." "Remember who you're talking to. I know the difference between right and wrong more so than many adults do." Nitz smiled and nodded at Olivia while waving. She waved back and turned to wait patiently for her mother. He glanced back at her one more time before moving in full stride to the front doors of the airport with his rolling luggage in tow. He wades through the numerous people and the sunlight immediately drenches him as he sets foot onto the sidewalk. "Not as warm as Cali, but still nice." He paused, trying to decide how exactly he wanted to get to Mikey's restaurant. He briefly considered jetting out in one of the many taxi cabs lining the curb just a few yards to his right, but he thought better of it and began his trek to Enterprise. A rental car would afford him more freedom; freedom he hoped to parlay into spending as little time at home as he possibly could. It wasn't that Nitz hated his family. He really did love them, but he was just tired of the drama. He had no idea if his father was able to stay on the wagon, his mother's cancer was in remission and he had no clue what the deal was with his brother. He'd heard he was back living in Boston, abandoning the life he built out in the North West. The only certainty was his sister, whom he'd talked to for a few short minutes on the phone. She seemed happy and it confused him. It even caused him to wonder if he misjudged what his family became sometimes. Fat chance he knew, but slight as it may be, it could be legit. |~o~| He slid the gear shift into park and killed the engine of the rental with one quick flip of the key. He slid out of the drivers seat after opening the door and lightly closed it, pressing the button on the small key chain remote to lock the doors. He turned and slid his sunglasses up from over his eyes and took in a sight for sore eyes. Mikey's Chowder restaurant, largely unchanged since he last saw it certainly made him feel all warm and fuzzy inside. It was almost like he was home and his actual home was just a stop in between. He'd felt bad for not keeping up with Mikey over the phone, but he knew his good friend would understand. He knew the deal when Nitz went overseas to train and that one day he'd suddenly show up again. Unless Hailey had already told him, he certainly wouldn't expect him back now. Last time he was gone, it was quite a bit longer than a year. He slipped his sunglasses back on and stepped up on the curb. He pushed the door open and took in the uncompromised aroma of the chowder he'd drooled over many evenings while watching TV in Venice. He looked around a little as the door slowly shut behind him, seeing a few people enjoying their personal favorites. He couldn't recognize anyone, but he looked through the small serving window into the kitchen and saw Mikey hard at work chopping up some ingredients for his next batch. A small smile developed in the corner of his mouth as he pulled out a chair and sat in his usual spot. His longer black hair covered his eyes somewhat and he was sure that Mikey wouldn't recognize him, nor take the time to actually look before he asked about a beverage. The owner of his favorite establishment in Boston backed out through the double kitchen doors with four full bowls of chowder balanced in his hands and on his forearms complete with rolls. He hurried over to a table in the corner and delivered the hot food to the pleasure of the guests without spilling a single drop. Nitz held the menu up in front of his face leaving just enough room to see just when Mikey would be coming his way. His friend wiped his hands on the apron tied around his waist and made a motion towards Nitz's table. He covered his eyes the rest of the way with the menu leaving only his hair to distinguish himself. Mikey approached the table and reached into his pocket to remove a notepad and a pencil, using the eraser to briefly scratch the site of his head under his ball cap. "Hey pal, what can I get for ya?" Nitz laughed a little on the inside, doing his best to not be recognized. He didn't clear his throat before speaking. "What's good here?" Mikey almost rolled his eyes at the question but retained his composure, feeling somewhat happy that he'd attracted a new customer. "Well depends what you like. My famous New England blend is what sells the most and some people like the-" "Like I don't know what's here you fuck." he smiled brightly after dropping the menu from his face to look up at his longtime friend. Mikey did a double take, more worried about being sworn to by a patron. "You gotta be kidding! Nitz?!" Questioned the original creator of his nickname. "I'll be goddamned! Is that really you?" Donnelly rose from his seat, wrapping his arms around his friend who immediately returned the favor. "In the flesh bud." "Christ." Mikey took a seat right beside him. "I didn't think you'd be back so soon this time." "Not back exactly, just a visit." "How so?" "I've been living in Venice since January." "Venice? You ain't no surfer dude. What's the deal?" "While I was in Japan I got a message and went to see Letty on the West Coast." "That's right! Little Leticia. I haven't heard the name in years!" He smiled, remembering the times they all spent together as kids. "How's she been?" "Very long story that I'll get to, but help a brother out man. I can barely stand going a few days without a bowl when I am here... just imagine how I've felt for a little over a year now." "GLENDA! Need a bowl and a beer out here!" It was a common happening for Mikey to talk to his customers so yelling out to the kitchen for his wife's help was also just as common. In a matter of minutes, she would emerge with his food and he couldn't wait. "Been droolin' for this stuff ever since I bought the plane tickets." "So what's the deal man? You livin' the life out there in Lipstick City?" He playfully slapped Nitz's arm. "Good as it can be. I miss being around here, but I don't regret it." "You know you've become what you hated about your brother." "Jesus, low blow much dickhead?" After the arguments and fights between Nitz and his brother Chad, he'd figure Mikey would take it easy on him. But it was their way... pull no punches. Nitz did leave home and is now living out west much like his brother was doing, a fact definitely not lost on Nitz. "Just sayin'. I know the circumstances are different. But let's call a spade a spade. You can bet he may say the same about you." Nitz nodded as he heard footsteps approaching from behind. An icy cold beer was placed in front of him off to the right and a bowl of chowder was placed directly in front of him with a fresh baked roll accompanying the tasty dish. Then a large pair of breasts complete with a plunging neckline and deep cleavage invaded his vision. "Whoa-HOLY SHIT!" He yelped, winning some attention from the other people enjoying their meals. He backed away in surprise and looked up to see the much more youthful face of Mikey's wife Glenda. She looked 21 again and at 35 that was quite an accomplishment based on how she looked before. Mikey married an older woman much to the envy of some of their friends a few years back. On top of the wrinkle-free complexion, the bulging breasts that were mere inches from his face were a new addition. "Hey Nick." "Hey yourself... what the-... I barely recognized you Glenda." "Like 'em buddy?" It was a rather inappropriate question, but Mikey and Nitz had always talked freely about each others women in the past. Glenda was used to it. "Like them? Surprised with my eyes bulging out pretty much describes it." "I got them a few months back. Friends bought me a makeover and this was the next step into surprising Mikey. He had no idea what I was planning." "Some woman." Nitz added as he spooned the first taste of the chowder into his mouth, almost melting into his chair as his taste buds lit up with excitement. He enjoyed it for a second and then swallowed. "Sure wouldn't mind coming home to that." "Play your hand right and she'll show you in private." Mikey laughed and his wife punched him in the shoulder lightly for his dirty comment. Nitz just stared at him, jaw agape in amazement as Glenda made her way back to the kitchen area. "What was that all about?" Nitz questioned, taking a quick bite of the fresh roll that he dipped into the bowl. "She was going through something of a mid-life crisis at a younger age. So she got the makeover and got the chesticles and surprised me with it all. Ever since then life in the bedroom has been one non-stop roller coaster ride that'll eventually kill me." "Yeah ok, but that doesn't explain the show me in private comment." "We've been swingers for a couple months now." He couldn't hide the smile sneaking it's way out. "Bullshit! Glenda?! I don't believe it." "Ask her yourself brother. Like I said, crazy roller coaster. We've been with a married couple or two. She's been playing with the guys and the girls." "Ok now I know you're shittin' me. Glenda would never go muff diving." "She has and she loves it. Sometimes I think that if she wasn't with me, she'd be a full blown lesbian." "I step out from Boston and I come home to a strange new world." Nitz shakes his head and takes a swig of his beer as a chaser to the chowder. "Enough about that. Hailey or anyone else know you're coming?" "Hailey knows. She wasn't gonna tell anyone." "Did she tell you about Scott?" Nitz frowned, expecting bad news. "I haven't heard from Scott or about him for a long time. Isn't he still playing football?" "Oh crap..." Mikey put his face in his hands and deeply sighed. |~o~| The rental car was parked a couple blocks away. Not really for a specific reason, but after the news he'd heard from Mikey, he wasn't in much of a mood to see family. Not like he was before, just now he felt really down. He was gonna put a mask on that though. He turned the corner and walked under the trees, his hands in his pockets and his head hanging to watch his feet make every step. Every now and then he would look up into the newly developed leaves in the trees swaying back and forth with every light breeze. He looked a few houses down and could see his parents home... his home. All the lights were on and he could see silhouettes moving behind the partially drawn curtains. Checking out the bushes next door, he noticed that a path was still made through them leading into his back yard. They all used to use it as kids to sneak in and out of the yard without being seen from the windows. As he stepped between the branches, he had a flashback to being younger. The memories of those days made him wish he was that age again. No worries, no problems, no adult issues. Things just slid off his back when he was a kid, just like most kids. Kids wake up in the morning worried about what cereal they would eat or what games they would play outside after school. Adults wake up worried about paying bills, their health and other important assorted adult issues. Being young lacked the stress of everyday life. He broke through the final bush and stepped into the backyard. A figure was visible in the corner of the yard under a large tree by the fence, a puff of smoke extending into the hair above. Hailey was sitting on the same stump she always sat on as a kid. Everyone knew it was Hailey's tree stump as kids. Everyone else sat on it but when Hailey was around, it was hers without asking someone to move. That very stump used to be her favorite tree. She stared out the window as a toddler and watched the branches move with the winds and heard the rain repel down off the leaves. A bolt of lightning struck that tree during a freak storm and it needed to be removed. The Donnelly's never paid the money to have the actual stump dug out of the ground, so she claimed it as her own. Not much light shined in the backyard so he wasn't seen as he emerged from the bush. But Hailey heard him as he stepped on the gravel and the broken twigs on the ground. "Welcome home Nicky." He could make out her smiling face. He loved his sister, truly he did and with a feeling he couldn't fully explain, seeing her made him happy he came home. It even made him forget about Scott for a short time. "Thanks Hail." He approached the stump with his hands still in his pocket. Hailey took a drag from the cigarette and the burning ash lit up her face a little before she stopped and blew out the smoke. "You smoking again?" "I tried to quit. I'm gonna try again soon." "Try harder, that shit's suicide." He sounded like a parent and his sister took notice. "Yes Dad!" She laughed briefly before sighing. "I'm guessing from your attitude that Mikey told you about Scott." Nitz's head immediately sank, looking at his shoes in the grass. He didn't have to answer her, his actions spoke all the words needed. "Sorry I didn't tell you on the phone, but I figured news like that would have stopped you from coming back home." "You're right." He paused, raising his head to look directly up instead. "What exactly happened?" "He was playing football in a league around here after graduating from college. He was running back like always and he was tackled pretty hard. He wasn't moving. He's been a paraplegic ever since. But through some therapy and training, he's been getting feeling back and has been walking a few steps every day." "I don't think I could go visit him..." "Oh come on, you have to Nicky." "I don't wanna see him in that vulnerable state." It was childish he knew, but something made him feel strange. Back when they were kids even, Scott was always the natural athlete. He could play virtually any sport and as he grew up, he chose football. His parents weren't into it at first, but it grew on them. His mother didn't like the violence but she cheered when he won and was upset with him when he lost. Hailey rubbed the butt of her smoke on the side of the stump, putting it out and stomping it into the soil as she dropped off the stump. She approached her younger brother and wrapped her arms around him, smothering him with a warm hug. He returned the favor and relaxed instantly. Hailey was really the only thing pulling him back towards home and possibly the only family member he wanted to see in Venice. She released her grip and stepped back, giving him a look. "You ready for this?" He sighed deeply. "Would you believe me if I said yes?" "Hell no! Hehe." Hailey took his hand and led him to the back door of the house. It was now or never. To Nitz, never seemed more favorable. |~o~| "Well color me shocked." He sat on his perch on the old stove in the kitchen, his favorite place to sit when he was a kid. He tried to make sense of the scene in front of him, but he just couldn't do it. After entering the house and sharing hugs with his parents, he couldn't stop staring. His mother Trina looked fantastic. The cancer she was suffering from gone almost completely and she looked younger than ever. But as good as she looked, he was not the surprise. Cameron, his father, didn't look anything like the person he remembered. He had a smile on his face, pouring a cup of tea for his wife and himself before setting the pot back on the counter where he got it. He then took a seat right beside her and spooned a single scoop of sugar into his tea. He had lost all his extra weight and colored his hair, leaving a small bit of gray on the sides. He looked younger, but most importantly he looked healthy. "I've been clean since the bottle incident Nick my boy." His father explained, holding hands with Trina. It was like they fell in love all over again, like they were teenagers again. Nitz could remember the blood stained carpet and furniture and the lights of the ambulance flashing through the window. Cameron barely survived that suicide attempt and if it wasn't for the quick thinking of Nitz, he wouldn't have. His mother smiled. "It's great to see you son. Hailey was saying that you've been staying in Venice with Leticia. How is she and her father?" His mother took a sip of tea after she spoke and it gave him just enough time to gather his thoughts. It didn't feel like it, but he was talking with his parents. It was nothing like he ever remembered. "They are doing well. I've been training more as they own a gym out there. I've been working for Mr. Mendoza at his condo." "Doing what?" "Security. No big deal really. Big wig business men coming in and out for meetings." He wouldn't dare get into the slightly dirty aspects of his job. He wasn't exactly breaking the law, but it wasn't exactly morally correct either. "Sounds like fun. Always wanted to go out there." His father said as he stirred his tea. "See Nicky, told you things were good." Good? He didn't see this as good. He saw this as an alternate reality, kind of like Back to the Future where Marty's father stood up to Biff in the past and back in the present both his parents were successful, healthy and more youthful. No, not kind of like it. Exactly like it. He didn't take it the wrong way, like his leaving caused his parents to get their loves together. Instead he was actually enjoying being home. It wasn't that long ago that he was sitting in the same spot, talking to his mother and sister about breast cancer with his father mumbling drunken words from his La-Z-Boy in the living room. "I'm speechless Hailey. I never thought this was what I'd see when I walked through the door. The only question I have is..." He couldn't finish his sentence as he heard someone coming up the back steps. A second later, the door opened and in stepped the one person that Nitz didn't want to see at all. He had hoped it wouldn't come to this. Hailey was the first to speak. "Chad, I thought you weren't going to be back until later." She was clearly surprised to see him, like she planned in advance for him to not be there. "I wasn't going to be but I-" He stopped as soon as he could feel a set of eyes staring absolute death at him. The last time the two brothers came face to face Nitz had just punched Chad square in the face, a punch more than three years in the making. It was for leaving while their mother was sick, leaving his poor innocent girlfriend behind and basically only thinking about himself. Now he was back in Boston and it wasn't like he was expecting to avoid this moment the whole time he was in town. No, it was more like he was scared to lose his cool in front of his parents and Hailey again. "Nick." Chad looked at his brother, not knowing what to think of the daggers he was getting in return. Hailey was concerned things would go too far and the evening would end in disaster. Of course, Nitz's big mouth did nothing to help problems. "Asshole..." It was the only word he spoke as he hopped off the stove, his steel gray eyes locked with a purpose on Chad Donnelly.
