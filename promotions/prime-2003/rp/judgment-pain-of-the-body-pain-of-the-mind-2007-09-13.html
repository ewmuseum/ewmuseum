Judgment: Pain of the Body, Pain of the Mind

13 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/13/07 “So this is Chandler Tsonda, the loser.” DiPendo’s office smelled the same, a refreshing sterility. Faint cigar smoke loitered near the door I always came in through. Even the shrink needed a no-holds-barred escape from normal life. Hell, when normal life is listening to the crazies talk about how Daddy was a rolling stone, I’d light up a couple sticks myself every now and again. “You watched?” Dr. DiPendo’s curiosity regarding Chandler Tsonda the wrestling entity was perplexing. He had to fix my head; the body was already willing. To hurt, to suffer, to repent. To make use of physical ability given to the top one percent of the top one percent. The doctor nodded slowly and deliberately. Not an old man, DiPendo was a wise owl, old in experience. From the spectacles slinking down his nose to the way he subconsciously flicked his top lip with his tongue when listening intently. I always half-expected him to ask me how many licks to take down a Tootsie Pop. “If you’re gonna lose,” I started, “you might as well lose a crapshoot like that one.” I chuckled to myself; it really was ridiculous. “You, me, Rayne, and all the functioning humans on Earth realize I was one-upped only in the category of sneakery. He didn’t outwrestle shit.” DiPendo looked at me incredulously. “What? Am I supposed to express my inner-angst in a family-friendly way? No four-letter words?” “No,” he calmly replied, “but that’s a surface reaction. Not interesting to me and now,” he looked down at his watch, “…costing you money.” “Doc, I know it looks far, but getting down off the high horse won’t kill you.” I was there to rack the man’s brain and here was hubris burning through all else. His facial expression rarely changed. The matter-of-fact treatment was like a game; could anything get a rise out of him? “Pride before the fall, Chandler. And if you want to pal around, I can ask for autographs and you might as well light your money on fire. Everything I do is to pry open your unaccommodating psyche.” It didn’t feel like scolding, but I somehow still felt the transfer of guilt. Not for the money, of course, but for the stubborn, renegade complex. “So read me the Cliffsnotes on my state of mind right now, then. Just how does losing chip away at the marble effigy of Chandler Tsonda?” These challenges had become a customary part of my visits; he bullied me into uncomfortably personal epiphany, so I consistently put his psychiatric skills on the spot. “You still want to care too much.” I almost went to interrupt, but a passive wave of the hand cut me off and DiPendo continued, like he was reading stats of a box score. “You want every loss to crush you inside; you want to hurt, even when beaten by a competent foe in an unsporting manner. But I think you’re still faking it. You can’t muster up real pain, only misspent, self-eroding anger.” An obscured smile slipped across my face, quickly disappearing as I met DiPendo’s eyes. The pain train kept rollin’ through. “That’s why losses make you vulnerable, both as a wrestler and as a person. Lose once, lose every time. That’s you. One chink in the armor splinters outward until the whole damn thing crumbles.” I snorted out a self-conscious laugh. “You’re laughing right now because it just dawned on you: this is the crux of your failure.” Watching the wheels spin in this man’s head was comparable to watching a great marathoner run; silent cogs were clicking, as his eyes stayed beady and focused narrowly ahead. Halting for a moment, DiPendo chose his next words carefully. “And I would be afraid. Very afraid that everyone in your life is content to watch you feeding the beast and continuing down the road of inevitable implosion. Anger begets loneliness.” Dr. DiPendo had risen to the occasion. My silence, a rarity, hung as loudly as any words could have. Anxiously, I shifted my jaw from side to side, trying to take everything in while letting the doctor mentally shifted back to the defensive. “That’s all?” I grinned weakly at DiPendo, whose stoic face returned nothing. “I’ll tell you what I’m afraid of: dying without an heir, slit wrists, leeches, and Panzer tanks. Fuck the rest of it. A man makes his own future.” For the first time, DiPendo looked genuinely disappointed. Like HIV positive disappointed. Not his face, but his shoulders dropped their alert attentiveness. “You haven’t listened,” he sighed, “to me at all.” The old Chandler Tsonda welled up inside me, forcing out clenched fists that I hid by cracking my knuckles. This was not his usual call for mental stimulation, but an alpha male challenge. I excitedly opened my mouth to counter. “The good doctor tells me what to fear now. Can I get a comprehensive list of what emotions to feel and when? I might mistakenly turn into a human or something.” My voice had turned bitter cold, like an old flag whipped about by a blizzard. This office had never been good in terms of chalking a white line and saying this is personal and all else is merely good psychiatry. “Chandler,” he pleaded, completely sincere but not desperate, “…it would take more than I’ve got going on upstairs to predict your future. I don’t even dare predict your match on Sunday. But I’ll be damned if I can’t see the footprints down a certain road right now; I can see them because they’ve been tread too many times before. Humor me, if you will, on a mythology kick.” No words coming immediately to mind, I just flippantly prodded him onward with a flick of the hand. He ignored my noncommittal involvement in his current digression. “The Norse god Odin was near omnipotent. He knew that his death would come only at the end of all things.” Desperate to cut in and seek the shortcut to the moral of the story, I snuck forward in my chair. “There came a point where Odin was faced with an opportunity to consolidate all his power. He was offered the chance to see the future. The price was one of his eyes.” “Doc, c’mon, I promise I’ll be good if you cut out the-” “Stop thinking and just listen. Odin pondered the deal, reluctant to part with his sight. From his throne, he could see the whole world and losing an eye was no small sacrifice.” DiPendo was griot and excited child all in one; the confident doctor conveyed the tale to me in measured prose, but his voice struggled to stay even, like he was learning the story all over again with every word uttered. “Ultimately, he made the trade, giving up an eye for the ability to see the future.” DiPendo danced around the point some more, raising a finger. “But there was a catch: Odin didn’t receive a magic portal to look forward in time. The gift of sight was given to him by extending to him all the knowledge of the past. He learned to see the future by knowing everything that had ever happened before.” The point was evident: there are only so many roads we can all travel, most of which land us in familiar territory to someone. Life has no original ideas anymore either. The good lapdog plays along to fool master into thinking he runs things. “So you can’t see the future, but the fact that you can sense it from past experience should be bad omen numero uno for me?” DiPendo cocked his head just slightly enough to let me know that this was a new strain of thought. As he thought up an appropriate scholarly response, I looked down at my hands as the fists unraveled themselves, flopping open like weathered petals. “I get paid whether you self-destruct or not. I can’t bring you back to harmony. But your demons would have a field day if you shrugged this off.” He snuck a glance to his cigar window, deservedly longing for that Cuban branded release. “I hate to be the doomsday crier, but I think we both know you’re content to coast along right off the cliff’s edge.” “The hyperbole runneth over.” After significant mental lifting, the doctor now cracked a wry, fox’s smile. I let DiPendo see me look down at my watch, then pulled my head back up. “And I’ve still got half a session left. Wanna solve world hunger?” “I know it’s out of the question,” he near-whispered, “but this once…could you just tell me, somehow, that you believe me? I don’t care if you lace it with sarcasm. I understand vulnerability is not your strong suit.” My heart pumped a little faster, flailing rapidly to escape the ribcage. Feeling my throat drying up, I went for the gusto, offering up what I could. “I get it. I get you. And I want to help you see these things. I appreciate you digging into the bag of tricks, pulling out mythological parallels, throwing the facts in my face. It’s important to me.” My throat fought back, catching me in mid-thought and forcing a pause. Barely tethered sanity is a terrible thing to waste. “There you go. Here’s the part where you bring in my multi-million dollar offer from the Arizona Cardinals and I cry in front of Roy Firestone.” The switch was back on; shielded behind my normal self, I watched DiPendo touch his tiny notepad for the first time. It was indifferently blank. Sensing he still wasn’t ready to say anything, I filled the silence. “What do you think about Sunday?” “I think it’s an important step.” It was good to be back in a world where Dr. DiPendo was certain of so many things. “You and Mr. Williams have a checkered past. This is probably as good a release as any.” I wanted to believe that, but what would end? My hate for Hoyt was eternal, I was fairly sure. And I wanted to like Rayne, who had no reason to campaign for my inclusion in the match. Perhaps this was the phenomenon known as honor. “I can’t see the future either, doc. But I don’t really know. Whoever wins this match is on the fast track to the Universal Title. So that’s a dream, I guess.” I wanted to sound more confident, but my voice trailed off, making the proclamation sound like a little kid’s aspirations to one day be an astronaut. “A man should be excited about the unlimited possibility beyond him.” A man should, but wrestling ain’t your average slice of life. One minute you’re uncertain, the next you’re irrelevant. “And if I lose? What then?” “‘If your foot causes you to stumble, cut it off; it is better for you to enter life lame than to have two feet and to be thrown into hell.’” I waited for an explanation. DiPendo looked more playful than usual, finally detailing his proverb. “From the Gospel of Mark. Pretty good advice, no?” “Ehh, I’m not much for the Bible. I’ll wait for the movie.” I’d had enough melodrama for a day without offering up my religious ruminations. “No offense.” “None taken, I’m a man of science. I don’t completely disregard the wisdom within just because I don’t agree with the zealots.” In a funny sort of way, faith was the only thing I had left. Not in some magician from Galilee two thousand years ago. But in the natural way of things working out. At some point, you have to believe in something. “If my foot causes me to stumble, Monday will be a sad wake-up call. Wish me luck.” DiPendo closed his notebook and put it down next to him, rising with the spry energy of a much younger man. The confidence in his face was less contained than usual; perhaps he believed in me. He extended a hand. “Good luck, Chandler.” I shook his hand, nodded to him and started out of the office. A question lingered, but one that would not be answered Sunday or any of the coming Wednesdays. The future was the question. Terrifying and limitless, the future stayed with me. Did Rayne and Hoyt have these lingering questions? Soon, all the psychology of it wouldn’t matter. And three men would end up in a ring. And one wouldn’t need to worry about the future…he could bask in victory for a sweet, short moment. The preparation of the mind was done. It was time to bleed.
