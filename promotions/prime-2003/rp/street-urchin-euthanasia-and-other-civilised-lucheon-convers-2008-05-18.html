Street Urchin Euthanasia and Other Civilised Lucheon Conversation Topics

18 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 18 May 2008 As Union Jack passed through the impressively gigantic wooden oak doors and entered the rather sizeable ballroom, he nodded favourably at the elegantly attired doorman who wore a blatant false grin upon his face and didn‘t try all that hard to make it convincing. Jeeves, Union Jack’s ever faithful butler, raised an eyebrow and nodded, very definitely impressed. “Did you note the fabric, sir?” He asked, glancing back over his shoulder for a second look. Union Jack nodded, smiling. “Of course I did, Jeeves. It was made entirely of the finest velvet. Very elegant. I may ‘borrow‘ the idea for a future soirée I have in mind.” “I’ll make a note of it, sir,“ Jeeves told him, discretely removing a small, black notepad and an exquisite silver pen from the inside of his jacket pocket. He clicked down on the ballpoint and scribbled down the note as instructed. As he sifted through the crowd of very important, and undoubtedly very rich, people, he noted with great satisfaction that nobody turned around to glare at him, despite his decidedly unorthodox attire. He wore a ten thousand dollar tuxedo, crafted by the very best Italian designers, but obviously that was not out of the ordinary in a crowd such as this, what made him standout was the fact that he wore his Union Jack wrestling mask, with his red, white and blue sparkling sequins over his face; standard attire for him on any occassion. Ordinarily, this would be a major talking point of any important charity event such as this, but only the richest of the rich could both tolerate and perhaps even applaud his little eccentricities in the same breath and that could only mean one thing: they’d already accepted him. Word had already spread throughout the barrage of the wealthy and powerful in attendance about an eccentric British aristocrat who turned hand to the colourful world of wrestling, probably even before he’d, or more accurately Jeeves had, RSVP’d their very generous invite last week and naturally they were already enraptured with him without even being formally introduced. It was only the common adolescent waiters and waitresses that he caught ogling him quite openly. He glared at the latest with distaste as he swiftly scooped a slender glass of the finest champagne and took a gratifying sip, allowing the intoxicating bubbly liquid to roam around his mouth pleasingly. “Good, sir?” Jeeves asked, with an inquisitive raised eyebrow. Union Jack nodded. “The very best. Be sure to enquire about their supplies.” “Very good, sir.” Again Jeeves extracted his notepad and pen and scribbled another note. “Remind me, Jeeves,” Union Jack began glancing around the room, idly searching for his assigned table amongst the two dozen or so laid out, all focused towards the grandiose stage and podium at the forefront of the room, “what is this a charity event for, exactly?” “I believe it’s aimed at helping underprivileged children, sir,” Jeeves answered, beckoning over a uniformed seating assistant. It was a teenaged girl with freckles and braces, not even attempting a false smile and she certainly wasn’t rushing to give her aid. Union Jack raised a speculative eyebrow. “Ah yes, that’s right. This is for the bloody street urchins, isn‘t it?” “I believe the more preferred term is ‘Orphans’, sir,” Jeeves explained. “Calling them ’bloody street urchins’ will undoubtedly alienate you from the rest of the guests.” Jack nodded. “Understood.” The seating assistant finally made her way over, she was chewing gum furiously and had a completely disinterested expression plastered across her face, but at least she made a little effort at sounding sincerely and helpful. “Can I help you, sir?” “Yes,” Jeeves told her. “My master Lord Wilberforce requires to be seated.” The chewing seating attendant glared at Union Jack over Jeeves shoulder, cocked her head to one side and glared at him for the longest time. “Is there something wrong with his face?” Union Jack’s eyes widen. “WHAT?” “Is he horribly disfigured or just is he just ugly or something?” She continued, in between aggressive chews. “How DARE she talk to me in such a manner!” Union Jack exclaimed, clearly outraged. “Jeeves, I order to you to punch her in the face!” The girl glared at him puzzled, unsure of whether to laugh in face or call security or do both. But Jeeves knew better; he knew that his master was perfectly serious and thus needed to dissuaded from such an heinous act. “Sir, I hardly think that’s appropriate, I--” Union Jack shook his head. “Okay, I’ll do it then! For god‘s sake, Jeeves! Do you have no backbone?” Union Jack drew his arm back and sized up the punch, but suddenly paused on the spot and turned to Jeeves once more. “Could you at least hold her, Jeeves? I don’t want have to do this twice!” “ARRRRRGGGGGGGGHHHHHHHHHH!” The girl ran away screaming, with tears streaming down her cheeks. “He’s going to punch me in the face!!!!!!” “Quick, Jeeves!” Union Jack bellowed after her. “She’s getting away!” Jeeves sighed. “I don’t think chasing down the hired help and punching her in the face would be good for public relations.” “Says you,” Jack replied, “I’d say if our esteemed hosts heard what that--that…bloody chav said to me, they’d be joining in!” “Doubtful, sir,” Jeeves told him, “the event organisers are using some the orphans as staff tonight. You just threatened to punch in the face one of the very people this charity event is set up to help, sir.” Union Jack froze on the spot. “WHAT? We’re surrounded by them?” Jeeves nodded. “Yes, sir.” Union Jack shook his head. “We are surrounded by street urchins pretending to be event staff?” Jeeves nodded again. “No, sir. They ARE the event staff. The organisers thought it would ‘cute‘.” “Wait…where’s my wallet?“ Union Jack pats down his tuxedo jacket and pants frantically. “I can’t find my wallet! These bloody Chav’s have stolen my wallet!” “Sir, you don’t have a wallet,” Jeeves informed him. “You don’t carry spare cash around with you, remember?” Union Jack nodded, visibly calming. “Yes, yes. Thank god. You’re right. But if I DID have a wallet, these peasants would‘ve no doubt stolen it already!” “Most likely, sir,” Jeeves agreed (it was a part of his job description, after all). “If you so despise these ‘street urchins’ as you call them, then why pre tell are we attending a charity event for them?” “Two reasons, Jeeves,” Union Jack began, heading off toward a table; not much caring whether he was assigned to it or not. “Firstly, this is why to ‘break in’ to the higher circle of company I’ve already become accustomed to as a wealthy aristocrat and secondly, Jimmy Bonafide.” Union Jack eyed up the people he deems to be the most important in the room and quickly seized a spare seat, hurling away the laminated nametag set out on the table before him, flanked by beautifully folded lilac napkins, haphazardly and without drawing too much attention to himself. Jeeves stood beside him, hands behind his back and wearing that same dignified expression he always when in coming of esteem guests. Nevertheless, he considered his next question to be important enough for to disregard his well-practised etiquette. “Jimmy Bonafide, sir? What does that-that…common criminal have to do with this?” “Calm yourself, Jeeves. Show some decorum, will you?” Union Jack demanded, conveniently forgetting his previous outburst involving punching an adolescent seating assistant in the face. Immediately Jeeves righted himself, standing upright and glaring vacantly into the distance at nothing in particular. “Jimmy Bonafide grew up on the streets and it made him the thug he is today. If more people like me donate to a charity like this, we can prevent thuggery from even being issue. Either that, or we try to get a law passed in that retched White House place that enables us to cull them at birth…” “Well, that might’ve been a more preferable option for Jimmy Bonafide, but culling them ALL at birth, sir?” Jeeves questioned. “Isn’t that a little drastic…even for you?” “You’re talking like a Welshman, Jeeves!” Union Jack declared, desperately attempting to keep his voice lowered. “Where’s your English stiff upper lip? Where’s your god given desire to hold the rest of the world at the barrel of the gun as we once did?” Jeeves shrugged -- or more accurately, what counted for a shrug given his gentlemanly manner. “I’ve not really given it much thought to be honest with you, sir.” Union Jack nodded. “And that’s precisely why you’re a butler Jeeves and I’m an aristocrat.” “Very good, sir.” “Now leave me, Jeeves and make the appropriate preparations for Ultraviolence,” Union Jack told him. “We must be prepared to face this street thug in battle on his own terms or he may cry like he did the last time we duelled." Jeeves nodded and made his way out of the charity event, leaving his master to plot the downfall of street thugs everywhere.
