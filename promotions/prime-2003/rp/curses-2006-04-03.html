Curses

3 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/3/2006 It’s fucking beautiful outside, with a hint of cautious breeze enough to wake me up. Getting up isn’t pleasant, though: it’s just another week with K-Wolf. No, that’s not the set up for a trash talk promo, it’s the way things are. In terms of comparisons, she’s a virus…with bad fashion sense. This hundred-pound roadblock is one of the biggest of my career; it’s fucking embarrassing. Good thing there’s more to life than wrestling. Days like these don’t come too often so it’s worth it to savor one when you get it. I’ll think about K-Wolf and Jappy Pants when the time comes. Let me just- The Sidekick awakens from its slumber. “Hello?” It’s Gil Lavenstein. Here’s a guy who never calls with good news; seeing the number on the caller ID is like a warning to clench your cheeks and brace for impact. “Listen, Gil, it’s a wonderful day; I can probably get away with some bronzing cream today, so let’s get down to business.” Gil doesn’t shoot the shit; he aims, checks his weapon, and fiddles with the scope before he can take the shot. “No man, I don’t know ‘what would be really great’. I only know that that phrase means you’re gonna ask me to do something gay.” I don’t wanna say Gil’s a weasel, but he’s squirming and squealing over the fact that I know he’s gonna ask me to do something ridiculous. Today is a day for outdoor cardio, San Diego girls in short skirts (I don’t remember why I live here, but this must be one of the reasons), and a busting-out of the $14.99 strawberry daiquiri mix. He tells me I’m being stubborn. “Gil, you’re being a fucking ass. It’s masochistic to spend a day like this doing remedial office work. I don’t have that same self-defeating Jew attitude to do it. I can’t. I won’t”. I don’t pull punches; if I’m gonna pay the guy to just sit in an office and not fuck my career up, I reserve the right to cuss him out. “Don’t you dare ask how I can question the company. Nelson and Motoki will put my death-defying stunts on highlight videos and make a killing without giving me a dime. Loyal foot soldier, I’m not.” It might make sense to point out that Gil’s the reason I’m not getting the royalties like the Wolfendens, Novas, and Hoyts. Ugh, there’s the wench of the skies showing up randomly in my head again. No way am I going into the PRIME offices. Not today. Not a fucking change. * * * * * * Dateline: San Diego State University. Year: 1994. I’m waiting in the lobby of a cheap restaurant, having finished a burger and fries, not-too-hard on my paltry wallet. I model in the time between (and sometimes during) classes. There’s not a lot of hope that I’ll finish things here at SDSU, but things are mostly enjoyable. I’m trying to get enough under my belt that if this whole modeling thing doesn’t work out, I’ll know how to do something. Stacy walks out of the bathroom and I inconspicuously check for residue under her nose. Hey, I fuck around with the green, but I don’t want some cokehead broad dying in my dorm room. I just don’t need one of these trust fund chicks dying in the poor Asian kid’s room. Yeah, I forgot to point out she’s beautiful. I get with the Stacy’s, Natasha’s, Victoria’s (don’t you love how parents just know these girls are going to be stone cold foxes and name them accordingly?)…prime pickings. Not the cheerleaders, but I roll with the next tier of girls; only the athletes get the cheerleaders…it’s just a revolving door with them (I tried out for the gymnastics team, but uniforms were uber-homo). But enough about the girls, this is the girl. She’s short with petite features. Everything is on a smaller-scale. She told me over dinner that she used to want to be a model, but every tryout they told her she was too short. Well, that might’ve been the story; I was sort of in and out with the whole listening piece, but I think it makes an excellent back story, true or not. She smiles, flashing brace-corrected teeth over at me, and does the sort of half-hand grab, then moves past me towards the door. I gauge that I’m supposed to follow and within minutes we’re in her miniature apartment, undoubtedly paid for by mom and dad. I know I’m in when she pulls off her high school lacrosse jacket slowly, releasing soft, tan arms. As much as I know it, I don’t want her to think that I’m the kind of guy who knows when he’s golden. I hate it, but for a frosh, arrogance is a fatal mistake. College girls are not wowed by pricks younger than them; they can go to high school for that. So I’m admittedly not my total self here; I just might not get away with it. Stacy fixes herself a rum and coke and walks past me on the couch, perusing her own DVD collection. She makes several “hmm” noises, more or less asking me to enter my opinions. “What are you thinking about?” I could really care less what movie we “watch,” but whatever makes her feel comfortable while she’s getting invaded is cool with me. “Well, I’ve got Iron Monkey, Once Upon A Time In China…killer fight scenes in that one, and the Bodyguard from Beijing.” Oh dear gawd, no kung-fu movies. I can do campy chick flicks, but how am I supposed to screw watching some ninjas flipping and kicking? I don’t know who can make sweet love watching a bunch of guys prancing around in black robes, but I’m not among them. “Well…which one?”Hey there rock. Hey there hard place. Lemme just squeeze right in there, thanks. “I don’t really know much about kung-fu.” She looks back at me and winks, telling me in no uncertain terms that it doesn’t quite matter if I know anything about the movie. Stacy elects to put in Once Upon A Time in China, chugs the rest of her drink, and grabs a sit very, very next to me. “Don’t worry, it’s not, like, an Asian thing. I’m a pretty big kung-fu buff.” My last hope goes out the window; I can’t start calling her a racist and hope to get laid, but what chance is there to escape the kung-fuck? Some crazy stringed instrument plays the opening credits and her hands are creeping and grasping. The juxtaposition of her hands and the bizarro sitar-sounding music is otherworldly. I really am trying to focus on the beauty who’s migrating towards the driver’s seat on my lap, but it’s harder then it should be. Muscle memory is carrying me right now, lifting her shirt above her head, exposing low B-cups. It’s still uncomfortable setting her up for this while the first major fight scene begins to come together. “Do you want me to turn this down?” Maybe she’ll catch the hint that it’s just too weird. She seems pretty into it; maybe she won’t care. “No, my neighbors have kids. I don’t want them to hear us,” she giggles. Except it’s not coy; it’s not sexy. I pretty much want to leave. “I’m sure they’re asleep,” I lie. She stops pulling off my belt and looks me in the eye. “Chandler, if you want to do this, you’re going to have to deal with the rules here. I’m not fucking you in your shitty little dorm room or in my car, so unless you’re looking for some five-finger knuckle shuffle practice, just deal with it. Guys can just zone it all out when they’re doing it anyway.” How wrong you are, my dear. I grin and bear it. And…just like that we’re doing the deed. I don’t need to share the details of fluids, moans, and angles of bodies. Still, there’s Jet Li and forty other unnamed Chinese guys doing battle while I’m trying to get this whole simultaneous orgasm thing right (and let me tell you, it’s a doozy to try). “Keep going!” In this haze, I can barely tell whether that’s the dubbed American voice in the movie propelling our peasant hero onward or Stacy begging for more pleasure. This was a big mistake. Fuck, I hate ninjas. * * * * * * I’m in the PRIME regional offices, but I don’t like it. It’s really just a floor of a ludicrously nice office building that’s been rented out and festooned with PRIME logos, pictures of Ian English, replicas of all the belts that Kyle Lamen hasn’t ever won, etc. Gil Lavenstein is nowhere to be found, but a silent intern leads me to a regal office with gold trim on the door. Inside is Tara Baginski, a regional agent for PRIME, she tells me. I still don’t understand why I’ve been coaxed from my comfortable apartment to come down here. “Okay, now Chandler, I just spoke personally to Toshiaki Motoki about what I’m going to propose to you, so you know that this is coming from the top down and it’s, hopefully, something that works for the interests of both parties.” “If they’re even considering giving me a paycut”- Two things happen right here. Tara raises her hand to cut me off and the door opens again, except it’s not any business associate or pimple-faced intern kid. It’s a walking poster for Lily Pulitzer. “Ugh, I was doing community service and you pull me into the office?” I wish I’d taught Ellie that sneer, but it appears to be the result of a Kannon education. Bewildered, Tara has to ask. “You were doing community service?” “Well, yah, I was fixing my pool. Well, I wasn’t fixing it, but some drug mule guy was.” Tara, like many women, doesn’t find Ellie amusing. However, I find being around her exhilarating. If there’s ever been a female version of me, it’s her. She’s fashionable, crude, and calculatingly brilliant. Tara almost points out that Ellie is not describing community service. “Ellie, you look marvelous. Doesn’t she, Tara?” The regional agent just grumbles as Ellie sits down, obligated to push her skirt down, due to its eensy weensy size. Scarce few inches of her legs are covered and, as usual, she knows she looks good. “Sure thing. Well, now that Mrs. Kannon-Hall is here, I can pitch the idea to both of you.” Before Tara’s even done speaking, Ellie’s looking at the things on Tara’s desk…and by looking, I mean touching. “Well, the idea is that…Ms. Kannon-Hall, can I help you? “No, I’m good. Keep going with your little speech thing.” Thoroughly frustrated, Tara just decides to continue. “Well, see, you know that PRIME is becoming ever more mainstream, but it’s important we don’t lose sight of the fact that we’ll always have a cult following, especially in Asia.” I can sorta see where this is heading, but I’ll give Tara a chance. “However, the Japanese rarely open up to our gaijins, except for your good friend Mr. Lamen.” A small smirk sits on her face for a millisecond, contented with her small petty victory in her unfulfilling life. “What we’re looking for is to tell your story, along with including several highlights packages.” She knows what she needs to say to start selling this little package. Ellie has pulled out her Sidekick (truly after my own heart!). “The video will get a small DVD release here, but we’re talking probably a hundred thousand units for the first shipment alone. We would give you $15,000 for coming in, doing interviews and voice-overs and so on. That would be in addition to a three percent take on the DVD sales.” I just don’t quite get it. I’m not sure those numbers say that I’m not the only person on the DVD. Tara knows it too. And she knows that I know. “Well, who else is going on the DVD?” Tara grimaces, then puts back on her fake, toothy smile. “Well, right now we’ve locked up Kenjiro Ito, Kyle Lamen, and Xin Xin Xiong.” Dear God, what did I do to join that motley crew? Tara keeps herself measured, breathing slowly. “Lemme just get this right: you want me to go on a video with the guy who cheated me out of the Dual Halo, Super Tumor, and the…stereotypical mysterious Asian ninja?” Cue cringe. Bad memories. Ellie looks up quickly. “Channy, no using the n-word!” “Chandler,” Tara chides, “this is a great opportunity to get exposure and, for some reason, you’re still popular in Asian markets. The Dual Halo and the Lamen thing are building you up and you don’t want to leave behind any fan.” She’s right, but biting my tongue is nary impossible. I’ll be associated with those three douches for a long time to come. When they’re toiling in the midcard, they’ll continue to be raised up by the mention of my name. Then again, there’s always the chance I put the ninja to shame…AGAIN (cue up mental footage of X3’s dive off the DH and then my superior dive to eliminate Kyle Lamen) and be linked to him only in my continual superiority. This week floats back into my head, but there’s no time to worry about Wolfenden (again) and Jappy Pants. Losses only matter if they’re financial. PRIME is ready to open up the bank (only a little) for one of their superstars and that’s what matters. That, and that I don’t lose to “X3.” “Fine.” And just like that, I’m inextricably, in the truest sense of the word, in.
