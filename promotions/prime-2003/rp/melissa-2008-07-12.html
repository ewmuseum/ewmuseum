Date: 7/12/08 “Missy,” Brandon hoarsely spoke, the rhythm of his voice broken by an anxious pause. “I, um,” the answering machine gave his tone an electronic quality, each breath into the receiver a white wash of static. Sitting on one of the kitchen’s barstools, Melissa feebly cupped a ceramic mug filled with coffee made from day old grounds as she vacantly traced the island countertop. Her eyes thirsted for sleep, but her husband’s disappearance wrought only worry with each shifting attempt to rest throughout the night until she simply gave up. Her brain throbbed as a sleep deprivation headache caused the muscles in her neck to tense up; she was in the throes of a morning murkiness she hadn’t felt in years. Even through the mental fog and faint crinkling of the answering machine’s speaker, he husband’s voice was oddly clear. “I got picked up last night. By the police.” It was far too clear. “I, uh, I can’t figure out how to say this, um,” on the last word, the thin line of composure Brandon had been tight rope walking snapped. She could hear his plunge into harsh, unintelligible sobs. Her grip tightened around her coffee cup, the waves of aroma playing second fiddle to the acrid reality she had been warned about years earlier by her parents. “I need you come down here Melissa. I can’t take this anymore,” he spit out before a wobbling inhale interrupted him. “I need you to come down to the Central Community Police station. They got me on, uh,” he couldn’t help but skitter away from the reason. She pushed away from the counter, stomping her feet into the vinyl floor as her bottom lip trembled in barely restrained rage. “I can’t tell you!” His guttural roar did nothing to calm her. She was numb, but the flesh of her face felt as if it would crack from the torrid heat of her temper. “Just say it already!” she roared, coffee sloshing onto her forearm as she launched the cup against the wooden cupboards just above the dishwasher. Even as she felt her skin being seared, she refused to acknowledge it. Paroxysms of rage vibrated throughout her entire essence. “Just,” her lips didn’t respond immediately, causing a momentary pause. “Fucking say it already!” He couldn’t hear her. He couldn’t see what his indiscretion had created. “I am being held here on charges. Charges of, uh, on uh,” each wail was punctuated by wet snorts. “I was caught with a prostitute, okay? Pangs of nausea rapidly fluttered inside her stomach, slipping past her growing child. “They also said I had some cocaine and painkillers and some other shit but I’m telling you Missy, please, I’m begging you, PLEASE forgive me.” His mewling apology broke the dam, the rush of bile escaping through her nostrils before exploding from her gaping maw. It splashed in a downpour all across the steel of the kitchen sink. *** His most profane of abuses was once again crystal clear. She was falling after his fist exploded against her cheek. The thunderclap immediately broke her jaw; her crumpling body dropped limply into the carpet. She struggled to bring her knees up so she could clasp onto them. She needed to hold onto something. “Just please forgive me, okay?” While this was not as overt as what he had done to her three years ago, it was perhaps just as callous. “Just please get me out of here.” The message died as the blood from her soles began to pool around her planted heels. *** It was hot in Austin, the Friday bar rush easing into Saturday’s early morning hours. She was twenty-two and ambling through her Junior year without a definitive focus on the future. This had been the first year she started leaving her dorm room for any sort of social context. Her parents rode her constantly about getting out and meeting people. The University of Texas was one of the largest campuses in the United States; there had to be people she could get along with. The Lone Star was a popular dive sandwiched between smoke shops in Campus Town. The neon orange sign outside the doors had a bull blowing smoke out of its nostrils along with a star branded into its forehead. There was a live band strumming poorly constructed bluegrass melodies in the middle of a sweaty throng of zealous students who wanted to forget about everything else save the intensity contained within those drunken moments. Melissa had pieced together her style to accentuate her curves. Her khaki shorts were dangerously short, and her bust strained against her white and pink blouse. She completed the look with black leather knee-high boots with spikes that clacked with each step on the concrete. Her pace was washed over by the rowdy dissonance of the tightly packed dance floor, effectively cancelling out her original intention of wearing the heels to sound her presence. Pushing through the crowd, all she could think about was getting another drink to cool her throat. She hadn’t danced to a single tune but was already covered in a thin sheen of perspiration. Her glasses were constantly fogged over. She needed to get to the backroom, where the mob wasn’t there to make every single movement a chore. There never was a line for drinks in the back. Crossing past the loaded restrooms, she saw the inebriated couples pressing their bodies against each another as if they wringing out every last drop of their libidos. She sneered unseen towards them, her heart aching with envy. Melissa Garvin hadn’t had a relationship save a few flings in elementary school. Nobody thought the fat girl was attractive. She’d lost fifty-nine pounds over two years, worsening food allergies and traveling by foot the main culprits. Even so, the whispers of her once chubby appearance still remained as a haunting echo left to resonate every single time she looked in the mirror. This was her forth trip to the Lone Star, and she had come here to make an impact. But, like always, nerves got the better of her. As she made her way past the loaded corridor, she was caught in the sparse comfort of her destination. A few couples dotted the landscape, having conversations in close proximity thanks to the booths they sat in. Her curiosity of the people surrounding her caused her to look like a foolish gawker vacantly staring at sets of people she’d never seen before. It was the set of eyes staring back at her that she wasn’t prepared for. They weren’t a couple; at least she didn’t think so. Austin was more progressive because the University was right along the way. They both had long hair, but the Asian was not her flavor. Her heart skipped the moment she caught hold of those green eyes peering in her direction. The pair exchanged a word, then a chuckle. He had certainly dressed the part; tight white t-shirt, blue jeans and boots. He was a mountain of granite with long dirty blond locks and an underwear model veneer. He was the kind of man that she would touch herself at night fantasizing about. And just like in her fantasyland, he was rising from his seat and making his way towards her. She beamed a smile towards him. “You with anybody?” he questioned as his giant paw gently nudged her shoulder. Melissa was frozen. “I, uh,” her entire body broke out into uncontrollable trembles, her skin feeling far too hot. “No, I suppose.” “You suppose?” his chuckling at her response caused her cheeks to become flush. “Settle down, hon. I don’t bite.” With a premeditated crane downward, his lips flashed near her earlobe. “Unless you want me to.” He reset himself deliberately, the cocky smile she saw and the faintly spoken flirtation causing a fluid of white hot heat to grasp her heart. All she could feel was lust. “Oh yeah?” every syllable was drenched by her quivers. All he could do was laugh coyly. “Don’t you go anywhere. I’ll be right back.” He left her there for the moment, joining the line to get into the restroom. She couldn’t help but try to fan herself off, trying her best to collect her composure. The Asian’s gaze was fixed towards the wooden table, his cigarette becoming a long line of ash. He looked back towards her, and began gingerly walking on wobbly legs. When he was within spitting distance, she could smell the weed fumes from his clothes. Cringing away at the stench, she was startled by the firm shake of her shoulder. “With Brandon Youngblood,” his chuckling demeanor instantly broke into somber sentiment. “Caveat emptor.” *** The blood from her wounds smeared in trail leading out of the kitchen. It ran through the main corridor of their flat, then to the left of their bedroom. The white carpet inside was discolored by her footprints. Brandon had made the cradle while he had power tools as collateral when he was renting out the ring he had been using at his wrestling school. He had varnished the wood and brought it home as a big surprise. For the first time in months, she was shocked by his ingenuity; he hadn’t showed any real emotion since Christmas. Planted on the ottoman, she pressed her heels into the carpet and bent over to wrap gauze around her feet. She couldn’t feel the pain though. She couldn’t feel anything. They were not allowed to change the color scheme of the room, which had given her cause to be miffed. She wanted to set the room up just right with the right colors and the right settings and the right stimuli. The crib was all set up with a mattress and sky blue blankets. The rocking chair was plushy and could be slept in comfortably. Brandon had insisted that the baby be allowed the freedom of its own room. He didn’t understand her concerns. He didn’t read up on the risks. While she was terrified at the prospect of her unborn child being out of her sight, he just acted aloof to everything. Her eyes remained focused on the mahogany. Even the most thoughtful of Brandon’s contributions were now made empty. *** Brandon was a quick mover. He’d taken her virginity in the uncomfortable backseat confines of Chevy hatchback not forty minutes later after they had first met. For Melissa Garvin, the entire drunken experience was magical, as if it had come from a Hollywood script. He was the kind of man that made other women intensely jealous, the kind of man who made her feel good because she felt he was way out of her league. But that didn’t stop the overriding issues she still had with her own self image; if anything, the echoes grew more intense. Brandon was a wrestler and his Asian friend Alastair was too. They had been back and forth between Texas and California for well over a year, and were akin to brothers. Her dream boy was sparsely around, but every single time he was back in the Austin area, he was quick to find his way into her dorm room. Alastair would smoke cigarettes on the grounds of the University of Texas while they had sex. Youngblood was a frugal guy, and had somehow become the beneficiary of inflated spending by hungry promoters in an arms race to gobble up young talent. He’d been in the business for a drink of coffee and was already getting paid in the high six figures because entrepreneur Reed Young knew no better. “How’s about we get an apartment?” he asked one night after a string of dates in the south Texas area. It was right before Spring Break, and all she had to look forward to was a trip back home. “Can get something around campus, and anytime I’m around we don’t have to worry about your RA’s.” Her parents didn’t even know about them, and probably would not have approved, but she didn’t care. She was a grown woman, and it wasn’t like she was going to have to pay for the place. Brandon had enough to buy a house in the area if he wanted. Had enough money to slip the ring on her finger, which he said he wanted to do two months in. Said nobody made him ever feel the way she did. “Of course he told you that,” Alastair confided with her on the eve of her Senior year. She’d gone all the way, convinced her worrying parents that she was okay and that her relationship was healthy. Lied to them about what he did for a living. Said Brandon was an investment banker. Their first apartment was within earshot of the Lone Star. And while she was wondering about where to stow her bike away for safety, Youngblood’s road buddy had decided to approach her. They rarely spoke because his breath always reeked from chain smoking. Another reason was because Brandon had planted the seed that Al had a crush on her. It was two in the morning, mere days before class was set to begin. And here she was, outside of his apartment in only boy shorts and an oversized sweatshirt. The neon steer looked at her through the trees. Her bare feet tracked mud around the concrete stoop thanks to the premature walk she had taken seconds before. She refused to look Alastair in the eyes. “Brandon tells every single girl he’s with that same line of bullshit. What? You think that with the kind of cash we both make that we go exclusive?” She refused to believe a word he said because every word wounded her fantasy and hammered home her worst fears. There wasn’t any way to prove whether or not Alastair was telling her the truth; maybe it was a power play from a close friend turned rival. “Just like that clean and sober line he hits you with,” Al chuckled as he lit another menthol off the embers of his last. “I got a cookie jar filled with cocaine back in California says that’s bullshit.” Melissa had grown tired of biting her lip. “Fuck you, Al.” “Oh fuck me?” he grew indignant as he sauntered along the concrete with the rest of the moths under the lamplight. “No bitch; fuck you! I have to put my dick in a vice every single goddamn time we come through this shithole of a state. And why? Because Brandon has to ride his fucking whale!” His eyes were dilated fixtures, the thick haze of tobacco brushing into the trapped space between them. You don’t know what he brings home.” She couldn’t take it anymore. “Just shut up! Just shut up, okay?” Melissa couldn’t help the resonance his words imprinted on her shaky confidence. Her eyes became heavy, her chest shuddering to cease her from croaking as she tried to move away from Alastair. “Look,” he reasoned, his hand reaching out for the small of her back. The faint touch shocked her, causing her to push away. Standing face to face, Alastair tried his best to calm her down. “I’m an asshole, alright. I get that. But Brandon,” the cigarette made it too hard for him to breath. With a fierce rip, he tore it from his lips and threw it into oblivion. He panted for a few moments, his yellow teeth catching in the light of the street lamp. “Brandon is nice now because you give him something. But once you start getting to know him, he becomes unrecognizable. He’s cheated on you. He’s lied to you.” “Why do you care?” “Because you seem like a good kid, that’s why. And because,” stumbling over his words, he pawed his lips and looked away. “Because I like you. Even though I give you a hard—” That was all she needed to hear. But even the hopes contained within this precious life were dimmed by the shroud of its father. All Melissa could think about was her soiled seed. But was this any worse than what had transpired before? At the end of the day, he was still the dream partner; the one other women envied her for. Each blow which perverted his once handsome face eroded the image she had composed of him, and beyond that image she had nothing. Realities of incompatibility hit with fracturing velocity; beyond the sunset rendezvous and the beach front weddings nothing truly held them together. But her child was going to change that. Her son was going to change everything. At the heart of the matter was the fact that she was still shocked by the effrontery of her husband’s infidelity. Plasma seeping through her bandages, she still felt bound to the vows of her marriage. As vile as Brandon could be, she still held them sacrosanct. Brandon had requested her presence in his time of need. She was obligated, she reasoned, to follow through with his request. She pet the circumference of her stomach. Deep inside her, their son was thrashing. *** “It’s not your fault,” Brandon cooed into her ear, his strong hands stabilizing her body weight. Covered in sweat, she tried her damndest to shove away from that grasp, but her arms felt far too heavy and her knees had filled with lactic acid. The muscles of her mouth were so focused on keeping her airway open that she couldn’t emote. She was frozen in place. If she could, she wasn’t sure how she would look at her husband, who massaged her with reassuring swabs. His words were ignorant. She couldn’t believe she had agreed to quit her teaching job right as she was close to earning tenure. Brandon reasoned with her that this was the way they could spend more time together. If they were both on the road, there wouldn’t be any separation between them. They’d been only catching each other in glimpses for years. “Just five more pounds, alright honey? Just a few more and you’ll look just as pretty as those runway models. Just five more.” And he was right. Oh so right. *** She received worried stares from the peanut gallery of neighbor faces. Hobbling foot drags caused her to shamble in a syncopation beat to the parking garage, and for some reason, the entire world was vacant. Numbness had given way to a bone tired ache. Everything throbbed. Her whole world was towering sheets of gray and shadow, the sun barely peering in between the parking structure’s openings. The gauze picked up a mixture of cement dust and leaked oil. There was nothing more important to her entire life than him, and he needed her more than ever. It was just the due course of such fragmented relationships; obligation was more important now than ever. Even this transgression couldn’t break her marriage. It could be mended. He could do as he did before; whimper about change and love and passion and all those beautiful pieces of flimsy romantic filler which melted her distorted and broken heart. And he would change. He always changed! But she had lost sight of the actual, her focus more on the impractical and optimistic. She turned the key in the ignition and input the directions to the police station on her GPS. The check book was always stowed away safely in the glove compartment. Adjusting her rear view mirror, she stopped as she caught the glimpse of her cheek. Turning her head fully, she stretched the skin of her lower jaw. She’d examined it in the past but never before did it have the repulsive qualities which now made her cringe in her seat. For the first time, she saw the band imprint intersecting the scar his brutal kiss had cauterized into her cheek. *** It was the first time she had ever seen him bleed. I’m fine!” He fired a slap across her face, and when he saw her eyelids shoot open, he slapped her again. When she began to quiver, he caught her with the heel of his hand. He threw her down, limping away with only a deflated chorus to accompany the sound of his boots. “I knew this was a mistake. I knew you were a mistake.” *** Brandon paced back and forth in his holding cell, muttering to himself. She was coming. She had to be coming. Legs giving way underneath him, he couldn’t help but feel the steel bars drawing closer, threatening to constrict him. For the first time in his miserable life, he needed his wife. But she wasn’t there. *** Melissa walked to the top level of the parking structure, and pushed herself up onto cross section of concrete dangling over the edge. And as she stared downward at the miscarriage of traffic, she couldn’t help but think of every single time she’d buried her fingers down her throat to make her husband happy or how many times she battered her own stomach because she never could make herself thin enough. There were no more tears to be shed. The lies were apparent and spread out before her. It just was unclear whether she could blame him for everything of if she had to shoulder some of the responsibility. The breeze caused her feet to sway in the sky. There were trees on the street corners and an ocean of concrete with towering high rises acting as Babelian waves. It all seemed so inviting. Melissa Garvin never realized, until on the precipice of her mortality, that she’d lived such a dangerous lie. And that there was no second chance in her life; the scars from the physical and emotional presence of Brandon Youngblood had become permanent. Her dream man had become malformed. Twisted into a mess of beaten and raw flesh, his face barely recognizable compared to when she first had met him. And with one glance in the mirror, she finally realized the same could be said of her. Her son rapped in a staccato beat as she pushed her palms into the overlap, her mind ready to take the six story plunge.
