Ordered into his locker room like a child, left to stalk and glare at the door, listening to the muffled barbs beyond it as she handled her problem. The Deville couldn’t believe it. Since when was he a sidekick? Since when was he a subordinate to come and go at the crooking of a finger? The worst part about it was the realization that it didn’t really bother him, that he liked being at Karina’s beck and call, enjoyed being at the mercy of her whim. There was a release in it, a freedom, and when they were alone together, when she relaxed enough to let her true beauty shine through, it was like someone had magically cranked the volume up on life. It was nothing at all like when they were in public. There was no telling him what his business was. There were no curt commands as if he was a mangy but obedient hound. Forcing himself to stop grinding his teeth, Deville conceded that it bothered him much more than he pretended. Why does she have to be such a bitch? He couldn’t tear his glare from the door, picturing the modern valkyries on the other side engaged in their verbal war, slicing at one another with tongues sharp enough to draw blood, likely mocking him at every opportunity. He should go out there and lay down the law with Karina, then give that soul-devouring Aimz a real Columbian necktie. He harboured no doubt that her mouth had been around enough to warrant one. Stop talking to that fucking tramp and get in here. Despite the mental commands he projected through the door, the muted arguing continued. It was small consolation that he’d see Aimz in the cell later on that night, that he’d get a chance to cripple her before her big match with Karina. That would give him no meagre sense of satisfaction, but it paled beside the motivation behind it. Aimz was inconsequential without Karina. It was Karina that was important. As the door whipped open and the K-Wolf stalked through, slamming it behind her, Deville prepared to unleash his tirade. This time it was she who beat him to the punch, however, briskly demanding, “What are you planning to do in that cell?” as if she had cause to be pissed off. “What?” Deville challenged incredulously, perilously close to sputtering. “You heard me,” Karina snapped. Planting herself directly in front of him, she regarded him with narrowed eyes. “What are you planning to do in that cell?” Silence reigned for a long moment as Deville glared, his face a thunderhead. “I was thinking of going down there and reciting some poetry,” he finally said in a too-calm, much-too-thoughtful voice. “Maybe take a nap. Perhaps I could—“ “Don’t touch her,” Karina’s cold voice interrupted like a knife. “Oh, of course not,” Deville mocked, “why ever would I touch a wrestler I’m in a match with?” Karina folded her arms underneath her breasts and gave her head a slight, warning shake. “Don’t play coy with me, Angelo. You think I don’t know what you’re planning? You’re planning to go out there and hurt her in a misguided attempt to protect me.” The Deville had no answer save for glaring down his nose defiantly, almost insolently. “Do you really think I need your protection?” An edge entered the K-Wolf’s voice that Deville didn’t like. Her back stiffened even more than usual, bringing them eye-to-eye as her hands shifted down to her hips. “Do you really think I want your protection?” “Damnit, Kari,” Deville growled, “we both know you don’t want or need protection. It’s just that . . . for fu-- . . .” Faltering, he turned his head away from her, torn between what he knew was right for him and what she knew was right for her. What could he say to her that he hadn’t already? A knock at the door, the opening to the most annoying knock in the history of mankind, wrenched both of them out of the moment, whipping both of their heads around to glare at the door. Deville held his breath in the pause, then cringed as the last two raps sounded, completing the clichéd calling. “Don’t go anywhere,” Deville told Karina shortly, trying to call up a smile and failing. “This won’t take long.” Stalking past her, he added to himself a whispered, “Not fucking long at all.” Karina watched him disappear through the door with a blank expression, everything simmering beneath the surface encased in her mirage of serenity. Well, okay, her hands were balled into fists at her sides, and she was still rigid enough that pigeons might try to perch on her had they no fear of freezing to death. And her breathing came in stunted huffs. “John Johnson?” she heard Deville bark dubiously through the door. From the pitch of his voice, she knew his tolerance was hanging by a thread, the same frayed string that held his patience. “Are you serious?” Sighing, Karina shook her head and looked at the ceiling as if for help. She was going to have to go out there soon, before things got out of hand and this Johnson fellow got hurt. ~><>…<><~ When Angelo finally emerged from his locker room again, his suit replaced by the conflicting black and white trim he’d wrestled in for years, there was less than five minutes until his match. His Hell in a Cell match. His favourite type of contest. Despite his eagerness for the competition, for the opportunity to test himself against five of the world’s best and the incomparable Dragon Silver, the smile on his face was subdued. “As far as you’re concerned,” his darling K-Wolf had told him, poking him in the ribs with a finger, “there’s only six of you in that cell.” “Kari,” he’d protested, “she’s vermin. I never touched you. I never laid a finger on you.” “You chose her over me.” Deville wanted to tell him that he hadn’t, that he had gone out of his way to not choose either of them, but he couldn’t. Williamson should have reasoned it out on his own, and if he was thinking clearly, he would have. Besides, Deville knew that if it came right down to it, he would choose Karina. “So in order to prove to you that I’m loyal . . . I have to betray.” It wasn’t a question, but Deville stalked past the gaping Williamson to let him mull it over, having already wasted too much time before his match. Trying to teach someone was one thing, but when they thought they already knew more than the master, when their motivation wasn’t pure in the first place . . . Eleanor caught up with Deville as he swept toward the gorilla position, looking frantic and alarmed, seizing his arm in the same way Williamson had. “Don’t trust Justin,” she hissed, shaking her head to emphasise the negative. “He’s not your friend. He’s only in—“ “I know,” Deville cut her off with simply, coldly, shaking free of her hand and disappearing through the curtain as his music began to play. He did know. He had known all along, from the first time Williamson confronted him in his dressing room falsely offering friendship. Williamson wasn’t with him out of respect, or a desire to learn, or even an attempt at a true alliance. Williamson aligned himself with The Deville for the same sycophantic reason everyone aligned themselves with him. With The Deville there was always an eventual collapse, an implosion to rival the Big Bang for star creation. Williamson didn’t want to befriend him, he wanted to use him, to capitalize on his name and reputation. Williamson wanted the betrayal. He sought it out, waiting for his chance to be the wronged hero against the overbearing Deville. That was the first impression Deville had of Williamson, when the man was still The HIT. That was why Deville had sent Eleanor to ride with him, as if he needed confirmation of his suspicions. As if he needed proof that people saw something more – something less – than a potential friend when The Deville entered the room. He was used to it, and he played his part well, but he couldn’t help wishing for a piece of something genuine. Surely someone out there looked at him and saw something beyond what his fall would bring them. He supposed Eleanor was one of those people. She was only out for his success and well-being. That made it worse if not better, though. He had betrayed her, had exploited her as everyone else did him. On every level possible he had used and abused Eleanor Kannon-Hall, yet still she remained loyal to him. She still kept his best interest at the front of her mind. That wouldn’t last forever, however. Eventually she would find out, and when she did, he would have yet another friend with a dagger up her sleeve. He would have yet another person waiting to lord over his corpse. Did Karina see him as a stepping stone? Instant guilt washed over him for the thought. Knowing her, he knew she was incapable of it, couldn’t even imagine her considering it . . . but still, when hadn’t it happened? He loathed harbouring the suspicion, but he couldn’t shake free of it. What would he do if Karina turned out like all the rest? The scene was PWC’s Last Man Standing, from the Hershey Park Arena in Hershey, Pennsylvania, on July 15, 2003. The match had been building for months, since PWC’s first-ever payperview, Capitol Punishment, where Angelo Deville had been slated to compete for the vacant World Championship. All of the fans had known their Universal Champion was going to walk out with the gold, and with the alternatives of Sebastian Toomes, Clay Vendetta, and Frankie Bones, they couldn’t have been happier to see it. The Deville was a paragon of wrestling, a champion on the international stage. With him on the roster, PWC proclaiming another inaugural champion would have been a mockery. To the collective chagrin of the Universe, The Deville never won the title on that fateful night. He never even got to compete for it. Instead, he was arrested half an hour before it was set to go down for the murder of Jason O’Neil, whom Deville had thrown off Brooklyn Bridge in the lead-up to Capitol Punishment. The authorities ignored it at first, dismissing it as professional wrestling nonsense, but when one of the PWC superstars called it in as a real crime, saying he feared for his life, a SWAT team descended on the arena and removed Angelo in chains. That PWC superstar had been Magnum Wolf, affectionately known by the PWC faithful as The Package~! due to his love of incredibly tight-fitting jeans. Deville spent the better part of a week in jail before he was allowed to post bail, and when the preliminary hearing was finally held, only one person was surprised The Deville got the case thrown out. That one person, again, was Magnum Wolf. He couldn’t believe it. He had taken a huge gamble to get rid of The Deville, to clear the path to the top, and now it was blowing up in his face with the ferocity of a tommygun. Deville went after Wolf immediately, hunting him down on the next edition of PWC TV, but the wily Wolf had a backup plan. He had more dirt on Deville, gathered through his skills as a private investigator, and if anything unfortunate were to befall him, that dirt would inevitably see the light of day. When Deville balked at the threat, Wolf went for the kill, blackmailing him further, telling him if he lost another match in PWC, no matter how, it was Deville’s funeral, not his. For several weeks The Deville had no choice but to assist the nefarious Wolf, interfering in matches on his behalf, crumpling him with a savage brass-knuckle shot to get disqualified when the two were booked to face off. Wolf’s goal was to win the Last Man Standing tournament, thereby becoming the number one contender to the World Title, and with Deville as his infallible insurance policy, he was well on his way to making the semi-finals for the payperview of the same name. Audiences were stunned. The Deville was being strongarmed, yet no one was going missing, no one was being poisoned or maimed. One of the greatest and most callous manipulators they’d ever seen was being marched around like a marionette, and as far as they could tell, he was doing nothing to cut the strings. Of course, things were rarely as they appeared. Two weeks out of the big event, boos welcomed the predictable sight of Angelo Deville during another of Wolf’s qualifying matches on PWC TV. Some of them cheered, for Deville had taken his own slant on how he was to help Wolf, entering the ring and literally ripping him apart, leaving him laying in the ring a bloody mess, winner by disqualification . . . but most booed. As much as they liked watching Deville put the hurt on Wolf, they hated seeing the man win, mainly because of the implications behind the methodology. Their Crimson Angel had been reduced to a puppet, his ruthless malevolence exchanged for modest servility. So when Deville entered the ring and hit Wolf’s opponent before going through the traditional stomping, the arena roof almost popped off from the eruption. The Deville beat Wolf to a bloody pulp as they cheered him on, eventually chasing him clean out of the arena through that swarming throng of drunken revellers. The match went down as a disqualification loss for Wolf, meaning he was out of the Last Man Standing tournament and his hopes for a contendership were dashed. Meaning Deville would rather seal his own doom than continue to help Wolf prosper. The Deville was back. The next week Wolf threatened Deville again, let him know it was already too late, that his fate was already written, but The Deville just chuckled and reminded him that extortion was a felony. He could have Deville arrested if he wanted, but he’d take the fall as well, and Deville made it very clear how unfortunate of a time Wolf would have inside a prison, especially trapped there with him. Wolf was screwed, and he knew it. He didn’t want to go to jail, especially not for the extortion of a man with Angelo Deville’s connections. If he was going to do that, he may as well just hang himself now and forego the torturous agony leading up to it. The Deville knew it, too. With Wolf’s schedule cleared for the payperview, convincing Starr to feed him to Deville in a Hell in a Cell match was as easy as Deville putting the idea on the table. Wolf tried to worm out of it, tried to get Deville arrested again, but this time the law laughed in his face. They didn’t believe him and they refused to let him waste any more of their time. It was a classic case of the Wolf crying wolf. The only hope he had, the hope he clung to as strongly and desperately as life itself, was that PWC was too poor to afford a cell. The fledgling federation could barely afford to keep the lights turned up all the way, there was no chance they could even rent something of that grandeur. It would have to be a cage match instead, meaning all Wolf had to do was escape. He wouldn’t be trapped in a cell with The Deville, the mobster he’d turned in to the police, the champion he’d tried to make a mockery of. It would only be a cage, a cage he could climb out of. He expected to take a lot of punishment, but eventually he would seize his moment and escape. Eventually there would be a chance for him to flee to safety. It would be brutal and bloody, but at least he would live to see another day. When The Devil’s Don purchased the cell with his own money and donated it to the promotion, a solid structure of stainless steel with no doors and no means of escape, Wolf began to wish he really was dead. Terror made a masterpiece of Wolf’s face as the cell continued its slow plunge. The cell locked into place as the shirt hit the floor, as the gladiators steeled themselves for their murderous campaign. If Magnum really was a wolf, his ears would have been drooping. Cheered on by the bloodlust of the crowd, Deville stretched the tie between his hands and grinned at his victim, starting to advance across the ring. Wolf hesitated only a moment, eyes wide with dawning realization, then ran for his life. For a little over half an hour The Deville did everything short of literally murder his opponent. He choked him with his tie, tethering him to the turnbuckle and leaving him to hang from the makeshift noose. With Wolf choking and passing out, Deville obliterated his swelled head with chair shot after chair shot. He executed every one of Magnum’s finishing manoeuvres, going for the pin with every one, breaking it up himself each time just shy of the three, yanking Magnum’s shoulders from the canvas by the tie knotted around his neck. He even locked an unconscious Wolf in a sleeperhold, forcing the referee to go through the motions of lifting Wolf’s arm three times. There was no doubt it was going to drop after the third lift, but again Deville broke the hold in the nick of time, telling Wolf and the world at large that he wasn’t through with him just yet. Both men were as caked with blood as the ring itself, and it was little more than a slick crimson skating rink. More was added to the pool with each passing second, dripping from Wolf’s hair and moustache, burbling from the lacerations across his chest and shoulders. The Deville’s pleated dress pants glistened and clung to his legs with it as he stalked across the ring. When Deville finally reached down and grabbed Wolf’s ankles, straightening and posing for an instant to let the crowd’s ecstasy wash over him, there wasn’t a mind in the arena or watching at home that didn’t know the match was over. The Soprano was one of the quickest, most incapacitating finishers in the history of the wrestling world. Specifically banned in three separate federations, it acted as a perpetual low-blow, smashing the testicles and driving them into the opponent’s body, making it feel as if they’d relocated in the throat. Most people passed out within the first five seconds. No one had ever broken free of it on their own steam. When The Deville locked The Soprano on inside a cell it was only a matter of moments before the bell proclaimed him victorious. Planting his feet against Wolf’s crotch, Deville rolled onto his back and perched him high above the canvas, his feet ready to be the anvil to the hammer of Wolf’s torso. His arms and upperbody corded and trembled as he threw his head back, straining with all of his might. The tap was academic at this point, less than a breath away, and the raucous crowd knew it. They could feel it, the mark in them knowing that justice was finally being done, and they loved it. Only Wolf never tapped. Instead, as if perched on a comfortable bicycle seat, he looked down at Deville and smiled. The Deville knew it was coming; the whole thing had been his idea, he’d written the entire script. Pushing up through his heels, flexing just for the show of it, he wasn’t putting any pressure into the move. With how he had it locked in, it wouldn’t have been any more painful than if Wolf really was seated on a bicycle. Outrage near to panic painting his bloodied face, Deville bent his knees and launched Wolf through the air, then did a nip-up despite his exhaustion. Storming across the ring, he snatched Wolf up by the hair and reached into the front of his pants, pulling out the white athletic sock that gave The Package~! its charm. The crowd went nuts. The fact that the sock would offer less protection than a jock strap, that even a jock strap wouldn’t nullify the excruciating crushing pain, didn’t matter one lick to them. Wolf was still outsmarting The Deville, still getting the better of him, proving himself to be among the best. Magnum Wolf was the first person ever to counter The Soprano. Stuffing the sock in Wolf’s mouth, Deville wound up to level him with a haymaker, but as the blow came hurtling forward Wolf ducked under it, twisting behind Deville, then spun him and planted him with The Alpha Driver, Wolf’s primary finishing move. Wolf collapsed onto him for the pin as the referee, almost as drenched with blood as the competitors, slid to the mat to make the count. The walls of the arena swelled with the excitement of the crowd as they shouted along with the slapping of the canvas, eager for the moan that would escape them when The Deville kicked out. “Again!” Deville ordered through clenched teeth, kicking out at two, and Wolf was more than happy to oblige, scooping him up and spiking him with yet another vicious Alpha Driver. The Deville was next to unconscious, his muscles uncooperative hunks of lead as Wolf covered him again, but still he managed to throw a shoulder up, his growl more like a whisper as he instructed Wolf to do it one more time. Once more, Wolf was more than happy to indulge The Deville, picking him up and nailing him with his other finisher, The Point of Authority. When Deville awoke seconds later, Duran Duran’s Hungry Like The Wolf was blaring through the arena, nearly choked out by the thunderous applause and shouting of the crowd. They weren’t cheering because Magnum had overcome all the odds, or because he was a hero, they were applauding the sheer spectacle of it. The fanbase of PWC knew the whole thing was a work and they appreciated it as such. With The Deville down and Wolf standing tall over him, they knew what they were seeing. The Deville had put Magnum Wolf over. For likely the first time in Wolf’s career, the fans were chanting his name. And Deville couldn’t be happier. This was what he wanted. This was what they had planned for, and it went off perfectly. As most of the smarks crowding the arena knew, Wolf’s and Deville’s relationship actually extended back to Five Star Wrestling, where Wolf had been ring-fodder and Deville an undefeated champion. It was Deville’s first taste of angled wrestling, and based on his talent and savvy, along with his dominance in the non-worked GTT3, he was pushed through the roof. On the other side of the spectrum, Wolf was treated by the company as an afterthought, as Aaron Roberts had publicly made clear on countless occasions since. Deville had tried to take Wolf under his wing while they were there, talking to him behind the scenes about joining the stable he was about to form, the stable that would all but take over FSW, Mob Rule. When Wolf refused, opting instead to form a group with people more on his level, management was so disgusted that they fed Wolf to the Primetime Champion on the very next show. Deville wished he could say he took it easy on Wolf in that match, since FSW was another federation that was completely worked, but he had heard rumblings backstage that Wolf resented the idea of jobbing to him. Because of that, Deville treated it like a real match, actually beating Wolf senseless before locking him in the Soprano that would end the squash. Having always felt bad about it, not to mention the anti-push that resulted for Wolf, when he saw Magnum being used to put over the brand-new Karina Wolfenden in PWC, Deville approached him with a plan. A plan that would see Wolf not only manipulate and outsmart The Deville, but a scheme that would climax with Wolf doing the double-impossible. Not only had no one ever countered The Soprano, but The Deville was also undefeated in cell matches. Everyone in the arena knew it, too, and their heavenly applause shouted that they appreciated what he’d given. Soaking it in, staying down to let Wolf revel in the biggest victory of his career, Deville knew he’d done the right thing. Until that moment, Magnum Wolf had been a joke, a sideshow comic relief gimmick that grew stale faster than a number one hit on pop radio. He had occasional moments of side-splitting entertainment or soul-wrenching pseudo-introspection, but no one had ever taken him seriously, especially as it related to wrestling skill. From that point on, however, Magnum Wolf was supposed to be the man. He’d toppled the untopplable in a match where it was impossible to do so, by doing what no man before him ever had. In spite of their backstage camaraderie until that point, Deville didn’t hear anything from or about Wolf until a few months later, when Wolf appeared in the global Old School Entertainment wearing an Angelo Deville mask. The disrespect stung Deville a little, but if Wolf needed to use him a little more, he supposed it couldn’t hurt. In truth, he hoped it worked out. When Roberts refused to return his phone calls, however, even after Deville had shown up in the company himself, he began to second-guess his generosity. Neither man stayed in OSE long, with Wolf getting fired and Deville handing in his resignation after trying and failing to make something work with Rhett Locke and Ivan Stanislav. They never once spoke backstage. Their paths didn’t cross again until Rush-Pro Wrestling, when Deville joined up to help Wolf maintain the championship he’d just won, injecting himself as a non-wrestling entity to take out the owner of the company, Christian Xavier Kannon. Kannon had been trying to make Wolf lose the title, going so far as to threaten stripping it, so Deville appeared to make sure that didn’t happen. It was a great kayfabe angle for the die-hards, and once again, Magnum Wolf was his best friend. Or, more accurately, Aaron Roberts felt compelled to rekindle his relationship with Angelo Deville. Until the company collapsed less than a month later. Once again phone calls went unreturned, and once again The Deville was left to wonder what manner of man he had sacrificed his name and reputation for. Months later he finally heard from Roberts, when the man showed up at a promotional appearance Deville was doing for the fourth instalment of the GTT. Despite the man’s penchant for disrespect, Deville tried to be friendly, but Roberts cast the blame for RPW’s failure at his feet, accusing Deville of ruining his career. Deville protested, reminding him that he’d done everything in his power to make him a success, to make him a star, but Roberts spat at his feet. “You never gave me anything,” Roberts sneered, the defunct RPW Championship strapped around his waist. “Fuck you, Angelo Deville. You never helped me. I did it all myself.” Deville felt like his lungs were filling with ice-water. His whole body began to tingle with rage. The bottom of his stomach didn’t just fall, it felt completely hollow, gone, as if there had never been anything there. Heart a scorched lump in his throat, he stared at the derisive Roberts for a long moment, reliving the past in horrific slow-motion, trying to reconcile the situation before him. He’d given this man the most precious thing he possibly could have, for no better reason than he wanted to see him do well, and now . . . “Fine,” Deville growled, standing up, “I never helped you.” If that was the truth Roberts wanted to perpetuate, The Deville wouldn’t lose another wink of sleep over him.
