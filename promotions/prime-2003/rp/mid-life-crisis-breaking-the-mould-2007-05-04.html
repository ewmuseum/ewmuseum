Mid Life Crisis - 'Breaking the Mould'

4 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/5/07 It’s hot in Missouri this week. Not the kind of heat that just burns you right up, but the kind that likes to simmer closely to you, build up a nice energy and then explode without warning. It’s the kind where the sweat slowly builds up until it’s too late to notice in time. Humidity is high and then some. Right now, I’m stuck in my hotel room, unable to go anywhere. It’s not my fault per se but I’m the one who has to burden the consequences. You see, after that triple threat match, I may have gotten just a little bit excited about how things were going. A night out on the town was fair enough, but the beer and vino began to flow too easily. Loose tongues were rampant between myself and Barney. Words were spoken, some which shouldn’t have been said and apparently it drifted back to the booking committee. Needless to say, they weren’t too happy about either me or Barney giving away precious plans, whether or not they had decided on them. For sure, that set things back. There was also the fact that my knees were acting up once again. They cause me to cramp quite easily at the best of times, but when they start to niggle at you it’s just downright annoying. I’d visited the local doctor in St. Louis since I’d arrived but it need constant attention. Movement was not a positive for it so right now I was stuck in the hotel room, only able to sit and wait for a call that Barney or Dave would be up to talk to me. I hated being unable to do what I wanted; I felt like a caged animal in the room. The heat pouring in wave after wave made even more and more irritable and I cursed myself that this was the karma for blowing the prospective wad of the booking committee. Even now Dave was talking to them, trying to grovel them back into some sort of position where we could get going again. He was not happy to say the least, the reduced airtime I’d received had not pleased him one bit and so all in all I was not exactly getting along as swimmingly as I should be. So all I could do was to wait. Wait and hear about my fate. *** ‘Have you any idea how much of a blunder this has been? Sweat was rippling down my back. Stupid wrestlers and their idiot mouths. They always love to brag about their potential and talk themselves up but they don’t have a single bone of business in their bodies. How could they even begin to think that they should have their own choices with the booking committee when they can’t even keep a secret? ‘I understand of course. I’m trying to sequester the offenders for the moment so that they can’t do any more damage.’ ‘And do you think that’s going to be enough?’ I stopped myself and wondered if they weren’t already talking to one another now, conversing about the whole programme without even bothering to keep it quiet. The two of them just couldn’t be trusted. I was starting to realise why Barney had never made the big time before this - he simply was a motor mouth and would jump at the chance to feel important. It didn’t matter who’s trust he was breaking because he would always be in with the boys. Thank God there wasn’t any other wrestlers in that nightclub that night because otherwise there would have been some serious trouble. ‘I think it should be. Besides I’ll take care of them. I know that Murphy is willing and is probably beating himself up over potentially screwing up his last shot at the big time. He won’t get out of line anymore. At the worst, this has actually solidified him to the plan. He won’t question it anymore.’ The committee were all sitting around in what was a stretch of a conference room for the hotel. They all crammed in, pushing their cokes and sprites up against the fried food that I couldn’t understand people eating. It was vile, unfulfilling and disgusting to even look at. Nevertheless it was a market that would probably never fade, at least not in my own time. Irrespective of pet peeves, I was confident that the bookers were slowly coming over to my side of things. ‘Hmm…well, we can’t say that this idea isn’t good…that’s why it hurts us to know that lots of people could have already heard about it by now. We want this to be unique and blow up huge when it hits.’ ‘Of course, I wouldn’t want it any other way.’ *** Barney had taken his time to amble up to my room but he eventually made it. Being part of the committee had meant that he had to dwell halfway across the hotel away from the wrestlers. They didn’t want news travelling fast about anything it seemed, literally or figuratively. The usual flip-flops and Hawaiian shirt were present and hanging limply from his bulbous body. A pretty visible stain caused his shirt to look like it had been attacked quite viciously by the hamburger in his mouth. Other than that differing mark it was the same old Barney. ‘How’s the legs?’ ‘How’s the exercise?’ Barney grinned as a mouthful of supposed beef filled his grin. ‘Touché.’ ‘What are you here for Barney?’ I didn’t need the crap of him meandering around the subject and wondering what the hell he was going to say if anything at all. ‘Well it’s…ah I’m freaked out Jack. I don’t know what they’re gonna do!’ ‘The bookers?’ ‘Of course the bookers! This was my one shot! My one shot! And I could’ve blown it all away by messing with a couple of hookers.’ Barney slumped to the end of the bed and pulled out a snickers bar to chew on. He was like a cow chewing the cud with it, mulling over the taste as it rolled from one side to the next. What a way to comfort eat. ‘You’re not the only one Barney, you’re not the only one.’ He looked at me with a little bit of disgust. It surprised me. ‘You’ll still have your job; what’ll I have?’ *** ‘So it’s settled then.’ I breathed a huge sigh of relief. Despite the major screw up that the fat man and the idiot wrestler had made, the bookers had believed the hype. They’d taken in the spin and all the potential that was within the project. They saw that with the way things would pan out they’d be able to make a mint on Murphy before it would be too late. Nobody was under any delusion that Murphy had wrecked his body in order to get to here in the first place. He’d be able to wrestle for sure for years to come, but he’d be a shell and a sideshow act. The time was now to capitalise on the star power he had left in an active wrestling capacity. Murphy of course didn’t know this, but his contract was one more suited to a character on screen who didn’t get into as many pitched battles as he did; he was given a non-wrestling contract to begin with. If he knew that, there’d be another liability to contend with. For now however, he had his deal and he’d accept it. I on the other hand have my platform. I took the briefcase I had expertly laid out in front of me and closed it shut. ‘IF that’s all gentlemen I have news to give to our incumbents.’ Murmurs came from the mass of the booking committee and dismissive hands ushered me out. That was fine, I had work to do. *** A knock on the door alerted myself and Barney who was finishing his third snickers bar. He jumped up into fevered excitement. Before we could answer in strode Dave, power suit and all with a stern face. Barney crept up to him nervously, getting right up against the armpit of Price. He looked up, fiddling with another snickers bar, to ask the question. ‘Well?’ ‘Well what? If you’re wondering, the aftershave is Armani.’ ‘No the…you’re messing with me aren’t you?’ Dave’s response was quick and dry. ‘Yes.’ He pushed by Barney and went to address me directly, popping open the briefcase and handing me a pile of papers. ‘What’s this?’ ‘The next stage, we have work to do after all.’ ‘Really?’ ‘Really Jack. They were convinced that talent like yours doesn’t come along very often. They need you just like you need them. It’s too special an opportunity.’ Barney hobbled over with joy and tried to hug Dave, who nimbly moved out of the way. Barney barrelled into the toilet, shutting the door on his way. ‘Anyway, there are some things that need to be taken care of. Are you ready to do what it takes Jack?’ ‘Of course, I’m ready for anything.’ ‘Well, I’ll have the supplements ready for you in the morning and then I’ll let you know about the shoot for the preview videos.’ ‘Supplements?’ ‘Yes Jack, supplements. It’s all in the documents. It starts as soon as you take those tag titles right?’ ‘Right.’ I knew what he meant but I wasn’t sure if he was being serious or not. I didn’t have time to ask him that question though as he began to leave. ‘Excellent. I will speak to you when you’ve got that gold round your waist. I have another meeting to go to.’ And with that, he was gone. Changes, changes, changes…but did I really know what I was getting into? *** Later that day… Standing outside the hotel was making me nervous. The film crew would be here soon and I didn’t want to fuck this up. For once, I was actually going to interact with people who had my kind of degree. I wasn’t born and bred on wrestling, hell no. I couldn’t stand the thing to be perfectly honest, but I knew how to write a compelling story. Wrestling was fine and all for developing the soap drama skills, but I was serious about my writing. I didn’t need to while away my years writing fairytales for bullshit rednecks to lap up. I knew I could be a star and bring in the proper money writing for television or even movies. First however, I needed to break in. That’s where the film crew comes in. They’re coming in to film some vignettes for Murphy with the alterations to his character. Promos pretty much but the real reason they’re here isn’t for him, it was for me. It was my stepping stone. I padded my briefcase, knowing full well about the scripts and ideas that were locked within. They would be unleashed on the world, that I was sure about. I just needed the right vehicle. For now, wrestling would have to do. It’s a shame, but the idiot wrestler hinges on the success of my rise. If it works, I know of interested parties watching me. They’re considering it a test of my skills. It’s like how they do with teachers in France. They ship you to the deadbeat schools to see if you really have it in you and if you succeed, well they move you to the more prestigious schools where things matter. Wrestling is that beaten up old school. I could see a truck pulling up with plenty of production tags along the side. My chariot to the ball had arrived it seemed. It’s only a matter of time before I get the golden shoe.
