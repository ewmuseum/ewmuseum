Date: 10th July ”All truths are easy to understand once they are discovered; the point is to discover them.” - Galileo * * * * * He was welcomed to their world like a wolf to the flock. Jason Natas; a tattooed man born from blood and thunder, existing among white picket fences and picture-perfect two-point-four children families like a grotesque pimple on the Mona Lisa’s face. For many a man, such a quaint, suburban neighbourhood was a picture of sheer bliss. Quiet and pleasant, close enough to still technically be in the city, but far enough away to make for a calming post-work retreat, there were no crammed shopping malls or bustling sidewalks here. Only rows and rows of almost identical houses, built with wooden exteriors and sizeable front lawns. The 10mph speed limit made it safe for the children to play in the streets after school, and the neighbourhood watch scene meant that crime was almost completely non-existent. A thoroughly decent place to live, unless you happened to be one of the most reviled men in professional wrestling. Every time he returned from the road he felt like smacking himself in the face for pulling himself from the city, but fate had made Aidan Chase's disappearance a necessity. He couldn't have stayed in The Bronx, for there were far too many vultures circling him now. Cordell Abraham had been on his case for months; first for turning his back on the New York kingpin's underground fighting ring, and then for shooting his son – his own flesh and blood – in the foot. Now, with the disappearance of the young man who was surely under Cordell's wing, The Anti-Superstar was a marked man. The place made Jason sick. Everything was so clean, so well-maintained. Mom stayed at home all-day, taking care of the house, while Pop arrived back to his postcard home and his perfect family at five o' clock every afternoon. Everyone seemed to be on first name terms with one another, and they strolled through the streets in their Dockers and sweater vests with their sixty dollar haircuts smiling , waving, and exchanging idle chit-chat like everyone was their best friend. Jason didn't know how much longer he could go before he inevitably smashed one of those middle-class bastards straight in his pretty little nose. Part of him wished he'd stayed in The Bronx, but he could only fight them off for so long. It was too easy for them to find him there, and there was no way in hell he'd ever be able to take on every last one of Abraham's goons. They'd find him here eventually, he was sure of it; but he doubted that even a man of Abraham's brutal reputation would take his way to this pretty little slice of Americana. Still, the suburbs made Jason uneasy. Every time he stepped out into the sun he felt as though a thousand pairs of beady little eyes were watching him, and today, as he stepped out of the 'hood's only convenience store with a bag full of Budweiser, was no different. His Dr. Marten's hit the pavement like a ton of bricks and he walk down the street at a brisk pace, looking to attract as little attention to himself as possible. Unfortunately for PRIME's Anti-Superstar, though, a rough, rugged professional wrestler with an unshaven jawline and a small facial tattoo was always going to attract more than a couple of curious gazes when walking through such clinical surroundings. He walked passed a group of children, who immediately stopped throwing their ball to one another to watch this beast of a man stagger by with all the grace of a baby elephant. Their glare didn't linger for too long and soon they turned their attentions back to their game; it was the disapproving glances of parents from behind blinds and curtains that got Jason riled. Saw them all the time, Jason did; peeking through the tiniest gap in the window, only to make a hasty retreat when they noticed The Anti-Superstar staring back at them. Most of them he put his chin up and carried on his way, sometimes he smiled back with a black-tooth grin and gave them an over-emphasised wave, just to freak Daisy and Tarquin out a little bit. Still, he could hardly blame him. He was an alien in their world. A poison from the inner-city ghetto, sent to wreck everything and ruin their lives. There was no doubt looked him up on the internet when they'd found a professional wrestler was moving to the area, and they probably knew all about his reputation and criminal record. Hell, Jason wouldn't be surprised if some of them had started a petition to get him moved out of the area already, such was the clash between the citizen and his surroundings. Jason was fine with that, though; if they disliked him, if they feared him, then he had nothing to worry about. But he was getting a bit tired of being stared at like a circus act. He turned the corner to his street. Saw his new two-floor house standing about fifty yards away. The skies were blue, the air clean and full of the sounds of birds chirping and children playing. Families gathered behind closed doors to celebrate the end of the week, while Uncle Bob cracked-out the lawnmower across the street and his neighbour pulled a set of sprinklers from the garage. Everything in suburbia was perfectly aligned. Except for one thing. Jason had to squint in the sunlight to see it, but he was sure he saw a long, black vehicle sitting in his own driveway. His own car was white and tucked safely away in the garage, and he wasn't expecting any visitors. A couple of steps further along the street confirmed his suspicions but he didn't break his stride. Something was wrong. People just didn't come to visit Jason Natas. Had he been found already? Had Cordell Abraham tracked him down so quickly? He hadn't left New York entirely, so it was a distinct possibility, albeit one that he chose not to entertain for long. He put his head down and carried on his way. Best to ignore the vehicle. Best to pay it's occupiers no attention. Jason turned onto the driveway and strode towards his house, but as the Mercedes' front door flew open and nearly knocked him off his feet, there could be no more ignoring it. * * * * * “This problem is becoming rather troublesome,” Cordell Abraham wheezed as he reclined on his leather office chair. The strained joints creaked as this bulbous waste of a man pulled his short legs from beneath the desk and propped his size tens on top of his rich mahogany desk. “My patience is growing thin.” Sitting in the bowels of his boxing club, the career criminal plucked his cigar from between his porky lips and let folds of smoke gently waft through his yellow teeth. Cordell Abraham was a vile waste of a man. Unattractive in every department, his belly hung with the weight of a million Hershey bars and his oily, greasy complexion was that of a man who long-forgotten the meaning of the word “exercise”. His olive face was further marred by small splodges of liver spots on his face, and his thinning black hair hadn't turned white or grey with old age – it was turning yellow. It took great effort for the heavy-browed, green-eyed porker to lean forward again, and he did so with a muffled, uncomfortable groan. Tipping his cigar on the edge of his ashtray, he cast his gaze across the two trusted foot-soldiers that stood before him. Both had faces that were beaten and battered around the edges, shaped like potatoes that had been used as soccer balls, bearing the scars of men who'd weathered the storms brought forth by a life on the streets. “I think it's time we put an end to this, once and for all,” Cordell said, the folds of fat under his chin rippling at even the slightest movement of his jaw. He flashed his lieutenants a look that told them he meant business, and adjusted the wine-red tie that was tucked beneath his grey waistcoat and above a pressed white shirt. Abraham may not have been a good-looking man, but he was certainly well-dressed. “Anything you want, boss,” said the leather-clad man on the left in a dull, emotionless baritone. He was a beastly man of at least 6'4”, with a crooked nose and at least three missing teeth. His jaw moved at an awkward angle as he spoke, perhaps the result of one too many right hooks. “We gon' do it.” The goon beside him nodded in agreement. “Just give us the word.” Abraham exhaled, his chubby chest heaving like a giant swell. “He's taken a step too far this time. Don't care where that Chase kid is, or if he's even still alive; he was useless as muscle, too nervy, not cold enough... but he was effectively one of us. That's why we gotta put Jason Natas out of his misery.” “... not to mention shootin' Junior's fuckin' toe off!” growled one of the goons in an obvious play for his boss's approval. “An' turnin' on us, makin' us look like a bunch 'a fuckin' pricks,” said the other in a shrill, buzzsaw voice. He was skinnier than his cohort but no less mean looking, with three small rings piercing his left eyebrow and a thick goatee beard. Cracking his knuckles together, he smiled. “Lemme handle this one personally, boss.” “Frank, Frank, Frank,” began Abraham, “your enthusiasm is encouraging.” He paused, tapping his sausage fingers against the surface of the desk. “We have to be a little bit careful with this one, though.” “Whaddaya mean?” Frank's face contorted with confusion, and his cold, grey eyes seemed to sink even further into the back of his skull. “Boy needs to learn who not ta fuck with.” “Frank's right, boss,” said Frank's baritone-voiced colleague. “He been runnin' rings aroun' us for years, almost. This ain't just come club ya can up an' leave, ya know? Yet he waltzed out like he had a choice, an' now he's prancin' 'round on national television like he's worth a fuck. Prick wasn't even close to the best fighter we had, man, an' now everyone's thinkin' he's badass 'cause he can throw a couple of fuckin' homos aroun' a wrestlin' ring? Fuck that.” “And he shot yer own son in the foot. Yer own flesh and bloo-...!” “Enough!” snarled Cordell, smashing his fist into the desk. The two goons knew better than to press their boss further. If they had it their way, they would have put Jason Natas out of the game on the very day he announced he was leaving to forge a wrestling career, but Cordell surely had his reasons. The kingpin was breathing heavy, deep in contemplation. Removing Jason Natas was nothing like getting rid of Joe Average or Johnny Lunchbox, no. He was pretty well-known now; PRIME had brought him into the public eye, and if he were to suddenly disappear it would be national news at least. This situation was delicate. Had to be handled carefully. The answer came to Cordell quicker than he thought it would. “The agent'll do it.” “That blonde, city-slickin' motherfucker?” Cordell nodded. “It's perfect.” “Perfect?” quizzed Frank. “Fucker ain't killed a man in his life – how's he 'sposed to kill one of his own clients an' carry on livin'?” “That is why it's perfect,” Cordell said, drawing a crooked smile across his wet lips. “It's too obvious if we do it – the cops pry hard enough and they're gonna find out all about his past association with us, and that's if they don't already know about it. Gotta get someone from the outside to do this, see.” “He'll rat us out.” “No. No, no. He's too much of a chickenshit for that. Knows we've got people everywhere who'd cut his throat if he even breathed a word to the police.” Cordell took a deep breath. Sucked on his cigar. “He'll take the punishment on the chin. No need for for anyone to even think we had anything to do with it. He ain't no rat.” Baritone's facial muscles loosened. He nodded his head, feeling himself come around to Cordell's way of thinking. “You think he'll do it, though?” Abraham cracked a sinister sneer. Leaning back in his chair, cigar sitting between his lips, he summoned a terrible cackle from the deep pit of his gut. “A man'll do anything if you stick a gun to his head, John.” * * * * * “Little early to be crackin' open a cold one, don't ya think?” Detective Harry Stone said as he observed the red Budweiser label through Jason's shopping back. He'd almost knocked the Anti-Superstar off his feet when he'd opened the door, and when he slipped out of the driver's seat he found himself standing mere inches away from one of the most feared men in professional wrestling. “'Spose a man like you ain't really one to care, though,” Harry smiled, removing his aviator shades from the bridge of his nose and sliding them into his breast pocket. “You know what these bastards are like, Harry,” said Harry's partner, Stan Callaghan; the homicide unit's resident man mountain. Gently closing the car door, the bald giant folded his thick arms and leaning across the car's roof. “They got nothing to live for, so they fill the void with liquor. Makes 'em feel better about their shitty little existence.” When Jason looked up he realised he recognised the cops instantly. The one standing before him – the slightly chubby one with the suspenders and the cheap, grey suit – he was the one that had brought him into the station to quiz him about the apartment fire a couple of weeks ago. And the tall, slap-headed one? He must've been his lapdog. Jason Natas had never been particularly fond of police – particularly police whom he'd brushed with in the past. “Fuck you piggies wantin' now? Already told ya I ain't givin' damn 'bout the apartment.” Detective Stone looked over his shoulders, admiring the quaint little chunk of suburbia that Jason had carved out from himself. “Looks like losing your apartment was the best thing that ever happened to you, truth be told.” Harry smiled at Jason, mocking him. Natas could tell from the queer glimmer in Stone's eye that he knew something that Jason didn't, and Jason wasn't entirely comfortable with that. Still, he clenched his fists together, draining as much stress as he could without actually popping Stone. “What, then?” he huffed. “Busy man like me ain't got time t'be jawin' with a couple 'a bacon-smellin' motherfuckers like you.” “Heh, yeah. I'm sure Jason Natas, professional wrestler, has one hell of a hectic like when he's not on the road,” scoffed Callaghan condescendingly. Stone couldn't help but chuckle at his partner's quip. “Just wanted to ask you a few questions about Aidan Chase is all,” Harry said, turning his attentions back to Jason. Natas felt the blood in his veins turn to ice. Abraham and his clowns were already hounding him for what had happened a few short months ago, and the last thing he needed was the police on his case. He soon calmed. They don't know a fuckin' thing, he told himself, otherwise they'd be marchin' ya away in cuffs already. “What 'bout him?” “Ain't nobody heard from him in a couple weeks,” replied Callaghan. “People are getting worried.” “Fuck makes you think I know anythin' 'bout it?” “Well,” began Stone, “he did torch your place of living...” Jason's face twisted with indignation. “Fuck are you tryin' t' get at? Told ya I ain't wastin' 'nother thought on that fuckin' place, an' you shouldn't either. Don't know where yer hidin' the fuckin' prick, but he sure as ain't tied up in my closet, so get the fuck out the way an' stop wastin' my time.” Without a moment's hesitation Jason pushed passed Harry Stone, making a deliberate effort to clash shoulders with the Detective. He quickly removed his keys from his pocket and slid them into the front door. “What's the matter, Jason?” bellowed Callaghan. “Ain't you gonna invite us in for a couple of beers?!” Jason didn't hear a word of the officer's call, as soon he'd slammed the door behind him with such force that it shook the whole frame of the house. Harry quickly composed himself after being knocked and pulled his shades from his pocket, sliding them onto his face. He looked across at Stan. Saw his glance was returned. “You think those are the actions of a guilty man?” asked Stan. “Abso-fuckin'-lutely,” nodded Harry Stone, unable to suppress a smile. “Now let's find us some evidence.” * * * * * They’d kill Nathaniel if they found out. He knew that dwelling on the thought would do him no good, and if he spent too much time agonizing over it then he might just end up killing himself, but he couldn’t help himself. As a businessman, he’d been subjected to so much stress that he’d almost become immune to it. But this was something bigger than the management group he shared with Najera and Jewell. Going to the cops had been a grave mistake. Jason was guilty; he was sure of it, and if Detective Stone pried hard enough, he soon would be too. Nathaniel’s troubles with his client would be over and he’d be able to breathe-easy at work again. The Potemkin Group would be free of its most troublesome acquisition and business would thrive. But Jason Natas was the least of his worries. If Cordell Abraham smelt the blood in the water then he’d be done for. And how it terrified him. He’d taken the boy, Aidan Chase, to the walrus of a man’s base of operations with the hope of eliminating Jason, their common enemy, and Chase had lost his life because of it. Now he was in their debt, under their employ. He couldn’t afford to be caught co-operating with the blue and white of New York’s finest, not with a man as powerful as Abraham watching over him. Sitting in his conservatory, Loftman cocked his head back and downed the last of his single malt. An electronic buzz emanated from his pocket, and retrieved his phone with a pale, quivering hand. The name “Abraham” flashed back at him from the screen and he dreaded whatever might be asked of him next, but he dared not reject the call.
