Date: Timeless “We need to talk.” The simple statement, delivered to her from her own front porch, stunned Eleanor Kannon-Hall. Sleep fled her narrowed gaze, giving way to wide green eyes lost in thought, or perhaps the paralysing absence of it. Opening her door to discover a sopping wet Angelo Deville, his always-immaculate hair frazzled by the moisture in the air and licking down his forehead, dripping, would have been enough to inspire Eleanor to dust off her diary and chronicle the event. The words that followed, however, offered from a downcast face without any bluster or preamble, were the last thing Eleanor could have ever expected, and she was still having trouble wrapping her mind around it, convincing herself she’d heard what she thought she had. “I’m sorry.” Fat droplets of rain made a curtain around the porch, creating a private little world of serene quiet, somehow made more soothing by the soft drumming on the ceiling overhead. Eleanor didn’t feel soothed, however. She felt the furthest thing from it. Angelo raised his shiny, slicked face and peered at her – into her – intently, eyes flickering back and forth between each of hers. He had an umbrella in hand, but it hung unopened, and judging by his soaked state, had been that way however long he’d been in the rain. Eleanor couldn’t guess how long that had been, but it had certainly been longer than a walk from his car, which she spotted not far down the road, to her porch. Eleanor could think of any number of reasons for Angelo to be sorry to her, but she narrowed her eyes suspiciously and tilted her chin back. “For what?” Angelo swallowed. Eleanor actually saw his Adam’s apple sink, heard the distinct click of his throat seeking reinforcements. He opened his mouth, then clamped it shut and shook his head. Dread crept across Eleanor’s mind like a spider. What could Angelo Deville have done to give Angelo Deville pause? “For everything,” he said at last, quietly, meeting her stare fervently. His tongue darted out between his lips, unnecessarily wetting them. “For everything I’ve done . . . and for everything I’m now doing.” Tightening the drawstrings of her bathrobe in a vain attempt to block out a chill from within, Eleanor stiffened even more. “You’ll need to be more specific.” The Deville looked down with a sigh. “I know,” he muttered softly with another almost imperceptible head shake. “I . . .” Cutting off, his face did a brief Picasso. “Do you mind if we talk inside?” Blinking, Eleanor looked over her shoulder into her house, the home she cared for and shared with her husband, who hated this man. “I don’t think that’s a good idea,” she allowed slowly, thinking up excuses – reasons – why he couldn’t come in. “Christian’s not home and . . .” “I know,” Deville cut her off with, his tone too weary to be sharp. “It won’t take long,” he added quickly, nodding, steering her mind down a different path. “Please?” Eleanor bit her lower lip as she backed into the front alcove, gesturing for him to follow. Her inner voice screamed at her not to let him in, to slam the door and lock it, then barricade it, but her body moved in contrast to her thoughts, as if possessed. The Deville was pestilence on legs, but she was his manager, and for whatever reason, he was trying to open up to her. That last thought made her want to slap herself for being silly. She led him down the hall into the kitchen, then retrieved a towel so he could dry himself off. Draping it over a chair instead, he flopped down into it and twined his fingers together on the table. Eleanor’s offer of refreshment, more an excuse to put off sitting with him than a testament to good hosting, was met by a quick shake of his head, and he practically bristled with impatience when she tried to insist. “Are you sure you won’t have some orange juice?” she chirped, whipping the fridge door open and practically climbing inside. “I made it fresh this m—“ “I drugged you.” Again silence descended but for the pattering of the rain. Eleanor straightened from the refrigerator and let the door swing shut on its own. She took a long moment to turn around, to settle her sights on The Deville, sitting at her kitchen table looking bedraggled, his eyes burning fiercely with desire. But desire for what? To make her understand? To make her forgive him? It was as unreadable as The Deville himself usually was. “I’m aware,” came the brisk, tight-lipped response. Eleanor folded her arms over her chest to loom, then felt a stab of guilt when Angelo flinched. The shame was murdered almost instantly by the discovery of its presence and the resulting revulsion. Why should she feel bad for him? He had drugged her. “So you could immunize me to the rabies, right?” “No.” Deville flinched again. “Well, no and yes. That was a reason, but . . . it’s difficult to explain. I’d like to try, though, if you’ll sit down.” Instead of moving to take a chair, Eleanor arched an eyebrow and seemed to root herself in place. Angelo sighed, looking flustered, then began explaining himself, going over the real reasoning and the details behind it, telling her how the whole thing had been a scheme designed to bring about his rematch with her husband, Christian “Xavier” Kannon. Eleanor wanted to tear him apart when he unveiled how he’d used her as a pawn, how he’d framed her for a crime – attempted murder, no less! – just so he could blackmail his way into a wrestling match. By the time he finished, Eleanor still hadn’t taken a chair, so Angelo stood and took a step toward her. She backed off instantly, moving so the counter was between them, planting herself within arm’s reach of the wall-phone. The Deville stopped mid-step at the reaction, then showed her his palms in a hopeless attempt at comfort. “I understand if you no longer want to be my manager, of course. I’ll arrange whatever type of severance package you want, with bonuses for—“ “Why are you so obsessed with fighting Christian?” Eleanor was concerned for her own well-being, but as her husband had so accurately and infuriatingly pointed out before she got into this mess, it had never been about her. It was Christian who was in danger. She had been placed in danger as well, but the madman’s crosshairs had never wavered, had never once settled on her. Her danger was superficial next to Christian’s. “We need to have a match,” The Deville responded simply, heatedly. “You already had a match,” Eleanor reminded him in a guarded tone, “way b-- when you were Universal Champion.” The Deville shook his head slowly. “No we didn’t.” “Yes you did,” Eleanor reaffirmed, trying to keep patience in her voice. “I was there. Remember?” “That wasn’t a match. That was—“ Deville forced himself to take a deep breath when he realized he was on the brink of shouting, then went on in his cool, lecturing way. “When I first received that call from Xavier, I—“ “Christian,” Eleanor broke in to correct him. Angelo pursed his lips, but said nothing. “When I first received that call from Christian . . . I was on top of the world. Literally. I’d just won the tournament and people were harassing me non-stop to set up potential fights, begging me to grace their federation with the title, to bring to them the spectacle my name promised. Most of the opposition they wanted me to face, however . . . well, suffice it to say I didn’t see Chris Storm versus Angelo Deville as a dream match. I wanted Ivan Stanislav, or Violence Jack, or Xavier Kannon. “So when Xav— Christian called me and told me he wanted the first title shot, I was ecstatic. Assholes like Ron Starr were going around trying to demean my tournament victory because Inphino Blitz fluked his way into opposing me in the finale, saying that if someone with real talent – like Kannon – had been in that championship match, I would have fallen. This was my chance to show them they were wrong, to show myself that I really was the best.” When Deville paused, Eleanor offered a weak smile. “And you did that.” The Deville barked a humourless laugh. “I showed up to the event expecting the biggest night of my life. I never went public with it, but the whole reason I originally applied to PCW and suffered through their academy was because I’d seen you and Xavier in action, because I knew I wanted to compete on the level he did. Because I wanted to know what it was like to meet him in the ring. I didn’t find out until just before the match, when Tyler Nelson and Xavier Kannon approached me in my dressing room to detail how I was going to be ‘put over’, that the match was going to be a work.” Despite the situation, Eleanor couldn’t help rolling her eyes. “Wrestling is a work, Angelo.” “Not for me!” Deville snapped. “Not for him, either. Not normally. You can tell me his career was worked if you want, but I watched the matches. I know they weren’t. When Xavier Kannon met you in the ring, you were fighting for your survival, you were struggling to keep up with the best the game had seen. When you woke up and heard his music playing, you knew you’d been beaten by the best. And if you beat him . . .” “Once again, you did beat him.” The Deville was already shaking his head when she opened her mouth. “I never had the chance. He robbed me of it, Eleanor; he dangled the prospect in front of me and then cruelly snatched it away just as my hand encircled it. Instead of usurping his crown I was given his “charity”, hoisted as a puppet for all to see.” Deville’s fists were clenched at his sides, his words saturated with bitter regret. “He robbed me,” he repeated, “and I robbed him. “For the next year and a half I begged him to have a real match with me, to let us find out once and for all who should have won, but every time he refused. He even cited his back injury as reasoning once or twice before going on to win my Universal Title. When he actually announced his retirement . . .” “Angelo,” Eleanor began in a cautious tone, “Christian is injured. He can’t wrestle anymore.” Hesitantly, ever-so-hesitantly, she took a step toward him. “The man you want to fight isn’t there anymore. In his place is my husband, a strong man if there ever was one, but a man whose back you’d break in the ring. Leave him alone, Angelo. Please. He isn’t in any condition to face you.” “I . . . I know that. But . . .” Cheeks rippling, eyes glaring intently inward, Angelo seemed ready to explode. The explosion came in the form of his fist slamming the counter, making Eleanor jump. His next words, however, choked more than spoken, named it as frustration and misgivings rather than rage. “Why wouldn’t he fight me, Eleanor? Wasn’t I good enough to face him, to give him the match he deserved? I swear on my life I’d make it respectable, Eleanor. I’d give it my all. I’d make it . . . I’d . . .” Eleanor wasn’t aware she was hugging Angelo until his weight collapsed on her shoulder, his still-wet clothing soaking into the cotton of her robe. He wasn’t crying, or really even hugging back, just slumping against her, asking her why, demanding to know why her husband didn’t respect him enough to spill his blood on some canvas. She felt ludicrous doing it, but she rubbed his back, shushing him, comforting him. “It’s alright, Angelo,” she whispered. “It wasn’t that. Christian knew you were the best. That’s why he wanted that match, to show the world that you were next in line. He—“ “Fuck the world,” Angelo spat. “I don’t give a damn what the world thought it saw. I know better.” “He did it out of respect, not a lack of it. If anything—“ Both of them jerked at the sound of the front door opening, of someone wet shuffling through it, their shoes squeaking on the tiles. Christian’s voice rang out clearly, conversationally, echoing through their modest home. “You wouldn’t believe what that old bastard is charging for cantaloupe today, Ellie. He actually expects f—“ The sudden stoppage of speech, along with the crashing to the floor of the bags he’d been holding, announced Christian’s entrance to the kitchen. Eleanor jerked back from hugging Angelo like he was a bonfire, eyes wide with alarm and mouth agape. The Deville took a step back, turning as he did to face Christian, a habitual grin creasing his face, veiling what had been going on. “You son of a bitch,” was all Christian said, his face twisted furiously with outrage and betrayal. The mountain wasn’t world-renown, being little more than a jagged hill in comparison with some of the more notorious mountains, but it still lorded over the world like only a mountain can. The peak was a relatively flat piece of rock barely ten feet in diameter, with sheer drops on two opposing sides. The other sides stretched down and up again, giving way to the rest of the Gatineau Mountains. From one of those sides a hand breached the serenity, gloved fingers clamping down around a jutting piece of stone, displacing the blanket of stone. Another hand appeared a foot and a half over, fumbling around for a hold before settling down and heaving the rest of the body up. Wearing a toque, goggles, and a puffy black snow-suit, with ice caked on his face where the skin was exposed, saddled down with a huge pack and trailing a rope from his waist, Angelo Deville crested the mountain and lunged to his feet, tearing off his cap and eye-protection and breathing deeply of the air. Mountain-climbing was a pleasure rarely undertaken by The Deville, but it was one of his pleasures nonetheless. He loved the sheer majesty of seeing so far, of looking back down and realizing what he’d accomplished. The air was cleaner, more vibrant, as if that normally breathed was some inferior bottled variety. He knew it was thinner being so high, but it was so pure, so invigorating, that he’d argue its fullness with anyone. Strolling around on the zenith as if it was his backyard, Deville filled his lungs with the precious atmosphere, savouring it, enjoying the peace of hearing and feeling only the wind. It whipped against him, threatening to tear him from his safety and launch him hurtling to his death, but his feet were as sure as a mountain goat’s. Even leaning over the edge with his eyes closed, he’d only fall if he made himself. And falling was the furthest thing from his mind. His heart thudded in his chest as he scratched the ice from his face and followed his rope back to the side of the cliff. For him, there was only one thing better than surveying his dominion from the view he deserved, and that was having someone to share the feeling with, someone who loved it as he did, a partner with whom to bask in the glory. As another gloved hand came up over the top, Deville scooped down and seized it, pulling another snow-suited figure panting to the top. “Jesus Christ,” Justin Williamson mused after Deville pulled him up and he’d had a chance to look around. He looked back down the mountain, at how far they’d come. “Now that was hardcore.” Following Deville’s lead, he removed his toque and goggles, discarding them beside those already down. The Deville smiled and nodded, thumping his chest once with his fist. “Do you feel it?” he asked almost fervently, guiding Williamson to the very edge of the cliff. “The jumping jacks in your stomach, the wings in your lungs. The world at your feet.” He could, and nodded that he did. It was impossible not to feel. “I try to shape my life so I feel this all the time, so that every moment is special.” Williamson yelled when Deville’s hand suddenly shoved him in the back, flailing his arms across his body to grab The Deville, to pull him over the ledge with him, forgetting they were tethered together. The Deville had grabbed him at the same time he shoved, however, and his echoing laughter named the ill-conceived joke. “You feel that? For me, that’s life.” Shaking free of The Deville’s grasp, Williamson put a few more feet between himself and the plummet to his death. “Yeah, well.
