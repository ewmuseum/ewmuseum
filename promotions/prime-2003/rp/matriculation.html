Date: ReV 205 Troy Douglas had been told that the air conditioning in some of Columbia University's aging academic buildings were in desperate need of repair. The beads of sweat rapidly pouring off his forehead and onto his white Affliction t-shirt were just the personal, physical proof of that. When he walked into the building five minutes ago on this warm, but certainly not stifling, September New York day, Troy was dry as a bone. Now, as he tapped his right foot against a crack in the creaking hardwood floor, Troy's white shirt was rapidly taking on a translucent tone. The ancient building seemed to trap any humidity and amplify it twenty fold like a greenhouse on steroids, and racing up and down stairs and across hallways had done little to ameliorate the situation. At least he'd finally found the room he was looking for. Two hours earlier, he'd flown into New York, and before he was sucked in to doing yet another interminable round of press for his autobiography, Troy had wangled some free time for dinner with Samantha Wade. So, he rushed - or, rushed as best as one could whilst stuck inside a barely functional New York cab - to Columbia and scrambled across the campus to Sam's office ... only to find he was 20 minutes early, and she was still in class. Which left Troy Douglas no recourse but to wait, stand and sweat as the time passed before Sam's final lecture of the day ended. A lecture he could overhear every time the figure eight he was cutting around the hallway passed near to room 113. "So," came the familiar voice of Samantha Wade from behind the barely-opened door, "can anyone tell me the significance of the Kennedy-Nixon debates?" Silence. "Anyone?" she asked. "Bueller?" From the hall, Troy Douglas stopped dead in his tracks and chuckled. It was an old joke, he knew, but one that Sam didn't make sound as brutally passe and cliche as it actually was. Of course, maybe Troy Douglas shouldn't have laughed. "It was the first debate that truly showed the power of television in politics," came another female voice, this one younger, but still incisive. "Kennedy came off far better on TV, and even though those who listened to the debate on radio often thought Nixon won, the contrast between the two on camera was huge." "Exactly, Caitlin." Of course, Troy thought. Caitlin Bueller. Ain't that an unfortunate last name to get stuck with while in school. Absconding from his established pattern, Troy backtracked a few steps, drawing parallel with the crack in the door and looking through to see a familiar tangle of red hair bobbing up and down as Samantha Wade looked at her notes. She was at the head of a group of 20 or so students, most of them feverishly scribbling down notes as Sam plowed through the next phase of her lecture with aplomb. The topic, of course, was the latter stages of the 1960 presidential election, a sort of gateway to Sam's class on American history in the 60s, a particular fascination of hers. The class was, for the most part, giving her their undivided attention between wipes of their brow and looks to the clock for relief from the heat. Seeing their fatigue, Samantha swung her head toward the clock. "Alright," she said, "looks like that's it for today." Quickly, notebooks and pens were stuffed into backpacks, and Troy stepped back from the door to avoid being trampled by the stampeding herd of co-eds that would soon burst from the classroom. Before they could storm out of the room, however, Sam's voice piped up over the fray once again. "Remember, Bryce can send you copies of the slides if you need them, and required reading is ACTUALLY required! See you Thursday." Then came the rush, and Troy stepped aside as the stream of students started their exodus from room 113 and out to Starbucks and games of Ultimate Frisbee on the quad. Sam, however, stayed behind along with her teaching assistant, the aforementioned Bryce Hawthorn, an eager and earnest British grad student, collecting papers off the desk at the front of the room. Taking his opportunity, Troy slipped past the horde of students and positioned himself directly in the doorway, standing silently as Sam Wade swept up her pile of notes into her trusty green corduroy bag. It was Bryce who looked up first, and Troy merely flicked his right hand at him in a curt wave. "Looks like you've got a visitor, Prof." Samantha barely registered the comment for a moment, needing a prompt from Troy in the form of a clearing of his throat. That was enough to gain her attention and get her to whirl around. Her eyes caught his, and she smiled. That was all he needed to see. ***** In Greensboro, North Carolina, there is a house. In the house, a spare bedroom has been converted into an office. Is that office, there is a desk. In that desk, there is a drawer that can only be opened by a key. In that draw are sealed, unmarked envelopes. Letters to someone that can never open them, read them or even know they exist. From time to time, Troy Douglas turns the key and drops another letter into the drawer. Two days ago was one of those times. Dear Lauren, I know it's been awhile, although the fact that I'm saying that is silly anyway, considering you'll never see this, but I can't go forward in my life without telling you about this, and this is about the only way I know how. I know you probably would've wanted me to move on a bit earlier than 6 1/2 years down the road, but you know me, always the sentimental type, always clinging to things. Truth is, there are two reasons I never had any desire to start seeing other women once you were gone. First, I just didn't think I'd ever find somebody who in any way measures up to you. I put you on this pedestal, and trust me, you'll always be there. You've held a special place in my heart since we were seven years old, and I didn't know if I'd ever be able to believe someone else could come close to filling that kind of void. Second - and I know you'd call me silly for this - I was scared. Scared that by letting someone else in, I'd be pushing you out. That by trying to start over, I'd be deleting our relationship like someone running a bar magnet over a floppy disk. But, here's the thing. It took a long time, but there's someone new in my life. She's not you, I don't think anyone possibly can be, but Sam's fantastic, and more important than that, she does something that nobody since you has been able to do. She makes me smile. I've been down a lot of roads since February of 2003, Lauren, and I've gone down a lot of tunnels. It hasn't been until recently that I found another light at the end of them. I won't be long, I don't want to be or need to be, I just need to let you know that even though I'm finally seeing another light, yours was always the light that guided me safely home. I love you, and I always will. I know that now, no matter what path I take from here. Yours forever, Troy
