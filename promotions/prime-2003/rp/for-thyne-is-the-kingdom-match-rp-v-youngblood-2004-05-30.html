For Thyne Is The Kingdom (Match RP - v Youngblood)

Date: 30/05/04 For Thyne Is The Kingdom.Written: 30th May, 2005. 'Yours is the first face that I saw,I think I was blind before I met you,Now I don’t know where I am ,I don’t know where I’ve been,But I know where I want to go.' - Bright Eyes. I - The Two Towers. The two skyscrapers dominated the cityscape. Ego-strokes of architecture, felating the tastes of their founders. Awe-inspiring protrusions of glass and steel, watchtowers for the populous below... Towers of Babel, making a grab towards Heaven. The Wolfendens, and the Devilles. No matter your surname, you belonged to one of them. For as long as the modern attention span could recall, it had been that way. Technology progressed, the city rose higher from its streets, currency inflated, but nothing really changed. Stage-coaches became cars became S.U.V's. Muskets became pistols became semi-automatics. But no others could become akin to a Wolfenden or Deville. The sounds of the street carnival below reverberated up the structure of Wolfenden Plaza, delighted screams of children, entwined with raucous merriment of the adults. Complimenting their joy, the sky deepened its blue, while the sun gave golden highlights to everything below, painting the city in an almost gaudy palette. Red brick became glowing terracotta, grass infused with lush vibrancy. Brightly coloured balloons danced up the breeze's steps outside the tower, distracting the workers within as greens and reds skipped through their peripheral vision. Atop the tower, in the vault-like room below the spire, Lawrence Wolfenden watched a lone balloon pass through his view. The King of all he surveyed, the Wolfenden family patriarch temporarily forgot any thoughts of meetings, deadlines, or profit margins. Behind the pinstripe, under the greying sideburns, he was a humanist. That one balloon, probably chased after by a child until it was far out of reach, made him wish he'd had more time to spend with his own children. More time away from... away from this. He was the wise, level-headed entrepreneur, a stark contrast to the charismatic, shady Deville. Where Lawrence had build slowly from what he inherited, rewarding the people, trusting them to one day reward him in kind, Angelo had played the Hare to his Tortoise... and it was yet to be seen whether he'd lose the race. Money from questionable means, in plenty, bore Deville and firm foothold, and from there his silver tongue had worked its magic. Where Lawrence had employees, Deville had followers. Some would say Zealots. Mutterings of past retributions kept them in line, while his wrath kept those he presided over in check. Whether by command or initiative, the two families had sparred for decades. From petty theft to industrial sabotage... a twisted sense of respect ensuring no lives were claimed. The city was their chess board, pieces captured and traded, the Kings protected at all costs. They both seemed to know where the other was capable of moving, and what they'd be sacrificing with each choice. Across the city, on a clear day, it was said that Wolfenden and Deville would stand, over a mile apart, but knowing they were looking the other straight in the eye. It was the way it was. As it always had been. But that was soon to change. 'To be that carefree,' Lawrence's laboured words bemoaned as he watched the balloon disappear into the sun's blinding cloak. Eyes lowered to the streets, distant from his pedestal. Floats navigated the streets, stalls lined their route, and the first smile broken that day had infected all around it. Music, amplified only by the passion with which it was played, helped sweep the crowds along, while cannons fired streamers high into the air, sending the brightly coloured, unravelling paper down into the precession. They'd painted the city in bold colour, while Lawrence watched on from his colourless tower, containing grey suit after grey suit, and a personality to match the tone. He was growing weary of it all. Trapped in a moral dilemma, waiting for the salvation of a third option to spring up. A deus ex machina that would warp the world into his vision. He wouldn't wish his life on his children, so would hold the position selflessly. There was no pride in his role, not anymore. Lawrence's son, Maximus, was eager to take the helm, having idolised his father. But although wise in the formal, educated senses, all the schooling couldn't give the young man the kind of edge that only instinct can usher forth. An expansive knowledge of law and business wouldn't act as eyes in your back, while an appreciation of the arts was no substitute for being able to sniff out a lie. And then there was Karina. The Queen on the city's chess board. Unlike her brother, she learned from experience rather than text, and could more than match his intellectual traits physically. But in being the polar opposite, she too had to bear her weaknesses. A head hotter than the sun, and a lack of discipline that lightened Lawrence's hair before its years. Karina had no desires to be a leader, no tolerance for command structure. Where Max was the public face, the prominent sibling, Karina could walk unnoticed... both through his streets, and into the territory of others. II - Connection. Body crouched, her visored eyes gave an instant layout of the floor. Clocking each of the guards, Kari managed to use their directions to give her a moving image of what was in front of her. The black outfit clung to her sleek frame, bulked out by padding and utilities. Even her hair was dyed darker than the fabric, scraped back and restrained with ties and clips. The only skin showing was a lightly freckled back across her nose, everything else, concealed. Her heart-beat slowed, no racing pulse at the prospect of danger, no pores even feeling the first, salty tingle of perspiration. Her 6 foot frame spread-eagled, gloved hands and tread-heavy feet gripping her to the walls, while her back arched up to touch the ceiling. Closing her eyes, she then nodded in time, counting down, hushed, under her breath. 'Three... two... one...' Springing into action, the Wolfenden daughter released the clamps of her extremities, then pushed off the wall with her right foot. Spinning through the air, she used the extra speed of rotation to fling her foot at the oblivious guard's head, catching him plush on the jaw. Karina landed, crouched, barely throwing up so much as a grain of dust from the floor as she caught the limp guard. A clean kick, barely felt before he passed out. Her style, to a tee. In methodical fashion, assured that there would be no other guard with a line of sight to her in the next 14 seconds, Karina unbuttoned the man's sleeve pocket to retrieve his swipe card. Tucking it into her wrist bracer, Wolfenden then removed the man's weapon from its holster, removed the clip, then tucked the clawless weapon into her belt. 'Eight... seven... six...' eyes swung back and forth like the pendulum counting her down. 'I hate long waits.' With a roll of the eyes, Kari removed the ventilation grate, then effortlessly swung herself down into it, just placing the cover back in time to hear the concerned words of the man's workmate. 'Gray?' was followed by pounding footsteps, until a leg was seen stooping beside him. With everything in place, Karina's explosive legs kicked out, sending the grate flying as her feet ploughed into the guard's right knee. Rather than letting forth a roar of pain, only the beginnings of a wince could escape before Kari's legs followed up. The sweet spots of her feet knocked the man's jaw limply in both directions, before he passed out on the pillow of his unconscious colleague. Going through the same routine, Wolfenden took the card, then disposed of the empty weapon down the vent shaft. Un-attaching the guard's keys from his belt, Karina handcuffed the men’s belts together, back to beck, before keeping the keys for herself in a rear pocket. 'Didn't anyone ever tell you not to play with guns?' she regretfully sighed, hating the contraptions. 'Now, 6 seconds to do with as I please... um.... yeah.' With the countdown complete, stealthy, silent steps took her to the corridors' junction. On tip-toes, she pressed herself against the wall, fingers drumming down the time on her thigh. 'Now, time to make a good first impresi-... ugh, I have to cut it out with lines.' Flinging her exploding body around the corner, Wolfenden unleashed a Roundhouse Kick towards the approaching guard's head, only for his reactions to surprise her. More out of desperation than training, he managed to get an arm up in front of his jaw, only to suck air sharply between his teeth as the impact sent a sharp pain down the joint. As he went to double over, Karina bent her legs just enough for powerful thighs to thrust her up, then kick up to score a backflip kick to his jaw. The guard's head snapped backwards, and his body crumpled to its knees in front of the crouched Wolfenden. With a poke of the finger to his forehead, Kari left the young man to unfold on the floor. Another claimed card and discarded weapon later, Karina stood straight, listening attentively as she surveyed what was before her. She looked every bit the action girl, right down to her natural stance, always ready to burst forward. A streamlined body's tone gave hints through her outfit, still managing to carry grace along with its physique. At the junction, she paused. 3 guards, 4 corridors. Turning to the un-patrolled corridor, Karina narrowed her eyes in suspicion. It had the most rooms, and, at its end, had her destination. 'Hmmm, nearly had me there,' she smiled under her half-mask, before a control on her bracer began circling through vision modes... handily showing beams criss-crossing the floor. Rather than risking getting caught in the infra-red spider's web, Karina tore the mask covering her mouth off, then slid her visor up, revealing all of her lightly freckled face. Pale pinks lips were moistened by her tongue, before Karina lowered her head, staring out of the tops of her eyes at the path ahead. Focussing on what was in front of her, Wolfenden neglected to check behind her. Standing over the fallen guards, making no effort to un-cuff them, was one of Deville's deputies - Magnum. A smile formed under his moustache as his arms folded, eyes preferring to scan over the rear view of Wolfenden than look for a radio. He'd been watching her on the monitors ever since she'd crossed the river... he just wanted to see her first hand. The deputy of The Deville, besotted with the daughter of Lawrence Wolfenden. A lick of the lips accompanied a stroke of the moustache, before he heard the faint groan of one of the fallen guards. Snarling down at the oblivious man, Magnum cracked his heel down onto the guard's jaw, then watched with a smirk as his head dropped down limply. 'I'll be seeing you soon, Karina,' went his sinister whisper. 'Maybe I'll even bring the handcuffs.' Stepping backwards into the shadows, Wolf's lecherous glare watched as Karina built up speed, before hopping over the beams, using her endless legs to spread over the hallway. Soles gripped walls just for long enough to propel her along, barely in contact for more than half a second, until Kari pushed off into a forward roll just as she was about to lose her rhythm. Tumbling down just an inch shy of the final beam, Wolfenden rolled through onto her feet, then dusted her black outfit down. 'I miss picking locks,' she grumbled, before flightily, tip-toeing steps carried her down towards the target door. It came as naturally to her as the alphabet or simple arithmetic. While Max learned of money laundering and business law loopholes, Karina went about picking locks and martial arts. Able to immerse herself in the scenarios, Karina improved by always aiming for the impossible. She thought like the security systems, knew how she'd protect things against herself. Observing the key mechanism of the door, Karina fathomed it immediately. It was a system she'd pondered a couple of years ago, before something grander took over her imagination. Ignoring the card-swiper, Wolfenden thumped her knuckles against the panel beside it, causing a click to loosen it. Swinging the cover open to reveal a keypad, Kari retrieved the cards from her sleeve pocket. 'Employee 432, 563, 181...' she mused, breaking into a grin of recognition. Using the sequence of the last number as her order, Kari keyed in the 9 digit code, before closing her eyes triumphantly as the welcoming clunk of the locking mechanism released its grip. A satisfied nod led Karina through the door, before her whole body then froze, smile instantly straightening across her pale lips. 'I believe you've came for this?' In flawless, Bond villain style, Angelo Deville swung his chair to face Karina. In his right hand, a small, aged, purple leather box was tossed, his cobalt gaze highlighting it. Karina's body reluctantly returned control to her, yet she stuck with its policy of statueism. Eyes should have been on the prize, but rather they were locked onto Angelo, transfixed. Such a prominent figure in her life, in her family's history, had only been seen through media, or from a safe distance. He was no longer blurred, or two-dimensional... his chiselled angles were there for her to see. 'It was my mother's,' she softly hissed his way, eyes not breaking from his. Waiting for him to make his move, Wolfenden reached around, untying her hair and letting it cascade down. Fists were clenched, but stance was on the back foot. 'Yes...' Deville confirmed, snapping the box open to give a peak of the jewel. 'A regrettable acquisition from a naive follower who believed it would have been of value to me.' Hopping up from his seat, Deville's eyes matched Karina's, face devoid of any fierce snarl, or menacing scowl. He was the calm amid the storm of his reputation. This too, was the first time he'd seen the daughter of his rival close-up since she was but a child. The subsequent years were most kind. Paralysed by his stare, Karina just bit her bottom lip as he approached, oozing towards her as he removed the necklace from its case. 'There are places it would look far better than in my vault.' From under bushy eyebrows, Magnum watched Karina emerge from the vault room, hair untied and cascading down her back. In her hand hung the necklace, which was then tucked into her belt pocket, zipped securely, back where it belonged. Far from the serene creature that waltzed through the defences, Karina seemed ruffled, cheeks tinted rose beyond their usual, pale skin tone. Magnum's teeth ground as he watched Deville emerge, eyes only for the fleeing daughter of his rival, given a trouble-free exit by his security override. 'Don't get used to her, Angie,' bitter words spilled. 'In fact, don't get too used to anything...' III - In The Shadows. The shadows welcomed Magnum. Every contour of his face seemed chiselled for poor light, while prominent brow left menacing eyes veiled. Like a rodent, his steps were scurrying, darting around the amber wash of the back-streets' lighting. Little sniffs of the air soured his expression, even a man as unsavoury as himself being unused to the tucked-away, forgotten parts of the city. Concealed within a brown, hooded overcoat, the muggy night left Magnum's brow moist with perspiration, beads nesting in his bushy eyebrows and moustache. 'Psst,' he blew through his bleached teeth, eyes scanning for movement in the darkness. The walls in the old quarter of the city were far from the rich, red brick of the new. Stones larger than Magnum's head made up winding streets, while every step was risking a trip over the jagged paths. The stench of the river left him gagging, the humid night amplifying the eye-watering aroma of the restaurants' rotting, dumped waste. 'When this is all over with,' Wolf grumbled as he pulled the side of his gaping hood across his nose. 'I'm having this all demolished.' 'And there shall be none to oppose you, Magnum Wolf.' The words couldn't fail to send a shiver up Wolf's spine as his heart skipped a beat. 'I really wish you wouldn't do that...' From the jagged steps leading down towards the now-redundant, south docks, the figure emerged. The bald head seemed to reflect even the craters of the moon, while his eyebrows took a steep angle down to the bridge of his nose, locking the stern expression in place. Magnum took relief in discarding his coat, then pulled the t-shirt from his clammy skin. As he inched closer, Wolf checked all around him, knowing all too well the sneaky, disloyal nature of the city. 'You have no need to worry,' the bold, echoing voice assured the snivelling Magnum. 'This city lives by commerce, opens with the stock exchange, and closes with television's primetime. It's grown complacent, accepting what is as what will always be. There's only disdain shown for tradition, while convenience is worshipped by all. They've grown complacent, and that will be their downfall.' The words were spewed with contempt, while Magnum's expression made it clear he wasn't buying into the philosophy. Instead, he'd simply been bought by it. 'Um, yeah, that's what I was thinking,' he mumbled, before reaching into the back pocket of his overly-tight jeans to retrieve an envelope. 'I think you’ll find everything I promised there, Mr Youngb-' 'Youngblood,' the slimy traitor was cut off with, as the envelope was ripped from his moist grasp. 'Just Youngblood.' 'Now, like we talked about...' Magnum hung the coat like a cloak from his shoulders, before hands rubbed together in salty, clammy glee. The sweet, heady scent of expectation cut through that of rotting meat and decaying fish, brining a sinister sparkle to his chestnut eyes. 'Yes,' Youngblood's voice spoke, being felt through the aging stone. 'For this, you can ask of us as you wish.' A mixture of smirk and smile formed under Magnum's moustache. 'I don't care what you're going to do to this city, and some bits I probably don't want to know. But. The Wolfenden daughter - Karina - is not to be touched,' the request slithered from his serpent's tongue. 'Other than by the man who'll selflessly save her, if ya know what I mean?' The wink was sleazy, in fitting with the distasteful surroundings. 'Understood,' Youngblood nodded, sliding the envelope inside his jacket pocket. 'We'll be in touch.' 'Sure thing, chief,' Wolf beamed with a raised thumb, before concealing his face with the hood once more. Youngblood watched the snivelling man sneak off, sticking once more to the shadows, before turning back to the stairs. 'When it's time, allow me to be the one that kills the slimey excuse of a man...' Mixing a scoff with a chuckle, Youngblood descended into the shadows, towards the disembodied voice. 'He'll be all yours, along with much, much more... Maximus.' IV - The Reason? The streets under the shadow of Wolfenden plaza were sedate. Free of buildings or business, they formed an asphalt moat around the King's castle. It was the kind of silence that Karina Wolfenden needed, only a light breeze rustling in the trees to soothe her frantic mind. She was face to face with Angelo Deville... as close as any Wolfenden has ever came to striking a blow. He was defenceless, in his own home, just inches away from her. The speed of her feet would have left him unconscious before he'd noticed a muscle twitch. But what was the justification, other than his name? Did simply not being under the rule of her father make Deville her enemy? Was the oblivious theft of her late mother's jewellery any worse than the crippling financial blows of Max's corporate sabotage? As a child, she knew no better. They were taught rhymes mocking the Devilles, and were taught that the word of her father was the truth... fair and just. The river that divided them acted as a mirror, those on the other side believing the opposite. 'I've known nothing different,' the torn Wolfenden reflected, always more of a free spirit than her sibling. 'But what if...' Collapsing down onto a bench, Karina looked to the stars, unsure how to order her thoughts. 'There has to be something we share, something we... Ugh.' She feared not even her father had the answer. Why should she loathe Angelo Deville? Who started the rivalry that divided a city? What was stopping her... what was stopping her extending the olive branch? 'And what if,' her pondering continued as she unbuckled her equipment, shedding all but the figure-hugging black outfit. 'And what if I refuse, until I know what I'm working towards.' Between the white of her father, and the black of Deville, Karina was immersed in the grey. From her belt's pouch, Karina retrieved her mother's necklace, just staring at it in the case. Taken from her before Karina got to know her, it was natural to ask at a later age if Deville had killed her. Such was the hatred held for her father's rival. At times it surprised Karina that it hadn't been written into history as the fault of Deville. Everything else seemed to be. 'Just...' she sighed, turning back towards the river, staring at the distant lights of Deville's tower. 'Just do something to prove me wrong, please.' V - The Change Of Heart. The table may have housed the opinions of Deville's closest aides, but they were under no illusion that only one man's word mattered. To the right of Angelo's vacated seat, Magnum Wolf sat, arms crossed almost secretively as he listened to the ramblings of his cohorts. Circling the desk, absorbing the information from his eyes and ears, Deville seemed distant... about the same distance from the report as he was from Wolfenden Plaza. 'I think this is a threat that we need to investigate further, Sir,' went the advice from the man opposite Magnum. 'We've only heard a few whisperings, but they're whisperings from those we need to worry about. They're not the powerful and influential... they're the cunning and untrustworthy.' Magnum did his best to hold a chuckle in as his eyes rolled, disinterested. 'Word is they're traditionalists,' another chimed in with as Deville looked out across the city, thumb and index finger stroking hiss stubbled chin. 'Old-school methods, brutally effective. They take things apart, piece by piece. It's a science to them. They're not going to be the kind who stay in the shadows for long, Sir.' Before Angelo could turn away from the window, Magnum leant back in his chair, shaking his head belittlingly. 'Why do you trouble the man with rumours and whispers,' he mocked, looking around the table. 'Do you seriously suggest that there is a family who can challenge Deville? Are you going to insult our leader by claiming that there is anyone who is a threat to his position? I say we find whoever is polluting our intelligence and teach them the cost of such treacherous lies.' 'Sir, I fear these are more then rumours,' one tried to overcome Magnum's blunt dismissal with. 'They aren't just coming for us... they want the whole city. We understand they plan to move against Lawrence Wolfenden. And, Sir, if they have the means to come at both of you, they must b-' 'They must be crazy!' Wolf thrust himself up proclaiming, almost breaking out into wild laughter. 'These Youngbloods may be a threat to the weak Wolfendens, but there is not a man on our side of the city that could even THINK about moving against Angelo without me knowing.' Dropping back into his seat, Wolf gestured the previous speaker away, dominating the table as only he knew how. Breaking his gaze away from Wolfenden Plaza, Angelo turned to face his advisors, scanning each of them for concern, before resting on the tutting Wolf. Pacing around the table, Deville was deep in contemplation, not just having selfish concerns any longer. 'This city is fragile,' Angelo commented. 'As opposed as out views are, Lawrence props up his half of the city.' 'Then WE should be the ones to knock it down!' one eager aide proclaimed, only for a cold stare by Deville to cool his emotions. 'We have little to gain by tackling two enemies at once,' Deville lectured. 'Our concern should be with whoever these Youngbloods are. And maybe, rather than opposing Wolfenden, we could be of assi-' 'Is this the same Angelo Deville that I've followed all these years?' Magnum then challenged, rising from his seat once again. 'The Angelo Deville I knew than would have smited both with one motion of the hand, not sought to reconcile with one enemy to beat another. Have we grown soft in this stagnant ceasefire that we seem to have given in to?' The spite in Wolf's eyes was plain to see as they locked onto Deville. Every divided loyalty, and every queasy feeling of jealousy was exploited to add weight to his words. 'We should be building this empire rather than just trying to match the Wolfendens,' Wolf continued, seeing how some were subtly nodding along to his words. 'Or these days, do we fraternise with such filt-' Having heard enough, Deville flung his right fist at Magnum, a row of knuckles enough to silence the motor-mouth. Magnum fell into his chair, seething as blood flowed from a cut lip. Those around the table drew in sharp breathes, uneasy in their seats as they watched Angelo, his stare trying to force any truth he could from Wolf's eyes. 'Forgive me,' the smarmy Wolf then grovelled. 'I spoke out of place.' The minds of Deville's aids were suspicious mechanisms, and Magnum knew that all he needed to do was plant the smallest doubt, and it would be consumed as fuel. Angelo shook his fist, cursing his loss of control. Licking the blood from his lips, Wolf's brows arched, trying to usher further words from his leader. 'If Lawrence is worthy of such a following,' Angelo held firm with, trying to save face, 'then he should be able to deal with whatever threats come his way.' VI - The End Of History. Despite his convictions, Deville couldn't help but feel sheepish as he crept out of his own stronghold. Behind him, Angelo knew he left suspicions and doubts. He too was growing weary of the stalemate, the mundanity, until the sight of his rival's daughter lifted him. He couldn't deny the connection, and neither could she... but could such a scant bond hold over the distance at which they were held? 'I'm going crazy,' he muttered as a mantra. For years, he'd have been happy to take just a mere cent from Wolfenden, just so his rival couldn't have it. Just so he'd know that Deville got one up on him. And now, he was risking his life to save the life of that man, hoping that one day, it may lead to the hand of his daughter. 'Oh, how tiresomely predictable...' Deville hadn't heard the footsteps behind him, and the shadows cast by the Wolfenden Plaza left no clues on the floor. Before Deville could even turn, let alone go for a weapon, he felt a clubbing blow to the back of his head. Angelo staggered forwards a few paces, before swinging a wild right that was swatted away. In the scant light, Angelo couldn't make out anything but the outline of his opponent, and couldn't seem to land a lick. Fighting dirty Angelo went to thrust a knee towards the man's groin, only for an elbow to connect with his jaw first, sending him tumbling to the ground. 'Youngblood, I assume?' Deville growled, hopping back up. 'No real need to remember the name,' came the cocky retort, 'you and Lawrence won't be around to need it.' 'And just how do you plan on getting in there?' Angelo enquired, cracking his neck. 'Me, I was going with the front doorbell, you?' The grin across Youngblood's face widened. 'Oh, I have an invite. VIP pass.' Trying to catch Youngblood off guard, Deville surged forward, landing a right to his opponent's brick-like chin. Barely stunned by the blow, Youngblood lifted Deville from his feet, then drove him down into the paving slabs, wanting to feel bone crush under his weight. Doing his best to ignore the pain shooting up his spine, Deville thrust up with a headbutt, landing on the bridge of Youngblood's nose, but not hard enough to break. Growling, Youngblood grabbed Deville by the collar, pulling him up to land a string of hard punches to the jaw, only for Angelo to get second time lucky with a knee to the groin. A spotlight then flickered on, illuminating the duelling duo. 'Tell Lawrence to get out of there!' Deville appealed towards the light, only for Youngblood to score with a running knee to the side of his head. Watching the fight unfold, Maximus Wolfenden did indeed call his father, but the message was far from what Angelo requested. 'Father, it's Karina, she's... she's been hurt, they've found her out the front!' With Deville kneeling, stunned on the ground, Youngblood pounced, smashing his head off the concrete. Uncontested blows rained in on the side of Angelo's head as he tried in vain to turn and fight back. It was when the punches stopped that he had half a second to think... before he felt the gun barrel press against his temple. 'Where is she? Where's Karina?' then came the booming, Metatron voice of Lawrence Wolfenden as he burst out the front of the building. Eyes instantly gravitated to the spotlight, where he barely had enough time to recognise Deville, before catching sight of the gun. Instinct had Lawrence reaching for his holster, but he never stood a chance. With a security system design by his own daughter, Lawrence had no need for guards, and with nobody to cover his back, Youngblood had a clear, inviting shot. 'Welcome to the end of history,' spat into Deville's ear, 'for thyne is the Kingdom.' As the gun went off, Deville was left grabbing his right ear, which had been only a few inches away from the weapon. The spotlight went off as Youngblood released Deville, and vanished from the path of Angelo's wildly swinging right fist. Gritting his teeth through the pain, Deville staggered towards Lawrence, trying to wipe the blood from his face as he crawled in the dark. 'Dad...' then ripped though him. 'DAD!' The anguished words of Karina Wolfenden sliced across the ominous night as she emerged, eyes wide and transfixed on her father. By his fallen body, a trickle of blood flowed through the paving slabs, giving a crimson border to his dropped weapon. 'This isn't...' Angelo went to appeal, bloody hands held up, and Youngblood's weapon just a few feet behind him. 'Just li-' Hot-headed at the best of times, Karina gave up control to her grief as she lunged forward to pick up her father's weapon. Six aimless shots were let off in Deville's direction as he fled, lost in a mixture of shadow and tears as the young Wolfenden dropped to her fallen father's side. V - Free Thought. There were no guards to challenge Karina's combat prowess, and no security systems to test her knowledge. Everyone had been sent home, and all open doors formed a path to the building's summit, where Deville sat, looking out across the city in turmoil. Across the river, there was no street parade like the previous afternoon, just grief and anger. The black being worn a depressing contrast to the vibrant colours Lawrence had inspired. Taking a sip of scotch, Angelo was almost tempted to offer a toast to Lawrence... even in death their differences prevented him from the token of respect. 'How did it come to this,' regret ushered from his mumbling lips. Just as an ember of hope had danced on the breeze across the city, it had been extinguished, and a raging fire of destruction set to spite it. As the elevator's bell chimed behind him, Angelo rose from his chair, removed his tie, then slid his shoulders free from his jacket. Before the doors had even opened, Angelo's cuffs were released from their links, allowing the sleeves to be rolled up. 'Will my word mean anything to you?' he appealed, seeing Karina's reflection in the glass. 'Washed my father's blood from your hands yet?' she hit back, blanking any appeals. 'I wouldn't want you to taint it with your own.' Clad in her tactical black attire, the solemn Karina strode towards Angelo, ripping her gloves off. Letting out a reluctant sigh, Angelo opened his body up to her. 'I won't fight you,' he informed, holding arms out to his sides. 'Is that what my father said?' Karina snarled, the gap between them closing rapidly. Before Angelo could reply, Karina flung her fist at his face. Reacting quickly, Deville managed to block, but couldn't avoid the kick to his side that followed. As Deville winced, Karina's left fist looped over his defences, connecting with his prominent, beaked nose. Staggering back, Deville managed to keep the next duo of punches at bay, but as blood gushed from his nose, a Roundhouse Kick crashed into his temple. Stumbling away from the fuming Wolfenden, Deville retreated to behind his desk, only for Karina to plant both hands on its cluttered surface, then launch herself over to score another kick to Deville's blood-soaked face. 'Always back down from a fair fight, do you?' Kari pressed as Deville again staggered away, blood soaking his shirt. A cough from Deville expelled blood from his throat, and before he could offer any words in reply, Karina's foot impacted off the back of his head, sending him crashing into the book shelf. Lost in the battle, Karina flung a fist towards his already broken nose, but Angelo managed to dodge his head away just in time. 'Will you listen to me!' the spluttering Deville appealed as Kari's fist caught his ear. 'Check the security footage! I tried to sto-' Karina cut him off with a furious kick to his ribs, leaving The Deville huddled over, gasping for air as a second kick thumped against his jaw. 'Why?' Karina demanded to know, scoring another punch to his ruined nose as he desperately began to claw himself away from her. 'After all this time... why?' Reaching behind her back, into her belt, Karina pulled out the one weapon she'd sworn never to brandish, and purposefully pointed the barrel down between his appealing blue eyes. Head locked in place, staring back through the sight at Karina, Angelo coughed up more of his own blood, breaths gargling. 'Because there has to be more than this,' he wheezed, blood starting to run down into his eye sockets. 'Because I don't want to stand opposed, distant from you as I was from him...' All her life, she'd known nothing but hate for him. It was natural, subconscious... the reaction before the thought. She was taught the reaction without the reason, the emotion without the trigger. In times of anger, it was there to override rational thought, perpetuating the cycle. Now, she had a chance to break out... a chance to build something new, something better from the debris. 'Because...' Deville continued to splutter. 'And now, because, his death shouldn't further this, it should end it... Karina? Karina... karina... ka-' 'Huh?' the disorientated K-Wolf expelled as Angelo's words crept into her ear. Squirming free of Deville's hold, Karina snapped her body around, eyes darting around the unfamiliar surroundings. 'Are you okay?' the concerned Deville enquired, flicking on the bedside lamp. 'You were thrashing around and mumbling something about your father.' Embarrassment swelled in Karina's cheeks as she took deep breathes, firmly anchoring herself back in reality. 'Um, yeah, fine... I tend to, yeah, that.' 'I wouldn't mind it so much,' the relieved Deville remarked as he flicked the light back off, 'but you kick bloody hard, even in your sleep.' Karina felt herself pulled back into Deville's comforting clutches as the covers were wrapped back around her. Eyes closed with a relieved exhalation as she effortlessly drifted back off to sleep, safe that her own mistrusts of Deville had been overcome... and rather dangerously, having left the imminent peril of Youngblood trapped in the rapidly diminishing memory of the dream.
