This Is Your Brain. This Is Your Brain On The Forsaken.

10 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 10th, 2006 We can dance if we want to,We can leave your friends behind.‘Cause your friends don’t dance, and if they don’t dance,Well, they’re no friends of mine. There was a smile on Clyde’s face as he strutted down the sidewalk, leaning back while his legs made unnatural strides before him, all the while listening to the Best Of Men Without Hats CD James had given to him for his birthday the previous day. And why shouldn’t he be smiling? After all, he had just captured the Universal Title from an unbeatable adversary, ripped through Teh Greatness on his own to win the Tag Titles for himself and James, and run over his GTT5 opponents like a speeding car through a school zone just minutes after the final bell. Clyde was on top of the world, and his stuffed package showed that. Walkins: We can dance if we want to, something, something, fine! As long as we have music, we’re never gonna lose it, everything will come up Clyde! He spun on his heels, performing a twirl in the middle of the crowded sidewalk, before coming to a stop with his hands forming guns in the direction of the nearest female. Winking, Clyde clicked his tongue, prompting a shriek from the woman, followed by a mad dash across the street. Walkins: Hmm. Must be a thespian. Oblivious to the fact that the stuffing in his crotch had started to go south, Clyde continued on along his merry way, singing replacement lyrics to the music and hitting on every woman he saw, his pseudo-package bouncing against his lower thigh with each step. He was nearly home, and all would have been well had he gotten there in time, but just before the green light could turn red and allow him to cross, a mother stepped out of the nearby corner store with her child pushing a doll in a stroller in front of her. With the light changing to yellow, Clyde bit his lower lip and wondered if he should brighten their day with the package of a thousand glories, or head straight home like James told him to. The light went red and a noise signaled to let blind people know it’s ok to cross. Clyde had little time to make a choice. Walkins: Hey, ladies! Check out this Christmas miracle! Winking with his left eye, he gave them the double guns and an enormous thrust, causing the stuffing of his package to stretch his pants nearly to the point of ripping, almost touching the little girl’s stroller. By that point in time, both looked horrified, but the kid let out a scream not heard by anyone before when the stuffing dropped down his leg and rolled to a stop at her feet. Little Girl: Mommy! A dead kitty! Grabbing her daughter tightly around the arm, the woman gave Clyde a disgusted and fearful look, before dragging her child down the street and away from the insane man. Meanwhile, Clyde poked the dead kitten with the toe of his shoe. Walkins: I don’t see what they were so upset about. It was alive when I put it in there. - Back at the house, Clyde slowly unlocked the door and breathed in the air of a fresh party. Truth be told, his birthday party consisted of James giving him a used CD for a present and mumbling something about having a good night before going to bed. But, for Clyde, it was the first birthday where he ever felt like a true winner, and no lack of party could ever change that for him. However… “SURPRISE!!!” Walkins: Ahh! Burglars! If you want the combination to my safe, you’ll have to torture James to get it! Clyde pulled a handful of thumbtacks out of his pocket, hurling them into the darkness of his home. As the lights were flicked on, the man whose made more random appearances in Forsaken roleplays and segments than the Lord Jesus Christ stumbled around the living room with a tack sticking out of his eye. The rest of the party guests only paid mind to the spectacle for a moment, before resuming the festivities. Farwell: No, Clyde…this is a surprise party! Happy birthday! Walkins: But…it was my birthday yesterday. Why are you throwing me a party today? This doesn’t make any sense…unless…you forgot my birthday, didn’t you? James huffed out a sigh, already becoming annoyed with Clyde’s suspicious nature. Farwell: It’s a party. Take it or leave it. Walkins: Is there cake? James nodded. Walkins: And presents? James nodded again. Walkins: Good presents? James lifted his hand, tilting it to each side. Clyde wondered if the ratio of good presents to bad would be worth it. Walkins: Fine, I’ll accept. But, if this party is anything like last year, consider yourself no longer my friend! Cut to January 9th, 2005. Clyde and James sit on the couch, James with a huge grin on his face, and Clyde with an unsure expression on his. A couple of big burly men wheel a giant cake into the room, before leaving after getting James’ signature on a few papers. Farwell: You’re gonna love this, Clyde. I know it. The top of the cake prepares to pop open, and a struggle ensues, resulting in a hacking cough from within the cardboard effigy. Suddenly, a foot kicks out the side, and an elderly stripper with more wrinkles than an elephant’s ass climbs out, awkwardly grinding against the cake the entire time in an effort to seduce the paying costumers. Working her way to Clyde, she takes a drag from a cigarette through the hole in her throat, removing her false teeth and placing them on a nearby table as she dry-humps his leg. James can hardly contain himself, and starts vibrating from trying to maintain his laughter, his face going red and contorting as he attempts to keep from breaking out, while Clyde just looks worried. Back to The Forsaken. Farwell: Don’t worry, there’s no strippers this time. I think she liked you, though. Clyde’s brow furrowed, and he shot James an angry look before pushing past him, while Skywolf still staggered around the room, trying to pull the tack out of his eye. - After having sifted through poor-to-mediocre presents for the better part of fifteen minutes, Clyde rolled his eyes and set the Gameboy Color on the table in front of him, sighing as he took in all the gifts given to him by friends and casual acquaintances. Old Nintendo stuff, Digimon toys, those stupid Lego knock-offs that are far too flimsy to actually be of use, among a plethora of other awful presents. Needless to say, the surprise party took his day down from being great to being crap in all of half an hour. But, there was still James. Sure, he got him the CD already, and never gave the best presents in the past, but this time would be different. Farwell: Here’s mine, Clyde. We saved the best for last. Tearing the wrapping paper off in a feeding frenzy of greed and selfishness, the excitement on Clyde’s face disappeared when he saw what James had bought for him. Walkins: Oh. Farwell: Oh? Walkins: Thanks, I suppose… Clyde sighed as he placed the unwanted present by his side. Ha! I’m a poet and didn’t even…ah, screw it. What’s the point? Farwell: Come on, Clyde. I thought you’d like it. I mean, there are plenty of fun things you can do with a potato gun, I’m sure. Walkins: Name one thing. Farwell: Like…ah…you know…shooting things with pieces of potato…and stuff… Walkins: Ok, let’s give it a try then. James waited for a moment, then hung his head. Farwell: …We don’t have any potatoes… The vein in Clyde’s forehead bulged slightly, and his eyes glazed over with intense rage. Standing up, he brushed the excess wrapping paper from his clothes, turned to James, and slowly began to speak. Walkins: You are the worst best friend ever! You don’t care about me, all you care about is yourself! The only reason you even threw this stupid party was to make fun of me! Farwell: Oh, puh-lease! I arranged this party so you could have a good time after all the hard work you’ve done, but you just can’t accept anything that isn’t up to your standard. You’re the selfish one, Clyde. All you care about is yourself, and calling me the worst best friend ever is one of the stupidest things you’ve ever done! I’m the best friend you could hope for, and even if I’m not, there are way worse best friends you could be lumped with. Cut to Dragon Silver sitting on a stool over in the corner, drinking a beer while a fetus sits on the stool next to him. Holding its feet together like babies do, the fetus looks over at one of the other guests, deciding to see if he can get a rise out of him. Fetus: Hey, you. Yeah, you with the ugly face. I’m talking to you. You’re the grossest thing I’ve ever seen! Turning from his own pitcher of beer, a face from OSW that just about no one would recognize glares at the fetus. Freddy: Where the hell do you get off, you disgusting little creature?! Fetus: All over your mother! EHHH! Lunging for the fetus, Freddy is held back by his brother, Dougie, who struggles to keep the big man from destroying the fetus that just continues to try and gross everyone out with its antics. Dougie: It’s not worth it, man. Just let it go. Meanwhile, Dragon Silver motions to one of the hired AWC wrestlers posing as waiters carrying around various alcohol after having polished off the last of his beer, taking two more bottles from the tray as the fetus rolls around on the edge of the stool while holding its feet together, before falling right off. Back to The Forsaken again. A look of regret over what he said crossed Clyde’s face as the anger subsided, while James’ expression softened and he realized that maybe a potato gun wasn’t the best birthday present. Maybe a birthday present needed to cost more than a buck fifty. Farwell: How about the two of us ditch this party and head on over to McDonalds? My treat? Clyde choked back a tear. Walkins: Sniff. You had me at hello. James’ eyebrow raised. - At McDonalds, Clyde and James both entered through the doors simultaneously, each man with their respective PRIME Tag Title over a shoulder, and Clyde with his Universal Title around his waist. The two strutted along as if they owned the place, making sure to shine their gold in the faces of those standing in line before them, pushing past children and elderly alike before reaching the counter. Farwell: Yeah, I’d like an order of small fries. What do you want, Clyde? Walkins: Let’s see here…I’ll start off with a Big Mac for an entrée, and then a side of Chicken McNuggets, a large Coke and fries, and I’ll top it all off with an order of everything else. That should do. James gulped as he looked into his wallet, the enhanced paycheck for champions not having arrived yet, while the group of people behind them started to complain. Mob: We were here first! Yeah! Get to the end of the line! Clyde turned around, eyeing the first person he saw. Walkins: Do you have any idea who I am? I’m the PRIME Tag and Universal Champion! I’ve defeated the undefeatable! I’m Captain Clyderisma, the Prince of Pwn, Clyder-Man, whatever you want to call me…but the simple fact remains that…I’m Clyde Walkins, BITCH! Holding his hands in front of his face to block the spray of spit from Clyde’s mouth, James shifted his head back to avoid the intensity of the yell. Farwell: I know who you are, dammit! Tell them! Grinning nervously, Clyde turned slightly further, aiming in the direction of the angry mob. Walkins: Everyone heard what I said, right? A few nods came from the group. Walkins: Good. Well, it applies double to each of you. Clyde looked around, trying to hide his anxiety, while also searching for a fast exit. However, an exit wasn’t needed, as Clyde’s rant seemed to satisfy the mob, who, instead of assaulting The Forsaken with foreign objects, asked for autographs. Well, mainly from Clyde. James sorta got pushed out of the circle of people surrounding Clyde, while they scrambled for autographs from the man that beat Tchu, as well as a number of others. Walkins: Now, now. There’s no need to push, plenty of Clyde to go around. Clyde basked in his newfound glory, while James leaned against the counter, shooting the shit with the pimple-faced clerk that greeted him. Farwell: Look at all of them, trying to get a signature from someone they didn’t care about last week. Not you or I, though. Nope, guys like us don’t need reassurance from anyone else. Clerk: I’m a girl. James tried to hide a smile, but failed. Clerk: And your order comes to $211.96.
