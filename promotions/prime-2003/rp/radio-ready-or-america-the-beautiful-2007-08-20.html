Radio Ready: Or, America the Beautiful

20 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/20/07 SOUND AND THE FURY Oh, irony. Sweet, patriotic, ironically unironic irony. We begin our vignette today with something of a parody. Please bare with me as I’ve had two glasses of Jack Daniels and the cretins at our production company are trying to get me to read from cue cards. Ahem. Sound and Fury presents…Real American Heroes. Real American Heeeeeeroes! Where did that voice come from? I’m the only narrator here! Oh, sorry. I’ll continue. Today we salute you, Mr. Over-The-Top Comedic Wrestler. Mr. Over-The-Top Comedic Wresssstleeeerr! In a world where men battle for supremacy and pieces of gold while wearing ridiculous form-hugging tights, you exist to point out just how funny the whole situation is. Think big man got excited! They huff and puff, using their unnatural muscles and gimmicks such as angry anarchists, angry foreigners, and angry people with good hearts to try and get themselves and their sport taken seriously. You? You dress as something different every week, just to mock them. Look so good in costumes! It doesn’t matter how ridiculous you look, what you say or do…because at the end of the day, you know you’re just getting paid so fat guys can have a dream. Keep reaching for the sky, fatty! So crack open a nice cold beverage of your choice, Mr. Over-The-Top Comedic Wrestler. Because in the land of the brain-dead, the self aware brain-dead is king. Mr. Over-The-Top Comedic Wressssstleeeeeeeeeeeeerrrr! Soundandfuryproductionsneworleanslouisiana. Well, that was fun… And had basically nothing to do with anything. Seriously, Ian, Gil…why did I just whore myself out to do some stupid ripoff commercial? “Haven’t you ever thought about fading up to see what we’re dressed as?” is Ian’s smug reply. No, I hadn’t, actually. I’ve only been a narrator for five blooming years. Yes, blooming. Fade up. Ahhh, now I see. Ian and Gildenstern are standing at a street corner, each looking short of breath. Ian is wearing full army gear from WWII while Gildenstern is wearing a dress shirt and coat on top and…um…the British flag on bottom. Actually, I take back what I said. I don’t see. What the hell are you guys? “Well isn’t it obvious?” asks Ian in frustration. …No. “We’re American Heroes!” shouts Gildenstern, putting his hand to his throat to check for a pulse. There’s one there, of course. American Heroes, huh? Anyone in particular? “Yeah. I’m Patton, or did you want me to give an impassioned speech in front of an American flag? I’LL DO IT!” shouts Ian. “HE’LL DO IT, I SWEAR! HE’S BEEN PRACTICING ALL MORNING!” corroborates Gildenstern. Fine, I’ll take your word for it. And you, Gilly? “Hehe, I love it when you call me Gilly, God.” After a brief pause, he gestures to his clothes. “I’m Winston Churchill!” …Winston Churchill. “Uh huh,” Gildenstern nods happily. But…uh…right. Okay. Whatever. So, advance the plot a little…what are you guys doing? “Well,” Ian says, still obviously out of breath, “we’re running for our lives from that guy who got mad at us for stealing his gimmick.” Oh, yeah, from last time [<--CLICK LINK IF YOU DON'T REMEMBER -Ed.]…you mean you’re still running from him? Ian shrugs. “Well, he had the decency to wait about four weeks for us to do a few wrestling shows, and then another few hours to change costumes. Really considerate.” Mr. Over-The-Top Comedic Wressssstleeeeeeeeeeeeerrrr! Cut that out! Sorry, Mr. Narrator maaaan! …do you always have to sing? I’m trapped in the studio please help meeee! So, anyway…as our heroes still stop to catch their collective breath, a rumbling comes from the hills from the South, and with the rumbling comes an angry man walking leisurely with his magnifying glass to the ground, looking at the ground for, presumably, clues of some sort. “Still no sign of the villains, Sound and the Fury, but I have hope,” Bobby Ottilio mutters, his eyes so focused on the ground that he does not realize his targets are merely feet away. “The world favors justice, so it won’t be long before my magnifying glass leads me to revenge!” he says triumphantly before crashing magnifying glass first into Gildenstern’s British pants. “Zounds~!” shouts Bobby (the tilde is silent), jumping backwards and looking straight into the eye of his quarry. “Oh ho, I’ve got you now!” he says. “Quick, Ian, do something,” says Gil, hiding behind his much smaller tag team partner. “You!” Ian yells, poking Bobby Ottilio in the chest. Bobby, shocked, backs up a few steps, giving Ian the room and motivation he needs to keep going. “Now, I know what you want to do with us. You want to make us die. You want to make us die for our country.” Bobby’s anger drops quicker than Integer Chris’s balls as he looks to Ian in confusion. “I didn’t want to make you die for your…” “Well, listen, private,” interrupts Ian, “want you to remember that no bastard ever won a war by dying for his country. He won it by making the other poor dumb bastard die for his country.” Uh oh. Gildenstern looks up and whispers, “I told you he was practicing, God.” Ian, meanwhile, continues: “I know you are wondering whether or not you'll chicken out under fire.” “I am?” asks Bobby, unsure of himself. “You are. Don’t worry about it, I’m sure you’ll fight and die a worthless death and no one will miss you.” Gil tries to restore reason, saying, “But that’s not part of the…” “And as you’re lying there, bleeding and being as ugly as the day you were born, you’re going to remember that you are a loser. And America loves a winner, but they will not tolerate a loser.” Ian pauses, then says, with bravado, “I guess what I’m trying to get to you with this speech is that you’re a terrible person and instead of trying to kill Sound and the Fury, you should be killing yourself.” … That was the worst butchering of a great speech I’ve ever heard. “Wait til you hear my ‘ask not what your country can do for you’ speech,” says Gil proudly. But…that was Ken… Nevermind. Whatever. Meanwhile, Bobby Ottilio looks down at the ground in impeccable sadness. “Maybe…maybe you’re right, General.” He grabs the gun from Ian’s side and tries to end his worthless existence. Ian, stop this nonsense right now! “What? Fiiine…” says Ian, then takes the gun from Bobby’s hands. “Don’t kill yourself, all right? Someone might miss you and I don’t want them suing me. Why don’t you hang around us instead?” Bobby sniffs. “Around you guys? But you guys are my mortal enemy!” Ian smiles. “Tell you what. You can follow us around and have hatred and contempt in your heart for us, and we’ll pretend we don’t know.” After a brief pause, Bobby smiles and hugs the two heroes. “You guys are the best. You two really are American Heroes!” But Gildenstern…he’s not…ugh… Fuck it. Bobby and the “American” Heroes walk down the street, discussing very important stuff as they do. “Have you seen the kid who was on the cover of the Nirvana album? He’s all grown up now,” says Bobby. “Yeah,” assents Ian and Gil simultaneously. “Ever feel dirty now that he’s older because you’ve seen a dude naked?” And we mercifully fade.
