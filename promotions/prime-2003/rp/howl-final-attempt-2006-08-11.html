Howl (final attempt)

11 August 2006

Date: 8/11/06 ((We're going to try this one more time. I don't know why the end keeps getting clipped off. My apologies, but here goes nothing.)) “Howl” Eighty-nine hours ago, Damien was winning a minor victory that would carry with it some momentum going into the PRIME PPV, and the WATCH clusterfuck for some title he wasn’t even sure he could name. It wasn’t that he wasn’t grateful for the opportunity, he was; very grateful. He just pictured things differently. First off, he imagined that his first title shot would be a little more…intimate, maybe. He pictured the gladiator-style man-to-man showdown. He pictured walking down the aisles and into the lion’s den. Two men enter; one man leaves. It was supposed to be the Thunderdome, baby. He wanted a face to pin up to the wall in the gym. He wanted to carry a log across his shoulders in the snowy Russian mountains haunted by the flashing images of that primordial enemy killing his best friend. Damien Cruz wanted to snap up in the middle of the night, trying to escape the laughter of that Behemoth Nemesis. Damien found it hard to stand in the driving rain, stare up at the sky, and scream eight names into the eternal darkness. The other thing that got under Damien’s skin was the idea that he was no further up the PRIME ladder than he had been when he was a week in. He knew that he was the reason for that. He understood that he had missed a few opportunities and let a few great chances slip between his fingers. He knew that, and made allowances for that fact. However, it was still hard to make peace with. Whether it was his doing or not offered little resolution. Instead, it became a muddy stew of self-anger, resentment, jealousy, insecurity, and desperation. He wanted to prove everyone wrong. He wanted to prove the nay-sayers wrong. He wanted to prove the critics wrong. He wanted to prove wrong the media, the fans, the analysts, the investors, the veterans, the new guys, and himself. He wanted to drive the message of his presence by any means necessary. He could, would, and wanted to return to the roots of his career. It would please him to no end to unravel the barbed wire and plastique-covered tables that he had come to know so well in Asia. It would also please him to walk into that ring, and put on a clinic. Meet every offensive strike with a sound and impenetrable defense. He wanted to overwhelm all defenses with a punishing offense. The Latin Assassin had spent more than a decade in the ring, honing a craft that so few understood, sacrificing and squandering the things that most people center their lives around. Wealth was immaterial to him. A normal, functional, healthy romantic relationship served as a crutch for as long as a literal crutch did. As soon as his body was able to return to the ring, so did his mind, his heart, and his spirit. Sylvia was great, she just didn’t understand. Anyway, she was with David now, and she was better off that way…and even if she wasn’t, she was an adult and he was not her keeper. She’d be back if she felt the need. Damien thought about his parents. He had given them a home in Lake Tahoe, and allowed his father the convenience of retiring early, and taking care of his wife. Ivan and Lydia Cruz were going to be fine, and it came at the cost of their son bleeding like a stuck pig in front of a million strangers. If it was a moral dilemma for anyone involved, then they could sell the home and Ivan could go back to doing file work for the American-owned sneaker factory that was in the center of the town, and crippling the local economy. No one wanted that, so things remained unsaid. A warm meal and his mother’s care packages once in a while were all Damien asked for. Damien had a brother, but he died when Damien was two years old. At least, that’s how it was told. Other versions say that Javier was kidnapped for a ransom that Damien’s father could no way afford. When the kidnappers realized that Ivan was not who they thought he was, they never called again, and that was the last of that. Dead, kidnapped and raised by another family, sold into slavery, or left on the side of a Mexican road twenty-four years ago basically amounted to the same thing. If Javier was still alive, he was no longer the son that the Cruz family knew him to be, and some possibilities are better left ignored. After all was said and done, it was easier to be grieving parents than to die from anticipation and false hope. At least, that’s how it was told to Damien. Which was a strange philosophy the more Damien thought about it. It was maybe tied into their strict Catholic up-bringing; an upbringing that Damien drifted away from. He wasn’t angry at God. In fact, his closest friend, idol, teacher, mentor, and adopted father was a Catholic priest. A man named Father Morales. Father Morales was a former luchadore who had left behind a life of drugs, women, and excess for the simple life of humble service to God. Father Morales served God until his last day. It would be a year in February. Damien attended the funeral and delivered a heartfelt and unapologetic a eulogy. It was a moment that had become fable, canon, and hearsay among those in attendance. But for those who didn’t understand who Damien was, it was hard for them to understand who Father Morales was. Sure, they cried, but they barely mourned. After Father Morales’ death, Damien considered walking away from the ring for good. Out of respect and perhaps as a way of finding closure. It was, after all, Father Morales who had given birth to The Latin Assassin, and perhaps Damien should have put that part of himself to rest along with his Teacher. But he didn’t for a lot of reasons. He didn’t because he understood that The Latin Assassin was not a part of him; it was him. More and more, he was The Latin Assassin, and Damien Cruz was a part of him. Damien Cruz mourned his mentor’s death; The Latin Assassin pulled on the tights the next evening and lost a hard-fought match. Damien Cruz loved Sylvia Castelles and was ready to offer her the life that he knew she deserved. The Latin Assassin bought a ticket to Glendale Falls, and assumed she would follow him if she was really worth it. Damien Cruz had a lot of reasons to be unsure. The Latin Assassin closed his eyes and jumped. Often. Through burning tables, through light rods, onto thumbtacks, into steel ladders, over his enemies, and toward the Bigger and Better Tomorrow. It hurt. After the cameras were off, the lights dimmed, the blood dried and flaking, and the gathered massed parted, there was only the ache, the aftermath, the sound bites, the replayed moments in his head, and Damien to make sense of it all. The Latin Assassin was dormant once again. As the tape was peeled from his oft-bloody or bruised knuckles, as the pads were rolled away from the swollen joints, as the blood was washed from the back of his throat or from the wound that was opened somewhere on his body, so was that invincible Assassin who didn’t have to think about the medical bills, the inter-event traveling, or the fact that there was hundreds of men who would take Damien’s place if he couldn’t get up and perform the next day. And it got hard sometimes. His muscles felt like wet leather, his bones felt like tinder, and his head pounded from the back forward, in starburst pulses. Pop a few Advil, take a few extra minutes in the steam room, and wrap the tape a little tighter. If not, call it a career and go home to an empty home. Was it always worth it? Well, it didn’t always matter. What is or is not worth it is decided years before you decide that you want to make a living by doing what so many others have spent their lives avoiding. Pain as a by-product is one thing; pain as the central instrument of your profession is a whole other ball of wax. And then there was the business end of it. Let it never be forgotten that the epic battle of wills between the Homerian Warriors takes place on a million-dollar stage with a couple million dollars worth of sound, lightening, pyro, video, and digital editing equipment. The marketing department will want a catchphrase, a trademark pose, a media-friendly danger, and a Wow-factor at every show. Want the ABC deal? Well, that’s Disney, and they’ll be damned if there will be any Diva pillow fights or First-Blood matches. But then there’s the core demo of 18-35 year old males that live for that stuff. What does the company do? They hire Ivy League business graduates to figure that out; but know that what they decide, the performer does. Climb a twenty foot ladder to perform a leg drop that would hurt at four feet because it looks great in replays. If you injure yourself, get the shot. If you injure him, get the shot. If you miss, get the shot. Beyond all else, get the shot. Superstars are made when unnecessary pipes are left lying around the hallways to be used in a Falls-Count-Anywhere match. Why are they using lightweight aluminum pipes? For the off-chance that the match spills out into the halls, of course. Why are there bingo hall-style folding tables left under the ring, nowhere near any place that would possibly use them? Because ECW took place in a Philadelphia bingo hall and space was limited. Damien knew that he was a product like everyone else on the roster. It was time he started to find the fun in it. And it used to be fun. He spent four years learning his art form in Mexico. At 18, as soon as he was able to afford a plane ticket, he flew to Asia to complete his evolution. Three years later, with stories of the glitter and gold of the American circuit, Damien was doing backyard wrestling matches and underground fight clubs throughout the Southwest. He had a ticket back to Cielo Azul in his back pocket the day he agreed to meet with a local promoter out of Texas. IHWL was where he had his first exposure to the American audiences. He did a seventy-four minute Ladder match, and was paid eighteen dollars and a voucher for a room at a nearby motel. Other nights, it was a ticket to the buffet. One night Damien pulled a battle royal, won, and was paid seventy dollars. He ended up owing the own a hundred and thirty for breaking one of the bleachers. It was shitty indie work, but word got around and The Latin Assassin began to find his stride. Five years later, he was a proud member of PRIME. Well, maybe ‘proud’ is too strong a word. He was proud of his place in PRIME, he just questioned if the feeling was mutual. There was that incident early in which security asked to see his ID pass while he was in the locker room, suiting up. Other than that…yeah, it wasn’t a bad place to be. He’d done B- work, he’d admit it, but was barely pulling a C. If there was a time to turn that around, Colossus III would be a good place to start. If he fell short, he’d start to seriously consider walking away for good. It was time. He knew that his body was still able. He still felt hungry. He still felt eager. But at some point, Einstein’s definition of insanity must be taken into account. As the saying goes, it is insanity to perform the same experiment over and over again and expect a different result. It had occurred to him, walking away wouldn’t be the worst thing in the world. He was looking to retire under one of three conditions: irreparable bodily damage, signs of cognitive deficits, or his thirtieth birthday. There were two others, but one had already passed and decided against on the ride home from Father Morales’ funeral. The other was the birth of a child; and there had been a scare. That condition seemed more relevant until Sylvia came home from the hospital and reported that it was just a fallopian cyst, and would be fine after a run of antibiotics. Damien was relieved that he didn’t have to tell her that he planned to raise a child without giving up his livelihood. It might have been dishonest, but he hugged her, and resolved himself to another day at the offices now that the forthcoming child was no longer coming forth. He often wondered if she knew better, but after she left him with a letter weighed down by her engagement ring, he figured it was a moot point. The point that was made and valid was that she read between the lines with or without Damien’s message being deliberate. She was gone, Damien was the long-suffering loner, and everything was as everyone liked it. Damien was in no position to feel cheated. He was simply living the life that he was determined to live; all others be damned if they could not understand that. As she was quick to remind him the last time they had spoke, he was who he was long before she was a part of the equation, so…it was that type of ironic justice that made Damien wish he were more careful with his self-agonizing speeches. At least, and it said a lot that he had, he offered her an explanation. Debra, the woman who had found the chink in his armor and the route to his bed was not so fortunate. Damien literally ran away from home. She’d call his house and find the phone disconnected. She’d stop by and find the loft empty with a business card for Amanda Hearst, the real estate agent that was showing the place, taped to the door. Debra would stand in the hallway with a home-made casserole or a hastily-ordered pasta dish in her hands, wondering what she had done wrong, or failed to do right. But some things are hard to explain, even to a woman who had heard every excuse before. Damien felt angry at himself for allowing that situation; a situation that he had seen coming from a mile away, to fall in on his head. He was embarrassed that it happened and ashamed by the fact that he lacked the fortitude to come clean to her like a man. Being a thousand-plus miles away from a bad situation is the worst way to try and correct it. He could probably wait for her to forget, and despite being very cold and immature, it was wholly plausible. She had the attention span of a open field hunter. Great at catching what she set her sights on, but smart enough to give up the long shot. She would zero in on the weak, the vulnerable, and the easily-caught. Apparently, Damien had fallen into that category, and he felt ill to think that were even remotely true. It was true, and Damien closed his mind to the thought whenever it crept up on him. He’d been doing that a lot. His recent social obligations gone sour, his career that was lack-luster at best, the distance he felt with the family he had, and the connection that he felt with the family that he had lost. He had a student and friend that was quickly surpassing him in both ability, but also wisdom and insight. Che Reyes was his last remaining link to his past, and a few awkward conversations with him have made even that bridge a weary one to approach. He didn’t blame Che, and he didn’t allow himself to take the blame, he just accepted it as another sign that things were not as easily packaged and dealt with as he thought. Growing pains, he imagined, but still painful. So what does one do when they feel like they are missing the most important parts of their lives? For Damien, he relied on instinct. It was something he always had. In the ring, in his life, and in the greater world around him. Tuck in your chin, keep your fists at eye level, and keep your feet moving. But he was getting tired. Very tired. There is but so much a man can neglect before necrosis starts to spread. Like a moss, it will crawl. It will cover the healthy as well as the weak. It will creep into the crevices, and roll out from the places that are supposed to be sacred. It was a slow process accelerated by dark, moist, ignored spaces in our thoughts. And then, it’s everywhere. Damien Cruz had spent more than a year pretending that everything was fine. But it wasn’t. Not yet. When his name came up, and he was selected to be in the WATCH match, there was a pull. A tug. A flash of movement in an atrophied area of his mind. The shadows drew back for a moment. When he won the qualifier, it drew back a bit more, and remained at bay long enough for him to remember what he was allowing to be forgotten. Damien walked away from 103 with a new-found determination. It wasn’t a new sensation, just one that felt new again. And for as long as the feeling remained, he would allow it to direct his next steps. He would follow the sunlight in a strange act of heliotropism. He would be the man that he sold himself to be. For as long as it worked for him, he would fight to preserve that feeling. And if that lead him towards the WATCH match, and if it resulted in the title he was being offered, and if it lifted his name and renewed his reputation, and if he grew stronger for it, and if things fell into place like clockwork, and if he was given the opportunities that he had missed in the past, and if everything became what he wanted, then that would be okay, too…because all he wanted was to feel like things made sense again. And so it is…
