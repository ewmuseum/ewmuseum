Coming Up Roses

17 January 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-17-10 Things were coming up roses for Cyrus Sutherland, though he would never put it in such poetic terms. His entrance into PRIME could not have come at a more opportune time. It was the start of the New Year and he was the new face on the roster. Not only that, but Culture Shock was fast approaching and now, ReVolution had moved to a more curse-word friendly station: HBO. This at least ensured that most of Sutherland’s tirades would not sound like Morse code. However, this wasn’t all that was going well for Cy Sutherland. Devin Shakur, a man Sutherland knew very little about, had definitely made his presence known to the newcomer insofar as becoming one of the Board of Directors. Did this effect Sutherland? Not necessarily. But it did signal a changing of the guard as a whole, for better or for worse, and this lent itself to Sutherland becoming better acclimated with the federation. Yet, perhaps the best turn of events during Sutherland’s tenure in PRIME thus far came from none other than Tyler Rayne himself. There was a concern, in Sutherland’s mind, that he would somehow become lost in the shuffle. This concern would have transcended Sutherland to the rest of the roster when he had planned to make every violent attempt to be noticed. This wasn’t needed. Tyler Rayne, for better or for worse, had decided to bump shoulders with the suspendered Sutherland, and that granted him a great deal of direction. And a great target. With ReVolution 215 out of the way, 216 marked the wrestling debut of Sutherland, who had been itching to get in the ring and crank on the pain. Now Sutherland had a perfect recipe: He had a clean slate, he had a match, and he had direction. All thanks to Devin Shakur, Tyler Rayne, and Father Time. There was one small problem, however. The Culture Shock Tour was a pain in the ass. Far be it for Sutherland to complain, but starting off in Buenos Aires sure was a tough pill to swallow. This then followed up with a trip to Saitama, Japan, to wrestle at the Saitama Super Stadium. “Great, first I deal with Argentinians, and now Japs…” Poetic as ever, Cyrus Sutherland trudged with a duffel bag over his shoulder along a horizontal conveyor belt within Haneda Airport, one of the closest airports to Saitama. Sutherland had a lot to do. He had to train. He had done some preliminary study on his first victim, Troy Douglas, and now he had to get to his hotel. He dwarfed most of the Japanese men and women who populated the airport and as he moved in the direction of the conveyor belt, he was rapidly gaining speed. With his red stack of hair springing up toward the ceiling, one could think he was a comet flying through the terminal. Japanese dove left and right as he stalked along the conveyor belt, only to step off onto an immobile piece of tile, and then step back onto another conveyor belt and move at another quickened pace. It would be a lie to think Cyrus Sutherland didn’t hold a grudge. It’d be a lie to say he wasn’t petty either. He knew that Tyler Rayne probably did him the biggest favor possible by bumping into him. No, not because Tyler Rayne was a popular wrestler in the federation and it would give him exposure. Rather, it was a favor because now Sutherland knew who he had to stomp straight into the ground. As crazy as Sutherland could be, however, he was also very mindful of the situation. At the current moment, Tyler Rayne was a sideshow. This was befitting in Cy’s mind, because he had acted like a clown from the get go. Troy Douglas was the primary target now. Most of this momentum now rested upon Troy Douglas’ shoulders. If Sutherland was able to demolish Douglas his momentum would continue. If Sutherland failed? His momentum would halt and he’d have to start from scratch. Cy Sutherland wasn’t in the habit of repeating himself. “Keep your eye on the ball, Cy…” He muttered to himself as these thoughts ran through his head. Just what was the ball that Sutherland was chasing? He didn’t really interest himself in a title. He didn’t even interest himself in any fame. He just interested himself in wrestling. To feel that adrenaline was like a drug to him. To make someone bleed and scream in pain? It was an aphrodisiac. It was a crying shame that Sutherland couldn’t experience that all the time. But Troy Douglas? He was his own little drug dealer now. He was going to give Sutherland the high of his life. He was going to get hurt. Badly. Sutherland couldn’t fucking wait. Everyone always wants to know what you’re up to when you’re the new guy on the block. The world of professional wrestling wasn’t any different. One wouldn’t go so far as to say that Sutherland made a splash in PRIME. He was unknown and there were flashier, more established stars on the roster who easily overshadowed him. Nevertheless, the insatiable appetite of the press to get a hold of the newest acquisition was still palpable. Judging by the throng of chattering Japanese at the end of this current conveyor… another interview was on the way. Now, Cyrus found himself trapped on the conveyor belt. He wasn’t in the mood to pander to the public. He hated the public. They just got in the way. Screaming kids and their t-shirts, signs, parents, rednecks, he could do without them all. But now, he couldn’t turn around. A cursory look around the terminal found himself trapped and set to collide with the gaggle of Japanese press at the end of the conveyor. “Mister Sutherland! What did you think about ReVolution 215?” “The Spamburgers tasted like shit…” he growled in response. The reporters chattered in their native tongue and English speaking translators screamed over them. It was a cacophony. “Cyrus! What about your altercation with Tyler Rayne?” Sutherland snorted as he tried to shoulder his way through the group of five foot interlopers, “What about him? You stand on the tracks when the train comes through you get run over. He’ll get his…” “What about your match with Troy Douglas?” It was too much. For all Sutherland knew they knew karate and were ready to take him down. They had him surrounded like little midgets, screaming and gnawing at his boot heels for a sound byte. “Listen. All I can say about Troy Douglas is it’s unfortunate he has to get in the ring with me. Because I’m going to wipe the floor with him.” Sutherland’s forward momentum had stopped and, after a moment, he let his duffel bag fall to the floor. Rather, he let his duffel bag fall to a Japanese onlooker, who then fell to the floor with it. The bag itself hit the tile floor a moment later with a metal clang. One could only imagine what Sutherland had in it. “Look kids, I gotta get to my hotel, if you could all just spread out that’d be dandy…” Sutherland said with a sneer as he gazed at the grazers. “I really need some room to breathe here…” “Don’t you have something to say to the Japanese people? What do you think of their beautiful airport?” “Listen Toyota, I don’t speak Japanese. They wouldn’t understand a damned word I said!” “We have interpreters!” It was a low, soft growl that rolled through Sutherland’s chest. It was all but imperceptible amidst the noise of the press. It certainly was something an interpreter wouldn’t understand. This was unfortunate. He was getting irritated. “Listen, folks, you want me to talk? I’ll talk. I’ve never backed down from speaking into a microphone. It’s great to be in PRIME. It was great to be backstage in that shithole in Argentina. I’m happy to finally have a match against none other than Troy Douglas. The man’s been around the PRIME locker room for a while, and I can’t wait to eviscerate him in front of an international audience. You know, I said I don’t speak Japanese, but I’m sure the Japs know the international language of bones crunching and bitches screaming. I hope so, because they’re gonna hear it when I stomp a hole in Douglas’ face.” The Japanese man, buried beneath Sutherland’s duffel bag, began to stir as the cameras moved in closer to Sutherland. He gripped his red suspenders in his hands and rocked back on his heels. If he wasn’t such a whack job, he’d almost look refined. “Here’s the news people, and I hope the rest of the roster can hear this as well: I’m staying. PRIME is now my home. And I am going to make sure every person backstage knows it. It’s gonna start with Troy Douglas. Not only am I bigger than him, but I’m stronger, and I’m in better shape. He’s a broken down, battered up, washed out has been.” “Those are some bold words, Mister Sutherland, but when it comes to in ring technicality, would you say Troy has you beat?” “Of course I’d say he has me beat. I’ve never been one to claim I’m a successful mat wrestler. Douglas has what little bit of respect I can offer him. We’re the same height and I only outweigh him by fifteen measly pounds. It’s gonna make for a good match, but I promise to make it better, because I’m gonna make him scream like a girl on international television!” He grinned to himself and straightened his back a bit more, “But as you’ll all find out, I’m as tough as they come. Someone like Troy Douglas might think they can walk in, on my debut, and take the wind out of my sails, but he’s in for a rude awakening.” He winked, “You can bet on it… now… if you’d let me through…” “Mister Sutherland, if you could…” “Listen Suzuki.. I said let me through…” “Just one more…” With a roar, Sutherland’s voice cracked and his body erupted with sound, “LET ME THE FUCK THROUGH!!!” Like Moses parting the Red Sea, the group of reporters parted before him. With one fluid motion, Sutherland scooped up his duffel bag from the squirming Japanese man beneath, and headed off again. He could hear the chatter of the press behind him. Hopefully that was a good enough sound byte. He hoped “Let me the fuck through” would translate well into Japanese. Sutherland was completely right, at least in his mind. Troy Douglas was a common name amidst fans of PRIME. He was something safe. He was something common. This brought a twisted grin to Cyrus’ face, for he was not something common and he was not something safe. On the contrary… he was unpredictable, uncommon, and unhinged. First, Sutherland would make an example out of Troy Douglas. Then, he’d go back after that clown, Tyler Rayne, and make him pay for getting between himself and that Spamburger…
