Date: UltraViolence RING! “Ugh.” Craig rolls out around in the bed, hoping to silence the continuing noise with no use. As he rolls over once again, he finds his cell phone placed oddly on the corner of his bed. He picks it up and looks at the caller ID display which clearly reads Warren Green. He pauses for a moment, wondering what his doctor would be calling him for, before flipping it open and answering the phone. “Hello.” “Morning, Craig. Sorry to wake you up so early.” “That’s okay. I just wanted to inform you that I have the resu— ~~~ His hand brushes the metal of the doorknob and he stops dead in his tracks. Craig just stands there, his thoughts racing through his mind as he tries to figure out what the doctor's going to say before he walks in the door. The past few months had just brought bad news after bad news, and he didn't know if he could take it any longer. He didn't know if he could have the weight of something depressing on his shoulders days before his upcoming UltraViolence match. He takes a deep breath in, the chatter behind him becoming rather obnoxious, and finally opens the door before walking in and seeing Warren Green just sitting there looking rather somber. "Hey, doc," Craig says listlessly before moving to his usual chair and sitting in it. The doctor welcomes him with a simple wave of the hand before Craig notices that he's on the phone. He just sits back and looks around the room, admiring the different degrees the doctor had accumulated over the years. He nods his head, almost acknowledging each one of them, before he hears the clicking of the phone. As he looks back at Doctor Green, he can see the annoyed expression on his face. "Afternoon," the doctor responds, something obviously troubling him. Craig looks at him peculiar, but brushes it off as a personal issue and decides not to pursue it any further. "So, doc, what's the good news?" Craig asks him, but by the tone in his voice, he knew that the doctor was going to be the bearer of bad news instead. His left hand grips the armrest as he glares at the doctor, patiently waiting. "How's your shoulder feeling?" the doctor asks as his eyes glance over to the injured left shoulder. Craig rolls it a few times, feeling the pain travel up his neck at a lightening pace. "Oh, you know, just peachy. Any chance we're going to be able to operate on it or do I just have to go through the rest of my life with a gimpy arm?" The doctor just pauses as he opens up a folder on his desk and reads a few notes to himself. "I'm not one hundred percent sure yet on that. The tests that I ran to see how you'd react to such a surgery didn't come back as I would've liked. They were actually worse then I feared." The words don't sit with Craig as he sits up in his chair, nervous and scared. He licks his lips quickly, feeling his nerves causing every inch of his body to tense up. Slowly, Craig moves to the edge of the chair, wondering what could've been so bad about those tests. Each and every worse fear sets into his mind, and he prays that he's strong enough to endure them. Deep down, he knows that he's not. "So, hit me with the news doc. You've done it before, just rip off the band aid." His words sound confident, but his lips and throat tremble as he says them. The doctor's right hand wipes across his forehead, the anxiety beginning to get to him. He looks down at the folder before him and then looks back up at Craig. "The degeneration of your brain is obviously happening at a faster rate than anticipated. Even with your increased work schedule and the intensity of your most recent matches. I honestly can't tell you how long you've had MS, Craig, but I can tell you that it's going to kill you. I'm certain of it." Craig sits there for a moment, the weight and seriousness of those words sinking into his mind as he tries to figure out what exactly the doctor is trying to tell him. For months, he understood that eventually his death would come because of the life-changing disease that he was now living with. How was this news? What was the doctor not telling him? "Frankly, doc, I've known this for a while now. Can you cut to the chase?" Warren clears his throat before sitting up straight in his chair. "Alright, fine. The acceleration of your disease has left you with at best a year left to live." With hearing those words, Warren stops in his tracks as he sees the shock on Craig's face. His right hand has now gripped the armrest of the chair that he's sitting in and he feels like he could break it if he tried hard enough. He wishes that he could say something or do something. Some would say that they'd do anything in this case, but for Craig, it's a reality that he's ready to face at this point. "Nothing we can do?" Craig attempts to feign that he cares about his well-being, but truth be told, if he only had a year left then at least he knew what was remaining and what he had left to do. That thought, while morbid and grotesque, was rather comforting in an odd way to Craig. The doctor simply shakes his head and Craig's head drops, accepting his fate. Any other person would be devastated, but for years, Craig had stared death in the eyes and had won those battles. Eventually though, death would win the war. "Alright, then." "That's all you have to say, Craig?" For a moment, he thinks, trying to find something deep down that would resolve the man's emotions. He doesn't know if its years of just feeling dead inside or the fact that he has no heart left inside of him, but nothing bubbles to the surface. It's almost disappointing for he yearns to feel something there even as he accepts his fate. Feeling that anxiety, sadness, and anger all rolled into one would give him some kind of hope for a normal life. "I wish I had something more to say, but in all honesty, there's not anything I can really say at this point." Doctor Green just nods his head. "It's shock, honestly. In a few hours, the true weight of the news is going to hit you and you're going to feel different. I can promise you that." Craig just smiles at the notion. "I wouldn't be so certain, doc. Thanks for all of your help though." In one swift movement, Craig rises out of his chair and makes his way over to the door to the doctor's office. He opens it and takes a look at the world around him. As he walks out of it though and he hears the click of the door closing, he feels the trembling sensation of his hands. Fear. He might welcome fear with arms wide open, but the fear of embracing it scared him even more. Not because he would miss his life. More because of what he would leave behind. ~~~ "What're you hiding?" The words smack him in the face the moment that he walks in the door. Craig freezes as he closes the door, barely even remembering the drive home. His thoughts raced wildly around his mind and he had yet to settle down or really feel the immensity of the weight of death that had firmly planted itself on his heart. As he turns around, he sees Katie and Nicole both sitting in the kitche, awaiting his arrival. He takes a deep breathe in, hoping that tonight's not the night that he has to reveal everything to them. "What're you talking about?" He begins to walk towards them as Katie gets out of her chair and meets him halfway. "Haven't you done enough lying already? Craig pauses with his mouth wide open. "Katie, what the fu-- ~~~ A man sits there in stony silence with a glass of scotch in his right hand. He seems to be waiting for something as he just remains in the chair, appearing rather comfortable, before taking a sip of the alcohol. Across from him, a door opens and closes within a matter of seconds. With the dimmed atmosphere, the face of the man is not immediately known. The man in the chair continues to drink the scotch from the glass before acknowledging his arrival. "Well?" At first, nothing. Complete silence. The eyes of the man darken as he waits for a response as his patient wears thin. "Malik, he knows as you asked." Malik nods his head before raising the glass to his lips again and taking another sip from it. With it now empty, he puts the glass onto a wooden table that's next to him and then cross his legs as he rolls his tongue around in his mouth. "And? What was his reaction?" "He seemed unfazed. Almost as if he was expecting it." At first, Malik's mouth opens, but he stops himself before any words come out. He sits back in his chair, pondering those words in his mind for a second before coming to ease with them. "Very well. A calculated response from him. Knowing he only has a year left to live will push him in the right direction. Make him have to react much quicker and then will come the mistakes that he's sure to make. You did well." For the first time, the man steps into the light and looks at Malik. "And what about my family?" A smile appears Malik's face. "Your family will be returned to you in due order, Warren. Patience will be key, Doctor, patience will be key." A single tear rolls out of Warren's eyes, the pain he feels from being separated from his family. A smile creeps onto Malik's face as he sits back in his chair and can feel the invisible strings that he controls. Like a puppet master. To. Be. Continued.
