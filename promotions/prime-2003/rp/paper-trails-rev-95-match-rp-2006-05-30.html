Paper Trails (ReV 95 Match RP)

30 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 30/05/06 NB: Apologies to anyone who knows of The Creeps as a large portion of this RP (and hopefully many subsequent) are hugely inspired/ripped-off from their songs and ideas. “We all ready?” An almost familiar voice starts us off, the vocal strains more nasal and less gruff than usual. Everyone’s favourite Scotsman in PRIME (by default) looks round for the approval he needs to begin. There’re a few calls of ‘Yep’ and ‘Ready when you are’ followed by a cry of ‘Two minutes yet’, Adam nods in response, satisfied that nearly everything is good to go. He tosses his stub of a cigarette to the ground, grinds it out then runs through his final preparations before the tester video filming begins. Adam: Hey! You two, c’mere a minute. Two tech guys, conveniently wandering past the door to the studio that moment, pop their heads in the door, eyebrows questioningly raised. Adam: Where’d you say I was from? Tech #1: Ehh... North-East somewhere? Like, New England? Tech #2: I was thinking more, I dunno... He scratches his head, confounded. Adam: No problem guys, just curious, cheers for the help. The two techs shrug at each other as the contended PRIME star heads back to his set. The mid-Atlantic drift he’d, well... drifted into over the past decade or so in the States had scarcely covered his native accent. But the extra work he’d put in since receiving the mysterious envelope he’d been slid by Tyler Nelson at the previous ReVolution had paid off – two Joe Public specimens couldn’t detect a single trace of his roots. A voice calls to Adam, confirming that absolutely everything was now ready for him. Adam: Great; gimme one second though. He runs a hand through the bristly remains of his previously shoulder-length silver hair – the buzzcut proved most efficient in his morning shower, but definitely lacked the heat-retention that the occasional cold breeze warranted the need for. After a quick scan of the prop table in front on him Adam spots what he’s looking for; a bandana and boot polish. Two quick smears of the sticky black stuff under his eyes, a quick knot behind his head and he was all set. Adam: I’m ready now, let’s get this shit going! He jogs across to the stage that’s set up, a solitary camera aimed at it. Crouching down in front of it he closes his eyes, takes a few deep breaths and gives one last shout. Adam: Let’s roll! A shaky shot settles on our protagonist, Agent 138, deep behind enemy lines, his mind full to the brim with coded secrets. He uses an unidentifiable accent, covered even more so by the whisper he speaks in currently; Agent 138: This film is for my personal viewing only, to be verified by retinal and fingerprint scan before playback. He settles back a little, having lodged the tiny, top-secret design, micro-camera between two branches of the thick, foreign foliage surrounding him. Agent 138: This is Agent 138, Codename: Farflight, making a memory-dump for future reference. I’m camped outside a secret location, ready for infiltration on behalf on the CIA. Inside there are suspected to be sensitive files that could lead a paper trail directly to my discovery and subsequent termination at the hands of other agencies. I have no fear of death, carrying a cyanide capsule at all times, but my discovery could make life difficult for my superiors and I must hold preservation of the Agency’s dignity above all else. He coughs quietly, muffling it as best he can. Silently, he unsheathes his Bowie knife and looks around him, with the eyes of a hawk and ears of a bat, for any signs that his inconspicuousness has been impinged upon. After a good minute’s wait he relaxes (as much as a highly-trained agent can; which is on par with ‘Extremely alert and on-edge’ for most mere mortals) and sheathes his knife again. Agent 138: I shall commence the memory dump as of now; once at the height of physical training, my life felt incomplete in my current job of full-time professional athlete... of sorts. This was picked up on by what most people refer to as ‘The Powers That Be’ and thus a lucrative deal to leave the company known as PRIME ensued. In order to mask the exit of such a top draw my unhappiness in the company was highlighted and eventually I was phased out, with little to no disturbance to regularly-scheduled programming. Even when seemingly talking directly to the camera, our agent’s eyes are darting all around, searching for any sign of enemy movement or anything out of the ordinary. With hearing that could detect a pin drop at fifty paces his ears are constantly scanning the audible frequency range (and slightly beyond, having been subject to specialist CIA training) for any signs out outside life that could encroach on this Top Secret taping. Agent 138: It was them that my training began in earnest; I essentially gave full consent to become a human lab-rat for the CIA. I was sat in a room, drugged up and filled with information; codes and secrets pumped directly into my brain. My reflexes, hearing, vision and other senses were honed to super-human levels to become what has come to be known, on the inside, as a ‘Shock Trooper’. I’m the prototype and currently the only one of these Super Agents, with the distinct possibility of remaining that way due my current prowess. It’s unlikely any more will be needed once I have been fully tested and released into the field. There’s not a hint of arrogance or smugness at our agent’s superiority over not only his fellow man, but over his agent contemporaries, who simply pale in comparison. There is only the utmost sense chivalry and honour at having been privileged enough to have been chosen for this experiment. Agent 138: I had a memory chip implanted in my brain following the mental and physical preparation I had been given, this allowed for the switching between the false ‘history’ that was grafted into my cerebral tissue and the truth than I have just laid down here. The false history was required to fool my fellow wrestlers and boss that I was the same man I was when I left, but only slightly more determined and slightly fitter – indeed, at the time, I didn’t know that this was far from the truth of the full potential my abilities hold. While appearing so relaxed it’s easy to miss the fact that this super-agent has barely blinked once in the whole time he’s been filmed – truly a testament to the intense alertness and awareness he possesses, once harnessed inside a wrestling ring this man (though the term ‘man’ falls far short in his case) could justly be called unstoppable. Agent 138: Once my alternate life had been accepted by my peers, my human shortcomings exposed and my position in my job firmly held again the switch from ‘stay-at-home gay father’ to ‘CIA superhuman agent extraordinaire’ in the memory chip could be remotely accessed and flipped. Thus, from the last ReVolution to the upcoming one a new era shall have been implemented – an agent in the field where the CIA need one most; the ever-changing battlefield of professional wrestling. Unknown to the general public, the Cold War along with the first Gulf War were both settled this way. Indeed this is correct; in the face of the highly political, and increasingly expensive, world of high-tech warfare an agreement was made between the world’s top nations decades ago, hidden from their peoples, that war would not be fought with expensive weaponry but rather decided in the square rings of this illustrious sport. The battles that the Cold War appeared to lack were made up for by the East/West in-ring war that raged, hidden in plain view of the public. This was revised in the first Gulf War to include faked TV broadcast of war visuals, the part that was most obviously missing from the Cold War. Again this system was extended and revised under the latest Gulf War to be even less obvious to be happening in the ring and more obvious on computer-simulated news broadcast segments. Agent 138: Of course, my presence will be completely unbeknown to anyone within the world warfare stage that is PRIME as the memory chip is now under my control and I can easily recite convincing back-stories from what happened during my ‘leave’ of PRIME to ensure my cover remains intact. In the same way, my incredible strength, reflexes and so on will be downplayed to acceptable levels when needed, so as not to blatantly give away the enhancement I have been subject to. Decibels below what you or I could have ever hoped to have heard and a sound alerts Agent 138 to approaching danger, with speed beyond that of a cheetah he has his knife out its holster and into the body of the threat. Blink and you would have missed it, literally. Thankfully it was only the flapping of an awakening butterfly’s wings, the knife piercing it dead-centre through its body to a tree-trunk. Carefully our agent removes the knife from its off-screen receptacle and replaces it in his belt. Agent 138: Now my return integration to PRIME is complete the reconnaissance will begin – an easy start to ease me into the ways of this new-found strength, agility and mental prowess. This week a gambling bum will be under surveillance; his anti-American sentiment in his endorsement of and/or affiliation with an organised crime syndicate is detrimental to the health of this great nation that I have the pleasure of devoting my life to. In one key respect Agent 138 differed from all his contemporaries in the Agency – he is not a natural-born American citizen, but days straight of A Clockwork Orange style viewing of important American events on film – most of which are restricted to within select, high-ranking circles of the ‘inside’ only – have not only allowed him to catch up with but also to surpass those around him in his in-depth knowledge of the country he serves. Roswell autopsy footage and film of the shooter on the grassy knoll only scratch at the surface of what secrets this mind holds. Agent 138: As soon as the required information is found, destroyed and the perpetrating parties appropriately dealt with this ‘Gambit’ figure shall be further investigated. Transmission over. With a hand reached forward to the camera’s position Agent 138 kills the signal, plunging the screen to static. Unable to help himself any longer, Adam’s straight face breaks up and into a fit of laughter. Eventually he calms down enough to stand up a bit from the crouched position he has held himself in and wipe the tears away from the corners of his eyes. Adam: Absolutely great, guys – I think that went really well. He catches the bottle of water tossed to him and thanks the thrower; he’s just finishing a long drink when he sees a bystander has sneaked into the filming. Adam: Iggy! Ignatius stands with his arms folded, a decidedly solemn expression on his face. Adam’s about to ask what was up when Ignatius starts; Ignatius: “An agent in the field where the CIA need one most; the ever-changing battlefield of professional wrestling.” A broad grin cracks on the French-Canadian’s faux-serious face. Ignatius: Man, I thought I was gonna start pissing myself when you said that, I actually had to leave the room and double over – how the hell did you keep a straight face during that? Adam: I dunno, I’ve been going over it in my head all week – the humour’s kinda gone for me now. Ignatius: Yeah, and what’s with the accent and hair, or lack thereof of both? Adam shrugs a little, smiling to himself at the quick achievement. Adam: I figured a Top Secret CIA agent couldn’t really sound like he was trying to sell the Evening Times on Buchanan Street and be very convincing. I also figured that long silver hair might be a bit of a give-away when he was spying on the Reds. Ignatius slaps his buddy on the shoulder, shaking his head in disbelief. Ignatius: Well, you seem to be taking to it pretty well so far, I take it this is what Nelson wanted to see you about, then? Adam: Yep; I figure if Ferguson can convincingly act and portray someone else then why can’t I? Not like there isn’t scope for a bit of fun with a brainwashed CIA agent caught in a time-warp of paranoia, is there? Ignatius: As long as you don’t get too sucked into it. He winks and gives his buddy a friendly shot in the arm. Ignatius: I think you deserve a beer for that performance, wipe that shit off your face and we’ll hit a bar. Adam: Good cries, I’ll see you in a bit – think I’ll need a shower to get it all off yet. With that he heads for the dressing room he used earlier, taking off the bandana and camo vest on his way, decidedly pleased with his first run at acting.
