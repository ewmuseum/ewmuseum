Guardian Angel (Match RP - v Aimz, Rampage, Santos)

19 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19/09/05 Guardian AngelWritten: 19th September, 2005. 'You say that love goes anywhere.In your darkest time, It's just enough to know it's there.' - Jimmy Eat World. I - Give The Man Some Credit... There's a certain perception of wrestling superstars, one that is encouraged shamelessly by a small handful. As professional sportsmen and woman, appearing on national television week in and week out, enticing hundreds of thousands to shell out for pay-per-views, and adored by thousands of fans packed into over-priced arenas, they talk up the lifestyle they live away from ringside. For some it's talk of mansions, millions filling bank vaults, and enough jewellery to make Diddy mutter about just being a show-off. For others, it's instant recognition everywhere they set foot on the planet, usually due in part to starring roles in movies, or near-weekly appearances on the top weekly chat shows. And that's not even touching those who for some reason have a full camera and sound crew follow them around 24 hours a day, in case they suddenly think of a good insult while walking through a graveyard at midnight. As is always the case, perception and reality don't quite overlap. Especially since tales of making rent and hoping your pay was transferred into your account on time don’t really measure up to, 'these strippers recognised me from XHWF so gave us a free, Champagne-fuelled orgy.' Ah, well. On with the swearing. 'Fuck,' the weary K-Wolf grumbled as the dry skin of her fingertips flicked through the notes. 'Lost count... again.' Bloodshot eyes did their best to relay the numbers on the bills to her brain, which was already running at half capacity due to sleep deprivation. The whites looked cracked, where bloodshot red mimicked wheel spokes, while the usually vivid emerald of her irises was faded to a dull, lifeless shade. Tongue ran along dry, peeling lips as Karina continued mourning the loss of her chapstick from the luggage she'd packed. 'One hundred, two hundred...' she began once more, before the chiming of the elevator doors caused a frustrated sigh to be expelled, followed by Kari flicking back to the start. 'I really should learn internet banking.' Having decided she probably had somewhere close enough to her rent, the K-Wolf just rolled the haphazard bunch of bills up, and hopped out of the elevator, which for some reason, always stopped a foot short of the actual floor. Apparently her landlord's landlord was as slow at getting around to maintenance as he was. An overbearing smell of bleach sanitised Kari's nostrils as she headed down the hallway, worn sneaker soles almost losing traction on the still damp floor. Knuckles rattled off a tuneful knock against the dark green door as Kari slouched onto her back foot, money-free hand planting itself in her baggy black trousers' expansive pocket. A series of locks and blots then clunked, before a hefty yank dislodged the door from its tight-fitting frame. 'Yeah, yeah, I know it's late...' fired off her monthly excuse before the door was even open, red always being the trigger colour for Kari to eventually write cheques or draw cash. 'But I had a shit month, needed a holiday, so took two. But, yeah, it's all here, I think... might be a little short, or there might be more, so, shall we just say it's there?' For someone who must be raking in at least twice what Kari's PRIME contract paid, her landlord managed to dress down even more than her. An ill-fitting black sweater, open-weave enough before the plucks distorted it, was paired with jeans that took the authentic route to looking faded... most likely being worn for over 5 years. Thick rimmed, black glasses lurked under equally black, and even thicker hair, managing to mix the messed-up style with a highly authentic 'just got out of bed' look. Rubbing his eyes, as if the light exuding from the hallway was the first he’d seen all day, he took a few seconds to recognise his tenant... which always seemed to be a problem with someone as erratic with her look as Wolfenden. 'Um...' he paused, hand extending towards the money out of instinct, but fingers failing to grasp at the notes. Kari held up a hand to pause him as a yawn ravaged her slouched body, jaw almost separating to let the dramatic call for sleep out. 'Please don't say you want me to count it,' the K-Wolf then moaned, eyelids teasing closure as even the doorframe felt like a comfy place to nap again. 'I tried about 10 times, but I haven't slept since... what day is it again?' As her landlord rustled his thick bush of hair, he tried a few times to start his next sentence. 'Miss Wolfenden,' he addressed her politely with, far from his usually, angered demand for settlement. 'You're paid up for the rest of the year.' 'I'm...' went the kind of reply someone gives when they're not entirely sure they just heard somebody right, head cocked, bemused. 'Huh?' Even in her near-zombie state, the K-Wolf did her best to dredge through blurred memories, battling to remember supposedly important things from a few days ago, let alone a month and a half. 'Wait,' Kari slowed her stumbling thoughts with. 'Last month I had to pay you in a mixture of cheque, cash, alcohol and a lottery ticket... which, unless you got lucky with my numbers, doesn't cover 5 months rent.' Pausing, eyes narrowing as he cupped his right ear, her landlord did his best to top even his tenant’s wild imagination. 'Is that my phone ringing?' he turned away enquiring, as not even a hit of a ring troubled Kari's ear. 'You don't hear that? Anyway, I need to take this, so, see you in January... don't be late!' 'Bu-' Kari went to appeal, never thinking she'd live the day where she hassled him about settling up for the month. The door's slamming sliced off her word, before the locks, in turn, stifled her weary stutter as the K-Wolf blinked, wondering if she was actually asleep, or simply longing to be. 'If the same thing happens for the phone, gas, electric, cable, and internet, today is the greatest day since... ever,' words spilled out as sleep deprivation forced Kari into conversation with herself to stay awake long enough to get back to her apartment. 'Now, to go and sleep through it.' II - 15% Of Zero. 'Cheque, please,' Max Wolfenden requested with a click of his thumb to the passing, hurried waiter, causing a cringe from his little sister as she began to wonder if he'd pull on braces, or bring out a cell phone the size of a brick. 'Who actually does that anymore?' the wide-eyed, embarrassed K-Wolf demanded to know, since she was the one who actually liked eating there. 'And this is my shout.' Knowing his sister all-too-well, Max didn't even pause in pulling his wallet out, the smell of new leather overpowering even that of the garlic and basil still wafting from their plates. 'Is this the 'your shout' that involves me paying, and you scribbling an I.O.U on your napkin?' 'Haha,' she playfully took her brother's bait with, before retrieving a roll of notes from the pocket in her sleeve. 'Wait...' Max began his next mocking with, never able to resist picking on his little sister, even as they passed through mid-twenties. 'We're not going to see that woman over there looking for her purse in a few minutes are we?' After disguising a raised middle finger behind the half-empty wine bottle between them, Kari quickly recoiled it as the waiter delivered the cheque. 'There you go, Miss.' 'Thanks, Luca,' Kari offered with a smile, noticing the scowl with which the overworked young man looked to her brother with. 'You're doubling the tip up to 30% for the Gecko cra-... er, Luke, it's blank.' The waiter turned on dancer's tiptoes, then leant down to check the amount. 'Yeah, that's what's down for this table.' As Max reached a foot under the table, ready to poke at Kari's ankle and prompt her just to take the free meal, she somehow sensed it, kicking his foot away. 'Ow...' 'Yeah,' Kari agreed that much with him, 'but unless you started a 'random table eats free' offer, I think you might be slightly undercharging.' 'Hold one a sec,' Luca requested, grabbing the cheque and heading off towards the manager, still able to give the wincing Max and evil look. 'Sooooo...' Max chuckled from across the red and white checked tablecloth. 'First you tell me about your rent suddenly paying itself in advance, and now someone's shouting all your meals. Do I call you Miss Durdon, or just Tyler?' 'Huh?' Kari rebounded, despite knowing what he was getting at. 'My little sis either has a secret admirer, a mystery benefactor, or a hundred space-monkeys ready to take over the world for her,' he grinned, pouring the largest of glasses from what was left of the wine. Karina paused for a brief second, thoughts suddenly catching up to her as she entered her third hour of being awake. Something that had been said to her the night she went looking for Angelo, lost in a week of tears, alcohol, anger, and long-haul flights. 'You're Karina Wolfenden,' he'd told her. 'No offence, lady, but wearin' black and pullin' on a hat ain't a fool-proof disguise.' How did he know her? And why didn't a man twice her size at least take a swing when she pinned him to the wall by his balls. Unless... unless... 'Stay here a minute,' she coldly ordered, storming up and heading away from the table with haste. 'Hey, sis,' Max called after her, downing the glass he held up to her as a toast. 'You don't need to do a runner, it's free!' As Kari watched Luca and the manager retreat towards the kitchen, she slithered through the doors as they swung shut. 'Okay, what's going on?' Before either could protest about the K-Wolf being in the kitchen, the duo caught sight of the unrelenting look in her eyes, before turning to one another, as if to nudge the other into answering. 'And Luca,' Kari went to advise with the kind of potent smile that could be both reassuring and intimidating, 'there are lots of hot, sharp, and hot and sharp things in here.' 'Er, um, er,' the manager stuttered in his throaty way, as if he was ready to cough up a ball of phlegm. 'Service was lousy, we can't charge customers for that! We have a reputation to uphold. I should sack your waiter, who was it?' 'Him,' the tiring Kari bluntly pointed out, nodding to Luca. 'You're fir-' he went to exclaim with suitably Latino over-exaggerated gestures, before seeing that Kari's stony expression wasn't softening. 'Oh, oh, I nearly forget, silly me,' he then began to pluck excuses from mid-air with. 'We were our thousandth customer! You eat free all year.' 'You've been open longer than I’ve been alive,' the dry words of Kari rebutted instantly, wishing she could just get the manager to say his name... the name of the Crimson Angel. The man she'd wanted in her life so badly, but had forced to watch over her from the shadows. 'Just say his name, Rick.' 'I don't know what you talk about, Miss Wolfenden!' he feigned confusion with, tossing his arms to his sides and nearly dropping spoons and spatulas from their hooks. 'Maybe we put a bit too much vino in the sauce, huh?' Dropping her head to the side, the mischievous look that Kari's face could settle so effortlessly into emerged. 'So, if we fancied dessert and a few more bottles, that would be covered by this... 1000th customer thing? And maybe a little order for tonight?' 'Yes! Yes!' he grinned, gesturing to all the food being prepared in the kitchen behind him, while the chef and staff looked less than happy with the interruption. 'All year! 1000th customer!' Without saying a word, Karina's thin, pale pink lips formed the slightest of smiles as she gave a hint of a nod, then disappeared back through the doors. As they swung back together, the manager finally let out his breath, then wiped the beads of sweat from his forehead, patting dry the moisture trapped in his bushy eyebrows. 'Ugh,' he grumbled, loosening his bow-tie. 'One day I will learn to say no to that man...' Patting his boss on the shoulder, Luca headed back out to the tables with a teasing grin. 'Good, I've been waiting to take your job for the last year...' III - A 30 Minute Guarantee. From across the street, Karina watched. Sat in the bus shelter, legs hugged to chest, and engulfed in a black hooded sweatshirt, the K-Wolf put the wicked chill of the sharp breeze to the back of her thoughts. It had been trying to rain for the last 10 minutes, droplets blowing in the air, scouts for the deluge that was sure to follow. With I-Pod turned up to max, tinny, frantic drumming crackled from her earphones, attracting a quick glance from those passing the huddled up young woman. The building she was watching couldn't help but depress. Once proud men and women, clad in long, tattered coats that never left their backs, wandered in off the street, hoping they were in time for a bed. Like moths with torn wings, they flocked to the warming amber glow of the windows, but eyes barely left their scuffed, aging shoes. Karina remembered a Christmas past, before she'd set off into the world, where she'd been volunteered for the shelter. Whining and complaining filled the days before, but by the end of the night, there was a warm, satisfying feeling within her that no secret swig of the Christmas brandy could match. Chin rested on the backs of gloved hands as Kari's eyes drifted to her watch, counting off each of the seconds as they ticked down to nine. It was then that the grumble of a giving-out engine was heard, a sound that suggested that it would be followed by a choke silence a second later. Gingerly cruising towards the shelter was a red van, exhaust fumes sending a thick plume of pollution out into the damp night. The van came to a halt with the gargle, followed by a gasping wheeze as it settled. The driver's side door needed 3 attempts to open it, the handle having it's own anti-theft mechanism... no matter how unintentional. From the van emerged Luca, scarf tightly wrapped around his neck to give away the fact that the van's heaters packed up years ago. Leaning back into the van, he rummaged around as Kari watched attentively, before emerging with armfuls of boxes, holding them close to him for a few seconds to bask in their heat. After a knock at the shelter's door, he stacked the boxes on the doorstep, before returning to the van for another load. Slithering off her seat, Karina held up a gloved hand to the bus as it stuttered to a halt. A wash of heat brought colour to Kari's cheeks as the doors opened, and she hopped up the steps and dropped the right change into the driver's tray. With only a few stragglers left, the K-Wolf took a seat and squinted to see through the light reflecting off the windows. As the bus pulled off, she could see Luca trying to explain away the charity, before being taken aback as the woman lunged forward to offer up a hug of thanks. With the sight drifting out past the windows edge, Kari’s arms subconsciously hugged herself a little tighter as she was warmed back through, a smile being brought to her lips that not even her most self-conscious efforts could straighten. Angelo Deville had tried to fill the void he'd left in her heart with money, and as tempted as she was to throw it all back at him, wherever he was, it was too much of a gift to dismiss. Her feelings weren't receptive to money, but as the bus crawled through the streets of her hometown, she couldn't remember feeling this good about herself. Not even at home, curled up under a blanket with the fire on, cats nestled in beside her, could such a warmth of well-being be felt. This made her smile. This soothed the hollow ache of his disappearance. So if Angelo Deville wanted the K-Wolf to keep feeling good about himself, he'd better have left plenty in his account.
