A Day in the Life of a Wrestler

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Today “Say that again?” Killean demanded, trying to get the female voice on the other end of the phone to repeat what they said. “I’m Victoria and I am calling from the PAR Production Company.” She began, sounding a little distraught in having to repeat herself. “We would like to cast you in a local picture in Winnipeg. Filming will begin Monday afternoon.” Killean Sirrajin was driving from the Winnipeg International Airport to his loft in the downtown area when he got the call. Instead of really listening to the woman on the phone, he was silently trying to figure out why exactly he was the target of a film production company. “What are you asking me for?” Killean wondered, trying to sound a little hostile at the unwanted interruption of his originally relaxed day. “I have never done any acting… and if you begin to imply wrestling is acting… I sw-“ “Mr. Sirrajin I assure you we do not look at wrestling in a bad light.” Her cut off was swift yet it did the trick. Killean was silent on the phone, rolling his eyes in plain view of the vehicle stopped at the intersection with him. A child was looking at him through the slightly tinted glass. Killean gave a small smile which was almost ignored by he young boy as he turned back to his father. The older lady on the phone decided to continue. “Wrestlers are often asked to take part in movies, but this is a lead role.” “What is the movie about?” Like Killean really cared. He had no intentions of accepting the role anyways, but he figured he would play with their minds. “We can discuss that on your arrival here.” She coughed, a smokers cough. “Wait, I never said…” He was interrupted again. “We are located at 400 Broadway. Five o’clock is your appointment. Please be prompt.” With that she hung up the phone. “That silly bitch.” Killean threw the cell phone onto the passenger seat which was not occupied. He had banked on having a relaxing day and then this unwanted nuisance came up. He would entertain himself though, thinking he would thoroughly enjoy laughing at the script of this film. Acting was not his forte, made plainly obvious by his lack of gimmicks in the wrestling world. He would not be able to play the role of a clown, a dead vampire or even worse, a god worshiping crazy man, even though he had the experience of wrestling Hoyt Williams. -------------------------------------/ Sunday, October 9, 2005 @ 3:34 PM Central Time As Sirrajin picked out some dressier clothes than he is used to wearing, he couldn’t help but reflect back on ReVolution. Not so much the victory over Rampage, a hot young talent in PRIME, but on Matt Ward. Tchu had been in the third round for a week already and was given the week off after three solid performances in the ring. He definitely hadn’t lost a step in his two year absence from the squared circle. He was still on top of his game, just like the final days of the Underground. Back then, Tchu vs. Killean was a top match in the minor leagues. Not many people watched the sometimes sub-par wrestling the San Diego federation contained. Now, here in PRIME, the upper echelon in sports entertainment, even the prospect of Killean and Tchu squaring off in the ring ruffled the feathers of millions of people around the world. Killean held up a dress shirt. It was black with a lower collar. He put the shirt on, rolled up the cuffs and finished the package with a gold chain around his strong, thick neck. It clearly wasn’t traditional Killean style, but the get up didn’t look too bad. After taking a moment to tie his hair back, he grabbed his leather coat and broke for the door. Just has his hand turned on the knob, his phone rang. Clearly he wanted to just let it ring and get this whole movie thing over with, but his curiosity overwhelmed him. It could have very well been Amy. He really didn’t want to miss another one of her phone calls especially since he had been enjoying the time he was spending with her. He left the door shut and plucked the phone from his jacket pocket. It wasn’t Amy as he was expecting, instead he was rather perturbed with the text that scrolled across the digital screen. ‘Matt Ward Cell Phone’ This time it wasn’t Mary. Matt was the only person to use his cell phone so it was definitely Matt Ward on the other end. Killean really didn’t know what to do. On the one hand, if he didn’t answer the phone, it would show Matt that his mind games were affecting him and were knocking him off track. Matt was the master, as he was even able to play mind games with Seph. But then again, if he answered, he would have to listen to the smart remarks and the attempted mind games. Killean would not allow the mind games to control his mind, something that Matt Ward was not used to. That was his key most of the time. He out smarted his opponents. After thinking for a second or two, he pressed send on the phone and put it up to his ear. However instead of the common hello that he usually greeted with, he didn’t have time to say anything. Matt’s greeting came as soon as the phone reached his ear. “What took you so long to answer the phone?” He could just imagine the smile that graced the face of Matt Ward as those words slithered out of his mouth. Killean smiled as well though, knowing that the games would not work. “Matt, what exactly do you want to prove by calling me?” The scruff voice of Sirrajin boomed over the phone, banging into the ear drum of The Inhuman Being. But Tchu played it calm, acting like nothing was wrong. Another game… “What? I can’t call up a ‘friend’ to see how he is doing?” That smile had to be big as ever as Killean shuffled his feet, rather unwilling to play along. “Matt, it’s not going to work.” Killean began, sounding cool himself but also having a tone of authority. “Why do you play these games with me? You know it’s not going to affect me. I will walk into ReVolution in the Jewel in the Crown tournament and I will walk out in the semi-finals. Nothing is going to change and your stupid phone calls and silly comments won’t hurt my chances.” Almost as sly as the other comments, Tchu sounded off with one more line of gold. “They already have…” and he hung up. “Good memory.” Killean said to himself as he pocketed the phone. He remembered a session that he had with Tchu, just before his Universal Title defense against Boda. Tchu happened to say that a couple seconds of discussion or activity, whether it is friendly or not, can really side track an opponents thoughts. Killean was a little upset at himself when he responded the way he did. He should have tried to play the same games, throw the same smart ass comments back Tchu’s way. Now Matt knew that he didn’t remember, which means his mind really isn’t in the right place. “I knew I shouldn’t have amused myself with this movie thing.” He said to himself as he opened the door to his loft and broke into the hallway. -------------------------------------------------------/ Sunday, October 9, 2005 @ 4:45 PM Central Time “Demetrius Gage?” Killean asked. He was reading the script that was offered up to him. The main characters name was written right beside his. “That’s right Kill. You don’t mind if I call you Kill do ya? Of course you don’t.” The producer spoke fast and guarded, like he knew he was in control of the situation. But Killean had other ideas. “You know I haven’t signed a single contract yet. For you to even use my name in this script is…” But he was cut off again. Annoyance started to set in. “Is brilliant I say! We want to put Killean Sirrajin on the map outside of wrestling. We want to make you a star.” He was quickly approaching the brink of irritating. Joe Trinks was his name and producing stars was his game, or so the business card said on the desk that Killean was sitting in front of. He was looking over the script. “This script isn’t too bad actually. I…” Killean stopped himself. WHAT THE?! “Wait, why am I saying that? I don’t know anything about movies. I have no time to really watch TV let alone star in a movie.” The former Universal Champion said as he rose from his seat and planted the script back on the desk where it came from. But as he was ready to turn and leave the office, that was when Mr. Trinks appeared to work his magic. “Kill, wait! You know our goal here is to make the transition to movies rather seamless and we want to work with you. Your time is our time.” It really sounded like a lame scheme to get someone to shoot a Fruit of the Loom ad, so it definitely wasn’t washing with the Supreme Machine. “Listen, I am not an actor. I have no reason to even consider acting.” Killean tried to get the point across, but the producer was being exceedingly difficult and starting to get on Killean’s last nerve. “You have a reason, because you are a star.” He said matter of factly. Killean knew he was a star, but he was not about to use that to his advantage doing something he really didn’t want to do. Or so he thought. -------------------------------------------------/ Fifteen Minutes Later… “Mr. Sirrajin, this is the preliminary shoot. The main shoot will take place tomorrow.” He had been talking to Victoria for a matter of only thirty seconds and she was already on the brink of becoming the victim of a domestic dispute. She was even going as far as trying to give Killean some fashion tips. He was not the kind of person to talk to about fashion. He hated dress clothes and he hated business casual situations. Sirrajin is the kind of guy who would eat a big juicy hamburger over a steak and baked potato just to avoid going to a classy restaurant. “Ok ok, I get it. But leave my clothes alone.” He pulled away form her, undoing the collar on his button up shirt. “This is being done under my terms. If you don’t like it sweetheart, I can walk out that door right now.” Finally he felt a sense of control. Since the first time he set foot into the office, he hadn’t felt like he was in control of any aspect of this even though it was ultimately his decision. Victoria took a deep drag on her cigarette and exhaled, almost blowing the smoke into the face of The Supreme Machine. He hated smokers, not to mention smokers that didn’t have the plain decency to respect the wishes of others, sans the law. “You know, I could also report you to the city. Smoking is illegal in an indoor business setting.” He smiled deeply. His sinister side awoke as he wanted nothing more than to get under the wrinkled, nicotine infested skin of this boring old lady. “You could…” she said, but finished with a comment that possibly got under Killean’s skin even more. “But you won’t.” She butted out the cigarette in an ashtray as she walked to the door. She looked around the corner at nothing in particular and announced that Killean was ready. “The wrestler is ready!!” Doesn’t look at wrestling in a bad light huh? -------------------------------------------------/ Ten Minutes After That… As he walked around, reading from the script he held onto with almost white knuckled fury, he silently felt sorry for himself. Why was he doing this? He craved attention from the fans in a wrestling arena because he was doing something he loved and he was being himself. But acting was nothing something he had wanted to do since he was a kid. He saw it as a joke to him. Sure he liked to watch movies, but he never pictured himself as the next Clint Eastwood or, god forbid, Steven Segal. “I want to know what you plan to do about the lost shipment.” Apparently he was supposed to be holding on to the scruff of the neck of his peer, but he lacked the feeling. He just really didn’t want to be there. “I… I…” The younger man, about 23 years of age, struggled with the line. It had been his third screw up in 15 minutes. Killean was starting to become extremely frustrated with the man, more than likely because he had a month to prepare for this shoot compared to Killean’s five minutes. “CUT!!” The producer yelled. Killean threw his arms up into the air, trying to make is plainly obvious that this voice over was not going to work. “Can we just call it a day?” Killean begged, sounding like a whining actor more and more by the minute. He almost entirely disgusted himself. “Sure Kill.” Mr. Trinks said as he took the younger (wannabe) actor to the side. Killean turned to get a drink from the beverage table as he overheard the discussion Trinks and the young actor were having. “Good job kid. We should have this down in a couple days. I know the big guy is hard to work with, but…” Killean almost spit his cola across the room as he looked back to Mr. Trinks with enraged eyes. “Hard to work with?!” Killean dropped the empty cup on the floor and stormed over to where Trinks and the young man were standing. “This fool here, this so-called actor is messing up lines he should have memorized weeks ago and you say that I am hard to work with?!” Mr. Trinks appeared to defuse the fire quickly as he came up with a solution, one that actually made Killean feel bad for the young talent. “Ok ok, we will just cast someone else!” He said bluntly, making the younger man look at the producer like he was full of you know what. “Wait wait! I told you we need to do this under my rules otherwise I walk.” Killean took control of the situation. He suddenly felt like a father trying to stop a couple siblings from fighting. He hated that too. “The kid stays, long as you let me work with him myself, I am sure this can work out.” The producer threw his arms up in the air and walked away, pretty much leaving Killean and the young actor to do what they please. Killean turned to the younger lad and apologized. “Listen, I am sorry for saying that stuff about you. I really don’t want to be here so I apologize if I come off a little angry and frustrated. What’s your name kid?” Killean was trying to play the nice guy now. He did a pretty good job too. “My name is Bobby Sparks Mr. Sirrajin.” He took Bobby’s hand and shook it, formally acknowledging the greeting. “Call me Killean.” The Supreme Machine stood about seven inches taller that Bobby and also outweighed him by an estimated 100 pounds. Really, Bobby was not going to cause any problems with the much bigger and tougher man. “Listen, I’ll handle him. Do you enjoy acting?” “Why yes sir, I have wanted to act since I was in the plays in grade school.” Bobby started talking joyously about his affection for acting. Killean really did care if this kid succeeded and came to realize that he was only intimidated by Killean’s much larger stature in physical size and worldwide popularity. “Well stick with it kid. I stuck with what my dream and I accomplished more than I could have ever asked for. Keep your mind about it and it will work out.” Killean patted the young man on the shoulder and began to walk for the door when he was passed by Victoria. The short black haired woman was once again smoking a cigarette. Killean swore she looked like she was sixty, but he knew full well she was only in her mid-thirties, which disgusted him even more. Killean cleared his throat, which caused Victoria to turn and take notice. He simply made a smoking gesture with his hand a raised a knowing eyebrow. She however just rolled her eyes and raised something else, a finger in the direction of Sirrajin. “Awww now that’s not very nice!” He made a whiney sound as he nodded at the gesture, filing that one away for later use. He walked to the door but stopped when Bobby started talking. “See you tomorrow Killean.” The kid waved in his direction. “Maybe not kid.” Having waved back he disappeared around the corner and mumbled to himself. “Maybe not…” ---------------------------------------------------/ Monday, October 10, 2005 @ 6:45 PM Central Time Killean had thrown his luggage into the trunk of his car. He had secured a coach seat on a flight straight to Kansas City, Missouri which he planned on boarding right after he finished up his first shoot on Broadway in Winnipeg. Thoughts compounded his mind like an incessant pounding on a wall. He was thinking about ReVolution, about Matt and Mary and he was thinking about this needless hobby of movies. Actually he couldn’t even consider it a hobby, because he just didn’t enjoy it. Looking out his large window into the setting sun, he was feeling his first sense of comfort in days. He just wanted to come home and relax for a day or so before heading back out. Matters almost beyond his control changed that. But as the orange hue of the sun washed over him, he felt a large weight lift off his shoulders, like his problems were about to fix themselves and he wouldn’t have to worry about it. Almost on cue, his cell phone rang. He had just finished talking to Amy so he knew it wasn’t her. He had an idea who it might be, and his suspicions were confirmed when he looked at the display on his cellular. ‘Matt Ward Cell Phone’ Again, he was probably calling to play some more games, just two days before ReVolution. He just let the phone ring, leaving the sound to vibrate off his ear drums. This time he was not in the mood to hear the constant games that Tchu wanted to play. Tchu is not just a creation of the twisted mind of Matt Ward, Tchu was Matt Ward. Matt Ward was the Inhuman Being. When the phone stopped ringing, it sat silent for a couple seconds and started to vibrate and flash some lights on and off. It was signaling to Killean that it had a message, even though it was perhaps one that he didn’t have any desire to listen to. But he picked up the phone anyways and checked the message. Although it was definitely Matt, it was nothing like the message that he had running through his head. He always tried to guess what Matt would do next and he was mostly always wrong. This was one of those times. A simple message was left. ‘Please forgive me, Killean. It’s only gonna get worse.’ And that was that. Nothing to do with mind games, nothing to do with a rivalry that was over two years old. It was a simple apology with a message. Matt was trying to tell Killean that he couldn’t control his anger, quite sad for a psychologist. Killean put the phone in his pocket. He walked to the door. He just had one call to make on the way. --------------------------------------------/ Monday, October 10, 2005 @ 7:10 PM Central Time “But what are you talking about you won’t be here?” The annoying voice of Victoria bound over the phone line into the ear of Killean. She was smoking her traditional cigarette in her office, a pet peeve of the Supreme Machine. “You heard me.” Killean said on the other end of the line. “I am not coming for the shoot. You’ll just be forced to complete your silly movie without me.” “But you are the star, what about the contract?” Victoria’s voice annoyed Killean so much that he was just in a rush to get her off the phone. “Contract? If you look carefully I didn’t sign a contract.” Killean was laughing on the phone which kind of enraged the older, younger lady. “Oh and I made a phone call to help you guys. Hope you don’t mind.” And with that, the phone line died. Just after Victoria put the phone down, two police officers walked in the door. Victoria was about to greet them like she did everyone else, but she stopped. They noticed her smoking and just shook their head. Meanwhile, Killean threw the cell phone back on the passenger seat of the car as he made his way towards the airport. Soon he would be on a plane to Kansas City and away from these couple days that have turned out to be a complete joke. ------------------------------------------/ Tuesday, October 11, 2005 @ 12:35 PM Central Time Killean was met in the backstage area of the Kemper Arena by production crews from PRIME. Killean felt uneasy as this felt similar to the movie crew he abandoned just yesterday. But he knew this was his time to shine. This was his time to do what he loved and to be who he loved to be. He loved to be himself, Killean Sirrajin. “I don’t know if it was a coincidence or just pure irony, but I ran into a situation similar to yours Danny.” He was speaking in an interview to newer PRIME talent and movie showman Danny Ferguson. “Only I am not prepared to do something that I know I can’t do just for money.” Killean looked into the camera, being himself and doing what he wanted to do. “I am not about to stoop to such a low level to make more money or to expand my popularity. If people don’t love me for who I am, then so be it. But I am not about to become the next action hero. I am not about to become the next drama showman. I am a wrestler; I have been all my life. I have won accolades that most wrestlers deem unreachable. I have accomplished my goals yet I still wrestle. Why? It’s not for the money or the fame, nor is it for the awards and the accolades. It’s because I love wrestling. I love what I do and when people come in and try to make a mockery of it, trying to become something that they just aren’t, it bothers me. I guess you didn’t learn from the El Spiffy! Episodes.” The arena was quiet, not many wrestlers had arrived. No one had bothered them yet and no one was there to yell cut. This was Killean’s time. “I was offered a movie role this weekend and although I didn’t want to do it, I had a mind lapse and showed up at the studio anyways. But I woke up yesterday and dropped it like a bad habit. But why is it that Danny Ferguson cannot stay with what he is even remotely talented at? Don’t get me wrong, I have seen Hard Money and it quite frankly, wasn’t that good. I saw re-runs of that dry 80’s sitcom “Four is a Crowd” and it was really nothing to be proud of. But the fact is, wrestling just isn’t your thing, not when you have to step in the ring with a guy like me.” Killean sat, lacing up his boots for his practice run in the ring in a few minutes to warm up for tomorrow nights show. “You are like the fungus that grows on the bottom of a swimming pool. Sure it serves a purpose, but no one wants it there. That’s kinda like you in PRIME. No one wants you here, but yet you persist. I have studied your tapes and I don’t have a single piece of material that impresses me. Your wrestling matches are something to be desired too.” Killean smirked a bit to himself. Knocking Ferguson’s acting was nothing short of pleasurable to him and given the weekend he had, he could use some good humor. “Danny boy, I don’t pretend to be an actor because I know I am not cut for the job. So why pretend to be something you just aren’t? El Spiffy! was not your creation, you just capitalized on the popularity of the character yet you brought nothing to the table. What PRIME brass sees in you is beyond me, but I know I am not seeing it.” “Why even bother climbing in the ring with me Danny? My goal will be to tear you apart, to ground you and ensure that you don’t get up until that bell rings at the end of the match. I am bigger, stronger and more technically adept than you. Don’t pretend to be something that you’re not Danny; otherwise your night will feel like a bad episode of “Four is a Crowd” feels to me. You will feel pain, anguish and sorrow for your own existence in the squared circle. So when the curtain comes down on your dismal performance and the cameras stop rolling, you’ll be tending to your wounds. You’ll know why you are no longer in the Jewel in the Crown tournament and you’ll be hearing these words repeating in your head a million times over, eating away at your sanity.” “Why try to be something you are not?”
