The Good Life?

1 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Aug. 1, 2005 Ignoring the protests from your muscles and joints due to punishment and exertion, you force yourself to your feet. You blink rapidly, trying to regain your bearings as you survey the scene in front of you. Eight men, beating one another into hamburger in a structure enclosed by steel and glass. If anarchy were an art form, surely this would be considered its masterpiece. Then, suddenly, you feel a sharp pain in your back. “OOOOF!” The air is driven from your lungs.by the impact across your upper back. What the hell just hit you? You lay prone, blinking as white dots do a little spirit march across your field of vision, providing nice contrast to the blood-stained surface you lie on and the screams of agony echoing through the structure—though they are hardly heard over the roar of a crowd seeking blood as a side dish to their bread and circuses tonight. The answer comes as a pair of dark-skinned arms grab you and fling you headfirst against the cage. Weakly, you try to fight him off—grabbing for eyes, the throat, any fleshy part of the anatomy that can stop a man in his tracks no matter how tough he is. None of it works as strong hands envelop your head and start to push it back and forth against the metal grating. At last the pressure eases and you roll to a seated position, your body now SCREAMING in pain. You find yourself contemplating the now-forgotten bottle of oral Demerol as you raise a hand to wipe the sweat cascading down your face… Until you realize something. The substance on your fingers is too slick to be sweat. Suddenly, the blood is everywhere, pouring down your face, down the side of your head, obscuring your eyesight. You blink and wipe at the blood but it just keeps coming as someone else—a Diablo? Hawke? Nerezza? You’re too out of it to tell—nails you again. “Come on, move! Get away!” Your brain keeps sending messages to your arms and legs that go unanswered. Fight back, get a hand up, run away, just FUCKING DO SOMETHING!!! Instead, strong arms grab you and drive you down with a power maneuver. You’re not sure what he did to you. You’re not sure of anything right now. All your training, your acumen, your knowledge and technique seems to be seeping out of your skull along with the pint or two of AB-positive currently being spilt. You gag. The blood really IS everywhere, in every orifice on your face. It fills the back of your throat, and you repress a gag at the coppery taste of it. Have I been injured internally? You have no time to reflect on an answer as hands and feet dead set on meeting with your head and abdomen as many times as possible do just that. Help me, Azala, someone, ANYONE, just help me. I’ve made a mistake. Coming back to wrestling, coming to PRIME, befriending Amy then Azala, it was all a horrible mistake I understand that now just let me up, please stop please stop PLEASE STOP The scream inside your head becomes more and more shrill as… You open your eyes, feeling moisture on the side of your head and frantically running a hand up over your face and scalp. Just sweat… And the cloth bandage covering the 17 stitches in your forehead. With a sigh, you roll out of the bed, placing feet on hardwood floor, and flick on the light to illuminate the small bedroom in your shitty apartment in Charlotte. Friday, July 29 It’s been four days since Colossus II, and it’s four days before you’ll step back into the ring. Since Fallen Halo, the match that put the PRIME tag team division on the map, the match where Team Zambo had its chance to win the tag titles that had been denied for so long. Instead, you lost to the Diablos. AGAIN. The image of seeing Angelus and Baltasar escape that satanic structure with the tag titles—championship belts you felt within your grasp—leave a taste in your mouth even more sickening than the blood you remember swallowing during the match itself. You thought you had them beat. There’s no shame in it. You saw an opening against Knight, and you tried to capitalized. You failed. It happens. Lord knows you’re no stranger to doin the ole J-O-B. It’s happened before. Chances are, it’ll happen again. But what’s different this time? Why do you keep having the same dream, reliving the hellish combat inside the Fallen Halo, waking up in the middle of the night thinking that you’re still involved in the match, still covered in blood and gore, still trying to vainly fight off the brutal tactics of Los Diablos. And why is Axl Rose in your bedroom? The tones of “Paradise City” emanate from your cell phone. You grab the device, shutting off your old theme music—a different song from a different time—and pull it to your ear. “’Lo.” “Dave!” Dammit, this better not be another fan. How the hell do they keep getting your number? If it’s one of your students, you promise to stretch them until… “Hello, are you there?” “Yeah.” You run a hand over the bandage and your closely-cropped hair again. “I apologize for calling so early, but you’re not exactly an easy man to get ahold of with the travel and all. I’ve left seven messages at your wrestling school in the past four days, but you ha—” “Look pal, I’m not the only one that teaches there. Other commitments, you know? If you want to join that bad, we’ll be starting new classes in a couple of weeks.” Laughter. “Dave. Babe. Don’t you know who this is?” You pause. There’s only been one man to EVER call you babe—well, besides Steven Smith. “That you, Ricky?” “Alive and kickin’, brother!” “Look, if you’re calling to try and get me to work security for one of those pop princesses on your client roster, I’m afraid I don’t do that stuff anymore.” More laughter. Ricky certainly seems to be in a good mood today. “No, nothing like that. I was wondering… when can you get out here?” “Out… where?” “Philadelphia. I’ve got tickets to the Phillies when the Cubs come to town. L.A., Einstein!” Another chuckle—with a little more edge to it this time. “Listen, Rick, it’s not the best time. If there’s paperwork I need to take care of, just send it to my house.” “I’m not following…” “Hey, I understand. I became one of your low-priority guys when I hurt my neck. You want to cut all professional ties. It’s no biggie. But seriously, if you want to overnight me the papers, I PROMISE I’ll have ’em signed and—” “Dave! Baby, baby, baby, you’ve got it all wrong! We’ve got some offers to discuss, chico!” “Offers?” In another time, the same span of calendar pages when “Paradise City” could bring an entire arena of OSW fanatics to their feet—in cheers, hatred or sometimes both—Ricky Oliveros was the man making sure that the business of Dave Gibson would go beyond the squared circle. He was your agent, orchestrating media appearances and potential endorsement deals. He was making sure you’d be a good little Sellout months before you took the same character in front of the cameras. Now, he’s back. “Dave? You there, hermano?” “Yeah.” “Listen, I’ve got a first-class ticket with your name on it, just a point and click away. But if you don’t wanna come out, hey, that’s fine…” “No no! I mean… if you think the trip’s worth my time.” “Hey baby, Ricky O always delivers the goods. You know that, man!” “Right. Book the flight, Ricky. E-mail me the details.” “You won’t regret it, Dave. See you in sunny La-La Land tomorrow!” You hang up and start calling other numbers—waking up three of your advanced students to see if they can cover the classes for the next few days. The grumbles and discontent in their voices carries over the phone. You’ve been pushing the kids too hard lately, neglecting the wrestling school. But if what Ricky said was true, did you even NEED the school? Then again, where was he when things went wrong? More things to ponder as you shuffle off to the shower. Saturday, July 30You were “Old School” Dave Gibson—and for a while, you reminded everyone that a classic heel could still make it in this business. OSW was full of bad asses at the time. Guys on both sides of the fence with pituitary gland issues that REFUSED to look weak—in a match or on the mic. You, on the other hand, were willing to help make anyone look good. It was all part of the job, and if you had to, you’d sell for the goddamn Easter Bunny. As for guys like Ivan and Graphic Violence? They ATE bunnies. Raw. Turns out, Old School Wrestling was lacking in an old-school guy like you. And you took advantage. A feud with Hessian and his gothic wannabes in The Cult turned into a shot at the tag team titles. That shot at the titles turned into a reign at the top with Clay Vendetta. That reign ended in betrayal and led you to New School Wrestling—the rogue group that tried to “invade” OSW under the leadership of CEO Jane Reagan. That was the first time you changed characters—becoming “New School” Dave Gibson—and reminding everyone how to be a true heel, in and out of the ring. Other guys would break character once the show is over. If a fan approached them in the parking lot or in a restaurant and wanted an autograph, they’d sign it with a smile and a nod. Not you. A fan with a Sharpie was just another obstacle on the way to your rental car, a momentary appetite suppressant. Fuck the fans; you LIVED the gimmick. And meanwhile, other people started to take notice. That was when Ricky Oliveros, an up-and-coming agent at the time, came calling. You had big dreams from the moment you first stepped into the ring. You sent some headshots and clips of you in action and on the mic to several of the big talent agencies. Little did you know that your stuff got shunted off to a corner for some personal assistant fresh out of college. And little did Ricky Oliveros know at the time that he’d go from fetching coffee for clients to getting someone else to tote the snacks for his OWN damn clients in about six months time. Actually, Ricky probably did realize he was destined for more, but that’s neither here nor there. Back to the Book of Gibson… In the beginning there was wrestling. And slobber knocking and blood shed in the ring begat notoriety in the squared circle. And notoriety begat an agent, and the agent begat meetings, and the meetings begat auditions, and the auditions begat commercials, and the commercials begat royalties, which begat more royalties, which begat more royalties and more auditions and more commercials which begat MORE royalties. And the royalties upon royalties and royalties led thee to forsake the trappings of old. Get thee away from me, thou proclaimed as thou shed the tattered jeans, black T-shirts, one-bedroom apartments and Jeep Wranglers of yore. And lo! Thine past items of material significance hence were replaced by Armani suits, a large house in a gated community and a Range Rover with full leather interior, moon roof, CD and DVD changers, headrest TVs and $40,000 rims. And it was GOOD. When New School Wrestling folded, some of the other guys didn’t know what to do. You knew, though, that where one opportunity ends, another begins. No, not that one. Yes, best not to tell anyone about THAT particular opportunity. Better keep that one to yourself… forever if possible. Besides, if you can keep a secret from Amy and Jeff, deluding your own sub-conscious shouldn’t be too difficult… You decided to start living the gimmick. And “New School” Dave Gibson became The Sellout. You wore suits to the ring. You started to amass allies and formed Power Play. One WarGames cage match later and your star shone brighter than ever. More opportunities—in and out of the ring—emerged. Commercials, and the chance to help OTHER wrestlers get their own endorsement deals. Hey, you’d been burning up the airways flying back and forth to shoots set up by Ricky. Is it any wonder you started to play agent yourself with some of the other guys in the locker room? But meanwhile, everything was starting to catch up with you. You started living the gimmick a little too much. And you began a losing streak that’d make even Barry Horowitz cringe. And then, you were out of the game. Sidelined after a very strategic and well-executed piledriver by Matt Register onto the Apex Predator’s own Old School Title belt. Your Armani suit developed a sudden and unwelcome accessory—a neck brace. And the calls stopped coming. Now, you were the one trying to get in contact with Ricky. You were the one looking for some deals or other ways to make money. The bills were still coming in unchecked for the house and the cars and the suits, but the paydays from the office for wrestling had ended and that healthy bank account balance was threatening to evaporate. Maybe, you said, you could do some commentary on the side. Get your own talk show about wrestling and sports in general. Hell, you always had the gift for gab. “Sorry, babe,” he said. “We just can’t expect any big offers right now.” Ricky always used the first-person plural whenever he started to discuss business specifics. It was always how “we” need to discuss the particulars of “our” royalty agreement. After you broke your neck again, OSW closed, you had to sell the house and the Range Rover and began to contemplate the mountain of personal debt in front of you while giving equal pondering to a relapse back into the booze and the pills, you thought about ending it all. Hell, you’re not ashamed of it. But the thought of Ricky—speaking your eulogy about how thrilled WE are to be a part of OUR funeral—made you smile no matter how many times you’d eaten canned tuna fish and Minute Rice for dinner that month. On your first class flight across country, you try to remember all that. You try to remember how Ricky dropped you like a bad habit once things turned sour. You don’t begrudge him that—how marketable is a wrestler who’s a recovering addict, washed up at 25 with a neck held together by paper clips and snot that leaves him too risky for even grunt work as a stuntman? But you do your best not to forget. But the limo ride from the airport—with its cushy comforts and memories of a past life—make forgetting seem like a lot nicer of an option. “Dave!” Ricky comes around his desk and gives you, of all things, a hug. Then, “So sorry to hear about Marsha.” “They got the flowers at the funeral home. Thanks for the gesture.” “Hey, least I could do, man. Your mom was a good woman.” The sentiment rings as hollowly as it did in the funeral parlor. “Yeah… it’s hard without her.” “I can’t even imagine what you’re going through. But, we’ve got a lot to talk about.” So, business already. That was fast. “Right, I didn’t fly out here to watch a baseball game, that’s for sure.” “Man, the Dodgers are BRUTAL right now, aren’t they? Let’s take a seat. Man, that bandage is HUGE! Nasty wound?” “Got cut up pretty bad at the pay-per-view. Lost two pints of blood.” “We’ll consult our people. Got in-house makeup and wardrobe right now. Our reputation’s pretty strong there.” “So what you got for me, Ricky?” “Well, since the win over Brandon Youngblood—and that high-profile match at the pay-per-view—we’ve been getting several interesting opportunities. The old standards—commercial opportunities, mostly for the companies with the same demographic as professional wrestling—plus a book deal.” “Book deal?” “Yeah, wrestling books are all the rage these days. We should give serious thought to writing an autobiography.” “I barely graduated high school, Ricky. You know that.” “Hey, we can get a ghost writer. Writers are a dime a dozen, always looking for a quick paycheck. Should we start interviewing some potential authors?” “Some things stay with me. My story’s one of them.” “Hey, fair enough. We don’t want to do anything that makes us uncomfortable, you know?” “Right. So commercials then.” “Yeah, we’ve got offers from a tire company, Chili’s Restaurant—we did a spot for them back in OSW—and Under Armour.” “Under Armour?” “Yeah, seen the ads? WE MUST PROTECT THIS HOUSE!” “I’ve seen ’em. But… Under Armour, athletes wear that under their gear, right?” “Yup.” “I don’t wear shirts in the ring, Ricky.” “Well… have you thought about starting? The money’s pretty good…” “Let’s start with the Chili’s ad.” “Way ahead on that one. We’ve got a pre-shoot interview with them, sort of to talk creative content and all that, but since it’s the weekend they can’t squeeze us in until Monday. We’ve got a hotel all set up for—” “Stop.” “Problem, Dave?” “I can’t do Monday.” “Excuse me?” “I’ve got a match coming up. Two in fact. A six-man tag against The Imperium, plus a 36-man battle royal.” “Well, perhaps we can get PRIME to make some creative changes. We’ve done it before, remember? We just get them to make it a—what do they call it?—a handicap match.” “No.” “What’s that?” “I don’t do that any more. I don’t hang people out to dry. Not this time around. Not Azala.” “You’re putting us in quite a bind here, Dave… We need to really think about this before doing anything rash.” “Can we PLEASE drop the double-speak, Ricky?” “Fine.” He’s frowning but you don’t see many additional lines in his face. Botox? Possibly. “The fact of the matter is, most guys in your position NEVER get a second chance like this. You’re hot right now. The win over Youngblood, the pay-per-view. People are talking about Dave Gibson again. People you used to know. Important people. If you want to keep them talking, you’ll do this spot.” For once, you’re struck silent. “If I don’t… will you drop me as a client?” “No, Dave. But I also can’t promise any other opportunities will come along. But there’s something else to consider.” You look up as Ricky tosses a computer printout in front of you. You recognize the banner. Since when does Ricky read the online dirt sheets, any way? “Other guys in PRIME are noticing your star is rising, and they’re not happy. Look at the comments from Vetra. ‘He’s taking my spot?’ You’ve barely been there and you’re already leapfrogged some of the veterans. How long before somebody tries to break your neck a third time? And now you want to wrestle in a battle royal? You’re going to have a bull’s-eye on your back as it is—especially in a match like that. It’s time to start thinking about your future before your past catches up with you.” “Azala will have my back.” “Even he can’t fight off 34 men, Dave.” “You’ve made your point, Ricky. And I’ve made my decision.” Monday, August 1You made a wise choice in agents. Ricky Oliveros knows how to get things done. He got you on the redeye back to Charlotte with just a couple of hours’ notice. As you lift weights and work with your advanced students in the ring over most of the next 36 hours, part of you wonders if Chili’s has found the man for that spot yet. Probably so—probably another one of Ricky’s clients, knowing how your agent works, always having a backup plan. Backup plans… you’re lucky if you’ve even got a plan to begin with. You add more weight to the barbell before sliding onto the bench and beginning your third set. Unlike your dream, you now welcome the burn of exertion in your muscles. Pain now means the ability to deal pain on Wednesday, the chance to make Angelus and Baltasar realize they lucked out twice against Team Zambo, and the ability to make sure anyone who tries to cheap-shot you in the battle royal ends up regretting it. You became the Sellout in and out of the ring, and you said you were never happier. Now, you’re happier than you’ve ever been, living in your shitty apartment and driving a used Jeep Wrangler. The Sellout has decided he’d rather live for the wrestling. Ain’t that some shit?
