Date: 7/13/08-7/20/08 The spaces of Texas opened up to him, making him feel as if he was the only creature in the scorched terracotta expanse. Arid waves didn’t bother him. In the solitary embrace of this singularity, human comfort was made trivial. Sweat poured over his skull’s breadth, and he threw his arms up triumphantly, his wild hound ravings the only company he had. He pressed the heels of his hands into his knees as he took sharp breaths. A low haze of dust crept across the scree jutting out of the flood plain. With nothing of consequence riding his mind, he reasoned to climb it the old Indian way, and if the talus chipped under his grasp, he would gladly fall, if only because he was a free man. ---- “Right now,” Brandon groaned, “I want to get away from everyone.” For him, Los Angeles now stood as a collection of overcrowded avenues and false sky rises. Only able to reflect on reality while in his holding cell, he had plenty of time to ponder this sham of an existence. He missed the desert; missed the call of the untamable. He hadn’t realized it until he saw faint traces of humanity baked into an all too practiced concoction. They danced, oblivious of how out of control their lives really were. Youngblood had escaped it through violence. Breaking men and women with his hands like prisoners would with hammers to rocks. The lines constantly grew blurry between what was and what wasn’t real. They sat on an obnoxiously high floor. Lisa Durbin’s pitch fork heels bobbed on her desk, nylon showing through on her exposed ankles. She sat with a hawk scowl, viewing the city from her perch with a watchful eye. “You have to be thinking about your family, Brandon,” she chided in a New England cadence. He sighed, his attention more focused on the reflective material of her shoes. “Perhaps she’s the reason this even happened in the first place.” The statement broke her concentration. “You can’t possibly be…” she hesitated, perplexed by her client’s vacant stare. The muscles along his lips were relaxed and the flesh of his face was straight save the imperfections grafted into his skin from his line of work. She pulled her feet off and steadied them under her desk. Brushing hazel curls out of her line of sight, she rubbed the skin underneath her right eyelid. She felt a soft pain as she ran her fingertips across a hidden bump. “Whether she had anything to do with this,” she started as she drew her hands together, “you have to realize that if she plays a factor, you will lose”. He noticed the heavy eyeliner she sported underneath her eyes and suddenly felt even more disgusted. “I’m just upset about what happened.” “Well, your wife is in shock. Why did you tell her over the phone?” He steadied himself in his seat, his hands grabbing the steel armrests for support. “I got the answering machine.” Lisa cupped her forehead and began rubbing the foundation which helped her look ten years younger. “I’m not paid to make…” She hesitated again. Her tongue licked burgundy for a moment. I’m not paid to make assumptions, she lied to herself. The facts were in front of her; she had to defend a client in a cut and dry case. He’d been caught soliciting a police officer for sex. A urine tox screen showed he had cocaine in his system. Unless it was erased, there was a phone message where he admitted to the crime. She knew that message still existed; Youngblood’s domestic abuse case from three years prior showed how he treated his wife. In her shoes, Lisa would have already burned him at the stake. In reality, she would have never let it get this bad. Given that he was here, paying her salary, she figured Melissa tolerated his flagrant abuse for the sake of money. No logical woman would put up with the bad behavior of Brandon Youngblood unless it meant a few more diamonds, or a few more charge cards to max out. Lisa had made her mark with crueler clients thanks to displacing the blame from who she was defending. At this stage of her life, even something as simple as sympathy for another woman had to be shut off. She couldn’t risk it troubling her conscious. “I don’t want jail time out of this.” His eyes traced the large panes of glass encasing them both. “I came here before anything else. I need you to understand just how important it is that I don’t do time. I have a kid coming. I have to make amends with my wife.” While she’d been paid handsomely to forgo conventional human sympathies, it didn’t save her from being sickened by those she had to defend. She’d spent years crying into pillows. She’d never had a real relationship and she nearing her twilight years. But she’d gotten over the simpler things in life. At that moment, all she wanted to do was shake him. Slap him. Make him know that his child-like unawareness prevented him from seeing how damaging this situation really was. That he’d be lucky if she ever saw his wife again, let alone the child she was carrying. The thought of carrying his child nearly caused her to gag. “We’ll see what we can do.” That fucking idiot grin he wore caused a nervous hitch to go off in her lips. Hardened blood burst from her heart in waves. Varnished nails pressed into the arches of her hands. As he closed the door, she rose from her chair and stabbed her way across the carpet. Yanking the cabinet door open, she grabbed a bottle scotch and unscrewed the top. She too wanted to get away from everyone, but for different reasons. She took a swig, grimacing at first, but sighing as it burned its way down her throat. ---- The television was on, but Melissa hadn’t the foggiest recollection of what exactly was on it. Given the amount of sunlight, it might have been the evening news, but the sky had been overcast for most of the day. She stayed stagnate, her raw feet resting on the glass coffee table in the center of the room. The wounds were dirty and exposed. Pangs of hunger rocked her insides, but she didn’t move; she rather she rotted into the couch’s upholstery. Melissa had long since passed the point of no return, having gone crashing into the lull of a depression she refused to escape from. Hours before, she’d made up her mind that she would paint the boulevard. No matter how close she positioned herself, she couldn’t push off the edge with enough force. What was inside of her prevented this from happening. When the door opened, she didn’t know how to react. Brandon’s footfalls were anything but soft. “Melissa?” he asked the living room, but received no response. He didn’t need it; he saw her on the couch, reclined and expressionless as she listened to the genteel droning of newscasters. “Honey, I’m…” His sensibilities were counterfeit. Even as he crouched over and looked at her, she couldn’t help but feel what he was about to do was part of some machination he’d cooked up while stuck inside a cell. Just another mental game plotted to further torment her. She had no energy. What she had had been siphoned off, fed into the germinating consequence of two uncorrectable mistakes. She preferred it suck her dry. “Yes?” Melissa’s voice was limpid, her face serene as she broke away and looked her husband in the eyes with nothing but malice in her heart. Brandon didn’t break down. She respected him for his composure because she didn’t want this piece of filth attempting to pluck lilts over broken heart strings. Instead, he looked her in the eyes. “I’m sorry for this. And I’m sorry for everything.” “It’s okay. I believe you.” “I can’t…” Don’t you dare, Melissa thought. Brandon trapped her with a frozen caress along her cheek before brushing the contour of her neck. The air she breathed through her nostrils tasted stale on the back of her tongue. His eyes were glossed over, ripples of red strewn along the whites. “Everything is going to be okay. Alright hon? I’ll get help.” That was the same half-hearted plea he’d made before absconding into the Rocky Mountains with a Bible clutched to his heart. And it didn’t do any good back then. “It’s okay.” “I need you to realize that I’m sorry for this.” “It’s okay.” Brandon nodded his head, drawing his wife into an embrace. “This does me good,” he said in a sobering whir. “This does me good.” When he finally stopped holding her, he rose back to his feet, glancing out towards the gunmetal haze oozing over the city’s expanse. “When this is over, I think its best that we just,” his voice dissipated as he walked away towards the kitchen, “leave.” She narrowed her eyes to slits as her head stalked in his direction with a glower. The bastard didn’t even make mention of her tattered soles. ---- Before Texas, there was one story sardine can in Winnipeg. A marriage house for a couple who’s family had steadily outgrown it. There were two bed rooms, and one was for his mother and father. Brandon had to share space with his sister, living on a bunk bed coated with pink paint and bonnet blue ribbons. Then Lindsay and Tommy came along and right as he was starting to need a place to escape to, there was no space to be had. Candace snored and Tommy talked in his sleep. Lindsay was terrified of the passing cars because their headlights scampered through the closed cavities of drawn blinds. She called them ghosts and assorted an army of stuffed animals to liter open spaces on the floor. The old television they had in their room was habitually left on, the sonic ring from the working tube whistling ever-presently. He braved the black trappings of the basement with a blanket and pillow, sleeping on a slab of cold concrete because at least the space could be his. He stayed down there every night he could, even after waking up with tightly wound charley-horses. Eventually, his parents forced him upstairs. By that point, his reemergence in the room had wrought every annoying trait from his brother and sisters unconscious aggravations with heavy traits of malice. When the summer came, crickets would house themselves underneath the hoheria sprouts scattered in the wood chip mote. Their chirps were intense and became akin to the grinding of chalk into lastoplate. It was when he wanted a corner of his home that things always came to a heart stopping pitch. A blinding rage would overtake him, and each breath tasted of sulfur. His muscles would spasmodically twitch, and the world would become thimble small. He felt isolated in a mass of flesh and musty air. There was nowhere to escape; the panopticon was always trained on his movement. He became claustrophobic because of that house. And through his almost irrational fear, he grew misanthropic. It was why he adopted the path of a vagabond. Brandon Youngblood was afraid of being unable to escape. ---- “I can make it alright,” Brandon babbled as he grabbed the edges of porcelain, an ignorant smirk plastered across his beaten face. His entire upper body rocked back and forth as he stared deep into his own reflection. Steam rushed from the facet’s torrid hot water spits. The vapors massaged the stubble of his jaw. “I can make this alright.” But his voice was breaking, quakes interloping and causing hitches throughout his cracking façade. ---- Brandon wondered why everything in the district attorney’s office screamed mahogany. The place smelled of old book bindings and turpentine. He never kept suits for long, usually tossing them away after wearing them once. But he had kept one, and it constricted his skin. Profuse sheets of sweat pressed through the cotton fabric of his dress shirt, thankfully hidden by a navy blue blazer. Even as cold air was pumped through the ventilation system, the trappings felt humid. He didn’t remember the names of the two minions from the prosecution. One was a cute redhead with a tight bun which was starting to fall apart. She was a young thing, probably fresh from college. Her partner was an older gentleman dressed monochrome whose hair had turned silver. “I’m sorry, but my client states that he was apprehensive of the officer’s advances. Perhaps you should read the statement provided from the defendant.” Lisa was a good person, Brandon reasoned, because she had to deal with people like him. He didn’t feel either way regardless of that though; she was, after all, taking his money. The silver attorney rubbed the ridges of his forehead. “We’re not here to discuss semantics—” “Semantics?” Durbin interrupted with a slam of her fist. “You think the conduct of police is semantics? You think it is simply semantics when agents of the law entrap—” “I do not think a pat on the back constitutes entrapment,” the red head chided, a smirk crawling across her lips. Her jovial nature only served to anger Brandon; she was finding humor at his expense. “More than anything, your client’s willingness to be belligerent with the officer should mean an added assault charge.” Brandon rolled his eyes, opening his mouth to speak. His attorney shushed him. “I think pointing a gun at my client’s head shows just how dangerous the officer really was.” “We are not here to argue about the particulars of the situation, Miss Durbin. We are here to give your client a chance to answer for his crime in a…” he hesitated, pinching his chin between his thumb and index finger. “Well, I think what we can offer him is a significantly better deal than he’s going to get anywhere else.” “My client isn’t guilty.” Durbin was adamant in her assessment. Brandon wondered how others who were as guilty as he truly was reacted to hearing this kind of remunerated support. The redhead had a case of the chuckles. “He confessed on audio tape, which we received from his wife.” “And that won’t be admissible,” Durbin pointedly shot down. Silver Hair pushed forward a photocopied piece of evidence which was sure to be an exhibit used if Youngblood’s case were to go to trail. “He has a signed statement saying he solicited the officer to have sexual relations.” “And that statement will not be admissible either,” Durbin quickly retorted, a faux smile gracing her face. She had so much bravado; it made Brandon jealous. But the old man wasn’t about to be undone. “What about the drug screening which, at the very least, shows Brandon to have had enough cocaine in his system to bust down doors with guns like Tony Montana?” He struggled at the end of the sentence, stressing the final two syllables of the almost apt character correlation. “You think when we are done shooting holes through your statements and through the scenario as it took place that cocaine will make a lick of difference?” The older officer for the district attorney reclined back in his chair, resting his hands against the back of his head. “That charge alone is going to earn him three years.” Youngblood couldn’t take the haughtiness anymore. “That isn’t going to happen.” “Brandon,” the redhead began, “you have another conviction that you plead guilty to. That’s going to factor into the decision of how lenient a judge is with you.” “Hey!” Lisa roared, again slamming her hand into the table. “You speak to me, alright? I’m his legal counsel.” The seed had already been planted. As silence permeated the office, Brandon found himself locked in the same mental space he had been in for the last week. “Three years?” “Yes,” the older gentleman responded, “at least three years if you take it to trial.” Lisa patted his forearm, attempting to comfort him. But her palms were cold, her touch eliciting a shiver to shoot through to his shoulder. “Brandon, don’t listen, it’s the government trying to get their pound of flesh.” “Look,” wariness brought on from the proceedings was starting to show on the older prosecutors face. “Three years on the cocaine possession charge is enough. But he has another conviction. With soliciting a prostitute, his time increases. He’s looking at three and a half years at least.” The idea terrified him. “So what are you offering?” Lisa rubbed her eyes, a sigh escaping her lips. “Nothing worth taking, I can assure you Brandon.” The redhead, sensing an opening, immediately jumped forward. “Well, we are willing to wave the solicitation charge—” Durbin immediately interrupted, nearly unbolting from her seat when she heard the line of tripe. “That isn’t even worth opening your mouth about. You drop the drug charge and we can talk.” In a flash, Brandon knew the reality; if a deal was worth talking about, then his chances of escaping prison time was impossible. The prosecutors were quiet for a moment. Silver Hair finally broke the tension. “He rolls on who sold him the cocaine, we’ll consider it.” Youngblood scratched the back of his neck. “So, I tell you who sold me the drugs and I get off free?” “No.” The redhead’s voice was beginning to remind him of crickets. “We’ll drop the drug charge and allow you to do three to six in county for the solicitation charge.” Lisa just shook her head. “You think we can’t do better in court?” The lead prosecutor narrowed his glance toward Brandon, completely abandoning conversation with his defense attorney. “You willing to risk three to five years of your client’s life in a state prison on your assumption?” The redhead chimed in. “Brandon, you’ll miss the birth of—” That comment got Durbin out of her chair. “Now that is crossing—” “Shut up,” Brandon meekly mouthed. A catfight was beginning. “Well he is taking a very big risk and what we are offering—” “Shut up.” Brandon was more forceful this time. Slamming her briefcase shut, Lisa Durbin said what she had been waiting to say. “He doesn’t take it. We’ll see you—” “Shut up!” Brandon acerbically roared before calming down. “Three months?” The redhead began correcting him. “Three to—” “Three months.” Brandon wasn’t budging. Lisa wasn’t buying this for a second. “I don’t think my client—” But Youngblood was quick to cut her off. “No, I’m aware of what I’m doing. I have a family to think about.” After a few moments of deliberation, the decision was made. “Three months then.” It wasn’t about defending an innocent man for Lisa; it was about the money she was about to lose out on. “Brandon, I’d strongly hope you’d reconsider—” Three years was too much time. In truth, another minute inside a cage was too much for his psyche. But there were no clean getaways from this. “I’ll pay triple the fine if the,” he hesitated, “nature of my crime was kept under wraps. I don’t think it would be an issue to keep the gender I solicited under wraps.” The redhead groaned. “He’s getting off too easy.” Pointing a finger at his younger partner, the older gentleman countered. “Fifty thousand, then. Money like that can go a long ways.” “Brandon…” Lisa started. “We can win this case.” “Yeah…” Brandon lied. “I know. But I can’t risk it.” Hanging his head, Youngblood had no other options. He could already feel edges of the office tapering towards his shoes. “You got yourselves a deal.” ---- He’d signed away his freedom willingly. In the low light of the restroom, he stood as he had before, looking into the mirror at his reflection. What stared back at him was unrecognizable; a tattered remnant of a human being who’d fall so far from grace into perdition that the angels cried for Lucifer to stop his ceaseless torture. He was adulterous; the amount of times and the genders blurring together. The spike of infidelity had been beaten in with a ball peen hammer, the head violently jostled before breaking away, the wound cauterized shut so the sin would forever be buried inside. Once their son was born, everything would change. He was vain; the result of being handed too much too soon. While others had scratched around in the dirt to find their wealth, he’d walked into it and held companies for ransom. And worse than that, he cheated the industry which had given him his fame. The restroom was just like the office, all mahogany but with checkerboard laminate and the smell of air fresheners. The facet heads were shiny; their necks dipping like elegant swans. He was slothful; he’d already peaked, and in his own mind he was the supernova in which the entire cosmos cowed to. But those from the past had cast him aside, and those who were once below had all but superseded him. They had all cut rifts through the sky, blazing a trail of mastery which made what he considered high watermarks to be puny and frail. They would look upon him with sullen eyes. Some would cackle at how The Greatest emasculated himself. And history would remember him as nothing more than a failure. They’d chained him down before, but he had broken free. He’d been cast from their Eden, but somehow had slithered back inside. He’d evaded every single person who ever tried to end him, collecting their skulls as trophies. There was nothing left to fight for. He’d become what they’d always wished he would be. What they always knew he was. But he refused to give them that one last act of satisfaction. If he would die, it would be under the tight grasp of his own noose. There was one last act of defiance before the curtain went down; before this pathetic invalid faded from relevancy and into obscurity. His fingers rapped against the heels of his hands. He slackened his shoulders. Only consequence rode his mind. With a sudden burst, Youngblood threw his head into his glass visage. Ropes of red divided along the ridge of his nose and rushing down his nostrils. And if the gash was too deep, he would gladly die, if only because it was his last act as a free man.
