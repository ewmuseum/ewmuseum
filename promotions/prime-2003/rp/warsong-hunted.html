[Warsong] Hunted

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: ReV 199 The grass tickled his cheek, just below his left eye, as he tried hard to ignore the cramp that was starting to form in his right leg. The sensations, at either end and opposite sides of his body, were a distraction that he couldn’t afford. Not with only one chance to take the shot. Not with only one shot. He breathed out, softly, slowly, gently, from the corner of his mouth and the irritation against his skin slid away as the blade bent back against itself. Keeping his eye firmly on the target, through the sight, never blinking, he stretched out his leg and the muscle that was doing its best to stop him. Second by second he moved millimetre by millimetre knowing that the slightest noise, the first hint of intrusion, and his prey would be gone. All that time in preparation, planning and tracking would be wasted. Finally the lack of discomfort, the absence of pain, filtered through and he realised that the cramp was gone. Settling himself back into the depression in the ground that his body had naturally caused he took a deep, steadying breath and allowed his focus to travel through the telescopic sight as his finger muscle tensed in readiness. It took a second for his brain to realise that, though it looked like he could simply reach out and touch the deer that grazed nervously in front of him, it was actually close to three hundred yards away. Extreme range. Trees and bushes moving in the slight Westerly wind. A skittish animal. A difficult shot. Difficult but not impossible. Time stopped. Thought became deed. The trigger was caressed gently into submission and, finally, the shot taken. “Nice shot.” Rick James dropped the camera in shock at the softly spoken voice that whispered in his ear. Before he could cry out a hand was placed over his mouth, stopping the scream before it could form, and with eyes open wide he saw his best friend, his soul-mate, Jay Phoenix, lie down in the long grass beside him. “Shush,” Jay said through a smile. “You don’t want to scare him off now, do you?” “Scare him?” Rick squealed then, as the deer’s head jerked up, nostrils flaring, he gritted his teeth. “What about me – you nearly gave me a heart attack!?” “You didn’t know I was here?” “I thought that you were still in bed, where I left you!” “Couldn’t sleep. Wanted to see where you were sneaking of to at stupid o’clock in the morning.” “… and you couldn’t just ask me?!” “No – more fun this way; you should have seen your face!” Phoenix’s eyes clouded over, slightly, as he stared at the face he was talking about. His hand reached out, tenderly, and traced the still raw wound, held together by staples, partially hidden in Rick’s hairline. A large area of skin, around the eye and cheekbone, was still swollen and discoloured. Rick reached up, taking Phoenix’s hand in his own and gently guiding it away from the area. Squeezing it, he smiled. “Don’t do that, Jay. You promised.” “I can’t help it Rick, it’s just …” “It wasn’t your fault, we both know that, so don’t be taking on any extra guilt, please.” “It’s hard not to, Rick, if it wasn’t for me then Natas wouldn’t have …” “What about you, though?” Rick interrupted. “It isn’t like you got off without a scratch either and, as I recall, I just got staples, and you got those and a mild concussion on top!” “I am used to it, Rick,” Phoenix sighed. “It is my life – I signed up for it!” “It was your life, Jay,” Rick said, holding Phoenix’s hand up to kiss it gently. “It is our life now – or at least it was.” “Yeah, about that …” Phoenix’s voice drifted off as, with a frown playing across his forehead, he stared past Rick and into the distance. Without a word he slipped, silently, to his feet and moved at a brisk lope through the grass. “Jay …?” Rick stared after him, watching with a mixture of confusion and awe as Phoenix moved silently and easily closer and closer towards the deer. It wasn’t until spots started to appear before his eyes, and a burning sensation fired through his chest, that Rick realised that he was holding his breath. He let it out with a gasp, gagging as he tried to silence himself as the deer shook and snorted in response. For a moment Rick couldn’t see Phoenix, his attention caught up completely by the deer as it stamped a hoof, nervously, and then it was over. “…no!” Rick’s shout came a split second after Phoenix came up behind the deer, moving fast at a crouch. A flash of silver became a spray of crimson and, before he knew what he was doing, Rick was up and running forwards, the camera forgotten. The deer’s legs buckled, it’s eyes wide and then, with a bubble of blood from it’s flared nostrils, it collapsed to the ground. “How could you?!” Rick slammed his palms against Phoenix’s chest, pushing the surprised man back a step. “How the fuck could you kill that innocent …” Phoenix dropped the hunting knife he had been holding and caught Rick’s wrists, keeping him still as he tried to push him again, as gently as possible. “Look,” he said, forcefully, and then repeated more softly. “Rick, look.” Kneeling down, pulling Rick with him, Phoenix put his hand on the still flank of the deer, pushing some of the coarse hair out of the way to reveal a puckered and oozing rent in the beast’s skin. Rick gagged, a little, as the putrid stench of decay hit him. “What’s that?” he asked, leaning against Jay, his anger forgotten. “That, Rick,” Phoenix answered his hand flat on the deer’s side, “is what happens when people not as caring as you hunt animals with weapons rather than cameras. Some idiot tried to shoot this poor thing, didn’t get a clean kill and then didn’t bother putting it out of its misery.” Pushing his hair back from his face, leaving a streak of blood from chin to forehead, Phoenix sighed, gazing into the distance. “He was lucky, though.” “Why?” “It was just a deer. If this had been a bear then things would have been different; you don’t leave dangerous animals wounded behind you. You either leave them alone or you kill them. You finish them. Anything else just provokes them and a hunter stupid enough to do that deserves whatever he has coming. The hunter becomes the hunted.” Rick stood up, pulling Phoenix back to his feet and reaching up to try to wipe the blood from his all too serious face. As he stared into the eyes that he knew so well Rick sighed and then, with a small smile, cupped the blood stained face in both hands. “When did this stop being about the deer?” “What?” Phoenix stuttered. “I know you, Jay, and I know that look.” Rick stated, simply. “I know you well enough to realise that your retirement, our new life, is going to have to wait, isn’t it? You’re going back, aren’t you?” Phoenix leant in close, placing his head flat against Rick’s own as he breathed in, deeply. “Yes,” he breathed. “I’m sorry, but yes we are going back. He has to pay.” “Because of what he did?” Rick asked. “Because he hurt you?” Phoenix’s hand traced the outline of the staples in Rick’s temple, once more, then he looked up with tears forming streaks in the blood on his face. “No, because he hurt you.”
