Into The Abyss: Part III - Sweet Mercy

19 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 19-01-09 The dull throbbing sensation in his back greeted him like an old friend. The reunion brought a soft smile to his hardened face as he yawned and rubbed the sleep from his eyes, turning to the little digital alarm clock on the side cabinet and quietly groaning at the blinking green numbers telling him it was 0:00hrs. Glancing at the bright haze filtering from behind the closed curtains he guessed the alarm clock had somehow reset...or he'd travelled into the future to Greenland in the spring time and it was still the middle of the night. The idea of doing so raised a low chuckle as he gazed down past the white duvet with its subtle Charles Rennie Mackintosh design embroidered into it and sighed as a big pair of hairy feet stood to attention at the end of the bed. Lifting his head off a single large fluffy duck feather pillow that bore a black rose also in the style of Mackintosh, he observed that his feet were still nestled on top of a second pillow borrowed from its place by his head and moved to the seat of a little Florence Knoll bench. Only after he used his kit bag to add height did the pillow reach the level of the mattress and provide a tolerable comfort. The room itself was forgettable, as many in the life of a wrestler were. Shades of white and flowery designs printed on most of the furniture from the curtains to those pillows were induced upon any visitors to really capture that pastoral atmosphere. He thought it a shame the cleaner hadn't made an effort to scrub out a dark greyish brown stain on the pillow he used to rest his feet, the one reason it was nominated for foot duty. All illusion of the countryside died with that soiled pillow and even 'The Skiff' by Renoir, hung above the bed's plush silky beige-cushioned headboard couldn't save it. Pulling the curtains back the real thing wasn't much better; the most interesting feature was the highway breathing a little polluted life into the surrounding fields. Even the hills seemed to shirk into the dirt, too ashamed to reach for the sky. Sluggishly he pulled himself up out of bed, throwing the duvet aside and knocking the pillow off the bench in the process. Ruffling through his kit for his toilet bag he dragged his feet across the maroon carpet and nudged the door of the en-suite bathroom shut with his heel. Laying out his toothbrush and paste, wash cloth and floss he once again met his reflection in the mirror of the cabinet hanging over the sink. The face staring back suddenly beamed with a childish grin at the discovery of a blotchy purple bruise adorning the corner of his right eye, courtesy of a closed fist thrown by Jay Phoenix during their match at ReVolution 182. Between the pain in his back and the black eye Von Kelsig felt like it was Christmas, and it was as though the glory days were bleeding back through under the skin just as the bruise had blossomed. He had played the match through in his mind so many times between then and now that the few hundred miles he had driven to get to this little pit-stop motel had felt like a casual trip to the store. He later watched the cars racing past and throwing glints of morning sunshine into his face as he dressed in faded jeans and a black t-shirt, then threw a brown leather bomber jacket over his shoulders and left the room. Handing his room key back in at reception to a little old lady in a white shawl with a blue rinse that resembled Anne Haney he held back a chortle as she raised her little head up to look at him, only to see a stomach where the head should have been. Flicking her tongue across her lips she adjusted her half-moon spectacles on the bridge of her little button nose before shakily continuing her gaze upwards until finally she was looking him in the eye. Good old-fashioned wartime manner stopped her commenting or so much as reacting to the sight of the giant who had walked in the previous evening and quietly booked a room, paid in cash and disappeared until this moment. At first glance she thought the reaper had finally come to tear her from her humble little life at the motel that her late husband had converted with his own hands from the stately farmhouse they had lived in for many, many years. He turned out to be one of the most polite guests she had received at the motel, he reminded her Lenny from Of Mice And Men, and so she daren't shake his hand. "Did you sleep well, son?" She asked. "Very well, thank you." He replied, maintaining his airs and graces in easy understanding that his presence probably unnerved her. "Where ya headed?" She continued, for fear of an awkward silence that would put her across as impolite. "San Diego, ma'am. I'm wrestling there in a few days." He only just finished the sentence before it hit him that such information was pointless when her hobbies probably stretched as far as cross-stitching and cooking up home-made broth. She cupped her hands together and managed an impressed look, then glanced at the door. "Tell you what son, I ain't no expert on wrestlin', don't care much for sport of any sort as it happens. Do you travel much?" She enquired. "I'm on the road most of the year actually; in our business you're lucky if you get a month off to yourself." He replied, shifting on his feet as the conversation began to drag. "Must get lonely, hm?" She implored, to which he nodded. "Must get pretty hungry alot of the time then?" Again he nodded, albeit confusedly but breathing a soft sigh through his nostrils as she looked behind her to a little white wooden door. "Yeah, the stuff they serve at those diners isn't fit for the dogs, ma'am." But as he finished she disappeared through the door and for a moment Von Kelsig wondered if she just gave up and left him to leave as he came. After a couple of minutes however she returned clutching a large wedge wrapped in tinfoil and a big bottle of water in both her arms, and held them out for him. "Best blueberry pie in a hundred miles son, should keep you going for a good while." She smiled quaintly at him, offering him the goods. "I, uh...I," Her generosity took him by surprise and he was speechless for a moment. "...I spent the last of my cash on the room, all I have left are my credit cards..." He swallowed awkwardly as a cackle escaped her throat and her smile parted to reveal small, craggy teeth spaced few and far between in her mouth. "No charge dear, just a gift from one person to another. You do accept gifts don’t you?" She cocked her head and nudged the pie forward. He couldn’t remember the last present he’d received, the closest thing to that was the endowment cheque for two hundred thousand dollars that Violence Jack had given him to help buy the office building back in Detroit that he had turned into the nightclub he should have been running right now, instead of discussing pie with Mother Hubbard on the way to another wrestling show. Thankfully he’d paid Bruce back before the unfortunate business that occurred leading him to being back in PRIME... "Uh, thank you. I really appreciate it." He snapped from thought after a moment and took the pie, shooting her a smile and waving goodbye as he left. Making his way across the gravel parking lot to his rental car something suddenly caused him to stop dead and cock his head in puzzlement. His left butt cheek was vibrating furiously. Reaching back he slipped his fingers into the back pocket of his Levis and produced a sleek black Samsung cell phone, a random number blinking on its face as the vibrations continued in time with the quiet ring tone that sounded classical, possibly Bach or Mozart. The phone continued to ring until finally he flipped the face open and placed it against his ear. "Hello?" He answered quietly. "Hello, Von Kelsig." A voice beamed through his ear, slightly too high-pitched for the man emitting it. At once he recognized it as belonging to the one person he never wanted to hear from again but knew he had to listen to, and obey. The bright sunny day suddenly turned dark and grey and any butterflies fluttering in his stomach from thinking about his match were turned to spiders and scorpions clawing their way out of his gut. He closed his eyes tight and wobbled on his legs momentarily before regaining his balance and listening to the voice on the other end of the line. "It’s your buddy Reuben here, you remember old Reuben don’tcha?" He paused for a moment waiting for Von Kelsig to respond, but knowing he wouldn’t he continued on anyway. "I was expecting a phone call from you last Wednesday and you left me hanging boy. Why the fuck didn’t you call me?" Reuben asked with a sinister sarcastic tone in his voice. Von Kelsig was lost for words, only answering when he realized the cell phone he was holding wasn’t his. "I...I was busy, I’m sorry. Whose phone is this?" His words were met with a twisted guffaw. "Haw haw, haw! It’s yours! I had one of my guys sneak it to you sometime in the last 24 hours. I knew you were thick in the head but I didn’t know you had an ass like a Rhino as well! Are you telling me you only just found that phone?" Reuben asked, his voice a little calmer. "Actually yeah, how did you get-..." "NEVER MIND THE HOWS AND WHYS YOU FUCKING INGRATE, WHY DIDN’T YOU CALL ME?!" Reuben snapped suddenly, cutting Von Kelsig off. Quickly taking a deep breath he continued. "We have an understanding Von Kelsig, you only need to call me once a week but you damn well NEED to CALL me ONCE a fucking WEEK and check in with me or I send some of my friends after you to bring your sorry ass back here and we can go over the routine again so it sinks in! Do you understand?" Reuben snarled down the line. "I understand. I wired the money on Thursday so you should have it already. Why do I need to check in if you already have the money?" Von Kelsig asked nervously, to which Reuben laughed. "You need to call me because for a start, that wasn’t enough money...and also because I want to know you’re trucking along with that wrestling promotion every week making me money!" He enunciated the word ‘money’, to which Von Kelsig winced. "Look I can’t do anything about the wages; you’ll get what you get until I climb a little higher on the billing and get a better bonus each night. That’s out of my hands, and I need at least some of it to get me by from show to show. I already apologized for the missed call, I’m not going anywhere that’s the reason I forgot to call...I was just too caught up with wrestling again. Despite the circumstances I'm glad to be back in the ring again." Von Kelsig replied smiled to himself, thinking again about his match with dear fondness. "Well you better fucking enjoy it. You still owe me big and the interest is building every day my friend. You better keep making the money because if one week goes by without a payment or a phone call then so help me-...well, just look at your hands if you need any reminder. I expect a call Wednesday night, don’t disappoint me. Goodbye." And with that Reuben hung up, leaving Von Kelsig shaken in the middle of the parking lot. He looked behind him to find the little old lady standing in the motel entrance, waving meekly at him and smiling. He smiled back and held up the pie in his other hand, nodding his head in her direction. Turning back he made his way to his car and fumbled with the phone, the pie and the car keys. Slumping into the driver seat he slammed the door shut and held the wheel in both hands, resting his forehead against the rubber for a moment. Sighing heavily he eventually looked up and released his grip of the wheel, turning his hands over and gazing at the hideous scars of puffy white skin devoid of any markings or prints on his fingertips. Suddenly he was in a daze and the nightmare came flooding back............ Three Years Previously The agony pulsed through his fingers as the little creatures bit hard into them, their razor-sharp teeth slicing through skin and bone but drawing no blood. There was a thick swarm of them moving over his hands like ants, each one about the size of a ping pong ball on legs. The ping pong balls themselves were little heads the spitting image of the man in the chequered shirt who Von Kelsig hadn't seen in a couple of days. Little heads on crab legs that stared at him screaming furiously in high-pitched little whines before munching into his fingertips and delivering a nerve shredding bite that made tears roll down his cheeks. He vomited down his chest again and gurgled helplessly trying to shake the creatures off to no avail. He had been asleep when the man in the chequered shirt had sent one of his accomplices into Von Kelsig's chamber with the syringe, filled with more than just heroin this time. The resulting hallucination left him reeling in his chair, sliding on the seat amidst a thick pool of urine and sick that smeared the silk cushion of his throne-like prison, not that he would have noticed a damn given he hadn't had more than a few minutes of artificial light in what seemed like a month. The smell and the wet mushy feeling between his legs was pushed from his mind in favour of an image of the man who was holding him here against his will, an image that manifested itself into the little demon crabs biting at his wounds. Had he been allowed the privilege of light he would have noticed as his captor's accomplice had that his burnt fingertips were fast becoming infected after the treatment they had received at the hands of the man in the chequered shirt and his wielding torch. She looked ghoulish, her skin the very shade of death and her eyes set back into her skull surrounded by shadowy black sockets. She was skeletal in form, her elbows pocked with track marks that looked like little crusty moon craters where she had been injecting. God only knew what the rest of her looked like underneath the loose-fitting cotton vest and black miniskirt, topped off with a pink fur wrapped around her neck for that added touch of elegance. Her hair stood mostly on end where it hadn't been pulled out by the man in the chequered shirt who swore he'd pull her apart if she so much as sniffed the syringe that was meant for Von Kelsig. She entered the room and flicked the light on, and seeing him sitting there with his head lolled to one side drooling as he slept she felt nothing more than surprise at the size of him, bound to the chair by a multitude of cable ties. Nothing else filled the room, just him in the chair stained from throat to ankle with all manner of bodily fluids. The coldness of the room excited her nipples through her top, and shivering she walked over to him and knelt down beside him, fingering the crook of his elbow with a browned and bony index finger, the nail chewed to the quick. Glancing at the syringe she caught herself suddenly staring at it like Gollum eyeing the Precious. Finding a vein she injected the concoction of drugs into Von Kelsig before she stole it for herself, and stood up to leave before her eyes met the melted flesh of his fingertips. The rotting stink of pork overwhelmed her and she realized she was so strung out she didn't even notice it choking her senses on the way in. She held a hand to her mouth and winced at the sight of the fingers on both Von Kelsig's hands which were covered in black flesh and large blisters, some of which had burst and oozed pus that dripped onto the floor at his feet. Even in her mangled state of mind she knew she should probably report this to the man in the chequered shirt, and as she turned out the light and left the room, closing the door tightly behind her Von Kelsig stirred in his sleep and heard the muffled sound of a female voice calling from the darkness. "Reuben! Reubeeen! Something's wrong with his hands!" The voice faded into silence after a moment and as he dropped back into a deep slumber Reuben had dragged her into his office and beat her about the head and body, roaring at her and punishing her for saying his name in the presence of the prisoner, and her shrill junkie cries filled the air though no one came to her aid. Now as the blisters continued to burst and ooze and the skin seared with a pain that was new and scary to him he kept his eyes shut tightly, whimpering and dribbling as the fix of heroin amplified the hallucinations and slowly drove him to the brink of madness. The worst part was in recent days he had felt almost as sick without the drug as he did with it, and he knew the man in the chequered shirt's plan was beginning to work. He hated the feeling of the drug filling his veins more and more every time, and his negative approach left his highs as incredible lows which only got worse as he came down and though it persisted he would not succumb to his captor and beg for his life back and the opportunity to repay this man. Suddenly the door opened and light filled the room, stinging his eyes as the clicking of heels tapped against the concrete floor. He tried desperately to clear his vision but the tears had blurred them and in the light they looked as sickly as the sores on his fingers, red and crusty with the added glaze bringing a raw sheen to them. All he could make out was a blob of dark colours that stretched and contorted with every flicker of his eyelids. "Awake this time are we?" Her voice echoed through his mind and at once all he could think of was the name of 'Reuben', reverberating in his head in her soft tone. He remembered her, even from his sleep. "I came in this morning and you were asleep. I told..." She trailed off for a moment remembering the beating she had gotten and took a quick breath. "...I told him about your hands and he told me to give you these. I got uh-..." Again she trailed off as she produced a paper bag, reaching in and bringing out a couple of smaller plastic bags with labels prescribed to the aforementioned 'Reuben'. "Flu...clocks...a-cillin....Flucoxacillin? That and some ampi...cillin and topical antibiotic lotion...now you gonna give me any trouble while I do this or what?" She asked softly. "No-...no, just do it. Please." He choked, gritting his teeth as the pain burned through his hands. She approached him and knelt in front of him as she had done before, keeping slightly back from the pool of fluid at his feet and placing the pills, gauze, dressing and lotion by her side. His hands were secured palms facing up, and she uncrewed the cap from the tube of lotion and squeezed it generously over the burnt skin of his fingertips on both hands until the tube was empty. He couldn't feel anything in the tips of his fingers but a sensation tickled the palms of his hands as she wrapped his fingers in gauze and wound bandages around them until they looked like they were wrapped in big white mittens. Satisfied with the job she popped three flucoxacillin and three ampicillin and waited for Von Kelsig to open his mouth. Complying weakly, she dropped the pills in and brought a bottle of water out of the bag at her feet, twisting the cap off and tilting it against his lips. He supped copiously, already having chewed up the pills and swallowed them and now taking his fill of precious water while it was being offered. He stopped while there was some left and took a breath. She took the bottle away and secured the cap again, thinking to herself. "Whatever reason you're here, just do whatever he asks you to. It's better that way, trust me. A lot of good people, tough people...they've been through the same thing and caved in. They left this place willing to do some pretty awful shit for him. Whatever he's got you here for just give it up and go while you're still sane." She murmured the last part under her breath. "......" He couldn't find words for a moment. "...Nothing he can do to me can be any worse than any one thing I've suffered in my life. Let him try." Von Kelsig replied, noticing the last couple of mouthfuls still sloshing around in the bottle in her bony, pale hands. "Pour it on my face please." He said, his voice regaining a little smoothness now that his throat was moist once again. She cocked her head as though it were some sordid sexual request, but seeing the state of his eyes shrugged and poured the remainder of the bottle over his face. Blinking rapidly he cleared his eyes and breathed deeply as the water seeped into his pores and ran down his face, refreshing him in that moment. As his vision became clear he looked up and saw the girl standing in front of him, then glanced at his hands. They were neatly wrapped up and though still stinging were less painful already than before. He almost managed a smile as he looked her in the eyes again and in those vacant grey orbs nestled in the bruised sunken sockets of a junkie whore's head he saw the spirit of the angel of mercy looking back at him. "Thank you." He whispered, smiling at her now. He saw past her cheap clothes, her emaciated frame and her horror doll hair and saw good in her, though to her he looked like a gurning madman with tears in his eyes straining to hold a grin. "Don't thank me, I'm just doing what I was told to is all. Just means I get a discount on my next bag." She slurred slightly, raising an eyebrow at him and picking up the left-over medical supplies off the floor, dropping them back into the bag. Turning to leave, her hand hovered over the light switch before his gruff voice called after her. "Hey, wait." He started, thinking only for a millisecond about what he was going to say. "What's your name?" He continued. She turned with a puzzled look on her face and studied him, looking him up and down as he sat there in such a vile condition. Cocking her head out the door to find the corridor empty she looked back at Von Kelsig, glancing away for a second before meeting his gaze once more. She hadn't been asked her name in so long, and even though something irked her about him she felt obliged given his sorry state. "Gloria. My name's Gloria." Darkness filled the room once more as she turned off the light and left. He sighed as though living a dream, he had a name...two names in fact. He felt cleansed by the water and the medicine, though his stomach turned as the drugs in his body began to mix and he knew he would feel worse for wear in a short time. For now though he closed his eyes and sighed again a sigh of peace as he thought of Gloria...thought of her watching him as he took the man named Reuben apart with his bare hands in the slowest, most painful way imaginable. While he sat back and fell into a hazy dream fuelled by drugs, he had no idea that he was no longer alone. Someone was standing behind him, had slipped into the room and crept to his back... ...he was no longer alone.
