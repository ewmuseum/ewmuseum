Make No Mistake, This Was More than Just a Favor

15 January 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01-15-08 "I didn't buy you the drink," Easton repeated. He pushed open the door and exited the bar, wrapping himself in the breeze. His hands found the pockets of his jeans. His rental wasn't too far away, but this place was packed solid with tourists and partiers. Friday nights were shit. When he felt the breath on the back of his neck he gritted his teeth. "I don't care if you were the one who did it," the woman replied. Her hand laid on his shoulder. Her hair was longer than his and bleached blonde and swam across her face. He kept walking and she kept pace. "Just leave me alone." The bags under Easton's eyes were full of these women, women attracted to fame, women who knew how lonely the traveling life was and how far away a man's wife was likely to be. Easton didn't bother showing her the ring again. "Why?" He could smell the smile on her, full of nicotine. Her other hand found his hip. "Not gay, are you?" He snorted. "Yeah. I'm gay. What of it?" She laughed and he hated her for it. He stood at an intersection and waited for the light to turn. She slid in front of him, slinky black dress and slim frame and late-twenties desperation. His hand curled around her forearm when she tried to touch his chest and he held her at bay. "Getting kinda hands-on, aren't you?" She tapped her teeth with her tongue. Her eyes were so bright. "Can't a guy just go out for a drink without having sex?" Easton asked. She did not stop smiling. His eyes caught the changing of the signs and he let her arm go. "You're not gonna run away from me, are you?" Easton shook his head as he circled around her. "Gonna walk." He stepped onto the crosswalk on the edge of the crush of people and he could feel her at his back, hands on his shoulder and elbow. It was too late for him to think about hitting anyone, much less a woman who wouldn't see it coming. "You can't just run away from everything," she said. The buildings on the other side of the street were like the bar and the bar's neighbors: brick façades, olive-green overhangs, numbers in a script-substitute font, glass doors badly streaked and rarely lighted from the inside. Everything smelled like sleep. Easton exhaled. "I'm walking." "Come on, I'm practically throwing myself at you." Her arm slipped into the crook of his elbow like it belonged there and she let her weight sink, dragging Easton down a step. Easton stepped up, snuck and arm behind to catch her by the hip. With a bounce of his shoulders, he lifted her off the ground. She whooped like a child and wrapped her legs around his waist. For a while he carried her in silence. She stole kisses at his neck and ear and he cooperated, twisting his neck just enough to fake a struggle. She spat out his hair with quiet smiles. The crowd was thinner ahead of them. Her teeth closed around his earlobe, tugging but not reining, fingers pressing against his chest but not digging. He held her up, but he did not clutch her. Empty spaces outnumbered the cars in the parking lot four to one, but Easton's red rental was still in tact. She unlaced her legs from his waist and let her feet drip to the pavement, taking two choppy steps before letting her arms unwind from him. She tucked her hair behind her ear. Easton dug his keys out of his pocket and they jangled as they sprayed the moonlight. "Going back to your hotel?" she asked. Her hand was ready on the handle of the car. Easton shook his head and opened his door. "Hotel's shitty, no good," Easton said. He tossed her a look. "Where's your place?" She smiled and gave her hips a little shake and sank into the car just after he did. The doors clunked shut one after the other. "I'll show you," she said. "South, just out of town." Easton started the car and let it quake all around him before pulling out of the parking lot. She was reclining. She was putting too much effort into being innocuous, dragging up her skirt with a fingernail. Easton was driving. She drawled and leaned over when she gave him directions, letting him smell her smoke and perfume, both expensive. He could taste her screwdriver in the air. She kneaded his thigh, inching inward. Easton did his best to focus because he knew he needed to. She laughed through pressed lips and brushed the back of her hand against his crotch. She flipped down the visor on her side to look at the mirror. Lipstick appeared from her small black bag and she set to work. Easton brought the car to a stop in front of a high-rise, the maroon canopy of the place inviting them into the yellow light inside. She looked at him and lifted an eyebrow. He looked at her. "Get out of the car." Her eyes were more than attentive and she leaned for him. "Come on!" He met her hand with his in mid-air as she grabbed for his shoulder, but he prevented her from sliding her fingers between his. "Out of the car." She watched him. She sank back and let her hands rest on the bag in her lap. He pitied her a little. "What do I tell Justin?" she asked. Easton shrugged, both hands on the wheel again. "Tell him... tell him I tried to fuck you and I couldn't get it up." She laughed a little, lips shut. Her fingers clenched a little bit. "I'll tell him you cried," she said. Easton gave her a smile through the thick of his beard. "Make me sound like a bitch." She opened the door and put a foot out. "Like you aren't?" She waggled her fingers at him and slinked out of the car, slamming it shut behind her. Her gait was long, slow, hopeful that he would reconsider leaving her in this state. Easton put his foot on the gas. These apologies were tiring. Justin's sister was more than good-looking, but Justin had slept with Easton's wife. He was a friend. Easton could not forgive him.
