Burden In My Hand

13 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 13th Nov. “Man, turn this fucking shit off.” The young adult in the grey parka wasn’t pleased. Face rife with discontent he turned towards his cohort in the driver’s seat. “Giving me a headache. Fucking rap bullshit.” Without waiting for approval he reached for the stereo, only to have his hand swatted away by his burly accomplice. “Fuck off, Jay. This here’s the real shit.” The look in Cordell Junior’s eyes told Jay not to bother furthering his complaint; there really was no arguing with him in situations like this. Displeased, he folded his arms across his chest and slumped down low in the leather seat. A twenty-something with the complexion of a sixteen year old, Jay’s acne-ravaged face hardly made for the prettiest sight in the world, so it was perhaps wise that he chest to dig his chin into his chest, the over-sized collar of his parka concealing much of his blemished cheeks. He sat there, staring dead ahead through the windscreen, as some rapper he couldn’t remember the name of spat rhymes over a generic East Coast beat. Noticing his friend’s less-than-cheery demeanour, Cordell Junior scowled. “Fuck’s wrong with you anyway, dicksmack? Sand in your vagina again? Been sitting there with a face like hell all night.” “Why do you think, man,” Jay began. “How long’ve we been waiting; three, maybe four hours? The music’s awful, it’s fucking freezing out there, and we don’t even know if this guy’s gonna show. Shit, and to think I turned down a night at The Vice for this.” Shrugging, Cordell Junior was almost completely indifferent to protests he’d heard a thousand times before. Jay always got this, especially on the slightly longer, more drawn-out jobs. Junior couldn’t understand it; he knew the nature of the job from the moment he agreed to take it, and yet he still persisted in whining and complaining. Every damn time. Junior thought it best not to humour him. In a contrast to the disinterested slouch of his accomplice, he sat leaning forward, casting his eyes across the streetlight-illuminated urban landscape before him. He was not a bad looking kid, Cordell Junior. Certainly unlike his Father and, indeed, his slightly grumpy partner-in-crime. His brow was heavy, casting a slight shadow under his olive green eyes, giving him a natural “stay out of my way” look. Short, perfectly-trimmed black hair was combed back with the aide of some classic-look hair gel, and he dressed smartly in a long, black leather jacket and dark dress pants. “Man, where is this grubby fuck,” chimed Jay again. “Longer he makes me wait, the more I wanna hurt the prick.” “Take it easy there, partner. The plain ain’t to put him in hospital or anything. You know that,” explained Junior in the gruff, deep voice he’d inherited from his father. “We wait ‘til he turns up, we mess him up a little bit, then we get the fuck outta there. Simple. Dad wants us to ‘send a message,’ nothing more.” “Right. ‘Send a message’.” Jay rolled his eyes. “S’gotta be some pretty serious beef, though. Not every day we get told to rough up a guy who kicks the shit outta people for a living.” “Haven’t got a clue. You know how it is, disclosing details and all,” Junior began but his words seemed to meander off into nothingness as something outside the apartment block in front of their Hummer perked his interest. Tensing up, tightening his grip on the steering wheel, Cordell Junior leaned forward, squinting in the poor light. “What? What the fuck?” Jay quizzed, suddenly pulled his slouched from upwards. He glanced once at the scene outside and once at Cordell. “Junior?” “That’s him, man. Right there.” Junior thrust his finger forwards, pointing through the windshield. Outside, under a streetlight, a rough, dishevelled figure emerged from a traditional New York yellow taxi cab. Reaching in and pulling a bag from the backseat, the man slammed the door behind him and looked around the street carefully. “You sure? How can you tell from here?” Jay was unconvinced. “Get your fucking eyes tested, man,” Junior spat, his hand already on the door-handle. “That’s definitely him. Look!” Jay carefully examined the scene. The figure turned from the curb and disappeared into the apartment block. “I dunno, he looks smaller than that on TV.” “Come on!” urged Junior, foot out the door. “Let’s just get this over with.” * * * * * Humming the rhythm of a Suicidal Tendencies song he couldn’t recall the title of, Jason leaned over and picked his bag from the floor as the antiquated elevator came to an abrupt halt with a shudder, almost causing him to lose his footing in the process. He’d been up the lift thousands of times before, and yet he’d never quite gotten used to that little jerk at the end. Squeaking and creaking, the doors slid apart. Jason walked into the hallway, fatigued and almost zombie-like in his movements. It had been a long day and his body was just about ready to give up. The rigours of travelling between his temporary Seattle base, his New York City home and wherever PRIME was broadcasting from every week was slowly beginning to take its toll on his body. Not that he’d complain about it, of course; the last thing he wanted to sound like was a whinging crybaby like half the guys he’d shared a locker-room with. Just part of the job. He could accept that. He reached his door, number four hundred and six. Trying hard not to pass out there and then, Jason stuffed his hand into the pocket of his well-worn jeans and pulled out the keys. Without even looking he forced one key into the lock, but it wouldn’t budge. “Damnit,” he mumbled, pulling the key out and trying another. This time the key tweaked and shuddered in the lock mechanism, but he managed to get it open. The air in the apartment was cold and unwelcoming and the faint whiff of a trash can that needed emptied hung in the air like an unwanted guest, but Jason was relieved. His senses had all but shutdown on the flight over from the West Coast and a recharging of his batteries was definitely on the menu. Knowing where to throw his hand in order to hit the light switch, Jason managed to fully illuminate the room. The abode was modest; hardly what you’d expect a member one of the most star-studded talent rosters on the planet to call home, but it suited his needs. Small and compact, there simply wasn’t enough room to make much of a mess so it was easy enough to maintain, and he’d added enough trinkets and new toys to the interior to transform what was once an unpleasant, rundown pit into a clean, bearable home. Jason never saw the point in those fancy mansions anyway. What would be the point in spending a ridiculous sum of money on a home he’d rarely spend more than three days a week in? He often thought of the buildings his more-decorated cohorts called home and found great amusement in the pointlessness of it all. Fools. Tired and ready for bed, Jason dropped his bag to the floor. He kicked his leg backwards, trying to close the door with his heel, and continued his slow shambling towards his bedroom. But something wasn’t right. Instead of swinging into the frame with a satisfying slam, the door seemed to come to a halt with a soft, dull thud. Figuring that he must’ve dropped something in the frame, Jason cursed under his breath and turned around. There was indeed something stopping the door from closing properly, but it wasn’t something he’d dropped. It was a foot. Before he even had a chance to examine things further the door had swung open. A tall, burly kid stood in the frame, a steel baseball bat slung over the shoulder of his leather jacket and a look of coercion spread on his features. “Not so fast.” He lunged forward, swinging the bat wildly. It swung through the air with a swift swoosh, but, groggy as he may be, Jason managed to lean back, dodging the strike. Jason barely had a moment to gather his thoughts before the bat was swung at his skull again. This time he ducked and the swing sailed harmlessly over his skull. Then, in the doorframe. Another. Brass knuckles clenched tightly around his right hand, the parka-clad, pizza-faced young man charged into the room after his sidekick and slammed the door closed. Gotta move, think fast, Jason thought to himself as he backed off. He didn’t have a chance to ponder why these guys were so intent on bringing him down; he was outnumbered, he had to stick and move. The kid with the bat swung the thing like a lunatic. His blows were strong, hard, as if he had previous experience with a bat, but there was no precision whatsoever. Still, that didn’t make him any less dangerous. Jason stepped backwards into the kitchenette, but the other guy came around the other side, cornering him. It was then that PRIME’s Anti-Superstar got his first good look at his aggressors. There was something recognizable about the guy with the bat. That haircut. That heavy brow. That crystal-clear complexion. “Got’cha now, fucker.” That voice. It was all so familiar now. But the bastards were closing in on him like a couple of hyenas. No time for contemplation. The bat was swung and again Jason ducked. This time it crashed into the side of the microwave on the downswing, cracking the cheap, white plastic in the process. There. A moment. A chance. He left himself open for just a fraction of a second too long. Didn’t matter. It was enough. Jason lifted his forward, preventing the kid from swinging the bat again. Balling a fist, he drove his knuckles into the familiar-looking kid’s pearly whites, doubtlessly dislodging a couple in the process. He’d hit him with such force that the guy fell backwards, flat on his ass. Sonny Silver had taught him well. Judgement clouded by the sight of Natas putting a hurt on his buddy, the kid with the parka and the dumb-looking beanie hat charged at Natas, but Jason saw him coming from a mile off. The feeling of the brass knuckles being driven into his arm muscle was painful, but not nearly as painful as it would have been had the kid hit him in the face. Advancing, Jason punched his assailant in the stomach, winding him. That wasn’t enough, so he thrust his knee upwards into the kid’s ribs and drove an elbow into the back of his skull. Ol’ familiar was back to his feet but rubbing his jaw, pained. Jason had just enough time to come to his sense. Enough time to fully establish the identity of his attacker. “… Junior?” A face he hadn’t seen for almost three years snarled furiously at him, a trickle of blood seeping from his busted lower lip. “Fucking die you prick!” wailed Cordell Junior, diving at Natas. The kid was outmatched, completely out of his league, and Jason telegraphed the move before striking Junior viciously on the side of the head. His eyes rolled back, dazed, but Jason showed no remorse. He grabbed him by the throat and pushed him up against the worktop. Jason wanted to grill him, wanted to ask him what the hell was going on and what he was doing here, but he was consumed by the primal urge to knock the numbskull’s brains out. He pulled his fist back, ready to strike. Cordell Junior was breathing heavily and irregularly, almost snivelling. One more shot to the head and he’d be down for the count… “YOU FUCKING HOLD IT THERE, ASSHOLE!” A split-second before Jason was going to bash Cordell Junior’s skull in, the dirty-looking kid screamed behind him with such vehemence that his voice cracked. A familiar metallic click rung in Jason’s ears. Immediately he knew the score. There was a gun pointed at his head. He released his grip on Junior and slowly turned around. The kid in the parka was shaking, sweat pouring profusely from every pore on his body. His aim was unsteady and the look in his eyes certainly wasn’t that of a natural born killer. Jason refused to put his hands in the air. He knew how to handle these situations. “You move and I swear I’ll blow your fucking head off!” the kid wailed. Was he going to do it? He was reckless, no doubt, but did he have it in him to end the life of a fellow man? Jason took the risk. He stepped forward. “I said FREEZE!” The trembling intensified. Jason thought that the kid might just burst into tears at any second. Keeping a cool head and refusing to concede any weakness to a jumped-up punk who was clearly out of his depth, Jason furrowed his brows, his gaze cold and steely. “Think it’d be a good idea if ya put the cannon down, kid. Don’t want this ta get ugly, do ya?” “Shut the fuc-“ Now. He never saw it coming. Jason burst forward, grabbing the guy’s arm, trying to wrestle the shooter from his paws. The kid’s frame may have been skinny and wiry, but the grip he had on the pistol was tight, almost vice-like. Jason struggled with him, forcing the gun downwards. “HOLY SHIT! JAY!” Junior suddenly piped up, inadvertently spitting blood between his lips in the process. “WATCH IT!” Using his weight to his advantage, Jason backed Jay into the wall. “AHHHH!” the boy wailed as his spine was driven awkwardly into the corner. His fingers were sweaty and his grip loosened slightly. Jason managed to dislodge one finger, then another. Thrusting his shoulder into that of Jay, he drove the boy into the wall. BANG! A bullet escaped the chamber, passing through flesh and bone. “AHHHHHHHHHHHHHHHH!” The scream was almost blood-curdling. “FUCKFUCKFUCK! SHIT! AHHHHHHH!” * * * * * “So. Figured it’s about time you boys told me what the fuck’s goin’ on.” Jason Natas took a long, cathartic drag on the freshly-lit Marlboro Red and cocked his head back. His knuckles were skinned and his body was rampant with all kinds of small aches and pains, but, as usual, the nicotine took the edge away. “After all, ain’t every day a guy comes home to find a couple ‘a bat-wieldin’ fuckwits waitin’ for him…” Seated on the floor, his thick, tree-trunk legs stretched out before him, Jason was comfortable. He cocked his head to the side, dirty blonde hair rubbing against the brilliant white wall behind him. Tapping his fingers against the side of the pistol he did not shift his gaze away from Ray and Junior for a second. What a sorry state they were in. Both tied and bound to the radiator, neither were going anywhere. Crimson ran from the side of Cordell Junior’s mouth and hair that was previously perfect was now tousled beyond all recognition, but at least he hadn’t been shot in the foot. Blood poured liberally from the deep rouge hole in Jay’s shoe, seeping into the cream carpet below. The kid was breathing heavily, panting almost. He wasn’t in a good way; the blood loss was beginning to go to his head, and the fact that Jason had tied his arms behind his back wasn’t helping much. “Please, Jason! This wasn’t our idea!” Cordell Junior broke the silence. Jason snapped his in Junior’s direction. “Jus-… just don’t hurt us anymore! Please!” Jason nodded slowly. “All depends on how easy you wanna make this, Junior.” Again he tapped his fingers against the gun. Cordell saw this and panicked. “I’ll tell you whatever you want! Anything! Anything at all!” “Well, seein’ as this seemed t’go right over yer head a moment ago, I’ll repeat myself; jus’ what the fuck are you boys doin’, waitin’ for me t’come home, huh?” Now Jason grabbed the gun, wrapping it gently against the carpet. “Dad sent us! To-… to hurt you.” Cordell spoke fast, he was tripping over his words. “Yer Dad sent ya? Well no fuckin’ shit, Sherlock,” an unsurprised and uninspired Natas responded. “Knew that much, Junior. Jus’ wanna know what yer here for. Haven’t done ‘business’ with yer old man for quite some time now, but obviously he ain’t doin’ so good, havin’ ta send his own son out onto the frontline…” “We don’t know a thing, honestly! We’re told what to do and who to do it to, that’s it. No specifics.” Jason eyeballed the perspiring Junior. He pulled the cannon up, examining it carefully under the light. He looked up momentarily. “Sure about that, kid? The fat fuck sends you two round to fuck with me, seemingly out of the blue? Ain’t sure I can get my head around that…” “I swear! That’s all we know!” Junior was getting desperate now, Jason could tell from his voice. “Nobody tells us anything! We do the job and we get paid for it, no questions asked!” “’Cept it ain’t gone too well fer you boys this time ‘round, has it?” Jason asked rhetorically. “If he’s tryin’ ta drag me back into his lil’ web, then it ain’t gonna happen, kid. An’ I want you to tell that to yer old man yerself, alright? Word for word. I ain’t interested in what he’s got no more, so tell him to back off, ‘cause I ain’t one to be fucked with. Got it?” Frantically Junior nodded. Jason wasn’t too sure of his own words himself, but he spoke with enough conviction to put the fear of God into the kid. Cordell Abraham was, after all, one of the most powerful criminals in the city. Jason knew him well from his time throwing down in several of Abraham’s shady underground fighting clubs and he knew that he was nothing to trifle with. There’d be ramifications, he understood that. He’d injured the man’s son, and that wasn’t something that a man like Cordell Abraham would take lightly. But Jason stuck that thought in the back of his mind, there were more pressing issues at hand. For the life of him he could not figure out why Abraham had decided to strike. Since walking out on the podgy “businessman”’s blood-for-money fighting circle a couple of years back he’d had no contact with Abraham of his legion of goons whatsoever. It puzzled him greatly. He knew Cordell Abraham to be a man devoid of almost all honour, but from what he could recall he’d done nothing to incite the ire of the man. They’d parted on relatively amicable terms. Well, about as “amicable” as two of the most volatile personalities in the city can get. But one thing was for sure, the kid wasn’t going to give him any answers. Junior was scared for his safety, for his life. He wasn’t lying; he really knew nothing. A pained groan escaped Jay’s lips. The shaggy-haired kid closed his eyes as Jason turned his attention towards him, glancing down at the bodily fluid seeping from the hole in his foot. “Awwww hell, kid. Ain’t even gon’ get that stain out the carpet. Be sendin’ you the bil-“ Suddenly, a ringing sound. Polyphonic and loud, it was coming from Junior. Jason looked at him. “Fuck’s that?” “It’s my cell,” Cordell Junior awkwardly responded. Jason pushed himself up from the floor and stood above the kid, pointing downwards with the pistol. “Which pocket?” “Right.” Slowly Jason knelt down, placing the shooter on his knee. He pulled the flashing phone from the kid’s pocket, the word “DAD” blinking back at him in block capital letters. Quickly he decided what to do. “Alright, kid. I’m gonna put this on speaker phone, but you fuckin’ do what I say. No funny business or I’ll put a lump ‘a lead in ya, alright?” Nodding nervously, Junior trembled as Jason answered the call on speakerphone and dropped the device on the kid’s lap. “Son? Are you there?” It was the first time that Jason had heard Cordell Abraham’s craggy tones in what seemed like an age and the sound of it brought a cavalcade of unpleasant memories flooding back. Brawling in a dingy boxing club basement, reducing a guy’s face to mush, picking up another measly pay packet for risking life, limb and personal safety. All things he’d rather have left forgotten. “Yes pop,” Junior replied, doing his best to sound calm and composed. Skipping all the niceties, Cordell soon answered back. “Is it done?” Hesitating, Junior glanced up at Jason who nodded his head once. “It’s done.” “Excellent.” Click. The line was dead. Cocking his head back, Junior breathed a sigh of relief. Jason snorted. “Heh. Guess the old man think I’m fish food.” “Oh, no. We didn’t come here to kill you, just rough you up a little bit.” Junior’s gaze shifted to his accomplice. Jay wasn’t doing so well; he wasn’t so much breathing as heaving for oxygen and his eyes were clamped shut. “Look, you gotta let him go at least, man! He needs medical help! You gotta get him to a hospital!” Once again Jason rose to his feet, the gun still in his hand. Pondering his options, he looked down at the parka-wearing kid who’d tried to jump him in the kitchen. He could let the kid go. Send him away in an ambulance. But then again… “Fuck that. Should’ve thought about the consequences b’fore ya got involved, kiddo.” The look that Junior gave Jason was cold; one of pure disdain but Jason did not flinch. He had no remorse for these kids, no mercy. Sure, they were just carrying out orders, but they’d invaded his personal space and tried to injure him. He was far too bull-headed to let that stand. Everything else aside, one question still remained. Why? At the moment, it didn’t matter. Before he could seriously begin to figure out why Cordell Abraham was seemingly after his blood, he needed to figure out what to do with the two broken, beaten and scarred kids he had strapped to his radiator. * * * * * Brrrt. Brrrt. Brrrt. The cellphone buzzed and not quietly. Hopping up and down in rapid, miniscule movements, it vibrated around the top of the bedside table, almost falling off in the process. Brrrt. Brrrt. Brrrt. Still the sound of plastic on wood reverberated around the darkened bedroom. It caused him to stir, that persistent, unending hum. His head rolled and lolled on the pillow as he came to his senses and a tired sigh left his lips while his waking mind struggled to decipher the situation. Brrrt. Brrrt. Brrrt. Again it buzzed. Consciousness was coming back to him. He pulled his fists up from under the duvet and rubbed them into his eyes. Finally he was awake. Flicking a wayward lock of blonde hair from his eyes he reached across, grabbing the phone and pulling to close to his ear. “Yeah?” he croaked in the only voice he could muster. He stared upwards, admiring the deep, navy blue hues that dominated the moonlight room. “Mr. Loftman?” questioned Cordell Abraham on the end of the line. The signal was fuzzy and kind of grainy and the voice was rough, coarse, and tinged with an unnatural Italian-American accent suggesting that the man had watched far too many episodes of The Sopranos for his own good. “The situation has been taken care of,” Abraham stated matter-of-factly, his diction as smooth as an unlubricated lawnmower. He didn’t even wait for an answer. “Good,” mumbled Mr. Loftman. Already his eyelids were getting heavy. “We shall be in touch when we feel it necessary to make use of your…” he paused. “…’services’. A man of your standing and swing, I’m sure we can forge some kind of mutually beneficial agreement.” Now Cordell Abraham was hissing like a snake, his gruff tones full of the guile of a man up to no good. Loftman, however, was far too tired to notice. He’d been asleep for two hours prior and holding a conversation with one of New York City’s shadiest characters was the last thing on his agenda. “Good,” was all that he could muster. Hanging up, the overpaid businessman replaced the phone on the bedside table and pulled the covers back over his torso. “Nathaniel?” a soft, feminine voice floated across the bed. Fuck. Loftman prayed that she hadn’t picked up on a conversation that would surely garner a lot of questions. “What was that all about?” she almost whispered. In his head he breathed a sigh of relief. “Nothing, honey. Just business. Got back to sleep, huh?” Taking his word for it, Cynthia rolled over, draping a skinny arm over her husband’s chest and closing her eyes. He pulled his arm from beneath the duvet and placed it round to back of his partner’s head. Warm comfort began to rise from Nathaniel Loftman’s stomach, safe in the knowledge that somewhere on the other side of the city, Jason Natas was in pieces. The poker loss, the damage that PRIME’s Anti-Superstar had done to his reputation, the insults, the backchat. All of it was avenged, at last. At least that’s what he thought.
