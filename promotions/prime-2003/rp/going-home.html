Going Home

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: ReVolution 191 "Come on Dad, why don't you just go ahead and tell me how horrible of a son I am!" David's voice pierced through the awkward silence that had permeated for the past few moments. Shawn looked at his son, adopted and all, and felt great pity as he did so. "I've never said that you're a horrible son, David. Never. So let's not start putting words in each other's mouths, understand?" he asked David who flipped his hand in a non-caring way. "Fine, you didn't say it, but you sure implied it. I came home last night, hoping to make amends for my sins, and instead of embracing me you decided to shun me. I haven't seen my mother or sisters in years, Dad, years! No matter how much you paint me to be such a horrible person, you have to shoulder some of that responsibility," David responded to his father with an icy and accusatory tone and Shawn simply shook his head. "Me? You can't be serious," he began in a soft, angry tone. "You were the one who hated being confined in the house. You couldn't wait to leave and "experience" the world as you so put it. I tried to give you a life, stability, and all you did was spit those niceties back in my face. You’re the sole reason that you didn’t see your mom or your sisters these past few years." David scoffed at such a notion as he turned his shoulder to his dad. "That's because I never felt welcomed. Plain and simple, there you go. I felt like a stranger in my own house. I knew I was the adopted kid. You never hid that contempt in your eyes, that you could never have a son that shared your own genes. I was never 'yours' and you couldn't stand such a thought," David laid it out for his father. Shawn walked around to look at his son in the eyes. "David, you're wrong. You honestly couldn’t be more wrong if you tried. I love you, still do, and always will. I tried to get through to you because at the end of the day, I wanted you to be a man. I wanted you to feel more than just a kid who was rejected by his own parents," Shawn pleaded with him, but the armor around David was too thick to penetrate. "You think you can just do that by showing me tough love? Did you really think that I would become a man and not feel rejected if you tried to make me tougher? I was put up for adoption, Dad! My own parents didn't want me anymore! How much more of a man could I possibly be?! I had to live with that reality and still do. There's not a night that goes by that I don't ask myself what's wrong with me. I have to look at myself in the mirror and feel this heavy burden on my shoulders that I'm just not good enough," David said to Shawn. Shawn sighed at hearing those words as they came out of his son's mouth. "For the life of me, David, I can't put myself in your shoes. I can't imagine what you're going through, but son, if you go through your entire life feeling that way then you're never going to be happy. That's why you left here. That's why you left wrestling school. That's why you've come back home and just left everything behind in New York City. You're looking for validity that you matter in this world, and when you don’t find it, you flee," Shawn replied in a very aggressive tone as his words were rather pointed. David glared at his father, the pool behind as clear as could be unlike the feeling David felt. "Thanks, Dad, I forgot that you were a psychologist on the side," David responded sarcastically and Shawn saw that David was shutting down. "David, at what point in your life are you going to grow up? Right now, you're holding onto these childish ideas that you're still going to be accepted by your biological parents. I hate to break it to you, but they don't love you son. They're not your family, we are. We're the ones that love you at the end of the day," Shawn told his son, his words heavier than he had intended them to be. David's glare intensified as he moved closer and closer to his father. "Oh, so you love me? Is that why you're kicking me out of your house? Refusing to let me find my place in this world? Explain to me how someone who claims to love me could possibly do what you're doing!" he yelled back at Shawn as a fury in his chest began to overcome him. "David, you weren't here. Your sisters, they couldn't begin to cope or understand what was happening with you. Your mother, she broke down. She had spent so much time loving you, caring for you, and raising you that when she saw you walk out the door without a care or consideration in the world for what you were doing to her. It nearly killed her, David. She had to go to the hospital--," Shawn began to explain to David before he was cut off by a stiff hand in his face. "Don't you dare put that on me. That's just not fair," David interjected in a rather dark tone. Shawn's eyes turned dark as he looked at David. "You're so clueless. Do you know what would happen to your mother if you just left again like you did last time David? She would die!" Shawn yelled back at his son as the anger was starting to get to him as well. David just shook his head as he felt the contempt in his body. "Don't, Dad, just don't! She wouldn't die!" David screamed back, this time the veins in his neck strained as well. "She has MS! The emotional effect on her alone would cause her entire body to lock up, for her mind to cease to work, and she would literally die. Her mind can't protect her like it could before, David! Open your damn eyes!" Shawn screamed back and this time his words landed a brutal shot to David. David took a step back in complete shock and felt the vibes as they radiated from his father; Shawn had just made a vital mistake. "They don't know, do they?” David asked him pointedly. Shawn offered no response though. “No one else knew until now, didn't they?" David questioned Shawn once again and this time he simply nodded his head in agreement. David tried to take a deep breath in, but it hurt too much as a sharp pain assaulted him as he tried to do so. Shawn tried to wrap his arms around his son, but David pushed him back. "Don't you even think about it. You just blamed me for Mom's condition and now you want to put her eventual death on my hands. What kind of father do you think you are? No, don't even think about it. I'll just tell you. You're a shitty one. You're scum. I can't believe you would even do anything like that," David said to his father, and it was clear that his words offended his father to the point that the pain was etched into his face. David began to walk away from Shawn as his father desperately needed him. "David, please," Shawn began, but David spun around and glared at his father with dagger-like eyes. "Don't you even dare! Don't you even think of me as your son! I'm done with you. Trying to blame me?! What kind of man does that Dad? Not even my biological father would scoop to your levels and you should be woefully ashamed of yourself," David said as his words had difficulty coming out. David wanted to say more, but the tightness in his chest was unbearable by this point. He looked at his father, whose hands were on his chest, and David could see the emotional pain that he was experiencing. David turned his back on his father as his eyes landed upon the wooden gate that would allow David to exit without having to look at his family. As he reached the door though, he heard an odd noise that made him stop dead in his tracks. SPLASH! As David turned back around to look at his father, he found that Shawn wasn't there any longer. Ripples in the water gave it away as the door from the kitchen to the backyard frantically opened and Samantha ran out of it followed by her mother. David looked over at them before he could see the horror in their eyes. David exploded from his stance before he leaped into the air and dived into the pool. At the bottom of it was his father, eyes closed, and not showing a single sign of life. David quickly put his hands on his father and began to drag him towards the air. His father was heavy though and David felt the pressure of the water and his father's weight on top of his arms. He struggled mightily before he finally managed to drag his father out of the water and into the crisp air. He felt frigidness of the water against his skin and ignored it as he put his father down onto the stone ground and began pumping his chest frantically. David's eyes moved towards Samantha and he could see the fear in her body. "Call 911, Sam, go!" David yelled at her, but it didn't register with Samantha. "Sam!" This time, David's voice managed to get through to Samantha who bolted back into the house to get the phone. David looked back at his father and anxiety began to settle into David's chest, as he desperately tried to resuscitate his father. "Come on Dad, come on!" *** Rebecca looked at the wooden table with great sadness in her eyes. David's face looked sullen, beaten, and defeated as his arms were folded against his chest and he leaned back in his chair. For months, the reality of that day had just lingered on the edge of his mind and now that he had let it off his chest he was wrought with guilt. David felt like a terrible human being in that very moment. "So, you know," she spoke in a hushed tone and referred to her illness. David didn't know how to really respond to her remark. "Um... yeah," he responded in a matched voiced. "They still don't know, do they?" she asked him and he simply shook his head. "Thank you. Thank you for not telling them about it, and thank you for being brutally honest with me as to what happened that morning. It fills... this empty hole in my heart." Yet, he couldn't find the words to truly describe how he was feeling in this moment as his eyes shyly looked at his mother. He began to open his mouth, to rebuke her sincerities, but she spoke before he had the chance to. “I don’t blame you for his death, David. I truthfully don’t. That moment was bound to happen eventually. Your father, he died of a heart attack and that was something none of us could have caused,” she assuaged him. Still, that guilt remained with him, and he knew it would for a long time. Rebecca’s eyes looked around the room before she sighed. “I came here for a chance to clear my mind, yes. But, I also came here because there are a few doctor’s doing work here to help slow down the effect that MS has on the brain. This was my chance to see if I could have what would equate to a second chance at life,” she confided in him and he looked at her with curiosity in his eyes. “And? Did it?” he asked her and hoped for a positive response. She took a moment to answer his question. “No.” The heaviness in his heart remained. “So, now what?” he asked her and David could tell that wasn’t a question she was quite ready to answer yet. However, as the question hung in front of her like a tee ball on a stand, Rebecca knew that the answer was the most simplistic of the options before her. “Time to go home,” she said and joy slipped into her voice. David smiled at her before he nodded his head in agreement. David then rose from his seat and placed his hands on top of his mother’s. “If that’s what you want, Mom, then I’ll make the arrangements. We’ll be on a flight home tomorrow morning,” he informed her and she smiled at him. The warm thoughts of being able to see her daughters filled her soul with happiness, something she hadn’t felt in quite some time. “Thank you, David,” Rebecca replied and he shook his head. David didn’t need to say anything else. He had to make things right once and for all, and this was a step in the right direction. David turned away from his mother and began to walk towards the exit, but as his hand wrapped around the metal doorknob Rebecca stopped him. “David, I love you,” she said with genuine sincerity. David looked over his shoulder and smiled at her before he opened the door and walked out, which left her to be by herself for one more evening. *** The black, leather bag remained open and empty, void of the objects that should have filled it by this moment. Three hours had passed and David sat on the edge of his bed and just stared at it. That bag was a stark contrast to his mind. His hands rested on his knees as he continued to stare at it with no end in sight. A half-filled glass of alcohol sat next to him on the bed. He desperately wanted to be happy; not just in this moment though, but throughout his entire life. Yet, it had always eluded him, and now more so than any other time he felt it as it tore viciously at his heart. Even though he sensed the true way he should have felt, it remained a large mystery to him that not even Sherlock Holmes could have begun to solve. The cold edge of the glass met his lips a moment later and he felt the vacant taste of the alcohol as it hit his lips and slithered down his throat. Even that didn’t break him out of his daze though as he stood up and walked to his nightstand. On it was his iPhone and he picked it up before he began to look through a few of the contacts. As he found the person he intended to dial, David hit a button on his phone, and placed it to his ear. It rang for a moment before the person on the other end answered it. “Hello?” the woman answered with a sleepy hesitance in her voice. David glanced at the clock and saw that it was half past midnight at this point. “Hey, sorry to bother you,” he answered honestly. “No, don’t worry about it. What’s wrong?” she asked him without possibly knowing how loaded of a question it truly was. “Just having difficulty sleeping, Mary-Lynn. I was wondering if you could come over and we could, I don’t know, talk,” he responded to her and he could hear the gears as they worked in her mind. “Yeah, I’ll be right over,” his tag-team partner responded to him with an eagerness to her tone. He smiled as she agreed and proceeded to give her the directions to his house. As he hung up the phone though, the happiness that he had felt for that brief moment disappeared in the blink of an eye. His hand wrapped around the cold glass again and David finished it off as he sat down on his bed. Now, he had to just wait for Mary-Lynn Mayweather to snap him out of his funk. If she could.
