Date: earlier Opinions are like hard-on’s—boners, if you will—in that whenever someone has one, they always get the need to show it off; no matter how pathetic or ridiculous it is. And just like a boner, you can kindly get the fuck away from me with that shit. I don’t want to see it, hear it, and I sure as hell am not going to touch it you flaming homogay. It doesn’t matter to me if you think my erratic ideals are sending a bad message to society. It’s my fucking message and it can be perceived as a piece of art, if you ask me. You’ve got a problem, send the FCC a letter. We here at pro-wrestling are just begging to get the axe. Actually, I bet PRIME and the Playboy Channel would go unscathed in the mess, but it’d be funny to see people try and hit us. You think telling the world that “I’ve fucked bitches named Rita and Katrina who have put up bigger fights than you,” is a good way to get fans, Adam Dick? Of fucking course not, you idiot, but at least I’ve got enemies now. Because whether you agree with me or not; whether you think the shit I say is funny or offensive, you’re still sitting here, spitting in my ear about how you think I could tone it down a notch. The fact of the matter is, you’d be telling me a different story if I was Paddy O’Shea. That I’m likable but I could be more aggressive. Or if I was Chandler Tsonda, that I’m clever and witty but can tone it down with the pretentious attitude. Or if I was Tchu, that winning the Jewel in the Crown was a great achievement, but it’ll be hard to keep the momentum going. Get the point? You’d still be telling me the same opinionated bullshit. It all comes down to meaningless drivel that eventually runs through one ear and out the other. So take your stupid opinion and shove it in your girl-pants, you pansy emo bastard, because I’m standing up for ignorance everywhere. No one can sit there and give you a good piece of advice without sugar-coating it with their own slobbery opinion. They’re always adding the smallest influence of their own preference; at least whenever I think of the media. You get magazines and columnists on your back, harping on your dangerous style in the ring and how spontaneity can make for some pretty disgusting outcomes. So what is Johnny Q. Public thinking when he comes to my shows, eh? He’s thinking, ‘oh that Illustrious Face-Eater is a shit-eating prick! He can kindly just fuck off with his arrogant wrestling, thinking no one would get hurt. I hope he rots in hell!’ What the fuck did I do to him? Did I sit there and give him a Reverse Huricanrana when he was expecting Flying Headscissors? No, but he feels the right to bitch about it because he read a fucking magazine. Now you get a legion of these bitch ass know-it-alls coming to shows and gathering in masses, all there to piss and moan every single moment that you’re in the spotlight unless there’s a boot getting kicked up your fucking asshole, united in some intangible feeling of expertise on your situation. And what do the true Face-Eater fans have to put up with? True Facey-fans face more persecution than Anne Frank would at the Dome of the Rock, and that’s a fact. It’s hard for them to go to a show without hearing some asshole’s lecture about my reckless ethics and how it is killing the pure sport of wrestling. Sorry pal, but that ended when WWF let a douche-bag like the Undertaker get famous. However I’m often unable to defend this point, which leaves Facey fans wondering. Why am I such a devious bastard? Am I really that likable? Some fans are left to think, “Well this skinny jew journalist in my wrestling magazine tells me that Facey is a bastard, so he must be! FUCK FACEY >:O” And really, what am I supposed to do about that? Go on record and apologize for my ridiculous behavior? Any normal human being would and I guess it’s safe to say that I was about to reach that point. I was about to sacrifice my ideals, just so I can go to the top and have the fan base to keep me up there. And that’s respectable right? At least that’s what people were telling me to do. “For the good of your career.” They’d tell me. “Make moves now to keep you around for times later.” I would have eaten this shit up in Core Wrestling. Oh, you think the Piledriver is too dangerous? Fine, it’s gone. Stop it with all the springboard bullshit; it’s getting too out of control? You’ve got it. Don’t jump every single member of the roster in the midst of a riot that I happened to instigate? Whatever you say. Do you see how boring that would seem? Fuck that, I’m not going to stand here with my thumb up my ass just to get a marketable face, because those people aren’t out for your best interests, they’re out for theirs. You may think it’s all fun and games in the pursuit of the almighty dollar but that’s what these guys are trained to do; make money off of you. They don’t care what you do or how you do it as long as you’re cutting them off a piece of the profit for their “expertise”. Monkeys like this are trained to leech off of you. It’s because you’re around so many monkeys that you don’t see the help when it comes for you. Some times the right advice doesn’t come from the places you’d normally expect it to come from, and when you finally get it you don’t really take it for what it is: GENUINE. People often begin looking in the wrong places because of this. It’s hard to tell who’s friend and who’s foe most of the time. I think I’ve stressed that enough. The weak-minded are often swayed by fancy ribbons and shiny packaging, which just so happens to be the very foundation of what this society is built upon. Everyone has the need for name-brands and benefits. Essentially the root of this free-enterprise economy we’ve come to embrace has gotten the better of our form of free thought, as we live our life guided by national ad campaigns. I had the chance to jump on that train, you know. To be one of the people who tells you sheep what think whenever I speak if only by the way I say it. And he came in the form of Merv Baetelman. Now before, I’d have taken the bate of his bullshit with arms wide open. But this time was...no different at all. I fell for it again, just like I would have before. Like I said, it’s all about salesmen and how they’re able to make you see whatever you want to see. To them, it’s all about showing you the possibilities. The rest is supposed to figure itself out. But this is the real world. You’ve got the slimmest chance of making it big, so you may as well do you, right? “Your name is so incredibly wacky that it can be applied to just about anything!” I didn’t get it at first. “Andy Milonakis, Robot Chicken, the Geico Caveman commercials, half the shit you see on MTV. It’s all apart of this new generation showcasing the bizarre side of pop-culture. More and more, people are finding comedy in making people feel uncomfortable. All the borders that were there, are gone now. It’s the perfect time for someone like you, Mr. Dick, to make a name like the Illustrious Face-Eater as common as JFK.” IFE. It resonated for a bit. The fucker had a way of making things sound good. After his first business proposal, I told him I’d have to sit on it and think it over. That I’d call him back in a day or two and tell him whether I wanted to proceed or not. He called six hours later. “Listen, I know you said to give it a few days, but I just had a great idea! A way how we can make your persona appeal to the greater public!” To become a face, he meant. “Not a face,” he said, “but the next Anti-Hero! You’re the next Stone Cold!” So is every person with a pair of testicles in the fed, asshole! Of course, I didn’t say this then. I wasn’t even thinking it. Then, it all sounded good. “I just saw the humiliating events that transpired with Mr. Danny Ferguson. That bastard has been entirely too rambunctious and too aggressive since he arrived. He’s been stepping all over so many people’s nerves in PRIME’s hierarchy that they’d be willing to reward, most likely, anyone who’d be able to take him out.” I told him to continue. “Well, first you could play the victim. Ferguson has eliminated both Kyle Lamen and Dave Gibson almost back-to-back, PRIME wasn’t exactly happy with that. And now the entire situation with Danza...” See, things were going my way! ‘Actually, Merv,’ I tried to say, ‘I think I’ve got that end under control-’ “Nonsense.” Cheeky bastard. “If we abide by my strategy, we’ll have you playing the warrior that stands amongst the settling dust; the one man who has been able to stand after Ferguson has pushed them over. You can take the sympathy vote, where most fans we’ll side with you purely for the sake that you are AGAINST Danny Ferguson.” ‘But I’m a heel,’ I tried to justify, ‘I don’t get the fans sympathy.’ “Son, if you want to get to the top and you want to stay there, you’re going to have to make some moves. People are going to have to like you if they’re going to remember their face. And there’s no other way to get a memorable face than by getting ten thousand friends every Wednesday night on your side when you’re beating up that punk, Ferguson.” I was sold, as they say. That phone call was really all it took, but for the sake of not looking like a sucker, I tried to ask where he had experience. No one ever wants to look like an idiot; I made sure that I kept my idiot-levels to a minimum at all times. Merv told me about how he used to manage Killean Sirrajin, and James Edwards, my good friend Mike Wade, and even the Forsaken. Merv later retracted his statement of the Forsaken, I don’t blame him. He said, right before he got off the phone, he’d take the first flight out of JFK to see me. The truth was, he was already on the plane. The guy was banking on this job from the moment he heard I was hired. When he arrived, though, he didn’t even seem half-desperate. The guy played the part of a pro; a real bad-ass. He got shit done. Apparently, salesmen are always supposed to look like that. They’re so friendly, charming, fake. Within hours of the call, he was at my door. Knock knock with the chipper and eager ass smile, it was easy to hate him. That’s what made me love him. His go-get-‘em attitude was always rubbing off on me, to the point where I didn’t as about the papers in front of me, I just signed them and got shit done. He promised endorsements from all angles. Talking me through the process, he said that every superstar goes through preliminary bullshit stuff; it’s after you pay your ‘marketing dues’ that you really start to make money. I never heard of something like the marketing dues. But regardless, Merv began whispering things in my ear. Things like judging talent contests at state fairs; or endorsing local car dealerships; or appearing on day-time talkshows. It was after someone builds a foundation on the bullshit work that the true deals come in. And if my work synergizes with the wrestling stuff, I’ll be at the top of the money-making scheme. Merv even told me I could probably get my own Reebok. I’ve always wanted my own fucking Reebok! Who DOESN’T want their own fucking Reebok? So I signed every piece of paper he shoved in front of me. I’ll sponsor your homemade chili. And sure, I’ll cut the ribbon to the grand opening of your Nail Salon. And you, yeah, I’ll slap my name on these multi-purpose office tool! At least, I would have done all of this. The brand name IFE, that didn’t look like it was happening anytime soon. Contrary to what he was telling me, I didn’t feel like I was getting famous. Maybe that’s not something that hangs over your shoulders, but it was still bothering me. Nothing in my life had changed, not even the stack in my back pocket. Profits were still turning, gathering and waiting to be collected. My name was stamped on all this stupid bullshit and Merv was just pushing papers in front of me. “Focus on your endorsements,” he’d tell me. All of the sudden, I’ve got a Pulse Title shot. Against two cumbubbles and some furry clam, albeit, but I’ve still got a fucking Title shot. Two victories in, no losses, and they’re already starting to notice. This seed is planted in the depths of my brain, and I begin to think, “Maybe I need to keep at this wrestling stuff.” But no. Merv said, “Focus on your endorsements. Wrestling can only last so long; your money is what’s important now and forever.” I decided to let my thoughts stay planted. I let them grow. He could tell that I wasn’t satisfied, and decided to give me a more “hands on” approach. “Here, I’ve got something new for ya. Public speaking, at this community college class. It’s a sports broadcasting class, and they want someone to talk about the dangers of unprofessional sports.” Right off the bat, I have no idea what the fuck they want from me, but I say I’ll do it. Fuck yeah, pencil me down for talking to the kiddies! I was more than willing to share my expertise to the youngsters begging to hear my advice. So you could imagine how fucking insecure I got when I saw the entire audience (which was lacking in itself, mind you!) was on average the age of my dad. The podium was too big for me to look anywhere near respectable when using it. This in turn triggered my sweat glands to go apeshit, and all of the sudden my tuxedo have Raccoon eyes around the armpits. WHAT THE FUCK WAS I THINKING WEARING A TUXEDO TO A PUBLIC SPEAKING?! The whole thing was doomed from the start. Probably never should have happened, and never would have happened… if advice had come from a different person. See where I’m going with this? If not yet, you will. Just wait. Even in the beginning, there was this girl who had it out for me. There wasn’t something I said during my speech that happened to offend her. She just wanted to bite my fucking head off and she must’ve had it written on her calendar months ahead of time. Her purse sat anxiously in her lap, across folded arms and legs, as if it were a rabid animal begging to break from her clutches and tear someone’s genitals off. Her facial expression, her body language—hell, even her hair and make-up made her look like a bitch. You could tell she was unhappy, probably because she sat by herself. So when I finally began, I thought it was good. Well, as good as I could have hoped to have done, sweaty and insecure as shit and just waiting for little miss PMS to go bonkers over one false word. Considering the circumstances, I thought I was doing fine. “The problem with the backyard wrestling, is that it’s starting to create this stupid style that is becoming more and more popular every day. It started off that bullshit, oops, I mean bullcrap Hardcore era which brings about your ECWs and your XPWs, but then we start importing these Japanese death-match tapes. All the sudden these kids are in their basements mixing C4 plastique so they can powerslam their neighbor’s dog on to it with the trampoline. Then you’ve got such a huge problem that you don’t know what could’ve instigated it, and this just becomes a crappy situation. I mean, what the fuck are we supposed to do when these kids sue us for setting one of our co-workers on fire? I mean, if I’m not getting sued by the guy I set ablaze, why should I be sued at all? Because some brat saw me, took a cue and decided his friend wasn’t over with the fans enough? That’s ridiculous!” Now before I try and defend myself anymore, I’ll admit, I’m not the brightest talker. Especially when I’m defending an issue. And if it’s an issue I don’t particularly care about, I’m going to lose any argument without question. That should be said about anyone, but unlike most people, I’m not a salesman. I’m not good at dressing my words up to suck your dick. But still, this chick was ruthless. It was comparable to fishing for trout with your grandpa’s shotgun in his standard-sized fishing tank. I’m actually surprised at getting out a live, much less at how the situation played out. “Wait a second, are you saying that wrestling should turn towards a more professional direction?” She asked. This was the beginning. I smiled, hoping to catch her interests. “Yeah! Exactly! The kids these days obviously are too stupid to think for themselves, so the thing to do here, OBVIOUSLY, is uh, professionalism. Yeah.” “And how do you suppose we do that?” She said. Here eyes, they didn’t read any positive or negative light. It was hard to tell if she was going to skin me or lead me on. “Education!” A politician’s best answer. “We need to develop better training programs with the new recruits, a more professional training method, and we also need to hire more experienced talent from outside the promotions.” “Well, you entered Core Wrestling with practically no experience whatsoever. I’d hardly say you’re applying your standards to your own situation.” Yep. She was skinning me. “Or maybe you’re thinking of the time when you joined the Atlantic Wrestling Club; because you had training then, right Mr. Dick? Actually, that probably ISN’T right, seeing that you only had about six months of training in the ring with a formal tutor. This doesn’t really fit your requirements at all. What do you have to say about that?” “I… uh.” Fuck you, Merv! That’s all that I could think. “Maybe you have nothing to say. Maybe you’re just going to hide inside your own head because you can’t respond. You know what I think? I think you’re trying to bullshit us. If you were able to step outside yourself and sit through that bullshit we just forced ourselves to stay awake through, you’d think you were an idiot. There is no way that you can stand there and say you believe in anything that you just told us, because it’s a big fucking lie and doesn’t make any sense whatsoever. Hardcore wrestling is apart of your fucking genre.” I noticed the people in the audience had begun to look at her funny. They were starting to get nervous. At least I wasn’t the only one. “What are you trying to prove, anyways? That you should be the ones our kids look up to? Well get a clue man because even if we encouraged our kids to accept you as their role-model, by your stature alone you’d fail to come close to the Captain Americas and Stone Cold Steve Austins of the world. Maybe after a few needles, kid. And a haircut, I’d say you’d look pretty cute after a haircut. But until then you still look like a prick who doesn’t know what propaganda he’s trying to push.” She said I was cute. “I’m sorry?” Didn’t she just call me cute? “Don’t you have a match in PRIME tomorrow for a Title? You’ve got a two-win streak going and a title shot, and you’re squandering it on some fucking public speech? Are you dense or something? Or are you trying to lose this match. You should be out at a fucking gym! Jesus Christ, you are NOT the Illustrious Face-Eater—that is for sure. You’ve got to be some pussy ass mild mannered imposter who can’t find his separate his dick from his zipper. The guy on TV would actually have the pair to tell off a group of students who opposed his views, but no, this guy is trying to win a popularity contest. What you should do is go back to being the ILLUSTRIOUS FUCKING FACE-EATER and maybe you’ll start to make these changes you’ve been crying about.” I didn’t notice it at first, but by the way she was talking, she was a fan. So, I let the silence linger. Her speech ran longer than mine, apparently. Everyone in the audience been coughing to fill that awkward silence, until I finally decided to speak. “You think I’m cute?” “I think you’re a fucking idiot.” The instructor decided to kill whatever tension he thought existed, and pulled me back from the podium. He thanked the students for showing up, and then he thanked me for taking time out of my busy schedule to speak to them. Some kids waited around for questions, but I ignored them. Someone even went as far as to grab my sleeve; so I booted her in the gut. None of these mother fuckers mattered at that point, I just needed to talk to her. She was trying to get her things together quickly and bail the fuck out as soon as possible; I wouldn’t let her. “You said I was cute.” “I said you would be cute if you didn’t have that lame fucking haircut. Get it shaved, then call me.” Boom. The number was mine. She passed off this piece of paper, then stormed off and out of the room, cutting off a group of students waiting to leave. The bitch had attitude, that was certain, but it was the words that reflected in mind later. I fired Merv no more than five minutes later. It was a look back at who was actually giving me the more credible advice; some bitch off the street or a paid expert? Friend or foe? It was confusing at first, but the concept is clear now. Merv was a slime ball trying to capitalize off of me for his own sake. He was giving me tips, all with tainted with his dirty intentions and personal gain. I talked to Mike Wade, who told me that Merv was a predator. He liked to prey off new hires and rape them of their name for a month before someone finally told them of the scam he was pulling. Apparently, I failed to get the memo. So I’m not a popular guy at PRIME, I can live with that. I was the top dog at AWC, but all of the sudden in a world of Tyler Nelsons and Angelo Devilles and Emilio Rages, I’m fucking just another Cheerio in the bowl. There’s only one way to change that. And that isn’t by letting a guy like Danny Ferguson walk all over me, just because he has the name value to do so. Oh no. I show him what happens just like I’ve shown everyone else. It’s all been a short ride in PRIME so far, no one really knows what I’m capable of. That’s why I haven’t got the fan base. Looks like I’ve got to start by taking Danny’s. The note had her number, and then her name; Chelsea. Chelsea and Merv were both very crucial to my path in PRIME, and taught me different things. Together, they showed me that friend and foe are not always easy to recognize. People are always capable of saying a few nice words and scratching your back a little, but they are just as capable to sell you short. Take a look around; your friends aren’t always who you think they are, and those who turn out to be your enemies may end up surprising you. Chelsea showed me how valuable it was to remain true to the self. I’m not best making my name spread in waves by doing commercial spots. The Face-Eater is an ass kicker and life-sabotager! People hate me the most when I’m out destroying lives. And most importantly, Merv taught me the art of selling. Just in time to inherit a prize like the Pulse Championship. Because you see, there’s no better way of proving you’re better than the rest than with a gold belt strapped around your waist. Superiority and glamour, pressed in to one piece of bragging rights that no one can argue with. With a title like that, it’s the pro-wrestling business’ form of endorsing; putting your name on a product that everyone knows about. Free advertising. And no one is going to stay in my way for that. K-Wolf is gone, that’s a given. I’ve got to worry about Chris Collins. The guy who can’t even keep his support payments up. He’s struggled to keep his name in the spotlight since Core, has bounced around more times than Britney’s tits and all of the sudden has a shot at this Title? He didn’t do anything to win that match at the King of Kings, why should even get the right to use his voice? Like I’ve preached, I’ll practice my own form of street justice. AWC officials decide to reward him for incompetence, it is only right I balance out the universe by beating his ass for it. So Chris Collins has somewhat of a winning record, I can understand why he’d get a title shot, if you’re going to apply some rather insane justifications. But Chandler Tsonda? The guy I beat? I’ve got the fucking winning streak, not these chinky bastards. Why are they getting the same recognition as me? See, me of yesteryear would say it’s because I’m not presenting myself in the right way. My name isn’t on enough ads. Hah, I was so naive. But that was… last week. I’m a new man now. Now I understand that while a person like Chandler Tsonda and I may both be making waves, its all about how you sell yourself. I may have the winning record, but were both rambunctious loudmouths at heart. To any PRIME fans glancing from a distance, we’re two peas in a pod. Hmph. A pity. I’m going to have to beat the shit out of this other pea to show everyone why I’m better. So please, start paying attention to me; I’m starting to get desperate. God dammit I really do need to get a girlfriend. I wonder what Chelsea’s doing?
