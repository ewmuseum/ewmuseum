The Boss and The Brawler

10 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/10/05 1. The Boss “So what is this PRIME anyway?” James Kyle, sitting opposite his relocated English employer, licked his dry lips nervously. He always hated this part of the job, the part in which he had to address his boss. Throughout the nine years he had worked for Mr O’Donnell, he was generally always nominated to be the bearer of bad news to him. Whether it was by his superiors or his peers, he was the one that was coaxed into this sort of downgrading tasks all the time. It really shouldn’t have been a surprise though. He was from Sheffield in one of the richer areas and hadn’t come accustomed to the grimier side of life until he was around twenty-two. To look at, James was pathetic; is skinny build and pathetic comb over did him no favours nor did his thin black moustache that with its sharp black bristles highlighted and contrasted against the smoothness of his full upper lip. His clothes were tacky yet unnecessarily expensive, full pieced tan suits and distinct oak loafers. His mother had always commented on his frailty – a psychologist could have told him that the reason he did eventually join a crime organisation was because he had something to prove to his mother but of course it was his lack of processing ability that blocked him from ever knowing this. He was a weak man of weak character, seemingly unable to say the word no. In his blindness he had long since convinced himself he actually was truly valued and that these chores were simply tradition. The fact of the matter is, every great man had to step on somebody to get where they got. Kyle was one of those men with a footprint on his head. “Are you listening?” James had of course drifted away once again so no he wasn’t listening asshole. “Yes boss sorry. PRIME is-“ Mother says don’t lie James, it shows a lack of integrity. “-A wrestling organisation based in America. It is quite a large franchise with a number of key shareholders. It’s believed to be one of the leaders of its genre of entertainment.” James heard his words in his own head and grimaced. Compared to the coarse and mysterious tone of Mr O’Donnell’s voice, his own voice was simply feminine and high. You make me sick. Remember how mother used to call you her little girl? Ever wondered why? Did I ever tell you how much you make me sick? Because you do, you make me sick you pathetic crazy old bastard. Leave me alone. I’m working. And I’m not pathetic and I’m not crazy! Really? Then why the hell are you having a conversation with yourself! I..I- “Mr Kyle, are you even listening to what the hell I’m saying!” James looked up from his fixed gaze on the deeply varnished wooden floor. It wasn’t one of those jigsaw Mickey Mouse efforts he’d noticed once while daydreaming in a not so much different situation from this. Now however he was looking into the shadow smouldering O’Donnell’s desk. How the hell you could write in virtually no light, he would never know but at least the air of mystery was intensified. All Kyle could see was the outline of the mans silhouette, perched resolutely on the full size executive chair. Something about that scene made him think about a detective story he had once read and he yearned for a moment to be as respected and strong as that make believe character. Then he pushed the thought out of his head after reminding himself books were for queers. “Er…s-sorry s-sir, I was away there. What was it you s-said?” The boss sighed from within his dark stronghold most likely at how pitiful James was now becoming. Raise your voice, curse in anyway, sometimes do nothing at all, and he would begin his babbling. Nerves had plagued him in his youth through a stutter but he had suppressed it through the course of months and years. Of course it flared up again when he had to cope with a situation in anyway difficult. He never could do anything right his mother would have said. “I’ll reiterate then for your benefit. Why would he want to go there? Why this sudden resettlement of loyalty?” “Well…I…” James fingered nervously with the doyley type cloth, residing over the crown of the chair, desperately trying to breath from the clamping on his lungs, his coping mechanism seemingly now working at all. If only he was doing work on the road, he wouldn’t be in situations like this. “He’s getting paid more for a s-start…and I believe he enjoys it a lot.” “Haven’t we been good to him? Haven’t I been good to him!” James heart began to beat a little faster. He feared the rare moments he was in his boss’s company when he got mad. It had happened before and James has gotten a fat lip just for the trouble of being there. What was to say this wouldn’t simply be a repeat of that episode? James licked his bottom lip nervously, his tongue tingling as it passed over living ridges, circling the area that had once been a split. Wiping his sweaty palms on his beige trousers, James allowed the impending silence to devour him, and he fidgeted just that bit more. Sometimes silence was good. The spaces in between anger and action wasn’t one of them however. “Tell me Mr Kyle…when’s the last time you’ve been on full active duty?” Suddenly aroused with excitement, James cleared his throat as he thought for a second. Active duty…hm…well the last time would be when they were sent to beat up that witness at his house. He can still remember how he had bounced around like an idiot, hyped up with adrenaline. He can also remember how the witness had fought back whilst he went looking for life savings, heard something heavy fall as his incompetent colleague was knocked out. Most of all he remembers clearly how the witness then left him a bloody wreck when he walked in. Like ever injury he’d ever incurred, he could perfectly envisage how the squelch of blood had sounded as the mans fist sunk deep into his face. Active duty again? Did I say excited? Actually…maybe I’ll pass on that one. “Five years sir…give or take a month.” “Quite a while isn’t it? Well James, I think it’s about time you did something to earn the money I put in your pocket every week…” The distinctive sound of James gulping seemed to echo around the room. James scratched his head, moved about in his seat so he was in a prone position then asked nervously: “W-w-what d-do you m-mean?” There was a pause then a chuckle was heard. A laugh perhaps but a laugh that had sinister laced right through it. 2. The Boxer Alone in a makeshift changing room that was normally used as a female toilet sat Paddy O’Shea, deep within that place they have named ‘the zone’. The sound of a crowd can be heard from behind the door yet Paddy can’t hear a thing. He sits on the edge of a kitchen table swinging his legs. As childlike as this may seem, Paddy O’Shea is as far away from that manner as is humanly possible. O’Shea tightens his left and right fists, allowing the white tape to stretch and mould into the shape of his fists. This is bare-knuckle boxing. And this is the sport Paddy is well, with a heavy heart, soon leave behind. It was about six years now when Paddy began it, encouraged by his father Danny. Eight months later and he had made a name for himself as the tiny terror, operating under this alias to protect himself from the illegality of it all. He beat up fighters big and small, powerful and fast. Because he had something in kilos those men could never muster. And that was heart. He soon got recognised by the underground network and he remembers when a man he simply has grown to know as O’Donnell, offered to pay him obscene amounts of money at the time, just to win when he said win and lose when he said lose just to please his betting slip. It broke Paddy’s heart to do this but times back then were rough for his family and he had no other choice - O’Donnell controlled everything in Galway City, to turn your back on him was like turning your back on civilisation. Yet he grew to love the sport, even held onto it when he joined wrestling organisations. Held onto it right up until today infact even though he’s an AWC wrestler. But something’s changed for the first time. A little something called PRIME, a federation so big it will drain all his time and pay him incredible money for doing it. There will be no throwing fights there, like a haven for a tired warrior like himself, it will the best man who wins, not the one with the greatest odds on their back. He’s handed in his two week notice so after this, it’s adios Senor O’Donnell, pleasant days and long nights. O’Shea is jerked out of his thoughts by a rap on the door. “The doors open, come on in.” O’Shea looks up with those focused eyes as the door opens and a head slips in. Although he is unrecognisable, its plain to Paddy that this man works for O’Donnell because in this hellhole, its his pressed navy suit that makes him stand out from the crowd. And why is he here? Well why is one of O’Donnell’s associates usually here? To give him the verdict on the match no doubt. “You win tonight Paddy, odds are against ye after losing the last two. Make it the first round since it’s your last fight. Now hurry up, they’re waitin’ fer ye.” The man suddenly disappears and the door closes with a click. Paddy thinks for a few seconds then smiles. He’ll go out with a bang tonight and the romanticism of it grips him. Held by a sudden fever, O’Shea hops off his seat and swings a quick succession of lefts and rights, loosening his muscles for tonight’s encounter, allowing every muscle he can think of prime itself for action. He quickly zips off his plain navy tracksuit top, leaving him in three quarter length khakis and a white skintight vest then shadow boxes into the mirror, reinstating his strategy in his head. He bobs up and down, left and right for a few more seconds then strides towards the door, pulls it open and steps out. For a second, O’Shea thought he was back in PCW, stepping out from behind the curtain, imagining the twenty something crowd was really twenty thousand, amplifying and manipulating the muted clap he hears into a genuine roar as fans strive to bust their lungs for him. Then he blinks back to reality and instead he’s looking around a seedy, crusty old bar filled with alcoholics and gum beans. What an anticlimax, eh? O’Shea walks into a large square, bordered by white paint and looks over to the opposite corner into his opponents face. Big, rugged and most of all stupid. Didn’t know his name but he’d fought this guy before and lost, that’s probably why Paddy was perceived to be an underdog and probably why his opponent was beaming with confidence. At least he could now wipe that goofy grin off his face before he left permanently for America. “Ready fer another beatin’ wee man?” the opponent called over. O’Shea was in no mood for jokes though; he just continued to issue that mesmerising stare. Let the man keep his arrogance, it will make the fall that much heavier. The referee (who incidentally was also Paddy’s postman) called the two fighters into the centre of the ring, wearing his black and white striped T-Shirt he’d bought from Dunnes Stores for £4.99. “Okay Paddy, Hugo, no bitin’, gouging or low blows. Now hurry up an’ get this over wi’, aye’ve got a post round t’ do in fifteen minutes,” the moustached referee murmured. O’Shea took two steps back, as did Hugo, psued then initiated the fight when they heard the bell ring. O’Shea took a step in and tested the water with a timid jab. Aye remember him now…Southpaw. Aye love these type o’ fighters O’Shea grinned hugely at his opponent who stood about half a foot above him causing his own sneer to disappear suddenly. Best thing about big dumb fighters, O’Shea had discovered long ago, was do anything at all unpredictable and they got in a serious psychological daze. The man’s reach was perhaps ten centimetres past O’Shea’s, which to the inside fighter O’Shea is, was seen as advantageous. The two danced around each other, Paddy analysing straight away his sloppy footwork, his unbalanced stance. He wasn’t even holding the southpaw right for Gods sake. O’Shea saw his opening, stepped in and before Hugo knew it, he was getting literally cracked with close to naked bone knuckles, crunching off his cheeks and chin. O’Shea hit him perhaps seven times in two seconds before stepping out. Now that he had made his introduction into the match, he felt at ease and he would savour this euphoria just a little more, a high he didn’t quite get in the wrestling ring. The grit and grime and hard concrete floor just seemed to shine brighter than any of the pyros or stage lights a wrestling federation could offer. O’Shea weaved a little left then a lot right, bobbing up and down, a slightly more excited crowd shouting the usual cries for blood. O’Shea glided around the outskirts of the white line, Hugo, now seemingly recovered moving to meet him. O’Shea simply had to duck left to avoid a jab then cut upwards viciously to connect with the underside of his chin. Paddy let his arm fall to his side, allowing the muscle to return to neutral. Then O’Shea closed his eyes and felt the sound of the mans body bounce off the unforgiving floor reverberate up his body. He tried not to smile but he couldn’t help it. He was born a fighter, all that smile proved was that he would die a fighter too. He was deaf to the drunken cheers and screams and instead walked away, his eyes still firmly shut, almost by instinct to his changing room. He didn’t wait for the ten count or for the referee to confirm his victory. The match was over, and so was his involvement in the sport he grew to love. No more going home with knuckles the size of walnuts, or bumps protruding from his face like tennis balls. Maybe he would return someday, maybe not. But for now it was an emotional goodbye. He just had to get his kicks somewhere else from now on. 3. The Break-In “Okay I’m in charge so we’re going to do this my way okay?” James Kyle, unsure and high pitched as ever, was now addressing two other men. He sits in the back of a car at at least ten O’clock, the complete darkness giving this fact away. One of his companions sits in the seat beside him, the other in the driver seat, facing forward so we can’t see his face. Only a black pinstriped suit and a baldhead can be used to place some identity on him. “Okay, okay. O’Donnell wants me to lead this thing so me and you,” Kyle points to the young man sitting beside him, not more than twenty, looking back excitedly and unsure in a complex hybrid of emotions. “Are going to carry this out.” Now he gazes into the abck of the other mans head. “You stay here and keep watch. This is going to be simple – in, beat him up then out again. Couldn’t be simpler. Okay?” “Okay,” the boy beside him said, the other man staying silent in the front, which Kyle felt too intimidated by to some his sudden frustrated. Kyle tried to look at both men at once, his eyes darting back and forth. His hands were shaking and he couldn’t stop from fiddling with his ring adorning his index finger, turning it back and forth around his finger. Unlike the young man beside him, Kyle didn’t feel excited at all. He’d waited for so long to get another chance and here it was. Yet as the hours drew into the initiation, he found it impossible to shake off the sickness nestling in the pit of his stomach. The fact he knew that O’Shea was an extremely dangerous man didn’t help matters much at all. Kyle pulled a gun from the inside of his jacket and almost dropped it as he spun it around to check the clip. Here was another thing he didn’t like – guns. They were too heavy and made loud noises that made him jump. Something mother would have jumped at to criticise…oh the irony of it all. Satisfied with his piece, Kyle nodded at the boy and the two got out together of the car, closing the doors silently behind them. The two were at the top of a lane with only the moonlight guiding them. However their destination was clearly highlighted by another light, trying to hide behind the branches of a tree. An artificial light beaming from the O’Shea residence. “Okay. We’ll just walk down this lane, sneak up to his caravan and break in.” The boy beside him nodded. He was green and it showed with the stiffness in his walk and shaky composure. Kyle was just as bad however and seemed reluctant to move. Eventually he broke his mild paralysis and the two began walking down the lane, barely visible silhouettes beneath a watchful sky. Kyle found himself staring into the hedgerows, separating the lane from the pasture fields, wondering if a man could be in there somewhere waiting to pounce. This notion was something he’d picked up growing up in the countryside and something, like many childhood worries he had, had never seemed to have shaken off. The night was mild which would have been hopeful for the people around these parts considering the last few weeks dire weather. Despite this, Kyle shivered and quickened his pace until he was behind the tree overhanging the caravan. Just get this over with quickly. You know what to do. Ha! And what’s that then? Go in and beat him up obviously Oh yeah? And how’s that going to work? How is a pathetic weed like you going to beat up a boxer? With the help of Sparky over there? No…I’ve got a gun. Well whoop-de-doo! And you’re going to point it at him and starting punching him at the same time yeah? Please, you couldn’t formulate a milkshake never mind a plot. Kyle’s private debate was halted when the young boy beside him nudged him from his inner quarrel. He only had to look at the boys face to know this was the time. Kyle looked again at the light cutting through the window of the caravan, nodded and made his move. This was his chance, he wouldn’t squander it. And as the thought of his boss clung onto his mind like a piece of toilet paper hanging from the toilet seat, he realised that he couldn’t squander it. #~#~# Paddy O’Shea was to the rear of his caravan taking what he would describe as a leak when he heard it. He hadn’t bothered to get changed out of the attire he wore on his fight tonight for two reasons – a) he hadn’t really broken a sweat and b) because he was as lazy as the day he was born. But he was attentive, and he heard the fibreglass sliding door shift in its placing easily enough. He swung around in surprise, still in the process of urinating when he heard it, almost preparing to dart around the corner with it in full flow. Then the sudden injection of adrenaline began to wane and he caught himself on. A few seconds later and he was sneaking around the corner, his eyes coming out his ears just to get that peep around the corner. His breath was heavy now and he fought bitterly to silence himself. It was dark now but he could still various belongings of his, scattered amongst the long grass here, thanks to the light from his caravan basking the place with life. And there it was, almost as if sent by God, a claw hammer, he remembered now how he’d used to panel wood a few days ago. He always knew God was the vicious type and despite this potentially volatile situation, surprisingly he found it hard to suppress a chuckle. It was always the same, once he’d let go of his fear, he was completely focused on the task ahead willing to evolve and adapt into the conditions that he would be forced to meet. Now it was all business and Paddy felt calmer. Whatever they had, he could now rival. The only thing was (and what now dawned on him) that this man or group of men weren’t here for a breaking…afterall, who would break into a house with a light on? Paddy harnessed his fear once again and stepped out from around the corner, his eyes trying to scan everywhere at once. There was one in the caravan and near the door too, it was his that footsteps gave him away. And then there was another, around the otherside of the caravan. He knew from the shadow being cast adjacent to his position. O’Shea lifted the claw hammer up above his head and ran into his home. A number of gunshots were heard then for the second time that night, the sound of a body dropping. The boy ran around to the door as quickly as he could and was about to enter until the view in front of him forced him back. Now he was running away and as fast as he could. From behind a tree a man stepped forward, a baton in his hand. All this he had now viewed. And for the pleasure of what he had seen, he allowed himself to smile. 4. The Betrayal “So…its finished?” The voice is that of the same man who addressed James Kyle two days before. Once again, he sits in his throne in the shadows, viewing his empire in the darkness. His coarse voice is devoid of regret or guilt. Instead, there seems to be a hint of satisfaction, even pride. All this despite sending a man to his death. Across from him, sitting in the same armchair Kyle had occupied, is a man in a black pinstriped suit, topped off with a matching black shirt and grey tie. His bald head reflects the hazy orange light being emitted from a table light beside the marble fireplace. A thin smile is on the mans face, although you’d have to look twice to see the tweaking at the corners of his lips. The man slips a hand into an inside pocket and pulls a cigarette packet and lighter. “Indeed it is.” The man removes a cigarette smoothly and places it at the end of his lips so that it hangs down past his square chin. The man looks in his late fifties although this may not be the case – some of the lines now taken for wrinkles could just as easily be various scars and this doesn’t seem terribly unlikely as his chewed ears and mashed nose point out. He flips his lighter open and clicks it alight, then holds it to the cigarette tip and inhales slowly, the orange tip beaming in the dire surroundings, brightening with every drag. “So, is it true that nuisance Kyle is indeed dead?” “Aye” This time the man doesn’t hesitate with his answer. Instead he barks it like O’Donnell’s questioning could at all be confused for doubt. “O’Shea panicked and caved in his skull. Kyle died instantly. Aye seen it all from outside so don’ worry.” Suddenly O’Donnell’s face appears from the shadows, fading into the light, almost as if crossing over an invisible barrier. His face is slightly anxious no matter how much he tries to hide it. Like this man before him, he is bald but he’s older, a lot older. He doesn’t seem to smoke because of the pleasant odour in the room so it is difficult to calculate his age. However, he does look at least fifteen older than his associate, with crows feet and weathered, blood vesseled cheeks giving him away. “And Flynn…the body?” Flynn’s was looking at the design on his lighter when O’Donnell asked him and suddenly his eyes shot up, so rapidly that O’Donnell slid back into his seat again. He smiled at this reaction, seemingly the type of person who gets their kicks out of making others uneasy, no matter what their position. “Aye followed in, knocked O’Shea out then disposed o’ Kyle.” “Disposed of it?” “Like aye said, don’t worry.” O’Donnell had to question himself again and asked why he put this man as head of this mission, a man who had always seemed to g et the edge on him in terms of the mental battle. When he talked to Flynn, he felt common and cheap, all over somebody he paid. O’Donnell however pushed away this sudden surge of annoyance when he looked at Flynn’s face and was reminded. This man was good; one of the best and what you wanted done became testament with him. “Very well. I’ll update you when you get to America. And remember to keep our aim very clear in your head that-“ “-O’Shea is ours. And if can’ talk him into stayin’, we’ll have t’ convince him otherwise.” O’Donnell squirmed in his seat in disgust as Flynn now bared a full smile, showing an incomplete set of dentures, his cigarette gripped between his front teeth. He took another drag of his cigarette then put it out in the ashtray and got up, flattening out the creases in his trousers. “Aye’ll see meself out.” O’Donnell watched the man walk out and then close the door. Only when he heard his footsteps disappear did he breath a mental sigh of relief. That man is dangerous…and for the first time, O’Donnell questioned the gain of this job over the cost.
