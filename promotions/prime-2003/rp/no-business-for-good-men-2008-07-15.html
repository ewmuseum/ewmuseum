No Business For Good Men

15 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15/07/08 “He’s grabbing my hair, ref!” Kannon appeals to the unsympathetic official, clutching a clump of his dyed red mane and yanking his own head back to act out his opponent’s alleged indiscretion. “They don’t have rules around here anymore?” The referee’s disdainful stare silently lectures the Gold Patron Meritorious not to push his luck, but whatever judgement he emits won’t stick to the Teflon of Xavier Kannon’s conscience. With his distraction investment having reached maturity, Kannon barges past the official towards Jonathan Rhine, the sharp burn on Ellie’s nail across his one good eye still searing nerves as he rolls back into the ring. “Now put him out of his misery,” Kannon snarls up at the official as he drops a knee across Rhine’s face and stares straight into the ringside camera, repulsive smugness almost dirtying the lens. “One!” counts the sickened official, staring up at the triumphant pose of the Gold Patron Meritorious rather than Rhine’s limp shoulders. “Two! THRE- As the crowd explode, the official breaks out of his depressed autopilot mode just in time to break his right hand off of a collision course with the canvas. “Two!” he proclaims, the gesture to the time-keeper’s table enough to conduct the entire arena into a symphony of rousing cheers. With wild eyes growing wider as his jaw drops further, Xavier stares at the referee in disbelief. “No…” he refuses to believe, “no, no, no, NO!“ With a groggy Rhine stranded on his back, the fuming Scientologist pounces, finger nails as sharp as his snarling teeth clawing at his good left eye. “JUST GO HOME!“ a possessed voice growls from deep inside Xavier. “JUST GO HOME!“ Clouded in a mist as red as his hair, Kannon has to be dragged off of Rhine by the official, but not before he tries to claw around the New Life’s left eyeball for grip. “GET YOUR FILTHY HANDS OFF ME!“ seethes Kannon as the official drags him up to his feet. “DON’T YOU KNOW WHO I AM, PEON! I’M THE KING OF WRESTLING! I CAN DO WHAT I WANT TO HIM! YOU DON’T LAY A PATHETIC FINGER ON ME!“ “Oh, I know who are,” fires back the referee, his nose wrinkling up like he smells something unpleasant on the sole of his shoe. “Then you won’t even think about doing that again,” hisses Kannon with sinister intent. “Unless you want your family hounded out of their home and your name dragged through the mud, Peon.” Unable to hold himself back from the red rag that Kannon waves in front of his face, the official squares up to Kannon, taking the smug Scientologist aback. “Don’t you eve-” is all the referee has time to spit into Kannon’s face before a flash of red and gold crashes in through his peripheral vision, and the sound of a boot crunching into XK’s ribs is followed by a guttural wheeze. The next thing Kannon knows, the crowd are spinning all around him, then shooting up towards the sky… until the back of his head whiplashes against the canvas, almost popping both eyes out their sockets. As an agonising, drilling pain spreads throughout his skull, something dark and fuzzy passes across the arena lights above, then crashes down beside Kannon, jolting him back to life. The musky stench of ingrained sweat in the canvas act as smelling salts. Staggering up and over to Rhine, Kannon can’t help but break out into laughter at the sight of his visually-impaired rival’s human wreckage. Standing victorious over the crash ‘n’ burned New Life, Kannon draws his thumb across his throat, signalling the end for Rhine. Despite having hit the Hallmark hundreds of times, it’s never a case of just going through the motions… he savours it. The sound of human skull drilling into the ring, the deflation of the crowd, and as he closes his eyes, he imagines the sound of vertebrae chipping against one another. With a handful of Rhine’s blonde hair, XK tugs him into a standing head-scissors, knees clamping either side of the neck to cut off the blood flow to the New Life’s brain. Grabbing the gold waistband of Rhine’s tights, Kannon then pulls him up onto his feet, before tingling fingers lock around his waist. A slight bending of the knees gives Xavier all the leverage he needs to pull Rhine up, before his hands lock in a grapevi- This isn’t how it’s supposed to happen. As Kannon goes to lock his hands, nothing is there. Then he feels a hand tugging on his shoulder. Then he feels an arm across his chest. Then he feels his organs jump in his torso. Then the lights go out. … All of that? That never happened. ==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X Sadly, this was happening. By now, Xavier Kannon’s heartbeat has synchronised with the tick-tock of the clock’s hands. Dry eyes squint as harsh amber sun all but disintegrates the tatty blinds, illuminating a thick smog of dust that a lone desk fan lacks the oomph to disrupt. A few beads of sweat peek out across his forehead, sucking up the courage to tumble down his face as a flimsy booklet about amateur boxing limply flops in front of him, thoroughly failing to create much of a breeze. Welcome to the offices of the Missouri State Athletic Commission. Tongue hanging out, a wilting Gold Patron Meritorious again stares up at the clock, where the second hand ticks closer to the 12, building up leverage to heave the minute hand up with it. His tongue now covered in its own film of dusk, Kannon recoils it and expels a dry cough, before attempting to refresh his mouth with a glass of tepid water. As the late afternoon sun makes a special effort to stare in through the office’s window, salty perspiration blinds Kannon, prompting him to unfasten the last two buttons of his sweat-patched white shirt and wipe it around his eye sockets. “Sir,” the drawl from the homely receptionist asks, “we ask that visitors keep their shirts done up proper.” Disbelief wrinkling his face into trenches for the sweat to channel through, Kannon squints through the hazy, glowing reception. “If you didn’t have the one fan in this entire building aimed at your face,” he remarks with withering words, “then guests won’t feel like a dog locked in the back of a station wagon during a heat wave.” Just completing the sentence feels like an exertion for the Gold Patron Meritorious, and he slumps a little limper in his seat… the combination of a sweat-sodden clothing and cheap orange plastic almost sending him skidding off onto the floor. “Sir,” heavily made-up eyes narrow as a bulldog jowls frown, “as a lover of animals, I find that offensive.” “I find you offensive,” Kannon counters instantly, the heat and boredom sharpening the edges of his words. Then, if he believed the concept was anything more than a false promise to herd humanity’s bovine population into corrupt churches, Xavier would have been in heaven. The door for the Commissioner’s office swings open, and what amounts to an arctic blizzard sweeps through reception, freezing the sweat beads on Kannon’s forehead and frosting his dyed red hair. “Mr Kannon?” a phlegm-hindered, grumbling voice calls out through the dry waft of icy air. “I’ll see you now.” ==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X Basking in the full brunt of the air conditioning unit, Kannon - shirt now buttoned - looks a little heady as he feels the shivering beads of sweat retreat back into his pores. “You reported an unsanctioned bout aired on television?” The Athletic Commissioner tries hard to look as if he occupies a lofty role, despite surroundings that look like the remains of a garage sale. He sports a vibrant mustard jacket over a dark blue shirt, oddly then paired with grey trousers and brown shoes. His office doesn’t exhibit a much greater sense of coordination. Every item of furniture appears to be made from a different wood, the carpet appears to be two halves not quite matching where they meet, and from under the masses of framed photos and mounted belts, about 7 different colours of wallpaper can be seen. “Yes,” a revitalised Kannon eagerly confirms, spinning a DVD around on his index finger. “Yes I do.” Like an overly humble waiter serving his master, Kannon bows as he presents the DVD resting on his forearm to the Commissioner… gleeful expression faltering only as he stops to ponder if the office actually has as DVD player. Relief wipes any trace of anxiety from the Gold Patron Meritorious’ face as the static on the room’s ancient television set gives way to footage of PRIME’s ReVolution 164. Slightly doctored footage. The usual bright lights on PRIME’s ringside have been washed out to paint a gritty interpretation of events, the roars of the live crowd are blighted by dubbed-over drunk swearing and demands of bloodshed, while the identity of the man battling the eye-patched warrior has been obscured by blurred pixels swarming around his head. “Oh,” Kannon adds, as if he’d somehow forgotten the stack of papers bundled under his arm. “Here are some copies of the medical reports I ‘obtained’ for this show. As you will see, the half-blind freakshow they have scrapping like it was a monkey knife fight was never cleared to compete.” The Commissioner squints through the grainy footage, making out the tattoos of the opponent, and sneaking a quick look back to Kannon’s arms, exposed by the rolled up short-sleeves of his shirt. “As a fully trained professional athlete, I personally take it as an insult that such a match was permitted to take place,” Kannon offers up as judgement. “What message does it send to the children, I ask? The children! Do we spread this message through schools that it’s okay to just have a fight with the handicapped kid at recess? Is this Missouri, or is this Uzbekistan?” The heart-felt message comes complete with a pleading look to the Commissioner. “I asked myself this, Sir,” he continues, “and I told myself that THIS. IS. MISSOURI! Other states, more well known states, better represented states, more influential states, states with higher literacy rates, states people outside of here have heard of… THEY might allow such a match to stand, but not here. No. Not Missouri.” Wiping a tear from a faux-proud eye, Kannon follows through into a salute to the Commissioner. “That was you just getting pinned by him, wasn’t it?” is the less than rousing response from the Commissioner, who seems to be paying as much attention to the clock as he does the footage. “Yes,” Kannon admits, before throwing in his justification before the Commissioner can say anything further. “BUT. Imagine being in my position…” He looks to the name plaque on the desk. “…George, may I call you George? Good. George, imagine that you’ve been in the wrestling business for almost 7 years, you’ve wrestled all around the world, you’re a model professional who values nothing higher than the unspoken word between two professionals to fight the clean, fair fight. THEN imagine you’re thrust into a match with a one-eyed man, having to fight that internal, moral battle over whether or not you risk injuring them further by exploiting their disability? I was the man at risk in that match, George. I might as well have been fighting with an arm and a leg tied behind my back.” “Are you clawing at his eye here?” the Commissioner asks, rewinding the footage and checking his watch. “Ah, no! It may look like that, but…” Kannon thinks on his feet. “The bandage covering his eye had slipped and, well, I try to be a tolerant person who accepts peoples’ differences, but what can I say? It grossed me out, so I tried to pull the bandage back over it.” With his pride on the line, Kannon could sell white flags to the French. “Is that so…” the Commissioner remarks with obvious suspicion as he ejects the DVD… then unceremoniously drops it into the trash can beside his desk. “Um…” Kannon buys a little time with, not sure how to react. “I wholeheartedly concur, George! In the bin is where unsanctioned matches like that belong, so let us strike it from the records!” “Is this what you really think of the Missouri State Athletic Commission?” George bluntly asks, checking his watch and dumping papers into his briefcase. “That you can just come in here and have your losses revoked because we have nothing better to do?” “I can throw in a free Spiritual Audit,” offers XK with complete sincerity. “Mr Kannon,” is gruffly barked back at him, so that would be a negative. “I still have three amateur boxing cards to approve by tomorrow, and I now have to go home for my daughter’s birthday party and explain to her why there isn’t a clo-…” As his briefcase locks shut, a light bulb flickers on above George’s head. “You know, Mr Kannon, I think this Commission could be convinced to make a stand against the lack of pre-match medical checks…” ==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X==X Plastered-on white face paint cracks to highlight every scar and wrinkle that blights his face. Blue tears are painted flowing down his cheek, complimenting the droopy red frown that’s smeared around his mouth. Red tufts of hair stick out from under a curly green wig, and a chequered, baggy clown outfit opens the floodgates of his pores once more in the humid Missouri evening. “Mommy, I think the clown is broken!” Over the last hour, he’s had cream pies smeared onto his face, had a water pistol sprayed in his ear, been poked and prodded with sticks, kicked in the shins, been used as a trampoline, had lemonade spilt all over him, and fell off a unicycle almost fifty times. But, sat on the front porch, under the caked-on droopy frown, he’s grinning. To scratch his loss to Jonathan Rhine from the record books, this was more than worth it. To erase that moment of vengeful jubilation the half-sighted New Life must have felt pinning his shoulders down for the three count, he’d have done this every day for a month. Anyone who tells you that wrestling happens between four sets of ropes is lost in the time stream. This isn’t the 80’s. This isn’t a few hundred rabid, rain-or-shine fans crammed into a sweaty coliseum to see their athletic hero in the white trunks overcome the dastardly antics of the bad guy in the black trunks. The only honour left is honouring your multi-million dollar contract. The only history is what fans remember from last week’s show. The fans in the front row? Fuck them. That family in the second row? Who gives a shit. That old guy standing along the aisle? He. Doesn’t. Matter. Some kid sitting in his living room in South Korea during a big push into the Asian market? The others would be trampled underfoot to get him in front of his TV for ReVolution. In 2008, how do you avenge a loss? Train harder? No. Face paint and bright baggy trousers. A festering decomposition of a grand business that one was. No more fitting realm to be ruled over by it’s King of Wrestling… Xavier Kannon. It’s no business for good men. After all, he helped make it that way.
