A Dream Deferred (Born Again, Round I)

1 May 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 05/01/10 My father used to tell me stories about our Mayan ancestors. I’d curl up on his lap as he rocked back and forth in his creaking wooden chair, and I’d do my best to fend off the sleepiness as he weaved tales between heavy puffs of his cigar. He used to tell me how our people lived and how they died. He told me how they built a great empire despite the impenetrable jungles that grew between their cities. But the most interesting part, I recall with some strain on my memory, was the story of their sudden downfall. As I lay there, eyes gazing into the burning embers of our stone fireplace, he told me how a great empire, carved out of the wilderness, was crushed under the weight of its own greed. How the once flourishing culture left little trace of its former greatness, discovered later but only in the form of abandoned cities and crumbling stone temples. Needless to say, I’m familiar with the way that empires fall. Sure, not all empires are the same. They vary in certain aspects such as size, power, and longevity. But the basic story goes something like this: An empire is built on the backs of the unfortunate souls at the bottom of the social ladder. They work hard from sunrise to dusk because of their sincere belief in the bullshit fed to them by the rulers and their priest caste. But soon, not even the culturally imbedded fairy tales can cover up the fact that there’s not enough food for everybody. So the empire decides that it needs to expand, to gather more resources. Thus, the wars begin. True, it’s the poor who suffer the most in war. And true, it’s only the rich that squander the dividends of war. But there’s always a little something in it for the little guy. The promise of a better future, maybe. Another loaf of bread here and there. Or some other menial handout. But the facade does not last forever. Internal strife and external pressure begin to feed discontent from within. Soon, it all boils over and things begin to fall apart. Inevitably, the empire falls. And leaves nothing but ruin in its wake. …But then again, what does this fallen empire, a promotion like PRIME, expect? This is the same company that, during my tenure there, allowed the roster to be decimated and picked apart by lurking vultures such as Vampir Nosferatu, Chris Sloboda, and Tyler Nelson. Have they not yet learned the error of their ways? Nearly five years ago I was a defeated man. I was beaten down, depleted, and hopeless. After attempting to protect PRIME from these vultures, I was eventually forced from the very promotion that I had dedicated my life to. I was deported, to be more precise. I was booted out of the country thanks to some handy identity theft and green card-falsification by one Tyler Nelson. So fuck them, I think to myself. Fuck PRIME. They deserved what happened to them. They deserved the loss of roster members and the lower ratings they’d suffered in recent times. Like so many before and since my time, they had left people out to dry. They let a ruthless tyrant like Tyler Nelson come in and kidnap my daughter. Then they let him steal my 10% share in the company. Then they let the bastard steal my very identity and ship me off to Guantanamo Bay. If it wasn’t for my adoptive father pulling some strings, I would probably still be rotting in that hellhole today. And this was only months after I put PRIME over the top by bringing home the PTC Global Title for the very first time in the promotions’ history. Not to mention my match against Vampir Nosferatu at The Great American Nightmare. The Japanese Death Match was a grueling, vicious engagement that would go down as one of the most brutal matches in professional wrestling history. I was thrown 15-feet from the top of the PRIME-A-TRON. But did I complain? No. I could take it. Broken bones heal. Even neck bones heal. But being stripped of my 10% ownership of PRIME, disallowed to compete in the ring, and then removed from the country I had come to love? These are scars not of the flesh but of the soul. They do not quickly heal. And so what was my response last night when I received the call from PRIME’s front office concerning the upcoming Born Again tournament? What did I say when they asked me, “Emilio, will you please come back?” “Fuck it,” I said, without further consideration. “I’m in.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Emilio had not been taking it easy in the five years since his last foray into the squared-circle. In fact, at present he was on a mission so dangerous, so terrifying, and so important that it required his full and undivided attention and overwhelmed his very soul. It tested his resolve so often and with such ferocity that his special-ops assignment with the Sandinista rebels seemed like a routine chore by comparison. For although Emilio Rage had survived the brutal rape and murder of his village as a young child; although had fought in three civil wars and came out unharmed; had suffered broken bones and puncture arteries; had once even been branded by Peter-fucking-Vetra on live television; he had never before… Raised a daughter. “Oh, you are not going to school in that,” Emilio said, folding his arms across his chest. “Oh yeah,” Sylvia grinned as she gently stroked her hair with a brush. “And who’s going to stop me? A ‘professional wrestler’?” Emilio snickered. “Maybe,” he said, turning his attention back to the hot pan of scrambled eggs. “But as your father, my advice is that you change into a shirt that‘s not so tight and a dress that’s not so revealing. Unless,” he said as he flipped a pancake on the back of the grill, “you’re trying to attract unworthy suitors. Like, say, a ‘professional wrestler.’” Sylvia let out a soft chuckle and tapped her father with a soft jab in the ribs. Emilio feigned pain momentarily before he reached out and put her in a headlock. “This is the type of man you’ll attract,” he said as he lifted her above his head, “if you keep wearing clothes like that.” “That’s not so bad,” she retorted, giving her father a kiss on the cheek. Emilio set her down and turned back to the stove. He’d never admit it to her, of course. But deep down Emilio took great pride in his daughter’s beauty. Although she was only seventeen years old, her deep set cinnamon brown eyes seemed to command the authority of someone that was much older. And in light of what she’d been through in her short years on earth, she was undoubtedly beyond her years in some respects. Yes, he thought, she must appear to be quite the beauty. Her tall slender figure, her straight jet black hair, and her tanned brown skin; these features kept Emilio up late at night –had for the last several months- with the dreadful realization that he’d soon be fighting not just wrestlers in the ring but her boyfriends in the neighborhood as well. Her fashion sense –the ripped denim skirt, the tight white shirt- she must’ve got them from her mother. It certainly wasn’t from him. Ripped fatigues, swampy combat boots, and revolutionary bandanas. Does any of that say “I’m an articulate, intelligent, and beautiful young woman?” Probably not, at least in the high school hallways she roamed now. “I always thought you we’re funny, dad.” Sylvia took a seat at the dining room table and continued to taunt her father. “But you’re downright hilarious if you think you have a chance at winning Born Again,” she teased. She set back defensively and crossed her arms, preparing herself for her father’s sure-to-be-witty retort. It never came. Emilio sighed instead. “You’re probably right, dear. You’re probably right. This old body wasn’t built to take the beatings it has, let alone more of ‘em. But, as you know what they say?” Emilio asked as he passed his daughter a beautifully laid out plate of scrambled eggs, pancakes, and vegetarian sausages- her favorite breakfast. Sylvia grabbed the plate and quickly answered, “Yes. I do know what they say. They say when you’re hair gets old and gray, use Rogaine.” “What gray hair?” Emilio asked, defensively. “This one,” Sylvia said, reaching across the table to pluck a hair from Emilio’s head before he could react. The damn Jiu-Jitsu lessons, Emilio thinks. She’s getting too quick for me. With a broad grin, she hands her father the picked hair. Indeed, it is gray. Emilio can’t help but chuckle. It’s been an amazing five years for him. Instead of the constant travel and hectic pace of his past, Emilio had the rare opportunity to stop and catch his breath. On the frigid inlets of Maine’s frozen shores, Emilio had spent many hours in quiet contemplation of his past. He reflected upon his early days as a Sandanista rebel in Nicaragua, and all the poor decisions he made as a teenager. He reflected upon his work as a mercenary in his early-twenties, doing the bidding of clandestine government organizations, as well as extralegal organizations, such as the Japanese mafia. The things a man will do when he’s down on his luck and out of money, he thought, bitterly. He reflected upon his years he spent in the independent wrestling circuits across the globe, where he made a name for himself and managed a steady income, but nearly destroyed his body in the process. He reflected upon his tenure in PRIME as well, where he clawed his way to the top of the mountain but never received a chance to complete his dream of holding the Universal Title. But most of all, he reflected upon the sad day when he left his former lover, Maria, unaware that it was the last time he’d see her alive. Not knowing that she was pregnant with his daughter. And not knowing that it would take thousands of miles, weeks of mental torture, and ultimately twelve years to ever even realize that she existed. What these reflections all had in common was guilt. Guilt of leaving. Guilt of working for the wrong people. Guilt for not being persistent enough. And guilt for simply not knowing. Despite the nagging feelings in the back of his mind, there was, however, one period in his life that Emilio did not regret: the present. Emilio took the opportunity afforded to him over the the last half-decade to spend as much time with his daughter as possible. Not long after Hector Ragerio, his politically powerful father, had arranged for his release from Gitmo, Emilio seized the opportunity. For once in his life, he did things the legal way, too. He applied for citizenship, gaining it in less than a year. He went to Florida and took custody of his daughter and legally adopted her as soon as the paperwork could be filed. Lastly, Emilio moved out of the city of Boston and into the woods along Maine’s coast. The only regret he had after leaving PRIME was letting go Maya go. Well, not as much ‘letting go’ as simply not following up on his former lover. In the intervening months between his imprisonment and release, Maya had apparently taken off. When Emilio made it home that fateful night, rushing up the stairs of his apartment, he held his breath and reached optimistically for the door. The apartment was empty. And so was his crippled heart. He recovered quickly. The adoption of Sylvia and the subsequent move to their new home in country did the trick. In some ways he was even relieved. Yet, on some lonely nights he would lay on his hammock and look up at the stars –the same stars that Maya and him once shared- and contemplate what the future could have held for him is she was still in his life. But she was gone, and he was happy. And what right did he have to jeopardize that? She was probably back in Japan now, living happily in the apartment above the restaurant where they first met on that fateful day six years ago. Or so he liked to imagine. After setting down her cup of orange juice, Sylvia snapped her fingers in front of her father’s face, breaking his seemingly entwined stare-down with his plate. “Thinkin’ hard?” she asked . “Hardly thinkin’.” Sylvia rolled her eyes and got to her feet and threw the backpack over her shoulder. “Whatever, dad. I know that look.” She certainly did, he thought. “Don’t worry, you’ll do fine. You’re pretty tough,” she optimistically quipped, before narrowing her eyes and quickly adding, “for an old man, I mean.” “The Born Again tournament?” Emilio grunted and waved the thought away with a dismissive gesture. “I’m not worried about that. I’ve climbed bigger hurdles in my career. I’ve seen it all done before. Every move. Every trick. It’s not fair to the competition, really,” he began, as he stood up and underlined his point by striking a comical 80’s-style wrestling pose, flexing his biceps and looking up toward the ceiling like some invincible superhero. “The other guys don’t stand a chance.” “Please,” Sylvia retorted. “Don’t give me that. You stop worrying so much about that damn Universal Title. You’ll get your shot someday. Just…Just promise me one thing,” she began, staring intently into his eyes. “What’s that?” “That you’ll be safe.” “I promise,” Emilio said, pulling her up against his frame so that her head lay against his chest and she could feel the comforting beating of his heart. A heart that, Sylvia hoped, has millions of more beats left in it. “I promise.” With that, Sylvia planted him with a quick peck on the cheek before turning away to head off to school. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ It was only a three hour drive from his house in Maine to his old training facility Boston. But it seemed like a world away. The congested highways, the familiar smells, and the bustle of hundreds of thousands of people in the rat maze called a city. They weren’t as foreign to him as much as they were simply dreams of a distant past. A distant past that he was returning to. A world of travel. A world of intrigue. A world of monumental adrenaline rushes, heroic victories, and intense physical pain. In short, the world of professional wrestling. It was a world that had been both a blessing and a curse to him. But this time it would be different. This time he’d break the mental chains that held him back, that caused him to misplace his focus from his own goals and instead set his sights on other lofty, arbitrary duties. No more superhero, Emilio, he thought, bitterly. He no longer heard the call of duty. Emilo’s promise to Sylvia was sincere. There would be no playing around this time. His overdramatic sense of righteousness and his eternal search for justice that had landed him in too many sticky situations in the past; It simply would not do. No, this time he’d stick to his own business and focus on his goal of gaining the PRIME Universal Title. Oh, certainly he would take on any foe that had the misfortunate of standing in opposition to that goal. But beyond that, he’d keep to himself. No more heroic battles against the evils of PRIME. No more feuds, blood-wars, or otherwise physically draining and mentally taxing extracurricular activities. He was there to wrestle, and wrestle he would. Nothing else. He didn’t owe anything to PRIME. And considering what misfortunes they allowed to befall him as he waged a war in PRIME’s defense, if anything they owed him. But it was a debt that could be easily forgiven. Especially in exchange for the Universal Title. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Emilio let out a sigh as he came to the top of the stairs. He was tired from the long drive. And to be completely honest with himself, he realized with a pang of intrigue, he was also slightly winded from having rushed up three flights of stairs. That was something he’d have to remedy before the tournament began. Clearing his thoughts, Emilio reached out and turned the knob on the door to his new apartment. Or prison that was conveniently located above the gym he’d be training at for the next month, he thought, cheerfully. “Here goes nothing,” he said, opening the door. Something wasn’t right. As he stepped into the empty room, something to his left caught his eye. He swung around defensively, preparing for whatever shitty card god had inevitably dealt him this time. But it was just the wind. As the next breeze rolled in from the slightly ajar kitchen window and pulled the bathroom door the rest of the way shut, Emilio set back against the wall and allowed his body to fall to the floor. He reached up and pulled off his red bandana, letting out a sigh of relief. You’re too stuck in your old ways, old man, he thought. Paranoid, fearful, excited. But it wasn’t like that now. It doesn’t need to be like that anymore. The world he now inhabited was safe. It was relaxed. It was…predictable. A grin crept over his tired face. I should’ve taken up Mike on his offer for the joint, he realized. I need to chill the fuck out. He bit his lip, set his head between his legs and closed his eyes. And that’s when it happened. “Get on your feet.” Emilo’s eyes went wide. He was stunned. “I fucking mean it!” the voice barked, as Emilio clamored to his feet with the grace of drunken hobo. He could barely breathe now, trying to collect his scattered thoughts as he looked down the barrel of a shiny black 9-millimeter pistol. Standing not ten feet in front of him was a short, squat man sporting a white porcelain mask. It looked like something from the Phantom of the Opera, he thought. Who did I piss off now? he asked himself, cursing himself under his breath. The feds? Nelson? The Japanese mafia? He had paid off his debt to all of them. To society. To himself. And he had made an honest living for the last half-decade. So why now? Fuck me, he thought, realizing now how mentally unprepared he was to deal with the steps that he’d have to take in order to persevere his life. Nevertheless, his years of training took over and the calm set in, relaxing his tensed muscles. Here we go, he thought, bitterly. The man had a sleek black suit, with matching black slacks held up by what appeared to be a solid gold belt, and…dirty white Nike’s? “Surprise!” the masked man yelled, tossing the gun aside. “Mother fucker!” was Emilio’s response as he leapt through the air and closed the gap in one stride. “I should’ve known better! Patch, you son-of-a-bitch,” he yelped as he playfully tackled the man. “Yep. Shoulda,” said Patch, pulling Emilo into his guard with the sleek speed and grace that only a man of his training could. “But how?” Emilio asked as he helped his longtime friend back to his feet. Patch reached into his pocket and retrieved his cell phone. “Remember Vegas?” he asked as a mischievous gleam formed in his eyes. How could I forget, Emilio thought, but did not say it out loud. Although considering the variety and amount of alcohol consumed, ‘how could I remember’ was probably a better question. “Yes. Yes, I do,” Emilio admitted. Apparently he had drunk too much that night and provided his friends -Patch among them- with the cell phone numbers of some essential PRIME office personnel. Patch reached out and punched Emilio in the shoulder with such force that the former pro-wrestler winced. “Nah, I’m juts fucking with ya again! I’ve been following PRIME lately, heard rumor you might be coming back.” “But how did you know-” “Bob. Patch said, referring to the landlord who owned both the apartments and the gym underneath. “Really? Do you think that guy could keep his yap shut?” Emilio rolled his eyes. “Should’ve known.” He then extended a hand to Patch who shook it accordingly. “But it’s good to see you, my old friend.” Then, there was the moment of awkward silence which Patch took as a sign, reaching into a brown paper bag where he pulled out a twelve-pack of ice cold Labatt Blues. He tossed one to Emilio who caught it in one hand before tossing it back to Patch. “No, it’s all good…” Emilio began, pointing down to his stomach where his gut protruded slightly from under his shirt. “I’ve got a tournament to train for.” “That you do, my friend. That you do.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The night before ReVolution 222, Emilio could not sleep. He kept tossing and turning in his bed. He had just trained intensely for the last 26 days. He had successfully shed the pounds that he’d put on in his years of physical apathy. He had raised his cardio to new levels that he hadn’t before thought possible. He had perfected old moves and had worked out the kinks of a few new ones, too, complimenting an immense arsenal of technical pain. He was quite possibly in the best shape of his life. But still, he could not shake off the nerves. Is it because he feared losing? No. He was far too prepared for that thought. While the other wrestlers had their skulls beaten in at the Dual Halo, he had wisely built up his strength. Maybe it was the giddiness of coming back to the company itself, of once again basking in the cheers of tens of thousands of roaring PRIME-ates. But what’s to fear about that? Or maybe, just maybe it was just quiet recognition of the fact that this time around, it just might happen. That this time he would not leave, would not relent, would not be moved until that ever elusive gold was strapped firmly around his itching waist. Or maybe he just wanted to crack some skulls again. Yeah. That was probably it.
