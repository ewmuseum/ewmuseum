Pipe Down

26 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 26, 2008 “Just grant me the interview.” Bill Frisell's Nashville. Pretty album. Fits, kinda. “Why do you need to interview me?” “I don't.” “So drop it.” “But you do. We pay for our interviews.” “I don't need money.” “I know you don't. That's not what this is about.” “So tell me what this is about. I want to know. 'Cause you're just—” “Easton—“ “—just jerking me around here, you know?” “No, I'm not.” “'It's this, it's that.' You gotta come at me straight or I can't tell you a damn thing.” “I am—” “Making it real hard.” “Come on, Easton, don't dance.” I'm good at dancing. Sometimes. “Just remind me what the fuck I have to say to Rolling Stone.” “You've got... an opinion, you know?” She's got two left feet. “I've got an opinion.” “Mudhoney? Who enters to Mudhoney? And Kyuss? What were you coming in to when you won the HIW title?” “I don't remember.” “Wasn't it High on Fire?” “Could be.” “'Blessed Black Wings,' yeah. Who enters to that?” “People with taste.” “Exactly.” So maybe I'm not so good. “You already talked to Ferguson?” “Why would I talk to Ferguson?” Why would she talk to Ferguson. Like I have to say. “I haven't got shit to say. Talk to Troy, she's on top.” “So? I don't want to talk to Troy.” “Why not? She's got a hell of a lot more charisma or whatever, you know. Good on the stick, quick, witty, all of that bullshit. That's what you want, isn't it?” “Yes, and no. Troy's too good.” “And what about me? Are you saying I'm not good?” “Like goody-two-shoes good.” “You seen me pull any evil shit ever?” “Easton—” “Hear me out here. You write a magazine that aging rockers read—” “Hey...” “—but you want a guy like me, a guy who has done diddle-shit. You want me to talk to these... people? You want me to put on my ass-kissing boots for them? I shoved those boots aside a long time ago.” “You're not very funny.” “This is bullshit. I want no part of this, I'm hanging up the phone.” “Easton!” Click. Ring. Ring. Ring. “Hello?” “Easton—” “What! do you want!?” “Easton, what do you think the 5-Star title is? Is that diddle-shit? Is that what you think?” “It's a belt they put on has-beens like Jason Snow at the end of his career as a goddamn gimme. They put it on nobodies like Tyler fucking Rayne and Easton fucking Hall.” “Nobody thinks that.” “Everybody thinks that!” Everybody thinks that. “Nobody does, Easton, listen.” “No, I'm hanging up.” “Easton!” “What!?” “Do you not fucking get it?” “Get what!? Get that I've busted my ass for—” “Easton, people—” “—ten-some years, still not getting fucking respect? Just barely losing every big goddamn chance? I beat Devin Shakur and does anybody fucking care? No, because Devin Shakur is nobody, just like Rayne and just like Dusk and just like all of that shit! It's not gonna get me any closer to Troy or to Ferguson or to... fucking anyb—” “People like you, Easton, is that hard to understand? Are you just not used to it? What? What's the problem?” “Do I give a shit about people?” “What's the problem here, Easton?” Am I supposed to soul-search? “There's no fucking problem.” “You're not getting a cover story, Easton. Is that what you're worried about? You worried about becoming some kinda star?” “Why would I be worried about that?” “You sound pretty worried.” “That's ridiculous.” “Yeah? So grant me the interview.” Beat. “Why?” “Because you've got something to say, a little bit at least, and you need somewhere to say it. There's gonna be some overlap between the aging rocker demographic and the homoerotic voyeur demographic.” “Is that what you think this is about?” Beat. “Won't know unless I ask you about it.” “Fuck you.” Click.” Ring. Ring. Ring. “Do I get my interview?” “Yeah, you'll get your interview.”
