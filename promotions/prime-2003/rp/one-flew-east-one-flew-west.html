One Flew East, One Flew West

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Some Date Previously, on The Forsaken… Team B Member: The rafts are there because this is an island. If something goes wrong and the helicopter doesn’t make it back, this is our only way out of here. Farwell: Oh. Aren’t there sharks in these waters? Team B Member: Of course. --- Walkins: Oh, no! Grandpa! Wake up! WAKE UP! --- Wisker: We lost Martinez! Team B Member: Martinez! --- Clyde’s Girlfriend: I love you, Clyde. Over these past few days that I have known you, you’ve captivated my heart. Your childish sense of humor…your girlish giggle… Walkins: Teeheehee. Clyde’s Girlfriend: Yes, that’s the one. --- Clyde’s Girlfriend: Clyde…I don’t think…I have long left. Walkins: Don’t say that! You can’t die. Angels can’t die! They can’t! --- Farwell: I’m back, motherfucker! --- Black Guy 1: What’s he doing? Black Guy 2: He’s stompin’ the yard! Black Guy 3: I ain’t never seen steppin’ like dis befo’! --- Walkins: The doctor said I was autistic. --- And now, the conclusion… “…Over two years have passed since the incident at Raccoon Island. I’ve been hiding in France with a group of anti-Parasol operatives, and together we’ve been able to keep the C-Virus from falling into the wrong hands. My old life is over, but here I have found new purpose…” Clyde stopped writing in his journal long enough to place a hand on the unopened briefcase, staring hard at it. The source of so much trouble, and for what? It seemed Parasol had long ago ceased their hunt for it. All this fighting, all this hiding, and even the loss of his best friend amounted to saving the world from a menace that may never have existed. Raccoon Island was long forgotten. And so was James. A soft knock on the door brought his focus back to the task at hand. Into his room walked a black woman wearing what appeared to be some sort of police outfit, but what drew the eye to her was her ample derrière. And not just a regular ghetto booty, mind you, but a straight-up badonkadonk. Clyde: Labadonque! You’re early. Labadonque: Mais oui. Clyde, zere ees somezeeng you must see. Quick to get out of his chair, Clyde ran ahead of Labadonque, sensing something important was afoot…and also not wanting to get stuck in the doorway beside her gigantic ass, Three Stooges-style. What he saw when he stepped outside was almost more than he could have ever hoped for. Clyde: James! Rushing forward to give his friend a hug, Clyde found himself thwarted by a palm to the chest. The angry look in James’ eyes said it all. James: Two years and you didn’t even bother to look for me?! Clyde: I saw you get eaten. I thought you were dead… James: Well, I wasn’t! You left me to rot on an exploding fucking island! Clyde looked down at the ground, grinding the toe of his shoe into the pavement. Clyde: Sooo…how’ve you been? James: How’ve I been? HOW’VE I BEEN?! They framed me for the explosion at the facility! Because of you, I wound up in jail for sixteen months. And you’ll never believe what happened to me while I was there. The most delightful thing. Clyde: You befriended a ragtag band of inmates and assembled them on a football team to play against the guards, proving that the brotherhood of man can overcome all obstacles? James: I was raped. --- Here’s the story of two brothers, just like Rick and Scott, Except one of them’s retarded, and the other one’s not, James got blown up and eaten by zombies, Clyde floated on a seal raft through the Seven Seas, You thought they were dead, but they’re still alive, It’s gonna take more than that to kill… James and Clyde, James and Clyde, James and Clyde, James and Clyde! --- The roar of the engines reminded Clyde that he was leaving the place he called home for nearly two years. As he looked out the window from a thousand feet in the air, he couldn’t help but reminisce about all the good times he had with his new friends. Labadonque, the meter maid with a penchant for stopping crime by way of her plentiful buttocks. The ravenous Manda, a half-man half-panda genetic experimentation of the C-Virus gone horribly awry. Greg, their janitor. Together, they had all but eliminated the Parasol threat, and now finally felt safe enough to let down their guard. Clyde: He would miss them, but he knew it was time to move on with his life. Distracted from his book, James looks over with an expression of equal parts annoyance and concern. James: Are you dictating your thoughts in the third-person again?! Clyde: It’s for my autobiography! James sighed. James: Speaking of ridiculous ideas, what’d you do with that serum, anyway? I couldn’t help but notice you ran off with it and the only life raft on the island the first chance you got. Clyde: I was shot! James: And I got blown up and eaten by zombies, everyone heard the song. That doesn’t change the fact that you ditched me, so the least you could do is something worthwhile with it. Clyde avoided eye contact, suddenly looking everywhere but at James. James: …At least tell me you sold it to terrorists and made millions off my suffering. Clyde: Close, but kinda not. We’ve been keeping it out of the wrong hands since we joined up, and we’ve likely eliminated the last traces of Parasol in the process. Sure, it’s all but drained our savings, but it was worth it for the peace of mind in knowing that the world is safe another day. James: So you’re telling me that not only have you not made any sort of profit or gained any semblance of power with the most destructive virus in the history of the world, but you’ve spent all our money doing exactly the opposite? Clyde: Well, not all of it. I spent our last five dollars on this headset for the in-flight movie. They’re showing A Charlie Brown Christmas. James’ left eye started to twitch slightly, and an escalating rage could be seen in his visage. Clyde: I thought you were dead! --- As the plane landed and they collected their luggage, both men knew something drastic needed to be done to keep from becoming homeless. Clyde: Seriously, I thought you were dead. I’d have never done something like that if you were alive. James glared at Clyde. James: We both know that isn’t true. Picking up their suitcases, they each pondered upon a way to get to wherever they’d now call home. A cab was out of the question, but Clyde saw something that just might work. Clyde: Follow my lead. Approaching a nearby limousine, Clyde walked straight up to the driver, who held a sign in his hands reading ‘Queen Latifah’. James stopped walking and just looked around with shame in his eyes, muttering about how the plan would never work. Clyde: Ooh, child! Aren’t you a cold drink of water on a hot summer’s day! Now help momma with her bags, all this walking has wreaked havoc on my edema. The limo driver just narrowed his eyes in suspicion, eyeing Clyde up and down. Something wasn’t right here, but he couldn’t quite put his finger on it. Driver: If you’re Queen Latifah, what’s the premise of your next movie? Clyde: Umm…uhhh…I play a former basketball coach that tries to teach chimpanzees the value of teamwork and sportsmanship, but they’re the ones who end up teaching me. About myself. Clint Eastwood’s signed on to direct. Driver: Wow, word for word. Right this way, Your Majesty. James looked baffled, but he couldn’t argue with results. As the driver returned from putting their bags in the trunk, he opened the back door for them and led Clyde in first, kissing the back of his hand. Clyde giggled and blushed slightly. James died a little more inside. Inside the limousine, Clyde turned to James with a big shit-eating grin on his face. Clyde: From rags to riches in under five minutes. That’s a new record. James: We’re not rich, dumbass. We’re riding around in a stolen limo, and there’s only so long we can do that before the jig is up. Where are we going to, anyway? Clyde: Before we left, Labadonque told me about a former anti-Parasol operative working in Peachtree City. Apparently, he’s deep, deep, deep undercover. James: And he’ll take us in? Clyde: Iunno. But doesn’t a road trip sound like a blast? --- Clyde: I said I was sent here by Labadonque. Can you help us? The room smelled like dead hippie. Ashtrays and beer cans littered what could best be called a table, but would more accurately be described as three phonebooks placed side-by-side atop a row of cinderblocks. Cannabis and Che Guevara posters inhabited the parts of the walls not infested with various types of stains and possible mold samples. And all the while, a lone lamp sat next to a couch dating back to when Carol O’Connor was in his prime, emitting a low hum. Probably faulty wiring. A man with unkempt long hair and a straggly beard sat on that couch, squinting his bloodshot eyes and cupping a hand to his ear. Stoner: What’s that, man? I can’t hear you over the buzz of Jesus. Clyde and James exchanged confused looks and shrugged at each other. Meanwhile, the stoner smiled at the lamp and nodded, while in his mind Jesus winked at him and gave him a thumbs-up. Jesus: Keep it cool, kid. Stoner: Right on, man. Totally! Both men still shared their confusion, now shaking their heads and staring at the lamp. Suddenly, footsteps from upstairs began descending into the basement, following the sound of a door being closed. An elderly woman’s voice called down before the footsteps reached the bottom. Woman: Joshua, are you there? I need help bringing in the groceries. Stopping when she noticed Clyde and James, the surprise in the woman’s face turned to a smile. Woman: Oh, visitors! Joshua, you never told me you were having friends over. James: I’m sorry, ma’am. We were actually looking for someone named Frederick, but it looks like we have the wrong house. Woman: Oh, Fred’s my husband. Clyde nudged James in the ribs with the point of his elbow, as if to say ‘told ya I was right’. In return, James slapped him in the back of the head, responding with a wordless ‘no one cares’. James: When will he be home? We need to speak with him and it’s pretty urgent. Woman: He won’t. Fred died last year. A pause ensued, as no one knew how to follow up something like that. Of course, Clyde could always be counted on to shatter an awkward silence. Clyde: So…you’re saying you’re single? James just looked horrified.
