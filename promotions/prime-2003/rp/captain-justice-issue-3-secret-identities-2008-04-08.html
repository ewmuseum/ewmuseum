Captain Justice, Issue #3: Secret Identities.

8 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-8-08 The funny thing about being a hero? Nobody really CHOOSES to be a hero. Nobody wants to genuinely put their mind, body, and soul on the line for the greater good, but rather we find ourselves forced into these delicate situations where being brave and being the foundation for the lesser is the only alternative. This is a story that doesn’t involve having somebody getting rescued from the evil monster, foiling a bank robbery, or taking a nice, hot dip in a vat of ooze and becoming an Adolescent Hybrid Samurai Toad™ set out to rid the world of its evils. This is simply a story that begs a question: When the chips are down, who's left to save the heroes? I FRIDAY AFTERNOON, 4:49 PM Most early-April days in the city of Burien, Washington, found themselves on the receiving end of hideous gray clouds taunting the residents below with trickles of rain before the heavier falls came later in the day. This day would be no exception as the heavy breeze only aided the chaos outside, tacking on a biting Arctic cold to an already chilly downpour, rattling windows, trees, and kicking up debris every which way in the street. Already, talks of summer were amidst the populace of 33rd Place, longing for better days and the freedom to travel without rain hindering their progress. Residents were quarantined to their abodes as they could do nothing more than watch yet another day they could’ve used to run their errands get washed away by the horrendous conditions outside. The only thing making their particular situation worse was it was Friday afternoon when the normalcy of a 9-to-5 work week gave way to letting one wind down for the weekend before starting all over again. Instead, they found themselves trapped; Friday night plans getting cancelled eleventh-hour and leaving them with no choice but to lament their choice of habitat. The SOLD sign posted outside a nice mahogany-colored two-story was swaying to and fro, doing its best to withstand the dreaded elements. From within the aforementioned house, two particular sets of eyes peer out the living room window, watching the little sign jut around, taking bets about whether or not it could hold up much longer in the heavy gusts. “You know, Soren, we really SHOULD be unpacking,” the voice of Steve Knox blurted out, but still finding himself oddly captivated by that little sign. “We should really be doing SOMETHING to help out Rachel. If she comes home another day and sees that you ain’t done SHIT for unpacking, I don’t care how big you are; she’s gonna have your nuts in a blender.” “Dude,” his older brother whined. “The last truck isn’t even HERE yet.” “Do you know where procrastination gets you? Nowhere. Especially with that big, fancy wrestling… wait, I’m sorry…” He threw up overdone air quotes. “’Sports Entertainment’ career you got going on now.” “Hey,” Soren interjected, ceasing Steve’s attempt at a go-get-em-tiger motivational speech. Steve, a successful indy guy in his own right, winning titles all over the world at a very young age, was an expert in such matters. Not only that, but a sought-after commodity all over the Greater 48 because of such traits. “Are you aware we’ve got this shit backwards? Shouldn’t *I* be the one giving you the ‘rah, rah, rah’ speeches? You know, big brothering it up?” “Hey… who’s won more titles between us?” “Shutty. Who’s the one with the “big, fancy Sports Entertainment career” now?” “Pfft, please,” Steve scoffed. “If you want to dance around in underwear and flex muscles all night like you’re in the closet, that’s your problem, not mine.” “Oooh, the gay card. But, hey, I’d rather wear a mask and make gobs of money that pay for nice two-story houses and expensive engagement rings than trade headlocks in front of twenty-thirty people for pennies on any given night.” “That’s ALMOST good, but hey, we’ve got a PPV deal and we’re broadcast on over fifty-million homes now, so I’m doing just fine, thank you. Plus, what you and your fiancé do with wrestling masks after dark ain’t my problem. And besides, I didn’t HAVE to fly out here from Providence to see the place and help you move.” Both shared a heavy laughing fit, almost enough to make the tears pour before Soren relaxed and rubbed an eye. “Oooh, now the bedroom card. Well played, sir.” Beaming with victory for the moment, Steve’s eyes followed the raindrops pummeling the window before shifting his gaze to a stack of about five or six boxes. He took one of them and hurled it at Soren, catching it in his gigantic mitts. “Look at you, bro,” Steve began as he unpacked one of the boxes and emptied it on the coffee table. “You’ve got yourself a brand new job that’s gonna rake in the cash, you and Rachel are finally gonna tie the knot after six years – took you guys long enough.” “Indeed.” “So…” he studied Soren’s face, watching it contort into an unknowing grimace. “What’s bugging you.” “Dunno. It’s…” He tossed the box down before throwing himself back into the sofa. “Nerves, I suppose. Things are happening so fast. This time two months ago, if you were to tell me Rachel were to finally be able to afford a house, an engagement ring, and me being on the road a lot, being managed by Sonny Silver, it’s… it’s a lot to take in. I’m getting married in November, then I gotta worry about the road, and everything that goes on there…” He sighs. “Like I said… a lot to take in.” Unsure of what to say to Soren, Steve found himself equal parts confused and awkward, not sure whether to place an errant hand on his shoulder or give him another pep talk or what. Pep talks were easy, but Steve downright HATED the “emotional” moments. Pondering his next course of action to take, Steve patted him on the shoulder once, albeit uncomfortably. “Dude… I… uh… know it’s tough, but… hey, you pick yourself up… good.” Steve found himself instilled with a little more confidence as he started to articulate his thoughts. “You land on your feet like a cat, you and Rachel are meant to be, you know she understands, so quit your bitching.” Lightly chortling under his breath, Soren caught Steve completely off-guard. “What’s so funny?” Steve inquires. “You’re fucking terrible at heart-to-hearts, you know that?” He punctuated the statement with a kick of his foot on the table before erupting into another fit of laughter. Steve turned red in the face before pursing his lips together. “You’re a dick.” Steve grunted before he turned back to the boxes again. “Okay, SERIOUSLY… we need to get our asses in gear. This can’t stand.” Soren brushed the sentiments off with a wave of his hand. “Will you get the stick out of your ass, dude? We’ve still got me a little bit of ti…” BEEP! BEEP! BEEP! “…Goddamnit.” Irony decided to dish out Soren a mighty big bitchslap as the last of the moving trucks slowed along the main road before gently backing into the driveway. Meanwhile, Soren tried to fathom what side dish would perfectly complement the crow he’d be having later. “Told you,” Steve jabs. “Shutty,” was Soren’s only reply before he tossed on his jacket, ready to brave the Washington weather one more time. II STILL FRIDAY AFTERNOON, 5:35 pm A grunt escaped from the lips of Rachel Gwen as she contemplated blasting her horn along with the cacophony of other sounds alongside I-5 Traffic. With thousands of people eager to up and get the fuck out of Seattle to home and their loved ones, the long stretch of Interstate was more congested than a sickly man’s nose and evenly nasty. Each horn, each shout, each wave of the windshield wiper scraping against the glass, and each rumble of the engine did nothing to calm down her hot temper. “Comeoncomeoncomeon…” She peered to the neon green timer display marked “5:35.” Ugh. She was supposed to be home around 4:30 along with the last of the trucks. It was terrible enough she had to deal with a lot of HR hullabaloo and sign off on seven punch corrections at the last minute just because some newbie morons working at Puget Sound Pipe couldn’t fucking figure out how to use the time clock correctly. The radio’s ability to block out noise was on par with a coloring book to a blind man. The only programs on this time of day that were of any use were tacky rock music, THAT idiotic Soulja Boy song, and liberal talk radio. Given she wasn’t a punker, an ugly thug bitch who has no right to wear “CUTIE” on her faded t-shirt, or a left-wing whackjob, she found herself with no alternative but to be immersed into the madness around her. Running her fingernails along the leather interior of the dashboard, she decideed to make an attempt at getting hold of Soren. A few minutes with the future hubby would help her deal with the madness. Digging through her purse, she rummaged through its contents in search of her cell phone. Make-up. Loose change. Wallet. A… Rolo? (The candy, not the wrestler. That’d be a big-ass purse.) Ah, there! Grabbing her LG Trax, she flips it open and hits 2 on the Speed Dial, holding it up to her ear. The phone buzzed several times and the voice of Soren got to her, but it wasn’t exactly what she’s sought… “Hi, I’m Soren Knox. You know me from such phones as… well, this one. Leave a message.” …Damn it. Flipping the phone shut, she gallingly tossed it into the passenger seat. The grinding of the windshield wipers on full blast only increases the throbbing headache she was now cursed with, so she reaches for her Advil. Reaching for her Dasani bottle in the cup holder, she popped a pill in her mouth and is about to drink… BEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEP! In her reverie, she’d failed to notice traffic got going a little more and found herself the victim of a rather livid driver behind him in a Chevy Avalanche. After coughing up her aspirin and taking a respite to gather her air, she settles herself just enough to roll down her window in the face of the freak storm battering her fellow travelers… “ASSHOLE! I ALMOST CHOKED, YOU SON OF A BITCH!” She rolls up the window to drown the sounds of whatever comebacks or nonsense the simpleton behind her could muster. Feeling the last threads of sanity starting to unravel, she pulls the car forward and begins to accelerate as much as humanly possible. Her own quiet sobs seemed to occur in tune with the movement of the wipers. It shouldn’t have gone down like this. It should be her at home with Soren right now, unpacking the rest of their stuff. Not wrestling with her belongings, screaming at dipshits or nearly choking on her aspirin. Things should just be better, she didn’t need this bullshit. Rachel was supposed to be strong. She was the one that was in control all the time. She remembered so many times when Soren confessed one of the things he loved about her was ability to take charge of a situation and make it her own. She frowned before fixating her stare on the road ahead as she continued to rev the engine. Silently praying, Rachel merely hoped things would get easier and that this wasn’t some horrid sign of things to come. III FRIDAY EVENING, 6:04 PM Yuck. As Sonny watched his reflection in the mirror crinkle its nose, he realized that he looked utterly terrible this evening. Not physically, mind you. In his head, he was still the incredibly sexy alpha male that people fought to be like in their lifetimes, but something else was amiss. Wait… Riiiiiiiight. Clinkclinkclink. Attaboy, Sonny chuckled to himself as he dropped three ice cubes into his Scotch glass. The bitter ice burned his teeth and the Scotch, as always, seared his throat even worse, but he ignored it for the moment as he reached for the newly-tailored Mr. Silver costume he was set to debut very soon. Inspecting every inch of the suit for any abnormalities, tears, wrinkles, what have you, the nagging feeling in his stomach would not subside. Bzzzzzt. Bzzzzzt. Bzzzzzt. The phone. It’d been ringing off the hook for somewhere in the neighborhood of fifteen minutes, but he’d paid it no mind. This was his alone time; time to kick back and unwind. But there would be no kicking back of any kind and if he were any more wound, he’d be walking around his Seattle home like a Happy Meal toy. Sonny’s mind wandered to these last few weeks spent with the Knox kid. Soren, and the events leading up to him entering his life. When he was fed up with how PRIME was treating him earlier in the year and knew that now matter how he tried to avoid the inevitable screwjob that would transpire because of Blaine Blair and Lisa Tyler, he made a fight-or-flight decision to walk out on his contract. No longer would be subjugated to their iron clutches or their little “rules.” So he walked out and that would be it. For about a week. A manila envelope was sent to his doorstep from Prometheus, LLC., in it a brand new contract calling out for a managerial role. However, a proviso in the form of “We want Mr. Silver, Chairman of PRIME, not the Rogue SOB that nearly tore down the federations walls many times over” was attached to said contract. Yet another fight-or-flight decision was made where he had 72 hours to reply: either suck it up, pucker your lips and kiss some hardcore Prometheus ass or stand by your convictions until you become as penniless as the fans you ridicule weekly. Soren Knox was far and away the best student he’d trained. The kid didn’t have much in the way of ring psychology, but when it came to charisma – more importantly, crowd psychology – he had a Masters from Harvard. He also had that look and that passion that Sonny once had… maybe it was a soft spot, but that’d just be hearsay. For now, Sonny knew this kid had the “it” factor that would truly translate into an abundance of success, given he had the right outlet. Most importantly, Soren didn’t ask for a penny more than what Sonny proposed to him. Sonny would’ve given this kid the moon and the stars to allow him to represent him, but he asked for little-to-nothing in return; only to be shown everything Silver had been taught. He felt his stomach do more knots as he set down his Scotch glass. Gently hurling himself into the black leather couch, he cursed to himself. What the fuck had he done? Soren Knox was a very laid-back, easy-going guy, genuinely into every aspect of this business for its fans, not because of money, a TV deal, or any of the fancy-pants world indy kids could even dream of. Every nicety the kid exuded was neither disingenuous nor condescending. He was a rarity for this business: pure. And Sonny’s evil would no doubt taint it one day. No. Sonny’s head bolted up, still ignoring the shrewd vibrations of his cell phone in order to process his predicament. There was still hope for that kid. If Sonny could guide him to the Universal Title fast enough… maybe, JUST MAYBE, Sonny would leave him be. When he won that title, he’d be his own man. Sonny wouldn’t be around him all that long. But, he’d have to do this carefully. If the kid knew any of the rumors surrounding Sonny’s very being were true, his plan would go up in smoke. Yes, he told himself. This was the right thing to do. For Soren’s sake, he’d better win that title sometime soon. A victory over a PRIME Hall of Famer and a former Jewel in the Crown winner would get him on the right path. I’m not a bad guy. I CAN do this for him. He won’t be like me. Bzzzzzt. Bzzzzzt. Bzzzzzt. For what had to be the umpteenth time, he watched his cell phone vibrate across the kitchen counter, snarling as he could make out the “XK” initials flashing on the surface. The bastard was probably calling in to bitch about what went down on ReVolution 156 when the fabled “Enemigo Play” rolled him craps. He wanted so badly not to answer that phone and continue to put Soren through this… but… that kid was his ticket to be welcomed back without having to eat crow upon his “retirement” from the business. He could manage him to victory and finally be done with PRIME. Plus, it was either back to the spotlight where he belonged or another night of emotional self-immolation to deal out. And he wasn’t a big fan of that Emo shit. BEEP. Sigh. Back to work. “Mr. Silver, Sports Entertainment Liaison to PRIME and home of the Whopper. What’s your beef…?"
