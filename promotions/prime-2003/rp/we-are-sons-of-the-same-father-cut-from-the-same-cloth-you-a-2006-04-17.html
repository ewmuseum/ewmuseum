We Are Sons Of The Same Father, Cut From The Same Cloth You And I

17 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4-17-06 Okay, fine. So I haven’t been in a hardcore match for about four years. And in my career I’ve been in what, two? Three, maybe? I’m just not a blood-and-gore type of guy. I never have been. But…I am INTENSE. And Vangelus, or whoever booked our matchup, chose to play to my strengths. Steel cage? Bad. Ladder? Great. But I’m intense, alright. Name another superstar who’s been booked EVERY SINGLE WEEK since the Great American Nightmare? Who’s wrestled every PRIME show since the year started? Me, baby. Me. I mean, nothing’s more intense than dying, right? * * * * * Spokane’s not too terribly far from the North Pacific Wrestling Academy in Seattle. And this is where Kyle Lamen and Jason Core are in a ring, stretching out, and talking. “Kid, you’ve gotta understand…this isn’t what…” Jason Core’s voice is cut off by Kyle Lamen’s, full of fervor. “Jason, are you kidding me? Are you honest-to-God kidding me?” Jason was more than a little stunned by his protégé’s outburst. “Tell me that you’re kidding me.” Kyle turned away from Jason, and pulled his arm up over his head, and across his back, stretching out his shoulder. “When we get down to it, it’s a ladder match. All I have to do is get to the top of the ladder. Forget all the in-betweens.” “Kid, you’re not all that good at climbing ladders.” Jason stood nose-to-nose with Kyle, close enough so that he could breathe his hot breath right in Kyle’s face. “It always seems you get tripped up when you’re about to reach the top.” The words stung, and Kyle backed away slowly. “Jason, I know I keep losing it at the big moment…but I took out Karina, I took out Archibald MacGregor, and I’m DAMN sure going to take out Vangelus Olsig tomorrow.” Jason backed off, and smirked a bit. His weathered, and slightly creased face crinkling along the crow’s feet aside his eyes. “Oh yeah, Kyle?” He crouched a little low, and with a quick motion, pulled his shirt off and tossed it out of the ring. “Show me.” Kyle threw himself at Jason with a corkscrew kick, and Jason took it hard on the side of his arm, purposefully. Kyle crashed to the mat, but Core still stood, at least until he fell upon Kyle like the hammer of the gods, raining big fists on the prone Lamen. “Jason, what the fuck!?” One right hand potatoed Lamen right above the eye, his teacher showing no signs of stopping or slowing. “Unfortunately, inside you is the blood…hnfff…of the greatest family of brawlers in wrestling. The Cretin Family. Hrrrr. The Lamen Brothers.” Kyle brought his hands up to try and cover his face instinctively, and it absorbed a few of the blows. It gave Kyle the impetus to pull a leg off and shove Core away, allowing him to kip up to wobbly feet. “Show me the things I never taught you. Show me the you from the bar fight, except without trying to armdrag that drunk kid hitting on Miho.” He breathed heavy. Kyle breathed harder. “Show me the Lamen in you.” Kyle thought back to the few experiences he had with his father and uncle, as the air fought for time in his lungs. He took a weary step towards his friend and mentor, who eyed him with trust and mild disappointment. “Jason, I don’t…” Kyle’s foot hurtled into Jason Core’s midsection, and the wind rushed clear out of Jason’s stomach. The young Infinite Gauntlet Champion tried to throw a few punches around Jason’s face, but they didn’t work, and in a fight, you use what works. So instead, he grabbed Core by his head, and drove a few knees into Jason’s forehead. The first almost knocked him over, and the second would’ve if Kyle hadn’t steadied him for a third. Core tumbled to the mat in a heap, but half a laugh escaped his split lips. “If your father could see you…brawling like this…” Kyle smiled slightly in pride. “…he’d be awfully disappointed!” Core’s good leg snuck out and kicked Kyle in the leg, dropping him to one knee. The massive Core rose like an ocean wave and cracked his forearm into Kyle’s face. But this time, the blow didn’t knock Kyle over. Kyle fired back one of his own. “C’mon…Jason. You wanted a fight, now let’s do this.” And Kyle fought, fought like a brawler, like a fighter instead of a wrestler, for the first time. * * * * * Outside the NPWA’s main entrance – which was a wide garage door - an older, but lanky man stood in the chilly rain, watching Jason Core and Kyle Lamen spar in the center of the ring. Though they fought, they carried themselves like family. They watched out for one another, even while delivering stern blows to the head, and the shoulders of each other. It reminded him more than a little of how he used to handle his big brother Brad. Only meaner. With light tubes, and thumbtacks, and lots and lots of pain. They could beat the hell out of each other, and afterwards, like he was sure the kid and the older guy would, they’d go out and be family, and be closer than close. They’d drink beers, and pick up girls, and understand. But Brad was off doing something-or-other, so it was just the kid. That’s the only family left now. He wanted to walk into the training warehouse, slide into the ring like he used to, skidding halfway across the mat then bounding to his feet. He’d shake Kyle’s hand, then kick him in the gut like a good heel should. They’d do the catch-as-catch can thing for a while, feeling out, then get into the hard and mean of it. The part of wrestling that’s raw and intense. The part that his family always had an inclination for, bred into them on the mean streets of Chicago’s South Side. His blood rose, his hands tensed, and his feet even itched a little, inside his damp and ratty old shoes. Even if the rest of his body had forgotten the thrill of the wrestling ring It was no longer his place in the ring. And it was never his place at all, with his son. Jason Core was more of a father than he’d ever had the chance or even the inclination to be. The adrenaline slowed, and the blood pounded softer. The arthritis and war wounds throbbed, memories of the battles won and lost. Johnny took a final moment to scratch at the bead of water that escaped the hair behind his ear, before finally saying something. Instead of the loud greeting he had planned, it was spoken only to himself. “Ah, hell.” And with that, Johnny Lamen took the hastily-wrapped present he was carrying for his son Kyle, the Lamen family fluorescent light tubes, and wandered off, down the misty Seattle streets, and alone. * * * * * ‘The Dialogue Between Fathers And Sons’ Oh father, you don’t know.How the day will run.How the wind will churn.How the twilight breaks.Oh father, you don’t know.When the sun will rise.When we’ll trod the grass.When the day will start. Oh son, you don’t know.How the rain does fall.How the sky does crackHow your feet will slip.Oh son, you don’t know.When we’re out of touch.When the time has gone.When the end will come. * * * * * I think that’s why you bother me so much, man. It’s like looking into a life-size mirror that’s gone warped and wrong somewhere. The risks, the passion, the pride. Don’t think I don’t see a lot of myself when I look at you…flaws, strengths and all the dross. But don’t think I won’t recognize our differences. The differences that make me greater. We’ve both fallen into darkness, Vangelus. I’ve just had the strength to find a light. We’re both sons, Vangelus. I’ll just be beyond my father’s spectre. We’re both addicts, Vangelus. I’ve just already kicked the habit. We’re both killers, Vangelus. I’m just killing myself to live. We’re both dying, Vangelus. I’m just doing it faster. We’re both going to enter ReVolution 92 with dreams of walking out, under our own power, with the PRIME Intense Championship. Cementing our legacies. Claiming the glory due us. … I’m just the one who will do it.
