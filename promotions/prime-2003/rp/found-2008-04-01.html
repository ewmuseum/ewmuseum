Date: April 1, 2008 I'm not sure what to expect as I climb the concrete steps to the three storey former apartment building and current Phan Hiroshi Ultimate Karate dojo. It is a martial arts academy where the instructors teach a stripped down, simplified version of the style known simply as Golden Phoenix. It is one of the three North American masters of this style that I've come to see. Ironically, David Cross reflects the style in that he's been reborn from the ashes of his life, and he's done this at least three times. It's been many years since David and I last spoke. The last time we met, it was just before he joined the Fans Wrestling Organization for a show at the Yad Eliyahu Arena, now known as the Nokia Arena, in Tel-Aviv, Israel. He wasn't scheduled to wrestle on that card, but he wanted to be there on the off chance he could fill in for someone who was unable to perform. Over a cup of green tea, David and I discussed his love of professional wrestling. He told me of the horrible accident in his back yard during the filming of a homegrown wrestling match. His friends had assured him that by jumping off the roof of his house, he would hit the burning table they’d set up with such velocity that he would pass through it without being burned himself. Someone had put too much gasoline on the table. The end result was David lying unconscious with his head in a puddle of burning gasoline, losing his skin for the first time before regaining it after numerous plastic surgeries. It was horrible memories much like those that David was hoping to put behind him, replacing them with memories of fans cheering for him all over the World. The show in Tel Aviv would open the eyes of not only the Wrestling community, but the Global community as well. He felt a little guilty in hoping that someone would develop a case of the runs, perhaps a stomach flu, so that he could show what he was capable of in the ring. Still, if the opportunity presented itself he wanted to be ready. No one got sick. Unfortunately, due to a suicide bomber, a great many innocent people were killed. I had heard rumours about the post-bombing chaos. Someone said David had been found bloody and broken, screaming at the top of his lungs with something clutched to his chest. It took four people to pry his fingers apart. When they did, the charred and gruesome remains of a child's hand fell to the ground. It had belonged to a fan that Cross had just signed an autograph for, seconds before the explosion. The whole time, even when being strapped to the stretcher, David just kept screaming. Following his life after that experience, it seemed that David had completely snapped, and understandably so. At one point, after slicing off half of his face with a shard of glass, he was institutionalized for a number of months and placed in the care of Dr. Edward Menzel. Somehow he escaped from the psychiatric wing of the hospital he was confined to, rejoined the fWo, and eventually ended up in Japan. One might come to the conclusion that David had returned to the remote temple of the Golden Phoenix to escape the World. I like to think he went there not to escape anything, but to find something. It seems like he found whatever it was he was looking for. Despite his new facial disfigurement, he returned to North America and put on an impressive display in PRIME's Dual Halo match at the recent Culture Shock pay per view. His wrestling debut in PRIME came almost five years to the day of the bombing in Tel Aviv. I think it's safe to say that he's back where he belongs. As I enter the dojo and remove my shoes, I can't help but feel like something's not right. Maybe it's the pristine interior of the building, with its white walls, golden banners, and a large jade statuette of a large bird. This looks nothing like what one would expect upon passing by all the graffiti and garbage just outside the front door. While the streets were filled with urban decay, inside the dojo was a feeling of serenity and solace. This was certainly one book that was not to be judged by the spray paint and vandalism on its cover. I sit quietly on one of the wooden benches propped against the wall and watch a middle-aged Asian man, wearing a classic white kung fu shirt with baggy black pants tapered at the ankle, wiping the large canvas training mat with a white cloth. The canvas is faded, mended in some places, and the reddish brown stains of dried blood reveals that the dojo isn't always as serene as it appears at first glance. The man pauses and presses the cloth across his sweaty forehead. At that point, he notices me. Looking very tired, he struggles to find the patience to address me with any degree of courtesy. "Can I help you?" I tell him who I've come to speak to, and ask if he's here. The man drops all pretence and goes back to his cleaning. "He's on the roof," he says with an exasperated sigh. "Go up the stairs and take the fire exit at the top." I retrieve my shoes and carry them up the three flights of stairs. Just as I reach the door marked "Fire Exit", I hear the patter of heavy wet raindrops drumming against the metal door. I drop my shoes and slip them on my feet, but decide to wait for my host to come in from out of the rain. It's cold outside, and the rain has to be little more than wet snow at this point. I wait one minute, then another, and I begin to wonder if he's even outside at all. I push the door open a crack. Immediately, several wet droplets splat against my face and I feel the chill run down my spine. Goosebumps rise all over my arms and neck, and my breath gets caught in my throat. No, he can't be out there. Still, I open the door a bit wider just to make sure. The clouds, dark grey and heavy with moisture, seem to have rolled across the city and swallowed the taller buildings. Looking up, I feel as though I can poke them if I had a long enough stick. The roof itself is covered with tar and crushed rock. A fan vent roars nearby, probably for some refrigeration unit, and I shiver under my now soaked jacket and shirt. Sitting on those crushed rocks, shirtless, leg folded in the lotus position wearing the same style of pants as the gentleman downstairs, is the man I've come here to speak to. At least, I suspect it is. He's wearing a mask that covers most of his head, black fabric with a white cross where the face should be, his unkempt black hair crowning the top of the headpiece. Any doubts I might have that this masked man is, in fact, David Cross, disappears when I see the horrific scars that cover his torso. His lean muscles seem relaxed despite the cold wetness, and even his damp mask sags off his head. He doesn't move as I shout from the little bit of shelter I have in the doorway of the fire exit, "David Cross?" I try again. "David?" Still nothing. Maybe he's sleeping or, given the plummeting temperature out here, dead. I start to wish I had that long stick so I could poke him, rather than the clouds overhead. I shout over the hammering rain one last time. "Crucifix?" The head lifts, and water droplets spray from the mask where his mouth would be. "Ah, the Serpent has entered the Garden of Eden," he croaks, pausing to drink in my look of surprise. "Sorry. Most people see this thing on my face and expect me to start spouting scripture." "That's a reasonable assumption, isn't it? After all, you're wearing a religious icon." "I'm wearing a Roman torture device that happened to have the best publicist in the world - the Bible." He points a finger in my direction and curls it back into his fist. "Pull up a pile of cold wet stones and have a seat." "I'm okay where I -" "Come. And. Sit." By the time I cross the roof to where Crucifix is sitting, I feel as though I've been dipped in a giant bucket of ice water. My arms and chest are beginning to tremble slightly, and it feels like only a matter of time before my teeth start chattering. "How long have you been sitting out here?" I ask him as I sit down. He looks up at the clouds. "Not sure. The sun disappeared a while ago, so I've pretty much lost track of time." "I take it the cold doesn't bother you." Crucifix looks at me. "After all the fire balls, explosions, and hot lead I've had in my life, the cold is a welcome change of pace." We exchange pleasantries; he asks about my family, I inquire about his best friend and current coma patient, former professional wrestler "The Mecca" Alex Creed. "Alex is resting," he tells me. "I see him every day or so. We talk - well, I do most of the talking." "Do you think he can hear you?" "I know he can. I told him about my eleven eliminations in the Dual Halo match and he filled his catheter. I had the same reaction right after the Dual Halo match." "I understand you might be a part of a four-way scramble match on the upcoming ReVolution card. Is this part of your preparation?" "Not really. I mean, I beat eleven men before. How tough can it be to face three?" "It's not quite the same thing." "It never is. That's why I don't need to prepare for it. I go into every match like water, fluid and conforming to whatever openings I can find in my opponent's game plan. When I do -" He blows against the inside of the mask, spraying a mist of water in my direction. " - I drown them." I barely feel that the rain has stopped. My skin is pale and numb, and my teeth are chattering. I wait to see if my host has something to tell me. After all, he's the one who asked for this meeting. I watch as he slowly pulls up the bottom of his mask, lifting it past his ruined mouth as he stands up. He takes a breath through broken teeth, exhaling a frosty cloud of air. He seems pleased with my discomfort. I decide not to wait for him to speak, and I stand as well. “How have some of the others reacted to your return from the Land of the Rising Sun?” I ask, watching the scarred lips spread into a horrible smile. “Oh most people are okay with it. A majority of them haven’t seen how handsome I am under this mask, and they’re not sure how to react to me. I usually just play the fool.” He shrugs. “It seems to put them a little more at ease.” “Should they not be at ease?” “If they haven’t wronged me, they’re free to laugh at my antics.” “What about those who have done wrong by you?” He replies quickly, with little humour in his voice. “Then I laugh last, and best.” My shivering is becoming more animated. David’s smile, poking out from underneath the visage of a horrible torture device, seems less friendly than it did a moment ago. “Look at you shaking like that,” he says. “Let’s get you inside before you catch your Death.” I take a quick look at my watch. “You know, perhaps we can get together another time. It’s getting late, and I should probably change my clothes before I catch pneumonia.” David nods. “Of course.” He lowers the mask, covering his mouth again. “Well you know where I am.” He walks me down the stairs, leads me around the edge of the mat where the Asian man has just finished scrubbing, and stops at the door. We shake hands, say our goodbyes, and I leave the dojo. My car is parked one block over, and as soon as I round the corner I pull out my cell phone. I press the number one on the pre-programmed dialling menu and find myself talking to a receptionist after two rings. “I’d like to speak to Doctor Menzel,” I tell her. “It’s urgent.” “May I ask what this is in regards to?” she queries. “I have information about one of his missing patients.” “Sir, if you would just come by the office –“ “It’s Cross. David Cross. I’ve found him.” There’s an awkward silence on the other end of the line. “You... you know where he is?” “I do,” I tell her. “Put him on. Now.”
