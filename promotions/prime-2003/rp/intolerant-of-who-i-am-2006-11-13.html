Intolerant of Who I Am

13 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/13/2006 “My daddy can beat up yours!” “Nuh-uh!” “Oh yeah? Prove it!” “What's your daddy do?” “My daddy is a mechanic!” “My daddy is a fighter!” You know how the childhood arguments go. This is better than that, and so on. But little Andrew Stewart, he isn't backing down. His dad is the toughest man on the planet, even after he's gone into a semi-retirement at the age of forty. Shawn is still in the shape and skill that Randy Couture was when he retired, and Shawn still does a few bouts a year. “No, your daddy is a wrestler! Wrestling's fake!” Now, bear in mind, Shawn didn't hide who he was from his son. He showed some tamer bouts to his child only a few weeks before, now that he thought his son was old enough to start understanding what his dad had done. He watched as his dad took on some members from the old OSF in bouts that ended in a submission, most of them in a choke. They were tame fights, and Shawn wanted to start having his son understand, and to start teaching his son to defend himself. “My daddy was a fighter first! It was real!” Andrew was an average sized child, with his strikingly dark eyes and raven-black hair inherited from his mother. His longer frame and bold face was like that of his father. And so is his marked stubborness. “It wasn't real! He was a wrestler!” “Before that!” “I'm right and I'll prove it!” “You can't!” That's how a schoolyard fight got started. David had the height and weight on Andrew, and he swung first. Shawn had already started teaching Andrew how to punch, to get the muscle memory going, in the tradition of the original Tae Kwon Do style. It gave Andrew focus and helped offset any kind of adverse health conditions that people harp about on children. David's first punch bounced off Andrew's shoulder, while Andrew's return punch took the bigger boy right on the end of the nose. David staggered back and Andrew pressed the attack, charging shoulder-first and laying David out on the ground in front of him. Andrew kept punching the bigger child's face, smacking his knuckles into the boy's cheeks, jaw, nose. * * * “Mr. Stewart,” came the voice from the principle. She had graying hair, a tan suit and the kind of attitude that was snooty, superior and made you wonder if she had ever attracted anyone to her romantically in her entire life. Shawn sat there, very uncomfortable in the tie he had around his neck. Ties always made him feel like he was being strangled. But there he sat, looking his woman in the eye and incredibly unhappy over the whole affair. “I have to say, you should know that we don't tolerate that kind of behavior in our school.” “I hope you have the facts straight...” Shawn started. “Mr. Stewart, you will let me finish,” she said imperiously. “This is not the kind of problem that can be solved by hitting it.” “Okay, listen, lady...” “Your son,” she interrupted again, “broke the nose of another student. Now, I don't know what kind of morals you teach your son, Mr. Stewart, but this is the kind of event that could have a child expelled from this school. The kind of influence you seem to have...” “Shut up,” Shawn cut in. “There's something you need to know. You are really pissing me off with this attitude, lady. I've never seen someone being so intolerant and prejudiced in my entire life.” “Well I never!” “I'd be willing to bet you have.” Shawn started ticking points off on his fingers. “My son got into a fight, which is nothing new, ever. Second, the other kid swung first, so Andrew defended himself. Third, you have flat out accused me of making my son into a little demon and want to expel him because of who I am.” “You will not speak to me in such a...” “Oh my God!” Shawn bellowed back. “Have you listened to yourself? You just talked to me like I'm an ex-con!” “For all I know, you have nothing in your past but being a pit fighter, Mr. Stewart.” Shawn's jaw dropped incredulously. “Pit fighter? First of all, I only ever fought in certified organizations that followed the athletic rules and regulations. Second, I was a cop, lady.” “Your profession means that Andrew has no place here, Mr. Stewart.” * * * Waking up in a cold sweat is one of the most shocking experiences that can happen in life. You're jerked from a deep sleep to waking up, shivering and not knowing where you are. Shawn's hand found Nicole lying next to him, still sleeping soundly, and he started to get a grip on where he was. He very gently slipped out of bed as to not disturb his wife and walked from the bedroom of their apartment, down the hall to their kitchen. He pulled open the door to the refrigerator and reached in, pulling out a bottle of water and turned towards the stairs. Now he's gotta get some frustration out. He hit the stairs and as he stepped out the door of his apartment, he snagged the roll of tape he kept on the banister on the way downstairs and cracked the water. He took a long drink and started wrapping his hands up. He would love to have a son. He'd love to help raise a productive member of society, to have a son that he could teach, that he could learn from, that he and Nicole could nurture and all that sappy, mushy garbage. But he was terribly afraid exactly of what would happen. That people would judge any child of his because of his reputation and legacy as a hard-hitting, no holds barred fighter that had taken the fighting and wrestling worlds by storm. He started unloading punches into the worn heavy hanging bag in the back of the gym. His taped hands pounded into the rough canvas exterior. The bag began to rock and swing, and so Shawn threw in some jabs to keep the bag boxed into his range and not letting it get away from him. His hands flew, going hard and fast, making dull thuds and loud smacks. The chain rattled against the bare metal rafter that it hung from. How could people be so judgmental and intolerant without even knowing a person? He tried not to judge until he at least spoke to a person or saw how they behaved. But he was never subject to the same courtesy. His fists kept swinging, hammering away in an effect to bleed out some of the frustration he was feeling. Oh, he was intolerant of certain things. He was very intolerant of Sonny Silver, but that's because he knew Silver and had since Silver had first moved into the big time. The two had always hated each other, so intolerance there was expected. Shawn's fists hammered the bag more. No, honestly, it seemed to be encouraged. Shawn's fists hammered the bag, but began to slow. His hands kept hitting, but felt like lead. Shawn's eyelids kept pushing down, his knees buckling. His hands gripped around the bag as he slid down as he began to drift away. He collapsed on the floor, sweaty and exhausted. Light footsteps came downstairs as Nicole, in her blue flannel nightgown, brought a blanket and a pillow. She placed the pillow under Shawn's head and gently laid the blanket over of him. She stood there, looking at her husband, then lifted the blanket, laid down next to him and snuggled close to go back to sleep.
