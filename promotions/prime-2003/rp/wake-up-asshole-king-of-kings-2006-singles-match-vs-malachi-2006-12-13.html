Wake Up, Asshole (King of Kings 2006, singles match vs. Malachi)

13 December 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 13th, 2006 A ray of light shone through a nearby window, projecting a small square on the floor. Slowly, that ray moved across the room, eventually reaching the unconscious body of a haggard looking Asa 'Fro' Fountain. Shirtless with a pair of jeans and black boots still on and one hand grasping an empty bottle of Jim Beam, the once bright and excitable Asa that the world knew and loved lie train-wrecked from the night before, drooling on the floor. The sun-ray eventually found its way across his closed eyes which, naturally, woke the sleeping Fromaniac. With a few blinks, a moan, and a smack of the lips, he rolled onto his back. After staring at the ceiling for a moment, orienting himself, he sat up, rubbing his head through his gigantic round afro. He lifted the bottle of Jim in front of his face, peering through the clear glass. Finding only traces of backwash in the bottle, he sighed and pitched it into a corner. He looked around the room, and after finding no trace of his female companions from the night before, he lumbered to his feet. After a quick stretch he reached for his wallet. He frowned when he found there was no trace of any cash or plastic. "Ain't this a bitch. Damn broads." Stuffing his wallet back into his pants he made his way out the door, kicking his boots off in the hallway before stumbling into a nearby bathroom. He grabbed hold of the sink upon entry, turning on some cold water and splashing his face. He looked into the mirror, his rough, unshaven face staring back, drips of water trickling through his big, puffy beard. Asa reached for a razor and shaving cream, but stopped, looked at his beard a little longer, and put the razor and shaving cream back. "I think I'll keep it." After brushing his teeth and taking a long morning-after piss, Asa made his way into a messy living room, bottles of alcoholic nature strewn about. He grabbed a shirt draped over a couch and threw it over his head, pulling it down over his gargantuan afro. "Where's that damn phone..." Under a pile of chip wrappers Asa found his cell phone. He flipped it open and punched in a few numbers. "Yo, American Express? Yeah, it's Asa again. Yeah, they took my card again. It'll be in Wednesday? Good, thanks suga." From his kitchen area Asa flipped on the TV, which happened to be on FX, which happened to be showing an advertisement for PRIME Wrestling. Shots of Rich Rollins, Angelo Deville, Tchu, Lindsay Troy, and all the other big stars flooded the screen. Asa rolled his eyes and turned the TV off. "Prime. Buncha jive shit, that's what. Ain't what OSW used to be..." As luck would have it, Asa's eyes fell onto a note tacked to a cork board. In scribbled writing it read: Chet: (phone number confidential) Asa frowned, then grabbed the note, crumpled it up, and pitched it in the trash. "Gotta walk off this hangover." With that, Asa threw on an old coat, grabbed a pack of cigarettes, and headed out the door into the streets of Los Angeles, California. * * * * * * * Asa set a bottle of Jim Beam onto a counter inside a local convenience store. The clerk, an old, balding white guy, lifted an eyebrow to the Ayatolla of Funk n' Soula. "Can ya put that on my tab, brotha?" asked Asa. "Some broad took your money and cards again, didn't they." "You know I'm good for it, man. Got a new card comin' in Wednesday, I'll pay up." The clerk shook his head. "I can't keep doing this, Asa. This happens to ya on a damn near weekly basis." "This'll be the last time, pops. I promise ya, man." The clerk heaved a heavy sigh. "Last time, Asa. You gotta get yourself back in line." "Thanks brotha, won't happen again." Asa took his bottle and headed out the door, the clerk shaking his head as he left. Asa ambled down the streets, cigarette hanging out of his mouth and bottle in hand. It wasn't long before he came across a homeless man sitting on the sidewalk. He was a black guy, old clothing hanging off his skinny body, nasty gray hair hanging off his face. He spoke to Asa in a taciturn, Uncle Ruckus kind of voice, for those of you who watch the Boondocks. "'ey son, gota buck 'r two ta spare?" Asa stopped to study the man, taking a drag from his smoke. "Nah, cat daddy. No cash on me." Asa turned to leave, but the old man piped up once more. "'ey, wait a minute. Turn 'round." Asa turned back to the homeless man. "What?" "Yer that wrestla guy from tha tee vee, ain'tcha?" Asa laughed a little, looking away from the man. "I was. Years ago." "Ya was? Well, whaddya doin' now? "I'm retired, brotha. Restin' and relaxin'." "From the look atya yer a drunk, jus' like me," retorted the old man, pointing a bony finger at Asa's bottle. Asa glanced at his beverage, smiling a little. "Yeah, guess I am." "'ow old are ya, boy?" "Just turned forty years old." "Fo'ty years old? An yer retired? Ain't tha' sum shit." "Yeah?" "Hell yeah, boy! I werked my ass off 'til ah was sixty sumpthin' years old! But fo'ty, damn, what you do with yoself 'sides gettin' drunk all the time?" Asa paused. He couldn't answer the question. That's all he did, got drunk every night. "Can't say I do much else." The old man huffed. "Looks ta me that ya gotta git yerself back 'n line. What's keepin' ya from wrasslin' these days anyways?" Asa gazed off into nothing, thinking back to the old days. He glanced back to the old man. "I ain't too sure, man." "Ya got half ya life in fronta ya, son. Don't go endin' up like me, last think ya wanna do is start beggin' fa change on the streets. Time ta wake the hell up! Be a fighta! Don't be a begga..." A grin came across Asa's face. He nodded, then knelt down, putting the bottle in the old man's hand. "Ya think so?" "'ell yeah! Lookatcha! Ye got health! Ye got enuff youth ta keep ona fightin'! But whateva, I'm jus' some drunk on tha streets n' shit..." "What's ya name, brotha?" asked Asa. The old man squinted hard. "Roy, why?" "Thanks brotha, I think ya mighta just saved my life. Take care of yaself, I'll see ya again." Asa left the bottle with Roy and headed down the street, walking much faster, a new kick to his step. "Thank ya, son!" yelled Roy after Asa, but The Fro was around the corner and heading home. He opened up the bottle Asa gave him and took a long swig. "T'day's mah lucky day..." * * * * * * * Asa burst through his front door, tossing his jacket and heading straight for the trash bin. He dug around and retrieved the little scrap paper he threw out earlier. He found his phone and dialed the number. "Yo, can ya put me through to Chet, suga? Yeah, yeah, I know he's a busy guy, but you tell 'im Asa Fountain's on the phone. Nah, wait. You tell 'im The Fro is back. Tell 'im to tell Prime to get theymselves ready for Fromania..." End.
