Preparing for the bomb

14 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/14/07 POSITIVES As Told by Ian Nackedy It’s easy to continue to beat into everyone’s head what a bad guy my father is. There’s ton of evidence, every piece more incriminating than the last. All of the information I’ve cataloged on my father’s existence with me culminates in an image of a man who seems to be one without redemption or virtue. But being his son and coming from his seed requires me to have at least a little bit of a soft spot for him, and I guess that I do. Pops Nackedy isn’t a man completely made of negatives. There are a few good qualities, too. They all relate to the business. When he was our manager, he did a lot of things wrong. Things I’ve already gone over so many times that in my head Pops Nackedy is the equivalent of Darth Vader, Sonny Silver, Boss Hog, and all of the other most evil people on the planet. But he did some things right too. He prepared us mentally. He prepared us physically. He knew how to make us better. They’re all qualities of a great father, and he didn’t use him to that end. But at least we got something out of it, for a short while. PREPARATIONS As Told by The Spectator Ian Nackedy lifted weights, as always. Since he decided to take wrestling seriously, he spent almost all of his free time in the weight room. He felt safe there, secure from the rest of the world and, most importantly, from… "Hey, son!" …interruptions. Ian shelved the bar and sat up, staring at Pops Nackedy with a grimace on his face. "Do me a favor, Pops. Go to the door frame right now." Pops looked at Ian, confused. "What?" "Just do it." Slowly, Pops walked to the door frame and stood there, his head tilted to the side in a universal sign of confusion. "Now what?" Ian pointed at the door frame. "Look at the sign right by the door frame." Pops Nackedy read the sign silently at first, and then aloud to show he understood Ian’s message: "‘If your name is not Ian, Gil, or Ridiculously Hot Babe With Intentions to Heighten Ian’s Pleasure Zone, stay the fuck out.’" Pops looked back at Ian with his own grimace. "Nice, son. Real classy. No one would ever name a girl that." "Well, if she was here to heighten my pleasure zone, I’m sure we could temporarily change it." After a pause, Ian shrugged. "What do you want?" "Oh." Pops suddenly looked down at the box he was carrying and his face lit up, making him look ten years younger. Ian scowled as he thought he saw a flicker of his own face there. Before he could dwell on it too much, though, Pops had started his explanation. "Well, I know that you’ve got your big match coming up. And that even though you’ve done some great things with Gil, that this is the biggest match you’ve ever been in. So I wanted to help you out." Pops stepped up to one of the weight benches and dropped the box onto it, picking up some tapes and holding them in Ian’s face. "I got some videos of the two most recent Jewel in the Crown winners, Tchu and Tony Gamble. I figured that it would be helpful on a metaphorical level, because it will help you see how these guys possessed the heart of a champion; and the more obvious practical level, because you’re facing Tony Gamble in the first match." Ian grabs one of the tapes and nods. "Thanks, Pops. This actually would help a lot." After an awkward pause, Ian places his hand on his father’s shoulder. "You…you did well." As Ian walked past Pops, grabbing the box, his father could only smile widely and sit on one of the benches, relishing in the fact that, for once, he had done something right. GRINNING: A SIMILARITY As Told by Ian Nackedy Something tells me Tony Gamble watched old NWC tapes. His mannerisms, his mystique, his whole balancing act between serious and comedy reminds me of what me and Gil have done forever. The similarities are staring me in the face as I watch every match, every appearance, every Gamble sighting my dad could get my hands on. Tony Gamble is successful, Tony Gamble is dangerous… Tony Gamble is Ian Nackedy watered down. I’m not downsizing his importance, or listing his shortcomings, or calling him a midget – okay, well maybe I am. But these tapes that I’ve gotten on The Grin have worked perfectly in both ways my father intended. First, the metaphorical. Gamble has that nature that begs you not to take him seriously. His smile, his act, his entire plane of existence in the wrestling arena just screams harmless. And then, before you can even realize it, you’re being played. Tony Gamble is a snake hiding in tall grass, a wolf salivating in sheep’s clothing, a midget hiding in a basket of old clichés. He gets you by making you sleep on him, and it’s the same thing that’s kept me alive so far in the tournament. So what Pops was trying to make me see is that Tony Gamble’s path to victory could very well be my own. This trick of not being taken seriously has gotten me a victory over the PRIME killer, and it very well could get me a win over the former Jewel in the Crown champion. By using the weaknesses that I see in his ring work, and in the aforementioned fact that I possess many of the same characteristics will help me pray on an opening and take it. That, of course, is the physical aspect. My dad got me covered on this one. TARGET PRACTICE As Told by The Spectator "I am not putting on those jester tights and getting into that ring with you," Magen Nackedy said firmly. Ian’s sister-in-law was a very easy-going woman. She had endured the randomness and zaniness that inviting Foster’s two brothers in her home had unleashed. She even didn’t mind Five Year Old Integer Chris, for when he wasn’t trying to get her phone number he served as an excellent companion to her own son Connor. But to the act of impersonating Tony Gamble she was very opposed. "It’s just not happening, Ian. Not now, not ever." "Oh come on," Ian pleaded, stepping between the ring ropes and dropping down in front of her, his face trying its absolute best to appear serious. "I need an accurate sparring partner to simulate the effects of my match." "And your tag partner won’t suffice?" Ian shrugged. "He doesn’t fit the height requirement." Magen shook her head. "I’m sure no one else has made a joke about his height before. You’re really original." Ian laughed. "You’re right. People say that he’s short on every Revolution. And it doesn’t give anyone big laughs, or humor awards, or anything like that. You know what else it doesn’t do?" "What?" Magen sighed. "Change the fact that he is two feet tall. Now get in the ring and fight like a man." Magen threw her hands up in the air, leaving the weight room without a retort. Ian sighed, then leaned against the ring apron. After a moment, Pops entered. "I can probably help you out, son. I’m not quite that short, but I’ve got the weight requirement met." Ian grimaced, saying, "I’m not going to make you step into the ring, Pops." "Nonsense. You need help, and damned if I’m not going to make up for all the terrible things I’ve done. Haven’t you always wanted to take down your old man?" Ian’s smile grew so far across his face that it could have been mistaken for Gamble’s. "You just said the magic words, Pops." DIGGING GRAVES As Told by Pops Nackedy For every punch I take, I come closer to redemption. For every bump I take, I come closer to acceptance. For every tooth I lose, I come closer to being human. For every scoop of dirt on the grave I dig, I come closer to being a father. Ring the bell. FIGHT CLUB As Told by The Spectator Ian Nackedy smiled as he started off with a right hand to his father’s jaw. What had started as a joke was now becoming his dream come true. "Nice, son," Pops Nackedy said before getting a harder left to the other side. Ian didn’t pull his punches. He sent another one to the midsection that caused Pop to reel towards the ropes. Ian stopped thinking. He sent kick after kick, punch after punch, ending in a big suplex that had Pops grunting in pain. Magen came back and shouted something, but Ian didn’t hear. He was over his father in a flash, mounting across the torso and slamming down right and left hands. Ian’s cheeks slowly became speckled in red. "STOP!" yelled Magen from a million miles away. Ian continued to barrage Pops with punches, his face becoming more and more blank with every punch. The tirade lasted for minutes until Ian was finally too weak to block it out any longer. Looking down at his father’s mangled face, he sighed and blinked twice, then stood up. Magen slid under the rope and began wiping Pops’ face with a towel. "Ian, what the hell are you doing?" she screamed. Ian looked at her, his eyes still not completely taking everything in. He looked back at his father, who was struggling to sit up. "Pops, are you okay?" Magen asked. After wiping his face of the blood, Pops looked up to Ian, liquid that could have either been blood or tears dripping from his eyes. "It’s okay." After a second, he smiled, his teeth striped with a film of crimson. "Those were some pretty good strikes. You’re better than I could have hoped for. I’m…I’m proud of you, son." Magen did not look so proud of Ian. "That was disgusting, Ian. What is wrong with you?" Ian looked down, still dazed. "I…" was all he could muster before shaking his head and walking out. BEING HUMAN: ANOTHER SIMILARITY As Told by Ian Nackedy I decided the real world was getting too much for me, so I went back to the tapes. Tchu this time. Man, this guy is a beast. I have passing knowledge of Ian English and Munson, the other Jewel in the Crown winners, but as I watch more and more footage I can see why my dad chose these two. He chose more recent winners, guys who have things that I can see, that I can use to help me in my quest to the top. Tchu was all about passion, addiction. He wanted the competition more than anything. He knew what it meant to him, and he used it to fuel himself to victory after victory. There were stacked odds, and often obstacles from both in and out of the ring to tend to. But he was focused. He was determined. He was also a lot better of a wrestler than I am. I guess I’ll have to work around that. But even in that, Pops was smart for giving me some of his videos too, because I see the metaphorical side of studying Tchu. He was a guy who went on to be the best. After going through this Jewel in the Crown, he wasn’t mired in another one the year after. He won the title. He became the man. He had something that Gamble doesn’t have, that I don’t have yet. The passion to win. The need to win. And it’s something I need if I want to beat everyone in my way. And thanks to the beating I just gave Pops, I see it in myself as well. His nickname was "The Inhuman Being," and sometimes to get what you want, you have to be a little inhuman. You have to do things you regret. You have to beat the shit out of your father. He said he was proud of me, and I know he absolutely meant it. Because he wanted this to happen. And that’s why I say that Pops isn’t all bad. He’s a great manager. With these videos, he’s prepared me for this match better than 1000 hours in the gym ever could have. And I thanked him by kicking his ass. But without doing so, without kicking Dad’s ass, without Dad kicking his own ass to get me those videos, I’m pretty sure I wouldn’t be here right now with the chance to kick everyone else’s ass at all. DROPPING SHOES As Told by The Spectator "Here, sit down," Magen said, bringing Pops next to the sofa and putting a towel on the seat so he didn’t bleed on it. She went to the refrigerator and grabbed an ice pack as he shook his head. "Magen, you aren’t listening to me," Pops said. "I’m fine. Ian’s just a little enthusiastic, that’s all. Getting geared for his match." "That’s not true and you know it," she said, giving him the ice pack and a bottle of water. "He’s still angry, and I think he’s treating you really unfairly. It’s really getting out of hand now, and if you want me to I’ll…" "No, no, dear," Pops interrupted, his breath coming out in a slow wheeze. "I’ll be fine. So will he. Everything’s going to work out okay. All I need is a little time to lie here with my thoughts, and I’ll be okay. Can you let me have that, please?" "Sure, of course." Magen stood up and shot one melancholy look at her father-in-law before leaving for her bedroom. Pops laid the ice pack on his forehead and watched the door for a few moments. Pulling his phone from his pocket, he held a button down and waited a few seconds, feeling safe enough to close his eyes. "Yes," he said lowly. "I’m here. So are Ian and Gil. No, they don’t suspect. I haven’t found it yet. I’m still searching. Give me a few more weeks before you – how did you describe it before? – ‘drop the bomb’. Okay. Bye." Pops hung up and lifted a bloody hand to his cheek, cringing at the pain.
