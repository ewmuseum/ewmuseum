Interrogation

29 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 29, 2008 A late night rendezvous with Lindsay Troy in a hotel room. Hot. Throw in a lawyer, and what you have is the least appealing threesome ever. After his match in Omaha, David Cross received a note from Lindsay Troy responding to the message he had sent to her. She arranged a meeting in her hotel room to discuss Alex Creed, given her personal proximity to the situation at hand. That night, David told Lindsay everything – Alex remaining in his coma, being poisoned by some unknown party at the hospital, and more importantly, being released into the care of his father. One would assume that being in a father's care would be a good thing. Vic had money, power, and the means to help Alex if he chose to. The one slight hiccup was that Vic was a bit of a sociopath with somewhat homicidal tendencies. During one of their last father - son reunions, Vic attacked Alex and tried to bury his thumbs in his son's eye sockets. Now Alex was comatose, helpless, and in very real danger. Assuming he was still alive. David had no means to combat the restraining order that Vic had levelled against him. Lindsay did. She unleashed the fury of her own legal beagle and was confident that they would get Alex back, or at least get him out of the monster's lair. The woman Alex once called his 'unofficial big sister', who had taken the young Creed under her protective wing on more than one occasion, was ready to unleash Hell on Vic's poor lawyer much the way Lindsay herself had on the Main Man. David hoped to be able to sit in on that meeting. He'd even bring popcorn. At the conclusion of their meeting, David thanked Lindsay for her help. There was one more avenue Cross felt he could pursue to help things along on his end. If it paid off with information of any kind, LT would be the first to hear it. It wasn't until Cross was in the lobby that a question popped into his head. Vic had restraining orders out on many people. Pretty much anyone who had ever had any contact with himself or Alex had been put on that list. So why was there no paperwork at all on the one person who had helped Alex out repeatedly, not to mention take out the human Juggernaut in NFW - Lindsay Troy? -==- When he stopped by the Eighth Precinct, David's intent was to talk to the detective in charge of Alex's case. There was a hit-and-run incident that needed to be solved, and so far he hadn't heard if anything had been discovered on that front. Marilyn hadn't been told, but it seemed like that was par for the course. No one seemed to be talking about anything involving Alex Creed. David wanted to know why. The police officer at the front desk smiled when David asked to talk to the Detective in charge of Alex's case. He was young, couldn't have been out of the Academy for very long, and still had that ambitious look in his eyes. He was going to make a difference! Following him back to an Interview room in the back, David passed by other officers who had some years under their belt. Some had the futility etched into their faces, defeat in their eyes, but still they soldiered on. Cross respected them for that - it wasn't just anyone who could fight a war they knew they couldn't win. The young man motioned for David to enter the room. "The detectives will be here shortly," he said. "Have a seat." The door sounded a little louder than he would have expected when it closed behind him. The lights were bright, which only enhanced the appearance of the mirror stretching across the far wall. David looked around, saw the table, two chairs with black plastic seats, a stack of four more chairs in the corner, egg white walls, robin's egg tiles on the floor. The room looked fine. Where was this feeling of anxiety coming from? It was lurking behind a curtain inside him, and he couldn't understand what it was doing there in the first place. He walked over to the table, touched it with his fingertips. That's when he realized what he was doing. He wasn't taking stock of his surroundings. He was convincing himself of where he was, or rather wasn't. Eyes. He felt eyes on him, just like they were when he was admitted to the psych ward many years ago. He remembered the cold, the dark, the drugs, can't scream can't move trapped torturerapepain - Standing in the hallway outside his room, Dr. Menzel had watched terrible things happen. Studied them. Wanted them. No no no, he wasn't there now no sir! He was in a police station, in a room, not trapped not - "Are you all right sir?" David blinked, gasped in a breath. His fist was clenched tightly, white knuckles pressed against the table top. Across from him stood a balding man in his late thirties, a hint of a paunch hiding behind his unusually wide tie, with a steaming Styrofoam cup in his hand. "Yeah I'm fine," David replied, slightly embarrassed. He sat down on the plastic seat, elbows resting on the table, fingers interlaced. That seemed to satisfy the man's curiosity. "Well Mister Cross, I'm Detective Simmons," he said. "I'm just waiting for my partner." He watched David pull out the chair and sit down. "You look a little on edge. I hope it isn't the room. We don't want you to feel intimidated." The word 'you' had just barely left his lips when the door opened. The body that passed through the frame was the largest African-American David had ever seen. It was like the rich soil of the planet had broken off a piece and molded it into a behemoth with shoulders so wide he turned slightly to step through the doorway. His head seemed to grow out of his neck, and his hands looked as if they could have palmed a basketball net. His attire - black t-shirt, jeans, and some sort of boot that had to be custom-made - was definitely not regulation. "Well it's a good thing you don't want me to feel intimidated," said Cross. Detective Simmons sat down in the chair across from David. “This is my partner Detective Moses. Now I'd like to thank you for coming in today –“ Moses was across the room in four large strides. If he hadn’t been smiling the entire time, Cross might have considered jumping to his feet. “My nigga!” Moses said and slapped Cross on the upper back. David literally saw stars. “Uh, hi.” Simmons didn’t appear impressed by his partner’s behavior. “You'll have to excuse Detective Moses. He just transferred from our Gangbuster Unit and he's still acclimating to the civilized life.” Moses stood at the side of the table. He looked at his partner and jerked his thumb in David’s direction. “You know who that is? That’s the Steel Serpent! Bad ass mofo from Japan!” There was something in Simmons’ eyes that made David feel a little uncomfortable. “Is that true?” he asked. Unsure what the proper reply would be, Cross tried to be as clear as he could be. “I did wrestle in Japan under that name, yes.” Moses clapped his hands together, still smiling. “I told you!” “My partner is a wrestling fan.” The way Simmons said the word ‘wrestling’, he might as well have said that his partner was a fan of rolling around in pig shit. Detective Moses wasn’t finished. “I hear he be bangin' bitches an' having them butt out cigarettes on his dick!” The land mass turned his head to where David shifted uncomfortably in his seat. “You did that?” Behind the gauze was a very red face. “Well those days in Japan were some crazy times.” He noticed that Detective Simmons was looking more and more disgusted with him. Great. “Uh, I thought we were discussing the Alex Creed case. I understand you were working it.” A pad of paper was set down on the table, and Simmons began flipping through the pages. He clicked the end of his pen as he read his notes. “We were . We've come upon a few dead ends and were hoping you could answer a few questions for us.” “Absolutely,” Cross said. “Whatever you need.” Simmons continued to look at his notes when he spoke. “Good. You can start by telling me who your friend Alex was dealing to.” David opened his hands, but his fingers remained intertwined. “When you say 'dealing', are you referring to cards or something?” Moses folded his arms in front of him. “See that? Bad ass AND smart ass all rolled up inna one.” Detective Simmons didn’t share his partner’s amusement. “I'm talking about drugs. He WAS a professional wrestler, wasn't he?” David leaned forward, digging his elbows into the top of the table. “So all professional wrestlers deal drugs?” “Oh don’t play innocent with me, buddy.” The bitterness dripped off every word Simmons spoke. “Professional wrestlers live the fast life, and they’re always shooting up with something – either steroids or painkillers. You wrestle for PRIDE, don’t you?” “No, that would be PRIME.” David tried to lighten up the situation by almost singing “Number One by Definition!” Simmons leveled his gaze at him. “That’s the dumbest thing I’ve ever heard.” Mission so very not accomplished. “Your own website brags about wrestlers using drugs. So don’t play innocent with me!” David sighed. “Thanks Hoyt.” He looked up at Moses, hoping to see a friendly face at the top of that mountain of mass. The smile was still there, but it looked to be fading fast. Simmons stood up and snatched the note pad off the table. “Look, we arrested one of your wrestler buddies and raided his home. We recovered enough steroids to jack up an entire wrestling promotion! And when we ran the prints on those vials,” he said with a self-satisfying smirk, “guess who the owner of those prints were? Your good buddy Alex Creed.” Impossible, David thought to himself. “Did the guy say that Alex sold the steroids to him?” “Your boy didn’t say nothin’,” Moses said, his smile now gone. “An’ I asked him pretty hard. Feel me?” “My boy? I don’t even know who you’re talking about!” Simmons rolled his eyes as if he’d heard it a hundred times before. “He got popped, and a week later Alex was assaulted. Coincidence?” Assaulted? Say what? David thought. “His girlfriend, Alison Bartley, also disappeared.” Girlfriend? He had a girlfriend? “Along with their two month old baby girl Amy.” His WHAT? David’s head was swimming. Simmons placed both of his hands on the table, palms down, and leaned toward him. “I don’t care about you,” he said in a level tone. “I don’t care about Alex. I care about finding that two month old baby that didn’t do a damn thing to anyone.” “He... he had a baby?” How could you not tell me something like that? Two sets of eyes stared at David, waiting for him to look up from his hands. Before the next move came, there was a knock at the door. Detective Simmons glanced over at Moses before walking over to the door. He opened it a crack, mumbled something, then said “I’ll be right back.” David heard the door close. A moment later, he felt an eclipse fall over him. He looked up out of the shadow and saw Moses had turned that smile upside down. “Now you an' me, we aight. But I'm feelin' like maybe you startin' t'disrespect me. That happens, we ain't gonna be so aight anymore.” His hands tightened around themselves. “Straight up, I have no idea what you guys are talking about. I thought Alex was a victim of a hit and run.” “That was a cover story, to keep him safe ‘till he woke up. Someone beat your boy almost to death.” He held up his index finger. “One guy, with his bare hands. Motherfucker almost as bad as me, pull that shit off.” One guy? Bare hands? David filed that away. “Girlfriend? What do you know about her?” Moses’ frown softened. “Not much.” He glanced over his shoulder at the mirror, then back at David. When he spoke, he lowered his voice. “We found her in a ditch two days ago. Dude that did your boy did her too. Bitch looked like she’d been backed over by a dump truck.” “I want to help. I really do. But Alex is as straight as they come. There's no way he'd deal drugs, take drugs, have anything to do with drugs. He was the one kid who Just Said No. The smile returned to Moses’ face. “You aight, Snake." Detective Moses stepped back from the table when he heard the door open. Detective Simmons returned to the room, closed the door behind him, and walked up to the table. His partner slowly retreated to the opposite wall and leaned against it. David knew what Simmons was going to say a second before he said it. His eyes gave it away. “Mister Cross, please take off your gauze.” “I’d really rather not.” David looked behind Simmons. “I’m allergic to mirrors. They make me break out in ugly.” “We're discussing a case here and I'd like to confirm your identity. I'm aware of your facial injury –“ Apparently Detective Moses was not up on current events. “You get fucked up, Snake?” “My understanding is that the covering isn't essential.” Simmons sat down again, setting his closed note pad down in front of him. “It's not acting as protection.” “Tell that to your lunch,” David quipped. Simmons was not in a quipping mood. “Remove the gauze please, or Detective Moses will do it for you.” The sigh came from deep within his chest when David started to unravel the gauze bandages. The entire time, his eyes never left the large mirror across from him. Simmons watched his eyes as David started to reach the end of the line. Skin, pink and scarred, started to show until all of the bandages were piled in a small mound in front of him. Simmons wrinkled his eyes in a wince as David smiled at him with a mouthful of broken teeth. “Better?” he asked. Moses slapped his leg and laughed. “Damn Snake! If yo' bitch ain't gonna shave her shit, don't go down there!” David actually smiled at that. He leaned back in his seat and folded his arms in front of him. Detective Simmons found his voice. “Now, ahem, we were discussing the case.” “We weren't discussing anything,” David said. “You were talking out of your ass.” Simmons snapped back. “You people think you’re so much better than everyone else because you’re on TV. You think you’re tough because you beat people up for a living?” “No, I think I’m tough because I butt out lit cigarettes on my dick. I think I’m tough because Alex Creed’s father tried to kill me by shooting me in the face with a shotgun.” Cross tilted his head ever so slightly to the side. “What about you, sport? You a tough guy because you got a badge and a gun? You a tough guy because you make your alimony payments on time?” “How did you know –“ “Ring finger on your left hand has the pale strip where your wedding band used to be. You have shoulders that say you used to work out, but your gut says that you’ve given up. Now you find your power in your authority. You keep Moses around to do the heavy lifting.” Moses watched to see if his partner was going to leap across the table. Simmons didn’t do it, but the red in his face and the murder in his eyes said that he really, really would like to. “Damn Snake,” he said a little louder than he expected to. Steel Serpent looked up at Moses. “My nigga.” There were no notes being taken. Detective Simmons hadn’t touched his note pad, hadn’t done anything except stare at David. “I think I’m done here,” David finally announced after letting Simmons stew and Moses had looked at the mirror enough times to tip off that they had an audience. “I don’t have anything for you, and since you have nothing for me I’d say our business here is finished.” He stood up and rolled his head from side to side. Simmons stared at the empty chair, replaying his life inside his mind. David walked to the door and said as he passed by the seated Detective, “If I find anything I’ll let Moses know.” "Hey," Moses said, "ain't you gonna wear that gauze out? David looked at the pile of gauze on the table as if he didn't know what it was, or how it had come to be there. "Think I'll just wear what I've got." -==- Standing on the other side of the mirror outside the Interview room, Dr. Menzel watched interesting things happen. Studied them. Wanted them. Captain Jeff Zazeski stood slightly behind the doctor. “Was that to your satisfaction?” Dr. Menzel nodded. “It was.” “And?” The doctor turned to the Captain. “I should have David Cross off the streets and back in an institution in two weeks.”
