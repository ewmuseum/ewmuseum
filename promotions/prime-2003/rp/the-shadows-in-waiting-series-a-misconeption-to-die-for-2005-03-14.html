The Shadows in Waiting Series: A Misconeption to Die For

14 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/14/05 There he was. She had long flowing hair. The dark silk fell down around her shoulders and embraced her in a hug. She spoke cautiously and tenderly, turning every word over in her mouth, fidgeting with the exact enunciation in her mind, before she let it roll off her tongue, flawlessly. “Are you sure?” she asked with a slow, cool voice. The static electricity built up under his skin and caused goose bumps to rise along the length of her arm. Her skin was smooth and the color of light chocolate. As a wave of anticipation flowed over her, jolting her spirits, still, she hid her excitement behind a cool veil of solitude. “Well?” she asked again after a purposely patient wait. The bouncer turned and gave her a cocky glare. He had tight ripped blue jeans and a black tee-shirt that read “SECURITY” across the front in big white lettering. A golden tongue ring bobbled up and down as he spoke in quick, raspy sentences, but all the while she couldn’t concentrate as the light bounced across the top of his bald head. “Yep. I’m sure. I’m sure I’m sure,” he added, unfolding his arms from his chest. Several dark strands of hair from his bulky chest grew over the collar of his shirt. “Right over there,” he said, pointing back toward the same table. Maria followed the man’s finger and her gaze automatically shifted back to the table where the three young men set. And there he was, sitting between the other two, just as she imagined. Tall, dark skinned, and amiable. Although the round curves of his muscles shown through his white shirt, and his masculine presence was unmistakable, she could see the intensity and passion of his green eyes as he peered inquisitively up at the stage. He was a good man. Just like she always knew he was. He shifted in his seat. He must have sensed something. His eyes turned to look up and meet her’s. Maria turned away shyly. “Thank you,” she whispered to the guard. The guard simply nodded, too focused on the stage to be distracted by formalities.. Maria took the young girl’s hand and pulled her into the Women’s room. “Come on, dear. We’ve got to fix you up...” A full meal on a plate, and only chopsticks to eat it with. An object in motion stays in motion unless an external force acts on it. Emilio, however, was not that force. Not in terms of defeating Angelo Deville, not in terms of derailing Chris Slaboda, and certainly not in terms of stealing the torch from the Dark Age as a whole. This rejuvenated pack of savages reaped the rewards of their Culture Shock surprise in Boda. With the Universal Title around the waste of one of their own, this cadre of malign intent felt a sense of entitlement unparalleled by any other individual, or group of individuals, on the roster. They called the shots as they saw them and what they couldn’t have they simply took. Branded. The PRIMEal Rage Championship, the ranking insignia of PRIME’s historic Alias division, was scarred, blemished, de-elevated forever. The Dark Age’s crude geometry now adorns its once pretty face, cursing it forevermore. Emilio had tried bleach, tried soap, tried heat-intensive care, and hell, he even tried holy water and had it blessed by a Boston Dieses priest. But for what? For the feeling of helplessness to breed again in his led belly and sink his heart further into his chest with the knowledge that the war he helped start wasn’t over. That it never would be. That he couldn’t win it. And the only way it would ever stop would be if one of two scenarios played out: The Dark Age was gone forever, or PRIME was. Emilio’s loss on ReVolution 57 was of little concern. He gave it his all, and ask any ringside fan, sports commentator, or professional analyst in this business and they’ll tell you the same thing: Emilio looked the best. Emilio dominated. He could have, and should have, won that match. But a quick slight of hand -another crude move on the part of an egomaniacal Brandon Youngblood- and Deville scored the tainted victory. No bother. For at that point, Emilio could have left the arena with his head hung hi. He proved that he was one of the best the promotion had to offer, and the trio collectively proved that PRIME was one of the best promotions that the sport had to offer. If it had been left at that, being ranked among the best of the best, it would have been fine, a fitting end to the show. If that were the end. But no. As Vampir Nosferatu bitterly commented, “So it appears you can run from the wolves den, my friend, but that doesn’t mean that the wolves won’t come after you.” “...The wolves...come after you.” And that’s exactly what it was. A hunting. A kill. A gang rape. The predatorial pack of PRIME went for the killing off of the weak. Indeed, Emilio had just finished his match. And a blind-side from Dani Furher...more salt to the wound. They beat him down, choked him out, crushed his skull, and decimated his spirits. Then, to complete the slap in the face, not only Emilio Rage, not only Hin See and Taun Pham and the front office, not only to PRIME or Playboy TV, but to every man, woman, and child watching around the world, the wolf pack collectively pissed on the trophy of Emilio’s success, the fruit of the Alias division. A belt which, when PRIME was in a slump, signified dozens of great matches in which some of PRIME’s best competitors gave it their all. Rumor had been floating around for a while now. Speculation in the backwaters of pro-wrestling bloggers said that Emilio was tired of defending his title, ready to move on with his career. But that title is his career. While other championships and straps of gold may come, hopefully in the near future, this will always be his title. When the final bell tolls and the historians close the books on PRIME forever, it may very well be written that Emilio was the greatest Alias champion ever. So Peter Vetra, a pawn in the emotional chess game of the Imperium, a group of scum who’s looking for the attention they haven’t received -and don’t deserve- in the ring, if he’s feeble minded enough to hop into the squared circle with a hungry, seething Emilio Rage, on the prowl for revenge in any form, whether it be an injury to the Dark Age or a symbolic ass-whooping to a member of PRIME’s “other” evil jobber boy scouts, Vetra may very well find himself pinned for the one...two...three. For the third straight time. Now, however, there are more pressing matters at hand. The PrimeTime Central Global Title match, a match with Ozric Mortimer -who is in every way as dangerous as the likes of Youngblood and Deville -and then of course, there’s the simple matter of a few personal items Emilio has found in his luggage... Needless to say, Emilio has a very fascinating few weeks ahead of him. Startling Realization. The faint riff of the trumpet danced melodically about the lodge. Men with long Cuban cigars and pressed dress pants set idly about, failingly whistling to the bebop tune, passing their scotches back and forth between hands. The poets came up one by one and unashamedly orated their art onto the def ears of the drinkers. Poker games started up in the back, and the small group of college kids, dressed in the their khaki pants and Dave Matthew’s tee-shirts, angrily sifted their way up to the front in an attempt to listen in on the speakers as a pool cue on the back pool table bounced off the eight ball, nailing it into the corner pocket. Game over. The laws of physics seemed to bend in waves synchronous with the band as the smoke hovered slightly over the head of the men instead of rising to the roof. A weepy eyed dame gave a tale of her dead cat, Chuckles, and the horny jocks clapped, hoping to be rewarded in return for their appraisal of the trashy poem. Unable to endure another regurgitation of Robert Frost, Emilio stood quietly to his feet. Under the dim lights of the ceiling, his skin seemed darker than usual. His rippled muscles extorted their mass under the wrinkles of his white Red Sox jersey. A cigarette crater punctured a hole through the side of the shirt -which was a gift after the World Series- forming matching circular stain to compliment his own black eye. Although he had recently returned from Japan in good spirits, the physical manifestation of his run in with old enemies haunted his bruised body. His chest heaved slowly in and out, highlighting the strips of his sepulchral ribs. It ached to speak. “Alright buds, I’ve got to take a piss,” he informed his two friends as if it held some level of importance. They simply nodded back, eyes fixed in concentration on the bosom of the blonde who took stage. Emilio added, jokingly, “save my seat okay?” This produced a slight chuckle from the lips of Michael, the fellow fighter, who set back in is seat with an open fifth of Cyclone in one hand, and a cigarette in the other. But Patrick Orbison, or “Patch,” as they called him, ignored Emilio, rapping his fingers on the table to the beat of the bongos. “Aight. Be right back.” Emilio cracked his neck to one side to work out the healing kinks. He meandered off toward the rest room, dragging his languid frame. “You think he’s alright?” Patch asked, pretending to be concerned about the friend of his friend. At first, Mike didn’t answer. Patch was in a pretty serious relationship with a Mike’s cousin, Sheri. Patch and Michael had been mutual acquaintances since high school but it wasn’t until recently, after meeting at the Dojo. that they became good friends. In fact, enough so to rent an apartment together. But six weeks ago, Patch met Sheri. And from then on out, everything began to chnage.. Their friendship began to sour. “Fuck you,” Mike replied, looking up at toward the stage. A trombone solo began playing. Patch looked down at his Guinness and shrugged. “Po-jazz is for pussies, man.” Mike tossed him a malicious glare. “I’m going to get another drink,” Patch announced while quickly sputtering to his feet. He slid the chair back under the scratched table and headed toward the front of the bar. “Suit yourself. Ass.” Patch ignored this as he wove between drunkards and toward the exit. Hid good friend was starting to get on his nerves and he figured there was no better way to get back at Mike than to fuck his cousin. Long and steady. He laughed. A deep grin came over his face showing his childish dimples. But he loved Sheri. Very much. Just as he reached the doors, something caught his undivided attention: a pair of eyes peered up at him from afar... Brilliant brown embers, an offbeat amber color, embraced his seething retinas. For a moment, Patch store back in awe. He had never seen such a beautiful young girl. But quickly recovering, he pretended not to notice, peering down at his watch. The gaze, however, wouldn’t allow him to leave. By the stairwell, a little brown-skinned girl stood peering up at Patch with an inquisitive frown, her thick red lips arched on either side. Her long brown hair fell in waves around her scrawny shoulders and dangled at her back, arched in tight gnarled curls. She opened her mouth as if to speak, but closed it in silence, biting her top lip. Patch shrugged the aura of impulsive, illogical excitement aside and began meandering his way toward the girl. He danced carefully between the beer-holding patrons, their breath hot with the scent of malt liquor and smokes. By the time he had reached the staircase and perched next to the girl, she turned away with a tuft of her own hair in her mouth. “Excuse me?” Patch asked, in visceral whisper. She looked up at him and smiled. He wanted to ask her why she looked at him in such a way, throwing fire into his face. Why her penetrating glare seemed to be starring at something in his soul, searching for something familiar, and yet, elusive as a shooting star. But all he could mutter was... “Excuse me. Do you...would you mind telling me where the restroom is?” he asked, although his voice came off as squeaky and his thoughts ridden with wonder fay beyond the taming question. The little girl turned and pointed. “Thank...thank you,” he muttered. He gently made his way around her with his eyes focused on the “MEN’S” sign on the top of the doorway. Unfortunately, Emilio was nowhere to be found in the bathroom. Patch was hoping to walk back out with the big man, a shield from the glare of that ponderous young girl. Something deep down told him that Emilio was his only source of protection, as if he held some bond with the girl that would keep her (evil?) presence at bay. But he had obviously split. So after a quick face-washing, Patch relieved himself of a tired yawn and turned to exit the restroom. “Damn.” The girl was waiting for him. She rose her hand like Moses and instantly, a long, tender finger pointed a hole through Patch’s chest, crippling his inclination to move. He store back paralyzed, abrasive, confused as she continued to point. A light sweat formed over his face and his languid heart began to float freely in the heavy cavity of his chest. The “You are my father” emanated from her lips. A question? Or maybe she this was an accusation. Patch blinked. And before he could even contemplate the simple motion of breathing, the girl took him by his hand and led him out of the lodge... He felt odd abandoning his friend. But even odder, it seemed, allowing the quiet girl to climb into the back of his gray Volvo. She had a fierce, mean determination in her eyes and they followed his every move. His knees turned to gelatin and his blood turned to an acidic sewage, burning a hole in his gut. His belly ached. His head hurt. He wanted to protest, to shout out, to scream. But her apparent sense of entitlement and heir of absolute confidence kept him quiet as if he knew, deep down, that it wasn’t in his place to speak. So he started the car and peeled out of the parking lot, and it wasn’t until minutes after leaving the lodge that she spoke again. She turned and gave him a soft smile. He breathed a sigh of relief as he turned the wheel to bring the car onto the street that led to his home. The glare seemingly unfroze him from his position of utter helplessness. “Do you believe me?” she asked, as if she were in question herself. He paused before delivering a quick, assured answer. “No.” He focused in on the road ahead, slowing down at a crosswalk as an old woman in a gentle blue coat made her way tiredly across the street. The girl slightly giggled “What?” Patch asked in alarm. But she didn’t answer. “Do you...do you have a name?” “Sylvia,” she responded, running a hand through her long and beautiful hair. She blew a stray strand of her black silk away and pulled it back, tightening her hair into a bun. She wouldn’t make another statement. “So,” asked Patch, after a long pause, “why would you think that I’m, well, your father?” He raised an eyebrow dramatically as he turned to face the young girl, hoping to startle her into whatever confession it was that she needed to confess. But she simply smiled. “You used to play chess with grandpa,” she said in a conversationalist tone, “well, with your father, I mean.” And then added, “I like chess.” Patch simply shook his head. “Yeah, well...everyone likes playing chess.” “Not everyone. But some people,” she said, holding her ground defensively. “Some people,” Patch agreed. “But,” she added, with a slight hint of sentimentality, “not everyone played on a marble chess board with hand carved pieces.” This got Patch’s attention. He looked back at her through the mirror. She smiled. “And grandpa’s chess board was missing one piece, a rook, so he replaced it with the little toy revolver from Clue. And you would play on soft Sunday afternoons under the shade of the Oak behind the south porch,” she finished, breathlessly. “Maria told me...” Patch slammed on the breaks. His knees shook uncontrollably. The girl’s smile hit close to home. “But I thought you’d be taller,” she said, feeding him more fodder. And just then, out of the corner of his eye, Patch caught a glimpse of Sheri on the sidewalk. Patch shrunk back against his leather seat. “What’s the matter?” the girl inquired, her face contorted in an annoyed stare. “Th-that’s my wife,” he finally stated in a hushed whisper. And then Sheri turned, ready to cross the street. She followed the gleam of the headlights from the strange car which parked over the crosswalk illegally and traced them back to the driver. And then, before averting her eyes, a painful wound haunting her white face, she looked over to the passengers side where the girl tried to slide down and hide. Then, she bolted across the street. “Shit!” Patch banged his hands hard on the steering wheel, honking the horn. “This is gonna be hard to explain...” Preparations. Sheri was taking a hot bath. She snuggled up in the confines of the rounded Jacuzzi with a cheap romance novel in hand.“Passions of the Sun,” or some generic title. She closed the book and tossed it aside. After a sip of her smooth strawberry wine, she laid back to feel the bubbles as they crept gently over her skin, and she emerged herself in the hot water of the deep basin tub. Her toes tickled the chain to the plug and she let out a sigh of relief as the warming sensation came over with. Suddenly, the phone rang. “Shit!” She knocked over the wine. The strawberry liquid puddled up with the shards of broken glass on the white tile floor. Sheri sighed. “That’ll leave a stain...” She scrambled for the phone again. Click. “Hello?” “Hey Sher, it’s Cath!” a voice announced excitedly on the other side. With a quiet chuckle, Sheri laid back in the tub and kicked up her legs. “Sher?” the voice repeated. “Yeah,” Sheri laughed in a school girl’s voice, “I’m here, hone. What’s up?” “Oh,” started Cath as Sheir imagined her sister’s face contorting in a painful frown, “I was just in town with Jay...I thought it might be nice to stop in and see my sis,” she said, alluding to the situation. The subtly was not beyond Sheri’s grasp. “Jay?” she asked, with an angered sigh. She flickered back her hair, suddenly, sending a thousand droplets of water into the face of the fogged mirror. “Yeah...” “Oh honey,” Sheri said in a soothing tone, “I’m so sorry.” She poured herself another glass of wine.” “Don’t be,” replied her sister, rashly. “He was a creep...” My sentiments exactly, Sheri wanted to say, but didn’t. You see, they were so much alike, Sheri and Catherine. The twin sisters had always seemed to find themselves in a pickle, a game of sour relationships with men they once thought to be “the one.” But it always turned out otherwise. So hearing this was a sharp jab in Sheri’s heart. They seemed to share the same soul. Indeed, they even looked identical. Cath knew what her sister thought. “Well, on the bright side, maybe you’ll introduce me to that playboy of your’s...Screech?” she inquired, playfully. “Patch,” Sheri answered, laughing. “And yes, you’re welcome anytime dear...you know that. And Patch,” she said, taking another sip of wine, “yes, he’ll be back tonight.” “Great! We’ve got lots to discuss...” “Well,” Sheri said, trying to get back to her bath, “I wouldn’t want to hold you up then...” Cath got the picture. “Oh...well, yes. I’ll be over in about a half hour, is that okay? I mean,” she added as an aside, “that’s if I don’t get run over by freaks who like to run through crosswalks,” she reported, only half kidding. “Already happened once.” On the other end, Sheri simply laughed. “Yes dear, that sounds fine. Half an hour is fine Yes, yes, alright...I love you too. Yes, bye.” Sheri sighed. She hung up the phone and slid back into the tub with her legs sticking up over the top of the basin. Here goes nothing...or something. Patch pulled uneasily into his driveway. A light sweat trickled down his hot face rubbing salt in the mall blisters that protruded from his dry, reddened cheeks. “Sorry,” he said in a weak voice. He spoke as if he were going into anaphylactic shock. “Christ,” Sylvia asked, almost more embarrassed by the condition of her own father than out of concern for him, “are you okay?” He simply nodded his head and cut the engine with a turn of the key. “Wait here,” he said, with no apparent sense of authority. “This going to be some tough explaining...” “What are you going to explain? That you’re my father?” Patch put his hand on his chest and took in a deep breath. No more wheezing. He took in air at a steady rate, attempting to control his breath. He looked at Sylvia and rolled his eyes in a gesture that almost said “oh yeah, I forgot.” “Well, that remains to be seen,” he calmly stated. “No, no it doesn’t. It’s a fact, Patch. Something you’re going to have to get used to,” said Sylvia with her arms crossed angrily over her chest. She looked over at Patch and gave him that awful look. It was the first time this evening she had raised her voice. “Look, hone, I know absolutely nothing about you! Who you are, where you came from, what you’ve done. All I know is that you obviously have some...well, semi-legitimate reason for believing that I’m your father, and we obviously have some connections. But as far as I know, it ends there. For all I know, this could be a set up. Mike, that bastard, I bet it is!” He slowly unclenched his fists though as tears began to swell in the corner of the girl’s eyes. She began to weep, softly at first, and then uncontrollably. “Hey, Hey I just...ah, I don’t know! I mean, I don’t remember having any...er....bad experiences back in my late-teens, I mean, assuming that’s how you were...Heh.” He banged the steering wheel again. “I-I just don’t know.” At that, he slowly stepped out of the car. Giving the girl one last look -she was curled up in the middle of the seat now, burying her face in her lap- Patch took in a deep breath and slowly trudged across the driveway and to the bottom of the apartment staircase, leaving soft imprints in the light snow. Whether he could explain it or not, he had to do this. Besides, Sheri would have to believe him. Believe him in terms of the fact that Sylvia wasn’t a date or something. I mean, all that the girl knew about him proved something...right? But still, hopefully just not enough to make her awful truth come into fruition... Set in motion. Senor Ragerio, Hector Ragerio, the bad man, the bad father, sat at his desk in quiet contemplation. He ran the length of a long Cuban cigar under his nose, and then satisfied, lit it up. He blew out a long, protracted puff of smoke and looked out the window. Production was up, but the workers were tired. Frustrated mothers rounded up their energetic children as the males set to work in the fields, kissing their wives goodbye as they handed them straw bags of bread, cheese and water for the day. Dogs rolled in the mug, digging holes to protect themselves from the oncoming heat. The guards set around cleaning their guns or playing poker. Senor Ragerio leaned back in his desk and considered his future: His one son, heir to his throne and all the icing it entailed, was missing, gone off to find a better way of life. To live without “stepping on the backs of others,” as he so crudely put it. But he had no idea of the consequences. Senor Ragerio’s small empire was but the tip of the family iceberg. And who was he, some “celebrity,” to throw it all away? Senor Ragerio angrily clenched his fists. Mr. Bush, in continuation with is father’s “war on drugs,” had to at least keep the outward appearance of authoritarian control. So randomly, Senor Ragerio was forced to sacrifice one of his compatriots and allow them to become a trophy head for the U.S. Department of Justice. It was always sad to see a cousin or good friend go, but it had to be done And here he was, that “Rage” - or whatever his ridiculous pseudonym was- the most untrustworthy and unsavory of all his acquaintances, and yet, he still felt it in his heart to keep him safe, to one day give him the keys to the empire. What a good dad, Senor Ragerio thought, smiling. Rest assured, a head from the State Department told him, his assets were safe. So long as he returned their favors. “Mucho caliente,” he remarked with a wave of the hand, fanning himself from the heat. Suddenly, there was knock at the door. “Ci.” The door opened and in came an advisor holding a small sheet of paper. “Sir, we just got word from feds,” said the man, breathlessly. “The girl just touched down in the States on Thursday. The exact details,” he informed, shoving the paper toward his superior, “are right here.” “Thank you,” said Mr. Ragerio, gently receiving the paper. The plans had been set in motion. A misconception to die for, the finale. The door was unlocked. Patch carefully stepped inside and kicked off his soggy shoes. The lights were dimmed and the only intelligible sound came from the glow of the living room television. Some winter advisory forecast. Patch knew he was in for a dog fight. He drew in a deep breath and held it ‘til his nerves subsided and the led in his belly seemed to grow lighter. He wiped the sweat off his brow. Rounding the kitchen corner, he saw that the tea water was boiling, the white bubbles rising over the top of the kettle and soiling the electric stove top. Patch ran over and quickly turned it off. Well, he thought, he’d at least have ammunition in his barrel as well...But soon, the hurt in his gut came back. How to explain, how to reassure, how to convince his girlfriend that he wasn’t the man she thought he was, but yet, at heart he still was, and that if he wasn’t, well it wasn’t his choice. And all the while the burning thought that maybe it was a misunderstanding, confusion, all a sick joke, played out in the back of his mind But no, the parallels were too great. He was the girl’s father. But as he made his way quietly into the bedroom, something caught his eye: The bathroom door. It was open. And as he stepped in, his heart sunk in his chest. There Sheri lay in the tub, still, her arms and legs over the edge of the tub. He couldn’t see her head; it was buried in the water. And a sanguine pool of red, scattered with shards of glass, formed a pool on the tile floor. Patch buckled at the knees. He had to use the frame of the door to keep himself steady. The acid refilled his chest like a thousand-gallon fish tank exploding in a closed quartered room. And then, slowly, methodically, he used the wall to pull himself back into the living room. He kept his knees bent as it to give him more leverage as he crept along like a decrepit old man. Without giving it another thought, he headed toward a small wooden cabinet at the rear. He pulled out the plastic gun. And a moment later, lifting it into the air, he fired.
