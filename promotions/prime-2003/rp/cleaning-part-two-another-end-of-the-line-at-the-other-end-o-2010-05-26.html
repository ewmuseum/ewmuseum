Cleaning, Part Two: Another End of the Line at the Other End of the Line

26 May 2010

Date: 5-26-10 Cleaning A Garbage Bag Production Part Two: Another End of the Line at the Other End of the Line *** The Grass Is Always Greener *** Loose refuse spilled from the mouth of the overturned trash can, and the lidless profanity echoed through the alleyway. I was out there swearing up a storm, and under normal circumstances, I wouldn't have even been in that situation, but I was only back in town for a few days, passing through from somewhere to somewhere else. I always went back when it was on the way, even though there wasn't much of a place for me left. “Fuck!” I kicked the can for emphasis, angry how embarrassed I was, but Frank didn't hold it against me. He just watched the fit rage on leaning against a telephone pole with his arms crossed. I figured he was enjoying the show. “Fucking shit!” The alleys looked pretty much the same as they always looked. From the street, the fronts of the buildings looked nicer, but the city was doing a rush job of building itself up that was more interested in hanging some curtains than fixing the windows. “It'd be one thing if he sold us shit for the price of shit, you know?” Frank knew it was rhetorical, and he wiped the grin off of his face with his thumb. “But the motherfucker has the fucking audacity to stand there with that dumb fucking grin on his face and act like he's doing me a favor.” Frank nodded along in assessment, but his slight display of empathy wasn't helping me cool down any. I kicked a plastic bottle down the alley and watched it skid across the pavement. “I got burned, Frank.” I looked at him, and it made him uncross his arms. “Every time I buy from this motherfucker, it's always shit. I swear to god, man. One of these days, I'm going to teach this motherfucker a lesson. He thinks he can fuck with me? I'll show him.” This time Frank laughed, and I wasn't really in the mood to look for the humor in the situation, but I knew there wasn't much else to laugh at besides me. I took a deep breath and sat down on the overturned trash bin. Frank walked over from his leaning spot and handed me the joint. I puffed on it, and it didn't seem to be doing much, but I kept puffing anyway. You smoke enough of anything, and it'll fuck you up somehow. “Let me ask you something.” I handed the joint back to Frank. “This shit getting you high at all?” “Not in the slightest.” Frank drew in deep and exhaled routinely. “Say, man. I got a question for you.” “Yeah?” “If this guy never has any good shit and he keeps on burning you, why don't you just stop buying from him?” There were several answers, really. He was easy to reach, and he always had something to sell, given that he probably didn't have too many clients anymore. Despite that, he was still around, and that was rare. I figured he'd always be around when I couldn't get a hold of nobody else. “I don't know, man.” I suppose there wasn't really a valid reason. “I just figure that one of these days, he's bound to have some good shit for me.” “Why?” “Karma.” I shrugged. “Ain't that how the universe is supposed to work?” ---- *** Another End of the Line at the Other End of the Line *** ---- Tim Shipley was at the bar that night, and without the cameras around, his charismatic gestures actually worked to endear his audience to him. Johnny never would've guessed that the two were about the same age. Each one seemed older in a different way: Tim with his heavily designed sense of class and Johnny with the swift attrition of recklessness. The two didn't drink together. Johnny took a seat in a corner booth and watched Shipley at work. Johnny grinned over his straw at the Englishman, and failed to figure out it was the grooming as much as it was the accent. “He's a crafty bastard, ain't he?” The dialect poured from his mouth like cement as the recently dethroned Scottish king slid his long frame into the booth across from Johnny. “Hey, Andy. How's it going?” “Can't complain, brother. How about you? How's the new job fitting?” “It's different.” Johnny shrugged and stirred his drink with his straw before sipping some of the whiskey down. “But I'm getting used to it.” The two of them had always gotten along as their paths crossed back and forth, but despite a loosely interwoven history and mutual respect for the others' work ethic and skill, they didn't have too much to say to each other. They were different men, and they were both different men than they were. “Hell of a run with the belt, by the way.” Johnny finished the rest of the whiskey. “And hell of a match with Roberto.” “Thanks, Johnny.” Andy chuckled humbly. “I fathom it'll be nice to have the weight off my shoulders for a while. I'm just looking forward to spending some time with Marvin. I appreciate you writing me off the tapings for a couple of weeks.” “It's no problem. You deserve the break.” Johnny chipped at the ice with his straw and stared off to Shipley at the bar, who was in the midst of some charming story that commanded the attention of the nearby crowd. “You sure you're cool with Shipley putting you out?” “Yeah, man. Why wouldn't I be?” Johnny was still getting to know how genuine Andy's humility was, and while part of him appreciated that, the other part was a staunch realist. “I don't know.” Johnny shrugged again. “I guess it's just that Shipley's done a great job with Just and all, but he's only had one televised match in GCW. What I'm saying is that nobody saves you a spot on top of the mountain.” Johnny had the relics to prove it, and he lacked the accomplishment to prove otherwise. He couldn't count the times he clawed his way back only to hang there until there was space on the peak. He always fell from the highest point he could, the aches and pains of landing serving as reminders that he'd been close at all. His subscription painkillers were appropriate consolation prizes. “I'm tired of fighting the same old people.” Andy was perceptively stubborn and stubbornly perceptive. He was far more versed with how the company worked than his new boss. Still, he had faith in Johnny's ability to right the ship. Andy had more faith in Johnny than Johnny, and Johnny was by no means faithless, or at least Andy gave off that impression, which to Johnny, was far more important than the unabashed truth at the infancy of his reign. “But don't get me wrong, boss. I'm not taking my eyes off the prize. I'm going to keep that spot safe. Trust me.” “I didn't mean it like that, man. I'm just a little drunk.” Johnny looked away from Andy and back at Tim. He bit his lip and shook his head. “I don't know. It's just that this was your first title run, right? I mean, you busted your ass for a while, and pretty soon, everyone knew you had it in you.” “That's right, I guess.” Andy chuckled humbly again. He was such a good guy that Johnny really didn't know how to react, but Andy didn't really know where Johnny was going with it either, and ultimately, neither did Johnny. “And even though everyone else figured you were going to get some gold sooner or later, you still had to prove it to yourself, right?” “Of course.” “I don't know, man. I just don't know. You know I quit drinking for a few months once?” Andy smiled politely, but his smile slowly faded as Johnny sucked melted ice through the straw for any trace of missed whiskey. “Yeah. It felt great, man. I'd beaten the monster and all, but you know what happened? I knew I could do it, cause I'd proved it to myself. So guess what?” Johnny knocked over his glass very purposefully. The ice slid across the table, most of it going right on over the edge. Johnny stood up and wobbled. “I drink now, cause I know I can quit, and I don't want to try again and find out that I can't anymore.” “Easy, man.” Andy stood up quickly and grabbed Johnny by the arm to keep him steady. With his free hand, he signaled for a waitress to help with the spill. He sat Johnny back down in the booth, and Johnny submitted without putting up a fight. “How much have you had to drink?” “I don't know. I'm sorry. I'll be fine.” Johnny was embarrassed. Andy obviously didn't want to encroach, but Johnny was too drunk, at that point, to notice. “You want me to get you a water or something?” “Nah. Don't worry about me. I'll just go home and sleep it off.” “You sure?” “Yeah.” “I'm going to go say hi to Tim, then.” Andy looked at Johnny, trying to show just the right amount of concern. “Keep it safe, brother.” Johnny watched Andy Murray walk off towards Tim Shipley. Johnny and Tim locked eyes briefly before Tim turned away after surveying the commotion of the commissioner's outburst. Though Johnny couldn't explain why, he realized that he and Tim had a lot of things in common. Try as they might, neither of them was Andy Murray, and from their times in charge, both had the vision to see that about themselves. Both were looking for a place where those in charge wouldn't. Both would settle for convincing everyone else, or anyone else, short of themselves. A busboy came by with a broom and pail to sweep up the ice, lest someone fall when it melted. *** Bright Lights and Good Dirt *** I don't know why I was there that late in the locker room after the Halo. It was my worst finish in one of those things, but for some reason, it felt good to go out that early. It didn't feel great, but it felt better than it could have, I supposed. I thought everyone else had left. The lights were off, and it was silent for a while. I got up to leave, but I heard what sounded like faint sobs. I threw my duffel over my shoulder and headed towards the door, past a few banks of lockers when I saw him there, and my muscles relaxed as I sank back down onto my feet. He noticed me there enough to compose himself in front of me. Then Brandon Youngblood punched one of the locker doors as hard as he could, denting the metal and undoubtedly fucking up his hand. He ignored the pain completely. “Fuck, Johnny. Fuck.” I couldn't congratulate him for a match hard fought or offer condolences for a glory barely lost. There wasn't much I could do. I knew what it was like to finish second, and I knew there wasn't much I could say that he'd want to hear. “I was this fucking close.” He pinched the fingers on his good hand together. “I'm always this fucking close.” “I know, man.” I put the duffel bag down on the other end of the bench and sat between Brandon and the bag. “I'm thinking of hanging up the boots, man. I really am.” Brandon looked at me as if he wanted me to dissuade him, but that wouldn't have helped things any. I knew in the locker room, he had a reputation. I'd heard that Youngblood never backed down from an argument. I'd met him a few times, and though I could see how he had been the subject of many controversies, I never had reason for quarrel with him. I figured I'd let him speak out, and busied myself searching through my bag for a half finished fifth of Jack Daniels. I unscrewed the cap and handed the bottle to Brandon. He gulped some of it down, wiped his mouth with his forearm, and passed the bottle back to me. “What the fuck am I supposed to do? I've tried everything. I couldn't put down Snow. I couldn't take the Dual Halo. Do you know how hard I've been training for this shit?” I nodded and screwed the cap back on the bottle. I set it down between us in case he wanted any. He did. “You mind?” I shook my head. He grabbed the bottle and worked the cap back off, grimacing as he turned it with his punching hand. “I'm just sick of giving this all that I have when I get nothing back in return.” “And you're ready to cash in your chips over it?” “I might as well before I've given them all away, right?” I shrugged. I really couldn't tell him what to do with his chips. I had enough on my plate wondering how to spend mine. The thought of walking away had occurred to me several times. I'd been able to walk away before, but I always ended up coming back. Wasn't much I could do with all those unspent chips when I was away from the games. He slammed the bottle back down on the bench, not so much intentionally. He just lacked the control of his body thanks to the alcohol quickly hitting his bloodstream and the emotion slowly draining through him. The bottle was nearly empty. “I'm sorry, man. I'll buy you another one.” “Don't worry about it.” I looked over at him, and he had his head in his hands. He looked defeated, and with all the losses I'd suffered, I envied that. I couldn't “What the fuck else can I do, though?” “I wish I could tell you.” I looked around the locker room. I felt like a tourist. “I don't know what the hell I'm still doing here either.” “Even with a boss job over at GCW, you still can't stay out of the ring, can you?” “It ain't easy to stay out of it, especially when the choice is yours to make.” I could see that he was calming down, even as the liquor worked its way through. The whiskey was no match for the grueling drain that the last two nights took on his body. His mind. His soul. “What makes you think you can do it?” He didn't respond right away. He just shook his head and looked at the dent in the locker. He looked down at the strange bumps forming on his fist where he punched in the metal and rubbed the bumps with his free hand. “I can't afford not to.” He looked at me and laughed, probably for the first time in a couple of nights. “This shit is driving me insane.” “Tell me about it.” I nodded along and unscrewed the cap to take down the few sips at the bottom of the bottle. “I just don't want to be known as the guy who overstayed his welcome. I've never been able to grab the brass ring, you know?” He laughed again, this time more desperately. “People say I'm one of the best in the game, and I started believing them early on. Hell, I believed it before they did. But when it comes to proof, what accomplishments can I list?” “Man, you know it's not about that. It's about what you give when you go out there, win or lose. The people who were there to witness your fights, man, they don't care about what you've won. They care about what you do.” “For now, Johnny, but not for good.” I nodded along with him. I saw his point. “When the dust settles, and people look back, they'll only remember what the records show after memory forgets. I just figure I'll save myself the trouble. I don't want to drag my name down as one of those guys who dragged his name through the dirt, trying and trying until it kills me.” “It'll pass, man. It'll pass.” I sighed, and looking around, I lit a cigarette in the PRIME locker room. I knew the brass there hated when I did that. “You know, I thought a lot of the same things after I lost to Desade in the finals.” “Yeah?” “Yeah. I went into the tournament swearing it'd be my last hurrah. I was going to do my thing, and then I was going to leave and never look back.” “Why didn't you?” “I figured, how can you dirty up a name like Garbage Bag Johnny?”
