Making Plans (RP Vs Killean for CIII)

11 August 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/11/06 Chuck's Plans Thursday, August 10th. 2:10 P.M. “He’s lucky I didn’t press charges. God damned lucky!” Chuck stormed back and forth across the living room, each of his steps put down in aggressive form. It was only the third time he’d talked to, and the first time he’d seen Mary since her estranged husband had turned his face into a crimson mess of blood, tears, saliva and snot. At her pleading request, he had agreed not to file charges against him… and what did he have to show in return for it? A couple lingering cuts that left him answering “oooooh what happened to your face?” questions every day. “I know. And I can’t thank you enough for that. But this whole thing has been crazy for me. Ever since I moved out back in December… my thoughts have been all over the place, and I just don’t feel like I can…” “Mary” He stopped his angered romp around the living room, “All I’m asking for is another shot at dinner. Just something simple in a nice, quiet, romantic restaurant. Hell, I’d settle for semi-romantic. I just want the opportunity. You can’t sit around forever waiting for Matt to come apologize and try to make something work out of all of this.” “I know… I just… I don’t know when the right time is to say ‘ok, that’s enough, time to move on’. It’s not that simple.” “It doesn’t have to be… not just for us to go out on a simple little dinner. Nothing has to be set in stone. You don’t have to have made up your mind for all eternity. Just give yourself the chance to get out of the house and have some fun while you do think about everything.” “I dunno…” She ran her fingers through her hair with an exhausted sigh. “C’mon… we’ll catch a goofy, stupid movie. Something completely off the wall and entirely unromantic.” “I’ll think about it.” “Fair enough.” He stood up from the couch, took a step towards her and wrapped his arms around her waist, putting his chin on her shoulder. “It’ll give ya chance to think about something other than Matt.” “Yeah.” “Alright, then I’ll give you a call in a couple of days. We’ll set a date and pick a movie.” “Ok.” Mary rested her chin on the top of his shoulder and stared past him with heavy eyes, just gazing at a blank wall. “I’ll talk to you then, I guess.” * * * Spring, 2003 ”You want to what?” “I want to quit my job.” Mary pulled her sweatshirt over her knees as she nestled onto the sofa with Matthew. “And try your hand at writing a novel?” “Yup.” Her tone was decidedly more casual than his. “I thought politics was your passion? You do remember that Poli Sci was your major and creative writing was just your minor, right?” “Of course! My memory isn’t that bad.” She gave him a childish punch in the shoulder, then kissed him on the cheek as if in immediate apology. “And I enjoy my job… it’s just an unnecessary stress sometimes. I love to write, and I just want to give it a shot.” “But quitting your job…” He shook his head in disbelief. “We’re financially secure.” Mary began to run her fingers through his hair, stopping occasionally to twirl a few strands around her index finger. “I was making ok money. And with them having to honor your contract for R4W, that’s bringing in a really nice sum every month.” “Yeah, but that expires soon. It’s good money, but it’s short term pay.” “Still, we’re secure for the moment. Enough to allow us to get by just fine while I give writing a shot. If it doesn’t work out… I can go back to my old job.” “And you’ll be able to do that… just march back into the office and say ‘ok, I’d like my job back, thanks.’ I dunno…” He pulled his left arm, pinned between her and the back of the couch, and wrapped it around her shoulders once he had tugged it free. “… you really think they’ll just hand ya the job?” “No, not necessarily just hand it to me with a ‘whatever you want, Mary!’… but they might let me back on. It’s not like I’ll be leaving on bad terms. Besides, if they don’t, I’ll be able to go a similar job elsewhere.” “Sounds like you’ve already made up your mind.” “I know it sounds kinda crazy, Matthew, but I’d really love to give writing a chance. It’s always been something I’ve enjoyed doing. And lately, I haven’t had the time to do much of it at all.” “Ok.” He kissed her on the forehead. “Ok?” “Ok. I say go for it. Nothing should stop you from doin’ what you love to do. Besides, I read some of your stuff you wrote in college… and it was fantastic.” “So I have your support.” She softly slid away from him, simply so she could see the look in his eyes when he gave his answer. If he tried to lie, she would be able to tell in a second just by watching. They gave away absolutely everything, and all his deception disintegrated when he couldn’t hide his eyes. “You have my complete and total support.” She gazed as she listened. He was telling the truth. * * * Plan A Thursday, August 10th. 3:03 P.M. The Plan was simple. Go to the house of his in-laws, sit down with Mary, and explain to her that if she couldn’t support his desire to follow his passion, then it was time for both of them to head in their own direction and stop hanging onto premonitions of impossible hope, just wasting time while nothing ever happened. First, however, he had to make it out of Stacey’s place alive. He shifted a step to the right, out of the way of the cup that rocketed towards his skull. The plastic missile ricocheted off the wall, spiraling back across the room, spraying its contents across the carpet like some sort of out of control sprinkler. “She has the nerve to walk out on you because she ‘hates’ wrestling so much, but then her hypocritical ass shows up on ReVolution last week?!” Apparently, hurling her drink across the room hadn’t been enough of a release. Stacey turned and punched the wall. There was a loud ‘cracking noise, though Matt couldn’t be sure if it was the dry wall or her first that had been the source of the discomforting noise. “God damn it! I can’t believer her!” “Stacey…” Matt opened his mouth, but let it hang in useless silence. He hadn’t expected quite the vicious reaction that he was witnessing. Still, he had come to Stacey’s house when she had called him specifically so he could tell her… face to face. Standing there with his mouth half open, drool rolling down his chin and collecting in his goatee… he was accomplishing nothing. Wasn’t too much unlike the last eight months had been. “Stace, calm down.” “Calm down?! I’ve been here for how many months? Just waiting patiently, trying to let you and Mary work things out… trying not to push myself into the middle of things, no matter how much I may want to… and she’s going on national TV making a mockery of you… of everything you stand for! She’s standing in the middle of a fucking wrestling ring, trading verbal jabs with the father of my child! Calm down?!” “Ok… stupid suggestion, I guess.” Matt stared on as Stacey paced back and forth, dragging her feet across the same stretch of carpet so many times, he figured it must have been lighting the bottom of her bare feet on fire. “God damn it!” “Look, Stacey… I know that you’ve been hanging around here because of me. And I know that you don’t have much to show for it, and that you’re trying to straighten out your life and I haven’t done much of anything to help make that happen and…” he was rambling, cracking the knuckles on his left hand in his right palm. “I’m sorry about all that.” ‘Sorry’ seemed to be the red light to her aggression. Slowly, she stopped pacing, noticed the now empty glass laying on the carpet, and leaned over to pick it up. “So, what now?” As she stood, cup in hand, the look she gave him somehow made it easy to answer. “Now, the plan is to go see Mary and tell her that it’s probably time we went our own ways. Last week on the show was the first time I had talked to my own wife since before Christmas. Even then, we said just enough for me to explode and her to throw it back in my face. I figure moving on from that, isn’t lettin’ go of much.” “So what does that mean for us?” She collapsed onto the couch, rolling the cup between her hands, staring into its barren bottom. “Doesn’t mean anything for us right now. I figure I better take things one step at a time, or I’ll probably end up knocked on my ass.” Matt started for the door, “Anyways, you called… so I figured I’d at least stop by and talk to you face to face.” he turned the door knob and stepped into the thin space that wasn’t indoors and wasn’t outdoors, but before he could put foot back into acknowledged existence, Stacey called for him to stop. “Matt,” she took her gaze off of the cup and shot it in his direction. His eyes met hers, though they lacked the same genuine luminance of hers. “I can’t speak for Mary, but I now I’ll be rooting for you this Sunday against Killean.” He left the void of the space in the doorway and moved outdoors. “I’ll be seein ya around, Stace.” * * * Summer, 2004 “You promise?” “Absolutely” He reached down and grabbed her hand as he drug his feet through the sand of the beach. The occasional waves from the ocean that crashed ashore swarmed around their ankles. He loved getting away to their little beach house in Florida. Walking along the ocean with Mary was probably his favorite pleasure in life. If all went as planned, at the end of the week, the last night of vacation, they’d be doing this same thing under the moonlight… and she’d have a ring on her finger. It just seemed like the perfect time to hang up the boots. “Thank you, baby. I can’t tell you how much that means to me.” Mary swung her flip-flops in her free hand as they ambled along to the rhythmic sound of the roaring waves. “Every time you’d step foot into that ring, it would have me a nervous wreck. I just kept waiting for the night where something would go wrong and you’d wind up seriously injured, permanently injured, or even d…” “Stop. Wrestling’s not that dangerous.” He knew she would make some sort of objection so he moved the conversation along, putting out the fire before he could start it. “Besides, I’m gonna hang it up… dangerous or not… there’s no more worries, sweetie.” For a couple of minutes, the two walked in silence, occasionally glancing over to the other and smiling. His seemed effortless, hers were more those of uneasy doubt. It took him till the second smile to realize that there was something strange to their nature. “Somethin’ on your mind?” “Just like that” Mary took her eyes off of the endless, minuscule grains of sand beneath her steps and looked at him, face to face while she finished the sentence. “you’re retired? That’s it? Just like that?” “Why not? Wrestling isn’t the only thing I’ve got going for me” he winked as he leaned over to kiss her on the cheek. “I have plenty of plans for my future that don’t involve professional wrestling.” “What kind of plans?” “You’re impatient.” “Yes, I am” This time, the smile that spread across her face was lighter, not troubled in any fashion. “And as long as you’re retired, I’m happy too.” “I’m retired. So be happy. It’ll be good for me… good for us. It’ll just be nice, ya know?” “Sounds like you’ve suddenly become quite the fan of retirement.” “Yeah.” As the sun began to sink towards the horizon, Matt reached into the pocket of his white button-up shirt and pulled out his sunglasses, and casually slid them on. “I’m enjoying it already.” * * * Plan B Thursday, August 10th. 3:34 P.M. There was nothing quite like the smell of a thunderstorm rolling into a summer afternoon in Ohio. Sometimes, if you were lucky, you could even taste it hanging in the air as it approached, dancing across the Midwest. As he made the drive to Mary’s house, Matt left the windows down. Despite the rain that began to fall, sporadically as small drops in random places, he couldn’t pass up the opportunity to let the oncoming storm fill his car and ease his senses. The Plan was simple. Park in front of the house, ring the doorbell, and the second Mary answered… apologize. Tell her that he was sorry, that he had been a fool, that he had made a mistake and realized it was time to fix it. There and then, he would quit. Make good on an old promise. Let it all go and return to his life with his family. For the past eight months, he had abandoned his responsibilities as a father and as a husband. It didn’t matter that Mary had walked out on him… in stepping into the ring every week, each night breaking the promise he had made to her, he was telling her to go ahead and leave. It wasn’t fair to her… even less fair to his one year old daughter who was growing up with one parent, when she had every opportunity to be brought up in a full, loving, and… Before the thought could finish processing in his mind, it was jolted out the back of his brain. Just as his car had been rolling to a halt, it was suddenly propelled forward, coming to rest under the red light it had meant to stop short of. The sound of grinding metal pushed into his ears, disrupting the soothing way the storm appealed to his senses. When the noise ceased and the unwanted lurching of his car had passed, Matt took a moment to realize what had occurred. And then, as the rain began to fall more forcefully, his anger began to rise. To his right, he saw stopped traffic, waiting at the intersection as normal… the only difference was the concerned looks on the faces that peered through the repetitive passing of windshield wipers. To his left, he saw the bumper of a large pick up truck, the headlight just above it shooting right into his window. The truck couldn’t have been more than a foot from his drivers side door, not enough room for him to even exit the car. Through the raindrops on his own windshield, he saw a man hop out of the truck and run in front of his car, over to the passenger’s side door. “Are you ok?” The man leaned in through the open window. His voice sounded heavily of concern. “Yeah… I’m fine.” “Are you sure?” “Yeah.” Matt unfastened his seatbelt. “Let me out. I can’t get out on my side.” The man withdrew from the window and took several steps away from Matt’s car. Sliding ungracefully across the console and the passenger’s seat, Matthew pushed open his door and let his feet land violently on asphalt. “Who the fuck hit me?!” He slammed closed his door and immediately spun on his heels to answer his own question, turning to see who had plowed into him at the stoplight. From a white Ford Taurus just a few feet behind Matt’s Solstice, a man sheepishly stepped out into the street. The look on his face was one of sheer panic. “I… I’m s…sorry sir.” He held out his hands in pleading fashion. “My CD player started skipping and I looked d…down for just a sec… just a second.” “Just a second?” With quick, deliberate steps, Matt marched towards the cause of the accident. “That second almost got me killed! You pushed me into the middle of the intersection!” Arriving at the trembling man, Matt grabbed him by the collar and yanked his line of vision towards the road crossing. Do you see?! That pick-up came within twelve inches of ramming right into my door! That god damn monster truck came within a tenth of a second of killing me… because of your stupid fuckin’ CD player!” “I… I’m sorry!” The man reached up with his hands and tried to break Matt’s grip of his shirt… but to no avail. “ “Sorry?!” Matt shoved the man back, pinning his against his own car, and leaned in bringing his burning face to within inches of the man’s terrified eyes. “I could have been killed!” “Whoa whoa! Calm down!” The man from the truck rushed over to the scene, convinced the rain wasn’t going to do a thing to extinguish the fire that looked ready to erupt uncontrollably. “C’mon… calm down!” Matt felt a hand on his shoulder pull him away. For some reason, he allowed the tug to release his grasp. “I… I’ll take care of it. I’ll pay… I have insurance.” Despite hearing the stumbling words of the man who had hit him from behind, Matt ignored any significance they held. Instead, he turned his head in the direction of the intersection. There, he shut out everything around him and focused on the sight of the massive truck mere inches from his car. Then, before heading back to his damaged vehicle, Matt made one last comment to the man who’d caused the mess. “Ya know, guys like you shouldn’t be allowed on the road.” Even before he finished the sentence, he started to step away, knowing that it was pointless. Everyone did that sort of thing. He’d taken his eyes off the road more than once to change a CD, or grab his sunglasses from the glove compartment. The truth was, an afternoon drive could be dangerous. There wasn’t anything in life that couldn’t kill a man. * * * Thursday, August 10th. After the accident. Before the Apology. Killean. Fuck. It sometimes blew his mind that in the span of just four years, Killean had managed to be his biggest enemy, and his closest friend. Then again, life was often strange that way. It was hard to imagine that four years could all lead up to one night, and despite the occasional feeling of disbelief, there was a definite high (not of the Nova/Facey persuasion) that came with thought of finally stepping into the ring with The self-proclaimed ‘Supreme Machine’. Not that anything guaranteed that would ever be enough. The pressure on both men’s back was enough to cripple them. Killean was the champion… the hall of famer… the man who had been hired four years ago solely for the purpose of taking Matt out of the game. If he failed… he would want another fight. And Matt… it had been the year of Tchu in some people’s mind. To those believers, CIII was quite simply put, his official crowning. If he failed… one year of successes would become an afterthought. Wasted time to mimic his personal life. One way or another, personal or professional, he needed something to show for the past year. Unless frost could form on the burning embers of hell… and he could have both. The pressure to walk away from a violent battle wasn’t anything he couldn’t handle. The pressure to make the right decision was what kicked his ass. Matt pulled to a stop in front of the house, and turned the key, silencing its hum. Suddenly, the million thoughts in his head sounded a whole lot louder. * * * Plan C Thursday, August 10th. 4:04 P.M. He rang the doorbell, then pushed his shoulders back, straightening his posture, like he were some sort of salesman. It’s Important to have a ‘Plan C’. Sometimes Plan A just doesn’t work out, and Plan B… well, there’s probably a reason it was Plan B. As he heard movement in the house and saw a shadow move passed the window towards the front door, he abandoned the nervous look on his face and shoved a smile through the tension. Maybe he was selling something. Through the glass pane in the door, Matt could see that it was Mary who had come to answer the bell, and as the door swung up, he spoke before there could be an awkward moment… at least, he hoped. “Hey, Mary.” “Matthew” She wasn’t saying his name so much in shock, but because it was just nice to be able to look him in the eyes and say it. “I was wonderin’ if we could talk. I thought maybe we could grab a bite to eat, maybe head to the…” “No… c’mon in. We can talk here. Got the house to myself right now. Mom and Papa took Georgie to the park for the afternoon.” Mary moved to the side and Matt took the offer without saying a word. She closed the door behind him and followed his steps to the living room. For a moment, Mary thought about telling Matt to have a seat on the couch while she got him something to drink, but as her husband came to a stop in the middle of the room, she remembered that he didn’t like to sit unless he had plans of staying somewhere for a while. “I guess some things haven’t changed over the last eight months.” “Naw” he smiled, not forcing it this time, “your mother’s couch is just too hard. It’s like sitting on a stone slab.” “I know I know… you like the big comfy couches that swallow you whole when you crash into ‘em.” “It’s nice bein’ comfortable.” “Yeah… it is.” So much for avoiding any awkward moments. As Matt stared across at the woman whose face he’d seen one solitary time since Christmas, he figured it was pointless to try and find some way to delicately delve into the reasons that had made the last year what it had been. So he didn’t dance around it, didn’t give easing into things a second thought. “I want to say to you…” he paused, rubbed the back of neck, harshly, then finished, “… that I’m sorry.” “I’ve been waiting a long time to hear that.” She sat down on the concrete couch, though he didn’t follow suit, and she didn’t expect him to. “A long time.” “And I should have said it a long time ago. The truth is… I really screwed up by letting you walk out and just expecting you’d come back if I sat around on my ass and just pretended you weren’t gone.” Matt looked down at his hands, the same way he had done the day he’d first asked her out to dinner and a movie. “That was the biggest mistake of my life. I had a responsibility as a father and husband, and I just ignored ‘em all. That was stupid… cuz I love you, and I love my baby girl. The second I came home to an empty house, I should have been out the door, headed over, making strides to push through things, and I…” “Matthew” In some way, it pained her… despite how many nights she had dreamed of this moment since she’d left him… to hear him sounding so humble and so afraid of his own failures. “I should be apologizing too. You aren’t the only one with a phone. I could have called you at any time. You’re not the only one with a car. I could have come home on any given day. And I didn’t. We’re both bullheaded… always have been. It’s why I came to the show last week… to prove some sort of stubborn point.” “I guess some things haven’t changed over the last eight months.” “No,” a small laugh escaped her serious tone. “Still stubborn as ever. And I still love you.” “Good. I was really hoping you hadn’t gotten to hating me. It would have really sucked if the drive over here would have all been for nothing.” Suddenly, he fell silent, thinking back to the accident. “Matthew, is something wrong?” She looked up at him, very much worried about the change in the tone of things. “No… no, I’m fine.” He closed his eyes briefly and gave his head a quick twitch, trying to shake himself back to where he had intended to be. “Anyways… wish I would have done this a lot sooner. Ya know… apologize for shutting you out, running you off, and then not chasing right after ya.” “It doesn’t really matter.” She stood, took a step towards him and wrapped her arms around his waist, burying her face in his shoulder. “Right now you’re standing here, and that’s good enough for me.” “Good.” “So…” she kept her head against his shoulder, didn’t loosen her grip a bit, “we’ve apologized for all that stupid stubbornness that kept us apart for eight months. Did you maybe come to tell me that you were retiring from the ring… for good?” “No.” Matt rested his chin on the top of her head and stared past her with heavy eyes, just gazing at a blank canvas. “I came to tell you I’m gonna win the Universal Championship in three days.”
