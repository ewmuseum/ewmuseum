North and South

Date: 16/1/06 North and South There is no feeling so alone When the one you’re hurting is your own. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ What put the blood on his hands? What put the blood on his hands? Is that the blood of a slaughtered hare? Or is that the blood of a man? Paddy O’Shea was eighteen years old when he first picked up the armalite, took aim and fired. What put the blood on his hands? He killed, that point has long since been established, and he did so through a hazy inclination of where he wanted to go but just wasn’t sure of the correct way there. Freedom…the liberty of man, bestowed in the kindred hearts of Catholics and Protestants from Mulan Head to the Macgillycuddy Reeks. Without thought for consequence, he fired a volley of metal shards into the air, as a veil of crimson red descended before his vision. Merciless and cruel, he fired again, watching his comrades die at his feet, he wouldn’t think of the consequences. All those British soldiers that stood before him, he would not give a passing thought to, not when they were alive and not when they were dead. What put the blood on is hands? He never did stop to think, that these men…whether they were British soldiers, Loyalist paramilitary members or even just ordinary protestants who had decided their interests laid with the empire; he never thought that he was killing his own. Never thought of the consequences. You want to talk of division? A religion divided a nation…through the actions of both sides of which O’Shea was an eager participant. Kicking and screaming, men were dragged into resent of each other, hate bred hate. What of those consequences? What put the blood on your hands Paddy O’Shea? ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ “Aye’m…sorry…” Paddy whispered, the cold January air nipping out. Standing on the footway that borders the goliath bridge of Burntollet, the planes of his arms resting lightly on the guard rail that encloses the traffic, Paddy breaths a sigh, as he gazes out over the water which whips pleasantly at the sides of the stone levees. It was evening now and the sun was approaching the final descent of its arch, yet the light was still considerably bright in what was generally an unremarkable day. What was he sorry for? Well, that is the question. What is he not sorry for would formulated a less long winded answer. Was he sorry for his past, his present…even his future? Right now, so confused with the pain he was feeling, he decided he would group them all with a single word, in the hope that the memories would leave him in peace. He was sorry to the town he had left behind just a week ago. Bundoran, the land of honorable O’Shea men, who had cut from the earth, life. Soaked in sweat and smitten in toil, they had dug deep, through muck and shit, planting their crops. Hacking through the wilderness, they made themselves the kings with their own two hands, dragging their children along with them. They were men of principle and valor, of wisdom and nobility. Never before had his imagination succeeded in grasping the true extent of their accomplishments. Yet, now Paddy stands sorry. Because he’s forgotten the face of his Fathers and forsaken their legacy. He did so when he ran away in terror of the daughter he never knew he had, and from the wife he never had wed. Paddy sighs again. Cowardice. Check. Embarrassment. Check. Disgraceful. Check. He could almost hear the gossip of the people from two hundred miles away, as he they summed up everything he was in a handful of words. Gossipers, let them talk…that wasn’t what bothered him. But how could he have done it again, run away when trouble reared its ugly head. How could he have just stood silent, then when you turn your back, he’s running away again. How could your response to the knowledge you’re a father, inspire flee? I just need time to clear my head he said to himself, I’ll be back in a week. Monkeys in every jungle in the world fell out of their trees with laughter at that one. This wasn’t a brief holiday from his home, this was self-imposed eviction. He wanted to be home so much but, right now, he just didn’t have the balls to face any of it. Fucking coward. With his head bowed, Paddy glances out across the river once more, through his eyelashes. The lash of the sea drummed into his ears and this was a distraction from everything else, so that was good. There was something else he was sorry for, he now realized; he was sorry he had ever contributed to the divide of this fine city. The river, that divided a town now only by geography but by religion and political stance, this river was a gaping scar that had never healed. It stood like a trophy of partition, segregating and boxing. Apples in one box and oranges in the other. So what happens if they ever mix? Republican Catholics to his left, Loyalist Protestants to his right. And he’d contributed, because he’d took arms, he’d waged war on the other. He had made this scar, his doing, maybe not all his work but he was the brick that finished the wall. Why did he do it? How could he have separated and boxed? Goddamnit, this wasn’t what his country of his dreams was supposed to look like… Then something hit him…hit him so hard he swayed backwards dumbfounded that he hadn’t thought of it before. Redemption. I want to reach out…over the bridge…and feel your hand across the water Could he…be forgiven? Walk with you along an unapproved road, not looking over my shoulder I mean, some cuts are deep but they can heal, right? I want to see… Maybe we can be a nation? I want to hear… Please tell me we can. I want to understand your fear Or is too late…? Paddy gazed to his right and was instantly taken aback by the red, white and blue of Union Jacks, flying above houses throughout the residence. As he turned to his left, he could have sworn they were the same houses, only the flags were those of the tri color…maybe it was too late… But we’re North and South of the river…. How did it get so far? From all around, the sound of a Church bell boomed throughout the mountain entrenched valley that- ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ -made him shiver to his bones. Something wasn’t right, that bell said that something wasn’t right. Listen to the bell, it’s always right! Standing on the footway that borders the goliath bridge of Burntollet, the planes of his arms resting lightly on the guard rail that encloses the traffic, Paddy breaths a sigh, as he gazes out over the water which whips pleasantly at the sides of the stone levees. He’s eighteen by the way, same scene and everything. Except it’s night time. 1988, the year of our Lord, and all around, there is silence. Despite the city being vibrant (in their respective corners), on this bridge, this outstretched hand begging the other to embrace him, pulling them together, the bullets forcing them away, all around there is silence. There is something unearthly majestic about the ground on which his feet stand. The stench of burned rubber and rotting oil, it only adds to the sense. It shouldn’t, these are man made odors…but for the people of this town, this is something that can’t be found on either side. These are the only smells that they share. And so Paddy stands in the cold night air, allowing the clouds of condensation to whisper back into the air. Never before has he held a rifle at this stage. Right now, he never thought he would. This bridge is something he spends time on, but won’t across. Sometimes he hopes the love between the side wasn’t lost, that it was merely mislaid. That someone will emerge over the brow of the hill, and meet him halfway. Maybe. One things for sure, he won’t be like his Father or his Grandfather or his Uncles or neighbors. He can’t allow the badness to it’s anymore of it’s own way. By God, it’s got so many already. Maybe he was naïve and foolish, maybe it was something to do with the fact he had only ever crossed this bridge a handful of times (as reciprocated in the complete lack of vehicles), but he believed that everyone on both sides felt like he did now. That if someone only reached out, like this bridge, they could meet and maybe there would be no flags, no songs. Afterall, there’s some ground not worth taking…some connections just not worth breaking…right? Yeah, maybe he was naïve. The sound of a cough wandered towards Paddy’s ear, the noise coming from his right…Paddy looked over and saw the outline of a single silhouetted figure, an intruder to his privacy or did someone want to meet him halfway? Paddy seen the uniform first as he slipped out of the shadow. It was a policeman and Paddy suddenly realized he was sweating. The hard blue uniform, the overshadowing hat and the hard scrape of boot against pavement all became noticed to Paddy in that order. Policemen who never would be dear to either side. In that time they only stood for corruption and bigotry but in Paddy’s optimistic head, he wanted to extend his hand. Even to them. As he drew closer Paddy noticed the stern face, emotionless and raw. His eyes narrowed almost into slits, he continued to stare at Paddy as he walked past him. He was analyzing him see, noting his skin color and any clothes that might provide clues. He wanted to sort and box him. Wanted to know if he was a damn Catholic before panning him over the head with his baton. Paddy looked away hoping he’d pass quickly, forgetting already his recent objectives of unification. He supposed some people just weren’t interested no matter what you were offering. And he did pass, walking around the rear of Paddy as he continued to gaze out over the scar. Then he paused. “What’s your name?” the man rasped into Paddy’s ear, a spray of spittle tickling his skin. The thick Belfast accent was clear, especially because his lips were practically talking right to his brain, he was that close. John..er…Henry…er….www.randomfakename.com “Ian.” Not bad Paddy. “Ian what?” Er…insertenglishnamehere “McGonagle” Shit. Paddy thought he could almost hear the ticking of the man’s brain as he formulated that Ian and McGonagle are immiscible, oil and water blah blah blah. Bloody taig, bloody fenian Irish bastard. Those cogs sure are loud, hm? “Ian McGonagle is it.” It wasn’t a question, it was a statement laced with loathing. Paddy tried to say yes, failed, so simply nodded. The policeman sneered, his two thick black bushy eyebrows drawing into one. He paused for a second, made to walk away then he grabbed Paddy by the collar and slammed his face down onto the guard rail. “Over here!” he screamed to the right. The Protestant right. All this before he’d even hit the ground. In a slight daze, he heard the sound of running footsteps drawing closer. He took a quick glance to his right, his head swaying stupidly, then got put back down to the ground as a boot connected with his forehead. He was kicked again so that he landed on his back. Then the boots started raining down, on his ribs and face. They like the face, the bastards. Paddy was kicked again and he landed on his stomach. He tried to raise his head but couldn’t, his chin only resting on the concrete instead. He did catch one thing though before he was kicked into unconsciousness and that was the sight of the policeman, walking away whistling. Reaching out? I’ll fucking wrap by hands around you neck! Without considering that this could easily have happened on his side with a young Protestant boy playing victim, Paddy decided to make a new series of objectives: The O’Shea Manifesto1. Get a gun2. Kill police3. Kill Protestants.4. Kill strangers.5. Kill anyone who looks at you funny.6. Kill anyone who might eventually look at you funny.7. Kill anybody and everybody.8. Kill9. Kill10. Kill ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ There was a badness,That had its way.Love wasn’t lost,It just got mislaid We’re North and South of the river. How did it ever get so far…all the killing, and by God you’re damn right he killed. I mean, this man is an icon for people all over the world. H e had fans who see him as a role model. Would they still try to sculpt their lives into that of his if they knew he was a killer. He had contributed so much hate into this city, it had polluted the streets into discrepancy. And all those that died, he was a part of it all. Of course, he didn’t deserve the beating…but do you exchange a kick with a bullet? There was no justification. As the sun begins to set over the river in the distance, Paddy wonders. As the light split’s the water into a kaleidoscope of glorious orange, the sun climaxing in intensity, he considers. He has two choices: walk North or South…or bring the two together and end all directions with there only being the commune for all. “Aye’m sorry…“ Paddy whispers. The sound of a cough drifted towards Paddy’s ear. Paddy glanced to his right and sees a figure drawing near. The person wasn’t clear at all, what with he/she playing the foreground to the departing sun, it’s departing rays still enough to blind. The figure seemed to stumble rather than walk he decided as he squinted. Then the figure moaned and dropped to concrete ground with a thud. Jesus Christ… Paddy darted over to the person quickly and was nearly knocked for six. No…no way. This can’t be…it’s not possible… Paddy fell to one knee to make sure it was who he thought it was…it was…it was him…the man that had made him a killer. That goddamn police officer…from 18 years ago? Easily in his 50’s, white and frail…the once angry bushy black eyebrows, just wisps of white cloud. “H-h-hel…” There was blood here and lot’s of it…perhaps the gaping hole in abdomen has something to do with it Paddy, you idiot, you could fit your fist in there. The police officer’s eyes were desperate. He had been shot… (From his side) And had stumbled his way into retreat…into the only ground that they all shared. And he needed help right now. The sound of the Church bell echoed throughout the valley. What? Why the hell should I? He made me a killer, he nearly had me kicked into a carrot. Why should aye help him now, eh? Paddy didn’t have to ask that question, he already knew the answer. Redemption. Paddy looked into those desperate, pleading eyes. They were begging him for redemption, just like he had begged for early. The answer was right on his bloodied face. “H-help m-m-me…puh-puh-puhlease.” Yet still Paddy hesitated. 18 years it had took Paddy to find this man, he had looked everywhere, the man had a bullet assigned to him for God’s sake. Yet it took eighteen years for Paddy to find the man he had first met when he was eighteeen years old. All he had to do was wait for him. He deserves t’ die The cold thought was a resurface of the old Paddy…but it didn’t seem harsh. Paddy looked at the man’s waist and saw a pistol and a walkie talkie in the other. The Church bell rung out once again. Are you going to talk or shoot? The gun or the tongue? Paddy slammed a fist down onto the faces cheek so hard he was sure he had cracked the bone. God, tha’ felt good. Then, without another hesitation, he pulled the walkie talkie from his belt and radioed in. “Man down on the Burntollet Bridge. Get here asap….” Then, without another word, Paddy dropped the walkie talkie, got up and walked away, pulling himself from the scratching fingers at his ankles. Redemption…it seemed so sweet as Paddy walked away. And he smiled. This wasn’t walking away…this was walking back. It’s time to see that daughter of his. And through the air, the Church bell chimed out in joy. The Shepard had got his lamb back. END
