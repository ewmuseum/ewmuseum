Victory

25 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-25-09 Fusenshoff wakes up in a cold sweat. His hands are shaking. The room smells like every other hotel room he’s ever slept in. The linens smell almost fresh. They masquerade as new, but induce a lingering suggestion of endless washing that can’t completely hide the sweat from hundreds of strangers who’ve slept in them over the last few months. Bland, totally inoffensive framed photographs and paintings hang on the white walls. They were chosen just for this reason. No one could possibly be insulted by them and yet no one can relate to them either. They’re landscapes and canvases with no meaning, completely uninspiring. People steal fleeting glimpses of them as they head to the mini fridge or drop off their luggage. They’re ordinary and practically invisible. Yet Fusenshoff sees himself in them. He’s been seeing life differently over the last week. His life has fallen into a slumber, like he’s just woken from a long hibernation. When he walks down the sidewalk he sees women in business attire screaming at cell phones like they’re wind instruments. Couples are walking in parks, chatting casually with one eye always on their children who chase pigeons. The wrestler walks past, as intriguing as a calm wind. He saunters unnoticed, carrying nothing but his lonesome idleness. It was always easy to ignore these emotions before. He was an empty coffin with liquor-soaked linings. It’s a comfort he’s grown accustomed to, yet now dreads. The physical toll sobriety breeds during this withdrawal period has been impossible to ignore. Now in the bathroom, Fuse reaches for his toothbrush. An unsteady hand forces him to drop it into the sink. Frustrated, he looks at himself in the mirror and sees a pale ghost staring back at him. Clear as a cloudless day, Fuse realizes what’s haunting him more than anything right now- he’s utterly unhappy. ::One hour later:: He’s found a pleasant distraction. A building amassed with now-crumbling old brick is his solace. It stands in a rickety old town- risen, beaten down and now submitted to droll obscurity. Yet there’s a life just inside the walls of this old gym few care to partake in. Those that do are usually filled with either blind rage or an unfaltering, willful ambition. Fusenshoff steps inside and takes a look around at his surroundings. The walls are littered with posters of old boxers. Colored paper with forthcoming and past events hang prominently in hallways. Worn out heavy and speed bags hang just a few feet away, with plenty of room between them and the ring which takes center stage. It’s mud and blood-stained. The champion logo hangs off the apron. Inside two men wearing sparring helmets and gloves slowly and methodically exchange blows. Their footwork is imperfect, but studied and practiced. An older man who leans over a turnbuckle turns his head slowly to look over the newcomer. His toothless grin and surprised eyes meet Fusenshoff’s as he jumps off the apron and walks toward his visitor. "I’m Tyrese. It’s nice to meet you." He says, greeting Fusenshoff. "Nice place you’ve got here." Fuse responds. "Thanks, but this place is a shithole." The man takes Fuse’s coat and hangs it on the rack a few feet away. "We don’t see many new faces in here, especially white ones. I should warn you, you don’t leave my gym without either working up a sweat or picking up your kid. You’re obviously not a single mother looking for her teenager, so get ready work, my man." "Now you’re talking." "You box?" He questions. "Not for a long time. Even then it wasn’t anything serious. I’m here to clear my head, even if that means it’ll be throbbing in a couple of hours." The wrestler replies. The old man laughs. "Okay, well then I think I know just the ticket. You’re not looking for training, just a release. I think I can help you with that. I haven’t had the chance to put a licking on a greenhorn for some time. I could use the practice." "I was thinking one of the young guns up on stage over there might be a better fit. Medicare’s been going to shit for years now in this country." Fuse grins. "Well now," the man roars heartily, "them’s fightin’ words son. Trust me, I’ll do just fine. If it’s a beating you’re looking for, I promise you’ll get exactly what you wish for… go warm up on the heavy bag for a minute. I’ll be right with you." The men exchange smiles as Fuse drops his duffel bag, pulling out a set of heavy gloves. Twenty minutes later Fuse can smell his own sweat, which still reminds him of Jack Daniels. He’s been going at the bag hard. With each punch his problems are released into the leather. Out of the corner of his eye he sees Tyrese approach. "Well big man, ready for an opponent that fights back?" Fuse is breathing heavy now. "Music to my ears Tyrese." "Do you have a sparring helmet?" "I think I’ll pass." Tyrese laughs louder than ever and throws a few punches at the bag. Fuse whirls his arms around before losing the heavy gloves for his Everlast boxing gloves. Tyrese winks at him, grinning, as the two crawl into the ring. Taking their respective corners, Fuse bites down hard on a brand new mouth guard. He punches his fists together as the two meet in the middle. The two start off sizing each other up before a headstrong Fusenshoff throws a few fists. His punches land hard but Tyrese isn’t phased. His third swing is a hook which leaves him open and Tyrese lands a hard straight to Fuse’s cheekbone. Stunned, Fuse takes a jab and a hook to his midsection, but doesn’t budge. Instead he goes for a right which Tyrese easily blocks. Bouncing around, Tyrese shakes his head as he stares at the wrestler. Fuse finally gets his hands up, protecting his head. They go on like this for half an hour. Tyrese gets his rest while circling around Fuse, who stands pat in the center of the ring. Fusenshoff gets his brains bashed in over and over again, but never backs off. Tyrese starts to worry he’s in the ring with a man who’s a glutton for pain, but he knows those eyes. He’s seen them before in men who just want to forget; men looking for a distraction. Rarely has he seen it in men as tough as Fusenshoff. He has the perfect medicine. Fuse chokes every knuckle sandwich with a look that begs for more. "Alright, I think you got what you were looking for. Besides, you’re making my arms tired." Fuse looks at him, sporting a swollen eye and a fat lip. "Too much for ya old man?" He laughs. "Yeah. You’re a real killer." "I got a few good shots in." Fusenshoff rebukes. "My wife has better technique man. What’d you say your name was?" "Fuse." "Seriously? Well, you sure lit a fuse. A fuse to a dead firecracker." "I feel ten times better, so its all the same." Fuse remarks. "Well man, I’m beat. Kicking your ass is a real workout." He says. "I’ve been hearing that a lot lately." "Say man, I’m gonna shower up then grab some lunch. You feel like joining me for a drink?" There it is. The moment he’s dreaded for weeks now. Immediately images flash in his head of smooth liquor in a rocks glass. The smell hits him like a ton of bricks, overpowering the stench of sweat that drowns the room. Bartender after bartender flashes through his mind- just faces without names. He pauses for a while as Tyrese looks at him questioningly. Fusenshoff licks his lips. "Well? What do you say?" Tyrese wonders. Another pause. "…Another time Tyrese. Thanks for the offer." "Sure man. Come back any time." Tyrese says, halfway to the showers. Fusenshoff leaves the gym and greets the fresh air. The sun beams down on his face. He’s knows he’s leaving that gym with a victory.
