Captain Justice, Issue #6: Never Look Back

26 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6-26-06 FRIDAY, JUNE 20TH Alkai Beach, Seattle. “I can’t believe you talked me into this crap. I better be getting some MAJOR road head for this.” A full moon bathed the inhabitants of the Akram property in its glow, adding some extra light to an already dazzling horizon. The scene was every bit as hectic as it looked, with numbers dipping into the hundreds flocking in the beachside property. Friends, family, and friends-of-friends mingled with one another over the momentous occasion of holy matrimony. Mere days prior, local Philanthropist, Ethan Akram, announced his engagement to three-year girlfriend and close friend to both the Knox and Gwen families, Sheila Rogers. Thus, much partying and copious amounts of booze from an open bar were to be had by all. One such individual was only beginning his second Mai Tai, tilting his head to watch a flock of beautiful ladies in matching white dresses traipse by his table. Soren Knox nodded to himself in approval. Hell, just cause you were on a diet didn’t mean you couldn’t look at the menu. “You nag too much, you know that?” was Rachel Gwen’s return fire to her fiancé’s tetchy complaints. Stirring the lone olive in her martini, her eyes cast over to Soren, watching him shift uncomfortably in his chair, donning his blue dress shirt and black slacks. Inspecting her own full-body sanguine-tinted dress, the buxom blonde beauty leaned back in her chair, taking in a whiff of the salty sea air… and nearly gagging when a hint of seaweed punched her right in the olfactory senses. From his seat, Soren chortled. “Seaweed, huh?” “Yeah.” Soren tugged at the collar of his shirt and undid one of the top buttons that had been strangling him for the better part of three hours. After his windpipe gained freedom from his tight-collared prison, he indulged himself in a breath of slightly cleaner beach air. “You know, I don’t see why we didn’t go balls-to-the-wall with OUR party after we got engaged. As much as I love your parents, morning buffet at Muckleshoot Casino with a parade of wrinkly people is NOT a venue fit for our greatness.” “Well, SAW-REE, Mr. Big Wrestling Star!” She retorted. “Not like we had the money before-hand. Ethan’s hand a hand in money ever since he was born. Parents were rich, he dipped into the right stocks here and there. Full ride to Stanford, became a hotshot attorney, now he’s turning profits just by sitting on the can.” “You know…” his voice trails off after a sip of his green-colored concoction. “What?” “Nah, nevermind,” Soren replied, though the anguished expression plastering his face belied his words. He allowed the moment to simmer down, but Rachel wouldn’t give up on her line of questioning that easily. “What?” Rachel repeated. “Could this BE more awkward? Me and Ethan don’t get along. AT ALL. In fact I nearly put his head through the wall for flaunting his money in my face just because I’m not some rich boy begging for handouts. Hell, the last time you saw Ethan was back in business school when you were sleeping wi…” “Hey!” Her finger sprung into action, indicating Soren needed to recant his question, lest he feel the wrath of a size-six heel up his sphincter. After peering around their table to make sure no other ears could pick them up, she leaned closer to her fiancé. “I was dating him, thank you. The sleeping part? Only a few times. And you think this is easy for me? I’m here, mainly because of Sheila. We’ve all been friends since we were nine and if one of her Bridesmaids doesn’t show up to this party, she’ll never speak to me again. By proxy, I’ll never speak to YOU again!” “Okay, okay, okay, I gotcha. I won’t prod,” he twanged, giving her an eyebrow-only notice before whispering a jab to himself. “Damn cliquey women…” “Besides,” Rachel’s infectious smile curled upwards, deflecting Soren’s interest away from his Mai Tai. “You know how I feel about you. I thanked you enough for this in the dressing room at the clothing store the other day.” The couple shared a laugh amongst themselves that died off quickly as a waitress hurriedly cut through the bustling crowd to snatch their empty glasses. Soren’s eyeballs continued to carouse the gaudy spectacle of lights, laughter, and people dancing to the DJ’s fourth or fifth rendition of “Four Minutes” by Justin Timberlake and Madonna. Oh, how Soren Knox loathed Justin Timberlake. “So where are Party Boy and Party-Girl-To-Be, anyway?” he asked. “Sheila was off gallivanting with her twin sisters upstairs, no doubt swimming in a sea of colors she’s picking out for the wedding. Ethan…” “..IS RIGHT BEHIND YOU!” At the sound of the familiar voice, Soren’s blood bubbled, almost a reaction reminiscent of a Kill Bill moment. He glanced up and was almost blinded by the freakish whites of Ethan Akram’s dental work. The he beamed in a way so over-the-top, he’d gone full-circle to boring as piss, then back to top again. Seriously, at any moment, Soren Knox was expecting an entire musical troupe to pop from Ethan’s ass to sing whimsical show tunes, but the thought was dashed as Rachel stood up and politely hugged the gracious host. “Ethan, hi!” Rachel greeted warmly. “God, it’s great to see you!” He embraced her – a little too long for Soren’s liking – then shook hands with Knox as he rose from his seat. “And you’ve gotta be Soren. The wrestler guy, right?” “Yeah, the wrestler guy,” Soren said, slightly melancholy. “I don’t mean to pry,” Rachel interjected. “But this whole shindig… all you?” Ethan merely raised his hands to let the world know “it was ALL me!” with a smile that could be seen from space. “That’s great! I’m glad you’ve done well for yourself.” Soren gave Ethan a once-over in his black business suit and tried to picture the same guy from years before, but it was almost impossible. Standing proudly at an impressive 6’2” and more than filling out his suit with muscles, his appearance was more in the vein of a linebacker as opposed to a corporate attorney. Gone was his Jesus beard, now replaced with an incredibly clean-cut hair-do and frosted boy band spikes adorninghis head. He appeared to have taken a complete non-stop bath in Man Tan or a similar substance as he was looking considerably more bronze these days. Pfft. Probably just plastic surgery, the cunt. “Hey…” Soren offered. “Thanks for inviting us, dude. And congrats on your success. That’s always nice to see work pay off.” “Not a problem!” Ethan was practically oozing bullshit from his pores. “Hey, I’ve heard you’ve been doing great in that wrestling league thing! Guys like us need to use our talents for the greater good.” And here, Soren felt, was where the other shoe was about to drop. “God gave you size and strength for physical labor; he gave me the REAL power of the mind. It’s… it’s a great feeling, you know?” Sensing there was danger afoot, Rachel deliberately threw herself in front of both her past and present beaus before Soren’s trick right hand decided to act up. “You kids enjoy yourself! In just a bit, we’re bringing out the movies from our trip to Cancun, so you better get ready!” “We will be,” Rachel punctuated her statement with a sharp elbow to Soren’s ribs. “Won’t we, honey?” Soren flatly complied. “Oh, yeah… sweetie, with bells.” “Catch you later!” And with that, he vanished into the rowdy crowd, leaving happy couple to their lonesome. Soren let out a deep sigh of relief and brought his elbows to the table with a dull thud. “You actually DEFEND his crap?” Soren spit. Rachel raised a silencing hand up, an exasperated look crossing her face. “I’m sorry. He’s a lot to take in, but I know how you get around him and for the love of all that is Holy, we don’t need it here. I know, I’ve heard it before. You’re a proud man, you’re macho, you don’t beg for anything. I get it, but If you’re not doing this for Sheila, then do this for me.” Realizing there was no fighting this, Soren sunk into his chair, defeated. “Fine.” “Thank you, Soren.” Her gentle hand clasped his own massive palm. “Besides, it is what it is. Sometimes, we’ve all gotta do things we don’t want to do for the greater good, right?” Before he could answer the question, his eyes couldn’t help but fall upon the engagement ring he planted firmly upon her finger not more than a year ago. Drumming the fingers of his free hand on the table, he let out a hesitant sigh. ~ ~ ~ June 30th, 2008 Sheridan Suites, Seattle, WA 9… 10… 11… How high did this stupid elevator go? Trying to do whatever he could to take his mind off the impending task, Soren ran a sweat-soaked hand through his grungy brown hair, muttering quietly the entire way up. He’d worked out this entire spiel for the last three weeks since his second check to the Silver Lining Gym bounced like a rubber ball on pavement. Still practicing to himself, he tried to get the correct verbiage down, but like any nervous rookie performing for the first time, he was choking right before the opening act. Already, he declared noiselessly to himself that he was going to bomb this and get kicked out of the school before word one. The final ring of the elevator’s bell played in tune with the huge swallow of air Soren took before he exited the car. On floor twenty of the suites, the blue-collar Seattle native had to stop and adore the splendor of a world he’d not seen since his childhood with movie star father, Earl Knox. The checkered tiles were immaculate with a capital I. Nary a speck or hint of dust polluted the sparking walls, decorated from top-to-bottom with various artwork Soren himself couldn’t craft to save his own life. As he continued to follow the chartreuse drapery lining the walls, he couldn’t believe that he – some scrub working at a warehouse 9-5 – would ever get the chance to rub elbows with this kind of scenery in his lifetime; and it was jut a fucking hall. However, a hall created with money he was never going to have. Soren finally made his way to the door that was housing the man he’d come to see, he tried to capture every last faultless detail before he got kicked out on his ass with the dregs of society again. Never had a wooden door terrified him so, but he mustered up enough courage to knock… “Soren?” Jumping out of his skin, Soren was shocked by the visage of a man he couldn’t picture closing himself off in such a ritzy place. Before him stood Sonny Silver, clad in a pair of black dress pants with same-colored shoes, a blood-red shirt and a thick plastered cast on his right hand. His beard and hair were a bit more unkempt than last he saw, making him look like a domesticated Davey Crockett. Soren raised an eyebrow when he noticed it wrapped around his left hand. “Sonny, you okay?” Soren was drawn to it like a car crash; he couldn’t look elsewhere. “You look like hell.” “Oh, this?” Silver jested with a rather dreary tone. “Old work injury, let’s just say. Something up?” “Uh…” He tried to think of anything poignant as to why he was here, but he was drawing blanks. Time for a full-scale retreat. “I think I came here at a bad time, Sonny. I’ll get going.” As Soren turned on his heel, a hand devoid of a cast wrapped around his bicep. “Wait. You… you came for something important. It’s written on your face.” Soren, not sure how exactly to respond, uttered a weak chuckle. “I told Rachel to stop drawing on my while I was asleep.” Sonny jerked his thumb in the direction of his suite. “You want to come in for a few?” A heavy, regretful sigh. “Sure.” ~ ~ ~ “Shit’s been BAD, Sonny. REAL bad,” Soren told his mentor as he eased himself into the black leather couch, taking a glass of Scotch from Sonny’s hands as he plopped down in the recliner across from him. “Money’s tight. REAL tight. Hours are rarer to find than I’d like them to be, it’s taking everything short of selling my blood to some black market quack. I can’t afford my rent, I can’t afford to even be a part of this gym. My checks for monthly school enrollment have bounced.” “And.. you want me to show you some leniency despite the fact said checks have bounced?” Sonny’s cross face didn’t contradict his feelings a bit. “Well…” he acquiesced. “Yeah.” Sonny sat forward, leaning against the table before him. “Look… as much as I want to help you, do you really think I’m the right guy to go to? I like you, Soren. I like all my students, but I don’t train for free. Do you know what kind of shit I’d get into if I keep letting you wrestle for free?” “No, no, no, you’ve got it all wrong… I’m sorry, this is stupid. I… I shouldn’t have done this… I just need SOMETHING.” His breath slowed and his eyes shut tightly. “I… I don’t have a dad anymore. My family and are a bunch of pricks who haven’t helped me do anything since I was sixteen. I’ve got a relationship with a girl that I think is quite frankly, falling apart unless I make something of myself some time soon. I... look, I’m sorry to show up on your doorstep and give you the Hallmark shit, but I just need one more month, Sonny. I’ve wanted to be a wrestler as long as I could remember. It was one of the few good things I remember when I was with my Dad and not doing this… I can’t sit here and regret things for the rest of my life.” Sonny remained quiet while Soren’s emotions continued to pour. “I’m asking you, man-to-man, doing everything short of getting on my knees, to let me have another month to get you some cash and you won’t regret it one iota.” “Soren, I… I don’t know.” Throwing himself further back into the couch, Soren hung his head in shame and kept a vigil on the floor, not wanting to be greeted with what had to be a disappointed look from the man that’d helped him start the career he sought for the last year and a half. As he did so, Sonny stood up and approached the window until his face was mere inches from the glass pane. Scotch glass in his free hand, he crunched an ice cube between his teeth as he remained focused on the Seattle skyline. “Sonny?” He tried to get his attention, but it was no use. “If you want me to go…” The PRIME superstar calmly reached into the drawer of the nearby coffee table and from it, a black book trapped in his hand. Soren watched his trainer scribble something down on the paper and forcefully tear it from its book. “Sonny, wha…?” His jaw nearly dropped nineteen stories to the lobby when Silver slapped the paper against his chest. “It’s not a lot, but it’s the best I can do for you right now, kid. This should cover your rent.” “What the…?” he queried. “Sonny… this is $5,000 dollars!” “It’s business, Soren,” he explained. “You possess a trait that I don’t see in nearly 99% of wanna-bes and posers I have knocking down my door: integrity. A lot of fucks just sit her and add filth to my halls just for being here. You? I don’t sense bullshit.” He slaps Soren’s chest. “You’ve got heart, kid. Don’t let anybody tell you different or try to corrupt it.” “Sonny, I can’t take it… I just needed…” “Hey,” he grinned with a wink. “If you don’t take it, you’re not allowed at my school anymore.” Still taken aback by his mentor’s generosity, Soren stood up with his focus not leaving the check. With this money, he’d have enough to cover rent for at LEAST some time. Quite a few ‘some times’ at that. “Do something nice for your girl while you’re at it,” Sonny added. Shaking Silver’s hand frenetically, the smile couldn’t be wiped from Soren’s face as he laughed happily. He was sure the people down below could hear him trampling around like a child, but he honestly didn’t give a fuck. “Don’t worr!. First things first, I keep my shithole apartment. And Sonny…” with a sincere tone, he stood proudly. “If you ever need anything EVER, Sonny. Name it.” “Hey, worry about yourself first, kid.” Snatching his leather coat off the couch it rested on during his stay, a feeling of euphoria washed over him as he prepared to make his exit. Now standing over his charge, Silver placed an uneasy hand on Knox’s shoulder as he let the last drops of Scotch burn his throat. “Soren…” Sonny prevented Knox from leaving just yet with an arm around his own. “Yeah?” “Tell me, Soren… what do you know about the place I wrestle for… PRIME?” ~ ~ ~ “Soren? Soren, snap out of it, hon!” Awakening from his haze, he turned to his fiancé with eyes that didn’t stare at her, but through her. “Beg pardon?” “I just said we all have to do things we don’t want to do,” Rachel scoffed at having to tug Soren’s head out of the clouds. “Come on, you’ve been there, haven’t you?” Gripping her hand tightly, he rubbed a thumb over her engagement ring and presented a warm smile. “No. Just tonight.”
