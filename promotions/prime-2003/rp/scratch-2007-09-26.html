Date: 09/26/07 SOUND AND THE FURY Not your father’s crazy gimmick team Ian I don’t remember losing the fire before this. Well, there’s a history lesson in here somewhere, but I’m not up to going through an entire backlog of ancient history, even if this is only an internal monologue. Suffice it to say that the first time Gil and I went through this adventure of randomness, it took us over a year to start to get tired of it. And as soon as that happened, we dropped the ball and ran home. Well, it’s happening again. Maybe it’s because we aren’t winning any matches. I’m sure that connection is more than possible. But what’s the point, you know? We beat up some team who just debuted, and maybe get a nod over the CHAAAAAMPIOOOOOONNN OF THE WOOOOOOOORLD and some condescending douchebag with no charisma, and then we get to face some comically polarized wrestlers for some title belts that really don’t mean much in the scope of things. It’s possible, and I’d be lying if I said that I wouldn’t mind having some gold in our camp once again. Because the gold is justification. You might not believe it, but not many people really approve of dressing up as lawyers and Spartans and other things every week. These people believe in some stupid value called consistency, or reliability. These douchebags also wet their beds every night, but I guess that isn’t the point. Point is, we’re always blown off as some stupid goofy team who never delivers when it counts. We used to prove those people wrong all the time, but now we’re doing nothing but putting on the clown shoes and showing them how right they really are. And that does much towards losing the fire. Sure it would be great to hold the belt and show everyone that even though we’re a joke team, we’re a team that you have to take seriously lest we fuck you eight ways from Tuesday. But there’s not much to get me excited if failure looms at every corner. I don’t know. The question here is: once you’ve climbed the mountain, and then drop off of it for three years, is it really worth all the effort to get back up there and see that your flag has started to wear thin? Well, I don’t know yet. If I did I’d be doing more than having some stupid conversation with myself, I’ll tell you that much. Gil This isn’t fun. We used to really awesome. I know you wouldn’t get it from what we’ve been showing you recently. I mean, we can’t even beat gay guys here. Sheesh. Back in the NWC, we beat so many gay guys that it was a hate crime. Police would question us every night after our matches. Seriously. Look it up if you don’t believe me. Okay, really, I’m making it up. But seriously, we used to be good. And for me, I’m fine with dressing up as whatever. I’m fine with losing often, because the glory of this game has always struck me as rather silly. I’m even fine with being set on fire if it’ll get the people to laugh. But I’m really worried about Ian. He never says anything to me about it, but I know all of this frustration is making him really upset. He told me a few years ago how content he was to being the best that NWC ever had, how holding the record was good enough for him to never want to put the tights back on and give it another go. But things changed, obviously. He must have seen his brother’s tournament comeback and think that maybe it was his time. I don’t know. I tried talking him out of it. Because honestly, as much as I like wrestling and making people laugh, I don’t need this. I can do anything for a living. I only do it for him. Thing is, he loves this thing as much as his brother did. Maybe more. Yeah. He loves this game probably more than anyone I’ve seen. And it kills him that we haven’t been doing better. You know how many times we lost in the NWC? Twice. In fourteen months. And we’ve already lost four times here. Maybe five. Math was never my strong thing. Anyway, all of this losing is really starting to hurt him. Maybe it’s because the competition is harder here, or because we haven’t really gotten the ring rust off yet. Or maybe we’re not as good as we used to be. Or worse, never as good as we thought we were. It’s like being the king of the playground in 8th grade and then coming back three years later to find out that the people after you have changed the playground so now there’s a puppet theater where the monkey bars used to be and all of the new kids don’t respect you at all, and they can beat you in wrestling matches.
