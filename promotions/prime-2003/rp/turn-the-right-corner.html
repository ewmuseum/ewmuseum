Turn The Right Corner

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Too late I breathe deeply as the last crescent of the sun finally dips behind the skyline. My day is about to begin. With a flick of the wrist, I toss the lighter into the air. It catches the end of my cigarette just barely before falling, closed, into my outstretched palm. A neat trick. One of those stupid things you practice over and over when sleep no longer takes up space in your day planner. The end of the cig hesitates for a moment, flirting with the kind of hesitation that ever facet of my life wallows in. It ultimately decides to appease me, just this once. Closing my eyes to make sure the smoke has no orifice from which to escape, I take my first REAL breath of the day. Smoking will only kill you if you live too long. Anyone with a pack of reds and a set of balls will be fitted for a pine box long before some dipshit in a lab coat hassles them about tar buildup. You can tell it from the way a real smoker takes that first drag, sucking in long and deep. Like if they tried hard enough, they could suck that flame back and light themselves on fire. A cigarette is a temporary stay; only spontaneous combustion can bring out the true relief. Keeping the butt perched inside my upper lip, I start moving. It won’t be long before the bars open up and give me the usual Tuesday respite. Or maybe today’s Monday. I curse my knee joints for their disloyalty. After all we’ve gone through together, I still have to coerce them to keep plugging along into the new day. It’s nothing that a few numbing beers won’t cure, but I can’t help but think that- “Hey Rourke!” Goddamn motherfucking shit. I stop in my tracks and turn away. Maybe the shadows on my face will mask my identity. Maybe, for once in the history of mankind, this ruse will work and he’ll think I was an illusion. “Rourke! Mickey Rourke!” I’ve never been one to set a precedent. “Hey dude! It’s me! It’s Ferguson, man! Danny Ferguson!” The groan escapes unhindered. It sounds like a cross between a territorial lion growl and the air being released from a balloon. This will be a chore. I often welcome the unexpected twists that life provides. Anything to break up the haze of monotony, a haze created from blurry, inebriated vision and a thick cloud of tobacco smoke. This, though, I hadn’t signed up for this. I turn towards the pipsqueak as he approaches from behind. Before I even look to see if it IS that sniveling shit, Danny Ferguson, I demand to know what he wants. “Aw, c’mon, bro, can’t I drop in and see how one of my boys is doing? We gotta stick together, man. Irish actors.” If I didn’t regret being a guest at that fucking convention five years ago before this instant, I finally had my reason. One appearance, one autograph session, one quick payday…none of it was worth this. He looks me up and down. I shake off the dress-down and demand to know how he found me. “You’re the only guy on the block with thought balloons floating over his head, Mick. Just kidding. I talked to Emilio, and he put me in touch with Charlie and Chuck said you hang out here these days.” He pokes at the prosthetic chin that has long since become a normal part of my anatomy. I pull away and consider what the two goons behind him would do if I snapped his index finger like a crayon. “So, you feel like getting out of character any time this year, man? I know that Sin City II got greenlighted and all, but I didn’t peg you as the ‘method acting’ kind.” There are worse things in the world to be typecast as than the kind of motherfucker who’d step on a kitten’s neck if it suited his personal goals. I consider expressing this theory to him, but the less personal interaction I grant to the punk, the better. I start walking. 50 yards to freedom, or the kind of interstitial freedom you get between raising the bottle and putting it down, when you forget anyone else is in the room. Unfortunately, subtle hints fall like raindrops on this guy. He follows. “Mick, I need some help.” Of course he does. Having another human being seek out my company without some sort of favor expected in return would be crazy. “I’ve got a match this week. A bar fight. An IRISH bar fight.” He pauses. This is where I’m supposed to offer some question that gives him the OK to explain everything. I don’t afford him that luxury. It takes him at least two minutes to finally speak up. “I need to prepare. And I don’t mean taking another Nebraska Trip. I need to learn to drink.” The NTs were popular recreational activities among nutless manchildren like Ferguson and his boy-actor cronies. I’d taken a few back before 8 ½ Weeks. Back when it was the tough guys who beat the shit out of grain farmers, not the guys who wanted to BE tough. Makes you wonder what this fuckin’ world is coming to. “I’d like to think I can hold my alcohol, Rourke, but this is different. I don’t have the tolerance these other guys do. I can’t hang in there. I need someone to teach me how to hold my liquor.” If there’s a reason why I won this sweepstakes, I don’t want to hear it. We’re almost at the bar. I grunt and cough something awful up, spitting it right in front of the shithead’s feet. “All I need is for you to teach me the tricks. To make me the kind of Irishman I know I can be.” My hand wraps around the thick handle to the watering hole’s door. He won’t follow me in because he knows the kind of place it is. Unless you walk inside with someone to vouch for you, you shouldn’t plan to walk inside at all. This would be my escape, as if I needed an escape from someone like him. “I’m buying.” And suddenly he’s my best friend. I toss the remainder of the cigarette to the ground and leave it for someone else to rub out. I swing the door open and gesture for the boy king and his entourage to enter. If he’s writing the check, then I’ll have enough free juice at my disposal to forget this night even happened. In my dictionary, that’s success.
