A Boy Named Sue (A Wade Elliott and Lindsay Troy joint. Revolution 133, Tag Team Match vs. Devin Shakur and Nitz Donnelly)

July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July, 2007 Her bare feet hit the carpet with a soft thud, the mattress creaking like an ancient door on rusty hinges. The bedside clock read one hour past her flight's departure time and Lindsay Troy groaned upon the realization that she slept, insomnia be damned, through not only her wake-up call but also the alarm on the clock and the alarm on her cell phone. This feat was in and of itself a rarity, and the beating at the hands of Sonny Silver surely played a part in addition to her match with Elliott and Gamble. With a sigh of self-determinacy, she eased herself the rest of the way off the bed and stretched as much as her very sore ribs and arms would allow her. The grogginess in her head would wear off much quicker than the pain would, but a hot shower would alleviate some of that. She let her weary feet lead the way... ------------- His body made a funny jerk, an awkward snort escaping his mouth. He blinked, rolling from his stomach onto his back, staring at the ceiling as he left unconsciousness. Instinctively, he reached for a nearby dresser, expecting his hand to fall onto a set of keys. They did not this morning. He closed his eyes and let out a low growl, slamming the bed with his forearms before throwing back the sheets. The theft of his truck at the hands of Nitz Donnelly was not only a slap in the face, but now an inconvenience. With no plane ticket to speak of, or desire for, he would have to improvise. But not before a "hearty" complimentary breakfast sat in his stomach and black coffee coursed through his veins. He threw on an old t-shirt and a pair of dirty jeans. The scent of scrambled eggs and sausage patties entered his nostrils. He let a pair of heavy boots lead the way. ------------- "Darlin', if ya don't mind me sayin', ya look like sheeit." Troy's hand was inches away from securing a banana nut muffin when the lazy Southern drawl nestled inside her ear, making her momentarily turn away from the breakfast treasure sitting in a wicker basket. At a nearby table sat Wade Elliott, coffee mug in hand and a forkful of half-runny egg waiting to be picked up and shoveled into his maw. "Drifter, I'm amazed you can see anything at all after staring up at the lights last night. You sure you're not blind?" Wade took a big swig of black coffee, releasing a guttural sigh of approval through his teeth. "I'm seein' stars, but the eyeballs are workin' just fine," he replied. "Just figured I'd make sure," she swiped the muffin before anyone snuck up behind her and grabbed it first. "Wouldn't want Lisa to pitch another fit, this time about me ruining your career after a flash pinfall robbed you of your eyesight." "Oh, if that were the case I'd probably just wrestle blind." Wade took a big bite of egg and toast, chewing twice before swallowing. "Long as the paychecks kept comin'." "Mmm," was Troy's non-committal reply. She stopped at the cereal station, took an individual box of Cheerios off the stainless steel spinner and moseyed over to Elliott's table. Not waiting for an invitation to sit, she plunked herself down and eyed Wade's breakfast with a hint of disdain. "I can't believe you're actually eating half-cooked egg." "Ever tried it?" "I may like my steak still partially mooing when it's on my plate, but I don't like my chicken still clucking." "Well, I'm partial to a little life in everythin' I eat off the farm..." Wade picked up a thick piece of bacon, eyeing it hungrily. "'Specially the pigs," he finished, stuffing it into his mouth. "Indeed." Wade took another gulp of coffee while mopping up running egg yolk with a piece of white bread. He chomped it down in a few bites, earning another disgusted looked from the Universal Champion. He sucked down the rest of his coffee and pushed his plate away, releasing a heavy, satisfied breath from his mouth. "Whelp," he said, starting to stand. "With my truck stolen I'd best be rentin' a rig for the ride to L.A." "That puss-filled boil couldn't have gotten far before he got pulled over for driving like an asshole." Lindsay tore open the cereal box and started crunching on the tiny oat circles. "You file a police report last night?" "Shit no," answered Wade, rubbing his stomach. "Cops ain't gonna do right by Nitz, and they sure as hell ain't gonna do right by me. I've got my own plans to take care of Nitz, and I don't need to give the police reason to take a gander at my record, if ya git my meanin'." Troy cocked an eyebrow skyward. "So you're just going to let him get away with jacking your car? Like he needs any other reason to think the spotlights burn just for him." "He ain't gettin' away with nothin', darlin'. He's gonna git all manner of ass whuppin', an' if there's one place I know I'll find 'im, it's at Revolution." "Given the thing with Sonny, I guess I shouldn't start preaching about waiting that long to get retribution. But this isn't just a beatdown. Little shit stole your ride." Wade stared his piercing blue eyes into the just as intense hazel orbs of Lindsay Troy. "Yer god-damn right," he said, hands on the table. "And I'm sure as shit gonna run 'im over with that ride." Lindsay looked around the hotel's eating area to see fifteen pairs of eyes on Wade, parents looking horrified and others murmuring amongst themselves. "Suggestion, Wade. It's probably not wise to threaten vehicular assault in front of a roomful of strangers." Wade peered around the room, then pushed himself to full upright position, tossing his drifter's cap onto his head. "They're just strangers." "Who can attest to this outburst as premeditation should you do decide to make Donnelly into New England Roadkill." She looked over her shoulder and shot the crowd a look, whereupon they averted their eyes and went back to their business. Wade popped a Marlboro Red into his mouth, ready to be lit upon exit of the building. "What's gonna happen is gonna happen," he replied, tossing a ten dollar bill onto the table. "But I won't even make it to Revolution without a rig. I'm off to see if I can rent me some manner of truck. Have yerself a good flight, darlin'. I'll see ya in L.A." Wade pushed his chair in and took a step toward the door. "Funny thing about flights..." He didn't get far before her voice called his attention back. "...when you miss one there's no guarantee just when there'll be another available one for you to jump on." Wade stopped. He let the words tumble around in his skull for a minute. "Y'know, we driftin' types cover lotsa ground, but mosta the time we're wishin' there was someone to shoot the shit with..." He turned his head back toward the Queen of the Ring. "Need a ride?" "So long as you don't pull from that flask while behind the wheel, I'm down for a mini-road trip." ------------- "You been wrestlin' ten years?" The two cruised up the highway, Troy comfortable in the passenger's seat of a 2007 Honda CRV, and Wade less comfortable behind the wheel. His left arm dangled out the window, ashing a cigarette butt while steering with the right. "Eleven, actually. Had my first match right before my twentieth birthday. Couldn't tell you how I won the match, but the guy was banged up for a week after it." Troy readjusted the Red Sox visor on her head and crossed one jean-clad leg over the other. "Never imagined I'd still be doing it at this point in my life. You're still a relative rookie, right?" Wade snorted, passing a small Sedan before moving back into the right lane. "Think I got 'bout a month under my belt. First sanctioned match was 'gainst some feller named Huff..somethin'. That Chris Williams tournament. Wrestled a little bit in high school before I dropped out. Did some low-class MMA stuff fer a bit. Don't ask me why I'm in PRIME. Talent agent thought I had what it takes, n' here I am." "Hey, sometimes all it takes is one person to notice you. But a month?! Christ..." she trailed off as they passed a road sign for an upcoming rest area. "And you got thrown in there against me and Smiley. Blaine or Lisa must see big things for you in the future." "Guess the fuck so. Kinda weird getin' situated with it. Happened real fast. One day I'm tossin' cardboard boxes into shippin' trucks, the next I'm gettin' big ol' paychecks n' wrestlin' folks I only seen on the TV." Wade took a final drag from his cigarette, flicking it out the window, eliciting a glare from Troy. "Course this Donnelly cunt ain't makin' things any easier." "I've seen a thousand Nitz Donnellys step in-between the ropes, rattling off the same lines out of Heelology 101 that all the others did before them. He's not even trying to be original." The cap of a water bottle was unspun and Lindsay took a long drink. "He won't get anywhere if he doesn't try to rewrite the book and he's too arrogant right now to succeed at it. He's all flash and no substance." "May be the truth, but he does a helluva job to git under the skin." Wade lit up another cigarette. "'Sides...he ran 'is mouth 'bout my ma. By itself that deserves a broken jaw." "Mom's a bit of a sore spot for you, then?" Wade stared straight ahead, stifling a bit of what appeared to be anger with a breath through his nose. "Yeah...real sore." "Mmhmm..." Civilization and desert rolled on by before Troy decided to continue the conversation. "Family's a sore spot for a lot of us." Wade did not reply. He kept driving, needle pinned at seventy-five. "You the only kid too?" A pause. "Yup." "So you're the only child, Ma's a line you don't cross, your clothes look like they haven't seen a washing machine in a month and you live out of your truck. Did I hit all the high points, Drifter? We weren't struggling for conversation a few minutes ago." Wade's teeth were grit behind curled in lips. "Yup. Hit the nails on their heads," he slowly replied. "Alright." Lindsay sighed. "I see you've pulled yourself into your shell so I'm gonna try and sleep a bit more. Contrary to what my sharp wit and bubbling personality might make you think, I'm still in quite a bit of pain from the Fuckhead." She pulled the visor down until the brim reached the top of her Michael Kors sunglasses. "I've got directions on how to get to the hotel. If I manage to actually hit an R.E.M. cycle and I'm not up by the time we're close, you get one free punch to my arm to wake me up." Wade kept his hands at ten and two. "Sure." ------------- She wasn't quite sure how long she'd been out for, but the steady, smooth travel of tires over pavement was successful in Troy catching some winks. Her body had somehow curled itself into a fetal position in the bucket seat of the CRV and she was now facing Wade, who hadn't seemed to move much at all since she last glanced in his direction. Troy's sunglasses had fallen down the bridge of her nose in her sleep, and after taking a deep breath to signal her conscious state once again, she replaced them over her eyes with the tip of her finger. "Guess I caught that R.E.M. cycle after all." "Yeah, ya been out fer a good while," was Wade's reply, taking a big swig of coffee from a Styrofoam cup. She looked outside at the landscape and noticed no great change. "Doesn't look like we've crossed into California yet. Have we?" Wade was silent for a moment, finishing off his coffee before lighting a cigarette. "Nope. Not yet." "Ah. Alright. You want me to drive at all? You've been behind the wheel for awhile." "I'm all set, thanks." It was at that point that the two passed a sign reading 'Las Vegas - 50 miles.' Wade remained stalwart, eyes fixed dead ahead. "Vegas?" Troy questioned, then looked back over her shoulder at the road sign now becoming a part of the passing background. "We're going gambling before hitting LA?" "Not quite." "Oh. Well, I didn't take you for a Wayne Newton fan but if you are, I guess that's OK..." Wade exhaled heavily, adjusting his posture in his seat. "Got some business to take care of first." Lindsay looked at him and thought back to the earlier conversation where Elliott mentioned not wanting to get the police involved with his car being stolen for fear of them looking at his record. She very slowly took her sunglasses off and folded them in her hands. "What kind of business, Wade." It was purposely not asked as a question. "Goin' to see my pa," he replied, choosing his words carefully. "Here's where I'd make a crack about you introducing me to your family before we've even had a first date, but I know a veiled comment when I hear one." Her eyes narrowed out of instinct. "The song and dance about wanting someone to shoot the shit with on the road, the comments about a police record and the off-hand remark about your Ma may not all be clicking on the same cylinder right now, but I know you're not telling me something. Don't bullshit me, Elliott." Wade curled his lips in, tensing his arms as they gripped the wheel. "You know who yer pa is, Lindsay?" he asked "My father died when I was nine," was her short reply. "Yeah? Well, to me, my pa died before I was fuckin' born." "And you're....what, going to Vegas to tell him that and bringing me along for the ride?" Wade growled before slamming on the breaks. The car skidded for a good fifty feet, leaving long streaks of black on the road. They jolted to a stop in the breakdown lane, the screeches echoing through the desert. Wade turned to what appeared to be a somewhat shocked and pretty angry Lindsay Troy. "No! I'm gonna beat the god-damn piss outta him! He'll be real god-damn lucky if I don't put 'im in the fuckin' dirt!" He stopped, breathing heavy. He kept his eyes fixated on the nasty glare from Troy. He turned away after a moment, eyes set on the horizon, hands gripping the wheel of the motionless car. "All I know 'bout my pa is that he knocked up my ma when they was eighteen and went runnin' like a dog with its tail 'twixt its legs, and the pain, n' the bullshit that we had to deal with growin' up is worthy of a good fuckin' trip to the hospital, at the very fuckin' least." More silence. Dust swirled next to them, a tumbleweed here and there. Wade moved back to sitting position, eyes away from Troy. "I weren't bullshittin' ya 'bout havin' someone to talk to," he continued after a moment. "It weren't no fuckin' song n' dance. But I'm thirty-one years old, just found where my piece of shit father has been hidin' all these years, and I ain't 'bout to forget about 'im." Lindsay continued glaring at Wade, then turned in her seat to face the front of the Honda. The digital clock on the dashboard slid from 7:16 to 7:17 before she said anything. "I need a minute." Troy unfastened her seatbelt and opened the door, slamming it behind her before traipsing away from the CRV over the desert ground. Wade didn't watch her leave, instead keeping still for a few minutes. He soon heaved himself out of the car, lumbering around the front to take a seat on the hood. He retrieved his brown hip flask from his back pocket, spinning the top off before taking a swig. He adjusted his drifter's cap, eyes on the waning sun. Lindsay finally stomped back over to him, standing directly in his line of sight while he remained on the hood. She folded her arms over her chest and waited until his eyes met hers. "You probably don't want to hear it, but I am sorry for what happened to you. Abandonment like that is something that I can't imagine the pain of because I've never had to live through it." Wade kept his eyes on hers, taking a swig of bourbon. "Well, I never had to bury no-one when I was nine, so I offer ya the same sentiment." He offered her the flask, which she accepted and took a drink from. "I went out there," she motioned with her head, "to try and figure out why it seems like I've let myself get dragged into things this past year. The thing with Fuck You, now the thing with Danny and Caesar and now this. And I had no answers for myself. Maybe I'm just that kind of person who is always drawn to, and into, problems, whether I want to be or not. But it's almost dusk and I'm not going to stand waiting in the middle of a desert for AAA or someone to come pick me up to get me out of this mess. So here's the deal. I'll stay, but I'm driving the rest of the way there. And you'd better not get thrown in jail because if I have to bail someone else out within a three-week period, I'm going to be even more pissed off than I am now." "Hey, if I get arrested I won't blame ya fer leavin' me there." "And if I do, then I'm going to get bitched out by Mini-Me. That's probably going to happen anyway, regardless." Wade looked at her, befuddled. "Mini-Me?" "Lisa Tyler. I swear she's watched promo tapes of mine and is trying to emulate my style." Wade took his flask back. "That title on yer shoulder...who wouldn't?" "I wouldn't want anyone to be just like me. The titles and the recognition are nice but the headaches aren't always worth it," she shrugged. "So, you ready? I want to eat something before you go all smashy-punchy on me." Wade stood up, replacing his flask in his back pocket. "Yer drivin', so I'd better go with ya, 'less I wanna walk to Vegas." ------------- "I guess the money that should have gone to child support wasn't enough to find him a better building." The darkness of evening went unnoticed under the bright lights of Las Vegas. Elliott and Troy sat in the CRV in a parking lot behind an unsavory looking building. Wade's eyes were narrowed, staring at what appeared to be the back door of "Snake Eyes Tavern." He took out his flask, quickly drained what was left, and wiped away the droplets in his thick goatee with the back of his hand. "Wait here," he grumbled to Troy, opening the door and starting to step out. "Whoa, wait a second Cowboy. You're making me wait in the car outside of this shithole?" He stopped, turning toward her. "This ain't yer problem, and I ain't 'bout to drag ya in further. Wait here." "Need I remind you that you consciously and deliberately drove me eighty-five percent of the way here before letting me in on your secret? I'm not going to be the side plot in your revenge scheme while you go in there alone and get shot, or stabbed, or choked, or who knows what else. So fuck you, I'm going too." She yanked the handle of the driver's door and was about to jump out when Wade grabbed her arm. "Don't. Fuckin'. Follow me," he said, the hatred boiling in his eyes. "You going to hog-tie me to the seat to stop me?" "If I fuckin' have to," he growled. He stepped out, slammed the door shut, and lumbered toward the tavern. Lindsay watched him walk inside, nearly ripping the door straight off as he did. She locked the doors and climbed into the backseat where her suitcase was, somehow compacting her body between the front two seats so she wouldn't have to get out and draw even more attention to the SUV with out of state license plates. "Fucking pig-headed, razor-backed, half-drunk hillbilly meathead..." she cursed while pulling out wrist-tape and a small leather sack, which she untied and let a few throwing stars fall out onto her clothes. ------------- Wade stepped into the tavern. It was a nasty little dive; old floorboards, scratched up tables, dim lighting and not much more than cheap whiskey and cheap beer behind the counter. Though by the look of the patrons it didn't seem there was a need for anything more. Lucky for Wade, he fit right in. Tipping his drifter's hat low he stepped past the tables and toward the counter, where a gangly fellow with long, stringy gray hair and a long fu-man-chu mustache stood cleaning dishes. Wade stopped. He caught a look at the bartender's eyes, the piercing blue giving away his identity. "Sonnova bitch," muttered Wade. He collected himself, stepping forward and taking a seat on a stool. "What's yer poison, stranger?" asked the bartender, in a voice not unlike Wade's, though less deep. "From the look of ya, I'd guess the same yers," replied Wade. The bartender smiled, eyes still focused on his cleaning. "Bourbon it is!" The bartender happily grabbed a bottle of Jim Beam, pouring a tumbler and sliding it into Wade's rough hand. He looked into the eyes of the new fellow, then stopped, squinting a bit. "Well now," he said, studying Wade. "Somethin' familiar 'bout you..." Wade bore a hole through him. It clicked. The bartender's eyes widened. "Shitta-god-damn..." he whispered. "Evenin', Pa," said Wade. Wade grabbed a nearby beer bottle and smashed it against his father's head, covering his face with blood and staining his gray hair. Wade gripped him by his collar, broken bottle pointed at his throat. Many of the patrons stood ready to fight, but stopped to see the predicament of their bartender. A few fled. "Coulda...'xpected a hug from my long lost boy..." said Wade's father, groggily. "SHUT YER FUCKIN' TRAP, YOU SONNOVA BITCH!" roared Wade, arm taught, ready to slice into his father's neck. "Rrrghh...whaddya want from me, son?" asked Wade's father, blood flowing down his face. "I want the fuckin' thirty-one years of fuckin' confusion n' not knowing where the fuck I come from back! I want Ma to have the eighteen shitty fuckin' years raisin' me without nobody to help 'er back! But most of all I want some fuckin' answers before I open you up!" In the insanity of it all, Wade's father managed to chuckle. "Oh, fer fuck's sake, son, whatta you wanna hear? That I ran off when I was eighteen after gettin' some broad pregnant..." Wade pushed the bottle into his dad's throat, dangerously close to penetrating. "Another word 'bout my mother n' I'll do worse than kill ya." "Well Christ, boy! I was eighteen fuckin' years old! Weren't ready to raise no kid! You woulda done the same!" "HORSE-SHIT!" "No it ain't! Don't fuckin' kid yerself!" Wade breathed heavily through his nose, eyes fixed on his father's. "Ya come walkin' in here, all piss n' vinegar like yer the only asshole who grew up without a dad, an' fer what? To kill me? That gonna git ya yer thirty years back? That gonna help ya sleep better? Don't bullshit yerself, boy." "RAAAAAAAAAAGH!" A roar from behind caught Wade's attention, some drunk with a beer bottle ready to smash it over his head. Wade, holding tight to his father's collar, turned, throwing a heavy boot into the chest of the assailant, causing him to fly back through a window and into the parking lot, gasping for air among thousands of glass shards. Troy had no sooner snapped the top of her phone down when the bar patron flew through the air with the greatest of ease...or not...and landed within fifteen feet of the Honda. Another curse escaped her mouth as she opened the back door and ran over to the man. "Sir? Shit, glass, sir? Did you break anything?" "Ermmmm...." Troy looked up and saw Wade with a bottle, holding presumably his bloodied mess of a father by his shirt collar, and about ten other guys ready to bum-rush him. "Shit, this has 'Boy Named Sue' written all over it..." she muttered and started to walk over to the open door when the drunk tugged on her leg. "You sure are purty. You's an angel?" Lindsay rolled her eyes and jerked her leg away, then walked inside the bar. "BACK THE FUCK OFF, YOU LIMBER-DICKED COCKSUCKERS!" bellowed Wade, threatening the lot of the bar with the broken bottle, a vice-grip remaining on his father's collar. Wade's dad stared wide-eyed at the the window he just witnessed a man fly through. "Ho-lee shit! Looks like my genes made one helluva man!" Wade whipped the bottle back to the neck of his father. "SHUT YER FUCKIN' MOUTH!" yelled Wade. "Sure looks like I picked a bad time to get thirsty." Wade turned to see Lindsay in the doorway, as did the rest of the bar, only most with more carnal thoughts on their mind. "Get the fuck outta here, Troy! This ain't yer fight!" "Wade, you made me wait in the car and didn't even give me one of those Mad-Libs books or hand-held Texas Hold 'Em games to keep me occupied. I got bored. " "Shoulda stayed bored!" "Shit boy! Lookit that pretty lady ya roped up!" "Fuck off, y'piece of shit!" Wade smashed his fist into his father's nose, splattering blood all over and sending his head back, only to swing forward, thanks to Wade's unrelenting grip. "See? This looks infinitely more fun," said Troy. "Glad I decided to join in." Lndsay sauntered over to get a better look at the damage, but had difficulty seeing around two tall, double-wide patrons. "You guys mind scooting over a bit to let a lady through?" The two bumbled out of the way, giving toothless grins to the Queen of the Ring. She returned their grins with a curt smile and stepped over to stand next to Wade. "So, this is what a deadbeat dad looks like up close." "Yer startin' to piss me off, Troy. Get yer ass outta here!" said an enraged Wade. "I say we let 'er ass stay!" laughed his groggy father. "RAAAAAGH!" Wade smashed his forehead into his father's nose, further breaking it, sending him to the brink of unconsciousness. "Ughh...or she could...leave..." "Much to everyone's chagrin, I'm not leaving," Troy remarked, putting her hand on Wade's shoulder. For the first time, everyone in the bar noticed her hands with a throwing star taped right across her knuckles, the points jutting out when a fist is made. "You're pissed at me, Wade, and I'm pissed at you, so now we're even. Beating the shit out of your father may make you feel better now but it's not going to get thirty-one years back. Behind your Hulk-like rage, you know that's true." Wade made no motion to recognize the wise words of Lindsay, only stared directly in the eyes of his battered father. "Give me one good god-damn reason not to kill you where you stand," he asked, low and cold. "Because, boy, whether ye wanna hear it or not, yer just like me," was his father's bloody-mouthed reply. Wade could only stare at him. "He's nothing like you." Wade's father's eyes rolled over to Lindsay, whose returned his look with an emblazed one of her own. "You're not a man, you know. Men take responsibility for their actions, something you chose not to do at eighteen when you got a girl pregnant and ran off instead of doing the right thing by her and the baby. That may make you a child, but you're not a child either. Children say they're sorry when they've done something wrong and are reprimanded for it, but you haven't done that. So if you're not a man and you're not a child, you're not a human being. And if you're not a human being, a compassionate, remorseful human being like you should be, then you're nothing more than a shitheel who has had this beating coming to him for longer than you thought possible." Lindsay took a step closer to the old man and glared down at him. "You deserve his rage. But you don't deserve his time. He's a man, who takes responsibility. He's more than you'll ever be." He twisted his face, looking like he might spit a bloody glob at her. "Don't even think about it, because I'll carve your face up so fast you'll look like a dead ringer for Danny Trejo." "An' I'll stomp yer jaw into a million fuckin' pieces!" hissed Wade. "And the rest of you," Troy whirled around to address the patrons, "don't you have wives, or girlfriends, or boyfriends, or a bottle of Vaseline to go home to? Get out of here." They shuffled out, some quicker than others. Wade watched them go, then slowly turned his head back to his father's bloody face. "Thank her," he growled. "For fuckin'what?" spat his father. Wade gripped tighter, choking his bloody mess of a dad. "Thank her for savin' yer fuckin' life!" he spat back. "Cuz you'd sure as shit be dead n' rottin' if she hadn't come in here!" There was no reply, only the blank face of a man trying to figure out the words of his enraged son. "He can thank me by not pressing charges." Both men looked at her. "What the hell makes you think I won't?" asked Wade's father. "Because you'll be arrested, tried and convicted, might I add, for negligence and not paying child support. Or something more legalese, I forgot what Clay said in all this excitement. Regardless, you'll lose your bar and your livelihood." Both men continued to look at her. "I called my lawyer after you yelled at me to stay in the car," she shrugged. "Helluva woman, that one," said Wade's dad, turning back to Wade. "You ain't lyin'," Wade replied, eyes still on Troy. He let go of his father, who fell onto the bar, spitting blood all over it. "You stay here n' rot, you fuckin' piece of horse-shit," said Wade. "I ever run into ya in the days comin', I will fuckin' kill ya." "And I may not be around to save your skin again, old man. So, I'd say it's in your best interest to stay where you're familiar." Troy looked over to the bar. "And for Christ's sake, get some rum in this place. Musty beer and well-liquor is no way to run a bar." Wade turned without a word, heavy thumps sounding across the floorboards as he left. Troy followed after. They crossed the parking lot and stepped into the CRV, Wade behind the wheel, and quietly left the scene of such havoc. ------------- The CRV cruised West toward California, the sun indicating mid-morning. They drove without a word, eyes on the horizon. After a few minutes, Wade finally broke the silence. "Thanks, Lindsay." "You're welcome. Although, I wouldn't be surprised if your father or some of the other dregs figure out where you and I work and rat us out that way." "I ain't gonna worry about it." "Maybe it's best that you don't," she said. Her phone chirped from inside her purse and, after a moment of rummaging, she pulled it out and flipped the top open. "Text from Blaine." When she said nothing more, Wade looked over to see her smirking. "What?" he asked, one eye on the road, one trying to see the text. "You and I get the Cunt and the Commie Emo on Wednesday." Wade turned back to the road, a grin on his face. "Well...don't that just lift the spirits."
