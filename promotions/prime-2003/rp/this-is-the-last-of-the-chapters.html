This is the Last of the Chapters

Date: 2007 gives way to 08 December 1st, 2007 Morning Green. Green. Red. Red. Red. “God damn it!” His fist crushed down on the plastic concoction and, though, there was no cosmetic harm, the sound of rattling pieces echoed from inside the machine. “Piece of junk.” “What’s wrong?” Mary [name withheld] stepped into the family room, a look of concern on her face. “What are you yelling about?” “This stupid fax machine.” Matt folded his arms across his chest and sighed, his eyes burning a whole through the contraption, as if he were going to intimidate it into functioning. “It’s got one job… one thing it’s supposed to do… and the hunk of crap can’t even do it.” “Ok. First of all, it’s a fax/scanner/copier/printer all in one… so it has a few talents.” She pushed past her husband. “Secondly, what are you trying to fax?” “It’s contract stuff for the PTC Unified Title match. Squaring away some details.” “Well, if the fax machine isn’t working,” Mary grabbed the papers and looked them over before folding them up in her left hand, “how bout you just pitch the thing in the trash.” “Are we going to do this again?” He reached out and snapped the papers away from his wife. “I’m not wrestling full-time anymore.” “By this point, I had thought you weren’t going to be wrestling at all.” “Mary, this is it for me. This is the ultimate prize. I’m goin’ on thirty-two years old. I only wrestle part-time anymore… this is my first and last shot at the Unified title. It doesn’t get any bigger than this.” “That’s the problem, Matthew! Some gold belt means a helluva lot more to you than it should. To you, some tin on a leather strap is as big as it gets.” “That’s not true.” “Then do something to show it!” Mary paused to take a deep breath, and she rubbed her hand across her stomach as she did so. “You’ve got a two-year old child. Another on the way… and at this rate, you won’t even be able to play with your kids by the time there old enough to run and tackle! Your knees are shot, Matthew, and more wrestling won’t fix that.” “You think I don’t know my knees are a wreck? Mary, if this were any other shot at any other title… I wouldn’t even bother, but I swear to y…” “I’m tired of swearing to me… I want you to do it for me! Just one time I want you to toss an offer in the garbage, instead of feeling like you have to pounce all over it. For once…” Her words stopped, like a broken watch, running fine one moment…nothing the next. Again, her hand reached for her stomach, and through her shirt and her frustration, she could feel everything go wrong. The family room began to melt away, its blur broken up only by the blinking red ‘error’ light of the fax machine. “Mary… baby, what’s wrong?” His tone was of genuine concern, not forgetting, but no longer caring of the spat just seconds before. “I think it’s the baby.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The pieces of crudely deteriorated blacktop crunched under the soles of his shoes. Shifting his head from side to side, Matt took in the abandoned environment around him. He’d seen an actual ghost-town once, on a week-long summer vacation to Montana when he was 14. This was the deadest thing he’d seen since. It had been ten years since the old stadium had served as the playing ground for his hometown high school football team. It’d been three years since the soccer team had moved on to greener pastures. Still Wert Field had a nostalgic aura about it. Located right in the middle of town, nowhere near the school itself, it had been common place for field goals and extra points to send footballs landing on the front porch of Mr. Runger’s old place, or bouncing on the hoods of moving cars that were zipping past the stadium on Route 36. It was quaint. Simple. There was no parking lot anywhere around, just a small patch of grass where the team buses could dock for the evening. All the fans parked up the street near the ice cream shop, grabbing a slushy on their way to the game. Then they’d fill the old bleachers, stomping on the cold steel surface, shaking the concrete foundation beneath them. And behind the stands, the junior high kids, far too suave to actually watch a single snap of the game, loitered around the concession stand, cursing and spitting and smoking… whatever was cool at the time. The smell of pretzels and popcorn always lingered in the air, even if it did occasionally mix with the stench of urine from the stone-age restrooms. Now… all the buzz of Friday night life was gone. Weeds uplifted chunks of asphalt through its various cracks and crevices. The grass of the field was probably shin-high. And underneath the seating on the home-team side… empty of teenage angst… the smell of popcorn ceased to exist, though the pungent odor of stale piss remained. Matt hadn’t stepped foot on the grounds in nearly a decade. But, through everything that had changed (and it was difficult to distinguish what was more dissimilar from ten years ago… himself or the stadium), he felt a sense of familiarity. It was soothing. And he needed his mind at ease. The last few months had brought about a lot of differences in his daily routine. His life had changed. So had his brother’s. And it was time to tie up the last loose end. He snuck a peek at his watch. 8:08 Glancing around one last time, Matt headed up the ramp towards the bleachers to wait. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Afternoon His focus shifted the second he caught his brother, in the corner of his eye, coming through the door. Matt hopped to his feet, his stride carrying him across the room in a fraction of the steps it should have taken. “Scott… Laura.” Their names were the only thing he thought to say. “How is she, Matt?” Laura reached out and touched her hand to his arm, a sympathetic way to her demeanor. “I dunno. I’m waiting for the doctor.” “How far along is she?” Scott looked around the room, trying to find someone in scrubs who could give them all the answers they were searching for. “She’s just seven months, isn’t she?” “Yeah.” His reply was nearly lost in the pop of knuckles as he cracked them against the palm of his hand. “I’m sure everything will be fine. You should sit down...” Laura pointed to the chair Matt had just departed “you look exhausted.” He was. Matt couldn’t remember how long he’d been at the hospital… maybe just an hour, but the panicked rush to the emergency room had been draining. He’d darted through the house, searching frantically for Mary’s bag, finally locating it right where it was supposed to be had he bothered to look their first. Phone calls to relatives followed as he stumbled out of the house, his daughter in one arm, a bag slung over his shoulder, leading Mary to the car with his free hand. His mind had been such a mess, that it had taken him three times as long as usual to secure Georgie in her car-seat. The shaking of his hands had just been too much. “Yeah, sit down, bro. Try to relax.” Scotty’s voice betrayed his attempts of comfort, nervousness punctuating each of his words. But there was no chance to sit and try to level his breathing, steady his hands, or catch any sort of rest. No sooner had he turned and taken a step towards the chair, then the doctor arrived in the room. Laura and Scott joined Matt’s side, engulfing the doctor in barrage of questions. They listened as he spoke, as much with hands as words, informing them that he and his staff had administered a drug to try and delay labor, how it had failed, that they were going to deliver the baby. There hopes seemed to deflate, and someone must have closed some blinds in the room, because things suddenly seemed darker. “Matt, I know you’re concerned,” the doctor put a hand on his shoulder, “but this sort of thing happens much more often than people are lead to believe. Lot’s of women go into labor before nine months rolls around. I need you to stay calm, and if you’d like… you can follow me.” He turned his attention to Scott and Laura. “I’m afraid I can only allow the husb…” “We understand.” Scott shook his head and Laura’s hand. “If you’ll come with me, Mr. [name withheld], we’ll deliver this baby.” Without hesitation, Matt spun on his feels and followed the doctor, not a half step behind. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ He sat about the thirty-yard line, maybe fifteen rows up… the type of modest seats he usually found himself in for kickoffs. But it was good enough. From his location, he could see the old entrance near the locker room facilities, and he’d spotted Hicks before he’d even entered the stadium. On the other hand, Robert Hickens, better known to everyone who would listen as ‘Hicks’, seemed to be having trouble locating the lone individual in a sea of empty bleachers. “Rob!” Matt yelled down to the rough looking soul. “Jesus, dude… I’m sitting right here.” The scraggly haired weasel looked in the direction of the voice, shook his head, as if he needed to acknowledge where the words had come from, and started to make his way up the stands. Each step left a ringing echo in the nothingness around them, as he skipped up the metal benches, ignoring the stairs just a few feet away. Hicks was one of those guys… no real job, just a bunch of shady dealings that involved gambling, of the mostly illegal variety. There was probably a little bit of drug pushing too… though he’d only ever confess, “hey, I’ve sold a little bit of weed a time or two before… but who the hell hasn’t?” He was no big time criminal… just some insignificant creep far too small to ever register on any federal agent’s radar, but slimy and big-time enough to ruin the occasional life. “Mattman! Been a few months… how ya doin?” Hicks took a seat just to Matt’s left, one row in front. “Good. You?” “Eh, same shit, different day.” Rob scratched at the graying mane atop his head. “Sometimes different shit on the same day.” “Real deep Hicks.” “That’s about as philosophical as I get, pal.” Pulling a roll of Lifesavers from his pocket, Rob popped one in his mouth as he looked around. “So what’s up… and why did we have to meet here? Couldn’t you have treated a guy to lunch?” “I’m not gonna treat you to anything. I just wanted to let you know that it’s over.” “Over?” “Yeah,” Matt placed his hands at his side, the metal chilled beneath his hands. “I wanted to let you know, that I’m retired from wrestling now.” “No shit?” “And I wanted to let you know that Scotty finally has his life turned around. He’s kicked the gambling habit, and that means I don’t need to see your face again.” “Hey,” Hicks held up his hands in a manner that was somewhere between a halt and a plead for help, “wait a minute, Matt.” “No. For the last half a dozen years, I’ve been using pay-outs from wrestling matches to square away my little brother’s debts to you. The last grand you got from me is the last grand you get from me. There’s no more gambling. There’s no more wrestling. He’s being the husband he’s supposed to be. I’m happy being a father. Things have changed, and as far as I’m concerned… that means we don’t need to deal with each other anymore.” “I’m not likin’ what I’m hearin’.” “Whatever. You can keep going about your business for all I care. Just expect yourself short a client.” “Oh come on!” Hicks laughed as he practically choked on the words. “You really think your brother won’t be right back at it? Scotty likes to bet… more than he’s got. He’s one of those people who’s always gonna owe money to guys like me, and…” Whatever the rest of the sentence was supposed to be, it was stifled by the clang of skull to metal as Matt’s right hand suddenly exploded from its docile state, slamming against the side of Hick’s face. In a fluid motion, his palm drove Rob’s head into the empty space on the bench next to him, wedging the rat’s cranium against the stinging cold steel. “I’m serious, Hicks.” “JESUS! What the FUCK are you doing?!” The scream came out in a garbled mess. The lifesaver and spit came spewing from Rob’s lips as he tried to force the words through restrained jaws. “Scott’s finally cleaned up his act.” Matt stared down over Hicks without even moving from his casually seated position. “He’s got a beautiful new wife. He’s done with your bullshit, Rob, and I’m done giving you a cut of my paycheck every time you claim he owes you money. Things are different now. I don’t want to see you anywhere near Scotty. I don’t want hear your name again. I don’t want to remember what you look like. Ok?” “Y…yeah… ok, Matt.” “Matt…hew” Evening The sound of her stammered voice pulled him away from the stadium, placing him with a jolt right back in the hospital room, beside her bed. He folded the notebook in his hand and leaned out of his seat, brushing a bit of hair away from her eyes. “Hey, sweetie.” “The…the baby. Matthew…” “The baby’s fine. Everything’s ok.” “Hunter…” “Hunter Dean [name withheld]. He’s fine, sweetheart. We’ll see our baby boy soon. Now, you need to take it easy, let the doctors do their thing. Just rest.” “How long… was I out?” Mary warily lifted her hand and rubbed her eyes. “Just feel exhausted.” “You were asleep for maybe three hours now, I guess.” As he answered, he kissed her softly on the forehead. Staring at her their in the bed, eyes heavy, hair a matted mess of sweat and tangles, he smiled. “S..sorry. You’ve prolly been… bored.” “Naw. I’ve been reading a great story.” Matt lifted his hand, clutching the notebook in its tight wrap. “It was in the bag.” “Oh.. that…” “You turned my ol’ college roommate, Hicks, into quite the sleazy small-time felon.” “Heh,” the softest laugh he’d ever heard escaped her lips. “Creative liberty.” “Well, I promise I won’t say anything to him.” He looked at the story in his hand and lifted it a bit. “Is this your next piece you’re writing for your publisher? It’s really good, baby.” “Nah… it’s nothin.” Mary tried to push herself into more of a seated position, but the energy just wasn’t there. “Just somethin’ silly I wrote…one afternoon to clear… my mind. Didn’t even give it an ending.” “It’d really mean the world to you if I quit wre…” “It’s just a story.” “Our story. You should finish it.” “No.” The corner of her lips began to arch their way up, just the slightest bit. “It was a few words I wrote one day… for no reason. You can toss it in the trash.” “Ok.” Matt watched as her eyelids collapsed and a slow breath moved her chest. Again he kissed her on the forehead. “Go back to sleep, sweetie.” And she was already there by the time he slunked back into the chair by her bed. As she worked on recovering her strength, he let the notebook unfold and opened it back up where he’d left off. Trailing down the page, he saw the words give way to blank lines. Rubbing the back of his neck, Matt closed his eyes for a moment. When they reopened, he turned them towards the trashcan next to their bag on the floor. A smile burst across his face, and he reached down with his free hand, rummaging through the satchel till he located what he was searching for. Pulling forth a pen, he did his best to steady the notebook across his knee, and he began to let the words flow, finishing their story.
