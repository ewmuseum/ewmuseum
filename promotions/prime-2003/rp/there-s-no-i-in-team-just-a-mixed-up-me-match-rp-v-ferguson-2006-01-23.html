There's No 'I' In Team... Just A Mixed-Up 'Me' (Match RP - v Ferguson/Nelson/Farwell)

23 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23/01/06 There's No 'I' In Team... Just A Mixed-Up 'Me'.Written: 23rd January, 2006. 'I've spent my whole life surrounded,And I've spent my whole life alone.I wonder why I never wonder why,The easiest things are so hard.' - The Bravery. I 'Hold the elevator!' With worn sneaker soles almost losing traction on the freshly-mopped floor tiles, Karina Wolfenden did her best to sprint and slide down the corridor. Ignoring the harsh burn of the floor's detergent attacking her nostrils with each hurriedly-inhaled breath, Kari somehow managed to keep her footing, just as legs threatened to disobey her, and drop into the splits position. Seeing the two barricades of steel creep together, trying to eclipse the sneak peak of what awaited her inside, the K-Wolf found a higher gear. Channelling all her weight down into her left leg, Wolfenden almost burst her muscles with kinetic energy, before she exploded off the tiles with a lunging leap. Contorting herself in mid-air, she just managed to slip through the closing doors, shaving fibres from the back of her sweater as not even a spare inch was to be found. 'Thanks for holding,' was scoffed with breathless sarcasm as Karina found a bit of wall space to lean against. While not claustrophobic in the scientific sense, the K-Wolf became overly conscious of her surroundings. A need for personal space left the air feeling stale, recycled through others lungs so that it weighed heavy in hers. Like a fish flapping around on a boat's deck, not even heaving a breath in through her mouth could tackle the fuzzy feeling creeping through her head. Slowly, the elevator's gears began to turn. The slow, hesitant whirr did its best to taunt the K-Wolf. Brow furrowing as she tried to fathom the maze of entwined white, Karina untangled her I-Pod's earphones, before ducking them under her choppy blonde, blue, and pink hair to tuck them into her ears. Dropping the I-Pod back into her sleeve pocket, Kari did her best to relax back against the elevator’s wall as the feeling she so hated, as if her diaphragm hiccupped, told her that she was descending. As ambient drums crashed throughout her skull, the eyes of her 5 fellow passengers gravitated towards Karina. 'Sorry,' she looked innocent in appeasing them with, having neglected the fact that it had barely been used at anything other than full volume. Tapping the volume down button through the weave of her sweater, Kari kept pressing until every set of eyes had returned to politely staring at the floor. With a surly look to her, Kari folded her arms... years of decibel abuse having left her so that barely anything could be heard at what should have been a comfortable level. Ding! Looking up through her fringe, Kari clocked the floor number. Sixteen. Only another... sixteen to go. 'Oh joy...' she mumbled under her breath as the doors opened, unaware that detached from the ambient volume, the comment was heard by everyone else at normal volume. Through the doors walked, or rather waddled, a portly man in his 40's. With a heavy case in either hand, red was already burning through his cheeks from the exertion. Karina watched as he dropped the cases, beads dripping from the moist handles. As the doors sealed them in once more, he ran a hand through his thinning hair, sending perspiration splashing down at Wolfenden's feet. Focussed emerald eyes watching them queasily. The stairs would have been the wiser choice, she felt like kicking her own shin in thinking. Swallowing her insides once again as the lift jolted into its descent, heightened senses almost caused her eyes to water. The already stale stench of the elevator had taken on a sharper, sweater tang. Her narrowing eyes watched as the man again ran a hand through his hair, mopping the sweat from his forehead. She imagined the dark patches under each arm... sweat trickling down his back. Stairs, I'm sorry for ever doubting you... Needless to say, the constrictive company of strangers was not something that the K-Wolf tended to bask in. Even the plane journeys that criss-crossed her across the globe were only made tolerable by what awaited her at the eventual destination. The ground floor didn't quite hold the same prestige. And at least she could drink on a plane without getting funny looks. Ding! Fourteen. It was going to take years. It already felt like more than a few months. Picking up the slippery handles of his cases, the man waddled out, leaving the original six. Not that they shared any sort of bond that would exclude him, apart from maybe anti-perspirant. Letting out an audible sigh, Karina was already starting to fidget, re-crossing her arms, and switching her stance back and forth. With her head bowed, bored watching toes wiggle through sneakers, she looked out through her fringe to where a couple of those she shared the elevator with were engrossed in conversation. Lip-reading, she made out a few words, when mouths weren't obscured by flicks of hair, or overly exaggerated - bordering on charade-level - hand gestures. Retreated into herself, the close confines almost caused paranoia to creep in. Her eyes scanned back and forth, not quite sure if she saw someone look away just as her gaze cancelled out theirs. When the doors opened in the lobby, she'd once again be the continent-hopping, mountain-taming, ass-kicking K-Wolf. But with her wings clipped, she passed back through tame, and into the realm of timid. A very, very unwelcome location. One she hated. Loathed. Even more than Japan. Sodding 'Lost in Translation.' Thirteen. Within herself, Wolfenden ran through excuses as to why she hadn't visited Eric Evans that afternoon... something that wouldn't normally have triggered the hint of an inkling of guilt if she hadn't actually gone and said she would. Well, she'd replied 'sure, whatever' when asked if she would be. The all-conquering force of randomness had forced them into the same team, so that supposedly meant that she had to be social. 'Next time I do contract negotiations,' the K-Wolf had sighed with her stereotypical roll of the eyes, 'demand a 'no random teaming' clause.' After all of a few seconds thought, she decided on a travel delay. Twelve. 'Um, about the tag thing...' she'd appealed to Motoki. 'I'd like to be excused.' 'And why would that be?' a cocked eyebrow tried to tempt the reason. 'Well, this whole thing was because everyone decided they'd resort to basic animal mentality and fight everyone else for no reason...' Kari recalled, scowling at the trigger for Toshiaki’s announcement. 'Only, I wasn't one of them. So, no company bonding exercise needed.' 'Is that correct,' Motoki pondered as the K-Wolf looked pleased with herself. 'And why was that?' 'Oh, I'd left early,' she really should have stopped with, 'hanging around when I'm done with my match isn't really my thing...' Needless to say, Toshiaki Motoki wasn't exactly presented with a sob-story of an excuse that would melt even his frosty heart. Eleven. Beginning to relax a bit more in her surroundings, the K-Wolf closed her eyes, letting the loops trickle out through her earphones. Closing her eyes, she finally began to breathe through her nose, while her palms lost their clammy feeling. The body heat of her fellow passengers seemed to diminish as serenity was once again imposed upon Wolfenden's world. Until... 'Ooomph!' The smooth descent down the shaft was shunted uneven, one cable seemingly jamming, forcing the metallic box to be jolted to the side. The man leant against the wall beside Karina, his legs crossed as he casually reclined, was instantly knocked off balance, and was thrown across her and into the wall next to the doors. Her eyes shot open just in time to see the flash of his red t-shirt blur into her vision, before a string of expletives came as an encore to the tinny bump. 'Arghfuckshit!' he seethed, grabbing his forehead where it whacked against the wall. Karina seemed a second or so behind everyone else, retreated back deep to calm herself, having to scramble back up into the present. Across from her, the pair who were eagerly talking before had gradually flicked up the volume scales and were now yelling at one another. The man, pulling off smart casual with a grey turtleneck under a black suit jacket, pushed his hands down as if to order calm, but done in such a fierce manner that the medium negated the message. 'I am calm!' was screamed back in his face. As the floor seemed to rock ever so slightly - just enough to notice, not enough to soothe - leaving Kari's feet probing, trying to find good balance. Long, deep breaths failed to slow the heart that was on overdrive, while the slightly off-kilter interior only served to make her feel as if the walls were starting to creep back in. By the doors, the elevator’s eldest occupant, a woman flirting respectfully with her 60's, continuously thumbed the emergency alarm button. With neatly bobbed, greying hair, and handbag hanging daintily from the cup of her elbow, the lady never lost her poise and composure, leaving it three seconds between each pressing. Maybe she was just as nervy as the K-Wolf, and the repetition was a physical take on a calming mantra. 'Relax,' Kari told herself behind closed eyes. 'Relax, relax, relax.' Despite the panicked voices criss-crossing in front of her, Kari tried to focus only on what the I-Pod was pumping out through her earphones. Timing her breathing to the beats, the cold sweat that threatened to break out in full force across her forehead retreated back. 'Relax,' Kari continued to order herself. 'Just listen to th-... oh fuck.' The K-Wolf was never one to let things recharge, whether they be batteries, or herself. With her audio barrier breached, she was opened up to her surroundings with full force. 'Press the alarm!' 'She's been pressing it!' 'Maybe she's not pressing it right!' 'Oh my God, he's bleeding!' 'Look, just ca-' 'I can't be near blood!' 'Well he's coping, and it's his head that's split open!' Short of having to work more than one night a week, this was the K-Wolf's nightmare. Nowhere to spread her wings, far above the ground but devoid of the freedom altitude usually gave her. Running a thumb around the neck of her sweater, Kari tried to relieve the constricting of her throat, a psychosomatic asthmatic feeling leaving her windpipe feeling tied in knots. 'I think she's going to vomit...' Peeking out from under the blonde, blue, and pink, Wolfenden saw the dismissive look the words were delivered with. Already, the other 5 had become sewn together by some kind of convict's bond. As if being trapped within a few feet of one another should naturally foster such camaraderie. Having handed over alarm pressing duty, the elderly lady had already delved into her purse, and was engrossing the others in pictures of her seemingly endless pride of Grandchildren. In between ooohs and ahhhs, their attention was channelled into concern for the bleeding man, each taking their turn in telling him to keep the pressure he was already applying... well, applied. Even with no sound coming out through her earphones, Wolfenden left them in, nodding her head along to an imaginary beat. 'If it falls,' began the woman closer to Karina's age, but with a look more akin to a porcelain doll subbing for a mannequin. 'We all need to jump just as it's about to hit the ground.' 'No way does that work,' a dismissive scoff doubted her with. 'It's true!' she decreed. 'It happened to my friend Melody once!' There was a scathing, belittling - relieving - snarky remark just bursting to escape the K-Wolf, but pursing lips did their best to hold it in... almost turning inside out in the process. 'It's to do with gravity and in... inversia, or something?' Pursing became biting. 'We should so do that,' the bilge continued relentlessly. 'I'll count us down.' Biting became full-on Vampirism. Maybe, somewhere tucked away behind a dead-end in the labyrinth of her subconscious, the K-Wolf wanted to yank her earphones out and join in the conversation. Perhaps, just perhaps, there was a side of her that wasn't comfortable sitting out on the sidelines, watching with cynicism rather than wholeheartedly joining in. But wherever those facets were, they were too far from the surface to see the light of day anytime soon. Throwaway conversations weren't her thing, and friendships came with far deeper foundations than just being stood next to someone. They'd moved beyond grandchildren, and on to grandchildren's pets. Patch got run over. Everyone let be known their sadness for his passing. The K-Wolf's eyes crept upon as her head dropped back, mouth agape, and focussed on the hatch above. Only the few shreds of common sense that bound her acted as gravity to tie Kari's feet down, and stop her clambering out, then clawing her way back up to the eleventh floor. How she missed it so. 'She doesn't talk much, does she?' Kari heard the lift plummeting survival expert ponder, thinking her ears were being blanketed of all sound. Stood perfectly still, Kari was climbing the walls inside. She couldn't see the sky. There was no breeze. The oxygen was becoming third-hand. Ambient body heat baked any refreshing qualities from their air. It was forcing her to think... she hated that. Guilt. She'd lit the fuse of Baltasar, and he'd exploded all over PRIME. Her wounded pride set her on a blinkered pursuit of revenge, and it ended in the Red Devil's humiliation. Tens of countries around the world saw him coughing and spluttering in sewage, splashing his own vomit around, while the bodily stench of whoever preceded him at the bowl only spewed forth more. On every file-sharing program on the internet, the clip was being downloaded from an exponentially growing number of sources. Karina had wound him up, pointed him away from her, and watched him shoot off on a rampage. The Imperium she had little sympathy for, Keeton she couldn't care about... but Evans. He was to be her tag team partner, and he was left laying in hospital at the hands of a man she'd driven to madness, then let loose. And she couldn't even spare an hour to catch a bus or train and try to act anything but cold for a change. She should have been accepting the unfamiliar feeling deep in her stomach, embracing the guilt as a liberation. But instead, all she could think, on the surface levels above all others, was that she now had a convenient excuse. Well, it's not like the PRIME application demanded team-players who excel in a group dynamic. 'Maybe we should try yelling?' it continued. 'Someone might be able to hear us, and send for help!' 'Can anyone get reception on their cell?' 'No.' 'No.' 'No.' 'I don't have one, dear.' 'What about her?' 'I don't think she speaks English.' Obviously, the sarcasm of her thanks for holding remark had obliterated any English it contained. As they continued to talk just for the sake of it, pretending to be interested in things they'd already forgotten before they'd finished their overly-acted nodding, Wolfenden felt almost dirty for wanting to join in... just a little. A boredom threshold so low that it could just be taken as being the ground no longer being quelled by grinding teeth and incessant fidgeting. Unable to hold the latent desire to socialise in any longer, defences breached, she waited impatiently for a gap into the conversation into which she can forcibly interject herself. 'This is just like Lost.' '...HOW?' 'It just... is.' It was then that her lips parted, air whistling in ready to be expelled over excitable vocal chords. Then, a rumble underfoot. Her diaphragm hiccupped. Ding! Ten. Tension released, Kari almost sprung off the wall, striding into the harsh, heavenly lights of the corridor. 'I think I'll take the stairs.'
