The Melding of the Roses

29 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12 - 29 - 2005 In a violent place we can call our countryIs a mixed up manAnd I guess thats me.The sun's in the skyBut the storm never seems to end. Its a place of sorrow but we call it a home.And the darkest thoughtsYeah, i guess they're my own There's wealth in the bank But there's nothing to show inside.It's cloudy now. Monday4:22 P.M. The sky looked down at me like two daring eyes darting towards a fire-blazing circus ring supported by two nail-embedded totem poles. There was beach surrounding me and two palm trees keeping a circular shade around my body. It was warm, although a breeze would pick up every ten minutes or so. There was even a dock to swim out to in the middle of this lake I was staring out at. It was as if UPS packaged up perfection and threw me inside of it to sweeten the deal. I was no longer threatened by personal demons that endlessly washed up on shore in front of my feet. No longer were there pits of damnation being shot at me like steel arrows. No longer were there machines mapping out my eternal rest before my very eyes. No longer were there random enemies chalking my name up on international shit-lists. For once in my life, I managed to escape the monster. Whether it was temporary or permanent didn't matter anymore. This was my fraction, my block, of sanity. This beach. This warm air. The magnificently suffocating joy. This was all mine for the taking and because of that, because of that, I didn't care about what lay ahead of me once I returned from paradise. I let out a breif sigh and slid myself out of the white beach chair my rump was sinking into. I was completely satisfied until I noticed something particularly strange. There were three similar looking boats docked to the left of me. A pack of men, dressed in black suits and ties, were exiting simultaneously off each of them with matching suitcases that had gold emblems on the front of them. This wouldn't have caught my attention had I not seen three of these men walk past me when I first arrived at the resort. I wouldn't have cared had none of the other incidents in my life happened to me. I tried to convince myself that I was going through one of my common atomic episodes of paranoia, where everything mentally crashes onto me and mercilessly stomps my existance into the ground until I look like a patty of turnip, and I was somewhat successful in doing that until these boats showed up. Monday12:25 P.M. Ozric: 'Reservations for Nicholas Brocken?' Leaning against the pine counter, Ozric looked down at a nervous receptionist. Fingers trembling, she would delete faster than she'd type. Ozzy wasn't much for serving assistance or taking the reigns of a guide, but he couldn't help himself. Afterall, he was much obliged to do the same for his mentally ailing mother and father through their time of existance. Ozric: 'You know, if you're having trouble with the computer bullshit, I can just give you my signature and you can have your manager look it over. It's been a long week and I'd just like to get to my room.' She stopped and gently placed her fingers over the home keys. A delightful smile painted across her face as she looked up at Ozzy with welcoming eyes. Clearing her throat, she pushed a circular gray button on the monitor to shut it off. Receptionist: 'What's your name? You look so God-damn familiar.' Ozric: 'I don't think you're supposed to curse towards customers, Receptionist lady.' Receptionist: 'I was only doing what you were doing to me.' There was dead silence. Both Ozzy and the receptionist were like two frozen lions; caged in the Alaskan terrain with the intentions to kill eachother. Just before he could open his mouth a gentlemen, clothed in a luxurious suit and tie, nudges his shoulder, causing Ozzy to bang up against the desk. Turning around, he noticed the man look particularly sketchy. Just a bit overdressed for such a casual 'getaway venture'. Then again, nothing was casual to Ozzy anymore. Ozric: 'Now who the hell is that?' Receptionist: 'He's part of the corporate scum sun-bathing around here.' Ozric: 'Why are they here?' Receptionist: 'I suppose that's something I know, and something you need to find out for yourself.' There was a knock on the wall directly behind her. She didn't move an inch, as if she was trying to conceal whatever the noise was. It reached Ozzy's attention, though. He moved a couple steps to the right only to see a small gap in the fortification. The receptionist followed Ozzy's every move while keeping a close eye on his reactions. Receptionist: 'Excuse me, Ozric, but---' Her pulse stopped. She crossed the crooked step. She turned around and walked over to where the gap was and blocked it with her presence. Acting as if she was filing papers, she made an attempt at ignoring Ozric and ending their conversation completely. Ozric: 'What did you just call me?' Receptionist: 'I called you Ozric..' Her eyes squinted. Her breathing slowed down. She moved closer and closer to Ozric until she was face to face with him. Licking her index finger, she slid it down both of his cheeks and inside the crevice between his lips. She started to giggled -- A giggle that mortified Ozric and forced him to back step a bit, but he couldn't. She grabbed a handful of his face and mushed it like a baby's and started to sing the melody to 'BINGO'. There was a victim who had a nameAnd Ozric was his nameoO-Z RIC O-Z RIC O-Z RICAnd OZRIC WAS HIS NAAAAMEO Receptionist: 'You're gonna' die a painful death without ever knowing why it happened to you. Now tell me, Ozric, how do YOU feel?' What? Switch blade's gotch' yer' tongue? I said clearly, HOW DO YOU FUCKING FEEL?' Pressure. The feeling of a 75-piece orchestra simultaneously tuning their instruments. The reverberation smacking against the auditorium walls. Growing louder. Distorting eardrums and dilating pupils. Louder it grows. The pressure rising. The whistle, like boiling water in a tea pot, sounding off at glass-breaking peaks. Ozric's eyes closing and opening again only to see the receptionist drastically changing her body-language again and again and again. Staring into Ozric's eyes and screaming the 'BINGO' melody until everything pops like thumbtack to balloon. Muffling his ears with shaking hands, Ozric opens his eyes and notices a bubble-gum chewing, blonde-haired receptionist staring in amazement. Receptionist: 'Are you, like, sure you're feeling all right? I mean, you really looked, like, upset and stuff?' He said nothing and leaned to the right a bit to make sure the gap in the wall wasn't there. Of course, the wall was as normal as night and day, the receptionist was as phoney as ever, and paradise was just a few short steps away. Ozric: '..Yeah.. I'll be all right, I think..' Receptionist: 'k', cool. Because I was really hesitating on calling for help because I never did that before. But, you really like, scared me.' Ozric: 'Well, that's reassuring, thanks.' Monday4:30 P.M. Two and a half million thoughts, eight shot-glasses of tequila, and four hours later -- I sit here alone, looking at the sun smiling back at me. The smile was questionable and I would've imagined it pissing on me if I wasn't tipsy. The feeling was much different than being drunk or high. My long-term memory was still in tact, but as I sat in this beach chair, I realized that I couldn't remember where I was or when I'd gotten here. I got to my feet and stood still, watching the water rise and fall, and carried onward down a sandy path to my sleeping quarters. I was starting to notice the sudden darkness in the sky, the vacancy of drinking stations and various cabanas, the emptiness within the pool. There was a couple sitting on one of the resting benches beside the path I was walking on. They were smiling, laughing and having what seemed to be a wonderful time. I embraced their happiness as much as possible, mainly because I felt like an anxiety attack was peaking. Ozric: 'God, it's empty around here.' They looked at me with concern. Guy: 'You sure about that?' Ozric: 'Excuse me?' I turned around and, to my absolute shock, saw the livliness of the resort again. The cabanas were all opened. There were more bodies than the Holocaust in the pool. Music was bumping and grinding and people were drinking themselves to a daze. What the fuck was going on here? I turned my head and immediately gasped. The couple was no longer a couple, but two of the suit-and-tie gentlemen I had seen twice already today. Gentleman: 'Is there a problem Nicholas? Or wait, shouldn't I be calling you.. OZRIC?' I back stepped until I bumped into the cold hand rail. Smoke started to rise around both of the mysterious figures and it nearly blinded me. Coughing and tearing, I drop to the floor like a swatted fly, crawling on the cement in hopes that I could get away from whoever was after me. My hands, trembling in fight against the desolation, searching the grounds for something to help myself up. A curb, a bench, a shoe? A black shoe, to be exact. I looked up, only to see a balding man with circular eyeglasses staring at me with a monsterous grin painted across his face. Holding a cane, that had roses painted around it, the man slammed it down onto my right hand. Older Man: 'We have a couple of questions to ask you, Nicholas.' Ozric: 'No! Get the fuck away from me!' I linebacker-charged into the man with recklessness etched like indentations in my brain. I could hear his body slap against the ground but I didn't dare turn around. My legs started to move like propellers being forced by motors. I wasn't controlling my escape any longer. It was as if I was out of my own body. I could see the fear in my eyes. I could sense the adrenaline dropping off of me like mortars. Breathing in. Breathing out. The world was either against me or I was against it. Every possible angle was thought out to a 'T' by these individuals. Each turn I'd make would serve consequence. Each door I'd open would reveal another hint, another reason, a bigger picture to provide annotation. Voice: 'Don't make this harder on yourself. You're only making it easier for us! Run Ozric run! RUN OZRIC RUN! RUN OZRIC RUN!' A wimper from the hopelessness shot out of me and the outcome of this dementia distracted me enough to keep me from noticing the stairs that were coming up ahead. One step. Two step. I looked back but could hardly see a thing through the strange mist. Three step. Four step. Anxious to discern whether or not I got away from them, I thought about stopping for just a second, but popped the bubble and continued. Five step. I snapped out of my daydream just as my right foot stepped off flat ground, sending me into a barrel-roll down the stairs; smashing my head, scraping my knuckles and ramming my forehead into the final step. Ozric: 'Oh my FUCKING God, they're gonna get me. They're gonna get me. Get up you pathetic, washed-up, piece of shit! GET UP!' There's never a reasonTo the pain you recieveThere's never an answerTo the connivance you percieve I was bleeding and bleeding badly. My jaw was torned apart, as was my forehead and both of my knees. I could barely stand but knew I had to if I wanted to stay alive. I had never thought of any of this as being anything more than a game. Until today, I let them do what they were doing because I knew I couldn't stop it by myself. This is all just massive game of life or death. I can see Pat Sajack giving me this douche-chilling smile and telling me the categories I have to pick from. LifeDeathEscapeRevenge Pat Sajeck: 'Now, Ozric, SPIN THAT WHEEL!' My room! I could see my room! #135 with the red paint-chip peeling door and gold knocker. I couldn't believe I was just a few feet from a shelter to hide inside of! I'll call the police and the resort manager and everything will be settled. That's what I'll do. That's what.. I'll do. ..Is that what I'll do..? Monday4:59 P.M. He sprinted the few feet and reached in his back pocket for his entrance card. Exhaling, he feel his blood-pressure lowering. His heart-beat was back at a normal rate and the cold sweats were bowing out. Everything seemed better and clearer now. He slowly slid the card until the red light turned to green. Gripping onto the door handle, he closed his eyes and inhaled a tank of breath. This could go either way and he wasn't anticipating anything at this point. He opened the door and gazed. A morsel of red roses were scattered all over the room. On the floor, on the bed, taped on the walls. He turned to the left, where the bathroom is, and saw the same thing. Roses in the shower stall, in the sink, inside the toilet, and all over the walls. The Melding... Ozric: 'Huh?' There was a nebulous whisper that seemed to come from the bathroom. Ignorantly, he went in there to check on things. He slid open the shower curtain, only to find nothing. Everything was pushing him farther and farther to the edge. These voices probably weren't even real. This whole day was probably a facetious lullaby in a book he read long ago. He had quit drugs, so this couldn't have been one of his vivid tripping experiences, and even his episodes with the demon within hadn't gotten this odd. He walked past the mirror and paused just as he coming to the door. He swallowed hard and turned his head. There was a figure reflecting off the mirror, which meant he was directly behind him. A scream, that went from mewl to bellow, shot out of Ozric as he turned around and charged ... air. Nobody was there. Sigh. He turned back around only to be blinded by a white flash, sending him into the shower curtain, unintentionally pulling it down and bringing him to his back inside the stall. ..Of The Roses. A mysterious individual with a camera can be seen in front of Ozric, laughing hysterically. Camera Man: 'This is all for you, Nicholas. Like we said, we have a couple of questions to ask you.' Ozric's focus was lost in the decaying of his mind. The world seemed so small now to him. He was now just a simple beholder whose innocence has been fried like an egg. Dilated pupils. Living in fear. There is never an end to any of this, but this time, he wasn't sure how to escape it. He could see signs hanging inside of his room now, like a diorama of words that symbolize his existance. Pressure rising. Absence in reality. The immortality of the pain inside his mentality was all too much for him now. His eyes closed and his back dropped, signaling the end of his run to be free. The magical rejuvenation that he worked so hard for was now being crushed like a piece of construction paper at the hands of these men -- The Melding of the Roses. Suddenly, the circular eyeglass-wearing man comes into view, standing over the absent-minded Ozric with cane in hand. Camera Man: 'Sir, I believe he's fainted.' Older Man: 'Well, wake him up. We've got work to do.' Camera Man: 'Yes sir.' The older man nodded and flipped a coin with his index finger and thumb. He turned around and walked out. The room was now filled with photographers, men whose faces were concealed with masks, suit-and-tie individuals, and strange equipment. Equipment that looked just like torture devices. This was but only the beginning as far as they were all concerned. To Ozric? This was but only another chapter.
