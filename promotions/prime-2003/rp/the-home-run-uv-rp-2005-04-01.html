The Home Run (UV RP)

1 April 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 04/01/05 It’s funny…well, funny is the wrong word. It’s strange…there’s a whole mentality about professional wrestling, a whole take on the industry and its well-publicized employees that never sees any light shed upon it by either the business itself or the fringe media that so loyally cover the shows and chase the rumors. See, the wrestling world portrays itself very self-importantly. The media swarming the press conferences, the jam-packed arenas, the jet-setting playboys (and girls) who fill the ring once or twice a week to put on a show, the pyrotechnics, the music…it’s all meant to overwhelm you, the spectacle in itself so grandiose and epic in its proportions that you can’t help but get sucked in. I won’t lie…there have been times when I’ve walked onto the ramp and the crowds have gone crazy and I did feel like, for a moment, I was the king of all that I could see. But it’s back to this image, this image of professional wrestling as so important to the world… …God, how do I explain this? Let’s try brevity: There are tons of people out there who don’t give a shit about wrestling. They don’t care about Angelo Deville or Karina Wolfenden or Boda or Killean Sirrajin…to these people, wrestling will always be “wrasslin’,” and its fan-base will always consist of fat, hideously disfigured red-necks. These are the people you never see…anywhere. The foggy, deceptive lens of the camera filters them out…they aren’t welcome in the tiny PRIME-iocentric galaxy where all that is of importance revolves around the sun of a company Sebastian Starr created in 2003. It’s that self-importance thing. We have to protect ourselves from them, lest they wound us worse than any chair-shot or suplex…by discrediting the profession to which we dedicate our souls and our very bodies. We bleed, and maim, and die for this shit…so, it stands to reason that we don’t want people raining on our parade. Unfortunately for me, the forecast for March 31, 2005 predicted 100% chance of fucking thunderstorms. “Why don’t you want to go?” Ariel asked Nova from the bedroom where she was pulling the straps of a black dress over her shoulders. “I dunno,” Nova replied with a shrug from the bathroom where, in addition to taking the Browns to the Superbowl, he was trying to finish a story in the March edition of Reader’s Digest where a young deaf boy stricken with tuberculosis had to find his way out of a canyon with only the help of a blind, three-legged goat, and a day’s worth of Chef Boyardee Ravioli. The extra cheesy kind. “I just feel like I wouldn’t belong there…they’re all your friends anyway, and lawyers, to boot. I just don’t see myself being a heavyweight in the conversation department.” “But I told them all you were coming,” Ariel replied disappointedly, “please? I haven’t seen these guys in a while, and I would really feel more comfortable if you were with me…and they all really want to meet you.” “I bet,” Nova offered sarcastically with a roll of his eyes. “They do!” Ariel protested, “You don’t want people automatically passing a judgment on you because you’re a wrestler, so don’t do the same to them just because they’re a group of my friends from law school.” “I’m not passing judgment, Ariel,” Nova responded coolly, “only saying…” He paused for a moment to collect his thoughts and deliver a devastating oral retort that would leave his wife’s hair blown back and the logic of her argument in shards scattered across the fluffy green carpet, but could retaliate with nothing more potent than, “only saying it might be weird.” “Get dressed,” Ariel commanded, and that was the end of that. Sitting in the front passenger seat of the car, Nova couldn’t shirk an ominous feeling that was slowly creeping its way into his head about this little outing of his wife’s design. The idea to have dinner with her old law school pals first struck Ariel when one of them, a man named Peter Dunhill, had called her out of the blue to see how things were going. Nova had happily prejudiced himself towards the man from the moment he picked up the phone and heard Mr. Dunhill’s voice. Nova fired out the usual “‘ello’?” and was met with “Yes, is Ariel available?” “May I ask who’s calling?” It was the way the guy said his name that really pushed Nova’s buttons, for some reason. “Yes, this is PEE-ter DUN-hill…I’m an old friend of Ari’s.” He said it just like that…PEE-ter DUN-hill, not so much stretching out the first syllable of each part of his name as simply pounding them in authoritatively, as if, though they had never met in person, his name should carry some amount of weight with Nova. “Right. I’ll go get her.” Ariel stayed on the phone with PEE-ter for what felt an eternity. Occasionally, the sounds of excited yelling or laughter would echo out of their bedroom upstairs, where Ariel took the cordless to talk in private and not annoy Nova with only half of a conversation. The end result of their expensive long-distance reunion was that “the blanket-busters,” as Ariel called her group of friends, were to convene at the spring residence of Amy Durant (another of the “blanket-busters”) in upscale Lansing, about an hour and a half away. “Why do you call yourselves the ‘blanket-busters?’” Nova had asked when he first heard his wife use the term. “It’s just a thing from school,” Ariel explained, “the beginning of our second year there, Amy helped break a case where a blanket search warrant-that’s where a search warrant authorizes…” “The search of more than one area,” Nova finished for her, “yeah, I know what a blanket search warrant is.” Unlike his wife, Nova hadn’t learned about blanket search warrants from law school, but rather, from his illegal activities as a young man. He and his friends had to study up on the codes and regulations so that the cops couldn’t push them around or get them to consent to anything technically illegal. “So Amy helped break a case where a blanket search warrant was implemented illegally, and it was big news around the school,” Ariel continued, “so after that, everyone started calling us the ‘blanket-busters’.” Nova thought the name was stupid, but said nothing. Given the company he kept, Ariel had a more-than-full arsenal of silly names to retort with should he ever demean the chosen moniker of her college crew. The drive to Lansing was uneventful enough…but Nova definitely knew when they were getting close. The corner stores, apartment complexes, and general buzz of city life gave way almost instantly to expansive lawns and the quiet serenity of wealth. Houses sporadically dotted both sides of the recently-paved road, each given plenty room between its nearest neighbors on both sides…as if less than half an acre on each side would leave the families in question scandalously close to one another. “Let’s see,” Ariel peered at the signs naming “lanes” and “circles” that split off from the main road, “Abbey Circle is what Amy told me to look for…ah! Now, the third house on the right…and we’re here!” Ariel bumped the Altima up onto the steep driveway and pulled in behind a black Lexus. The house itself was massive, a testament to the artistic possibilities of light-gray brick meshed with white wooden porches that jutted out in a strange pseudo-symmetry from different parts of the house and windows that bespoke a lavishly decorated interior. “Jesus,” Nova whispered, awe-struck, “and you say this is her ‘spring home’?” “Amy has done very well for herself,” Ariel replies politely. “I’ll say!” Nova agrees emphatically. Nova could see a woman standing on the front porch waving to them. Ariel returned the gesture excitedly and they began walking toward the front of the house. On the porch, the woman (Ariel greeted her as Amy) embraced Nova’s wife and the two of them huddled together, talking quickly and quietly. Amy looked over at Nova and amiably shook his hand, touching her own left to his left arm, before leading them both inside, asking the get-to-know-ya questions like how was the drive? Oh, yeah? Not too bad, huh? Strange…its, uh…ya know, sometimes the traffic is real bad…this time…of day. Except it wasn’t awkward. Amy was a nice woman, and Nova liked her off the bat, her casual demeanor elegantly underscoring the fortuitous wealth she had accumulated for herself in only a few years. Nova felt himself letting his guard down as he strolled through the hallway bedecked with photos of friends and family. Nova’s eyes caught on one with Amy and Ariel that couldn’t be less than five or six years old. He felt his heart stir as he looked on a happier woman, without the worries she had accumulated, exponentially, on almost solely his behalf. As soon as Nova turned the corner behind Amy and his wife, and stepped out into the spacious living room, he instantly felt the fool for getting so caught up in the “How’s your father?” that he forgot not to look like an idiot. All eyes trained on the new arrivals, and Nova felt a tidal wave of relief surge through his body as everyone got to clamor around Ariel, their whack-job friend who completed the law program at NYU with flying colors and then ran off with a wrassler. Then it began, slowly at first. “Well, hello!” a clean-cut man with short black hair said to Nova, extending his hand out. “Oh, everyone! This is my husband, Chris!” Ariel shouted over the din. Nova had to give her credit for the enthusiasm she managed to muster. With that announcement, Nova became the center of attention. Strangely, though Nova had traveled to other continents and made his living standing in front of thousands of people with whom he shared no personal connection, but now, in the living room of an old friend of his wife, surrounded by people who had made a living out of making a living, he felt strangely shy. Ariel introduced him…the first man to shake his hand was Gordon McDermott, the slightly balding man next to him was the infamous Peter Dunhill (the most wealthy of all the gang, according to Ariel), and behind Peter was Charles Liddy. The women in the room’s names were Sandra Liddy (the only inter-group coupling that had ever amounted to anything, Ariel told him), Amy Durant (who Nova had already met), and Fara Sicore. The names floated in one ear and out the other. Nova could remember none of them. “So who’s ready to eat something?” Amy offered, and the proposal was met with unanimous consent, as well as a sigh of relief from Nova. Finally he could gain his bearings. Seated around a large mahogany dinner table set with gold inlays, Nova could feel the brewing of a grill session. The women were all nice enough…very relaxed, and their relaxation would have infected Nova…if it weren’t for that damn Peter Dunhill. Nova still didn’t like him. He felt like Dunhill was fucking with him. And McDermott, too, though he almost felt more like Dunhill’s crony. Liddy was a good guy. “So what do you do for a living, Mr. Vega?” Dunhill asked as Amy and Charles carried in trays of food. There was a moment of uncomfortable silence. They had all heard what they each at first prayed to be rumors…Ariel had fallen out of favor with influential members of her family, and to make matters worse, she had taken up with a professional wrestler. Nova knew they knew, and he knew that Dunhill schmuck only brought it up to hopefully get some entertainment out of it… …some diplomatic entertainment. But if it was a game Dunhill was after, Nova would play ball. Batter up. “I’m a professional wrestler.” On the first pitch, Nova sends it toward the short stop…will they be able to field it? “A wrestler, eh? Wow…that must be…interesting.” They fielded it. One out. Dunhill was carefully trained, of course. Years spent manipulating the pitches and tones of his voice like a clarinet in the courtroom payed off in little situations like these. Dunhill was careful not to add the sarcastic tone into his voice…probably so he could deny guilt later on when the girls and maybe Charles would chastise him for the remarks. Bring another one up to the plate. “Yeah, I’ve never regretted it. I’ll have all the stories I need to keep the grandkids up past their bedtime for years.” BAM! He had clocked one deep into left center with that one. Everyone at the table but Poopy-Pants Dunhill was laughing. Would his outfielders be ready to catch it? “Yeah…heh…I bet.” Weak sauce. Nova got a man on base with that one, easy. That’s right, cock-sucker. Who’s got your witticisms, biotch? Nova couldn’t help but smile triumphantly. Dunhill the Learned was not to be deterred, however, and the catcher called for the curveball. “Ari and all of us are friends largely in part due to NYU. Where did you go to school, Mr. Vega?” “Peter,” Sandra hissed quietly. “What? It’s just a question, Sandy,” Peter replied with a shrug, “we’re just having a conversation.” Oooh, this mother-fucker knew where to pull his punches…but Nova knew his wife was crossing her fingers and praying to leave at the end of the day without incident, so he decided he would kill this shit-don’t-stink high-baller with kindness. “You can call me Chris…and I never had a formal post-secondary education.” Graceful. A clean hit getting his man on first to second and the batter to first. The other team was getting antsy. The catcher called for the change-up. “A self-made man…I can understand that.” Switching tactics. Interesting. Here Nova saw the chance to put Dunhizzle on the defensive. “Can you?” Very quiet, very passive, but still there, and everyone at the table heard it. Dunhill remained quiet for a minute or so. Nova had loaded the bases, and he knew it. He knew Nova knew that knew it. Nova knew that he knew that Nova knew that he knew it. Dunhill looked about ready to say something, when Amy offered to show Ariel some photo albums. Sandra invited herself along… …and that left Herr Dunhill, Chuck, and the McDermott character who spent most of his time giggling at anything and everything that came out of Dunhill’s mouth. If he hadn’t seen their wedding rings, he would’ve figured they were into each other…not that Nova cared, either way. “Care to go out on the back patio, Chris?” Charles asked, and Nova grinned and nodded his assent. Unfortunately, the Brothers Asshole decided to follow suit, and they all adjourned to the circular table on the back porch, where Charles, Dunhill, and Nova lit up cigarettes. Dunhill took a drag from his and stared at Nova for a moment. “So…Chris…who do you, um, fight out in the…ring, is it? Killer mutants, or Communists, or…” “I’m not an idiot,” Nova interrupted him stiffly, “and I’m not gonna embarrass my wife at her friend’s party.” “Well, I suppose…,” Dunhill began, but Charles cut him off. “Tell me one of your stories, Chris.” Dunhill looked as though he might try to re-insert himself, but Charles kept the questions about Nova’s profession coming at a rapid-fire pace, soaking in with genuine interest everything Nova had to tell him… …and so the next two hours passed, Nova telling Charles stories and Dunhill sitting in silence, brooding. His sidekick had gone inside a while earlier to keep company with the girls. “That’s where he hit me,” Nova said, pointing down the length of an enormous pink scar as well as other places where various metals or plastics had been inserted to replace what could not be stimulated to regrow. “My God,” Charles replied, aghast, “with a piece of brick tied to a baseball bat?” “It sucked,” Nova agreed with a nod, “worst. Rehab. Ever.” It was then that Ariel poked her head out onto the porch. “Chris? It’s time to go, honey.” They all filed back into the house and began exchanging good-byes. Nova was shaking Charles’ hand when a voice only slightly louder than the others drew his attention. “Chris!” The catcher was calling for the heater, even though Nova had the bases loaded. Nova turned around to look Dunhill in the face. “Chris, I truly had a wonderful time…are could I call you Nova? Yeah, I’d like that…or maybe Magnum Pee-” Nova didn’t mean to hit him as hard as he did, and he almost flinched as soon as he felt his fist deflect off of Dunhill’s chin…almost. Home run. Grand-slam. The crowd goes wild. Take me out to the mother-fuckin’ ball game. Dunhill hit the hardwood floor like a sack of bricks. He lay there, moaning and summoning only the strength to lift an arm to shield his face. Everyone froze, all eyes eventually making their way over to where Nova stood. He raised his eyes and met their gazes unafraid, unapologetic, before nodding, turning, and walking down the hallway and out the front door. Standing by the car, lighting a cigarette, he looked down at the ground…and smiled. … I guess I should have something to say, right? I mean, this is it…the final chapter of the story that has been my life for the last eight months. I’ve fought through all the bullshit and come this far to see the person responsible for what happened to my family punished, even if it means destroying someone who was once my best friend, my closest confidant, my brother. I derive a certain satisfaction in extracting the meaning from Pay-Per-View titles, and I feel this is no exception: Ultra Violence…more than just hook-line, it represents the final conclusion, the final solution (Nazi slogan notwithstanding) to my quest…ultra violence. Everything left to be settled, settled in a wrestling ring where two men beat the holy hell from one another until a winner is declared and a resolution is reached. I face a man who once inspired respect in the wrestling community, and who valued competition and sportsmanship second only to his friends, and whose legacy was destined to be heaped at the top of the pile with the greats. Instead, he’ll stand across the ring from me a husk, devoid of any of the qualities I once admired so much and aspired to attain for myself. And I’ll stand across the ring from him renewed in my determination to append to every punch, every slam, every elbow drop or diving clothesline, six months of personal anguish that you caused me and mine, and when I stop swinging, I will have washed my hands of him, in his own blood, and he and I will be finished forever. “Now the time has come, time!There are things to realize, time! Time has come today, time!Time has come today, time!” - “Time Has Come Today,” The Chambers Brothers
