The Regulator (Dual Halo - 2009)

4 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 4th, 2009 Late September, 2008 Somewhere in Arizona Awkward, the buzzing of a lone fly among miles and miles and miles of silence. No traffic to crunch the dirt and gravel underneath tires. No wind to shift the frame of the house. No movement from the son in his chair to creak the floorboards. And nothing at all from the dead mother in her bed. Only the fly. She was frozen in pain. Her rotten body was stiff, limbs locked in the last moments of life, joints twisted violently to fend off the Reaper. Her right hand curled upward, middle and index finger clinging tightly to a cigarette butt that had long since burned out. Blue eyes stared at the ceiling, wide in terror, while her jaw set her mouth in an eternal, silent scream. The fly flew close, but never to land. He sat on the other side of the dingy room, on a modest wooden chair, just as lifeless as the dead woman in her bed. Like hers, his piercing blue eyes were open wide. Roughed-up hands on dirty jean-clad thighs. Work-worn boots sat flat against the floor, and a drifter's hat rested on his knee. When the human body experiences the emotions of despair, anger, confusion, hatred, fear, remorse, contempt, disgust, relief, neglect, and love all at once, it will do one of two things; It will break. *Rushing into the man's left eye reveals himself inside the room, eyes burning with destruction as he ravages everything in sight; boots through drywall, fists through glass, hurling chairs into splinters and overturning dressers.* Or... *Rewind.* ...it will shut down. And for Wade Allen Elliott, statuesque in his chair, he did just that. * * * * * * * Northern Arizona He couldn't even see where he was going. He couldn't see the trees along the highway headed north. Or the passing traffic. Or the rising sun over the horizon. He couldn't hear the rumble of his black Chevrolet, nor the concerned whines of his canine companion, Angus. Only the memories, flooding back. * * * * * * * Pine Ridge, Alabama 1988 "Wade Allen Elliott, would ya screw yer damn head on straight fer once? Her sharp, yet elegant voice trailed after his stiff, determined frame as he stormed out the front door, screen door slamming behind them. "Made up my mind, Ma! Git used to it!" "And where ya gonna go?" she asked, strands of beautiful brunette hair whipping in the wind. "Wherever the hell I feel!" he replied, tossing a ratty knapsack into the bed of his red Ford Ranger. "With what? Yer good looks?" she snapped, planting her hands on jean-covered hips. "I'll figure it out," he muttered, pulling a red John Deere hat over his tussled brown hair. She rubbed the bridge of her nose as he swung the driver's side door open, hopping inside and firing the engine. He popped a Marlboro red into his mouth as his mother stepped to the driver's side window. "Yer no better'n your Pa, Wade," she said, eyes gleaming with the sudden onslaught of tears. "Chip off the ol' fuckin' block I guess." She stepped away, one hand wrapped around her waist and one over her mouth as Wade put the rig into gear. The tires spun, kicking up dust and spitting it into the hot, dry Alabama air. She watched him roar off down the road, leaving her and the run-down trailer behind. She lifted her head, fighting the tears as she set a cigarette of her own between her lips, lighting it with a cheap Bic lighter and breathing in deep. "Just like his Pa..." * * * * * * * Nevada His heavy stare shifted suddenly, drawing across an elaborate sign reading "Welcome to Nevada!" His trance momentarily deterred, his keen blue eyes fell across the orange and reds of the early morning sky. Until he passed another sign. "Las Vegas, Nevada, 96 miles." And again, the memories came racing. * * * * * * * Las Vegas, Nevada 2007 "What's yer poison, stranger?" asked the bartender, in a voice not unlike Wade's, though less deep. "From the look of ya, I'd guess the same yers," replied Wade. The bartender smiled, eyes focused on cleaning his cup. "Bourbon it is!" The bartender happily grabbed a bottle of Jim Beam, pouring a tumbler and sliding it into Wade's rough hand. He looked into the eyes of the new fellow, then stopped, squinting a bit. "Well now," he said, studying Wade. "Somethin' familiar 'bout you..." Wade's sharp blue orbs bore a hole through him. It clicked. The bartender's eyes widened. "Shitta-god-damn..." he whispered. "Evenin', Pa," said Wade. Wade grabbed a nearby beer bottle and smashed it against his father's head, covering his face with blood and staining his gray hair. Wade gripped him by his collar, broken bottle pointed at his throat. Many of the patrons stood ready to fight, but stopped to see the predicament of their bartender. A few fled. "Coulda...'xpected a hug from my long lost boy..." said Wade's father, groggily. "SHUT YER FUCKIN' TRAP, YOU SONNOVA BITCH!" roared Elliott, arm taught, ready to slice into his father's neck. "Rrrghh...whaddya want from me, son?" asked Wade's father, blood flowing down his face. "I want the fuckin' thirty-five years of fuckin' confusion n' not knowing where the fuck I come from back! I want Ma to have the eighteen shitty fuckin' years raisin' me without nobody to help 'er back! But most of all I want some fuckin' answers before I open you up!" In the insanity of it all, Wade's Dad managed to chuckle. "Oh, fer fuck's sake, son, whatta you wanna hear? That I ran off when I was eighteen after gettin' some broad pregnant..." Wade pushed the bottle into his dad's throat, dangerously close to penetrating. "Another word 'bout my mother n' I'll do worse than kill ya." "Well Christ, boy! I was eighteen fuckin' years old! Weren't ready to raise no kid! You woulda done the same!" "HORSE-SHIT!" "No it ain't! Don't fuckin' kid yerself!" Wade breathed heavily through his nose, eyes fixed on his father's. "Ya come walkin' in here, all piss n' vinegar like yer the only asshole who grew up without a Pa, an' fer what? To kill me? That gonna git ya yer thirty-somethin' years back? That gonna help ya sleep better? Don't bullshit yerself, boy." "Sure looks like I picked a bad time to get thirsty," came an angelic voice. Wade turned to see Lindsay Troy in the doorway, as did the rest of the bar. "Get the fuck outta here, Troy! This ain't yer fight!" "Wade, you made me wait in the car and didn't even give me one of those Mad-Libs books or hand-held Texas Hold 'Em games to keep me occupied. I got bored. " "Shoulda stayed bored!" "Shit boy! Lookit that pretty lady ya roped up!" "Fuck off, y'piece of shit!" Wade smashed his fist into his father's nose, splattering blood all over and sending his head back, only to swing forward, thanks to Wade's unrelenting grip. "Give me one good god-damn reason not to kill you where you stand," he asked, low and cold. "Because, boy, whether ye wanna hear it or not, yer just like me," was his father's bloody-mouthed reply. Wade could only stare at him. "He's nothing like you." * * * * * * * Las Vegas, Nevada Late September, 2008 Snake Eyes Tavern The thin, bony hand of Earl Davis wiped the puddles of cheap whiskey and cheaper beer from the top of his bar. His long, wiry hair and mustache hung low, his eyes heavy with exhaustion. Another night finished, another handful of patrons properly intoxicated. Time to lock up. Almost. His gaze lifted at the sound of his front door creaking open. He placed his rag on the edge of a sink as two heavy thuds brought a large-framed silhouette of a man into the tavern. "We're closed," said Earl, his haggard voice spitting quickly and harshly, head turning downward to return whiskey glasses their shelf. "Evenin', Pa." Earl stopped, eyes wide. He stood frozen and hunched for a moment before closing those eyes and slowly standing up straight. A pause. "Come to finish me off, boy?" No reply. Only heavy stomps of steel-toed boots against creaky floorboards. Earl inhaled deeply through his nostrils and tilted his chin upward, proud. "Then come on an' git it over with." The silhouette stepped forward and into the light, revealing the face of Earl's son, Wade. It was their eyes. Damn near identical. That piercing blue that was impossible to turn away from. The world stopped turning, frozen in place as father and son stared into one another's very soul. Not a blink. Not a breath. Not a heartbeat or brainwave or contraction of a muscle for minutes upon minutes upon minutes. Until Wade's lips curled inward. "Ma's dead," he said, quiet. Dream with the feathers of angels stuffed beneath your head. The Regulator's, swinging pendulum. The proud, rigid expression of Earl Davis slowly melted to a stone cold visage of emptiness. His eyes released from Wade's hammering gaze to stare past his son and into nothing. Wade stood a moment longer, then turned, steadily thumping across the bar and out the door without another word. Come with me and walk the longest mile. Anyone who might have seen Earl at this moment might have believed him to be dead on his feet. He was a standing corpse, hands planted on his bar, eyes staring forever forward into void, two words tumbling through his head over and over. "Ma's dead." When the human body experiences the emotions of despair, confusion, pain, remorse, regret, anger, hatred, and love all at once, it will do one of two things; It will shut down. *Rushing into the man's right eye reveals himself in front of a gravestone, though his hollow stare travels straight ahead. He is alone, his surroundings a barren wasteland of black and gray.* Dream with the feathers of angels stuffed beneath your head. Or... *Rewind.* Earl reached under the counter, retrieving an old, black handled .357 magnum revolver. He methodically cocked the hammer back and lifted the barrel to the side of his head. The Regulator's, swinging pendulum. ...it will break. Bang. Come with me and walk the longest mile... Excerpts taken from Asa Fountain and [name withheld]'s "A Boy Named Sue."
