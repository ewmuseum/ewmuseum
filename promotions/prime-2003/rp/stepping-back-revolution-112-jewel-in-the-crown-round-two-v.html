Stepping Back (ReVolution 112 - Jewel in the Crown: Round Two v. Jonathon Winters)

Date: Timeless “You’re a father.” They were the sweetest words he never heard. He didn’t even get the stark truth of “I aborted our baby.” Instead he found out from one of the just-above-scumbags he’d hired to keep her under constant surveillance, muttered to him between bouts of sighing and gesticulation. Angry and disgusted, but shared out – understanding to the very core. ~><>…<><~ Rich Rollins once told me – albeit in more flattering words – that I was like a father to him. He looked up to me, aspired to be like me, thought it an honour to trace my steps, to overwhelm my footprints. In turn, I admired Rich Rollins. Not because of those words, and not just because I saw myself in him, but because even with his flaws he was everything that was good about me. For the longest time he sought my approval. He would fly six hours just to run an idea by me in person, to tell me excitedly about his plans, cataloguing my every hint of a reaction. He would ask me about mine, his grin proving beforehand he knew I wouldn’t tell him. Not all of them, at least, nor even the half. What I did share with him he would fawn over, never cruelly to mislead, but simply because he saw things as they were not, through a tint he adjusted as needed. When I agreed to stand by his side in the Universal Wrestling Federation he shook my hand and thanked me. It wasn’t just a gesture. He saw my help as a testament to our respect, our friendship, our undying union, and he appreciated it. A little time can change a lot of things. Last year, after defeating me in his dream match, he stole an award of mine, marking it clearly an act of disgust, the manifestation of disrespect. His contempt eventually saw my trophy melted down to a set of brass knuckles . . . which I’m pretty sure he threatened to brand into my forehead if I ever showed my face again. ~><>…<><~ Angelo could never face Karina after that, after learning how she’d flipped out over the positive pregnancy test, immediately subjecting herself to a torture-rack’s worth of skateboarding. In truth, that was any amount of skateboarding to Deville, but she’d actually thrown herself down stairs and slammed herself into walls, trying to force her body to reject his seed. It worked. More serious thought was put into killing her than anyone else before. Until he figured out why she did it, he was certain it was the only sensible option. Even with the knowledge he still considered it. Strongly. She spent countless afternoons and nights trying to track him down after he vanished, maybe out of guilt, likely out of revenge for vanishing. Her inquiries went unanswered, as she always knew they would. He had abandoned her, as her instinct had rightly told her he would. ~><>…<><~ The crowd was bored, but it wasn’t the fault of the gladiator in the ring; Spartan had nothing to do with it. He’d been told a few days ahead of time that his opponent, Angelo Deville, was gone without a word, and everyone in attendance heard the rest, that he was moving on by forfeit. All that remained was the formality of it, and decked out in street clothes, likely sleepy behind his mask, Spartan didn’t seem ready for much more than that. Sympathy for the Devil by The Rolling Stones The silent crowd was clueless to it, but The Deville had something else in mind. He ignorantly thought it a great ruse, a scam merely run amok from lack of forethought. If nothing else, he knew he could slither his way free of it, knew he could come out unscathed with shiny scales on the other side. He sauntered out onto the stage, a lordly smirk with a body attached. He felt the hatred and played to it, fuelled it, urged it to rage as he always had. He waited for the tugboat-sound. He ached to play the game they all knew so well. Silence echoed, pulsing in the stillness. Someone coughed. “Yeah, fuck you too, pal!” The first dissenting voice, from deep in the bleachers but heard with crystal clarity, was soon joined by others, and then a cacophony. The swagger became a stagger, nearly planting him flat on his face. This wasn’t the spirited jousting of markdom. This was the impassioned rejection of those who knew too much, of those who knew the truth. He’d feigned offence, turning his back, and when he peeked to see the effect, found his bluff most brutally called. Everywhere his eyes fell a sea of backs was shrugging. They would look and be blinded no more. In the blink of an eye he’d become a mockery of himself, and he knew perfectly well – as they did – the how and why. The only redeemable quality left to him – honour – had been seared out by petty schemes and false pride. Abort. Honestly, looking back, that had likely been just another charade all along. Now that it was gone they despised him, for real, with reason. Abort. A deadly truth tempered by lies. Abort! Unable to face it, he fled.
