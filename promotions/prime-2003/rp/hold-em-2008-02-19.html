Hold 'Em

19 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-19-08 No rain, but many dark clouds. Easton had given up looking at the sky. His clothes hung against his body as if sodden as he emerged from the truckstop bathroom, working his belt closed, slipping it through the loops. A swirling breath of lifting winter cooled the air and swam through his bones. There was a lack on this stretch of highway, near to nothing on either side of the road but the ground, no cars coming or going. Marja, stout and tousled-blonde, without a hat but dressed from head to toe in denim, stood near the tow truck, jabbering with a taller man. A smile touched her unpainted lips. She caught Easton from the corner of her eye. "You wanna gimme a minute?" Marja hollered, turning lazily towards Easton. "Got some things to discuss." Easton let his eyes slide lazily over to her companion, noting that gut, noting the scuffle that crawled over his face. She drove the tow-truck, she made the rules. He shrugged his shoulders, remembering he only needed to be at the arena tomorrow. "Thanks, hun," Marja said, giving him a small flick of her fingers and sinking back against the metal of her truck. Easton's hands pushed their way into his pockets and he began to tramp over the rugged ground towards the longhouse-style diner. White paint peeled and a sign propped on the flat roof promised eats in dim capitals. Easton didn't want eats, but he went inside anyway. The door was light and swung breezily behind him as he stepped in the diner. His feet sent little shivers through the tile. A woman looked up at him from the counter, her hair shot through with gray and her face shot through with wrinkles, her whole being tied back to mock youth. Easton dipped his head, half a greeting, and slunk off towards a table. The leather imitation on the seats was peeling off, maroon giving way to whitish stuffing, and the tables were gouged through to the wood beneath. He forced himself into the seat and on his heels was a young man, thinner than he and without muscles, his hair black and straight behind his head. When the man turned around to face him, Easton took note of his beak of a nose and his blue-eyed smile. There was no pretense of wealth or station; "Hey there, friend," the man said, inviting himself to sit across from Easton. Easton didn't raise an eyebrow or set his forearms on the table. His hair, clumped and dirty, swung in vines over his face. The man appraised Easton again, thinking to rise on account of his face, but deciding to stay by looking at his black leather jacket. "Ah, what brings you here? Ain't from Yolen, are ya?" "Nah." The man smiled, leaning forward and placing his elbows on the table, his left hand slightly clenched. His hair seem to swing forward, hood-like around his face. "So?" "Towed. Car blew a tire." The man laughed and shook his head. "Damn shame. Well, I'm Lee." The clicking of heel on tile grew into being and then stopped. Easton followed a sudden sharp look from Lee and saw the wrinkle-marred waitress from behind the counter standing almost at Easton's shoulder. He did not look at her. She took a step back, and when Lee's eyes didn't pull back, she turned and walked away. Lee flashed Easton a smile and lifted his hips a bit, reaching behind his back. "I was wondering," Lee said, bringing his left hand back up and slapping it on the table. He turned it to the side, opening his palm to the fluorescent lights. "If you wanted to throw dice." The dice showed three and two. Easton looked from them to Lee. "I don't gamble." "Come on, uhh... you got a name?" "I don't give it out like that." Lee smiled, hiding his lips under his hand for a second and shaking his head. "Alright then, Clint. I know you ain't got shit else to do. Test your luck." Easton sighed, tasting his beard, looking down at the dice again. "You don't want to listen." Lee glanced off to the side and shifted his hips again, swinging his right hand behind his back. "I heard you," Lee said, nodding. "But no, I didn't listen. I'm just, you know, trying to offer you a friendly game of dice. I hear the game started up in Bristol." Easton didn't start when he felt the depression against his knee. He didn't let his eyes waver. "You spell Bristol with a P?" "You're catching on." He could probably hobble to the ring if he had to, if it was a grazing shot, but this was point blank, and it'd probably blow his kneecap right to shit. "How do we do this?" Lee used his left hand to scoop up the dice, clattering them against each other in his hand. "We dice a little bit. Then you pull out your wallet, empty it out, and gimme the cash." Easton nodded. He wondered what Jeanne would say when he got back to New York. She'd probably be half-glad that his career was over. Not that he would let it be over necessarily. "You gonna roll 'em?" Lee laughed and tossed the dice, letting them tumble over and come up ones. Lee's eyes lost their wine and revelry. "Don't you dare laugh." Easton picked up the dice neatly, twisted his hand over and began to shake the dice. "Wasn't going to." He tossed the dice, three and four. She would probably kiss it. She was that kind of woman. "You want a coffee, Lee?" called the waitress, back behind the counter. "No thanks, Eez." "What about your friend?" Lee looked at Easton and Easton looked at the waitress. "I'm alright." The hard press of the pistol hadn't been necessary. Lee picked up the dice and rolled, two and one. Lee's face screwed up. Easton plucked the dice from the table. A cane wouldn't be so bad. It'd look dignified. Easton tossed a six and a one. He'd be out of the running for the 5-Star belt for sure, but that was never all that important. He'd done enough, hadn't he? Lee made himself laugh as he picked the dice back up in his awkward hand. "God, you got some kinda luck," Lee said, shaking his head, jamming the pistol forward, clacking the dice. "Where you from? Vegas?" "North." "New York?" "Just roll." Lee just rolled, and he rolled two sixes. Lee's eyes dipped down to the dice and rose again. "Guy like you gets this kinda luck and he doesn't stick around long. You got a trick?" Easton shook his head, picked up the dice, and rolled a six and a five. "I don't talk so much." Talking to PRIME management would be a bitch, though. At least they'd let him off easy. He wasn't famous enough to make more appearances. Lee snarled and picked up the dice again, but he didn't shake them. "Take out your wallet. Tired of fuckin' talking to you." Easton eased up the seat, dipping his hand into his back pocket. Lee watched him closely as he pulled out his brown leather wallet and flipped it open. Out came five twenties and some odd ones, all uncounted. He tossed them nonchalantly on the table. Lee used his awkward and to pull the bills back towards him. "Pleasure doing business." Lee pushed his hand into his pocket. The flimsy door swung open and Marja stepped into the diner, big and blonde as before, her hair a little more off-kilter. The tall man she had been with before wasn't around. Her eyes found the two of them. "Lee." Lee lifted an eyebrow, sinking back against his chair. "What's up, Marja?" "Not much, Lee." She watched Easton push his wallet into his back pocket. "I'm ready to go if you are." "Yeah, I'm ready." Easton scooted sideways out of the seat and straightened out his jacket. He didn't look back at Lee when he made his way out of the diner. No use. Marja's steps were heavy as she walked towards her rig. Easton's head was inclined, hair hanging over his face, shoulders hunched. "He rob you?" "He did." "That's Lee's gimmick." Marja turned around to face him when she reached her truck. "Why'd you let him? You coulda stopped him." Easton shrugged his shoulders. "What for?"
