What we fight for....

26 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/26/05 It was so good to see Janina. I have very few people in this world who have ever cared about me, and no one has ever done as much for me as she has. Without her I would not be where I am today, and I may not even be on this earth. These are the thoughts that are running through Walczak’s head as he walks down the street to get back to his apartment. These are words that mean a lot in the life of Tom Walczak. He has not opened up to many people in his life, and thus never truly felt feelings for anyone. Janina was one of the few and possibly the only person in Walczak’s life that has seen Walczak’s emotions. He never had a mother when he was growing up, but Janina provided him with that in the later part of his life. Without her he would have no guidance whatsoever. He would have continued to roam the streets, and his life probably would have ended on them long ago. I didn’t realize how much of my life was shaped by this lady. Even though I wanted to compete in that fighting tournament I would have not went if she was 100% against it. Her approval put me in my profession, and while I know she doesn’t always approve of what I do I know that she still supports me. She gives me the strength to keep going when I really don’t see a point anymore, and before her that was something that I never had. Walczak takes out his keys as he reaches his apartment building. He heads towards to the elevator, and heads up to the fifth floor to his three bedroom apartment. He gets inside, takes off his hoodie, and sits down in his chair with his eyes closed. Without her I would not be sitting here. I would not be in PRIME, and if I was fighting I would have nothing to fight for. She is the reason I am in this business. A man can have all the abilities in the world, but if he has nothing to fight for than he is not going to win. I remember my first fighting experience, and how it wasn’t any of my physical abilities that helped me win, but it was her…. Walczak starts to remember that day when he first fought. It was held in a dingy part of town, and the venue wasn’t anything to brag about. There weren’t many people there, but most of them were celebrities, and to add to the peculiar setting there were many television cameras there. There were also some fighters there from higher ranks hoping to get a look at some of their future competition. Walczak had breezed through his first round competition, knocking his opponent out in under a minute. However, there were many good fighters there and there was a good chance that Walczak’s size alone was not going to carry him to a victory in the tournament. …Man I can’t believe that my first win was so easy. I thought that there would be more suitable competition here. That guy was scared before he came into the ring, and it was over quick. Man I’m good. Walczak seems to be getting a little bit cocky, but in some sense this confidence may be good for him. He is by far the youngest competitor in this tournament, and he also has the least experience. This confidence may be able to carry him for the next couple of rounds. Suddenly one of the other competitors comes over to Walczak.“Yo.” Walczak looks up at the man. The competitor is a rather large man standing around 6’3” and is solidly built. He seems to be a well-kept man, and he has all of the best fighting equipment. This is much different than the hand-me downs that Walczak is wearing. After taking a quick glance at the man Walczak puts his head down and stays in his zone. “Hey! Look at me when I’m talking to you.” Walczak did not mean to be disrespectful to the man, it is just that he has not yet fully grasped the English language, and does not like speaking it. “No speak English.” Those are the only words that Walczak manages to mutter and they come out very broken.“Well, just my luck I got a big dumb foreigner trying to come in here and win my tournament.” The man starts to laugh and so do a number of people in the surrounding area, but Walczak has no idea that he is the subject of their laughter. He just closes his eyes and pays no attention to the man. “What’s the point you’re not even worth it.” The man begins to walk away, but is stopped by a teenage boy. “Mr. Charles can I your autograph?” “Sure kid.” When Walczak hears that name he now realizes who the man was. It was Christopher Charles, a former Heavyweight Boxing Champion, who has decided to use this as a tune-up to launch his new fighting career. Walczak had seen this on the newspaper, and had seen the man fight before, but he didn’t put it all together until right now. Now he knew why there had been so many cameras and celebrities. However, it would have been better if this information was never revealed to Walczak. He had confidence inside of him before this, but now all was lost. I didn’t think that he was actually going to fight at this tournament. I thought he was just promoting his new career, and I thought it was going to be all for publicity. How can I possibly beat a former champ? Now granted the man was over 40 now and had slowed down from his peak years nearly eight years ago, but the fact was he was the man to beat. He had more fighting experience then anyone in the tournament, and he knew he could win it, and this thought kept running through Walczak’s head. There is no reason to worry I can beat this guy. Yeah, that’s the attitude I should have. Nothing is impossible one lucky punch might be all I need. The fact is Walczak remained worried. He could no longer close his eyes and go into the relaxed state he was once able to attain. His eyes wandered around the room looking at the competition seeking out who might give him a problem, and then he heard the loudspeaker… “Walczak, Ramirez please make your way to the ring.” Walczak hears his name and slowly makes his way to the ring. As he takes off his sweats and loosens up he eagerly awaits his opponent. Finally a man standing near six feet tall makes his way to the ring. The man has an athletic build, but is not that intimidating. Both men make their way towards the center of the ring where the referee is waiting. “Alright guys, you know the rules I want a clean fight, and if I say the match is over than it’s over. There are three five minute rounds. Get into your corners and lets start this thing off.” Walczak heads to his corner, and no one is waiting for him. This is the biggest difference between Walczak and the other competitors. All other fighters have someone cheering them on and helping them out. However, for Walczak there is emptiness. The bell rings and both men head towards each other. They move around the ring slowly with the Ramirez taking quick jabs at Walczak. While Walczak is bigger than his opponent he is much slower. The jabs start to turn into harder punches and Walczak starts to stagger a little. The first round ends with almost no action coming from Walczak and his opponent feeling very confident. Man what am I doing? My head is not in this match at all. I can beat this guy his punches do nothing I am just not focused. These are the thoughts that run through Walczak’s head as he sits in his corner. He takes a gulp of his water, and then stands up waiting for the bell to sound. As the bell sounds Walczak’s opponent charges towards him, and is laid out by a vicious punch from Walzcak. This was done more out of instinct then anything, but it gets the job done. The referee calls for the bell as Ramirez lays on the mat unconscious. The crowd erupts behind Walczak, and a smile appears on the young man’s face. Walczak leaves the ring and heads back to the spot where he sat before the match. Man that felt good. I didn’t think that I was going to get out of there. I got lucky in that one, but I really have to remain focused in order to win my next match. Walczak sits there anticipating his next match and there are many people who come up to him throughout the next couple of minutes. “Congratulations Tom.” “Good fight man.” “What a hit!” These are some of the things that people say as they walk by, but all that Walczak can do is respond with a head nod because he can barely understand what they are saying. When people start to realize this they start poking fun at Walczak. “Nice win foreigner.” “Good job you dumb Polak.” “I’m surprised you knew what to do when you got in there you stupid shit.” These are what the comments grow into as more and more people pass Walczak, but to Walczak everything sounds the same. He responds with a head nod all the same and goes back into his same relaxed position. Then the director of the tournament walks up to Walczak, and starts to speak to him. “Walczak, the two men who were supposed to fight in the other quarterfinal match in your bracket can’t continue, so you get to go straight to the final.” The man spoke in a slow droll so Walczak was actually able to pick up most of the words, and understood that the next match he had would be for the title, but he did not know yet who his opponent was. Pretty nice, I get a free ride to the finals. Hopefully my opponent gets tired out before we fight and I could use that to my advantage. However, that wasn’t going to happen. “Ladies and gentlemen, your winner by knockout in a time of 11 seconds Christopher Charles.” Everyone who was there goes nuts, and although the place was not completely filled with people the roar filled the entire area. Walczak looked over and saw who had won the match. His heart sank as he knew whom he would have to face. Don’t worry about it. If you win you win if you lose you lose. That’s all that you can say. You weren’t even expected to go this far, and here you are in the finals. Your first shot at fighting and you already made a splash. There is nothing to be afraid of if you lose. Walczak’s confident attitude had completely changed, and was now an attitude of mediocrity, and settling for second best. There was an hour intermission before the last match to give Charles a chance to recover, so Walczak went to get some refreshments. He was greeted with rude comments on his walk over. “You suck you big Polish goon.” “Charles is gonna fuck you up you piece of shit.” “Go back to fucking Poland you moron.” While Walczak’s English was not very good he was not stupid, and he knew that these were demeaning comments. He goes over to the refreshment stand where they take care of the competitors and grabs a Gatorade. As he opens the cap Christopher Charles walks right by him. “You don’t stand a chance you little punk.” The comment reverberates in Walczak’s head, and this time instead of scaring him it gets him angry. He throws his unfinished Gatorade to the garbage and walks back to his secluded spot. Why the hell is this guy talking so much trash? It’s just a fight, but he has to make into more than that. He has to make it personal. I am sick of all of these people talking down to me because I do not speak their language. It angers me that I have come so far in this tournament yet still I have little respect from the crowd. No more of this playing for second. I am going to get into that ring and give that bastard the fight of his life. Walczak is starting to gain his confidence back. He walks back to his area in a fervor and blocks out all of the noise that is around him. He gets back to his area, but instead of sitting in a relaxed position he paces around thinking about his upcoming match. This is the first time in my life that I have wanted to accomplish something. I am determined to do this money or no money I want to win. That is all I want to do right now. I want to take his words and shove them right back in his face No one approaches Walczak before the match because every member of the media is surrounding Charles, and his entourage. All the fans are either watching his interview, or outside catching a breath of fresh air. Walczak pauses to look at the interview being conducted. “Do you think that your opponent has a chance?” “Not at all.” “Is there a reason for this?” “He just doesn’t have the right stuff. I saw his earlier fights and was not impressed at all. He is a big dumb goon, and I have faced many of those throughout my career.” “He has to have some skill to have made it this far, doesn’t he?” “He doesn’t even have the skill of speaking.” The crowd erupts with laughter, and the anger just continues to grow inside of Walczak. “Listen after the match you will all be asking me how he even made it into the finals. I guarantee a victory and that’s not even going out on a stretch.” As the last statement is made Charles leaves the crowd and goes to his own private backstage area to prepare That is a complete sign of disrespect. He is assuming that the match is over. He has waltzed through his competition up until now, but that will be no more. Walczak sits there thinking of what he can do to his opponent, and what skills of his he can use to get the upper hand. Then the time came. “Walczak, Charles, make your way to the ring now.” Without hesitation Walczak makes his way to the ring. There is nothing on his mind except this fight. All previous confrontations, good or bad, are in the past and he is living in this moment. “Gentlemen, come into the center and shake hands. I want a good clean fight. This one is for the tournament championship, and the $1,500 purse. Shake hands and head back to your corners.” Walczak heads back to his corner and sees the emptiness that it contains, but that means nothing to him now. He looks over at the corner of Christopher Charles, and it seems that the entire population of the arena is on his side. The bell rings and he makes his way out. Jab after jab Charles wears down Walczak. Walczak is trying to block his opponent, but to no avail. The first round ends, and Walczak is reeling from the blows. He can barely catch his breath before he has to go out and start the second round. This guy is killing me. There is nothing to fight for anymore. Just as these words come through Walczak’s mind he gets hit with a hard right hand from Charles. He stumbles into the ropes and is tackled to the ground. He quickly gets his legs around Charles abdomen, but the damage is already down. Charles starts to wail on Walczak, who does a fairly good job in deflecting the major blows. He arches his back and stares into his empty corner. There is one difference though. His corner is no longer empty. Janina is there pounding her fist on the canvas telling him to get up. This lights a new fire in Walczak. He frees his legs and kicks away from Charles’ grasp. He gets to his feet and unloads a vicious fist to his opponent. Charles flails to the ground, and is pounced on by Walczak. Punch after punch is delivered by Walczak wit no defense attempt made at all by Charles. The bell sounds behind Walczak, and he is pulled off the limp body of Charles by the referee. His hand is raised, and Walczak experiences the first true triumph of his life. “Janina! Janina! Thank you for your support.” She climbs into the ring and hugs Walczak as tears swell up in her eyes. Walczak’s right eye is nearly shut, but to him it is all worth it. Finally some of the attention is on Walczak as the media comes over to him. “Excuse me, Mr. Walczak is there anything that you would like to say after this dramatic battle.” “Jescze Polska Nie Zginela!!!!!” These are words that still ring true in the life of Walczak. Slowly he realizes that he does have reasons to fight for, and that all is not lost in this world. A phone call rings and rattles Walczak from his deep thought. “Tomek?” “Yes.” “It’s Janina.” “Hey, whats up?” “I just wanted to say. Never forget that there is always something that you can fight for.” “I know…….
