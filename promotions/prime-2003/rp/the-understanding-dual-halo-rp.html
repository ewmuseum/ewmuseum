Date: Numerous November 23, 2007 The sun was beaming down as the waves were making their wonderful music. He loved the Pacific Ocean and even though she saw it for the first time, Desiree was drawn to it. Since arriving a couple days earlier, they went down to the beach every morning. It wouldn’t have happened any other way because the little girl practically insisted it. Short of screaming bloody murder, she would do everything she could to ensure her morning walk in the sand and salt water. Killean kept her in the very shallow parts, watching her every move so she didn’t try to sneak out further. She was happily running through the water, her little feet carrying her anywhere she wished. She was an energetic little tyke, almost wearing her father out on a daily basis. She bent down to pick up a sea shell but threw it back into the water when she realized it was broken. She had already started a little collection back up in the room. She plugged up the sink in the washroom and put her shells in there to clean them with help of course. Killean was thinking that he might have to buy a new bag just to take sea shells home at the rate she was collecting them. So what of Papa Killean? He had been to Mazatlan, Mexico three times in the past three years. However this was the first time he had taken his daughter. His tropical style shirt would have been an eyesore anywhere else but down there he had blended in perfectly. He even took the advice of some of the nearby residents that he had gotten to know and bought himself a pair of thin canvas pants. He was closely monitoring the actions of the little Sirrajin as he also enjoyed the feeling of the clean sand beneath his feet. He was swinging his sandals back and forth in his hand as he walked. He looked like the typical father, much different than how he looked over two years earlier when the little girl was left on his door step. “Daddy?” “Yes my dear?” He bent down to talk to her. “Where do sea sell... sea schmells come from again?” She had the hardest time pronouncing the words, always bringing a bright smile to her father’s face. “You asked me that yesterday Dizz... and the day before that too. They’re like little coats for a sea creature. It protects them.” He explained, the little girl listening intently. “Oh yeah.” She proceeds to clean the sand off a shell in her hand and then points out into the seemingly endless ocean. “They all come from out there?” “That’s right. Far, far out there in deep waters too.” “Do people live out there too?” It was such an innocent question that would have sounded extremely strange coming from an adult. But from a child it had a certain meaning behind it. Her inquisitive mind was constantly churning, waiting for what she was to learn about next. “Only in far away lands. People can’t live in the ocean sweetie.” He figured that would satisfy her curiosity, but he couldn’t have been more wrong. “What about Ariel?” “The Little Mermaid?” At that exact second, he remembered the day he walked into the living room of his loft to see Desiree watching the Little Mermaid on the TV and in her hands she had a fork. She was using it to groom her hair. “Yeah. She lived out there. Did she collect sea coats too?” “Heh, no darling. She had friends who wore them though.” “I want a sea coat... big enough for me!” She jumped in the water playfully. While he watched, something on the beach caught Killean’s eye behind her and he tried to direct his daughter towards it. “Look Dizzy! What’s that?” Killean began to run with her, her interest peaked as well. There was something sticking out of the sand only a couple dozen feet away. As they approached it, Killean could tell what it was and that it was safe. Desiree bent down to pick it up, revealing it’s complete and round form to her eyes. “Weird sea coat Daddy.” “That’s a sand dollar baby. Hard to find in one piece too.” “Sand dollar?” She asked, looking oddly at the large coin like souvenir. “Do they work in the drink machine? I’m thirsty.” He couldn’t help but laugh that time. He dug into his pocket, producing a five Peso coin, equivalent to almost fifty US cents. “These work in the machine, that one you take home with you.” He plucked her out of the sand and held her in his arms as she kept observing the sand dollar. She then looked out to the ocean again. “What are the places out there like Daddy? The people places.” He stopped and took a long hard look at the endless sea of water with his daughter. He remembered what it was like to travel overseas and to visit the countless other cultures. Japan was one of his favorites. He also had a fondness for Australia. All the memories forced him to remember the wrestling business even more. If it had not been for that, he would not have seen all that he has. He had tried to forget about the invitation he had received from Lisa Tyler. They were passing through town on the twenty-eighth of the month and Lisa called up Killean upon hearing about the date. But talking about far away places and figuring out exactly what business took him the world over brought all the things he stored away back to the front of his mind. He took a deep breath and looked down at his daughter. He cuddled up close to her. “Well they’re almost as pretty as you Dizz.” He took one of her cheeks in between his fingers and jiggled it, winning a laugh from her. He put her down in the sand and she started to make a beeline for the resort. After stealing another glance at the ocean, picturing the worlds beyond, he followed. |~o~| December 21, 2007 Killean sighed as he hung up the phone. It took three letters delivered by FedEx, one complete with contract for him to finally call Lisa Tyler back. It was the fourth time since he retired that he turned down a contract return offer by the brass of PRIME and he hoped silently it would be the last. Yeah, high hopes huh? He ran his fingers through his hair and walked over to his desk. He gathered the other two envelopes with the one he was carrying and began to feed a sheet into the paper shredder. He could see the shredded paper dropping into the bin below and let out a sigh again. As he started to put another sheet of paper through, the door to the loft opened and in walked a rather attractive redhead. She had a business suit on and her hair was held out of her eyes by numerous hair pins. The rest of it cascaded down her back and shoulders. She was not the typical female that you would normally have seen with Killean though. His normal date would have been beyond sexy with long slender legs, a size two waist and a chest to make a Hooters girl jealous. Well this woman had an average sized bust line on top of what was definitely not a size two, but a size twelve waist. Her curvaceous body was certainly not invisible when she turned to the side unlike some women he used to date. This was basically, your typical attractive and average female, not some magazine model made sexy by how society as a whole viewed them. She put her briefcase down on the floor by the closet and made her way to where Killean was standing, her high heels making a clicking sound every time she took a single step. He wrapped an arm around his shoulders and kissed him on the cheek. “Hey hun, whatcha’ doin’?” She said as he returned both gestures and proceeded to feed yet another sheet into the machine. “Just shredding these letters, along with the contract that came today.” “They just don’t know when to quit do they?” She said as she patted him on the back while she made her way to the kitchen. To make things a little clearer, Jenny Hastings was a part time attorney at a local law firm in Winnipeg. She was born on March 13, 1978 making her twenty-nine years old. Killean and Jenny met on the day of Killean’s twenty-ninth birthday. Both of them had terrible dates that night and ended up at the bar in the lounge to down one last drink for the night. They began to talk and hit it off immediately. Her baby blue eyes drove him wild from the beginning, like looking out into the ocean. Here they were, six full months later in a stable relationship. She was working a lot which caused Killean to stay at home with Desiree most days of the week during normal business hours. He did spend a few nights a week at the club, which was now completely redesigned into one of the town’s hottest nightspots. “So how is your sister and step mom? Did they get settled in all right?” “Seems like it. Talked to them about half an hour ago and they love the new condo.” Back when his father passed away, his half sister Cadence and his step mother Hilary had taken Killean up on the offer to stay with him. Just a few days ago, they had moved into their own place. Cadence was attending the University of Manitoba following up on her dream of a nursing degree with the help of her big brothers financing. Yep, you got that right. The old coot Thomas Sirrajin fathered another daughter when he was the ripe old age of forty-three, which made Cadence nineteen now. Hilary was younger than Thomas as she gave birth to Cadence when she was thirty-six. Killean’s father passed away at sixty-two years of age. “That’s wonderful. It’s so nice of you to split the cost with them. They couldn’t stay with you forever like you said.” Jenny said carrying half a premade tuna sandwich into the living room. Killean was just feeding the last couple pages of the contract he received into the shredder while he tossed the FedEx envelopes into the recycle bin beside it. “Well that and we have talked about living together for a while now.” Killean said as he dusted off his hands as the final sheet of paper was sliced into hundreds of tiny pieces. He made his way over to the couch from behind, putting his hands on Jenny’s shoulders. He began to lightly massage as she ate. “I’m just glad it’s actually happening now. When are the moving guys supposed to be here with your things?” “Another fifteen or so minutes I think. Desiree should be waking up from her nap anytime now right?” She said as she took the final piece of sandwich into her mouth, wiping the crumbs from her fingers. She sighed and leaned back a little more, enjoying the massage. “Well if she isn’t, the moving guys will wake her I’m sure.” “When they’re done, I’m going to get you one more special Christmas gift on my way to work. Anything you want.” She placed one soft hand on Killean’s. “Got any ideas?” He thought for a second, but he found it to only be a distraction. He cleared his head for a moment and looked over to the paper shredder bin. The pieces of the beige paper the contract was drawn up on stood out from the other white sheets. He then glanced over to Desiree’s room, which was down the hall. He had chosen fatherhood back when he left PRIME. He had chosen his daughter. So what else could he possibly want? He stared awkwardly back at the beige strands of paper in the bin as he let his hands relax, clearly losing his concentration on the massage he was giving. Jenny turned her head to see what he was up to. “Kill?” Jenny asked, snapping him from his trance. He turned back slowly, his head hanging just a little as he smiled. “Surprise me.” |~o~| February 9, 2008 The display on the flat screen TV was changing from menu to menu every couple seconds. Obviously, Killean could not find anything good to watch on TV. Unfortunately it was the standard considering the writer’s strike still going on, but rumor had it that it may end soon. Regardless, it was a terrible day outside, the snow was falling and the sun had no chance of peaking through. All in all, it was a day to be lazy on the couch. Killean settled on a channel, but a few minutes later his eyes began to flutter shut. Soon he was fast asleep on the couch. With Desiree down for a nap, he would have sufficient time for a little shut eye. It seemed like an eternity later, but some light was now shining in the window and straight into the face of the Hall of Fame superstar. He started to wake up, trying to clear his eyes. He rubbed them. “Dad? You awake?” “Excuse me?” Killean thought, still trying to gain focus. A grown woman seemed to be standing a few feet away from him and she called him Dad? He cleared his throat. “Pretty much.” “Good, cause I wanna ask you something.” Her shoulder length dark hair briefly covered her features when she sat down and then her very beautiful face was exposed. Killean found himself rubbing his eyes, seeing a little bit of Stacey’s features in her face. But how could this be happening. If she looked any little bit like Stacey, then it had to be Desiree. Killean felt like pinching himself or slapping himself, but he was frozen in time, listening to his not so little girl talk. “Mom told me some things...” She started. “Mom?” “Well Step-Mom... Jenny... your wife... duh!” She said laughing. “Anyways she told me some things, like that you stopped wrestling all those years ago because of me, because you wanted to be a good dad. Is that true?” “All those years ago?” He thought while shaking his head. He placed a hand on hers but he then pulled it back and looked at it quickly. It was like he was seeing double. One older and somewhat wrinkled hand and the other was his own. But he never questioned it. “Baby, I wanted to be here to devote all my time to you. I wanted to be the best father there could be.” “But Dad, you would have been the bestest ever anyways!” She kissed him on the cheek and gave him a hug. He slowly returned the gesture, almost like he still didn’t know who he was hugging. “I thought it was what you’d have wanted.” “Oh come on Dad. Mom said you loved wrestling.” She giggled and took her purse from the coffee table and made her way to the door. She turned and smiled brightly at her father. “If that were true and what I’d have really wanted, for you to stop, then I would’ve been wrong.” She closed the door behind her as she walked into the hallway. Killean rushed off the couch and to the door, almost ripping it from its hinges. He looked out into the hallway, but no one was there. Back in the room, the light that shined into the room disappeared and the snow was falling outside again. Killean wiped his face and turned back into the room and did a double take as he shut the door. On the couch, where he just came from was... him. This man, this virtual double of him came into focus as he rubbed his eyes again. Gray hair covered his hair and his beard. The older Killean had on a pair of reading glasses. “I never needed glasses.” He walked over to the couch to find this aged duplicate fast asleep. He looked around nervously and reached out to touch the gray locks of hair. His hand basically went right through it, like he was trying to touch a projection. Just then, the old man opened his eyes and sat up swiftly. The bloodshot look was almost sickly. It stuck in his mind, burned into his memory. Suddenly, without a split second to think, he was in that spot on the couch. He was wide awake now, looking down at his hands and finding no abnormalities this time. He shook his head, trying to clear it but he still had those bloodshot eyes terrorizing his vision like a plague. He got up from the couch and made his way to Desiree’s room. He turned back briefly to make sure that his older double was not still there. He approached her door to find the little girl he loved to be still asleep in her crib. He sighed deeply and went across the hall and looked in his old weight room. The weight machines and free weights had not been used in a while. He walked over to the bench press and dragged his finger across the material, leaving a brighter streak in its place. The equipment was collecting dust. He remembered the vision of the old man, the aged man that appeared to be himself. He was tired... And he seemed broken. |~o~| February 15, 2008 Killean seemed to have extra energy that day. The weights and equipment no longer had a layer of dust on them. In fact, they were clean as a whistle except for the occasional sweat droplet. He was looking out the window at the street below as he curled some weights, watching the cars go by. The sun was trying to peak out from behind the clouds. The very faded reflection in the window told the whole story. Killean was pushing the envelope, working out like a mad man. This wasn’t some Sly Stallone in Rocky drinking raw eggs thing either. This was pure, unmistaken adrenaline and raw power instead. There was a throaty grunt every time he curled a barbell, every time he bench pressed and every time he punched the punching bag. He would follow up an hour and a half worth of weights with an hour of full on cardio including exercise bike, treadmill and elliptical trainer. All that and it was just the first day. He did it at the same time day by day afterwards. Desiree was down for her nap and Jenny was busy at the office. He worked a little harder every day, trying to rid himself of the memory of that old man he had seen. It was why he didn’t look in the mirror. Every time he did, he saw those bloodshot eyes, that aged body and that broken soul. It was like the mirrors were taunting him. He put the few mirrors in the closet but he left one out. Every day when he was finished his strenuous workout, he looked in that mirror to see if the broken old man would vanish. Every day, he saw the same sight. It made him want to try harder. But what happened leading up to February 15th that inspired this? His old stomping ground was sold. Tyler Nelson was no longer involved with anything and shortly thereafter, his phone rang off the hook. After a couple days of not answering it, he finally relented and his interest was immediately piqued. New owners having their assistants making phone calls was a rather tacky way of doing things, but if they handled all their business situations like their discussions with him, then they were a very streamlined and determined group of people. Killean had more offers and letters to shred in those couple days, but then his fax machine came to life one morning and out came a beige piece of paper, followed by a white letter. This time, he didn’t have any documents to shred. He never told Jenny, Cadence, Hilary or even his best friend Vegas about the faxes he received that morning. The only people who knew anything were Killean and the people at the other end who received signed copies back later that afternoon. The old man in the mirror also knew. The weeks passed and the image in the mirror was different only a month later. With intense work out sessions and endless will the broken man in the mirror gradually disappeared, no longer taunting the younger. Gone were the polo shirts and the soccer dad look. Gone was the doubt that he made a mistake way back when. Gone was the old man in the mirror. Instead, an impressive physical specimen stood, black dress shirt on, sunglasses in their place and arms crossed across his chiselled chest. He was never a broken man, but merely damaged and the repairs were flawless. He was still in amazing shape prior to his “awakening”. After, he had that extra skip in his step. He had more energy.
