Road to Recovery Chapter 1: Fear and Loathing in Milwaukee

8 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/11/2010 Milwaukee, Wisconsin Yesterday The silvery cascade of moonlight broke through the half-closed blinds with unknown purpose, painting a sizeable portion of the motel room in a surreal shade of blue. Jacob McKail drank the simple of beauty of it in, finding it to be somewhat comforting during yet another sleepless night. There was too much on his mind for him to sleep; he told himself that the blame lay with the constant worrying about the hell that was his life, but deep down he knew the truth. He was scared. PRIME had offered him a lifeline; a way back from the baron wastelands that was his wrestling career. PRIME had decided to take a chance and offered him a reasonable contract for a permanent spot on their roster. Of course, he snatched out of their hands. He thought his wrestling career was long over; it’d been a whole seven months since he’d last stepped foot inside the squared circle and truth be told, he never truly thought he would again. He’d been provided yet another chance to redeem himself and this time he was going to make damned sure he didn’t fuck it up. Tomorrow was the first day back on the job and he had a long forgotten tingle in the pit of his gut that was causing him some discomfort; it was a strange amalgamation of fear and excitement. It’d been so long since he’d felt this way, he’d forgotten how to deal with it. His system was in shock, seemingly unable to cope with the stress this emotion was putting him under. McKail sat up in bed and buried his head in his hands. What the hell is wrong with me? He couldn’t come up with any kind of sensible answer to the question, no matter how hard he tried to conjure one. He’d fought and wrestled in over a thousand matches before. Sure, there would inevitably be some ring rust, but it wasn’t like he was going in there blind. No, that wasn’t it. It had nothing to do with the prospect of stepping back into the ring again, nor was he questioning his ability to compete at the highest level again. He was scared of losing it again; losing all control of his anger and senses and inflicting a bloody rage upon anybody in the direct vicinity. Indeed, the last time he did grace the squared circle, he beat a fellow wrestler half to death in a drunken rage. It was an action that cost him dearly; the wrestling industry consequently blacklisted him for a time and it sent into a seemingly fatal downward spiral, the likes of which he never thought he’d recover from. But to his own surprise, just as everyone else’s, he did recover. *** The Bronx, New York 17th June 2010 Bundled into the back of the police van, the stench of piss was the first thing to assault McKail’s senses. The second was dark red stains smeared haphazardly upon the white walls of the van. Blood, most likely. The rather portly police officer escorting McKail to his metallic prison caught him staring at the obvious blood stains and chuckled to himself. “Boy, did we have a good time last night!” McKail decided to take his comment only partly as a joke and remained silent as the police officer shunted him further into the van. Hands cuffed behind his back, McKail slumped down onto the bench and embrace the ice cold touch of the metal against his upper back. He was the first to be dumped in the truck, but had seen three more parked close by. They’d obviously planned this raid for a while. “No, man, no!” The voice from outside the van yelled. “Don’t put me in there! I didn’t do nothin’! I was just watchin’, see?” The cops hauled a skinny African-American man into the truck and dumped him on the bench opposite McKail. The obese cop directed his fat face towards McKail, his hand on his nightstick all the while. “Move up,” he said. McKail barely made out a grin underneath of his thick ginger moustache. “We got more o’ your friends to come and keep you company!” McKail ignored him. He should be angry; angry at the police offers unnecessary jibes and at his whole damned life. But he felt nothing. He hadn’t felt much of anything since he was blacklisted from the wrestling industry. Now, he was forced to earn a living on the streets fighting for the entertainment of blood thirsty citizens looking to earn a quick buck. He couldn’t fall any lower. Or so he thought. The other prisoner clanged his feet against the floor and desperately fidgeted, trying to break free of his steel restraints. “This is bullshit, man!” He declared. His angry eyes focused upon McKail, but really he was addressing anybody who’d listen. “I didn’t even do nothin’!” He nodded at McKail. “Hey! What’s wrong with you? Why do you do somethin’?” McKail glared at him, but said nothing. “Don’t be givin’ me that cold silence or nothin’,” he said. “You ain’t my damned girlfriend or nothin’. I know you wrestlers are all ‘roided up. Break these things, man!” “Hey!” It was the overweight cop again. He threw another two men into the back of the van and pointed angrily at the loud African-American gentleman. “Shut the fuck up! I know your history Reggie. This is your third strike, motherfucker. You’re going down!” Reggie shook his head. “But I didn’t do nothin’ man!” Reggie nodded at McKail again, this time for the police officer’s benefit. “What about him?” The cop shrugged. “What about him?” “He was one of the fighter’s, man!” Reggie blabbed. “I was just watchin’! He should be the one going down for this!” The cop chuckled and shook his head. “You’re one dumb son of a bitch, you know that?” “What, man?” Reggie questioned. “What you talkin’ ‘bout?” The cop just continued to shake his head and slammed the van door shut. “But I didn’t do nothin’, man!” Reggie continued. “I DIDN’T DO NOTHIN’!” McKail knew that he should have been angry. He knew that he should have been furious with Reggie. But he didn’t care. He was numb inside, he felt nothing. And he didn’t much care if he ever felt anything ever again. *** Lakewood, Colorado 2 Weeks Ago The diner brought back some nostalgic memories for McKail. The folk who ran the joint were trying to sell the nineteen-fifties greaser image as a theme as most diners all across America liked to do these days, but judging by the quality of the leather clad seating and the paint bubbling and peeling from the walls, the place was probably the real thing passing off as a theme. The diner in which he first met his late wife, Mary, had the same god awful turquoise and white interior. She worked there as a waitress at a twenty-four hour diner near his crumby apartment in the Bronx. He didn't usually go in there, but he came home late from a small-paying indie gig in some school gym out in the suburbs one night because they paid him an extra twenty bucks. He was starving and he knew damned well he didn't have anything in his fridge to eat. She was fresh out of high school looking to earn a few bucks over the summer to help pay for college. It seemed like a whole lifetime ago now. “Yo, Jake,” Elle said, snapping him out of his nostalgic reverie. “You here?” “Yeah,” he replied, tearing open a brown sugar packet and pouring it into his coffee. “The place just reminded me of somethin' is all.” “Like I was saying, you’re different, Jake,” Elle noted, emphasising her point by waving a maple syrup stained fork in his direction. “I can’t put my finger on it, but I can tell.” McKail tried to hide a smile and failed. Elle had always been able to right through him; to her, he was an open book and lot more vulnerable than he cared for. Once upon a time she loved him and he inevitably fucked things up. “I came here to apologise,” McKail told her, taking a sip of sweet coffee. Elle only stared at him wide-eyed. “Apologise? You...?” She paused to fully digest the concept. McKail suspected she was only teasing him, but allowed her the time to play it out. He owed her that much. “Are you dying or something?” McKail allowed a chuckle. “No. I’m growing up. I have changed, Elle.” He paused, choosing his next words very carefully. He felt a lump the size of a tennis ball form in the back of his throat. He choked it down the best he could and tried compose himself. “What I did was unforgivable...I’m sorry.” It had been almost a year since McKail lied to and betrayed Elle. He blew the only shot he'd had since Mary died for true happiness and it'd been eating away at him ever since. Up until now he'd just been piling it onto all of the other shit he tried to forget and let the booze take care of the rest, but now he'd decided to face his demons and apologising to Elle was the first thing on his to-do list. Elle delicately placed her fork upon her plate and reached across the table and took his hands into her own. “It’s good that you’re taking this step. It’s good that you came all the way out here to see me and apologise to me face to face, but it wasn’t necessary. I forgave you a long time ago.” McKail shook his head. “I don’t deserve it.” Elle removed her hand. “Jake, if you don’t truly believe you deserve my forgiveness then why did you ask for it?” Tears welled up in McKail’s eyes. He hadn’t felt this bad, hell, hadn’t felt this anything for a long time. It was times like this he realised why he always turned to the booze to fix his problems. It helped him to forget; it helped him to avoid all of the pain. He was such a coward. “Because it was the right thing to do,” he told her, pawing at his eyes to dab away the tears. “And because you deserved to hear it.” “Jake,” she began, looking at him like he was a complete stranger, “what the hell happened to you?” McKail smiled. “I got better.” *** The Bronx, New York 21st June 2010 “Jacob?” Archie Steiner called, squinting as he tried to sift out his client amongst the shadows. “You in here?” He glanced back at the correction officer who pointed at the next cell along with his nightstick. “Thanks.” The officer only nodded a reply. Steiner approached the next cell along and found Jacob McKail sitting on his bunk, leaning against the wall, one leg hugging his chest and the other outstretched. His head was pressed against the wall and his eyes were closed. He didn't look all that good. “Judge was in a good mood,” Steiner beamed, leaning against cold metal bars of the cell. “Because you got off light my friend.” “Yeah?” McKail grumbled, his voice hoarse and his eyes still closed. “Well, you’re not looking at any jail time at any rate,” Steiner told him. McKail opened his eyes and glared at him, curious. Obviously the news wasn’t something he was expecting. “No?” Steiner grinned. “Thought that would get your attention.” McKail had to admit that he was good. Steiner worked for one of the largest law firms in New York City and upon hearing of McKail’s troubles, offered his services freely. Naturally, McKail turned down his help the first five times it was offered; partly because he didn’t really care who represented him nor what happened to him and partly because he was naturally paranoid by the offer of free representation. Who wouldn’t be? Steiner’s standard rate was over two hundred bucks an hour and he was offering to help McKail for free. He smelled a rat, but when Steiner explained his fascination with wrestling and how used to idolise him growing up, McKail finally agreed. Even if he was being fucked over for some unknown reason, what the fuck did he have to lose? “There’s a catch though, right?” McKail asked. “Yeah, well,” Steiner began, “they aren’t exactly going to let you off scot-free, Jacob.” McKail nodded. “Of course. So what is it, Steiner?” Steiner winced. “You ain’t gonna like it.” McKail sighed. “Just spit it out already.” “You have to spend a minimum of 5 months in a specialised rehabilitation facility,” Steiner explained. “You gotta get clean, man.” Strange as it may appear, the full gravity of the situation had only just hit him like hammer to the side of the head. Without the booze there would be nothing to hold his demons at bay; there would be nothing to suppress all of the painful memories that he'd accumulated over the years, there would be nothing to suppress the last few moments he had with Mary before she passed on. And he had 5 months of forcibly clean living for it to torture him. McKail buried his head in his hands and then slowly ran his fingers through his hair. “Fuck...”
