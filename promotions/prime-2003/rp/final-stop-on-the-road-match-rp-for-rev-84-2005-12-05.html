Final Stop on the Road (match RP for ReV 84)

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/05/05 ~Tuesday, December 6, 2005. 2:57P.M.~ Your mind… your memory… is your identity. If you put on a few pounds, someone pats you and the stomach, laughs, and says ‘looks like you’ve been eating well lately’. But they still know you, still catch up on old times and share old stories. If you shave your beard, people rub your face in awe of how smooth it is, as if they’ve never felt a clean shaven face before, but then they strike up the same old types of conversation and laughter they’ve done a hundred times before. Women get married, take the last name of their new husband and his heritage. You change the very label you have carried your entire lives. But it doesn’t change you. People still recognize you… still treat you the same way, you still treat them the same. Your name can’t be your identity. There’s always another John or Jane Smith out their. Thousands of them. You are your mind… your memory. For an individual to lose the very thing that gives them their identity is a surreal and numbing thought, but its actuality is far worse. With the same name, they can become nameless. With the same face, they can become faceless… total strangers to everyone who swore to know them best. No matter how the memory suffers, the way things change is immense. Sometimes it’s the loss of all past memories leaving only a new future to try and start again… doctor’s call that retrograde amnesia, but those affected call it hell. Other times it’s the loss of the ability to form new memories, shattering any potential for a future, leaving only the past to cherish… psychologists have labeled this anterograde amnesia, but is more accurately known as a nightmare. That nightmare had been the life of his family for the past three months… at least, he thought it had been about three months since the accident… maybe it had just been one infinitely long day, with no true beginning or end. Who could really say? Three months was fast approaching the point of no return… over halfway there. It was standard medical opinion that patients who hadn’t recovered from their amnesia and regained their full use of memory by the six month mark were likely to suffer from the amnesia permanently. Memory could return at any time really, and contrary to pop culture’s portrayal, no one had to get bashed on the head or “re-experience” their tragedy in order to regain their mind. If damage to a part of the brain had occurred, the amnesia would likely be permanent, but if the onset had occurred through shock or a minor bump or a traumatic event… memory could return without much of a definitive reason why or without a grand defining moment that changes everything, curing all problems. But after three plus months, even the knowledge that Mary’s short term memory could return in working order at any moment was little comfort to him. Still, there were nearly three months till the point of no return, anything was still possible… the best and worst case scenarios. And three months into the nightmare, the same three months into his rebirth in the world of wrestling, things were beginning to take a form all their own. The Great American Nightmare was fast approaching and things were beginning to look increasingly uncontrollable in the world of PRIME. It had been just a little less than a week since the last bit of chaos had struck between The Supreme Machine, The Inhuman Being, and The Other son of God. And that was just the picture as it involved the Universal Title. That didn’t even take into account what potential blow up could occur between Ignatius Lisieux and Ozric Mortimer, or the quite personal feud that had developed between Emilio Rage and Tyler Nelson. There were plenty of other problems sparking a general impression that things were about to erupt in violent fashion within the confines of PRIME. As it related to his own well being, ReVolution 83 had been as much about survival as anything, though he knew he would be labeled the bad guy for leaving his friend, The Supreme Machine as he called himself, alone to deal with each and every member of Imperium. That was fine, he could deal with that. The good graces he was beginning to find with some of the fans and a few members of the PRIME crew… they weren’t doing him any good, and if leaving Killean alone in the ring to suffer whatever fate Imperium saw fit was going to ruin his good graces… so be it. Matt was fine with that, and a lot better off than he would have been with the fans on his side but a few broken bones courtesy of his TGAN opponent. Self-preservation was key. Survival of the fittest, in this case smartest, as Darwin had put it. Hoyt probably wouldn’t like hearing how he’d taken part last Wednesday night in a bit of Darwinism. But as far as Matt was concerned, that was essentially what it had been. It was Killean’s choice to come down to the ring and involve himself in the business at hand, knowing full well that Imperium had the numbers. Matt knew that much, which is why he had simply rolled out of the ring and headed to the back… the numbers weren’t in his favor, so he wanted nothing to do with them. Killean should have figured the same. Besides, he didn’t have to answer to Killean, or to Hoyt, or to the fans. The only person he felt he would have to justify himself to was Mary, and no matter how much he wanted the opportunity… succeed or fail… to justify his actions to her, it just wasn’t possible at the time being. Which left him in an intriguing situation… no strings attached, nothing to justify and no one to apologize to. “Matthew,” The words snapped him out of his zombie like state. He turned his stare away from the suitcase that lied half-zipped on the edge of the bed and towards the sound of his awakening. Mary stood in the doorway, leaning against the oak slab, her hand resting on the doorknob. “what are you doing, sweetie?” “Just came in here to check on Georgie… she’s sleeping peaceful as can be. Hasn’t made a sound aside from one little sigh since I came in here.” He looked over towards the baby lying quietly, eyes closes, in her crib. “She usually does sleep well. I can’t believe how peacefully she naps sometimes.” “Guess she just likes to sleep.” “Must get it from her father.” Mary walked over towards her husband, wrapping her arms around his waist and kissing him on the shoulder. As she did, she noticed the suitcase lying in front of him, and her confusion was too strong to ignore. “What’s with the suitcase?” “Well, when I came in here to check on Georgie, I remembered I had forgotten to pack a couple things, so I was adding them…” “Where are you going?” “Just away on business for a couple days. Actually, I probably won’t even be gone thirty-six hours. I’m leaving this evening, and I’ll be back by Thursday afternoon.” “How’s business going?” “Its going good.” He cringed a bit at the thought that Mary was still under the impression he was working as a psychologists. For all he knew, she may have been thinking at that very moment that he was being called to testify in some criminal case, the defense wanting his ‘expert’ opinion that their client was legally insane, or not competent to stand trial. As the thought occurred to him, he figured it might be the safest way to go. In the absence of any truth worth a damn, it would be a logical and simple explanation. “Been called to testify tomorrow in a case about the fragile state of mind of an individual accused of two counts of assault. Defense wants me to get their client off the hook.” “Was the guy actually suffering from insanity at the time of the crime?” Mary released her hold on him and lightly pushed him on his left arm, giving him the necessary clue that she wanted him to turn and face her. He accommodated. “As much as the next guy.” “That sounds like the exact response I’d expect you and your sarcasm to give.” “Yeah, and it’s the exact response I’d like to give while testifying. But I don’t think that would go over to well with the defense of the judge, and I can’t say I want to find myself in contempt of court.” “And your wife and baby girl wouldn’t like that either.” She gave him a tug on the shirt and shot him a look that said, ‘play it straight and stay out of trouble, mister’. “I have no intentions of getting locked up behind bars.” “Promise?” “Yeah.” It was the first time in several weeks, maybe since the accident, that she had asked him to promise anything. He was just glad it was something he could answer ‘yes’ to, and actually mean it. “I promise, baby.” * * * ~Tuesday, December 6, 2005. 5:22P.M.~ “Did I tell you Scotty won five hundred bucks on an instant win lotto ticket?” Matthew carried the plates from the small table in the kitchen over to the sink, rinsing them off briefly before sliding them in the dishwasher. “I thought Scotty had given up all forms of gambling?” Mary followed his steps, carrying the glasses in her hand, dumping the remaining mixture of melted ice cubes and flat cola down the sink, then handing them to Matthew to load with the plates. “He did. That’s the kind of luck the kid has been having lately. He just found it laying on the sidewalk outside the Speedway Gas Station. A lotto ticket, unscratched, just laying there free for the taking. Scotty picks it up for kicks, scratches away, and next thing he knows… five hundred dollars richer. I tell ya, my little brother has had quite the run of luck going here lately.” “Good for him. He had more than his share of tough times and bad breaks. He’s worked hard to clean himself up and I think its great that he’s finally catching a little bit of good luck. I just hope he doesn’t take that as a sign to run back to the casinos and gamble away his paycheck at a card table.” “I don’t think anyone has to worry about that. He seems quite the changed man. Just a few weeks ago, he was telling me how I needed to find God and…” Matt caught a glimpse of the clock on the microwave. “Damn! I gotta go, baby. I need to be at the airport by six at the latest. My flight leaves for up state at seven.” “Flight to catch?” “Have to testify tomorrow in court for a double assault case.” “Ok, then get going. I’ll finish loading the dishwasher.” She took a pot from his hand and kissed him. “I love you, sweetie. Good luck with the case. I’m sure you’ll wow the jury.” “Thanks.” He ran into the bedroom and grabbed his suitcase, then came back through the kitchen, stopping to kiss Mary one last time. “I’ll see you in a couple days. Love you.” He headed through the living room, and out the front door, stopping to yell back “bye baby” before closing it behind him. After he had left, Mary finished loading the dishwasher and set it to run on ‘normal cycle’. Content with the cleaning process, she dried her hands on a nearby towel and headed into the living room to play with Georgie while she watched some evening television. Rolling around on the floot with Georgie, the television remained mostly irrelevant background noise, that was until, through the baby’s laughter, she heard a name she wasn’t accustomed to hearing, and hadn’t heard for quite some time on television. Tchu. The name blasted through the room as part of a commercial for the wrestling organization PRIME… the one she knew Killean had joined over a year ago. The spot advertised an upcoming PPV event… a ‘great american nightmare’… then followed it up by promoting the “final stop” on the road to the PPV. An event in Lexington, Kentucky… the very next night… headlined by a six man tag match featuring a list of names, only two of which struck her memory. Killean Sirrajin. And Tchu… Matthew Ward… her husband. So much for the court case.
