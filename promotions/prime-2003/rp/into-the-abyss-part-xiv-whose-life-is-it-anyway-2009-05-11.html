Into The Abyss: Part XIV - Whose Life Is It Anyway?

11 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-05-09 "Where did it all go wrong?" He stared into the bottom of the tankard as the frothy remnants of a cold pint slithered down the side of the mug and gathered at the bottom. Glancing up he regarded Gloria as she spread out on the black leather sofa and nuzzled into the cushion at the armrest. Slipping from her fingers, the dying ember of the cigarette threatened to break away from the ashen tail of the cancer stick and stain the smooth varnished floor. At the last second she salvaged the butt and dropped it into the crystal ashtray atop a swivelling glass coffee table. Downing the froth and wiping his beard, Von Kelsig reached for the Guinness tap and tilted the steel mug as he poured himself another. Gloria reached out blindly, grasping at the air around the neck of a half finished bottle of vodka. Eventually her fingers grazed the glass and at the moment of contact her hand snapped around the neck like a Venus fly trap around a juicy fly and she slurped down the bitter spirit. The veneer of stagnation was rife in the den, highlighted by the overhanging waft of warm alcohol and tobacco smoke that would be expected of the atmosphere in any hovel. Beyond the fusty veil however lay a scene that would betray the assumption that life had continued on a downward spiral for the societal misfits. While Gloria downed vodka on the imported Italian leather, Von Kelsig leaned over the polished mahogany of the bar and rest his head upon his hand, then lazily tipped the steel mug to his lips. Rivulets of froth curled down his thick beard and with each gulp he felt a little more pleasant. The bar was nestled in the corner of the room, the wall behind it adorned with whiskies, spirits, gin and rum, while various lager, ale and beer logos were embossed onto the taps in front of him. A few framed nostalgic posters from numerous breweries hung above the bottles on the wall, while the rest of the room was decorated with a few paintings depicting certain ominous scenes. Across the room from Gloria a 70" LCD screen hung upon the wall, while a pool table stood at the far side of the room from the bar. By all accounts the setting for their haggard inebriation could have been any roadside bar befitting of the Bad Dog himself, never mind Von Kelsig and his beau. The lack of punters and bar staff coupled with the prominence with which he splayed himself across the bar indicated that this room wasn't a dead-end joint at the bottom of that spiral; it was his private bar room. "Hm?" Gloria offered a queried grunt, shifting onto her back and looking down her nose at Von Kelsig as he sat up and cracked his back, wearily rubbing his eyes. "Where did it all go wrong?" He growled, stretching his arms out and groaning. "What's the matter?" Gloria asked, checking the timer on the Blu Ray player below the big screen. 02:31. "What's the matter?" He echoed, stumbling off his bar stool and carefully staggering over to the sofa. As Gloria pulled her feet back he plopped down beside her, reaching for the vodka and taking a swig. "Life is the matter. Look at us, look where we are." He held the bottle up, gesturing at their surroundings. "We're in a nice big house in the country Angie, our house." "No, I mean...in life. All we wanted was a normal life. Correct?" He turned to her, his eyes narrowing sorrowfully. "Yeah, and we have it! Angelus when I first met you you were tied to a chair and smacked out of your eyeballs. We're sitting in a bought house, you're in the best shape of your life and your day job is to be a T.V. star! What's not normal about that?" Gloria pulled herself up and cuddled into him, taking the bottle of Smirnoff and necking a couple shots. "What's not normal?" He glanced at her, surprised by her naivety. "When did we last spend any time together?" "I, uh..." She cocked an eyebrow and gazed at the floor, finding in her inability to recall such a time that she had an idea what Von Kelsig may be suggesting. "Can't think? I'll tell you, it was at the start of the year. I was at a show at the end of December to gauge fan reaction for the board, then came back and we spent another week and a half together before I had to fly out to Phoenix for my first show on the fourteenth of January. Apart from the last week we haven't been together for more than a few days because I've been touring pretty much non-stop since that first show. Hell, I had to plead with them to let me take a couple weeks off before the next show, which by the way I've got to jet out to promote tomorrow. That's why I got buried under a gas station, baby." "Oh, right. I guess you got a point..." Gloria stared blankly ahead and took another sip of vodka. "So what's normal about that?" "It's a better life than the one we had, Angelus." She replied sternly. "Of course it is, but that's what I'm getting at. The life I had was a normal life, away from all the touring and the bumps and the fame. I was working reasonable hours and the effort was minimal compared to my wrestling career. Clean up in the morning, paperwork in the afternoon and oversee business at night? It was great! The club was always busy thanks to the regulars, then there were the fans that had seen me on television who would come along for a good time. The place was jumping!" Von Kelsig rest his head on the sofa and reminisced about the glory days of the Slaughterhouse, staring into the spot lights on the ceiling. "Yeah, it sounded like you had fun..." Gloria chirped in, pulling Von Kelsig back from the day dream. "Exactly my point. I had a normal life, and Reuben Blanco put a stop to it. The worst of it is nothing that I went through was my fault!" "Whose fault was it?" "The bastard that married my momma..." Von Kelsig sighed. "My father owed a great deal of money to a shark, a shark that worked for Blanco..." "I know, you've told me before hon'..." She kissed his chest and pulled his arm over her as she put the vodka bottle aside. "Fate threw Blanco at me because I'm not supposed to have a normal life. A decade ago it was different, I was free to go where I wanted or live how I pleased...but now I've got someone, I've got you Gloria. I never ever imagined feeling the way I do about someone like I do you, which makes it even harder to leave here every week and go off across the country, or the world, and wrestle a show." "But it's fine Angelus. I don't mind!" Gloria interrupted. "I know you'll be back at the same time every week, and even if we don't spend a lot of time together we still spend time together! It's not like I sit at home every hour of the day missing you, I keep busy at the salon and I'm taking extra shifts at the bar on the nights you're away from home. I still miss you but I can live with it if it means we can live in a nice house like this and have a normal life." "A normal life, hmph." Von Kelsig scoffed. He thought about the journey they had been on to get to this point. It had been over two years since their stint in rehab, and what was supposed to be a ten day detox took a few days longer after a chemical imbalance was diagnosed in Von Kelsig and immediately treated. The spectre of Reuben Blanco hung over them like a threatening thundercloud until they finally settled in Memphis. With little more than a couple changes of clothes and a clean bill of health they moved to Tennessee, taking up residence in the Peabody hotel and parting with over $7000 to live there while looking for work and taking extra nibbles from Von Kelsig's account whenever they had to. After his gift of a makeover, Gloria took on the job of receptionist in a local salon and part time shifts at a bar a couple of blocks away from the hotel. She didn't need the income but it gave her a little responsibility and purpose in life. Gone now was the wily white mane of hair, refined to a sleek bob. Her natural hair colour turned out to be chocolate brown, and in Von Kelsig's eyes she looked even sweeter. With her addiction long since kicked to the kerb, her once grey and lifeless eyes had sparkled back to a bright shade of green and her emaciated frame became a canvas of womanly curves that Von Kelsig fell in love and lust with. The only noticeable change in his appearance was a heavier build thanks to the return of his appetite after rehab, and an increase in muscle mass when he resumed training for the independent shows on which he was known as The Scarecrow, a fiend who wore a hessian sack over his head for every match. If only the fans knew. "But it's not normal...not the kind of normal I imagined." He finally piped up several minutes later, the break in silence startling Gloria from a light slumber in his arms. "Who, huh?" She yawned. "Maybe I don't deserve normality, or maybe I'm not supposed to have it. I tried leading a mediocre life and look what happened; it was snatched away from me. I'm not supposed to be just a face in the crowd, a member of the public." "Don't be silly honey." Gloria whispered. "Everyone is public." "Maybe it's all for a reason." He continued, ignoring Gloria. "Maybe I'm supposed to be Hessian, be on television for a purpose..." "Well of course you are, silly!" Gloria furrowed her brow and sat up, looking Von Kelsig in the eye. "Why wouldn't you be who you are? What a weird thing to say..." She chuckled and melted back into him again, getting cosy while he finished the last of the vodka. "I tried to be different, I tried to be someone else and it didn't work. Through the most twisted way imaginable I've been brought right back to where I started. It's like I'm destined to be this, like I'm not allowed a simple life, and why should I argue? It's blatantly obvious I'm supposed to be this...whatever I am." "I tried to be just a regular member of the public, be content with a "membership" that allows me to run the rat race and expect disappointment around every corner with what? A couple vacations and a nice car to distract me from the fact that I'm working almost exclusively to fill some other bastard's pockets? Are we all just drones working for some higher power that doesn't have a single concern for us other than how much money we can earn them? I'd rather slum it as a wrestler and work for me." He growled, palming himself in the chest. "What does the economy have to do with it?" Gloria asked, hiding a chortle. "A lot. There's just so many factors pointing me towards that damn ring. Companies like PRIME are a safe bet in these times because the crowd needs that fix to get them through their week, which is where I come in. I mean you don't see the alcohol and tobacco companies going bust in the credit crunch do you?" He asked. "I don't know...I just kinda go with the flow. I'm not into politics or business or whatever I just ride it out and pay a few dollars extra when the government start flapping. But if it's as bad as you say it is I dread to think what our kids will have to go through..." Gloria trailed off, her eyes wide in the hopes that Von Kelsig had noticed what she had slipped into her little rebuttal. "Exactly, is that what our future generations are being set up for? Is that why education is so important?" Oblivious. Disheartened and with a quiet sigh she buried her face into his chest once more. "Honestly, did Newton have a price in mind when he discovered gravity? Did Einstein demand compensation for his theories? Did Edison sell off his inventions for profit? Who gave permission for this knowledge to be sold to those that can afford it? Why are universities and institutes of higher learning charging kids to learn? How is humanity supposed to advance when its stuck in a capitalist mire?" Von Kelsig nudged Gloria, exciting a sleepy groan. "Because money talks, baby..." She slurred a little, the vodka finally hitting home. "I don't want that! I want a peaceful life but I'll be damned if I'm working for the Man in order to achieve it. So maybe I need to be more than just a member of the public, maybe I'm supposed to suffer like this and repent because I want my own selfish little paradise, when the selfless thing to do is live in Hell for the sake of others." "What do you mean, Angie?" Gloria asked, running her hands over his body as the effects of the alcohol began working its magic. "I guess what I'm trying to say is maybe I'm meant to struggle in life, maybe I'm sacrificing it for you, for others. I never had a meaningful relationship before I quit my job, then I hit the skids and find you and suddenly I'm wrestling again. Maybe the reason I'm so dominant in the ring is because it's my purpose. I'm not claiming to be Christ, but if I look back on my life there's an obvious pattern." "It's just how talented you are sweetheart, and don't let all that crap get you down. We're here now, you and me, regardless of time I'm happy." Gloria pat him on the stomach, cuddling him tightly. "But it's too distinct to be chance. On the street I'm a piece of shit, in the ring I'm a God. It's like opposite ends of a spectrum. People fear me, respect me and cheer me. I'm sending out a message to those fans, I'm a goddamn symbol of power. The fans, the people, they all come to see a wrestling show and then I walk out to the ring and shoot on the way I see things and how things should be done, then that bell rings and those things get done. Those folks walk away witnessing a show of authority and power and dominance and it festers in their mind. They go about their everyday lives and think about what they've seen and sometimes it'll find a niche in their existence." "What do you mean, like some kids going out and play-wrestling and breaking their friend's neck with a-...a Helle-...Helle..." "Hellevator honey. It's a Canadian backbreaker rack modified into a Ganso bomb...if that's easier to remember." He half-smiled, unable to see the perplexed look in her eyes as she stared at the floor but aware of it nonetheless. "But yeah, something like that." He continued. "Some poor sap might speak up against his boss at work, some kid in school might stand up to a bully. Someone somewhere will at some point benefit from something I've done...and then there's you darling. I don't even need to remind you what we've done for each other, you've given me a whole new lease on life. Someone to care for and love...I didn't even know I could have that kind of relationship with someone." "Aw, you're sweet......let's go to bed." Gloria grinned, a mischievous twinkle in her eye as she gazed up at him. "Soon baby, I'm just wrapping my head around this..." Von Kelsig shifted on his seat as she turned and lay her head across his lap, and let out a comfortable moan as he stroked her hair. "I think I was born to do this. Heh, the more I think about it the more sense it makes." "What's stopping you getting a normal day job again?" Gloria uttered, her eyes closed and her tone soft and monotonous as Von Kelsig stroked her cheek. "What, and risk losing it all again? I got lucky thanks to Tyler Rayne...real lucky." He bit the inside of his lip and gazed across the room, recalling just how lucky he really was. "Who's Tyler Rayne again?" Gloria asked. "Baby I'm going to start bringing you to the shows so you can see it all for yourself, we're gonna fill that pretty little brain up with some wrestling knowledge!" Von Kelsig chuckled. "What and push out all the fashion and girly things just so I know whose tights you're getting to grips with each week?" "Tyler Rayne got me my job, remember? Called me up at the end of last year and gave me the green light? He won that big match, the one I wrestled in back in March?" "Ohhhhh the goldfish tank match! Oh sure sweetie I remember now!" Gloria beamed, eliciting a sarcastic face palm from Von Kelsig. "Back to what I was saying...I got exposure now, I'm a known face on the street. Blanco is still out there and I don't doubt for one minute he's still got me on his mind but he can't do shit to me without it being traced now that people recognize me again. He actually got in contact with me a couple of weeks after I started at PRIME and hassled me for more money to pay him. I had to ask Rayne to step in and give me a little help..." "What did he do?" Gloria asked, holding a hand out and waiting until Von Kelsig clasped his large mitt around it before lowering it to her chest. "He called up pretending to be John Doe again and told Blanco to back off in case he scared me into running or doing something stupid, and set him up with more territory to focus on. Like I said though unless I disappeared back into that "crowd" there's nothing he can do to me, or you. I suppose that's just another consequence of that mediocre life. I guess the path I'm on is just fate." He sighed again and gazed at one of the paintings across the room of a dark stormy tumultuous sea, the waves crashing against a cliff atop which stood a lone figure lit up against a flash of lightning in the sky. "So does this mean you don't wanna spend time with me now?" Gloria narrowed her eyes, squinting up at Von Kelsig and smiling. "Of course I do sweetheart, but when I think about the circumstances...I guess I can live with the schedule, if it means avoiding all the trappings of public life consider my membership cancelled. Let me be a role model to the public that I can't be a part of, if it means I keep you so be it. Don't get me wrong I'd love to give you the good life, 2.4 kids and a white picket fence and all that jazz women dream of..." He stopped in mid-sentence as Gloria hushed him with a finger to the lips. "None of that matters, if we can do this a few days or a week out of every month then I'm happy, baby." "I know...but God only knows if guys like Wade, Chandler and Dawkins can get so much damn time off and have such a high billing on the cards I can damn sure get some of that thrown my way. You know Glory-baby, I've been the most consistent worker since the start of the year and..." "...everybody's talking about Hessian." Gloria interjected. "So you keep saying, you sure are buying into your own hype Angie, what with all those junk yard stunts and putting that poor guy through hell." "Poor guy nothing. I'm being paid to be a Champion so I'm going to be a Champion people remember. Plus that "poor guy" would be dumb enough to involve his girlfriend in his work, I wouldn't dream of putting you in harm's way like that baby." Von Kelsig added, chuckling at the thought of the feud with Jonathan Rhine. "Oh, how sweet of you......who are those guys you mentioned?" She asked, rubbing her eyes again and yawning. "My thoughts exactly..." Von Kelsig muttered under his breath. "Hm?" "Oh, nothing darlin'. Wade's the redneck, Chandler's the Asian chap and Dawkins is the Hawaiian. You've watched the shows, you know who they are!" Von Kelsig said, nudging her. "I know, but it's late and I'm tired..." She mumbled. "Let's get you to bed then sweetheart." He said, rising up with Gloria in his arms. Nudging the light switch with his elbow as he left the bar room he emerged from the darkness into the light of the hallway. Crack! Von Kelsig ducked and hissed, his eyes shut tight and holding back the thudding pain coursing through his forehead as he connected with the door frame. Looking up at him, Gloria's face softened and she reached up to pat his boo boo while he cursed under his breath and made for their bedroom with his love in his arms. Laying Gloria down on the king-sized bed he undressed and slipped under the covers with her, clapping his hands and killing the light. Buying into my own hype indeed, ha! he thought. "What about you baby?" Gloria muttered suddenly, clutching her pillow. "What about me?" He whispered. "Who are you?" She asked, regarding the characters on the wrestling show. Von Kelsig thought about the painting of the stormy cliff face and the lone figure down in the bar. "I'm the one they're all afraid of." He grinned. ---- She woke suddenly, staring at the ceiling and blinking away the strands of sleep trying to fuse her eyelids shut. Solid blue sky filled the convex skylight above the super king-sized bed, with not so much as a wisp of cloud in sight. Snapping her head to the right she checked the time before running her hands over her chest and realizing she was still wearing the same cotton tank top and underwear. Throwing the duvet aside Gloria stretched out like a feline as the door opened and a bearded face peered in, complete with a smile fit to rival the Cheshire cat. "Arrangements have been made. Get dressed and pack an overnighter." A quick flash of his teeth and he was gone. A curious grin grew across Gloria's face as she cocked an eyebrow and rose up out of bed chasing after Von Kelsig in her undies. "What do you mean arrangements?" She squealed, catching up with him on the stairs and leaping onto his back, latching around his neck and pressing her cheek against his neck. "Glory we don't have time for this. Get dressed and pack a bag, we're leaving in an hour." Came the order in a stern tone from the big man. Gloria slid off his back onto the stairs and tip-toed back to the top, still confused. "But...I...you've got your show and your promotion deally. Why have I got to pack?" She asked, maintaining that slight high-pitched squeal that hinted at hope. "When are you back to work?" "I'm supposed to be back at the salon tomorrow but I can call in some vacation days if you've got something planned........." Gloria leaned in, almost trying to draw Von Kelsig's plans out telepathically. "Alright, look..." He rolled his eyes, his barrel chest heaving with a sigh. "I made some calls to the office and got you passes and tickets for the show, including my promotional work. You're gonna need time off until the 25th or 26th." He couldn't help but feel happy for her as her face lit up with delight at the news. "Now go and get read-..." "OKI'MGOINGBABYILOVEYOU, WOOOOO!!" She was gone in a heartbeat, and Von Kelsig chuckled heartily as he made his way downstairs, laughing harder every time he heard the sound of a drawer or door creaking open and slamming shut as Gloria whizzed around the room like the Tasmanian Devil. He had given her an hour to be ready for them to catch a cab to the airport onto Minneapolis. She was dressed, packed and on vacation in twenty minutes after calling in a holiday from the salon. She emerged at the top of the stairs clad in Ugg boots, jeans and black designer turtle-neck, thin enough to accommodate the weather while giving the illusion of winter chic. The trim little package was wrapped up in a buttoned leather jacket. An iron grip with both hands helped little in dragging the large burgundy suitcase behind her, even if it was on wheels. On reaching the stairs she pouted and waited for Von Kelsig to step up and take the burden from her. The automatic male reaction kicked in and he stepped up before he thought to help. He was learning fast from this relationship just how women yielded the power of persuasion, and Gloria was no different to any other female. The call to Talent Relations to procure passes for Gloria made him think about his relationship with her. Through their hardships they had come together, and their little nuances and attractions drew them even closer. It wasn't that he was embarrassed to take her along to shows or personal appearances, but when guys like Tsonda and Rayne were courting supermodels and pedigree bitches around Von Kelsig felt almost inadequate to be presenting a former junkie as the love of his life. He barely entertained the notion, paying more mind to spending time with Gloria than suffering the backlash of personal opinion from any colleagues or peers. He could have his pick of the women. Women. Not the faked up gold-digging whores, the girls, that tried to tickle the fancies of guys like Tyler Rayne. Level-headed, mature, intimate, passionate women. He ended up with not so much a diamond in the rough as the roughest diamond, but Gloria was a gem nonetheless. After lugging her case downstairs, he propped it up at the front entrance and checked the ground floor over once more to be certain all was well. The house was nestled in woodland on the outskirts of Memphis, recommended to him by staff at the Peabody as a fine location for a celebrity such as himself to bide away from the trials and tribulations of public life. Indeed, a few minor television personalities occupied neighbouring houses to his, separated by the few acres of land around the property that offered the idyllic privacy craved by such personalities. That said, the house was his pride and joy. It was a love nest worthy of Cribs, that in itself a newly learned term imparted on him by Gloria. Their possessions, the layout and the look of the new home meant everything to them as a means of visualizing that freedom and life and love they so strongly desired while suffering torment at the hands of Reuben Blanco. Therein lay the reason he checked each room to ensure no freak accidents would occur in their absence, and with the all-clear embodied in a stiff thumbs up they left their little country manor for Minnesota and a week of PR and preparation, not forgetting Gloria's re-education of all things PRIME. ---- Gloria spent the best part of the journey bombarded with a torrent of trivia, a deluge of knowledge. Truth be told Von Kelsig had tried to familiarize her with his career on several occasions since they had left rehab, but while she retained little to no information that he passed on, her heart melted for the way he nattered on about bumps and spots, counter techniques, comparative statistics and the dog-eat-dog world with the passion of a boy. His devotion made her feel safe and secure, even if he was arguing why his beard should be the length it was and not shaved to as he put it, "mouth muff". Von Kelsig was set in his ways and she appreciated that and sought solace in the fact he never deviated from his beliefs. If she harboured any regrets about their relationship it was the circumstances through which they met, but at the same time if it hadn't been for those conditions they may never have ended up together as they were. The banality of life for a wrestler was lost on Gloria, who found the experience of flying and checking in to the hotel quite pleasant. If she had to repeat the process every week of the year for the last ten years she would have carried herself with the same hunched disdain as Von Kelsig. Only the soft skin of her hand clasped in the gentle grip of his brought an occasional smile to his otherwise stoic expression. Von Kelsig's first appointment was a radio interview at The Score 690 with Steve Czaban in the morning. She had barely gone a day in his boots but getting up at 5:30 in the morning to get ready for an interview at 7:00 had put her off the idea altogether, and that understanding brought a much greater awareness and appreciation for the life Von Kelsig was providing her with. The cab waiting for them in front of the hotel was plastered with stickers for The Score, and doubled as a private hire vehicle when the driver wasn't careering around Minneapolis collecting fares. The trip to the station was smooth however, and Gloria managed fifteen minutes or so of drifting in and out of a nap before the taxi came to a stop outside the front of the station on Utica Avenue South. "So what do you do here?" Gloria enquired, skipping alongside Von Kelsig as he paced methodically through the lobby of the building to the reception, seemingly lost in thought. "Oh, well..." How to sugar coat the dreary pre-show promotion, he wondered. "Sometimes it's just a case of going in and answering questions, sometimes they want you in-character but they always want us to sell the show like it was legal dope, especially around the local area to help drive business." He concluded. "Is today an in-character day?" She asked with no hint of sarcasm. "Yeah actually, it is. It's a morning slot so they know a lot of kids are listening whilst getting ready for school and whatnot. They get a better reaction if I'm heavily in-character so I lay it on thick for 'em, maybe spar a little with the hosts and so on. It's fun." He assured her, as he declared himself at reception and was pointed in the direction of the studio. The experience wasn't as entertaining as Gloria imagined it to be, as she listened to the interview from beyond the plexiglass-walled studio. On occasion she glanced around and smiled as Von Kelsig really threw himself into character, making use of hand gestures and poise to feed off the morning crew and portray Hessian effectively over the airwaves to the listeners. Czabe played the part of the sarcastic host, getting in on the fight talk and even taking Von Kelsig up on the challenge of making him wince with his best open-handed chop. Needless to say Czabe's hand was red and stinging before Von Kelsig's chest had even begun to welt. Chewing bubblegum and flicking through a copy of Cosmo, Gloria seemed more interested in the secrets to healthier looking hair than the pain Jonathan Rhine was going to suffer at UltraViolence. Steve Czaban sold the event like a pro, calling on all the listeners in the key demo to make sure they had tickets for the pay-per-view on the 24th at the Target Center. A few more in-character threats to Rhine and the offer of a chop to Czabe's "rippling-for-all-the-wrong-reasons chest" and Von Kelsig was out of there by 7:50. "That was great!" Gloria beamed, jumping up out of her seat and planting the magazine onto the flat-pack coffee table with a thud. "Thanks baby." Von Kelsig grinned back, nodding for her to follow as he made his way out of the suite down the corridor back to the main entrance. As they entered the lobby once more, Von Kelsig groaned at the sight that greeted him beyond the front doors. A large group of kids, anywhere between 15 and 19 had gathered in front of the station and were peering through the glass for any sign of the Murder Show. A curly-haired kid in a beanie was the first to spy the Intense Champion approaching them, and chinked his lip ring on the glass as he called out for Hessian. That set off a chain reaction and soon the whole squad of kids, who would end up being several minutes late for class by the time they got to school and college respectively, were cheering for the Intense Champion and forming a semi-circle of pumping fists and pens and paper around the entrance. "Yikes." "Don't worry." Von Kelsig reassured her. "Just act like you're my agent or something, squeeze straight through to the cab and don't pay any attention to them. I'll ham it up and maybe we'll get out of here nice and sharp." She took a deep breath as Von Kelsig stepped ahead and opened the door for her. The semi-circular student body swarmed closer as Gloria pushed past them to the waiting cab. Thankfully they pushed past her to get to Von Kelsig, and she was at the cab in moments. Glancing back she watched as he held his hands out in a futile attempt to calm the crowd, brushing away the pens hovering around his face like wingless dragonflies. She giggled at the thought of him stuck in a crowd of children and turned to the cab, opening the door and sliding in. With one hand on the door handle she greeted the driver as he welcomed her back and made a comment regarding Von Kelsig's intimidating radio persona. She engaged in conversation with him and seconds later pulled hard on the door to slam it shut... ...Unaware that Von Kelsig had somehow pulled himself through the crowd to the cab, signing autographs and taking photos on the way to reach the safe haven of the cab. What made her aware of his presence at the door was the sickening crack that his fingers made after they wrapped around the top of the door that Gloria promptly slammed shut. Crunch! At once the crowd hushed. Gloria was stunned into silence. There before her, lodged in the closed door were four fat fingers, and the tip of a thumb around the incline of the jamb. She gasped, unable to put into words the shock that she was feeling. The kids surrounding Von Kelsig were just as quiet, some flipping out their cell phones to capture the moment on camera. All the while, Von Kelsig himself was burning through a range of emotions. The first was a startled fright at the sensation of his fingers jamming, followed quickly by anger and then suppressed by an overpowering calm. Hessian. He told himself. Hessian wouldn't yelp. Don't yelp. His face flushed red, but the expression was almost vacant. Behind his pursed lips, his jaw was locked so tightly it threatened to cramp. The kids surrounding him were in awe at the sight of the 7'2" man with his hand caught in the door, seemingly unaware of the agony that should have been coursing through his fingers. Inside the cab, Gloria's stomach turned as Von Kelsig calmly bent down to look through the window. He brought up his other hand and maintaining the composure of a monk tapped lightly on the window. "Excuse me ma'am? Would you mind?" Six words delivered with the propensity for tranquillity often displayed by...oh, Superman? At least in the student's minds. Gloria had gone slightly pale at the sight of the fingers wiggling in the closed door, and with an unnecessary carefulness popped the door open and watched in awe as Von Kelsig removed his hand from the trapping and simply wiggled his fingers into a fist. A deep welt ran along the fingers and the bones felt like fragments of granite scraping around, though he held the expression of the Man of Steel and turned to the kids who were only now talking amongst themselves after witnessing the accident. "Keep that in mind when you're watching me go toe to toe with Jonathan Rhine guys. It's called UltraViolence for a reason." With that he disappeared into the cab and closed the door gently, waving goodbye to the kids with the broken hand while they saw him off behind the lens of their megapixel cameras. It was only when the cab reached the end of the street and turned the corner that it filled with a monstrous guttural howl of pain, and Von Kelsig shook his hand as though it were on fire, tears welling up in his eyes. It was then that Gloria understood perfectly what Von Kelsig had meant when he talked about his life and the life of Hessian and the clear distinction between his physical pain and the pain that tormented his soul, all in the name of a better life for her. With her. She held as much of his massive frame in her little arms as she could manage as they drove back to the hotel for Von Kelsig to begin preparing for his match. To prepare for Jonathan Rhine. To prepare for Hessian. To prepare for The Murder Show.
