One. Two. Three. Ring The Bell.

15 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 15th, 2005 One Dinner’s awful. Then again, I’d be hard-pressed to find a time when it hasn’t been. Clyde cooks like shit, and the last time we were paid enough to eat a real meal…well, shit…let’s just say it’s been a while. And if you expect me to don an apron and go all Martha Stewart on this poor excuse for dog food, you don’t know me very well. Come to think of it, maybe that’s a good thing. You don’t want to know me. You don’t want to know the man I am inside. You just want to know the guy you see on TV each week, who gets hit in the balls with something or beaten on by another wrestler because his idiot partner made it happen. You don’t want to know a damn thing about me that doesn’t have to do with comedy, because I don’t exist to you outside of that. But maybe I’ve painted myself into a corner. Hey, that’s completely my fault. I can take full blame for that. I’m the one who stuck myself with Clyde and somehow managed to get over in an industry where technical wrestlers are shat on day in and day out. I’m the one who wrestled through injury to entertain the fans who paid good money to see me. And I’m not gonna say I’m the only guy to ever work hurt or take one for the team in order to get a job, because that would make me a liar, but what gets me is that after all we do, people always find a way to bitch and moan about how we don’t wrestle to our full potential. Jesus Christ. First off, you’re the same group of people who don’t want to see us if we’re not falling into something, and now we don’t wrestle well enough for your liking? I’m sorry, but you cannot have your fucking cake and eat it too. I’ve sacrificed my pride and dignity to get some laughs out of the crowd. I’ve sacrificed my body by pushing it to the limit each and every week. I’ve sacrificed my life by giving it up for you, and you still want more from me. You want more than I can give, and you keep asking for it again and again. Enough is e-goddamn-nough. I don’t know what it is with you smarky fucks, but you’re the biggest bunch of whiny dickheads I’ve ever had the displeasure of hearing about. Nothing’s ever good enough for any of you, is it? If we’re being too funny, we’re not good enough in the ring. If we’re too good in the ring, we’re not being funny enough. If we’re funny AND working our asses off, you’ll still find something wrong. Like maybe we don’t have the right look, or we need to magically be twenty years younger, or something. Anything. Because it’s all about complaining with you people, isn’t it? And through all of this, I shovel crap down my throat and pretend one day it’ll get better. Clyde smiles at me between mouthfuls, and all I can think of is how fucking mundane everything’s become. It’s all just one big loop, and we keep going through the same motions, the same days, the same lives, and not a damn thing changes. Nothing ever gets better. No one ever stops complaining. We all live, we all die, and then one day someone digs us up and pretends we were great heroes because they don’t know a damn thing about us beyond what they want to believe. Talk about déjà-fucking-vu. Two Drip. Drip. Drip. The tap’s been leaking since we got to this place, so I figure I may as well drown it out for a while. The water burns at first, but I stop feeling it after a few seconds, and slowly my eyes close. Sirrajin. Vetra. Williams. Sure, thinking of three other men while in the shower might not be the straightest thing you can do, but when you’ve been in the business as long as I have, wrestling isn’t far from mind. And speaking of wrestling, that card has got to be a mistake. Former and current PRIME champions on each side, and then one-half of a team that’s seen about three wins in the last year. Either PRIME hates Killean as much as it hates me, or someone upstairs is puffing a little too heavy on the wacky tobaccy. But I’m there, and I guess that’s that. The smart thing to do would be to look at this thing as I would any other match. And since I’m not Clyde, that’s exactly what I plan on doing. First off, let’s take a look at my opponents: a guy who can’t seem to stop winning, and a guy who can’t seem to…well, let’s just leave it at that. So maybe Petey has more losses than any three wrestlers in PRIME have had matches, but the bastard’s still got something like ten wins under his belt. That’s still more wins than I’ve had matches, at least. Which isn’t saying much right now, all things considered, but still… And then we have Hoyt. I remember that guy from a few years back when we tried to rid OSW of a certain female lupine and her band of merry chums. Granted, that didn’t work out the way we planned, and maybe if he had spent more time worrying about wrestling matches instead of wrestling religion away from sanity, we’d have succeeded. But maybe it’s for the best. After all, we wouldn’t have gotten to see her walk out of PRIME if her career had been cut short like that. And that’s a memory we’ll all hold dear to our hearts. Which brings me to my partner. All I can say is, thank god it’s not Clyde. Anyone but him. After that shit he pulled last week, he can’t be trusted for a while. But Killean, on the other hand…he could be someone a little more beneficial. Yeah, the guy got his ass handed to him by Hoyt, but I’m almost certain he bested God’s Left Nut on at least one occasion. Besides, he was trained by the guy who beat the guy I trained, so there has to be some kind of forced connection in all that. Now this is where I come in. I honestly don’t know why or how I’m in the match, but I do know that this week I will be giving the fight of my life. This is my chance…this is my last chance to show the world that I’m not just some guy to point and laugh at, and I’m not just some guy who is destined to watch others rise while I fall. I may not have some catchy name or flashy gimmick, but I’ll be damned if the world doesn’t see something from James Farwell that they’ve never seen before. PRIME. Wednesday, November 16th, 2005. ReVolution 81. Mark it on your calendars. That’s the day I beat your Universal Champion. Three I had to get out of that place. Clyde was driving me crazy with that damned Wookie of his, and if I had to spend another minute listening to how it apparently killed a small dog last night, I’d finish Clyde off myself. But the walk did me good. It’s been a while since I really got out and walked for a while. Kind of ironic that I picked up my first pack of cigarettes in a year halfway through it, though, but the feeling you get from that first drag…it’s indescribable. Just like there’s not a damn thing else in the world. And for a moment there isn’t. There’s nothing but the smoke filling my lungs. And just like that, I realize things aren’t how they used to be. After a few excruciating seconds that feel like I’m bringing up an organ or two, I throw the pack into the bushes. I can’t fall back into old habits now, not right when I’m so close I can taste it. I don’t even know what ‘it’ is, but it’s on my lips, and it’s something much more addictive than nicotine could ever hope to be. It’s a taste of something that far too many people have had before me, but it still tastes just as sweet as if I were the first. Greatness. Stardom. Immortality. In one night, everything can change. In a single night, I can go from being no one to being better than everyone. All I have to do is push myself beyond what I am. For one single night. All I have to do is give every fan in the world the things they’ve been asking for. The hopeless, inhuman feats they’ve been demanding of us for years. For one night, all I have to do is the impossible. All I have to do is be the best in the world. For one night, I will be Superman. Fuck Kryptonite. Ring The Bell So I guess this is about the time I’m supposed to come to some sort of realization--an epiphany, if you will. I’m supposed to have it all figured out and wrapped up in a neat little package, because everyone likes finality. Everyone likes reason and closure and all those little things that make us warm inside. Sorry to disappoint, but I don’t deal in revelations. I deal in fact and realism, and the truth of the matter is, I hate all those whiny little fucks who spend their time on the Internet talking about how so-and-so is holding someone else down, or how bad a match was, or how much better they could do if only someone would buy one of their goddamn backyard wrestling videos. But in the same moment, I love them. I have to, otherwise I wouldn’t put up with the bullshit that I do. I love the fans, and I love the business, but at the same time, they’re the worst part of it all. They push us past our physical capabilities. They hate us for no reason other than to be spiteful. They disrespect us, and place impossible demands on us night after night. They spit on us when we fall, and wait for us to stumble when we’re on top. They are the reason we die young, and stick ourselves with needle after needle, and force ourselves to wrestle just one more match than we should. They’re the death of us. And yet, I wouldn’t give up the thrill of being in the ring - being in front of all those same fans who could love me one night and hate me the next for no reason other than popular opinion - for anything else in the world. Goddamn, I’m sentimental.
