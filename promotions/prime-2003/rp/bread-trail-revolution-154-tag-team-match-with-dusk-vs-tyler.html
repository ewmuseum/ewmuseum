Bread Trail (ReVolution 154, Tag Team Match with Dusk vs. Tyler Rayne and Chandler Tsonda)

15 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March, 15th, 2008 Post Culture Shock 2008 – Night Two The low rumble of satisfied fans filled the night's warm air in Monterrey, Mexico. Thousands of attendees exited the Arena Monterrey like a colossal herd of grinning cattle, branching off in every possible direction in search of friends, vehicles, and after-show beverages. Among the massive ruckus, one remained quiet, her brown leather booted feet pointed in a straight, specific direction. Her thin red lips formed an amused grin, adjusting a worn, red ball cap over her wavy, much redder hair, taking confident steps across the street. “Well ain't that somethin'...” * * * * * * * “She sure is takin' 'er time...” “Show a li'l patience, Cliff! Cain't rush these things.” “Could've at least gone fer a cold brew...” “We'll grab a cold one just as soon as Becky gits back. Hell, I'll even buy ya the first round.” “Yer a lyin' sonnuva bitch, Horace.” “Don't I know it.” Cliff's monstrous frame shifted restlessly in the front seat of their green, 1995 Dodge Caravan. Horace sat comfortably in shotgun, cowboy boots resting on the dashboard as he picked the grime from his fingernails away with the tip of a large Bowie knife. The loud rolling of the van's side door turned their as the beautiful, fiery frame of Becky stepped inside, taking a quick seat behind them. “Guess,” she said with a curt smile. Horace rolled his eyes, returning to his frontier-style manicure. “Ya found 'im drunk in some bar an' finished 'im off to save us the trouble?” guessed Cliff, drenched in sarcasm. “Nope,” she replied happily, revealing a thrice-folded piece of paper, the letters “P-R-I-M-E” on the cover. She handed the pamphlet to Horace, who furled his eyebrows in confusion. “Yer shittin' me,” he mumbled, opening the pamphlet. “Wade Elliott is in fucking PRIME,” she revealed, folding her arms over her chest. Cliff suddenly regained interest, leaning across to get a better look at the paper. “Since when?” asked the big man. “Don't know,” answered the redhead. “But it's true. I saw him compete in some huge match with fifty something people. Did pretty well for himself.” “That where you been this whole time?” “Shee-it, how fuckin' stupid can ya be!?” hollered Horace, his mouth in a toothy grin of disbelief. “Son've a bitch just gave us a fuckin' bread trail to follow! Look! There's a show schedule right 'ere!” “Don't forget, the asshole still thinks we're all fuckin' dead.” added Cliff. “Probably why he wasn't scared to put himself in the spotlight, with his real name even,” said Becky. “Well, we ain't fuckin' dead, are we?” piped Horace, folding the pamphlet and tucking it into his jeans pocket. “Turn that key, Cliff! We're headin' back 'cross the border!” “Where exactly, boss?” asked Cliff with a thin-lipped smile, turning the key and bringing the Caravan to life. “The Lone-Star State!” whooped Horace, tipping his tan cowboy hat upward. “Dallas, in particular!” Cliff flicked on the headlights before pulling out onto the street. He stepped on the gas, propelling the van in a northern direction “This man-hunt just got a whole lot simpler,” said Horace, returning the Bowie knife to his fingernails. “Now it ain't a matter've where...” Becky swung her legs onto the van's bench seat, lounging against the window and tipping her hat over her eyes. “It's a matter of when...”
