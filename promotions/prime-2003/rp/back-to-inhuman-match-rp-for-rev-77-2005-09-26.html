Date: 9/26/05 There had existed some sort of inhuman nature in the foundation of his being, and from it had been born many emotionless, and in there own way grotesque, events. Though it had consumed his soul in a way that seemed eternal, he had found himself miles from cold and unfeeling, in a place that was far more frigid than his heartless world had been. Somehow, she had steadily done what he once had known was only wishful thinking on his part. She had taken a great, but ultimately impossible idea and had made it real. She had made a human being out of an inhuman being. Every moment that passed with her, he had loved each bit with a forceful passion. He had breathed with appreciation every bit of life she had fiven him, and now… with it gone… he longed to cleanse himself of its memory. And to do so, he had stained his hands with the blood of one old friend. Though the first steps in the process of cleansing himself of his human nature had begun at ReVolution 75, and carried through to a second victory in the six-person tag match, the real beginning of his return to the cold indifference he thrived upon had come with one swing of steel and the splitting of one skull. And the blood that tainted his hands, rolling across his palms, between his fingers, was the blood that signified the true beginning to his rebirth as the inhuman being. It was, perhaps, unfortunate for Killean Sirrajin that his path had first crossed that of Matt Ward’s nearly three years earlier in the Underground. Though, likely, The Supreme Machine’s true misfortune laid in the second time the men had crossed path, a year ago, when Killean had sought help and guidance from Tchu. The first rule of Psychology was to never compromise the patient/doctor relationship. It was essential, that the only present relationship was that of doctor to patient. Romantic partners, family, friends… none should ever find themselves serving double duty as a psychologist’s patient. Matt had put that code of ethic on the back burner, sacrificed his reputation in the world of psychology… all in order to help a former enemy who had found himself fallen on ‘hard times’. That’s what Killean thought they were… hard times. That’s why Killean had needed the help. He had needed someone to talk to, so he could make it through his ‘tragic’ loss. Tragic loss. Hard times. Right. Slowly, Matt reached up and pulled a couple of the few remaining knick knacks and decorations from the large oak shelving unit in his office. Former office. The top of his desk was a barren tundra, the sunlight pouring in from the windows sliding into the grain of the wood, its distinct glow separating it from the rest of the room. The drawers were also empty, their contents caged in various boxes that sat stacked like pillars near the doorway. The only of his belongings that remained in the room were a few odd things along the top shelf. Among them was his Ph.D. in psychology, and the B.A. he had received at Bowling Green State University years before he had ever imagined carrying it through to a doctorate. So it was with little fanfare or any sort of emotion that he found himself giving it up. His lifeless thoughts were interrupted by a knock at his cracked open door. Immediately following the knock, the sound of hinges creaking struck his ears. Why knock if you’re not gonna bother to wait for someone to tell you its ok to come in? His annoyance quickly evaporated. The door had been left hanging open a couple inches, besides, it wasn’t his anymore. “Hey, you must be Matt. Name’s Roger.” The man made his way over to Matt, stepping between the columns of packed boxes. “Good to meet you.” Roger extended his hand. Matt accepted the offer. “Nice to meet you, too.” With a quick, vacant glance, Matt took in a senseless first impression of the man who would be calling the office home. Roger was a short man, slightly heavy, but two-thirds of America was, so coupled with his George Clooney haircut and standard black suit, white shirt, and red tie… he was all in all a very average man. Not that anything was wrong with that. Average was good, but also gone. “So, what do you think of the office? Probably a dumb question, if you’re paying the rent for it, you must be pretty happy with it.” “Haha.” Well, the laugh wasn’t average, it sounded like a cross between a weezing sound and the scream you might make if someone smashed your hand with a scolding hot frying pan. “Yeah, it’s a nice little place. I like the hunter green walls and the wood accents. Plus, the location is great and the rent is affordable.” “Yeah, I always liked the place. I looked around for about a month before I found this one, and I never second guessed the choice. It was a solid little place.” “So, with all that said, the realtor told me that you had only had the place for about a year, and had rather suddenly put it back up for rent on the market. If you don’t mind me asking, why ya ditchin’ the place?” Matt didn’t really mind the question, though he couldn’t say he really desired to get into the true reason as to why he was abandoning the office. Perhaps Roger would accept the response that would likely only raise more questions, and just let the issue die. “Well, I’m finished with the profession of psychology, so I don’t really need the office anymore.” “Done with psychology, huh? You look pretty young? How long had you had your practice?” “About as long as I’ve had this office.” Of course, no luck, Roger was the prying type. He should have figured as much, afterall, he had been a psychologists… wasn’t he supposed to see that sort of thing in people? Besides, weren’t most people too curious by nature? Just another reason to give up on the profession… he wasn’t even any good at it anymore. “So, I guess a little over a year.” “So after all suffering through all those years of schooling, what’s got ya wanting to call it quits?” “I’ve just had enough of it. Other things have come up in life, I guess you’d say. And on top of that sort of thing, is the general fact that I was a bad psychologist.” “Did your patients end up crazier than when they came in?” Roger unleashed another one of his disturbing laughs, despite the fact that nothing he had said was funny. “No, but I don’t think I was helping anyone all that much. I also kinda broke the code of ethics to pieces. I had an old… friend… who came to me for help, and I counseled him for several months.” “Yeah…” the humor in Roger seemed to have vanished. “I imagine that wasn’t a very good idea.” “Don’t worry, I took care of it.” * * * ~ An hour later ~ He closed his cell phone and put it back into his pocket. His younger brother, Scott, had left a voice mail message saying something to the affect of… 'Hey bro, saw what you did to Killean at PRIME. I dunno what the hell is going on, but I’m going to be in town next week and I definitely need to know what the hell is going on. So keep an hour or two open for lunch or dinner or something. Later.' Scott had moved out of town several months earlier. He had finally found a girl who had settled his crazy, and at most times, stupid soul. His partying, his gambling… all of it had taken a back seat to a girl by the name of Laura, who had managed to turn his life around. That was something Matt, nor his mother and father, had been able to do for Scotty. He and Laura had moved due to a change in Laura’s job, but Scott had adjusted quickly, finding a job of his own, utilising his journalism degree at the city’s main newspaper. Scotty had held the job for three months now… and for him, that was the best he had managed since losing his job with a Pro Wrestling magazine that he had landed straight out of college. Scott had a lot going for him. He was putting his education to good use, he had beaten his demons, and he had a beautiful girl to come home to at the end of every single day. It was all the same for Scotty, every day, the same thing… but each time it was sweeter because of the memory that preceeded it. For the first time in his life, Scott had a lot going for him. It made Matt fucking sick. Scott was going to ‘talk with him’? Scott was going to try and sort out his life? It’s illogic nature defied possibility, and yet it appeared to be just one short week away. “Sir…” the words reminded him that he was still alive, breathing at least. “would you like a refill?” The waiter stood over the table with one hand behind his back, the other extended, motioning towards the empty glass that sat in front of Matt. “No… I’m good… thanks.” Matt watched as the waiter smiled, nodded, and walked across the rather desolate restaurant. He let his gaze follow the waiter to one of the only two other tables that were in some way inhabited. The particular table was occupied by a lone man, sitting in his hundred dollar polo shirt and khakis, sipping a cup of coffee, reading what appeared to be an issue of ‘Time’ magazine, but Matt couldn’t be sure. He had seen an issue of ‘Time’ in years. The gentleman appeared to be giving the waiter a hard time. What could possibly be so important that might accompany a cup of coffee escaped Matt’s comprehension. Still, apparently for this guy, something was worth throwing a fit about in public. Odd to think that a man could hate his life and carry so much rage over the fact that his damn croissant was a couple minutes late being delivered. It’s a roll. Some bread. It’s supposed to be out in five minutes, it shows up in eigh instead. And people flip out about it. Why let it slide when there’s always the option to start yelling and cursing, slamming fists on the table, so silverwear and dishes ring the hatred through the entire restaurant. As if the biggest catastrophe in life that has to be faced is a fucking roll that took a couple minutes too long to prepare. It was worthy of Matt shifting his glance, somewhere away from the revolting stupidity. But that was hard to do. Across the restaurant, at the only other populated table were a young couple, probably in their late teens, but perhaps early twenties, just with too much innocence to make them look like they belonged anywhere but at the high school football game on Friday night, holding hands under the bleachers, just killing time flirting until they meet up with their friends after the game at the local Casano’s Pizza. As the two sat at the table, the boy leaned across the table holding out his hands, spreading his arms, most likely pleading his case in an effort to atone for some mistake. She seemed to be rather unreceptive, and her lack of immediate forgiveness only seen to enrage him. A moment later, Matt heard a string of profanities, a yelled whisper, intended to express his anger but with the hope that it would reach the ears of no one else. Unfortunately, those words made it Matt’s way, yet they fell on deaf ears. Two kids, barely out of school, probably arguing over whether or not he had stared at the girl they had passed coming into the restaurant. She was mad because he had wondering eyes. He was mad because she was right, but it wasn’t a big deal. It was just looking, not touching, not hurting anybody. It was a world ‘falling’ apart for a set of eyes locked onto one little ass that they shouldn’t have been. It was worse than the guy having a heart attack over his missing croissant. A bunch of problems that didn’t amount to a bit of anything. Again, Matt looked away, staring off aimlessly at whatever bit of the back kitchen area he could make out. As his vision blurred, and he longed for a belated roll or a jealous girlfriend, the bickering young couple he had noticed earlier made their way over to his table. “Sir, you’re Tchu from…” “Yeah.” It had been a while since anyone had recognized him while out and about. In the nearly two years it had been since he had last graced a television screen, he had faded from the public eye. PRIME had once again introduced him to some measure of fame, and for the first time in a long time, he was faced with the task of being the stunning celebrity. In his last run as a professional wrestler, he would have grinned and posed for pictures, then shook hands and said he had to get home to his babyto curl up and make new memories… but there were no new memories to make this time. “That’s me.” “My girlfriend and I were just wondering if maybe we could get an autograph and a picture or two. We’ve got a camera with…” “No. No autographs and no pictures.” “Wh…why not?” “Cuz you two were sitting over there screaming and yelling at each other, complaining to each other, and I was sitting over here trying to enjoy my dinner. You don’t have a damn reason to come in here and be upset about anything. You’re over there hating life, I’m trying to choke down a bad deal. Now you’re asking for autographs? If you want a picture, give me the camera.” “What?” The boy looked utterly confused by the sudden, odd change in conversation. His girlfriend looked nervous, perhaps even a little scared. “Give me the camera.” “We wanted to get a pic…” “Camera.” Matt held out his hand. Without saying a word, the girl reached into her purse and pulled out a small digital camera, handing it to the inhuman being. “Now stand together and smile like you ought to be.” The couple took a step closer together, and the young man put his arm around his girlfriend, both forced smiles through their confusion. Matt fumbled with the camera, finally hit the right button, and watched as the flash exploded in their eyes. For another minute, he studied the camer, then put the lense cap back on and returned the camera to the girl. “Did you say something about an autograph too?” “Yes… please.” The boy fumbled for a pen in his pocket. Matt took the pen and grabbed an unused napkin from the table next to his. He scribbled on the paper, then handed them back to the boy. “There ya go.” As the young couple stood frozen, Matt grabbed the check from off the table, gave it a quick look and left enough cash to cover the bill and tip. Then he started to exist towards the restaurant, but not before turning back to the two. “A new picture is like a new memory.” Matt left, saying nothing more. “That was…” The young man had no real idea how to describe what had just occurred. “Let me see the camera, sweetie.” He held out his left hand and the girl placed the requested object in his grasp. With a quick flip of a switch, the boy turned the camera on and opened the screen to check the new picture. When he hit the ‘back’ button to view the most recent picture, nothing popped up on the sceen. He looked to the top corner and saw that the memory card said that no pictures were stored on its memory. “What the hell?” “What is it?” She leaned over his shoulder in curiousity. “There’s no picture. He must have deleted it by accident or something.” Instinctively, he checked the other souvenir they had received. On the napkin Matt had left them were the simple words… There are no more memories * * * ~ Twenty minutes later ~ He pulled into the drive, his mind fixated on his upcoming JITC match against Tyler Lopez. It was the second round, the next step towards the finals at KoK. It was necessary to be like every other. His chance to escape. And further escapes required further victories. Fight, escape, win, fight escape win. It was a cycle, but like all cycles, it was crucial that each step was hit in stride. Matt turned the key and the engine silenced, coming to stop. He decided to leave the boxes in the trunk, headed into the house, and was greeted at the door by Mary, holding Georgie in her arms. “How’s my baby?” He kissed Mary softly on the lips. “I’m good.” “And how’s my other baby?” he softly touched the head of Georgie with his fingertips. “Tired. I was just getting ready to put her to bed when I heard your car pull up.” “Sorry I’m so late. The guy who’s moving into my office was a little bit on the talkative side.” “Moving into your office?” “I’ll explain it in the morning, sweetheart.” Matthew kissed her again, this time on the forehead. “Ok, well, I’m gonna put Georgie to bed, I think she’s pretty tired. Then I think I’m heading to bed too. Might watch a little television… but I’m pretty exhausted. “Alright. I’ll join ya in a few minutes. I just want to grab a quick bite to eat. Dinner was a little lacking in decency.” Mary nodded in acknowledgement then headed to the bedroom. Matthew walked into the kitchen and made his way to the fridge. On the front was a note that said 'Killean called.' He pulled the paper off of the refrigerator. “Baby” he yelled in to the bedroom, “what did Killean say when he…” Matthew stopped in mid sentence… it would be useless he remembered. “What did you say, Matthew?” “Nothing, sweetie.” He looked at the paper, then flipped it over. The back read… 'Killean says no need to call back. He’ll see you on Wednesday. He promises.' Then a note to herself… 'Remember to tell Matthew'
