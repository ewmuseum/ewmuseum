Date: Feb, 2005 GloriousWritten: February, 2005. 'My anger is a form of madness,So I'd rather have hope than sadness... And you said something,You said something stupid like;Love steals us from loneliness.' - Idlewild. I - I'd Rather Have Hope Than Sadness. Despite the apathy the drenches her outward persona, there are things - and people - in life that Karina Wolfenden cares for. Deeper than she'd ever let on, or maybe even admit to herself. 'Ugh,' she'd regularly groan when an answering machine message gave the date of the next family function she was obliged to attend. And each warning of a visit from her parents would usually garner an eye-rolling, 'oh, yay.' But they were her family. They shared more than just a surname with her. To most, she was simply Karina Wolfenden - the wrestler - the unsuspecting young woman turned stiff-kicking daredevil of the North American Indy scene, turned surprise national star with PRIME. All they knew was what she wanted to show them on camera... and nothing else would filter through her cold, ice-queen exterior. To Lawrence and Sandra, she was the daughter they'd watch grow. From fertilised egg, to blossoming, strongly independent young woman, their memories made the timeline in-between. And then there was Max. 'What's the best way to beat a double homicide rap?' she'd prank call him at his firm with, usually 5 minutes or so after the aforementioned warning call. Despite being her polar opposite, he'd been the one person who helped her most through the teenage years. The back-and-forth, sibling jabs were still there, both happy to try and score points on the other, but Kari knew she could always rely on him. When she needed someone to stand beside her and support her opinion, he was there. When she needed a buffer between herself and the often overbearing parents, he took on the role, steadfastly. And then there was Henrik. 'This will work,' she assured him when they were first parted. 'I've never made anything work before... really... but this will.' Karina's attention span was something which had distanced her from fiends, seen her flunk out of college, and left her never having worked a proper job - for even a day - in her 23 years of life. She was all boundless enthusiasm initially, before novelties wore off, and the apathy of familiarity set in. Relationships with Karina were hell to be part of... even for those who'd overcome her initial, barbed exterior and actually made themselves part of her daily life. 'Busy,' she'd leave on their machine, 5 minutes before they were meant to meet. 'Be back in a few days.' Her life was lived at break-neck speed, and anyone wanting to be an obstacle would need to take the full-brunt of a collision. Henrik was different. He wasn't the one doing the chasing. He wasn't the one bobbing and weaving his words to try and make them penetrate Karina's closed-off ears. He wasn't the one trying to convince her that things would work, and that it was worth the effort expended, and the time invested. Karina was the one who laid herself bare - emotionally. Karina was the one left fumbling for the words to properly release the feelings bubbling under her frosty exterior. Karina was the one who risked rejection, risked having her very soul shoved back down her throat, forcing her to gag. 'If that is what you believe,' he spoke right back into her sincere eyes, their noses joining in union. 'Then I can only believe it, completely.' That was 3 months ago, when what was meant to have been a carefree jaunt to Lapland during a wrestling time-out had her flying back a different woman. Wanting to escape from it all, finding purification in the vast lands of the Sami, Karina had only met him by sheer universal will. A poet, something which he would never advertise amongst the Nordic macho of his village - to the point where even his flimsy pad of paper was safely hidden - he would find inspiration in the endless scenes that surrounded him. While many needed sounds, sights, and experiences to fuel there imagination, all Henrik had to do was stare out into the nothingness that bordered his life, and the words flowed effortlessly. He lived on the outer rim of the galaxy... where only a few would stray, never to return. And that was his angle - a man who only knew what he could see, and had to imagine the rest of the world, naively. It was there, floating in the blustery void between his world and the unknown, that he met the alien visitor that was Karina Wolfenden. In a village of dull, mat brown hair, and simple, animal hide clothing, she was the living equivalent of a bizarrely lit UFO. With a mane of platinum blonde and shocking pink, and with jet black and regal purple padding out her sleek frame, she instantly set his senses ablaze... optic nerve almost shorting out as the vision it had never seen the like of before burnt into his brain. He didn't want to share her with the village, and she'd underestimated her admittedly scant need for human contact, so between their respective universes they sat. Hours passed in the blink of an eye, conversation without pauses writing their joint autobiography in one another's minds. She was as foreign to him as he was to her, so different that they couldn't help being intrigued, almost craving for that little morsel of knowledge that might help them understand. To Wolfenden, a man of such duty to his family and village was so odd that it was almost inhumane, Dickension at the closest comparison she could draw to it. To Henrik, she was such a free spirit that an angelic hue practically formed from the flittering snow around her. If they'd met anywhere within a hundred miles of one another, then it would have been at least 4am by the time they went their separate ways, already having arranged to meet up the very second they were both free. But with what seemed like the missing part of their soul stranded on the other side of the planet, they had a choice to make. He was bound to his life until his brothers were old enough to take on his duties. She was due back in the states the next morning, and knew deep down that she couldn't live his life. He couldn't offer her much, but he could promise that one day, he would be hers. She had little that he'd value, other than her fidelity, and an equal promise. 'I... I can't,' were the regretfully stuttered words that then broke her promise. 'I know what I said, and I know what... I know this is going to seem selfish, and that you won't understand, but I don't kno-' 'Kari,' he cut her off with, words warm and reassuring when she was expecting harsh and bitter. 'We might not have seen each other that much, but I know you. I know when you're happy, and... you're not.' 'I made a promis-' she went to explain, almost trying to convince herself that she was making a huge mistake. 'And I'm offering you a way out of it,' his hushed words spoke right into her thoughts, having known somewhere deep in his stomach that this moment was coming as soon as he saw her arrived, unexpected, at the spot they'd first met. 'I'd rather let you go, knowing that someday it could still be... than hold you to a promise that would only ruin what we have.' 'We can, still,' she did her best to assure him, sniffing the crystallised air to fry and keep back the tears... even from the one person she could cry in front of, in the arms of. 'All you'll ever have to do is show up.' 'You don't need to make any more promises,' his comforting words advised, as woollen gloved hands tightly locked into hers. 'No regrets, no apologies, just the chance of something in the future. If it's meant to be, it will be... you've made me believe that to be true.' Resting her head on his shoulder, which seemed to have been sculpted by God himself as the most divine of pillows, Karina closed her eyes... wondering if whether she'd done the one thing that would give them a hope of being together, or made what could well be the biggest mistake of her life. 'No regrets,' she sniffed in reply, realising that the blissful feeling she was enjoying would soon be but a memory. 'No apologies.' II - Not My Kinda Scene. Other than the straining grunts that serenaded the lifting of boxes, there was silence. Nearly all of Karina Wolfenden's worldly possessions were imprisoned in cardboard, taped shut, and scruffily labelled. Barely five months had passed since they had first filled the shelves and adorned the walls, turning a generic shell of Magnolia into something that passed as 'home'... for the brief moments she was actually there. Going to college was never her choice, rather a place she was herded to under the direction of her father... who was already starting to lose his homely grip on the most free-spirited of the Wolfenden offspring. Straying characteristically far from the footsteps her elder brother had trodden into life's topsoil, Karina had enrolled on a New Media course at Maine College of Art, one of a shortlist compiled more to rile her parents than reflect career options. Brochure after brochure was greeted only with apathy, no courses hooking her wandering attention, and no locations drawing the world-walking K-Wolf towards them. New Media was a rarity... something which she honestly thought could hold her attention. Well, for the first year at least. Alas, it wasn't to be, and Karina's typically stubborn, often infuriating personality failed to mesh with her new surroundings. Or, to be a little more factual, she failed to mesh with the attendance requirements. 'I'll take that one,' Karina broke the fuming silence with, taking a box of her most precious, personal artefacts from Max. As she left the room, then headed down the empty hallway of Portland Hall, Max gave a pleading look to his father, asking, 'go easy on her, will you?' The stern look he received in reply told him that the strictest of his parents was rapidly losing patience with his increasingly independent offspring. Lowering his head, and knowing right away what the look meant, Max sighed and heaved Karina's television up into his mildly muscled arms. 'Just remember,' Max added, trying to add a tone stern enough to break through his father's authoritative exterior, 'this wasn't her choice. She only went through with this stuff because you wanted her to.' As Max departed the room, he hastily strolled down the hallway to catch up to his sister, who had just headed into the stairwell. Doing his best to keep the grip on the TV, he reached the stairwell door in time to wedge it open with his foot. Hearing the thudding steps behind her, Karina turned at the foot of the stairs to see her concerned sibling emerge. 'So,' Karina sighed, tiredly, leaning back against the wall as if the frosty atmosphere had weakened her. 'How mad is he... really?' Crouching, Max placed the TV set down on the landing, just managing not to get his fingers caught underneath its awkward base. Searching for the right words, an obvious act of sugar-coated diplomacy, he took a seat on the top step, resting forearms on knees. 'Well,' he began, in a tone that told his sister to brace herself for the least pleasing on answers. 'He said a whole three words on the drive here.' Closing her eyes, Karina dropped her head back against the wall, for the first time worried that the seemingly unbreakable - no matter how contorted or stretched in became - bond between father and daughter had started to terminally fray. 'And they were?' 'Turn that off,' he coldly stated. 'Nearly didn't need to. Tone of his voice nearly fused the radio.' In 19 years, he had never laid a finger on her... never even raised a hand to feign a strike. Karina couldn't even remember a time he'd shouted, or even noticeably, memorably raised his voice. Lawrence Wolfenden had a way of portraying his feelings that didn't require aggression, and had no need for volume. If body language were literal, he would be fluent in many tongues. Just putting hands on hips would let someone know exactly what they needed to know. His eyes broadcast their own semaphore... the merest pupil dilation spelling out his intent. 'This is so not good,' her frustrated voice exhaled, sliding down the wall so that she was left seated against the skirting board. 'This is so not good on way too many levels.' Perched above her, Max looked down on his worried little sister, who for the first time in her life was coming to the realisation that she'd fucked up. 'Well, what did you expect?' Looking up from under her choppy fringe, Karina gave him a look of, 'geez, thanks.' 'I know you didn't even want to come here, or anywhere else,' Max accepted, doing his best to play the middle-ground. 'But he still paid for it all. All you had to do was turn up for a few stupid classes and do enough of the work to pass.' 'It wasn't just a case of tha-' she went to explain, but despite being her staunchest ally in the family, even Max had to sometimes question his sister's actions and motives. For the first 3 weeks, she'd actually made a real effort to go along with the syllabus and timetable. While never early, Karina was never rudely late, and only missed a class because her alarm reset after the power went off overnight. But sitting and listening wasn't what she was designed to do... and not what she'd expected to be doing on the course. She'd wanted something to express herself with... figuring that was the only way she'd get through a whole course. The subject of her work would form a visual diary of her life, the medium being the means in which she could channel all the excess thoughts and opinions she'd built up over the years. But one thing she was never comfortable with was communicating verbally to strangers, barely able to keep a lock on their eyes as she talked. Karina's work wasn't for them, it was for her. She knew what it meant, what messages it carried, what arguments it formed... whether anyone else understood was irrelevant. 'Then what was it a case of?' he interrupted, growing annoyed with Karina, and the mood she'd worked their father up in to. 'Kari, you had attendance of forty percent... that's 2 days a week. Where the hell have you been the rest of the time? Everyone else would have had classes the other days, unless they were all like you...' 'I haven't been away THAT much,' she protested, still doggedly fighting her corner. 'I had like three long weekends in France, a wedding down in DC, and Gemma wanted me to stay with her for a week at Florida state... and, I needed a break, so I took a few days in Montreal.' 'Jesus Christ...' Max sighed. 'Do me a favour and do NOT share that with dad, okay?' 'Wasn't exactly planning on it,' she quipped, honestly. 'How the hell did you afford this anywa-' Max went to question, before things fell into place. 'Wait... wait... don't say everything he'd been paying on your credit card was charged to cash?' That uneasy sensation that things were starting to pile up on top of her began to was over Karina, making her feel nauseous. That sudden, stomach-jolting realisation that things can't be turned back, or corrected without anyone noticing. Her head dropped, and Max shot to his feet, pacing agitatedly back and forth atop the stairs. 'And I assume he thought this was all money for books, clothes, field trips and food,' Max almost lectured down the stairs. 'Am I correct?' 'I didn't lie to him,' she bluntly assured Max, taking on an inflection that betrayed her sense of being cornered. 'Funnily enough,' Max almost laughed, wondering how on earth he could help his error of judgement prone sister out of the situation, 'I don't think that’s going to win you much leeway with him.' 'This is so fucked up,' Kari breathlessly grumbled, head cupped in her hands. 'What's your advice? I think I've already demonstrated how well I know him...' 'Well,' Max replied, as if his suggestion was to be the most obvious thing in the universe, 'saying sorry wouldn't be a bad start.' 'Hey,' Kari snapped right back, her usually confident voice wavering a slight - but noticeable - amount, 'I didn't ask him to let me come here. This wasn't my idea, it was his.' 'And that makes it all better?' Max exclaimed, finding his sibling's stubbornness every bit as infuriating as his parents. 'If he wants to accept that I fucked this all up, then I will,' she argued her corner resolutely with. 'If he wants to cut my card up, he can. If he wants me to explain exactly where I was instead of class, then I'll tell him. But I'm not going to apologise for who I am.' 'So this is who you are?' Max probed. 'This is who you want to be? For how long? Another ten years? Twenty?' 'I'm not apologising,' she firmly re-iterated, grabbing the box with both hands as she rose to her feet in defiance. 'I'm not who he wants me to be. I'm not ever going to be... so if I say sorry now, then I'm going to be apologising for the rest of my life. And that's something I will not do.' Before Max could even think of what to say in reply to his sister’s impassioned words, she was storming down the stairs and out of sight. And would remain true to her word. Unapologetically. III - Of Crimson Angels & Fallen Halos. Karina Wolfenden and her body worked in unison. Her subconscious was running a constant diagnostic, relaying data to her brain, which was analysed as rapidly as neurons and their transmitters could relay them. She felt the lurking, stuffy effects of a cold days before it blocked her nose or infected her throat with its abrasive, lingering touch. She could feel her muscles stretch and strain, every nerve impulse going to her cerebellum amplified to deafening volume. She felt far more than touch. And walking down to the ring, Karina Wolfenden knew her body was not in good shape. Every step sent a sharp pulse of pain shooting from her right knee, washing over the whole of her body like a wave of agonising sonar. Facial muscles had to be locked in place to mask the discomfort, her lips almost turning inside out as they pursed, harshly. The painkillers she'd taken to try and drown out the searing scream of her nervous system did little to smother the sensation, only making her feel a little more distant from the body she controlled... as if her footing had been lost, and she was left clinging on with one hand, scared it would fly out of control. Despite the bagginess of her black pants, emblazoned with her famous pink lightning bolts, the heavy strapping beneath was prominent for those who knew what they were looking for... and one such man stood in he ring, a sadistic smile already warping his thin lips. Angelo Deville. 'You don't have to go through with this,' PWC management tried to drill home backstage, since the match was signed before her knee had been blown out. Had it of been a lesser opponent, maybe one of he local, raw talents that the Circuit gave bookings to, Karina might have stayed at home. Honest with only herself, Karina knew the joint needed another week to ten days to fully recover, but anyone suggesting such to her would only invoke and silent scowl to warn them off. Karina Wolfenden never aimed for the safest trick, the tamest slope, or the softest landing. Asking her not to face Angelo Deville would be like trying to convince her that taking the last 2 steps in one bound was like a Base Jump. Incomparable. Stood dead centre in the ring, his awareness between the ropes such that he could get the equidistant measure to within an inch, Deville unfolded his arms, placing his hands on his hips. Clad in his trademark black and white halves, with the red trim that went with the moniker of the Crimson Angel, Deville's narrowed eyes scanned every inch of his opponent as she navigated the aisle. He'd watch Karina Wolfenden, scouted her, replayed entire matches in slow motion so that no detail was left unanalysed. Her movements flowed, her legs moved in sudden, snapping motions, and her eyes of burning emerald could project her very soul into an opponents mind. All he could think while watching her climb onto the apron was, 'who is this?' The big game hunter, ready to set his sights on the prize of his life, had just locked onto nothing but a wounded antelope. Karina Wolfenden would have vaulted over the top rope, or executed a lighting-quick springboard to assault him early with one of her famed Dropkicks, always managing to land back on her feet, instinctively, effortlessly. Before the pretender to herself had even stepped tamely through the ropes, Deville was leaning out the other side, calling for a mic. 'I'm sorry,' he apologised... maybe for the first time. 'I don't know who you are, but I was meant to be facing Karina Wolfenden tonight.' Every bit as wily as Karina was quick, every bit as calculating as she was brave, and ever bit as mischievous as she was stubborn, Deville knew how to psychological jab someone with only a handful of words. Trying to keep the facade up through her body language, Karina just stared back at the Deville, but the burning emerald was down to smouldering embers. As Deville paced towards her with a swagger that almost pleaded to be accompanied by a pin-stripe suit and a Tommy Gun, Karina stood her ground, face almost entirely veiled by a cascading pink fringe. But to a man as strongly welded together as Deville, with the persona of sharpened steel and of similar wit, her stance was nothing more than amusing, bordering on cute. 'Now, I don't want you to start thinking that I've spawned any of these morals that you people seem to be bound by,' his slick voice reeled off from his slick, lubricated tongue. 'But I'm going to cut you a break here.' Scraping the hair from her face with a gloved hand, Karina took a step forward, ready to square up to Deville. 'Don't think this is for your benefit,' he quickly cut her back down with, his words carrying such a tone that even the blind could have seen the smirk distorting his lips. 'This is solely for my sense of sport.' At a natural six feet tall, extended a couple of inches by her elevating soles, Karina still had to look up into Deville's eyes... and was almost lost in them. Set under a prominent, brooding brow, they carried an arctic chill... the blue of a clear, snow encrusted morning, swirled with a grey so cold that it would be warmer to swim in liquid nitrogen. When alert, uninhibited by the medication overriding her sense, Karina's rapturous eyes may have defrosted his ensnaring glare... but not today. 'I'm going to give you five minutes of Deville,' he then proclaimed, with inflection that made his own name sound grand beyond most people's imaginations. '300 seconds of unchallenged onslaught to put me away, before I even start thinking about which move to warp you into a human pretzel with.' If there's one thing that Karina usually doesn't do... it's what she should. She should have accepted that her body was in no real state for a match against anybody. She should have backed down from Deville, knowing that even when fully fit, she'd have the fight of her life just to keep up with him. She should have known that when Angelo Deville says he's going to do something that doesn't involve glory for himself, then you don't trust a word of it. But she'd hurtled down the slope, and was on the crest of the jump... you don't stop once you've reached it. 'So, Kari,' he beamed with equal menace and charisma, flicking her fringe to the side so he could look right down into her mottled, cloudy eyes. 'What do you sa-' Ever since she'd realised that the things that made her feel alive were the things most likely to kill her, Karina Wolfenden had shared an almost intimate relationship with her body's pain receptors. Over the years, she'd learned how to land to absorb impacts, found ways to lessen the deafening alarms of pain with breathing and mind techniques... and all for just such an occasion. In the blink of an eye, she'd hopped back, while in the same motion, flinging her right foot up at Deville's pointed jaw. The sudden snapping of her knee joint forced a wince through the still-setting concrete of her face's mask, a pain only intensified as the ripples of the foot's connection were fed back through the limb. Caught off guard, Deville went to drop limply backwards, just managing to take a staggering step backwards to balance himself. With a couple of limping paces, Karina closed the gap between them, accepting that her leg was now going to hurt like hell whether she continued to fight him or not. Hoping that the joint would take all of her 155 pounds being channelled through it, Karina then arced her left leg up on a graceful flight, before sending the heel smashing down into Deville's cheekbone, swatting his head to the side so that a fine rain of perspiration shot off his tanned skin. Skirting around a competitive black-hole who's gravity perpetually ravaged her body, Karina couldn't resist, and gave in to it, letting the only escape route fold itself out of existence behind her. With the passionate fury that personified her style, the K-Wolf threw herself at Deville, landing blow after blow to his head and body. As each kick landed, every nerve impulse seemed to be re-routed through her knee, building up a pain so sharp that it threatened to pierce her cerebellum. The minutes counted down, and gradually, every fan in the building had risen from their seats... like a laboured, curious Mexican Wave. After the first minute, the mocking smirk had been removed from Deville's lips as a split sent blood pouring down his chin. After two minutes, a little sparkle went from his wintry eyes as Karina's heel pounded their socket. After three, his dominant stance crumbled when she finally managed to kick his legs out from under him. 'KA-RI-NA! KA-RI-NA! KA-RI-NA!' they chanted, turning their vocal chords into puppet strings to help lift the K-Wolf. But even though their energy helped lift the pain-killer-induced hue that was tinting her vision, their stamping sent shockwaves up through the ring, adding even more seismic pain to her knee... which was already ballooning, the strapping starting to feel like a tourniquet. Almost hopping on one leg, Karina made her way to the corner, and began to drag herself up to the top turnbuckle. Teeth ground as weight was channelled through her knee during her ascent, to the point where a dust of enamel could have quite easily been expelled from her mouth. Once halfway up, she couldn't turn back, even when a sound from behind Karina tipped her off that Deville was rising. 'Gahhhh,' she hissed inwards as one final effort rose her to the top turnbuckle, only for Deville to stagger towards her, hand extended. Having found it hard enough to set her balance as it was, the hefty shove from Angelo Deville was only ever going to send Karina plummeting down to the outside. Instantly, the crowd erupted into a rapid tirade of jeers, the stuffy arena air itself turning to pure hate. 'Go limp,' was all Karina could think as she rotated uncontrollably on her way down to the thin mats. 'Go limp... Go limp...' Some fans in the front row turned away as Karina hit the floor, a thud that sounded anything but cushioned forcing itself out from underneath her wrecked body. Having foolishly attempted to try landing with a roll, her right knee had given out under her the millisecond her toes had hit the floor, before being contorted to an even more agonising angle as her body followed through. Then, for the first time since she had entered the wrestling business, she screamed. Unwillingly, untainted, and uneasy to hear. 'ARGHHHHH!' pieced the tympanic membranes of those at ringside, the fierce, focussed sound enough to make their ears bleed. It was the scream of a young woman who may have felt the first, tingling sensation of fear claw its way up her spinal column. Grabbing her knee, Karina thrashed about on the mats as if she were having a fit, her face screwed up so tightly it has already taken on a red hue where the blood vessels were being forced to the lightly freckled surface. No matter how tightly she held it, or whatever position her body warped itself into, the pain would not subside... Such was the agony, it was controlling her breathing, rather than letting Karina's breathing control it. Within seconds, a swarm of black and white surrounded her, panic riding on the frothing waves of their voices, all under the watchful eye of the other man in the purest and darkest of colours. Knelt on the canvas, leant on the middle rope, Deville just watched... taking enjoyment in the raw, primal noises being made by his agonised opponent. But somewhere under the smug visage was a part of Deville that knew she had a chance to walk away. She had to step up to be pushed down. And despite her body locking itself down with searing agony, her thoughts unable to focus as pain shredded them with it's diamond-sharpened blades, Karina had no apologies for herself. Injuries happened. No sorrow. No regrets. Her knee would heal, and over time, the all-encompassing, concentrated ache that paralysed her would be all but a memory. If she'd walked away, turned the match down, then the damage to her fierce pride would take far longer to repair. She'd made her choice, and there was nothing to be sorry about. Not that she knew the words to express it with. IV - Body HITs. It was where it had all started. It was the moment that came perilously close to ending the wrestling career of the Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid. A veteran of the brutalist Death Matches that the most sadistic Japanese promoters could create... the sorts of matches where your skin was still growing back two weeks later. His forehead was a woven tapestry of scar tissue, while his body carried the sickening scars of barbed wire, routinely embedded in, then slashed through, his flesh. Through these wounds had poured more blood than any man was ever meant to bleed, causing canvases to look like the floor of a busy slaughterhouse. He was heralded as being on the toughest, bravest, most resilient workers in the wrestling business. Some were amazed that he could still walk down to a wrestling ring, others shocked that he'd still want to after all his body had been through... but when he left the building, it was in an ambulance. All due to, however unwittingly, Karina Wolfenden. Not that she knew any of this as her weary opponent dragged himself up onto the top turnbuckle. After six minutes of stiff, back and forth action, Karina Wolfenden was starting to lose her focus. Usually a serene depiction of concentration when in the ring, the Trapezoid had eroded away her icy diamond exterior, finally letting the fans see desperate eyes staring at the referee's fingers following a close two count, or her jaw drop when a flurry of her hardest kicks couldn't keep him out for ten. As if she were seeing in echoes, every movement took a split second to relay itself through her optic nerve, like several frames were shadowing one another. She saw seconds in one image, the trail of the HIT's right hand fading out of time behind it, before it clubbed the back of Karina's head. The impact jolted her eyelids shut, letting a second right hand pound her out of the echoing darkness. Where balance was usually her closest ally, it had deserted Karina as she started to feel the unsettling whisperings of gravity trying to distract her. Stood on the middle rope, straddling the top cable, Karina was mirrored by the HIT. Normally, the rampant popping of the crowd would fuel her energy-guzzling body, helping keep her topped-up, but their cheers echoed distractingly through her throbbing head, almost bursting out of her skull's crown. Flaccid forearms were all the K-Wolf could offer as a defence, her bracer bouncing off the HIT's scarred forehead. Rapidly blinking, Karina tried to focus her vision, but her opponent's tattooed body, the blue mats beneath, and the multicoloured fans danced a pattern akin to a kaleidoscope. The crowd then took a collective gasp, holding hands tightly against cheeks, as Karina toppled backwards, only for the foot hooked under the rope to act as her safety harness. With pain screaming through her skull, the sudden jolt of the fall only amplifying it, Karina then felt the HIT grab her hair... using it to harshly winch her back up. Stepping back over the rope, the HIT gripped Karina tightly, the pressure such that she struggled to get a breath in. Then, as her competitive instincts faded, and her senses started to switch off one by one, something deep down in the K-Wolf, an unknown reserve of unconscious energy, took over. As if someone else was grabbing her foot and acting as puppet master, Karina's right leg looped around, then sharply cut back up... pummelling the HIT's groin with her heel. Then, like the natural, survival instincts that rule over nature, Karina used the fumes she was running on to slap on her Whirlwind Romance grip... wrapping her arms around the HIT's upper body like a sash. Muscles almost creaked as her legs bent, barely enough ATP left in her heavily perspiring body to allow the layers to slide over one another, before searing pain shot through them as she exploded off. For a brief, panicking second, it felt as if the HIT wouldn't release, until fingers could almost be heard snapping as he was prised away from the buckles. Closing her eyes, Karina went limp - save for her grip on the HIT - and just waited for them to crash against the mat. But rather then feel the dull, even thud of canvas underneath her, something jabbed into Kari's shoulder... then expelled a sickening crack as the HIT's unprotected, prone head ploughed down into it. Wincing as the intense pain pierced through her arm, Karina barely managed to roll over and drape herself across HIT's chest. The three count pulsed up through her body, each hand slap like punch to her shoulder, before an all-telling sigh of relief from Karina accompanied the tinny ring bell...telling people all they needed to know about the match. With the adrenaline being depleted by Karina's racing metabolism, an ache set in all around her body, leaving her barely able to roll out under the bottom rope, oblivious to the medical team sprinting either side of her. Nobody had even told her about the HIT's fate until the next week, by which point, Karina could barely remember what had happened in the match. It wasn't her fighting the HIT on the top turnbuckle, it was her primal survival instinct, that whisper in her ear that told her, 'you have to do this, or he'll do it to you.' He'd pushed her to her body's limits, and worryingly beyond. He'd brought the chair into the ring earlier, and initiated the battle over the top rope. He'd failed to pull a single punch, and relished the battle. He'd been wheeled away with a broken neck. He'd known the deal. He'd prepared to do the same to her. He'd have to wait a long while for an apology from Karina Wolfenden. That, or earn one. V - Are These Words From The Future? Gideon Daray Poe stood at the bedside of his wife. Staunch, resolute... yet broken behind the facade. Where crimson streaks had once ran through his jet black hair, now only grey highlighted it, running down through his make-shift, scruffy beard. He hadn't shaven in 3 days, after all, he hadn't been home since bursting through the hospitals doors, fear and grief etched into his pale skin. Life with Karina was always one of worry, especially when their respective lives had kept them apart for so long... but every panic had always turned out a false alarm. That fact had almost lulled him into a false sense of security, making the realisation's stiff punch to his heart all the more devastating. The woman he'd known was one of colour, vibrancy, and eventually a beaming smile. He'd found the key to her protective shell, the fault in her cold diamond exterior, shattering it to let the real K-Wolf free. Where once she was only content with her own life, never looking far from her bubble, she had begun to infuse all around her with optimistic joy. 'Don't make me do this,' Gideon - or Gidz, as she'd teasingly dubbed him - pleaded under his breath, looking out of the corners of his deep-set eyes to where there children sat in the corridor. 'You're not... you're not meant to di-... you're just not.' Through the glass, between the blinds, he saw his mother-in-law, Sandra, read his look, before turning back to his children. Sebastian and Molly, twins, had been the final piece in the frustrating jigsaw that was Karina's life. Everything looked finished without them, but once you looked closer, they were needed to make it complete. Far from the traditional parent, Karina had wanted their early years to be full of lavish sights and intriguing cultures, so took them on tour with their father's band - Cadaver Theory. Being bound to responsibility began to seem natural for her, but despite that, she relished her moments of freedom his retirement from music brought her. Slopes and rings had been forsaken for days in the park, or trips to the coast... but for old times' sake, she needed to open up the throttle on the scarlet Ducati 748 she'd had since her 18th birthday. She'd always promised him that her reckless side was behind her, and she'd held frustratingly true to her constrictive word. It was the careless actions of another that had left the broken K-Wolf ready to depart the world. Tubes and dressings tainting Poe's final image of his living wife. Swallowing, Poe dropped his head, dabbing around his moist eyes with the overly-long sleeve of his sweater. The talk, the mechanical, strained breathing, and the haunting beeps failed to register... all he could take in was the sight of it all, the sight of his wife slipping quietly out of the world. 'Goodbye,' he exhaled, giving up his fight against the tears as Karina's gave up her own battle. From under the cascading, charcoal tinted hair, he looked back out to Sandra, and shook his head. As she herded Molly and Sebastian away to anywhere she could make a reason for going to, Poe walked to the glass, butting his head against it. As the Doctor waited respectfully to announce the time of death, and the nurses looked at him with unpatronising pity, Gideon watched his children leaving, already full of more questions that were good for them. Just like their mother at that age, and for many after. Before he took what would be a haunting, memorable look at Karina, he wanted to convince himself that she still lived on. Molly already had her starting emerald eyes, and Sebastian was rapidly discovering the stubborn streak that his mother so infuriatingly possessed. He may have been left to raise them alone, but he held enough stories of their mother in his cherished thoughts to keep her in their lives. 'When you get to heaven,' he whispered, no longer trying to stem the flow of tears as he turned back to where Karina lay. 'Give 'em hell.' As her reborn, virgin eyes crept apart, a blanket of pure, brilliant white was there to welcome her. Devoid of shadow and perspective, Karina still somehow managed to make out the details in the monotone landscape... not there to see, but rather to believe. Like a fine, silk veil hung across vast nothingness, walls looked as if they could be walked through just as easily as the wispy, morning air she'd once strolled in. Out of instinct, her diaphragm plummeted, but there was nothing to be sucked into her lungs... an odd sensation of choking, when there was no oxygen debt to quench. Shuffling forward, it was like living in a hologram world. Nothing of substance sliding in and out of her vision, casting no shadow, flickering as her resuscitated mind struggled to take it all in. As her eardrum began to solidify, a distant whisper began to nag her. A whisper that was brewed light years away, carried through nothingness towards her. Lulled into a false sense of security by the serene subtlety, Karina was then nearly knocked off her feet as a deafening, metallic clunk echoed within her skull. A ripple was released, pulsing through the dream cityscape, and causing her floaty white gown to be ravaged, Crossing her arms, Karina rubbed cold flesh with her palms, but there was no friction to warm her shivering form. There, in stark contrast to the hologram surroundings which phased effortlessly through one another, stood a pair of sturdy, decorative gates. The light reflected off the surface, giving them a sheen of brilliant white that both matched their surroundings, and caused them to stand out unmistakably. They were real... if anything, including her, was. Reaching a timid hand forward, Karina felt them before fingers had even made contact. So real that they expanded beyond their physical form. 'Repent,' the gates resonated with, pronouncing the words through Karina's own bone marrow. Karina went to yank her hand away, but it seemed drawn to the bars... a magnetic force that was almost stronger than the muscles and tendons of her arm. Pulling herself away, Karina tumbled backwards, almost falling through the mental projection of the floor, before dragging herself back up, getting a footing on pure light. So this was it; the end. Life hadn't scored the knock-out blow that her often reckless guard had always left her vulnerable to, rather taking her out with a sucker-punch. And after one defeat, she was being called on to surrender fully, truly. Before her was an eternal paradise, universal bliss that her words held the key too. All she'd have to do to become ageless, endless, was shed the moments that had made her. Abandoning the unique chrysalis to become a uniform, conforming butterfly. Then, in one final, eternal act of defiance, she raised her goose-pimpled arms up from her sides. Symbolically, she took a deep breath of vacuum, then dropped backwards. Like trying to see the picture behind a magic eye puzzle, Karina extended her thoughts through the light, concentrating on what was behind it, allowing her body to phase through. As the light passed through her, it felt like ice running through her veins, before a veil of darkness was cast all around her. Looking up at a distant ball of light rapidly disappearing into the distance, Karina crossed her arms across her chest, then closed her eyes. Tumbling through zero gravity, a fierce heat below seemed to disrupt the worlds either side of the void, creating a gradient that pulled her down. Thoughts, dreams, and stubborn opinions were disintegrated just as easily as flesh in the inhuman heat. Karina had departed the universe as she lived. Unrepentant. Unapologetic. Glorious.
