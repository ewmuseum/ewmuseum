[Endsong] - A Picture Paints a Thousand Words

11 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11th February 2009 The grass underneath my back tickled my skin like a lover’s fingers. The gentle caressing was strangely soothing, as I lay prone, staring up at the clouds that rolled overhead. From my vantage point, with the sounds of the waves crashing against the rocks so far below, it almost felt as if I was floating; as if I were being carried in the arms of Father Sky himself. A wave of dizziness, vertigo brought on by a sudden belief that I was truly held aloft, broke the spell and I realised that I was where I was. Lying flat where I had feel on the edge of a cliff near the rented house on Hawaii. So slowly that I hardly registered my own movement my hands, flat at my sides, clenched into fists and pulled at the grass and earth below me. I swallowed by a cry, feeling the sting of fresh tears, as the memory of my father played around the edges of my mind once more. It had felt so real, so vibrant; I could smell his hair as if I just buried my face in his neck. I could feel the touch of his hand on my face as he reassured me that everything would be ok. There were times – months sometimes – that I never even thought of my parents but here, today, I had seen him as clearly as if it were yesterday. The pain of loss flooded through me again, crashing against my heart and soul in waves. The booming of my agony joined in staccato rhythm with the ocean and the waves; I wanted nothing more than to lose myself in both … become them rather than me. No matter how hard I wished, what I wanted, I stayed who I was and where I was. Staring at the sky through the haze of my tears I wished so desperately for rain knowing that, should the heaven’s open and the water wash over me, I could pretend that it was nature itself that wet my cheeks rather than the weakness inside. Real men don’t cry. It was something that had never been said to me but something that I knew far too well. I knew that, were he still here, my father would have laughed at the thought. He had cried so often. When he was happy, when he was sad, and – simply – when he felt like crying for crying’s sake. He had been a proud, strong, loving man and one who had never been ashamed of his emotions. He had taught me as much as he could, in such a short time. Seven years of lessons, however, can be untaught – unlearned – by twenty-five years of absence. Of grief. A heart hardened by loss – a childhood … childish … promise to never allow oneself to feel that pain again – led to that was so tightly controlled that everything on the palette of emotion had become shades of grey. Never too happy, never too sad. Just being. Never allowing anyone too close because, as the seven year old inside the adult knew without doubt, the closer they were the harder the loss would be when they left. … and they always left. It was only in the moments alone, moments like these, where reality came to call and the things locked deep inside broke free for a time. Here and now, with no-one around, the tears could fall. The screams could cry out. The anger could boil and … from the deepest, darkest corners of his soul … the desire to simply leave it all behind could surface. The edge of the cliff was so close, the waves on the rock so inviting. The lure of the past called louder than ever before while the memories of the pain – from then and now – threatened to overwhelm him. So why was it, when he stood on the precipice and had the choice to fall forwards into forever or backwards into one more day did he choose the harder road? Why didn’t he slip off the edge, into the sea, and wait for his father’s arms to embrace him once more? His own personal trial of tears beckoned and, he knew, it could be a short road or a long one. He had been walking it all his life, trying to cope with the loss of his parents and fight the World alone – a wall of stone around his heart to keep all pain out. … to keep all love out. Love. Pain. The seven year old inside his heart couldn’t separate the two and the man he had become had never bothered trying. The memory of the last day of Summer with his father, the tale of the bones and the medicine shield, roared back to life as fresh tears rolled down his cheeks. "They may, Jay" his father said with a small shiver. "Don’t worry, though, I am not going anywhere for a very long time!" "Don’t worry, though, I am not going anywhere for a very long time!" “I am not going anywhere for a very long time!" “I am not going anywhere” “anywhere” It hadn’t been meant to be a lie but the words had shaped his life. His father had said he wasn’t going anywhere and then he died. He was there and then he was gone. He had taken his mother with him and left the boy alone. He had filled the boy’s heart and life with love. With everything. … and then it was all gone and life became a daily battle to simply get through. Then it had become nothing at all. The seven year old had not know what he was doing but he had shaped the adult’s life. The choice to protect his heart from pain – from love – had ensured that he had lived a life without joy, without hope, without love. Unbidden his father’s voice came back to him. “Immortality is living a life worth living, taking chances, risking it all. Live and love.” Sitting up, hugging his knees, he pulled his wallet from his back pocket and pulled out a small photo from within. The tears fell, stronger, but a smile played around the corners of his mouth as, staring at the photo with wonder in his eyes, Jay Phoenix knew what he had to do.
