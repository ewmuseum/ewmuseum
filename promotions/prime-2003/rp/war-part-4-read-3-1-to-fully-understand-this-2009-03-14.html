War (Part 4)- Read 3.1 to fully understand this

14 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 14th ...The race was on to find Larry Connors. If Steven got to him first, his life would be saved. If Christian got to him first, the results would be devastating. March 14th, 2009 11:25 PM Listening to the cacophony of sounds on a busy Sunday night in Raleigh, Larry Connors paced the abandoned rooftop of Woodlawn Apartments, smacking the service revolver against his bleeding temple. For the last forty-five minutes, the former detective had gone through several emotional boom/bust cycles. In one second, happiness flowed from his body and a jubilant smile creased his lips backwards. He was happy that Cynthia and Maria had escaped the clutches of Christian Daniels without experiencing prolonged trauma. Neither had to experience the shock and anger of losing their grandfather and father by the same hands. Cynthia also never knew that her mother perished in such a malicious fashion. The next second, his mind was consumed by their deaths. Devin's arm wrapped around Maria's neck. Christian sliced Cynthia's throat and left her exposed for Larry to find two days later. His body was sickened, and the vomiting induced powerful headaches that brought Larry down to his knees. The throbbing of his head brought memories of the anguish suffered by loved ones. It was only a sliver of what they actually sustained, but he still endured the pain. More tears were inevitable, mixing with the vomit on the rooftop and streaks of blood that decorated his forehead and tip of his nose. Staring down at the cement, he wondered where Cynthia and Maria had ended up. Were they in heaven along with his other relatives? Was Mike Connors bouncing his granddaughter around with God behind them? Were Cynthia and Maria being eaten alive by worms six feet under? Larry vomited again and started through another cycle. At least they were gone, he thought to himself. They couldn't see him like this. He wouldn't think twice about pulling the trigger if either caught a glimpse of him now. His mind departed many days earlier and was still missing, more than likely never be found. The first forty-eight hours were critical to finding any missing entity. Larry tried to rise and face the world again but his Jell-O legs made it difficult. The pistol rocked back and forth in his hands, unable to stay locked in a grip. A sheen of sweat covered his hand, further loosening his grasp and sending the gun onto the roof. "SHIT!" A single bullet fired toward the brick wall at the opposite end of the rooftop, startling Larry and making him dive for cover. Two seconds elapsed before he nervously looked back and forth, removing trembling hands from his head and pushing off the nearby wall. His vision became a blur, almost as if an unseen power was spinning him around at twenty miles per hour. Once he stood up, Larry couldn't grasp a hold of anything and dropped down to his knees. He remembered the appalling smell of vomit when his body released another wave that covered the cement. "I'm sorry Cynthia...I'm sorry. I...I...failed you as a father..." Weary arms couldn't hold the two hundred pounds of body weight off the ground any longer. Sinking down next to his vomit, Larry ignored the blood moving into his eyes. The physical pain wouldn't allow him another moment of staying awake and forced respite. He wasn't committing suicide in the traditional way. He was letting mental terror tear away at his insides like a cancer. He needed someone to save his life, quickly. March 14th, 2009 11:28 PM Shooting down back roads at close to one hundred miles per hour, Devin Shakur was against a clock. Helping Christian tonight was essential for the operation against the Connors family. If Christian was able to successfully accomplish his mission, Devin would be one giant step closer to achieving complete freedom from his past. The GPS gave him the next instruction, "In two hundred feet, turn left onto Clayton Road." Putting all of his weight into the steering wheel, Devin felt the car adjust to the unnatural shift. He recalculated the vehicle, slapping like a five year old girl at the wheel in hopes of nailing the inside corner. Ten feet over and he would receive extensive admonishment while writing out a check for $400,000. "come on! Come on! COME ON!" The back wheels screeched against the last available space of pavement, missing the grass by inches, and Devin breathed a huge sigh of relief. Fist pumping with his free hand, Devin put more gas into the vehicle and reached the car's top speed of one hundred and twenty. Going at such a high rate of speed, the duo wasn't more than fifteen seconds away from Woodlawn Apartments. Devin was playing the role of lookout. If anybody came around that reeked of law enforcement, he was to honk the horn repeatedly and Christian would have to leave no matter what. If he heard something over the police scanner about Woodlawn Apartments, Devin was to honk the horn incrementally every time the building was mentioned. If the number got to five, he had to get Christian out the hard way. Christian barely acknowledged the twists and turns of the vehicle, keeping his eyes shut and focused on the moments ahead. With Cynthia no longer in the equation, his mercy factor drastically decreased and the macabre bastard who hated everybody outside his family resurfaced. Tonight, Christian had to bring his A game to the table or risk being set back several years. Slamming the brakes behind the complex, Devin opened the passenger door and allowed Christian to exit from the vehicle. Larry's body barely acknowledged the sound of Christian's door slamming fifty feet below. When his eyes roamed and spied the pool of vomit and blood at his right, Larry rolled over and looked up to the sky. The pain of not eating anything in over seventy two hours made his head feel like a brick. His hand blindly smacked around for the gun, unwilling to turn and find the exact location of the weapon. His fingers recoiled when the aberration of metal smacked dry fingertips. New streaks of blood slid down the front of his fingers while he rose to a seated position. Every muscle in his being begged for more sleep, but Larry knew nobody lived in the apartments. To hear a car door so close was suspicious and necessary preparation needed to be made. Stepping over a pizza box full of bugs and rats, Christian weighed his options. He could either walk around to the front of the building and break through the door, running a heavy risk of being seen. If police officers heard Larry was on the roof and realized the most nefarious criminal in the history of the state was entering the same building, they would flood like a swarm onto the scene and Christian would be trapped. The other option stared Christian in the face, a dingy and aged ladder which could fall off the hinges at any second. It was a direct line to the roof and the next death on his list. Christian placed a hand on the ladder and tested the durability, shaking the thin object back and forth. After deducing that it could hold him, the monster started to climb. Larry cowered like a child, curling himself into a ball and hiding away from the lights of the city. He did not want to be saved by Steven or any of his other friends. The idealistic situation would be to put a bullet in his brain, leaving them to deal with the aftermath of finding and burying his corpse. Whey they did, the poem he considered a suicide note would be found in his right jean pocket, nestled next to his driver's license. Christian continued his climb, checking on the ladder's stability every few feet. The prospect of a massive fall didn't trouble him, but the thought of Larry escaping did. With his little background in actual psychology, Christian knew Larry was a distraught human being that could turn into a completely different man at the drop of a hat. Once he got up on the roof, Christian needed to ensure Larry he was in control because otherwise the situation could go completely awry. Cradling the service revolver in his hands, Larry felt the unknown individual getting closer to his newfound haven. The steps grew louder, causing Larry's patience to get shorter. He pointed the barrel at the ladder, only intent on using the weapon as a scare tactic rather than a killing device. If Steven had somehow found where Larry was, Frank Moraco would be to blame. The kid couldn't keep a secret and when Larry waved the gun in his face to indicate the seriousness of his plight, Frank nearly faint in the street. Larry sighed and began to trail off in thought. Why do people care? Why do people all of a sudden take an interest in death to those who are troubled? Nobody thought enough to save Cynthia and Maria... ...Nobody Larry held back tears and grimaced while the anguish ran through his body again. Christian placed his hands on the curved portion of the rail and pushed up, sticking his head onto the roof, scanning for any sign of Larry. The thought of ambush crossed his mind and he dropped back down for a moment of thought. Larry could be waiting in the wings with an excellent shot. Christian knew the level of paranoia coursing through Larry's body was off the charts and he didn't want or need that kind of interruption. The biker considered the best place for someone to hide on the roof. There weren't many enclosed or even partially enclosed spaces, and hiding behind the door was too obvious... The darkness Christian peered onto the roof one more time and scanned fifty feet to the right where light didn't penetrate. Upon first glance he observed nothing out of the ordinary, but gave himself a moment to adjust. He was looking for movement of any kind. A hand dropping. A foot moving accidentally out of place. Faint words that weren't meant to escape. Or an entire body shaking like an autumn leaf. Christian found his man and pushed the rest of his body onto the roof. Larry still couldn't deduce that his daughter's killer was approaching, but nonetheless drew the gun, more sweat than before enveloping his hands. Meanwhile, on the other side of town, Steven Connors hadn't made it five hundred feet, passed out over the passenger's seat of his parked car outside Mikey's Bar. His determination couldn't overcome the amount of alcohol consumed in such a short period of time. The key was stuck in the ignition but he was unable to start the car. Due to the large amount of cars that used the bar for parking whenever a college party occurred at the fraternity houses nearby, nobody thought to call for help or wake him. Christian started his walk over to the darkness, one hand behind his back reaching for a knife in case he needed to defend himself. Should Larry fire a warning shot, Christian would fling his knife in the darkness and leap into the fray. It wasn't how he wanted to go about business, but contingency plans in his line of work were nothing short of mandatory. He took one more giant step and looked down at Larry Connors. Christian saw the nine millimeter pointed at chest level and had to halt his hand from extracting the blade. A long moment of silence elapsed between the two. Christian knew Larry didn't have the mental strength remaining the pull the trigger, and Larry was still trying to figure out who exactly was in front of him. His eyes open and closed repeatedly from the physical pain. Christian broke the silence, "You'ra pathetic excuse for'a human bein', Larry." The massive emotional mood swing Christian assumed Larry would take upon seeing him was accurate. Emerging from the stupor, Larry sprung to life and caught Christian off guard with the butt of the gun. How much gall did Christian Daniels have to seek Larry out in such a condition? Larry hadn't felt the sensation of adrenaline in three days and was going to finish what should have been done a long time ago. Charging forward, Larry slammed the gun into Christian's head again, sending him backwards. The blows weren't compromising enough to knock someone of Christian's size down, but they weren't weak. Christian didn't wish for them to continue rocking him backwards. When Larry went for a third shot, Christian caught the arm, swept Larry off his feet, and threw him onto the cement. Larry flipped over backwards and went back to his disoriented state. Christian stalked over, hands unclenched, and stood over his rival. Realizing he was on the defensive, Larry pointed the gun upward and used it as leverage to stand back up. A trail of blood descended from the back of his head onto his torn dress shirt. The warmth brought Larry back to lucidity for a moment. His entire body shook back and forth, jolting like an epileptic on crack. For the first time in a long time, Christian was witness to the breakdown of a man who had his entire life shattered. "WHAT..." Larry could barely project his voice, exhaustion forcing itself back to the forefront, "...WHAT ARE YOU DOING HERE, DANIELS?" Christian stood the complete opposite of Larry, stalwart with a cool and calm demeanor. He was seldom rattled even with a pistol pointed at his face. "I believe the better question is...What are you doing here, Larry?" "You...God, how fucking sick are you?" Larry tried to move, but his legs wouldn't carry him the necessary distance. Another wave of vomit made its way up his body. He couldn't stare at the man who cut his daughter's throat. "YOU HAVE NO RIGHT TO BE UP HERE!" Christian moved forward, sending Larry in a backwards panic while keeping the gun pointed forward. Larry fell down, unstable legs not relenting in their fatigue. He used his hand to create distance, swallowing back the inevitable vomit. With a roll of his eyes, Christian advanced forward, "If you were gonna fuckin' shoot me Larry, you'da done it by now. We both know why yer up here." Larry couldn't control himself any longer and emitted frustration in the form of a primal scream. Christian's blinking increased and for a split second, he felt pity for the man. If history were to get involved, Christian was the one who instigated the rivalry between the two families. Daniel Connors decided to raid the Death clubhouse in 1991 and paid an unfortunate price, being in debt to Christian. Eighteen years later, his grandson was facing the same low level biker who tackled Daniel during the botched raid. But Christian had a job to do and his background wouldn't allow straying from the task. Even when Larry vomited again, Christian didn't change facial expression. He walked over and knelt down next to Larry, who averted his head. "Look'atcha, what the hell's the matter with you, kid? I ain't ever seen someone so fucking miserable in my life." While keeping his head turned away and refusing to wipe his tears away, Larry responded, "YOU...KILLED MY DAUGHTER! YOU KILLED MY WIFE! WHAT DO YOU EXPECT ME TO BE FEELING?" Christian walked around the vomit and assumed the same stance as before, determined for Larry to look him in the eyes. It gave him a small sense of satisfaction. "You killed my brothers and hunted Dev like a dog for killing his dad after the man abused him endlessly. What did you think I was gonna do?" Larry mustered enough strength to crawl a small distance away from Christian. The monotone of his voice was nothing short of frightening. Hovering over small pools of blood, Larry shut his eyes and screamed, "YOU MURDERED EVERYBODY IN MY FAMILY! MY GRANDFATHER! MY FATHER! MY UNCLES!" Moving his gaze up, Larry saw Christian's military boots standing in the pools of blood and had no choice available but to stare up at the seven foot behemoth. "YOU..." When his eyes locked with Christian's, he could no longer prevent from bawling in front of Death's leader. The tears quickly reached his chin and dropped onto the cement. His voice transformed into a whisper. "You killed my daughter. You killed my daughter...How could you do that to me? ...After you know what it's like to have a son." Larry's arms gave way and he fell at Christian's feet. The biker heard the sobs and had to withstand his own wave of pain. Having to leave Caleb created post traumatic stress that he hadn't been able to shake, regardless of years. Despite their many differences, both men held one common belief, time never healed any wounds. Christian grabbed a hold of Larry's shirt and yanked him up to a standing position. Larry tried to shove away, but lacked enough power to break the grip. His head was forced up to meet Christian's, who dropped down a foot so they could be eye to eye. Christian never liked telling someone the truth in any other fashion. "It was logical. I wanted to make a statement both you and that asshole brother of yours would get. I didn't want either of you, that wouldn't have been worth it. Your wife was already gone and killing some distant relative would have been pointless." Larry was mortified, Christian was treating this like any other speech. "So your daughter was the perfect solution. You shoulda never taken her out Larry, otherwise you wouldn't be here. It's on your head for actin' the fool and leaving her unprotected so you could grieve. Women ain't worth that, no matter how long you've loved 'em." Larry grasped a hold of Christian's wrists and used them for support structures while he rose to his full height. He still wept uncontrollably and couldn't hide such a level of disgust. "Your...Your fucking sick. How does God let people like you live?" Christian cracked a grin in Larry's face, furthering the psychological anguish, "Balance, kid...balance. But I'll make ya a deal. You wanna make right by your family, do 'em proud? Then kill me. Right now. Dev won't get up here in time to save me. One shot's all it takes." Christian's move was the equivalent of a poker player shoving all his chips in on a bluff. He knew Larry didn't have the internal strength to pull the trigger. He also had an essential tell on the situation, but didn't want to let Larry in on that detail yet. Not with one more trick up his sleeve. Larry dreamt of being presented with the opportunity to kill Christian ever since the Devin Shakur murder case started. Some nights he fantasized about the sadistic ways he wanted to torture Christian and make him suffer for killing the most important people in his life. Those possibilities only intensified with Christian's reappearance, and subsequent murder of his wife and child. Christian ate away at his soul and Larry finally held a weapon in hand that could extinguish the main nuisance of his life. But he couldn't do it. Christian shook his head and folded tattooed arms across his chest. "Hmm, alright, perhaps I gotta do this, give ya some more incentive. I'll make it more symbolic, eliminate the gun from the equation." Larry watched as Christian went into his back pocket and pulled out a switchblade that wasn't the most silver blade in the universe. He offered the weapon to Larry, watching the young man curiously hold the weapon up and inquire why it was covered in two colors, silver and crimson. "Let the same weapon that killed your life end mine." It was covered in crimson because Cynthia Connors blood hadn't been washed off. Looking at the macabre smirk on Christian's face sent Larry into an unfathomable rage and he bolted ahead, brandishing the gun and swinging it with all his might. Christian received the blow on his chin and took a step back, ready for another. "YOU SON OF A BITCH!" Larry cried. Another strike slammed into Christian's chin with much more force. Larry wound up and struck again, and again, and again, and again. Christian kept his poker face in tact, refusing to show any signs of pain and give Larry semblance of revenge. His chin and jaw were incredibly swollen from earlier blows and Christian felt a few teeth jarred lose. The strikes continued until Larry's body fell onto the cement. Christian walked over to the knife and placed it back in his pocket while Larry recuperated from the punishment. Placing a hand up to his chin and checking for blood, Christian strolled over to Larry and caught an unsuspecting shot to his throat. Christian couldn't hide the pain, doubling over and releasing a series of coughs. He didn't want to spit any blood out and give authorities a definitive reason to investigate him, claiming they could put him at the scene of the crime. Showing quick recovery time, Christian only needed fifteen seconds to turn his body around. When he did, Larry showed teeth and pointed the pistol in his direction. Christian turned his head to the side and motioned for the trigger to be pulled. Larry cocked the gun back and Christian emitted a snarl. "DO IT! PULL THE TRIGGER! MAKE YOUR FAMILY FUCKIN' PROUD YOU MISERABLE SHIT!" When Larry wanted it the least, the shaking in his hands came back. "COME ON! WHAT ARE YOU, A FUCKIN' CHICKEN? MAKE IT HAPPEN! YOU'VE WANTED THIS SO BAD! HERE I AM MOTHERFUCKER! DO IT!" A second hand came around to steady the gun. Larry perspired and, once he saw Christian's grin, knew the illusion was over. Larry knew he couldn't pull the trigger. Christian knew he couldn't. Christian wound up his burly fist and knocked Larry to the ground, breaking his jaw from the sheer force of the punch. Christian returned to command, backpedaling Larry against the wall of the roof. The biker stalked ahead with a purpose, ready to finish the job he came to do. In the background, Christian heard the sound of Devin honking the horn. He groaned under his breath. Someone heard the screaming and made a complaint to the police. They were on their way. Larry backed himself into the corner and was left with absolutely no where to go. His adrenaline rush started to wear off while Christian switched into the next gear, the final gear. Booting Larry in the midsection, Christian removed the gun from his hands. Larry swore while Christian picked up the gun and held it against his own head in mockery. "Funny thing about bein' in front of guns all your life...You get that feelin' when one's empty and ain't got no more bullets in it." With the gun still at his temple, Christian pushed down on the trigger. Nothing happened. He sighed in disgust and brought the weapon down to his side. "You only brought one bullet up here, figured it'd be easy to put it in your head after all the shit ya been through." Christian said, crouching down and getting in Larry's face, "Probably went off with all that shaking and shit ya did. Fuckin' amateur thinkin' I ain't gonna know that shit." Christian put the gun back in Larry's hands and placed his arms across his knees. The fear had come back full circle for Larry and he was in the tail end of an emotional swing. Tears streamed down his face and fatigued muscles screamed at him for more respite. He cradled up into a ball and backed further against the wall. "Let me bottom line this for ya son...Even if ya had enough of a sack to kill me when ya saw the knife, ya know that ya ain't ever gonna win. Put me down and ya still don't make it off the fucking roof. Devin down there got a few rounds in his gun, he'll whack ya and haul before yer boys get here to save ya ass." Devin smacked the horn twice and stuck his head out the window, "HURRY UP GODDAMMIT!" Christian ignored his brother's concerns, bringing Larry's head up from his chest. "Look at it this way boy, at least ya ain't gotta deal with me anymore if ya off yerself. Ya ain't gotta deal with anybody. It's society's problem'ta take me down...And the upside'ta it...All them people you been cryin' and whinin' over will be right there for ya. Ya can hold em, hug em, kiss 'em, tell 'em ya love 'em anytime ya want. You stay here..." Christian smirked and bared teeth again, "I ain't even begun to do the worst shit I can do to you. I'll draw it out even longer, terrorize all yer friends, brother, Maria's family, make yer life so fuckin' miserable, dig all yer secrets up and expose em. You'll become the villain in our lil' story and anybody that puts you out will be the hero. Sad fact of life, but everybody around us is fuckin' gullible, lovin' change, and most of 'em hate cops anyway." Rising up to his feet, Christian dismissed three honks of the horn and reached into his pocket, retrieving a small box. "I'ma make this easy on ya boy since yer too stupid to have back up plans." Christian turned his back and fiddled with the gun while Larry rocked back and forth. He did his best not to completely explode. The perfect avenue to escape was present, climb down the stairs which were only a hundred feet away, and outrun Christian. But a part of Larry didn't want to. A part of Larry was content to stay on the defensive. His resolve had been completely sapped. The will to live slowly evaporated even in Christian's presence. A smack in Christian's hands resonated on the roof as the menacing biker turned around, flipping the gun back and forth. "It's loaded." Christian held the box to Larry's face and gave the distraught man a good look at the word 'Ammunition' on the front before pulling back and pocketing the remaining bullets. "You know where to put it." Nonchalantly extending the gun forward, Larry became transfixed and seized the weapon. Christian was presenting the ultimate insult to Larry and the entire Connors family. By putting the bullets into Larry's gun and handing him the loaded weapon, Christian would effectively stain Larry's legacy should the young man go through with the task before him. Larry had nothing left to live for and knew the end was not near, but here. Valuable seconds to Christian's escape passed before he flared his nostrils and tilted his head, annoyed that Larry was taking such a long time. Larry slowly brought the gun to his head and placed it against his temple. He gave a hard swallow and shut his eyes, prepared to end the pain once and for all. "Nah uh," Christian said, shaking his head, "You ain't goin' out like that. Your gonna look me in the eyes when you do it." Releasing a powerful grunt, Larry opened his eyes and gave his most menacing stare at Christian. The biker folded tired arms and waited for the inevitable. Devin honked the horn four times in a row. Time was really running out. Keeping his eyes locked on Christian's, the two exchanged a nod. Larry put pressure on the trigger and slumped over onto the cement, head smacking off with a thud. He didn't go out on his terms, but at least he went out. Both men got what they wanted out of the confrontation. Larry will get to see his family in heaven while Christian and the Smith crew move one step closer to complete liberation. Christian removed the gun from Larry's hand, also taking the bullet next to Larry's head. While crouched, he delved into Larry's pocket, searching for his wallet, and found a worn and folded piece of paper. Christian pushed off his legs and rose to a vertical position. He unfolded the note and found a familiar poem, one read many times in his life. Remembrance is a golden chain Death tries to break, but all in vain. To have, to love, and then to part Is the greatest sorrow of one's heart. The years may wipe out many things But some they wipe out never. Like memories of those happy times When we were all together. Christian stared at the note for a long second and back over at the corpse of Larry Connors. Folding the note back up, Christian gave three nods of his head before turning his back on the scene.
