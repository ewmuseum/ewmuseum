A Return To Zenith: Chapters I-III

15 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Posted 15/09/06 Chapter I: Outcast It had nary been a month since the once-glorious ruler of Zenith had been deposed, once and for all, his loyal subjects shocked and dismayed to see his defeat at the hands of a foe he had long held off battling. But his displacement as monarch had come long before; at the hands of an evil force pushing and kicking its way to rule the bountiful land. The invading force was full of in-fighting factions and former allies of the great king Lisieux turned tail to side against him in the everlasting battles of the past few months. Through it all, his right-hand man; the honourable knight Sir Adam, had stood firm by his side, but to no avail. Prince Lisieux had finally fallen for the last time, long after the cries of “The king is dead, long live the king!” had aired. Despite his fervent dissociation from them now, Sir Adam had long ago been at one with the tyrannous forces, a fact he now hung his head in shame for. But his crimes had been repented upon a thousandfold and more. Yet, despite this, he had still spent his time from his return to his recent dismissal locked in a middle court jester role, something that nearly drove him away from the fine lands entirely. But his release had come with the banishment of Prince Lisieux, at the next meeting of the crown court Sir Adam had been issued with a notice of eviction – his tormentors had grown weary of his jester act and decided to exile him. It was not the end of him, though. Not by far. In his hand he held a newly signed paper, giving him safe passage through the dark lands that plagued many a would-be entrant into Zenith all the way through the outskirts of the Ay Llyas municipality to the central court. There he would unfurl the second paper, one hidden away in the innermost pocket of his riding cloak, entitling him to challenge the Duke of Ay Llyas to a duel. He had duelled against the Duke before, a fine man – his past shrouded in mystery – but the contest had been a farce and had eventually led to Sir Adam’s removal from the kingdom, though in a secret deal which provided him with the precious documents he now carried. Chapter II: A Land Divided The two-day trek from the hilltop camp to the gateway into Zenith would be a tough one, in every thicket of trees a bandit awaited; ready to steal the coveted belts of gold that gave credentials to the claims staked to the counties laid out in the kingdoms. In the past few months a spate of such thefts has terrorised every county Lord and, though none would admit so, each one held their Title deed closer with every report of another theft. The belts of the far off twin-county of Tagg had finally been rested upon a pair of shoulders, though not without some devious goings-on, in last month’s annual jousting meet. The control of the much debated semi-circular Spirit Of Intense Glory grounds had finally come to a conclusion, only for the winning jouster to fall from his horse on the ride back and disappear in a bog, his body still missing to this day. The matching grounds of The Sky Of Five Stars, framing the central castle on the other side of the moat encircling it, had been with a dutiful, if selfish and vain, Lord for a while now. His place was challenged and duly defended at the jousting competition, an ageing former Count not proving his worth enough to reign once more. Even the district of Al Lyas had come under siege in this age of robbery, though once again a rightful owner was now settled upon. Despite the obvious, and tempting, allure of the Title deeds to Spirit Of Intense Glory and The Sky Of Five Stars, Sir Adam’s thoughts had no place for these estates – his main concern being in the most outlying lands of Ay Llyas. They are the ones through which every traveller into Zenith must path, as a way of proving themselves worthy, in essence, to lay claim to being a legitimate and proud resident of Zenith. As with every region of the land, there were ways to avoid passing through the land of Ay Llyas on the way to the central courts and castle. The legitimate route of Tagg was one not often used; indeed, spates of contest for the ownership there were few and far between, but usually happened in a series within a short time before the despot settled down and remained the Title deed holder for many moons. But every now and then a new face would appear in the realm of The Sky Of Five Stars or the tract of Spirit Of Intense Glory, someone who had somehow wiled his way past the Ay Llyas territory, through past connections, bribes or sheer blind luck. It had even been known for a traveller from outside to pass through all the outer lands, without so much as even meeting their Lords, to challenge the king directly, but this was fast become just a tale from days of yore. Sir Adam had only eyes for Ay Llyas and the grand castle itself. The castle, hidden in the innermost grounds, central amongst all the other principalities, held his gaze because of his familiarity of walking their halls under its former governance and his contempt for their current (and illegitimate, in his eyes) ilk. He knew those stony corridors well, and he planned to deliver merciless vengeance with his blade upon the traitorous and villainous alike for his Prince’s displacement and degradation at their hands. However, upon his arrival there he wanted good and solid grounding for his mission; he wanted proof of his worth, not for his own name and honour, but also for that of his fallen ally and confrere. The man who had seemed so much like a kinsman as to negate any need for bloodlines between them. And thus his passage through the outermost grounds was necessary, the thinly-spread land encircled all within it, bar the far-off Tagg district, and so defence happened often, with the previous Lord tending to move on into the lands of The Sky Of Five Stars or Spirit Of Intense Glory. Basically any route into the land required passage through Ay Llyas, and this suited Sir Adam perfectly. Chapter III: The Road Back To Zenith (Is Paved With Former Glory) After a quick survey of the road ahead Sir Adam geed his horse and set off, calling to the four winds to give him Godspeed on his journey. Riding the rough-shod road was tough, but years of duelling and jousting had toughened the man – he had been riding under the banner of Zenith for longer than any other in the kingdom, though still without having laid his hands upon an ownership of any of it’s four districts. With a pat of his cloak, to double-check the precious papers, Sir Adam let slip a rare smile – he had his challenge awaiting him. Fresh and rested, Sir Adam was more ready to take up his place with the Viscounts and Earls than ever before – he rode as his own man, he had no-one to hold him back. Though he would never have let on to his Prince Regent that he had ever entertained thoughts of this nature, he secretly held the belief in his heart than he had been held back from eminence by his loyalty to his Prince. As he rode, memories of his past tenure in the land flashed through his mind like a mace whirled overhead. He remembered his last duel in Zenith before he packed up to leave for lands afar, over two years previous. He had come up against a close friend and ally, even more mysterious than the current Ay Llyas Lord. The dark-cloaked and masked adversary had topped him, leading him on to a successful bid to topple the king and take the throne for himself, but the thought of this did not dampen the spirits of Sir Adam – instead it lifted them. He had carved himself a spot in the upper echelons of the high courts of Zenith all that time ago, two years on he felt more ready than ever before to start from the lowest of rankings and climb through. He knew he had to do all he could possibly do to bring the good name of his Prince and - dare he imagine it - himself to their rightful prominence, and taking the land of Ay Llyas as his own was step one on the way. As the border between the outer lands and the dark surroundings of Zenith loomed, he swung his hand round to his side and released his blade from its leather prison. The sword was old, but well tempered from years of battle – his Prince had held a blade shiner and sharper than any other when he first rode into the land and took it by surprise, but the speed of his ascent reflected itself in his weapon’s corrosion. By the time of his expulsion the edge was dented, blunted and battered. Holding his own blade before him, Sir Adam could see these features – but on a lesser scale; he had kept it well maintained and had more than enough use left in it to complete his quest. The blood of his fallen foes and the sweat of his own hand discoloured the handle, but to him that only proved his right to wield the blade. His upcoming opponent had his sword varnished in blood of a mere fraction of the number of men that Sir Adam had cut down. Re-sheathing the weapon with a satisfying swish, as the edge ran down the length of the casing, Sir Adam gave his silver-white mare another spurring and tucked his head down, to lower his profile and use the work of the Gods of the wind to his advantage. “Onward! Destiny awaits!”
