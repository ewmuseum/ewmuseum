Avoiding Intense

10 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/10/09 You can’t have a moment where you don’t doubt yourself. It’s just not in your programming. It has happened every time you’ve ever won a championship. You take your belt home, and it’s the same ritual. You imitate the ring announcer shouting “AND NEWWWWWW CHAMPION…” and then you spin several times, raising your belt for all of the invisible fans to see. You usually jump on your couch as if it’s a turnbuckle post and pump your arm a few times in victory. And then, as soon as you get over the euphoria that washes over you, you think about everything else, and you promptly forget about your meaningless title belt in your little federation. In SCCW, it was your infidelity that brought you back to the real world. When you finished so strongly in the Dual Halo, it was the fact that Katie wasn’t with you that dulled the celebration. And now that you’re the Intense Champ? It’s a familiar doubt, one you haven’t felt since you won another belt: the FUSE Custom C.H.A.O.S. Title. That was your first belt since going pro, and it was regarded with a lot of praise. After all, you won it in your third match and you had taken down two veritable FUSE legends to get there. There was a lot of buzz about you, and holding that championship, the second largest in FUSE, gave you a lot of pride. It made you feel like you had finally made it. But even through all of that, there was still doubt there. Doubt that you just couldn’t shake. ---- 6/10/07 “I’m proud of you, Jonathan. I really am, so I hope you won’t take this the wrong way, but…are you really going to be that guy who carries his belt everywhere? ‘Cause I won’t lie, babe: that’s a little pathetic.” Katie’s smile betrays her scathing words as she sits across the table from her beau and newly minted FUSE Custom C.H.A.O.S. Champion. Jonathan matches her smile and pats the title lightly. “Not forever. Maybe just for the first few weeks. This is special to me.” Katie nods, reaching across to grab his hand. “I know, and you deserve it. I’m really happy for you. I wasn’t just saying that.” “Yeah.” Jonathan then looks down at his title, and his smile slowly melts away. It’s a gradual action, one that moves so slow it takes a while for Katie to notice it. When she does, though, her concern is immediate. “What’s wrong?” “What?” he says, suddenly looking up, as if even he didn’t realize he wasn’t smiling. “You look troubled all of a sudden. Is it what I said? Because I really didn’t mean…” He interrupts her by holding up his hand and shaking his head. “No, it has nothing to do with you at all. It’s just…” He stares at the title again, and sighs perhaps a bit too loudly. Other eyes drift towards the table. “The title?” she asks. He shrugs. “Yeah, it’s just…I don’t really know what it represents. You saw what Clinton Sage told me. He can hold that Universal Title knowing exactly what it makes him: the best this place has to offer. And Ethan Frost is the Livewire Champion, which means he can hold claim to being the best technical wrestler on the roster.” “And your title doesn’t represent any of those things?” He looks at her for a second in disbelief: it’s hard for him to grasp that his girlfriend still doesn’t understand the details of his belt. “The Custom C.H.A.O.S. Title…this hunk of gold right here…” he holds up the title, which is covered in gold plated debris – tape, icons of steel chairs, an image of two men falling off of a ladder – “this thing just means I can take a chairshot to the head particularly well.” Katie shakes her head, trying her best to be supportive. “No, it’s more than that Jonathan, you’re more than just a…” “But I’m not. I won three matches to get this belt, all more brutal than the rest. I didn’t get to show my technical prowess, or my high flying ability, or anything else that got me the contract in the first place. As long as I’m the champ of this division, I basically just have to hit people with blunt objects and hope they don’t hit me harder.” “So drop the title.” “It’s not that simple,” Jonathan sighs. “This title is a double edged sword. On one hand it represents all of the hard work I’ve put in, and shows that I have accomplished something special. On the other hand: what is that accomplishment really saying? That I can take lightbulb shards in my back and still win a match? It’s just…I don’t know what this title says about me. I always pride myself on being a good guy who fights for what’s right. But how good is a guy who brutally attacks his opponent with whatever he can get his hands on? How is it right to bleed opponents dry in some sick and twisted gimmick match?” Jonathan looks across the table at Katie for a satisfying answer, one she can’t hope to give. ---- And now, exactly two years later, you find yourself in the same situation. A lot has changed in two years. You’ve built your name from simply a hardcore fighter to one that has become recognized with great things: championships, stables, truth and honor. No one can say that Jonathan Rhine isn’t a good fighter, because the past two years have proved that and more. But as you sit in your home, looking semi-proudly at your newly won Intense Title, you feel the same sharp edges on each side. You came into PRIME like a man on a mission, and with your victory at UltraViolence you fulfilled that mission. You wanted to take out the biggest, baddest dog in the yard to prove you still had your bite. And you did. Hessian had not lost to any one man in PRIME since arriving, and you were able to keep him down. The title you now hold represents one of the biggest wins in your career over one of the biggest names in wrestling. But the other edge is there, and it’s a little bit sharper this time. Not only have you carved yourself back into the niche of the hardcore wrestler, but your personality has slowly started to form itself into the mold that niche has created. In FUSE, you hadn’t really done anything that bad aside from hitting your opponents with hammers and stop signs. But those were sanctioned matches. Some of the things you did to Hessian…those were not sanctioned. You try to tell yourself that you were pushed into that position, that you were playing the part of the cornered animal who has no option but to lash out at its attacker. But that doesn’t change the fact that you tried to harm – and perhaps even kill – another human being, no matter how nefarious that being was. Ever since the Xavier Kannon rivalry, you were beginning to wonder if maybe all of the things you had been through was turning you into a worse person, someone who isn’t above hurting the people he has issues with. And your recent ordeals with The Murder Show have pretty much confirmed all of those concerns. Now it has gotten to the point where you no longer are content with just wondering. You turned your life around once before, and you’re determined to do it again. So you pick up the phone, you dial a number, and you make a pledge to get yourself back to the person you were when you started out. So to get there, you start the only way you know how. At the beginning. ---- 6/10/09 “…Jonathan?” “Hey, dad.” There is a long pause on the phone as Gregory Rhine tries to think of a suitable opening line. One that shows how angry the family is that Jonathan has neglected them. One that is perhaps a little witty, to make Jonathan feel even worse for moving away. He even considers simply hanging up to show Jonathan that it doesn’t matter how many titles he’s won, this family doesn’t need him to survive. Instead, Greg Rhine says, “It’s good to hear from you, son.” And so Jonathan begins the first step on his path back to morality: mending bridges and then (hopefully) crossing them.
