Date: The progression of seven years Time is an ever-progressive element in our lives. It always marches forward, trampling over our memories and everything we hold dear. Nothing can ever defeat time, nor can anything really stand up against it. No man can really fight against time, not without breaking the very foundations of everything humanity had come to understand about its existence. Progression is, among many other things, the act of time moving forward. Through progression, people age. They grow wiser, but they grow older. They grow more experienced, but they grow less unpredictable. And eventually, after the progression of a certain amount of time... The clock stops. ...and people die. * . * July 7th, 2001; Providence, Rhode Island It was a typical summer in Rhode Island, on a lazy Friday. The only thing atypical about this day was the light rain that was slowly getting heavier and heavier as a thunderstorm slowly crept closer to Providence. The rain was keeping most of the Knox family indoors, away from their courtyards. It had been two months since Earl Knox left for Hollywood. Soren Knox had graduated high school and was already getting scholarship offers from top universities. Steve Knox was leaving his football team to tackle becoming more of a wrestler, even finding a couple of dates to work. Meanwhile, Simon Knox was content on just waiting for his father to return home so he, too, could follow in his footsteps. Everybody in the mansion were doing their own thing. Samantha Knox was in the kitchen, working on dinner for that night. Soren Knox was looking at all of the different college offers he had received. Steve Knox was in the gym working on his weightlifting. Jeffrey Knox was slouching around in his own room, watching TV and generally not contributing to much. Siobahn Knox was working on her homework. As for Simon Knox, he was in his room and lifting weights, trying to prepare his body for the horrendous amounts of torture that his father would no doubt subject him to in the attempt to convince Simon that he was not ready to become a wrestler yet. His white shirt was clearly covered in sweat, which was slowly dripping onto his jeans. Over in the corner was his bed, and that's where his rather uninvited guest, Lieselotte Weber, stationed herself. Lieselotte had her brown hair in a short ponytail, she wore a short-sleeved pink shirt and a pair of denim shorts. Simon had long since figured out that Lieselotte had some sort of strange fixation with him, but he didn't pay her too much mind. She tended to creep him out at times, and he still couldn't really understand anything she ever said, but he figured she was harmless enough. Regardless, Simon needed to continue his training. Work, work, flex, build that muscle, build that endurance. Lieselotte was half-paying attention to Simon's efforts and half-paying attention to her English book. Because she spent some time paying attention to Simon, she found it hard to focus on her book, but she really didn't want to leave, either. Finally, though, Simon had enough of his workout. After all, a twelve-year-old probably didn't have that much endurance to handle full-on body building quite yet. Simon looked up to see Lieselotte laying on his bed, shook his head, and simply left the room to go take a shower. To his annoyance, he heard his door open and shut behind him, which meant that Lieselotte was following him out. He paid her no mind, and he walked until he got to the bathroom. Once there, he stopped to turn to Lieselotte, "Are you going to follow me all the way to the bathroom?" She stopped, looked at Simon, and then quietly shook her head. She walked past Simon and the bathroom, and just continued on her way. Simon shook his head in confusion, and then stepped into the bathroom. Shower, shower, scrub, get clean, build those movie star looks. With the shower completed, Simon left the bathroom with only his shorts on and a towel over his head. He was intent on returning to his room and finding a better shirt, but then he caught a voice coming from the lobby of the room. "How... how did it happen?" a shaky, distraught voice asked. It clearly belonged to his mother, Samantha Knox. Curious as to why his mother sounded like she did, Simon peaked out of the lobby and saw that Samantha Knox was talking on the phone. Samantha Knox was a tall, lovely woman in her mid-40s at the time. She had short brown hair, and she was very sensitive to the cold, which caused her to dress up a lot more warmly than someone would normally. Despite it being the summer, Samantha chose to wear a brown sweater and black pants. At the moment, from what Simon could tell from a distance, she looked like she was shedding tears and having a hard time actually composing herself. "A-an accident?" she asked to the person on the other side of the phone. Simon had this terrible feeling at the pit of his stomach. He had a terrible feeling about this conversation. "...T-thank you." she said, a voice that sounded like she really didn't want to thank anyone at all. She hung up the phone... or rather, it was more like she dropped it onto its hook. She fell to her knees at such speed that she needed to put her hands out underneath her just so she didn't smack her face on the floor. The tears flowed and would not stop. Probably against his better judgement, although he would have to face the information sooner or later, Simon approached his mother and knelt down beside her, before asking, "What's wrong, mom?" His mother simply responds by suddenly grabbing him into a hug, crying onto his shoulder. Simon could only make out a couple of words that she spoke, but he only needed a couple of words to understand what had happened. "He's dead, Simon..." Samantha Knox muttered, holding Simon tighter, "Your father, he's dead." Simon Knox would later say that much of what happened for the next five days after this conversation was nothing short of a blur. He did not remember anything between this day and the events of five days later, the shock of losing the one he admired the most was simply too great for someone as young as himself to bear. * . * July 12th, 2001; Providence, Rhode Island Simon Knox didn't really know the circumstances of his father's death even five days after the fact, the only thing that he did know was that he died in some sort of accident on the set of his new movie. Details and circumstances were not the things that were on Simon's mind at the moment; the only thing he had was grief. Today was the day of the funeral, and as one might imagine, the mood was about as cheerful as a funeral can get. Which was to say, the very opposite of the word. Moods were somber, the dress was black, and the soft sound of music fitting for a funeral was being pumped through the speaker systems in the funeral home. The Knox family and its large number of members shambled through the front doors of the home, looking as if they were just going through the motions. The three brothers, Steve, Simon, and Soren, all came in wearing black suits. So, too, did Jeffrey Knox, who wore a suit for what had to have been one of the very few times in his life. The girls, Siobahn, Lieselotte, and Samantha, all wore black dresses. Samantha Knox, in particular, was wearing a veil over her face, which did nothing to shroud her sorrow. The actual funeral parlor was decorated almost like it was sterile, the walls, floors, and ceiling were white and shiny. The only actual features of the room were the benches that the family could sit in, the organ in the corner, and, of course, the casket where Earl Knox laid. The main family soon met with other loved ones who had also gathered in the parlor, such as distant relatives, and people that Earl had met over the years. Simon recognized some of the people that weren't related to him. Bob Woods, a promoter of a western territory in the Washington and Oregon areas who had been a long-time rival of Earl Knox's, was standing around with his daughter, Rosemary. He was talking to another man, Kyle Morris, who was another rival of Earl's during his heyday. The two were seemingly sharing stories about Earl Knox and his accomplishments in the ring, but Simon really didn't pay attention to what any of them said about him. Instead, Simon let the rest of his family talk to the other family members, and slowly approached the casket. The casket was opened, displaying at least the upper part of Earl's body. His eyes were closed, his face flushed, as if he wasn't really asleep and was instead simply dreaming. Such a thought hit Simon Knox with a heavy blow. Suddenly, he couldn't walk straight, as if he was carrying the entire weight of the world. The pressure of his feelings, which had been contained for the last five days, had become too much for him to bear. He held his head with his hands, perhaps trying to keep those feelings bottled up, but the involuntary tears were already falling fron his eyes. He felt something warm take him from behind. Despite the tears in his eyes, he managed to turn to see Lieselotte trying to comfort him. "I am... so sorry." she said to him. Simon managed to carry the weight of the world that had crashed down on top of him just long enough to sit down on one of the nearby benches. Lieselotte sat down next to him, but stayed quiet like she usually did. He couldn't really focus on anything except the fact that his father was dead. He couldn't do anything except cry. However, eventually, Simon stood up. It came so suddenly that Lieselotte was surprised at the movement. Simon turned to her, and although he still looked utterly sorrowful, he still nodded to Lieselotte. "Thanks." he said, before he turned around, "I need some air." Before Lieselotte could say a word, Simon Knox had left her side and bolted for the door. He blew by the rest of family quick enough that they knew something was wrong, but none were quick to find the words to comfort him. Simon Knox's world had been forever changed, and it would take him years to figure out where he would go from here. * . * November 29th, 2004; Providence, Rhode Island Jeremy Odessa was a former wrestler who now spent time training the new generation. As a trainer, he was best known for training High Flyer. However, he was also known for his rivalry with Earl Knox in his heyday, and the two developed a mutual respect for one another... enough so that Earl trusted Jeremy with Steve Knox's training in his stead. Jeremy's business as a trainer was usually much greater in the summer than in the late fall and winter months, and this year had been particularly tough on him. Jeremy used to live in Pennsylvania, but he moved to Rhode Island as early as three years ago, and had opened a gym in Providence. Closing up his office, Jeremy fished inside his pockets to look for his keys, when he heard the sound of footsteps approaching from behind. He made a glance towards that direction, seeing the approachment of a man who looked to still be in his adolesence. The young man had brown hair that grew just over his ears. He wore sunglasses, a collared white shirt, and a pair of khaki pants. He looked fairly professional for just a kid, so Jeremy suspected that something was up. "School's closed, kid." Jeremy said, "`Least for the winter." "Reconsider it." the man said. His voice was low, nearly devoid of emotion. "Okay," Odessa said. He paused for about one second before adding, "Nope." Strangely, the demeanor of the man standing before him didn't waver, not one bit. Jeremy was finding it hard to figure out what this guy was really thinking, and part of him thought that he'd probably be really good at poker. After a few seconds of being glared at, the man spoke to him, "You look like you have something to say, Odessa." "That's Mr. Odessa to you, junior. What's with your attitude? You think you can just walk up and tell me to 'reconsider' reopening my school just for you?" he asked, "Who do you think you are?" The only thing that came from the man is a short, though obvious sigh. He reached up to his face and removed his sunglasses, casually bending them into a closed state. "Let me tell you a story. I admired my old man, he was kind of a professional wrestler. Like you. Couple of years ago, he made me a promise that he'd train me. I was... thrilled at that prospect. All I had to do was wait. Wait, and he'd come back, and he'd introduce me to this business the same way he introduced my older brothers. ........And I've been waiting for three years now. He's not ever coming back." "And what's this got to do with me?" Odessa asked. For some reason, the man standing before him was somehow familiar to him, but he couldn't quite put a finger on why that was. "You haven't figured it out yet, have you?" the man asked, "My old man was Earl Knox." Jeremy blinked. "You can't be... Simon, can you?" he asked. "That's right." he said, "I'm Simon Knox." "Then I assume you're wanting to follow in your father's footsteps, like Steve is?" Jeremy asked. All this got out of Simon was a soft chuckle, more of a dismissive gesture than that of actual amusement. "That's what you think? I'm just going to go right into wrestling because dad did it? You think too small, Mr. Odessa," he said, emphasizing the "mister" part just because of Odessa's previous remark about his name, "I'm aiming to do things exactly as he did." "'Exactly as he did?'" Odessa asked. Another sigh from Simon Knox. "Become a wrestler, become highly successful, enough so that Hollywood comes knocking. That is my goal, Mr. Odessa." Simon said, running a hand through his hair as he said those words. Jeremy stared blankly at Simon Knox for a few seconds, before he responded, "Why not just... I dunno... go drive to Hollywood, liver in near-poverty a couple of years, and do a couple of auditions like everybody else?" Once again, Simon did that chuckle that Jeremy knew he would come to hate if he actually let Simon talk him into training him, "Because I'm not everybody else, Mr. Odessa. I'm the son of a great wrestler and actor. All I'm asking you to do is train me, and then you can go on with whatever else you do these days." Jeremy pondered this, before he decided to ask his next question, "Well, you're what? All of about fifteen years old? You're a bit young for this." "Sixteen, actually," Simon corrected him, "And don't give me that bullshit 'you're too young' excuse, Mr. Odessa. You trained my brother after my dad got through with him, and he was younger than I am now." Jeremy paused, and sighed to himself, "Are you always this difficult?" Simon smirked, "I'm just a product of my generation, Mr. Odessa. Now, I can make it here as often as I can, and I'm still willing to shell out the cash. You don't have anything to lose other than some time. You got plenty of that, don't you?" The sounds of grumbling were heard from Odessa, but he nodded quietly in defeat. He knew that there was no legitimate reason to turn down Simon Knox's request, even despite how much of a jerk he was in actually making the request, "Alright. I'm only doing this because you're Earl's son. Show up tomorrow at 10AM, and don't be late." Simon Knox smirked again, "I won't be." * . * April 18th, 2006; Salt Lake City, Utah It had been a year and a half since Simon Knox began to train in becoming a professional wrestler, and nearly five years since his father's death. Simon's road to wrestling glory had not been as easy as he originally anticipated. He was not only being held to his father's standards, but also his older brother, Steve's, standards, and in some cases was just having trouble finding work. In most cases, it was because of his age, Simon was still seventeen years old. Being a home-schooled child, Simon didn't have too much of a need to actually stay in one spot, and Simon was travelling more and more recently. For example, today, Simon Knox was visiting another wrestling school, that of Curtis "the Hammer" Brown. Curtis was a professional wrestler between the 1950s and the 1970s, whose heyday was, in fact, long before the heyday of Earl Knox. Curtis was in his early 70s, and did not actually oversee his own wrestling academy these days. Rather, the job was left to Curtis's older sons, Robert and Jordan, whose wrestling stars faded much faster than their father's, or even that of their younger brother, Allen. Simon Knox was only here as a guest, asked by the Brown brothers to show others his style. What the brothers did not tell Simon was that they had wanted Steve Knox, but rather, they received the wrong phone number. When Simon gave his name and mentioned he too was a wrestler, the brothers decided to just let him come by anyway while they tried to acquire Steve's phone number. If Simon had known that this was the case, he probably would never have shown up tonight, which would have thereby altered the very course of Simon Knox's career progression. Simon didn't even have to step in the building for something to happen, as when he parked in the parking lot near the gym, he saw a young man being physically dragged out of the gym by the two tall black men that ran the gym, Robert and Jordan Brown. Simon calmly made his approach from the parking lot, though by the time he got to the gym, the Brown brothers were already gone and that young man just stood their, fuming. "God damn you both!" he shouted, "So what if I'm only sixteen!? What's that matter, you god damn jackasses?!" The kid kicked the air, obviously fuming, "You know what? You know fucking what!? I never wanted to train here, anyway! Who the fuck ever heard of the Brown brothers, anyway!? You guys are pathetic losers who couldn't make it in the business, so you sit around bullying the new guys! Fuck you! Fuck you all!" Connor threw the double deuce at the doors of the gym. By this point, Simon was now standing there, and he was ever-so-passively looking at this young man. "I don't know what the doors did to you, but I don't think they hear you." Simon said, sarcastically. This caused the young man to snap his head towards Simon Knox, looking at him angrilly. "Shut up! I can yell at whatever the fuck I want!" the man said. "Fuuu," dismissed Simon, as he stepped up a little more towards the young man, "So you can. So, why are you yelling at a door?" The young man looked at Simon and realized that he might not have been around for what happened inside. He kind of calmed down, but it was still obvious that he was livid, "Oh, those motherfuckers in there, they told me I was too young for wrestling. Said they wouldn't train me." "Most trainers don't usually give a shit how old you are as long as you got the money," Simon suggested, "So, how'd you managed to piss them off?" The man paused and thought about it for a bit, "Well, uh... maybe I kinda started it... but still! I paid them in advance for this shit! And this is how they treat me?" Simon nodded, and looked at the doors of the gym. He decided to ignore the building for the time being and talk to this guy, "Hmph. So they kicked you out because you raised some sort of ruckus?" "I swear to fucking Jesus, that other guy couldn't suplex his way out of a wet paper bag." the man said. He crossed his arms and then spit right on the door to the gym in disrespect, "What're you here for, anyway? I hope you're not planning to train here, these fuckers ain't worth it." "I was asked to teach, actually. I'm Simon Knox." he said. "Huh." was the man's only response. He turned to look at the man who maintained a passive expression, but scoffed, "Pfft. You're not that much older than me." "No," Simon said, before something dawned on him. Perhaps this could work out for him after all, "I bet I know somebody who could help you, though." The man raised an eyebrow, "Yeah?" "What's your name, anyway?" Simon asked. "Connor O'Reily." "Connor, huh?" Simon asked, rhetorically, before he proceeded to ask a much less rhetorical question, "Tell me. What do you know of a guy named Joey Malone?" The look in Connor's eyes upon hearing the name told Simon Knox everything he needed to know. * . * August 1st, 2008; Charlotte, North Carolina, eight days before Colossus V, Night 1 Within a ten days, Simon Knox and Connor O'Reily, who had finally gained national recognition as the "Princes of New England", would challenge Team VIAGRA from the PRIME Tag Team Championships. It had been a long, ardurous road for the two men... one man was a high school dropout who very nearly got himself kicked out of a wrestling academy because of his irresponsible and unpredictable nature, the other man was a dismissive jerk who was embittered at life after losing the man he admired the most. Their personalities were somewhat similar in their abrasiveness, yet their motivations and attitudes were both different. It could be considered amazing that two men who came from very different backgrounds could gel into the unit that they became, particularly with a young woman in the background who was trying to keep them in line. All it took was time. It took the progression of time to temper Simon Knox from the impressionable child that he once was into the cold, unemotional bastard that he became, and it took the progression of time to bring Simon Knox and Connor O'Reily together as the Princes of New England. Would they be able to reign at the top of PRIME as well? Only time could tell.
