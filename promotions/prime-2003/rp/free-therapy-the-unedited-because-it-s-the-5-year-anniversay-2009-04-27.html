Free Therapy (The \"Unedited because it\'s the 5-year anniversay of the first date with the wife\" edition)

27 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/27/09 “Okay, so what’s wrong this time?” At the very least, Jared Sykes had expected to make it through the door of the unobtrusive building that served as a wrestling school before being accosted by its owner, and his former trainer, Darren Stracker. What Jared hadn’t counted on was that because of the sudden and pleasant change in New England weather that the man he wanted to see would be enjoying the day outside. The words caught him off guard. “I… why did…?” Darren smiled, chuckling to himself. “We haven’t seen you in a few months, kiddo, not since you had your little emotional crisis over the Searle kid. I figure you came by then because something was bothering you, and I figure that something’s got to be on your mind this time. Tell me I’m wrong.” Jared fell silent, slipping his hands into the pockets of his hooded sweatshirt, and giving Darren the answer he was looking for. “Alright then, kid, come on inside.” “It’s not that something’s wrong, really,” Jared said, stepping through the door and into a hallway lined with photos, “There’s just been something on my mind for the last few days, and I wanted to get your opinion on it.” “Is that so?” “Yeah. I figure that of all the folks I know, you’re the one who’s going to give me the honest answer, and tell me what I need to hear whether or not you think it’s what I want to. I need someone I can trust.” Darren stopped in his tracks, and half-turned to face Jared. “Wait, you’re not coming out are you?” “What?” came the exasperated response. Jared too froze in his tracks. Eyes wide and jaw agape he stared at his mentor. “Dude, no. Not even. Where the… What the hell would make you say that?” “Wouldn’t care if you were, to be honest. Just figured that if it was the case better to get the hard part out of the way now. My son, Marty, he’s up in the office and the last thing I need is to explain something like that to a 7-year old.” “Well, then I’m happy to put your mind at ease. Jesus.” The two men continued on their pace and soon came to the end of the hallway. The corridor opened up into a wide space separated down the middle by an invisible line. On one side an 18x18 training ring was set up and surrounded by padded mats. The other side was home to free weights, weight machines, and a half dozen pieces of cardio equipment. “Place looks different than the last time I was here.” “Yeah, we bought the space next to us and knocked down some walls. I’m working on moving the locker rooms into some of the new space, and maybe even put in a small steam room. Figure the current lockers will make for a nice classroom, and maybe even a little media center.” “Oh sure, wait until I graduate before you make add the good stuff. Do you have any idea what a pain in the ass it was to balance a notebook while trying to keep my ass from going numb on the ring steps?” “Jared, you graduated ten years ago. If you were still here I’d have probably killed the both of us by now. Besides, you almost scared half your own class away. You think I’d still be in business with you as a student?” Playful jabs like this were not uncommon between the two men. Though it was rare that they saw each other, and though Darren was fourteen years older than Jared, the two looked on each other as brothers. Regardless of how much grief Jared was known to cause, Darren Stracker was one of only three people whose words he would follow without question. The two turned the corner into the training area and ascended a narrow staircase to the office whose windows looked down into the heart of the building. Jared closed the door behind him. “Uncle Jared!” Martin Stracker was the first of three children for the Stracker family. By all accounts he was a miniaturized version of his father, a near perfect clone: they had the same auburn hair, the same cool grey eyes, and even at 7 years old Martin was starting to show signs of his father’s wit. “What’s up, Mini-Marty?” “Uncle Jared, look at what I drew,” the smaller Stracker said, leaping out of his chair with a brightly colored sheet of paper in-hand. “Is that a robot… on a horse?” “That’s not a horse, that’s a Pegasus. He has to ride on it because he was injured in battle against the aliens, and his jetpack is broken.” Everything about Martin’s tone screamed “duh”. “Martin, I thought you were supposed to finish your homework,” the elder Stracker’s voice was calm yet stern. “You know the rules: homework first, playtime after.” “But I already finished my math.” “And the other subjects?” “But English is so boring. None of the stories I have to read are interesting. “Homework first, playtime later,” Darren folded his arms across his chest, his body language a clear indication to his son that this was not up for negotiation. “Your uncle and I have something to discuss, so I think now is a perfect time for that English homework. Don’t you think?” Martin sighed in defeat. “Yes, Dad,” he said, putting his headphones on and opening his English textbook. The two men stepped away from the small meeting area that had been set up in the office, and where Martin now resided, moving towards the desk. Darren took a seat in his chair, with Jared slouching in one of the seats opposite him. “Alright, Sykes, spill it.” “You’re going to think I’m insane.” “Well to be honest I already do, so now that that’s out of the way, spill it.” “I think I’m losing my mind.” Darren shook his head. “Look, do I have to keep making the obvious jokes, or are you just going to come out with it and tell me what’s on your mind?” “It’s about a dream I had. At least, I think it was a dream.” “I had a dream last night,” came a tiny voice partially buried in an English text, “I had a pet dinosaur, and…” “Martin. Homework. Now.” Darren’s eyes locked on his son, but Martin did not push the issue. He silently turned his gaze back to the text before him, at the very least to give his father the visual satisfaction that his son was working. “Sorry about that, Jared. Didn’t mean to derail you there.” “No, it’s no problem. I remember that age. I was a maniac.” “Of that I have no doubt, but, you were saying?” “Oh, right, the dream. You know, to be honest, I don’t really know what it was. I woke up the other night a little after 3am and found a guy in my living room.” “Someone broke in?” “No, see, it wasn’t like that. Guy wasn’t dressed like a thug or anything, and he was just sitting on the couch, like he was waiting for me. Knew stuff about me that only I, and maybe like two or three other people would know. He brought up Calvin, and Merv’, and then started spouting off about the future, but that’s not what made it weird.” “Seems pretty normal so far, at least for what I expect a Jared Sykes dream to be.” “Okay, so maybe this will be weird enough for you: the clock – none of the clocks, actually – they didn’t move. Didn’t tick away the minutes. They just all read the same time until he left. It was like everything stood still.” Darren leaned back in his chair and shrugged his shoulders. Given his history with Jared this was well within what he would consider the confines of normal. “After he left, I couldn’t fall back to sleep, really. I mean I drifted in and out for a while, but…,” Jared cut himself off, “You know what, screw it. None of that is important. The thing that’s really driving me nuts is what he said.” “And that would be?” “He said I was afraid, that I lived in fear. He said that everything I went through last fall was like a, what did he call it? ‘A psycho-emotional catalyst’ is I think what he said. He said that I’m destined to live alone and miserable because I’m too afraid to change things in my life that would make it the life I want. Am I making any sense?” “Oddly enough, yeah. I think it’s a decent enough point that your dream person made, and it’s obvious that this has got to you.” “That’s just it, I think he’s right. Or worse, I’m afraid that he’s right about me being afraid. It’s like some bizarre Mobius strip running through my head that I can’t get out of.” “So I figure that you want to know what I think? Whether any of this is true, and whether you actually have something to be concerned with?” “Ideally, yeah. I could call up Sebastian, the shrink the NWC hired to follow me around, but I don’t want some clinical, touchy-feely answer here. I need to know what’s up.” Darren leaned back in his chair and scratched at his goatee, considering the question. For a moment the room was silent save for Martin’s hushed mutterings about his English teacher and how stupid his homework was. Finally he spoke. “I think he might be right to a degree,” Darren paused for a moment, wondering whether Jared would try to refute the statement. When no response came, he continued, “But I don’t think that’s always been the case. I know when you told me what happened when you went overseas after the Council closed that it really screwed with you, and I know that you certainly had your issues after what happened with that Pierce woman, and maybe it’s made you a little guarded and a little more cautious, but who wouldn’t be cautious after a pair of incidents like that?” “No, I hear you.” “And to be quite honest, I don’t think it’s necessarily fear. You’re not the same kid I met in ’99. No matter how much of a pain in the ass you can be you’ve matured a lot since then, and while I don’t know what happens in your life day-to-day I sincerely doubt that you’re always looking over your shoulder and peeking under the bed for monsters.” Across the desk there was silence. Jared simply nodded along as Darren spoke, taking it all in. “You’re older. You’re arguably wiser. You’re more responsible in taking care of yourself and the people around you. That stuff’s to be expected, Jared, but if you really think that it’s fear, if you’re doing this all because you’re afraid of what your life will be if you don’t look for the potential of bad things to happen, then your life is going to suck. That’s all there is to it.” “Okay.” “Okay? Really?” “Yeah. I came here looking for honest, and you gave it to me. Now I just need to figure out what I have to do to fix things. Need to figure out where to begin.” Darren smiled, and folded his hands behind his head. Jared, who was still trying to digest the advice of his mentor, was visibly surprised by the sudden and dramatic change in body language. “Umm, you’re smiling. You know that, right?” “I do.” Darren’s smile widened. “Okay, for the record, you’re scaring the shit out of me right now,” no sooner did he say the words did Jared gasp, struggling to get out an apology. “Oh man, I’m sorry. I forgot your son was here.” “I can tell you where to begin.” “Seriously, the smiling. Can it stop? You’re kind of creepy.” “I’m smiling because this is just too good.” “What is? What the hell are you so happy about?” Darren stood from his seat and stepped over to the window, gesturing Jared to follow. As the blueberry king got to his feet, Darren was already pointing out the window of his office down into the ring on the other side of the glass. “Do you see who I see, Jared?” “No – fucking – way.” Even the slap to his head from Darren didn’t bring him back to his senses. Jared Sykes’ brain had shut down at the sight of her. “Your dream person wants to tell you about Justine Calvin? Well there she is. Why don’t you go say hi.”
