Walk With Me In Hell (I)

6 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6th May ”For I have sworn thee fair, and thought thee bright, who art as black as hell, as dark as night.” - William Shakespeare. Pulling the BMW to a halt and dropping it out of gear, Nathaniel Loftman pushed a breath mint between his teeth and crushed it between thirty thousand dollars worth of dental reconstruction. The engine\'s gentle purrrrrr hushed as he twisted the ignition and reclined in the driver\'s seat. He took-in his surroundings with a disgruntled sigh. The docklands wasn\'t the most aesthetically-pleased of locales on the best of days, but on such a damp and dreary afternoon it were about as attractive as a pig in a tutu. Sandwiched between unending stacks of rusty freight containers and the murky, grey depths of the Atlantic, the vehicle sat on a cracked bed of concrete and weeds. It was Sunday afternoon and there wasn\'t a soul to be found. The workers had finished their six-day weeks and left piles of empty crates and a few abandoned forklifts scattered around the place. Not a single boat cut through the harbour\'s swell, and the staff office to the west may as well have been plucked from Chernobyl and dropped port-side. Feeling very much like an extra from The Wire\'s second season, Loftman killed the REO Speedwagon that seeped from his speakers and found himself alone with nought but the gentle pitter-patter of rain against his car\'s roof for company. He sighed. Leaned forward and tightened his grip on the steering wheel. The superagent hadn\'t been looking forward to this moment and the weight of it clogged his gut like a ball of lead. These weren\'t the type of people he liked to do business with and having them mix with Aidan Chase could end in disaster for both parties. Loftman knew it, but he had no choice in the matter. He was indebted to them, and that small fact was enough to eliminate any argument his restless mind could conjure. He waited, his patience slowly dissipating with every passing second. If there was one thing that Nathaniel couldn\'t stand, it was waiting, and Aidan Chase should have arrived ten minutes prior. Loftman had intentionally left the office a little bit later than he normally would just so he wouldn\'t have to wait around on the docks like a desperate lowlife waiting for a prostitute, and yet there he was. Chase\'s lack of punctuality was something that he could understand, of course. The kid sounded like a mess on the phone; his voice shaky, wavering, and understandably so. This was a man that had unleashed a terrible wave of vengeance upon one of the most notorious professional fighters in New York City. A man who knew that it was only a matter of time before Jason Natas uncovered the truth behind his terrible deed. A man who knew he was in trouble. Regardless of Chase\'s situation, Loftman became worried for his own. Much like himself, Cordell Abraham was not a man to keep waiting. If he turned up to the meeting late – or even worse, without Aidan Chase – he\'d be in some seriously dangerous water. So he breathed a sigh of relief when he saw Aidan\'s beaten-up white Audi pull up in his rearview mirror. Slowly crawling between freight containers, The car came to an eventual halt alongside Loftman\'s significantly snazzier ride. Rolling down the window as Chase lowered his, Loftman nodded. “Took your time.” He didn\'t get the verbal response he was looking for, Nathaniel. Just a blank stare, and the slightest of nods. “Come on, we don\'t have all day...” “Alright, lead the way,” Aidan croaked; his voice sounded even more hoarse in-person than through a telephone speaker. “I\'ll follow your lead.” Loftman shook his head. “Nuh-uh. Get-in; we\'ll get their quicker with one car.” “One car?” Aidan\'s face twisted with confusion. “I can\'t just leave this thing sitting around here...” “What, you think someone\'s gonna be desperate enough to steal that piece \'a shit? We\'re only gonna be gone for a couple of hours max. Besides, look around you; ain\'t a single soul going \'round”. The Potemkin agent paused, looking for compliance from the young would-be wrestler. “Just get in the Goddamn car.” With a heavy sigh Aidan Chase finally unclipped his seatbelt and pushed his door open, narrowly avoiding a paint-scraping collision with Loftman\'s automobile. Locking the door behind him and shuffling around the hood of the BMW, Chase finally slid into the passenger\'s side as Loftman fired-up the engine. Aidan plopped his body down in a chair significantly comfier than he was used to and pulled the door shut. It was then that Loftman got his first good look of the kid\'s features. He was in a mess alright; with bloodshot eyes, thick stubble and dirty clothes that hadn\'t been washed in at least week, Aidan Chase was about as far removed as possible from the enthusiastic, bright-eyed kid he\'d tried to replace Jason Natas with in PRIME months prior. “You look like shit, kid.” “I know,” Aidan said. “You said that the last time...” “Heh,” Loftman said, thinking to himself that nothing had changed. “Buckle up.” He put the car into gear and pulled away from the dock, ready for whatever lay ahead of him. * * * * * “Going up,” said the distorted, electronic voice as Detective Harry Stone hit the button for the seventeenth floor. Stuffing his sausage-fingered hands into the pockets of his grey jacket, he let his heavy chest heave with a long, weary sigh. Going back to a crime scene was never the most exciting tasks, but unfortunately for Stone it was a necessary evil. He needed to know who it was that decided torching Jason Natas\'s apartment was such a bright idea, and while traipsing through piles of ash and charcoal wouldn\'t give him anything concrete, it might at least point him in the right direction. “I still think this is a dumb idea, you know,” said Stan Callaghan, his bald, built-like-a-brick-shithouse partner. “We must\'a looked \'round this place three or four times already, and CSI couldn\'t find shit with all their fancy equipment. We ain\'t even got a microscope.” “We need anything we can get, Stan. You know that.” Harry glared at his partner. Stan was big - way big. With shoulders broader than anyone else on the force and standing at least four inches taller than the average human being, he often reminded Harry of one of those guys from the UFC. “I\'m not expecting to find anything pertinent either, truth be told.” “Then what the hell are we doing here?” Callaghan\'s moody scowl was accentuated by the furrowing of his heavy brow. “Because at the moment, we need even the slightest flash of inspiration. We gotta build-up as much stuff on this guy as possible if we wanna follow-up on his priors, and I get the impression that there\'s more to this arson than Jason Natas is giving up.” He pauses, pondering. With a cheery bing the elevator came to a shuddering halt. “He\'s up to something, Stan. I can smell it.” With a rusty squeal the doors slid open. Following his partner into the hallway, Stan Callaghan walked as he talked. “You think he torched his own place?” “No. No chance; stupid bastard didn\'t even have the place insured.” “Well, who then?” Stan asked. The two paused as they reached the door they were looking for. Harry glanced at his partner. “A man with a history as, ahem, colourful as Jason Natas has gotta have accumulated more than a few enemies during his time on this planet. If we can begin to figure out who he pissed-off enough to wanna do this, we can find more dirt on the fella.” Harry reached down for the doorknob. Much to his surprise the door swept open upon the slightest touch. Immediately he dismissed any suspicious thoughts; this part of town was notorious for break-ins, squatting, and a myriad of other petty crimes that he had absolutely no interest in. So Harry entered the apartment, trying his best to step around the piles of ash scattered around the blackened floorboards like pixiedust. He walked through the charred apartment, glancing it\'s surroundings with a morbid curiosity. The place was totalled; it\'s destruction total and absolute. Smoke had left giant black smears on surfaces throughout the apartment, and the flames had been severe enough to blow out the windows and leave a giant hole in the wall separating the lounge and the bedroom. In fact, the only things that suggested this world of ash was once a home were the burnt-out shell of a television and the darkened springs of a long-destroyed couch. “Ain\'t nothing changed, Harry. Place is still as grim as it was two weeks ago,” Stan said, trying not to get soot all over his brand new loafers. “Look at this place, Stan,” said Harry, ignoring the protests of his partner. “It\'s completely annihilated. Wiped-off the face of the earth. Whoever did this didn\'t want to frighten Jason Natas, they wanted to send him a serious message.” “Heh. No shit...” “Can tell from the spread that they torched this place good. This wasn\'t no \'petrol through the letterbox\' job. Somebody knew what they were doing.” He bowed his head, treading carefully through what remained of the lounge. “Whoever did this must\'ve really, really wanted to see Natas suffer...” Stan saw him before Harry did. Standing in the ruined bathroom doorway, clad in denim and leather. “Shit, Harry...” “... our boy must be in some serious shit to piss someone off this bad...” “Harry!” “... thinking it was probably someone who\'d do-...” “HARRY!” And then Harry looked up. First at his partner, and then towards the shady character across the room than he was pointing to. Jason Natas was glowering at him. * * * * * “Where are we going, anyway?” Aidan Chase asked the question he\'d been waiting to spill all morning. He was suspicious. He knew that Loftman was a man with the resources and contacts to help him, but the fact that the usually ice-cool superagent had hardly said a word was gnawing at the back of his inner cortex. “Wait about one hundred and twenty seconds and you\'ll find out,” Loftman retorted, pulling the BMW into a quiet backstreet that Aidan didn\'t recognise. The situation was eating away at him like a terminal illness. He was a good kid, Aidan; never before had he found himself in any kind of trouble with anyone, and yet here he was, meeting shady businessmen at the docks, allowing himself to be trafficked towards a destination unknown. But he was a desperate man, and you know the kinda things that desperate men tend to do... right? Jason Natas had brought it on himself. Yes, Jason had nobody to blame but Jason for what happened to his apartment. Sure, it was by Aidan\'s hand, but Chase would never had done it if PRIME\'s Anti-Superstar hadn\'t bulled him out of his PRIME contract. He often thought of how sweet life might be if he\'d never met Natas in the first place. Rolling large with every hefty paycheck, travelling the country with Amanda by his side, never having to worry about overdrawn bank accounts and unpaid bills ever again... He swallowed back such fantastical visions like a jagged pill. It tore him to shreds knowing that his brush with Jason had cost him his career, and, indirectly, the love of his woman. Jason Natas had taken so much for him, and in return, Aidan had to take something back from Jason. He couldn\'t let it rest. He couldn\'t. It was an impulsive act, torching the apartment. He did it without thinking of the consequences, and now that they were upon him he knew he was in trouble. Soon enough, Jason would remember his face from that altercation in the alleyway, and he\'d come looking for him. Looking for revenge. With Jason\'s reputation and penchant for violence, that wasn\'t something that Aidan Chase could deal with, so he called the only man he knew with a bigger dislike for Natas than himself – Jason\'s agent. And so, there he was. Sitting in the passenger seat of Nathaniel Loftman\'s company BMW, heading for a meeting with a man Loftman promised could “make his troubles disappear.” So preoccupied with his own thoughts was Aidan that he didn\'t even notice that the car had pulled-up in a parking lot, and that Loftman had already unbuckled. “Alright, here we are...” Loftman pushed his door open and put one foot on the ground when he noticed Chase was in a daze. “Eh, hello... Earth to Mr. Chase...” “Oh, uh. Sorry.” Aidan said, shaking his head, regaining his composure. He left the vehicle and, with Loftman strode up to the curb. Before them was a building of red bricks and grey cement. The sole window on the building\'s front was barred and an old sign labelled “Abraham\'s Gym” hang above the door, it\'s paint beginning to chip and flake. Somewhat surprised that Loftman hadn\'t taken him to some place a little grander, Chase turned to the agent. “What, this is it?” “Yes, Einstein. Now hurry up.” Aidan looked up again at the sign and shook his head. Loftman was already halfway through the door. * * * * * “Well, uh. Jason. This certainly is a pleasant surprise...” That\'s what Detective Stone said to the brazen wrestler after he\'d caught his breath. “Touché, piggy. Was just gon\' ask what the fuck ya think yer doin\' in my apartment...” Jason snorted as he pushed his body away from the doorframe. Showing little regard for the mess on the floor, he slowly, heavily, paced his way towards the detectives. “We\'re the cops, asshole,” Stan suddenly barked. “We can come and go as we please.” “Zip it, Conehead. Ain\'t nobody askin\' for your opinion,” said Jason. Callaghan parted his lips to utter another venomous response, but found himself being cut off by Stone\'s “take it easy, Stan.” A reassuring glance from his elder partner was all the convincing that Callaghan needed to back down. “Leave it with me. I got this.” “Aye, baldo. Stan\' in the corner and keep playin\' that third wheel role,” Jason sneered, intentionally trying to pull the strings of the short-tempered Major Crimes detective. He continued across the ocean of burned personal effects, eventually reaching a spot a couple of feet away from Harry Stone. Pulling a cigarette from behind his ear and lighting it, Jason looked at a man he\'d became very suspicious of from under a furrowed brow. “You ain\'t told me what yer doin\' here, yet.” “The investigation is ongoing, Jason. You know that.” “Matter of fact, I don\'t. Figured when I told you guys I didn\'t know shit you\'d fuck off and attach yerselves t\' some other bullshit case.” “Thing\'s aren\'t that simple. You didn\'t think we\'d just take your word and can the whole thing, did you?” Stone smirked. “Shit, you have any idea how bullshittin\' to the lieutenant that\'d take? Nah. Let\'s just say we\'re looking into things a little more closely. Exploring a few new avenues.” Jason nodded, exhaling cigarette smoke through his nostrils. Stone and his slaphead buddy were up to something. He knew it. “Wastin\' yer time, piggy. Prob\'ly jus\' some crazy fuck messin\' \'round with fire an\' things he shouldn\'t \'ave. Not worth losin\' any sleep o\'er; lord knows I haven\'t” “That\'s kinda strange, if you don\'t mind me saying. Your flat\'s been burned down, you\'ve lost all your worldly possessions. Figured you\'d be a little more... upset.” “\'Upset\'?” Jason scoffed. “Fuck that. Shit\'s all material; ain\'t nothin\' that can\'t be replaced. B\'sides, this hotel room they\'ve gone hooked me up in\'s much nicer than this place ever was.” Harry nodded, watching the ever-stoic wrestler take another long suck on the cancer stick. Jason was good; he\'d clearly done this before. He wasn\'t giving them anything, no matter how deep Stone pried. He\'d prepared himself well for the inevitable shitstorm that followed, but Harry knew that Jason knew more than he was letting-on. They always do, guys like Natas. You don\'t come from a background like his by blubbering everything you know to the cops, after all. “You know that shit\'s gonna kill you, right?” Harry said, switching his method of attack. He pointed towards Jason\'s cigarette. “Eat shit and die.” Charming, Stan Callaghan thought as the human chimney stack puffed smoke into his partner\'s chest. He tensed up, considering reaching for his firearm. “Well, I guess somebody woke up on the wrong side of the bed this morning,” said Stone. “Just got more important things t\'do than stan\' \'round here talkin\' to you two dicks is all,” Jason said. He shrugged, making his way passed Stone, passed Callaghan, and towards the door. “Oh yeah? Who are you being paid to beat up this week, then?” Stan suddenly asked. “Your Mother. Easy, shitstains.” With such a warming parting shot Jason departed, abruptly and untimely. Stone brought his hand to his jawline, scratched his old, calloused fingers against his rough, greying goatee. From the moment he\'d laid eyes on Jason he could tell that he was looking for a way to weasel his way out of the conversation without looking too suspicious, and he\'d done just that. Perhaps he wasn\'t expecting them, perhaps he was hiding something... Harry felt a giant paw clasp at his shoulder. “Well, that went well...” Stan Callaghan said with a hint of sarcasm in his deep baritone. “Fuck\'s that guy up to?” “I don\'t know...” he paused. “But I intend to find out. C\'mon, let\'s take a look.” * * * * * Jason didn\'t have the patience to wait for the elevator to come back to his level so he took the stairs. He darted down them with haste, like a worker ant. Scurrying his way through the building he once called home, Jason made his way to the underground parking garage and made for his vehicle. Visiting his ruined apartment had been more fruitful than he ever could\'ve hoped. “Somebody knew what they were doing,” he thought he\'d heard Stone say. A repeat offender. Somebody who\'d committed such foul acts before. The list of potential suspects in his head was long, but eavesdropping on the detectives\' conversation certainly helped him narrow things down. He started the car and drove out of the parking lot. Jason was going to visit an old friend. He was on his way to Cordell Abraham\'s place. * * * * * The gym was rife with the musky funk of too many sweaty bodies crammed into a space far too small to contain such odours. It was the first thing that hit Aidan Chase as he stepped through the doors and it stung his nostrils like a bunch of nettles. It was just as dusty and run-down as it\'s ramshackle interior suggested. The varnished floorboards were well on their way back to matted bleakness and the boxing gear was hardly top of the range. Groups of would-be athletes were ducking and diving their way around the two rings that had been set up in the middle of the room, while instructors and trainers barked orders at the fighters throwing their taped fists into heavy bags near the changing rooms. It was just about the last place on Earth that Aidan thought Loftman would be taking him to. Loftman seemed to know where he was going. He weaved between young boxers and pieces of equipment towards the other side of the room, and Aidan saw no reason not to follow him. Without a knock the blonde-haired superagent pushed open a door with a frosted window and ushered his young accomplice through into the room. The sight that greeted Aidan was a curious one. Standing beside another door were two musclebound heavies, each clad entirely in black and with brown leather gun holsters strapped around their “Abraham\'s Gym” shirts. They eyed him up with curiousity; sized him up good, glancing up and down. Something told Aidan that there was more to this place than just boxing. “You Loftman?” barked one of the heavies, vaguely recalling the description that his boss had given him earlier. Nathaniel nodded, rolling up his shirt sleeves. “Yeah,” he said. His voice quiet. Hushed. “Boss been waiting for you, go right in,” the heavy said as his partner-in-crime twisted his wrist and opened the door for Nathaniel to step through. Aidan responded to Loftman\'s motion to follow him by stepping through the threshold. The heavy closed the door behind him, and Aidan Chase found himself standing inside an office. It was certainly well-decorated with a deep, maroon carpet, cream wallpaper and rich, mahogany features. Aidan could tell that a great deal of time and effort had gone into making the place look nice, even if it was a rather stark contrast to the rest of the building. On the walls were hung numerous photographs of championship-winning boxers and there was a well-stocked trophy cabinet at one end of the room, but it was not these features that were the most notable in the office. No, it was the man sitting at the desk, staring incessantly at he and Loftman. He was bulky - round, almost – with layers of fat building up under his chin and a belly that John Goodman would be proud of. His hair had turned that milky yellow colour so prevalent with those over the age of sixty and his greasy, olive complexion was dotted with ugly liver spots. What he could see of his outfit – a striped white shirt with tight black braces – and his well-trimmed moustache made him look not unlike Don Corleone. “Mister Loftman,” wheezed this bulbous walrus of a man, pointing chubby fingers towards the two free chairs sitting free at his desk. “Be seated, please.” Without hesitation, Aidan followed the lead of Loftman and sat down in the chair under the watchful gaze of two further gun-totting meatheads who bookended the ageing Godfather-wannabe before him. “I don\'t like to be kept waiting, you know,” the mound of a man breathed. He leaned forward in his chair, clasping his hands together. Pausing for a second, allowing Loftman a moment to gather his thoughts, he interjected when the agent parted his lips to speak. “No matter. I\'m sure you have your reasons.” He turned his attentions to Aidan, and Chase immediately cast his gaze downwards. Being stared at by such a well-guarded character made him feel incredibly uneasy. He shifted his weight back and forth in his chair. “This is the boy?” Loftman nodded. “This is Aidan.” He cocked his head to the side. Look at Chase. “Aidan, this is Cordell Abraham.” For the first time, Aidan looked Cordell Abraham dead in the eye and found more lurking behind those sharp, green orbs than he wanted. He inhaled, tried to say “hello,” but nothing came out. There was something about the man; something in this eyes that told him he was not to be trusted. He knew that Cordell Abraham was a dangerous man, and that the armed guards were not just for show. Aidan Chase began to realise the severity of the situation he found himself in. “S\'matter kid? Don\'t you talked?” said Abraham, directly addressing him. “He\'s just a little bit worried is all,” Loftman explained, diverting Abraham\'s attention back towards him. “Worried? What about?” “Well, you know...” Again Cordell turned his focus back to Chase. “You worried about that punk Natas, kid?” Aidan nodded. Swallowed hard. “Yessir.” This drew a chuckle from Cordell Abraham; a man who himself had more than a few dealings with PRIME\'s Anti-Superstar in the past. He reclined in his plush leather chair, looking at Chase through his thin-rimmed glasses. “Well kid, I can assure you that from now on, Jason Natas won\'t be bothering you. See, he and I... let\'s just say we aren\'t strangers. Used to fight for me, see. Kid was a hell of a brawler \'til he saw stars and decided he wanted to be a wrestler like those fools on the TV.” Cordell paused, bowing his head. “Jason Natas did me wrong. We\'ve been poking at him for months.” Cordell Abraham\'s head shot up. He was looking at Loftman. “Unfortunately, with our limited man-power he\'s a tough guy to track down. But if what Mr. Loftman\'s been telling me\'s true, this should be a mutually beneficial relationship.” “I just want this shit gone,” Aidan said, his tone cold. Unforgiving. “I don\'t care what you do to him; I just want that prick out of my life. ASAP.” Cordell Abraham looked at Aidan Chase. He could tell from the look on his face that the kid was dead serious. “We can do that, Mr. Chase. No question. I can promise you that after we\'re done, you won\'t ever have to worry about this parasite ever again. But it\'s not gonna come for free, I must warn you...” Aidan gulped. He knew that one was coming. “... somehow, someway, you\'re gonna have to pay us back,” continued Abraham. “This isn\'t the easiest of game\'s to be in, you know. You want your back scratched you\'re sure as hell gonna have to scratch mine. Understand?” He thought about it. A lot faster than he should\'ve, but he still thought about it. And he came to the conclusion that he wanted Jason Natas to go away, even if it meant being indebted to an apparent gangster for the foreseeable future. “I understand.” * * * * * The Anti-Superstar pulled his vehicle around the corner and began the final straight towards Abraham\'s Gym. He hadn\'t been back to the establishment since he\'d turned his back on the street-fighting game the first time around, but he still knew the route like the back of his hand. He wasn\'t quite sure what he\'d do when he got their and he knew that Abraham\'s guard would be heavy, but torching his apartment was the straw that broke the camel\'s back as far as his strained relationship with his former “employer” was concerned. He\'d thrown the cops off his scent, left them alone in the apartment, hopelessly scouring for evidence. Meanwhile, with the knowledge he\'d accumulated, Jason set about plotting his revenge, away from the watching eye of New York City\'s finest. For weeks he\'d seeked the identity of the arsonist and finally, through his eavesdropping, he\'d unravelled the mystery. Cordell Abraham. A man who\'d been his scourge since the very day he turned his back on his shady underground fighting group. The man had made his presence felt through threats and visits from his numerous braindead goons in the past, but never before had he made such a bold, volatile move. Cordell was going for checkmate, and Jason couldn\'t blame him; he had, after all, shot his own son in the foot little over six months ago, but he\'d be damned if he was going to let that bastard get the better of him. Jason had it all thought out. He flicked on his indicator to turn into the parking lot, but stopped just short when he recognised an eerily-familiar car sitting in front of the building. He squinted, pushing his foot down on the break, when he recognised the number plate. “What the...?” his car was almost at a complete halt now. It was his agent\'s automobile, and if that wasn\'t bad enough, he was getting into it with a muscular kid with a face oh-so familiar. And then he remember the alleyway, their bloody meeting just days before Culture Shock. The kid who was supposed to take his place in PRIME was getting into his car with his agent outside his greatest foe\'s base of operations. Things didn\'t seem so black and white anymore.
