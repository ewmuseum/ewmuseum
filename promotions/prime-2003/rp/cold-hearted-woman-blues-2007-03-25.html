cold-hearted woman blues

25 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25 March 2007 His head felt tight and knotted, more so than it had in recent memory. If the news had not gotten to him about carbon monoxide-poisoning, Jackson thought he might just pass out there in the driver’s seat of a shitty jalopy that was due to the rental place four days ago. Instead, he pushed the car in reverse and back to drive and straightened his position inside the parking space. He got out and walked heavy-legged into his apartment building, thinking comic book stink-lines of cigarette smoke and alcohol followed close at heel. He slid the key into the lock and jiggled the knob about. It opened without a turn but he took no notice. He put the keys on the small table beside the door and flicked on the light switch. The room filled with a dim yellow the color of tooth decay and nausea eddied around his gut. On the far side of the room sitting on his dinner table stood a new bottle of bourbon. In the lights it looked like days-old piss and that smell which was not there raided his nostrils. Jackson jackknifed in half and retched and dry-heaved until his throat was raw. When he stopped and walked to the bottle and steadied himself with a hand firm upon the table, he saw the note taped to it. He sat and pulled it off. In a hand maybe too steady for whom he knew the note was from, it read call me and beneath that was a phone number to a motel he thought had been bulldozed years ago. Under that was reimburse, please. # That cost forty-five dollars, she said. Oh, and fore you ask, no delaysies. You still owed me ten bucks til I found your stash. You’re a sumbitch, Carys, Jackson said. Now I know you aint got respect for the past or memories or nothing of the sort, but what you found there was just a scrapbook. Heh. Either you got brain damage from too much booze or I heard you slurrrr. He palmed the side of his face and scratched his fingers along his stubble down to around his chin. I’m tired. Long day. Sure. Had a couple of those myself, she said. Last one was a real doozie and I’ve been coming round since. How’s that bottle lookin? Her eyebrow arched as she leaned forward against the table. You want the honest-to-God truth? he said. It looks like a big ol bottle a shit and just thinkin on it is givin me the runs. Really? she said. If I saw a line right now, I’d be climbing my way into heaven. Fantastic. She tried to proffer a chuckle but just sighed a long breath. Her eyes darted about the room without focus. Her bravado, gone. It burned through her confidence like a rocket does fuel. An air of paranoia and a concrete slab of self-loathing hung upon the creases in her once-beautiful face. He said, Why’d you come round here now? Mizzou aint exactly your place. She rubbed her nose. Aint nowhere my place. Well then, Lil Orphan Annie. You got to know then comin down here don’t give you the privilege to take a jab at how I talk. I don’t go on over to England and say mate or pub. And I got good teeth. He bit the fingernail of his index finger. But I’m sorry to hear it regardless. Still would appreciate an answer, though. After a moment, she said, Good teeth. Yah. Newsflash, newsflash! She coughed and stood up for emphasis. Throw away your toothpaste and mouthwash today. All you need is a gallon of spirits a day to keep that plaque away! She walked a tight circle. Dude, you couldn’t be more of a bucktoothed ninja if I smacked you in the mouth with a hockey stick. Hyuck hyuck. He laughed but cut it short when his throat caught fire again. Yeah yeah yeah, hon. Lemme tell you. Day I moved up here I thought, Goddamn. These people here fill up and overflow the ugly pool three times over and they still enough left over for a party. But really? They the same as in New Orleans or I guess in London . . . maybe a little better than in London. He rapped his knuckles on the table near the bottle. But how bout we stop this now. Maybe I’m tired but I aint too slow. How come you leapfroggin away from the point here? She still hadn’t sat down, but she needed to curb her nervous energy. She sat atop her shins on the chair and tried to get some sort of focus on the situation at hand. Fuck it. Here’s the deal, my slight-of-mouth friend. You remember when we first met? When we finally managed to speak instead of mock, I mean. How hard would it be to capture that again? How much would it cost? Your maidenhead. How about the ten dollars I stole earlier? Sure. They worth about the same, anyway. Cute, she said. Now, if your alcoholically retarded brain will allow . . . Cast your mind back to that first day we spoke. When we spoke about everything that had happened in the past--the stuff we could talk about, at least. I told you that I didn’t have a clue who you were. I think the exact terminology I used could have been an elegant Aren’t you that fucktard who cost the footy game? Yeah, he said. I remember I thought you were this big bitch. She laughed. Yeah, I thought I heard you say that. Hard to tell between the racking coughs and I wasn’t too sure. But back to the facts: I knew who you were. Hell, wrestlers like you sorta brought me into the whole thing. Well. Well? Now I’m touched, really, he said. But what the hell does this have to do with--oh, I dunno--anything? Wrestling! she said, sweeping her arms out in a majestic grace. Right, he said. You hopped back up into the drug saddle, cowgirl? Not since I went to rehab, cumguzzler. I’m serious! And I’m happy for you. I am. But if you got even the smallest idea that I can or want to do this again, then I’m goin to say you just a lil crazy. She wanted to pat his hand, but restrained. Jackson. If you can learn to talk again you can learn to wrestle. You just need someone to beat the need back into you. He shook his head and stood up from the table, the chair scraping across the linoleum floor. Girl. I never had no problem talkin and I damn sure know how to wrestle. But aint no way I want to. Beat me as hard as you want--don’t mean I want to do it. Could you quit with the gibberish? she asked. Lemme translate so you’ll understand, Señor Inbred. Hyuck! Whelp, probation it don’t want hear bout yall lil after-schoolin hobbies dere and if you want to keep sayin no to helping me then I guess you’d be real-time ragtime fucked, huh? Type of fucked those blues guys wish they could sing about. Jackson quit pacing. He stood tall above the table and looked down at it and at the bottle of bourbon. The bottle seemed to him to be staring back with nothing short of accusation and then he felt as if he were looking up at it. He stepped back from it. With a measured tone, he said, You cunt. You godforsaken bleedin cunt. She muffled a giggle with her hand. Now, now. Sticks and stones may break my bones--but whips and chains excite me. She sat up straight. What you’re failing to realize is that you’ll get money for this. Ruin your body on TV in front of the world, not with the drinky slumped down in some shitstain bar. Plus, you’ll have merchandise. Isn’t that fun? Foam hands holding whiskey bottles! I-- He sat down hard. Listen. I--Christ. Carys, I got a life. I can’t be doin this shit. You’ll see sense, she said. She hit her hand down on the table. It rattled. You’ll have to. You’ll be there. Cox Arena. San Diego State University. California. In front of twelve thousand fans. You will not let them down. You moan about you having a life? Now I’m giving you reason to live it. Your ticket is already in your mailbox. Ciao. # She slammed the phone back into its cradle. She got up from the table and the phone and she sighed. She tested the bed, found it rigid, rolled over to face the wall, and pulled the sheets up over her head. # He said, Fuck, and looked around at his shitty one-room apartment. The drapes hung askew from the window and the wildflower design matched nothing else in the room. The brick walls had been chipped at. The floor, too. He stood up and grabbed the bottle by its neck. He whipped it into the wall and it exploded against it. Bourbon and glass ricocheted back and in the sickly light it looked almost cinematic. He screamed--fuck fuck fuck fuck fuck--and punched and groped at the table like a lunatic rapist until his hands and head throbbed. Fuck.
