Date: Unknown “'ey, Davey, look o'er there!” “What?” “Lookit that feller sittin' at the end've the bar, know who that is?” “...looks like any other ol' prick to me.” “Oooh no, no no no, that ain't no prick Davey. That man, that man's Wade god-damn Elliott.” “What? No it ain't.” “I'm tellin' ya right now, that's Wade fuckin' Elliott.” “...shit, maybe yer right...” “I know I'm right, ya stupid sumbitch!” “What the fucks he doin' here?” “Dunno, but I'ma find out.” “What? No, leave 'im alone. He's a surly fucker.” “Naw, he's a wrestler! That's all just TV mumbo jumbo, he's prob'ly just a regular ol' joe like you n' me.” “I dunno, Kev...” Kevin took a last swig of his beer before pushing himself to his feet. He adjusted his oil-stained truckers cap as his shorter, cleaner bar companion, David, stood from his stool. “Let's jus' go over an' say 'hi,'” said Kevin, smoothing out his blonde goatee. “Maybe he'll sign my hat!” “If ya say so, Kev...” The two took easy steps through the dive. The character on the far end sat relatively still, uninterested in the upbeat tunes of The Lost Trailers. It was a busy night for Harry's Bar & Grill, but even on a busier night, the appearance of someone well-known was a rare spectacle. The two approached. The man's face was hidden under a dirty brown drifter's hat. His rough paws wrapped around a tumbler of whiskey. He could have been asleep, only the heaving of his barrel-chest proved any signs of life. David and Kevin waited a moment, then Kevin removed his hat and took a step forward. “Excuse me...” he started. “Mr. Elliott?” No response. “Wade? Wade Elliott?” His chest heaved. “The Bad Dog” turned his head to the right, his steely blue eyes half closed, his goatee-surrounded lips curled inward. He adjusted himself in his stool as he addressed the two men. “What.” “See? What'd I tell ya?!” said an excitable Kevin. ”Kev,” said David, hitting him in the ribs with an elbow. “Oh, right. Mr. Elliott, we cain't tell ya how good it is to see a feller such as yerself havin' a few in our very own bar!” Wade didn't blink, didn't move a muscle, didn't take a breath. “We're big fans've yers Wade,” he continued. “We was hopin' we might buy ya the next round! Or maybe git an autograph!” The Blue Collar Brawler breathed heavily through his nostrils, his lips curling in further into a nasty grimace before parting to reply. “Leave me the fuck alone.” Elliott turned back to his drink, assuming his original position. David and Kevin stood a bit struck, Kevin's mouth hanging agape. “See, I told ya,” said David. “Let's git back to our drinks.” “Yeah, s'pose yer right, David,” said Kevin, his voice obtaining a harsh tone. He turned his attention back to Wade. “Guess I shoulda known that a big fuckin' star like Wade Elliott ain't got no concern fer us fuckin' low lifes, huh David?” “Kev...” The Southern Sparkplug's eyes furled, his teeth locking together. He stood up, tipped back the rest of his whiskey, and tossed a $50 bill on the counter. He turned his rugged frame to the pair, stepping in close. “Go fuck yerself,” he spat. With that, The Bad Dog took heavy steps forward, hitting Kevin with an intentional shoulder as he walked by. The two watched, dumbstruck, as the 'Bama Bruiser left the bar, the roar of his black Silverado sounding in the night a few moments later. “Well ain't that fuckin' somethin'?” said an angry Kevin, wrenching his hat back onto his head. “C'mon, Kev,” said David, patting him on the back. Let's git a drink.” * * * * * * * Wade found no joy or enlightenment in the sunset. His body stood frozen cold, leaning against his truck, parked on the dirt of a scenic view stopping area. His eyes stared off into nothing as the oranges, reds and pinks swirled in the sky. Angus sat beside him, whining and turning his head up to his troubled master. Wade took a deep breath in, and quickly exhaled through his nose. “What the fuck'm I doin'?” Angus turned his head back to the view. Wade shook his head slightly, the realization of the life he was leading and the happiness it didn't bring sinking in. “Fuckin' hell with it,” he grumbled. “I'm goin' home.” So he climbed into his truck, let Angus into the passenger's seat, and drove away, leaving the colors in the air behind him.
