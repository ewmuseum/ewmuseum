Writer's Block (Match - ReV 76)

21 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/21/05 LEGEND:White = Current HappeningsGreen = Roleplay 'You say that love goes anywhere.In your darkest time, It's just enough to know it's there.'- Jimmy Eat World. I - Give The Man Some Credit... There's a certain perception of wrestling superstars, one that is encouraged shamelessly by a small handful. As professional sportsmen and woman, appearing on national television week in and week out, enticing hundreds of thousands to shell out for pay-per-views, and adored by thousands of fans packed into over-priced arenas, they talk up the lifestyle they live away from ringside. No, that’s crap. Popular culture paints the picture that addiction can be overcome. It happens all the time on movies and TV shows; a character gets hooked on booze or drugs, decides to kick, goes through rehab and seemingly is fine for the remainder of his days in the collective consciousness of mass media. They make it seem like people get over alcoholism like the flu; as if a rehab stint can cure a cocaine habit like a flu shot. This sucks too! The delete button was quickly becoming my friend. Maybe it’s time for a break, I’d been staring, eyes glazed over, at the same screen for the last hour and a half. Nothing. I couldn’t think of ONE solid RP idea. It was sick, really. “I have to get this damned thing written!” I declared as I pounded my right fist on the desk. I rubbed my eyes with the thumb and middle finger of my left hand as I stood up. I paced about the small office-like room scattered with cheap, pine furniture and buried in books. Books like ‘Contemporary Ethics,’ ‘Introduction To Psychology,’ and ‘The History Of Western Civilization.’ I’m sure it’s painfully obvious that I was a student. A student stuck between second and third year hell. Didn’t do so well in the previous semester. “Maybe I should have some breakfast…” I trailed off in my thought but soon found myself in my kitchen anyway. Dishes littered the counter and sink, but I didn’t really care. I just wanted to get something in my stomach. I pulled open my dish cupboard and… only three plates were left. “Dammit.” I knew this meant I would have to clean something. Was it worth it? I saw a bowl lying at the far end of the sink and reached over to grab it. Three or four flies scattered as soon as their perch was disturbed. Looking at the inside of the bowl I noticed a reddish smear… cherry? Strawberry? Something sweet, the flies seemed to enjoy it. I cranked the warm water on and placed the bowl under the flow as I rubbed it out with my free hand. “Looks clean to me,” said a voice from the back of the room, “bachelor clean!” “Single forever at the rate I’m going.” We both laughed… perhaps it truly is better to laugh than to cry? “How’s your day, Robb?” “Finest kind. Making breakfast, are you?” “How could you tell?” “You’re doing dishes. That’s a rarity at best. That only happens when you want to eat and can’t afford to order out.” Robb said as he sunk down into the couch. He snapped the remote up off the coffee table and flicked on the television. “It’s nine o’clock, man? Isn’t it a little early for that?” I quizzed as I rolled my eyes. I placed my bowl on the counter and popped open the fridge to retrieve the milk. I grabbed the carton and pulled – too light! “What? The way you go on you’d think that TV was a beer or something.” “Yeah well… we’re outta milk. You got any cash on you?” Robb stood up to check his pockets, but, alas, nothing. He simply looked at me and shook his head, then, in one motion, sat down and resumed The Channel 9 Morning News. I closed the fridge, “Dammit.” “What’s up?” Robb inquired over Colleen Christie. “I need some food. I’ve got mad writer’s block, I can’t think of a damned thing to role play about and it’s due tonight. Where’s the milk?” Robb let out a small chuckle, “That was scattered. We’re outta milk. Oh, have you seen this?” He turned up the TV and I turned my attention to it. Colleen, all dressed up and looking as hot as ever, was speaking into the camera, “… and in local news; Yesterday afternoon yet another convenience store was knocked over and all it’s patrons robbed. Police have no suspects at the present time. Anyone with information should call 1-800-555-TIPS.” I shook my head. “Yeah, that’s terrible, but what does it have to do with milk or my role play?” “That could BE your roleplay!” “What are you on about? That’s stupid! A role play about a corner store robbery? How would that even work? Blood isn’t a thief,” I paused for a minute… then a mad fit of inspiration came upon me! “What about a song lyric!? You know, as a spring board from which to work?” “Like what?” Step up to me... You wanna be a big time player...It's not to be... Look at all the snide ass - actin’ like a wise ass... I'll fuck up your face and you'll never look back... So when you fall to the ground... And finally get back to reality... And no one at all is around...So tell me how does it feel to be the enemy...- Sevendust Robb shrugged, “Sorry bro, but that’s crap, man.” “Yeah, it is.Look, I only have debit, and I need milke, so I gotta go up to the 7-11. Wanna come along for the ride?” “Why not. Just let me get a shirt on and we’re off.” With that Robb stood up and got dressed, as he did I gathered a notepad and a pen, just in case inspiration struck along the ride… and we were on our way. Within 12 minutes we were at the 7-11 and I headed straight for the back to get my milk and Robb was fixing a Slurpee. I walked up behind him and his Orange-Mango Madness… “I thought you didn’t have any cash, Robb.” “I don’t… but you have debit.” “Why would you assume that I would spend money on you when--” “EVERYBODY HIT THE FLOOR!” hollered a voice from the main entrance. Two men dressed all in black and wearing balaclavas brandished guns and informed us of what we were to do, step-by-step. There was probably about 15 or 16 of us in the store at the time, including the two clerks. Robb and I were tucked up against the counter by the Slurpee machine. We began to whisper a plan… or at least that’s where I thought Robb was leaning… not so much. “D’you think these are the guys from the news?” Robb inquired with almost an excitement. “How can you ask that with a smile on your face!?” “D’you think we might get on TV?” He almost squealed the question. “You’re worried about 15 minutes of fame!? These guys have guns!” “You’re just being irrational. They haven’t killed anyone yet.” I could see this was going to be a circular argument at best, “Look, let’s just lay low, maybe we can get out of this with our lives and our money.” “And your milk.” “Yeah… and my milk.” “You know, Conrad, if they take too long that milk might spoil.” “Hey Robb?” “Yeah?” “Shut up.” “That was unnecessary.” Robb retorted sharply. Then, suddenly, the jig was up. “YOU TWO! BY THE SLUSHIE MACHINE!” “It’s a Slurpee,” replied my roommate. The importance of that piece of information at that moment in time eludes me to this day. In any case we stood to our feet. “Put the milk down!” demanded one of the two men. I obliged. “Empty your pockets onto the counter!” the man screamed at us. We did and then he asked for my wallet. “Can’t we talk about this--” I began, but when his prompt reply came informing me that my life meant very little to him I decided it best no to barter. Besides, I had nothing better to offer. So they took my wallet. And everyone else’s. And the money from the register. And the cigarettes. …and a phone card? WTF? Then they disappeared out the door and hoped into a blue sedan which quickly sped them off. I decided, though, that I needed my wallet back. I had to write an RP, but I needed food, for which I had to have milk, to purchase which I needed money, which was in my wallet. And so the story goes. I tugged at Robb’s shirt. “C’mon!” “What? Where?” I plopped the 4-Litre jug on the counter and leapt out the door. Robb stopped at the counter and put his Slurpee beside my milk, then turned to the clerk, “We’ll be back.” “ROBB!!” I called out, hastening his departure. He ran out the door after me and we slid into my bad-ass 1990 Chevy Cavalier and began the persuit. It took us up King George highway. “This is so exciting! A real-life car chase!” Robb was a fan of the dramatic, it would seem. I couldn’t be arsed… I just wanted some milk. It was about then that I noticed they were slowing down and resuming a normal driving speed. Trying to blend in perhaps? They obviously didn’t notice we were following them. I decided it would be best to keep it that way. As we followed them, though, we began to notice the terriroty was looking more and more familiar. “Conrad… we’re almost at our house.” “I noticed.” Then the car did the unimaginable… it turned into our driveway! “Haven’t they punished us enough!?” I declared. Robb had a different view on it, “Maybe now they want your cereal. You oughta just go with toast.” “No!” “Good ole toast!” “NO! Now shut up and let’s see what these guys do her--” Robb and I sat stunned for a moment or five. It was Tino and ‘Hacking Guy’ from the upstairs suite. “Now we know why they stole the phone card.” Robb announced matter-of-factly. “Huh?” “Those calls to Fiji aren’t cheap!” He concluded. I figured he was right. A lucid thought was a rarity with Robb. I decided to go with it. “Hey, Robb, you got that paper I brought out here someplace?” “Yeah,” he answered, handing it to me. I started writing. Inspiration was rare, it’s true, I had to capitalize when it presented itself… even if it be in the midst of a robbery investigation. It was a ridiculous idea, but one which I felt was just crazy enough to garner the interest of my fed-head, who, due to the nature of my day, was likely not going to even have an opportunity to read this until much too late. It seemed like quite a bit of writing, but it only took me less than a minute to jot it down in my college short-hand. Now, back to Tom Clancy-like espionage! Robb and I waited several minutes before pulling up to the curb in front of our house as usual. I put the car in first, pulled the e-brake and shut down the engine. Then Robb and I hopped out just in time to see Tino and Hacking Guy stroll out of their front door to sit on the front stoop. “Hey guys!” they called out to us, “How’s it goin’?” I decided to play along, feeling we had better odds that way, “Not so great. We just got mugged up the street at the 7-11.” “Oh no, that’s terrible! I hope the cops catch those guys. I’ve seen them on the news. Was it the same guys?” “I can only guess so.” I replied. “They get their 15 minutes!” Robb whined, “I want mine!” “Shut up!” I nudged. “Can we PLEASE do a news interview after?” “Fine.” “Well,” I started, to Tino and Hacking Guy, “I’ll catch you two later. I gotta go in and phone my bank and the Social Insurance Card people and… well, you know how it is with the Government.” “Yeah,” Tino replied, “It’s tough.” So Robb and I went down into our basement apartment, secure in our belief that our thieves knew not that we knew what we’d known about them. When we got in the door all Robb could talk about is what a rush that was. I ran to my computer to start typing. And I wrote! Before I even realized it I’d had 2,471 words by the end of this sentence! Quite a few, long hours passed and eventually we decided to head out under the cover of darkness. Besides, we knew that Tino and Hacking Guy liked to watch CSI: Miami on Monday nights. We came to the conclusion that from 10-11 we’d have run of the house if we stayed quiet. So we did. We snuck outside and popped the insect screen off the window in the spare room and slid in as softly as we knew how. Once inside we examined the spare room and found nothing. So we went to Hacking Guy’s room. (Which was directly over top of my room, might I add. And he didn’t get the name Hacking Guy by being super quiet in the middle of the night and sleeping silently, thank-you-very-much!) Again we found nothing. Which left only the living room/kitchen (unlikely) or Tino’s room! “To Tino’s room!” I declared. We tuck-and-rolled behind the couch and sat tight for a second to make sure we haven’t been caught-out. … And we hadn’t. And then we played the waiting game - Holding out until an explosion or a loud scene to make a break across the rest of the living room and into the oddly placed master bedroom. CRASH BANG! This was our cue! Success! I began my search in the dresser drawers and Robb started his in the closet. After about 10 seconds Robb blurted out “Jackpot!” It wasn’t long before our neighbours were in the room with us. Hacking Guy charged at me, but I ducked and he went flying into the wall. One down, one to go. Tino then charged at Robb and kicked him hard in the arse. I laughed. Then he turned and punched me in the mouth. I stopped laughing. I reached out and grabbed a lamp and smashed him over the head with it and knocked him unconscious! “That’s not true!” “It’s my story!” “I thought it was meant to chronicle the day’s events!” “That’s how I remember it, Robb.” “Whatever.” I dug through the closet and found my wallet and went back to 7-11 and bought my milk. Hell, I was on top of the world so I bought Robb a new Slurpee. And we phoned the police. Tino and Hacking Guy, who we later found out is actually named ‘Pablo’, were arrested and whatever was found that could be identified as belonging to any one person was returned. And me? I got an RP out of the deal. When I finished that RP and hit the ‘Submit’ button I let out a deep sigh of relief and drank the milk left at the bottom of the bowl. “That’s how you’re ending it?” Robb asked. “Yeah. That’s how it ended!” “Oh, now you’re suddenly worried accuracy?” “Attention to detail, Robb,” I started back smartly, “is what elevates my writing above the common man!” “You gotta go back and put an explosion in someplace!” “No,” I said, “I don’t ‘gotta’ do any such thing!” “I think you do!” Then Robb spontaneously combusted, near gasoline when he went outside to have a smoke. “That’s cold, Conrad. Real cold.”
