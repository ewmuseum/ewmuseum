Interrogation

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Craig killed Mr. McMahon June 22nd, 2008 “Gentlemen, I know that you are very busy people, as am I, so I’m going to come right out and save us all a lot of time. I killed Mike Grimwood.” Never in the wildest dreams of Larry or Steven Connors did they anticipate the sight before them. Resting comfortably across the interrogation table is Devin Shakur, Marlboro cigarette dangling from mouth, legs interlaced over the middle of the table. Dressed in the world renowned black Armani attire, Shakur spins a Bic pen in between his fingers while staring blankly at the detectives. His eyes are void of remorse. His face lacks any distinctive read one could possibly accumulate, and he just confessed to the hottest murder in North Carolina. “So,” Larry Connors is taken aback by the sudden forthcoming of Shakur’s words. Not a single tactic was used to induce the confession. “You are saying that it was you who killed Mr. Grimwood, am I hearing that correctly?” The million dollar smirk creeps onto Shakur’s face, his body incapable of hiding it any longer. “Yep, sure, did. Walked right into that fucker’s office and beat him until he wasn’t breathing anymore. Greatest moment of my life and I’d do it a million times more before I let him live another day.” “What, uh,” Steven chimes in, unable to contain his excitement any longer. This would be a defining moment in both he and his brother’s police career. Now he needs to keep Shakur talking, praying to God this wasn’t some kind of ruse. “You killed Mr. Grimwood because of what he did to you in another lifetime, as your father, Anthony Shakur, correct? Shakur knew they were playing to his ego. He knew that once Christian found out he went behind his back to commit sheer treason to the family, protection would be in order. But this was something he needed to get out. Devin was tired of being a third rate pawn in the game of life, his life. “I killed that prick because he had no idea how to be a parent. I killed him because every night until the age of 15 he treated me like I was a chore. I killed him because I’m tired of holding a connection to the Smith family. It’s their fault to begin with that I’m in this shit.” Larry looked over his shoulder, ensuring nobody from outside the window was listening in. “You know Devin, since we haven’t had the time to start recording, I tell you what... We go back and do this professional like...If you can give us the whereabouts of Christian, Chris, Michael, and all the others in the Smith family...We might have a nice spot in that Witness Protection Program for you.” Ah, Southern hospitality. Devin swings his feet off the table, straightening himself up into a more professional stance. Not taking a moment to think, he extends his hands to Larry Connors. “That can definitely be arranged.” Larry returns the handshake. “Larry,” Steven says, leaning up against the interrogation room door. “You won’t regret this.” Larry continues “We’ll make sure that you are completely safe. Just make sure to tell us where the Smith’s are-“ Steven attempts to interrupt again, this time with more vigor in his voice, “Larry!” Without hesitation, Devin responds, “Well hell, I can tell you where they are right now. Little place near the North Pacific Ocean in Taiw-“ “LARRY!” Steven shouts, ensnaring Larry from the nirvana state he was just consumed in. The thump of flesh against skull shoots Larry Connors completely back to life. He’s barely able to catch the cup of coffee clutched in his right hand before it burns his new tan dress shirt. Steven looks down at his brother with disgust and disbelief, “Stop lazying up the work place. Shakur just walked through the door and he’s headed our way, get ready.” Well, perhaps not their wildest dreams. --------------- Despite the numerous hours of practice and reading up on interrogation Do’s and Don’t, Devin knew this wouldn’t be easy. No matter how many times he listened to Christian’s voicemail advice, there was no substitute for more time. Having waited almost a month, citing work scheduling conflicts over and over again, Devin couldn’t duck the police any longer. He made a mental note on the way over to add C.P Cantrell to his ‘Murder’ list for forcing a home state Colossus promotional appearance. Perhaps this process would be a little less strenuous if he hadn’t committed the crime, but one can only take so much from the vine before being called selfish. With an officer flanking each side, Devin makes his way toward Interrogation Room B. Each step feels choreographed, straight out of a play, and Devin’s eyes trail every few seconds to make sure his feet keep pace with his fellow walkers. A lot has changed since the last time Devin was escorted down these halls in 1996. At the age of 18, Devin was rebellious, naïve, and glaring at everybody who dared look at him the wrong way. Nobody in the office cared about his well being because he was just a small fish and they were after the giant shark that was the Death biker gang. Now with every cubicle he passes, Devin instigates more chatter. Not everybody within the department is a wrestling fan, but he has dual notoriety, a genius of the business world along with aforementioned wrestling career, so it’s seldom he goes unnoticed anywhere in his hometown. Turning the corner, Devin sees the detectives leaning up against the wall next to Interrogation Room B. Making his best attempt at keeping a straight face, he can’t believe they were able to slither through the emotional attachment aspect and be the ones to do the actual interrogation. The officers slip down into a nearby room while Larry and Steven close the gap and each extend their hands to Devin, keeping their professionalism at least for the time being. “Mr. Shakur, I’m Larry Connors!” Devin puts his hand forth and Larry shakes it firmly, “This is my partner Steven Connors!” Steven grasps Devin’s hand and leads him inside the interrogation room. Scanning quickly, Devin notices Larry is dressed to match the color of the room, a light tan. Everything from the carpet to the wallpaper to the ceiling matches Larry’s attire. In the upper right corner is the actual table, long enough to seat an eight person party. The two video cameras are stationed where the walls meet right behind the door, and west of the interrogation table. Larry gives a hand gesture to Devin, almost forcing him to take the seat that backs him up against the wall. Steven isolates Devin by pulling a chair on the short and narrow side. With all parties seated, Larry folds his hands across the table and gives a courteous smile to Devin. “You are a very hard man to track down, Mr. Shakur.” “Everybody else I know says the same thing.” Devin replies back with just the right amount of arrogance and timing. Not too quick. Not too slow. Larry and Steven both gave each other a glance. They are relatively green in interrogation and had requested assistance of their own from a superior. Their supervisor said they should exude confidence and control, keep the approach direct, and most importantly allow Devin to talk. Larry starts the questioning off, trying to keep a casual tone. “So, Mr. Shakur we’ve got some questions for you. Do you have any connections to Mike Grimwood, CEO of The Braintrust?” Devin would have to lie from here on out, luckily this part was well rehearsed. He needs to respond solemnly, putting enough conviction into his voice to eliminate any suspicion. “I had a quasi-business relationship with him when I was CEO of Apex Technological Institute. Not exactly in the same field, but we did lunch once in a blue moon and saw each other occasionally at charity events. We weren’t exactly best friends but I knew him.” “Can you be more specific about once in a blue moon. Was that quarterly, once every two months, when?” Steven chimes in. Devin turns his head and stares Steven down while formulating up an answer that fits his previous one. “I would say...Maybe once every three and a half months. He wasn’t exactly a public figure, like a Howard Hughes if you get my drift.” “What was your professional and personal opinion of Mr. Grimwood?” Larry asks while keeping his hands folded across the table, a position of power. “Never had a reason to hate him because I so seldom saw him. Always was nice when around me, picked up the check every time we went out, and donated a good amount of his money to the community, can’t make an enemy out of that.” Devin says, chuckling lightly afterward. He wanted to keep the questioning on a consistent level and try to ease into the personal route that was sure to come down the pipe soon. Christian told him to expect questions ranging from “How are you?” to “So, Mike Grimwood, A.K.A Anthony Shakur, your father, how bad’ya wanna gut him like a fish?” The detectives force themselves to share in the laughter, a ploy to try and relax Devin. Both were still trying to get the feel for sitting across the room with a cold blooded murderer. “So you were an acquaintance of Mr. Grimwood’s I guess we can say for the record.” Steven asks. Devin nods in reply, “I think that would be fair to say.” Larry wanted to vomit at the lack of candor. This was the moment they had salivated over, dreamt about, and they were starting to let it slip away. This was their one connection to Christian Daniels and the Smith family. If Devin ended up caught and in custody, no doubt the Smith family would come roaring in, guns blazing to try and free their childhood acquaintance. Their father and uncles needed closure, even from beyond the grave, and Devin was the key to getting it. “So, Mr. Shakur, can you describe to us your day on May 24th. Be as detailed as your memory can allow.” Larry shoves forward. Steven wishes he would have sat closer to his twin brother so he could kick him underneath the table. Devin notices the frankness and gives an internal fist pump. He didn’t make the first mistake. “Well, I woke up to a 10 AM phone call from my boss informing me that if I wasn’t on the next flight to Chicago, my ass would be handed to me on a silver platter. Sorry for the profanity, but these are details.” “Of course” Larry responds, keeping a watch on Devin while writing down notes on a small pad. “I was planning to come in on the day of the show and avoid publicity. To the people who watch our wrestling shows, I’m not exactly a well liked and respected person so I figured nobody would want to speak with me. Anywho, I begrudgingly packed my bags and started out the door when I remembered that Mr. Grimwood wished to meet with me-“ Larry interrupts, failing to notice his brother’s evil glare. “When and how did he say he wanted to meet with you?” “Ran into one of his secretaries a couple days prior, said he wanted to meet with me. Shannon...I can’t remember her last name. So before heading out on my flight, I made a quick stop over to Mr. Grimwood’s office-“ “About what time was that you would say?” Larry interrupts again. Devin takes a moment to “ponder the specific time”, realizing that Larry is slowly transitioning into a rapid fire approach, not giving him time to completely finish his thought process before pressing forward. “I would clock that around 12, 12:30. Meeting didn’t take long, maybe fifteen minutes. I was out there and at the airport by 1:15, made the 2:00 flight, was in Chicago for the rest of the day. First time I’ve been back in the state since.” Steven jots down a note on his notepad while Larry leans in a little closer, getting more personal. While he might have made the first mistake, Larry just got Devin to admit that he was inside the building on the day of the murder and within the time range. “What did you two discuss in the meeting? Try and give me the minutes like they would at the beginning of a club session.” Devin now felt the need to switch to a rapid fire approach of his own, toning down the details and answering the questions specifically. He began to get in the groove of the lies. “He was working on some new project that involved the Wake County school budget and wanted my analysis on the technological aspects of it.” “What does that entail?” Larry shoots back. “Mr. Grimwood wasn’t exactly a computer guru by any means. My institution specialized in computer technology specifically.” Steven needed to ease his brother off. Devin’s switching indicated to him that soon Larry would get too personal, and Devin would say the one sentence no interrogator ever wants to hear, “You need to speak with my lawyer.” Sliding his chair over, Steven gives Devin some much needed space to breathe. Devin stretches his legs out, a sign of relaxation and exactly what Steven wants. “In the time that you knew Mr. Grimwood, albeit limited, did he ever express any knowledge of people who wanted to harm him?” “I think you are going a bit too Hollywood there, Mr. Connors. Our relationship was strictly business and nothing else. He never enclosed anything of that sort to me, and I’m not exactly the kind of person who would sit idle on it should he decide to do so. Like I said, lunch, charity events, and the rare meeting were all that we saw of each other.” Steven gives a nod and took down some more notes. Larry glances over at his brother and takes the initiative to continue his line of questioning. “You are aware that Mr. Grimwood was savagely murdered on May 24th, the day that you saw him, correct?” Steven couldn’t slap his brother in front of their interviewee, but he desperately wanted to take a five second break and do so. Devin keeps his calm, having a decent grasp of who is who now. “I’m aware of that, definitely not the way I want to go out.” “Are you also aware of the fact that his time of death was relatively around the time you were there?” Larry continues forward. Devin tilts his head to the side and gives a frown. Steven wishes he could do the same. “Mr. Connors, I’ve seen enough police shows in my day to know that people who are supposed to strictly provide information aren’t given this line of questioning. They are seen as suspects in the eyes of the people questioning them. Is that the case here?” Larry goes to speak but his brother cuts him off, “Nobody is insinu-“ “Because with the constant interruption that is how it appears.” Trying to get back into control, Larry speaks over his brother, “Mr. Shakur-“ Devin keeps his foot on the gas, “And if that’s the case, there need to be a few more suits and one less of me in this conversation.” Steven and Larry have reached a crossroad. If Larry continues with his line of persistent and antagonizing questioning, Devin will be out of the room within sixty seconds. If they decide to backpedal, both sides now know that Devin is considered a suspect while in the interrogation room. They have to make a decision and fast, otherwise this case won’t get solved for years. Steven clears his throat, “Can you excuse us for a minute?” “Alright” Both detectives slide their chairs back and step out of the room. Knowing full well that the cameras are still on him and the high probability someone has been assigned to watch this, Devin doesn’t become tense. He holds the position that he is in, doing his best to reduce any twitching that would be visible to the cameras. Meanwhile, Larry and Steven go down the hall and into a spare interrogation room not being utilized. Steven is furious to say the least. “WHAT THE HELL?” “What?” “Are you completely oblivious to what’s going on in there?” “I would say that we’re making good progress.” “Good pro-“ Steven stops himself and slaps his brother in the back of the head Three Stooges style, “GOOD PROGRESS? In five fucking minutes you’ve managed to take him from PERSON WITH INFORMATION to thinking he’s the PRIME SUSPECT! That is NOT progress!” Larry scoffs at his brother and now turns up the intensity on his thermostat. “You ever think as soon as he found out we were looking for him that it was ever a matter of person with information? Come on, he might look it, but he’s not stupid, Steven!” Steven holds both hands up to his brother’s face, “Get your damn head out of the television for a second, Larry. This is not CSI and we’re not solving this crime in an hour with commercials. THIS. IS. REAL!” Larry retorts, “So what were we supposed to do, Einstein? Get his trust and then call him back until we got him to confess? You are the one who needs to GET REAL! We’re on the verge of losing this because it took him 20 some odd days to get his ass down here. We’ve gotta strike while the iron is hot!” Steven sighs out loud and follows that up with a furious grunt, “And do you know what logic you have to back up your ludicrous line of questioning? Nothing! We’ve got two eyewitnesses who saw him go down that hall. For all we know they could have had a meeting. What, are we supposed to go in there and say...Yeah, Devin, we know you have an association with the Smith family who killed our dad and uncles, and we know you killed Grimwood. Confess now!” Pointing a finger at his brother, Larry shoots back, “Don’t go there” “I’m being reasonable here, Larry. This is our chance! Do you want this to go to the lawyers? We can’t hold him and he’s already close to that. Yeah, we know what our guts are telling us, but we can’t present that as fact. Records indicate he HASN’T talked to the Smith’s since they fled the country.” Rolling his eyes, Larry responds, “You and I both know that’s a load of bullshit.” “It might be but it’s our word against the phone company, and unless we have some solid evidence, nobody is going to believe us. Don’t you get it, Larry? We’ve got to leave him wanting to come back, otherwise he’s going to walk out that door and we will have to deal with his lawyers.” “Well we’ve already got him thinking he’s a suspect, might as well do something to advance that. What else can we do at this point?” “Larry, I...There’s a wall straight ahead and you are about to run dead into it. You know what it’s called? COLD CASE!” A long and tense pause happens between the two brothers. Steven rubs the sweat off his brow onto his pant legs. Larry doesn’t return fire with a belligerent statement. Instead he raises a finger into the air. After an additional moment, his mouth curves into a smile. “No, no, I’ve got an idea.” “What, point a gun at his head and force him to confess or we’ll kill him?” “Just go to the monitor and watch.” “What are you planning?” “Trust me, this is going to work. I assure you.” “I can’t be assured of anything with you at this point.” “Just. Trust. Me. This will work, promise.” “Fine” Dispersing out of the side room, Larry and Steven head in opposite directions. Steven goes to the left where the officers who escorted Devin were watching the verbal exchange go down on a monitor. Both of them exchange a condolence kind of look, sympathizing with having to deal with a headstrong detective. Larry heads back into the interrogation room. Just before opening the door, he takes a deep breath and composes himself, running over his improvisation idea once in his head. If this failed, the repercussions would be massive. Giving a confidence nod to himself, Larry turns the door and finds a perturbed Devin Shakur staring back at him with a hint of impatience. “Sorry for that brush up earlier, Mr. Shakur. It’s been a rather long day at the office, and crime always rises when the weather is hot.” Larry says, adopting a more friendly tone before taking his original seat in front of Devin. “Do you have anymore questions? If not then I should be going.” With the smile of a car and ulterior motives of a car salesman, Larry replies, “Just a few more and you’ll be free to go.” “Alright, ask away.” “It has been established that, at least according to the ME, that you were at Mr. Grimwood’s office on the day of his death.” “Apparently” “And it has been established that you were there around the time of his death, which was approximately 12:34.” From the monitoring room, Steven sighs, “Get to the point, Larry. You are losing him.” “You are still considering me a suspect, aren’t you?” Larry completely ignores Shakur’s inquiry, going for his ace in the hole. “Mr. Shakur, what if I was to inform you that we have obtained video tape showing you not only entering Mr. Grimwood’s office before the murder was committed, but also of you leaving Mr. Grimwood’s office seconds before his death was made official by the coroner?” Steven and the two officers stationed in the monitoring room all hold the same expression, disbelief. Larry just went out on quite possibly the biggest limb conceivable without a single ounce of legitimacy behind it. He’s presenting Devin with a stone cold bluff and hoping he’ll crack underneath the pressure of possibility. Throwing his arms up in surrender, Steven says, “Jesus, Larry, what in the fuck are you on...” He wants to finish the diatribe. He wants to go in there and give his brother the worst tongue lashing ever concocted from one human being to the other. But he doesn’t. He can’t. Because Devin doesn’t respond. He sits there, stone silent with a blank expression on his face. One of the officers tips his cup toward the monitor, “That takes some nuts, right there.” Steven gives a thoughtless nod to the officer and stands there, shell shocked. He never envisioned that such a brass tactic would be capable of taking down someone like Shakur, who seemed all but ready to bolt just a few minutes ago. Now the wrestling superstar is staring at the all too eager detective foaming at the mouth, ready to cuff and book him for first degree murder. Like a poker player faced with an all in, Shakur locks his eyes on Larry Connors. This kid was absolutely dying to get vindication for the death of his father and uncles, a crime Devin had no part in whatsoever. He was just associated with the people who partook in the executions. The death of Shakur’s father, “Mike Grimwood”, was nothing more than bait. Steven and the officers in the monitoring room are on edge, waiting desperately for Shakur to say something. Larry sits there, mimicking Shakur’s poker face and adding a condescending aura. He put all of his chips into the table with absolutely nothing behind him except a world of confidence and now it appears to have paid off. A small smirk begins to emerge on Larry’s face, barely visible to the surveillance cameras. Shakur meanwhile is still drenched deep in the proverbial thinking tank, blinking being his only movement since the accusation. Three long minutes has elapsed and Larry can no longer allow Shakur to sit and ponder. He moves in for the kill. “Mr. Shakur, do you have anything-“ “Show me” Larry either doesn’t hear or doesn’t want to acknowledge the comment, “Pardon?” “Show me the tapes” Steven facepalms, “Oh shit” “Mr. Shakur, I don’t have to show you the tapes because you and I both know that they-“ Devin folds his hands across the table in a similar manner to Larry at the beginning of the interrogation. “Show me the tapes because I don’t believe a fucking word that just came out of your mouth.” He’s been in front of cameras long enough to know when someone is talking for affect. Larry stumbles for a moment, unable to come up with a proper retort. Now Devin knows he’s got the young detective against the ropes. Sliding his own chair back, he stands, leaning down over Larry and showing a menacing pair of eyes. “YOU WANNA LAY DOWN A CLAIM THAT HUGE? SHOW ME THE GODDAMN TAPES! BRING A TELEVISION IN HERE RIGHT NOW AND SHOW ME!” Like most people who get caught bluffing, Larry can’t save himself. The actions have already spoken for themselves. “That’s what I fucking thought!” Devin moves away from the table, all too willing to head for the door and more importantly the exit. Steven rushes down the hall, trying to save face and his brother, “MR, SHAKUR!” “No, I’m fucking tired of playing this bullshit! What do you got? YOU GOT CIRCUMSTANTIAL BULLSHIT! Oh I meet the man on the day of his murder and ALL A SUDDEN I’M THE PRIME SUSPECT? FUCK THE BOTH OF YOU! When you get something more than intuition, you fucking come to me. Otherwise, you better get a good lawyer because I’m suing the SHIT out of this department!” “MR. SHAKUR!” Larry stands up, attempting to give chase. Steven has to restrain his brother and the pair looks at their only viable lead turn back to face them one more time. “IF YOU HAVE ANYTHING CONCRETE, STOP ME FROM LEAVING THE BUILDING! STOP ME! YOU HAVE THAT ABILITY! IF YOU HAVE SOMETHING LEGIT, STOP ME FROM WALKING OUT THE FRONT DOOR!” Turning on his right foot, Devin storms out of the building swearing the entire way toward his green pick up truck. Neither Larry nor Steven move from their positions, both dropping their heads collectively and looking toward the ground. They blew this one. Bad. ------------------------- Storming down unfamiliar roads with his air condition on full blast and Gatorade pouring down his throat, Devin Shakur stops off the side of the road and reaches at his left leg, shifting the excess part of his pants away and acquiring a small device attached to a wire that goes all the way up to his belt. A minute, barely visible microphone “You get all of that?” The grainy voice of Christian Daniels responds on the other end, “Yeah, we got it.” “Fucking ridiculous” “We’ll handle it from here, good quick thinking in there, kid.” “Yeah, don’t make me do that again.” Shakur yanks the microphone from his belt buckle and flings it over to the passenger seat, rumbling down the back roads and plugging in coordinates into his GPS system. ----------------- Taiwan Sitting around a living room, Brandon Smith closes a laptop computer and places it underneath his right arm, moving up the stairs of the Smith residence. Looking around the room at Jason Hardy, Michael Blaze, Mallory Daniels, Rachel, Chris and Matthew Smith, Christian stands up to his full seven feet and folds his arms across his broad chest. “That whole exchange took half an hour, tops. Anybody else have an argument as to why we shouldn’t go back now to end these pricks once and for all?” Nobody can formulate a response, not even the defiant one from two weeks ago, Jason. “Good, now let’s run through a checklist, because we’re going back real damn soon. We got parachutes?” Jason nods in affirmation, “Check” “Lined up transportation when we get there?” “We’ll get it” “Hideout?” “We can get that too.” “Plane?” “Yep” “Disposable pilot?” “I’m sure we can get one.” “Let’s plan, kids.”
