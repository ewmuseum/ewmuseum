Fathers and Daughters

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: ReV 177 Wind raced across the rooftop in great, sweeping gusts. Bitter cold stung at her porcelain grimace. Invisible lashes from an icy whip. Her raven hair was helpless to resist the pull and tug of the wind gusts. It fluttered and snapped about her stony expression. The searing howl of wind was her moonlight serenade. The suffocating darkness a blanket from the world. Katterina Wylde stepped from those oppressive shadows, ignoring the unforgiving chill that crept like a spider up her spine. The wind would not relent as she marched assuredly out to the middle of the rooftop. Stars struggled to twinkle through the wispy grey clouds above. Detroit glistened below her. Car headlights and flickering streetlamps became the luminescent heart monitor of her city at night. The city below. There, above the hustling heartbeat… there was no life. There was Katterina. There was darkness. There was silence and stillness. And then… there was a voice on the wind. “Katterina, my daughter. How pleasant to see you again.” His smooth voice floated on the wind. It caressed gently against her ear. A comforting and inviting tone that promised far more than he would provide. Compassion. Love. Understanding. The Dark Angel swallowed deeply, pushing aside an unexpected flow of emotion. The hairs on her neck prickled. The wind had subsided. The tempest downgraded to little more than a breathy whisper. “Father.” Her only acknowledgement of the siren’s song upon the wind. Her eyes were narrow and sharp. Focused on the movement of shadows. The play of distant lights against the rooftop. She strained to listen through the quiet. Beyond the silence to something… Oh fuck it. “You can come out now. The spook show’s not going to work on me.” His bemused chuckle came softly from behind. She craned her neck ever so to watch the approach of… absolutely no one. She should have expected. Katterina turned back to face. He stood with hands clasped behind his back, long nails curled around pale skin. His hair, darker than the night itself, fell magnificently around his shoulders. Danced with grace and beauty upon the wind. His back remained turned toward her. His gaze fixated upon the Detroit pulse. “I was wondering when he’d send you to find me.” “No one sends me to do anything.” “Denial is an ugly thing, my dear. That degenerate has had you gallivanting around this globe for years now, doing his biding.” “If Tyler wanted to see you-“ “He’d be shit out of luck. If Rayne could have found me by now, I would have seen him. Our encounters are derived upon my whim, Katterina. Not his.” “I didn’t come here for him.” “Because of him.” “Did you kill that girl?” The question tumbled out of her mouth before she could stop it. “You might have to be a little more specific, dear.” “The reporter.” “The Jap.” Her hands curled to fists. Tightened. “Yes, I killed her.” “You told me you were done with that.” “I lied.” There apparently wouldn’t be any subtlety this evening. “The human race is weak, Katterina. They are sheep. Cattle. Livestock for my slaughter. You and I… we are superior. We are better than them. I have lived centuries amongst these mortals. One meaningless life-“ “Meaningless?” “Yes. The girl’s life was insignificant. She could have lived for many years. Died of old age and withered away. She would have made no more impact upon this world than she did in the few years she survived before I found her. Her only purpose was my purpose. A means to an end.” “What end?” “The destruction of Tyler Rayne. I thought that would be obvious by now.” “But… why?” “There are too many reasons to count, my dear. Tyler Rayne is an arrogant, insolent little roach. He always has been. He always will be. He represents the worst this society has to offer. A self-important asshole. Takes what he wants. When he wants. No regard. No remorse. No respect for the world and its wondrous gifts. For those who came before him. He took from me the one thing I loved. And I have taken great pleasure in slowly, torturously taking those he loves away from him.” “The one thing you loved? Tyler never took shit from… Oh Christ. You can’t be serious.” “What else did you think this was about, Katterina?” “Not fucking me. You… what do you even care? You abandoned me! Left me to fend for myself with a mother who… You may be my father, but you were never there. You never-“ “I am your father. My blood in your veins. You have a legacy, Katterina. A destiny. Together you and I could-“ “Oh, spare me the Darth Vader bullshit. I could never-“ “Never what? Be like me? A murderer? Like your precious Tyler hasn’t taken lives in his time. Like you haven’t taken lives in-“ “That’s different.” “The lives you took were less important than the ones I have? You killed a rapist and I killed a reporter, so somehow you’re better than me? Did his parents weep any less than hers? We are the same, Katterina. No better. No worse. Good and evil are ridiculous notions impressed upon these mortals to keep them in line. To keep the flock in order. Such ideologies are beneath me. They are beneath you, as well. You have a power, Katterina. A gift.” “Power that came from a stupid fucking book. Jakob and his bastard broth-“ “Fuck that plebian. Fuck The Fallen. I’m not talking about that childish bullshit. I’m talking about your blood, Katterina. Your lineage. That book would have done nothing if not for the greatness coursing through you veins from birth. A-“ “I don’t care, Se7yn. I just… don’t care. I gave you a chance. I let you back in. You told me it was over. You were going to be better.” “I am better.” “You told me the killing was over. That you’d fight the hunger.” “I did. For a spell. Where did it get me? I never stood any brighter in your graces. I tried to play the hero. I tried to help these mortals. And what did it get me? Nothing. I walked these streets. I watched these lives. These ridiculous, meaningless lives. And I realized something. I realized that I was born to something more. Something… higher. I am the predator. They are the prey.” “So you decided to prey on Tyler? You, who claim to love me so fucking much, your precious little daughter… and you insist on destroying the life of my best friend? The only-“ “What did you come here for, Katterina?” “I came here to stop this.” “What’s set in motion cannot be undone.” “So I’m starting to see.” “It’s almost commendable, really. That you came here to beg for his life. An attempt to appeal to my familial sensibilities. If you’re not willing to embrace my blood, however… you can hardly expect me to embrace yours. Go back to your master, Katterina. Tell him you have failed. Tell him I am coming and there is nothing he can do to change the inevitable. Tell him to make peace. If he can. There is not much time left.” Similarly, there was nothing left to say. Even in her own muddled thoughts, Katterina was unsure of what she’d come here to accomplish. Everything Tyler had said was true. Something hidden beneath the layers of guilt and hope had known. But she wouldn’t let herself believe. Couldn’t. Until she’d heard it from the mouth of the monster himself. The wind began to pick up again. This time, though, the frozen needles could not penetrate her shell. She was too cold inside, too hard, to feel the bite of a blustery fall evening. The toe of her boot dangled over the edge of the roof. She did not turn to see if he was still there. She did not turn to see if he was watching curiously. Waiting for the reply. She spoke to the wind, much like it had spoken to her a few minutes before. “I didn’t come here to save his life, father.” Katterina Wylde stepped off the edge of the roof. She let the night embrace her. “I came here to save yours.”
