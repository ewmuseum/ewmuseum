A Blast from the Past

11 February 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11 Feb 2010 Cigarette smoke hung like an acrid cloud of cancer within the Solyanka Club in Moscow, Russia. This was the next stop on the Culture Shock Tour and ReVolution 218 had recently come to a close. Cyrus Sutherland had wound up sitting at the bar, glancing at the Russians and foreigners alike with careful apprehension while he considered the events of the evening. It was an interesting place, the Solyanka Club. It had been built only a few years ago and though the charge to enter was twelve dollars, Sutherland was a foreigner, and as such he was admitted into the club for free. A strange policy. The only other policy that seemed to be present was the fact that the more outlandish the outfit you wore the better the chance you had to get in for free. This meant that there were plenty of odd looking folks dancing around in the club. Much to the chagrin of Sutherland. Cyrus Sutherland wasn’t much of a dancer. The strobe lights and thumping beats; they only seemed to accentuate the headaches he felt from time to time. It was like the entire club was a microcosm of his psyche. Or was it a macrocosm? His eyes narrowed as he considered this thought for only a moment, before pushing it out of his ear for someone else to digest. In any event, Sutherland wasn’t here to wax eloquently about his mental difficulties. He scanned the crowd to spot a group of young kids doing shots of what was undoubtedly vodka, a few ne’er do wells leaning against the wall, a possible illegal transaction going down at a nearby booth, and Elise Ares dancing in the center of the dance floor. Sutherland shook his head. The girl was as crazy as he was, if not perhaps even more. There was a huge circle of people built around her watching her move in ways Sutherland didn’t think possible. It was as if there was an invisible ring around her. Any time someone stepped within that ring, Ares lashed out with a string of Russian profanities that would have made Sutherland blush if he knew just what the hell she was saying. She was cocky. Arrogant. Interesting to watch. The display reminded Sutherland that they were on the same team and that made them all the more dangerous. She was, after all, the 5 Star Champion for a reason. She had a sharp tongue and wit, but she was also physically adept. This brought a smirk to Sutherland’s face. That was his kind of friend. He was glad she had come to the club with him following their match. She could speak Russian whereas he couldn’t. Sure, Sutherland had no doubt he would have been able to buy a drink on his own, but that would have been the extent of his interaction with the Russian people. One fella looked like he had some sort of condom on his head and wore a plastic tanktop and shorts complete with thigh high boots and his nails were painted black and white. Sutherland shook his head. No doubt that freakshow got into the club for free. It seemed like a travesty to allow anyone, dressed like that, to get anything free in Sutherland’s mind. He seemed rather poorly dressed for the occasion as he mulled over his choice of clothing. It was more utilitarian than anything else. The t-shirt, blue jeans, and black suspenders seemed rather simplistic compared to the flashier fashions he witnessed in his stead. At least he didn’t have to dress like a jackass to get into this place for free, being American was good enough for the Ruskies. The bartender slithered his way closer to Sutherland and wiped down the area in front of him. Sutherland nodded, "Beer me…" he said loudly over the din of the music. A moment later there was a frothy glass of beer in front of Sutherland and he drank it down with a few gulps. Ares was slam dancing or something out there now, and the people were cheering her on to the beat of the music. Cyrus slurped the froth off of the top of his lip with his lower lip and drummed his fingers on the table. The fact of the matter was that Sutherland, despite gaining the support of Chainz, Ares, and the Osaka Street Cutters, still hadn’t made the impact he had envisioned when he joined PRIME. Granted his shenanigans this evening at ReVolution sure did a lot to ease his concerns. After all, he not only had a group of wrestlers to back him, but Devin Shakur seemed to be on board to, at least for the present, back him against his nemesis. Tyler Rayne, for better or worse, certainly gave him a direction to move toward, but he had hit a few bumps in the road. The honest truth was that Sutherland wasn’t as good of a wrestler as he could be. Troy Douglas proved that point soundly. Tyler Rayne’s blow to his jaw was a period to the entire statement. The match with Troy Douglas, a veteran of PRIME, certainly snapped Sutherland back into some semblance of reality. At least the reality that the rest of the world accepted. In Sutherland’s mind, he’d just waltz into that ring and beat Douglas within an inch of his life and be done with it, but that was hardly the case. Douglas not only was faster, but his in ring presence was stronger. He didn’t hit as hard as Sutherland, and his namby-pamby willingness to play by the rules was annoying, but he didn’t just beat Sutherland. He creamed him. The fiery ginger ground his teeth in frustration while Ares did some sort of cartwheel flip thing and then screamed for the rest of the masses to get away from her greatness. Sutherland knew at the same time that he was running with a pack of cut throat wolves all the same. He didn’t trust Ares, Sloan, or the Cutters as far as he could throw them. It made him uneasy. He had thought, and perhaps been concerned, that his overture to Devin Shakur would have been frowned upon by the rest of the crew. But they didn’t seem to be fazed. The fact that Shakur would orchestrate a match in their favor against Rayne made things all the better. Play to win. That’s the rule that Cyrus Sutherland had always lived by. Shakur and himself had a common enemy: Tyler Rayne. What was the harm in playing the field and seeing how it shook out? "She is quite the dancer, is she not?" came a higher pitched voice behind Sutherland. He turned slightly to see who had spoken and if they had spoken to him. Standing there was a dark haired man, angular features, pale skin, and sallow cheeks. He looked unequivocally Russian. He offered a grin to Sutherland and tipped the black hat atop his head. He was a smaller man, definitely not an athlete by any stretch of the word, but his person was obscured by a black trench coat. It wasn’t zipped, and the white shirt and thin black tie beneath was a bit strange to Sutherland. Perhaps some sort of an 80’s throwback or something? "Who?" "The girl on the floor over there…" the man said as he pointed with two of his fingers, which also held a lit cigarette that only added to the cancer cloud hovering over the club. "You know her?" "How’d you guess?" Sutherland replied as he turned slowly on his chair to face the man, "And who are you?" The man grinned and shrugged his shoulders, "Call it a lucky guess…" he said softly, a tinge of a Russian accent in his voice, or perhaps just Eastern European. Sutherland couldn’t tell. They all sounded the same. "I am joking!" The man continued, raising his voice to speak over a loud portion of some sort of techno song, "I am Russell! I am an American!" He paused, "Might I join you?" Cyrus shrugged his shoulders, "It’s not my club, join me if you want, just don’t piss me off. I’ve got a lot on my mind." "Indeed, it would appear so!" The man said carefully as he leaned against the bar, next to the bartender. The tender quickly approached Russell and spoke to him in Russian, only for Russell to respond quickly and shoo him away, "You are Cyrus Sutherland, yes? From PRIME wrestling federation?" Now, Sutherland would have liked to have thought that he was already world famous, but he certainly wasn’t. If anything, most people probably changed the channel when he got into the ring. It was the way the game was played when you first started off in wrestling. He knew that from the start. But it was pretty strange that Mr. Russell here would already know who he was, "Yeah, and how would you know that?" "Oh, I do not know, my friend… actually I lied again. I do know!" Sutherland narrowed his eyes as the man, who cheerfully laughed off the menacing look, "I am wrestling fan, truth be told. Dual Halo competition went through Russia last year, if I recall correctly. That and… to be honest… I am not just Mister Russell. I am Agent Russell: CIA." This brought Sutherland’s right eyebrow up a bit higher, "CIA?" Russell nodded and leaned in closer to Sutherland so their voices were all but drowned out amidst the music, "I am born from this part of the country, yes, but I work for United States. I only tell you this because… I’m aware of what you went through…" Sutherland’s eyes searched the smaller man carefully, studying different portions of his features, penetrating his features, "What I went through? The fuck are you talking about?" "There is no reason to be coy, Mister Sutherland. We are aware of what happened in Iraq. You weren’t just a grunt. And what happened up here…" he said carefully as he tapped the side of his head, "…was a byproduct of that mess." It was a dangerous thing to bring this up to Sutherland, and Russell was well aware of it. He could tell right away, his trained eyes studying the man, that he was growing angry. Apprehensive. Paranoid. It was all in his dossier. Sutherland was a dangerous, unpredictable man. But Agent Russell knew a thing or two about paranoia. And he also knew that his partner, seated not that far away at the bar, was more than capable of not only handling Cyrus Sutherland, but half of the club at the same time. It brought a smile to Russell’s face. "I don’t know what the fuck you’re talking about, Russell." Sutherland said as he leaned closer to the man, "But you’d do well not to talk about that shit to anyone else around here, or anywhere for that matter, or else I’ll shove that hat down your throat and make you shit it out." Russell leaned backward, careful to make sure Sutherland’s beer soaked spit didn’t hit his white shirt, "Now now, Cyrus, there is no need to threaten me. I am simply saying that we’re watching you, and we understand what you’re going through. You have nothing to worry about." "Who said anything about being worried?" His eyes traveled along Russell once more, and then back over his shoulder toward Ares. She was still dancing her heart out. "Watch that girl, Sutherland. She’s a Cuban defector, you know…" "So fucking what…" "She isn’t willing to stay in her own country for whatever reason… who is to say she won’t stab you in the back someday?" "Who’s to say I won’t do it to her first." There was a pause as the two men looked at one another once more, and then Russell laughed, "Hee haa! You’re exactly right! That’s what we expected of you!" Russell took a pull on his cigarette and he and Sutherland continued to watch Ares, who was doing some sort of a cross between the Macarena and the electric slide, "You know it is interesting… thinking on how she defected and fled from that communist hotbed in Cuba…" "Big fucking deal…" Sutherland replied, "And what do you care? What are you doing here anyway?" This brought a wily grin to Russell’s face. Sutherland didn’t need to know that this fellow was a mystery wrapped in an enigma. He also knew not to believe a damned word he said, but it didn’t hurt to ask. Russell was all too quick to answer, "Oh," he said with a wave of his hand, "let us just say we’re watching to make sure those communist dogs don’t garner any power here. National security. Democracy. All of that… uh… jazz…" Cue the shifty eyes. "So then, Mister CIA…" "Shhh!! Don’t say that too loudly!" Russell replied as he brought one of his hands to the top of his round, flat hat. With a roll of his eyes, Sutherland leaned a bit closer to the smaller man, "I find it hard to believe that you simply found me here, on accident, and just so happened to start talking to me…" He stared at the Agent with a cautious look, "Or do coincidences just happen often in your life?" "To be completely honest, though we are aware of you and what you’ve been through, we were interested in seeing the girl there." "What? See her dance?" Sutherland asked as he turned on his chair again and looked at Ares. "You know, Russell… she’s pretty and all but you can find other girls with bigger cans and less clothes who will dance specifically for you… provided you have the cash. And you get to leave the joint smelling like baby powder…" Russell scoffed and flipped up the collar of his trench coat, "Be that as it may, Sutherland, I’d prefer not to smell like the cleaned behind of an infant…" "Listen pal," Sutherland began, "she’s the 5 Star Champion of PRIME and one cocky, arrogant bitch." Russell crossed his arms over his chest and paused, watching the girl dance for the crowd. He shook his head. She reminded him of someone. Someone who’d have loved this loud, garish scene. Techno music. Noise. Chaos. "That she is, my friend. That she is. I never understood why it is people call this catastrophic rubbish music…" There was another long moment and Sutherland took time to gaze around the establishment once more, all but ignoring Russell’s comment. He had noticed, however, that the sight of Ares had somehow ruffled the American agent’s feathers. It was curious, to say the least. More than anything, however, he was looking for Russell’s partner. After all, there was no doubt in Cyrus’ mind that Russell wasn’t here alone. Even if he was packing heat somewhere beneath that trench coat. CIA ops don’t move about alone. No doubt he was being watched elsewhere… or Ares was. It brought Sutherland’s head to turn to look at Ares again and he considered, for a moment, if perhaps he should mention that the CIA douchebag was specifically here to watch her. With all the people dressed up as they were, it was impossible to decide if anyone looked particularly conspicuous. Russell had dressed like a typical guy, for the most part, save for that thin tie that even made Sutherland cringe. But then Sutherland considered himself… he too looked like a typical fellow from the street, and he sure wasn’t a CIA agent. It was hopeless. Or was it just misdirection on the case of Russell? A loud fanfare exploded from Russell’s breast pocket and quickly he fumbled to open his cell phone and answer it, in Russian. Several onlookers raised their glasses, hearing the music. Sutherland narrowed his eyes and studied the man again, who chattered into his phone and shut it. "Ahem…" "Not exactly the quiet type of ringtone, Russell. Good thing you weren’t sneaking up on someone…" Russell laughed the jab off and cleared his throat, "That was… interesting news." He paused, "It would seem your next match has been announced: Hessian." Hessian. "Son of a bitch…" "Da… you could say that." "I did say that." "It was figure of speech!" Russell said with exasperation. "However, Hessian has too much going on to think clearly on your match with him. Use that to your advantage. He is a sound wrestler and sadistic as they come. Think of it as a chance to really… expand." "Expand?" Russell scooted his bar stool closer to Sutherland and leaned in. The two didn’t make any attempt to hide the fact they were conspiring. "You operate best outside the confines of the rules, Cyrus Sutherland. So does Hessian. But he’s bigger than you. He’s more experienced. He is also one of the most sadistic people you could encounter. However, you have a collective behind you. You have a group of people willing to help you overcome this individual. The collective will always overcome the individual, Sutherland." "What?" Russell inhaled and after a moment he shook his head. His fingers moved to the area between his eyes and rubbed. The combination of the music and the Cuban defector in the background filled him with concern, "In any event, I think it is time that I go. I’ve spent too much time here. It was nice to have seen you here, Mister Sutherland. I don’t wish to leave on a dubious note, but we’ll be watching you." "Hey now… let me ask you one question for you, secret agent man! What’s your first name?" "Oh ho, my boy, that is national security… I cannot say!" "Wait a minute!" Sutherland answered, "How do you know all this shit?" However, it was too late. Russell had already gotten down from his chair and straightened his jacket and hat. He grinned and turned on his heel without another word. Russell paused, not more than a few feet from Sutherland, and waved behind him, "You got your one question, Sutherland, and I have answered it to the best of my ability. Good luck with Hessian. He’s emotional wreck and has been seen crying like girl. A fellow of his size and stature could do without being a crybaby. But do try to make him cry again." He tipped his hat toward Sutherland, "Das vidanya, Cy." Cyrus watched the small agent move off and shook his head. He focused his attention back to the bartender, "Beer me…" In his peripheral vision he could still see Russell leaving, and as he slapped the back of a man sitting far along the bar, the man stood and grew larger and larger. He was a massive man, in a matching trench coat and hat. He screamed agent, though hardly an agent who could sneak anywhere. He dwarfed everyone else in the club and together the duo walked out of the club. Perhaps the local clientele had recognized the huge agent or perhaps they just knew he wasn’t about to move around a person, but everyone got out of his way, some of them actually speaking toward him, only to receive a curt reply in Russian in return. Feds. What a pain in the ass… Sutherland thought as he stared toward the door. Russell had turned at the doorway and saluted at the group, before spinning on his heel and striding right out the door. The lumbering man behind him lowered his head to avoid the top of the doorway and held onto his hat, and then the two were swallowed up by the Moscow darkness. He didn’t need his personal shit getting in the way of his wrestling. He didn’t need any of this. And he certainly didn’t need Hessian to stomp a hole into his skull. Troy Douglas got lucky and Russell was right. He did have a group behind him. Sutherland didn’t care about the rules and he didn’t care about winning fair. A victory against Troy Douglas would have been helpful. A victory against Hessian would be phenomenal. Sutherland continued to stare off in the direction of the goliath who had left with Agent Russell, though he was now left with his thoughts. The man he had just witnessed was roughly as large as Hessian, if not bigger. That kind of sudden perspective really brought things home. Hessian. His history with Hessian only extended as far as his history with Chainz and Elise Ares, but it was simple enough. Hessian had hung out with that duo, and they had unceremoniously thrown him out of their cadre. How ironic, he had to think, that now he had taken the place of Hessian. The beast had hit rock bottom and if there was a chance for Sutherland to best the beast, now was the chance to do it. His thoughts were interrupted by the hand of Elise Ares slapping hard on his right shoulder, "You just gonna stare at that door all evening, Cyrus?" "Huh?" He said as he turned to look at Ares, "You done cutting a rug over there?" He nodded in the direction of the dance floor, where other freaks had begun to fill in the large gap she had left. With a grin, she slapped the bar and yelled something in Russian to the bartender and he promptly brought two shots of vodka. She lifted a glass as Sutherland took his own, "To one hell of a match, Cy." "You bet, Elise." The two clinked the vodka glasses together and both did their respective shot. "Saw you chatting with someone earlier… who was that?" Elise asked him, taking the moment to quickly catch a camera pointed in her direction. She offered a wide grin as the bulb went off, and reverted back to her conversational veneer. For half a moment, Sutherland considered mentioning to her that the agent, who knew who he was, was actually keeping an eye on her, but he instead shrugged it off, "Just some fan..." Elise Ares considered this for a moment and shrugged, "C’mon then, Cy, get out there on the dance floor, will ya? I had the match and I’m dancing!" "Naw, I think I’m just going to sit here, I have some shit to think about…" "Suit yourself…" She responded as she turned and screamed in English, "All right people, make a path, I’m coming back!" Like Moses parting the Red Sea, the dancers split for Elise Ares and granted her the wide berth she demanded, and she slipped right back into another dance on the floor. Sutherland shook his head. It nagged him as to why Russell wouldn’t reveal his first name. Sure he could have given him an alias or simply lied, but no, Russell instead chose not to say anything. What a bastard. "What was his first name…?" Sutherland mentioned aloud as he rubbed his chin. "Alex." The voice came from the bar, and as Sutherland looked down at his beer, the amber fluid simply shimmered. Sutherland narrowed his eyes and stared at the beer… …and the beer stared back…
