Musings of a Whole Freaking SHO

11 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/11/05 If you're hearing this, you must be a member of the Van World Order. If you say that you're not a Van World Order member, then you clearly didn't listen to the last line. After all, you'd have to be a diehard fan of mine to come to my website and download the podcast. If you don't like me and you're still listening to this, then, well... you must be one of those masochistic types who enjoys self-torture, and I'm all for helping a person seek enjoyment; please, continue to listen if that, in whatever way, gets you off. To start off, I'm sure that you saw how great that Teh Greatness has been. We got the Tag Team Titles back. It was a crock how we got robbed at Tropical Turmoil. To win the belts from Los Suckablos, then Civil Disobedience comes out and acts like they deserve a shot when, who did Scott and I beat in the first place to get there? Say it with me. C-I-V... well, you get the idea. They come out, then Pranks With Attitude. comes out when they dropped out of the mini-tournament in the first round. Second match happens, blah blah, we lose the tag titles. And it's because CD pinned PWA. We weren't even in the deciding fall. That makes 3 consecutive wins for Masters and Hawke over Nerezza and Gaz Green. Someone answer me this: since the fall, perhaps, of 2002, has this been ANYTHING to celebrate about? It's pretty well established in OSW, and then when they tried to go it on their own after that neither with very good results, that the two aren't just shells of their former selves as a team, but as individuals as well. I mean, damn, neither of them really acts like they want to be a wrestler anymore. Civil Disobedience... well, they do want to be wrestlers, but that's only because they have bills to pay. Not a damn one of them have any marketable skills outside of performing hammerlocks, so if they're not working a match they're not eating. Except for maybe Nerezza, but it'd be a bit of a problem when a blood test turns up like a bazillion different types of DNA, none of which being his. Point is, Teh Greatness suffered a grave injustice, but we didn't get an immediate rematch because, well, the whole Jewel In The Crown thing was going on and that took precedence over everything. I mean, I'm sure whoever is actually running the damned place now (I heard that there was some backstage shifting of the power, not that I care because, well, honestly I never really talked to the former boss; Adrienne handles that stuff) would've liked to have a full set of 32 singles wrestlers and leave us tag team guys the hell alone. But it didn't turn out that way, and a bunch of us were thrown into the big tournament. And the winner will get... actually, I never looked that up. I think a title shot or something. I lost in the first round against Tyler Lopez. But as we've all seen, it was more of the case of me handing him the victory. He needed it. I mean, I have more heat in the crotch of my pants than Lopez has for his whole career right now. I took the fall to a Diving Headbutt not because I was outclassed in that ring; no, it was because I saw the kind of funk that he'd fallen into. What was everybody saying? That he'd lost his drive? Well I looked him right in the eye, in that ring, and said, 'Vroom vroom muthafugga.' He went on to lose in the second round. Here's where we go onto a little detour and I explain something. I wasn't even expecting to get any kind of play-in action when I took the loss. I was fine with handing it off to Lopez and going on with my day, career, life. Not because I don't care about PRIME, but honestly. I'm Vangellus (short 'u', two l's, fockers!) Oxios. The Whole Freaking SHO. I didn't need the Jewel In The Crown like he needed it. After all, Lopez, and damn near everyone else in that thing, is a singles wrestler. Not like me. 'But Van, winning the JITC is still a great thing. It can lead to a lot more noteriety and maybe even some singles title shots.' Do I look like I care? Think back; how did I enter PRIME? With Gabriel Brown as part of Teh Pwn. We were a tag team. When Gabe decided to take the ball and run with it (all the way to a different home field), did I quit? No. I wanted to stay here in PRIME and continue making my way in this place. And lo, what did I do? I recruited a replacement partner. Anyone even vaguely interested in PRIME, understand this. I am a member of Teh Greatness, the Tag Team Champions of PRIME. I work with Scott Matthews because that's what I want to do. I'm not looking for a stepping stone (or launching pad, as the case may be) into singles super stardom. Hell, I could do that at any time if I wanted, and hey, let's not forget, I'm a legitimate fighting superstar around the globe. PRIME is small-potatoes compared to the kind of press and money that I could get the world over in all sorts of fighting venues. I'm here because I have a goal. First, to entertain the fans. Second, to maintain a successful tag team with my partner. The first and second actually go well together, so there's no need to change up the formula. Scott and I are the tag champs. No tournament is going to split us apart, no singles accolades will change that. So as we come to the Quarter-Finals of the JITC and I'm the only member of our team who's still in competition (Scott lost to Killean Sirrajin, but he's a former Universal champ so now shame in that), the only primary tag team wrestler left in the thing even, what does this change? Not. One. Damn. Thing. Teh Greatness is still going to be a force to be reckoned with, ESPECIALLY now that we're under the tutelage of the accomplished Justin Williamson. Think about it. Really. Scott Matthews and Vangellus Oxios. It's not like we couldn't defend ourselves in a fight, but we are still rookies in wrestling and professional wrestling is supposed to be a whole different beast compared to a street fight. And still. Look at the list of teams who have been defeated by two guys who know 4 wrestling moves between us. Forsaken. Civil Disobedience. Pranks With Attitude. Los Diablos. Okay, so there's only four teams, not our fault the division just kinda sank to crap after Gabe and I showed up and suddenly we went from like 9 teams to 3. Still, if PRIME hires teams who are at least decent in terms of chemistry and wrestling ability, that means that there's something about Scott and I that enable us to defeat much more skilled pairs, all of whom had been together longer than we have. Could we lose the tag team straps? Of course. But the scary thing is that even when you consider that little point, the fact remains that we can only go up from here. It's now the second week of October. We're a third of the way done with the month now. And we find Teh Greatness as two-time PRIME Tag champs. We've only just started. I like wrestling, and apparently my partner doesn't mind having a job that allows him to legally bash people senseless, so we aren't going anywhere. Get used to seeing us here for a long while. Jewel in the Crown? Nothing more than a feather in the cap for PRIME's hottest new team to come along since Manifest Destiny. And since neither myself nor Matthews seems to be too high on the idea of betrayal, look forward to a long time on top for the Random Pairing That Could. It was 12:18am when Vangellus Oxios, Adrienne Diamond, and Teshigahara Emi walked into the Damon's Bar and Grill. Of course, for the late hour only the bar was open, and at that there were only a handful of other people there. Mostly some PRIME crew who had already arrived, getting some drinks and exchanging stories from home before they hit the road to make it to the next show. As they entered, Adrienne commented, 'This place is awfully... red.' Indeed, the leather barstool covers, walls, placemats and coasters, as well as the tiled floors were all red. Secondary color present in the napkins, stripes of the wallpapering, and stairs, was white. Seat covers green. 'And you probably look like a floating head to the guys in the back,' Van joked, as Ms. Diamond herself was wearing a red business suit. Emi, as always clad from head to toe in her white robes, sniffed at the air. 'What is that smell?' Oxios himself took in a big whiff of the air, it having a pleasant and reminiscent quality. 'Pork lard. Man, this takes me back. Must be emptying out the fryers.' 'Ugh...' the bodyguard shook her head. She hated food that was created to have negative nutritional value. Though the dining area was no longer open for the food service, they all still took seats to the left from the entrance, where there was a substantial amount of booth seating. Glass walls around around the three fascades (back wall separating the bathrooms and kitchen), they had a clear view of the outside world. The night sky was clear and bright from the moonlight. They were 50 miles northwest of Toledo, Ohio, traveling along the edge of Lake Eerie. The air was cool and crisp outside, the winter months soon approaching but this area of the nation already feeling the full onset of fall. 'Okay,' Oxios clapped his hands. 'I'll get some drinks. What does everybody want?' Adrienne thought about it. Emi was the first to respond, asking for a glass of water. Soon enough, Van's manager voiced her pleasure at a Long Island Iced Tea. 'Come on,' Van gestured around his person to the restaurant as a whole. 'This is Damon's. The best they've got is Grey Goose or Gekkeikan sake.' Diamond clicked her tongue against the back of her teeth. 'Tsk... if that's all they've got then I'll go with the vodka.' Though Emi didn't drink, she didn't mind if Adrienne did. Often made her more adventurous in bed. Taking off his blue denim coat leaving only his jeans and a white sleeveless tee, Vangellus left them at the front of the table-dining area, crossing the 10 feet of tiled floor to the bar that was angled to face the door from the parking lot. They wante the first thing that people thought about when they entered the place to be the alcohol, not the food. A good thing, as the chain isn't known for the quality of the menu. 'My good man,' Vangellus approached the bar, sounding like a wealthy snob. At least he had the wealthy part right. 'I would like to purchase beverages for myself and my consorts. Please, prepare a pitcher of water, a vodka, and a virgin strawberry daquiri for our refreshment. Thank you.' The disheveled bartender, called Longshanks by the regular patron for no particular reason, wiped a beer mug dry. He seemed to ignore Oxios. 'Excuse me, sir?' Van tried again. No response. Finally, he broke character with, 'Yo, pops!' Longshanks casually looked up from his cleaning. 'What's happening?' Oxios snapped his fingers. 'Make with the drinks.' The unattentive Longshanks had a tattoo on his left forearm. It was a clock, sans any hands. A common prison tattoo; one loses all sense of time when isolated from the rest of the world. On the other arm, however, was something more inspiring of pride: a Marine tattoo. The stout old man, still having a head of black hair to top a hard head and frame a taut face, displayed both of them as he reached underneath the counter to get the glasses. 'That'll be $16.75,' he said bluntly. Vangellus scratched his chin. 'Perhaps we can haggle?' 'Haggle?' he arched his brow. 'Yeah,' Oxios nodded his head with a smile. 'I try to convince you to lower your price by giving you a counter-offer, then it goes back and forth like that until we agree on a price.' '$16.75, and that's final.' 'Come on, they're haggled in Morocco for centuries.' 'And it's made them the economic powerhouse they are today.' Vangellus met his match in the man in the torn and faded Grateful Dead shirt. Without another word, he handed the man a $50. Back at the table, Adrienne stretched out her arms over her head. She needed the tension release, dealing with travel and having to manage Vangellus's career over the phone all day. Interviews, personal appearances, commercials... it was all a lot to juggle. Oxios couldn't do it himself. He didn't do it at all; that's what he had the much more capable Diamond for. 'I'm surprised you're still awake,' Emi commented, arms folded with her wooden sword resting horizontally between the two seats at the booth. 'You've been up all day.' A deep breath that almost brings a yawn. Adrienne watches the cars pass by on the highway only a hundred yards away. 'Comes with the job. There's a lot of renewed interest in Van now that he's a champion again.' 'Yes,' Teshigahara nodded. 'I will admit he seems to be back in fighting form now.' She folded her arms over the table, leaning forward to rest her weight. 'It was a great idea going to Scott Matthews for a tag team. It's Van's best idea since I started working for him.' Emi he was looking out the window herself, but was more fascinated by the moon in the sky than the manmade contraptions rolling about below. 'I'd been meaning to ask you. How did you meet him?' Adrienne remembered that day. It was the first real job interview she'd had. And it paid off for her in spades. With a smile on her face same as the day they'd met, she recounted her story. It was three years ago. I was attending New York University in the northeast with a major in marketing and advertisement. Very big school, pretty well known. I'd tried to get along without a higher education for a couple of years after high school, but it wasn't working for me. It was career day. Normally there are a bunch of companies who come to these things. They set up little tables for themselves, have packages to tell you all the great things about their company. Why you should come work for them. That kind of thing. And it was the same. Xerox. American Airlines. Dell. All the giants of industry. I was just wasting time there after I'd seen all the other tables, looked at what the companies had to offer. Really it was more about what we could offer them. No big deal, they're all like that. As I was eating my lunch, I noticed another table kinda off to the side. There was one person sitting there, and he just had his resume there, and a bunch of pictures of himself. Even a VCR/television set up so he could show us clips of him in action. I passed by like four times, wasn't really interested. But I was done by that point, so I stopped by and checked it out. Van was marketing himself. Looking for a manager. Thing that sold poor little me was that he wrote me a check on the spot. And I've been working for him ever since. Vangellus returned with their drinks. 'Hey, we were just talking about you,' Diamond said. Vangellus responded, 'So that's why my ass itches.' Back again. Thought you were rid of me, eh? Well, too bad. It occured to me that if I was going to talk about things concerning myself and my team that I might as well do it a little more focused on this week. After all, if you only talk about the past people get the impression that you're not aware that you live in the present. It's the week before King of Kings, the next pay-per-view on the PRIME schedule. This will probably be the best PPV we've had since Tropical Turmoil. In fact, I know it will. Give that a moment to sink in. Yeah, it's a bad joke. Sue me. The card's going to feature many attractions. Killean Sirrajin and Matt Ward (I refuse to call him 'Tchu') will finally wrestle each other. They've got a long history together, and it'll all come to a fore soon enough. Thankfully for that, since focus group polling indicates that approximately 16% of the audience gives a damn about those two. At least they haven't dominated the programming with their tripe. It gives the people time to worry about the important things. Like deciphering Psymon's promos. There have been some people asking questions. I hear the fans talk. They're unsure about what's going to happen with a certain someone. Well, I did some checking, and rest assured: Karina Wolfenden will be the same anti-social jackass that she's always been since RPW. She may look down and out like she's been for the past month or so, but to liven her up for a moment just walk up to her in a real friendly voice and ask, 'How's Angelo?' If you're not a wrestler, you could probably get a nice lawsuit out of the impending ass-kicking. Hoyt Williams might finally wrestle a title match; if he loses, Brandon Youngblood and I are starting a petition to have his title reign counted at zero days... except in Youngblood's case he's saying WHEN Hoyt loses. And for everyone who's foretelling the end of the world, mark your calendars: the beginning of the end starts today for the fact that BY and I can agree on something. And, at King of Kings, there will be three matches regarding the Jewel in the Crown tournament. Two semi-finals matches and the final. I'm not much for predictions. No, not for that dumbass Yogi Berra saying. I just don't like to leave something unfulfilled. I don't like failing to meet a goal. That's why most of the time I don't talk about winning a match. Then I won't have had anything to live up to going in (and so nothing to have failed to do if I lose). I'm cool like that. But you know what? I'm going to lay claim to one of those matches. Now, fact is, out of the pairings, I really don't care about Aimz/Santos and Sirrajin/Ferguson. No matter who wins isn't a concern for me right now. But I will say this. We're going to see one of the semi-finals matches as Vangellus Oxios against Karina Wolfenden. I say this with a pretty good amount of certainty. I've looked at it, on paper, past matches, so forth. That's how it's going to turn out. K-Wolf... yeah, she is a bit of a prick. Her trendy outfits and criminally-pink hairstyle has tricked the world into looking past the fact that she's got the charisma of a jar of peanut butter. No one, not even Karina herself, would deny that if held at gunpoint and forced to hold a normal conversation in order to save her life, she'd ask for the bullet in the skull so as not to get blood on her outfit. Still, she does win matches. Much more often than not. Paul Cain is good, but he hasn't shown me that special something yet. And as for me, well, I'm coming up. I've hit a hot streak, I tell you. Not since Rush Pro Wrestling have I felt this good and this confident in my ability. After giving away the first round victory, I come back in the Second Chance battle royale. Right there, that was the first turning point for me. I survived. And not just by being a bom-ass fighter. Look back at it closely. I was ejected from the ring. Twice. Both times, I stayed in the match by using my head. First time by using the guys I was thrown down onto as a stepping stones (literally) to get back into the ring. My feet didn't touch the floor, and I knew that. So why roll off of them and in turn eliminate myself when I could stay on them and get up, coming right back into the match? And then in the second case, well... I apologize to the referee for misting him in the eyes (and thanks to Nerezza for having a misting attack in his repertoire so that I could make better use of it than he ever did), but it gave me a litle moment to relax. And Chris Collins... well, he can bite my glorious golden ass if he thinks that he accomplished anything in that contest. That was step one. Step two came the week after. I was in a match with Mark Blackwolf. Now, earlier in that show two weeks ago that saw me winning TWO matches (you can't see me, so take my word that I'm doing a pelvic thrust right now), I called out Blackwolf for pretty much no reason. Just an excuse to talk about someone. I was emulating Deville at the time, and he did that all the time. Whoever's booking must've thought that it was money to go with that (Man Sucks Wang... how could he NOT have wanted to punch my face in after that?). So when we both survived in the second chance match, it was booked. Vangellus Oxios versus Mark Blackwolf. The time before, I didn't really watch Blackwolf. I knew I didn't have to. It's because of his background. What am I? A wrestler? Well, by occupation. But by style? Definitely a fighter. If it's one thing I know, it's fighting. That's what I was doing before this. That's how I got my start, that's how I got famous. I was winning bouts, and not just in my own weight class. Mark Blackwolf is big. Too big. At 6'11' and 400 pounds, he's got it at his fault that his attack base is too high, his movements are too slow, and his fighting style really doesn't suit a guy his size. And I proved it. Not just that, but I proved my own strength. Blackwolf outweighs me by a whole other person, and that person would have a weight advantage over me. Watch that match and you'll see two things. First is a guy at ringside in a blue shirt getting head from his girlfriend. I can't believe that they picked that time and place for it, but they were ejected afterward. Second, you'll see me; scrawny little Oxios, taking it right to the UFC fighter. You've got to understand. I'm seen as the weak link in my team. Scott 'Rampage' Matthews. He's 6'6' and 350 pounds. People are going to look at him and call him strong and tough just by seeing him. It was different for me. I'm 6'4', but only 185 pounds. I'm one gangly-looking muthafugga, I'll admit. So it's different for me. Someone like me, I'm supposed to go out there and outspeed the guy. I'm supposed to be bouncing off the ropes every which way and landing triple backflip corkscrew dropkicks before finishing him off with a 900 Splash. That's not what happened, though. People who didn't know what I can do had to be shocked when they saw me walk out there and beat Blackwolf senseless. I knocked him down and out with my fists. He was supposed to be the harder hitter. Maybe he was. But he dropped first. Him. The bigger man. I take that as a good thing. Really, I did fall out of that tag match way too quickly. I fault myself for that. No big deal, we still won the match. But I don't want people to think of Scott as carrying me. I'm not helpless. Not by a longshot. So it moves into this week. I'm facing Matt Ward. They say that he has a good chance to win it. Very good, based on only a handful of matches in PRIME. And the hype seems to focus around the reason why he's called 'The Inhuman Being.' Sumbitch is tough. He almost doesn't feel pain. Drop him with a suplex and he'll shrug it off like it's nothing. The guy walks through the kind of offense that his opponents try to toss at him. I should be worried. But I'm not. Simple reason for that, going back to what I am. I'm a fighter. I don't care about making Ward feel whatever I do. Purpose isn't to get him to scream 'Ouch!' That's what a wrestler does. They try suplexes and powerbombs and all that stuff. They try to submit people. And why? Because the victim is supposed to be so disheartened by all the pain they suffer that they just can't take it anymore. They CHOOSE to lose. Ward is supposed to be able to keep that from happening. He's supposed to ignore the pain. He can't choose to lose. Losing isn't an option for him. All I have to say is: So? I'm not interested in inflicting pain. I'm just going to knock him out. If he's got dead nerves and can't feel the hits, that doesn't matter. Unaffected by pain isn't the same thing as invulnerable or invincible, and he sure as hell doesn't have to make the mental decision to acknowledge that he's knocked out for it to be so. Vangellus Oxios. Matt Ward. ReVolution 79. The Inhuman Being is going to find out a very important fact of life: if it's awake, it can be laid to sleep. Forcefully.
