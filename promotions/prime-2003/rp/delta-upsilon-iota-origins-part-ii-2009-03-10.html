Delta Upsilon Iota: Origins (Part II)

10 March 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March 10th, 2009 Tallahassee, Florida April 19th, 2002 "You touch me again, I swear to God, I'll break your fucking neck, little man." The stern conveyor of such ominous assurances occupies almost the entirety of the double-wide door frame, his almost inhumanly broad back effectively blocking the respective views of the belligerent masses gathered outside. Without full comprehension of the events transpiring within, the crowd grows increasingly restless, anxious to perceive both the source of and solution to their continued outdoor purgatory. As the sounds of ricocheting beer pong balls, flipping plastic cups and crumpling aluminum cans spill out of the crudely shuttered windows, a distinctly condescending voice sounds forth from beyond the hulking behemoth. "I've broken my neck before, and truth be told it really isn't that big of a deal. Which is good, because it turns out that your mom really likes it when I pop my neck out of joint and do that little thing with my tongue." "Go fuck yourself, Korver." "That's Plan B, asshole. Which is unfortunately becoming more and more likely the longer I have the fucking missing link dominating my front door. It's going to be sixth grade all over again, thanks to you." "The fuck are you going to do about it?" "Well, initially I'm just going to try to talk you down, but I have my doubts whether or not that strategy will bear any fruit. If my appeal to your innate sense of decency indeed falls on deaf, or simply ignorant, ears, then I will be forced to lower myself to your level and rely on the only means of communication by which I am guaranteed to convey my message." "And what message would that be, frat fuck?" CRACK Dozens of wood fragments cascade down over the bulging traps and shoulders of the lumbering behemoth as his body goes completely stiff, sending his gigantic frame toppling backward onto the throng of underclassmen crowded behind. As the dust settles on the newly horizontal and wholly unconscious mass of humanity, a lone figure replaces him in the doorway. And the splintered fraternity paddle in his right hand ties a pretty neat little bow around the proceedings. "Class of '83. Good year." Tossing the paddle at his fallen adversary's feet, Korver nods his head, signaling the commencement of cleanup duty from the herd of nearby pledges at his right. As all evidence of earlier physicality is hastily removed, the vacancy left by the halved paddle is quickly filled in the form of a twenty-four ounce can of Busch Light. "Much appreciated Pledge Simpson, you may not end up a Phi Delta Theta after all. Nice work." "Thank you, Brother Korver. But it wasn't really my idea, sir." "So then whose was it? Or are you just skipping around campus, giving all of our beer away like a certified 'Let me win. But if I cannot win, let me be brave in the attempt.' retard?" "Actually, sir, it was that big guy over there." "Knock the motherfucker out and he sends you a beer? Unbelievable." "Not that one, sir, the one over there in the cowboy hat." As the petrified freshman points him in the right direction, Korver turns his gaze to a shadowy figure leaning against the far wall, smoke drifting upward from beneath his black ten gallon Stetson. Sending the pledge on to more menial tasks, Korver makes his way across the yard, quickly arriving at his intended destination. "I'm not sure how I feel about random old guys sending me alcohol. No offense." "None taken. But I ain't nearly as random as you think I am. In fact, me being here ain't random at all. You see, you and I have a lot to talk about, Mr. Korver." "So says the motherfucking Marlboro Man?" "Not quite, hoss." Exhaling a final cloud of thick smoke, the man tilts his hat back to reveal his previously shielded visage. "So says Duke Williams."
