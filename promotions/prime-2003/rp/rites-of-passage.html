Date: This week From the morning edition of the Charlotte Observer, July 16, 2005 OBITUARIESMarsha Gibson, 59, died after a lengthy illness. She is survived by one son, David. A wake will be held at Northwest Funeral Home beginning at 6 p.m. followed by a funeral service the next morning at 10 a.m. You broke your share of promises over the years. You promised Clay Vendetta you would always stick by him. You promised loyalty and monogamy to various girlfriends. You promised yourself you’d become one of the biggest stars in wrestling. But in the end, at least they can say you upheld the promise you made your mother. “Don’t you dare do anything like that when I die,” she’d say whenever she’d just been to a funeral—an occasion which became more and more frequent in the last five years of her life; her family and friends were notoriously hard-living people. “Big funerals like that? I don’t see the need. They say it’s to honor the dead? Well honey, it don’t matter if you honor me or not then; I’d rather folks do it while I was still around to appreciate it.” “Mr. Gibson?” You jerk back awake to see the middle-aged woman in business attire leaning over you with a concerned look on her face. “Are you all right, sir?” “Yeah… yeah, fine.” But really, you’re not fine. You’ve barely slept or eaten for weeks now—-aside from one 20-hour hibernation following your victory over Brandon Youngblood (and how’s THAT one for all the dirt sheet writers who said you were washed up?) After waking up you flew right back to Charlotte, content to orbit around the bedside of your mother and wait for her to die. Hour after hour, you sat in the ancient recliner by the bed—became so familiar with the piece of furniture that you came to recognize each crack in the upholstery, every creak from the chair as you moved. You waited, and continued to wait. Your business became an afterthought. You handed over your basic classes at the wrestling school to your two best students, and canceled advanced classes indefinitely. A couple of guys—-spot monkeys who probably went home and watched Chris Benoit matches before they jacked off-—demanded their money back. You paid them. Hell, what do you care? It’s only money, and you’ll have plenty of it now that you don’t have to pay the lady sitting across from you any more. “We’ve taken care of everything,” the hospice director said. “As you can imagine we deal with this situation quite often. However, there was a question regarding the personal effects?” “I’ll take the pictures that were in the room. Donate the rest to Goodwill.” “Very good, sir,” she said. “Now if you’ll just sign here…” You scrawl your name and get up, feeling the lurch in your belly and a sudden sense of vertigo. You right yourself quickly, but you realize the woman is eyeballing you. She thinks you’re drunk. You know better. Right now, being drunk would be a lot more fun. But you had your chance, didn’t you? Had your chance to numb your sorrows in the finest painkillers Nova could afford. I guess there’s something to be said for progress. “And Dave? Don’t you go wastin’ money on some big fancy coffin either. No point in it. Might as well cremate me…” “Mr. Gibson, I’m sorry for your loss.” You grumble some sort of acknowledgment as you walk down the hallway of your mother’s Methodist church with the clergyman. “No matter how good or bad a life we lead, each of us must take that trip to face final judgment,” the preacher opined. “All we can do is take solace in the knowledge that this is part of God’s plan—just like everything else.” God’s plan… Yes, well that explains everything, doesn’t it? The Supreme Being glanced down at the kindest and finest person you’d ever known, took away her lungs, raped her mind and took away everything that she loved, THEN let her hang on the precipice of death for a week and a half before finally cutting the string. Sounds like a heck of a plan, all right… “I’ll have the eulogy fully prepared,” the minister said in assurance. “Glad to hear it.” You’re almost to the door, and you’ve got the handle in your grasp when… “A-HEM, there is one other matter, though.” You turn around, eyebrows cocked inquisitively, to see the preacher adjusting the glasses on the bridge of his nose. “As you know your mother believed in the tradition of tithing for her church. Ten percent became custom. Considering her length of membership here, I was only curious… did she make any allowances in her will regarding her wishes for the church?” Try as you might-—and admittedly, you don’t try hard—-you find yourself unable to replace a laugh. It comes out like a harsh bark without an ounce of humor to it. You’re still laughing as you leave the minister standing there, still awaiting an answer that he hopes will swell his coffers. Your mother worked for years as a seamstress, and picked up odd jobs on the side whenever things got tough. There was never much, but at least you always had food on the table. Meanwhile she was still able to scrimp and save, taking 10 cents out of every dollar she ever made and putting it in the bank. Once the Alzheimer’s became full blown, you went through everything in a matter of months. Forget sports cars, Armani suits or narcotics. No lifestyle is more expensive than that of an American that requires continuous medical care. “Whatever you do, don’t make my wake such a somber affair. At least put some food there for people to eat. Nothing helps people grieve like filling their bellies together, hehehe” “Your mother was a good woman.” The man speaking to you right now is wearing a suit that smells like mothballs. You extend your hand and clasp his, mumble a “thank you” and send him on his way. No one ever told you about this part. Then again, unless you’ve had a close family member die, you figure no one ever really knows about it. It’s the night of your mother’s wake, and judging from the line of people, it looks like a buffet of the bereaved, and you’re the main course. There’s no coffin, just a big photo collage of some of the best shots of your mom you could find. You tried to pick as many as you could where she was smiling, you always liked her smile. And it was something to keep you occupied. So what if you went another night without sleep? They continue on, an unending parade of people in dark colors—mostly elderly folks from church or your mom’s old bridge club or the mail specialty shop where she worked every May and December to make some extra money. You know very few of them, although they’re all very eager to make sure you know the relationship. “I went to high school with your mother, David. She was a fine woman. I know it’s hard right now, but this was her time.” “Well look how big you’ve grown! I lived next door to you and your mom back when you were just a kid. Take heart, son. She’s in a better place now…” “Sorry about your mom, Dave. Listen, can I get an autograph?” Damn smarks. How’d they find this place any way? The fan’s attempt to repeat his request is cut short as a meaty brown-skinned hand grabs him by his blue polo shirt and lifts him off the ground. Azala tucks the guy under one arm and heads for the door. “Fans leave Dave alone now,” you hear Zameer say. “No autographs!” he adds, wagging one finger ominously at a little blue-haired lady busy making the sign of the cross. You enjoy the murmur that goes through the line as they see the Snack Daddy play at crowd control, before returning to the substantial catering spread you paid for—or what’s left of it. Azala showed up to pay his respects, and stood by you for the first few well-wishers. (“That’s what being good partner means!” he said with pride) But you saw the way he was eyeing the deviled eggs at the back. “Listen pal, why not go get something to eat?” By the time you got to “to eat” he was already working on a full plate. Hey, your mother loved food. She’d be happy to see Azala chowing down. Part of you wonders at why specifically she’d request a catering spread for her wake. It’s not like she knew you’d be professionally and personally paired with a gigantic African who carried a passion for all things deep-fried, deviled, glazed and cheese covered. Did she? Motion at the corner of your eye ceases your reflections. Across the room, you couldn't help but notice a growing clearing in the crowd of family and friends. What’s this now? You step forward from the line only to see a streak of bright red. You’re disturbed by the reaction—-a combination of rage and elation swells within you—-and turn away from the line immediately as that same little blue-haired lady reaches for your hand. “So sorry about Marsha, dear,” she says, before leaning closere. “That large Negro at the back… I could call the police if you want.” “That’s fine, ma’am, he’s a friend of mine.” Frowning, she continues on. 'Hi.' You stiffen—no not like that! Fucking pervert—and turn to face Amy. 'I'm just here to pay my respects. You know how much I fucking hate these things, so don't give me hell for it. I'm doing it because she was a good woman the few times I'd met her and her son USED to be my best friend, not because I'm twisted enough to think you'd want me here.' Everything about her—-the outfit, her body language and sweet Jews for Jesus are those CHOPSTICKS in her damn hair?-—screams uncomfortable. You know how much she hates funerals. Despite all that’s happened lately, you realize what you must mean for her to come here tonight. And suddenly, that makes you realize exactly what you’ve lost. 'I... I really miss her, Amy. She's all I've got.' 'I know how it feels. Err... uh, not my MOM, per se - but I've lost a lot of friends. More than most people. But this isn't about me, it's about her. Uhh, I'm sorry? I don't know what the hell to say to someone who's lost that. And I'm not going to cry, because that's just not what I do... but you know how I feel. I guess I am sorry. I would've taken this sooner than wishing it upon you.' You can see the concern in her face, and you can understand why. You’re all too aware of the dark circles under your eyes from too many consecutive nights of little or no sleep. Your diet has ranged from not eating at all to eating pure crap—well not ACTUAL crap, unless you consider that one night you had pork rinds and Mountain Dew for dinner. And how long has it exactly been since you’d been to a gym? Even your own? But right now, even that didn’t matter. Your eyes are starting to burn. Oh Christ, not here. Not in front of all these strangers. 'I can't...' Get it together, dammit! 'I've got to get out of here, just for a minute. I can't handle any more of this right now...' 'C'mere.' A pair of slender arms wrap around you in a tight hug. 'Let's get outta here for a sec, big bro.' She takes you by the hand and leads you into the parking lot and away from the door, where a few of mom's old friends are lingering, swapping stories and smoking. Cigarettes at a wake for a woman with severe emphysema. Nice touch. If somebody gets shot walking home from the movies you don’t see people pulling out pistols at the funeral, do you? Now you’ve got Amy, and you’re leading her through the parking lot, threading through the parked cars, just trying to get away, away from the funeral home, away from the pictures of your mother and away from her but she’s not there anymore is she? She’s gone, burnt to a crisp, and the only person in this whole world who loved you unconditionally has been reduced to a cloud of dust lying in the cheapest urn available. You stop. And you start to cry. REALLY cry, for the first time since that night in the hospital chapel, for the first time since she died. Through the blur of your vision, you can see Amy bite her lip as you crumble to the pavement, grabbing at your stomach and sobbing uncontrollably. She makes no move to comfort you. She just stands there, fists clenched--as if ready to defend him from anyone who dares to wander into the scene--and lets you finish. Deep down, you love her for that. 'What am I going to do... She was all I had. My only family... The only person in this whole world who gave a damn about me. And now she's gone. I've lost them all. Clay, my mom, every girlfriend I ever had... and you.' 'She wasn't the ONLY person who cares about you, Dave. That's ridiculous to say. So many people do, even if you don't know it. I know I do, no matter how hard we fight.' The flow of tears stops. There’s snot dribbling from one nostril, your eyes are bloodshot, you’ve gotten your best pair of pants filthy and yet, somehow, you strangely feel better after the cathartic cry. It feels good to let go, doesn’t it? And speaking of letting go… 'Amy, about what happened last month... I thought--well, I didn't know what I thought. I was confused... confused about you and me, about what I wanted. I'm still confused. But when I walked in and saw... that... I'm sorry. I'm sorry for the way I reacted.' It feels good to get that off your chest. And when you see the look on Amy’s face, you realize she needed to hear it. You realize that you need her—not as a lover, just in your life whatever that means. Maybe that’s the one good thing that can come from this whole mess. “Amy!” a smooth baritone voice calls out across the parking lot. Instantly, you feel a flush come over your face. Because, even before you turn around, you know the voice. “Oh shit,” you hear Amy mutter as you turn to see Killean Sirrajin approaching in all black, wearing an open-collared shirt and an Armani sportcoat. 'Sorry,' he came up from behind, planting a kiss on her cheek, 'I had trouble finding the place.' “YOU! WHAT THE FUCK ARE YOU DOING HERE?!” One small part of your brain registers surprise at seeing the Supreme Machine stop dead in his tracks. There’s no wariness in his eyes—no rage at the words spewing out of your mouth. Just alarm. The rest of your brain has given over to pure adrenaline. Your body seems to act of its own volition as you shed your sportcoat, dropping it carelessly on the asphalt, and approach Sirrajin with murder in your eyes. “Dave, he’s with me, OK?” you hear Amy call out behind you. “But he isn’t WELCOME. You should know I wouldn’t want him here.” Now you’re nose to nose. “That’s just like you, isn’t it? I’m Killean Sirrajin and I can go wherever the fuck I want. WELL NOT WITH ME! YOU CAN’T COME IN HERE, WHILE MY MOTHER IS BEING BURIED, AND PULL THIS SHIT! UNDERSTAND?” Sirrajin shakes his head sadly. “Do you think this is the best way to honor your mother, Dave?” You let loose with a howl, and you’re not quite sure where it comes from… somewhere deep inside you, letting loose all the remaining anger and sadness and shock and dismay you’ve repressed since you got the call. You take a swing at Sirrajin, who blocks it and puts you—firmly but not with all his strength—facedown against the hood of the nearest car. “STOP IT!” You stand up when he releases like a dog called on by its owner but before your reflexes set in for the fight back, Amy's between you both. 'He's with me, okay? And he'll probably continue to be with me. If you can't accept that, then you aren't the friend I thought you were. You two have never done anything to one another, and I don't WANT you to have reason to hate each other.' She looks at Killean with the same ferociousness she's casting your way. At least he's getting shit, too... right? 'And if EITHER of you touch the other, I'll kill the both of you. Got it? I don't want to see grown men acting like little boys in a high school cafeteria. Dave, we'll leave. I hope you can see past your fucking iron curtain and realize that I meant no disrespect or harm by having him come today. I think you should appreciate the fact that he wanted to come to respect the loss of a fellow wrestler, not just as my fucking lap dog... but whatever, it's your choice. I'm gone.' That said, she turns and starts a speedy trek back to her car. Killean's glare lingers on you, but he isn't far behind when she flees the scene and soon enough, you see the 'Happy Couple' arguing in the space between their parked cars. “Dave, I’ve thought and thought about your question. And truth be told, I don’t have an answer. Not a full one anyway. I just want you to dispose of my ashes the way you think is best. After all, no one knows me better than you.” You wobble a bit as you walk into the ocean until it’s up to your knees. The sun feels good on your bare chest, arms and shoulders as you gaze out onto the crystal blue water. Behind you stands the lush green landscape of what most people would call paradise: Oahu, the largest of the Hawaiian islands. Your mother always wanted to go to Hawaii. Yet at the same time, she was afraid to. She’d heard of people who went and wept upon departure—-knowing they’d never be back there again. Now, she’d get to stay. You tip the urn over, pouring the ashes out into the shallows of the water. You watch as some are taken back out to sea, while the others are mixed in with the sand underneath your bare feet. A friendly hand claps you on the shoulder. “You OK?” You nod and turn to face Jeffrey Wayne, wearing only a pair of cargo shorts. “Thank you for this. I think she would’ve liked it.” “Happy to help any way I can, Dave. You know that. Sorry I couldn’t make it to the wake. Brody had this bad cough, and I didn’t feel right leaving.” “I understand, man. You’ve got a family to take care of…” “Hey, I didn’t mean to imply—” “I know you didn’t.” You pat Jeff on the back and turn to face the water. “I wish you’d decide to stay another day. I know you need to get some workouts in before Colossus II, but I’ve got a gym here… Plus, it might do you some good. Get out of Charlotte for a while?” “Yeah, but there’s one more thing I have to do.” ”No matter what Dave, I’ve led a good life. I was never glamorous or famous but I always do my best to help people. And I love my work. I know most folks might thumb their nose at it but I always enjoyed what I did. Being a seamstress, working with my hands, creating something from nothing. Remember that, son. It doesn’t matter how much money you make. All that matters is that you do what you love, and live how you want.” A trunk sits before you on the floor of your shitty apartment. Inside is your mother’s legacy—-clothes she made but never sold, not because she didn’t want to but because she liked them enough to hold onto them. Truth be told, you DO love what you do. Even with all the pain and trouble it’s brought you. Through the concussions, the lost blood, the hours on the road, countless meals of eggs and hash browns at Waffle House at 3 in the morning, two broken necks, backstage politics, unions, betrayals, beatings, title reigns, wins and losses, you wouldn’t trade a second of it. Already, the memories are starting to grow a little distant. After all you lost her a long time ago when her mind went. The eyes that were once so bright and vibrant went dull and lifeless. The laugh that could fill a room was now a long-forgotten echo. But you would honor her the only way you knew how. You open the trunk and find them, rubbing your fingers across the smooth fabric. Before you left to make your pro debut, your mom worked in secret on your ring attire. Plain black trunks, extending to knee level, with “Old School Dave Gibson” emblazoned on the back in electric blue. You never got to wear them. That was the night “Sexy” Nick Stevenson decided to work his booking magic, and dress you up as the Redneck Ninja, and the first time you crossed a promoter—-but not the last. By the time your real debut came around, the tights sadly had been left behind. That night an IOW scout came out, and slicker, fancier ring attire was commissioned. Then, it was the tattered blue jean shorts at the beginning of your run in OSW. And as the matches piled up, you forgot. Until now. Until Colossus II. Silently, you vow to honor her-—her skills, her gift and her love-—for the rest of your career. And it would all start at Fallen Halo. Written in blood--yours or your opponents’, it didn’t matter. And punctuated by the dull wet snap of bones breaking in your grasp. Mom, I want to be a wrestler… “Like those guys on TV?” “Yup!” “Like that Flair fella?” 'That's right.' Wariness on her face is replaced by a wry smile. “He’s kinda cute in those tights.” “MOOOOM!” “Hehehehehehe.”
