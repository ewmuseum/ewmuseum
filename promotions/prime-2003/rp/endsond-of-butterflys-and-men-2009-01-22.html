Endsond - Of butterflys and men

22 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 22nd January 2009 Hawaii He tried to scream. His mouth stretched open - wider than should have been possible with teeth showing like perfect white cliffs in a red ocean – a gaping maw of frustrated agony as no sound emerged. He could feel fear become terror and terror become – something else, something more. His body stiffened, tightened, each and every muscle readying itself to fight or flee. He didn’t know which it would be; he didn’t know why he would need to. He just knew without knowing that his body wanted to do something. Anything. Ice filled his veins where once blood flowed. His spine and knees turned to jelly and he knew that, had he been standing, he would now be lying prone. As it was, as he was already lying prone, he curled – foetal like – and cried salt tears as he waited for it to end. End? What to end? Finally, with supreme effort, he whimpered. Nothing much, barely audible even if anyone were listening, but something. A newborn babe, taking its first breath, would make more noise. An old man, breathing his last, would make more noise. The whimper, almost no noise at all, brought him to his senses and let him know where he was – who he was – and what he was. The question – what had to end - saved him; the question woke him. In knowing that he was scared, in knowing that he was terrified, and in knowing that he asked himself the simple question of ‘why’ he realised that he was dreaming. In so doing he awoke. Eyes rimmed with tears and sleep were heavy and slow to open; a rainbow of muted colours obscured his vision as he struggled to see once more, to escape – fully – from the nightmare that had held him and escape to … … where? Taking a gulp of air in through both his open mouth and his mucus filled nostrils he tried to draw the breath deep into his lungs – to fill them completely – and clear the cobwebs from his mind and memory. Sitting up, slowly, arms clenched around his knees he looked around him. The sheets on the bed were crumpled, dishevelled – they looked how he felt. Clothes scattered on the floor around him. The light curtain across the window barely moved in the still night air but still, despite the sanctity and security of where he was he didn’t feel safe, didn’t feel secure. His body jumped, he gulped back a scream before it could become anything more than a whimper, as the door opened and light streamed in. Silhouetted in the door, limned in light like an angel in all its glory, he stood there for a second that lasted a lifetime. The sheets were crumpled in a clenched fist, tears starting to fall unchecked as the angel – as he moved swiftly, assuredly, to the bed. Arms wrapped around a shivering body, skin to skin. Fear ran, chased away by something stronger until all that was left was that which was always there. The emptiness. The despair. The memory. "Jay," he said, words warm on my ear, "It’s ok, it was just the dream again. I am here." * * * Detroit They say that bad things come in three, don’t they? I mean I am not even sure who ‘they’ are but I know that I have heard that saying. Me, personally, I have never had an issue with the number three so don’t know where or when – in the dim and distant history where myth becomes folklore becomes truth – three became such an evil number but there you have it. Right now, in this moment, I may have found out why three is such a bitch. My best friend in the whole World, someone who means more to me than life or death, just changed things – everything – our World – by a simple form of three. Three things. Three words. I. Love. You. It only happens in bad movies or even worse novels, doesn’t it? Boy meets girl, boy and girl flirt around the edges, one declares undying love to the other and the other – normally the boy, I mean we are jerks after all – doesn’t know what to say or says it too late or – worst case scenario here – doesn’t say anything at all. … for the record, ‘ditto’ doesn’t count! I always thought that, in real life, it never really happened. Surely if someone loved you enough to tell you that they loved you then you would know how they felt without the words and, if it was real, you would feel it too. You would know that you loved them just as much. Words wouldn’t be needed. Would they? Then again, in the real World or in the movies, you didn’t normally have two guys – best of friends, close as brothers – sitting in a hotel room after one had just asked to die. You definitely didn’t have those two guys – did I mention best of friends and as close as brothers – sitting there while one tells the other that he loves him. Love. That is in interesting word. You can love hotdogs – they can be your favourite food. You can love long walks and moonlight serenades. You can love Sinatra. You can love your parents, your dog, your Country, your job. You can love your friends. You can love your best friend. I know. I loved – I love Rick. I truly do. I have always known that. He has been there for me since – well since forever, basically. I was there for him. He was there for me. That is what best friends do. Now, I know that our relationship was – is – different. He was brought into a World that isn’t exactly normal. First of all the whole ‘living on a reservation’ deal isn’t for everyone. Not even my People – a lot of us just pick up, move out, and live in the real World. Some of us – like me and my Grandfather – not only lived as far removed from that ‘real’ World as possible but stepped our toes into the pool of the ‘other’ Worlds from time to time. Being brought up as a medicine man – or Shaman – will do that for you. Yes, magic, the spirit World, good, evil, the whole shebang. It’s real. … the Easter bunny, however, isn’t. Sorry. Rick had stuck with me through all of that. He had stuck with me in the crazy business called ‘show’ … or more exactly wrestling. Same thing, really. He had always been there. … even when my life was ripped away, my heart – body – mind – soul – all taken from me and I was raped from the inside out by a malevolent spirit that wore me like a glove and played my life like a cheap tune on a cheaper piano. Even then Rick was with me – or at least he was looking for me. If it wasn’t for him I would still be someone else. I would still be Ember. Thanks to him Ember is gone. Dead. And I am not. I am alive. Alive. He thinks that he did me a favour, then, by saving me. By giving ‘me’ back to ‘me’. I should be grateful, I know that I should. The thing is that when Ember had me I didn’t know who I was or what was happening. It was like being in a dark room, with my eyes closed. I couldn’t see. I couldn’t hear. I couldn’t feel. I didn’t know. Now? Now I know. Now I remember everything that he did with my body. Everything that he did to my body. Everything he did in my body. I remember it all. I can’t forget. Three minutes ago the man who never gave up on me – the man who I love more than life itself – told me that he loves me. … and I know that he doesn’t mean it in the way that two guys would normally say it – there are no hearty backslaps and shoulder hugs here. He doesn’t say it like two guys in a Hollywood movie would say it. Yeah, I know that there is that movie, ok, but there is no way in Hell I am going to compare myself to a cowboy. I am the ‘good guy’ remember – the Indian! He said he loved me. He looked into my eyes and saw my soul. He opened himself and gave himself. To me. Three little words. … and I choked. I looked, I stared. I saw his offer, I saw his soul. I took it, ripped it, screwed it up and gave it back to him. Broken, torn and ruined. I watched as hope died and realisation sat in. All I had to do was look inside myself – see my own soul – and realise that perhaps, just possibly, I had always known how he felt because I felt the same way. He wasn’t just my best friend, almost a brother. He was my everything, my soul, my beginning and my end. … but, if I had looked into my soul, if I had wanted to give it to him, I would have seen the truth and had three different words to give him that would sum up the real ‘me’ that he had saved. That he had brought back. Broken. Torn. Ruined. So I tried to do something, anything, to console him – as he had always consoled me – and didn’t. I simply didn’t do anything to ease him. I chose three very different words. "Kill me. Please." * * * Hawaii "I don’t know," I whispered into his shoulder, tears streaming down his skin and evaporating into steam with the heat of his body. "Am I a butterfly or a man?" Rick’s arms tightened around me, pulling me into his embrace as if by the simple strength of his arms he could chase away everything that assaulted my mind, everything that haunted my dreams and filled my heart with despair. I knew he was only trying to help me, I knew that I could even let him help me but – selfishly and stupidly – I pushed him away. Pain flickered across his eyes but I didn’t notice it – well, I pretended not to notice it just as he pretended, as quickly as he could, that nothing had happened. Sitting on the edge of the bed, light streaming across him from the open doorway, he fixed a small smile on his face as he watched me. "So, was it the dream again?" he repeated. "I don’t know," I mumbled, pulling the sheet up to my neck and wrapping my arms around my body, forming a cocoon of silk about me. "I don’t remember. I never do." The frown disappeared as fast as it appeared and he sighed. He knew I was lying. I knew I was lying. Still neither of us mentioned it. The first time it had happened, back in Detroit on the night he convinced me to live – or I decided, at least, not to die – he had lain there, fully clothed, and watched as the terrors of the night contorted my body. I awoke to see him holding me, shouting my name, and the truth spilled out. I told him what my memory had brought back – some of it, at least – and I saw the shock, the revulsion in his eyes. He had tried to tell me that it was just a nightmare, just fantasy brought to life in my head through the trauma. It wasn’t real, he had said, it couldn’t be. I knew he was lying. He knew it too. Since then I just didn’t say what my dreams brought me. I just prayed that they would stop. The day after the last night in Detroit he had brought me here, to this small island in Hawaii. He had said that it would help. He had said that we would – together – get through this. Three nights in and the nightmares were as bad as ever. "Can I get you anything?" I stared at him, knowing that I could answer and start to heal the rift between us or could answer and keep it still divided. He just needed a sign, anything, that I would accept his help – accept him – and he would give it. He would give it all. "… no." He didn’t even sigh. He even smiled. At me. For me. Even though I had twisted the knife deeper in his heart he smiled and tried to make me happy. Walking to the door he looked back. "What did you mean about the butterfly …" "I’m going to sleep." I cut him off and he closed the door. Quietly. In the darkness I sat and watched the night, waiting to fall asleep and dreading it when it came. Did it bring me nightmares and fractured memories or did it show me who I was … who I am? Was I the butterfly or was I the man?
