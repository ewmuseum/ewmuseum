The Mirrors of the Wolf

4 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4 November Kazys Jankauskas looked into the mirror at the foot of his hotel room bed. Staring back at him was a pair of eyes which, if not terribly dark in color, were fairly so in expression. His fingers went to straighten his tie, a dark black number with minuscule stripes of a delicate slate blue. In a suit like this, a man almost had to take himself seriously. It was rare that Kazys went out. He was doing so tonight only because he needed some time to think. And what better way to think than to fill yourself with alcohol? He grimaced, and his reflection in the mirror did the same. It wasn't the best logic in the world, but then, even something as stupid as binge-drinking before a match as big as the one he was about to be involved in was better than just sitting in your hotel room talking to your reflection. “Shit,” he swore under his breath, much as he did every time he thought about the situation. The truth was, Kaz thought to himself, he wasn't a singles wrestler. He didn't figure that was any great secret, though perhaps fewer people knew than he gave them credit for. It was a good mask, selling that title shot. A good mask to put over one's pride to say that you were selling the title shot for the money. It helped, too, that Elise had been so pissed off about it. It made good cover for the fact that wrestling on his own was possibly the most uncomfortable thing Kazys Jankauskas could think of to do. Reflecting back on it, he didn't know how the hell he'd gotten here, nor, sometimes, why he stayed. He'd signed up to be Elise's bodyguard, not her tag team partner. The lure of money had been enough to get him to sign up for the gig at PRIME in the first place – that, and Elise telling him that he'd be a natural at the sport if he only gave it a shot. His size, his already good physique... Kazys rolled his eyes. If nothing else, as Kaz had seen recently, Elise knew how to stroke a man's ego. In some small way, despite himself, he'd been had when it came to her convincing him to go forward with this. Only she hadn't told the whole story. The whole 'eventually, someone's gonna make you wrestle on your own' story. Kazys had never even really watched wrestling as a child. Had never had the opportunity. It was nice enough when you had a partner who had been absorbed in the business all her life, practically as a family duty. Elise knew strategy. Elise knew fundamentals. All Kazys knew was that if he followed Elise's general plan, only carried it through with his own finesse, things worked out really well. “Jewel in the Crown,” he muttered, sniffing, rolling his eyes. This wasn't his kind of thing. He didn't want this. But then, he hadn't wanted his cousin to die at the TV tower in Vilnius, either. And he hadn't wanted to ever see Daimon Vytautas again. Sometimes you didn't have a choice about what you wanted, and what was actually going to happen. He knew that well enough. The only thing to be done about it was to see it through somehow. To, as the Americans put it, “suck it up.” In this case, he was interpreting it literally, and deciding that the expression referred to a copious amount of vodka. ***** Rasputin's 11:10 PM Kazys wasn't particularly into the jet-set crowd. Why he'd come to a bar specifically aimed at invoking that feeling was beyond him, but he'd seen the place a few days ago, and suffered some morbid curiosity about the sort of bar that required classy attire. He'd never worked at one in Miami, which was obvious given his collection of embarrassing tropical-patterned shirts. A million bars in Miami, and he had to find the one that was hell-bent on embarrassing him by making him look like a giant mountain covered with yellow hibiscus. That was all out the window now. Now, he looked like a giant mountain covered in expensive fabric and Italian leather. Nobody said there weren't any perks to making a few good paychecks from this place, he reflected. There was a cute brunette next to him. Her hair looked redder than it probably was because of the bar's bloodred windows, further accented by the clean white lighting inside. She'd been fidgeting on her stool for about twenty minutes now, casting him glances every now and then that indicated her interest. In a way, it irritated him, the way that he felt he was a curiosity for most people who saw him- a very large man with a very funny accent. “Excuse me,” she said, in a lull between the loud sounds of people talking, “but that's a nice suit. I'm ah... betting you didn't buy it around here?” It was the most curious pickup line he'd ever heard. He tilted his head. “No... I don't live here, in any case. Been here for a few days.” Her accent was different from what he'd heard called the Southern drawl down in Florida. But then, people in New Orleans as a whole seemed of a different stripe than other southerners he'd met. She tilted her head as he spoke. “And you don't sound like you're from around here, either.” He shook his head, glancing over at her. She seemed rather interested in him, for someone who'd just sat next to a random stranger in a bar. “No. Miami once I moved here, and Vilnius before that.” Her brow furrowed. “Vilnius? Where's that?” She looked like she was having to concentrate awfully hard. He sighed. “Lithuania.” “Ahhh... I had a best friend who had Polish grandparents. They had some uh... colorful sayings about Lithuanians.” Kazys smirked. “Poles would.” “Good pierogies too,” she mused, seemingly mostly to herself. “Wouldn't guess you had too many Poles around here,” Kazys commented. “Too warm.” It was practically too warm for him, especially for November. The girl shrugged, though. “We get all kinds of people down here. I mean, it's not too hard to understand why they'd want to stay.” Kazys shrugged. He supposed that was true. If nothing else, the Big Easy was a unique place, though he hadn't heard much favorable for people who wanted the safe, settled, suburban sort of American life. Then, he wasn't one of those people. “So, what's your name?” she asked. He realized that he'd been silent for a few moments. He rubbed his ice cold glass of Vodka between his fingers, the sweat coming off its cool surface under the heat of his fingertips. “Kazys Jankauskas,” he said, nodding to her, and taking a sip of his vodka. It slid down his throat, cool as a glacier. “You?” “Emmy,” she said, then blushed. “Well, Emily. Emily Dixon.” He nodded. The warmth of the vodka flooded through him, and he was struck, once more, by how attractive she was, by how well the classy strapless dress she was wearing fit her. She obviously knew a thing or two about clothing. Which was a weird way to introduce yourself... He tilted his head. “So, the... suit thing?” Emily colored. The tinge of rose in her cheeks surpassed the blush she had obviously used. He wondered, again, why so many women were preoccupied with making themselves look good artificially. “I work as a personal shopper for some of the richies here. I guess you could say I'm up on fashion.” He tilted his head at her, giving a curious sort of scoff. “How do you wind up with that gig?” “You fail at fashion design school, that's how,” Emily said, taking a dainty sip of her screwdriver. “I didn't do so hot my last semester, wound up claiming I got mono and dropping out. And with my experience, uh... that's where you end up. My parents had a few friends that got me in on the ground floor, and from there I've been slaving away at it.” Kaz wondered if it was common for people from this city to tell strangers their entire life story. He'd run into a lot more of that in America than he had in Lithuania. But then, he hadn't worked in bars in Lithuania, and alcohol really seemed to loosen Americans' tongues. “I guess I can understand that,” he said. He couldn't understand that at all, truthfully. He turned from the woman, taking another sip of his vodka. He didn't understand how people could mix vodka with so many different things, and completely mask its taste. “So, I like you,” Emily said. Kaz raised an eyebrow. “You uh... do?” “Yeah,” Emily said, nodding. Kaz wondered how it was that she could, considering he'd barely talked. Mostly listened to her. “You want to head out somewhere?” she continued. “I know some really good dance clubs a little way down into the Quarter...” At that, Kaz smiled wryly. “You don't want me dancing, trust me.” She nudged him in the ribs. It was a surprisingly hard nudge for a woman her size, but then Kaz had learned not to underestimate how hard women could hit. Daimon Vytautas probably wouldn't ever underestimate that again either. “Oh come on, it can't be that bad!” “Trust me, it can,” Kaz said, breaking into laughter. “First thing you know, I'm stepping all over your feet. And with feet as big as mine, trust me, that hurts.” At that, Emily threw her head back and laughed. Kaz could see a pair of drop-crystal earrings hanging against her neck, and a necklace glimmering against her tanned skin. “Well,” she said, leaning closer to him, lowering her eyelashes. He'd seen that look before. “We could always go back to my place and have some coffee. I'd like to get to know you a little better.” There was something about the whole situation that seemed wrong. No, felt wrong. “I uh...” “C'mon,” she said, leaning a little closer to him. “I can show you some of the real New Orleans... away from all this stuff...” She tilted her head to indicate all the people around them. Evidently she found them as fake as he did. But, as grudging as the feeling was (he was a man after all), it was still there. Guilt. Like he had no business indulging in this, when he had something so all-consuming to think about right now. He leaned closer to her, his lips almost touching her ear. “As much as I'd love to, I have a plane to catch at 5 this morning. Tonight's my last night here.” Emily suppressed a shiver. He could see a line of goosebumps on her neck. “C'mon, Kazys, don't you like to live on the wild side? This might be your only chance ever to be in New Orleans... you don't want to waste it, do you?” “Don't take it personally honey,” he said softly, pulling out his wallet, and flicking enough cash onto the bar to cover his tab, plus a decent tip. “It's not that I'm not interested. But I can't afford to screw myself over...” Before he could catch himself, he pressed his lips to the top of her head as he stood up to walk out. It wasn't a typical Kaz gesture. Nor was it a typical Kaz gesture to let someone down gently. As he turned to walk out the door and out into the noisy New Orleans streets, he could feel Emily's eyes on him. For some reason, it wasn't hard at all for him to turn and walk away. ===== Kazys didn't know why he'd lied to the girl. There was no reason to act like leaving New Orleans was so imperative. He could have just had his fun with her, and called it a night. Why he didn't was a mystery. It didn't make any kind of good sense. Except that while he'd been drinking, while he'd been considering it, Elise had come up in his mind. Elise, who had successfully passed through her first Jewel in the Crown round (even if with his help). “What the fuck, Kaz?” he said, looking at himself in the mirror as he loosened his tie. His fingertips ran through his long, dark hair, slightly tangled from sweat in its under-layers. Even if it wasn't too terribly hot compared to summers here (so he'd heard), there was still a good deal of humidity. His vodka-clouded eyes stared back at him. They didn't really give him any answers. He wasn't so drunk as to pass out, but he also wasn't so sober that he could quite remember how many rounds he'd had at Rasputin's or at the significantly divier place on a more Eastern part of the city, whose name he couldn't remember. He scrubbed his face in his hands. That served to blur his vision further. Was it competing with Elise that motivated him? The fear that, somehow, if he let his guard down and lived that wrestling high-life for just a moment, he'd lose his shot? He didn't want to go out in the first round – but competing with Elise before hadn't brought him anything but hardship. What would he do if he had to face her again? Lay down for her in the middle of the ring? Lose all his pride by giving her what she wanted, so that he didn't have to hear her bitching? Where would that have gotten him? Emily's face lingered in his mind. A pretty girl, even prettier in the lingering haze of alcohol. And he, a man who had his needs, needs that hadn't been met since he'd broken up with his last girlfriend, Therese, in Miami. “Stupid, Kaz, stupid,” he grumbled at himself, yanking his tie off and flinging it to the dresser in frustration. Something inside him told him it would have been wrong to go back to Emily's apartment. It would have been wrong for him to sleep with her. But why? A dawning feeling sickened him. His face paled slightly in the mirror, ashen undertones making deep circles stand out under his eyes. Was he in love with his partner? It seemed like hours that he hung there, clinging to the idea and trying to suss it out in his mind. It would have made sense – Elise was a wanted woman. A beautiful girl, a former model, a talented wrestler, a driven competitor. And he was responsible for her safety, around her nearly ten hours a day, at the least. Wouldn't it have made sense for a lonely guy like him to attach himself to her? To go for what most guys would have wanted if they'd been around her all the time? He knew, though, that he wasn't like that. That he was a consummate professional. That he knew, extremely well, how to keep his business separate from his personal life. And no matter how much time he spent around Elise, their association was definitely business first. They weren't tag team partners because they were friends. They were tag team partners because they both knew they needed the other one to get anywhere in this business. Plus, there was the fact that... well, after getting to know Elise, what could you love about her? Smart and driven as she was, that often translated into an unattractive bullheaded stubbornness, a jealousy of any successes that Kaz might have had on his own. Besides, as he'd seen in operation with Daimon, she was scheming, conniving, utterly ruthless. All he'd have to do was let his guard down once, and she'd pounce on it like a shark in bloody waters. He knew that. He knew it well. And he found it to be an intensely despicable quality. He'd known too many people in Lithuania who would sell others down the river to profit, and in no case had he ever found it understandable, much less admirable. The fact remained, though, that he was giving up a lot to stay here in PRIME, with Elise. He was giving up a lot of his personal life, a lot of his chance to see the world, to make a better life for himself, by doing what he was doing. Staring at the inside of gyms. Parading around in wrestling rings so fans could throw trash and spit on you. Wandering around with Elise at official events, enduring her cringeworthy behavior, and never being able to breathe a word of apology to anyone for it. Kazys was no shrinking violet. He didn't find the need to apologize for any of his own actions, so maybe that was something he and Elise had in common. But he couldn't make sense of the rest of it. He and Elise were tied together somehow, maybe in some way that would never make sense. As bullheaded as they both were, it had become apparent that they needed each other. What sort of need that was, though, was another question. Though he didn't know the answer to that just yet, Kazys was beginning to understand now just why it was that the whole idea of the “lone wolf” wasn't much more than a lie. They traveled in packs for a reason.
