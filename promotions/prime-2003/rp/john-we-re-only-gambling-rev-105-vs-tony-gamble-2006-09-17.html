John, We're Only Gambling (ReV 105, vs. Tony Gamble)

17 September 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/17/06 Jingle-ling-a-ling! The bells dangling from the main entrance door to Hosscat’s Family Buffet & County Pawn jingle merrily as Nova makes his way inside. He waves to Hosscat, the older gentleman with shoulder-length graying hair and a huge handlebar moustache like the cowboys Sam Elliott always plays on the big screen standing behind a cash register and gun rack. Hosscat gives him a friendly nod. “‘Ay, Brutheeerr!” Nova hangs a right and heads over to one of the booths in the restaurant-portion of the establishment where a slightly disgruntled friend has been waiting for over an hour, with only shots of rum and scowling to amuse her. Lindsay Troy. The “Queen of the Ring”…and the Queen is searching for a way to not rip the Rising Star’s head off at the moment. “What. The. Fuck.” She’s not having a ton of success at the moment. “I’m sorry,” Nova says as he approaches the table, his arms held out inoffensively, “traffic was a fucking bitch, I swear.” Nova sits on the couch in his hotel room, bloodshot eyes glued to Everybody Loves Raymond as smoke swirls and floats in the air above his head. “AH HA-HA-HA-HA!” he guffaws, slapping his knees, “that is so exactly the way it really is!” It goes to commercial, and Romano’s delightfully blunt voice bleats out of the TV speakers: “Hey, where ya goin’? Keep watchin’ the TV, we’ll be right back!” Nova snaps out of his comedy-induced trance and stares down at his watch. He winces, but then stares up longingly as the show resumes. “Oh, five more minutes! Five more minutes and I’m headed out the door!” “I’m sure it was,” Troy replies curtly, “but all the same, a ring on the celly would be nice…especially since you asked me to meet you here.” “Indeed, indeed,” Nova says with a nod as he takes a seat and shakes a smoke out of his pack, “again, I’m sorry. But aren’t you happy you did? Is this place the shit or what?” He lights his cig and puffs away. “Have you been destroying the buffet like I’m five seconds away from doing?” “I waited,” Troy spits through gritted teeth, “I thought it would be the courteous thing to do.” “Absolutely true,” Nova concurs, an anticipatory grin spread across his face, “this place’ll fill you up and hammer a few more nails in your coffin lickety-fuckin’-split. Let’s go punish some grub.” He gets up and practically jogs over to the formidable hot bar. Lindsay stares after him in disbelief, expecting perhaps more verbal reparations from the Rising Star. Forty-five minutes later… Lindsay Troy’s spoon slices slowly through a flaky scoop of vanilla-chocolate swirl, and her eyes follow the motion lazily as her head rests on her palm with all the weight of a visit to Hosscat’s. They move up and focus on Nova, who’s still holding fork and knife in Barbarian Horde fashion, napkin hanging as a bib from his shirt collar. Bits of food litter his beard, but he’s too wrapped in culinary euphoria to notice. Plates lay strewn about the table in stacks like fallen adversaries. “I can’t believe you’re still alive, much less continuing to shovel chicken ‘n’ dumplins into your gullet,” Lindsay says, a mixture of awe and disgust shining through in her voice. “Ah’m ennit fortha long hawl,” Nova mumbles, mouth full. “Ah fuckin’ luff *gulp* love this place.” “Ugh, I’m full as hell,” Troy moans, “great. I’m really glad I skipped on wine-tasting with Joey tonight to meet you out here at the sty and clog some arteries.” “It’s all part of my brilliant plan to win you over,” Nova says with a wink, “it must be working, right? You’re here at the sty with me instead of out knocking back chardonnays and gewurtztraminers with your man, right?” Lindsay rolls her eyes. “Nova…what’s up? You told me you really wanted to talk…I’m here. Let’s talk, or I’m waddling the hell out of…” “Alright, alright, alright!” Nova says, holding up a hand, “I was just shitting around, you know that.” Lindsay nods. Indeed, it was the Rising Star who had requested this rendezvous. Because for all the superficial flirting, cracks about Melton, or biting repartee, Nova and Lindsay Troy are friends, and she is a woman, and that means he can talk to her without feeling like a complete pussy, which is a very hot commodity in the business of professional wrestling. “I…I just don’t understand it, even still,” he says, running a hand through his hair, “I’m losing sleep over this crazy shit. People don’t just explode, Lindz.” Troy shrugs, her brow furled sympathetically. “I don’t know what to tell you, Nov. No one understands it.” “So much about that makes no sense that it’s almost hilarious somehow, in a dark, fucked-up way.” “Well, that was Facey in a nutshell,” Lindsay replies with a shrug. “Touché,” Nova admits, “but I just…I don’t really know what I’m doing now. Tchu and Killean left it out on the mat. Lisieux and the K-Wolf definitely left it out on the mat. Facey escaped in a hot-air balloon, and then blew up in front of me after raising my hand. I can’t just brush it off, ‘oh, another big PPV, another crazy goddamn spontaneous combustion.’” Troy nods knowingly. “I’m not saying you should do that. But you’re gonna drive yourself crazy thinking about it, because I’m guessing the only person who might know anything about it is currently floating in the form of tiny dust particles above the Raymond James stadium.” “Lindz!” Nova protests, taking a drag from his smoke and putting it out. “What?” She asks innocently. “This is a person we’re talking about here,” Nova scolds. “Jesus, I’d hope if I spontaneously combusted that I’d get a little better than the ‘Dust Particle’ treatment…” “Two things,” Lindsay answers, hand extended in a peace sign, “one…you’re not Facey. Two…the odds of you spontaneously combusting are practically non-existant.” “A few weeks back you’d probably be singing the same tune about Facey, but it happened,” Nova replies, his eyes widening as his fork searches blindly for another tasty dumplin’, “and now we know it can happen for sure, instead of just hearing it from Dean Cain! My number might be up soon…” “You’re being ridiculous,” Troy interjects matter-of-factly. Nova scratches his beard, realizes there are left-overs camping in it, and begins picking them out. “A little lower…you got a…yeah, it’s gone,” Troy offers helpfully. “Thanks,” Nova says as he lights another cigarette. “I don’t know where I’m headed now. I feel kind of lost…like I’m just floating around.” “I know that you shouldn’t be ruining that beautiful blonde hair with gray streaks over what happened at CIII,” Lindsay says in her best effort to boost the Rising Star’s spirits. He’s very susceptible to flattery. “Do you really mean that? I switched conditioners,” Nova says brightly, running his hand through his mane a second time before giving himself a shake, “Oh, no you don’t! I know what you’re trying to do…attempting to lighten my mood with the flattery you know I’m susceptible to! Did you think me unawares?” “I should’ve known better,” Lindsay says dryly, her eyes rolling for the seven-hundredth time since entering this cultural hellhole. “Look, Nov…you need to concentrate on the here and now. You have a match in a few days. You should be getting ready for it.” “Gamble?” Nova replies with a snort, “Lindsay, Tony Gamble is a lot of impressive flash and no substance. Did you know that custodial services at the Raymond James Stadium offered Winters a job on the spot after witnessing how skillfully he used Gamble to mop the floor? The guy has been losing, Lindz.” “I don’t remember you walking out of Colossus with a mark in the ‘Win’ column,” Lindz retorts, cocking an eyebrow at her friend. Nova stares at her for a moment. “That hurts, Lindsay, but need I remind you that my opponent escaped in a hot-air…” “A hot-air balloon, I know, I know,” Troy finishes, “well, all I’m saying is Gamble’s no slouch, so sitting around puffing J’s and lamenting the recently incinerated isn’t going to do you any favors come ReVolution 105.” “Shit, Gamble’s the last thing I’m thinking about right now,” Nova says as he looks up at the waitress who’s returned with the checks. He takes his (“Thanks”) and looks at the “Queen of the Ring.” “You about ready to cash out?” “Half an hour ago,” Troy groans, and they both stand up and proceed over to the cash register where Hosscat waits, smoking a cigarette. “Nova, have you ever thought about maybe the reason you’re unhappy with your direction is because you don’t take chances?” “Whaaat?” Nova squeaks, “Bullshit. I take tons of chances.” “No you don’t,” Troy replies simply, “you nod and smile and play the game how the cards are dealt to you. I bet you never try to shoot the moon when you play Hearts, do you?” “Everything alright, brutheerr?” Hosscat inquires amiably as he takes Nova’s check. “A-plus, my man,” Nova says with a grin, patting his belly before turning to Troy. “I do well enough without trying to shoot the fuckin’ moon.” “Of course you do,” Lindsay agrees with a nod as she hands Hosscat her ticket and flashes a smile, “you do fine. But not spectacular.” “Yeah, well some of the dumbasses who try to shoot the moon fail miserably,” Nova fires back, “and then you’re fucked.” “Sure,” Lindsay replies, signing her receipt and turning back to Nova, “but those dumbasses take the gamble and risk getting fucked for the big pay-off. (to Hosscat) Thanks.” She turns and makes her way towards the exit. Nova stands at the register for a moment, lost in thought. “Yer friend’s leavin’, bruther,” Hosscat offers helpfully, and Nova snaps out of it. He gives the odd old man a nod and walks after Troy. “How are you comin’ at me with all this shit, anyway?” he says irritably, “been reading too many of those metro-magnet spirituality books Melton has spread out on all your damn coffee-tables.” “Whatever you say,” Lindsay says with a shrug, “but you wanted my opinion and you got it.” “Duly noted,” Nova replies with his own eye-roll. He stops in his tracks as he sees a slot machine a few feet from the door. Troy walks a few more feet and then stops when she realizes Nova isn’t following. She stares at him quizzically as he gives the machine a once-over before walking up to it and slipping a quarter into the slot. He gives the lever a pull and watches the columns spin. BAR…BAR… …Lemon. “Figures, right?” Nova says bitterly as he looks up at Lindsay. “Sometimes you get fucked,” Troy admits, shrugging her shoulders. She walks over to Nova, removes a quarter from her pocket, and slides it into the slot. She pulls the lever (while Fanboys everywhere bust their rocks on the innuendo). CHERRY…CHERRY…CHERRY. Several quarters spill out of the machine into the dish protruding at crotch-height. Troy looks down at the quarters, and then up at the Rising Star. “But sometimes it pays to gamble. I’ll see you in a few days, Nova.” Nova’s eyes are locked on the quarters, and he doesn’t notice Troy turn and walk away. When he looks up, she’s out the door and walking to her car. “Uh, thanks for coming out and meeting me, Lindz!” Troy doesn’t turn around, but gives a blind wave. “We’ll have to get together again some time!” Nova calls out, “Have dinner, talk wrestling…make babies…” He watches her leave, and then his eyes fall back on the quarters in the tray. He knows that he has too many vices as it is… …but maybe he should pick up gambling.
