45 Minutes to the Wasteland (with Danny Ferguson and Wade Elliott, ReV 143 vs. the Fuck-lets)

18 October 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 18, 2007. Very early It’s nearly 3 a.m. along the aptly-named Providence-Worcester Turnpike, and the place is barren. Not too many folks are out driving New England highways early on a Thursday morning, so that makes the deluxe party bus rocketing down Route 146 stand out just a little more. Three-hourly rentals at Carl’s Crunk Caravans were only about $500 less than a full night, so Danny Ferguson had decided to splurge for the extra time. This was not always the plan; rather, a more spontaneous choice that he’d arrived at shortly after Dametreyus Fuqueiawytas - bodyguard, volunteer firefighter and de facto driver for all Team Ferguson ground transportation - had downed his fourth-straight pineapple upside-down cake shot at the Cadillac Lounge earlier that evening. According to police reports that would be filed a couple weeks from now, this may or may not have coincided with Dam attempting to ‘make it rain’ on the Lounge’s main stage and managing only a light drizzle. Faced with the prospect of shelling out a week’s Hard Money royalty check or getting behind the wheel and driving himself the 40 miles from Providence (site of ReVolution 142) and Worcester, Mass. (site of ReVolution 143), Danny promptly opened his wallet. Sitting four rows back from the driver and surveying an entire bus full of security guards sleeping off the last several hours, he let out a sigh. "Money well spent." He sunk into a seat and leaned it back as far as he could go. Across the aisle, Lindsay Troy kept her eyes fixed out the window. She sort of half-laughed but never turned to look at her former tag team partner. "Considering the kind of cash you dropped to get those boys smashed in the first place, it sounds like the smartest fiscal decision you made all night." Faced with the prospect of shelling out a week’s per diem for cab fare to Worcester or riding in a bus full of temporary personal security gorillas who reek of Jim Beam and something fruity, Lindsay reluctantly threw her bags on board and grabbed a seat near the front. She watched a road sign for Woonsocket pass by outside the bus and shook her head. "I should have stayed back." "You really wanted to get one more full night in at the hotel?" Danny inquired, burying his shoulders into the plush upholstery and keeping his eyes trained on Troy. She still didn't look over. "No." A sigh. "But with all the investigatory bullshit that's going to go on over the next few days, I probably should have stayed within city limits." "It's not like we're going across country here, Legs. They've got your cell anyway." Lindsay responded with a shrug, looking away from the window only when something weighted nestled itself in her lap. She looked down to find Angus the Bluetick Hound getting comfortable next to her, body stretching out on the cushions and head looking to be scratched. A tired groan came from the back. Troy looked over her shoulder, presenting a weak grin at the sight of two long, dirty-jean'd and steel-toe boot clad legs shifting in the aisles, the tired, rugged, possibly drunk, gravely tone emanating from the throat of what could only be "The Big Dog," Wade Elliott. Troy turned her attention back to Angus, smiling as she scratched the pup behind his ears, eyes once again drifting to the window. "Eugh, where's the coffee?" came the waking voice of Wade, walking down the aisle and taking a seat next to Angus and Lindsay, causing the dog to shift a little to make room for his large master. "You'd be taking your coffee with mostly Jim Beam anyway," was the Queen's quiet reply, her smirk visible through the dimmed party lights of the bus. Wade offered an amused chuckle. "Yeah, yer probably right." He looked over to his friend, joining her in the scratching of his dog, much to Angus' delight. "How ya holdin' up?" he asked with sincerity. Troy's face fell slowly, keeping her attention mostly on the dog. "It was a '68 Lamborghini Miura." She could hear Danny trying to mask the sucking-in of his breath. "And it was my father's car." Wade nodded his head. "Damn shame." Lindsay sighed for the umpteenth time, it felt like. "But hey..." She looked up, locking eyes with the Drifter. "...you weren't in it." "Small miracles, right?" "Pretty fuckin' big one if yer askin' me." For the first time all evening, Troy genuinely laughed. The Southern Sparkplug shared the chuckle with Troy. Angus looked from his master to the Queen of the Ring, curious as to what was going on before being lulled into a semi-conscious state with a belly rub from Troy. "Thanks for coming along, Wade." "Y'needed all the company y'could get," the Big Dog nodded. "So who woulda pulled some horse-shit like that?" asked Wade, continuing the conversation. "Silver? Some fuck I ain't met yet?" "It reeks of Sonny; Craig Miles did something similar to him in the fWo," Lindsay stated matter-of-factly, her tone turning serious again. "But I don't think it was necessarily him." Danny snorted. "I don't need to play a detective in a Ben Affleck movie to figure out that Sonny had something to do with it." "I'm not saying he didn't, Danny. But my finger of suspicion doesn't fall only to him." Wade took in the information, curling his lips behind his bushy goatee, eyebrows scrunching together. He leaned his head closer to Troy's. "Lettin' ya know ahead've time so you ain't surprised: it turns out it was Sonny, I'll fuckin' kill 'im." "Guess that's why you're the Intense champ, Drifter." "I’m a little outclassed in this trio, what with not having a belt and all," Danny shoots them both a sly smile. Wade gives him a snorting response, just enough of a laugh to acknowledge that Danny said something funny. Lindsay furrows her brow a little - not bothered at all by the implication, but certainly wanting a follow up. "Does that bother you?" "Huh?" "I mean, not the ‘no belt’ thing per se," she continues, biting her bottom lip until she had the rest of the sentence worked out in her head, "but does it bother you what people are saying?" "People are saying things?" "Come on, Danny," she groans, sitting up. "I watch Access Hollywood in the hotel when nothing else is on. They think you’re playing second fiddle to me over this champion stuff." Danny nods a little and looks out the window at the rest stop they were passing. "We’ve talked about this before." "I know we have, and I’m not questioning your motives anymore." She looks over at Wade. "I’ve tried to be better at just accepting friendships for what they are," she starts, then casts her gaze back to Ferguson. "I know why you can’t and won’t accept a title shot; what I’m asking is, does it bother you that you can’t?" "Some people say the Oscar for Supporting Actor is more prestigious than the Lead Actor one," he chuckles. "It’s all about outshining your co-stars." Troy gives him a mock offended face that fades to a smile. "Fine, I see how it is. This from the guy who once famously informed me that PRIME was not an ensemble drama but his own starring vehicle." "Yeah, well," Danny stammers, stalling for a comeback, "the showrunners went in a different direction." All three share a quiet laugh. There’s a pause in conversation when no one knows what to say next, so Danny finally gets around to the business of answering Lindsay’s question. "You spend a lot of time being cutthroat in Hollywood, walking over anyone and everyone to get where you want just so you can get there, look back and say you’re sorry. I’ve had belts. I might even get the one you’re holding one day, but I’m willing to wait my turn." "I ain't the veteran in this here trio," interjected Wade, turning his head to Ferguson. "But with all the fuckin' cunts runnin' 'round in this business, don't seem like a bad idea to cut in line once in a while." "Patience is a virtue that I don't have often," the Universal Champ said as she unfolded her legs from her seat, "but I think Danny knows that he'll be happiest when he gets his shot on his terms. Am I right, Fergdor?" "Hit the nail on the head, Legs." Wade offers sarcastic incredulous looks to his companions. "Odd sort I've found myself runnin' with," he snorts. "All this talk've 'virtue' an' 'true happiness' ain't so familiar to ol' Wade." "It's not really familiar to Danny either, so you're not alone," came the quip from Troy. "Gee, thanks. And need I remind you that it's unfamiliar ground for you too." "It is," Lindsay said, and gave Danny a wink. "But I'd rather tread it with the two of you then tread it by myself."
