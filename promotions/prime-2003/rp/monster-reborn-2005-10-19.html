Date: 10-19-05 So my debut didn’t go as I planned. I didn’t get the win that I wanted, but that’s all right - going against three debuting superstars in Prime - which was bound to happen. What happened afterwards was something I didn’t expect. I felt a presence inside of me that I’d been familiar with since I was seven years old. A presence so deadly that I thought I kept it locked inside for a better part of a decade. I thought I controlled the monster inside me. Until last week. After my match against Paddy O’Shea, Tyler O’Neill and the Illustrious Face-Eater, I felt fine with my loss. I congratulated everyone on a good match, patted Face-Eater on the back and left the match. As I got into the back, something inside of me snapped, and I went on a volcanic rampage. I started fighting with backstage personnel, committing vandalism by destroying Prime’s backstage property and I’d gotten into fights with four of the largest backstage security personnel I’d ever encountered. It took those four backstage security personnel, Kenji Iwakura, the four co-owners of Prime and two tranquilizers to apprehend my savage actions. I was handcuffed, taken to a psych ward and was released the next day under my own resonance. If it wasn’t for Kenji Iwakura explaining my situation to everyone in Prime, my contract would’ve been terminated that night. Today I’m visiting with Sylvia Madison, my personal doctor and licensed therapist since I was sixteen. When I was let out of some mental institute in Chicago, Sylvia acted as my guardian, since I had no family that could take care of me. There was my birthmother who gave me away when I was born, but she couldn’t be contacted - or didn’t want to be contacted - so Sylvie took me under her wing. She took care of me, loved me and we had a...thing...for a few years. When I was eighteen, she helped me find my own place to live and I went out living on my own. I still kept in contact with her when I had problems, and she promised me that she would be there for me if I ever needed her. Today would be that day. I parked my Land Rover in front of Sylvia’s office. She was standing there waiting for me, wearing a black, tasteful skirt, a royal blue blouse and knee-high leather boots. Her elegant blond hair framed her face, resting softly on her shoulders. Her blue eyes were crystal clear and innocent. After dealing with deaths in the ER and nut jobs everywhere she went it’s surprising that her innocence never tarnished. And she had the experience of someone who’s been in the profession for twenty years! That’s pretty astounding for someone who’s twenty-nine years old. I got out of my car and I could see her sparkling smile. I walked up to her and gave her a hug. I got on my tiptoes, since she’s six-five, and gave her a kiss on the cheek. “We meet again, Sylvie,” I said, smiling at her. “And on time this time,” she answered. “Last time you were half an hour late.” She giggled, reminding me that I was caught up in traffic when I made an appointment with her late last year. I chucked ruefully. “You knew that I was caught up in traffic. The roads were atrocious back then.” I walked over to her office door. “So...shall we begin our appointment?” I opened the door for her, putting a debonair smile on my face. She giggled and curtsied. “Very sweet of you. Let’s begin.” We walked inside; I closed the door behind me as I watched Sylvia walking over to her chair and sitting down. The office looked like your basic office. To the left-hand corner of the room, there was an inclined chair for patients to lay on, with a chair on the opposite side, facing the chair. Hung randomly on the walls are Sylvia’s certificates on MD graduations and psychology graduations. See what I mean when I said that it was pretty astounding for a woman who’s twenty-nine years old? It only took her only took her four years to get those accolades. A genius intelligence quotient definitely worked wonders. On the center left-hand and center north walls sit bookshelves full of psychology books, some she’d written herself and some from other psychologists all over the world. In the center of the room, two armchair facing each sit on opposite sides separated by a long coffee table. “Have a seat, Simon,” I heard Sylvia say. I walked over and sat on the opposite chair, facing her. She crossed her legs, folding her hands and placing them on her lap. She has her clipboard for note-taking to her right on a little stand. “So,” she began, acting as professional as possible, even though she didn’t need to. She’s the most professional person I’d ever met. “What’s been going on the last time we’ve met?” “The same old thing,” I answered. “Except for the fact I almost got fired from Prime last week.” Sylvia raised her eyebrows. “What happened?” “I had a ‘breakdown’ of course, snapping after losing my debut match last week. I don’t have the slightest comprehension why I did that, because I was okay with my loss. Something took over inside me, Sylvie. It was a feeling that I felt before.” Sylvia’s eyes widen. She instantly understood for I had said momentarily. “You mean---” “Yes,” I replied, sighing. “It’s returned. And at a perfect time, I’m assuming.” My therapist looked at me quizzically. “What do mean by that?” “Remember our last session when I told you what happened to me at LOW?” She nodded her head. “You said you were screwed out of the LOW title.” I let out a sigh. “Precisely. And my deep-rooted hatred for Jamester must’ve trigger my inner monster. You saw me challenge Jesse Jamester to a Sadist Match last week on Revolution, did you not? She nodded her head again. “Sounds like that’s what triggered your uncontrollable rage, your ‘monster’ as you say. I don’t know what to do to help you there, Simon. Obviously prescribed medication that I gave you in the past didn’t fully pacify your monster. Now with you challenging Jamester to a match, your subconscious was awaken, and your monster wants to destroy your hatreds, like what you did to your foster family.” I shook my head, sighing. “You had to remind me of that didn’t you, Sylvie? I doubt that I’ll kill Jamester.” “You certainly will if you can’t control yourself. Who knows what you could do if no one can stop you. It scares me that you’re a liability everywhere you go. I’ve been advised to lock you up, but I can’t do that, you’re a sane member of today’s society...that is insane.” She finished with a chuckle. “Yeah, I supposed,” I answered, chuckling myself. “It’s like I’m the only one in world who’s normal.” Sylvia cleared her throat and went back to her state of professionalism. “Is there anything else I can do for you, Simon? Anything else you want to talk about?” My face harden into a state of seriousness. “There is actually,” I began, “and promise me what I’m about to tell you doesn’t leave this office.” “That depends on what you’re going to tell me.” “I figured you were going to say that.” I looked straight into Sylvia’s eyes, seeing nothing that would betray my trust in her. “Remember the murder that happened last night? The one that happened to Alice Swanson?” Sylvia nodded. “The prostitute, yes. What about her?” I sighed. “I don’t know what better why to tell you this. I might’ve been involved in her murder.” Sylvia’s eyes widened. Could Simon be the one who killed her? No, it wouldn’t been possible. He hadn’t killed anyone in ten years. Perhaps Simon was joking and was leading her on. But the look on his face said otherwise. “Tell me everything. Everything that you remember.” “Well,” I began, hesitant at first, but being with Sylvia made me feel comfortable enough to continue forward. “This is what I can remember...” Yuriko and I had a fight over what I couldn’t quite put my finger on; I’m not even sure that Yuriko knew what we were fighting about. Yuriko was in a bad mood all day today, all she wanted to do was pick a fight with me. She was on her period, she was cranky, cramping and didn’t want anything to do with anyone at that moment, which was understandable because I’ve experienced her attitude plenty times when she had PMS. After Yuriko and I had our fight, I left for awhile, giving Yuriko her space. We had agreed that when she had PMS, I would leave the house for awhile until she calmed down. She and I had a few encounters in the past, and she’s a great person to be around with, when she isn’t sexually promiscuous. “Hey, baby,” Alice said, wrapping an arm around my shoulders, kissing me on the cheek. “How’s it goin’ tonight? Did you and the missus have a fight?” I don’t know how she did it, but she could always read my mind. “Yeah, unfortunately,” I replied, taking a swig of my beer. “Over what, I don’t even know. I don’t think she even knows. It’s that time of the month, you know.” She nodded in agreement. “Can’t blame you there. Are you doin’ something later? Do you want to talk? Maybe---” She was dragging her finger along my chest--- “a little fun?” I brush her finger off my chest. “Sounds tempting, but I’ll pass.” Alice usually didn’t take no for an answer. Don’t you ever call me a slut!” She stormed out of the bar, followed by some commotion from the bar patrons, who were watching the little incident that transpired. I felt my anger growing. I never felt this mad since the Fristik family screwed me out of the LOW title. I got up, watching Alice leave the bar with fire burning in my eyes. The next thing I knew, I blacked out... It’s now my time to play. I’m finally out and I put Simon in his place. He was getting weak; he left that slut punch him right in the face. So I placed him in a comforting slumber while I do his dirty work. I looked around to see where I was. Of course, I was in a bar.
