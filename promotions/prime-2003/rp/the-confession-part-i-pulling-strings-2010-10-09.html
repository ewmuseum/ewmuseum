The Confession: Part I - Pulling Strings

9 October 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09-10-10 “Why?” He asked hot on the heels of yet another slug to the jaw. As Michael's head snapped to the side the giant reeled back again, baring his teeth. “Answer me.” The next shot found its mark dead centre of the bulging red welt that fused the fallen Monster's eye shut. A mix of blood and pus spurted from the swollen wound, trickling down his cheek and staining Von Kelsig's knuckles. Wiping it off in disgust he grabbed Sloan's jaw, squeezing hard and studying the misshapen, discoloured face before him. Michael looked up at the giant with his good eye, sucking in shallow breaths and expelling them in laboured huffs. He had been falling in and out of consciousness ever since being chokeslammed from the top of the ladder. Von Kelsig responded with a thunderous clap across Sloan's face, incensed by the silence. “Damn it, why?” Von Kelsig reached beyond Sloan's line of sight to retrieve something. Producing the crinkled old piece of paper he opened his mouth apprehensively, eyes wandering over some of the words. The veil of television had heavily diluted the rumours. He fixed a mean look on Michael. “What are you?” Sloan never flinched but under the surface, even in his weakened state, he was panicking. A new kind of pain was racking his body, so intense that he struggled to understand the giant. Quick as a light switch a smile formed on his lips that spread a burning sensation across his face. Still he grinned. Von Kelsig resisted the urge to fire another row of knuckles into that twisted mouth. “I want to know what the fuck's going on here and I want to know now.” To drive the point home Von Kelsig dangled the note in front of Michael. A morbid thrill shivered up the Monster's damaged spine as the giant leaned into his face. “Gloria.” Unable to form anything beyond a whimper, Sloan's eye glazed over, rolling back up into the socket. The colour drained from his face as he fell back into unconsciousness, provoking a roar of frustration from Von Kelsig and another slap to the face. Clambering back into the front seat he gripped the wheel with both hands and pushed his weight back into the sponge. Staring up, his eyes began to water. Blinking away the tears he slammed a fist down on the wheel, muttering to himself as the ambulance tore through the streets in the opposite direction of the hospital. “You'll talk Sloan...I guarantee you will talk.” ---- “Y'ain't got no business bringin' him here...” “Trust me this is legit. I couldn't go anywhere else.” “Anybody see yer bring 'em here?” “No one, I made sure of it. Called up a few of your guys to help out. If anything leaks it's come from them.” “Why'n the fuck don'tcha take it up with five-oh? Why here?” “I can't involve the cops man, it could come back on PRIME...and between us all there's probably enough dirty laundry to choke up the courts for years. Can't take that risk.” Christian Daniels paced around the table, examining Sloan's body. It had been wrung hard by the fight at Colossus, harder than any of his previous matches by a long shot. Yet there was a strange vibe emanating from his face; a calm amidst the agony pulsing through his flesh that disturbed the Biker. “I'ma tell yer straight, ANY fuck ups'n it's on yer head.” While Von Kelsig contemplated the Biker's word, Sloan's eyelids began to flicker. The transition from dream to reality was seamless, bridged by a fatigued grunt from the Monster. Sedatives and bandaging confined him to his vegetative state when before the crippling pain had left him incapacitated. A fat bluebottle buzzed aimlessly around his head, dropping occasionally to sample the crusty scabs still dotting his face. He wanted to ask where he was, but as clearly as the words formed in his mind they fell from his lips as a garble. Turning their attention to the man on the table, Von Kelsig and Christian Daniels watched quietly as Michael's head lolled from side to side, trying to gain some kind of bearing. “Compus mentus yet, Sloan?” Von Kelsig swatted the fly away from Michael's eyes and clicked his fingers over the Monster's face. Blinking slowly in reaction to the noise, Sloan moaned dazedly. Daniels scoffed, noticing tiny blood spots patterned along the wrapping around his torso. “Ya used our guy to fix him up?” “Yeah, been nearly two weeks since he operated.” “An' still lookin' like shit?” “That's the idea. You definitely know the right people.” The Biker furrowed his brow and nudged Von Kelsig in the ribs, nodding towards Michael. The giant bowed his head in humility, wincing at the thought of unintentionally feeding Sloan information. “So whaddya need me f'r anyway?” Von Kelsig scoffed, shooting the Biker a knowing look. “Don't be naïve, you know how to deal with these situations...” ---- Five Months Ago After the bitter winter, spring had dragged April, May and June in, half dead and gasping for breath. The Culture Shock juggernaut had long since rolled out of London, having left its stamp of destruction on Wembley. Another loss to Chainz and another top ten finish in the Halo meant business as usual for the giant, whose appetite for destruction had been stifled by events back home across the pond. With the death of his mother weighing heavily on his mind he couldn't muster the spirit to give a blow-out performance at the pay-per-view, settling for second best and, in the case of the Halo, eighth. The onslaught from the Monster continued at ReVolution 223, and after a vicious assault the Murder Show was forced to take medical leave to recuperate from his injuries. He was scheduled to return in July, but no one had heard any word from the giant since his departure. His voicemail was filling up with messages from the front office demanding that he get in contact, but a dark depression had sunk in and work was the last thing on his mind. His mother had been taken from him, and combined with Sloan's mission to end his career Von Kelsig was giving serious consideration to throwing in the towel on his livelihood, on his life. Through the healing process he chased his medication with whiskey, despising himself for falling into the cliché that he would pity another for suffering. Unable to bring himself to even visit the cemetery he spent his days behind closed doors, quietly rotting away while the world turned without him. The self loathing took a turn for the worse in the middle of the month when he picked up his cell phone with the intent of calling Shakur and asking for his release. It took nearly half an hour to build up the courage, or perhaps the cowardice, to push the dial button on Devin's number... “Angelus, where the hell have you been? The board's been chasing my ass twenty-four-seven trying to get a hold of you. You know you're due back next month and you've got a ton of catch-up to do for this arc.” “Dev, I need to talk to you. It's important.” “We're talking right now. Spill it.” “No. It needs to be in person.” “Shit. Okay look I'll be at home for a few days next week. Come on over and we'll sort everything out. I'll let the directors know you're still alive. You alright?” “Yeah. See you next week.” The dial tone filled Shakur with dread. He wasn't Jason Snow, but Hessian was still a big draw and from the sound of it he was going the way of the Original Villain. He couldn't afford to lose another talent like the Murder Show when other companies were prospering so well and PRIME was struggling to hold its own. Von Kelsig spent the next week in limbo, at peace with the finality of his decision. He knocked back the drugs and alcohol as usual, only now they helped fuel his determination to see this through instead of clouding the pain he was feeling, mentally and physically. He left a day early to make the journey up to see Devin, giving him that little bit extra time to fine-tune his approach and the monologue he had been working on since the phone call. It broke his heart when he bumped into fans of his, knowing he might lose them all after the meeting. Always the professional he pushed his woes to the back of his mind for those few minutes of handshakes and autographs. He owed them that much. Shakur's house was as he expected it to be. Large, luxurious and extravagant, the only difference was its setting. He imagined it to be some hilltop mansion in view of all the world, not hidden away behind tall trees and imposing hedges. It wasn't L.A., but Fort Beverley Hills would have served as an apt title for the residence. Trudging beyond the wrought iron gate, his legs felt heavier with every step. He felt the shakes running from the soles of his feet all the way up his spine, and it took all his strength to push forward knowing what was beyond the front door of the property. I can't do this any more...I can't deal with it...I can't work with all the constant shit...I can't...I can't...I can't... The words burned in his brain as he arrived at the front door. He reached for the large iron knocker, pausing to wipe the sweat from his palms. Turning his hands over he stared at them, at the hands that had wielded grotesque weapons of destruction and championship belts alike through the years. After today they'd be good for nothing except an ass scratching or a bottle opening. He reached for the knocker again, and drew breath. He flicked his wrist and chapped the door once... ...at the exact moment that it burst open, sending him staggering back as a lean man half his height propelled through the opening and smacked hard into his body. “DON'T FUCKING LET HIM GET AWAY D!!” Von Kelsig recognized Shakur's voice echoing through the halls within followed by the booming footsteps of Christian Daniels, who he could see storming after the man in matte black now stood in front of him. In an instant it all came together; Shakur's command, the furious look in Daniels eyes, and the strange ninja trying to push past him. He couldn't make sense of what he was seeing until the balaclava-clad man produced a shank stained in blood, aimed squarely at his heart. He delivered the punch as Zeus would a bolt of lightning. Daniels skidded to a halt at the door as the intruder's head snapped back, his body following suit. He hit the deck hard, the blade sliding across the marble floor past the Biker. Still in shock at the sight of it all, Von Kelsig could only gasp as Christian produced a silenced Desert Eagle and without hesitation put a single round through the woollen mask. The giant barely had time to react before Daniels grabbed him, pulling him inside and slamming the door shut. “Angelus?! What the fuck are you doing here??” He turned from the dead man to see Shakur hobbling towards him, holding a hand over his shoulder. Blood seeped through his fingers, bright and red against his usual black outfit. Pointing at the intruder's corpse, he opened his mouth to reply as Daniels holstered his weapon and rushed off to begin the clean up. “Fuck! Fucking prick!” Devin looked into the lifeless eyes of the man on the floor, groaning and cursing under his breath as Daniels returned with a plastic sheet and a bucket full of detergents and other liquids. He took a clear unlabelled bottle and squirted the solution around the puddle of blood blooming around the man's skull, containing it immediately. Throwing the sheet over the body he ran back out of the foyer, muttering to himself. “What the fuck Devin...” Von Kelsig stared blankly at Shakur, who grabbed him by the arm and pulled him towards an arched opening into a sitting room. “Get in there.” Shakur took one last look at the corpse, wincing from the injury on his shoulder. Daniels returned carrying a large black sack and a bundle of clothes, whistling to Shakur and tossing him a couple. Nodding in thanks, Shakur placed the cloth against the wound and limped after Von Kelsig. Upon seeing him, the giant stopped pacing back and forth and pointed out to the foyer. “Don't say shit. Sit down.” “Who was that??” “It doesn't matter who it fucking was.” “Doesn't matter?! Dev, I just watched Christian shoot someone through the face, what the fuck is going on here? Why are you bleeding?” The Man in Black slumped into a sofa, gazing into the middle distance as Von Kelsig sat opposite him, his heart pounding from the shock now instead of his earlier fears. “Shit.........shit.” He placed his head in his hands and sighed through his fingers. The last several minutes played over in Von Kelsig's head, and he waited in silence as Shakur muttered to himself, his eyes moving rapidly from left to right. “Shouldn't you call the cops?” Shakur couldn't help but smile. A momentary reaction that sent a shiver down Von Kelsig's spine. “If only it was that simple,” Shakur said, looking the giant in the eye after another several minutes of silence. “Look...” Von Kelsig held his hands up, dreading the truth that seemed to be emerging, “I don't know what's going on here. I only came here to talk about work. This...this has nothing to do with me. As far as I'm concerned you were defending your property.” Shakur dabbed his wound, sliding the cloth under his shirt and pressing it down on his shoulder. Behind him, Daniels was hefting the large sack, now slung over his shoulder and filled with a distinctly human form, into the back of the house. “That'd be simple enough, except for the fact that there isn't a thief alive that could get into this house without me knowing.” “What are you saying?” “He's sayin' it weren't no fuckin' cat burglar.” Both men looked up at the entrance to see Daniels wiping his hands off with a cloth smudged in dark red marks. Shoving it into his pocket he walked in and threw himself into the sofa next to Shakur, who cocked an eyebrow expectantly. “Couldn't tell yer who he was, but I know who sent 'im.” Leaning over, Daniels whispered a name into Devin's ear. Von Kelsig looked away, but couldn't help listening carefully to try and catch any part of the accused's identity. “Motherfucker......” Daniels shifted in his seat, eyeballing the giant. “That,” he motioned back to the foyer, “don't leave this fuckin' house. Y'understand?” “D, let me talk to him. Go sort this shit out.” Keeping his gaze fixed on Von Kelsig, Christian rose up out of his seat and left the room. Shakur chewed on his thumbnail, deep in thought. Every so often the giant looked out into the foyer, replaying the event over and over in his head and forgetting all about his reason for being there. Forgotten at least until Shakur opened his mouth. When he did, Von Kelsig regretted ever thinking about quitting. “You came here to talk about work Angelus...so let's talk about work.” ---- It bothered the Biker that this giant of a man should tell him his business, but circumstances dictated that they must co-exist if the truth of that fateful day five months ago were to remain secret. Von Kelsig had kept his side of the bargain, keeping his mouth shut in exchange for Shakur and Daniels' assistance in dealing with Sloan. Watching as Michael's eyes rolled around beneath his eyelids, Daniels nodded to the exit and led the giant out of the room, closing the door firmly behind them. “If what yer tellin' me's true...how'n da fuckin' hell'd nobody figure it out 'til now?” “Same way nobody's figured what kind of business you and Devin are involved in...” Daniels grunted, “told you never ta bring 'at up.” “But you catch my drift?” The Biker offered another grunt, gazing back at the room containing Sloan's broken body. “Folks ain't gon' believe he just up'n disappeared...” “Ah,” Von Kelsig held a finger up, “I've already thought of that.” Pushing past Daniels, the giant led him away from Michael's room down a flight of creaky, cracked stairs. The Biker followed him, along the musty hallway at the bottom to a door through which lay another set of stairs that carried them into darkness. As Christian's feet hit the ground light filled the basement. It was a far cry from the dingy shit hole upstairs. The walls were freshly painted, the floor clean and the furniture almost new. Looking back up at the door Daniels noticed that on this side it was made of steel, complete with double locks. From the hallway it looked like any other door in the house, with paint flaking off of it and cracks running down the wooden surface. Von Kelsig had been busy in the last fortnight, and had turned the old safehouse long since abandoned by the Biker into a bonafide hideout. Impressed with the renovations, Daniels watched as Von Kelsig's pointed finger moved from the light switch to another door on the opposite side of the room. “In there.” Christian made his way across to the medieval looking oak door. Gripping the handle he pushed his way into the chamber beyond with Von Kelsig at his heels, and stopped dead at the sight before him. Sobbing into her hands on a single bed surrounded with personal effects sat a very familiar face. Gazing up, her panda eyes met Daniels and for a moment she was still. The sight of Von Kelsig brought more tears to her eyes and once more she buried herself in the duvet of the bed. A half-finished letter lay on a small cabinet at her side. “...His wife? Ya got his fuckin' wife hostage too?” “I wouldn't say that...” Von Kelsig smiled, “call it collateral.”
