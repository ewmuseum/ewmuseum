Homecoming (ReVolution 152, singles match vs. High Flyer)

14 February 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 14th, 2008. Valentine's Day. Albuquerque, New Mexico. Valentine’s Day. “Alright, let’s git the fuck out’ve here.” The army issue duffel bag containing the few belongings of “The Bad Dog,” Wade Elliott thudded into the bed of his shiny black Ford F-150. The morning sun shined brightly over the buildings of Albuquerque, New Mexico, offering a golden hew to everything in sight. PRIME’s Blue Collar Brawler popped a Marlboro Red into his mouth before stepping around to the passenger’s side of his truck. He lit the cigarette with his silver Zippo, leaning against his rig as he blew smoke through his bushy brown goatee. “C’mon, Angus!” Across the parking lot and behind a tree of the Hyatt Regency Hotel, where the Drifter had spent the night after PRIME ReVolution’s one hundred and fifty first broadcast, stood Wade’s canine partner in crime, Angus the Bluetick Hound, one leg in the air, relieving his doggy bladder before the long drive ahead. “Friggin’ mutt…” grumbled Wade, flicking his cigarette ash on dark asphalt. “We’re burnin’ daylight, pup!” Finished, Angus trotted out from the behind the tree, taking his sweet puppy time as he padded across the parking lot. “What’re ya draggin’ yer ass fer?” Angus looked up to his master with judgmental eyes before turning them back toward the ground. “Oh, don’t gimme that horse-shit,” said Wade, tossing his finished cigarette butt and opening the passenger’s door. “Quit yer belly-achin’ and git in.” Angus offered another droopy eyed gaze before slowly clambering into the passenger’s seat. Wade slammed the door closed with a grunt before trotting to the driver’s side and taking his own seat at the helm of the rig. Angus kept his eyes out the window. “What, yer mad at me now?” asked a frustrated ‘Bama Bruiser. No reply from the hound. Wade snorted through his nostrils, shoving the key into the ignition. “What, ya think I was out’ve line tellin’ Rayne off? I gave that shit-stain his chance, an’ he proved he ain’t nothin’ more’n a fuckin’ cunt.” Angus turned his head and raised an eyebrow. “Oh, ya think I was bein’ an ass towards Lindsay.” Angus turned away. Wade released another snort. “It’s her own fuckin’ fault. She wants to spend her time ‘round Rayne, then she can go the fuck ahead, but I ain’t gon’ stick around an’ watch em’ bat their fuckin’ eyelashes. Got along just fine by my lonesome before, I sure as hell can do it fer a good long fuckin’ while longer.” He turned the key, the big truck roaring to life. He adjusted his brown drifter’s hat, keeping the hot Albuquerque sun out of his cold blue eyes. He shoved the clutch into first gear and pulled out of the Hyatt Regency Hotel parking lot, rumbling off down the road. “I ain’t got nothin’ to feel sorry fer, an’ got nothin’ to be ‘fraid of…” Angus heaved a sigh through his floppy jowls. * * * * * * * “Well well well…” Her long, striking red hair danced in the wind atop the roof of a building across from the Hyatt Regency Hotel, her sharp blue eyes watching the black F-150 cruise down the road, heading east. She snapped one last photograph with her thin digital camera before tucking it into her baggy blue overalls, the shoulder straps dangling from her waist. She tossed a faded red ball cap onto her head, adjusting it as she turned away, swinging her hips with a sly grin while taking dirty booted steps towards the roof’s ladder. “Look who came home…” * * * * * * * “Now, tell me why we’re takin’ this one again?” Cliff smothered the playing cards with his monstrous hands, creating what was once an organized game of solitaire into a mess of diamonds and clovers. He was a mammoth of man, close to seven feet tall, a muscle bound farm boy with a bald head and just as clean shaven face. His deep set beady brown eyes focused on the faded pack of cards as he took care to shuffle them properly. “Cuz there ain’t no other jobs to take,” said the man sitting across from him, cowboy-booted feet resting on the table. “Dunno, Horace,” said Cliff, deep baritone voice pushing through thin lips. “Seems like we’re wastin’ our time.” “’Course we’re wastin’ our time,” Horace affirmed, a large, tan, wide brimmed cowboy hat sitting on top of his face to block from the flickering light above them, arms folded behind his head. “But I wouldn’t mind a drink’re two when we’re finished.” “’Bout all this pay’ll get us…” “Forget about the old job, boys.” Cliff turned his head at the pretty sound of a female voice heading their way. “An’ why’s that, Becky?” asked Horace under his hat, taking feign interest. “Guess who just came home,” she answered, sashaying across the concrete floor of the dingy basement. “The fuckin’ Messiah?” guessed a sarcasm-drenched Horace. “Just about the opposite,” she said with a smirk. “Take a peek at this.” She tossed her thin digital camera onto the table before resting her dainty palms against it. Horace tilted his hat up, revealing a chisel-jawed, stubble covered visage. He grabbed up the camera with rough fingers, bringing it close to his brown eyes. “Well I’ll be a horse’s ass…” he awed, a toothpick sitting between grinning yellow teeth. “Let me see,” said Cliff, taking the tiny camera between his beefy fingers. He squinted at the screen, causing his lips to curl upward. “He just left,” continued Becky. “Heading east.” “Shot of Jack says he’s headin’ fer El Paso,” said Horace. “Two shots says San Antonio,” added Cliff. “Four shots says both,” finished Becky. “Well now!” roared Horace, kicking the card table over and hopping to his feet with glee, pulling a shiny revolver from the back of his jeans and giving the chamber a spin before snapping his wrist, slamming it shut. “Load up yer guns an’ hop on yer horses, boys an’ girls! We got ourselves a manhunt!” The other two chuckled, Cliff loading a double barreled shotgun with red shotgun shells, and Becky thrusting the bolt of a long barreled bolt action rifle into place. “Best keep runnin’, Wade,” said Horace, flicking his toothpick to the floor and eyeing the small image of the Southern Sparkplug with a toothy grin. “There’s a whole lot to be ‘fraid of…” Becky stepped over to Horace, blowing a kiss at the camera. “Happy Valentine’s Day…”
