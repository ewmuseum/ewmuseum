Doing A Line (ppv rp)

14 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 14, 2005 i. This was a mistake. There are too many people around… probably twice the number you thought would be here. What were you thinking coming out here? It seemed like such a good idea at the time, sitting at home alone in your apartment, which—after spending a good portion of the past three weeks on the road at ReVolutions, promotional events or in Nova Scotia training with Amy—remains just as shitty as you remembered it. At least it was private, though. That just wasn’t good enough for you, was it? Nooooo, you had to come downtown and succumb to temptation. Oh crap, here comes a big group. Young people. Specifically, male. The type of people who Jane Reagan used to call the “core audience.” In other words, they’re the people most likely to point, then shout, “Hey! It’s Dave Gibson!” Think fast! You pull the brim of your baseball cap down as far as reasonably possible and keep your head down, pretending to have a sudden and complete fascination with your shoelaces as they traipse past. Phew! That was a close one… You should just start walking. Keep walking. Head for your car. Drive home. Jerk off. And get some rest. After all, you’ve got a big weekend coming up, one of the biggest matches of your career. Sure, it’s no main event. There’ll be no main-event bonus to pocket, no title on the line, no longstanding grudge to settle in front of thousands in a torrent of spilt blood and sweat. No, something different is at stake this Sunday. Survival. Back in the present… you see some motion at the door and allow yourself a brief sigh of relief. If you can get off the street you should be safe. Inside it’s dark and discreet. No one will recognize you then. Not inside, when they’ll be distracted watching their own private fantasies spring to life in front of them. You glance at your watch again, and see it’s been about two minutes since the last time. You start cursing under your breath, until the person in front of you starts to turn around with a funny look on his face. You clam up, and put your head down again. What if someone’s watching right now? What if some wrestling fan—or worse, somebody that runs one of those Internet dirt sheets—is in the mass of humanity that goes ambling past intermittently in ones and pairs? What if he’s got a digital camera? Your heart starts to hammer at the thought. That’s the last thing you need. Dave Gibson, on the eve of his first pay-per-view appearance in PRIME, outed! … As a Star Wars Geek™. ii.At least you can blame Amy for this. Your most recent trip to the Great White North to instruct the Red Raver in the fine art of technical wrestling had produced some interesting results. Amy had actually kept pace with you step for step inside the ring, and then, later that night almost overdosed on a malevolent narcotic cocktail of cocaine, ecstasy and Red Bull. After bringing her back around, you and she talked for hours. The end result? She agreed to come visit you, for the first time, in good old Charlotte, North Carolina after the pay-per-view. She seemed to like the idea of a good, low-key mini-vacation. You like the idea of having her close at hand, after this weekend’s scare. You can understand why she went back on the drugs. Yes, you know about that all too well, don’t you? To this day, waking up in the middle of the night in a cold sweat… not angry, not sick… But THIRSTY. It’s not a jovial “kicking back with a cold one during the game” kind of thirst. It’s not even a “I just ate five hot peppers” desperate kind of thirst. It claws at your throat—harsh, irritating—like your throat’s been coated with sandpaper or you swallowed a handful of freshly cut hair. You might be a wrestler, but you’re also still an alcoholic. Screw putting that “recovering” horseshit in front of it; when you crave booze as often as you do, the old label still fits perfectly. You’ve got your own demons to battle, and they’re tougher to get ahold of than a greased-up deaf guy. Can you help Amy in the shape you’re in? Can you really? Maybe not… But you can try. And you can start when she comes to visit. But maybe you should’ve checked the dates. Because this morning, you got an e-mail… “Dave, did you forget what Wednesday night is? How about picking up some tickets to the midnight premiere? Aimz…” Yep, you’re not the only closet Star Wars fan on the roster. You ARE, however, the only closet Star Wars fan who, after sleeping late and packing for his flight in a hurry, left one very important item in Canada. “P.S. You left your phone charger at my place. I’ll bring it to the pay-per-view.” Dammit. You didn’t even realize! You’re not exactly the type of person to gobble up cell phone minutes in mass quantities. Only then, after reading the e-mail, did you go for the phone. Maybe it still has enough juice to call the Cineplex and buy some tickets online. Flip it open. Dammit, what’s the number? Where’s the phone book? Back to the Internet… where’s Google? Type it in… A-HA! Flip the phone open again. Here we go… 704… 643… 94— SPYOOOOOOOOOOOO SONOFABITCH! You start to throw your phone against the wall but think better of it. Instead you toss it, dearly departed battery and all, onto the couch. PRIME pays well, but not that well. You’ve already used all the money they paid you for your I4 appearances to pay some debt and set Mom up at the hospice. Your credit card’s maxed, so you can’t buy the tickets online. Just write Amy and tell her what happened. She’ll understand. Hell, it’s not like you’re DATING. Rent something instead. But, it IS the last Star Wars movie… You continue to grumble as you find your shoes. iii. You’ve used plenty of different definitions for heaven over the years. It could be a good steak. Or a freshly opened bottle of Jack Daniels. Or the feeling you get after winning a match. Or a blowjob. ESPECIALLY a blowjob. For now, though, heaven is a ticket window manned by an overweight Asian teenager in a badly fitting maroon vest. Just seven people to go, and you can get your tickets and head back home. Make that five… As you step closer to the window, you see a sign taped in the bottom right corner. “Help Wanted… Inquire Within” The sign makes you realize that by the time this movie comes out, you could be looking for work. You and the rest of Team Zameer. Team Zameer… Shit… How did that happen again? You don’t have anything against Azala—hell, with all the food stains you’ve gotten hanging out with him, your dry cleaner probably would like to give the big guy a big sloppy kiss—but weren’t you the obvious choice to lead the team? You might have lost your match, but last week was a good one for your team. You’ve opened up a huge lead on the competition. But it still all comes down to this week. One match. An old-fashioned elimination tag format. On paper, you’d seem to have the advantage. The Black Lotus guys are a full-time team, and you and Azala both have had more than your fair share of tag bouts. But it’s not just about experience. As much as you hate to admit it, especially after all the hours of training, it’s also about luck. And maybe, last week the team used up all its mojo. It’s not fair… you just stepped back into the limelight. You forgot how much you missed the reaction of the crowd. Whether they cheered or they booed, you got off on their reaction so long as they had one. And the buzz it gave you was better than the finest bottle of 15-year-old scotch. You also forgot how much you missed competing in the ring. You enjoyed beating Magnum Wolf—who’d seemed very un-Magnumlike lately—but in a weird way you enjoyed losing to Karina more. You and she stole the show. Plus, you picked up some scouting information for Amy’s use. Wait, hold on a second… “Next please?” the fat girl’s got an eyebrow raised, inquisitively, as you sidle up to the window. As your mouth says “Two for Revenge of the Sith” and your hand reaches for your wallet, your brain makes a sharp right turn and takes a shortcut down an unpaved neural pathway. Maybe you’re not upset because you’ll miss the crowd. Maybe you’re not upset because you’ll miss the action. Maybe you’re not upset because you’ll miss the money, or the fame. After all, business is booming at your wrestling school. You’ve got a full class, and a waiting list of several dozen. It’s amazing what a few weeks of TV time will do—especially on the Playboy channel (there’s that core demographic again.) Maybe, you’re upset because you know that if your team loses this week… You’ll have to say goodbye to her. As you pocket the tickets and start walking to the car, you ponder this revelation. It’s not like you’ve got a thing for her… Do you?
