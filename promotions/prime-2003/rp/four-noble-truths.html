Four Noble Truths

Date: Who cares Buddhism revolves around the cycle of life, better known as samsara. A person can go through life and death many times, yet come back every time void of a particular perspective. Dukkha (suffering) emanates with every blink of the eye until the cycle can be shattered by achieving nirvana. According to the faith, there is a righteous path that Buddhists must put themselves upon. This path contains four characteristics that allow one to join in the company of the enlightened upon completion. They are called the Four Noble truths. The following are the Four Noble truths, according to Devin Shakur. I- Dukkha is life Present Day The only word to describe my father during childhood was horrifying. There was no corner dark enough, no space small enough to contain my body that he could not get to. In the midst of many attacks, the only thing that I saw when I shut my eyes was my mother. Her arms were outstretched, her last ounce of energy being exerted in order to try and save me, to ease me into death… And then it stopped. My vision became blurry, I had a pounding headache, all of my joints ached, and I wanted to go back to being free of pain. If you ask me of my opinion as I write this, I do not believe that my father wanted me to realistically die. He harbored enough strength to strangle life out of me whenever he wished, or to extract the necessary amount of blood from me so that I would not rise again. He always stopped at the brink, giving me a glance of a better life before pulling me back. There were always scars upon my body after an attack. Whenever I had a free moment, I tried to wash them away with soap and water. Feverously I scrubbed away at my skin like no tomorrow existed. Yet, nothing would rid of them. They were permanent, never to go away unless I could tear my skin off. Believe me, that option was considered many times. The next day they would have brothers and sisters to join them. Soon an entire community of horror rested itself upon my body, and it never stopped. No matter how loud my screams were, no matter how many tears I cried, and no matter how much blood I shed, the scars would never stop coming. The common word today is torture, but I knew it by a different term when I was growing up…life. The darkness became my companion many nights. It accepted me for who I was and never judged me. It never cared that I prevented my mother from continuing on a life of happiness. It never cared that I was considered Satan by my father. I was able to be liberal with the darkness. I told it some of my stories and allowed it to alleviate some of my mental pain. I curled up in its endless abyss, letting it rock me to sleep, telling me that it would be okay one day… My father prevented me from ever achieving nirvana as a child. I died each night after what he put me through, and resurrected the next day exactly the same. No reincarnating as something different, just the same old Devin Shakur. I had to come to terms that life was dukkha. II- Dukkha is rooted in desire Reflecting December 12th, 1989 Whenever my eyes opened in the morning, I longed for emancipation. My dreams were clogged with daring escapes from "Alcatraz". In reality, instead of having the San Francisco Bay to contend with, I had to go against the legs of my father. He had more strength, stamina, and speed than I did. He stood feet taller, weighed hundreds of pounds more, and found it in his being to do the unspeakable. The only thing that I worked with was dream, and when the highlight of your day consisted of staring into darkness, the dreams in hindsight weren’t shit. I remember the first time that I struck back at my father. I was just eleven years of age, and since it had been my birthday recently, the punishment that I had received was simply unbearable. My skin stung as if being attacked by kamikaze bees fulfilling their mission to the master. A year ago, I learned to stop crying because that would only invoke the fury to intensify that much more. Pretending to pass out did not work because he had a sense to know when I was faking. I guess when someone has shaped you to become their personal slave for eleven years they have a better interpretation of your actions than most. The room was bitter cold due to the lack of heat in the home, so the lashings that I took were like being stricken with ice. I was so accustomed to this kind of punishment that I cannot remember when my skin was broken and crimson started to pour out onto the bed. We were thirty lashes in. I had only seventy to go and I was receiving the usual admonishment from his Puritan mouth. Jonathan Edwards, of "Sinners in the Hands of an Angry God" fame, would have eventually told my father to shut the fuck up if he had to endure that kind of ranting everyday. My father had the tendency to fixate on one part of the body for a long duration. For the last week he poured the pain into the middle of my back. Upon receiving the thirtieth shot, my eyes rolled into the back of my head, and I swear I heard a voice. To this day I cannot ascertain if it was my mothers, but it told me to fight back. Frederick Douglass, at the age of 16, defeated his master in a two hour struggle because he was sick of being beaten. The master proceeded to never physically confront him ever again. When I turned around to face my father, letting the blood run onto the bed sheets, I had one thought in mind, get out. My right foot extended forward just below his waist. Once it connected, I heard the air escape from his lungs. With limited knowledge of fighting at the time, I could only do to him were things done to me. I threw my flailing hands at his face, hoping to see his skin break, hoping to hear him groan in agony, and hoping to hear him cry. Thirty seconds later, I left him in a heap and made a run for the front door. Just as I was about to step outside, he grabbed my right ankle with his foot and pulled me back inside. What occurred next was only verbal, not physical. I had finally won a battle. However, my one nanosecond of freedom was short lived, because like any mortal I wanted more of it. The lengths that I went to only escalated the beatings once they resumed. My desire was liberation, and I was left with no way to excommunicate it from my being, therefore I had to suffer. III- The way to rid of dukkha is the cessation of desire Reflecting January 19th, 1991 Twelve years of age was I when a new avenue of exploration manifested itself to my eyes. I had just received another five score of lashes, the blood just dried upon my back, and I was guaranteed to have at least four immigrants into the community of white lines. My father gave me a demand, yet to me it was more like a request, one that had no prior precedent, I was to go and get the mail at the end of the driveway. This would be my third exploration into the outside world, where I could look up at that large orange object and wonder how big it really was. I heard that it could bring heat to a vast amount of people at a single given time. Once or twice I watched the object while it descending behind the trees. I believed that it had to sleep and get away from all of the stress that humanity presented. Jubilation in my step was hard to come by, but when I walked up the driveway, I received that sense of liberation: The vast amount of trees in every direction, how I wondered where they came from and how they were able to become so tall. This was the area which regular people were able to roam freely, without being physically taxed for their wish to walk. I took the mail out of the mailbox, put it under my right arm, and went back toward the house. To the left of me I heard laughter from a pack of neighborhood boys. They often played elaborate games of hide and go seek, or threw a football out in this area. Their laughter intrigued me since I did not see anything worthy of that emotion. Perhaps it was something that one of them said which sparked the outbursts. Today, a wrench was thrown into normalcy. The one with the jet black hair saw me walking back toward the house and shouted in my direction. At first, I assumed that he was giving out some kind of command to one of the others, but I did not see movement from any of them. The second shout was followed by a point directly at my being, which got me to stop. I looked over at my home, ensured that the coast was clear, and headed over to interact with the foursome. The shouter with the jet black hair identified himself as Chris Smith, and his comrades were Brandon, Matthew, and Jason. They asked me of my name, to which I responded Devin Shakur. Curiosity got their tongues as their first question was why I never appeared outside? My response should have been worded more carefully, but my mind was not fully cognitive. I said that my father did not allow me to exit the house. Without changing inquisitive expression, Chris asked why? At this point, I put my hands behind my back, as I had seen my father do that a couple of times, and said because he wished for me to stay inside at all times so he could keep an eye on me. They all seemed to understand that and did not retort. What happened next drastically changed my outlook on the world. Jason asked me in a coy manner if I wanted to throw the football around with them, something that I dreamt about. The lack of company in my life was leaving me with mannerisms that bordered depression-esque. My response was, of course, no and I thought that would be the end of the ordeal. At the time I failed to hear my father open up the front door, come down the stairs, and stare at the five of us. The next question belonged to Chris, who asked if I wanted to sneak off once my father was asleep to play video games. Ghastly thoughts ran roughshod through my head as I knew of the repercussions if I were caught going against my father. The other three persisted. They claimed that he would never find out. I sincerely doubted the validity of the claim, knowing my father better than the four of them put together. I later came to the realization that this was a prime example of something called peer pressure. When I said I would try, they told me to meet them at the end of the street once the clock struck midnight. I obliged with a nod and turned around to see my father’s cold eyes staring a hole through me. Chris and company faded into the background while I walked back to the house. Upon getting in my father’s physical presence, he asked me what I was doing with them. I told my first lie when I said "We were just talking". Soon, they would know my dark secrets. I considered this to being converted to a witch ala Salem in the 1690s, going out into the woods late at night and being introduced into what reality was really like. This is cessation of desire because I was no longer going to crave freedom. I was going to actually experience it. IV- Enlightenment can be achieved via finding true happiness Present Day The majority of Western civilization would balk at the notion of having to comply with the Eightfold Path in order to be able to eliminate dukkha. Right speech would eliminate free speech. Right understanding cannot be universally compliant. One never completely understands everything in their life. Right motives…Who can deem the motives right, and can that be seen as accurate in the eyes of others? Right actions and behaviors…You get the point. I’m a categorical imperative bloke myself. My categorical imperative that applies for all is to achieve enlightenment, one must find true happiness. Each person is entitled to their own conclusions as to how to resolve their conflicts, but true happiness must be achieved. The cards that I have been dealt are not exactly those of the envied. Only now do people wish they were in the position that I was in, and it took me twenty five fucking years to get on the right path. Yet, I am still not satisfied today. The cosmological portions of life are pish-posh because I am lacking an internal metaphysical structure. I lack the one thing that a lot of the world rejoices in on a daily basis, love. What would give me that love? The porcelain angel that calls herself Sun Tzu Once I had a woman who I managed to manipulate to the point of marriage, only for me never to have the chance to enjoy her company. She was viciously murdered by an unknown creature, leaving me without anybody to rest my head up against when the times get rough. For the longest while I have thought that I could handle that burden myself, but it is lonely when you are holding a conversation with yourself as a means of appeasement, not to mention pathetic. Despite whatever is said, I am not drawn to her based upon the fact that she is attractive. That is only an addition to the natural beauty that she possesses. She is of a rare breed that really does not adhere to whatever standards are the norm in a society. I look at her and see the personification of independence, and someone that will go to any length to keep that title upon her shoulder. She shunned me many a time and it has been suggested that I move onto someone different. There are other women that would not resist as hard as she has, nor who would shoot me for interfering in one of their affairs. A man who was looking for a quick fix could work his charm on other women, but I am not looking for that. They laugh at my attempts to garner her attention, but I have gotten closer to her than anybody else. She has gone from not knowing who I am, to wishing not to kill me. I’m ahead of 99.9 percent of Western men on that one. By her constant rejection, I can tell she is a card that life does not want me to have. What would I be able to do if I had her in my corner? Becoming her friend is just one step in that direction. Having to humiliate myself by wearing a People’s Liberation Army uniform, doing mundane and degrading jobs is just another. Soon she shall see that I am not out to demean her or anything of the sort, I just want her to love me like I love her. She is what I need to have in order to reach enlightenment. I will go to the ends of the earth for her to love me. Nothing will stop me from making her my true happiness
