You're the Juicebox Guy

29 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-29-2006 Diego rocked back and forth on the unstable chair, a feeble attempt at landing the fourth stubborn leg firmly on the tile floor. “Fuckin’ shit, what are we doin’ here?” The previously unseen man seated in the chair opposing him sighed and slowly shook his head. He slowly placed both his hands on the table separating them, setting his gangly fingers in shallow pyramid. “How many times do I have to tell you?” The man questioned, as if seeking a reprimand from a child. The guy had that look, like he thought he was better than everyone else for some odd reason. Something about compensating for his past or some bullshit. “Why don’t you try telling me for the FIRST time?” “We are here,” the man says as he whirls his finger in a small circle. “To find you a suitable replacement, so when you disappear for your PRIME events no one will the wiser.” Diego, however lazy and unorganized he may appear on the exterior, handles very serious matters in the daily grind of the Delgado Estate. “You mean someone has to, you know,” Diego struggled to comprehend the statement, his eyebrows arched. “Get crunk, and sit in my room rocking the 360?” Okay, so I lied. He does nothing, ever. Like you expected any different. His father, the man notoriously known as El Delgado, has a legend to uphold in the game that Diego has never shown the slightest interest in. Two men, one family, two completely separate lifestyles, their only bond the slowly fading memory of their fallen wife and mother, respectively. One day Diego will bear the right to take over for his father, but his cousin Xavier Torres has taken over the duties destined for him until he is ready. Xavier, the right hand man to El Delgado, has grown to more of a son to Delgado than Diego has. The man nodded slightly, another small sigh escaping. “Precisely.” Diego held his hands in front of his body, seemingly attempting to balance the situation. “Aight, I got ‘at part. But... why here?” For the first time, the rest of the room becomes focused in plain sight. “Well, where else in Chicago can we find mass amounts of Latinos?” The man said, barely able to contain his sly grin. Taco fuckin’ Bell. And not even one of their legit Taco Bells, it’s got a damn KFC attached to it! “You’ve GOT to be shitting me,” Diego sighed and covered his face with his hands, a feeble attempt to hide his embarrassment from himself. “Look around, the people eating are white, those idiots making the food are white, and fuckin’ hell, the guys outside spreading the mulch are fuckin’ white too!” “We’ll see what we can work with,” the man’s voice was quiet, nearly a whisper, trying to bring the conversation back to the table and away from the people craning their necks towards Diego’s outburst and racial slander The duo scanned the room, their eyes darting from face to face before settling back onto each other’s. Together, they sighed. “Fuck.” “Great idea,” Diego couldn’t finish the sentence, as either the man hadn’t told him his name or Diego simply hadn’t taken the time to deposit the name in his memory. “Fan-fucking-tastic. You do know I have a match at 113, and I can’t miss it.” “I know. We’ll find someone.” Diego began to tap his finger incessantly on the tabletop. “Took those idiots this long to give me a shot, hell if I miss it. I’ll be waiting ‘till next Christmas to get another shot.” In the midst on his complaining, Diego hardly noticed the presence now looming directly next to their location. Diego slowly raised his attention to the nuisance, hoping to chase it away. He failed, annoyance prevailed. “Sup niggas?!” Diego closes his eyes, crinkled his nose and shook his head slightly back and forth as if attempting to dispel a foul smell in his nose. “Fuck is wrong with you?” “Sorry, yo. I don’t speak Mexican so y’all gotta bear wit me.” Diego shifted annoyingly in his chair, glancing slightly out the window as a small grin crept across his face. His gaze returned from the window as he focused back upon the pencil pusher assigned to him. “I think we have a winner, give this idiot the prize.” The now angered kid took a deep breath, puffed out his chest, and then readjusted his uniform as he began to sweep underneath the table. “Sue says ya’ll gots to buy some shit or get out, yo.” “Who’s Sue?” asked the pencil pusher, half aware of the conversation. Our g’d up Taco Bell associate pointed towards the front, surprisingly his fingers held no bling. “Oh, she da boss.” They collectively turned their heads towards the front of the restaurant, where an angry lady donning a name tag scripted ‘Sue’ was having a heated discussion with an equally angry man that towered over the counter. Before the thoughts arrived in anyone’s mind, the man shouted out in a rage. “Who know who the hell I am?!” the angered customer shouted. “I’m Mike fucking Ditka, that’s who! If Mike Ditka wants Hi-C, Mike Ditka gets Hi-C!” He slammed down an oversized cup, spraying a red liquid over the dumbfounded manager. The liquid stayed far away from the trademark Bears sweater, however, it seemed to know better. As he took a step back from the counter, the red sticky disaster covered every square inch of the counter space, floor, and person... except him. Oh shit, it was Hi-C. The pencil pusher finally showed a sign of life. “No shit! That’s Mike Ditka!” Diego nodded, not holding half the affection for this man, barely recognizing the name. “He play for the Bulls or something?” “That’s not important right now,” he said as he made his way towards the counter. “All that matters right now is we’ve found the answer to our little problem. Pack your bags, you’re heading to ReVolution.” “What?” Diego attempted to follow the energized man, but shouted behind him instead. “What the hell are you talking about?” “The answer.” Diego folded back into the chair, still unable to balance the damn thing. He slowly began to wipe his eyes, the gravity that he had no idea what was going on began to weigh on him, before he noticed the kid was still standing there. He bent down toward Diego, and spoke softly. “He happy now, don’t tell ‘im I stole this loot.” The young kid produced a small, sleek black wallet before thrusting it in his pocket with a coy grin. What. The. Fuck.
