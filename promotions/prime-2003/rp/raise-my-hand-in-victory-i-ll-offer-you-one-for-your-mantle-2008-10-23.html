raise my hand in victory & i'll offer you one for your mantle

23 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 23 oct 08 “Wake up!” shouts the voice. With a metallic prod to my rib cage, I startle from my slumber. My head feels hollow and muddy. Sit up, finding myself in unfamiliar surroundings. My consciousness kicks in, I realise that this alien environment was in fact the confines of an 8 by 4 cell at the local PD. Prodding me with his nightstick was a man wearing a name badge that read “Randall”. He seems to be enjoying the fact I was inside the cell. His cheeks, fat with apple pie and hot dogs, reddened with the second and third jabs as if they exhausted his chubby arms. “How was yer sleep, champ?” he mocks. “Where am I?” “Tha’s what all y’all say when ya git the mornin’ wake up call, champ. Yer in the Albany PD clink, champ. We found yer in the Motel 6 and brought yer in fer some questionin’. Figurin’ you was too hopped up on celebratin’, we stuck ya in here to sober up so’s we could ask ya some questions when ya woke,” he clarifies. Motel 6? Why did they take me from my motel room? My memory didn’t seem to be helping me out much, let my face rest in the palm of my hand. Then I saw the change in design on my white t-shirt. Mostly it was red. Not paint red. Not dye red. Blood red. “The fuck is this?” I cry, tugging at the front of my shirt for further investigations. My heart stops dull thumping in my chest a few moments. The tiny hairs on my neck prickle with anticipation for memories to come flooding back. But they don’t. Instead my head feels thick with the reminiscence of last night’s festivities. Fuzzy emptiness surrounds my thoughts and I’m just staring, with my jaw gapin’ wide, at this blood my clothes are drenched with. “Y’all don’t remember, champ?” “Quit callin’ me that!” “Ya don’t ‘member what happened?” “No, I don’t remember. Tell me, dammit!” I bark. Shakes his head with his eyes closed like there was something far too important for a lowly cell checker to expose. His head directs my attention to a door with a window in it, in which I can see the to-ing and fro-ing of cops with too much on their plate and too little budget to get more folks to help them out with the buffet. “Don’t think ‘tective Renner gon’be happy if I spoil his thunder now, would he?” My care factor for Detective Renner’s “thunder” borders on zero and fuck all. Stand up, eyes never venturing far from the stains on my shirt. Randall puts a chubby hand on my chest, other hand pushing back and resting on his piece. His happy-go-lucky expression changes to one of unknowing bravado. “Whoa, Nelly! Slow down there, buck-o! Where ya think yer headed, hrmm?” “Ain’t headed nowhere. Tryin’ to figure out the fuck’s goin’ on. This mine?” “What yours?” Randall chokes out through his confusion. “This blood. IS. IT. MINE?” Squints his eyes, trying to figure out if I was legit or it was all a ploy. “You really don’t know?” “This ain’t a fucken gameshow, Pillsbury pin-up. Who’s blood is it?” Randall looks over his right shoulder. Checking for prying ears. Then his left, and then right again, just to be sure doubly. He draws breath and shuts his eyes, drawing on a confidence he never had. Then he exhales all over me. “Samantha Gideon’s.” - | s h e p | - Push through the curtains with the Just Wrestling Championship title belt in my hand. The leather strap drags on the ground after me. Dumbstruck, my prosthetic covers my gaping mouth. I was the Just Wrestling champion! Walk down the corridors and the guys who’d normally pay me no attention at all stand and smile, shouting congratulatory remarks and offering high fives. I can barely respond to the adulation, just wanting to get back to my locker room. Finally push through my locker room door and find a familiar face staring back at me. Samantha Gideon. Grinning from ear-to-ear, wearing an ankle length trench coat that is tied at the waist. I glance fleetingly down the halls, left then right, before stepping in and pulling the door closed. “What’re you doin’ in here?” “Thought you might like to celebrate.” “With you?” Her hand toyed with the ties of her coat, twirling them around. Her thigh playfully pushed through the front of the coat to reveal it’s nakedness. Felt a warmth in my cheeks as my ball-sack began twitching like I were on the way to a full scale salute downstairs. “Wow. Celebrate, huh?” Gideon circles me as I make way across the locker room, her eyes taking sly glances toward my prosthetic every now and again, when she thinks I’m not looking. Her fascination gives me the heebie-jeebies but I try to shrug it off and enjoy my moment. “How’s it feel… champ?” Can’t help but smile in response to her investigative lines. Shrug, trying not to disclose my own infatuation with self-gratification right at this moment. “Bet it feels real good, don’t it?” “Guess so. Been a long time comin’.” “How long?” “Too long. That’s long enough.” Samantha ran her index finger across her bottom lip. Feel the blood rushing away from my head as she looked me up and down. My body glistening with the sweat of success seemed to be stirring her own loins. “Where you staying?” “Why you askin’, Sam?” Her pause was long enough to answer. Her naked shoulder shrugged beneath the coat. Her eyes playfully danced with wanton lust and her chest heaved with her breath quickening. “Never fucked a champion before.” Response took me by surprise and I choked a laugh more consumed by shock than humour in the situation. “Neither have I.” “Let’s make the dream come true for one of us.” I decided it was time to make more dreams come true. - | s h e p | - Detective Renner drew back on his cigarette with the intention using it to grant him some sort of epiphany to the situation. His fingers tapped in succession on a manila folder on the table, causing me the utmost of irritation but granted the situation I was presented with I was more than willing to be forgiving if I were able to leave any time soon. “Now, Mr. Shepherd, yer tryin’ ta tell me that ya don’t got no idea on how this blood come ta bein’ on ya?” Shake my head. Silence was the voice of the guilty man. I didn’t know if I was guilty or not. “No idea, hoss.” “Now ‘scuse me fa findin’ this a little hard ta git me neck ‘round but we got a dead broad on our hands here, Mr. Shepherd, and my only witness --- more ta the point my ONLY suspect --- has no recollection as ta what happened at that Motel 6.” Renner rises to his feet with a puff of smoke, pushing through his own cloud to stare into the mirrored window. Trying to be deep in thought but was no doubt using some type of facial communicatus to the viewers while he had his back to me. “This is one heck of a situation ya gone an’ got yaself inta, Mr. Shepherd.” The corners of his mouth turn down in a ploy to use his silence to draw information from me. I’d watched Goran. Had an idea on how these things worked. Frowny mouthes don’t draw blood from stones. “We need some sorta explanation, y’know?” “I understand, Detective. But I ain’t the type’a guy that goes round talkin’ ‘bout subjects he don’t know nothin’ about, right?” “Right.” He muses with his own thought infrastructure. Putting together the clues; what he knows and assuming about what he doesn’t. He sucks on the end of the cigarette as if it was still going to give him that epiphany. No epiphany. “Let’s assume, Mr. Shepherd, ya ain’t the one to kill her-“ “I like this line of thought.” “Let’s assume there was someone else.” “Don’t you got experts that can tell you about shoes and fingerprints and trajectory and shit?” Renner nods. “We do. But do we need ‘em? I mean, let’s look at the facts. We got us a dead broad an’ it looks ta me… not bein’ an expert or nothin’, but it looks ta me like a hate crime.” Confusion infiltrates my mind and must twist itself into some capacity on my face because Renner continues. “In my years, Mr. Shepherd, the clues don’t point to a feller so bad as they point to you.” “To me? The clues point to me?” “See we got some prelimin’ry findin’s on the body.” I cringe at the thought of a person I know being referred to as ‘the body’. “Your semen’s all in her. Stomach and the like.” I always cringe at the idea of riding bareback. Looks like there’s a new way to keep sex safe. Death, more often than not, hinders the procreation process. “And then there’s the way she died, of course.” “Whaddaya mean of course? I fucked her. So what? You never been laid before, Detective?” “Been laid plenty of times, Mr. Shepherd. But I ain’t never hacked their hand off after I fucked ‘em!” My blood chilled within my body. I shivered and felt my guts wrenching. Renner flipped open the manila folder and grabbed a Polaroid, frisbeeing it at me. The Polaroid hits my chest and dances as it floats to the floor. Staring up from the photo was Samantha Gideon, no longer with the dancing in her eyes. They were vacant. Empty. Dead. Her right hand had been brutally severed on an obscure angle. Her face did not appear to be that of someone who had been barbarically conquered and butchered. There was an air of quiet peace that seemed to resonate on her face. The corners of her mouth upturned into what looked like a smile. It didn’t look panicked. It did not look contorted with pain or anguish. It was peaceful. Like something that were supposed to be. “Hell of a thing lookin’ at a dead person, huh?” Renner’s voice interrupts. Finally breaking the fixation, I had on the Polaroid I simply reply with a nod. “’Specially when ya lookin’ at a broad ya gone an’ killed yaself, right?” “You’ll get a promotion for this brilliant deducation, Sherlock.” “Just workin’ the clues, Mr. Shepherd.” Shake my head. “Statin’ the fucken obvious is not working the clues. What is this? A cheap remake of the Fugitive and nobody bothered to tell me? Blame the one-armed man, right?” Renner scoffs. “Look, Detective. You got a job to do. I can appreciate that. But you’re going about this all wrong.” “How’s Anger-holics Anonymous goin’ for ya, Mr. Shepherd?” Detective Renner’d been hanging onto that dagger for a while and he seemed to be relieved to have gotten it off of his chest. Snatching up another sheet from the manila folder, one that has my picture stapled to it, he clears his throat. “Lemme read yer record, Mr. Shepherd,” declares Renner. “Brock Shepherd. Professional wrestler. Current Just Wrestling champion. Did I offer my congratulations?” “Fuck you.” Renner continues to read. “Suspected of peddlin’ anabolic steroids. Broken up with long time partner, April Monday. Father of one Adam August Shepherd. Allowed to visit once a week. Currently undergoin’ anger management. Known alcoholic. Amputee; missin’ right hand.” The detective looks up from the record he was reading. He whistles a sarcastically impressed whistle. “Well lookee here. Looks like we got an angry drunk without an arm havin’ lady issues. That sound right to you.” I couldn’t have done this. “Would you say, Mr. Shepherd, that person I just read out sounds like someone who’d go carvin’ the hand off a broad?” It wasn’t me. “Doncha think, Mr. Just Wrestling Champion?” Was it?
