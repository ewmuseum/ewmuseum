Chapter 11: And so it is ... and always shall be (Overkill Lavelle vs. Dusk)

Date: June 2007 “There is nothing either good or bad, but thinking makes it so” - William Shakespeare (Hamlet) ---- Kansas, Hume Headquarters Good morning Kansas. Estelle Loughlin here with the final poll count on our states elections. Roger Dumont turned with a raised glass, a crooked smile on his worn features. The tweed coat and leather elbow pads accentuated the old scholar that lay in the depths of his withering image. Rising his glass high, he cheered. “To our next Governor!” “Cheers!” replied Shirley Boxer, a well-mannered and diverse business woman with an astute nature in picking the weak points in a person and building on them to gain a profit in politics. Ballets came in at four a.m. this morning … Dante Hume sat with a taut expression on his usual handsome features. His eyes squinted at the television screen, focused on the plastic blonde holding all the answers to his aspirations and desires. Taking a deep breath he sat stilled in the aromatic flavour of the old study – the scent laced with old books, once smoked cigars and a hint of whiskey. In the background he heard the phone ring but ignored it. His trip to Washington had been expedient and the outcome he wanted came to be. Nothing would compromise his position … nothing. “I believe we should celebrate, Dante,” exclaimed Roger with a husky voice. Dante moved his head, ever so slightly. “Don’t count your chickens before they hatch, old friend.” “Ah true, but nothing wrong with allowing yourself to be merry once in a while, Dante.” Dante turned with a scowling upper lip. “Do not disturb me, old man. Do not.” Raising his hands in a submissive manner, Roger sat into the old leather seat and waited in silence. Knowing full well that when Dante had a mood swing to keep out of his way. The next Governor of Kansas by 430 votes to 429 … Senator Ronald Lang Shirley’s eyes widened and her mouth was instantly dry. One single thought laced through her: ‘Oh fuck’. Dante swirled with venom shoving the plasma television off the glass stand. A fountain of sparks and electrical surges erupted. “One vote. ONE VOTE! That was my seat. Mine damnit!” Roger remained calm. “Please, Dante, relax. You still have a seat in parliament.” “It’s that damn brother of mine. If I hadn’t wasted that time in Washington … I could’ve voted here. He’s ruined me.” “I can ask for a re-count.” “Spare me the humiliation! You ruined me as well, all that campaign rubbish.” Shirley jumped up out of the chair, the sunlight behind reflecting off her red hair, a fire burning in her eyes. “Stuff this job. I quit. You’re on your own.” “FINE!” he screamed with passion. Roger watched as Shirley stormed out of the office, slamming the door behind her. “Where’s the damn alcohol. I need a drink.” “Perhaps you should –“ “Get the hell out of my office, Roger,” he ordered. Roger got up and moved toward him but stopped when Dante turned and pointed with a sharp twist of his arm toward the door. “I said get!” ---- “Conscience is but a word that cowards use, devised at first to keep the strong in awe” – William Shakespeare (King Richard II) ---- Washington D.C. The Inquisitor News Room Pierce Lavelle sat at his small desk in the Inquisitor and gazed at a mound of paper, cataloguing everything he could find on Mary Hume. In the back of his mind he longed to uncover the truth and change the image that had been bestowed on her as an adulteress. Instead, in another pile he gazed at photos of those he loved and cared for. His conscience was torn. His mind was weak. With bleary red eyes and a stubble covered chin he picked up his waste bin and chucked the files on Mary Hume into the thrash. It landed with a resounding thud on the wooden floor; he couldn’t risk all he cared for, not for any story. In front of him he saw an e-mail from Lisa Tyler, reminding him of his match against Dusk. He pushed that thought from his mind, unable to concentrate on two things at once. “Pierce Lavelle, my office, now!” He looked up to see his Editor, Lindsay Fallen, stood with her arms folded at her door. A look of determination shone brightly from his brown eyes. Nodding his head he rose from his chair in defeat. Moving toward her he saw her upper lip quiver and her hair flew around her shoulders like a hive of angry bees. His shoulders slumped and he sighed: ‘I’m in for it now’ – he thought. “Please, take a seat,” she ordered as she sat behind her desk and took a sip from the luke warm coffee. Obliging he sat down; he began to fidget under her scrutinising gaze. He was dressed in a creased suit and his once crisp blue shirt had become nothing more than a creased and stain covered top. His tie hung loosely around his neck, conveying the image of a sleepless and disgruntled employee. “Are you going for a new look, Pierce, because the employee notice requests a proper uniform of sorts? Not the creased ensemble that you’ve been wearing for three days straight.” He nodded and looked up toward her. “I’ve just been having some difficulties getting anything together for the newspaper. My mind’s been elsewhere.” Lindsay Fallen leant into her reclining leather seat, the cool fan on full blast, sent chills against his sweat lined forehead. “You haven’t written a solid piece of writing for this newspaper in over two weeks. Are you on sabbatical?” “No.” “Then why the hold up? I’ve a paper to run and can’t afford to waste time on slackers!” With a shameful sigh he sat forward, hands clasped, and looked into her hazelnut eyes. “I’m sorry, Ms. Fallen.” Lindsay’s hands rested on the table as she inched closer. Shutting her laptop she managed a weak smile and lit a cigarette. The lighter struggling to keep a licking flame as the wind from the fan blew a strong gale against her. “A little birdie told me about a project you’ve been working on, apparently pro-bono. I want you to write that story, understand.” Pierce’s eyes rose wide and he shook his head. “I c-c-can’t … personal reasons. Please, not this story. I’ll write any other-” “You will write this piece on the cold case, or you can pack your stuff and leave. Either way, you’ve a choice to make, Mr. Lavelle.” Nodding his head, he knew he was dismissed by the cold glance he’d received. Stumbling out of her office he felt warm, tense and his breathing increased. Loosening the tie even more he moved toward his desk and with sweaty palms picked up the telephone. “I think it’s time we met,” he said quietly. “Our Lady’s Church on 32nd street.” Feeling defeated and at a loss for words, he grabbed his car keys and left the news room. ---- “So they lov’d as love in twain. Had the essence but in one. Two distinct, divisions none.” – William Shakespeare (Poetry) ---- Washington D.C. On route to Our Lady’s Church The car felt moist, despite the air-conditioning unit working tirelessly to cool the humid air, he felt sticky and debilitated. He put it down to nerves and switched the air-conditioning up another cycle. With his eyes glued to the road he couldn’t stop thinking about the morale dilemma he’d been placed into. You’ve got a choice to make … Her words rang clearly through his mind, causing him to ache, and a ferocious headache to build. He knew it was not as much a choice he’d have to make but a decision based on pieces of paper still at his desk. The traffic lights switched to red and he found himself stuck in a back log of traffic, bottlenecked all the way through Capitol Hill. Switching the car into park he allowed the cool air to wash over the beads of sweat that ran down his neck and stained his collar. Closing his eyes he took a deep breath. He felt his mobile vibrate in his pocket and a hesitation rustled through him. He gripped the vibrating Motorola. Flipping the phone open and hearing the sweet voice on the other end he sighed with relief. “Not who you were expecting?” she asked. “Just having a really bad day, Dawn.” The lights switched to green and he cranked the car into drive and moved off, watching the cars behind him he indicated left and began to move off. “You want to talk about it?” she asked, sweetly. “My Editor is pressuring me. I’m at a crossroads.” “You want me to pay her a visit?” Pierce smiled for the first time in a few days. “Have you been watching the Godfather?” “I was trying my best Michael Sloan impression,” she replied. “I know you’ll figure it all out. Just try to remain positive. I trust you.” Swinging the car down 30th Street he found another line of traffic and cursed as his meeting was to take place in a few minutes and the more he drove, the more he found he was getting nowhere fast enough. “I looked into the tape when I got back to New York, I’m afraid I have some bad news. Sorry to discuss this now, but I’m on the clock.” “I understand. Could you get anything?” he asked. “Whoever he was, he used a very hi-tech decoder so when his voice was analysed it was muddled but forensics were able to pick up background noises,” she announced. He heard her shuffling through some files. “There are car horns, music and something odd …” “What?” he asked as he swung his car into a parking lot. He could see the church across the road and locked his car. “Kansas News show was in the background, a weather report.” Confusion furrowed his sweat lined brow as he stepped in to the mid-day sun. Sliding his glasses on to shield his eyes from the intense sun light he moved with a swift pace and relished the cold breeze that blew against his cheeks. “I have no clue who it is, but I have a feeling I’m about to find out.” He heard the tone in her voice change, worried, she spoke. “What do you mean ‘find out’?” “It’s going to be fine, Dawn. You just look after yourself and I’ll see you on Tuesday.” “Pierce … Promise me you won’t do anything stupid.” He stopped at the church gates, his body frozen in place. “I won’t if you won’t. I’ll see you soon, sweetheart.” He shut the phone and with a firm hand gripped the wrought iron gates. ---- “There is a tide in the affairs of men. Which taken at the flood, leads on to fortune.” – William Shakespeare (Julius Caesar) ---- Washington D.C. Our Lady’s Church Carefully he climbed the steps of the church, shaded by the towering columns, cylindrical and daunting as they watched over him, covering him in darkness. The brass handle felt cold under his touch as he opened the door. A droplet of sweat dangled from the tip of his nose. As he stepped inside, he immediately became overwhelmed with the smell of incense and the heat. The licking candles by the statue of Mary guided his path through a small refectory. In the midst of the basking sunshine, he stood in the centre of the church, bathed in an array of colours dancing on the floor around him. In the distance he saw an old man knelt in a row of seats, his head dipped into clasped hands. Remaining cautious but abrasive, he approached. The man’s head raised and he spoke, a quiet and dulcet tone. “I haven’t been to mass since the day you were christened,” he said, his words echoed around the emptied church. “Although I hear your sister converted to Hebrew. Your mother never liked my choice in making you both Roman Catholic.” Pierce stood at the edge of the row, frozen in place. In the pit of his stomach he managed to muster enough might to speak. “I didn’t come here to discuss my up-bringing; this is a lot more serious than that.” Rising from the padded knee rest, his father sat on the uncomfortable oak seat and gazed at the altar. “I know why you are here, son. I’m glad you called.” “I’m not, but it is you that has put me in this position and now I need your help,” Pierce admitted as he sat down on the seat, shoulder to shoulder with his father, he gazed to the mural of the Holy Trinity and held a stilled heart. “Call it the conscience of an old man, but I have changed. I will do anything to help you, son.” “Are you aware my family, friends and girlfriend are targets?” Pierce questioned and watched his father nod his head. “What I want from is the truth … about everything. We’ve been apart so long our past is forgotten, I just want to know what really happened to Mary Hume and who the hell is threatening me.” His father, Bradley Lavelle, pulled from his long coat a few sheets of folded up paper and clasped them in his wrinkled hands. In his other hand he held a small silver chain carrying St. Anthony. The papers were frayed, worn over time. His eyes were no longer hollow and cold, they held a remorseful glow in the summer sun. “I did not kill Mary. I loved her very much. I got her killed because I wanted her to come away with me, to take the money with us and start over. My plan had been originally to rip them off, but that changed when I met her. I was young and in love,” he whispered to the floor. “On May 11th 1978 I had rented a motel room just outside the small village where the Hume’s had their mansion. I had two plane tickets and was waiting for her. Joseph Hume was a man brought up in a world of fortune and his father taught him that in life you have to either kill or be killed as the world is an unforgiving place. When he found out about Mary’s affair and her new born child, he followed her to the motel room and I watched on helplessly as he shot her.” “So you were innocent?” Pierce asked, listening to his father’s story. “Not entirely. I was scared, young and foolish. He gave me the money she took as payment for me to keep quiet,” he said with a whimpering sob. “I took the money and fled. To this day I’ve held onto these four pages outlining in greater detail the sordid truth.” “Why didn’t you tell mom or me any of this? Why didn’t you bring it up two weeks ago when you confronted me?” His father turned with a quivering bottom lip. “I tried to deal with this quietly, save the humiliation of my mistakes, because I made another mistake that night. I left my child behind in the hands of that murdering recluse. You have a brother, Pierce. A man named Dante Hume. He is the one behind the threatening tape. I tried to handle this myself but he isn’t the once kind and gentle man he used to be, he’s lost and confused.” Pierce stared at the altar, looking away from his fathers prying eyes. He couldn’t understand his feelings, a surge of emotions fluttered through him. Thoughts filled him like a sponge soaked in water. “Why didn’t you mention any of this? You knew the truth and you stayed silent, even after I’d been warned. Does he – my brother – live in Kansas by any chance?” “Yes,” he replied softly. His father dipped his head, guilt stricken. “It wasn’t your burden to bear, son. I’m sorry I got you involved.” “I’m paying for it now,” he replied. He let the comment linger in the silence of the idyllic church; a quiet and gentle atmosphere embodied them. With a shaken hand his father placed a weak grip on Pierce’s shoulder and nodded his head. “I’m proud of you son. I know I wasn’t a good man to you, Marie and Becca, but you are a good man son. I’ve watched you wrestling since you first started, and I am proud to call you my son. Just know one thing … I do love you and your sister very much, and I hope one day you can understand all this and forgive me.” Pierce turned with worried eyes, for the first time he looked at his father without fear or anger. “I – I – What’re you going to do, dad?” With a weak smile at being called ‘dad’, Bradley Lavelle spoke with passion. “You should write this story. Give Mary the peace she deserves and clear my name. Be the man I know you are …” he inched closer. “…I love you son.” He embraced his son with a tight grip, necklace sliding from his fingers. From his pocket he produced a tazer taking Pierce off guard in the middle of an embrace, driving it into Pierce’s arm, the tazer came to life, and an electrical current pulsated against his arm. Pierce’s eyes went wide and he slumped into his father’s grasp. Placing the implement back into his pocket, he slid his son into an upright position and placed in his hand the sheets of paper. “I will fix this. I am sorry son, for everything. One day I hope you will forgive me,” he whispered into his son’s ear and kissed his forehead and draped the cross of St. Anthony necklace over his son’s head. “He will protect you now, son.” Kneeling down once more, Bradley Lavelle gazed toward the cross that rested above a golden chalice and whispered a small prayer. Oh heavenly Father, lead me on the path of right. I walk alone and stumble in the dark. Show me the way and I shall follow you. Let me find peace in mine own heart, and save me from mine enemies. Blessing himself he moved away from the row of chairs and down toward the entrance, he looked back once more to his son with a saddened feeling. He felt the weight of the Glock in the small of his back as he gripped the door handle, and with a mournful sigh he opened the door. ---- “When sorrows come, they come not as single spies, but as battalions.” – William Shakespeare (Hamlet) ---- Washington D.C. Our Lady’s Church With a sharp tingle in his arm Pierce awoke to a filled church, the evening sunshine cast a shadow over him. His eyes focused on the sheets of paper now clasped in a firm grip, he felt a cold metal against the back of his neck and his finger tips looped around the necklace now splayed over him. His father had given him quite a shock but he understood now, worry spread through him as he rose from the row of seats, despite the looks from the people listening to the Priests sermon, he ran struggling to keep the hurt he felt from welling up. Stood atop the stairs he watched as the world around him moved with a fluid pace, time passing quickly. Stood in the cooling evening air he gripped his phone and ran toward his car, pressing his father’s mobile number he listened to it ring … … And ring … And ring The person you are calling is not available at the moment, please leave a message after the tone. Pierce felt a knot rise in his throat, fumbling with the car keys, he left a message. “Please pick up the phone, dad. Don’t do anything stupid. Pick up, dad. Pick up.” ---- “As he was valiant, I honour him. As he was ambitious, I slew him.” – William Shakespeare (Julius Caesar) ---- Kansas City, Hume Headquarters A few hours later … With his image mirrored by the crackling thunder in the glass structure, he moved with dripping clothes down the long glass corridor. His heart pounded inside his chest and the gun felt heavy in his feeble fingers. The room was quiet, the building deserted and the weather roared a mournful cry above him. He froze by the thin double doors and gazed upward toward heaven, his only witness. With a strong grasp he opened the doors and entered a darkened room, the smell of booze rampant in the old study. Shuffling into its quarters he saw his son seated in the leather chair with a whiskey bottle in his hand, slumped forward and sobbing mournfully into his lap. As the thunder cracked it illuminated the study – strewn with books and antiques, which now lay in pieces on the oak flooring. “I – I – I – lost … everything! It’s all his … fault,” Dante whispered through sobbing breaths. “I knew … I knew you’d come.” “Let’s go home, Dante. Forget the campaigns and your step-father, we can leave.” Dante’s head rose, his dark eyes laced in red and salty tears. In his hand he held his father’s old Smith & Wesson .38 Police Special. The very gun used to kill his mother. “Don’t you see, I am home. This is who I am, who I was destined to be.” “No, this is not who you are. You don’t threaten people, you’re a good man, Dante.” Dante laughed between heaving sighs. “I’m not my brother, dad. I killed my step-father and I’ll kill my brother.” “We can fix this all, son. We can go to the police and end your suffering. We can sort it all out.” “And go to prison? No. He deserved to die. He deserved to …” he sobbed. Bradley Lavelle inched closer, Dante still frozen on the chair, swaying from side to side. “I won’t let them do that, I promise. We can leave here together and make amends. Tell the truth, son.” Dante rose wearily from the leather sofa and with a shaking hand pointed the gun at his father. “I’m not … stupid!” he groaned. “You … You love him more. That’s why you want me gone, because you think I’ll hurt him.” “No Dante. I know you’ll hurt him. You threatened my real family, that wasn’t smart.” “What’s so goddamn great about your real son?” Bradley Lavelle shook his head and shut the double doors behind him. “He’s a good man, Dante. He doesn’t deserve to have our misfortunes. He knows everything. I want him to write our story.” Dante chucked the whiskey bottle aside, fury rising within him. “NO! He can’t … He’ll ruin everything I’ve hidden. I won’t let him.” “I let you down, son. Not Pierce. I left you behind.” “He ruined … me … He ruined … everything,” Dante mumbled between sobs, a drunken slur for words. “He must … pay.” Dante watched as his father raised the silver Glock; it gleamed in the moons unkind glow. “I can’t let you hurt him or anybody else, son.” He pointed it toward his son with a shaken hand. “Put down the gun son, you are a kind man.” “No dad…He can’t write the story…He can’t.” “Please, Dante. Listen to me, son. You’re better than this,” he directed. Dante inched closer, crying and shaking his head. “You’re naïve. What happened to the man that hated his children? Why are you trying to help your son that hates you?” Bradley Lavelle put the gun down; he couldn’t hold it at his son. “He doesn’t hate me, son. He missed me. He forgives me. Who will forgive you?” He raised the gun at his father, aiming it at his chest. “You can never love me, father. I know … now. I know.” “You’re right, maybe I can’t ever love anybody again, but I can forgive son. So can you?” “No, I can’t let you do this to me. I can’t.” Bradley Lavelle held his son’s gaze. “What are you going to do, son? Shoot me?” Dante managed a crooked grin; an evil smile laced his lips. “Actually … yes. If I can’t have you. He can’t. Kill or be killed right father …?” Bradley Lavelle’s eyes went wide, terror struck him. Words of a past had come to haunt him. He watched his son’s eyes, empty and cold. “Damnit! Stop this madness, Dante.” Bradley raised his own gun and moved closer to Dante but Dante stood with a shaken grip and Bradley watched as the thunder behind illuminated the monstrous image of his son, his hair strewn and matted. Tears dried on his cheek. Alcohol laced into his top, he smiled. BANG! The trigger went, his hand bounced back, the bullet left the barrel and thundered toward Dante. The Smith & Wesson .38 Police Special exploded as Dante fell to the floor with a slow and painful fall. The bullet hit Bradley in the chest, a burning sensation ripped through his skin. Dante’s screams of pain echoed around him, but he felt cold and tired, the bullet piercing through his chest. Gazing upward at the sky above him, Bradley Lavelle closed his eyes. ---- “Men at some time are masters of their fate.” – William Shakespeare (Julius Caesar) ---- Five Days Later Washington State Penitentiary Pierce Lavelle sat in his car and gazed at the tall gates of the Washington State Penitentiary, his hands gripped the steering wheel tautly, his fingernails digging into the leather rim. Sighing he gripped the St. Anthony necklace for reassurance, and placed it back on his neck. Holding the Inquisitor newspaper in his grip he entered the cold and uninviting prison. His eyes bent to the ground, fixated on his shoes as he moved with a swift and sturdy posture. An officer approached him, his uniform a deep brown and laced with sweat marks. Removing his cap he gazed at Pierce Lavelle. “Can I help you?” “I’m here to see Dante Hume,” he replied. The officer nodded his head and gestured for Pierce to follow. Ignoring the jeering and chanting by the inmates, Pierce followed the officer to a small unit of cubicles. Each cubicle separated by thick plastic and a small telephone as a means of communication. He stood frozen a moment, eyeing the room with hesitation. “Cubicle three, he’s been waiting for you.” Pierce heaved a sigh and moved forward. He inched closer to the cubicle. Raising his eyes he came face-to-face with Dante Hume and sat down. Hume was dressed in an orange jumpsuit, the silver glint of handcuffs shone in the dirt covered lamp above them. His left arm remained in a sling, his fathers aim had been off and Dante had been shot in the arm. Dante held a chilling smirk as he gripped the phone. Pierce held the phone to his ear; sadness lined the pit of his stomach. “It’s good to finally meet you brother. I didn’t see you at the trial,” Dante said with a menacing lisp. “I was there,” Pierce sat vehemently. “I was good, no? All that acting about the abuse I got. Court bought it,” Dante sneered into the phone, his eyes hollow. Pierce inched closer. “No, they didn’t. You’re in here aren’t you?” “Look at me, little brother. I get three meals a day and warm bed. This isn’t punishment,” Dante said with a snickering laugh. He inched closer, his breath moist against the plastic. “How was the funeral? Did you cry for a worthless man?” Pierce remained in position, anger welling up inside him. His thought of coming here to see the man that caused so much hurt and pain in his life was now leaving him nauseous. He longed to be back in his car. “He may not have been perfect, but he changed and he tried to be a good man. That’s more than I can say for you or any Hume member.” “A good man! You sound just like him, you know that?” Dante said. “Such miserable bullshit. Nobody is always a good man. How many mistakes have you made in your life, little brother?” “Everyone makes mistakes, Dante. You’ll pay for yours.” Dante inched closer, his eyes lifeless. “Why did you come here, little brother?” Pierce held up the Inquisitor against the plastic window, the headline read: A SECRET EXPOSED AND A DEATHLY TRUTH REVEALED As MARY HUME’S PAST COMES BACK TO HAUNT THE HUME FAMILY … Dante’s eyes rose wide. “He said you’d do it. I didn’t think you’d have the guts.” “They deserved to have their story told. Everything’s in there, Mr. Hume.” Dante laughed into the phone. “You fail to see the important thing in all this little brother.” Pierce looked at Dante with piercing eyes. “The court has ruled the death penalty for the murders of Joseph Hume and Bradley Lavelle. There is nothing important here, but justice. You are going to pay for what you’ve done,” Pierce said, coyly. Dante continued to laugh, Pierce’s words having no effect. “Don’t you see littler brother, now we are free. We are free!” Pierce put the phone down and slid the article through the grating; he walked away and could hear his brother’s wailing cries from behind the metal bars as he was escorted back to his cell. Pierce left the prison with a sigh of relief, his heart raced in his chest. ---- “Pray now, forget and forgive.” – William Shakespeare (King Lear) ---- Washington, D.C. Lindsay Fallen sat behind her desk, basking in the sunshine, with a giant smile across her face. In her hand she held the latest edition of her newspaper and read the page one article. The very article that had caused wide spread recognition for the Inquisitor, but that thought and recognition came at a price for the author. Sitting up, having finished the article Lindsay stretched her arms and opened her two-way. A blinking light rose and she spoke. “Hailey, is Pierce in yet?” “Yeah, he’s at his desk, you want to speak with him?” Hailey asked. “Yeah, we still on for lunch?” “Of course.” Putting the phone down Lindsay sat back and folded the paper up. There was a knock at the door and Pierce Lavelle entered, dressed in a clean shirt and suit trousers he held a small smile on his face. Fallen stood up and approached. “It’s good to see you up and about,” she said as she shook his hand. “Please, take a seat.” Pierce did as instructed and sat into the leather seat he sat in no longer than three days ago. Feeling a little less on edge, he looked at Lindsay with curious eyes. “How are you doing, since –“ “Since my father’s death? I’m getting there, just looking after my family at the moment. His funeral was two days ago.” Lindsay noticed how he seemed more confident in himself. His appearance and approach to life had all changed and she realised her choice had been right. She only hoped he’d appreciate the promotion she was about to give him. “I know the article came at a price for you, but as a career move, you have risen greatly.” “I didn’t do it for the work. I did it for my father and Mary Hume.” “I understand that and that’s why I have this proposition for you.” Pierce’s eyebrows furrowed and he inched closer. “What proposition?” “You bring something to this newspaper others don’t. You bring passion and truth to the untold stories. So, I want to give you the opportunity to become a freelance journalist for this newspaper.” “Freelance? Meaning I can move all over the place and take files from anywhere.” Lindsay Fallen sat forward, hands clasped with a grin. “Exactly. So what do you think?” she asked. Pierce smiled, a little relieved at now being able to focus on his wrestling career without the time constraints of a nine-to-five job. “Thank you.” “Just one thing, how are your air-miles?” “Not too good at the moment, why do you ask?” Hailey entered the room with a big grin; she placed a hand on his shoulder. “Because Lindsay and I think you should go to New York. Enjoy life and whenever you want, write a story. Your bedroom will still be at the apartment and we’ll still be here, but you deserve to go to New York.” Pierce’s eyes widened. “You guys got me a freelance job in the Inquisitor branch in New York?” Lindsay smiled and nodded her head. “Yeah, we did. Now don’t say I’m not nice.” Pierce smiled and shook Lindsay Fallen’s hand. “You have the rest of the day off Lavelle, so enjoy.” Pierce exited the Inquisitor with a smile on his face as he turned into the parking lot. With his mind clear of pressure and worry, he could focus on his upcoming match and looking after his family.
