Token of Rejection

Date: Last week June 12, 2005 Errands. Hell, you never liked them. Get in, get done as quickly as possible and get out. That’s always been your strategy—whether it was a trip to the grocery store or the barber (at least that explains the close-cropped haircut people have given you grief about for so long.) The door opens automatically in front of you and you walk into the store. The smell of industrial cleanser and something else… there, from the display case to your left—christ, who would need THAT many cinnamon buns? You pull your baseball cap low and head deeper into the store. Down one aisle, then the next. Then, straight ahead, you see the counter currently being teenagered—“manned” would be too kind a word—by a stringy kid with a rash of pimples across his forehead. “Yeah, here for a pickup,” you say as you hand over the photo slip. The kid nods and goes to check without saying a word. That’s the way you like it, especially on this errand. He comes back with a large envelope. You open it, check the contents and nod as he punches up your total on the cash register. “That’ll be $15.47.” Tucking the envelope protectively under your arm, you turn and start looking for the picture frames. And promptly take a left turn into the women’s clothing, as some dietetically advanced lady is modeling a pair of stretch pants. Shuddering, you leave the department, wondering why they can’t lay out every Wal-Mart the same way. June 13, 2005 Inbetween settling into your seat and reaching for the other end of your seat belt, you don’t hear the guy next to you talking until the third time he repeats himself. “What was that?” you say. “I said you don’t look that old.” You look up and see a round-headed man, with beady eyes behind glasses, staring at you with a slightly dim smile on his face. He’s chuckling at some inside joke you’re either too tired to grasp or too disinterested to care about. “You’re seventy… 7D, get it?” The guy is pointing at your seat label, on the other side of the armrest you share. “Yeah… yeah, I’m definitely not 70.” “HAHAHA I got ya there, didn’t I?” “Um… sure. You sure did.” “Hehehe, I got you!” The guy claps his hands twice before pulling a battered Louis L’Amour paperback from a fanny pack around his waist. It appears your seatmate has that combination of extreme friendliness and a slightly dim intelligence. On a flight from Charlotte to Vancouver. Oh, goody. You pull down the armrest as discreetly as possible and go to your bag, double-checking the placement of the item in the plastic bag. “Sposed to go under the seat.” You pull down one flap of the plastic bag and take a look at what’s inside. They did a good job with the enlarging. Looking at the picture, you can almost feel the friendly warmth of the sunshine. The warmth of the faces and body language of the people in the photo is evident enough. You found the picture a few weeks back, after Amy stood you up on the promised visit to Charlotte. Just another shot in a bloated pocket photo album. You’ve met one of her brothers—the powerfliter with the arms twice the size of yours who threatened to “crush your head like a grape” if you hurt his sister. He smiled when he said it, which makes you think it was just a good-natured joke. At least, you hope it was. You’ve been waiting for this. The perfect gift for Amy. Even before you had money, you always thought you were a pretty good gift giver. Not just getting people what they wanted—any schmuck could get the hot CD or Xbox game of the moment and make somebody temporarily happy. But, when your cousin got pregnant, you got her and her husband a Diaper Genie. Something they could use, and use every day. Three months later, the kid was born and five months after that, your cousin ditched the man but kept the Diaper Genie. Luckily your self-esteem was high enough that you didn’t mind the perpetual association between yourself and baby shit. You? You liked gifts with memories. Wasn’t it obvious? And there were plenty of memories here. You didn’t expect Amy to see the photo and run into your arms. You hoped for it, but you didn’t expect it. By now, life had thrown too many curveballs of disappolintment down the ole pike for you to think it’d be that easy. But… at the very least… she’d see that you cared. And that was something, right? “Hey mister,” another tug on your sleeve. “Sposed to put your bags under the seat. That’s the law on airplanes—like bucklin’ your seat belt.” “Right.” You force a smile onto your face and stow the bag carefully to protect the framed photo. “So what you gonna get to drink when they come by, mister?” “Ummm… nothing.” Your seatmate’s face falls. “NOTHIN?!” “Yeah… I’m kinda tired.” You shut your eyes and wish for some quick sleep. But as soon as your eyelids drop, you see the cold stare of Clay Vendetta burning a hole through your mind’s eye. “Oh boy! Shark Tale! I know every line of that movie…” It’s gonna be a LONG flight. Even before you had money, you liked to think of yourself as a pretty good gift giver. June 16, 2005 Above all else, you’re a creature of habit—and it didn’t take long after joining PRIME to adopt some new ones. After your debut with the rest of the I4 guys, you slept late and didn’t bother to set an alarm clock. Wanted to give your body plenty of time to heal itself. But last night you couldn’t sleep at all. You tried counting sheep, taking a hot shower, watching TV until you drifted away. Nothing worked. Briefly, you even considered raiding the mini bar for one of those tiny bottles of bourbon. Hey, it was just one bottle, right? Thankfully the rest of your brain—the rational part—reasserted itself a few seconds later. Truth be told? You couldn’t sleep because you were excited. No, that wasn’t entirely right. You were NERVOUS. But it was a good kind of nervous. A Christmas morning kind of nervous, the same sort of eager anxiety you felt before making your presence felt in the first Mt. St. Hell’s End Match in your OSW debut—the night you really arrived in the business. After picking at a room-service breakfast, reading the same two pages of your book 20 times over, packing your bags and storing them downstairs with the bellman for your shuttle to the airport for tonight’s flight home, you decided you’d waited long enough. It was time. You kept the framed picture in a plain plastic shopping bag, more so no one else might make notice of it. The last thing you wanted was for Amy to find out about your present ahead of time. Your brain wanders off on some unpaved neural pathway and you dimly register what’s going on around you and what your body does in the meantime. Taking the elevator to her floor. Walking down the hall to her room. Then, knocking on the door. Not hearing any response, you try the handle, jiggling it back and forth. Nothing. “Ames! It's Dave. Wanna, uh... lemme in?” You hear something. Maybe a whisper? Hard to tell from out here, but definitely a reaction. “Just a sec, Gibbo. Gotta... uhh... need to get some clothes, yeah...” Seconds later, the door opens and you see your good friend clad in the finest bed linen the hotel has to offer. An interesting wardrobe choice… “All right, what’s going on?” you say. It might be a fancy hotel, but you start smelling the rat the moment you step into the room. Seeing Amy stand there, slightly rumpled and red-faced clad only in a bed sheet, was the first hint. “And don’t even think about playing innocent with me. Finding a naked Amy wrapped up in a bed sheet is like finding Michael Jackson leaving a nursery school—you just KNOW somebody’s been up to no good.” Gibson keeps his stern look, but it quickly dissolves into a smile. “Dammit… I can’t play bad cop with you. We are NEVER allowed to play poker together. Seriously though, what’s up with the hot chick burrito look you’ve got going on?” Then there was the smell. That familiar smell of sweat and… ''I... uh, what - I'm not allowed to sleep naked? It's not my fault that you like to prance in here at all hours of the morning, expecting me to be awake.'' Oh yeah, something is definitely not right. ''First of all, it's three o'clock. Second thing is that you told me you needed to get some clothes on... but a shirt's the last thing I'm lookin' at right now. Spill it.'' Amy bites her lip nervously in reaction. ''Have you been drinking, Dave? Yeesh, you're way too hard on me when you get like this.'' What was that? For a second, as you stare at the petite redhead, all you can see is red. How could she say that? How could she go there? You feel your right hand drawing into a fist. “No,” you say, trying to choose your words carefully before you say something you regret. “I. Have not. Been. Drinking.” You look down at the bag, then at the almost defiant look in Amy’s eyes, and realize this was a mistake. “I had something I wanted to show you, but maybe this isn’t the best time. Neverm—” CLATTER You slowly turn to face the bathroom door. “What was that?” Amy’s talking a mile a minute—something about shampoo. You’d know more if you were actually paying attention. You’re more focused on reaching for the doorknob. She dives at you as you reach. ''Relax, man... it wasn't anything. Now, back to me being a bitch...'' Slam! A slim hand presses up against the side of the doorway next to you, and the other hand presses against the other side. Now, you KNOW something’s going on. “That sounded pretty loud for a shampoo bottle,” you say trying to sound casual. “LOOK, Dave, don’t change the subject,” she replied. “I’m bus—EEEP!” Poor Amy. She’s learned a lot about wrestling in the past few months—but she can still be susceptible to the most basic amateur wrestling tactics. You hoist Amy onto your shoulder and push the door open. And just like that, the bottom falls out. Back on the immediate plane of existence, you sense Amy twisting out of your grip. You, however, are too busy staring down the Universal Champion standing in the middle of your friend’s bathroom. ''Look, Dave...'' Amy starts to say, ''You're my best friend, I would've told you really soon. It's just... this, it just happened, you know? And I didn't want anybody to know about it until I knew how to deal with it. It wouldn't do public opinion of me any good at all, and it might even hurt Killean's reputation. That, and I didn't need you thinking less of me or making any assumptions that I was... well... the kind of person who engages in this stuff, and...'' CRASH! The framed photo hits the floor hard. You can’t deal with this now. Not any of it. You’ve got to get out of this room, out of Winnipeg, and you’ve got to do it right now. As you depart, you dimly feel the glass of the frame crunch under your heel—and you wonder if something in your heart gave at the same time. * * * * * “Hey! I know you!” The same fanny-packed, L’Amour fan is sitting on the other side of the aisle as you drop into your seat. “We were on the plane here, I remember!” he said with a broad smile. “Yes, that’s about right.” “You have fun on your trip? I love it here!” “Nope. No, I really didn’t have much fun, pal.” “You do a lot of flyin’?” It’s important for a man to know his limits. You can take getting beaten down by Los Diablos in the middle of the ring. You can take Azala stuffing a dead boar, whole, into your locker. As it turns out, you can even take a betrayal by the woman you care for—at least without pulling an O.J. right there on the spot. But after all that, to be on one of these planes, with this guy, AGAIN… That, you most definitely cannot take. “Yeah, I fly a lot for my job,” you say, awaiting the answer. “Oh, what you do for work?” “I try to beat men into oblivion and, if I can, break their arms or legs.” It was a nice and quiet flight home.
