Denial

15 June 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 15th, 2007 “Never underestimate the power of denial.” - Wes Bently The police report just sits there before him, the words never changing. He had read the damn thing ten times now and yet the words on the paper remained the same. The variables of the world could change a billion times, yet those words that haunted him so relentlessly kept telling him the same thing; that the amount of blood in the carpet of his house confirmed to them that Sarah could no longer be living. Craig had tried to find one of those variables in the police report, but none existed. Not to them. Yet, to him, they were there. You just had to find them. Craig pulls himself up out of his chair inside of the hotel he’s been residing in for the past two weeks, and slowly walks over to a nearby window. Two weeks had passed since Sarah had been supposedly murdered in Craig’s own house. Her body though had not been there after he returned from the hospital after being shot in the midsection by an unknown man. Instead, there was just a note from the man he had saved Sarah from, telling Craig that since he had taken from Daniel that Daniel had taken from him in the form of Sarah’s life. However, it was easier to tell someone that a person was dead then to prove it. The police report weren’t facts or proof that she was dead. Instead, they were made by a bunch of detectives who could care less if a former drug addict was dead or not. That was why Craig believed that she was still alive. He had to believe it because once before he thought she was dead, and she turned out not to be. He owed her this much, especially after everything she had experienced in his life. Tonight would be another evening of PRIME ReVolution, yet he could care less. He had been given rather specific instructions these past weeks on when to show up to the arena and when he would have to leave it. He was not to interact with members of the roster unless instructed to in the form of a match. By PRIME he was seen as a liability and the only reason they hadn’t released him yet was that he had a contract that was iron-clad. It only lasted a year though, and quickly that time was coming. He was certain his time in PRIME wouldn’t last for long as Lisa Tyler and others had it out for him. However, he was certain that in the end he would get the last laugh. In this moment though, he could care less about a silly match. Instead, he remained focus, awaiting word from the several detectives he has working on this case while following leads of his own that he has yet to share with anyone. Then, almost as if on cue, his cell phone rings nearby. Slowly, he walks over to it before putting the phone to his ear. “Hello?” he says as he continues to look out at the window, trying to make sense of it all. “Craig, its Martin,” the man on the other end said. Craig’s face or reflection in his voice changed not one bit. “What have you found out?” Craig asked the man on the other line, hoping that there was good news. Yet, from the sound in the man’s voice, he knew it could be anything but. “The leads you provided me with are cold. Daniel Santiago has disappeared off the map. How does a multi-billionaire disappear of the map?” the detective asks him and Craig can only remain silent. A man with that kind of money could easily disappear. If Craig wanted to, he could disappear for life as well. So, now he had to think of a man like Santiago’s stature in terms of power and money. It sickened him, but he knew it was the only way. “Fine, but keep looking,” he tells the detective before hanging up the phone. He throws the phone onto the bed behind him before looking out at the window again. Even with that detective’s leads going cold, Craig had one lead that he knew for certain wasn’t cold. It was his last ditch effort because if he went to this one lead then he knew that Daniel would know that Craig was digging around. He would wait until the last possible minute to use it if he must. Until then, he just sat around, the seconds ticking by. If Sarah was alive, time was running short. For Craig, he knew that, but knew it would take every second possible to find her. Last time, he had to be patient. This time it was proving to be harder. The only difference this time though was that Craig intended to take no prisoners this time. He would kill everyone who got in his way. Without question. ---- Days had passed by and there was nothing new. It was starting to eat away at him, and the anxiety that was rising in his soul was getting worse and worse, almost to the point where it was going to explode out of him. For him, it was the worst kind of pain he could possibly experience. Every fiber of his being was on fire, and nothing he did could calm himself down. He was starting to reach a boiling point and eventually fireworks were going to be set off. The past few days, he had been taking calls, trying to make sense of everything, and lay out the land before him. He had one detective feeding him information on Daniel Santiago, letting Craig know about his past and his involvement in Craig’s life and Sarah’s. Another detective was following up leads on the supposed murder of his daughter while another one was digging into the happenings of that evening where he was shot. Slowly, information was starting to pour in about different things, but nothing concrete where it concerned Sarah. That was proving to be the most difficult part of it all. He didn’t know what do with himself any longer. As he sat in another hotel room that PRIME had provided for him as they continued to travel around the United States, he wrote his thoughts and notes on pieces of paper. The intent was to help him sort everything out and keep track of it all. Unfortunately, it wasn’t helping. His mind was jumping from thought to thought so fast that he was unable to keep up with himself. However, slowly he was putting the pieces together only to find out that there were other pieces to find out there. With every second he spent on this, he could feel the anxiety build within himself, ready to just explode. It was killing him. So, instead of letting it kill him, he decided to make the pieces fit so that he had some of the answers he desperately sought. He decided to start with the shooting, yet there was a knock on the door. Craig gets up out of the chair and walks to the door of his hotel room cautiously, checking out the peep hole to make sure it was all legit. Outside stood a middle-aged man, a man Craig had hired two weeks ago. Craig quickly opens up the door and the man steps in. “So, what have you found out?” Craig asks, desperately wanting to skip the pleasantries and get straight down to business. The man drops the bowl hat he was wearing on top of his head onto the tables and runs his fingers through his greasy hair. He’s obviously a little tense about the situation he is in, but as he hit walks over to the bar within Craig’s room he begins to feel better about his surroundings. Meanwhile, Craig is just standing in the same spot by the door, looking at the man, and waiting for an answer. Preferably one that will make him a happier person then he was at that moment in time. “Nothing good. Nothing that’s going to make you happy,” the man says as he opens up a glass bottle of an unidentifiable drink and pours himself some in a small scotch glass. The man, before Craig even has a chance to ask him another question, drinks the liquid hard and fast before placing the glass back on the stone counter. He then turns to Craig with a weird look on his face before he walks over to a chair at the table where he placed his hat and sits down. “What’s the supposed to mean exactly?” Craig asks, not interested in mincing words with the older detective. The man though is rummaging through his coat looking for something that’s got Craig even at a loss for words. Then, as he pulls out the pack of cigarettes he looks up at Craig with an answer. “Just that. Everything I tell you, you’re just going to get upset about,” he tells Craig as he pulls out a cigarette and lights it up before placing it in his mouth. Craig, not interested in playing these games, walks over to the man and snatches the cigarette out of his mouth. “It’s no smoking,” Craig tells him before crumpling the lit cigarette in his hand, leaving some burnt ashes in Craig’s hands. Yet, he doesn’t care as he stares the man in his eyes, knowing that his red eyes will intimidate any man, even speaking that which he is uncomfortable speaking about. “Now, speak,” Craig tells him and the man just looks back upset for having his cigarette crumpled up. “So be it. Where should I start?” the man responds, a little upset that Craig snatched his cigarette out of his mouth. “How about with the important things? I always thought that was a good start,” Craig responded sarcastically, growing tired of these games rather fast. The man looks back at Craig with a roll of the eyes before deciding to just spill on everything. “No word on Sarah. Nothing. We don’t know if she’s dead or alive. The world doesn’t even know, at least the world that pertains to Daniel Santiago. If he killed her, then he’s being rather quiet about. Extremely quiet to be exact because this is the kind of thing you brag about, but there’s nothing. He goes and kills the daughter of a celebrity and yet he just sits back and says nothing, that’s weird. What’s even weirder is that those who are even close to Santiago haven’t heard from him. I mean, extremely close to him,” the man says, taking his time with each sentence to make sure that he words everything correctly and doesn’t miss a thing. Craig takes it all in, analyzing each word, and finding this all hard to believe. He paces around the room, trying to find a good reason why Santiago has gone off the map. “Where could have he gone? Why would he be hiding? Do they know any of those things?” Craig asks, hoping to dig further into the subject. However, it would seem that the detective he hired knew nothing up to this point. “Those who would know where he is and what he’s doing are extremely baffled by all of this. They say they have never seen this kind of behavior from Santiago. Anybody else and he would’ve been howling about this kind of news. Instead, he’s doing the complete opposite. Yet, they don’t think it’s out of fear,” he tells Craig before resting back in the chair. “What do they think it is?” he asks, perplexed by this piece of information. “They think he’s got something up his sleeve. Something not even pertaining to you to be honest, but some kind of business deals that has gotten him in this place where he is just waiting to unleash. Perhaps upon you,” he tells Craig to which he just simply nods his head not even ready to broach that subject quite yet. Instead, he needs to move on. “Now, what about the person who shot me? Is there anything there yet?” Craig asks him, his tone growing short as he does not like the lack of information he is getting so far. “That lead has gone cold,” Martin says, sitting there coolly. “What?!” Craig yells out in response. “That might be the coldest trail yet. People know that he was hired by Santiago, but his name is a mystery. A mystery that I don’t think anyone could possibly crack because Santiago only met him twice from what I’m hearing, and this guy had a serious vendetta against you. He wanted you dead, much more so then Daniel, and would’ve done this even if Daniel hadn’t asked him to. The only question is why he didn’t actually kill you because he had a chance to do so and instead he took a shot that was less than certain to kill you. From what I’m hearing, that could also be another huge reason why Daniel went into hiding. The assassination was botched and he can’t find the guy who botched it, which is why this mysterious guy is probably in hiding as well. He knows the gauntlet is about to come down on him too,” the detective responds. Craig just continues to pace around the room before he slams his fists down hard into the table. Martin jumps back slightly, not foreseeing this kind of response from Craig. “You need to bring me back answers. Not these tidbits of information like I’m a fucking parrot,” he begins to tell Martin in a cold tone. His eyes then land upon Martin, wanting to make sure his next words are crystal clear. “You do better or you and I are going to have some problems, you understand? I was shot, my daughter was killed, and there are people to answer to these things. I want their names. I want their location. I can’t go snooping for these answers, not anymore. The stakes are too high now and my name is being looked at closer and closer with each passing day by those that I don’t even know now. You take care of this part and then I will take care of the rest. Otherwise, I’m going to put you on that list of people I need to find and take out permanently, and that’s not a very long list right now.” The man just sits there, his life threatened, and gulps hard. His mouth is extremely dry as he gets back up and just simply nods his head, unable to form the words in his mouth. He walks to the door, opens it, and leaves the hotel room getting a very clear impression. A beast had been awoken inside of Craig, and it was ready to come out and play. ---- Silence. That was the word on the street as another week had passed by and not even a single word. The anger inside of Craig was starting to boil up as the hope started to disappear. He needed some kind of sign that his daughter was still alive out there so that he knew he just had to find her. Unfortunately, the signs weren’t coming to him. He was beginning to get desperate as he pulled his rental car into the parking lot and stepped out. The night air was actually nice and cool for this time of summer. It shocked him slightly as he walked towards the building before him, hoping to find some answers inside. He knew he had reached the end of his rope as his feet carried him up the concrete steps and through the threshold of a building he wasn’t quite familiar with. A church. Even with his memories back intact, he couldn’t recall the last time he had walked inside of a church. It was a mystery to him as he wasn’t one versed in religion for the mere reason that he couldn’t prove it himself. He was a logical man who needed real answers before him to make sense of things instead of buying into things even though he had experienced God in a number of ways that people couldn’t possibly fathom, even killing him he had come to find out. Yet, in this instance, he needed something above him to help him find his way for he had become lost along the way. As he walks through the sacred sanctuary he notices that there are a few other people in the building looking at him, recognizing him, and he becomes uneasy with it all. Yet, he pushes his fears away as he walks towards one of the pews and sits down, looking at the beautiful architecture of the building. If there was one thing that Craig liked about churches it was the fact that there was history in the buildings, history that astonished even him. After a few moments admiring the church, he decided to get down to it, and just bows his head as he hopes no one hears him speaking to God. “Dear Heavenly Lord, I know you and I don’t have the best relationship. How could we when I killed you and all? But, I hope you don’t hold that against me in this moment of need. I know you’re still up there, doing what you have to do, and I need your help on this one. I need you to give me faith and hope that my daughter is still alive out there because it’s slowly starting to fade. They say you do things for a reason, but I see no reason for this one. I need my daughter after not being there for her, for letting her get to the point she had in her life. I need to make it up to her, but I can’t do that with her dead. So please, just give me a sign to continue to have hope in all of this. You and I have unfinished business, I know that. You have questions about my past and I want to find those answers, but I need help from you to get to that point, otherwise I’m going to lose myself, and that scares me. If I lose myself now, it could very well be the end of it all, Lord, and we both can’t have that. Not now. Not with the way things are right now,” he finishes saying, before looking up and just nodding his head. He gets up out of the pew, a few of the people still watching him, and he begins to walk towards the door. Being inside of the church gives him this weird feeling, one that he just can’t seem to shake. As he walks through the threshold of the church once again, he feels a vibration from his jeans. His cell phone. He reaches inside and pulls it out, seeing that it’s a private number. He immediately answers it to find that it’s Martin on the other line. “Craig, we need to talk. Meet me at this address as soon as possible.” Then, he gives Craig an address that Craig instantly memorizes. Then, he tells Craig the final piece of information, the only piece of information he needs. That it’s about Sarah. ---- Craig couldn’t remember a time when he had actually gone to the country of Turkey. He might have gone there because of a connecting flight, but never before had he seen the country. Yet, this wasn’t one of those times where he wanted to enjoy the city. Instead, he had business, and he wasn’t interested in that which was around him. He was interested in getting to his destination and finding out the answers to the questions he seeked. This was going to be one of those times. As his car pulled up to the house where he was told to meet Martin, he could see Martin outside smoking a cigarette. Craig immediately turns off his car and gets out of it. He then walks up to Martin and takes the cigarette out of Martin’s mouth who looks on in protest. “Hey! What was that for?” Martin asks Craig who just looks back at him. “Smoking in Turkey is dangerous, or so I hear. Trying to make sure they don’t shoot you right in the streets,” Craig says before walking inside of the house with Martin following behind him. “So, what did you find out?” “I found a guy, Sharif, who claims he’s got something to show you. Something he needs to show you,” Martin tells him and Craig looks at him suspiciously. “How’d you find this guy?” Craig asks, worried about the answer that is going to come out of Martin’s mouth. “I didn’t. He found me,” he replies and Craig immediately knows that something is wrong. “Shit,” he says, holding his right hand up to his mouth as a man walks out from a bedroom. In the man’s hands is a manila folder that he hands Craig. Immediately he pulls out the contents of the folder. A dozen photos. He starts to flip through the photos and without a moment’s notice drops them on a nearby table, not even able to hold them any longer. The top photo of the bunch, the one that made him drop them, is a picture of Sarah lying on the floor with a knife in her stomach and blood pouring out of her. All hope leaves him. “Where’d you get these?” Martin asks Sharif, knowing that Craig is in no shape to ask the questions he desperately seeks. “My cousin. He got them. He told me how to contact you too,” Sharif responds, having to pause a few times to find the words in English. Craig just stares at the pictures, his red eyes nearly burning a hole through them. In a brief second, the denial that he was holding onto just vanishes and is quickly replaced with anger. Pure rage. He turns to Sharif with one smooth movement and immediately slams his forearm into Sharif’s throat as he rushes him into the wall before holding him up off the ground. “What kind of games is your cousin playing here, Sharif?” Craig says through gritted teeth, his tone very terse and glottal. “N-none,” Sharif manages to squeeze out. “H-he wasn’t there. He was g-given these!” he says, feeling the air in his body quickly vanishing. Yet, Craig doesn’t care and intends to kill Sharif right there and then. But, a hand comes from behind him, landing on his shoulder. Craig, not in control of himself any longer, releases Sharif, spins around and tosses Martin through the nearby table. All Craig can see and feel is rage. He is tired of these games, tired of Santiago, and tired of not doing what he should’ve done a long time. He walks over to the table and looks down upon Martin who just lies their shocked and frightened. “You’re fired,” Craig tells him coldly as he picks up the pictures. He then turns back towards Sharif who is holding his now bruised throat. “And you, I’m not done with you. You tell your cousin that I need to talk to him, and soon. Very soon because he’s got questions to answer, and if I don’t like his answers,” he begins to say before deciding to walk back over towards Sharif. He then grabs Sharif’s head and makes him look into his fire-filled red eyes. “I will burn you, your cousin, and this country to the ground,” Craig says before pushing him into the wall and walking out of Sharif’s house. Outside, everything to him is blood red. It’s the only color he can see as he stumbles towards his car, unable to hold himself up any longer under his own power. He gets inside of his car and just sits there as he feels the pain exploding into his chest in a way that even he can’t control it. Then the palm of his hands slam into the steering wheel repeatedly as he tries to take out the aggression on something, hoping that he’ll be able to breathe easier with the tension out of him. It’s no use though. It remains in his system for minutes. The pain just worsens with each passing second as he’s unsure of what to do any longer. He has lost everything he known and can’t handle it any longer. His only choice is to do that which is inevitable. Destroy everything in his path before he destroys himself. There was only one word for it. Kamikaze. To Be Continued.
