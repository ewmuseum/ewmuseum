Destiny & Coincidence (Part II)

25 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Jan 25/06 …Just like I was not meant to suffer a hand-spasm in my left arm and spill my drink all over the right leg of my old friend and current colleague, much to his chagrin. Embarrassment swept across me as John looked down at his soaked-through denim and then shot a cautionary glare my way, before breaking the tense atmosphere with a classic Mr. Incredible smile. “You think that’s when you know you’ve had too much to drink?” he chortled in my direction, grabbing a towel from the bar area and beginning to wipe himself down. “Okay, so if you promise to get onto the OJ’s with me and leave that nasty stuff behind the bar for the rest of the night then just maybe I’ll allow you to do some of the talking!” I nodded with a relieved expression towards him and inquired, “What is it that you want me to talk about?” “Well heck, I’ve taken us from when I met your Dad until I lost touch with him, so maybe you can fill in the blanks for me… but first I’m gonna have to go and dry off these pants and take a whiz, too… This stuff still goes straight through me. You get the next round in…” I laughed to myself at the thought of two glasses of iced-down orange juice being entitled a “round”, but the fact I was struggling to see straight or talk without slurring my words meant that John was probably right and so I beckoned that cute little waitress over towards me – who I wasn’t at all interested in, despite the impression I got from Johnny, who seemed to be thinking I was. In fact, women were the last thing on my mind with a huge six-man tag match upcoming and the almighty Dual Halo at Culture Shock just a few short weeks away. PRIME was my priority first and foremost, despite the pressing need to fix my physical condition. “Can I get a couple of OJ’s, please?” I smiled towards her, struggling to maintain my balance on the high-stool. She issued an amused smirk in my direction before cheekily adding, “Ah so it’s finally caught up with you, has it? I have to say that you’re especially cute when you’re disgustingly drunk!” “I have heard that one before, believe it or not… I prefer to think I’m just especially cute all the time, keeps my self-esteem levels high.” “What are you two youngsters looking so happy about?” interrupted a dried-out and whiz-ed out Johnny Noble, fresh from the Men’s WC. “I hope there’s been no flirting going on while I’ve been away.” He tipped a sly wink in my direction and followed-up with a small encouraging dig from his elbow into my ribs. Momentarily I completely lost my balance as I jumped with the shock, but he grabbed a hold of my shoulders to avoid me making a scene by falling on my ass in front of the entire population of the bar – which to be honest was now only about seven anyway. The barmaid returned to her duties after a warm, embarrassed smile to John, who delivered a reassuring glance in my direction. “Yup you’ve definitely had plenty.” His attention then turned back to the barmaid, “Short-stack, get me a strong coffee over here a-sap! …If you’re gonna be tellin’ me stories then I want to make sure that I can understand whatever you’re gonna be sayin’.” I couldn’t help but feel embarrassed at the state I had let myself slide into in front of a man I used to consider a senior family member, but it seemed that more and more lately my nights had been ending in drunken stupor and with me fumbling dangerously trying to find a vein on my left arm to inject into. Even when I was supposed to be making a good impression I couldn’t break the cycle. “Okay so, shoot… it’s getting late.” I stumbled around in my mind, trying to find the right story to tell and the perfect way to start it. My immediate memory of around just after the time John left the tours was when I was in Japan with my Dad, it was the first time we didn’t have a nanny with us to help him out, so it sticks out more-so in my head because it was just me and him, the way it was meant to be. “Right… here goes nothing… So it’s Japan we’re in, not long after you leave the tours, maybe around nineteen-eighty-seven? You remember the huge Tokyo Dome show that was booked that year? Wrestlers from all over the world came to take part and my Dad god lumbered to work with Keiju, a superstar sumo…” Tokyo - 1987 This was a summer day with a difference in the capital city of Japan as excitement and tension engulfed the warm air with its embrace. The huge Tokyo Dome was hosting perhaps the biggest single wrestling event the globe had ever seen as the best wrestlers from all nations made their way to the land of the rising sun for what might be the biggest night of their lives. Along with the masses came Philippe Lisieux, a strong-styled technician from Quebec, Canada, who was widely acclaimed as the best Canadian talent at that moment in time. He also brought with him his son, Ignatius, who was a mere seven years old… “Dad, Dad… how much longer do I have to sit here!?” the small child was becoming restless as he perched on the dressing room table, watching his father getting suited and booted for his big showdown with a Japanese sumo star. His father finished tying up his left boot as he turned his attention towards the fluffy-haired ragamuffin, who was sporting a blue WWF tee-shirt with baggy white swimming shorts and green and blue Teenage Mutant Hero Turtles sandals. “I’m sorry I can’t focus totally on you tonight buddy, where’s Josh these days anyway?” A frustrated expression spread over the youngster’s face as he explained to his Dad, “Josh had to go back home to his family because his Dad broke his Elvis!!” The French-Canadian star chortled to himself, “He broke his Elvis?” “Yeah!! Josh said it’s next to the you-know-whatty!” Cue a light-bulb popping up over the head of the Father, “Oh, you mean his pelvis. I understand now… well that’s a real shame, you guys seemed to be getting along real good.” His accent was a severely diluted Quebec dialect after years of living and working in the United States and across the globe, it had become a real mish-mash of dialect and idiom. “Well it really sucks that he had to go!” bawled the youngster, folding his arms in a melodramatic mini-tantrum. The charming scene between father and soon was soon shattered as the door to the dressing room was angrily flung open and a huge behemoth of a man entered the action. Keiju was a heralded sumo wrestler, quite probably the most popular and famous of all sumo stars at that precise point in time and he had a big, big attitude problem – especially when it came to working with skinny, white French-Canadians. A translator quickly followed Keiju into the grey dressing room and then an old-nemesis appeared at the same time, perching in miniature form on the shoulder of the five-hundred-pound monster – but this time it was the man’s child who could see the apparition. Keiju spoke quickly and forcefully into the face of his far smaller opponent, the translator seemed to be struggling to keep with the pace. The Japanese vocabulary sounded harsh and intimidating, but the smaller men kept his ground as his son cowered in the background, worried for the safety of his father. The translator began talking as Keiju stopped and stared a hole through the man that stood firm before him… “Philippe Lisieux… tonight you will be beaten by Keiju, the greatest Japanese sumo wrestler the world has ever seen. You will not put up a fight and you will not make this difficult for Keiju. It is his destiny to beat you and prove himself in the eyes of the world.” Lisieux shook his head, distressed at what he was hearing. He took a step closer in towards his Japanese opponent for the night and stared directly into his eye line, the French-Canadian was even slightly taller than his counterpart. “Listen here, pal… I accepted the invitation to this match to boost my global reputation, not to do a screw-job for some over-inflated Geisha girl. If you want to win, you’re gonna have to earn it.” The translator muttered quietly into the ear of Keiju, who erupted with a roar upon hearing Philippe’s response and smashed his hands against his huge stomach. He began shouting in Japanese and pointing towards Ignatius, who was now hiding under the dressing room table, rather than being perched atop of it. Philippe turned with an agonising look towards his son, not needing to hear the translation of what Keiju seemed to be threatening to do. Without warning the huge sumo turned on his heels and stormed out of the dressing room, cursing to himself in his native tongue. Philadelphia - 2006 “…I don’t ever remember being so scared in my entire life, for both myself and my Dad. Here is this absolute behemoth of a guy with links to probably ever bad brother Japan has, who could probably make a run for Emperor and get it, threatening me and my Dad, who are thousands of miles from home, completely alone.” Lisieux recounted the experience with an expression of stone and a face that was ghostly white, “I don’t remember exactly what or who I saw perched on Keiju’s shoulder that night, could easily have just been my young mind playing tricks on me, but when I told my Dad about it he freaked out…” Tokyo - 1987 After being shaken-up by the intrusion of the monster, it had taken Philippe Lisieux a good ten minutes to calm his son down, but little did he know that he would then be the one sent into a panic. “There was… something evil sitting on that man’s shoulder… something wearing black and pointing at me!” Ignatius sobbed as he clutched tightly to his father’s shoulder-blades. “What was it Daddy, it looked like it was coming to get me!” Philippe Lisieux was stunned into silence. His eyes grew wide with fear and he wanted ever so much to break the embrace and get him and son the hell out of Japan as quickly as possible, but his son needed him now and Ignatius always came first. Philippe held the reassuring hug and firmly fixated it, until the youngster was finally ready to let go. “I want to go home Daddy, this place is weird.” Philippe’s mind was ablaze with thoughts of Death and destiny and he became a whirlwind of activity, buzzing around the dressing room to collect all of their possessions before slamming them into his suitcase, grabbing Ignatius by the hand and making a beeline for the exit. “Come on son, I think you’re dead right… let’s go home.” The two left the dressing room together and Philippe made various alibis and excuses to various acquaintances and colleagues about getting food for Ignatius or dropping him back to the hotel as they headed towards leaving the arena, and Japan, as quickly as humanly possible. Philadelphia - 2006 “That sure sounds like your Pa’ alright, always startled by something haunting him… though he never told me what. It would just take the slightest thing and he was running for the hills. That never made no sense to me, your Dad was one of the toughest and bravest men I ever met, but he had some kind of monkey on his back.” Noble pondered to himself as I finished my story, he took a long, thoughtful gulp of his juice and just sat there in silence, desperately trying to find a solution to the problem. “He was always trying to dodge fate… did he never tell you about it?” I shook my head, “He never, ever mentioned that night again. It always seemed like he was trying to protect me from something, now that I look back on it. I remember hearing him on the phone to my Mom and I’ll never forget what he said… “The bastard is back and he wants Ignatius now, instead of me, I have to fix this…”” I then joined John in complete silence, two currently very lonely men feeling extremely unimportant and insignificant in the great scheme of things. The bar had become extremely empty, there was one old guy snoozing away in the far corner and that was it aside from the two melancholy souls that occupied the bar area. The barmaid sat quietly reading through a gossip magazine as a complete hush descended over the place, creating an extremely reflective and eerie atmosphere. “Look at us two here, after all of these years of not seein’ each other we’ve managed to drive each other into a little depressive slump… at least you seem to be soberin’ up fast!” John nodded towards my third cup of coffee, desperately trying to lighten the mood – I couldn’t help but smirk. “There’s nothing we can do about the past, I guess, maybe there’s just some things we’re not meant to know.” I exclaimed. “Sounds awful philosophical for a guy who claims not to believe in fate!” John bellowed in my direction with a chirp in his tone, but I was ready to bat this one away immediately. “I used to believe in fate, y’know? It’s not like I’m just some narrow-minded gump who can’t stretch his mind that far. I believed it was fate that I accomplished all I did with PRIME and then when it all started to slide I realised I had done nothing worthy of such punishment. There is no fate but what we make for ourselves, I suppose I’m more of a believer in karma these days.” John took a moment to ponder my response and he was quick to accept my stance, which didn’t surprise me one little bit, “Well whatever works for you, youngster, even if you did use a Terminator Two reference in there…” I raised my eyebrow, completely surprised that Johnny Noble could ever be so hip… “What!? …That movie was out in ‘ninety-two, I was quite a young pup back then!” protested my drinking buddy. “You were NEVER young John, you were the old from the day I met you… anyway we should really be starting to head off now, I don’t want to keep you from your slippers and pyjamas for too long.” Laughed Lisieux with a cheeky grin. “Hold it right there sport, you ain’t quite finished with the recollecting yet… I’m gonna get this out of you tonight no matter how much it depresses you, you have to talk about stuff or it’ll just eat you up from the inside.” “Tell that to someone who hasn’t seen a dozen different psychologists! …What do you want to know, anyway?” Noble paused in front of me for a moment, I knew exactly what was coming - the tension in the air was easily palpable. “How much do you know about how your Dad died?” “Ah, Jesus…” I moaned, really not wanting to get into this out of fear both of having John abandon me because of the way I acted and of having to judge myself all over again for the way my Father left this world. He put an arm on my shoulder and drank down his OJ, “I can stay here all night, I’m sure that little piece over there won’t mind getting to stare at you for another eight hours…” “Alright then, you asked for it… January 2004, back in Ville-Marie… My Dad had been in a bad way, he lost our house and everything in it because of his drinking and gambling and I resented him for it, it was the worst time in our relationship and it’s what killed him…” Ville-Marie – 2004 Ignatius Lisieux had taken up spying on his Dad right after he had lost the family home and everything in it in the early days of the New Year. At the current time the relationship between father and son had deteriorated to extreme levels and while Philippe Lisieux found himself living on the streets of Quebec with no money, food or shelter, Ignatius Lisieux was making waves in PRIME in his second month with the company. Raking in a small fortune after winning the Intense Title in his third match, the contrast between father and son could not have been greater. However, Ignatius still found himself looking out for his Dad, like a resentful guardian angel watching from the shadows. He wanted his father to be punished for losing everything that was sacred to him, but he also wanted to make sure the punishment wasn’t too extreme and so whenever he could, Lisieux was back in his hometown in clandestine form, watching over his old man. Philippe Lisieux was a mess. Running from fate for thirty years will probably do that to a man but he really was in dire straights. Completely broke and with a drink and gambling problem to fund he had taken to begging on the same streets that had once heralded him as a Canadian wrestling legend. Unrecognisable from the days when he had fans across the world chanting his name the man wore ragged clothes, smelled like he hadn’t been washed since birth and spent most of the day on his knees asking for spare change. As bitter as Ignatius was towards Philippe, he still hated to see him like this and he had no intention of allowing it to continue for more than a week, but that is when fate apparently dealt Ignatius another bum hand. Though the French-Canadian would most certainly put it down to nothing more than coincidence, these days. One day had saw Philippe awaken feeling worse than he ever had before in his life, worse than after taking a legit beating from three rowdies in El Paso, worse than being crushed by a Ukrainian pro-fighter in Russia and worse than when his wife left him alone with their baby son back at the very beginning of the eighties. Philippe Lisieux awoke to a familiar face, which came with a proposition for him. “Do you know that your son has just won an accolade in one of the largest wrestling promotions in the world? Or do they not get up-to-date news reports in back alleys these days?” Waking up to mockery from a man with no face and one item in his wardrobe was bad enough, but worse was to come for the hungover and bleary-eyed French Canadian who was shocked into statue-like stillness. “I’m tired of waiting Philippe, I’ve allowed the balance to remain out of kilter for far too long, and it needs to end today.” Death continued in fearsome monotone, getting ready to drop the bombshell on his victim. “Because of your refusal to comply we are down one Lisieux on my list, I warned you back when he was seven and you still refused to listen. You have run from fate for too long, so today I offer you a choice…” Philippe remained in stony silence, pain welling in his gut and thoughts flying through his mind – fear and dread were the predominant feelings on display. “Today you can die of natural causes… well as natural as I can make them appear… or today I can make a certain private jet encounter some technical difficulties and plunge into the ground in the north of the United States. A jet that will be carrying the current PRIME Intense Champion, your son, Ignatius Lisieux.” Death most definitely smiled towards his prey, fear shot through the veins of Philippe and seemed to burst out through his eyes which shot open into pure wide panic. “You can’t kill my boy, especially not now that he’s making something of himself… I can’t allow it. Take me, take me today and end this right now… I don’t deserve to live anyway…” Philippe’s face dropped as Death raised his right finger in the air and began to move it towards him, laughing louder and louder as it veered closer and closer. “I always win the battle Philippe, you cannot run from your fate… I have to say that you made a valiant effort of it, but fate catches up to everyone eventually. You were supposed to go a long, long time ago, Ignatius was not even allowed to be… but I see a bright future for that boy, I will allow him as much life as I can.” Death laughed one final loud burst of sickening laughter as his finger stroked over the shoulder of Philippe Lisieux… His body was overcome by a completely numb feeling and then simple nothingness as the French-Canadian slumped backwards into his covers and Death vanished into thin air. Just another homeless body on a street full of sadness and despair. Philadelphia – 2006 “He couldn’t even extend the courtesy of lasting a week on the streets, that wasn’t the man I knew. My Father had become something completely different to what you knew back in the day, John, he was a shadow of his former self. I resented him for the guilt he caused by dying then, it made me hate myself for allowing him to be on the streets in the first place.” A tear swept through my eye and rolled down my cheek as I valiantly brushed it away as soon as it appeared. I could see that Johnny Noble was shocked at what he had heard, he couldn’t pretend otherwise, he was disappointed at how much a father and son could fall out of touch with one another, he hated himself for not being around to try and help. But it was all fate, I thought he must have assumed. As much as Mr. Incredible should have been distressed by the story, in his typically optimistic fashion he saw the good in it. He sat in silence before he smiled towards me and patted me on the shoulder, it was a great feeling and an immense gesture. “Your Dad was a great man, Ignatius, he lived a great life… you can’t burden yourself with guilt over his death. It was he who lost your home and your possessions, as bad as it sounds you were doing the right thing doing what you did, the adult thing, your Dad was big enough and clever enough to look after himself – it was just his time.” In the heat of the moment he grabbed me and delivered a real man’s version of the “hug”, like any real uncle would have. With my Dad gone it seemed to me like Johnny was the closest thing I was ever going to get to having a father figure in my life, it seemed to be exactly what I needed right now. At a time when I was awash with painkillers, steroids and alcohol for the sake of competing in PRIME some stability in my life was absolutely necessary, but I wasn’t sure if Noble alone would be enough to save me. “The fact that he died then was coincidence, it wasn’t your fault” said John reassuringly, though I knew he was telling me exactly what I wanted to hear and perhaps not what he truly believed. I just nodded towards him, I prayed that it was just a coincidence, it’s what I had to believe in now. Coincidence was the only saving grace in these situations, it was the antithesis to fate and the thing that excused me from blame. That was all I could rest my assurances on, that destiny did not exist and did not have it in for me. We are masters of our own existences, nothing is left up to chance but is determined by our own thoughts and actions on a day-to-day basis. Ignatius Lisieux did no longer need the reassurances of fate and destiny when he won, when he lost and when he died, he would know that it had happened because he had taken the path that had led it to happen. Free will for all mankind. He hated the thought that some external force could be in control of how he lived his life, it was HIS life to live, nobody else’s. Maybe Ignatius was completely wrong and completely naïve, but for him at this very moment, he was in full control of his life. He was the man taking the steroids to rebuild his broken and battered body, he was the man becoming increasingly dependant on painkillers to make it through any given day and he was the man who chose to drink himself into oblivion on any given night of the week. Ignatius Lisieux had every intention of avoiding anything relating to fate, he would accept the consequences of his own actions as cause and effect, nothing more, nothing less. When Ignatius wanted something to happen, he would make sure that it would.
