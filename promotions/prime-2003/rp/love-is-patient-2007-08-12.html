Love Is Patient

12 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-12-07 Narrowing your eyes to the rising sun coming up over the horizon you’re pretty glad that you took a chance on those sunglasses that looked a little bit tacky. Fashion isn’t one of your strong points, but that notwithstanding, $5.99 at the local drug store was very well spent. You admit to yourself, as well, that the dozen roses would be a lovely way to break the ice since you haven’t seen her face-to-face in several months. Christmas time, to be exact. It matters not to you that the roses would only be confined to an undignified, slow death, forgotten in a tacky vase because your mother is too busy attending to other home improvement projects. You just hope she’ll accept your peace offering for not visiting her sooner. You pop open the trunk of the cab to get your luggage and drop it on the ground with a dull thud, kicking up dust off the sidewalk. After you pay the cabbie, he drives off back to the main road, leaving you to your own devices. You take a moment to stare at your surroundings and reminisce; everything is nearly just as you left it. The first glance at the neatly-trimmed garden tells the story of a woman striving for that ever-elusive sense of perfection. To have the greatest-looking garden in the entire neighborhood is to earn the adoration of friends, family, and any casual observer that just so happens to venture out into her part of the world. Her prized petunias and rhododendrons on either side of the concrete walkway continue to shine effervescently, creating the perfect juxtaposition of color and energy she spent years trying to capture. A wandering eye heads over to the koi pond. It’s amazing. You still can’t believe the monument to the work ethic of father and son stands so tall and strong after fifteen-plus years passed it by. In your younger years, not too long before Dad was diagnosed with Cancer of the esophagus, it was to be another one of his little projects. It was also to be one of those things where he’d throw in the fact that he taught you everything you knew about your craft in order to guilt trip you into helping construct the damn thing. In any case, attention was attention from him when it didn’t have to do with professional wrestling; something you ultimately sought and that was good enough for you. A gaze around the nearby vicinity shows that a lot of the folk on this block continue to make their homes captivating to the human eye. Under the watchful stares of some fruity neighborhood community gathering or cult or whatever that never appealed to you in the least, families put together all sorts of bells and whistles that turn an otherwise dilapidated old dwelling into something special. Mom being the head of the aforementioned Neighborhood Community Committee always brought about interesting tales, each one bringing a smile to your usually sour face. She would tell you of all the times she regaled her own friends with tales of the road where she’d brag that her handsome, strapping son was living his dream of going around the world as a big wrestling star. The six figures a year, the fancy house out in Hawaii, the ability to be viewed by millions as either a hero or a villain. Now those tales were all gone and replaced with the story of the emotionally deficient husband that would put his wife, daughter, and own brothers and mother on the backburner for a life he thinks should have belonged to him years ago. When she and her friends gathered for their weekly lunches, they’d chat about the latest Silver family gossip. "What was that no-good snake in the grass doing this time?" they’d ask. How dare he even think of leaving that sweet girl to raise a child of her own so he could live out some botched fantasy, they’d scream with contempt. And all the while Maria Lynn Silver nee Blenn would come to the defense of her one pride and joy, telling them all kinds of excuses ranging from simple protective fibs to stretched-out, horribly contrived lies. If it wasn’t something, such as the fact that he wasn’t quite right in the head, it was the truth of stress from your high-paid, over-worked, ulcer-inducing job. You even heard the rumors of "intimacy issues" come up despite the fact everything down there worked properly and had never failed you before. Anything could’ve been said during those lunches, but you didn’t know. Quite frankly, you never wanted to know. But the bottom line is that she was there for you when you were in times of need. No matter how much strife you inflicted upon yourself or others to live out the life you always sought, there was always one particular ally you could count on when the proverbial chips were down. That’s what brings you here now. Because you just to happen to be in said time. All is not as it should be in the life of Sonny Gabriel Silver and because of the choices you make, you’re left to crawl and beg back to your matriarch. Under normal circumstances, this would merely be one of those things where you’d plead your case and tell her why you’re not the bad guy in anything you do. You’d tell her that it was all the guilty party’s fault and that they suck, should burn in the hottest infernos of Hell while you remained a shining bastion of good. She’d tell you that everything would all work itself out in the end and that God’s will shall ensure all things happen for a reason. He would test your faith, but in the end, He did it to teach His children a lesson and you would learn a lesson and become a stronger person for it. This… this visit is far from a normal circumstance. What you’re about to ask her is well beyond any favor you’d ever asked of anybody in your lifetime. The words that would come out of your mouth the moment you set foot back into your childhood home will no doubt vex her. You bank on the fact that as your mother, she continues the trend of kindness that always leads you back to her. What bothers you most about this entire scenario is the fact you’ve done naught to earn it from anyone, let alone your own kin. Those final steps have your heart racing far worse than any crowd you’d ever been put in front of. Those people… you could do that in your sleep. That one time in front of a bingo hall, you actually did. They meant nothing to you. But enough stalling. It’s time for you to take those ultimate steps and do this. No matter how big or infinitesimal they may be, you cautiously approach the perfectly white-washed door. The scenarios play out rapidly in your head. She could say no, go hysterical, laugh. It beats on your brain profusely but with determination, you’re there. You turn the knob… ~~~~~ There. Done at last. The finishing touches on this hand-crafted ceramic jar were the most painstaking. Counting each stupid brushstroke meticulously until you’ve smoothed out the very last one eventually paid off. Sitting on your countertop is a perfectly crafted Porky the Pig Looney Tunes cookie jar. You had to slather together an awful lot of red and white to get just that perfect hue of light-ish pink down just right, but it shows. Well, that task is out of the way. By now, you’re just killing time until Sonny gets here. But you don’t want to sit around and do nothing or he’ll get on you like he always does when he comes by. He’ll think you’re stewing about the fact he hasn’t come to see you and that you’ve been sulking all this time. You understand that his life demands attention from everybody else and you made your peace with it when Richard left you for weeks at a time. It was nothing new for you, but enough about all that. Just stay busy, Maria. What else is there to be done around this house? You’re fairly positive you’ve turned this entire house upside-down in your never-ending quest to hunt down every single sneaky microbe and destroy it. Nary a speck of dust in sight or a dirty dish is visible to the naked eye. But you want things to be absolutely perfect. The last think you want is a repeat of the Great ’86 Christmas debacle when you spent so much time filing through boring ledgers and insurance forms at home, you momentarily forgot you had a life outside of number crunching and spent no time taking care of Christmas arrangements that were your responsibility. As both a last resort and a way to put your nagging OCD at ease, you decide to run through that mental checklist that’s never failed you before when company comes your way. Cat litter bin in the laundry room changed and cleaned? Check. Washer and dryer completely empty so clothes aren’t accidentally bunched together to collect obnoxious wrinkles? Check. Floors swept in the kitchen and dining room, floor free of debris? Check. Carpet vacuumed and none of Bella’s dog toys laying around? Check. Windows washed and scrubbed? Check and double check. You have the cuts from that stupid loose piece of wood you pried out to prove it. With all your major objectives achieved, it all comes down to the waiting game as you let your entire body weight collapse into the plush white sofa that Steven helped you purchase and move in last week. The joys of having young, large children never cease to amaze you when you need to get odd jobs done around the house. Uh-oh. You feel another family bonding recollection moment coming on. Well, it’s fitting since your oldest is currently poised to arrive at your abode soon. And he’s got that habitual lateness problem going on, so it’s not like you won’t have time to see pictures that have shown how far each and every one of your proud baby boys have come along. Opening up the hidden wooden oak compartment in between the twin-seated sofa, you search around with your bare hand until you find the object of your desire: The Family Album. The tome chockfull of Silver clan memories, experiences, and adventures was very near and dear to your heart. If one day, the Earth were to suddenly rise with fury and pull this house into the depths of Hell below, this book would be the only thing you’d take with you. You never thought shilling out a few dollars for some ratty old book you got at that flea market in Burien would become such a keepsake. With some elbow grease, ribbons, and some leftover fabric you had from when you knitted that sweater for your first granddaughter, Gabrielle, you would turn it into the beautiful album it is today. Thumbing through the index, a few pictures catch your eye. A few scattered baby pictures. Your late husband. The respective graduations from Highline High School for your four boys. Sonny’s wedding to Eme. Their first wrestling matches. Steven winning that big WBG World Title he held long ago, not to mention Rex and Timothy holding the same federation’s tag team titles. Heh. Your boys had grown to become such strapping young gentlemen and earned back all the work they’d put in to become successes. Call it weird for some parents to cling on, but this book commemorated every last thing to the minute detail that you were proud of her sons for. Each one represented good memories of your boys and you would be there for every single one of them. Try as you might, the gears turning in your head tell you nothing about where in this timeline things started to spiral out of control for your eldest son. As long as you’ve known him, he’s had this innate ability to be a master of his own destiny, so to speak, and attain control of the uncontrollable. You’ve never known him to be any kind of dependent leech. He wasn’t quite the social butterfly you would’ve liked him to be, but his dependence on only getting by on his own merit always impressed you and his drive to succeed served him well in his youth. He took on the world with an unmatched boldness that none of his brothers ever duplicated, but somewhere along the way things appeared to go wrong for him. Now, whenever you think of him, all you can see is two things in his life. You see things as they are and you see things as what they should be. In the oldest child’s case, he does deserve all the success that he’s every achieved in his life and he does deserve happiness. But instead, he shuns conventional American dreams in favor of his own childish youth pipe dreams and severs the ties he’s made to further those goals. Marriage, children, companionship, friends. All these things that would make any average person happy didn’t apply to your son. It was almost as if he viewed them as fallacies. Like he thought of himself as something other than human. And that idea chills you to the bone. In this time of grief you look to the other most important book in your life for your answers. You reach into that same compartment in the sofa and wield the Holy Bible itself. You set your photo album aside to thumb through that one particular passage that has always guided you before. You pray for him to find the salvation he doesn’t ask for but know he wishes for himself. You… huh? The door knob turns. Your son is here. Suddenly you bolt up from your seat and you have no idea what to do once he sets foot in the door. You start to sweat just a little bit and you’re completely unsure of what’s about to happen when your son comes to you now that you’ve realized some of the horrors that he must put himself through day in, day out. You don’t know why you feel this way, but from the moment he opens that door and gazes at you through some tacky-looking sunglasses, your motherly sense tells you that something is amiss. He drops his luggage on the floor nonchalantly just as you set your Bible aside on the nearby table. A weak "Hi" is all you can muster as he continues to stand there with some poker face plastered where a normal, loving face should appear. He’s always been good at trying to hide things from anyone else he knows, but what he’s dealing with now is not just any normal person he passes on the street. An unspoken conversation takes place between you and he. You can’t hide anything from one another. It’s just the way things have always been between you two and that will never change. Both of you can tell something is dreadfully wrong and that he's here for your help. A word doesn’t exchange before the two of you as you choose simply embrace one another with a hug. You don’t know what it is about this moment. Just moments ago, you turned to your book for answers to a question you didn’t quite understand. But you know, as does he. You both know that somehow, someway, things will be all right. Love is patient, love is kind. It does not envy, it does not boast, it is not proud. It is not rude, it is not self-seeking, it is not easily angered, it keeps no records of wrongs. Love does not delight in evil but rejoices with the truth. It always protects, always trusts, always hopes, always perseveres. Love never fails...and now these three remain: faith, hope and love. But the greatest of these is love. I Corinthians 13:4-8,13
