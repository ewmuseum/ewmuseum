Time Takes a Cigarette

27 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/27/07 It’s usually a happy and fun time when a girl is with her best friend and getting ready to see the first boy she ever kissed. But when the objective is to put a bullet in a vital organ of said boy, rainclouds tend to cover up the sunshine. Sun Tzu had been called to the office of Minister Shen, the head of Propaganda, once more. This time his pretty little receptionist signaled her to go right in. Good. Sun Tzu wasn’t in the mood for Shen’s petty little waiting games. The Minister of Propaganda sat behind his big desk of English oak, dressed in an Italian suit and smelling of French cologne as he tapped away on an American computer. It was almost like he was begging to be killed by a true Chinese communist like Sun Tzu. Shen looked up at her and smiled that plastic grin of his as he slid a file marked "CLASSIFIED" across the desk to her. He was so casual and nonchalant about it, like he was a waiter giving her a menu. Sun Tzu lifted up the file and opened it. A very familiar face greeted her. "You recognize him, my dear?" asked Shen, knowing the answer. It hadn’t been that long since she had seen that particular young man. "Yes," she replied coldly. "Confucius. He was in the class ahead of me." Although Terra Cotta raised the children together and gave them all the same training to be perfect soldiers, the motivations that drove the individual students varied wildly. For some, they were true patriots that were proud to serve the People’s Republic. Others out of a need for the family that they didn’t have. Some left it was all a grand adventure, others served out of fear of the penalty for failure. Sun Tzu… well, she had her anger to drive her on. An anger that pushed her to survive, to excel, and to hurt. And Confucius? Well, he had something else… apathy. Confucius was a natural. Languages, combat, espionage, torture techniques… he took to it all like a duck to water. Maybe that’s why he just couldn’t get himself that worked up about. He did it all because there was nothing else he could do. Sure, he could have rebelled against his training, but Confucius just didn’t care enough to risk his life. His superiors loved him. Someone with nothing to care about was capable of anything. No task was unacceptable, no target was untouchable. You could tell him to walk into a maternity ward and tell him to execute every newborn and expectant mother. Confucius would do it with one hand while playing Tetris with the other. That sort of moral vacuum didn’t come around that often, so the officers forgave little indiscretions. Sun Tzu saw one of them herself when she was just eleven. She had walked around the mess hall and saw Confucius, he must’ve been about fifteen then, smoking a cigarette. She stared at him in silence. "Yeah?" he said finally, not bothering to look at her. "That’s bad for you," she told him. Confucius chuckled, causing twin streams of smoke to shoot from his nostrils like a dragon in the art books. "I don’t think either of us will have to worry about lung cancer, girlie. We’re more likely to die from lead poisoning, if you know what I mean." Sun Tzu didn’t, but neither did she want to admit it. She walked over closer to him. "I hear those things make your mouth taste like ash." Confucius looked down at her and raised an eyebrow. His gaze made Sun Tzu feel very small and trapped, like a frog on an anatomy class. "Wouldn’t know. Why don’t you tell me?" He didn’t hesitate. He didn’t rush. He knew exactly how to move in. His lips touched hers and Sun Tzu felt a chill touch her spine as a fir was lit in her blood. With the same assured determination as when his face moved down, his tongue snaked from his mouth to her and caressed her. Sun Tzu felt like her whole being was flowing into his. And then he was gone. Sun Tzu stood there for a moment, eyes still closed. She never wanted to open her mouth again, not even to eat, drink or breathe. She just wanted to remember the feeling of him. "So how did it taste?" Slowly, Sun Tzu opened her eyes. Confucius looked at her with those cool eyes as he took another drag. "I… don’t know…" "Hmmm…" Confucius took out a pack from his pocket and held it to her. "Want one?" Cautiously, Sun Tzu reached out and took a cigarette. She gently cupped it in her hand, like a baby bird. "I’ll… save it for later." "Whatever." "Thanks." "No problem." Sun Tzu walked backwards away from Confucius. She tucked a rebellious lock of hair behind her ear. "I’ll see you around." "Hmmm…" Confucius had gone back to staring into nothing. After a nervous smile to someone that wasn’t looking at her, Sun Tzu turned and walked away. Now, many years later, the eyes in the photo gave her the same look that they did that day behind the mess hall. There was very little in the way of information in the file and what scant bit there was ended some time ago. Sun Tzu came to the inevitable conclusion. "He’s gone rogue." Shen smiled and nodded in his usual patronizing way, like she was a schoolgirl that had just gotten an arithmetic problem correct. "Indeed he has, my dear. His mission was to infiltrate a major heroin smuggling ring. Apparently something about them interested him more than we did and he became a drug dealer in reality. You’d think being a government assassin would provide enough excitement to hold his interest, but I guess different strokes and all that." Sun Tzu knew it had nothing to do with excitement. For Confucius, he most likely felt that if he had to act like a drug smuggler that it would be far easier to actually be one. He had his funny kind of logic. Now she just had to figure out Shen’s. "What does this have to do with Propaganda?" "His little gang has gotten sloppy. The police are on to them. The net has been cast and they are preparing to pull it onto the dock. Can't have the people knowing one of our elite secret operatives has gone bad. While I have full confidence that a soldier from Terra Cotta would be able to elude capture by such forces, you never what might happen. For instance, just the other day I saw one of those supposed super-soldiers beaten by some American new waver." It took a moment for Sun Tzu to realize that the grinding sound she was hearing was her own teeth. It all made sense. Sun Tzu wondered why Shen would want a face that had been seen all over state television the last few weeks performing a mission such as this. There were plenty of other agents that could handle this mission, even others from Terra Cotta. Shen knew she had shared something with Confucius in the past. Hell, not like Confucius had a particular desire to keep secrets. And so to punish her for her humiliating loss at the Dual Halo at the hands of Karina Wolfenden, Shen was making her kill her first kiss. One of the few bright points of her childhood and now she was expected to snuff fit out. Even though such manipulations made her want to snap the minister’s neck like a carrot being tossed into a stew, Sun Tzu knew he was in the right. She had failed herself and China when she had gotten pinned by Wolfenden. After months of proclaiming her superiority over the American, to lose in her homeland like that… the only reason Sun Tzu didn’t kill herself was because she considered her life to be state property. That, and it was hard for corpses to get revenge. "I trust you have no problems with this mission, my dear," said Shen, snapping Sun Tzu out of her thoughts of Wolfenden. "No, sir." "I thought not. Especially after how you handled that Deville lad.You're quite the cold-hearted one, aren't you, my dear? But then, any affection towards him was just ruse, eh?" Sun Tzu remained silent, denying Shen the peasure of her confirmation or her denial. Shen chuckled lightly before continuing. "We've decided to partner you up for this particular reunion. Confucius is a Terra Cotta operative like yourself and he has a uncouth gang of cutthroats assisiting him. So we decided you need some support." Shen pressed a button on his desk phone. "Please send her in." Sun Tzu heard the click of the heavy doors opening and turned to see just who it was. An attractive young woman walked in, close to her age. A little shorter, a little fuller figured, but with the same bearing as Sun Tzu. She looked into those dark eyes she once stared into so long ago. It was Lao Tzu. The two of them had been seent on a survival exercize, just a few years after Sun Tzu's encounter with Confucius. They had been dropped on a desolate mountain in the middle of winter with no supplies and one objective... don't die. Clearly, the two young girls suceeded, but not without an injury to Lao Tzu. But even more memorable was the night they had spent in a cave when an attempt to stay warm turned into something else. The memory of that night sent a pleasant feeling of warmth throughout Sun Tzu's body. Confucius was her first kiss, but Lao Tzu was simply her first. Lao Tzu gave her a slight nod, but Sun Tzu saw a twinkle in her eye that indicated that she too still had fond memories. The two shared the slightest of smiles. There was the sound of a throat clearing and the pair turned to the minister. The look in his eyes was almost predatory. "Ah, I see you two recognize each other. Since you're both professionals and well acquainted, I leave it to you to plan the handling of this situation. Good day, ladies." The two saluted the Minister of Propaganda, then turned and exited without another word. Sun Tzu could feel Shen's eyes leering at them both. Her skin didn't stop crawling until they were out of the office and quite a ways down the hall. Not a word was said until they stopped and pressed a button for the elevator. "It's good to see you, Sun." "And you, Lao." "It's been a long time. Well, other than watching you on television." Sun Tzu could feel the blush coming on. "Damn, Lao, don't remind me. I've got to go back there soon.I already have a match booked for the next show." "Aw, poor baby. Forced to be a star while the rest of us do real work. " Lao Tzu looked over at Sun Tzu with a mock pout. Sun Tzu giggled softly. "Oh, come off it, Lao. I didn't ask for any of this. I'm a soldier, just like you. Except all this exposure has ruined any career I had in undercover work. Really, I have no idea what I'm going to do after this whole wrestling fiasco is over." There was a soft chime ringing and then the doors the the elevator opened and the pair stepped in. As the doors closed, Lao Tzu let out a deep sigh. "So... Confucius." "Yeah." "Can't say I'm shocked. They should've kept him as a cleaner. His mentality clearly made him a risk undercover." "Too true., unless they though someone that doesn't care would panic when surrounded by people that would kill their own mothers." "Sun... we would kill our own mothers." "We don't know who our mothers are, Lao." "Details. So what was it like?" Sun Tzu looked at Lao Tzu, confused. "What do you mean?" Lao Tzu rolled her eyes. "The kiss, idiot! I know what happened behind the mess hall! All the kids knew!" Groaning. Sun Tzu covered her face with her hand. "No, Lao!" "We'll see about that..." Lao Tzu pressed the emergency stop and the elevator came to halt. "What the... Lao!" cried Sun Tzu. Lao Tzu pointed an accusing finger at her. "Talk, you subversive!" "Lao... " "I can wait here forever. I don't TV shows and autograph sessions expecting me." "You do have a mission, you know. We both do." "And I need as much intelligence as I can get. So fess up!" Sun Tzu sighed deeply, knowing when she was beaten. "Fine. It was... like an ashtray." "Uh huh." "It was. He had been smoking." "And that's just it? The kiss was like an ashtray?" "Yes. An ashtray. The most... amazing... and magical... and delicious... ashtray ever!" Both soldiers broke out in fits of giggling.It was one of the best feelings Sun Tzu had felt in quite sometime. For a moment she even forgot they were talking about someone they were going to kill. When they calmed down, Sun Tzu tried to look a serious as possible. "Now can you restart the lift?" Lao Tzu looked her right in the eye, an impish smirk on her face. "Not until you tell me one more thing, Sun?" "What's that?" "How do I compare?" Before Sun Tzu could answer, lao Tzu put a hand behind her neck and pulled her face towards hers. Their lips pressed together, and Sun Tzu soon felt Lao Tzu's tongue touching hers. She tasted clean.
