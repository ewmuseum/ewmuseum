A look into the past....(Part II)

18 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/18/05 …..Janina is talking to Walczak but nothing seems to be registering with him. He sees her lips moving, but is as if no sound is coming out. Once in a while he takes a spoonful of his soup, but seems mostly indifferent to the whole situation. “Tomek? Are you listening?” “Uhhh…yeah. Of course go ahead what were you saying?” “Why don’t you talk for a little bit?” “Yeah sure. What do you want to know about?” “Well, how about…” “I can’t do this I gotta find some stuff out that’s been on my mind for a while.” “Like what?” “Well, why did you take me in? I mean you didn’t owe me anything. You didn’t even know who I was, but you gave me an opportunity anyway.” “You’re right I didn’t have to do anything, but I felt like you came to me for help, and that’s just what I gave you. When you first came into me on that cold night I knew that you had nothing. You didn’t ask for help, and I knew that you wouldn’t so I just offered it. I came from a similar background, but at least I always had parents. I thought it was horrible that you had nothing at all so I wanted to just give you a head start.” Walczak looks somewhat satisfied with the answer, but he still looks like he has questions to ask. He pauses for a second to try and figure out what to say next and then begins to speak. “But why did you take care of me for so long? I mean I did a lot of shit that got you pissed off, but you just always told me to never do it again and then just took me back like nothing had happened.” A half smile appears on Janina’s face, and it seems as if Walczak has finally found something out. Walczak just sits back and waits for an explanation. “Can I tell you something that I have been holding back for a long time?” “Of course that’s what I’ve been waiting for.” Janina takes a sip of water and a deep breath. She then begins to speak to Walczak in a very emotional tone. It seems as if she has wanted to get this off her chest for quite some time. “I honestly had no intention of keeping you longer than two weeks.” The statement hit Walczak like a freight train. When he asked her why she had kept him he did not expect this. “I needed a worker to fill in for about two weeks, and I honestly didn’t expect you to take work that seriously, but you proved me wrong. You ended up being the best worker that I had, and after two weeks I said I’d let you stay a little longer. Then as time went by I just got to know you better, and realized all of the problems that you had and just wanted to steer you in the right direction.” Walczak remains speechless. He cannot believe this revelation that has just been made. He looks down at the ground trying to hold back the emotion. He calms down and begins to speak. “So all that time that you told me we were similar, and that you knew my situation you were lying?” “No not at all. I knew you had it difficult from the moment that I met you, but I also knew that in my financial state at the time I couldn’t take on a nearly full grown boy.” “Well, than you should have just said that I would have been fine on my own.” “Now don’t try to turn this around on me. I always helped you out when you needed me to and even when you were doing something that I didn’t like I supported it.” “What do you mean that you supported it? It was always my choice, and you had nothing to do with it.” “That is a blatant lie!” “Then give me an example.” “Alright. The most important decision of your life.” “What would that be?” “Remember when you came to me and asked me what I thought about fighting?” “No not really.” “Well, one day you came to the restaurant with a flier….. Walczak starts to think back to this time. His memory is fuzzy, but slowly he starts to get a picture in his head. It is the spring of 1991 just shy of Walczak’s 17th birthday, and he has just come into the restaurant with a neon green flier in his hand. “Janina! Janina!” Thinking something grave has happened Janina comes rushing out of the kitchen frantically looking for Walczak. “What! What, did something happen? Are you ok is everything ok?” Walczak starts hysterically laughing as he sees the look on Janina’s face. Being caught off guard she ran out of the kitchen disheveled and unkempt and the sight is all to funny for Walczak. “Nothing is wrong. I actually have some good news.” Finally catching her breath a sigh of relief comes over the face of Janina. She brushes her hair out of her eyes and looks at Walczak.“What is it?” “I finally found a way that I could make money outside of working here.” “How are you gonna do that? You have a lot of responsibilities around here, so how are you going to manage that?” “It’s a night job, and it happens every other week. It’s only two nights and the winner gets $1,500.” “What do you mean winner? What kind of job has a winner?” “It’s not your everyday job. It’s a fighting tournament.” Walczak cringes as those last words roll off his tongue, and he awaits Janina’s response. A terrified look comes over her face. Janina’s eyes open up to twice their original size, and she grabs her chest. “ABSOLUTELY NOT!!!!!! There is no way a 16 year old boy is going to a tournament to fight. There are grown men at these things, and you don’t want to get involved in it.” “I am twice as big as some of these men, and I know that I could hold my own.” At this time Walczak is pushing 6’9” and is a little bit over 300lbs. He is by no means a small boy, but the key word in that sentence is boy. Janina is right when she says that the tournament will be made up of mostly men, and that most of these men have much more experience than the 16 year old Walczak. “Plus you want to start school next year, and in order to do that we have to brush up your English. You won’t have time to do that if you are training to fight all the time.” “But….” “No and that’s my final answer.” Not yet defeated Walczak tries to get a second wind before making another argument. “I’ll make you a deal.” “What?” “I get to try this out in two weeks, and if I win or do well I get to do it again. However, if I do bad I will work the next two weeks for free and do all of the chores at home as well. Also I will do nothing but study English.” Janina looks as if she is mulling the idea over. The next couple of minutes go by without a word being spoken. Than Janina speaks with her decision. “If this is something that you really want to do than ok. You just have to promise me that you won’t get hurt.” “I promise.” Walczak smiles as he gives Janina a huge hug. In present day Walczak also has a smile on his face and is a little red as he turns to face Janina. “So you see, you did want my approval.” “I know. You always end up being right I don’t even know why I argue with you.” “I have been trying to figure that out since the moment I met you.” The two of them both chuckle as Janina places her hand on Walczak’s shoulder in a very motherly way. We are beginning to see that Walczak does not in fact hate all people, and there may even be people that he loves. However, most of this man’s life as well as his feelings remain a mystery and we can only wait to see what is revealed.
