Date: All over the place! NOW Jason had seen the slick interior of the Potemkin Management conference room a lot more often than he had liked to recently. He always dreaded being summoned to the headquarters of his three agents; and with good reason, a trip to floor 15 of the Henry Fields’ Tower in downtown Manhattan rarely meant good news for The Anti-Superstar. “So give me one good reason why we shouldn’t just cut the cord right now, Jason!” a more-than-slightly agitated Mr. Loftman bawled at him from the other side of the glass-topped table. “Why shouldn’t we just toss your precious little contract up and throw your peasant ass back out onto the street?!” His anger was not without good reason; it was only yesterday that Mr. Loftman himself had flicked on his television set, only to be confronted by the sight of a giant skeleton leaping from Jason Natas’ closet via ESPN. With his two partners – Mr. Najera and Mr. Jewell (all three of them still kept their forenames from Jason, for reasons that he had yet to comprehend) – standing either side of him, Mr. Loftman sat hunched over the table, eyes burning out the back of his skull as he glared at the scruffy professional “wrestler” seated before him. “’Cos I ain’t done shit, Lofty, and you fuckin’ now it,” snarled Jason in response. He had never particularly cared for any of the three Potemkin partners, but he found Mr. Loftman to be the most obnoxious of the lot. Mr. Loftman raised his eyebrows and stood upright as a sarcastic trickle of forced laughter dribbling from his lips. “Are you fucking kidding me?!” he barked, snapping from “good agent” back to “bad agent” in a split-second. “You’re a fucking DRUG DEALER, Jason! You think pushing heroine and crack cocaine is nothing! Do you have ANY IDEA how this looks on our fucking resume, you ignorant CUNT?!” Jason was finding the urge to throttle the life right out of the perfectly-groomed Connecticut native becoming harder and harder to resist. “Was a dealer, how many times do I have to fuckin’ tell you?” he snapped back, rising from his seated position. “That shit was years ago, ya hear? S’not like I’m hangin’ out back at ReVolution sellin’ little pills to Tony Rolo and Chandler Tsonda, is it?” “FUCK YO-“ “Jesus, would you both just shut the hell up!” Mr. Najera ordered, obliterating his trademark calm and collected exterior in the process. “This isn’t some school playground, you know!” “These are criminal allegations, for fuck’s sake!” Mr. Loftman scoffed, clearly unimpressed at being interrupted mid-rant by his partner. “We could be talking jail-time here!” “For us? Nonsense!” Mr. Najera dismissed his partner almost instantly. “We’ve done nothing! And anything that gets thrown Jason’s way? We can make those charges disappear pretty easily, you know that…” “Jason’s got a point though, Loft” said Mr. Jewell as he turned his attentions away from the floor-to-roof window he’d been gazing contemplatively from. “It’s like we said earlier; if what Jason’s telling us is correct, then it’s not so much of a problem.” “Oh, so the two of you are happy harbouring a known drug dealer now?” the angry Loftman retorted. “Well, no. Of course we’re not,” Mr. Najera began. “But the thing is, this nonsense seemed to happen before we brought Jase into PRIME. Technically, he hasn’t done anything untoward whilst under our contract, isn’t that correct, Mr. Natas?” “Fuckin’-A!” responded The Anti-Superstar, somewhat relieved that Najera and Jewell had diverted the attentions of Loftman from him. “Right,” Jewell nodded. “And we’ve got, what...” he paused for a moment to check the hands on his immaculate Rolex, “… five minutes ‘til we hit the press? Not exactly the best time for the Spanish inquisition. Hell, we’ve been sitting here for the best part of an hour, trying to come up with some gameplan for this, and all that’s happened is you two slinging mud across the table at each other!” Mr. Loftman knew that his partner was right. Refusing to acknowledge it out of sheer pride, he furrowed his brows and flashed a smirk at both of his colleagues to show his continued discontent. “We should probably move,” Mr. Najera announced, “shouldn’t be too heavy. C’mon guys, we’ll talk on the way…” All four of them left the room in silence, Mr. Najera leading the way, followed closely by Jewell, Natas, and finally Loftman, who was still trying to hurl insults at Jason, albeit through the questionable medium of telekinesis. The mere presence of the blonde tycoon behind him was enough to keep Jason’s blood pressure raised; he could practically feel the seething Loftman breathing right down his neck. In another scenario, he’d probably have knocked his block off already. ***** THEN He’d spent the past hour or so of his life surrounded by some of the most despicable men to ever set foot in New York City. Make no mistake, some of these guys came from very respectable backgrounds and occupations; lawyers, accountants, stock brokers, maybe even a couple of company executives tossed in for good measure. It was their choice of vice that lowered them to a level slightly higher than your average piece of faecal matter. Men like this met across the city almost every night, guaranteed. Stumble down the right alleyway and you just might find them circled like a pack of hyenas, looking to satisfy their carnal bloodlust. After watching their fellow human beings beating the living shit out of each other until one of them could no longer stand, they’d disappear off to their perfect, white picket-fence suburban homes to their beautiful wives and children, like nothing had ever happened. Jason loathed these individuals. Unfortunately though, as a street-fighter, he wasn’t exactly in any position to lodge a complaint with any degree of credibility. People will pay to see a man bleed; that was a proven fact. Having a bloodthirsty mob screaming for his demise was just something that Jason had to put up with. That night’s fight had gone pretty well for Jason. He couldn’t remember the name of the kid he’d just put in hospital, all he recalled was that he was African-American, maybe six feet tall, and not exactly built like a warrior. Natas could tell from the way he threw a punch that it was the guy’s first fight, and it didn’t take long for him to put the guy to sleep. His opponent had hardly landed a successful blow; in fact, the only thing that might suggest to the unenlightened that Jase had even been in a fight was a thin crust of dried blood that nestled itself upon one of his knuckles. Yeap, it had definitely been a good night (well, as good a night a street-fighter can have on the job), and it was about to get even better. Jason swaggered through the door of The Axe ‘n Cleaver, the thick waft of alcoholic fumes immediately catching him by surprise. Hardly the prettiest looking public house in the world, but Jason never had a lot of time for upmarket cocktail joints and pretentious chain bars in the first place. Typical for a Friday night in this part of The Bronx, the bar was a heaving mass of drunkards and he could hardly hear himself think over the top of the bar’s collective tales of drunken merriment. Still, he knew exactly where he was headed. He parted the mass of pissed-up New Yorkers and passed through them like Moses through The Red Sea. It took him a moment for his eyes to focus through the smoke-induced haze, the passive smoking of which had probably cost him at least a month of his life, before he finally found what he was looking for. “Evenin’,” he said, addressing the bulky, balding gentlemen propped up against the bar. “Jase,” he nodded in response, momentarily refusing to drag his attention away from the sublime buttocks of a barmaid at the opposite side of the room. “Hear you sent some boy home in an ambulance tonight?” Finally, Cordell Abraham turned to face the future Anti-Superstar, whose scuffed leather jacket glistened from the thin coating of rain on its surface. He pulled his half-finished cigar up to his big, fat lips and took a long drag on it, before letting the smoke slowly trickle out between the gap in his lips. Cordell Abraham, to be frank, was not one to fuck with. As the ringleader of a highly-illegal New York street fighting ring he had amounted a small fortune; you’d be surprised at how much the average white collar wage slave will pay to see a couple of junkies swinging punches at each other. He was dressed as impeccable as he ever did, with a black pinstripe waistcoat and white dress shirt underneath a brown sheepskin coat that he insisted on wearing despite the sweltering heat inside the bar. With his thinning hairline and large frame he had always reminded Jason of Tony Soprano. “An ambulance? Sounds about right,” Jason shrugged. Abraham knew exactly what Jason was here for. “Alright,” he responded; he didn’t really care enough to explore the topic further. Reaching into the breast pocket of his coat, he soon pulled out a small, brown envelope, candidly passing it in Jason’s direction under the bar. “It’s all there,” Cordell said. Jason glanced around the Axe ‘n Cleaver, making double sure that nobody was watching, before placing his hand on the envelope. However, much to his surprise, he finds that it does not move as he attempts to pull it towards him. He flashes Abraham a curious glance. “Oh uhhh, Jase, just one thing,” the Bronx’s Tony Soprano began, “I know about the fuckin’ pills, okay?” A faux-confused look so preposterous that only Stevie Wonder would be fooled by it spread across Jason’s face. Natas opened his mouth to speak, but before he could utter a word Cordell had dismissively raised his free hand in front of him. “Alright, I don’t want to hear any bullshit denials. I know, Jase, no point in denying it. Just do me a favour, keep that shit away from the fights, okay? We don’t want to give the cops another reason to be sniffin’ around. If you don’t, I’ma have to start pulling your paycheques. After that, I you starting paying me… in blood. You hear?” Jason nodded. A lesser man may have found himself trembling in his boots at such a threat; Cordell Abraham was, after all, one of the most powerful men in the New York City criminal underground. Just one word from this man was all it took, and before you knew it you’d be face down in a gutter, drowning in your own blood. “Good, good,” Cordell signed off, finally released the envelope to Jason. The future PRIME employee haphazardly stuffed it into his jeans pocket, before turning and walking away from the boss; he didn’t waste his breath by saying “goodbye,” Cordell Abraham never was one for pleasantries. As Jason stepped back out into the cold New York evening, gently patted his thigh, just to make sure he hadn’t been pick pocketed on the way out. He then checked his other pocket, gently padding down his damp jeans… yeap, the small, cylindrical tube was still there. It rattled quietly as he struck it. His primary pay packet was in the bag, not it was time for a little extra earnings. ***** NOW The three Potemkin partners fed him a constant stream of tips and hints as they lead him down the corridor towards the press room. They spoke over the top of each other, contradicting one another with every second word, but none of them noticed, they were too busy shitting themselves. Jason, of course, ignored it. The rate at which Loftman, Najera and Jewell were tossing advice at him made listening pointless anyway. Truth be told he was a little bit anxious himself; this would be the first press conference he had ever taken part in throughout his entire life, and he didn’t have the faintest clue what to expect. Perhaps if these bumbling idiots would stop talking over the top of each other, he’d find out. Besides, he pondered, it’s been at least a year, what could possibly go wrong? “Alright, Jase, this is it. Let’s do this…” The double doors leading into the press room swung upon simultaneously. Faced with the sight of a blinding wall of flashbulbs, Jason came crashing back down to reality. What could possibly go wrong? Everything. As the initial burst of flashing cameras began to dissipate, Jason sat down at the table set before him, second seat from the right. To his right sat Mr. Loftman, and to his left Mr. Jewell – Najera remained standing. Sitting there in his piece of shit $50 suit, Jason gazed out with disdain at the gathered mob of reporters. There was a heck of a lot more here than he had thought there would be, and just about every eye in the room was focused on him. He knew that they were here to rip him to pieces, because lets be honest, who doesn’t love a scandal? The situation reminded him of his old street fighting days… these men and women were, after all, here to witness the painful demise of a man’s integrity. The only difference was that the reporters were paid to be here. “May I have your attention please!” Mr. Najera loudly blurted, immediately hushing all chatter between the gathered masses. “We’re going to have to keep this short, ladies and gentlemen, but we shall try to answer as many question as possible. I’m going to have to ask you to direct your questions to either myself or one of my esteemed colleagues, and we shall do our best to answer on Mr. Natas’ behalf. Any comments or questions that seek no purpose other than to undermine and discredit Mr. Natas will be ignored and the conference cancelled.” Najera gazed around the room; more than a few of the reporters sighed with displeasure as he uttered that last sentence. “Thank you, and fire away…” ***** THEN ”FREEZE MOTHERFUCKER!” the gang banger roared as he pulled a shiny silver pistol from the pocket of his ridiculously baggy jeans. Some woman’s tights pulled over his head in a poor attempt at concealing his identity, he strode up and began to wave the gun in the face of the drug pusher on the corner of the street. “GIMME WHAT YOU GOT OR I’M BLAST YOU!” Jason Natas, aged seventeen, watched on with a morbid curiosity as the scene before him unfolded. He noticed the drug dealer put both hands in the air submissively, but he couldn’t pick up a word he was saying from the other side of the street. “BullSHIT you ain’t got nothin’!” The man with the gun yelled loudly, making him about as subtle as a brick to the small of the back. The assailant, keeping the pistol aimed at the dealer’s face, forcefully drove his hand into his victim’s jacket pocket. Within a matter of seconds he pulled out a small wad of cash and jammed it into his own pocket. “You know what we do to liars around here, huh punk?” by now he had the pusher back against the corner at which he had been supplying drugs to the city’s lowlife. BANG! A single bullet was all it took to end the young man’s life. Without warning the thug blew a whole through the dealer’s skull, splattering a thin line of crimson against his own grey hooded sweatshirt. As the dealer tumbled to a lifeless heap on the sidewalk, the robber immediately turned on his heel and ran away as fast as his legs could carry him. Jason hardly flinched as the dead man’s body give one final shudder as he passed on to the other side; it wasn’t the first time he’d seen a man die, and he knew that it sure as hell wouldn’t be the last. Something, however, caught his eye. A small, light object lay just an inch from the man’s body. It had diverted Jason’s attention enough to make him walk across the empty street towards the carcass, descending like a greedy magpie. He knelt down beside the lifeless corpse, picking up the object with his right hand. On closer examination, it appeared to be a small see-through sachet, containing some kind of white powder inside. It didn’t take Jason longer than a couple of seconds to realise that it wasn’t sugar or baking soda… It was then that, in the distance, the sound of police sirens filled the air. Startled, Natas rose to his feet, taking the sachet into the pocket of his trousers. He briskly walked away, the stash now safely in his possession. He had no idea whatsoever what kind of impact this one back of blow would go on to have.
