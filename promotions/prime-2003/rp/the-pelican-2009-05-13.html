The Pelican

13 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/13/09 Jazz brunch. Two words that anywhere else in America would be completely incongruous. But in New Orleans, it’s just another Sunday. It’s just like any other weekend at Arnoud’s: soft music plays out of the upscale restaurant, couples walk hand in hand in the decorated French Quarter, and Jonathan Rhine nervously chews his nails more than he does the eggs benedict in front of him. “You need to go see her,” says Aaron “Dith” Timble, the man who started out April as Jon Rhine’s life coach and has since been demoted to friendly acquaintance. He still feels the need to give out advice. “I can’t.” Advice that The New Life won’t listen to. “Listen, Jon. I know you feel guilty for what happened to her. And maybe she doesn’t get hurt by Hessian if she doesn’t get back with you. But just because of all of that…” Dith stops, thinking. “Yes?” Jon asks, his eyebrows arching. “Wow, sorry. Yeah, that does sound bad.” Rhine sighs as his friend pats him on the back. “But you know she still loves you. She can’t blame you fully for what happened; you didn’t drag her to that junkyard. She knows how sorry you are…she has to forgive you.” “No, she doesn’t. Katie and I broke up because the life I was living was too dangerous for her. Because the stuff that gives her nightmares and makes her shiver is just par for the course every Wednesday. Because I was too stubborn and selfish to put her before the job. And what happened at last ReVolution was a microcosm of that bigger picture. And in the big picture…I don’t think there’s room for us together.” ---- You’re not saying this to make Dith feel sorry for you. You’re not saying it for effect. You’re saying it because it’s absolutely true. You’re saying it because the guilt of putting Katie in that position is too much for you to bear, and most of all you’re saying it because the way you now feel about Katie is making think about quitting for the first time. You’ve turned down offers for Katie, and you have told her that you would give up wrestling for her if she asked. But you said those things knowing that she would never make you. And that was a good thing, because you’re relatively sure that if she had tried to make you, you would have fought. You would have resented her. And in the end, you would have left her for wrestling. Because at the end of the day, Jonathan Rhine is more of a fighter than a lover. And you’re not really sure if you want to change that. So you feel guilty, and you think about Katie in the hospital, and you worry about her. But at the back of your mind you know that it’s best for you to sit here and look nervous with Dith. Because the everpresent debate of wrestling versus love is all up to you… And deep down, you’re a selfish man. ---- “Okay, so you’re not going to be by Katie’s side while she recovers. As weird as that is, I kind of get it. What about Hessian?” Even at the mention of the name, Rhine’s eyes widen for just a second and look around, as if saying the name will summon the beast like Candyman or Bloody Mary. “What about him?” “You have to face him this week, and he pretty much admitted that he did what he did to Katie to get your mind right. To focus you, to make you get amped up for facing him. And I have to say, you don’t look very amped up.” Dith Timble isn’t inaccurate; Jon pokes at his eggs timidly, then lets his eyes droop towards the floor. He lets out a heavy sigh and nods. “I know, Aaron. I’m not focused. I’m not ready. All I can think about is what he did to Katie, but it doesn’t motivate me. It doesn’t make me want to destroy Hessian, take his title, and pay him back for what he did.” “What does it makes you want to do?” “I…” Jon looks up finally, and his eyes are without the determination that usually resides there. “I guess it makes me want to run.” During the ensuing silence, a soft saxophone riff trickles out of the restaurant, birds chirp, and others have idle conversation. But the silence at the table is too much for Rhine’s former life coach to bear. “So you don’t want to be with your girlfriend in the hospital. You don’t want to face the person who put her there. What do you want to do?” Jonathan Rhine finally eats a bit of his food, presumably to buy him time to answer the question. Once he finishes, he still says nothing. And that’s all the answer Dith needs. “What about all that talk about nothing being wrong with you?” He waits for an answer that won’t come, then asks another. “What about your talk with Sarah?” Again, Jon doesn’t answer. “What…what about being Jonathan Rhine? What about doing everything you’ve ever stood for? What’s going to get you to get back to doing what you do best?” Jonathan sighs again and pushes back his chair. “I don’t know. I can’t think of anything right now.” Seeing the disappointment in Dith’s face, Jonathan merely shrugs. “I don’t have an answer you’ll like, Aaron. I’m sorry if that upsets you.” He starts to stand up, but his phone begins to ring. Fishing his phone out of his pocket, he takes a look at it and audibly inhales. “It’s…uh, it’s Katie’s dad.” Dith Timble looks at his troubled friend and laughs. “I guess that answers my question.” ---- You know it’s a cliché to be scared of your girlfriend’s father, but that doesn’t make you any less afraid to answer the phone. Michael Malick has never liked you. He hates the fact that you are with his daughter. He hates the fact that you engage in a “senseless” sport such as wrestling. And he especially hates the fact that you are more wealthy and successful than him. All of this has manifested itself in an outright hostility whenever the two of you are in the same area. In fact, the two of you have come to blows at least twice. And now you’ve gotten his daughter injured because you wanted to show Hessian that you were just as intense as he was. You know that Michael will bring it up; you know this because it has tortured your thoughts all week. How your selfishness and your need to prove how tough you were essentially put Katie in the hospital. How losing sight of what’s really important might have caused you to lose it for good. And how trying to find out how to compete with the animal has caused you to turn into one yourself. You can also hear him saying the other clichés: stay away from my daughter, if I ever see you again I’ll rip you a new one, you don’t deserve her after everything you’ve done. And you can also hear yourself completely agreeing with him one hundred percent. Because it’s all true. So you tell yourself the right thing to do is to answer the phone, take his scolding like a man, and try to forget what it feels like to hold Katie in your arms, because you’ll probably never get to feel it again. What happens when you answer, though, is anything but cliché. ---- “Mr. Malick?” “Hey Jon.” His tone is solemn, and Rhine nods in acceptance. He understands that he has to deal with this, so he figures he’ll make the first move. “Before you say anything, Mr. Malick, I just want to apologize.” Rhine begins to walk towards the street. “I was terrible and selfish. I hurt Katie so much, and knowing that hurts me more than anything any wrestler can do. And I understand that you must want to rip me apart for everything, but I promise you that…” “Jon, do you know why the pelican was picked as the Louisiana state bird?” Rhine stops, his eyebrow raising. This was certainly not what he expected. “Excuse me, sir?” “The pelican was picked as the Louisiana state bird because John McKeithen, the governor of Louisiana at the time, was near Lake Pontchartrain on a windy day, and he saw a brown pelican alight on a cypress tree near the shore. The pelican was visiting a nest that had three chicks, all chirping in hunger. It must have been a slow day for food, and the pelican had nothing to offer her babies. So, with no other options, the pelican used her beak to rip open her own breast.” “Ew, gross,” says Jon, unable to avoid sounding like a middle schooler. “What’s the point of this story?” “You’ll find out if you listen,” Mr. Malick says sternly. “Anyway, the pelican ripped open her breast and used her blood and breast meat to feed her starving chicks. Governor McKeithen was moved by this act of selflessness and instinctual survival, and so he made the motion to make the brown pelican the state bird of Louisiana.” Mr. Malick pauses to catch his breath, but Jon has learned his lesson and does not interrupt. “The point of the story is, Jon, that sometimes we do everything we can to help the people we love. Protecting them and caring for them requires sacrifice and pain. But that same sacrifice keeps both people going. Because the chicks have the mother’s blood, they are able to survive. And their survival keeps the mother with a sense of purpose.” Jon nods. “I think I get it. I’m supposed to be the pelican…but if that’s true, then what is the sacrifice?” “The sacrifice is putting your body on the line. Allowing Hessian to put you through hell. Putting yourself through hell so you can return the favor. And through that sacrifice, you will be putting her needs in front of yours, which will in turn help you and your relationship.” “But I feel like beating his ass is also on my list of needs,” says Jon with a smile. Jon can hear Mr. Malick’s smile on the other end. “I didn’t say you couldn’t have fun while you did it.” The New Life laughs and shakes his head. “Mr. Malick…thank you. Thanks for understanding everything I’m going through. Even when I don’t understand it myself.” “Well, as much as we’ve had our differences, I know what’s best for my daughter. So go to her, son. Show her you’ll fight for her. Show her you’ll bleed for her.” Smiling, Jon makes his way back to the table. “I will, sir.” Hanging up the phone, Jon opens his wallet and tosses a few bills on the table. “This is my half, Aaron.” “Wait, where are you going?” The life coach asks. “To do what you said to do earlier,” he says, then walks away. ---- You’re selfish. You’re obsessed. You are more concerned with your job than your girlfriend’s well-being. Except according to someone who hates you, you aren’t. Strangely enough, the encouragement from Katie’s dad means a lot to you. Even though the two of you have never seen eye to eye, you have always seen where he was coming from. He was never illogical; his points always seemed to come from a sound place, even though they conflicted with yours. So to hear him say that your actions have been and will be sacrifices in his daughter’s name is a relief, and not just because you get to see Katie again. It’s also because you realize that you were dead wrong earlier. When you told yourself that you didn’t want to see Katie because you didn’t think the two of you belonged together, you were merely protecting yourself. Trying to make it easier on yourself. Because if you weren’t able to be with the woman you loved more than anything in the world, it was easier to tell yourself that you didn’t think it would work anyway. That you actually preferred your job over her. The truth is you can’t get to the hospital fast enough. ---- Katie Malick’s recovery has been laborious, but at least she’s finally awake. After a few days of bedrest, Katie is finally allowed to be moved to the recovery wing, where she can see the visitors who have been waiting days to see her. Except there aren’t any. It’s been nearly three hours since she was moved to this wing and Jonathan hasn’t showed up, called or sent word. And while she didn’t necessarily blame Jon for what happened, she knew him well enough to know that he probably did, and would be waiting for her with flowers and a sincere apology. Except he wasn’t. So Katie Malick sighs, closes her eyes, and tries to sleep. It doesn’t come, however, because her mind is too cluttered. She worries that Jonathan has changed his mind, that he doesn’t want to be with her. And after a few more hours of waiting, Katie has finally convinced herself of the worst: Jonathan Rhine isn’t coming, because he doesn’t care. Except he does. Standing in the doorway is The New Life, a bouquet of daisies in his left hand. Placing them on the table, he walks up to her and grabs her hand. “Katie…I love you. And I’m sorry for what happened. But I’ve been bleeding for you. Sacrificing for you. Dedicating everything I do for you. And I’m not going to stop. I’m going to keep bleeding. I’m going to bleed in that match for Hessian, and that blood and sacrifice will be to keep you going.” Katie’s smile shifts as she crinkles her nose. “That’s sweet, Jon…but a little morbid.” Rhine chuckles dryly. “I know, but it’s true. All of my efforts, all of my thoughts, and all of my actions go to you. And I can’t spend another minute dedicating myself to any other cause.” “Aw, Jon…” Katie opens her arms, letting Jonathan lean in for a long hug. When they release, she says, “I’m glad you came. My dad should be here soon enough. Did you want to stay to see him?” Again Rhine laughs as he stands up. “No thanks, I’ve actually got to go get some training in before the match. But, um…be sure to tell him thanks for me.” “Thanks for what?” He kisses her forehead and then begins to walk out of the door. “Uh…for raising the most wonderful girl in the entire world. See you at UltraViolence, Katie.” Jonathan Rhine leaves the hospital room and into the lobby, where the first thing he sees is the large blue Louisiana flag. He smiles as he stares at the pelican in the middle of the flag. He then makes his way out of the hospital, to continue to bleed and sacrifice as long as his body allows.
