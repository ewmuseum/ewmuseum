Things To Do Before I Die (Again)

7 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/05/05 I - Too Cool For School. 'MISS Wolfenden,' was the familiar, curt call whenever the young Karina's envious eyes drifted to the lush, outside world. 'Would you PLEASE pay attention.' Whether it be the glorious, hazy intrigue of the New York summer, or the crisp, tantalising winters, Karina squirmed in her seat in anticipation... counting down to the bell from the moment she reluctantly walked through the gates. Between the contrasting seasons, the amber hue of Spring and Autumn gave the world a Midas touch, goading sneakers to kick through mounds of desiccated leaves, or be cleaned by light, humid rain. The world began to call at a young age, and school only served to slap its hands, carrying the stench of formality, over her open ears. 'Karina,' was always said in a stern, tiring voice. 'I've moved you as far away from the window as I can. Now, take us from page 76, please.' She could never tire of the scenery around her, familiarity to the world breeding dreams of exploration rather than contempt. The scent of a spring morning, stumbling sleepily from the Wolfenden home, was heavy with sage and mint, while the reborn sun dried the air, readying its baking heat for the summer. The 45 minute walk home in the final months of the school year left her lightly freckled face beaded with perspiration, the hill towards her home a pedestal for the lower afternoon sun, no shade to taint its swansong over the horizon. Autumn brought the fermented hint of fallen, rotting fruit from the bushes where it had grown wild in excess over the summer months. While not even the bitter, arctic bite of winter, reddening noses and cheeks, could leave the young Kari with any resentment for the world in which she lived. 'Well,' went the usual scoff at her lethargic, distracted readings. 'Your enthusiasm never fails to under whelm me.' Despite her love of poetry and a blossoming admiration for Jostein Gaarder away from the classroom, it was English that left the future K-Wolf scratching her bitten nails down the desk in frustration. It was held in the oldest, eastern wing of the school... the lone room at the top. A permanently bolted door mocked the students, hiding behind it the staggered rooftop of the school. From its windows, which amplified the summer-time sun to leave students grabbing at their collars, gasping in stuffy air, the world beyond their school day could be seen all around. 'It will still be out there when school finishes, Karina,' her teacher mocked, dismissing her lust as laziness, rather than compulsion. 'Now, can you explain to me...' Even the constant whispers, teasing her name, couldn't distract Karina's longing stare from the outside world, where the fierce sun continued to bleach the already straw-tinged grass. She was a fair leap away from the unpopular kids, distanced herself from the rich, snobby clique, bored quickly of the popular core when they'd invited her in, and wouldn't let the current trends take her down the social currents to where the 'outsiders' lurked. People just didn't 'get' her. So, not much has changed really, has it? Never one for team exercises, Karina distanced herself from the after-school clubs, had little interest in the supposed rebelliousness of underage drinking, and spent all of the school holidays out of the country. An unapproachable demeanour helped keep all but a handful of close friends at bay, while an aura of mystique left a few even a little afraid of her. She liked that. 'What IS she doing?' they'd ask as their groups strolled along the paths between buildings, curious eyes focussing into bemusement at the sight of the young woman sat cross-legged on the lawns, smiling up at the sun in serene, welcoming silence. 'I so swear she isn't normal...' Karina Wolfenden may have grown to a full 6 feet, set foot on every continent, became a cult superstar on national TV, and changed her hair colour nearly 50 times since... but she was still Karina Wolfenden. Every urge satisfied, every desire fulfilled. That was why, finally, as the day's first war was waged between sun and dawn chill, Karina Wolfenden stood thru the looking glass, behind the bolted door that so intrigued her in youth. Every strand of lilac hair was restrained, be it by ponytail, braid, or hairclip... the only thing teasing the dry morning skin of her face being the delicate breeze that wafted the damp chill. The sound of velcro being ripped, then attached even tighter had no competition across the airwaves, Karina making sure every failsafe, no matter how ineffective should she blow it big time, was in place. 'To things that could have been left too late,' Karina toasted with the final swig from her water bottle, before discarding it to the forgotten rooftop. 'And to never putting them off again.' To say she'd been reborn would be too cliché, reinvigorated suggesting Karina was less than she was at one time. Instead, the K-Wolf was happy to pass it off as being true to her real self. The idea of the K-Wolf. That which came before. Staring at the sun as it continued its laboured climb onto the plateau of fields and unremarkable town landscapes, Karina veiled vibrant emerald eyes with mirrored sunglasses, masking the one, startling hit of emotion her face portrayed. Lips pursed together over Karina's tongue, moistening them as earphones settled into place. Thrashy chords of Idlewild accompanied flexing fingers, before the added layers of distortion signalled the zipping up of Karina's top to just under chin. 'If I know what I know...' blasted the suggestion into the K-Wolf's ears. 'Then no-one will watch over you.' A Modern Way of Letting Go - never had a song title been so apt for its listener. 'MISS Wolfenden,' Karina dryly mimicked. 'Would you please jump off the building.' Karina's right foot rolled forward onto its toes, pushing the K-Wolf's lithe body off. Heavily gripped soles ground their treads into the roof, building speed as she approached the edge. As the sight before her quickly darted through her peripheral vision, Karina almost felt deja-vu creeping in, having imagined the act so many times it had crept into her memory banks. Off the edge she leapt, gliding through the air as if the light morning breeze was supporting her flat, toned body. As quickly as feet touched down on the rooftop below, knees collapsed into a roll, her physically-peaked body a natural shock absorber. Barely a pace was lost as Kari exploded out of the roll, her focus locked onto the approaching jump to the adjacent rooftop. Bending her approach, Karina spring at the wall bordering the jump, embedding her treads into those of the brickwork, pushing herself off with a spin to land flawlessly on the rooftop across. It was something she'd been itching to do for 8 years. The memory always there, calling her back no matter the sights and sounds that had indulged her body over the years. Something had kick-started the drive, dragged her to the memories that helped forge her. Going back to make sure all the pieces were in place, letting her finally move on in the present. Karina wished the rerouting of her adventurous neurons was a conscious effort... but it took almost fizzling out of existence to throw herself headlong back into it. II - The Hidden Place. The bedclothes were still half falling onto the floor, the way Karina Wolfenden had left them as she dragged her weary body out from under them. Rarely one to rise before midday, the K-Wolf instead rose with the early dawn, an odd sense of purpose about her. As was typical, glasses, plates, and discarded clothing littered the bedside, but amongst their haphazard reflection of personality, the true insight into Karina lurked. With an uncapped pen next to it, leaving blue ink to be sucked up into the duvet cover, Kari's rarely-opened diary sat. The spine looked almost new, despite having been purchased years ago... only there as the final failsafe when Karina needed channel out the crap from her brain, through the nib of the pen. Hopping onto the bed, one of Karina's black cats, Psylocke, nestled herself into the ruffled duvet, not knowing that a few inches under her were the words that caused her owner to pack a rucksack and wake the feline with a slamming of the apartment door. Yeah, I'm back again. I'll try not to be too emo. And if I do, don't worry, I'll punch myself as punishment. Okay, with the usual forced-comedy intro out of the way, here's the deal. Six weeks ago I died. No nearly about it. Heart-stopped, rather annoying lack of breathing, and a resuscitation which I'm pretty sure wasn't all about keeping my dignity intact. Nobody looks good flopping around from electric shocks to the chest. And as for the whole eyes still open thing... I'm pretty sure B-movie zombie wasn’t a good look for me. Anyway, I'm still alive. Duh. And things are... well... here's where I start to sound stupid. Even more so than usual. I should be pretty damn glad to be alive. Really. It's not like I'd marked 'dying from an extreme allergic reaction' on my colander that day, and set about fulfilling the task to the best of my abilities. But since then, I've taken a step back to look at myself. And the verdict? Maybe I needed the near miss to get my shit straight. As I warned, more stupid than usual. I felt like I'd begun to apologise for who I was. I know I'm pig-headed, so I seemingly had to start doing things just to spite myself. Hell, I don't even own my own likeness rights until my PRIME contract runs out. I haven’t even left the country since it happened... it's like I’m grounding myself as punishment. I'm not Karina Wolfenden. I don't feel like her when I go to bed at night, I barely feel like her in my dreams, and it takes me a second too long to realise who I am when I wake. I sound like a fucking Linkin Park song. The adrenaline they used to yank me back felt like it was lingering in my system at Seven of the Best. The looks on peoples' faces when I walked through the doors only helped pump it harder around my system. I'd say they looked like they'd seen a ghost, but that would be too cliché even for this little 'life evaluation' piece. As scared as I am to admit it, Angelo has probably been the only think keeping me sane. That there is insanity itself. I bet Rage didn't have this problem. Shit, even I thought he was dead at Ultra Violence... not even I could have twisted into a safe landing from that fall, and no bone in my body could have stood up to what Vamp unloaded on him. But he calmly lets people know he's okay, then just laces the boots and winds round the tape like nothing happened. And now... I have to face him. And frankly, he's winning at the moment. It isn't even close. Maybe I just think too much... or maybe me thinking too much has me thinking I think WAY too much. I can never tell with this brain of mine, sometimes. I'd shrugged it all off until now. Gone for a ride to try and clear my thoughts, throw in an extra work-out to stop me sitting around for a free hour. But knowing Emilio is on the horizon has ushered in the dawn for me. I need to feel like myself again. Not the woman I was six weeks ago, doing my oh-so-embarrassing 'distance thing' with Deville before the bee landed on my neck. I'm talking the woman I was meant to grow up as. The woman I wanted to be. I have the chance, the means, and the funds to be Karina Marie Wolfenden as I should have always been. So, I'll be gone for a few days. Just when Psylocke and Betsy had gotten used to the sight of me again. I have some memories to rediscover. I asked Angelo for a loan, and he ended up giving me a card to one of his accounts, then instructed, 'do what I need to do.' One day I'll actually have to pay him a compliment. Anyway, hopefully the follow-up to this will be wayyyy less up-myself. Until then, I'm off back to school, and to see the millennium in again. - K III - Widescreen World. Giant toads sang their dusk chorus against the ocean's lapping melody. With damp grass darkening the grey cargo pants of the American traveller, the Fijian island of Taveuni let the day retreat over the peach horizon. It was the first populated landmass to see the dawn of the new millennium, and since that day, every sunrise or sunset had been a vivid masterpiece of pastel oranges and reds. Karina Wolfenden could remember the exact spot she sat that morning, but across the island, it was the setting of the sun that would inspire her. As 2000 gingerly crept into much anticipated existence, Karina made her new millennium’s resolution. To free herself of the Wolfenden family shackles, to slither her shoulders from the burdon of expectation, and to do so with no regrets. She'd been inoculated against the overbearing pressures, now she needed her booster jab. Feet dangled over the stumpy cliff, heels kicking against the rock, softened by moss. Discoloured by the mesmerising sight before her, Karina resolved to enforce her previous vow. While many would see her as fiercely independent, only Kari knew how often she'd questioned herself, deep down. The calm, serene exterior an all-encompassing mask over the battle of fiery wills that took place within. Even as the sun's light dwindled in the hazy distance, the day still seemed brighter than any she'd woken up to lately. 'You've spent your whole life saying it,' Kari mumbled too herself, brooding shadows formed by her sharp features. 'Now to start being.' Karina had stardom she seemed embarrassed of, love she was uneasy with, a shot at a prize whose magnitude threatened to dwarf her, and funds she felt guilty of spending. All the ammunition, but a subconsciously jammed trigger. The means, but a fear of achieving the end. A reason, but no justification. 'I am Karina Marie Wolfenden,' she proudly boasted to the orange highlights of the rippling pacific ocean. 'I died, and not even that stopped me.' The slightest hint of a wry smile angled her pale lips. She wasn't just making a renewed promise to herself, no, Karina was exchanging vows with the world itself. The sun her witness, the sky her bridesmaid, the ocean her minister. There was to be no divorce, no trial-separation, no pre-wedding nerves. A woman of the world was making a commitment that would bind her until the next time she died. 'To all that comes...' Resting her head on the backs of her hands, the content Karina Wolfenden let out a solidifying sigh, the relief exorcising the nagging doubts that had crept into her life. Whatever came at her now, the K-Wolf could be ready. Be it commitment to Angelo Deville, or Emilio Rage and the belt he displayed around his bruised and scarred waist, even the fame she was slow in growing into. The world was her ally, and with its encouragement, she could handle anything.
