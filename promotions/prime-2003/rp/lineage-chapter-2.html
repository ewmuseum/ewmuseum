Date: ReV 220 "...so there I am, just out of the shower, bare-ass naked, and all of a sudden Billy kicks down the door of my goddamn apartment and starts running around, screaming about how evil trolls are trying to eat his brain!" Troy Douglas nearly spat out his beer at that one. Mostly, he ended up coughing and spluttering a bit as he forced his drink back down his throat. Although, Troy was far and away the runner-up in this race to complete and total inebriation. Tommy Wade had already lapped him at least once, and was coming back around for another pass. "Anyway," Tommy said. "Turns out he'd taken some really bad mushrooms, then picked a fight with a midget at a bar. Little guy ends up pulling a knife on him, and 30 minutes later I've got a freaked-out sous chef breakdancing on my new carpet." "And you STILL let this guy work for you?" "If you'd ever tasted his lobster bisque, you would've too." Troy stared into his increasingly empty glass, shaking his head in disbelief. If the last month had taught Troy anything about Tommy Wade, it's that he was much like Elaine Benes. He had a vault of locked-up information, and apparently, liquor was the key. A very potent and rather expensive scotch, in fact, that was currently melting away the cash supply in Troy's wallet. Not that the cost mattered one bit, Troy could afford it, and the cost was well worth sitting through nine million stories about Tommy's bizarre chef pals for the few brief nuggets of information that really mattered. Ever since he showed up out of the blue in Japan, Tommy had told Troy his story in dribs and drabs, but Troy had the feeling his future brother-in-law was still reticent to part with the most important of details. Hence the booze. Troy figured that if he loosened Tommy's gears enough, eventually he might let something out. Crude and Machiavellian? Yes. Effective? Well, it wasn't before long that this little gem popped out. *** It was raining in South Beach, and Tommy's already worn-down Nikes nearly caused him to slip on the slick pavement of the latest in a seemingly endless series of narrow back alleys. Flailing wildly, Tommy balanced himself against the damp brick wall with his right hand, only to accidentally knock his phone out of his jeans pocket with his left hand. "Oh, Christ," he lamented as he saw the BlackBerry tumble to the concrete, flip over twice and land with a small splash in a puddle. To most, it was an exaggeration, but for Thomas Wade, it was the truth. His life really did depend on his phone. Two weeks worth of running, and Tommy still wasn't quite sure why he let text messages from a blocked phone number control his life. After all, for all he knew, the bookies and loan sharks looking for him might just be dangling him on a string, toying with him until they finally decided to cut him off. But, he wasn't dead yet, and he figured that being alive with one friend was better than being dead with none. Not really a hard equation to balance out, actually. Which is why, here in this dark, wet Miami back alley, he dropped to his knees and reached furiously for his phone, hoping that his own clumsiness and idiocy hadn't cost him dearly. When the phone finally stopped fumbling through his fingers, his heart sank. Nothing. A black screen. He pushed the power button again and again, praying that repetition might make a difference. It didn't. Finally, desperately, he resorted to that tried and true method of bending technology to your will. He held the phone firmly in his left hand, reared back with his right and whacked the battery casing. 3... 2... 1... Perfect. The phone blinked back to life, and after a few impatient moments of waiting, he resurrected the text message that had gotten him into this predicament in the first place. "D. BAXTER. 88 GRANT, MIAMI. BACK ALLEY." Slowing his breath, Tommy scanned the alley until he saw a lone blue door, the pain faded and scratched, the doorknob nearly falling off. But, stuck on it in beaten-up, peeling masking tape were two large number eights. "Well, this has to be the place." He'd taken to talking to himself. Not the worst way to keep his sanity when the only other communication he had was with his anonymous text-messaging sherpa. He knocked, half-dreading that whoever came out from behind that door - D. Baxter or not - would bring Tommy face-to-face with the barrel of yet another gun ... or worse. That was before a petite blond in a sharp business suit casually swung the door open, a serene smile plastered on her face. "You Wade?" Tommy nodded. By now, he was too tired to do anything else. "I'm Denise Baxter. Come with me." *** Troy had one question, albeit an obvious one. "Who's Denise Baxter?" The answer, however, never came. The sum total of all that whiskey had reduced Tommy Wade into a dribbling pile of goo, one that collapsed into sleep in this back booth of a crowded Sydney bar. Answers could wait, but not for long.
