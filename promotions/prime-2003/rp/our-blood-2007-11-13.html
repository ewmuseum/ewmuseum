Our Blood

13 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/13/2007 “The king goes as far as he may, not as far as he could.” -Spanish Proverb November 6th, 2007 CE 9:13 A.M. I knocked on the thick, panel door, mentally steeling myself. Boston’s bitter cold was kicking my ass, and my teeth chattered loudly. My breath dipped and danced out of my mouth in the early morning’s zephyrs. The grey in the sky had me convinced these people had never seen the sun. And it was only fitting that this cheerless day found me on my father’s doorstep. It had been less than a week since he had sent someone to find me. My estranged father slithering back into my life, for whatever reason, came at the most opportune of times. But I had figured things out at Great American Nightmare. Hell, my left arm was still nigh unusable from top-rope mischief. Only pride stopped me from putting on a sling. That, and a full fucking bottle of Vicodin. The man who answered the door was not Ettore Calvino, but one of those nameless Italian cronies who inevitably dies in the final scene of a mob movie. With no pretense of manners, this crook led me silently towards an ornately decorated oak door. I examined its engravings, looking for some mob motif, but evidently this house was old Boston. There was no time to remark on the irony of the Irish-Catholic imagery on the polished wood. The Paschal Lamb nodded its sacrificial head at me as I pushed the door open; the foreboding piled on thick. I have walked into my father’s office at different locations across the country. San Antonio two years ago. Los Angeles before that. Whatever member of the extended Lucchese family works above him is one picky wop. But every time I walk in, the office is somehow the same. That musty, wooden odor is admittedly stuck with the mahogany desk he refuses to throw away. That smell was there, but this time, other things were different. There were still no pictures of me, but a small silver frame held a picture of my mother. His old typewriter had finally been replaced by a working computer−the staunch opponent of technology had finally been overcome by the tides. But part of the difference was ineffable, some lost part of his mystique. I tried not to dwell on it, since I didn’t know the man anyway. “Hello, Chandler.” I had learned the smallest of his tics in order to hate them all fully. I heard his voice in nightmares kept hidden even from Dr. DiPendo. This was not that voice. This was a fragile, throaty thing that made me start. “Your voice…?” The words slid out before I could think about stopping them. He put his head down, those unchanging hazel eyes closing momentarily. It looked like he was smiling. “Tracheotomy.” Apparently, this matter-of-fact announcement was supposed to clarify everything. “About a year ago,” he clarified, “doctors found a benign tumor in my throat. It was nothing, though.” “I wasn’t worried.” “Yeah, well…” He trailed off very softly, now surprisingly quiet. In no mood to defend his fatherly misgivings, he waited for me to say something and declare I was ready to be civil. I wasn’t, so I extended the painful silence. “How are you?” He faked sincere pretty well. But you don’t send a hired hand to someone’s door at four in the morning without a damn good ulterior motive to be hiding. That voice coiled around my thoughts, stifling all else. Looking at him was like watching a favorite old movie, only to find that it’s been poorly re-dubbed in the same language. “I’m wondering why you sent for me in the middle of the night.” Despair yanked his lips into a curdled frown. “What, do you really want to know how I am? My shoulder hurts like hell, I’ve got an intensely important match that I should be preparing for, and I’m knee-deep in self lament that I even showed up here.” “You always were brutally honest,” he whispered, smiling. “The fuck would you know?!” Yeah, I know, you’ve seen this episode before. Chandler meets Daddy. Chandler gets sad and angry. See Chandler yell. Not this time. I’m not wasting another sliver of my life getting up in arms over Calvino’s shining failure. “You…you don’t know.” “Congratulations on that win, by the way. You look like a real athlete out there.” He was actually looking at me, when I expected an evasive turn of the head. I felt like he wanted me to know that he watched me from afar, the proud parent on the sideline. “You would’ve made a fine military man.” “Ah yes,” I chimed, taking the bait, “the opportunity to go across the world and father bastard children to abandon. Honor and glory abound.” The thought of me putting on an American military uniform was my mother’s greatest fear. And when we were back living in that poor Vietnamese neighborhood, I thought about it. I saw Blind Bao tromping around, had visions of my mother dying in that pitiful home and I thought about it. But every time I seriously considered it, personal demons regarding my GI arrested the inklings. I’ll admit, I’d left for the enlistment office more than once. But I never made it through the door. “I know there’s nothing I can do to make you forgive me. But can’t you allow an old man some silence on the matter? I have to live with what I did every day. Hell, I turn on the TV at the right time and I’m literally looking at my own sins.” I was quiet now, content to hear that mine was not the only life of unending repentance. “I ask God every day what I can do to make right with your mother. “Your answers are here on earth, not with some celestial being.” “You don’t believe in God?” He sounded shocked, as only a devotee of modern religion can. “I don’t believe in religion. You think a Palestinian preacher from two thousand years ago remains alive in spirit, merely to answers the whims of whiny malcontents?” Calvino’s face softened. “You speak so well. It makes me proud, even if you don’t want to hear it.” Who was this unfamiliar foe? He was avoiding my arguments, drawing me away from the point of things…this kindness was unnecessary in the face of my stoically preserved hate. “You’re smart enough to do anything you want after this.” “After what?” “Anything…you want.” His voice was somber and muted now. Where was this going? “I need something,” he confessed. I could feel anger crusading righteously through my body. The warmness of personality was all part of the setup. Schmooze your target before you lay the big one on him. “I would never ask…if there weren’t other…there are some circumstances-“ “The question here isn’t what you want me to do, but why you’re so hesitant to ask. That means you’re almost certain I won’t do it. I’ll save you the embarrassment: no. Get someone else to do your criminal errands.” My instinct was to get up, but curiosity compelled me to stay in my seat. “Please, Chandler, I’m your father,” he pleaded. The man looked like he was going to fall apart and just dissolve into primitive emotion. His eyes had finally changed. They were clinging to something, some hope. “I have always wanted the best for you. I need you to want…to put aside your hate. Don’t you see that an old man is begging you to help him?!” Were these the words of a deranged capo or a desperate father? “I wish I could help you, Ettore. I’m sor…I’ve got to go. I’ve got a flight to catch.” I stood up and faced that misplaced door. The state of things in this house seemed much sorrier than I had imagined. I tried my hardest not to feel bad for Calvino, but then two things happened that erased all else from my mind. First, I heard the old man’s guttural voice devolved into one sob, then squalls of tears. Second, above the maelstrom of his anguish, I heard an unmistakable sound. The tick of metal on metal. A hammer cocking back against the heel of a gun. I whirled around and was formally introduced to the barrel of a Browning 9mm. There were tears slipping down Calvino’s rough cheeks. “I’m sorry, Chandler! You don’t know how hard…but I need you to…yes, I need you.” Any ambient description of the event would be a lie; I thought of nothing but my own death. “Killing your son is hard.” “I’m not going to…I need you to agree to help me. One last thing and then…then no more. You wouldn’t understand if…you just wouldn’t…” He could only get out a couple words in between loud, weeping gasps. “Say you’ll do it so I can put down this goddamn gun. Say…” “I hate you.” “I know.” “I’ll do it.” The gun fell to the desk with Calvino’s exhausted wail. He put his head in his arms on top of the desk, that pitiable monster. Neither of us had a choice here. “What’s so goddamn hard about what you do?!” I was screaming, liberated to think out loud now that the gun was down and I knew he needed my life for something. “You’re the king! Oh, long live the king! You’ve got it fucking easy, you spineless prick.” We had no more words left. I sat down in the chair, Calvino descending into delirium in front of my eyes. What would I have to do? And what on the planet could bring the king to this madness? / / / / November 1st, 2007 CE 1: 18 P.M.. Ettore Calvino’s eyes were trained on the other man in his office. In the grey Boston afternoon, his office was shadowy and bleak; it should have been haunted, by the look of things. “Ettore,” the man started, “what do you think we should do about this?” The other man lingered in the shadows, leaning against the wall. His trench coat and bowler hat gave him an anachronistic air. They also helped hide his form; this was a man who was used to hiding in early fog of Boston mornings. “It doesn’t need to get out of hand,” Calvino retorted. “These things should be handled within our house. “The fuck does that mean, Ettore?! You’ve been unacceptably lax dealing with this type of shit. And now you tell me it’s a family affair!” His voice was rising, anger bubbling up quickly. “We’ve always handled things like these quietly,” he defended. “You come back from prison and change the way we do things like that? C’mon, Stevie, that’s horseshit.” Calvino seemed unsure with his words, halting mid-sentence several times and looking up at Steven Crea, a.k.a. Stevie Wonder. “Ettore, your son is going to need to get involved.” “What? No, Stevie. I won’t stand for it.” Despite his advanced years, Crea still had the gunslinger’s ways about him. Before Calvino could flinch, Stevie Wonder’s revolver was fixed on him. “I’m not talking about a choice here, Ettore. Your son is the only bait we can use to draw out our enemies. If you don’t realize that, you’re a fool.” Calvino gulped loudly. He was composed; his life was one of guns always pointed at men. Men killing men. Vietnamese killing Americans. Italians killing Irish. “I can’t, Stevie.” “If you say ‘I can’t,” again, then you condemn yourself to death, Ettore. Let that be known.” His tone said that those were the nonnegotiable details of the situation. “Can I put down this revolver?” Those eyes were full of hazel pain. The king goes only as far as he may. “Put it down. I’ll send someone for him tonight.”
