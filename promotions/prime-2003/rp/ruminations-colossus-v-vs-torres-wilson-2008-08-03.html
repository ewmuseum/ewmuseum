Ruminations (Colossus V, vs. Torres Wilson)

3 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/03/08 One Goose vodka tonic. Three Bombay gin ‘n tonics. Three shots of Maker’s. One-and-a-half modestly-sized joints. That’s how the Admin Star medicated himself prior to and during the customary PRIME pre-Colossus Staff Mixer, but it wasn’t the cocktail of liquors in his belly or the sweet, heavy ganj radiating the smell of skunk off his clothes that finally got Nova to relax and enjoy himself at the party. It was the realization of an old cliché. Nova had always referred to PRIME as “Home,” and during his drunker moments would oft liken the roster to a sort of dysfunctional “Family” that managed – albeit violently – to cohabitate with one another from week to week. But before attending the mixer – an event closed to active AND veteran roster members, open only to staff – Nova’s appreciation of that sentiment, of the concept of an incorporated entity as a family structure, was shamefully shallow and meaningless. Troy had warned him against going to the event, a rare lapse in judgment by the woman Nova had sought council from in the darkest moments of his life, and more times than any other female figure in his life. “You’ll be bored out of your mind, Caes,” the voice of the Queen chirped through the phone receiver. “Maybe, but that’s why I’m drinking before I go,” Nova replied, clumsily juggling the receiver against his ear while slurping a shot of bourbon. “Do what you want, but it’ll be me you’re calling in forty-five minutes for help when you’re on the verge of tears from conversations about over-budgeting, under-staffing, and that insaaaaaaaaane Christmas party two years ago,” Troy teased, “but hey, if CP makes an appearance, then it’s all worth it, right? Gotta keep that eye on the Enemy.” “Meh,” Nova shrugged, “honestly, I’m startin’ to come around on the guy. He’s not like the others. I feel like he might actually be able to understand that he doesn’t have to act like an asshole for people to think he means business.” “What’s this?” Lindsay mocked, “the radical leftist stoner…respecting an authority figure?” “Yeah, yeah,” Nova rolled his eyes, “well I gotta run to this thing, I’ll give you a shout later.” “Sooner than you think, I’d be willing to bet,” Troy quipped. “You have more money than me, so I’m not taking that bet,” Nova chuckled, “later, Lindz.” “Later, Caes,” Troy replied. But he didn’t call her back to rescue him from a dull party. He had a great time talking to staff from all levels of the backstage workforce that turned the gears of the PRIME monolith each week, and most importantly, his eyes were opened to the realization of just what the wider implications of that “family” concept were. There were people who had “known” him, observed him for years, encountered him backstage, made him in some small way a part of their lives…and he became increasingly humbled as it was made aware to him in the evening’s conversations. He talked to Ryan, who told him that his match with Brandon Youngblood at Colossus II had convinced him of two things: first, that he would never be a professional wrestler. Second, that he would stick with this company even though his shitty janitorial job made him want to kill himself, because he loved it. He’s currently taking business classes at night and working in the advertising department. Nova talked to Buck, and forgot about how in the midst of the Fuck You craziness, when half of PRIME seemed to fear him and the other half detested him severely, it was humble Buck from the loading dock who knocked on the Universal Champion’s locker room door, looked him in his hollow eyes, and asked him to write a college recommendation letter for his son, because he remembered a conversation they’d had when Nova was drunk in a bar one night and offered to do it. People knew him, more than the recollections of a hundred shows broadcast on TVs all over the arena. They knew what he liked, remembered nice things he had said to them, told him how scared they were when they saw Youngblood drop him on his neck or when he fell and hit his head in the Glass House match. He mattered, as a human being, and he hadn’t even realized it. He had always mistakenly identified the roster as his surrogate “family” though his encounters with many of them were often unfriendly outside the ring, and always quite physical within it. The wrestlers were his co-workers, and maybe three of them could tell you anything personal about him. These people from the staff, on the other hand, had carried him from one corner of an arena to the other, had listened to him ramble half-conscious, had applied ice packs to his head, cheered him when he stood up against bullies and shook their heads in bewilderment when he cast aspersions on the fans and the industry. They connected with him because he had been there a long time and took an interest in them, an interest in bridging the uncomfortable Mason-Dixon line of Superstars to the left and veritable NPC’s to the right. He loved being in their presence as a colleague. The Mixer was a hell of a time. But after Nova said goodbye to the remaining guests at the party and pushed open the doors to the damp summer air, the good vibes of a no-drama evening on PRIME’s tab were shattered by a series of camera flashes, and the inevitable vampiric media swarm. “Mr. Vega, is it true that your divorce with Ariel Martiarra was due in part to allegations of spousal abuse?” “Mr. Vega! There are reports of drug abuse that extend FAR beyond your on-screen advocacy of marijuana!” Torres Wilson had released Round Two of the interviews with Ariel’s parents. Nova hadn’t bothered to pick up a copy, and anyway it was all over the internet if he wanted to see what they said. He didn’t. They’d hated his guts since the moment he entered their perfect world and swept their daughter off her ordinarily sensible feet. He began to push past the assembled reporters, and then thought better of it. “What I’m most thankful for,” Nova growled, grabbing a microphone out of someone’s hands and staring into a camera, “is that I work in a place that sanctions and encourages the kind of physical retribution I’m going to be able to enact upon Torres Wilson for ever peaking you people’s interest in my personal life.” Probably not the best defense to Wilson’s image of Nova as the drug-addled, brainless monster, but Nova didn’t really care. Maybe he could find a way to accidentally break Wilson’s hands during their cage match at Colossus. No more typing up slanderous reports. But then, the shmuck would just dictate his filth into a microphone. Shoving past the tiny throng, Nova walked to his car in a nearby lot. Leaning against it, he lit a cigarette and stared up at the moon. This would be his fourth go-round at the biggest show on PRIME’s calendar. Also potentially his last sanctioned bout with the company. He couldn’t help but look back at his mindset, his position, going into Colossus IV. He’d felt at his wits’ end, frayed, spread thin…exhausted. He’d felt like he had to get out, escape, breathe clean air away from the place that only seemed to remind him, through his record of professional accomplishments, of all the ways he’d failed in other aspects of his life. Standing where he stood now in August of 2008, he could happily report he didn’t feel those feelings any more. He felt angry at Torres Wilson for making him a seemingly arbitrary target. He felt content with his position amongst friends in the PRIME Executive CABINET~!, leaving a different kind of footprint on the company in his managerial role. He felt excited to be in front of a capacity crowd again inside the ring, and vindicated that he would be able to drag Wilson from pillar to proverbial post. He felt better. And a year later, that’s all he could really ask for.
