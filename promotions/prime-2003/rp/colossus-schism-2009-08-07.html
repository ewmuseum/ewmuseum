Date: 7 August 2009 Downtown Boston 9:32 pm Red Devil Cocktail Club The name of the place sounded classy, like you'd find women in sequined dresses with handbags here, and men in suits. The reality was, the Red Devil Cocktail Club could have used a good washing up here and there. A new upholstery job on the worn bar stools. Elise was well aware that she outclassed this place. Unfortunately, the bartender did not seem to be aware of this, as the service here was relatively bad (when you took into account Elise's reputation). She twirled the stir stick from her last appletini between her perfectly manicured pink fingertips as she waited for the next cocktail to come across the bar. There weren't many other customers, or at least not many that looked to Elise like they were customers as good as she was, so she was, needless to say, getting a little pissed off that it was taking so damn long to make one cocktail. Elise was dressed like a woman who wanted attention. A rose red cocktail dress hugged her form, dipping down in the front to show off a not-quite-respectable amount of cleavage, accented by a diamond teardrop necklace that hung just to the hem that barely covered her chest. And not one. Not one bite all night. She was regretting now that she didn't force Kaz to come with her, even though, as he'd said, he'd “rather eat glass” than sit around “some fruity cocktail bar.” The greatest unfairness about women like Elise Ares, though, is that they so often get what they want. As soon as the bartender had delivered her appletini, fate struck in the form of a low, deep voice that resonated from behind her, and almost would have sent a shiver up the back of her neck – almost. If she hadn't immediately sprung into action. “May I have the pleasure of buying your cocktail for you, miss?” The voice was European, the phrasing classy. Elise knew somebody who was trying to sound like a high-roller when she heard them. And she knew pretty well which ones were just talking. It was all in the dress... And this one, she saw almost immediately as she swiveled on her barstool to face the origin of the sound, knew how to talk the talk and walk the walk. The gentleman who had spoken to her – because she figured that really was the best way to describe him – had short-clipped dark hair, cut like someone who was in business, gelled to suave perfection. His eyes were hazel, zeroed in on hers as she leaned back in the chair. His frame was large, not large in comparison to, say, her co-workers, but big enough that he still seemed somehow to dwarf her. In dress, he was stylish, but not... well, metrosexual. A tailored pair of slacks paired with a nice, but not overly nice shirt. A man's shirt. A shirt that said 'I'm the kind of guy who, if he wore a silk shirt to a place like this, would totally spill the butter dip for the crab all over it.' So at least he wasn't gay. Elise tilted her head, her dark hair spilling over to one side. Her eyes, she knew, were at their most beguiling this evening, outlined with a smudge of black and done in smoky tones to emphasize... well, frankly how hot she was. As though she really needed to do that in a place like Boston. “Modern women are supposed to be offended by that kind of thing, you know. I make enough money to pay for my own drinks, and probably for a round for everybody in this place, if I wanted.” The man nodded, raising his scotch glass to her. He took it neat. “A fair enough point. May I enjoy your company, then?” “Persistent. Either a good or bad sign.” “Maybe not a sign at all,” the man said, chuckling. “Maybe I'm just persistent. And you look a little... bored.” Elise rolled her eyes. “Good call. I don't think it would take much of a detective to figure that one, though, so I hope that's not your profession.” “Moving a little fast, aren't we? You don't even know my name.” Elise didn't like that little tinge of cocky. It was men like that – the ones who arched their eyebrows, who played it cool – that she liked to knock down a peg or two. While, of course, getting a little something for herself out of the deal. She extended her hand to him, palm down, as though she was royalty. She was, as far as she was concerned... and if this guy wanted to stick around, he'd learn soon enough. “Elise,” she said, plainly expecting him to show proper courtesy. He went for the cheesy move, of course, pressing his lips to her hand as he delicately took it in his own. “Elise. Lovely name. Daimon Vytautas.” Elise took her hand back, not without a slight hint of a snatch when it seemed like he might be lingering a bit too long. “That's a mouthful.” “So I hear.” Daimon chuckled, and turned to gesture to the bartender for a refill on his scotch. With no small amount of annoyance, she noted the fast service. The bartender, she decided, must have been gay to be so fast on serving Daimon. “No one's pronounced it right since I came to this country,” Daimon went on. It was again with some annoyance that Elise realized that he was still talking. There was, at least, something of interest in what he was saying, though. “Oh, another immigrant? Me, I'm not from here either.” “You don't look it, if you'll forgive me saying so,” Daimon continued. There was something annoying about the way he was so obsequious, when Elise could tell that he wasn't sorry at all for anything he was saying. “You don't have to keep apologizing. I'd be more offended if you thought I was one of these fat Americans,” she said, not bothering to keep her voice down. A couple of snide, irritated glances were winged her way no doubt, but Elise Ares had long begun to pass over those as simply the features of ugly people's faces, not particularly having anything to do with her or her behavior. “You've got an attitude about you,” Daimon said, shaking his head. “I don't know what it is, but you're not afraid to say exactly what you think. I like that.” “I wasn't exactly raised to be a shrinking violet,” Elise said, shrugging. “Why say anything if it's not really what you feel? If you can't handle it, that's your problem.” Daimon nodded, taking a sip of his scotch, which had started to sweat in his hands. “Agreed... it's one thing that Americans don't seem to understand. This whole issue of pleasantries, making small talk... never did it in my country.” Elise raised an eyebrow, leaning forward with interest. “What country is that?” “Lithuania,” he said, taking another sip. “The grayest piece of shit there is in Eastern Europe.” That caught Elise's interest. “Lithuania, huh? I ah... happen to know someone from there too.” “Oh?” “Yeah, he's my tag... uh... bodyguard,” she stumbled momentarily. Don't bone it this fast, Elise. You remember how to do this, don't you? At that, both of Daimon's dark eyebrows shot straight up on his forehead. “Bodyguard? What's a girl like you need one of those for.” “My family's got a reputation,” Elise said, smirking an arching a perfectly plucked brow. The grin got them every time. Never tell it all at first, reveal it slowly, reel them in... and then bam, right in the kidneys. The proverbial kidneys, anyway, this isn't some weird urban legend where you wake up in a bathtub full of ice. “A reputation for what?” Daimon leaned a little closer. “I'm sure you'd love to know,” Elise said, crossing her legs casually. “Unfortunately for you...” “What a fucking surprise,” came a familiar Eastern European sneer from behind Elise's right shoulder. “Imagine finding an old friend right here in Boston...” It didn't sound much like Kazys thought highly of this guy, Elise thought to herself, in the split second between the surprise intervention and Daimon's reaction. “Kazys...” The name was said with as much venom as possible. His narrowed hazel eyes were no longer roaming over Elise with a hungry wolf sort of gaze. And with good reason. 15 June 1997 A month into boot camp, and the last training group was due in the next couple of hours. Herd after herd of young men had been bussed in under Kazys' watch, his training group being one of the earliest to come in for this rotation. Not that he'd gotten any extra training out of it. Which was somewhat frustrating for a young man who was looking forward to a glorious life of crawling around in the mud and playing with guns. He was outside now, walking the small grounds of their camp after lunch. Lunch in the army was nothing fantastic, but neither was it as disgusting as he had expected. In fact, Kazys was finding that most things in life fell somewhere on the in-between. Neither as good nor as bad as they could have been. Neither as perfect nor as terrible, as interesting nor as boring. It was enough to make a guy go crazy, realizing that life tended to be mostly about living through mediocrity. That was, he supposed, why the army was a good thing for him. Even if, right now, it felt like he wasn't being challenged, this wasn't a life that promoted only existing, but one that promoted grabbing existence by the proverbial balls. That was something he could get behind, even if it turned out to be only imaginary. He was somewhat conscious that he needed the illusion right now, the thought that he was striving towards something, not just spinning his wheels now that school was done. A flurry of sudden motion caught his attention from the left, and he turned quickly, eyes zooming in on one of the smaller young men in the group, Paulius. Light brown hair, scrawny, but with urgent eyes. Kaz didn't think he was the type that would make a good soldier, but who the hell was he to say? “Kazys,” he said, his voice somewhere in between a whisper and a hiss. “Did you hear?” Kazys frowned deeply, obviously aware of the sudden urgency in Paulius' voice, but not interested in looking like a gossip. His curiosity, though, got the better of him. “I... hear what?” “About Daimon...” Kazys frowned. Daimon was his roommate, or at least he would be when they moved to field training. He'd been acting strange lately, sickly. He'd stayed in for the lunch hour, which meant he was gonna be damn hungry after PT, because dinner was late tonight. “Hey,” Paulius said, giving Kazys a hard shove. “You all here?” He pointed to his head. “C'mon, you need to see this.” They were off, then, at a brisk jog, headed towards the small barracks where the recruits were housed. And it didn't take long for him to see exactly what had happened. Daimon's stuff? Gone. Except for one bag. His trunk hung open, ransacked. His knapsack and gear hung over the bedpost, just where it had, completely useless. “He...” “Deserted,” Paulius finished. He had a tendency towards the overdramatic. “That motherfucker, if I ever find him again...” Daimon had known Kazys in grade school, before his family moved away to the country, but the two boys had always stayed friends, sharing mutual visits. But on all of their mutual visits, Kazys had never quite figured out how much of a scumbag, how much of a motherfucker, how much of a coward Daimon really was. Until now. ---- Chapter Two: Posturing For Position ---- "...I never thought I'd see the day where you lacked the patriotism to stick around Lithuania. " Daimon had a smug sense of entitlement to him about everything he said. From the way he stood to the way he spoke showed signs of elitism at its finest. Usually this was a characteristic that drew Elise to a man. Men who feel entitled usually are. Their bank accounts for the most part tend to back up the swagger that greets their company when they walk in the room. There was something different about Daimon though, and Kazys was almost like a dog picking up on the scent of Daimon's insecurities. "To meet you in America of all places," Daimon posed, taking this moment to wrap his arm around Elise uninvited. "I'm a bit shocked." "I'm not." Kazys answered almost immediately, "I could smell asshole in the air." "Daimon!" Elise interrupted getting up out of her seat and away from the quite uncomfortable position the two Eastern Europeans had put her in, "This is my bodyguard, Kazys Jankauskas. It looks like you've already met thought..." "A bodyguard!" Daimon reacted with a chuckle and a smirk, "Always the good little soldier. You found youself a good one, Elise. He takes orders very well." "I'd rather be a good soldier than a coward." Kazys responded immediately, "I figure if I get angry enough you'll run away. Just like you do from everything else." "Actually, I'm rather enjoying the company of my lady friend." Daimon responded, "Your boss is quite lovely." "Lets get out of here Elise." Kazys spoke up immediately. "I..." Ares paused and bit her lip, looking back at Daimon and then at Kazys Jankauskas. The PRIME money was good, but it wasn't THAT good. She could use the boost in the wallet and Daimon could certainly do that for her. After all, Kazys had no problems taking that Universal Title shot from her back on Revolution 200... why did she have to listen to him all of a sudden? Because he won a match? She was still the one paying the bills, and if you want the bills paid you have to have cash. "I think I'll stay here." "You can't be serious." Kazys replied, "This asshole?" "Just because you don't like someone I have to leave?" Elise started to become irritated, "What are you? My daddy?" "You don't understa..." "No, you don't understand Kazys." Daimon chirped in, "The lady said she wanted to stay. Now be a good boy and leave." "You smug son of a..." "KAZYS!" Elise jumped between Kazys and Daimon right as The Iron Wolf went to wraps his hands around Daimon's neck. Snarling, Kazys looked into the beautiful eyes of his tag team partner for answers. "Go." Elise Ares pointed towards the exit where Jankauskas just looked back at her, bewildered. Treating him like a dog, is this what his job title had come to? Tolerating her delusional arrogance and non-stop talking was one thing, but blatant disrespect to his position as a human being was another. He might've been a good soldier and did what was right for his country, but he was no lapdog. "Don't you ever talk to me like that." Kazys glared a hole into Elise before shifting his focus to Daimon, still wearing a smirk behind her. "As long as I pay you," Elise verbally puffed out her chest, narrowing her eyes as they plunged deep into the soul of Kazys Jankauskas. That is the place where the next line hurt the most, "I'll talk as I please." "Then you can take your money," Kazys responded taking two steps towards the door, "and go fuck yourself." Elise just watched as Kazys turned his back and walked away. Pushing open the door, he left the bar, left his job, and quite possibly left her life. Maybe things were better off this way, he'd served his purpose. She'd made it to PRIME, she'd been on television, and was currently enjoying her position in the spotlight. What'd he do in the end? Help her win a few tag team matches? Big deal. Tag Teams were the small bucks anyway. All Kazys was ever doing was holding her down in the end. Down from grabbing the spotlight from herself, from living her life as she saw fit, and most importantly from getting money whenever she wanted... however she wanted it. "Don't take it so hard, beautiful." Daimon said as he walked up behind Elise Ares and wrapped his arm around her once again, breaking her train of thought. "I'm sure a more obedient lap dog will come along. There are tons of fish in the sea, and half of them would never speak to a beautiful woman the way he talked to you." "I don't need him." Elise responded, almost as if she was trying to convince herself, "I can have anyone I want." Rays of sunlight sifted through the blinds between the hotel room and the sundeck, covering her half naked body with streaks of light. Reaching down under the covers which concealed her lowerbody, she pulled up her underwear wrapped around her right ankle into their proper position. Surprisingly she didn't wake the man laying down next to her, which she now realized slobbered like a St. Bernard in his sleep. Disgusted she wastes no time now rolling out of her bed sporting a matching pair of red bra and panties. He certainly wasn't the most attractive man she'd ever waken up next to, but she'd also seen worse. At least the money was good. Flipping through the money clip left on the night stand next to the man sawing logs, the money was better than she remembered. It's times like this where a normal woman would probably feel like a whore, but not Elise Ares. Whores don't get ATM cards, she thought to herself with a smile as she tossed it down into a cargo bag she had on the floor next to a white silk robe. Zipping up the bag, she smiled at the thought of the things men are willing to do when they're wasted with a beautiful woman. Picking up the silk robe off the floor, she slipped it on over her beautiful physique while pushing the cargo bag under the second bed with her foot. That's the bed that would've belonged to Kazys Jankauskas, had he ever came back to the hotel room that night. She hadn't heard a peep from him since he left the bar the evening before. Maybe it was for the best, maybe this is when her career really began. With all the chaos of a three-way tag team match, she might even be able to snag the Tag Team Championships alone. My, wouldn't that be a feat? She would never admit it, but without Kazys around she didn't quite feel the same. She felt almost... incomplete. Like she lost an appendage in a war. Tying the robe shut she walked around towards the bathroom, the door was hanging wide open apparently from the night before. Elise didn't particularly remember much. Walking through the door and taking a step onto the cold tile floor of the bathroom barefoot, she could've swore she heard a key card swipe in the door next to her. But I suppose being half awake would do that to you. No, she definitely heard it. The door cracked open and before Elise could even turn around to see what in the hell was going on she felt an iron grip around her left bicep, pulling her out of the bathroom and into the hotel room hallway. It all happened so fast she didn't even see what happened. She tried to jerk away helplessly, finally getting a glimpse at the colossal man who is dragging her along like a cavewoman. "What the hell are you doing here?!" She says sharply. With those words Kazys Jankauskas left the elevator, inside Elise Ares just watched in silence. In her head, she was wondering to herself if she was listening to what she was saying. The truth was she wasn't even sure if what she was telling him was true. The doors closed once again, leaving Elise Ares by herself where she dropped to the floor and leaned up against the back of the elevator. The end was drawing closer, and this time she wasn't the one pulling the strings. For the first time in her life she found herself in a position where she wasn't in control of everything around her. "Think Elise think..." She muttered to herself, "How are you going to get yourself out of this one?"
