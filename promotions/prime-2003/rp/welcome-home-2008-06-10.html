Welcome Home

10 June 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 10, 2008 When he first regained consciousness, David’s first thought was that he couldn’t see, or breathe for that matter. His second thought was that he couldn’t move his hands to his face, which only increased the panic beating in his chest. Helpless, choking in the dark, paralyzed, can’tbreathecan’tthinkcan’tmove - Like a mule kicking him in the head, David’s thought process jarred him back to reason. He regained his composure and closed his eyes, focusing only on his breathing. It slowed, calmed, and David took stock of his situation. He wasn’t suffocating, he was wearing a mask. Judging by the smell of dried blood, he guessed it to be his wrestling mask. That took care of the breathing issue. It took several long blinks before David realized that his eyes were, in fact, now open. It was just the room he was in was very, very dark. Okay, he was breathing and he wasn’t blind. Now there was the matter of his arms. He couldn't move them. No, that wasn’t accurate. He could move his arms, but they seemed to be held in place. His wrists were tied, perhaps? The initial panic of waking in a strange place was starting to fade, and David tried to piece together his recent history. He recalled riding in the ambulance after his match at UltraViolence. Plasma was being pumped into his body to replace the blood that had been pouring out of it. Next, he remembered being on a stretcher, hearing the wheels rattle as he was being rolled out of the ambulance. He wasn’t outside - a garage? The paramedics didn’t say anything the entire time, which seemed a little strange. For a moment, he thought that maybe he’d died and the paramedics had nothing to say. But then he felt the pain in his back, in his chest, in his head, all over. No, he was still very much alive. The wheels on the stretcher squeaked and wobbled while the paramedics pushed him into what David assumed was the hospital. But despite having the gauze wrapped around his head, he could smell that he wasn’t at a hospital. That antiseptic smell wasn’t there. He tried to turn his head, but there was no strength left in him. Someone said something as the paramedics passed by. It was a woman’s voice, and it was the last thing he could recall. She said “Welcome to...” Summervale. She said “Welcome to Summervale.” David felt a tremor of fear deep inside himself, a warning of the earthquake that was dangerously close to breaking through his self-control. His memories of this place, back when it was called Summervale Psychiatric Hospital, were horrific to say the least. His eyes darted left and right, trying to see something, anything, that might confirm his true whereabouts. “Hello?” David’s voice was hoarse when he tried to speak, the inside of his throat still slick with the sticky wetness of his own blood. He couldn’t see anything, no shapes or forms at all, but the echo of his voice told him that there was a wall close by his head. Other walls, or ceiling for that matter, seemed nonexistent. “Is anyone there?” A supernova of light stabbed David in the eyes. He clenched his lids together tightly, but the light still burned through the thin skin. His breath hissed through cracked teeth, and bubbles of spit smeared between his mask and his lips and chin. Somewhere at his five o’clock position, he could hear a woman’s voice. “Please remain still Mr. Cross,” she said sternly. With his burning eyes still closed, David tried to reach up to cover his face with his hands. His wrist restraints bit into his skin. “Get these off of me,” he shouted before breaking into a coughing fit. Gummy bits of his swallowed blood loosened and rose up into his mouth, but since spitting was not an option he choked it back down. A new voice, a male voice, came from his six o’clock. This voice David recognized immediately, and any doubts about his location were gone. The rising fear was held at bay by a stronger emotion - anger. Despite the burning, David opened his eyes and tried to see beyond the light that shone down from the lamp which hung less than a foot from his face. The man started to come into focus at the foot of his bed. His hair and beard, flecked with gray, was further confirmation. The white doctor’s coat was simply icing on the cake. “You are being restrained,” Dr. Menzel told him, “because you were quite hostile when you were first admitted.” David squinted at the doctor. “You want to see hostility? Take these straps off.” Dr. Menzel shook his head, staring at him with dispassionate eyes. “The restraints will remain for the time being.” The burning sensation was fading, and David forced his eyes to open wider. “Doc, I’ve got an itch under my mask. Could you come over here and scratch my nose?” The doctor looked over at the female linebacker standing beside him, apparently a nurse judging by the uniform she was crammed into, who then handed him a metallic clipboard. He flipped through what appeared to be several pages of notes. From his coat, Menzel pulled a pen and wrote something down at the bottom of a page. “Interesting. Your face is covered, yet you still show a marked level of hostility.” He looked up from the sheet and observed David with an analytical gaze. “I’ve observed that when your face is covered, you show less aggression than when your bare face is visible.” He explained to the nurse, “A defensive mechanism.” Anger and Fear were having a go with each other, and Anger was starting to wear down. Summervale, and the memories locked within its walls, were becoming more than David could take. “So it’s kidnapping now, is it?” David told the doctor. “Listen, why don’t you just take these off and let me go? I swear I’ll behave, and I won’t tell anyone about...” Dr. Menzel clicked the end of the pen, and the tip disappeared. “David, there’s nothing for you to tell. Besides, this isn’t kidnapping. You have to complete your therapy before I can release you. You escaped from the hospital before we could finish your treatment the first time.” David let his eyes drop, hovering around the doctor’s chest and nowhere near the man’s eyes. “Treatment, well that’s one word for it.” Menzel’s voice was hard, almost making David wince with his words. “Nurse, give us a minute.” He couldn’t look up. Shame had him now, and it weighed him down from the inside. His strength, what little he had left, was dissolving. This place, those horrible nightmares, the restraints, it was too much. Dr. Menzel was at David’s side, and with the light shining overhead it was far more difficult for David to look up at him. “Tell me how you feel about your last stay with us.” Tears welled up behind his mask. “Let me out of here,” David said softly. He tried to look up, but the light stung his watery eyes. “Please.” Dr. Menzel looked down at David’s tattered mask. “You need to talk to me about these things.” He watched David’s head turn away from him. “You can’t get better if you don’t open up to me.” “Six of your patients are dead because they opened up to you,” David said to the expanse of darkness to his left. “They were too far gone. There are some even I can’t reach in time.” David’s sobbing stopped long enough to summon his expired strength, struggling in vain against the thick leather wrist straps that held him on the gurney. “This is kidnapping!” he snapped. “Let me out! They’ll come looking for me!” The doctor continued to study his patient, and was barely able to contain his enjoyment of watching David squirm. “I understand you have obligations that need to be met. I’m taking care of them. A qualified surrogate will fill in for you, so your wrestling contract will not be violated.” The words stomped out what little fire David had left in him. His head flopped back onto his pillow, while the bright light resumed the assault on his eyes. There was complete defeat in his voice. “It’s so people don’t come looking for me.” The corner of Menzel’s mouth twitched upward. “Your therapy won’t be interrupted this time.” “It wasn’t therapy, you sick fuck.” David shut his eyes tightly, but the memories all came rushing back to him like a nightmare he’d never wake from. The doctor shrugged, despite the fact that David couldn’t see it. It was a trained response by this time, his reaction after repeated questioning regarding Summervale’s former employee. “I don’t know how Mr. Rocca got through our background checks.” “You got him through.” “Excuse me?” Menzel withdrew his pen once again and flipped through the pages on his clipboard, not listening to anything his patient was telling him. David could only see bright spots, but he turned his head to where he thought the doctor was standing. “You got him through. You gave him access to the drugs he used to incapacitate the patients before he raped them. You watched him do it and just wrote notes on your fucking clipboard! Why? Why would you do that?” His wrists feebly pressed against their restraints. “Why would you watch him abuse helpless patients and not do anything? You’re a doctor! You’re supposed to help these people!” Menzel applied pen to paper and looked down at his patient once again. His amusement was gone, replaces by a more studious demure. “How does that make you feel?” he asked. “Broken,” David sighed weakly. Menzel tucked the clipboard under his arm and stuck his thumb under the mask. With one motion, he pulled the mask off of David’s head and stared down at his red eyes, flushed cheeks, and blood and spit-smeared chin. He set the mask down on David’s chest, and watched as David’s twitching eyes slowly narrowed and rolled in his direction. “Now how do you feel?” he asked. “Like I want to take that pen and jam it so deep into your eye,” David said in a smooth, calm tone, “I could write on the inside of your skull.” “Interesting. Clearly we have a long way to go, David.” Dr. Menzel picked up the mask and pulled it back over David’s head, somewhat glad to cover those eyes once again. The way they stared at him, looking into him rather than at him... no, that would not do. That would not do at all. David stared up at the light, wishing it would burn through his head like a laser beam and finally put him out of his misery. He was trapped in this asylum, and no one was going to miss him. No one would look for him. “No one would even care,” David said in a voice barely more than a whisper. From somewhere beyond his feet, David heard a squeaking sound. He lifted his head off the pillow, and stared for a long while at the figure standing at the foot of his bed. The spots slowly faded as the minutes ticked by, and it slowly became clear that it was simply a coat rack that had been wheeled to his bed. A few more minutes and he saw exactly what it was that was hanging from the rack. A straightjacket. His straightjacket. No... “Sam left you a present,” Dr. Menzel said from somewhere beyond the light. “Recognize it? This was yours. I believe that’s your blood and urine on the front.” David started trembling, and Menzel watched the mask sink in and puff out, sink in and puff out, while David took panicked breaths. Pleased with the reaction, he added, “The dried semen is his.” “No, no, nonononono...” Menzel jotted down a few more notes. “Interesting. Try and get some rest David.” His footsteps echoed in the room, but were drown out by David’s sobbing. “Don’t forget who’s in control here,” the doctor told him before sheathing his pen. “Welcome back to Summervale, David.”
