Date: "Five Years in the Making" Chapter 1 2:32 a.m... There were a lot of times when Troy Douglas wished he didn't have to dream. Having spent the last two hours tumbling out of a violent, intermittent sleep on a rock-hard bed inside this particular dowtown Birmingham Marriott, this could certainly qualify as one of those times. As much as he tried to stop it, as much as he though it had finally stopped haunting him, the dream popped up at the most inexplicable and inopportune moments. That night. That call. For more than five years, he'd lived for every waking moment with the memory of that night and the aftermath. He'd thought that sleep, at least, would give him some respite, some time where he wouldn't have to live with images that had been burned in his conscious mind forever. Five years of tossing and turning, five years of counting an ever-growing collective of sheep, five years of trying every sleep-aid gimmick hawked by a 3 a.m infomercial had most certainly proved Troy Douglas wrong. Finally having had enough, Troy grouchily rolled out of bed and stumbled towards the bathroom, filling an extra-large glass with ice cold water. Clutching his sore back with one hand, he tore open an individual packet of aspirin, popped it in his mouth and quickly downed the glass. Setting the glass down on the faux-marble bathroom counter, Douglas turned off the light and staggered into the bed, flipping on the TV and collapsing onto the mattress. "Aw, shit." Having suffered many sleepless nights in a myriad of different hotel rooms across the country, Troy probably should have seen this problem coming. Nothing on TV at 2:30 in the morning. Fucking predictable. Click. ESPN. The 63rd re-air of last night's SportsCenter. Click. FX. Seriously, does the world need this many reruns of That 70's Show? Click. TBS. Major League: Back to the Minors. AGAIN. That'd only serve to give him worse nightmares then he was used to. Click. Cinemax. Well, he certainly wasn't going to get to sleep with THAT providing the ambient light and noise. Click. VERSUS. As entertaining as the concept of Chuck Norris' wacky fighting league was, the sight of that man's beard could keep him awake for days. And then, somewhere between the Food Network and the swirling mass of colors that is the Weather Channel, Troy's body, mind and eyes gave up and the veteran wrestler drifted off to sleep. It was an unconscious decision that he almost immediately regretted. Click. ... ... ... ... February 5, 2003 A hotel in Edinburgh, Scotland 4:30 a.m. local time The sun had barely begun to rise over the city of Edinburgh, but Troy Douglas had been in-and-out of sleep for hours. To be fair, this was nothing new for Troy. Chronic back pain from an ancient injury had made a consistently comfortable night of sleep a relic from the Dark Ages. The back was nagging him tonight, make no doubt of that. Taking a particularly nasty suplex at a show in Glasgow the night before had caused the pain to reemerge, and a steady supply of Tylenol and ice packs hadn't done all that much to quell the situation. But, the pain wasn't the only reason for Troy's insomnia tonight. As silly as this might sound, he had a feeling of dread and uneasiness that he couldn't quite get rid of. He didn't know what would happen, and he didn't know when, but he couldn't shake the inkling that something was going to go wrong. He tried to reject it as perfectly normal human paranoia -- which three years in the wrestling industry had given him a healthy does of -- but the intermittent rumblings in the pit of his stomach just wouldn't stop. And, sadly, the low mutterings of late-night Monty Python reruns on the TV weren't doing much to help either. After what seemed like a decade of rolling back and forth, trying to will his eyes to stay close, trying to forcibly induce R.E.M. sleep, something happened to ensure that Troy wasn't getting any more sleep tonight. Or for the forseeable future. RING! ... ... RING! ... ... RING! Three rings was enough, and Troy reached blindly towards his nighstand to pick up the phone. Still groggy and barely awake, he answered. "Whozzere?" The voice on the phone was low and somber, and Troy recognized it immediately. "Troy, are you awake?" Alan Bennett, the father of Troy's fiancee, asked the question in a hushed, measured tone. He was obviously holding something back. "Yeah, Alan. It's uh ... 4:30 in the morning over here. What is it?" Troy could here his soon-to-be father-in-law bristling on the other side of the Atlantic. After a pause that included many muffled tears, Alan Bennett finally came out with the announcement that would change Troy's life. "It's ... it's ... Lauren a-and your ... dad. A, uh, man broke in to the house and tried to rob it. Your dad came down to confront him, and the guy panicked and shot him twice in the chest. Lauren heard the shots and ran downstairs, and he -- he shot her, too." Then, in a hoarse, throaty whisper, came the phrase that would haunt Troy's dreams for years to come. "They're gone, Troy. They're dead." ... ... ... ... Click. He'd been laying with his arm pressing down on the remote control for nearly an hour now, the channel changing every few seconds while Troy drifted in and out of the worst recurring nightmare a man could ever imagine. And what made it all worse was the fact that this wasn't a fantasy. This wasn't a vampire, or a mummy, or a marshmallow man. This was reality. This was memory. And reality was the scariest thing of all. At last, he'd had enough. It was fast approaching sunrise in the South, and he had a couple of days before PRIME brought its brand of ReVolution to Birmingham. So he flipped the lights on, grabbed last week's Newsweek from the nightstand and began flipping through the pages. He'd take those images over what he saw when he closed his eyes any day of the week. He had time. Maybe sleep and Troy Douglas would come to a better agreement tomorrow. Click.
