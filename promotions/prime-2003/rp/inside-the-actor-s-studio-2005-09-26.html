Inside The Actor's Studio

26 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/26/05 INSIDE THE ACTOR'S STUDIO By Danny Ferguson FADE IN. EXT. SOMEWHERE IN MIDDLE AMERICA - NIGHT. It's quiet in Omaha on this Saturday evening, and the streets are fairly empty. Without a Nebraska game to keep everyone in the bars, life stayed quiet and reserved. It's the kind of calm American night that would be raved about on a Garrison Keillor radio speech in some description that would go on for several unflinching minutes and relate even the smallest detail like the blanketesque quality of the sky or the slow hum of passive farm machinery remindingeveryoneofhownothingchangesinthislittletownandthat' whatmakesitsogreatsweetJesusdoesn'tthisKeillorsonofabitchtak afuckingbreath?!?! We fade to a shot of a local drinking establishment, known to the locals as 'Walt's.' From across the road, we watch the half-full parking lot, and the blinking 'Go Huske_s' sign in the window. No one seems to go in or come out. That is, until a small group of shadowed figures walks in from behind the camera and stops at the head of the lot. FIGURE 1 You got a light? FIGURE 3 Yeah. He produces a lighter and holds it out, as all four men keep their backs to us. The first man stares at the open flame for a second, then shuffles uncomfortably. FIGURE 1 Um...you got a cigarette? The third man doesn't answer, but instead flips the lighter closed and puts it back in his pocket. The fourth man dig furiously into the pockets of his jacket, which we can see in the faint light as a faded denim, and eventually pulls something out. FIGURE 4 Found one! He holds it out to the first, who slips it between his lips awkwardly. He tries to let it hang there, white-trash-style. FIGURE 1 Ca- The cigarette falls out. He puts it back. FIGURE 1 Is- It falls out again. He goes to put it back in a third time, but the second dark figure, who until now has remained quiet, slaps it out of his hands. This prompts a very feminine 'tsk' reaction from the fourth figure. The first man sighs, and finally, the camera begins to pan, moving up and over the four men and spinning as it came the full 180, finally showing their faces in the light. FIGURE 1 - AKA TOPHER GRACE Whatever. I wouldn't have been able to smoke it if it's not a prop anyway. Grace, dressed in a white t-shirt and a ratty leather vest, runs a hand through the mullet wig that's glued to his head. A five o'clock shadow - more like 2:15 on his girly-looking ass - is starting to poke through. The second man is our hero, Danny Ferguson, sporting dyed black hair and a made-up scar down his cheek. He gives Grace a glance from his periphery and then ruffles his flannel shirt a little bit. Third is Ashton Kutcher...dressed exactly the same as he normally is, which is like a douche bag. He snorts and spits on the ground, and his fake mustache falls off with the loogie. He doesn't even bother to pick it up. At the end is Sean Hayes, commonly known as the super-gay friend on Will & Grace. As mentioned, he's rocking the full denim, in addition to a ponytail wig and a full (fake) beard. He can'ts stand still, and constantly fidgets to find himself a position that makes him look tougher. No, they're not filming. It's not a movie or a skit or a commercial. It's not even a run-through. This is a Saturday night in a town where nobody notices pretty-boy actors from the next slob. This is what actors do for fun. DANNY FERGUSON So this is the place? TOPHER GRACE That's the word. This is where Feldman killed that guy in '98. DANNY FERGUSON Nobody called any cops? No sheriff? ASHTON KUTCHER Guy he killed was the sheriff. SEAN HAYES That's unfounded. ASHTON KUTCHER So's your heterosexuality, Mr. 'I Just Play One on TV, I Swear' SEAN HAYES Dude, I fucked like eight chicks last night. Twice. For a total of sixteen. ASHTON KUTCHER Yeah, I'd like to see that. There's a pause, and then all three look at him, and he folds that tough exterior under the pressure. ASHTON KUTCHER Fuck you guys. Danny slowly steps forward and begins walking across his parking lot. DANNY FERGUSON As long as we're not going to be answering any questions in a squad car when this is over, I don't care who watches who have gay sex. KUTCHER & HAYES (in eerily similar tones and inflections) Hey! Danny ignores their reaction and stops at the door. He looks back at the other three. DANNY FERGUSON Ok...We go in, mind our business until the opportunity presents itself. Call it off if we don't have the numbers. Once the fight starts, every man for himself, and we rendesvouz back at the airport. Are we expecting anyone else? TOPHER GRACE Nah...Nick Cannon wanted to come, but if we brought a black guy with us, it'd be too obvious that we're looking for trouble. ASHTON KUTCHER Like the gay guy didn't clue them in. SEAN HAYES You talking in the third person again? 'Cause that's tacky. ASHTON KUTCHER I'll give you something tacky. Once again, the other three give him a look. He realizes what he said and shoves past Ferguson and into the bar. Without another word, the other actors follow him. CUT TO: INT. WALT'S - NIGHT. The bar is about as rustic as a tent in the forest, if said tent had something stale on tap. EVERYTHING is made of wood, including a fake bartender, that bears a striking resemblance to the actual bartender. Matter of fact, if it wasn't for one of them moving, you'd never be able to tell. Thank God that wooden one fell over. Danny surveys the room as he walks in. Just their luck, there are five guys in the bar. Two are playing pool, and another two are watching LSU/Tennessee on the TV, making sure to comment plenty on the weakness of SEC football. The bartender, as we've covered, is stoic. Then there's the fifth guy. Hulking and hunched on a stool, alone from all the others, he turns towards the group, and we see his face. It's Jim Pibb, Danny's bodyguard, and he gives his employer an unnoticeable nod before averting his attention. You didn't really think Danny would go anywhere without protection? SEAN HAYES (whispering) You sure we can do this?! DANNY FERGUSON (out the side of his mouth) We've gotta. How the hell am I going to prepare for a Bar Room Brawl without any prior experience? TOPHER GRACE He's right...we can't turn back now. But guys...um...how do we get started? We pause for a second to show that all four men are still huddled in a group in the center of the bar floor. The other patrons are starting to take notice. Finally, Kutcher breaks free. ASHTON KUTCHER Like this. He stomps over to the pool table. Slamming his hands down, he looks both players in the eye. Well, one at a time. Not together, that'd be creepy. ASHTON KUTCHER I got next. POOL PLAYER That's fine, bud, but we just got started, so it might be a while. Waiting for his opportunity, Kutcher grabs it. ASHTON KUTCHER Dude, you got a fuckin' PROBLEM?!!? The player, a trucker type in a black Metallica shirt and dirty jeans, looks up at him after glancing at his partner. POOL PLAYER Nah, duuude, I'm cool. Kutcher keeps staring at them, looking back and forth at both men, and then he pushes off and stnads back up. ASHTON KUTCHER Ok....good. He slowly shuffles back to the group, which has since moved to the bar. They try not to laugh, and he doesn't say anything. Danny, mustering up some courage, stands up and shuffles down towards the TV, mouthing 'watch this' to everyone else. Danny saddles up next to the two TV watchers as LSU intercepts Erik Ainge on the goalline to score another touchdown and go up 21-0. The two watchers scoff at the play. Danny moves in for the kill. DANNY FERGUSON You know, I bet the Vols could still beat the shit outta the Huskers. Back at the other actors, they all cringe and giggle, then catch themselves and look back towards Danny, who gives them a thumbs up. He waits for the reaction from his fellow TV patrons. One of them opens his mouth after a drawn-out pause. TV WATCHER Now ain't that the truth. Danny's happy reaction sours, obviously. The other man gives an affirmative nod. TV WATCHER #2 Huskers can't beat a damn drum with Callahan at the helm. Neither man even turns from the TV. Rejected, Ferguson slinks away and returns to his partners-in-crime. They all look around, trying to think of ideas. Danny happens to look down the bar and catch Jim Pibb laughing, but he holds his tongue and looks back to his peers. Finally, Sean Hayes has had enough. He stands up on his chair and yells out for everyone to hear. SEAN HAYES Listen, all you corn-eating, mama kissing, TV-watchin' blue collar redneck fucks! I came here for trouble, so which one of you pieces of shit is looking to get fucked!?! He breathes hard after the outburst, and the other actors have no choice but to shake their heads and see what the rest of the bar does. Unfortunately, they had all turned back to their other things. From the back corner, the bartender finally chimes in. BARTENDER I had a feeling 'bout you ever since you came in. SEAN HAYES Oh yeah? He hops down and cracks his knuckles. The bartender pulls out a piece of paper an a pen and scrawls something down. BARTENDER Gay bar's about a mile down the road. People make the mistake all the time; don't worry 'bout any problems. Dejected, Hayes shrinks back to the other actors. Kutcher is practically jumping up and down at the bartender calling Hayes gay, and he's all ready to point and laugh when the keep adds a postscript from off camera. BARTENDER (O.S.) Make sure your long-haired friend knows where to go, too. Kutcher stops celebrating and just looks in shocked indignance. There isn't any room for protest, though, as all four men are thoroughly dejected at their inability to pick a fight. TOPHER GRACE Guys, it's hopeless. We just picked a bad night. (to bartender) Can I get a Sam Adams? Everything in the bar suddenly goes silent, save for one last 'click' of two pool balls colliding. Grace looks around and finds everyone else in the place staring. The bartender, still standing in front of them, leans down to Topher with an angry, almost disgusted look on his face. BARTENDER What kind of man walks into an establishment like this, dressed as you are, and tries to order some fucking New England beer? Grace swallows hard, but behind him, the other three realize what's happening. From across the bar, one of the TV watchers answers the question. TV WATCHER A man lookin' to start some shit, that's who. The TV watchers push away from the game and stand in the center of the floor, where they're joined by the pool players. All four men have flipped the switch, and are ready for a brawl. Cautiously, the actors mirror their formation, ready to partake in the festivities. Ferguson scrounges in his pocket and pulls out a bill, handing it to Topher. DANNY FERGUSON Now we're talking. Grace, go put some music on. Stones, preferably. Topher skedaddles towards the jukebox as the bar patrons search for weapons. Sean Hayes grabs a bottle and smashes it over his head, a little 'too' fired up. Kutcher just cracks his knuckles. Ferguson remains stoic. Eventually, the sound of music fills the bar, but it's not the Rolling Stones. TOPHER GRACE Um, sir? There seems to be a problem with the jukebox. I, uh, I think it just took the wrong CD...and my dollar. 'First I was afraid, I was petrified. Kept thinking I could never live without you by my side...' The bar patrons start to advance as Grace pleads his case. TOPHER GRACE Sir? Um...Mr. Bartender? I distinctly remember hitting F19, and I'm pretty sure this is G19. Maybe it's a crossed wire, or a button error, something...I- DANNY FERGUSON Leave it be, Grace. He cracks his neck and starts towards the regulars. DANNY FERGUSON I've got rehearsing to do. The two sides collide like small-stage orcs and humans as Gloria Gaynor continues to light up the bar. 'And so you're back from outer space. I just walked in to find you here with that sad look upon your face...' Right at the fast part of the song, we cut outside, to the exterior shot of Walt's, and the shadows of commotion from inside. The shot slowly zooms out as the music and the scene fades.
