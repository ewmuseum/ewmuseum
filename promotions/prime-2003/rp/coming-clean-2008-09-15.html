Coming Clean

15 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/15/08 "Thank God you were in Boston." "I told you that number was only for emergencies, Matt." In the nearly four years since he'd first given Matt Johansson his cell number, not once had Troy Douglas received a call. That was, until tonight, when Troy stepped off a plane to a cryptic voicemail urging Troy to come to Copley Square. It was well after 2 in the morning on a hazy New England night, but after finding what had to be both the fastest and most noxious cab at Logan Airport, Douglas was here. He was tired, he was sweaty, he was pretty sure he left at least two pieces of luggage whipping around the carousel on an endless loop, but here he was, standing outside a hotel room with Matt Johansson and hoping there was a damn good explanation for this. "Trust me, Troy, this definitely qualifies." When the fellow wrestler opened the door, any of Troy's doubts were washed away, and there was really only one reaction. "God ... damn." Most of the suite was exactly how you'd imagine it. Immaculately clean, beds neatly made with crisp, hospital corners, the familiar tinge of air freshener cutting through the room. Hell, the little chocolate was still sitting in its perch atop a pillow. Of course, the situation in the bathroom was a little bit different. Sprawled on the floor, weeping profusely and hanging on the brink of consciousness was another wrestler with whom Troy had a longstanding relationship, Bryan Storms. It was the realization of one of Troy's worst fears. Just about four years ago, Troy saw a promising 22-year-old kid with unlimited potential, a self-destructive streak a mile wide and a cocaine habit rapidly pushing him down a very slippery slope. Matt, Bryan's none-to-sharp best friend and tag team partner, saw the problem, but not knowing what to do, he turned to Troy for advice. A lot of work and even more aggravation were required, but in the end Bryan went into a rehab facility in New Mexico and came out clean. That was nearly three years ago, and in the interim, Troy hadn't heard a thing to indicate the situation had changed at all. Until he stepped into the bathroom and saw his shirtless form barely crawling towards the toilet bowl, all the evidence pointed to Bryan being 34 months clean and sober. Obviously, something had happened to alter said situation. "Matt, what the fuck happened?" It was the only rational question Troy could ask. Matt almost instantly threw his hands into the air and began to shake his head. "Damned if I know. He left the signing early tonight, and when I got back here, he was on the ground. Except, like five feet closer." "Has he, at any point ..." "Nope," Matt said, anticipating the question perfectly. "I've been back on the road with him for a year and a half now, and ... nothing. He's been clean." "I still am." Troy's entry had apparently ripped Bryan from his near-total delirium, because he was finally able to break through the hysterics to put together a coherent thought. Responding immediately, Troy peeled Bryan, still shivering and soaked in a cold sweat, off the cold tile floor and propped him up against the sink. A quick glance in Matt Johansson's direction is all the cue he needs to quietly back out of the suite. Once he's gone, nothing much happened for a while, and that's pretty much the way Troy wanted it. As Bryan's heavy, jagged breathing begins to steady itself, the elder statesman just stands next to him and waits. When he was ready, he would talk, and from experience, Troy knew that's how it had to go. Finally, Storms broke the silence. "How ... the hell ... do I let this keep happening to me?" "You're the only one who can explain that, Bryan. I'm not your big brother, I'm not your high school guidance counselor or your guardian fucking angel. I can give you a hand, but I can't analyze your entire life for you." Follow this with the requisite pausing and looking aimlessly at the floor from young Mr. Storms as he searches for a reply. "It's just -- just ..." "Listen, just tell me what happened tonight." "I was at the signing, and I get a call on my cell. It's my dad, throwing the same load of crap at me he's been tossing my way for like six years; finish law school, quit throwing your life away, stuff like that. Only this time, the bastard takes it one step further." "What'd he do?" "He fucking cut me out, that's what he did. He told me I was a fucked-up junkie five years ago, and nothing I could do anymore would change that in his eyes. And then ... and then he tells me he's chucking me out of the goddamn family. I'm 26 fucking years old, and I got DISOWNED." Troy could only shake his head. Having never met Bryan's father, Troy wasn't necessarily qualified for full-on psychoanalysis, but from the fringe details, he could piece together the kind of man this was. For his part, Bryan just kept telling the tale of his last several hours. "So, I just got up and left. Not a word to Matt ... or anyone. And then, well, I just figured that if my own father still thought I was a junkie fuck-up, I might as well fulfill his expectations for one time in my life." "So you made a call." "Yeah. Old dealer friend of mind still operates around a few neighborhoods, and he had some good shit on him. I mean, I haven't touched this shit for three years, but when I laid eyes on it I wanted to just bury myself in it." It was an easy mental picture for Troy to recreate; Bryan, a bubbling swirl of rage, depression, guilt and desire, looking for the one means of escape that he'd always used before. He could imagine the young man reaching for the bag, trembling more every inch he closed in, ready to slip back into the vice that nearly killed him. "I was ready, Troy. I was going to dive headfirst into the shit, fuck all the fucking consequences. And then ... well, I don't even know what happened. My hand was around the bag, and all of a sudden I just ran away. Didn't even know exactly where I was going, but somehow I ended up back here and just collapsed. Call it God, Jesus, The Force, whatever the fuck you want, but something stopped me." Feeling a tremendous sense of pride, Troy offered one last piece of wisdom. "This may be complete and total Yodaspeak, but it was all you, Bryan. You made the choice. You saved your own life."
