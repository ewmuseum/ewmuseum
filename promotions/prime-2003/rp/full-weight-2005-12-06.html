Date: 12/6/05 Hometown crowds are always great. But the problem is, Scott isn't exactly a hometown guy in Lexington, KY. Well, it's as close as he'd get in the wrestling business, given that they never went to Pikeville. They couldn't really, there wasn't a venue for something large. But as it turned out, Lexington thought highly of Scott. They did whenever he worked in town. He loved the rush he got from a crowd that was fully behind him the whole way. So for the week proceeding week, he'd been in town doing promotional spots. Of course, it helped that Alyssa had gotten an apartment in town for the two of them. It was great for those layover dates and such... and frankly, he liked being able to bed down somewhere after a long flight, but he wouldn't admit it to her. So, as he drove down New Circle Road, heading to the 92.9 radio station, he was mulling over in his mind what it would be like. PRIME drew huge crowds... Scott hadn't ever worked a crowd as large as what PRIME drew in Lexington. The thought was incredible to him. He pulled off New Circle and onto Nicolasville Road. He knew it well: Alyssa's favorite shopping haunts were along this road: Fayette Mall, Lexington Green and so on. In fact, just a few days ago, he'd taken her to the comedy club to see Tim Wilson live. It was there that he'd talked with the comic to discover the man was going to be doing a spot at the same radio station. The Bear was a bastion of Central Kentucky country music, as well as radio shows like John Boy and Billy. PRIME had booked Scott to do a couple of promotional spots for the big show in town. He made his left turn and pulled into the parking lot of the station to a strange, strange sight. He was baffled as to why exactly there would be protesters at a country station in Central Kentucky. Regardless, he parked and pulled the keys, clipping the battered old carabiner to his belt and stepped out. The more baffling part was that the mob closed on him. Consisting largely of women, looking more in the range of soccer moms and such, they waved signs such as “No PRIMATES” and “Violence is destructive.” The were nowhere near as good as stuff like “Hell no, we won't go,” but they were still there. Scott tried to make his way to the front door, but all around him, he was berated and screamed at. “You're a monster!” “I can't believe they don't keep you in a cage!” “I'm ashamed to live in the same state as you!” “You ought to be ashamed of yourself!” “No more rage!” That last one caught on. “No more rage! No more rage! No more rage!” He started moving forward, his face obviously betraying his increasingly foul mood. While the mob didn't impede his progress, they sure as hell followed, chanting the same mantra at him, over and over and over and over again. He kept a firm grip on his temper, trying not to start ripping down signs and screaming back. But one thought kept permeating in his head: What the hell was going on? Ever since that night he'd stepped in the diner, people had been looking at him oddly. They kept away from him, spoke quietly when he was around or some of them got in his face and ridiculed him, shouting nonsense he didn't understand. He had never faced a Jimmy Del Marco, yet people were blaming him for ruining the man's life. His plodding steps took him forward until he finally came to the door. He turned, casting his gaze over the mass of angry humanity shouting at him and stepped inside. He almost locked the door behind him, but with a sigh, marched on. The receptionist at the front desk of the station looked up at the newcomer. The ruckus had her wondering who was coming in... and know she knew and why. She quickly motioned to the door on her left, above which the red light of “ON AIR” blazed. Scott regarded her with the same irritated look he'd given the people outside and marched his way through the door. “Church league softball fistfight, gettin' washed in the blood on a Tuesday night...” the song rang out. Tim Wilson was sitting there at the microphone, his guitar in hand and happily singing away with the big song of his latest album. Scott stood outside the booth, crossing his arms over the already-strained white t-shirt on his chest and smiled. Tim was a country boy with a biting sense of humor he could really enjoy. As the song wrapped up, he lifted his eyes to see Scott standing outside and waved him in with a smile. Scott pushed the door open and walked in. The DJ, Frank Seres, waved Scott on to an empty swivel chair and handed him a headset. “Just entering our studio, PRIME tag team champion, Scott Matthews. Folks call this guy Rampage, and from his size, that sound plain scary. How are you, Scott?” he asked in a sparkling radio voice. “Pretty good,” Scott replied, pulling a microphone closer to him. “I'm really looking forward to our show in Rupp Arena coming up.” “Hell yeah,” Tim cut in, setting his guitar down. “I'm gonna be there myself. This big ol' fella here sure is going to put on a show.” “It's been a while since you've been in an event in Rupp, hasn't it Scott?” Frank asked him. Scott reminded himself not to nod. “It's been a while since I've been on anything here in Lexington. I really hate that, too. The folks here are really good to me, and I like goin' out there to show them what I can do.” “We'll be back in a few minutes with Scott Matthews and Tim Wilson, here on 92.9, the Bear.” Frank flipped a switch and the strains of Kenny Chesney's latest started up. “So, Scott, what did you think of the little welcoming committee outside?” “What in the fuck is that?” Tim asked, setting his white fedora back on his head. “I wish I knew,” Scott replied. “People have been lookin' at me like a freak for at least a week.” “You mean you don't know?” Frank replied, genuine shock showing on his face. “You didn't see the 48 Hours thing and all? It's been picked up on all the networks.” “Let's just say I don't,” Scott replied. He'd seen the end of it, and chosen not to look for the first part. “I don't get much time to watch TV.” “Wait a minute,” Tim cut in, “all that crap is about this guy?” he asked incredulously. “Yeah, you didn't know either?” “Like the big guy here, I don't exactly get much TV time,” the comedian replied. “So,” Scott cut in, “what in the hell is going on?” “Oh man, you aren't going to like this.” the DJ replied. Scott sat in that small studio, his mouth agape as the DJ recounted what he could remember from the newscast. It was about how uncontrollable he was, about how he had even attacked his own partner, that he was separated from his family and so on. As time had gone by, apparently opinion/discussion shows like The View and even some political talk shows had really screwed up the facts as they went along. Somewhere the rumor had been started that Scott was put in a straightjacket and moved from event to event by the same security staff that surrounded him backstage. “Holy shit,” he muttered. “You're... I'm... where did this come from?” “Oh, that's not the part that have people up-in-arms.” Frank sat back as a new song started, a new tune off the Garth Brooks re-emergence-whatever-the-hell-it-is-thing. “You ever deal with a religious retreat?” Scott's teeth audibly clicked. This part he had seen. “No,” he responded. “But I did get in a fight with a cult.” Frank shook his head. “Wow, I don't know how they did it, but they've got you all framed up for attacking a religious retreat and beating up the women.” “Well, try this,” Scott replied, “go look at some arrest reports. Eyewitnesses say that I was attacked first and they found these freaks trafficking prescription drugs.” “Wait just a damn second,” Wilson cut in. “These freakos started a fight with you, got busted for movin' drugs and you're bein' blasted by everyone that can put their lips to a microphone?” “I guess so,” Scott replied sadly.
