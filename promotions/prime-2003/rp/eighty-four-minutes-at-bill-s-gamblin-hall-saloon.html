Eighty-Four Minutes at Bill's Gamblin' Hall & Saloon

5 August 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/5/08 (posted 8/3) Tuesday, August 5, 2008 ---- Now: The Door ---- The hotel room door was flimsy and painted red. Golden numbers declared it to be room number 7-47, and the guest therein found that to be an amusing fact -- something about Douglas Adams and the answer to his question of life, the universe, and everything, likely. She'd giggled and giggled when the clerk (a nice gent named Jack) had told her the room number, to the point that she snorted. He hadn't thought much of it, though, chalking her up as just another stressed-out businesswoman in town for a convention (they never stopped having conventions in Las Vegas -- and people think it's just a gambling town.). The woman in Room 7-47 lied on the king-sized bed, feet crossed at the ankle, a metal nail file in her hand and the telephone cradled against her ear. Her name was unimportant -- people had named her "Cozen" so long ago it had become her name. "I know! It's like...I mean, I get that Clooney is, y'know, rugged-handsome or whatever, but he's old!" the woman Lindsay Troy called "The Crazy" cooed. "It'd be like fucking my dad!" She exploded into a fit of giggles as she flopped onto her side, filing down her nails, fall of obsidian black curls splayed out on the pillows that fell haphazardly across the bed (always ask for extra pillows in your room -- there's just no reason not to). "You're horrible! No wonder she likes you so much." The strokes with the nail file were practiced, but the Faceless Fighter kept her nails filed to brutal points (especially the pinkies -- something about her tiniest finger being that sharp made her tingle in places she rarely spoke about). "Oh, yeah. She totally -- " In police television shows, the cops always kick a door down in the center, which is (as is the case with a good deal of television's "truths") a gross oversimplification. Too solid a kick on the center of a door will only result in you putting your foot through the door itself, not forcing it open (which should almost always be your goal when kicking a door open). Too weak a kick will not provide the right kind of push on the lever of its hinges to force it open to snap the lock -- something that's made worse if the door is deadbolted. No, if you want to open a door in a hurry, put your foot right above the doorknob when you drive through it. All the pressure of the impact goes onto the lock, and even if the bolt holds, chances are it will tear through the frame. The door to Room 7-47 exploded inward behind the size twelve boot of the silent giant named Ian Ogden. Cozen rolled off the bed at the moment the door was struck (not the moment it broke), without so much as a "Gottagobye!" to the woman she was speaking with. Coming to her feet on the far side of the bed, the Faceless Fighter held a pair of wide, butterfly blades -- one for each hand. The giant stood aside, making way for a small group of men clutching high-voltage stun guns and wishing they were real firearms. Groups like the Order of St. Julian test for physical prowess first -- can't do whatever it is the group is intended for if the shock troops are made up of asthmatic bastards who can't lift half their body weight. The problem with that is that speed simply brings you closer to the enemy than your partner, who might have had too many jelly doughnuts. The first two men through the door were -- quite frankly -- doomed. Cozen used the too-springy Vegas hotel bed as a jumping point, left leg bent at the knee for the first man. Her kneecap collided heavily with his teeth, cracking three, and she rode him down to the ground, using what was his nose before the maneuver as a pivot-point to rotate to her feet, both blades leading. The knives slid through black cloth and toned flesh, not even an inch under the second man's flak jacket, which only served to hold his insides in. The third and fourth men piled through the door as the Faceless Fighter stepped to either side of the first's head, gripping with her ankles as she twirled the blade in her left hand. The third man in the room came at her with the long-handled stun gun, blue-white sparks jumping across the contacts. Cozen beat him to the attack, striking downward, her blade digging into the man's wrist until it hit bone. She turned over, bringing the man at her feet with her -- the sound of his neck breaking under the torque slowed the advance of the fourth enough that the hooked point of her blade punctured his calf and not his thigh, as she'd intended, and got stuck among the corded muscle. The third man (the one with the injured wrist) came up to Cozen from behind, grabbing the taller woman around the neck. The Faceless Fighter turned with the thin man draped across her back pushing towards the fourth, hobbling one as he struck out with the weapon. The man on her back convulsed as electricity coursed through his body, and the Crazy's grin bloomed to life. "Ohhh, baby, you don't sound so good," she whispered consolingly as she shifted her grip on the remaining blade into a two-handed one, driving it between her own legs and up into her meatshield's lower, lower abdomen. With a twist, she left it there, gripping the back of the man's head, fingers twisted in his hair, and pulling him over -- not unlike a snapmare. The fourth tried to time his attack properly, tried to strike when the Crazy was weaponless. But she proved too fast, turning a one-handed cartwheel and wrenching out both of her blades -- the one that hung in the groin of the third man and the one that dangled from the thick calf of the fourth. A full 360-degree spin brought both weapons across the fourth man's throat and left the woman facing the fifth -- and the two Inquisitors who waited on the other side of the door. The fifth of the five men stared at Cozen. Cozen tilted her head to the side, yin-yang eyes wide and innocent. She twirled both weapons. The man turned away, running for the door. They test for physical prowess in the Julian Order and they test for mental acumen -- but it had been less than thirty seconds since the big man had kicked the door down and four men were dead or dying at her feet. And the other two seemed perfectly ready to simply stand there and wait until Cozen eliminated their minions. So, yeah, facing the Faceless One straight up wasn't on the list of the man's priorities. Unfortunately, the thin woman with a spray of short, frizzy blond curls and a thinner cigarette dangling from her lips disagreed with the man's list of priorities. **BANG** The report of her WWII-era German Luger pistol echoed weirdly in the room, but the man did not have time to wonder why -- likely due to the bullet tearing through his mouth and out the back of his head. Guinevere Maddox stepped through the door, trailed by the silent giant. Cozen wiped her blades on the fallen, never moving her unsettling gaze from the other two. "Impressive, lass," Guen said in her soft Irish brogue. The literally smoking gun rested on her shoulder as a tacit reminder that the Faceless Fighter had brought a knife to a gunfight. "But I've got twenny more waitin' in th' wings. How long till yer muscles ache an' yer breathin' gets soft an' slow?" "Only twenny, m'darlin'?" Cozen's voice matched the Inquisitor's; her smile tightened, rictus-like, against her face. "Yer gonna need a bigger boat." ---- Eighty-Four Minutes Ago: The Best Laid Plans ---- "Everything all right?" a kindly older gent in a Hawaiian shirt and broad brown sunglasses said as he approached the tiny blonde with a map splayed out on the hood. The constant influx of tourists kept the city packed, so any of the restaurants or attractions that brought people from the Midwest to Sin City had wait times that were just too long if a body could just say, "Eh, I'll go tomorrow." Plus, contrary to popular belief, one can see Barry Manilow too many times. So it was a rare Tuesday morning, the Fifth of August, that found Jack seated at a gambling table at Bill's Gamblin' Hall and Saloon, playing his game of choice: craps. Fuck people and their obscene, ESPN-fueled love of poker -- that's not a game of chance, but of strategy, and, sure, that might attract some people, but not Jack Petersen. He liked the rattle of dice of in his hands, the sight of the red cubes tumbling across green felt, and the intricate betting that went into making sure you always walked away with some money. That was gambling. Besides, it's not like cute girls would blow on your goddamn poker hand. In truth, Jack wasn't very good at craps (or any other game -- slot machines had a tendency to run cold the moment he sat down in front of them, for example). But he had a few hours to kill, and Tuesday morning was a good time to lose a few bucks before the crowds came. He'd lose himself in the dice, in the feel of the room, in the clatter of coins in nearby machines. Jack Petersen wasn't really an attractive man -- his forehead was just a little too long, his nose a little too wide, and his sense of style a little too outdated. So he chalked it up to drunkenness (some of these fucking tourists, man) when the two women teetered over to his table and smiled at him. The brunette was taller than the blonde, though Jack would never be sure how much of that was high heel and how much of that was leg that went on for days. "So, wait, how does this work again?" the blonde asked. A red bandanna held back her long hair, green eyes sparkling over the golden rims of her eyeglasses. She was casual in a ruffled tan skirt and a diaphanous, lacy blouse the color of the deepest part of the ocean. "I think you just roll the dice, right?" The brunette turned the brilliance of her smile towards the man, tucking a lock of hair beneath her fuzzy white bucket hat and behind an ear for a moment before she looked back. "Stacey, should we be playing a game we don't know the rules of?" "Maybe you shouldn't," Stacey said. "But I didn't lose all my money already, and I want to try." "Hey!" The brunette's eyes widened in mock offense. "I haven't lost all my money, and I wouldn't say that these jeans were 'losing' anything!" "Lily," Stacey explained patiently, like it wasn't the first time she'd said so. "They were two hundred dollars!" Lily swiveled in her seat, glancing helplessly towards Jack again. She half-rose from her stool, bootheel hooked in the metal footrest encircling the base of the stool, showing off the jeans in question. "Do you think these were a good investment of my money? Better than losing it at a game I don't know how to play?" "Well, it seems like he knows what he's doing, tell him to come over here and show us!" Stacey trapped the tip of her tongue between her teeth, sliding a small stack of blue chips onto the "8" on the felt. "Twenty-five on eight," the base dealer announced. Jack was up out of his seat immediately. "No, no, no," he scolded. "You don't bet a load on a single number -- that's the easy way to lose all your cash, lady." He circled around to the ladies. "Stacey," the blonde said by way of introduction. "This is my friend Lily." Jack was not a gallant man who kissed women's hands -- he always considered that the province of skuzzy guys. He did take the fingers of the brunette's left hand into his, smiling. "I'm Jack." "Well, Jack," Lily said softly. "Teach us about your 'game'." "That...I can do." He glanced to the side. "Nice jeans, by the way." "Hah!" Lily nearly bounced in her stool. "I told you!" Stacey rolled her eyes. "A guy liking your tight jeans?" she asked. "Imagine my shock." She smiled over at the man as he pointed out the various bets. "Forgive my friend, she's a bit forward sometimes." "I'm not bad, I'm just drawn that way," Lily murmured indignantly. ---- Now: The Bigger They Are, The Harder It Hurts ---- Guinevere Maddox pressed the earpiece of her radio in tighter, cursing softly in a language that the Faceless Fighter didn't speak (believe it or not, there were a few). Cozen tossed the weapon in her right in the air, but not very far. She caught it in a backhanded grip, bringing it awkwardly across her body to trace a red line above the big man's eye. It would have gone deeper if he hadn't leaned out of the way. Ogden let Cozen rotate all the way through, never letting go of her left hand. She ended facing away from him, and he seized the Faceless Fighter's right arm as she tried to reach backwards, locking his hands in a chicken wing grip and running her at the wall, facefirst. The impact jarred the second blade loose -- it also left an impression in the plasterboard. A wide-eyed boy, no older than twelve, stuck his head out of a door, an ice cream cone clutched in his hand. "What's goin' on?" "Oh, nothin'," Guinevere said quietly, not really looking. "Just some routine room maintenance. Stay inside an' it'll all be oveh soon." Inside the room, Cozen stamped down on Ogden's instep, breaking his interlocked fingers and freeing her left arm. The tall woman rotated, grabbing the ceramic lamp off the nightstand and bringing it over the bucket-like head of her attacker. "Why so serious?" She giggled, repeating at a higher pitch. "Why so seeeeeerious?" ---- Seventy-Two Minutes Ago: Fancy Meeting You Here ---- Detective Reginald Phillips of the New Orleans Police Department was what you'd call a workaholic. He hadn't taken a vacation day in nearly eighteen months, taken a personal day in eight or been sick in four (and before he'd been bedridden with the stomach flu, Phillips had gone even longer). After the disastrous investigation into the woman called Cozen (he hadn't been able to find a legal name for the woman on file with records that went back more than four years), the detective had become slightly...obsessive. Something wasn't right about the supposed kidnapping -- he felt like he was missing a piece to the puzzle, and it gnawed at him. His partner, Ed Knowles, thought that Reginald (never "Reggie") was going insane, and had said as much to the captain. The result: two weeks paid leave for Phillips, a black eye for Knowles. When the invitation from the Harrah's Hotel & Casino in Las Vegas had come, Phillips had been tempted to ignore it -- he'd almost tossed it in the trash. Trouble was, his wife had seen it, and if Beverly Phillips wanted something...well, Reginald tended to give it to her, because she put up with the long hours and the obsessive attention to detail that made Reginald a good cop. When they'd gotten to the resort, his wife checked them while the detective (he tried to remember he was the husband at this point) went to the slots by the door. It's easy to remember you're the husband when your wife is standing next to you, telling boring stories about work. It's easy to remember to be the devoted lover when she makes a show of packing those pretty underthings you like so much. It's hard to forget you're a police officer (even if you have no jurisdiction in the city) when a girl comes limping into the lobby, left eye blackened. A sliver of t-shirt was tied around the woman's thigh and she collapsed to a knee before continuing on. His wife was going to kill him, he knew, but Detective Phillips was out of the deep red stool before he even realized he was moving, looping an arm around the dark-haired woman's waist. She leaned against him gratefully. "Are you all right?" Detective Phillips asked, speaking as slowly and as clearly as possible. She nodded slightly. "What's your name?" "Wendy," she whispered, eyes fluttering. "Wendy Robinson." "What happened to you, Mrs. Robinson?" "I was next door at...at Bill's...and there was this tall woman with...with crazy white and black eyes and long hair." The girl wet her lips, a nervous affectation. Her lips quirked wryly, which caused another grimace of pain. "She has a knife, obviously." Detective Phillips blinked in confusion. "Mrs. Robinson, if you don't mind me saying so, this conversation has gotten a little strange." ---- Fifty-Eight Minutes Ago: The Crow's Nest ---- The man the wrestling world called "Andreas van der Wal" but whose mother called "Andrew McFarland" didn't like getting involved.
