Far away from anything

9 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/9/06 THWACK. Two pieces of cedar fell off the stump and clattered to the ground with loud rattles. Scott’s big paw grabbed another piece and set it on the stump. He hefted the oak-handled axe and lifted high. With a deep breath, he swung hard. The axe hit somewhat off-center and sideways, but the wood still split. Scott leaned the axe against his thigh and rubs his aching forearms. He’d hit more often like that than dead-on. They didn’t need any more firewood. There was quite a stack already on the east side of the house, facing away from the slope of the mountain. He wanted to do something. But Scott needed to get out of the house; he needed to think. Here they were, far away from home and not allowed to even do anything that was part of who they were. He and Van couldn’t wrestle, Lacey couldn’t go to school and his mother couldn’t work. Alyssa didn’t mind so much, and neither did Van. But it mattered to Scott. He wanted to do something. With a heave, he cracked another piece of wood apart. The improper impact was making his arms hurt and his hands ache, but it was splitting and it was occupying his mind from the endless stint of nothing. So, he grabbed another piece and set it up. But even he had his limits and dropped the axe to give himself a moment’s rest. He wanted to be in the ring, working on improving his technique. He wanted to be learning new moves and perfecting ones he didn’t have down. He’d seen the replacements that Van had found. Top notch was the first phrase that sprang to mind. The two of them were taking his and Van’s places and doing better in the ring than they were. And while he was stuck at this damned picturesque cabin in the fucking fantastic woods, there was no way for him to try to eclipse that in their return. These guys were real pros. Scott leaned back on big maple tree that cast its shadow over the driveway. How, how, how could he stand to compete with someone like Stewart or Green? Even if he were teamed up with one or the other, he wasn’t in their league. He’d be the weak link. Stewart was a world-class fighter that can take apart anyone without terribly much effort. Green was a former world champion, an incomparable wrestler. And he was a jackass that threw a hard lariat and a football tackle. Van was great, agile and a good brawler, but between the two of them, they didn’t have the expertise and capability that just one of their stand-ins had. “I know what your problem is,” came a rumbling deep voice. Scott’s shaggy head turned to see the massive bodyguard said from the porch. The top of his crew-cut almost brushed the roof on the porch, and his impossibly thick arms crossed over his chest. “Yeah, you know what my problem is,” Scott retorted. “Sure, big guy, why don’t you fix all of us while you keep us locked away on the ass-end of nowhere.” Scott rubbed at his wrists for a moment, then turned away. “Don’t need any advice from the walking prison gate.” “Yeah, you do.” It was a blunt, simply reply from the black-BDU clad giant. “Listen, you are a wrestler. You’re apparently not bad at it, given that you’ve had some belts and all that. But you aren’t as good as you could be.” He took a slow step off the porch, his heavy combat boot crunching on the dry leaves. “Yeah, okay, so what’s the big, bad FBI boy want to teach me?” Scott snarled, turning to face the bigger man. It was intimidating to him, to be facing a man larger than him. But he wasn’t about to back down. “Okay, you’re pissed. I can tell you don’t like me, little man,” Titan responded. “But I brought you here because an attorney wants you to testify against the paper-pants wearing freaks that have been all over your ass. So, pardon the hell out of me for keeping those sociopathic freaks from trying to kill you, your family, your dog and torching the whole county you grew up in.” Titan squared his shoulders and lifted his chin. “So, let’s try this again: you aren’t as good as you can be.” Scott exhaled, snorting through his nostrils like an angry bull. “Alright, you need to tell me what exactly I need to do in order to become the Universal champion.” Titan gave him a bland look and leaned down, grabbing the axe and lifting. “I’ve been watching you swing at this wood all afternoon. You’ve hit it precisely a handful of times. You’ve been hitting off-center over and over and your arms have got to be killing you.” Scott growled and looked at him, snapping his arms to his sides. “Okay, fine, so what are you getting at?” “You don’t know your own body well enough,” the giant replied. “You’re all emotion and power. Trust me, kid, power is good, but you gotta have some control in there, too, or else it’s wasted.” “I don’t think people I’ve been in the ring with would agree,” Scott replied. “No, I suspect not,” Titan conceded. “But here’s the difference. This is what you’ve been doing all day long,” he said, kicking the scattered pieces of wood. “But it could be like this,” he said. He simply extended his arm, bringing the axe-head down in the dead center of the wood on the stump. The wood snaps apart easily and the axe sinks into the stump. “You know your own body and you don’t need to expend that much energy to do the simple stuff. I’ve seen your tapes, you throw a hard lariat, but if you would just be more precise about it, they’d be even harder.” Scott looked at the man, taking his time to process what he thought. He looked at the axe, then at the big man. “How do you know about controlling yourself? You’re a big guy, you don’t need to.” Titan nodded to him. “Listen, I grew up in Israel. I joined the IDF and served. I was instructed in Krav Maga, okay? I learned how to fight in the most efficient way possible against as many people as possible. So I learned how to be the best combatant I could. Kid, you could make do with having some of that yourself.” Scott nodded. “Yeah…. Start talking.” * * * Alyssa sat at the window, looking out at Scott’s efforts. She knew why he was out there, hacking away at more and more lumber. He was frustrated at being locked away because he did nothing wrong and he hated being limited in what he can do. So he went outside and hacked away at wood in order to try to take his mind off what he was facing. She knew better than to bother him. She walked away from the window and flopped down on the bed they shared. The unlit room folded around her like a soft blanket in the light of the afternoon. She hated being stuck here, too, because it made Scott miserable. She was miserable when he was, she was happy when he was. And vice versa. She never thought about it before. She knew Scott loved her, she had said yes when he proposed, but the lightning bolt that struck her was new. They were reflective of each other, supportive… they were a real-life, no-fooling, honest-to-God couple that was getting married. It was so… alien to her. Just before she’d met Scott, she had been very much a hedonist, sleeping with whoever she was interested in at the moment. But she met him and they started dating. Then they got closer and closer… She didn’t expect to fall utterly in love with this massive bear of a man. But there it was. And she didn’t want to wait to marry him. So what could they do? They were stuck off in seclusion, there was no way to get a preacher, a justice of the peace or whoever and certainly no way to get a honeymoon in. But, in her heart, she was tired of not being married to him. She fiddled restlessly with her engagement ring. What to do… what to do…
