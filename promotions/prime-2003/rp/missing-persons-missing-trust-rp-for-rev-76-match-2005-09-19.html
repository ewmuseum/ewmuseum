Missing Persons, Missing Trust (RP for ReV 76 match)

19 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/19/05 It was like he remembered… to step between the ropes and, for just that moment, suspend reality. Amidst the roar of the fans, the screeching agony of the competitors, and the sicking thud of bone against bone… there was a quietness. A calm. A peaceful embrace that he found where others were blind. There, in the center of the storm, was an escape from the true anguish life brought down without sympothy. It was the addiction. It was what he had longed for, it was what he had missed… it was what he had found again. On the 14th of September, 2005, Matt Ward reintroduced himself to the world once again as The Inhuman Being, Tchu. For just a few short minutes, he had surrounded himself with a familiar experience that had never realy been gone from the center of his spirit. It had been important to him, to get his renewed career going in the right direction. For his career to immediately have begun careening disasterously in a way that mimicked his life, would have been fatal. So it was with great satisfaction that he had stepped out of the ring Wednesday night, having successfully secured his place in the second round of the Jewel in the Crown tournament. Four more victories remained in order to claim the title and its prize. But there was no need counting the future… there was no future to concern about. One step at a time. He hoped to replicate each of his matches with the same repetitive manner… to relive each moment again and again… those were the types of moments that were meant to be recreated and forgotten. Those were the types of days that needed to be just like the last time… the same old performance with the same old result… yet a new and unique feeling each time, as if it were the first time, every time. Now, the rebeginning was one step in motion, and it was vital that the second be placed with the same emotion and abandon. That necessary second step down the familiar path was coming sooner than he had expected. On the card for ReVolution 76 was his name, penciled in with five others. Aimz, Juan Santos, Rampage, Karina Wolfenden, Nerezza… five individuals, five strangers. It was something he wasn’t used to, something that… much like stepping between the ropes… he had been away from for what seemed like centuries. It had not been since his days with the N4O, nearly four years earlier, that he had trusted another individual enough to call them his tag partner. The Real Outsiders, with Seph, had been a series of highs and lows, but in the end had seen solid success… until the two had found themselves on opposing sides in the Underground. From that point on, he hadn’t stepped into the ring with anyone in his corner. Now, that was all going to have to change, at least, if he wanted the next step he placed to be as demonstrative as the first. Somehow, he had to find a last remnant of trust to carry with him into the match. * * * ~ Saturday, September 17th, 2005. 8:03P.M.~ She walked into the living room, sat down on the couch, pulled her knees up to her chest, tucking them under the baggie grey hooded sweatshirt she wore. The apartment was chilly, she thought, she should probably turn the heat up a little bit. Maybe I’ll do it in a few minutes. The same idea had crossed her mind fifteen minutes earlier. As she reached over and grabbed one of the large, comfortable pillows on the couch, she snatched up the remote off the coffee table and turned on the television, flipping through the channels as its soft glow rippled across the living room. There was nothing that caught her attention. Everything was reruns. The same old stuff she had seen before. All of it… she had seen it all. The TV remained on as she let her gaze roam across the room, and find their focus on the lights of the city, far off in the distance. Matthew was out there amongst those lights somewhere, but she couldn’t remember where. He had left about twenty-one minutes before, or at least… twenty-one minutes sounded right. Twenty-one was stuck in her head, whatever that meant. She hopped off the couch, curiosity now controlling her. Where had Matthew gone? Mary started to roam around the living room, looking for something that would remind her where he had gone just a few minutes before. There was nothing on the coffee table, nothing on the couch, or sitting on top of the television. The living room appearing hopeless, she made her way into the kitchen. The countertops were covered with a variety of different papers and envelopes. Bills, sympathy cards for her ‘unfortunate accident’. She let him open all of those. But among all the various notes and cards scattered across the granite tops, there appeared to be no trace of evidence as to Matthew’s whereabouts. At the small dining table in the kitchen, sat only the dirty dishes from the dinner that had just been finished an hour and a half before. No one had remembered to load the dishwasher. Mary made her way to the refrigerator. There were several notes plastered to its sleek black surface with numerous magnets. One was a phone number she didn’t recognize. Another was a note “remember to load dishes in dishwasher”. Nothing, however, seemed to be a note from Matthew saying where he had gone. She sighed, feeling defeated, and turned to head out of the room when a small yellow paper attached to the side of the fridge caught her eye. She hadn’t noticed it before. How long had it been there? Mary slid her hand out of the long sleeve of her hoodie and pulled the paper out from under the magnet, which pinned it to the fridge. It didn’t say much ‘Next step… 21st. No trust…’ That was the ‘21’ stuck in her head. She had seen the paper before, but what did it mean? And what did it have to do with Matthew? * * * ~ Saturday, September 17th, 2005. 7:54P.M.~ “You can’t just think that you’re gonna get back into the business and not have the occasional tag team match.” Seph turned off the television that he had muted when Matt had first showed up at the hospital ten minutes earlier. “Seriously, you ought to know by now that whether you’re a singles competitor or not, you sometimes have to do the tag team thing. It’s a business, Matt. You know that. Sometimes random tag matches help spark or further some good feuds. You can’t be best friends with everyone you ever have to tag with.” “I know, Seph” For the first time since arriving at the hospital, Matt took a seat. They had moved Seph to a better room, one a little less intense with the medical equipment. His condition had been upgraded, and he was hoping to be out in a couple of days. Until then, they had at least been kind enough to give him a halfway respectable room. As respectable as a hospital could be. “I just wasn’t really expecting to see any tag action. Its never really been my style.” “Hard for it to be, when you don’t really trust anybody. Jesus, it was a battle getting you to go for the whole Real Outsiders thing. And you knew that was something great ready to happen. Not too mention you were only being asked to trust your best friend in the business, and someone who you knew was in the same bullshit situation you were.” “Yeah, and even in the end, that didn’t last. It all fell apart and it was a monsterous collapse.” “In the end? No way, it didn’t last for years and years, but even now, you gotta admit…we had some serious success and a helluva lot of fun with the run.” It was the truth. Seph was right. When the two had found a common enemy to fight, namely their federation of employment and the Outsiders, they had found a gold mine. Within a matter of weeks the two had captured the tag titles, won number one contenderships, singles titles. They quickly began to dominate the N4O… and they had seemed unstoppable, defeated only by the folding of the federation. Things came easier, both as a team and as a singles competitor and the success had rolled in with furious speed. “Yeah, it was quite the run while it lasted.” “I’m tellin ya, dude… I know how you are. I know you put zero trust in mankind more or less. But the truth is, you haven’t been a part of the wrestling business in over two years, and even without that absence wearing on you, even without the hell you’ve got to face at home… regardless of all that, its just easier to make it through the god foresaken event with someone watching your back. You shouldn’t be so quick to dismiss the idea of ever allowing anyone to stand in your corner.” “It’s a nice thought in theory.” “It’s a nice thought, period. It’s just the truth man, you know that. You don’t have to go out of your way to put some undying trust in anybody. And I’m not tellin you to go and start asking the people you’ll be teaming with this week at ReV, ‘hey, wanna be my partner?’… I’m just sayin, keep your options open. Think about it. Get through your six person tag match this week… and go from there. But don’t just lumber through PRIME trying to avoid any and everyone.” * * * ~ Saturday, September 17th, 2005. 8:18P.M.~ She had no idea how long he had been gone. Or how long it had been since she had found the note on the refrigerator. It seemed like he had been gone for hours, and she figured it had been at least ninety minutes. Slowly, she paced across the kitchen floor and entered the living room. The clock on the wall said it was about a quarter after eight. Maybe he had been in a car accident? Perhaps that was why he had been gone so long and hadn’t called. The fear in her began to build. It must have been an accident. No, she was just panicking. If he had been in an accident, she would have heard something by now. The police would have called, they would have stopped by. They would have let her know that something was wrong. Nothing was wrong, no one had told her anything was wrong. Still, she worried. It could have been any number of things, and she couldn’t handle not knowing any longer. Mary walked feverishly over to the side table by the couch and picked the phone off of the base, and dialed Matthew’s cell phone number. She looked around the house, everything dark except for the eerie glow of the television. In her ear, she heard the phone ringing. Why was she calling Matthew? * * * ~ Saturday, September 17th, 2005. 8:17P.M.~ Matt crossed the parking lot in the cool night air. A soft mist fell from the heavens, and the street lights gave life to each tiny drop that fell in front of its existence. He watched them, seeming to float down to earth. Perhaps everything Seph had said was right. There was certainly a bit of truth in his words, perhaps more than Matt wanted to admit to himself. In a business as cruel as that of professional wrestling, it was entirely beneficial to have someone who would watch your back. Someone to cause some damage with, not against. There were plenty of enemies, to have one or two friends could go a long way to making traveling the path much easier. And in this case, starting his career again, finding himself on familiar turf, he was fortunate enough to know the perfect individual to watch his back and who would gladly accept the offer in return. There was someone in PRIME that Matt had worked closely with, who he had helped immensely over the last year. Now, he was sure the two would be able to help one another greatly. It seemed, maybe Seph had been entirely right. Matt pulled his cell phone from his pocket and began to dial the familiar number. As he reached his car, unlocked it and took a seat to escape the drizzle, he listened as the phone began to ring, connecting the call to his friend. As he waited for an answer, th cell phone was interrupted by a quick double beep. He had an incoming call. Matt pulled the phone away and saw that it was Mary. Although the call he was placing was rather important in his esitmation, he figured it best to answer Mary’s call, and try placing his call again in a few minutes. He hit the ‘send’ button, switching to the incoming call. “Hey, what’s up?” “I dunno” Her words on the other end were soft. “I can’t remember, Matthew.” He closed his eyes, saw the glowing rain falling from the black clouds. “That’s ok, baby. I’ll be home in a few minutes.” He closed the cell phone and stuck it back in his pocket. Maybe he wouldn’t make that other call after all.
