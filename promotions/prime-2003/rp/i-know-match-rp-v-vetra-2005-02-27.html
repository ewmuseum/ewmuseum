I Know (Match RP - v Vetra)

27 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 27th February, 2005. I KnowWritten: 27th February, 2005. 'One of these mornings,Won't be very long.You will look for me... and I'll be gone.' - Moby. I - The Answer. 'Her personality itself was so evasive, that you could look her dead in the eye and still wonder if you'd found her...' the husky, 40 Marlboro-a-day narrative went. In a world of grimy, bleached colour, life played out its epic, interwoven tragedy. Dull, stodgy rain pounded the cityscape, sending explosions of murky water up from the bottomless-looking puddles. Not even the harsh argon of the scant streetlamps could battle against the drowning atmosphere that descended down from the brooding, choking clouds. A man screamed obscenities at his once-beloved car as he tried to unlock its awkward driver's-side door, his hair so sodden that it could no longer absorb another drop of moisture... causing the bruising downpour to cascade down his scowling face. 'And when there'd been a glimpse of her reported, you knew that the Universe itself was intent of keeping her cloaked,' his recollection continued, still baffled to this day by the woman. The door slammed closed behind him, as he immediately felt the seat below him soak up the moisture from his glistening coat and heavy, water-logged jeans. Headlights flicked on at full beam, the brilliant gaze they expelled barely cutting through the almost solid, humid environment the city had been submerged in. Worn tyres spun freely on the slick road surface, before traction was begrudgingly obtained, allowing the car to shoot off down the emptied tarmac. In the driver's haste, his tyres ploughed obliviously through a dingy puddle, the spray decanting out the grit and dirt from the water on its way to washing over a passing pedestrian. Before he'd even had a chance to see the raised middle finger from the aggrieved woman on his mirrors, the mad had turned off the street and planted his foot... speeding away to a churning clap of mechanised thunder. 'Once upon a time,' his storytelling began, voice as bleak and hopeless as the scenes he was recollecting, 'she was given a gift; to ask for anything she pleased.' Although it was an effort entirely in vain, the woman still took a few potent strides after the car, spewing harsh and censorable language from her pale pink lips. Lowering her middle finger as her hand withdrew back to the warm, dry comfort of her pocket, the woman almost folded herself back into her long, grey coat... which seemed to camouflage her against the depressing monotone of the city. A scarf and ski-hat formed a second, reserve barrier under her engulfing hood, leaving only shadowed eyes and the bridge of her nose visible. 'But what some would consider a prize, another would call a burden,' was then forced out over the pits of harsh gravel that were his vocal chords. Out of everyone she passed, not a single, fleeting glace was aimed her way. Distracted by the pounding of the rain, and the stale, clogged-up atmosphere of the overcrowded city, everyone was more concerned with staying dry, or quenching their gasping lungs with rare, refreshing gulps of filtered air. 'As when you can have whatever you want, there'll be those who make it their business to deny you your wish.' Amidst the towers that stretched so high that their summits could no longer be seen through the tainted air, a maze of alleys kept the city sewn together, forming the tight stitches that were interwoven through its fabric. It was from one of these capillaries that observant eyes watched the woman's delayed progress along the crowded street, her hips darting from side to side as she navigated the ever-changing slalom course with serene grace. 'They saw no material wealth suddenly adorn her life, and they assumed she'd kept her wish in reserve,' his croaky voiced continued to recall. 'What they didn't know is that she'd already asked for the one thing she shouldn't of... the answer to a problem that should never be solved, the question that should never be answered. And that was the Albatross around her neck, the shameful secret she had to bare, and the nightmare she had to endure every night.' Stood under a waterfall that gushed down from overflowing gutters, he lit a cigarette, before exhaling the first, buzzing drag out to be consumed by the deluge. 'The hand is quicker than the eye,' his croaky voice just about managed to force out, before a gutsy cough seemed to reset his vocal chords. 'But her... everything was quicker than any eye I'll ever see through.' The fierce cherry of orange beaming through the ash seemed to be the only truly vivid light in the city, before that too was extinguished as the cigarette was tossed through the gutter's overspill, leaving it sodden and limp before it even hit the puddle that acted as the alley's floor. A thin stream of exhaled smoke was then ravaged by the wind forced between the walls, and dissipated out into the street... where the man's footsteps headed, soles slapping up dirty water that darkened the bottoms of his charcoal trousers. 'She didn't walk in any direction... she walked in misdirections.' Relying on his acute peripheral vision, the man shadowed her from across the street, staying close to the curb and risking a drenching from the traffic to limit the number of bodies passing between them. The constant drumming of the heavy rain began to blend in with the sounds of the city to create an almost soothing urban symphony. The downpour's rhythm section was complimented perfectly by the percussion of the car horns and slamming of shop shutters as their proprietors decided to call it a day. The whirr of police sirens blanketed the treble, while a deep, booming horn hurdled 15 story buildings with ease to let everyone know that 17:27 was soon to pull into the station a minute or two late. 'The cold, stark grey of the city was like a hall of mirrors,' the monotone, reflective voice recalled. 'You could have been looking at her, or you could have been looking at where she was 10 seconds ago.' He then felt the vibrating of his cell phone, tucked deep into his inside pocket. 'Shit,' the gruff voice grumbled, almost too deep to make out as he unbuttoned the top of his coat and delved a hand inside. The relentless rain saw the opening and used another rumble of thunder to add weight to its already heavy downpour. The white shirt he wore underneath had turned transparent by the time he'd retrieved his phone, flicking it open while making a hasty retreat to a shop doorway. 'Every eye of every creature seemed to be her lookout,' the gravely voice croaked out, 'if it could see you, she could see you.' Opening up the message he'd just received, the man's bottom lip detached from its upper, dropping in a mixture of bemusement and respect. Where am I? it read, causing the man to instantly shoot his gaze up and try to spot his target through the almost opaque blanket of precipitation. 'Dammit,' he cursed, voice so deep that the words almost burst from his throat. 'Huh?' Half distracted as his desperate eyes darted to every face on show, he went to check the next message that was causing the phone to vibrate in his palm. You'd have missed me if I was on time, it read. 'She may as well have grabbed me by the tie and dragged me to wherever she wished.' 'What the hell?' he growled, his entire body animated as he spun sharply around, chasing flashes of movement. 'You'd have missed me..?' Just as he was about to slip over the crumbling edge of doubt, and plummet headfirst down into the deep chasm of confusion, the train's horn signalled its arrival at the nearby platform. The moment the sound waves had crept through the buildings and into his ear, the man spun on his toes, sprinting back through the crowd as he exclaimed, 'the train!' Awkwardly, he tried to drop his cell phone back in his pocket as he ran against the rain, ignoring the heavy lashing against his face as he darted through the pedestrians. 'My pulse acted as a radar, quickening as I began to close in on her.' Encased in chafing dress shoes, the man's feet pounded the sidewalk, sending a fine spray out into his slipstream. Skidding on the slick slabs, he turned onto the cobbled street leading to the station, each bounding stride agonising as the stones embedded into his stiff soles. With the balls of his feet throbbing as bruises began to fester under marbling skin, he threw himself up the station's steps, then pushed the doors open with his momentum alone. Through the crowded foyer he raced, bouncing off awaiting passengers as his attention was distracted to the arrivals and departures board. 'Gah!' he exhaled as a pile of luggage sneaked in under his pre-occupied radar and sent him crashing onto the hard, tiled floor. Wet soles skidded as he scrambled up, not even taking the time to apologise before he set back on his course. 'I was like an addict needing my fix,' he dryly remarked. 'All conceptions of right and wrong were thrown out the window if they interfered with my goal. My cat found a new home when I no longer cared to feed him, bills came with red warnings as standard, and clothes carried the stale smell of unwashed sweat.' Matching the time on his watch to the times of the arrivals, he headed for platform 5, hurdling the barriers as raising both the attention and ire of station security. Like a lone cavalry charge into a tight rank of infantry, he crashed into the departing passengers, filtering through them until a rather more dishevelled version of his previous self emerged the other side. As he heard the sounds of departure start up, he tried to find an extra gear, almost toppling forward and grazing his palms on the concrete as he steadied himself. 'Wait up,' he called as the doors were about to close, but the sounds of industry powering the train engulfed his voice. 'Hey!' With giant strides, he made it to the nearest door, then made a desperate leap for it. Contorting his body in mid-air, he slithered through the lessening gap, just managing to pull the tail of his coat through as the door locked shut. Salty perspiration could be tasted, mixed in with the rainwater that ran from his face, while scorched lungs heaved the spent, carriage air in to sooth them. 'Sir,' a conductor addressed him as he approached, 'your seat is this way.' 'Bu-' he mumbled in breathless bemusement. 'Wha-... how?' 'This way, Sir,' he was simply prompted once more. Despite his wheezing and spluttering, lungs wishing they didn't come with their fine lining of tar, nobody seemed to be paying him attention. Automated, suited drones tapped fruitlessly away at their laptops, while the day's headlines were being re-read from newspapers whose pages had never been turned. 'Here you are, Sir,' the conductor gestured, pointing him to a table seat, opposite a woman whose mere appearance breathed soothing air back into his lungs, and closed gushing pores. 'Never had something so plain and unremarkable inspired such awe...' 'I finally found you,' the man exhaled as he collapsed into the seat opposite her. 'I finally allowed myself to be found,' she dashed any thoughts of achievement with, not even looking away from the window's scene of passing platforms and signs. 'That wa-' he went to ask, patting his coat where the cell phone rested. 'Yes,' she shot back instantly with a cocked eyebrow, surprised he'd even doubt it for one second. His body pulsed as each rotation of the train's wheels sent the vehicle lunging forward along the uneven tracks, yet the woman opposite remained motionless, serene, her body absorbing any shock. As the rain lashed against the glass, a fine spray infecting the carriage where one window wouldn't lock shut, the lights flickered... and he half expected her to vanish in the split-second the fluorescent dimmed. 'Every word I spoke was like teetering in the edge of a cliff... no safety net if I acted rash or unwisely.' 'Why?' the man pondered, wondering if the game of Cat and Mouse had been orchestrated from the start. 'Because you weren't looking for the question,' she stated, in matter-of-fact fashion. From under her rainwater-beaded hood, emerald eyes of truth glistened, while tufts of mesmerising lilac hair forced his gaze to follow its strands. 'Were you?' 'I-I wa-' he stuttered, pulling his attention from her hair, and back to her lightly freckled face. 'You were trying to find me to know the answer,' her words spoke directly from pale pink lips and into his brain. It was like watching a video where the sound or vision were delayed, and the formation of the words lagged behind on the screen. The movement of her lips was an afterthought, since he already knew what she was saying. 'I don't understand,' he asked with a furrowed brow, while partly distracted by a drop of water from his hair funnelling into his ear, dancing around the rim like a basketball before dropping in. 'The answer to what?' 'Don't you mean the answer to why?' she talked over the end of his question with, raising her feet up onto her seat, then hugging her knees close to her. Despite looking at least 6 feet, her body managed to fold up, concealed under her long coat and baggy trousers, to leave the exposed face looking awkwardly large. 'That wa-' dangled from his lips, never quite managing to free itself. 'That was what I asked,' she spoke to the rain-streaked window, trying to make out the scenery through the harsh, reflective glare of the carriage's lights. Shifting her focus an inch or so to the side, she then looked right into his reflection's eyes. 'I was given the gift of anything I wished... and I asked why.' 'Why what?' went his instinctive reaction, trying to avoid getting lost in her maze of cryptic word. 'Just why,' danced from her lips in reply, as her head turned back from the window. 'Why?' the man staggered blindly deeper into the maze with. 'Exactly,' she quipped back, managing to tease and jest with a hint of overbearing seriousness remaining in her voice. Fingers began to twirl locks of lilac hair around them, the calming, circular motion almost hypnotising the sodden man opposite. 'What was th-' he went to ask, before pausing to think if he really should. 'What was the answer?' 'Could there ever be an answer to such a question?' she made him ponder, emerald eyes constantly scanning the carriage, where everyone seemed to be shielding themselves from life's truth with broadsheet newspaper. 'I haven't slept in 6 days,' he confessed, removing the hat that shadowed his face to reveal blood-shot eyes, bordered by dry, red skin. Wrinkles that shouldn't have formed for another 10 years had already embedded themselves deeply into the contours of his face, while the finger that scratched his nose was dyed a jaundice yellow by chain smoking. 'I barely even remember dates or names anymore, when I turn on the TV I'm just staring at it instead of watching anything. I smoke just so I can taste something in my mouth.' 'It was as if there was a gaping hole in the universe, and I craved the thread to sew it back together.' With elbows resting on the table between them, the man appealed to her in a mixture of prayer and pleading. 'It's been 4 months since I was asked to find you,' he confessed, thinking back to when he assumed it would simply be a job of giving an address or an updated description. Before the 20 hour days, before his home turned into a developing lab, and before just a tantalisingly brief glimpse of the woman would threaten to burst a blood vessel in his brain. 'And 3 months since I realised I needed to find you for myself instead.' 'Even if you're ready for the answer,' she challenged, gesturing discreetly to the content and oblivious cattle that they shared the carriage with, 'what makes you think that the rest of this world is?' 'When was the last time you saw someone smile?' his stressed-out words questioned, not even bothering to lower his voice. Despite his outburst, not a single head was turned, or newspaper lowered. Even a sneeze that burst out from behind them seemed subdued, almost apologetic. 'Not some false laugh because they feel they need to. I'm talking a REAL smile, just walking down the street and having a fond thought, a pleasant memory...' 'And if I tell,' her words took on a grave tone with, finally pulling her hood back to show her lilac mane cascading down her back, 'there could be tears just as easily as smiles.' 'You have no right to keep the answer to yourself,' he forced out through gritted teeth, eyes marbled with unhealthy red. 'And you have no right to impose it upon everyone,' she judged in return, not even flinching as a passing train rocked the carriage with displaced air. 'It IS everyone's answer,' the man appealed, almost crawling onto the table in front of her such was his desperation to know why. 'There are things in the world that would drive us insane unless we found their solution... questions that had to be answered for us to even care about breathing, eating, and sleeping.' Even though her eyes were never closed, they appeared to open, emerald irises adorned with a pattern that made him wonder if it was natural. The harsh lights refused to reflect off her pupils, and the whites surrounding the stark colour were a milky, untainted wash of purity. 'But maybe the one thing meant to save us, will ultimately be our downfall...' 'And would they really want to know,' he heard in his head, without even a token movement of the woman's lips accompanying them as her eyelids finally dropped, 'that the world i-' When they opened again, the world was nothing but a distant afterthought, fragmented memories leaving her to piece it back together again. BEEP! 'If this wakes me,' the message she barely remembered recording the night before kicked in with, 'I will kill you dead.' 'Er, hi, Karina, it's Blaine Bishop... y'know, Hin See's Intern?' 'They, um, please don't kill me, please? They, uh, wanted me to call to see if you'd decided what to, er, ask for? After winning the Dual Halo and everything, but of course you knew that, since you won it and all. Um, so, yeah... and I was serious about the killing thing. Please don't...' Karina Wolfenden had a choice to make, and what she asked for would effect far more that just her.
