CONQUEST FOR THE CROWN</font EPISODE I - The Rough Ride to Rapid City The beauty of riding the track is that you know where you’re going. Not by maps or updates from the conductor or by looking through the cracks of the railcar; you feel the wheels grind and run against the steel beneath you, guiding you through terrain otherwise unbearable. Every man, woman, and child feels the rotating steel on steel of each massive wheel. The re-verb shakes the contents of the snaking trail of railcars, jerking back and forth dozens of times per second. Surprisingly, people are the contents and, even more shocking is the fact that they try to sleep through the violent experience of "living life on the rails" because it’s a low-cost, off-the-grid means of transportation. Even sitting there is physically exhausting. The smell rivals stale, rain-trodden trash that’s been keeping warm with the inside of the car turning into a sauna at points in these summer months. Along with smelling like sewage, inside the car it is dim and sight isn’t very good, which makes sense, since the car wasn’t made for people to ride in anyway. And comfort is nonexistent, old couches and cots wedged together are what people are sleeping on. Out the shadows of the front of the car is a sight that might seem strange given a different setting; big black boots stick out of the darkness under khaki cargo pants with two thick, hairy forearms that are the size of two fat ends of a Louisville Slugger resting on his knees. More toward the door is a young mother and her two young boys, who both have her lovely blond hair and sparkling blue eyes that shine through dirt covering their soft cheeks. Her face is dirty, like her boys’, but still holds a resemblance of how pretty she actually is and once was. In the corner at the back is a group of ruffians, outcasts, who have allowed these people passage for a price. A small crate is used as a card table as three men with gritty snarls on their faces peer through the light cast by a small lantern. One man slaps down a stack of bills, bring grumbles from the others as they dig into their pockets. The mother runs her hand over her older boy’s head, smiling back at his smiling face. He looks back to his brother, who bounces a ball at him. The older boy is thrust in the opposite direction though, and the ball as well, goes off course and into the lantern. It shatters at the feet of the three unsavory characters. One man kicks the crate over, drawing out his curvy, rusty knife. The rust comes from the years of use. The mother grips her boys, getting prepared to protect them with her life. "What did we say, whore?" the rider wearing a black patch over his eye snarls. "What was the rule?" He reaches out but she scoots back, evading his grip. He begins to laugh and show her his knife by holding it in many different grips. "We told you…and these little brats…nobody touches our cards, our money, or our light. Now, we’ve got now light… and you’ve got a big problem." She puts her hands together and begins pleading from her knees. "Please… please don’t hurt-" The snaggletooth lunges out again, this time grabbing her youngest boy, sand reels him in like s fresh catch. The boy squirms and fights, as does the mother, until everyone notices the tip of his hook-blade is pressed against the kid’s jugular. Tears boil over the mother’s eyes and her frantic state of mind is only shown through her silence. "Heh heh," the man croaks, "see that boys? That’s how you shut ‘em up. Grab the kid, threaten this little puke’s life, and she melts like fuckin’ butter. Beautiful how life works, ain’t it?" He switches the blade to her chin and lifts it up, gently, trying not to ruin her angelic face. They lock eyes, his in some creepy, psychotic love-bond, and hers, with all intent on getting her boy back. Another violent thrust of the car shifting direction sends the man back a step and the boy toward his mother. She leaps out and grabs him, but meets a gritty paw holding the knife, and the man wrenches back before jamming it into her forearm. Her scream echoes off the walls of the boxcar as the boys try to aid their momma but are quick to be stopped by the boxcar bully’s underlings. One man tosses the smaller kid into the shadowy front of the car and directly into the big boots. "I thought we were clear, bitch? You want me to kill your boy?" the leader swings the sliding door wide open, exposing the harsh reality that no matter what, nobody’s safe if that thing isn’t closed. She screams and reaches out, but once again is met by the blade. "You want me to throw him out into that?" He presses on her cheek, forcing her gaze to shift into the black night. Her mind fills with fear; half knowing that her boy would die, and the other not knowing what it is he will die from. The stench of bourbon seeps into her nose as the man lick’s her cheek. "Take off your blouse." Lightly tugging on the top button, he draws the knife back to her boys’ neckline. He nods his head toward her son, implying that things won’t go well if she doesn’t do as told. Slowly, timidly, she folds her hands together and begins to unbutton her blouse, exposing her perky breasts and conservative white bra. Her eyes stick ot the ground as the two disgusting men nudge elbows and grunt together, smiling like two pigs in shit. "Damn, kid," he says to the boy in his grip. "You’re mom is one delicious lookin’ piece of pie. Ain’t she boys? Hungry for some pie?" The man takes the knife and runs it down her bare chest and then under the strap. He yanks up, slitting the band. She trembles below the blade as he finds it utterly pleasurable. Making his way across her collarbone, he jerks up, again, slitting the band. She lifts her eyes to the man as he hooks her remaining hope of cover and grins. "I love pie," he whispers to her, blowing her a kiss. THUD THUD The man’s crazy gaze shifts to the shadows beyond the delightful sight in front of him. He cocks his head, glancing back to the others before taking a few steps forward. The young boy steps out of the shadows, timidly, and holds his hands in front of his chest. The men all start laughing. "Hahahaa, dang! You gonna hurt me for touchin’ your mom, kid?" he turns back to the other men. "That scared the shit outta me!" From behind the boy, a man steps out from behind him and stands to his full height – and towers over everyone and everything inside the car. Dropping his hood, he reveals his face. Covered by a thick, rigorous beard and long, brown locks of hair, his eyes are not hidden. They glow like burning torches in the darkest of night. "That was the point, Chump." He throws out a fist that catches the closest villain in the jaw, which cracks like a dry twig under pressure. The man’s head bounces off the wall with a wooden knock and sends him tumbling to his knees. In a flash, the big man is on the other side of the remaining two men. "Get him…" the leader clutches his crony and thrust him into the shadows. "Get him off my car!" The hobo tries to swing at the mysterious man but misses and catches a fist to the face, then gut, then face, again. The big hands wraps around his waistline and shirt just as he winds up and tosses him out of the doorway, bouncing to an uncomfortable coma. Silence consumes the car, besides the sound of the outside environment passing them at a steady 110 miles per hour. The big man breaks his hiding, stepping out directly in front of the man holding the knife. He looks at the knife then at the kid before he kneels down and hands the woman his overcoat. He rises back to full height and locks eyes with the smaller, weaker looking scavenger. "Get the fuck out of here, man," he snarls, pushing the blade toward the kid more, "This is my box. You got that? My box." Seconds of tension, assessment, and planning go by before the bigger man cracks his knuckles. He continues to maintain his poise by rolling his neck. A smile comes over his face just before he jumps in the air, grabbing the kid’s shirt when he does so. As the rail car shifts, the boy and his carrier go right as the knife and it’s wielder go left. The big man tosses the kid onto a stack of blankets and hay and whips around his right boot to catch the man directly in the face. He stumbles into the wall of the car, smashing his face and dropping the knife. The big man grabs him and tosses him by his neck into the opposite wall. Spotting the blade, the man makes a last ditch effort to snatch it but misses and catches another boot to the head. The big man lifts him up by his dirt-riddled shirt. He points a finger in the lone good eye of the leader. "Don’t swear in front of kids," he jams his fist in the good eye. "Don’t threaten people with knives," again, jamming the good eye with a knuckle quartet. "Don’t cut babe’s bras off them without permission," he nails the nose, busting it up and drawing blood. "Don’t swear in front of kids," he says, smiling, and jabbing his opponent in the face one more time. He performs an Irish whip on the man, sending him into the abyss of the night. A small glint of satisfaction runs over his face and he turns back to assess the damage. The mother holds her boys once again, brushing their hair but this time; all have their eyes on the large man before them. "Uhh… everyone alright?" The car jerks again, launching the younger boy out the door and sends a chilling effect down the hero’s spine. He swings his trunk-like arm out and grabs the kid’s shirt before leaving his feet. The few seconds as both bodies float in mid air, the two lock eyes; filling with terror as they get closer and closer to the edge, he thrusts his free hand out. "Hang on!" he shouts, swinging his other hand back at the doorframe. Luckily, his large fingers wrap around it and he swings out over the ledge, keeping his firm grip on the boy. The child’s small frame bounces off the side of the car, brushing it barely, and his legs dangle in the draft coming off the cars ahead of him. The man tightens his grip and then jerks the boy away from the car’s wall. He spins on the door frame, tossing the boy back into the car, and he rolls on the hard floor to the feet of his mother. She picks him up and clutches his head, fighting back tears of fear and joy long enough to look at her boy. His hair is blown out from the windy situation but not one is missing. "Thank you! Thank you so much," the mother whispers, hugging her boys and kissing their heads. "You saved my family… you saved me. I can never repay you, I owe you my life. You’re a hero." He grips the heavy door and flings it shut, shutting off the outside noise. A small smile spreads over his face but he remains humble. "No, no, no; in my line of work you have to step up and fight for what’s right, for justice. It’s contagious, so I guess I’m just doing my job, Miss…" "Desiree. Desiree Love," She smiles, batting her eyes rapidly, "But my friends call me Desi." "I’m no hero, Desi," he says, turning back to the wreckage and picks up a pillow. "I’m just glad I could help." She grins, still coddling her young, "What do we call you?" Slouching down against the wall, the big man props the pillow behind his neck and shuts his eyes. He nestles into a suitable position and a few seconds of silence go by, leaving her questioning if he can go to sleep that fast. He props one eyelid open and catches her aimlessly gazing at his furry mug, "My friends call me Mike." "The greater difficulty, the more glory in surmounting it. Skillful pilots gain their reputation from storms and tempests." -Epicurus</center Normally, a professional wrestler can find ways to work that exclude the dangerous adventure of riding rail cars as a guest of lowlifes. Posh limousines and first-class flights are the perks of being a world famous athlete not to mention the paychecks usually cash in a bit more than a bus ticket costs. This is no ordinary ride to the arena, though, and Mike Munson is not just another professional wrestler. Desi Love sits near the car door, slightly ajar for her boys to get some fresh air and sunlight in their sunny eyes. Mike sits across the car against the wall, peeling an apple with a ramhorn knife he’s had since childhood. The apple slice sticks to the blade as he brings it to his mouth and throws it back. The echo of the crunch draws Desi’s attention; her boys remain busy throwing rocks out of the car like grenades, making explosion noises when they land on the passing landscape. "What do you do?" she asks, eyes looking at the knife slicing into the apple. Mike’s eyes look up, surprised. "Say somethin’?" "I asked, what do you do?" Desi repeats, firmly. "you said you have to fight for people in ‘you’re line of work,’ so I was wondering… what do you do?" "Ohhh, heh, sorry," he takes another slice and chomps down on it. "I was thinking about a juicy cheeseburger. I’m starvin’." She giggles, showing she’s still got youth in her veins no matter how old her boys are getting. Mike grins, glancing at the kids and then to the sun drenched Western range of the Canadian Rockies. He tears up a little at the exposure to sunlight and the vast beauty of Mother Nature’s fine, fine mountains. Mike remembers she asked a question. "Well, that’s a complicated answer to an easy question," he chuckles through chewing his snack. "I’m kind of like…" Desi’s eyes grow twice their size, "Are you a spy?" "I wish!" Mike laughs then, in his warped mind he begins to entertain the thought of him looking like a spy. He likes it. His name would be Double-Oh Disco Stick. "Spies don’t have entrance music, though, so no thanks." "Do you kill people?" she whispers, leaning away so her boys might not hear. "It’s okay, I won’t-" "Noooononononono," Mike waves the knife around and the apple, signaling her mistake immediately. "I… I wrestle." "Really? You’re a wrestler?" Desi shrugs. Her tone is one of surprise, much as Mike expected, and some curiosity. "Well, that makes sense I guess. You are big. You are strong. You look… intimidating." Mike laughs. He and her both know what she means by intimidating. He nods, trying to be casual and polite. "That’s right. I’m intimidating. Actually, Miss Love, you might find it a surprise that I am not a ‘bad guy,’ as you might think me to be." A coy smirk spreads over her lips, "My oh my! That is a surprise. Who might you be then? The… Lumberjack Man? The Train Car Killer?" "No, mom," the older boy says. "That’s Big Bear. He’s a good-guy wrestler." Mike’s jaw drops. She looks at her boy with a precious smile, still not registering that her son knows their hero’s name. She looks back at him, with his jaw still hanging, and he gives a stupid smile. "Uhh…" Mike’s face begins to regain color. "You got it, champ." "So you are a wrestler…" Desi thinks for a few seconds while Mike begins to gather himself. "yet you seem surprised Devon knows you?" Mike bites into the apple after stabbing the knife into the floorboard beside him He nods and points; "That’s a smart little fucker…erm, smart kid you’ve got, Miss Love-" "Please, call me Desi," she interjects, reaching out to try and relay her wish. "and don’t swear in front of my kids." "Oh, right," Mike stops, putting his hand over his mouth with the ‘OH SHIT!’ eyes. He nods and then crosses his arms, nodding back at her boy. "It’s just that, well, I’m kind of... getting back on my feet, I guess? I used to be young and vibrant, full of life, and the man on the tip of everyone’s tongue in the wrestling business. I flopped. Which is why I’m surprised your boys know who this two-star nobody is even a wrestler." Desiree glances down, thinking about the problem at hand, and her eyes wander over to her boys. Mike’s head slumps back against the wall and his thoughts consume him, allowing silence to cloud the air. "Devon, honey," Desi breaks the silence as the boy looks up at his mother, eyes beaming like two little spotlights. "Why didn’t you tell Mommy who our friend is?" Devon looks at Mike, who gazes back curiously, and then the boy looks at his momma shyly. "Darren did it." "Did what?" she asks, looking at her younger boy. "He saw him first," Devon says through a little Canadian accent. "Then he told me it was the Big Bear and that he would save us from those chumps." She looks at Darren, then Devon, and then finally turns her attention to Mike, with her eyebrows cocked and questioning. "Save us from those… chumps?" "That’s right little dude!" Mike’s voice booms through the car. Devon jumps up with a giant smile on his face and exchanges a loud high five with the Bear Creek Brawler. "How did you know it was me, though?" The big blue eyes of the boy look into Mike’s, "Me and my brother remember you, Big Bear. You came to stop Sonny Silver and Chainz and once we saw you we knew, we knew you were coming back to save us, to save PRIME, it was like we predicted it! We knew you would come back! We knew! We knew! We knew you would! Didn’t we, Darren?" Devon’s little brother is already standing next to him and he is practically jumping up and down. Their mother tries to restrain the boys but really doesn’t need to, as they are simply jumping in place, "I knew it was you, Big Bear! I told my brother and said it was you and then you saved us! You are my favorite wrestler; I’m your biggest fan… bigger than my big brother! We knew you would come back! We knew you wouldn’t leave us!" Desi sits in disbelief that her boys love the man who sits in front of them right now, and even more unbelievable; the man her boys are going nuts for just saved their lives. Her heart begins pounding with each correlating thought that connects the dots for her. Her head turns slowly to meet eyes with Mike’s and the two exchange a grin of understanding. "Are you going to beat up Shakur again?" Devon shouts. "Are you?" Darren adds, shouting louder than his big brother. "Are you?" Mike looks at the boys and takes another bite out of the apple. He chews on the apple for a bit, teasing them, enough to draw in the mother’s attention as well. Everyone waits, interested in Big Bear’s response. He finishes chewing the rest of the apple and looks up after finishing his snacktastic treat. "I’m hopin’ it doesn’t turn into that for a little while, boys," Big Bear cocks one corner of his mouth, grinning, "Next for me is showin’ the world I’ve still got the moves to put together a nice and long Championship run to give PRIME a much needed dose of fresh air. I’ve got to prove I can hang with the great Jacob McKail first… then win that Jewel in the Crown Tournament… then win the PRIME Universal Title. After that… I think I can afford to pay the Boss another visit." Darren smiles wide, "You can do it, Big Bear! I know you can." "Thanks little buddy," Mike tousles the boy’s hair and smiles back. "I’ve been working hard to get ready so I sure hope you’re right. McKail brings it hard every time, just like ol’ Big Bear does, so I’m a’lookin’ forward to getting’ in the ring with him and givin’ you fans an instant classic to talk about." From the front of the train, a long winded whistle draws everyone’s attention to outside the car. The boys point and the mother watches as the rural hamlet of Rapid City, South Dakota comes into view. Big Bear stands to his feet with a smile. Having never seen Rapid City in his long journey on the road to Legendary status, he watches and begins to daydream of the many activities he’ll bide his time with. He hopes he doesn’t run into another pair of twins with matching devil tattoos again. That was an expensive mistake. The train’s whistle sounds off once again as the train begins to grind toward a stop. Big Bear’s eyes pick up and he looks out the train cart wondering about the trials and tribulations that will lead him to King of Kings and the Jewel in the Crown finals.
