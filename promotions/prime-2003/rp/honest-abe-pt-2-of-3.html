Honest Abe (pt. 2 of 3)

Date: November The site of this foul wench made Abraham Harris utterly sick. He had twenty-four wives. Incidentally, all twenty-four made up 100% of the female congregation of the Happy People of a Certainly Doomed Future. This was primarily due to the fact that Harris' paranoia had become a mere pedistal to egomania and the Happy People had become Happy Worshippers (or, as he saw fit, Happy Concubines apparently). "Leviticus. Chapter 20. Verse 10." The False Prophet's voice was low and hoarse. His ebon complexion feintly reflected in the candlelight of his small, private cabin. A type of privacy that wasn't granted to his followers. He had installed hidden cameras sometime ago. What he had seen did not please him. Abraham continued, "If a man commits adultery with another man's wife, both the adulterer..." She had been tied to a chair, the fair skinned gal. She was shivering as though the spirit of the Lord had overwhelmed her in a sort of Pentecostal rapture, the sweat from her worried brows colored with fear. "..and the adulteress..." She started weeping. She knew the scripture long, long before Harris' slow delivery of it on that evening. Though she never thought her life would end like this... then again, she had never thought that she would beg for it as hard as she did either. She interrupted, "PLEASE! PLEASE! You don't have to, I sware it will never happen again." Abraham Harris considered this for the briefest of moments. "I have punished Darryl as the Word of God said. It would be unfair, Violet, if I spared you." His repetition was to emphasize the dramatic moment, "both the adulturer... and the adulturess..." Violet had no words. Only quiet sobs. "...shall be put to Death." In his quoting of Scripture, Harris had walked around the desk and opened a drawer. A small revolver was drawn and he walked back toward his scared wife. "You were one of my favorites," he proclaimed with a sigh of regret. "I wish things could be different, I really do." "But I've bowed at your feet! I've slept with you, despite the fact that my ACTUAL husband was Darryl... and you MURDERED HIM!?! OH..." The reality of her situation had hit her harder than Abraham ever could. Even were her life to be spared, her husband... her life... they were removed now. The Fifteenth "Wife" of Abraham Harris was going to be killed in cold blood for making love with her own husband, whom she loved so dear. "I now see that Darryl meant a lot to you. And for that I'm sorry," Abraham said it in what could only be described, however sickeningly, as genuine compassion. "I would not have allowed you into my congregation, had I know--" "I see what you are now," Violet cut in. She grit the words through her teeth, shaking with tears falling down her face. Her voice, cackling with panic and absolute understanding. Finally, after so long, her thoughts were clear. How could she have been so stupid? The False Prophet was amused, "Go ahead, Violet. Tell me what I am." "You're not God," she spat. "No?" The False Prophet's tone was soaked with beguilement. "Than who am I?" Violet's eyes narrowed. She smiled pathetically. "You're the Devil." Honest Abe frowned. His eyes glazed over. He placed the barrel of the gun to her temple. Suddenly, he'd found sweat perspiring from his own furrowed brow. She repeated, "You're the Devil" bang ---------------------------------- Rhett Locke's sense of personal tragedy drew little sympathy from One and the Other. "You don't seem to understand," said One. "You don't have a choice. You opened the folder, you follow the orders, you don't give us shit about your personal morality. That's the nature of the business." The restaurant was greasy and full of the deep south. Grits, hogjowls and all kinds of soulfood was being served. One and the Other ate heartily of the chicken thighs and barbequed pork. Rhett Locke ate nothing. The Other continued, "You see. We're just like everybody else. We're just, doin' our jobs bro. Hell, we don't want you to die. We, as pure spectators, have grown a rather fondness of you to be quite honest." "It's true," interrupted One with a mouthful of pork. "You're pretty tough. We admire that in people. You've got that uhh... survival instinct." "No no," Rhett chuckled. "You don't seem to understand. I'm not killing fifty-six people." "You're always forgetting, dumbass." One said, mouth full yet somehow pronouncing everything perfectly. "If you don't, we're gonna kill your friends. Then, we'll kill you. You see, we like you. But we are not above killing you to save our own jobs. We like you, we love our job. Capiche?" "...Fifty-six people, though? Really? All of them?" The Other smiled, "Come now. Would you really need all that poison for one man?" Rhett sighed. He remembered the entire pocket full in his suit, "I suppose not..." The Albino was dressed for a funeral. Perhaps it was a sign of things to come for several others. ---------------------------------- Archibald Myers, though educated and scholarly in more ways than one, was not a quick study himself. His mild demeanor, wrinkley face and white, thin hair eluded that he was merely an old man. Though were one to look carefully behind those kind, blue eyes they may find that things aren't ever exactly as they seem. "What you did this evening, Abraham," Archibald began. His voice was aged and hoarse. The kind of voice that tells stories of Vietnam and smoking reefer with Neil Young. "It will scare the congregation. You musn't let what happened leave this cabin." Abraham had been looking out the window, hands behind his back and folded together. His dark complexion contrasted in a bold fashion with his white suit. It was the suit he'd worn since beginning the Happy People, despite the humid South Georgia climate. A fat cigar was in his mouth, lit and smoked halfway down. Harris' dark eyes narrowed. "Archie," The False Prophet smiled. "I know that you are my advisor and I appreciate what you're saying... but if you're going to continue giving me advice that I already know... perhaps you should step down." "Hey, you're the prophet. Just tell me this, then, all knowing and wise selfish fool... what will you be doing about that?" Archibald pointed to the dead body of Violet, brain residue and pieces of cranium left surrounding her neck. It had been there for two days, decomposing and smelling absolutely horrific. The smell did not seem to bother either man. Harris only shrugged. If one were to ask either Archibald or Abraham about the body and one were to get a truthful answer, both would reply with, "It is a minor setback in the embryonic stages of a plan with grand proportions." Archie had trained Honest Abe well. The problem was that each man's plan was so vastly different that one of them would be very surprised, very soon. knock knock knock knock knock knock knock, KNOCK KNOCK KNOCK "STOP IT," screamed Abe Harris. He marched toward the door like a pissed off marine. Though he opened it carefully and merely poked his head out, the kind of move a man makes when there is a dead body in plain sight laying on the floor surrounded by blood and brains. "May I help you, my love?" The lady was beautiful and stammering. A model of a trailer park's favorite gal who'd just seen a ghost. Her plaid, tied shirt brought out the beauty of her figure. Though her green eyes, normally endearing and holding the taint of a thorough brainwashing, held fear in them. "The tripwire... it went off... has it started, the war? There's a man... he's in the nets... will there be more?" Abraham turned his head and looked at Archibald. Archie's smile held a calm demeanor, "Ah, a trespassor." ---------------------------------- Before the private cabin of Abraham Harris, far in front of the small housing of the Happy People of a Certainly Doomed Future, in the way of the landmines and booby traps, an albino with long blonde hair was dangling in the grasps of a net from a tree's branch. He'd fallen for the first of very many security devices set in place by the paranoid Prophet. Locke felt rather stupid about the whole thing as well, the folder he'd been given by One and the Other had a detailed autopsy of the area. Even the very trap he'd fallen for, Rhett remembered in great detail. He had been dangling there for quite some time. Early morning had become late night. Did they even know he was here? Would he starve to death before they even noticed his timely arrival? Though as those thoughts creeped into his mind, something else had as well. "Hello, Mr. Locke." Rhett scrambled to find the voice. Ah, down below him. The questions began to pour in. How'd the man know his name? Why did he not even hear the man approaching? For some reason, be it reverence or chickenheartedness, nothing escaped his grit teeth but a whispered, "Hello." "You don't need to fear me, Rhett. I've known about your arrival for sometime now. I've been... to coin a phrase, expecting you. This moment is a defining moment in History, whether you know this or not you soon will." "...Abraham Harris?" Rhett took a shot in the dark. The man laughed, "That poor fool? Don't insult me with the name of that walking tragedy. You can call me Darryl." "...Am I going to die, Darryl?" The shadowy figure of Darryl shrugged, "Perhaps you will. I did." Rhett shuddered. "You did?" Darryl did not answer right away, "They will be coming for you very soon. They were waiting for a particular arrival of... and this is not an understatement, copious amounts of LSD. Brainwashing tool of the Gods... Sadly, you've been caught and quite a bit of it will be force fed to you. The kindof amount that drove Syd Barrett to La La Land. It is likely that under normal circumstances, you would be incapacited and locked away in a padded cell the rest of your days with the amount of drugs you're going to be given. I need you to make it through whatever it is they do to you, with your sanity in hand. You are the exact diversion I need." "Diversion you need for what?" Rhett's voice was breaking. The idea of being a distraction wasn't a pleasant one, especially as Darryl described it. "Revenge." "...You're taking revenge on these people?" There was grit teeth involved with Darryl's next statement. "They killed my wife, you see." All of this information was fingering Rhett's panic button. Sweat began pouring from his pores. His eyes were watering and his breaths were short. His heartbeat was fast and steady. "Why hello there, trespasser!" "Do not trust the old man," Rhett heard Darryl say within himself. Wait, within Locke? Rhett glanced up at the voice that called for him. Red eyes met kind blue ones. "I'm Archie!" His voice was kind and soothing. "Hello Archie." "You'll be let down and brought to supper immediately. I hope you don't mind turnips?" "You love turnips." "...I love turnips," Rhett followed suggestive orders very well. Especially when a seemingly supernatural being is delivering them. Archie smiled again, "Good. We all love turnips here. Farm them ourselves, infact." There was something very odd about being so nice to a man you just caught in a net for secretive purposes, Rhett thought. "Let him free, Abraham." "Shouldn't we just kill him? He's wierd looking. I mean, look at the bastard. I don't want that wierd fuck in my congregation!" Archibald's smile remained and he suggested once more, "Let him free, Abe." As Abraham Harris sighed and began to go through the necessary process to free Locke from his entanglement, Rhett could notice the strings attatched to Abraham's limbs and how Archibald so delicately controlled them.
