Pandora's Box: The Opening

30 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: October 30th, 2008 Prologue. The Jewel in the Crown Tournament... An event held year after year to see what man or woman can rise up and find their heart when in the PRIME squared circle. The first ever person to win JitC was Ian English in 2004. A name recognized with Hall of Fame prestige. Move ahead three years, 2007, Chandler Tsonda walks out of King of Kings the Jewel in the Crown winner. Fast forward a year later, we await the eve of the first series of tournament matches. What is most important in this history lesson is, every year the Jewel in the Crown tournament comes around, somebody walks into the limelight, somebody finds their calling. Only a few days out from the Great American Nightmare, Chandler Tsonda still holds the very title her earned the shot for since accomplishing the crown feat. Now though, it’s another’s turn to walk amongst the gold strapped gentlemen. King of Kings rings like a whistle to the rookies, while the veterans stare down a path they know too well. In this day in age, some say a man is given a chance for the opportunity to seize the ability to change his future… My future is already determined and there isn’t anything that can be done about it. That is, unless I say so. I’ll save the trash talk for the time when it matters, for now, see what changes I’ve made to move on, see what the world of Jesse Jamester has molded itself into, and tell me… This isn’t a man that’s going be the next PRIME Universal Champion. Story begins. “Life is a complicated mix.” Someone once said to me that and then went on to describe it as life’s blend was like mixed drinks. You have your multiple liquors that alone can do a range of different effects to anyone who consumes them. Yet, put them together and you have a concoction that’s effect will put even the best of men in his worse state, or best, depending on the person. I guess that’s why I ended up where I was in the bar that night, but then again it could have been the joint I smoked out back with some old heads. Who knows really, but it was one hell of a night and one I wouldn’t forget even in my intoxicated slumber. I remember it from the very minute I saw her. That four foot ten inch, give or take one, strawberry bombshell with the most captivating hazel eyes. She was drinking with some friends of hers, playing pool and laughing. Awwwh, that laugh, how it caught my attention as she ordered up another shot… A woman who could drink and look good doing it, wow, I must get this chick’s number I started thinking. Relaxing my arms, it’d been a while since I’d approached a woman, so the nerves were acting up and I felt I needed something to calm them. “A beer please,” I shot across the bar to the tender, dropping an Abe Lincoln for his quick service. A swig out of the fresh brew in a bottle, and I’m on my way to pull a move on this sweet thing I see standing there, waiting for her turn on the green velvet table. Pulling two quarters out of my pocket, I place them on the table for next game and take a step back. I’m standing next to her now… Hmmm, interesting, my nerves are gone. “So how about, if I beat you I get a date?” the words shoot from my mouth as I turn to her, bottle in my left hand. What the hell am I thinking goes through my mind, when in the world has that ever worked on a woman? Her eyebrows look at me quizzical, and she chuckles a laugh as she shakes her head no. “I don’t think you’re going to win, but okay, if you think you can,” she replies even though she seems hesitant. I take another swig from my bottle, smiling now that I have something promising to look forward to. She steps up to the table for her turn, taking her time to aim up the eight ball to the far left corner, and then unleashes her stick on the cue ball, driving the number eight into the corner pocket she sized up. Her opponent shakes there head and drops a twenty on the table, she reaches down and picks it up, shoving it down into her cleavage before turning to me. “You rack’em, I break,” with a hint of determination in her words. I knew this girl had skill, but I didn’t think she’d be so good. Right off the break she sinks a high ball, and then cranks out three more before missing on a bank shot. I step up, sink a low ball in the far left corner, waiting to see her reaction, nothing. So I continue, sinking another ball. Tough spot now, anywhere I shoot leaves her open. Hmmmm… I figure I’ll block her in, easy way to save myself from her dropping another ball. I slightly graze the cue ball and send it rolling so lightly up to my low ball, it touches and stops, no severe hit, just a standard block. She shakes her head in a knowing way of what I wanted to do, “You’ll have to do better than that cowboy,” and with that she hop shots the ball over mine and sinks another ball, but her ball scratches and she throws her head back, hair flying in the action, and I can’t but admire the beauty of her in the dim orange-yellowish bar light. “Nice shot,” I retort trying not to be a prick in my words. It’s hard though for a guy my size to come off as a ‘nice guy’ when I’m in a bar, so many guys are jackasses when it comes to drinking. Though I’ve learned my limit, I can be the worst when I’m putting down shots. Sizing up the cue ball for my next shot, I send it down the length of the table, smacking the bank and missing my target by half an inch or less. I shake my head and grab my bottle, chugging what’s left down. Again stepping up, she takes her time to aim, precise adjustments, and shoot, sink, boom, she’s two away from game over, not including the eight ball. “So what do I get if I win?” she shoots across the pool table as she bends over and sizes up the next shot. “Whatever you want,” I reply not sure what she means, but I figure she’s going to tell me to do something easy, so why not. A devious smile overcomes her as she sinks another and is set up for her last ball before the eight ball. “Whatever huh?” she says and then sinks her last one. Looking at the eight ball, she has to bank it to make it, and the only time she’s missed so far is on the bank. In my pocket I’m crossing my fingers, theirs no way I can lose this, not now, no way. She aims up, glides the stick on the skin between her index and thumb, one push, two push, finally she thrusts it forward and the cue ball goes the distance, it has the speed, it bounces off, sends the eight ball into the corner… and the cue ball follows. Scratch on the eight ball. “What luck!” she says as she throws her hair back and slaps the end of the table. Looking over at me while I try to compress my smile to a bewildered ‘wow, what luck of mine’ look, but she doesn’t buy it. “So what’s your name cowboy?” she says now with a lower tone, getting closer to me, “Another two beers Al,” she shoots at the bar tender, dropping an Alexander Jackson for his troubles. “Jesse. And yours?” I ask trying to keep my focus on her face and not her breasts. “Ashley, but my friends call me Ash,” she says back to me as she turns and hands me a beer, “So where you from?” That question was one I was hoping not to have to answer. Small talk isn’t my suit, and I know it only leads to more small talk, so I set my beer down, grab her beer from her hand and set it down aside my and take her hand. “Let’s dance.” Taking the floor we stole the night, everybody had their eyes on us, and I made it a point to prove to her that I wasn’t just another guy looking to sweep her off her feet and put her in my bed tonight. Though the thought did linger in my mind, I wasn’t going to jump the gun on this one, too many risks with the uncertainty of if I’d ever see her again after tonight. A few hours later we hear Al the bartender call out, “Last call!” and I know it’s time to wrap this gig up. “So let me ask you something,” I start out, she looks at me waiting for my next words, “can I get your number?” That smile comes back and I can’t resist but return the gesture, maybe it was the booze, but I felt like I was walking on clouds right then. She asked Al for a marker and took my hand, flipped it over, and wrote below the knuckles [detail withheld], then put Ash above it. Walking out of the bar that night I stopped to enjoy my only smoke for the night. It was well deserved and much enjoyed at this point. Seeing her one last time as she walked out, she winked in my direction, and I gave her one last smile under all my haggardness. Around the corner she disappears and I hear the roar of an engine. Curious of where this familiar noise is coming from, I walk up to the corner and see her putting on a helmet while she seats herself on a beautiful black with red outlined Harley Davidson. Dropping the butte of my smoked cigarette, I stare on as she roars the engine once more and then speeds off. What a woman. ****** Sitting in my hotel room I contemplate whether or not I should call the number that’s still as clear as day on my hand. If I call now I may seem to eager, hmmm, if I wait she may move on. Decisions… I flip my cell phone open and stare at the background, a wrestling ring. Should I be so worried about a woman when my career hopes of being in the greatest wrestling company to date is on the line? When would I get a chance like this one with PRIME again? Women were a dime a dozen, or so to a wrestler they are. You could throw your name out to any woman that was a wrestling fan and they’d jump on you in a heart beat if you had the body and sometimes, the credentials. Depends on whether they’re picky or not. Standing up and pacing the room in eagerness to make a decision, I cannot make up my mind. Call or not call, do or don’t, what the hell was so hard about this? I’ve faced men like Seymour Almasy, a proven legend in the ring and my nerves were never off the fritz like this. Hell if I could go toe to toe with Global Champions and take plunges from fifteen or twenty feet, then what was the big deal over a phone call? Shaking it off, I decided I had to. This couldn’t be the way I’d be remembered from a harlot like her. Dialing the number finally, 5-5-5-1-5-3-2, I hit call and wait for an answer. Riiiiiiiiiiiinnnnnggggggg. Riiiiiiiiiiiinnnnnggggggg. Riiiiiiiiiiiinnnnnggggggg. Riiiiiiiiiiiinnnnnggggggg. The voice mail kicks in. “Hey, this is Ash! I can’t pick up right now but leave a message, your name, and number and I’ll get back to you as soon as I can! Alright, bye.” Beep. “Hey Ash, this is Jess from last night just calling in to see if you made it home alright. Thought I’d get a hold of you before too late. Get back at me, the numbers five-five-five-eighty-five-hundred,” I say into the phone, hoping not to sound desperate but at this point I figure it won’t matter. I’ll be lucky enough if she returns my call, but looking on the bright side, at least she didn’t throw me a fake number. Plopping down on the couch, I take a look at my watch, plenty of time to relax. Switch on the tube, see what’s on… News… News… News… Damn it, isn’t anything good on TV anymore! Flicking through the channels, news every where, I can’t get a break- “What the…?” Turning the volume up with the remote I lean to the edge of the couch and watch the TV with my utmost focus. The reporter explains an accident that happened the night before around 2 A.M. in the morning to 3 or 3:30-ish. ”The woman riding this motorcycle took a sharp turn going around the bend and was caught off guard when the thirty-five year old Daniel Howard, coming home from the bar last night, hit her as he blew a red light. She is currently at Saint James’ Hospital in critical care, though believed to make a full recovery…” I was gone before the words recovery were spoken, grabbing my jacket and out the door, forgetting to lock it or even turn off the TV. I don’t know what had got into me but... I felt something, something so deep in my veins it might-as-well be blood. It was real, and real was hard to find in the life I lived. There was so many unasked and unanswered questions still, and yet I was running down the stairs of the hotel I was staying at, I didn’t care about any of that, I just had to make sure she was okay. Rushing to the street, I pushed two people aside, in haste, I swear, but they didn’t take much notice to my apologies. Throwing my hand up, I whistle for a Taxi, and one arrives, ahhhh, speedy service for a change. Hard to find a man who actually likes to work for his living, and to my surprise, it wasn’t a man behind the wheel of the Taxi, but instead a large black woman who had her hair corn-rowed. “Where-to?” she shot back at me while eyeing me up in the mirror. “Saint James’ Hospital! Get me there in less than ten minutes and theirs a fifty in it for you!” I reply while trying to gain my breath. “You got it!” she claims and with that the pedal is put to the floor and we’re off. ****** Arriving at the hospital, I rush the doors and run up to the first hospital employee I see with a name tag, “Cherri” is her name, and that’s how I address her. “Cherri, miss, I need to find an Ashley that was brought here, she was in a motorcycle accident!” I spit out all jumbled up, my emotions getting the better of me in my reluctance to seeing her. She points through the doors and says “Room 132-B.” I sprint through the doors not waiting to hear the letter. I figure it’s not that important, the room is only divided in two sections. As I run down the hallway, my heart races with memories of being in this position before, and it’s obvious to me that I could have prevented this from happening. Every time I’ve been to a hospital it was on somebody else’s behalf but because of something I had done. I didn’t want this to be another occasion like those, I couldn’t let it be, and I wouldn’t. Rushing up to Room 132 I look at the letters and see it says A-B-C above it, okay, so I was right, the letter wasn’t important. I go to open the door, but the Doctor in the room opens up it before I can enter and looks up from his charts to see my face. “Family?” he shoots at me, and I show a disgruntled look as I know he sees when I do so, and then lie through my teeth, “Yes.” Not bothering to check for identification, he opens the door up and lets me walk in, awwww, the smell of hospital rooms, one of the few smells I could live without. Walking around two beds I finally come up to Ashley’s. Even in her unconscious state she’s as beautiful as a phoenix radiating it’s colorful aura. The doctor walks up behind me, placing a hand on my back, and keeping the clipboard out of sight. “She’ll be okay, but we don’t know when she’ll wake up. You should wait for her, nobody else has showed up yet,” he says, but his words become whispers as I gaze at her and sit down in a chair next to her bed. Leaving us alone, the doctor leaves, and I take hold of her hand, cupping it between my much more rugged ones. “I… What do I say to you?” and all at once I’m stunned for words. Whatever the reason, I’m unable to say anything. Though I know when she wakes, something will have to be said, and until then… I’ll wait. Is it a curse of mine to live a life alone? For almost ten years I’ve suffered from heart break and heart ache, never getting too close anymore for the fear of letting Pandora out of her box and making the one’s I love pay for it. This woman, Ashley, she was somebody so alive, so bright, and so new to me… I had barely known her for but a few hours, but she already stole a piece of me away. What was I to say but that maybe the curse was real? Maybe I was ridden to live a life of sorrow for the sins I’d committed. That’s why I quit on faith before… was I wrong to do so? Was this a message to find God? Or was I trying to hard to make this connect to my life? The answers even I won’t know, not until she wakes up that is. So much to find out, so much to think about, and so much ahead of me, what will happen? I’m not sure… We’ll just have to wait and see.
