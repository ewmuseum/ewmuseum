A Work in Progress

4 July 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 4, 2005 This is my life splattered across the page. Self-indulgent and egotistical. An exercise in self-expression (read: futility). Jackson Pollack, eat your heart out. Right off the bat, though, let me tell you: this writing thing is harder than it looks. Sitting here, staring at the blank notebook sitting in front of me, I think that perhaps I may be in over my head. I wonder if this is how God felt before he let there be light. I wonder if when he was finished with creation, he was embarrassed of how short it fell of his expectations and shoved it in the back of a drawer somewhere, never to set his heavenly gaze upon it again. I can’t say I wouldn’t sympathize with him. I have definitely been there before. I'm not just talking about writing here... It's more of an impulse than anything, what's making me sit down here at the kitchen table in the middle of the night. I remembered back when Gwen and I were in couples therapy, how that quack of a doctor encouraged us to write down our thoughts in order to better help sort them out. It sounded so stupid. Actually, it still does...but my neurons are firing on overdrive and at this point, I'm ready to resort to anything. So....here we go.... * * * I've been back in the states for a little over two weeks now. When you're living here, when you've grown up here not knowing that there was anything different out there, you take a lot of things for granted. People on the street speaking your fucking language is obviously a big one, but there are a lot of little things, too. Like the other day, I nearly walked off the curb smack into the grill of an incoming car. They drive on the other side of the street here, which before I thought was the 'right' way but now strikes me as the 'wrong way'; where before, I thought Japan's was the 'wrong' way and now it's the 'right' way. Funny how things change. All this talk of right and wrong, though, makes me think that maybe nobody is right. Or wrong. While I'm sure a lot of ethicists (and politicians who style themselves as such) might disagree with me there, I don't know what they'd be able to tell me that would change my mind. There's so much relativity in the world, so many people with opposing views that think they're right, people believing utterly in each extreme, with supporters of a million shades of grey lying in between. How the hell is anyone supposed to sort it all out? I guess that's how I validate my present behavior. No matter how you think you should behave, there's always someone that's going to condemn you for it...so fuck them, who cares? You need to do what you need to do to get ahead. It's as simple as that, yeah? Gwen would laugh if she could hear me now. She'd call me a fucking 'armchair philosopher', before going on to pick my theory to shreds. She was always good at that. I'd think I had the whole world figured out and then she'd go around and stir up all the contents of my head until my vision of order and enlightenment made absolutely no sense at all. I've always had a thing for smart girls, but I think that maybe Gwen was too smart for me. She was the only one who told me not to go to Japan, thirteen months ago. She told me that I was just running away from my problems instead of trying to fix them. What she didn't tell me was that I needed to stay for her sake. Neither of us brought it up, but though it wasn't said aloud, it definitely became the underlying subtext of those last few weeks. In the end, I went anyway. But now, sitting here in my suburban American home, just like the many nights I spent sitting in Japanese hotel rooms, I think about how much I miss her. And even moreso how I miss my daughter... * * * The more I think about it, the more ridiculous I feel, sitting here, writing down my thoughts. What would the smarks think if they found out that hard-assed Seth Masters kept a journal? I should....I dunno...have better control over my emotions or something. Especially at this stage of my career, teetering as I am on the edge of my newest venture toward greatness. Crutches are for the weak, and like it or not, this is a crutch. And then that makes me think that maybe this whole persona I've created for myself, maybe that's just another form of a crutch. One I use to keep myself from falling into the same pitfalls of before. Placing myself in this established role, with established traits and reactions, and letting life play itself out. I step back and take a look at myself, and I think: how archetypical. The golden boy, loved by all, finds success only to become jaded and turn his back on the world. The whole looking out for number one thing, it's been done over and over again. How cliche, yet...I can't help it. Our lives are stories, plagiarized all. You can find these stories just about anywhere, if you bother looking hard enough. All it takes is someone to put the effort into teasing it out, taking the thread and weaving it through this event and that one until you start to see a pattern form. The media has been doing this for years You just have to be careful. When you’re arbitrarily picking and choosing the pieces that will pile up on the cutting room floor, you run the risk of creating an unfair bias. When it’s your own life you’re editing, you play a dangerous game with delusions. Even so, there’s something satisfying about cutting away all the superfluous bullshit to get at this core of meaning. Meaning! In life! How often does one stumble upon such a precious treasure? This is why I love storytelling, for the fleeting glimpses of meaning it bestows upon us in an otherwise nonsensical world. Just what the meaning of my story is….well, I suppose it's one part anti-hero, one-part deadbeat dad, one-part warrior in search of renoun. Then again, it's hard to label a story that's nowhere near its end. You never know when the focus might change. Whole plotlines have the potential to be blotted out on a whim. So at the moment I guess it’s what you’d call a work in progress. * * * The last time I saw my daughter, it was two days before I left for Japan. I think it was raining that day, but then again, I wouldn't be surprised if that's just my revisionist memory at work. I remember coming home from the gym, like I did every day, thinking about how I would break the news to Gwen. The flight was booked already, yeah, but that didn't mean I wasn't planning on telling her I was leaving. I just...didn't know how. When I walked through the door, I saw her sitting on the couch with her arms folded, as if she had been prepared for my entrance. Before a question could even form in my mind, I saw my plane ticket sitting on the coffee table. What would you do, if your child's father had booked a flight to Japan leaving in two days and hadn't spoken a word about it? What would think if, staring up at you from the ticket, you saw the words 'ONE WAY'. She had packed my bags for me, and they were laid out a few steps away from the coffee table. I could have explained myself, I could have attempted to remedy the situation, but seeing the look on her face, a mixture of bitter anger and sadness, I simply froze up. I let her usher me to the door, screaming insults all the way. I let her slap away at me, knowing I deserved it and not raising a hand in defense. And when she told me I was a miserable excuse for a father, I looked up the staircase and saw Renee, my sweet Renee, three years old and tears streaming down her face. God, just thinking about it makes my heart break all over again. I wonder if she knew what was happening as she saw her daddy walk out that door and out of her life, without even a goodbye. I can only imagine what Gwen told her. I stayed that night and the next at Trev and Shanda's place. On the second day we caught our plane to Japan. This was all thirteen months ago. I haven't talked to either Gwen or Renee since. What the fuck is the matter with me? * * * I want to concentrate on my training, but it's hard when you can't get a full night's sleep. There just seem to be way more important things to which one can devote their thoughts. They want me to appear on TV Wednesday night and be ready spout some drivel about how little I think of my opponents. Like I care about a washed-up prick like Gaz Green or a bush-leaguer like Brown. I mean, so what if the Invincible Gabe pulled the OSE belts out of a dumpster somewhere, and so what if the Jester was riding the wave during the dark years of PCW. Neither of those things means shit to me. All their accomplishments are trivialized in my eyes. Then again, I have to realize that although these guys may not be serious challengers, when the big opponents do come around, I'm gonna want to be ready for them. I should work on gaining momentum, on making a name for myself, on climbing up by stepping on the heads of those beneath me. Yet I can't. Not when I think about how I've been back for two weeks and haven't been able to muster up the courage to see my family, to face Gwen's chastising eyes, to have to explain my year-long absence to Renee. Could a four-year old possibly understand the complexity of my motivations. At times, even I don't think I understand them. There's always the chance they won't even want me back in their lives. And if they don't, can I really blame them? Who abandoned who, here? And so I sit. And so I think. Alone. * * * There are lessons to be found in all of this, I’m sure. Just what they are, though, I couldn’t tell you.
