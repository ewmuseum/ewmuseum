The country that holds us down...

25 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/25/05 Respect: what Walczak is going after, and at Tropical Turmoil that is what he hopes to achieve. Envy: a feeling that other people have towards Walczak. They want to be like him, and they know that it is an unattainable goal, and thus they are jealous. Suffering: what Walczak has gone through most of his life, and he does not want to suffer anymore. Pain: what Dave Gibson and Kyle Lamen will feel when Walczak takes them through hell at Tropical Turmoil Eagerness: the anticipation that Walczak has to his match with Gibson and Lamen. Cocky: the way that Walczak feels about his match. He knows that he cannot lose. He knows that he and Blackwolf will prevail, and he is not shy about saying it. Turmoil: The very event where all of this will take place. Walczak will leave behind previous ideas of who he is and go on towards dominance yet again….. We see a massive man walking down 52nd St. This man is none other than current PRIME superstar Tom Walczak. Walczak is walking at a leisurely pace, and is dressed in a pair of jean shorts and a wife beater on. He is not wearing his trademark do-rag revealing his bald head. A very sinister look is seen across his face, as he makes his way down the street. What a good day. I have not seen to many good days since I have been in the United States, but this is one of those days. Walczak does look unusually happy. When we look at his face a second time we realize that his look is not so much sinister, but does look more like joy. All is right with the world. Almost everything has gone right these past couple of weeks, and once Tropical Turmoil comes around the plan that I have set into motion will come to fruition. This could not have happened any better than it did. I originally just wanted to take out Dave Gibson, a man that truly sickens me. As Walczak starts to talk about Gibson the look of joy is no longer on his face. The look is now one of disgust. His nostrils start to flare and he starts to breathe harder as the thoughts of Gibson run through his head. I tried to give Kyle Lamen a chance to join my cause. I liked the spirit that the kid had, and I thought that he would realize the opportunity that it would provide for him. This would give him a chance to work with a former World Champion. Well, needless to say once he didn’t accept my invitation I was not going to let him get off scott-free, but I didn’t think that I would have a chance to get back at him so quickly. However, the little punk takes it upon himself to get caught up in the middle of things and goes ahead and signs his own death warrant. Walczak’s face is still a sight of anger and disgust as he describes his past encounters with Lamen, but he does seem to stop and calm himself down. There is no need to get angry He starts to breathe a little bit easier and a smile appears on his face once more. There is no reason to dwell on the thoughts of these two pieces of filth. Come Sunday they will be all mine, and I will have my way with them. Today is too nice a day to get stuck on these two instead it is better to revel in the genius of my plan and just relax. Walczak continues on his walk. He pays no attention to anyone as he passes, and he seems to be in his own little world. However, just because he is not paying attention to the people around him does not mean that the people are not paying attention to him. He is stared at by all different types of people, but none of them draw his attention. An attractive brunette walks by Walczak and tries to get his attention by hiking up her little red dress just a little bit, but to no avail he just stays on his path and is not distracted. However, as he walks past an electronics store further down the street something does catch his attention. A television on window display is running last weeks edition of Revolution, and none other than Angelo Deville is on the screen. Walczak can barely hear it through the window, but he knows exactly what Deville is saying. “And while I'm on the subject of insignificant people getting out of line, how can I ignore Ballsack and Blackwolf? If you guys want to make a name for yourselves, the way to go about it isn't attacking someone like Gibson backstage. As you two goons may or may not know, this is wrestling, and here in wrestling, we conduct our business in the ring. I know that might sound novel and confusing to you, but trust me, it's true.” The look of fury once again appears on Walczak’s face this time more intense than the last. He makes a fist and looks as if he is going to punch through the glass of the storefront, but he manages to hold himself back. Who does this guy think he is? I had no trouble with him before this, but he just crossed the line with these statements. This time it looks as if the fury in his face will not disappear. He gets angrier and angrier as he stares into the face of Deville. It is this type of arrogance that I cannot stand, but why get stuck on this he is not even my problem. Shockingly the look on his face does indeed seem to disappear, and while the joy does not appear once more he does seem to be more calm. Just then someone taps Walczak on the shoulder. When Walczak turns around to see who it is he sees nothing more than a disheveled man, and assumes that the man is homeless. The man’s clothes are tattered, and he is wearing a flannel shirt in the summer heat. His beard and hair are long and unkempt, and the general appearance of the man disgusts Walczak. “Change?” “Sorry buddy I don’t have anything on me.” “I know who you are. You have lots of money can’t you spare anything?” “I don’t have my wallet on me, and plus I don’t just give out money people have to work to get there money I don’t like beggars.” “Neither do I, it’s just that this world has never given me a fair chance.” Walczak is intrigued by the last statement, and he stays to talk to the man instead of just walking away. “What do you mean?” “Well, my parent’s were immigrants from Poland and they came over to this country to try and make a better life for me and my brother. I don’t really remember how it was in Poland, but it can’t be worse than the life I had here, and now I can’t even go back because I don’t have the money.” “Yeah, it’s the same old song. This country loves to keep immigrants down. It hurts even more because it happened to a fellow countryman. ” “Yeah, I want to go back there and maybe find my family. I just want a better life.” “How, did your life go down the drain?” “My family started off all right, both of my parents found jobs rather easily, and we were able to find an apartment in the city. My dad saved up enough money to open up his own Polish Restaurant, and we were actually pretty wealthy at one point, but then the politics of this country came in.” Walczak seems angered by this statement. The story sounds so much similar to his own that it hurts him. “I know the story all to well.” “They closed my dad’s restaurant based on bogus tax charges, and they foreclosed our home. We were forced to live in the street in the dead of winter. My younger brother caught pneumonia, but no doctor would treat him and he died at only six years old.” The man’s eyes begin to tear up as he tells the story, and Walczak puts his hand on the man’s shoulder to try and console him. “After the loss of my brother my life continued to get worse. About two months after the death of my brother I was walking around the city with my parents, and we were particularly happy on this day because my father had just received a job. Then as we were crossing the street my parents were hit by a car. I figured the man was drunk, but I didn’t know for sure. The last thing my dad did was push me out of the way, and saved my life. The driver just drove away and was never found because there were no witnesses in the area.” The tears that have been swelling up in the man’s eyes start to fall down his cheek. The man’s dirt covered face looks smudged and pathetic as the tears make there way down his face. “I can’t believe that story man. That’s horrible. It just angers me even more that this country allows things like this to happen.” “They didn’t take care of me at all and I have been roaming the streets by myself for the past fifteen years. I have come close to death many times, but I feel that if I give up without a fight that my parents’ lives will have gone away for nothing. My dad saved my life, and I live now only for him.” Walczak seems to feel sorry for the man, and a rare look of compassion comes across the face of Walczak. “This is what truly angers me about this country. If they do not like you they do not give you a chance. You make your own chance and then everything that you have earned they take away.” Walczak grabs the man around the shoulder and brings him in closely. It looks as if it is Walczak’s best attempt at a hug, but to the man it is nothing more than pain as he grimaces in Walczak’s clench. “What did you say your name was?” The man slowly backs away from the monstrous grip of Walczak and catches his breath. “My name is Maciej Solak. As far as I know my parents were from Krakow, but I do not remember my childhood in Poland. I do not know if I have any family left in Poland because my parents did not remain in close contact with our relatives there.” “I wish that I knew where my family was.” A look of sadness comes over Walczak’s face. Almost as if he realizes that he is vulnerable he speaks again in a more defiant tone. “But those rat bastards left me for dead years ago. They are just as bad as these Americans.” “Well, my family was a little different. They truly did care for me, and they wanted me to have a good life. That was why they came to this country. They left against their families wishes and this is why they subsequently lost contact with them.” “If they had only stayed. If they listened to those who were wiser in your family you would not be faced with the problems that you have today.” “I wanted to go back after my brother died. I did everything in my power to convince my parents to go back to Poland, but they did not want to face their families with failure but with success, and their own will to succeed is what kept them here….” Walczak looks at the man who seems to be angry. Maciej is holding back tears and clenching his fists. His body is completely tense and he cannot speak. “It is alright my friend. I am not like the people of this country. I want to help all the people succeed in whatever they want, and for that reason I want to help you.” A look of awe comes across the man’s face. “What do you mean?” “I want to send you to Poland, and help you find your family. I know someone there who can help you out, and you should find your family in no time.” “You have got to be kidding me.” “It is not your fault that you live the life you now do. For nearly the past twenty years you have been held down by this country, and I will not stand for that.” Walczak makes a fist and punches a nearby brick wall. He pulls his hand away and his knuckles are bleeding. “I have been held down by this country not once, but I will not let them do it to anyone else.” Maciej has no words to say to Walczak. His body is no longer tense and he latches on to Walczak. “It’s ok. We’re gonna get you set up, and pretty soon you will be back in a great country.” Walczak starts to walk away and is followed by Maciej. Maciej still does not speak, but just stares at Walczak with shock and joy. For the first time in a long time we see the compassionate side of the Polish Punisher, but with this compassion comes an even greater hate of “The land of the Free”. What Walczak does next only he knows, but it will not be for the good of the USA.
