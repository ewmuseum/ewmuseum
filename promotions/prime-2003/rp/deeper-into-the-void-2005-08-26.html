Deeper Into the Void

26 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 26, 2005 The room is dark, so dark that at first you can’t make out exactly what it is you’re looking at. There’s a form ahead of you, that much is clear. Judging solely from it’s piston-like motion, repetitiously thrusting itself out again and again with flawless precision, your first thought might be that you’re gazing at some sort of machine. The sharp exhalation of breath in sync with each thrust gives it away, though. The form that you’re looking at, it’s all too human. Embrace the void….everything is nothingness… Your eyes begin to adjust to the absence of lighting, but there are more immediate impressions at hand. The air in the room is hot, beyond uncomfortable, and it exhibits a quality of staleness one thought could not be found outside the realm of reality television. A faint dripping can be heard now and again, providing an erratic contrast to the mechanical backdrop of educated breathing. And the smell…well, the smell is all too human as well. There is no me…there is no weight… The room, it reeks of sweat, and as your eyes finally focus to the darkness it’s easy to see why. Though you can’t have been staring at him for more than a minute, the man in front of you has been lifting weights for what now seems like forever,. Perspiration is flowing freely, seemingly from every pore of his body. Rivulets cascade down his face, his shoulders, his arms, forming temporary pools on the bench before taking the long plunge to the floor: the source of the dripping sound you noticed earlier. His black tank top is saturated, clinging to straining muscles which never cease for an instant. Their motion is perpetual, as if trapped in an infinite loop of bench presses. There are no muscles…there is no pain… He stares straight up, his gaze belying a deep concentration, yet focused on nothing in particular. Between reps, there isn’t even the slightest pause. The fluidity of the motion, it’s almost poetic. Almost… Because the longer one looks, the less poetic the scene becomes…until it begins to take on an air of the grotesque. You realize that the human body, it’s not built to function in such a manner. With the amount of weight on the bar, it’s impressive that the man can lift it at all. To carry with the routine at such length, with no sign of fatigue, no show that it is having any effect on him at all save the sweat pouring from his body, it’s simply unbelievable…and disturbingly unnatural. Everything is nothingness. There is only…the Way. Seth Masters is all too human, yes, but from the looks of things it appears as if he’s striving to become something far more. Or far less… If to err is human, and to forgive is divine, what realm of existence does that leave to one driven by hate? ===================================================== Trevor Hawke never regretted becoming involved with Shanda Russell. Looking at her now, silky black hair falling in waves over her shoulders, gazing back at him with a wry smile forming on her lips, what was there to regret? A lot of guys in the OSW locker room had had their eye on the alluring interviewess, and for good reason. She was a woman of the type you don’t find often in the business of professional wrestling. She was professional…respectable…intelligent…and damn good-looking on top of it all. So naturally, Trevor considered himself a lucky man. But ever since she got accepted to the Masters program at the Chicago School of Professional Psychology, he’d be damned if she couldn’t get under his skin at times. “It looks like something’s bothering you,” she says, regarding him with a warm, yet concerned smile. “Why don’t you tell me what’s on your mind?” “What’s there to talk about? I’m fine.” Though trying hard, he can’t seem to completely eradicate the defensive tone from his voice…and seeing Shanda roll her eyes, Trevor immediately realizes he’s gotten himself into trouble. The woman, she can smell blood, and as gentle as her tone is, he can’t shake the feeling that there’s something jagged, something shark-like in her probing. Getting up from the couch, she walks over to him, beginning to circle around him where he stands. “I know fine, and what you are now is far from it. Something’s bothering you,” Looking at her, he braces himself. It’s times like these he can almost imagine the dorsal fin sprouting from her back. Stopping in front of him, she raises a mischievous eyebrow, letting out a laugh as she gives him a knowing shake of the head. “You know, you’re just like Seth.” “What do you mean by that?” Trevor shifts uncomfortably under her penetrating gaze, moving his weight from one leg to another, uncrossing his arms and then crossing them once more. “What do I mean?” she repeats pointedly, before beginning to pace around him again. She takes her time before answering, letting him stew for awhile as she moves behind him. “I mean the way you bottle up your emotions all the time.” He stares forward vacantly without an answer as Shanda places a hand on his bare shoulder. Leaning forward, she whispers into his ear. “It’s not healthy.” Lowering her hand, she hugs him from behind. He winces slightly in pain, his ribs still encased in surgical tape, having been injured by the monstrous spear he received from Rampage on Revolution 73. Yet Shanda’s embrace is a gentle one, and the pain quickly subsides. He can feel the silky material of her blouse pressing up against his back, sending a shiver down his spine. “I’m not like Seth,” he states, after many a moment of silence has passed. “Seth…he’s got problems I don’t know how to even begin to deal with.” “Now what exactly do you mean by that?” Shanda asks, echoing Trevor’s earlier question to her. Removing her arms from around her chest, two quick steps take her around to his front side again, where she looks him in the eyes with a revitalized interest. His response comes slowly, uncertain, almost against his will. “I just feel like lately we’ve been…I dunno…drifting apart.” “Really?” she says with surprise, more to herself than to him. She pauses for a second, looking slightly downward, absorbed in thought. Looking up again, she throws another question his way, and this time the concern in her voice is more emphasized. “Do you think you’re going to have to break up the team?” “No, no, I don’t think the team’s in jeopardy at all,” he says, after letting out a weak laugh. Again he shuffles his feet uncomfortably. “Maybe drifting apart isn’t what I meant. It’s more like…we’re on different wavelengths. Does that make sense?” “Maybe…” Shanda begins, slowly, looking confused. “Want to give me a little more?” “It’s just…well it’s hard to put into words.” “Try.” “Well, alright,” Trevor agrees, albeit apprehensively. A comfortable silence hangs in the air as he thinks of the right words to put to his thought. “I guess the past couple of weeks…ever since we got back from Japan, really…he’s just been acting distant as hell. And at first, yeah, I thought maybe it would affect his performance in the ring, that the team would suffer. But if anything it’s the exact opposite. He puts too much of himself out there every night. It’s like wrestling is the only thing he’s living for.” “But don’t you feel the same way?” Shanda asks. “Don’t you give it your all every night, too? Not to be difficult, I’m just not getting why his behavior is a cause for concern.” “Well yeah, I love wrestling. But it’s not life or death for me out there.” The side of Trevor’s mouth screws up in an uneasy expression. “For Seth, I think that’s the way it’s starting to become.” “Life or death?” “See, it’s like this. For me, at the end of the night, I can just let it all go. I can let a loss slide. I can occupy my mind with other things. Not to mention, I’ve got you to come home to.” “But of course,” Shanda replies, with an affectionate grin. “I don’t dwell on things because I have other outlets,” Trevor continues, not pausing to return her flirtatious gaze. “But Seth, he doesn’t have any of that. The whole Gwen thing is still tearing him up, as far as I know. He’s got no real friends left in America, outside of me…who he doesn’t even seem interested in talking to outside of work. So wrestling, it’s all he’s got going for him at the moment.” “Hmm,” Shanda raises her hand to her mouth in a considering expression. “Maybe I should talk to him…” “Trust me, that’s the last thing you should do.” “Why not?” “You don’t know Seth like I do. You definitely don’t want to be around him after a big loss. I’m sure that hasn’t changed. In fact, I’d be willing to bet he’s beating himself up over last week as we speak.” “Well maybe you should be the one to talk to him about his problems,” she replies calmly. “You do have a big match against P.W.A. coming up this Sunday. They’re definitely a team you guys are going to need to be on the same page going up against. “Fuck P.W.A.” Trevor spits disgustedly. Shanda’s gaze is pleading though, and it isn’t long before it yet again succeeds in disarming him. Rolling his eyes, he lets out a deep sigh. “Like always, you do have a completely valid point.” It’s all he can do to prevent himself from rolling his eyes again as he sees the look of satisfaction appear on her face. “Alright, I’ll go talk to him.” “You’re a good friend,” Shanda says with a smile. Walking over to him, she kisses him lightly on the cheek, before wrapping her arms around his head. “I’m just glad we’re alone,” Trevor smiles back. “This kind of thing, it could really kill my heel image.” ===================================================== What manner of demon has entered the body of one Seth Masters, pushing him to the outer limits of human exertion? “A man possessed” doesn’t describe his performance by half. Now working the heavy bag, both the speed and intensity of his punches are startling. The force of each blow connecting with the bag lets out a violent thump, but a sound of even more note is the whizzing of his fist cutting through the air en route to impact. One can imagine the atmosphere is parting in fear before the onslaught of Seth’s sadistic hand. Completely absorbed in his workout, Seth is in another world. He ducks, dodges, and sidesteps the attacks of an imaginary opponent before lashing out to strike the back once more with relentless ferocity. He moves with animal instinct, with mechanical precision, a focused essence stripped of all conscious thought. In fact, Seth is so immersed in this state that it is nearly a minute before he becomes aware of another presence in the room with him. “Your front door was open, so I let myself in,” says Trevor Hawke, walking across the room casually. “I didn’t know you were coming over,” Seth says emotionlessly. Stepping away from the heavy bag and stretching out his shoulders, he quickly reels off two more punches. He turns again to face Trevor with the most cursory of acknowledgements, before turning away and firing off two more punches. “Ever heard of knocking.” “I figured a more direct approach was needed,” Trevor replies, undaunted. Reaching the far end of the room, he leans up against the wall, crossing his arms as he regards his partner. “You’ve been anything but a social butterfly lately, you know.” “There’s a reason for that, you know,” Seth jabs back, snapping off the gloves he’s wearing on both hands and tossing them to floor. “So what, you have a problem with me or something?” Trevor asks with irritation, stepping in front of his partner to make sure the other man looks him in the eye. “Are you pissed because I lost the match last week? If there’s anything wrong between us, just tell me, man. “I have no problem with you. And it’s as much my fault that we lost that match as it was yours” Seth says, placing a comforting hand on Trevor’s shoulder…before using it to move him aside so he can continue walking past.. “So what gives? Are you ok?” Trevor asks, walking along behind his partner. Seth stops as he reaches the bench propped up along the western wall, grabbing one of over a dozen bottles of water which cover its seat. “Why do you ask?” Seth inquires, before emptying the first bottle into his mouth “Because…Christ…I’ve never seen you like this before.” “What do you mean?” Seth looks up at his teammate, incensed. “That you’ve never seen this kind of focus out of me? This kind of intensity?” “Hey, don’t take it the wrong way, now...” “Trevor, what was the fucking pledge we made when we decided to come to PRIME? What was the pledge?” “I…” “No….Fucking…Mercy!” Seth bellows, thumping his chest with his fist as he spits out each word. “That was the one condition I agreed to when you asked me to come back and wrestle in America. That we would show no fucking mercy. You don’t remember that?” “No…I do.” “And have we been following through with it? Have we been making good on our words?” “Well, I guess it’s a matter of perspective...right?” “Like hell it is!” Seth shouts. “Have we dismantled everyone who’s stood in our way? Have we left a slew of broken bodies in our wake? Have we struck fear into the hearts of our opponents each and every time they’ve seen their names across from ours on the marquee?” Trevor can’t answer, the violent outburst from his partner having shocked him into an abashed silence. Seth continues, now having whipped himself up into a fervor. “No! We’ve been in PRIME for almost two months now and where has it gotten us? Nowhere! We’re fucking nowhere! This Sunday night, we’re wrestling a pointless match against P.W.A. on PPV, a team whose asses we’ve already kicked on free TV, while Teh Pwn, Team Annoying V. 3, is taking our title shot, is going to win our belts when they go up against Los Diablos. “This wasn’t how things were supposed to turn out. Scott Matthews, Vangellus Oxios, these guys, they’re nobodies! Bush-leaguers! And they came away with a clean victory over us? Over Civil Disobedience? Are we really the has-beens that everybody keeps trying to label us?” Trevor stands there, staring at his partner, speechless. Looking at Seth, at the crazed intensity that has taken over his visage, the competitive insanity that has usurped his mind from all rational thought. Not knowing what else to do, he grips Masters by his shoulders and stares at him, face to face. “Hey man, it’s okay. We can’t win them all. You don’t have to kill yourself over it.” “I don’t?” Seth’s voice is filled with cold disbelief, his face a mask of disgust. Yet he doesn’t pull away, he looks Trevor in the eye, delivering his words with a measured intensity that removes all doubt of insanity. “You may be able to take these embarrassments in stride, but I refuse to. I’m no longer going to stand for it. I need to do something to change our situation here, so we can escape from this quagmire of mediocrity. And I’m willing to do whatever it takes to get the results I desire.” He is terrified, that he is not ashamed to admit. Anyone looking into the eyes of Seth Masters at that moment would feel the same. At this moment, Trevor no longer knows what to think. One thought is clear, though. He certainly would not want to be in the shoes of P.W.A. come this Sunday night. For he realizes that he is staring into the eyes of a man purged of all humanity, a veritable machine driven by hatred, with the potentiality to churn up and devour the world itself if left unchecked. Could he stop him? More importantly, would he stop him if he could? Because this new Seth Masters, he could very well be his ticket to success.
