Don't get crowded

2 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/2/06 “What are you doing?” The voice stopped Titan like a whipcrack. He paused, looking back over his shoulder at the source that had stopped him so suddenly. Profile 21 looked back at him, his face again utterly impassive and his hands folded behind his back. Titan looked out at the crowd ahead of him after the marked military man that was playing the role of an escaping fugitive. Titan glanced back out at the crowd, then took a step back. “Do you know why I stopped you, Titan?” Profile 21 asked him in that cold, completely unreadable voice. Titan could only give his head an imperceptible shake of his head. He had been told many times by this man to give away nothing in body language, to present only what you wanted seen in how you moved and behaved. He had been lectured to project only cool confidence, calm power and express only anger when it was of maximum importance. “I stopped you because you almost walked into that crowd, Titan,” Profile 21 said quietly. The shorter man always spoke softly and refused to repeat himself, so Titan had trained himself to listen very carefully. Profile 21 stood only about six feet tall, just a little above average, with dark hair that never seemed to move and black eyes that never showed emotion. His face could have almost been carved from marble, with the exception of the slight movement of his lips when he spoke. His eyes could easily be stone, though. “What you have to understand is that you are a target. You're a man of power in obviously military attire. And you never, ever know what waits for you in a crowd. Do not chase a man into a crowd, just let him come out so that you can again follow him and take him away from those that would interfere.” Titan shifted his eyes to the shorter man. “I wouldn't lose him if he moved into the crowd?” “You might,” he conceded. “However, if you've done your research, you won't. You'll be able to project where he'll go, how hostile he'll be and how quickly he'll give up. Do the research, Titan.” That was something the man never relented on. He repeatedly hammered home observations of small body language, he drilled into him methods for ferreting out psychological clues that could tell you something of how the target thought and would behave. Titan shifted his eyes back forward. “It's not just that someone might get in your way,” 21 said abruptly. “It's more than that. At least, it is here.” Those cold, ebony eyes scanned the crowd before him. “Here, Titan, you never know where someone's waiting to kill you. You never know when someone is making his way to the middle of a crowd to take out as many people as possible. You. Never. Know.” Titan suppressed the urge to sigh. Profile 21 was adamant in being aware of possible terrorist attacks. He never once missed an opportunity to point out when, where and how to be careful to avoid the possibility of an attack. The big man did not feel the same way as 21 did. “They're animals, Titan,” he said quietly, “never having a care for who they hurt or how, just as long as the numbers are high. But if you turn the same measures on them, they scream of violation of human rights and cruelty. They disgust me.” Titan couldn't take anymore. At this point, Profile 21 had been working with him for three months. “They are not,” he said, turning to face the shorter man. “Not all of them. For the most part, they're just regular people that just want to live their lives. Instead, it's a small minority that's guilty of what you say.” He glared down at the other man. Slowly, the head turned and those black, stone eyes regarded him coldly. “You believe that?” came the question. “I do.” “Good.” Titan couldn't help but slightly stiffen at the response. It was a shock that rocked him to his heels. 21 kept his eyes on him. “Yes, Titan, I said good. I was asked to train you to be like me, not to be me. Let me assure you, you do not want to be me. My life is absolutes, all of one, none of the other. Humanity is either innocent or scum and I have to boil people down to basically good or basically evil. Otherwise, I could not do what I am reknown for.” “And what is that?” came the giant's response. “Another time.” The head turned back to the crowd. Suddenly, his hand dropped to his side and he cleanly drew the firearm at his hip. The Glock barked once as a solid 9 millimeter bullet flew from the barrel and into the crowd. The air was suddenly electric with shouts as people dove for the ground. But one man lay on the ground, his arms at an awkward angle. His bearded face stared towards the sky with a blank gaze. His shirt was partially unbuttoned and strapped to him was a belt with two sticks of dynamite. The shorter man looked at Titan again. “Bang, Titan,” he said softly. “You're dead.”
