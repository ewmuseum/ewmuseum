'Stuck in the middle with you...' (ReV 92 RP)

17 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17/04/06 We begin in black & white. Not for artistic reasons, but because the image we see of Adam is on the monitor of a shoddy CCTV system, the camera in question mounted in the corner of the elevator that’s stuck between floors in the cheap hotel he’s staying in. It’s 12:43am and he’d planned an early night, a few drinks at the bar down the street till about 11 then wander back to the hotel to get an early night – he’d been sitting with his back against the back wall of the lift for over and hour and a half now, and nothing had happened since the gears had ground to a halt. Stevie and Earl, the two security guards doing nights at the hotel, really didn’t have much else to do than watch the dozing Scot. That’s why the job paid so well – long hours of nothing happening. And you had to keep alert somehow – hence Earl’s $15 bet that ‘the big guy stuck in elevator C will snap by 2am’. Stevie vehemently denies this and has $20 on ‘the fat, sweaty guy and skinny, posh bitch in B getting it on by the time things start working again’. Their attention was divided between the two monitors where anything was likely to happen – elevator A had no-one in it when it stalled and D had been out of commission for weeks beforehand anyway. There was no point in watching the main door; no-one tried to get in or out after about midnight, and the corridors were gonna be empty – people that used this place were almost always here on business or with a hooker. Either way it was in bed by ten and out again at seven. Stevie: You’re wasting your time on that screen, man – I’m tellin’ ya; the guy’s asleep. Earl: Nah, he’s planning somethin’... I bet he’s just sittin’ there burning a hole in those doors with his eyes until... Bam! He’ll go batshit and start beating his head off the walls or somethin’. Popcorn? Stevie: Sure. He grabs a few kernels and starts munching, each man glued to his respective set waiting on something happening. There’s nothing but the krunch-krunch of the bored staff slowly emptying the popcorn tub. Stevie: Bet you ten bucks that John won’t be here by 1:30. Earl: Ehh... nah. I’m pretty sure of that anyway. Back in the lift and, sure enough, Adam is nearly asleep – not planning his violent rage-filled assault on the steel walls trapping him in. In fact, at just about 12:51am he drifts off to sleep, and then the fun begins... The doors effortlessly and silently slide open, showing about three feet of the third floor, the two-foot thick ceiling/floor between the two floors and the lowest three feet of the fourth floor. Adam’s standing in the centre the elevator, unphased by the sudden unsticking of the doors. He kneels down on one knee to inspect the gap and is just about to try and slide though it when he hears something to his left. With a brief look up he’s pleased to see Ignatius waiting for him. He stands up and turns to face him properly and strangely isn’t surprised to see another Ignatius, casually leaning against the panelled side. Adam: Hey guys. Ignatiuses: Alright. Adam: S’happening? One of the Ignatiuses, the more currently recognisable of the two – his hair more styled, his face brighter and cheerier – jumps in before the other has a chance. Ignatius #1: Not much, just heading back for a protein shake and some yoga, might head out for a run before bed. Lazybones here is probably just going to raid the mini-bar. He rolls his eyes after a quick nod to his other self, who’s looking more dishevelled; bags under his eyes and a definite spacey glaze to his eyes. The other Ignatius sneers slightly, but doesn’t look too bothered by the comment. Adam’s about to respond to the perkier of the two when a hand reaches down through the fourth floor’s gap, the crisp & bright Ignatius looks up and smiles, seeing the smiling face of Johnny Noble waiting for him above. Ignatius #1: I gotta split; Johnny & I, we’re running buddies, y’know? You can come if you want, but... He’s already half-way out, just pulling his feet through the gap by the time he trails off, never bothering to finish the sentence before he’s standing up on the fourth floor and walking off, chatting with the aging veteran. Adam: Ouch... The paler of the two Ignatiuses joins in now, peeling himself from the wall to join Adam in the middle of the lift. Ignatius #2: Meh, I’m not surprised; he’s been doing that a lot. Adam: Yeah, I’d noticed... The dejection is evident in Adam’s voice, his best friend from days gone by had moved on, it seemed. Ignatius #2: Don’t worry about it; we can hang out if you want? His mini-bar idea didn’t sound half-bad to me. Adam grins and nods. Ignatius #2: Technically... I’m not meant to drink on this medication, bu- He cuts short as a buzzing and ringing emanates from his cut-off shorts’ pocket. Immediately his almost happy-go-lucky expression drops clean off, leaving a cold, hard face in its place. He takes the phone out is pocket and flips it open. He gives Adam the ‘two seconds’ fingers and drops to the floor to roll out the lift, hopping down from the gap he carries on listening to whoever’s on the other end of the line without saying anything. Stevie: Well, that’s it 1:15 and I don’t see John buzzing at the door to get in and fix the elevators, d’you? Earl: Nope, I say we phone ’im again at half past. Stevie: Good call. Oh yeah, your guy flipped out yet? Earl: Not yet, but we got another 45 minutes before you can claim that fifteen bucks. Stevie: I hope they hang off fixing things for a bit longer, there’s definitely chemistry between my two. Earl: Shut the hell up. Stevie: No, seriously, just watch for a bit. Earl swivels in his seat to have a look at the other screen as we head back inside the head of a certain dreaming Scot. Ignatius’ footsteps have just faded into the inaudible, prompting Adam to bend down and check to see if he can see Ignatius along the corridor anywhere. Just before he sticks his head out for a better view a chorus of voices from behind him chimes in; Motokis: He’s long gone. Standing up to see two Toshiaki Motokis would scare the pants off most in PRIME, but right now it seemed perfectly logical to the Scot. The one standing toward the rear of the lift, who had just looked over his shoulder briefly for this last comment, returned to his phonecall and began ignoring Adam as he dealt with business, presumably. The closer of the two looked to Adam with a mischievous grin on his face. Motoki #2: You’re longing for things which aren’t quite as good as you remember they were, no? The scheming Asian businessman subtly smirks with this. Adam growls under his breath and looks to his former paymaster, the man he happily worked under for the promise of higher cardings and more power, which all-in-all just lead to the loss of any respect the crowd formerly had for the hard-working Scotsman. Adam: Remind me why you’re here? It’s with another sly grin that his reply is dished out. Motoki #2: To remind you why you are here. You decided to return to PRIME for a ridiculously low cut of your previous earnings, in fac- Adam cuts in; making sure his boss knows his views on the financial deal they’ve brokered for his return. Adam: Y’think the money worries me? You shoul... The past-Motoki dismissively waves Adam away, causing him to trail off. Motoki #2: Allow me to continue; you were only in the “Invasion” payroll because you were part of the Ignatius deal, without you I couldn’t have him – you think I really wanted a hired gun whose nickname was ‘The Eternal Underdog’? Adam’s fists clench tightly in anger as Motoki scoffs. Just as Adam’s about to lunge for the jugular Motoki quickly drops and rolls out the lift, down to the third floor where he proceeds to scuttle away, still chortling away to himself. Adam half-considers giving chase before the ‘click’ of a cellphone closing reminds him of the presence of the other incarnation of the PRIME co-owner. He looks about the lift, obviously annoyed at whatever had just happened on the phone when he spots Adam again. Motoki #1: Oh... it’s you. His upper lip curls at the sight of the infuriated Scot, surprising Adam into forgetting his anger for a moment. Motoki #1: I’m surprised you didn’t take us up on the retirement pay-off offered after the Halo and I overheard the argument you and my counterpart had reminding you about the terms of the contract you’re on now. There’s a reason why the pay-off looks so promising next to the long-term we’re forced to offer. Adam: I’m here to wrestle; I think I performed well enough to warrant a roster spot, the contract doesn’t bother me. There’s a raised eyebrow at this comment, the two men’s opinions obviously differing slightly on the issue. Motoki #1: Hrmm, well... you’re still hardly the company’s top draw. Hopefully Killean will make you reconsider that deal, we’ll be happy to lay it on the table for you again after your loss this week. There isn’t a nasty smile or chuckle to accompany this, just a dismissive snarl to his voice. Once again Adam’s fists clench tightly as he watched a second Motoki scarper from him – this time out through the upper opening and away along the fourth floor corridors. Earl: Did you hear the door buzzer? Stevie quickly turns and looks at one of the far-off screens. Stevie: Ehh... must’ve, that’s John at the main door. Earl looks at his watch, squinting a little to try and make out the time on the tiny dial without his glasses. Earl: S’about twenty-five to two: looks like the chances of your two getting it on are slim now. Stevie: There’s time yet my friend, time yet. Wanna get the door? Earl: If you make another pot of coffee while I do. Stevie: Deal. While the rotund Earl plods off to answer the door and let John the repairman in, and as Stevie handles the all-important coffee duties, we’ll return to the head of the contemplative Scot still trapped in elevator C. How could Motoki have passed him off so easily? Granted he was going into the match under the banner of ‘Eternal Underdog’ again, but he knew the match had only been booked to boost his opponent’s self-confidence in winning a match under his own steam. Two cheap finishes on the trot couldn’t have been helping convince ‘The PRIME Cut’ that his decision to work more closely with Tyler Nelson was a good one – hell, Adam didn’t blame him for it; he’d been there and done that in his time – but the results so far weren’t exactly promising of future success. So Nelson had fixed a rather well-crafted match; Killean remembers Adam as a solid competitor and will go into the match prepared for a battle, even if he does expect to win it already, and Nelson’s hoping that the time out (plus the break in bookings since the Halo) has got Adam out of practice enough that it’ll be a walk-over. Ergo, Killean scoops an easy win but thinks much more of it that it really was. Can’t fault them fo- Killean: Ahem. Adam breaks from his trance-like space-out, blinks a few times to clear his head and see his opponent for this week standing in the enclosed little ‘cell’ with him. At first he finds it odd to only see one of him, but quickly accepts it as easily as the double-vision he had earlier. Killean: You seemed slightly lost in your thoughts there, bud. Adam: Just... a weird day, y’know? Killean smiles and nods, knowingly. Killean: How are things anyway? I haven’t seen you since well back in the day. Adam: Eh, it’s been alright. Ups and downs, but I’m pretty settled back in the game now. Umm, about our match this week... He trails off at the odd look Sirrajin is giving him. Killean: Nothing to do with me, man. I think you might be confusing me with my other half. He’ll be along in a minute. He adds this last bit almost as an afterthought. Adam slowly nods, again slightly puzzled by this all, but (as always) quickly accepting of it. Adam: So, you’re the Killean I knew? Killean #2: I think ‘knew’ is a bit strong a term. He chuckles, raising a smile from the silver-haired Scot. Killean: But yeah, I ‘knew’ you around the back anyway. Everyone did really, you weren’t a bad guy, it was just some of the shit you tagged on to that turned anyone off. Adam: Yeah... Adam falls silent with this, thinking back to his previous run in PRIME and his involvement as a henchman for the ‘Asian Invasion’. He had always been the ‘almost nobody’ backstage, no-one had much but ambivalence toward him – it was only ever his affiliations anyone had a problem with. He began to think back to the number of times he’d sat about watching matches on the screens backstage, beer in hand, with almost anyone that’d walked by and wanted to talk some shit with him. Not that there was anything wrong with being a sociable guy, but it did say something about how seriously his opponents took him or how little an impact he ever had on anyone. Maybe it was ti- Killean #2: You alright? You’re glazing over again. Adam snaps back to it, mumbling an apology. Killean #2: It’s fine, just wanted to let you know that I’m leaving now. I’ve got a bit of training to do, but good luck with your match. Adam: Thanks a lot man, I’ll see you. Killean #2: I think that’s the guy you were looking for there. As the big Canadian rolls out to head out at the third floor Adam looks over his shoulder to see his mirror-image waiting behind. Killean #1: Good to see you back, wasn’t certain you’d last past the Halo when I saw you in there. There isn’t particularly any malice in the statement, but it’s hardly as encouraging as it could be. Adam: Well, I wasn’t expecting quite the pop on my return that you got. S’been a little chalk-and-cheese with us on our returns – I’m the reluctant face-by-association and you took the unpredicted heel turn. Killean #1: Hrmm, yeah... He’s obviously uninterested in the polite conversation as it stands, and is looking round the little metal-walled room out of boredom. His attention falls back on the Scot once more; with a bit of a scornful attitude he addresses him again. Killean #1: I’ll be good to see you back in the ring, never did get to beat you last time round did I? You must be the one of the last PRIME Vets I’ve still to get a notch against. He chuckles a little, displaying confidence beyond what his opponent expected from him. Adam: Well, we’ll see yet, I suppose. Keeping his cards close to his chest was always one thing you could never fault the wily Scot on, and having a Joker played out of nowhere was always a possibility. Adam: We’ll see... Sirrajin curls his lip at this repetition, obviously not believing an upset to be a viable outcome Killean #1: So you said. Adam smiles a little to himself as the cocky Canadian hikes himself up and out the elevator, bored of his coming adversary already. Left alone again Adam is about to resign himself to his thoughts once more when he begins to itch on the backs of his hands, he tries to scratch them but the more he scratches the worse it gets; beginning to move up his forearms, past his elbows, hitting his shoulder before his neck starts to feel like it’s blistering up. Adam: Jesus, fuck, Christ, shit, fuck! He starts clawing at the burning, boiling sensation on his neck, but as he starts to try and scratch at his chest which has decided to join in the fun his hands numb up and fall limp. With the insane pain racking his back now Adam does all he can think to do and launches back against the wall and starts rubbing against it to try and combat the searing agony of his skin trying to creep off his bones. His legs collapse underneath him as the rash takes them from him. Sitting, defenceless, Adam holds his hands out in front of him to see his fingers lengthening and splitting at the ends, his fingernails popping off under the pressure. Just as his hands tear open fully to let the hand underneath come out Adam passes out from the pain. *Smack* He wakes up with a jolt as a stinging slap hits his right cheek. Getting his bearings again the Glaswegian staggers to his feet and nearly collapses back down again upon seeing himself standing in front of... well, in front of himself. Adam: Uhh... The past version of the Scot laughs at the current incarnation as he frantically checks his fingers for where they split open: nothing, not a single scar or even a drop of blood. Adam #2: Surprised t’see me, eh? The silver-haired Scot looks to his black-haired doppelganger with total bemusement painted across his face. Adam #2: I’ll be going in a minute, don’t worry. Thought I’d make sure y’were awake an’ alright afore I left. Adam #1: Right... thanks... Adam #2: I’ve got an ‘Underdogs Anonymous’ meeting to attend, I keep relapsing at step 9. But you should know that already, eh? The wily Scot chortles as he good-naturedly socks his future-self in the arm, encouraging a slim grin to creep into the corners of the ‘new’ Adam’s mouth. Adam #2: Ach, see – yer fine! Good luck with the Sirrajin business, by the way, you’ll need it. With that he gives a nod goodbye, falls to the floor and rolls out the lift. Adam’s left standing, still slightly phased after meeting himself from about two years previous. The parting comment rings around his head a bit, eventually giving rise to a broad grin across the Scot’s face. Adam: I won’t need luck... There’s a shunt as the gears finally grip properly again and the three working lifts jolt into action briefly, their doors creeping open a few inches at the request of long-ago forgotten hysterical button presses from when they first froze. Earl: Ah-ha! Thanks a lot for that, John, we’ll head round and meet the poor souls caught in the lift and offer company apologies and that shit. John: No problem. Get someone to gimme a call later tomorrow and I’ll come round and get another look at them once they’ve been running for a while. Stevie: Shall do. You want a coffee before you leave? John: Yeah – that’d be great, thanks. Earl: Stevie, look at this! It’s 1:59 and he’s going batshit in there, you owe me fifteen bucks! Stevie: What? The three men crowd round the tiny monitor to look in on the trapped Scot. He’s woken up with the jerk of the lift coming back to life and stood up from his crouched resting against the wall. Looking round he sees the doors which have opened a little, enough to see the gaps to the floors it’s stuck between and the ceiling/floor between them. Fed up with his confinement he’s decided it’s time to leave – even if the lift isn’t going to willingly oblige without some ‘encouragement’. Stevie: You’re joking... Earl: I call this snapping, don’t you? And I don’t see anything happening with your couple in the other one. As Stevie’s busy fumbling round in his pocket for his wallet Adam’s busy wedging himself into the slender gap between the doors. Once he’s got enough of himself in he leans his shoulders forward, bracing one door on his left shoulder and the other against his lower back, and pushes. Bit by bit the doors loudly creak apart, when there’s a bit more room he starts pushes them apart with his hands, standing between the two doors in a slowly widening Christ-pose. When he’s forced them apart a good few feet he reaches up and grips onto the carpet of the fourth floor, pulling himself up and out the lift. The Eternal Underdog had fallen and was left for dead down below. The Number One Son has returned to PRIME and someone was up for sacrifice this week, but would the fatted calf be ‘The PRIME Cut’ or the Prodigal Son himself?
