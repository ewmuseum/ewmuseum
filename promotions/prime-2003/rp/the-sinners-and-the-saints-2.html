The Sinners And The Saints [2]

Date: Today Everytime I have to repost this a baby is killed and eaten by immigrants. The Sinners And The Saints [1]___________________________I cackled madly into the downpour. I had won. I pulled the trigger. BOOM! And it was over. *** I don't cry. I don't scream. I don't even complain. Every time it gets to me, I just think of that bitch of a wife not having closure. I think of what I removed from Carcer. A big throbbing tumor. And I took a razor blade and removed the cancerous hole within this city. The second I arrive, they throw me in confinement. They want to drive me mad. I'm not going to give them that satisfaction. The cot is not something I would consider sanitary. It’s faded and dirty yellow. But it is better than the toilet nearby. I’m not even going to go into detail about how disgusting that thing is. I’m in an architecture of steel. The perfect prison. The floor is cement, but the rest of my world is steel. Unforgiving unrelenting metal that compounds my loss of freedom. Months pass. And I have to admit, it gets to me. I don’t scream or nothing’, it’s just that I want to. I want to go wild. Being in this little cell with no one around is torture. And the promise I made to myself when they threw away the key that I wouldn’t give them the satisfaction of seeing me crack starts to slip away. Suddenly, a note slips under the door. It’s from Lindsay. She tells me all about how school is going. How everyone looks at her funny. She shows me there is hope, even in this situation. But the realist in me says that she’ll stop sending these letters soon. She’ll grow to hate me or she’ll find better things to do with her time. I know at her age, I’d do the same. Every week, she proves me wrong. Every week, a letter slips into my cell, and I read it. Ten years pass. Every Tuesday, like clockwork, they come. It’s to the point now where I have to stop myself from shaking like a giddy little child when it shows up. She’s gone through high school. She tells me of how stressful the tests are. She’s gotten her heart broken. Sad little thing. She tells me of it in such a poignant manner. She could get this sort of stuff published. She’s got a gift for writing, I can tell. I used to read thick novels to pass the time when I was out. Or maybe I didn’t. It was so long ago. Every single week, she sends her letters. She’s the only friend I have. The only thing I got. Another Tuesday passes, and I sit on that cot that reeks of filth. I have to stop myself from hopping up and down like a damn retard. I sit there. Hours pass. My internal clock is usually a good judge of these things. Time passes. Nothing. It finally happened. She has forgotten about me. I collapse to the floor and curl into a ball. Ten years in this Hell and I have lost the only thing worth living for. I break my promise. I begin to sob. Softly. I don’t necessarily know how to go all out anymore. Ten years of suppressing my emotions has turned me cold. Suddenly… Light. The door to this Hell is opening. It has never opened. “Youngblood?” a foreign voice speaks down to me. Figure it is a guard or something. Who else would open that door? I pick myself up off the floor, push away the tears. “The board wants to see you.” *** I get a shave and a hair cut before I go in front of the parole board. Didn't do a real good job. My face is still full of stubble and such. My hair is gray as is my face. I can see the wear and tear of the years for the first time. I'm not as I remember myself to be. To say that the outside world frightens me is an understatement. You'd be the same damn way if you got stuck in my situation. They put me in shackles, like a dog. They clatter around as I walk between two guards. I don't look ahead. I just keep my head down and walk. No fuss. I'm sure this is just a charade anyway. Build me up and then rip me apart in the same instant. Give a man hope where there is none. They sit there, in their pristine suits and their clipboards. Routine kind of bullshit for them. They only have a man's life in their hands. A bunch of young kids not far removed from college, full of ideals and what a man like me should feel like now after all the years get stripped away. Like I should feel remorse or something. I don't. But I'll say I do to get out of this situation. I removed the cancer from Carcer. Why should I feel sympathy? It's those motherfuckers who should apologize to me for screwin' me for doing my job. 'Mister Youngblood, as new information regarding the Wolfenden case has come to light, it has become apparent that you were wrongfully imprisoned. Now, we can not give you back the ten years of your life that you lost, but we can set you free and clear your record. We can also provide you with shelter until you can get back on your feet.' One of them says. My eyes are still adjusting, so I couldn't tell which. 'The state is extremely sorry for this situation.' Another says. Bureaucratic piece of shit. 'So, I guess there is no other way to say this. Sir, you are now free.' Free? Was I dreaming? Was it over? Inside I was going wild like a child at an amusement park. On the outside, I just nodded my head. I made a promise not to crack. And I came through. Good for me. *** The fan whizzed slowly. The blades moved as if there was nothing to worry about. Not a care in the world. The Carcer City Police Department was not exactly bustling with life at about five in the afternoon. 'Why did it have to be today?' The Hispanic man asked as he messed around with the flaps of his coat. 'Would you rather the guy be stuck in that shithole some more? Come the hell on Emilio, you know the guy is a fuckin' hero. He don't deserve to be in that hell anymore than you do.' The man responding to him was leaning back at his desk, the smoke bellowing out of his nostrils as he lifted a cigarette to his lips. 'Jesus, Vega. I'm not sayin' its a bad thing. Just I'd rather it was tomorrow is all.' He fixed his tie as he looked at the outside of Carcer City. Rush hour traffic. The sun wasn't shining. It barely ever did. The clouds swallowed the sun and made sure to make everyone feel depressed as always. Carcer lived and breathed on that depression and helplessness, like a hungry animal. 'Why? You rather spend the night picking up dead leads on Angelo? For all we know, the line ends at Rolo. Emilio, your tryin' too hard to find the connect, man. There ain't one.' He blew out a smoke ring as he ashed the cigarette into a nearby ashtray. SLAM! The punch into the desk was hard. It startled Caesar Vega to the point where he almost feel off his chair. Emilio was standing over him, his face red with anger. His rage was building quite fast, as it usually did. He gritted his teeth, the sound like nails on a chalk board. 'You think some fucking gringo without any fuckin' balls is running this city? That after that damn Wolfy debacle that some piece of shit from nowhere just decided to rise up and make a name for himself?' Vega stood up, meeting that piercing glance with one of his own. 'You know I think you're right Rage! But the fucking signs aren't there. They've covered their tracks too well. The second we can get Rolo on something more concrete, it will start topplin' over. Remember, Wolfenden wouldn't have fallen if not for that hitman...' 'And what happens if he don't talk?!' Rage cut him off. 'Then we are screwed!' Vega just sat down again and chuckled. 'Calm down man. You're going to burst a damn blood vessel in that thick head of yours.' 'Chúpeme!' Rage responded. He took to turning his head and spitting directly after his comment. 'So, I take it you want to come along?' Vega looked up from his seat, nodding like a little kid. 'He's the reason I got into this kind of stuff, Emilio. He's a hero. He got rid of the worst mob family in the United States.' Emilio smirked. 'Yeah, and within the snap of a finger someone took their place.' He grabbed his keys and started walking. 'Get a coat on, Caesar. We're gonna go pick up the toughest cop this damn city ever seen.' *** You know that first breath you take after something stressful? I mean that first real breath you take in when you feel totally liberated? It's perhaps the most exhilarating experience in life itself. You feel at peace with everyone. At peace with the birds and the bees and all that good feeling crap. You never know what true freedom is until it is snatched from you in an instant. Like a flash of lightning. They gave me a good pair of slacks, some decent shoes, a white shirt with a plaid tie neatly rung around my neck like a noose, and my old trench coat I used to wear when I was on 'the job'. This jacket has been in lockdown just as long as I have. Poor thing. My eyes have finally adjusted to the world. Things have changed, but the weather hasn't. It's like a constant. The only thing I know in this world that didn't go out and get a face lift. This area of Carcer used to be filled with mom and pop joints, cheap motels built in. Most of those places are long dead, replaced by fast food chains and thrift stores with neon signs flickering out for attention. However, nobody changes the bulbs. Laziness is like the weather in this neck of the woods, I guess. The great thing about Carcer is the fact that they really didn't lock me up in a prison. See, here, we lock people up in these solitary cells in a large expanse within the cusp of Downtown and Uptown. Used to be a big joke that my parents used to tell me that we could go get some good Italian from Dino's up on North Colgate, which was just a little ways away from where we lived on Thrush. Maybe a couple blocks, actually. Anyway, as I was sayin', we could go to Dino's, which was this little piece of crap where you could get some middle of the road wine and meatballs, get a little tipsy, and then see my Uncle Lou. Uncle Lou was on my mother's side, and he was doing a couple life sentences for cutting up his wife and girls. He ground them up and hid their bodies in coffee cans. The joke was that you'd be crazy to even go near that place. The worst of our criminals go there. Well, the one's that get caught, anyway. And on the outside, you're liable to run into someone who knows your face because you look like someone who owes money to some small fish. Then you get taken back in the alley and get your kneecaps broken. No joke. I've seen it happen before. But that was years and years ago. They must have gone ahead and at least tried to cover the bad of the city with a layer of wallpaper. You're never going to see a problem totally fixed 'round here. More or less lucky to have the City Council put scotch tape on the edges of the wall paper to make sure the damn thing stays in place. They pack up your things in a neat little briefcase. Reason they do that is when you die, they can ship it to be auctioned off. You can't legally get rid of these possessions unless the prisoner expires. We had some guys who got paroled and then screamed about their rights. That's back when the slime ran the streets. We got rid of that though. Nobody was powerful enough to take Wolfenden's place in this city. He was the top dog, with all these small timers going at it under him. The night sky is coming, and with it a storm. While I might have been locked away for a decade, the senses still could tell me when a storm was brewing. And every time a storm brews in Carcer City, something big goes down. It's like a full moon or some shit, I swear. I stand there, raising my hand to hopefully catch a ride with a taxi, but nobody comes. Suddenly, some police car comes roaring down the road and comes to a hard stop right next to me. I didn't even need to know who the hell it was. Only one person on the force gets away with driving like that. The door opened up and out came this Hispanic guy who didn't look anything like I remembered. He used to have this fat on his face, and some of the guys on the force used to call him Little Burrito. Like I need to really go into that one. 'Brandon, I thought we put pieces of shit like you away, not let you out to start trouble.' Emilio laughed as he looked over me, his fingers pounding on the hood of the car. I look at him and can barely crack a smile. It feels so foreign to twist my face in such a way. 'And I thought you'd have gotten a much more comfortable job picking strawberries on the country side.' He responded with a chuckle. He didn't take too kindly to that. He gestured to me with an 'up yours' before yelling out 'Cojeda tú tú asno perra!' Not like I know what that means or anything. The guy still kept from being fully Americanized by using those little Spanish cuss words. He threw me a pack of cigarettes, knowing my addiction hadn't been fed for a decade. 'You got some cancer that's been missin' you.' I opened up the pack and saw he left but three smokes for me. I pulled one out and put it on my lips before looking at him. 'Could you be more generous?' I sarcastically retorted. He threw a lighter my way and I lit up, sucking in the sweet mixture of tobacco and whatever else they put in these things. I saw some kid with short dirty blonde hair in the front seat, nervously cracking his knuckles. I gave him a once over before exhaling. 'Rookie?' 'Nah, he's just a goodie good.' Rage chuckled. 'Yeah. Sticking him with a corner cutter is a great move. Keeps your ass straight.' I responded, trying to sound like I was joking, but we both knew better. 'Get the fuck in the back seat. They got you pushed up in a halfway?' I nodded my head. '543 Macy.' Rage chuckled. 'Funny how the past catches up with you.' I pop open the backdoor and take my seat. I really hate being in the back of a police car. Too many bad memories. When you've been on the other side, you usually want to stay there. Rage got in as well and he sped off. 'You see, they fixed up the downtown a little bit. It ain't so bad no more. Got some good minimum wage jobs for those damn hoodlums to work at.' Rage said absent mindedly as he took a sip from his cup of coffee. 'They never fix things in this city. Just cover them up. But we made a splash they couldn't cover up.' Rage chuckled as he nudged his partner. 'Yeah. Yeah you did. It only took a couple years for someone to move in to where Wolfy once was.' His partner was not pleased by his attempts to undermine me. 'Well, maybe if the police force wasn't filled with a bunch of pastry eating doughnut fucks like you, they'd be able to deal with the crime rate a little better.' You fight fire with fire. He gulped down his coffee. 'Seriously though, Brandon, things have changed. I changed. I ain't some little rookie no more. And I'm just steps away from cracking up the guys who took over in Lawrence's place.' His partner just looked at him. He wasn't a rookie, but he was young, I could now tell. Probably two years on the force, I'm guessing. 'You remember Angelo Deville, right?' He turned around in his seat, looking me in the eyes. I nodded. 'Yeah. Guy was a small time hustler back when we put the nail in the Wolfenden case.' He nodded his head. 'Well, we got a feeling that he's the new crime lord. We got nothing connecting him yet, but the guy comes out of nowhere and lives in a mansion and all that crap.' Emilio rolled down the window and chucked his coffee cup out the window. 'He's got ties, that's all I know. We just haven't been able to pin anything down about him. Fucker is untouchable. He's got half the city in his back pocket and an army of suits.' 'Not to mention the Trio.' His partner said as he put himself back into his regular seat. 'Trio?' I was interested now. Not because there was anything I could do about it, but because I remember the fun of trying to get all the jig saw pieces to fit together into a coherent picture. 'All we know is that where ever Deville is, he's got three mad dog killers with him. Remember Silver? He's one. But he's got two others we can't really get a bead on. And since Deville trusts them with his life, he protects them.' 'Most of the stuff on them is just legend.' The partner said nervously. 'That have anything to do with the fact that he's got the department working in his favor as well?' Lucky guess. Rage nodded his head. 'Some fuck has been undermining our whole investigation. But I got a task force working with me to get this taken care of. Pierce wanted to make sure we handled everything with extreme care.' 'How is the old man doing?' 'He's got a bum ticker.' The partner responded as he looked at the road. 'Rage, you got to turn here.' 'I KNOW WHAT THE FUCK I'M DOIN' CHUPAVERGA!' He took a sharp twist of the wheel and we went screaming down the road. 'Anyway, his wife left him. He's got ulcers. Poor guy. But he won't ever retire. Said so himself.' 'What's he got to live for?' I was more than interested. He was the one who tried hard as hell to get me out of the legal jam I once was in. 'The thrill of catching the bad guys.' Rage snickered. He hit the brakes hard and the car came to a halt, jolting us all out of our seats just a little. 'We're here. Hope you have some fun, partner.' I shook my head. 'Yeah. Great.' I got out of the police car and looked at the place. When I left jail, I thought things were better out here. Turns out I was wrong. *** What a shithole. The pipes are cracked and leak. The damn thing is falling apart. The lights in the place flicker on and off. The wood holding the place up is rotting away. Any single step could send you falling through the floor to god knows where. Nails out for you to snag your elbow on. There is a corner store just a little ways away. I throw my briefcase on the bed, which actually seems to be a step down from the one I slept on in the joint. At least when you laid on that one it didn't squeek and squeal. At least the old mattress didn't have holes in it showing the insides. My place isn't that much better. It's on the top floor of this shithole. The light flickers on like a street lamp on a city street nobody has the money to replace. There is this little rug over the wood floor to make the place look presentable. It really didn't do the trick. The rug used to be red, but now looked something else with the dirt and such dragged in on it. And the floor boards were now gray and creaky. Nails were up all over the place. There wasn't a window save the one in the bathroom, which overlooked the back alley to the place. The last occupant hadn't even taken the liberty to flush the toilet. The place in there was all tile, but some were cracked or missing. The toilet and sink were yellow and had black growths all over them. The mirror was cracked in a spider web. To say I wanted to vomit would be an understatement. But the place had a radio that I could at least use and a decent couch to sit on. I figured that the better thing to do was buy a pack of cheap beer and some more cigarettes. So, I go and do that, buying the cheapest beer and pack of my favorite smokes, along with a Zippo. The storm comes around, the rain coming down hard and fast. I haul ass and lug the stuff on in to the place in the paper bag and throw it on the bed, as I go and fiddle with the radio. I'm shocked the paper bag doesn't fall apart. I go over and turn off the lights and sit on the couch. I took off my shoes and socks, putting them together neatly next to me. I loosened my tie and threw my trench coat on the bed, grabbing my pack of cigarettes and lighting one up. The radio station came off of their commercial break and just went into playing a song. My man's...Got a heart... Like a rock cast in the sea...My man's...Got a heart... Like a rock cast in the sea... I smoked my cigarette, looking nowhere. I just was trying to figure out what I was going to do with my life. What I had to do and all that. I needed a job, and I needed to find my daughter... My god. How could I see her? I left her all alone for a fucking decade. I just can't pop back up in her life and act like nothing happened. Well no one told me about her...The way she lied...Well no one told me about her...How many people cried... They don't make a card for this situation. Or maybe they do now. But I don't have the money to do that. Maybe they'd take me back in on the force. Maybe my pension would kick in. Maybe the government would give me checks as payment for my incarceration. My man's...Got a heart... Like a rock cast in the sea...My man's...Got a heart... Like a rock cast in the sea... I know her so well, unless she lied on those letters. And even then, I wouldn't blame her. And even if she told the truth, there is only so much you can throw on a piece of paper before you run out. Well no one told me about her...The way she lied...Well no one told me about her...How many people cried... Makes me sick. Makes me sick that I had to leave her behind like that. But it's too late to say you're sorry...How would i know...Why should i care...Please don't bother trying to find her...She's not there... I smoked the cigarette, trying to feed that addiction. But suddenly, there was a knock on the door. I got up, flicked the end of my cigarette clean, and made my way towards the door. Figured it was Emilio doing the rounds for some odd reason. I opened the door. Standing before me was this beautiful young woman. I knew her. I was shocked when she didn't pull a gun out at me and blow my head off. Her clothes were wet, her eyeliner trailing down her eyes ever so lightly. I stood there for a second, my mouth gapping open. 'Daddy?' She cried. We both cried. *** We cry to wash away the years. I missed so much of her life. But that's over now. She's eighteen. My god, she's eighteen. I've missed so much. She sits on the couch as I crack open a beer and begin drinking, our tears subsiding. I wasn't there for her when she grew up. No, no I wasn't. But I'm here now. I'll be there when she graduates. She says she's near the top of her class. My god, her mother would be so proud. I'll be there, in a arm chair, smoking a corn cob pipe in my robe when she brings over her date for prom. I'll tell him to treat my daughter right, and we'll laugh at the things people laugh at. I can't remember those things very well, but I'll find them again just to be whole again for her. She wants to go to Harvard? I'll pay. Princeton? I'll pay. She wants to go to Carcer City Community College? I'll pay. I don't care. I'm not going to demand her go anywhere, or make things easier on my budget. I'll work two jobs for her. Hell, three. I smoke another cigarette and look at her. She looks just like her mother. Those soft brunette curls. Those eyes. I will make it up to her. I must make it up to her. I start by doing a very unfatherly thing. I throw her a beer, which she fumbles around with before cracking it open and taking a big swig of it. She giggles. Sounds like an angel. We drink and talk. Talk and drink. The beer is almost gone, and we laugh. I almost go through my entire pack of cigarettes. I will make it up to her. I just know it. *** A couple hours later and the buzz has worn off. I get up, making sure to watch my step. I walk over to the couch, wanting to see her face. She's not there. The cushions are a mess. There's some blood on the wood floor. BLOOD! 'LINDSAY!' I roar. 'LINDSAY!' She wouldn't have left. No way. Something was not right here. I got this growing pit in my stomach that tells me she didn't leave under her own will. That gut feeling is never wrong. There's nothing telling me she didn't just leave. Nothin' but that growing pit. She was kidnapped. I don't know why, and I don't know how. I was right here and it all went down. Stone drunk. Goddammit. GODDAMMIT! I sit on my cot, lighting up a cigarette. I inhale and exhale. Try to rationalize it. It's quiet. I can think. Why would this happen? The silence is broken. Something screams out to me. Sirens, like a manic depressive choir. They tell me something is amiss. They make that feeling within my gut true. They validify it. It's only been two hours since I passed out, not even before midnight. I'm good with the time like that. My internal clock does wonders. Nobody in this building could possibly know something is up. I grab my shoes and socks and put them on as fast as possible. My tie is still hanging loosely, but that don't matter. I throw on my trench coat. I walk over the bed and pop open my briefcase and reach for it. Colt, Single Action Army. Same gun I nailed Wolfenden with. They never took it in because I hid it. Their boots pound into the floor. Their coming this way. Within that briefcase is a box of bullets. I flip open the chamber and load up, giving a powerful spin for good luck. With a flick of the wrist, the chamber clicks into place and it is show time. I can't run from them. I can't hide. I can only play. Their footsteps stop. I hear a pounding at the door. Shocked that the damn thing doesn't fall over. 'BRANDON YOUNGBLOOD, YOU'RE WANTED FOR THE MURDER OF LINDSAY YOUNGBLOOD! OPEN UP THE DOOR AND SURRENDER YOURSELF!' I smirk as I load the bullets into my coat pocket. I click back the hammer. I don't respond. 'YOU HAVE UNTIL THE COUNT OF THREE TO OPEN THIS DOOR!' I'm not afraid. 'ONE!' I'm not afraid. 'TWO!' I'm not afraid. 'THREE!' Bring on the war.
