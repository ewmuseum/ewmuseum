This Is Why He Hasn't

19 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/19/2006 “Dinner was lovely, thank you,” she said, smiling at him. Titan really was still trying to figure out how to act with Stephanie around. It had been a long, long time since he'd had an inkling of a girlfriend and he still wasn't used to it. Titan smiled to her and opened the door to his Escalade. She took his hand and boosted her small frame into the vehicle and he nodded to her and gently shut the door. The big man's walk took him around the front of the big SUV and, fingering the keys in his hand. It was his vehicle, it was his life now. He still wasn't used to that, either. When he'd left the IDF and transferred to the FBI, they'd supplied him a car as well. He took a long breath and pulled the door open. The big Cadillac rumbled to life and Titan pulled it back out of the parking lot and onto River Street in Savannah. It was a pleasant night: light, warm and not too humid. He rolled down the windows as they drove past the coastline. Out at sea, there was a ship coming in, its running lights creating a beautiful tapestry of reds and whites. He smiled as he turned away from the historical district and heading north. 'Well,' he thought, ' it was about time that my parents had met this girl I've been seeing.' “So, where are we going?” Stephanie asked with a smile for him. Titan glanced over at her. “Well, we're going to head to my parents' place. I thought it was about time you met them.” The smile on her face grew even broader, a shining face under that dark red hair that just made his heart melt. It did a little bit to banish the feeling of dread in his guts. And that didn't make any sense. He'd stalked stone cold killers, he'd been in combat and in those situations, he'd been cool as ice. Now, his guts were tied in knots. A small hand patted him on the arm. “Are you okay with that? You know, if you feel like it's too early, that's okay. I know you want to take this slow.” He nodded to the lithe little woman. She was dressed in such a way that it didn't worry him about what his parents might think. She had a blue blouse with short sleeves that came halfway down her thin upper arms and a knee-length black skirt. She looked back up and him and wrinkled her nose. He could only think two words “damn cute”. He took a side road towards the coast and past a massive tree dripping with Spanish moss. The road curved in front of them, back and forth, and Titan took the turns carefully, maneuvering the top-heavy SUV carefully. That's when the thought struck him. His dad wouldn't be home tonight. “Well, there's something you need to be aware of,” he said to his little passenger. “Dad's not going to be there tonight,” he told her. “He's gone to meet an old friend that drove up from Florida.” “Well, that's okay,” Stephanie replied. “I'll meet them one at a time, then.” He nodded and pulled the Escalade into the driveway. The house wasn't huge, but had a wraparound porch with the white pillars that were stereotypical to the old southern plantation style. In the fading sunlight, the flower boxes out front still seemed to be basking in those golden rays. They were filled with bright, vibrant plants. The door opened and there stood Sue St. James, Titan's mother. She wasn't as tall as her husband, but seemed that way at six feet, two inches. His father had about six inches on her, giving an inkling of where Titan got his massive size. Titan stepped out of the car, smiling broadly at his mother while walking around the Escalade to the passenger side. With a gentlemanly flourish belying his size, he opened the door and eased Stephanie out of the car. To the big man, it seemed like a movie scene: long, slender legs that slowly reached to the ground, she flipped her hair back slightly, framing her face with a beautiful grin and graceful ease of her walk. He shut the door and turned towards his mother, Stephanie's hand in his. The picture was odd, a massive man with a slight woman... “Hello!” his mother shouted, waving from the porch. She was dressed casually and stood with the confidence of a rock-solid matriarch. “Come in, come in,” she smiled, turning and opening the door to the house. The two of them passed by, Stephanie smiling at her and Sue patted Titan's arm as they walked past. The inside of the house was marvelous. Hardwood floors stretched out, with small chandeliers providing illumination in each room. The decor was that of rich mahogany and redwood. Titan moved carefully out of habit, not getting his weight or girth near anything that might be knocked over. He worked hard at being careful in other people's homes. “So, you must be Stephanie,” his mother said, smiling. “Hunter has told us about you. But he's not said much.” In spite of himself, Titan actually felt his face redden. “Oh, well, he's so sweet and I was looking forward to this anyway,” Stephanie replied. “Can I get you anything to drink?” his mother asked... her voice seemed far away, or maybe underwater... He could hear his heartbeat, getting louder, getting faster. He shut his eyes and clamped his palms to his temples. “No, no, no...” As his ability to think slipped away in the midst of the oncoming turmoil, he gasped out one last thought. PTSD was brought on by traumatic events and triggered by similar events: sometimes body movements, sounds, visuals, sounds or maybe certain events. This was the reason he hadn't dated in so long...It had been a beautiful night in Israel. The air was dry and warm. The sun had just set and the moon shone full above. Titan had just joined the MP's, just cracked his first case that led to a cell being broken up. As part of his celebration, he'd finally asked out a beautiful girl he'd known for a while. She had said yes, and even offered to come get in so he didn't have to requisition a vehicle. They'd stopped by his parents' house to say hi. She had been lovely; jet black hair with a light tan. She had curves that could draw any man's eye, a smile that lit up a room and definitely a taste for the big man's company. “Hey,” he'd said as he came in the door. She walked next to him, resplendent in a flowing, blue gown that hugged in the right spots. “Mom, Dad, this is Stacy. She and I...” “Oh, don't tell us all that stuff!” his mother had said, smiling. “Stacy, your dress is marvelous!” His father had smiled at him. “It looks very nice,” he told the girl. “Where do you plan on going?” Titan felt considerably younger than his twenty-four years. He had just found his spot in the MP's, finally nestling in. His mother hadn't yet acquired most of the fine lines around her face and his father still had a head of mostly dark hair, just sprinkled with gray. The smalltalk had been pleasant before the two of them were heading out to a nightclub before grabbing a late dinner. They stepped outside, walking towards her car when it exploded in a terrific fireball. The explosion knocked both of them back. The windows of squat, wide house all shattered with the concussion wave. Titan hit and rolled, coming to his feet and drawing his custom-made .38 pistol from the holster on his lower leg. He scanned the area, holding his weapon at the ready and squeezed off a shot as a man began to run away from the bushes near the house. He staggered and fell with a shout. The door to the house burst open, and his dad dashed out onto the doorstep with a Colt model 1911 in hand. He saw the man down and leveled the firearm at him as he struggled with the shot in his shoulder. His mother was visible just behind him, a shotgun in hand. Titan's eyes scanned for more movement before he turned to Stacy... A long piece of shrapnel protruded from just beneath her left breast, soaking her dress with blood. Titan knelt next to her, lifting her head. She looked up at him, her eyes shaking. “I'm sorry, I really wanted to go out with you,” she whispered. With a final breath, her eyes rolled back and she was gone. The big man got up, his jaw set and his blue eyes cold as ice. He walked over to the fallen man and grabbed a piece of shrapnel off the ground. “Why.” It wasn't a question, it was a demand. “You...” the man gasped through the pain, “took down the cell. That had to be punished.” Titan snarled and grabbed him by the throat. He took the piece of shrapnel and sliced it across the man's torso. He screamed and it brought a smile to the face of Shadow Mountain. He threw the terrorist towards the flaming remains of the car. “I killed no one,” Titan snarled, walking forward. “They were arrested and put in prison, but no one was killed.” “You... had to be... punished,” the man gasped back. Titan put a boot on the man's chest and knelt. “I could just fillet you with this,” Titan said, waving the piece of metal in his hand. “It'd look like you got cut up by the explosion.” “HUNTER!” came the shout from the house. It brought his head up. His father was still there, pistol lowered. “Don't do it!” Titan took a deep breath. “I won't kill you,” the big man amended. “But you will go to prison, and you will learn why people don't mess with Shadow Mountain.” The big fist cracked into the attacker's jaw. He followed up by smashing the man's nose with the heel of his palm. Then, slowly, he grabbed the small pieces of hot metal lying around and shoved them into the open slash on his chest. When the sirens finally arrived, Titan stood up and looked at them. He lifted the man by the torn shirt and shoved him towards the officers. “He blew up the car, one casualty,” he said in a cold voice. He walked away, towards Stacy's body. He cradled her form and a few tears ran down his face. It was supposed to be a celebration... Stephanie and Sue both stepped away from Titan as he clamped his hands to his temples. He pitched forward, groaning loudly and swaying on his feet. He took a few staggering steps and seemed to list one way, then the other before he caught his leg on the plush leather couch and fell onto the coffee table. Poor table never had a chance, and shattered under his incredible impact. Laying on the floor, he gasped for breath. His head rolled back and forth, his eyes gazing far away. He muttered under his breath, sounding like he was having one side of a conversation. “Yeah, we'll go to that new club downtown. No, we should eat afterwards. That's provided I don't run everyone else off the floor.” He blinked and gulped audibly. Sue looked up at Stephanie. “Grab a pillow,” she instructed, “then help me hold his shoulders down. We have to keep him from moving too much.” Stephanie complied, grabbing one of the pillows off the couch and putting it under Titan's head. The both knelt, putting their elbows on his shoulders. Sue looked across her son's big chest at the girl. “You don't know, do you?” she asked. A shake of the head answered the question. “He hasn't dated for years because of this. He knew as soon as he brought one home, this would happen. It's a shame, he's an old-fashioned boy, but the last girl he brought home had her car blown up in our driveway and died from a piece of shrapnel.'
