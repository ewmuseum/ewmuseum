The Great Unwashed Up Close

8 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 08/07/2008 “We need to change your image,” the PR guy announced. He sat on the expensive leather sofa in Union Jack’s luxury penthouse hotel room, with his legs crossed and his fingers arched. His suit was expensive; probably Italian, but certainly the genuine article. Union Jack, sat opposite him upon an equally expensive leather sofa, raised a speculative eyebrow, so intense it was clearly visible under his red, white and blue sequined mask. “Change my image?” He glared at Jeeves who shrugged, equally perplexed by the statement. “What the bloody hell are you talking about?” “Look,” the PR guy began, “you want the fans to get behind you, you want to for them to like you, then we need to change your image.” Union Jack shakes his head. “I don’t want any of those bloody peasants to like me! I just want to be PRIME Universal Champion! It‘s near impossible! I tried to bribe my way to the top, but these bloody colonials are more honourable than I gave them credit for if you can believe that!” The PR nodded and produced a smarmy grin. “And how do you get to the main event?” He allowed the question to hang in the air for a time, before answering himself. “Through the fans.” “I believe he may have something of a point, sir,” Jeeves agreed. “You do?” Union Jack asked. He allowed the notion to swim around his head for a time, before deciding to hear the man out. After all, he had paid an absolute fortune for his services. “And how exactly does one go about changing one’s image, Mr. Barnes?” “Well, I’ve been giving this some thought,” he began, leaning forward in his chair, “and I think we need to lose the mask. We need to lose this whole British aristocratic ‘gimmick’ and go old school.” “What gimmick?” Union Jack asked. “What the bloody hell are you talking about?” “Wait,” the PR guy began, “you mean to tell me that actually are a British aristocrat?” “Of course, dear boy,” Union Jack replied, “I play Polo and partake of Caviar with the best of them.” “But your voice...” the PR man began, groping for words. “Well, the Queen doesn’t talk like that. She talks like Jeeves.” Union Jack rolled his eyes. “That’s because like Jeeves the Queen is a southern nancy. I’m from the North, therefore I have a Manchester accent.” “There’s more than one British accent?” The PR guy mulled the idea around his rather empty head for a time. “I had no idea. I just thought that you were from Australia or something...” “HOW VERY DARE YOU!!!!!!!“ Union Jack yelled, lunging out of his chair and directed himself toward the PR guy like a torpedo, his arms outstretched ready to throttle the man. However, Jeeves reacted quickly, deftly stepping in between the two, holding back his master competently. By now, the PR guy is out of his chair and all the way across to the other side of the room, his back against the wall and constantly edging closer toward the exit. He glared at Union Jack as the man visibly calmed within his ever-faithful butler’s embraced, wild eyed and completely bewildered. “What the hell was that about?” He asked. “What’d I say?” Jeeves rolled his eyes and Union Jack’s anger returned. “I’M NO AUSSIE KANGAROO FUCKING CONVICT!” This time Jeeves struggles to hold back his master. “I’LL KILL HIM, HE’S A BASTARD!!!!” The PR guy stood still on the spot and pressed himself hard up against the wall. Jeeves turned to him, still holding back the struggling Union Jack and shot him a stern look. “It would be advisable sir to get back on topic. I don’t believe I’d successfully be able to hold my master back, if another derogatory mark be made about his ethnicity.” The PR guy nodded. “O-ok-okay.” Jeeves turned back to his master and looked him in the eye. “Calm down, sir. You can’t blame Mr. Barnes for his idiocy. He’s American -- it’s the law over here.” Union Jack removed his eyes from the PR guy to glance at Jeeves and then nodded in agreement. “You’re right as always, Jeeves. He’s doesn’t know any better, he’s a bloody colonial.” Breathing heavily as the anger subsided, Union Jack slumps down upon the leather sofa and sighed. The PR guy peeled himself from the wall and cautiously retook his seat, never taking his eyes of the supposedly crazy British aristocrat. “Okay, so I guess there’s no gimmick to lose then, correct?” Union Jack nodded. “Indeed.” “Then perhaps, there’s something else we can do,” The PR guy said, with a knowing smile. “Leave it to me.” *** PFFFFFFTTTTTTT And with that, liquid was cast across the Union Jack photographs and all over the first five people queing for his autograph. “What the bloody hell...” Union Jack wiped away the remaining spittle and glared at the brown beverage in wonder. He held the glass up to eye level and unsuccessfully tried to remove the look of disgust spread liberally across his face. The fans brushed themselves down and cursed under their breaths, just as the convention centre assistant drew closer. “Is there something wrong, sir? She asked. She wore a bright red t-shirt and wore a name tag which had ‘Tina’ scrawled on it with black magic marker. “What...the... hell...is...This? Union Jack managed, still waiting for the taste of the damned beverage to remove itself from his pallet. “It’s Tea, sir,” she told him. “Just like you asked.” “Tea?” He asked. “This tastes like gnats piss and it’s bloody cold!” “Well duh,” she said. Union Jack developed a confused expression and studied her carefully, before turning to Jeeves who stood by his side. His ever-faithful butler leaned closer. “Did she just have a stroke?” Jeeves shook his head. “I believe she was just showing dissent, sir.” Union Jack nodded in understanding. “Explain yourself, wench.” Tina rolled her eyes. “It’s Iced Tea, of course it’s going to be cold.” “Iced...Tea...?” Union Jack allowed the notion time to sink in, but after a minute or two he gave up. “What in the name of Greek buggery is that?” “It’s like Tea, with ice in it,” she informed him, chewing gum and blowing bubbles all the way. “Jesus, where are you from, Guam?” Union Jack rose from his chair, with fire in his eyes. “Now listen to me you bloody colonial peasant, less of the sass or I’ll have Jeeves kick you in your ovaries.” He paused for a time, allowing his threat to sink in. “Because let’s face it, the likes of you shouldn’t be allowed to breed!” Tears formed in the poor woman’s eyes and she promptly darted away into the distance. Union Jack watched her run screaming for a time, but then got bored and sat back down again. “Next!” A teenager approached him chuckling. He wore acne and a black ‘I Heart Devin Shakur’ t-shirt and Union Jack took an instant dislike to the fool; just like he did every other peasant that had offended his eyes for the last fifteen minutes or so. The PR guy’s big idea was to give away free publicity pictures of himself and then sign the bloody things face-to-face with the fans, in order to garner their trust - he was hating every minute of it. “Huh-huh-hu, that was a skit wasn’t it?” The teenage idiot questioned. Union Jack shook his head. “What the bloody hell are you babbling on about, gobshite?” “That thing with the girl,” he explained. “That was staged, right? I‘m not a moron....” “Well that’s debateable,” Union Jack muttered, signing a picture for the teenage fool. “Hey, fuck you man!” The teenager yelled. “I’m not even here for you. I was hoping I could get Jeeves’ autograph...” Union Jack‘s eyes widened with rage. “WHAT?” Jeeves blinked at the teenager who held out a photograph of the butler in action and pen for him to sign it. Bewildered, Jeeves reached for the photography, but hesitated when his master gave him a disapproving look. “Fuck you, man!” The teenager yelled. “Sign this Jeeves, it’s alright.” Union Jack stood, grabbed Jeeves picture and tore it in half. “Bugger off, peasant!” The teenager left, deflated and Union Jack glared at the next poor soul in line. “Next!” The next autograph hunter weighed three hundred pounds Union Jack figured and when he waddled over, he chuckled. “Man, this is great! I would’ve been here sooner, but I just had a shit the size of a birthday cake and had to beat it to death with a damned shovel!” “Lovely,” Union Jack muttered, his face contorted with disgust. He grabbed a fresh photo and signed it, before turning to Jeeves. “I’m going to rip that PR guy‘s arm off and bugger him with it!” Jeeves nodded. “Very well, sir. I‘ll assemble the rubber gloves and Dettol.”
