"Drifter": The Wade Elliott Story - Part Three: Delinquent. (ReVolution 140, singles match vs. Sonny Silver)

Date: November, 1982 “Fifty in gas.” The Drifter's eye caught a shiny new pack of Marlboro Reds. “An' a pack of Reds.” The anxty young woman behind the counter rolled her eyes, turning to snag the pack of smokes. Wade's other eye caught a small dog treat on the lower rack. “An' one of these,” he said, setting the treat on the counter. “That it?” she asked with a lifted eyebrow, frustrated. “Yes ma'am.” Her black-painted fingernails tapped into her register. “Fifty-five fifty,” she claimed through a mouth full of gum. Wade pulled three twenty dollar bills out of his wallet, placing them in her hands. She snatched them away, swiftly placing them in the drawer and returning four dollars and fifty cents to The Big Dog's paw. “Have a good day,” she said, devoid of enthusiasm. The Blue Collar Brawler tipped his drifter's hat before turning and thudding out of the road-side convenience store, pocketing his smokes as he approached his shiny black Ford F-150, parked patiently by a pump. The joyful visage of Angus, the Bluetick Hound, hung out the passenger's side window, a floppy tongue dangling from his mouth. Wade opened the gas cap, stuck the pump inside, locked the lever and proceeded to wait, leaning against his truck as the digital numbers grew and grew. A whimper from Angus. “Oh, shit. Sorry, pup.” Wade produced the small dog treat, which was swiftly inhaled by the Bluetick. Wade scratched his dog behind the ears as it happily crunched away. “That's a good boy.” The sound of a loud diesel engine caught Wade's attention. He turned his head over his shoulder to witness a large yellow school bus chug past the gas station. The joyful faces of small school children filled the clear glass, their happy, high-pitched voices piercing through cracked windows. Wade could only shake his head. “Sure weren't like that fer me in school,” he sighed, talking to Angus while the bus disappeared down the road. “Don't think I was smilin' fer any of it....” He patted his pockets, released a frustrated grunt, and started back toward the store. “Forgot a fuckin' lighter...” * * * * * * * Pine Ridge, Alabama November, 1982 “You shut up an' leave me alone, Eddie!” “Whatsa matter, Wade? You chicken?” A small crowd of seven year old boys and girls gathered around the two combatants, their field of battle that of a run-down basketball court on the school playground. “Fight! Fight!” they cheered, with little fists pumping in the air. “What're you gonna do, Wade? You ain't nothin' but a yeller belly!” balked the larger of two known as Eddie. He offered a confident grin, well aware of his size advantage. Eddie wasn't rotund, but was certainly a thick little boy. The striped blue shirt he wore day in and day out signaled the name of Eddie Welch, the baddest bully to ever set foot on the Pine Ridge Elementary playground. “I'll knock yer head off, that's what I'll do!” screeched the challenger, little Wade Elliott. His tiny blue eyes burned as he pushed shaggy brown hair out of his face. A wiry frame sat behind dirty overalls, every little muscle clenched and ready to fight. “Yeah, right!” laughed Eddie, eliciting more chuckles behind him from his “crew.” “My daddy tells me you ain't nuthin' but a little bastard child!” “That ain't true!” “Sure is! Says you don't even know who yer daddy was! Says he ran off 'fore you was born!” “Shut up, Eddie!” “Make me! I ain't afraid of no yeller belly without a daddy! I bet he was a little wuss, just like you!” “SHUT UP!” In an instant, Wade picked up a nearby rock and chucked it at his adversary, cracking Eddie in the mouth. “AOWW!” yelped Eddie, clutching his mouth and bending at the waist, tears filling his eyes while blood filled his hands. The audience of half-pints roared as Wade sprinted forward, throwing a size-six Converse sneaker into Eddie's crotch. Eddie yelped again, falling onto his back, tears streaming down his face. “I HATE YOU!” screamed Wade, climbing on top of the wailing Eddie and proceeding to punch him in the mug with angry little lefts and rights. “STOP! STOP!” cried Eddie, covering his face in an attempt to thwart Wade's ferocity. “WADE ELLIOTT! GET OFF OF HIM!” The bellow of what could only be a school teacher caused the seven year old crowd to scatter in every direction, leaving the enraged little Wade to continue beating on his adversary. He managed a couple more thumps to the face before finding himself lifted in the air, one adult on each arm, and carried across the playground, angry little legs kicking helplessly. “LEMME GO!” Wade yelled. “HE STARTED IT! PUT ME DOWN!” Wade’s cries of anger proved futile, however, as the teachers carried him away, leaving the sobs of Eddie Welch behind him. * * * * * * * “I’m real, real sorry for my son’s actions, Principal Richards.” Hands in her lap, Karen Elliott sat across the desk from Pine Ridge Elementary’s principal, Ted Richards. Her eyes were on the verge of tears as the elder gentleman removed his glasses and rubbed his temples, closing Wade’s already growing file. He turned his aging brown eyes to Karen, folding his hands in front of him. “Ms. Elliott, yer son is in the first grade and has already found himself in fifteen fights.” “Sure, but most’ve them were…” “I know most’ve the conflicts were initiated by other children, but that ain’t an excuse.” “I understand,” Karen resigned. “He's dang close to bein' sent to an institution for juvenile delinquents...and the boy's only seven.” “I know, I know. I'll try n' knock some sense into him.” “Karen,” started the principal. Karen only looked down at the purple skirt covering her lap, her beautiful brown hair hanging around her face. Principal Richards returned his small bifocals to the bridge of his nose. “Karen,” he repeated. “I know it ain't no picnic raisin' a little one by yer lonesome.” Karen nodded. “Have ya ever thought about tryin' to find someone to help ya out?” Karen offered nothing. “I know Earl broke yer heart, but that was seven years ago, Karen. It's time to move on with yer life.” Again, the only sound was that of silence. “Ya think you could try datin' again? Find someone fer Wade to call 'Daddy?'” Karen lifted her head, an awful glare set on the Principal's genuinely concerned gaze. Tears welled in her eyes as her mouth opened slightly to respond. “No.” * * * * * * * The white wooden door creaked as she slowly pushed it open, taking a peek before stepping inside. On the edge of his modest little bed sat Wade, lost in a comic book, oblivious to his mother's entrance. She took soft steps, eyes falling over action figures and army men strewn about the room. She took a seat next to her child, who kept reading as if she wasn't there. “Wade...” “Leave me alone,” he snapped, eyes locked to his comic. “Wade, listen to Mama. You got in another fight today...” “HE STARTED IT!” yelled little Wade, throwing his comic book across the room. “Don't you yell are yer mother, young man!” “DON'T TELL ME WHAT TO DO, YOU BI...” SMACK. Wade froze as his mother's cold fingers slapped him across the face. Tears started to form as he sat, mouth agape, at his now enraged mother. “Don't you ever called yer mother that again, do you understand me?” she said quickly through grit teeth. Wade's lip quivered, but in an attempt to be too macho for his age, he narrowed his eyes and seethed through puffed cheeks. “I HATE you!” he spat, jumping off his bed and sprinting out the door. “Wade,” she called after him, regretting her decision to be stern with him. She stood and took quick steps to follow him, but he was out the door and running across the driveway before she could catch up. She stepped to the entrance of their white, run-down trailer, clutching the door jamb as she called after her son. “WADE!” she yelled to no avail. The little boy kept running, arms swinging at his sides as he turned out of the driveway and sprinted up the dirt road, dust in his wake. She released a heavy exhale, covering her eyes with her hand. After calming down with a few deep breaths, she reached inside the trailer to retrieve a pack of cigarettes and a lighter, popping one into her mouth, lighting the end and inhaling deep. She crossed her arms and shook her head as smoke drifted out the corner of her mouth. "Just like yer God-damned father...”
