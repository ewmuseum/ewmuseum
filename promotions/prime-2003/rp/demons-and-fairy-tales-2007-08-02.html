Demons and Fairy Tales

2 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/2/07 Danny's face looks like shit. Not in the Star Magazine "ladies without makeup how gross!" way, but in a way that he can only play 27-28 for another year or so. If he keeps aging like this, he'll be playing the married dad role for the next decade of his life. He traces a finger through some of the newly-discovered creases in his forehead and lets out a deep sigh. After a few more moments of studying his unfortunate maturities and making various grimaces to tighten his face and make them go away, he shuts off the light in the bathroom and walks out. It would be easy to blame all the extra lines and imperfections on Noa, or more accurately, Nova-induced stress, but that would involve applying the same revisionist history that he had been spewing on television for months. It was a broad brush, sure, but it wasn't necessary when the cameras weren't on. He knew that the rapid aging was equal parts PRIME and Hollywood. As much came from his bloodfeud with the Risen Star as from his fight with the football player, his agent and publicist leaving, his crucifixtion in the tabloids and the ongoing media tour to promote his increasingly popular movie. Not only that, but Danny knows that the battle with Nova could have ended at ReVolution 122. To say nothing against the colossal reputation of an American institution like revenge, sometimes it's easier than people think. An elaborate and occasionally articulate plot to eventually isolate and render un-functional a man who had accidentally sent you to prison for a crime you didn't commit, well, that makes sense. But when the aforementioned crime is legally defined as "malicious combustion", well, it takes a little bit of the wind out of your sails. Seems like kind of a stupid thing to hold a grudge over, or at least so well-planned a grudge as he had claimed. No, a more fitting reaction to this equal and opposite action would have been simply to attack the reactee at the advent of his finest hour, after defeating a previous nemesis and righting his own peceived wrongs. Of course, had Danny realized at the time that his sneak attack after the Nova/Chet Worth match single-handedly cut shot the exorcism of Nova's demons and stunted any philosophical healing process the Risen Star was enjoying, he might have at least considered giving his response a clearer direction. Perhaps if he had better defined himself as pro- vs. antagonist, the assault would have been interpreted as a realization of his own justice rather than a violation of Nova's. He had not considered the possibility that some would see the emo stoner as the star of the show instead of the bona fide actor standing across the fresh line in the sand. Returning to his living room, Danny squints against the cerulean glow of the television - left running on mute) and sinks into a leather recliner. After a few moments of reading the closed captions on E! News Daily, he abandons it for some heavier reading - the pile of scripts sitting on a stand next to the chair. The stack isn't as big as it was when Allan repped him, but there are options on the table, no pun intended. People knew how to find the Ferguson home and send a script along. Hell, if the star tours can find him, Paramount should know his mailing address. The difference between the offers now, though, is that he is getting them as option #2. He could flip through them and tell they weren't written with Danny in mind. One was written for Vin. Another one clearly had The Rock in mind. There's even one that looks to be pegged for Seth Green. Guess they didn't want to pay for his strength and conditioning program. Being second or third in line isn't the problem for him. If you don't learn to swallow your pride in this biz, you don't work. Any one of these can be refitted to him with a competent re-write and the removal of a few ethnic jokes. Besides, if you believe what you read in the trades, Danny Ferguson could take a video game adaptation and carry it into the black at the box office. He considered taking a terrible project just for the challenge. So it's not the content that's a problem. The problem is the shooting schedules. Start August 4. Start September 27. Start August 19. Start September 1. All in mere weeks. All during the next PRIME tour. The true effort of these past few maddening weeks has been to make these decisions quick and painless, but the Band-Aid has not come off easily. The public image of Danny Ferguson as sufferer of an identity crisis was less emotional and more practical - the professional meltdown surrounding UltraViolence was a clear indicator that he could not function as both a big-budget action movie star and a professional wrestler, but the problem had been pegged way back at the arrest in late 2006. He was poised to make a run for the highest echelon of success in both industries, but trying to maintain both would lead to a full-scale collapse. One had to go, and it didn't take a financial analyst to determine the earning potential for each. To put it bluntly, being accused of exploding another human being as an actor would not jeopardize his wrestling dreams, but the opposite was proving untrue. Ignoring, however, the differences in class and connotation between his two professions, anyone would find it hard to simply give up something to which they had dedicated the last two years of their life. Even with the misconception that Danny was somehow a normal person, this proved to be a roadblock. When you consider the ego and the tenuous terms of evaluation for in-ring success, it complicates things further. There's a strong comparison to be made between professional wrestling and the Boy Scouts, and it doesn't involve the kind of hack gay jokes that belong in a Jay Leno monologue. When you tell someone you were a scout, the first thing they'll probably ask is if you ever made it to Eagle. It's the only thing about scouting - besides the gay part - that the casual person would know. Likewise, when someone knows if you were a professional wrestler, they want to ask if you won a World Championship. Short of steroids and, again, the gay stuff, it's the thing every casual fan would know. The bottom line is that both clubs or distinctions, in the eye of Joe America, can be split into two groups: those who achieved the highest honor available an those who didn't. The damaged pride, as referenced, was an endurable pain in Hollywood, but only when it happens in private. In a papparazzi-driven media environment where walking around with untied shoes provokes a four-page expose, taking his ball and coming home would result in the kind of public scrutiny and ridicule that would forever be attached to his name. To leave PRIME with anything less than top honors would be a public admission of failure, and would backfire on his original intentions of joining: to bolster his action star persona. The backlash would be unbearable, a public neuering of his image as any sort of tough or strong man. For the rest of his life he can look forward to labels as a quitter, labels that would ignore the hypocrisy of those who applied them. So as easy as it sounded to simply walk away, his years of on-the-job PR training told him otherwise. A graphic of Danny's face flashes up on the screen and catches his eye. He grabs the remote, but then tosses it back down. Without the cadre of "his people" - the doers, see-ers and hangers-on that keep him in a makeshift bubble to guard from the public and media - he was more than aware of whatever they were saying. This chick wasn't going to tell him anything he didn't already know. One year ago, Kyle Lamen had taken a very good shot at injecting a little self-awareness into Danny's everyday life. He faced down all the goading and the prodding and the unfair odds that Ferguson and the A-List had applied to the situation, and he put Danny in his place at Colossus III. It wasn't so much the beating that he endured, but the actual act of losing, that took the greatest toll on the Superstar. The shame and guilt of being bested by the arch-nemesis he created was nearly unbearable. Surely he could not continue on in PRIME after such humiliation. How could a man show his face there again? Easy. Give him a title. The Intense belt literally fell in Ferguson's lap at Colossus, dropping from the waist of the robo-Illustrious Face-Eater as it disintegrated. Ringside fans could still recall Danny in tears, beside himself and doing his best David Hasselhoff (sans cheeseburger and alcohol) at the ringside area. The broken man was the absolute antithesis of Intense before being re-gifted the belt in a rare moment of coincidence. Less than an hour from reaching his darkest hour and being shamed away from PRIME forever, Danny's ego brought him back. At the moment, it was an opportunity to save face. In hindsight, it was an opportunity wasted. Giving up on the script, he keeps his eyes on the television. Fishing blindly on the table with the rest of the treatments, he finds his bookmark - a photo of a woman, roughly his age. Beyond the days of wallet photos, the picture appears to be printed straight off a MySpace page. Without giving the photo or the script another look, he marks his page and sets it back on top of the pile, focusing his attention on the graphic and the screen. Following ReVolution 122 and Danny's subsequent re-arrest for evading authorities (following his acquittal of the crime he never committed due to physical impossibility), the negative impact of wrestling's ridiculous logical disconnects on his more 'legitimate' acting career were apparent. His brief pursuit of the revenge kick had came and went, his debts were settled, but he couldn't leave without the recourse of quitting. To walk away is to admit failure. But to be driven away...to be driven away is to need no excuse. One of the most fucked up things about Hollywood is that you find yourself, most of the time subconsciously, trying to turn your life into one bad movie cliche after another. And you know barely anyone in town is terrible good a comedy, so everyone is writing their own tragedy. Danny's tragedy, he determined shortly thereafter, was to return to the scene of the crime, in more ways than one. The colossally devestating loss to Lamen was his lowest moment and the closest he had ever come to leaving. Not only was he too humiliated to go on, but the audience had effectively written him off. Losing to the crowd favorite and being considered inferior was far better than simply walking away. Besides, it could open up a new career path as a cinematic supervillain. Any scientist can lecture on the trouble in trying to duplicate results that were originally unintentional. Danny Ferguson is clearly not a man of science. As public perception gradually turned towards Nova's newfound Trouble With Danny, he used the script from last year's documentary to make his own vanity project. The Risen Star was now the arch-nemesis and all the other pieces - down to the re-emergence of the A-List - fell into place. Intention was less to recreate all possible variables of the original event; trying to stir the universe into action had as yet proved unsuccessful for Ferguson. Instead, he sought to recreate in Nova the same burning anger that had driven Lamen beyond the point of human limitation to win at Colossus. Danny's passive-aggressive exit strategy relied heavily on The Other Guy, and in getting him as 'fired up' as the original had been. Anything short of a completely faithful remake would surely fail him and leave closed his supposed window of opportunity (or at least remove the ladder from the other side of it). The television reporter - still on mute - finished talking about Danny and moved on to a story about crack or hookers or something equally trivial. Still sitting in the recliner, he flipped the television off. In the blinding moonlight, he caught his reflection in the 60-inch screen. His face still looked like shit. The final scenes of the role of his life were on the horizon. If his methods had put his co-lead in the right frame of mind to deliver as planned, then the final lines would write themself. Critics who had lauded More Than Hardcore called it Ferguson's magnum opus, but the man himself knew better than that. Sitting in the dark, he looked at the script and the half-visible printout of the woman's photo. In hours or less, his story would get the proper ending - not the one he particularly wanted, but the one he knew he needed.
