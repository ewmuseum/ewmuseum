Devil May Cry After He Reads This

16 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 16th, 2005 The streets are empty, and the night is silent. Save for a slight breeze now and again, all is still. In the distance, few lights illuminate the house windows along that very street, but not a single soul is seen or heard. Quiet. Still. Peaceful. That peace, however, comes to an abrupt end as a stronger breeze picks up in the air. Swirling around an intersection in the empty streets, the breeze fast becomes a miniature storm centralized between the two streetlights, gaining size with each passing second. Tree branches sway and lines of electricity emit from nearby power sources, pulled into the center of the swirling mass. And just as soon as it begins, the storm is over. Everything is silent once more, and all movement ceases. But again, the silence is broken, now by the sound of a foot stepping on solid ground for the first time in a long while. Heavy breathing comes from the darkness, and then a piercing howl never meant to be heard by mortal ears. Meanwhile, in the secret headquarters of The Forsaken (a.k.a. some house James won on a crooked game show), James stands in stunned silence at Clyde’s proposal. Walkins: So, what do you think? Farwell: I think you have some kind of learning disability. James finds himself needing to sit down, Clyde’s ‘genius plan’ having been stupider than he thought it could be. Somehow, Clyde managed to surpass his wildest expectations and catch him completely off guard. Farwell: I think I need a drink… Walkins: Don’t you like my idea, James? Farwell: It’s not that I don’t like it, it’s just that…well, it may not be the most legal thing you’ve ever thought up. And that’s operating under the assumption that monsters and demons actually exist, which, I can assure you, they do not. Clyde’s brow furrows. Walkins: They do so exist! I saw proof with my own two eyes when I struck LeStatt Knight down with my own two hands. Don’t you see, James? He didn’t die. Farwell: And that’s a good thing, or else we’d have another unnecessary lawsuit on our hands, thanks to you. Walkins: But that proves he’s a vampire! And if he’s a vampire, and he obviously does exist, then there must be others like him. Other vampires, and demons, and monsters. And it’s my job to slay them, just like I slew King ‘Pire LeStatt. Farwell: But I thought you said he didn’t die… Walkins: No time for words. Quick! To the Clyder-Mobile! James just hangs his head. Farwell: I hate this part. Cut to James and Clyde in the Clyder-Mobile, which, as we know from a previous episode, is just a Volkswagen made up of spare parts from obsolete vehicles. The wind blows through their hair (well, Clyde’s hair, and James’…head) as Clyde searches the streets for demons, while James sits with his arms folded over his chest. Farwell: This is bullshit. Why the hell don’t we have a roof? Walkins: It makes it look cooler. Farwell: Yeah, because we know a rusted Volkswagen isn’t cool unless the roof is missing. Walkins: You must’ve gone to the same car dealer I did. James sighs and turns his head to look out the side, trying to ignore the fact that Clyde’s shady driving just ran over a cat. Suddenly, a large dark figure rushes in front of them, narrowly missing being hit by the car as it reaches the other side of the road. Turning back, it looks to be carrying something, but disappears into the nearby woods before they can get a good look at it. Walkins: Did you see that?! I told you there were monsters! James, on the other hand, doesn’t share Clyde’s enthusiasm. Farwell: It was probably just a dog. Walkins: A dog? It was carrying a dead body! Farwell: Then it was probably just Lassie. Clyde turns off the ignition and opens the door. Walkins: I’m gonna catch it. Farwell: This isn’t Pokémon. You can’t catch it. Walkins: Fine with me. Don’t get famous. But we’ll see who has the last laugh when I’m on the cover on Time Magazine, and the head of Satan himself is on a silver platter next to me! Farwell: Clyde, even if that was a monster, I highly doubt it was the Devil. I also highly doubt your ability to extinguish the life of a spider, let alone the Prince of Darkness. Walkins: Don’t worry about me, James. I’ll be fine, just as long as I have my trusty weapon with me. Pulling a covered object out of his pants, Clyde proudly holds it high in the air. And then it starts to vibrate. Farwell: Are you planning on destroying evil with a dildo? Walkins: It’s a Wonder Touch 3000, thank you very much. And it said on the box that no one can resist it. Not even Satan. Farwell: It worries me that a sex toy would make that type of claim. But if you insist on doing this, I’m coming with you. Do we at least have some sort of real weapon in case it decides to eat us? Walkins: There’s a gun in that glove compartment. Raising an eyebrow, James reaches forward and opens the glove box, his eyes widening as he spots a semi-automatic handgun inside. Farwell: What the hell are you doing with this? Walkins: You don’t know how I roll. Sometimes those Pokémon conventions get a little crazy, and you need some protection. Farwell: From seventeen nine-year-olds? Walkins: Sixteen after a warning shot. Several minutes later, Clyde and James are walking through the trees, following the faint breathing coming from just in front of them. Holding the gun out at arm’s length, James’ arms shake wildly as he tries to maintain his composure, even while the sound of bones snapping fills the air. Walkins: It’s just beyond this clearing… Holding the vibrator above his head like a club, Clyde charges in first, unleashing his war cry. Walkins: I’m Clyde Walkins, BI-IGFOOT! RUN! Throwing the dildo at the beast, Clyde screams like a little girl and turns around on his heels, running in the direction they came from. And pushing James down in the process. Walkins: God save the Clyde! Rubbing the back of his head after hitting the ground, James turns and spots the gun a few feet away from him, but as he looks back at the beast, he sees it stands right above him. And then that same cracking sound is heard, as it breaks another twig between its fingers. Farwell: Ah, shits… Clyde doesn’t stop running until his legs won’t go any further, and he falls forward into the mud, skidding to a stop after seven feet of sheer pain. Cringing, he slowly turns over onto his back, breathing a mud bubble as he lies there in agony. Walkins: Oww…Clyde down… Above him, a curious squirrel looks down from its perch on the end of a tree branch. Holding a nut in its paws, it bites down a few times, then looks back at Clyde, who giggles at the friendly squirrel. Walkins: Hehehe…hi, squirrel. You’ll be my friend now that James is gone, won’t you? Looking up anxiously for a response, Clyde finally gets it. In the form of an acorn right between the eyes. And then the squirrel’s off, disappearing into the night. Clyde rubs his forehead and forces himself to sit up. Walkins: The squirrel’s right. I can’t leave James to die…after all, I can’t use his credit card if he’s dead! Now on his feet, Clyde scans the area for anything to use as a weapon. His eyes light up as he finds one, and then it’s off to rescue James. Back where they found the monster, James sits beside a fire as the beast forces a few grunts and makes motions with its deformed claw-hand. Nodding, James looks over at the lifeless body of a young girl with sadness in his eyes, then back to the monster. Farwell: So, she wasn’t strong enough to make it through? The monster sighs in its own way and shakes its head. Farwell: And your people wouldn’t accept her? The monster shakes its head again. Farwell: So, you tried to bring her here, to a less-civilized world, where the two of you could hide from the society that shuns you and share your love for the rest of your lives? Now the monster nods. Farwell: I understand. Even here, we live with that kind of prejudice every day, and it’s as unfair to us as it is to you. I’m sorry you had to lose someone you love. The monster hangs its head, another sigh escaping the beast. Farwell: But I think it might be best if you return to your own world. Humans don’t exactly have a good track record with other species… Just then, Clyde dives into view, wielding a thick log as a weapon. Walkins: CLYDE SAVE THE JAMES! Flying through the air, Clyde brings the log down across the back of the monster’s head, killing it with a single blow. Proud of his feat, Clyde stands with his hands on his hips and his head held high. James, meanwhile, shakes his head in disappointment. Farwell: How did I know that you’d manage to kill something that can survive inter-dimensional travel? Clyde shrugs. Walkins: It’s a gift. Farwell: I hope you know that thing was peaceful. Walkins: Then why was it carrying a dead person? Farwell: She was already dead when they arrived. They were from two different cultures, and came here to escape persecution. And then you killed him. Clyde gulps, dropping the log onto the monster’s corpse. Walkins: Well, at least they’re together again. James sighs. Farwell: Come on, Clyde. We’re going home. As the two solemnly leave the area, the fire burns behind them. And in the darkness, a shadow can be seen. A shadow of two beings standing together. A shadow of love. 12 Hours Later… Farwell: And you’re still planning on going through with this, even after killing an innocent creature? Walkins: Now I’m more convinced than ever that there are demons in our world, even if they aren’t all evil. It’s my duty to destroy the ones that are. And then the phone rings, causing Clyde to calmly walk over to it, sitting down on the swivel chair in front of a wooden desk. Swinging around and putting his feet on the table, Clyde lifts the receiver to his ear. Walkins: Devil May Clyde. And then the chair breaks, sending Clyde onto his ass as the table in front of him also breaks, but right down the middle, causing the cup of coffee on the side of the desk to fall right into Clyde’s lap. Yes, he screams. Like a little girl.
