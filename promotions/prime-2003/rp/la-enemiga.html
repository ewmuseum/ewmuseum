La Enemiga

Date: Revolution 161 Guadalajara, Mexico 72 Hours Ago His name was Miguel Almarez (well, his legal name was Miguel Juan Ricardo Almarez, but he went by Miguel -- "Miggy" to his friends), and he would tell people he had the best, most honored job in all of Mexico. He laundered the ring wear of the mighty luchadores. Every week, he was brought the ostentatious garb of Mexico's beloved warriors was brought to his shop, where Miguel hand-washed tights and intricate masks. He did all of the work himself and he was beyond meticulous. Everyone knew the story of El Elephante Gigante waiting for the man to deliver his freshly-cleaned costume before he won gold in the late '70's. Even though the job took longer than expected (you try to clean a fake elephant trunk and see how long it takes), no one pushed for El Elephante to forfeit the bout, because it was his very identity that was being steamed in a little shop in Guadalajara. Over the years since, Miguel's business had steadily declined as young grapplers saw less of a need for his (admittedly expensive) services. Why pay for getting your garb hand-washed when Laundromats will wash them for a lot less and with just as much care? It had fallen to the point that Miguel had to devote part of his store to the creation of a memorabilia museum, putting some of the most prized gifts he'd received from his clientele on display in order to make rent payments that used to be easy. Then he got the call -- Los Enemigos had hit the big-time, working as "enhancement talent" in the United States (for PRIME no less), and they desired his cleaning services. Sure, it was for nostalgic purposes, but Los Enemigos were an ever-expanding band of the young and the talented. More importantly, they were willing to pay Miggy in cash dollars. The laundryman was washing spandex when he heard the screech of tires outside his shop. So dedicated was he to his work, however, that he did not look up until the terrible crash-and-crumple of metal on metal. * * * * * San Francisco, CA Four Hundred and Ninety-Seven Days Ago (January 27, 2007) Judge Harold Barnes turned his impressive glare on the foreperson. "You mean to tell me you're still deadlocked?" His scowl turned down into a salt-and-pepper beard. The dark-haired woman standing up at the front of the jury box blanched noticeably under the weight of that glower. "S-sorry, Your Honor," she stammered, pushing the wide ovals of her bifocals up her thin nose. "Some people are having some difficulties with..." "Yeah, I don't care," Judge Barnes interrupted. "I figured sequestration would solve this. Obviously, that didn't do a damn bit of good. Get back in there and make a decision, you hear me? If I have to declare you all hung, I will personally assure you that you will receive another jury summons before -- " "Objection!" The cry came from a thin, blond man in a dark, pinstriped suit. The attorney flashed a disarming smile. "Your Honor -- you cannot threaten the jury to get them to produce a verdict." "Looks like I just did, boy." "It's Drew, Your Honor." "Whatever." The judge banged his gavel against the sounding block on his desk. "We're at recess." * * * In the viewing gallery, a short woman with a bob of light brown hair watched the drama unfolding in the courtroom, tapping the pink eraser of a pencil against lips painted hot pink. She watched the jury box and she watched the attorneys with dark eyes. When the judge declared a recess, the smallish girl slipped out of the courtroom. Her cellphone was out a moment later. "Good evening and thank you for calling the Elisabeth Foundation, my name is Trinity, how can I help you?" the brightly-voiced operator answered. It was one of those voices that the smile was a part of. One of those voices that people would listen to and they'd say, "Fuckin' plastic-ass people." "Trin, it's me. Put me through upstairs." "One moment while I connect you." * * * * * Six Days Ago Location Unknown Aiden Danvers ran. The streets of the city echoed with footsteps, hard-soled dress shoes ringing out against the cobblestones. Aiden Danvers ran like people were chasing him. This was a good thing, because people were chasing him. He turned the corner, slung his bag across his chest to protect its contents as he turned the corner. At the academy, they teach you not to look back when you're being chased because it slows you down. Turn your face to the front, put your head down, and run. One foot in front of the other, they were taught, is the only way to make sure there's nothing in your rearview mirror. It's so much easier to hear lessons like that when you're seated in a little plastic desk chair in a lecture hall full of people not trying to kill you. It's really damn hard to remember, however, when you're running through the streets at two in the morning and people with bad intentions were chasing you. "Why did I..." Aiden Danvers muttered to himself, breath coming in short gasps. "Why did I listen to her and take this job?" She'd given Aiden the look -- the one she usually used for people she was interrogating interviewing for the Bureau and she'd told him it was a favor for a friend. He didn't ask what kind of friend, because that woman's business was none of his. So he'd nodded and he'd smiled and he'd taken the two names and started nosing around. He'd learned the van der Wal family was a successful shipping family based out of the Netherlands. Their shipping company had gone back for a while -- we're talking no-shit Dutch East Indies Trading Company connections. Like with the fuckin' Pirates bad guy and running pirates aground in the Caribbean. No mention of Davy Jones, though, which was a bummer. He'd also discovered they (unsurprisingly) did not have names in their family tree like "Andreas" or "Siena," at least none that were young enough to be the two (increasingly notorious) assholes who served as the "handlers" of the former PRIME Universal Champion. Accessing the van der Wal family archive hadn't been easy, sure, but it was when Aiden had discovered the safety deposit box that all his hassle and all the red tape became worth it. He left his boss a message on her voicemail. "Have found something about our happy couple of freaks. Meet me at the usual place at the usual time." He knew she didn't especially like the cloak-and-dagger shit, but if what he had in the forest green Jansport backpack he was wearing across his chest was what he thought it was... That was why Aiden Danvers ran, why he was chased... that was another story. * * * * * 71 Hours, 46 Minutes Ago Guadalajara, Mexico Miggy Almarez burst through the front door of his shop, holding his hands in front of his face to ward off the heat coming from the flames in the car. "Dios mio!" he murmured, squinting into the wreckage of the car across the street. A silhouette moved in the passenger compartment of the car, pounding against a doorframe distended and mauled by the impact. The car (one of those American hatchbacks, red paint flaking off into wisps of foul-smelling smoke) wasn't cooperating, had transformed into a tiny steel deathtrap. Miggy Almarez would tell you he wasn't a hero -- he was a hero's dry cleaner. He didn't consider himself brave, not when he watched as El Bandito Rojo leapt over the top rope and onto his foes at the last show at the arena. He'd always wanted to be brave, had always wanted a chance to prove himself. He knew the fire engines wouldn't make it in time, and he knew the person trapped inside the tiny American matchbox on wheels would not also need an ambulance if he just stood there and gawked, mouth open wide enough to catch flies. So he raced towards the wreckage, giving the flames that licked at the ground a wide berth. The driver's side door had been crushed completely, but he was able to yank open the passenger side door with relative ease. The heavyset woman inside the wreckage groaned, an angry red line dripping blood into her eyes. She was dark-haired with a perm that was straight out of the late 1990s, and the jeans she wore were just tight enough to not be flattering to her wide hips. He stuck his head into the car, raising his voice a shade more than he probably needed to. "Are you all right?" he asked. The woman groaned, turning glazed brown eyes over her shoulder. She was seated in an uncomfortable position in the vehicle, he knew; no one in that kind of shape could bend that way. "I-I think I hurt my knee," she said, voice quiet and ragged, as if the pain was fraying it around the edges. "Can you walk?" "I... maybe if you'll help a little. But I think you're going to have to help me get out of here." A small thud came from behind them, causing Miggy's to flinch over his shoulder. "Hurry!" the woman cried. Miguel slid his arms under the woman's armpits, bracing himself and pulling gently. It took some effort, but he managed to get her out of the car. "We must move quickly, miss," he said as he let her lean across him. "We can only hope that the fire department will get here before anything severe happens." "I'm ready," she said, crying out softly as he let the weight rest back on her legs. Her left knee bent at an odd angle, and he accepted more of her weight against him. "Come on, hija, we can do this," he murmured as the pair competed in the most dangerous three-legged race either would ever run. "We can do this." Once they were clear of the wreckage, the girl slid out of his grasp, landing heavily on the asphalt. He had to let go or she'd drag him down, and Miggy Almarez wasn't a professional Good Samaritan, he was just a laundryman. "Ooh, sorry," he apologized, lips pursed together. "I didn't mean to drop you." "Are we safe? Out of the danger zone?" Miguel looked back behind him to the wreckage. Maybe it hadn't been flaming as bad as he thought it was, because the car just looked terribly banged up. "We should be fine," he assured her as he offered his hand down. "Come on -- up you go." The woman allowed him to pull her to her feet, leaning even more heavily against him. "I'm so glad someone was here," she murmured. "I would've been... I don't even want to think what I would have been." Miggy pulled her to his storefront, setting her down gently on the curb. "It's okay," he promised. "The authorities will be here shortly, and there's a hospital not too far away." She threw him a grateful smile, blowing a strand of dark hair away from her face. "I don't suppose you have any water?" she asked, lips twisting into a rueful smile. "Let me see what I can find," Miguel said, pulling the Los Luchadores Laundromat's door open, the soft tinkle of bells drawing a small smile from both savior and saved. The girl stretched her left knee out, curling the right and laying her elbow over it. She rested her forehead against her forearm as she waited for the police and the paramedics. * * * In the next day's newspaper, there is an editorial about how the tragedy at Miguel Almarez's shop could have been averted if Guadalajara's emergency personnel weren't so wrapped up with "tourism issues" like corralling the runaway partying that took place in the hotel zone. If the authorities knew that a select few guests had intentionally driven the party in downtown out-of-control, things might be different. Alas. * * * * * Four hundred ninety-three days ago (January 31, 2007) The jury foreperson's hands shook with nerves as she read from the small sheet of paper. "W-we the jury, in the matter of the State of California versus James Kennesaw Rodgers, on the charge of insider trading, find the defendant..." She looked up, pushing her bifocals up for what the watcher in the gallery figured must have been the 40th time in the last week. "Not guilty." The room exploded into what could only be termed "a ruckus". The crowd watching voiced their displeasure, standing and shouting (she joined them; though she didn't much care, sitting peacefully would have drawn undue notice, after all). Cameras in the courtroom snapped dozens of pictures in a matter of seconds; the blond man from days earlier wasn't there to revel in the victory (a white-haired man with thin glasses stood to clap Rodgers on the shoulder). Grins passed all around the defense team, but they weren't who the smallish girl watched. She took note of several sets of downturned lips among the jurors, though many tried to maintain a stiff upper lip. She watched the crow's feet around Judge Barnes' eyes spread and then contract, his nostrils flaring like a bull's as he banged his gavel sharply against the bench. "Order!" the judge shouted. "I will have order in this courtroom, or I will start dropping citations for contempt like they're going out of style." The room mostly quieted down -- Barnes' thunderous rumble broke their outrage with fear. "Any of you who don't think they can control themselves, I invite you to leave now before you really draw my ire." A few of the onlookers took the judge's advice, scowls locked into place. The fact that a small woman with a light brown bob was among them is only surprising if you realize she wasn't upset by the shocking verdict. She was impressed. * * * * * Six Days Ago Aiden Danvers smiled tersely at the bellman as he darted through the revolving door; he barely waited for it to be open enough for him to fit through. A quick glance at his wrist told him he was still twenty-five minutes early for his meeting - which meant she wasn't due to arrive for ten. He shot a nervous glance over his shoulder as he walked swiftly towards the hotel bar, but the lobby was empty at this hour, just a woman in khaki shorts and a Hawaiian shirt checking in. Even this drew his attention, but two young girls slept on a bench nearby, and he didn't think Andreas and Siena van der Wal had children. He stepped into the muted browns of the bar with a relieved sigh. Maybe he'd surprise her and have a drink this time; she'd once accused him of being "terribly boring" because he met a pretty girl in a bar when he never drank and he never tried anything. Aiden stepped up to the bar, letting his lips curl just a little bit for the barkeep's sake as he dropped his backpack at his feet. "What can I get you, man?" the man asked. "Just a seltzer water, thanks." He rested his elbows on the bartop, running his hands through his hair. He blew out another long sigh as a tall glass of fizzy liquid was set down on a napkin before him. He couldn't even dredge up a small smile of thanks before he went back to resting his head. It wasn't long before his lowered eyes caught sight of a pair of well-proportioned legs in pristine dress pants striding briskly up to the bar. "You're early," he muttered, not looking up. "Why do we even bother with a set time? Jeez, you're, what, twenty-three minutes early?" He stooped, pulling the notebook onto his lap. "You won't going to believe what I found," he said with a tired but still eager smile - the chance to tell someone what he'd found lit up Aiden's hound dog expression. He glanced up, finally... and his smile faded away, as quickly as it had bloomed. "Y-you're not -- " "The Alex you were expecting, I know," his visitor said with a small, tight smile. "Good evening, Mr. Danvers." The woman in white lifted a thin-fingered hand to sweep away a curtain of dark hair from her carved marble features. "I would very much appreciate a few moments of your time." His eyes flitted from side to side like a trapped rabbit, pinned by her stony gaze. "I'm actually, uh..." He curled away from the calm-faced woman with a nervous frown. "Actually waiting for someone." "I think we are both aware exactly who it is you are here to see, Aiden," the intruder said lightly. "So let us not play any games, you and I. As of this moment, that meeting can still occur. Do not do anything that may tragically change that fact." Alexandra Pierce smiled a pleasant smile that showed just a little too much tooth; there was a challenge in the woman's expression, a dare to defy her. To his credit, Aiden did not move until the redhead slipped an antique pocketwatch from her hip pocket, her brow softly raised. "Very shortly," she said, her whisper-soft and diamond-hard voice barely audible over the soft piano music tinkling in the background. "Your employer will arrive at a spot my employees are keeping a very close eye on. You do not wish me to advise them you are being... uncooperative. That would advance things between her associate and mine beyond either of our abilities to keep it civilized. Now, if I were you, I would bring my very valuable backpack and follow me. I promise I will return you to your employer in one piece." Aiden nodded numbly, easing to his feet. He followed the woman known as the Spider in the Web from the bar, and he was very proud that he did not flinch as she laced her arm around his for appearance's sake. * * * The trouble with Aiden Danvers' plan was that he mistook "I will return you to your employer in one piece" for "I will return you to your employer alive." * * * * * January 31, 2007 "The financial world is in shock that James Kennesaw Rodgers, CEO of Rodgers Consulting," a coffee-skinned reporter on the steps of the courthouse said. "The prosecution believed the case they had laid out was ironclad, but the jury disagreed after a tumultuous trial." In the long run, this verdict will not be remembered by many -- the media moved on to another story in short order. But, for this day, it was newsworthy. The girl with the light brown hair strode through the throng, a wolf amongst the sheep. Without thinking about it, she smiled when circling those few pleased by the trial's outcome and frowned around the majority, who seemed faintly displeased. Several jurors were surrounded by reporters, microphones jabbed in their faces; none seemed particularly pleased by this fact. The foreperson's face had contracted into a mass of lines and it seemed that the web work of wrinkles was all that held it together. The brunette found the woman she sought standing off to the side, thin brows furrowed over designer sunglasses. The woman wore a pink San Francisco Giants ballcap, a turquoise blouse with a scoop neck, a black miniskirt and a frown that not even the sunniest smile in the brown-haired girl's repertoire could vanquish. "Excuse me, ma'am?" the brunette interjected nervously. "I'm real sorry to bug ya, but ain't you one'a the jurors? Number...seven, I think." The other woman's frown cracked in half before she smoothed away the expression. "Are you some kind of reporter?" The girl covered her mouth to hide an embarrassed smile. "Me?" She pointed at her chest. "Oh, my heck, naw! My momma's been followin' the trial prrrrretty close, is all." "Ah." It was as dismissive as it sounds. "Sure it was fascinating." "Not...really. Kinda boring, actually. Though Mr. Rodgers' attorney - that Drew guy? He's way cute." A smirk snuck into the corners of Juror Number Seven's lips. "Mr. McFarland is quite a character, yes." She rocked up to her tiptoes -- not that such a maneuver was really necessary to peer over the young girl. "I was looking for him now, as a matter of fact. I wanted to congratulate him on doing so well, but I didn't see him at the reading. You haven't happened to see him around, Miss...I'm sorry, I didn't catch your name." The brunette put a hand to her chest, mouth forming a tiny "O". "Did I not tell you? I am so sorry." The way she said it made it sound like she said she was "sorra". "I'm Mitzi. Mitzi Melvin." She wiped her hand on her jean leg. "I'm honored." "Susanne. I don't know if I am supposed to tell people that, though." "Our little secret." Mitzi mimed locking her lips with a key. "I jus' gotta say...Momma watched a lot more than I did, but you're, like, gorgeous." The compliment dragged a full-on smile free of Susanne's worry. "Well, I wouldn't necessarily say that..." "Nonsense! Your eyes are the prettiest shade of green an' your skin!" Mitzi seized one of the Asian juror's hands, pulling her knuckles towards her face. "It's so smooth -- do you use lotion?" "Actually, I swear by this brand..." She twisted to get into her purse. "If I could have my hand back?" The two women -- whose names were neither "Susanne" nor "Mitzi," of course -- joke about it quite often. How chipper they both were at this moment. How read the smaller girl's face got as she let go of the Asian woman's left hand. How "Susanne" claims to have known something was up just because "no one in San Francisco is that nice". Whatever the case, Susanne turned to her purse and Mitzi twists to look as she slipped her ever-present #2 pencil up under Susanne's chin. It was a tiny, tiny scrape along her throat that straightened the taller woman in surprise. "Did you just poke me with your pencil?" Susanne said, slapping her hand against her throat. "Oh, my heck!" Mitzi said, displaying the implement's sharpened point with a sheepish grin. "I may've! I am awful sorry." "It's okay, as long as you promise I can't get lead poi--" Her knees buckles, eyes rolling back into her head as she collapsed onto the ground. "Oh, man!" Mitzi cried. "Somebody help! Gimme some help!" Susanne Yang -- Juror Number Seven in the James Kennesaw Rodgers trial -- reached for Mitzi Melvin's ankle until the smaller girl was shoved aside for medical personnel. Ten minutes later, an ambulance pulled away from the courthouse, siren wailing. Of course, its destination wasn't the hospital. * * * * * Sixty-nine hours and twelve minutes ago Guadalajara Miguel Almarez pressed a cold towel to the livid red knee of the curly-haired woman, who smiled around her wince. "Gracias," she said awkwardly. "Me llama es, uh..." "I speak English, hija." "Oh, thank God. I flunked out of Spanish." He laughed. "You're okay?" The dark-haired woman tilted her head a little to the left. "I-I think... I think..." Miggy's eyes narrowed with concern. "You think?" She swallowed. "I think I want to stand up." She reached up, grabbing hold of the banister. "Hija, do not strain yourself! The paramedics will be here soon." She quirked a grin over her shoulder. "If you want me to stand up right, sir, you should help me." With a sigh more akin to a grumble, Miguel Almarez stepped up to his rescued guest, putting a hand under her elbow to steady her. Once she was standing upright (and with much more alacrity than her leg injury should have allowed) the woman slid behind the elderly laundryman's back, thin arms snaking around his neck, hand joined. Many of her colleagues -- PRIME Hall of Famer Killean Sirrajin, the Model Citizen, even her nemesis Lindsay Troy -- have fallen under the relentless pressure of the Piano Wire, Cozen's version of the katahajime. They're awoken with smelling salts by the trainer and their first reaction is to hardly remember the Faceless Fighter's arms slipping around their throat. It's something when even a man as beastly powerful as Sirrajin is still feeling the hold's aftereffects an hour after ReVolution airs. Now imagine what something like that can do to a sixty-eight-year-old non-athlete. It wasn't a pretty trip into unconsciousness for Miguel Almarez, but nor was it slow. At least the Impressionable Impersonator was nice enough to hum a lullaby for him. "Hush little baby, don't say a word, Momma's gonna buy you a mockingbird," Cozen sung softly. "And if that mockingbird don't sing, Momma's gonna buy you a pretty, pretty gold belt." She canted her head to the side. "I think that's how it goes. Do you know?" Miguel Almarez provided no answer. * * * An hour later, flames licked the Guadalajara night, and the former PRIME Champion sat cross-legged with her back to the fire. A yellow legal pad lied across her lap, the tiny green nub of a pencil she'd stolen many weeks earlier from a waitress in Nebraska writing feverishly. * * * * * I am La Enemiga de Los Enemigos. I am the firestarter, twisted firestarter. If I am a loser, I will be Queen of the Losers. If I am a winner, I will be the Queen of the Ring - not the woman - JEALOUS BITCH!! Gave up her dream -- like Rapunzel, gave up her hair! -- to steal mine, like a reverse fairy godmother. If I must be an I - if there must be a me - let the me inside be the best me it can be. I will be Queen, defeat the usurper, let her eat cake until she chokes on frosting. I will have it all, back the way it was. Back the way it must be. The only time things make sense is when they don't. I am Sun Tzu. I am Lindsay Troy. I am Alex Pierce. I am Eleanor Kannon-Hall. I am woman; feel me claw your eyeballs out. I am Cozen. Cozen am I. Am I Cozen? In the end, there can be only one. You all must fall; I alone am God. Off with your head. I am the Queen of Lies. I am the Psycho-Killer - quest que c'est? I........am. Fear that.
