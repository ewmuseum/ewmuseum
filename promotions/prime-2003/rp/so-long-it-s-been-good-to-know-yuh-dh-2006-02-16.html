So long, it's been good to know yuh (DH)

16 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2-16-2006 “Hey, hey Woody Guthrie, I wrote you a song'Bout a funny ol' world that's a-comin' along.Seems sick an' it's hungry, it's tired an' it's torn,It looks like it's a-dyin' an' it's hardly been born.”--Bob Dylan ‘Song To Woody’ Hoyt was down on himself, down on the country, and down on the couch doing what most American’s do when they get down and that’s absolutely nothing. Reruns of television sitcoms create a mental prison of compliancy that just gives the mind enough to have a purpose, yet drains it of motive. Complacency, the American dream. Dare not do we dare to offend as it may create a ripple that bursts the complacency of man? For the ability to just sit and exist is now enough, is it not? Hoyt was a champion who was driven far beyond the grasp of most mortals, as he was a prophet. But just as Superman died, John Wayne cried, and Jesus Christ was crucified; even prophets, heroes, and outlaws can get the blues and die. Hoyt was aware of his funk but he didn’t know how to get out of it. He knew lying on the couch was not the solution and part of the problem yet he just couldn’t get up. Superman had Kryptonite working against him, John Wayne had time and cancer, Jesus had a disapproving government, and Hoyt had depression. Like great heroes he knew his enemy, he just couldn’t finish him off. If there wasn’t a counterbalance there would be no heroes. In other words if Hoyt killed the depression it would mean killing the man. Duke walked in and saw Hoyt’s condition and sat next to him with a disapproving plop of concern. He looked at his son then he looked at the rerun of Sanford and Son causing him to slightly chuckle becoming hypnotized by the TV’s glare, then complacent forgetting his concern of his son’s well being. Thank God a commercial break broke the trance. Duke Williams: Its time you do something. Hoyt Williams: I don’t feel like doing anything. Duke Williams: That is the best time to do something. Did you know the Great Wall of China, Microsoft, and McDonalds were all created by guys who just wanted to watch reruns of Sanford and Son but were forced off the couch by their pain in the ass fathers. Bill Gates lived in his father’s basement playing Dungeons and Dragons and other fantasy games until the day his dad Duke Gates made him go for a walk. On this journey into the sunlight Bill Gates found the answers to life and created windows. Hoyt Williams: Great! Now if he can just create a Windows that doesn’t crash. How am I going to win the Dual Halo, or get back on track? I have so many questions and nobody seems to have the answers. Duke Williams: Perhaps the answers are all around you and you don’t want to hear them. Sure Windows crashed but then he repackaged it and upgraded it, making him even more money. Why don’t you go for a walk the air can do you some good and maybe on a clear head you can find what you are looking for. So Hoyt did just that he got on his favorite Reebok jump suit and went for a walk falling for Dukes plan to get the TV to watch reruns of All in the Family. Hoyt breathed in the cold winter air and thought a lot about life, PRIME, and why Twinkie’s are so damn good. I mean sure they are high in calories but that tan cake with that cream filling is amazing. For Hoyt this would not be like any other trip and by the end of it more then the answer of Twinkies would be found. Hoyt passed by shops most of which were closed for the night. He peered into them and for no real reason other then to look. You can find a lot in shop windows like fashion ideas, new gadgets, the price of milk, and sometimes if you’re lucky even love. But answers to tough questions are not one of the things you can find in shop windows, regardless Hoyt leered in. A few ruffians were standing outside of the 24 hour gas station generally up to no good. Most people you would label as a ruffian tend not to be members of the church choir, studious students, or star athletes. As a matter of fact to say a ruffian was up to no good is a bit redundant, as ruffians are never up to good. Sometimes if the moon is right you may catch a ruffian up to some minor child’s play or some average good but never, ever, would you catch them up to anything fully good. Ruffian 1: Yo man you da fuzz? Hoyt continues walking past the young men ignoring them as he strides past. Ruffian 1: Yea, you in the jump suit you the po-po? Hoyt Williams: The Po-po? Ruffian 2: The police, the 5-0, cops, fuzz, flat feet, pig man. Are you a pig? Hoyt Williams: The 5-0? Ruffian 3: Are you a police officer? Hoyt Williams: I’m a lot of things my sinning friend, but a police man isn’t one of them. The urban youths laugh as Hoyt cries on the inside. These kids are a reflection of what Hoyt at one time in his life was, and what he looked to change about this cruel world. He smiled at the youths with a warm Jesus like grin before starting to walk on like a prophet with no direction home. Ruffian 1: Hold it Holmes. Hoyt Williams: Holmes? Ruffian 3: Yea man whatcha be doing out so late? Hoyt Williams: Seeking answers to the advanced questions of man. Ruffian 1: We can help you with that! Hoyt Williams: Really? I mean no offence but you guys don’t seem like wise men. Ruffians 3: Us, no. But we sell these little stick on tattoo’s that give people answers. Hoyt Williams: Well I guess it wouldn’t hurt to try it. Curiosity killed the cat and ruined a nice bean garden for Jack. This is where the beginning begins as Hoyt buys the stick on tattoos thinking that’s exactly what they are. But for the street wise reader you know that acid is often sold in this forum. Hoyt licks the back of the stick on tattoo of a white dove as the world begins to melt. “Come and listen to a story about a man named HoytA superstar savior, and a poor deity looking to exploit,Then one day he was looking for some food,And met some kids with a tripin' crude. Acid that is, blue gold, California tea.” Hoyt walked away from the ruffian’s with a twisted mind that seemed to float in front of him. He found Jesus, lost him again, cured cancer but forgot to tell anybody. All that happened as Hoyt crossed the street against the lights orders which to him looked like a beacon for a lost ship as opposed to a cross walk sign. He wondered like the Beach Boy’s song about a wonder lost in a daze of confusion. Perhaps now under this intoxicating state of insanity answers can be found. It wasn’t less then fifteen minutes later Hoyt found himself sitting next to a bullfrog that had a Harmonica attached to his face that he liked to blow between sharing the bottle of grape wine with Hoyt. Hoyt Williams: Frog man this is some killer wine! Jesus’ blood never tasted this good. I’m going to call you Jeremiah! The win drinking bullfrog spoke in a mumble that Hoyt couldn’t comprehend or understand. All he knew is the Bullfrog had answers and a message between making music on his harmonica, its just Hoyt couldn’t understand a single word he said but he did help him drink his wine. Hoyt Williams: And this is some mighty fine wine! Listen I have a match coming up that I have to win to be taken serious in to recapture my momentum. How do I win this match? The Bulldog shrugs his shoulders and blows sad into the mouth harp as Hoyt listens on. Hoyt Williams: That’s some deep blowing my little green buddy. Jeremiah you are one crazy frog! A stranger the world would soon know then forget stumbles into the ally. He is a young blond man covered in filth and grudge. His passport says Seattle but his eyes say heaven and his dirty finger nails tell were ever he’s from he hasn’t seen a shower in years. Stranger: His name is Bob, not Jeremiah. Hoyt Williams: You understand him? Stranger: He’s the voice and conscious of rebellious youth. Hoyt Williams: Why can’t I understand? Stranger: Because you’re not listening hard enough to the message instead you are trying to hear what you want to hear. Hoyt Williams: I don’t understand? Stranger: That’s my point man! Listen with your heart. Hoyt Williams: You’re bleeding? Stranger: Youth doesn’t last and neither does innocence. Just listen with your heart my friend I must seek my own Nirvana now. The stranger passes just as fast as he comes only now bleeding from his head exiting the ever change world. Hoyt wanted to hear more of what the stranger had to say but he also understood that for the message to be heard the massager must move on. Hoyt thought about what the young man said and realized the potential is the hook to a waiting world, while the exit before the peek keeps the message alive. Hoyt closed his eyes and listened to the frog Hoyt called Jeremiah and the stranger called Bob. Time’s were a definitely a-changin' Hoyt sat and concentrated on the frog. Soon the noise became beautiful music and the message and words become clear as Clay Akins sexuality. He listened to what the Frog had to say. Bullfrog: To find the answers seeks out Woody Guthrie. Woody holds the key, go west young man haven’t you been told California is full of whiskey, women, and gold. FIND WOODY. The frog goes back to blowing on his harp as Hoyt got up to seek out Woody Guthrie and the answers he longs for. Hoyt walks down highway 61 against the blowing wind as the Tambourine Man plays in the distance. What does it mean to find Woody Guthrie Hoyt wondered as he walked kicking a rolling stone? What seemed like miles on a road that went on forever Hoyt walked on. All he knew was he was heading west for the answers to questions he’s never really made clear. With a foggy mind and an open heart and seemingly the conscious of youth, he soldiered on. To expedite the journey Hoyt stuck his thumb out trying to hitch a ride. Most of the people driving by ignored him as if he didn’t exist. Truth is most strangers don’t exist they simply are the back drop of our surroundings. Man helping man is a tradition that was lost somewhere in the 80’s “me” decade and may never be found again. Just as Hoyt gave up on humanity an old semi-truck pulled over onto the curb and the door opened. A young man with good looks and dark hair greeted Hoyt with a smile. Hoyt hopped into the cab where the radio was cranking out an old crooner tune by Mario Lanza. Driver: Hey man hop on in! Hoyt Williams: Thank you. Driver: Where you headed? Hoyt Williams: California. Driver: Oh well I’m not going that far, I’m on a run from Memphis I’ll take you as far west as I can. Hoyt Williams: Thank you sir. What are you hauling? Driver: Rock and Roll. Hoyt Williams: Rock and Roll? Driver: I know man nobody has heard of it yet but when I deliver this load the world is going the change. They told me I couldn’t make it that this haul wasn’t worth the trip. They told me to stick to driving truck but I’m moving forward anyways. You dig me? Hoyt Williams: I think I do. Driver: If you want to realize your dreams you have to move forward despite the nay sayers. Fuck the world man, live the life you want to live. Life is what you make of it and your dreams can’t be shaken if you expect to succeed. I will deliver this load and its going to change everything. You understand me man, EVERYTHING. Hoyt rationalized what the diver said as he stared out at the country around him. The world is always interesting and calm when you look at it through a windshield. The kid did make a lot of sense but what the hell does a truck driver know. The world outside of the truck seemed strange as it flew by fast. Hoyt glanced over at the speedometer and saw that the driver was going over 200mph. Hoyt Williams: In a hurry? Driver: Live fast, love hard, die young. Man have you tried these Twinkie’s? Hoyt Williams: I love them I wonder what makes them so good! Driver: A Twinkie doesn’t care. You ever see the Twinkie guy in the package? He’s a cowboy made of a Twinkie with a lasso and an attitude, man. You see the question shouldn’t be what makes the Twinkie so good, who cares, all that matters is you like it. So enjoy it. Don’t worry about what makes the clock tick just keep your eye on it because Time always marches on. You understand me? If you’re to busy worrying about how then you are missing all that’s around you. Fuck How, just do it. Just like culture, people’s interests and tastes change just like Hoyt’s interest in this conversation. Hoyt closes his eyes and drifts away to the soothing sound of the young truck drivers voice. Miles and hours pass before he is woken not by the gentle smile of the young man but by a rather overweight man with a worn out face. Driver: This is as far as I go man. We delivered the load, changed the world, and I’m still not happy. This ride is over for both of us. I wish you luck. Hoyt Williams: Thank you very much. The truck driver smiled for a second with his boyish smile back again before snapping at the exiting Hoyt. Driver: That’s my line kid! Good luck. Hoyt Williams: Where I am? Driver: Memphis. Hoyt Williams: I thought we were going west? Driver: So did I but we’ve been going south this whole ride. They were right. Take care man. The driver takes off in a truck with a flat tire and memory’s of many miles behind them. Hoyt stands in the middle of the street confused and lost. The young truck driver had a lot to say, but he failed in helping Hoyt get closer to his goal of the West coast. Despite no direction Hoyt continues walking tired but invigorated by the conversation with the trucker. He knows he should be hungry but he isn’t he just keeps footing on. He passes small towns, corn fields, and blues clubs. Robert Louis Stevenson was a great author who wrote classics like Treasure Island. Later in his life he became a recluse and lived in seclusion on an island. When people would visit he would greet them in his bathrobe and slippers. Hoyt thought about him as he walked down the dusty rode. Perhaps Robert Louis Stevenson had the right idea but then again how much of life did he miss imprisoning himself. Hoyt didn’t want to become Robert Louis Stevenson and perhaps his father was right to get him out of the house. Down the road a ways Hoyt saw the figure of a man. Was it Woody Guthrie with the answers or was it another stranger. Hitchhiker: Hey stranger. What’s going down? The man is not Woody Guthrie but rather a young black gentleman with a guitar over his shoulder. He wears a top hat tilted to the left with a very sharp suit on. You can see the eagerness in his eyes as he approaches Hoyt with a big smile and a handshake. Hoyt is reluctant but greets the man with a warm smile reciprocating the handshake. Hitchhiker: Stranger its strange meeting you out here at the crossroads. Are you the man who is here to meet me for guitar lessons? Hoyt Williams: I’m just a wrestler and savior drifting looking for answers. Hitchhiker: Savior? Hoyt Williams: The son of God. The young mans eyes turn wide and white and bulge out of his head. Hitchhiker: Mister I’m yet to make the deal please don’t hurt me. Hoyt Williams: I’m not here to hurt you. Where are we? Hitchhiker: The crossroads U.S. Highway 61 and U.S. Highway 49 in Clarksdale, Mississippi! I’m supposed to meet a man who is going to teach me how to play guitar. Hoyt Williams: Can he give me a ride too, I’m trying to get to California. Hitchhiker: Back to the land of California, to my sweet home Chicago. Hoyt Williams: Ahhh yea, anyways I needs a ride I’m sure this guitar teacher will give me a ride. Hitchhiker: Mister, you don’t want a ride with this man. Sometimes you got to give it all to get what you need. I’m giving it all and you shouldn’t be here for this. Hoyt Williams: Nonsense no guitar teacher can scare me I’m the Pontiff of PRIME! Hitchhiker: What is PRIME? Hoyt Williams: It is the biggest wrestling promotion in the world and my vehicle of propaganda. Hitchhiker: Pardon me for saying sir but you sure talk funny. Hoyt Williams: I’m a fast talker and a living legend. Hitchhiker: Oh Sir, you sure are I believe. I’m going to be a star to some day just you wait and see. I’m giving everything for my career. What have you given? Hoyt stops and thinks about that question as a long black Rolls Royce rolls up in front of them. Hoyt watches as the eager young man turns a pale white as he is obvious nervous. The door opens and a snake wearing a suit slithers out. Hoyt Williams: Good luck. Hitchhiker: So long Mister and same to you. Hoyt takes the kids advice and doesn’t ask for a ride. The snakes eyes looked evil something was obviously not right with him. Besides the fact a snake has no arms and can’t teach guitar something appeared Machiavellian and evil about the snake. The light of an early dawn was on the horizon. Hoyt watched as the sun rose a true miracle of existence and one of the gifts of his father. Hoyt sat in a bare corn field and thought about his victory over Killean for the Universal title and what a special moment that was for him. Not to mention the magic that was going into getting there and the joy of victory. A powder blue 1952 Cadillac pulls up to Hoyt and the back door opens. In it is a tall, thin, pale man wearing a fancy cowboy suit laid back with a bottle of whiskey. He grins a sinister smile to Hoyt and motions for him to come closer. Backseat driver: Hey kid you need a ride? Hoyt Williams: I need to head west. Backseat driver: Get in the passenger seat I have the back all wrapped up. You look like you have a broken heart just like me. What’s your pain? Hoyt gets into the front seat and a faceless driver pulls away as sad old songs which are all solid country gold are blasting out of the speakers of the radio. The stranger lies back with a cowboy hat over his head, almost seems ghost like as both him and the driver appear to be transparent. The back seat driver talks with an uneducated country drawl. Hoyt Williams: I have no pain. Hoyt doesn’t tell the full story as he just smiles nervously to the ghoulish being. He is in pain his wife is a week away from having his child and he is at a low point in his career. The politics of the RISP is also in a bit of trouble as investigators have been nosing around those high in power in the organization. Backseat diver: You’re so full of shit man. Hoyt Williams: Fuck you. The thin man just laughs out loud as he remains laid back. He takes a swig of whiskey and offers the bottle to Hoyt. Our hero turns it down as his head is already spinning and he doesn’t trust the drifting cowboy who besides being wasted seems crazy. Backseat Driver: Why are you headed to California? A year ago the answer would be an exuberant yes, but after the shit with Aimz and Youngblood that question is not so easy to answer. Maybe this isn’t what is best for Hoyt Williams maybe he needs to move on. All these questions shoot through Hoyt’s head yet still no answers. Comment and lack of acclaim shatter his fragile ego and upset him. Hank: Now pardon me while I take a nap. Hoyt doesn’t sleep instead he listens to the songs on the radio. The old hick music has a soothing tone that pacifies his worried mind. A few hours later while the drifting cowboy is still asleep in the back seat Hoyt exits the car in front of a restaurant. Hoyt watches the car drive off carrying the body of a man who made the world cry. The marquee to the restaurant is weird and wonderful as a giant Sunday is written in black. The atmosphere around the greasy spoon is dark with bats circling ahead. Hoyt is hungry and has survived eating at a Denney’s after midnight so he’s seen weird before and so he takes the risk, entering the front door with the devil head door handle. You need to relax. The two men sit in silence for the rest of the trip as Hoyt again watches the world pass by, town to town. He smiles as he watches a kid kicks a can down the street and remembers a time in his life doing the same. Perhaps the frail brakeman was right and the answers are in the past. A black man with a backwards bandana and diamond nose ring begins to approach Hoyt. A cold sweat of fear overcomes him as he knows this could be the end. Black youth in poor areas of major cities tend not to approach you to sell you a paper. They tend to have more devious motives. Gangster: What you doing here? Hoyt Williams: Just passing by. And that is what Hoyt does he ran and ran for his life. A far cry from the comforts and self pity of the couch where this story began, to wanting to live so much he is running with every ounce of life in him. To truly understand if one wants to live he must face death. Hoyt ran, and ran, and ran. He ran for life, he ran towards his answers, he ran from his problems, ran from the poor, ran from the world around him. He ran, and ran, and ran. He ran from his father, ran from his past, ran from the Universal Title, ran from his talent. Yes Hoyt ran, and ran, and ran. He ran from love, ran from commitment, ran from danger, ran from the critics. Finally Hoyt stopped running and caught his breath. You can’t run for ever the body just doesn’t allow it nor is it healthy to the mind. Sometime you have to stop and face the music (pardon the pun) and the time was now to do that. The time to face the facts had arrived and nothing was going to change that. Hoyt walked into an old hotel named “California” where the man at the desk was dressed in black. Hoyt looked around and thought to himself what a lovely place as he continued towards the desk clerk in black. Desk Clerk: Hello, I’m Johnny Cash. Hoyt Williams: That’s it? NO metaphors or symbolism? I don’t have to guess who you are you’re just going to tell me before giving me some shitty advice. Desk Clerk: Yup, it’s just me because that is my style I just tell it like it is. I don’t waste time with symbolism and that other garbage I’m just straight forward. Hoyt Williams: Ok so what’s the advice you’re sure to pour on me in your deep voice like gods. Johnny Cash chuckles in that hardy laugh heard on numerous recordings from his past. Desk Clerk: That is my advice, be straight forward Hoyt. Enough of this bullshit of trying to be cute or creative. You got where you got because like me you just told it as it was and were funny and personal in the process. You’re not fancy, well structured, or metaphoric. You are honest and down to earth and people appreciate that. That’s all the advice I have for you. That and stay away from the cocaine. Hoyt Williams: You sure you don’t have anything else? Desk Clerk: Change your theme song to my version of the song. Hoyt Williams: Your version is to slow. Desk Clerk: Suit yourself hoss, it was just some advice from a guy who changed the world. Hoyt Williams: You know where I can find Woody Guthrie? Desk Clerk: He’s on the sixth floor. Would you like to check in? Hoyt Williams: Once I check in can I leave? Desk Clerk: You’ve heard the song of the Hotel California I see. No you can’t. Hoyt Williams: Then I think I’ll wait. Desk Clerk: We’ll be waiting for you. Johnny Cash fades like a ghost as Hoyt steps into the elevator. He waits what seems like an eternity to get to the sixth floor. The doors open on the dust bowl of the dirty 30’s in Oklahoma. Hoyt fights the winds and dusty haze as he travels on. Hoyt watches the suffering, pain, and destruction along with the fighting spirit of an inspired nation. A far cry from the world he lives in now of complacency and reruns. Hoyt spots an old building and walks into see a man with a guitar stroking the conscious of America. Hoyt Williams: Woody? Woody Guthrie: I am. You must be Hoyt. Hoyt Williams: Have you been expecting me? Woody Guthrie: I have. I know of your questions and I’m glad to see you have found the answers now go and win at the Dual Halo for me. Woody packs up his guitar and begins to walk away. Hoyt Williams: Wait one God damn minute you haven’t given me any answers. Woody Guthrie: All your questions were answered if you were listening. The answers are the journey. You see it’s the paths we travel that lead us to our answers. Advice, solution, and answers are around us everyday, you just have to look to see them. You have to get out and find them which is what you did today. I do not have the answers Hoyt Williams the world around you does and your journey forced you to see them first hand. What makes a man is the road he has traveled and the lessons he has learned. Congratulations, now you can win the Dual Halo and recapture your lost luster. Hoyt Williams: This is bullshit. Woody Guthrie: No its life, you can’t always get what you want. Let me ask you did you learn something today? Hoyt Williams: I guess so. Woody Guthrie: Then maybe your attitude is bullshit and the answers have been solved. Hoyt Williams: Maybe you’re right Mr. Guthrie I can and will win the Dual Halo because I understand what is at sake more so then any other member of the roster. I have been around long and seen more then anybody on the roster. I can and will win this thing I have Woody Guthrie, Jesus Christ, and God on my side!! Hoyt Williams is back baby tell the world and sing it loud! “I've sung this song, but I'll sing it again,Of the people I've met and the places I've seen.Of some of the troubles that bothered my mindAnd a lot of good people that I've left behind, saying: So long, it's been good to know yuh;So long, it's been good to know yuh;So long, it's been good to know yuh.What a long time since I've been home,And I've gotta be driftin' along.”--Woody Guthrie =w=
