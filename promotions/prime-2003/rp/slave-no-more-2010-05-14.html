Slave No More

14 May 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14th May The images were so vivid they felt like yesterday. Medics swarming the body like flies to light, and the blood in his own veins turning to concrete. It didn't take long for the crowd to realise that their hero wouldn't be getting up for a long time, and even less for them to turn on his aggressor. Jeers rained a debris flew as he backed away and slid out of the ring. Medics lifted the limp pile of skins and bones to slide a stretcher underneath, and he saw his downed foe's foot twitching as they set his body down again. It was too much. Fans scratched and clawed at him as he ambled up the ramp, guilt-stricken. He disappeared backstage and left the arena without even collecting his bag, but there was no running from what had happened. When he got home he locked the door and put his head down, still in his wrestling gear. The phone rang after a sleepless night; his quivering digits picked it up on the fifth ring. “Hello,” he mumbled. Then voice on the other end confirmed his greatest fear. At the age of twenty-four, Jeffrey Stone's childhood dream had turned into a nightmare. * * * * * “All great things must first wear terrifying and monstrous masks in order to inscribe themselves on the hearts of humanity.” - Friedrich Neitzsche. * * * * * Paul always forgot about the stench. He'd walked through the stairwell countless times but still the grody miasma of stale sweat and urine jarred him as it had the first time. Thankfully, he only had two floors' worth of tatty carpets and peeling wallpaper to pass through and his deep breath lasted long enough to reach the door. Paul wrapped his knuckles against wood that was in dire need of a varnish and stood waiting in the discoloured hallway for a few seconds more. Eventually the deadbolt clicked and the door opened as far as it's chain would allow. “Hello, Mallory,” Paul said to the gaunt face that peered through the crack. The door closed but was quickly reopened. She stood beyond the threshold in an old TJ Maxx dress, greeting him with a crooked smile. “Hey, Paul. How are you?” “Can't complain,” he said, shrugging. “Is my brother around?” Nah, shouldn't be much longer though. Wanna come in and wait?” “Yeah.” A nod. “Yeah, alright.” His brother's studio wasn't exactly The Ritz but Paul was just glad to be out of the pungent lobby. He took a good look around the modest abode as the door slid shut behind him. Rectangular in shape with a bed at one end and a kitchenette at the other, it was scantly bigger than most peoples' living rooms. Off-white walls dusted with mould and damp and a brown carpet that needed replacing twenty years ago surrounded their second-hand furniture, and the chair that Paul sat down on creaked unhealthily as it took his weight. “You want some coffee?” Mallory asked, scuttling over to the cluttered worktop. She was a curious thing. Short, bony, and with barely enough meat on her to feed a family of rats, Paul's brother's partner could easily have been confused for an anorexic. Her shoulder-length black hair was left straggly and unkempt, and her high cheek bones pushed awkwardly towards her round, green orbs. “Nah, I'm good,” Paul said as she pulled two cups from the drying rack. “You sure? Just made a fresh pot. Still warm.” “Oh go on. Black, no sugar.” Mallory poured the kettle's water into the cups and clanked a spoon around them one at a time. She came over to the table and handed Paul his mug, smiling. He took it with a “thank you”. “So, what're you looking for Alex for?” “Just thought I'd come over and see how you two are doin'.” Paul swallowed his first mouthful and tried not to grimace; he hated instant coffee. “And, uhhh. I got something for him.” He reached inside his blue jacket and pulled out a manilla envelope. Folding it out to it's full A4 size, Paul placed it on the table. Mallory pulled the envelope towards her curiously. It was sealed and her boyfriend's name head been neatly inked across the front. “What is it?” “Just some wrestling stuff I thought he should take a look at.” “He'll probably just throw it in the trash. You know how crabby he gets when we try to interfere with his wrestling...” “I'm not trying to interfere,” Paul said, “I'm trying to help. He's been wasting his talent in bingo halls and community centres for far too long. He's 25! He should be coming home to something a little grander than this by now.” His voiced tailed off as he looked around the room again, before snapping back round to Mallory. “No offence.” “None taken,” she said, acknowledging her surroundings. “I'll pass it onto him but I can't guarantee he'll read it. Hell, I don't even mention the wrestling to him any more. Just isn't worth the stress.” “Thanks, Mal.” Paul rose from his chair, and taking his coffee with him, stepped over to the apartment's only window. Baltimore had been threatening to rain since the crack of dawn, but the overcast sky had yet to yield a downpour. “How are you guys doing? In general, I mean.” “We get by,” she said. Though her living conditions were far from ideal, Mallory never sounded anything less than enthusiastic. “Got a couple extra shifts at the diner this month and Alex has been getting a lot of work down at the plant, so that oughta lift us outta the red for a little while.” She paused. “You seem to be doing alright for yourself. New shoes?” Paul turned, looking down at his shiny loafers. “These? Nah, just had them polished.” He looked at Mallory and sipped. Though she seemed oddly content with the state of their life, it saddened Paul that his brother lived in such squalor. Though he was no doubt content with his relationship, things could've been a lot better for Alexander Hughes. Paul knew little about wrestling, but he did know that his brother was good; damn good, in fact, and that he was working below himself. If it wasn't for the accident, Alex would've been on his way to becoming a household name by now. Paul sighed. He could not spend any more time hi his brother's downtrodden pit. “I gotta go, Mal. Thanks for the coffee,” he said abruptly. “But you only just got here,” Mallory protested as he set his half-finished cup down on the table. “At least finish your Joe.” “I got a lot to do,” he said, pulling the door open. “Take care.” Paul was gone before Mallory could utter a response. She pondered the suddenness of his departure only for a few seconds before noticing the envelope again. Alex didn't care for Mallory and Paul meddling with his career, despite their good intentions. She knew what would happen: Alex would come home, she'd give him the envelope, and he'd throw it in the bin without even opening it. Shrugging, Mallory pushed her thumb behind the seal and began to pry the letter open. Alex wasn't going to read it anyway; what harm would it do? * * * * * He strode down the street faster than usual: the clouds were growing darker and darker, and he hadn't brought a raincoat. Hurrying round a corner, Alex came to the street he was looking for and walked towards Selena Jacobs' house. The once pleasant building had fallen into disrepair. Moss clung to the redbrick walls and the front lawn was at least up to his own knees, if not higher. Caked in a year's worth of grime, the windows were far from the sparkly portholes they should've been, and the roof was missing a few slates from where they'd come loose a slid off. It was a shame, he thought; all the house needed was a week's worth of tender loving care and it would no longer stick out against the neighbourhood's tidy aesthetic. Alex placed his hand atop the mailbox by the driveway and went to pull it open, but something stopped him. A child's laughter from within, followed by the pitter-patter of small footsteps behind a brick wall. Selena's children. Sammy's children. There were three of them in total, and boy, what a noise they made. It took a shrill wail from their mother to calm them down, but even then their discontent was audible from outside. He paused. If there was anything more he could do to ease the suffering of the women behind those walls, Alex would have done it. For a moment he contemplated knocking on her door, getting on his knees, and begging her forgiveness, if only for his own sake. It wasn't enough, though. He'd give anything in the world to rewind the clock by two years and prevent this from even happening, but there was nothing he could do to reverse the damage he'd done. Alex could knock on Selena's door, sure, but who's to say she'd even open it? And even if she did, she had no reason to listen to him. Alex had ruined her life – he'd ruined her kids' lives – and she could never forgive him. Besides, what use was Selena's forgiveness without her father-in-law's? With a heavy heart, Alex pulled open the rusty mailbox as he'd done tens of times before. He pulled a stuffed envelope from his jeans' pocket and slid it inside, before flashing one last quick glance at the silhouettes behind the filthy windows. As he turned away from the property he felt a tiny droplet splash against his shorn head. It was raining. * * * * * It took the bus forty minutes longer than it should have to get back to Alex's block and the rain was truly lashing it down by the time he stepped outside. He jogged towards his building and then up the stairs, before sliding the key into his lock and twisting. Mallory had forgotten to put the chain on. “Hey!” she said as Alex walked briskly into the room. “You're back.” “Uh-huh.” The stubbled fighter pulled his sodden navy hoody over his shoulders and hung it to dry over the open bathroom door. The white t-shirt below was clammy, but not wet enough to warrant a change. Alex walked through the apartment and sat down on the end of the bed, leaning over to thumb the television into life. Tired, he crawled towards the headrest and propped himself up. “I'm making meatloaf,” Mallory called from the kitchen as her boyfriend idly flicked through channels. “Smells good.” Closing the oven door with a rusty grind, Mallory took off her apron and let it rest over a chair. She walked round to the bed and sat down beside Alex. His face was pale and his expression stoic. “Rough day?” she asked. “Eh, nah. Just had a few errands to run.” “Kinda errands?” “Just some work stuff.” Mallory accepted his answer with an unseen nod, though the lack of emotion in his smoke-ravaged drawl bothered her. “You okay?” “'Course I am.” “Just seem a little, I dunno... off?” “I just took a one-hour bus trip sat next to the fattest guy in Baltimore.” Alex turned, facing Mallory for the first time since coming home. “You wanna try getting pressed up against a window by Sammy Sweatypants for that length of time without wanting to throttle the fucker?” “Ewww, gross.” “Exactly.” Mallory glanced at the television: Alex had settled on a local news broadcast. “Hey, listen,” she said. “Paul came by earlier.” “Oh?” Alex's interest peaked. “How's he doing?” “Good, I think. Seemed it anyway. He brought you something.” Mallory slid her gangly frame over the edge of the bed and went over to the table. Picking up the opened envelope and traipsing back across, she handed it to Alex who dragged his attention away from the idiot box and took it with both hands. The first thing he noticed during his examination was the broken seal. “You opened it?” “Well, yeah. I figured you were just gonna throw it out anyway.” “That's not the point, Mal.” Alex scowled, his tone deepened. “This is my mail. Couldn't you have waited 'til I came home?” She sighed. “Just read it, Alex.” Though discontented, Alex saw no gain from arguing and pulled the documents from within. The first thing he noticed was the bold PRIME letterhead. The second was the heading, Born Again Tournament Information Pack. Immediately he looked up at his girlfriend. “PRIME.” He groaned. “Are you kidding me?” “They're a big company, aren't they?” “Yeah, that's the problem.” “'Problem'?” “Do you have any idea how many guys are going to try to sign up for this thing?” he said, exasperated. “When the biggest wrestling promotion swings it's doors open, people take notice. What makes you think they'd be interested in a mook like me, huh? Besides, I'm an indy guy. I don't do mainstream. Remember the Sex Pistols? In 1977 they were the most subversive thing in the world, and now John Lydon lives in a country mansion and advertises car insurance. That ain't me.” “But look here,” Mallory said, pointing to a block of text. “'All and any may apply: all applications will be considered, regardless of experience and background.'” “I know it says that Mal, but c'mon. Hundreds of guys are gonna apply for this--” “Goddamn it, Alex!” Mallory yelped, bashing a tiny fist into her thigh. “Would you listen for a change?! Maybe if you didn't keep turning your nose up at stuff like this we wouldn't be living in this fucking shithole. Now I'm sorry if you're upset that I opened it, but that's a damn good opportunity and you're not even considering it.” “I won't make it, Mal. I'm just not marketa--” “How do you know if you don't even call them?!” Mallory screamed. The volume took not only Alex by surprise, but herself also. She paused for a moment or two to control her breathing and regain composure, before sitting down and taking Alex's paw in her own. “Look, Alex: I know you don't like us interfering with your wrestling stuff, but this is a great opportunity and it probably took a lot of hard work for Paul to track it down. So you might not make it, but the least you can do is pick up the phone and register your interest.” “I don't know, Mallory.” “Please,” she said, squeezing his hand tighter. “For me.” Alex's chest heaved. He nodded. “Okay,” he conceded, “for you.” * * * * * The storm had passed, the clouds had cleared, and night had fallen. Leaning on a railing, he gazed out from the cold, dark waters of Baltimore's harbour to the sea of stars above. A picture of rare beauty to brighten the industrial sprawl around him. Though warmed by the Marlboro Red between his lips, Alex was beginning to feel the cold. It was with relief, then, that he saw his brother's car crawl into his peripheral vision. Paul pulled the vehicle up behind his brother and slid out of the door. “Couldn't you have just called, Jeffrey?” he said as he walked towards his younger sibling. “This is a bit too Michael Mann for my liking.” “I figured we should talk face-to-face – it's been a while, after all,” he replied between puffs. “And it's 'Alex', by the way.” “You're my brother: you'll never be Alex Hughes to me.” Wrapping his fingers around cold steel, Paul looked out to the horizon and took in two lungfuls of crisp, night air. His brother extended his cigarette carton towards Paul, who waved him away. “Nah. Trying to limit myself to a half-carton a day.” “Suit yourself.” A silence fell over the two, but it did not last. “So you're doing it? This 'Born Again' thing?” Alex nodded. “Gonna try at least. The fuss Mallory was kicking-up, man. Shit.” “I've been saying it to you ever since the accident, Jeff. You gotta recover. Four years ago you were one of the hottest up-and-comers in the indy scene, and now look at you.” “It's just risky, Paul,” Alex said, looking his brother in the eye. “If certain people find out that the man they knew as 'Jeffrey Stone' is out there making a living from a sport he was exiled from... well, I don't wanna think of what might happen.” “Of course it's a risk. Everything you do in wrestling's a risk now, man, but it's not like you've got much else to fall back on. You didn't graduate high-school, didn't go to college... this is all you got.” “It ain't that bad,” Alex shrugged. “Isn't it?” Paul raised an eyebrow. “I don't mean to cause offence, brother, but I've seen squats that are cleaner than your apartment. I'd be ashamed to live there on my own; let alone with anyone else.” “Mallory don't complain.” “I'm sure she would if she knew you were still siphoning $4,000 a month to Selena Jacobs.” Suddenly Alex's facial features turned sour. “What else am I supposed to do? It's not like I can just stroll up to her porch and make everything alright. 'Most all my paycheck goes to that woman, but it ain't like I got a choice.” “I'm sorry, I didn't mean to--” “Yeah, well. This is my hole to dig myself out of. That's why I don't like you guys interfering.” Another pause. Alex tapped a few millimetres of ash from his cigarette as his brother cleared his throat. “You told her yet?” Paul asked. “About what happened?” “Mallory? Nah.” “You know you're gonna have to eventually, right? How long's it been – two years? Can't keep hiding this from her forever,” Paul shit. “Shit, I bet she doesn't even know you changed your name.” “I'll tell her when the time is right,” Alex replied. He closed his eyes and sighed deeply. “Look, Paul, if all you want is to lecture me then I'd rather you didn't. I know I've made mistakes, but I know what I'm doing. Trust me.” “I know, brother. Just looking out for you is all. It's just, you've really let this whole thing ruin you. It's time to start digging upwards. I know that there'll probably be a lot of competition for this PRIME thing, but it's a start. You can't let our uncle's shadow loom over you for the rest of your life.” “Yeah, yeah. I got it.” “I'd best get going before Kim starts wondering where I am.” Paul pulled himself away from the railing and gently patted his brother's back. “You take care, Jeffrey”. And in a number of seconds Paul Stone had driven away into the night, leaving his brother alone with his thoughts. Standing up straight, Alex cleared his throat. His mind jogged through the state of his living conditions and the black hole his bank account had become. The weight on his mind hung heavier as thoughts of Selena Jacobs raising three children alone passed through his head, but it all came back to Mallory. The life he was providing her was barely fit for a cockroach, but now he had a chance to do something about it. Flicking his cigarette into the water, Alex smiled. It was time to take his life back.
