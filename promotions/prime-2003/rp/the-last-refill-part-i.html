The Last Refill: Part I

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: The week of Thanksgiving ~The Day Before Thanksgiving~ As he listened to the ringing of the phone, pulsing in his right ear, Matt stared down at the rust-colored prescription bottle in his hand and he sighed. locked his eyes on the very last line of font. Rolling the bottle back and forth with his fingertips, he let his eyes wander, skimming the medical mumbo-jumbo, but in the end, all he cared about was the last line of font, tucked away under the pharmacy logo at the very bottom of the label. 'No refills remaining' The ringing ceased, giving way to the gravely sound of Rob Hicken’s voicemail recording, and when it finished, Matt waited anxiously for the beep. “Robbie… it’s Matt. Listen, I’m runnin’ low on Valium and I don’t have any refills left on this prescription. Got maybe a half-dozen or so pills and that’s it. I was just hopin’ I could swing by later today or first thing tomorrow and get at least a couple week’s worth. Gimme a call when ya get this. Later.” He pulled the phone away from his hear and pressed the ‘END’ key, nothing to do but wait. Looking back down at the bottle, Matt gave it shake, the remaining pills ricocheting against the sides—the sound of an infant’s rattle, and as the medication came to a rest, he frowned, like a child having his favorite toy taken away. ~The Day of Thanks~ Around 10:00 PM “Wow” Matt looked down at his plate, eying the remnants of a slice of pumpkin pie, and he slunked down in his chair, trying to find some place for his expanded guts to go. “Dessert was a bad idea.” “I think it was going back for ‘thirds’ that was the bad idea.” “Probably right.” “It comes from years of experience. I’ve made more than my fair share trips to load up another plate. There are consequences.” Dean Ward gave his son a slap on the shoulder. “It’d help if you drank more diet pop.” “I don’t need to drink diet pop, Chris.” Dean shot his wife a look. “It’s all that fake-sugar substitute garbage that probably gave me cancer in the first place.” For the first time in several minutes, Mary Ward injected her thoughts as she gathered up the plates of pie crust, clearing the table. “Everything these days causes cancer.” “Well, I don’t know what causes it, but it pretty much stinks. That’s why you should get a check-up twice a year. Cuz unfortunately, they think it can have something to do with genetics.” Dean waited for an affirmative response, but his son sat with a blank stare. “So don’t say I never gave ya nothing.” He slapped his son on the shoulder, but it wasn’t until Mary looked Matthew right in the eyes and said his name that any response came. “Matthew?” “Huh?” he snapped back into the conversation. “Are you ok, sweetheart?” “Yeah. Yeah, fine. Must be the trytophan or whatever it is in the turkey.” “You sure?” Mary dumped the pie crumbs in the garbage as she looked back at her husband with concern. “Your eyes look kinda glazed over.” “Just tired. That’s all.” “Then we should get going.” Chris Ward pushed her seat away from the table. And started to grab a plate. “No, Chris. Matthew and I meant it when we said you two weren’t going to lift a finger. This Thanksgiving dinner was all on us.” “Just cuz I have cancer doesn’t mean I can’t do the dishes.” “Honestly, Dean, you two should go home and get some rest. We’ll clean up” Mary leaned over and kissed her father-in-law on the cheek. With a bit of a struggle, Matthew managed to peel himself from his chair and he walked his parents to the front door, kissing his mother on the cheek. “Thanks for comin’, Mom.” “Thanks for having us. And I’m cooking Christmas dinner. No arguments.” “Deal.” Matt turned to his father and gave him a solid hug, comlete with a heavy pat on the back. “Good to see you can still put away ten pounds of turkey, old man.” “Some things never change.” The two generations of Ward men exchanged a brief chuckle before Matthew’s parents headed out onto the porch, waving goodbye as they made their way to the car. Closing the door behind them, Matt turned and headed back toward his wife. “You want some help cleanin’ up?” “Nah, I got it. You promised Georgie you would read her a bedtime story. You better get in there or she’s likely to storm out here and drag you back there by the hand.” “That she might.” Something about the way he said it didn’t seem right. “You sure you’re okay, sweetie?” “I’m fine.” He winked and kissed Mary on the forehead before making his way down the hall toward the bedroom of his little angel. “Good enough to get through a Curious George book anyways.” ~The Night Before Thanksgiving~ He crossed the kitchen and picked up his phone, noticing the blinking light. As he flipped through the main screen, Matt saw a new text message waiting for him. From: Robbie Got your stuff. Swing by whenever With a faint smile crawling across his face, Matt reached into the pocket of his jeans and pulled out the same pill bottle that had rattled-away only hours earlier. Now, completely empty, it made no sound as he lifted the lid to the trashcan and tossed the bottle away. ~Black Friday~ Just After Midnite Mary put the centerpiece back in the middle of the table, tugging on its multi-colored leaves, trying to make each hue stand-out. As she surveyed the kitchen, she gave a nod of the head to no one in particular, content with the state of order she had restored, but as her eyes caught the clock on the wall, she sighed. 12:04 AM. Friday already, and on cue, she yawned and headed for the bedroom. To her surprise, Matthew wasn’t curled up under the sheets as she had expected. With an early flight out the next morning, she had imagened he would retire for the evening as soon as he’d finished reading to Georgie. Instead, the only thing she found in bed was his suitcase, lying open, still not completely packed. “Oh, sweetie…” He’d been in the business of professional wrestling for ten years, and in a full decade, he still hadn’t learned the art of packing a bag, how to avoid procrastination, or prioritizing for that matter. His Elite Championship title laid folded neatly in the middle of the bag, but a belt to acuatlly hold up his pants… it laid hanging off the edge of the bed.With the same rapid fire movements she had used to return the kitchen to sparkling state, Mary set to work, rolling up the belt and sticking it in the corner of the suitcase. Rearraging his socks and shirts in an attempt to make more room, a small black bottle rolled free of the clothing. Instintively, she picked it up and popped the cap. Inside, she saw what must have been a hundred pills, nearly filling the bottle to the brim. There was no need for a doctorate, the same pills had been around the house for the last few months, only in typical prescription bottles. They were Valium. As it all came together, she felt her stomach tie itself in a bundle, and the knot rose into her throat, forcing her to choke it back. For weeks, she had wondered, seem signs that made her uncomfortable, but an unmarked pill bottle was the proof in her hands. Matthew was abusing his prescription medications. Tearing through the clothes in the bag, she dug through the mounds of cotton till she found what she was looking for. A second unmarked bottle, this one with a different type of pill inside. Probably the Vicodin. Squeezing the bottles in between her fingers, she turned and stormed out of the bedroom, her heels nearly tearing holes in the carpet. “Matthew!” Her heart raced against her steps, seeing which could thump faster. Where the hell was he? “Matth…” As she passed Georgie’s room, she stopped dead in her tracks. Their baby girl was sound asleep in bed, the covers pulled up under her chin, and sitting in the chair at the side of her bed, Matthew sat in a slumber of his own, Curious George folded against his chest a couple pages from the ending. His head hung heavy, pulling his shoulders from the back of the chair, leaning him across Georgie. On any other night, it was an image that would have made her smile, maybe even run and grab the camera. But, as Mary looked down at the two unmarked bottles in her hand, there were no thoughts of capturing the moment to last. There was only the return of that lump in her throat. “…Matthew.” Reaching in through the doorway, Mary flipped the lightswitch on the wall, blanketing the scene in the same black of the bottles. Again she trudged through the house, this time with slower steps. As she entered the kitchen, she lifted the lid to the trashcan and tossed the pills in the garbage, right alongside the pie crusts, cold turkey, and the empty bottle with a label that read ‘No refills remaining’.
