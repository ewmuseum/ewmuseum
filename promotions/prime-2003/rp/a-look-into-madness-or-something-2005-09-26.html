A Look Into Madness. Or Something.

26 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 26th, 2005 So… Farwell lost his match this week. I guess you all know what that means, huh? No more continuity. You could’ve seen what happened next at the convention, but no. You had to have it your way and make James lose, and now I’m not writing the damn thing. You can blame Chris for not no-showing, and Sebs for not disqualifying his roleplay due to sheer lack of crappiness. Their combined incompetence has earned me yet another defeat. Damn them. Damn them to hell. … Sigh. I can never get a Goddamn break. So, instead of showing you what did actually come next that day, I’ll wait. And I’ll make you wait, too. Once Farwell picks up another win, the story continues. Simple as that. Until then, however… July 7th, 1974 “Sweetie, could you take the garbage out on your way?” Fade in to the typical ‘70s décor. Green furniture in the living room, peach wallpaper, flower prints in the kitchen, the whole nine yards. If you’re thinking of That ‘70s Show right now, you’re thinking too high class. Imagine something a few steps below the poverty line. Just about right for a man who works long hours as a janitor just to keep the rent paid, with a wife who’s only ever been a homemaker, and three children to look after. One of which wasn’t even his. But wait…three kids, you ask? Well, yes. But we’ll get into that a little later on. It’s not important right now. “Take it out yourself.” That, however, would be the one that’s not theirs. A fifteen-year-old James Farwell pushed out the back door, muttering an insult under his breath as he exited the house. Undergoing a slightly enhanced version of teenage rebellion, and displaying a full head of long stringy hair, James broke into a quick jog in order to get away from the house, only slowing when he knew he was out of earshot. Thank God I’m out of that damn house, he thought to himself while lighting up a cigarette. His foster parents didn’t know he smoked, or that he did any of the other things he did, but it never stopped him in the past. And it sure wasn’t about to stop him today. Fast approaching a group of teenagers standing at the corner of the sidewalk, dressed in much the same fashion as himself, James nodded in their direction and kept walking past them, as they quickly turned and flanked him. “You’re not gonna go chicken shit on us, are ya, Fartwell?” One of the older boys prodded James, hoping for some sort of reaction. From the look in his eyes, it was obvious he was looking for a fight. That, and he was hopped up on something. Maybe a few things. “It’s Farwell,” James replied matter-of-factly. “And the only chicken I see around here is you, Alphonse. Or have you already forgotten about Old Man Patches’ farm?” “He had a gun!” Alphonse snapped, but it was obvious his thirst to fight had been quenched. “And I’ve told you a million times to call me Phonse.” “Not happening,” was the half-assed response from James, who now had his full attention focused on the corner grocery store in front of him. Taking a deep breath, James made a move to step inside, but a hand on his shoulder stopped him. “You do this, and there’ll be something nice waiting for you when we get back.” A feminine voice. Thankfully. Her hot breath graced James’ earlobe, causing the hair on the back of his neck to stand. A golden-haired, blue-eyed, and pale-skinned goddess. And he was all hers. The next few seconds felt like an eternity, and then they were all wearing ski masks. The plan was simple: get in, scare the clerk, take the money, and get out. No one gets hurt, and no one gets busted. But you know what they say about the best laid plans. “Give me the money or I’ll shoot!” James shouted, holding the fake gun at arm’s length, shakily pointing it at the store owner. But the man wasn’t afraid, and instead reached down to quickly pull a shotgun out from behind the counter. A shot. Darkness. Screaming. Everywhere, screaming. Am I dead? And then a hand grabbed James by the arm, pulling him out of the darkness and back into reality. Already outside the store and running through the back alleys, James saw two of the boys with a few handfuls of money, and one with blood on his hands and shirt. He was the one who saved James’ life. He was the one who killed an innocent man, who was only working to make ends meet for his family, just as Clyde and James’ father did everyday. He was the one who gave James his first taste of the needle. And he was the one who would offer it to him again. James sat and relived every single moment of what happened, from the opening walk into the store, to the gun being leveled at his head, to the sight of his friends looting a dead man, to their very escape. And now here he was, in a place known only to him, sitting beside the girl he knew he loved but could never have. James looked at the needle in his hand, then to her. Her lips slowly parted, and her eyes let him see into her very soul. And at that moment he knew he wanted to die. “James?!” Clyde’s voice called out, rousing him from his half-sleep. For a moment, James had trouble differentiating between the dream world and reality, as Clyde’s voice had cut through the silence back then just as it had now. “What is it, Clyde?” James asked, groggily wiping the sleep from his eyes while peering over to the clock on the wall. “It’s two-thirty in the morning, for the love of God.” Sheepishly, Clyde rubbed the tip of his toes into the rug and gave the puppy dog eyes, trying to play to Farwell’s sense of adorability. He has none. Thusly, Clyde promptly dropped the act. “I was just wondering if you were asleep…” Walkins whispered, eyeing the room for any signs of movement. “And I obviously was.” James had begun growing angrier by the minute, and all he wanted to do was go back to sleep. Even if he couldn’t stop dreaming about his past, the nightmares had to be better than the reality of Clyde. “So if your merry excursion to my sleeping quarters is complete, I shall bid you adieu and give you my best well-wishes.” Clyde stood there for a second, completely lost. “What?” James just leaned his head back onto the pillow in a desperate attempt to fall back asleep. “Go back to bed and stop waking me up, Clyde.” “But Mr. CW is…he’s…gone…” Clyde’s eyes grew wider with fear. “Do you think that evil man stole him?” “I’m sure he’s just out on a midnight killing spree. He’ll be back by morning, Clyde,” James gave an uncharacteristically reassuring smile with his eyes closed. “Trust me.” But when morning did finally come, Mr. CW was nowhere to be found. Instead, all that was left of him was a note written in poor handwriting, with even poorer spelling and grammar. if u want 2 sea the woo…wok…monst4r again u will do as i say dont call the cops or ill kill him u will b sorrie ha ha ha send moneys know “Dear God…” James gasped. “Mr. CW’s been kidnapped by John Edwards!”
