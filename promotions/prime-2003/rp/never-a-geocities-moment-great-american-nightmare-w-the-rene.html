Never - A Geocities Moment (Great American Nightmare - w/ The Renegade v. Jason Snow and Xavier Kannon)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Timeless Dearest Xavi, Let’s do a quick overview of what you’ve “made” in this business I dominate. Money – Oh, wait, that’s right: my generosity last year saved you from the abject poverty your success wrought, liberating Eleanor from the shackles of whoredom, since you yourself lacked the stuff to bring home the butter. Of course, she continued working even after my million set you free, didn’t she? Maybe it was less about the money for your little “fuck ferret”. Maybe it was more about getting time away from the failure she made the mistake of marrying. The money you made? Shit. Keep in mind the next time you slice your sirloin that without my philanthropy you’d be choking down corned beef. Excuses – “Oh, woe! They won’t let me be me! I’m not Xavier! It’s Christian or bust!” That one worked out well for you. Who busted again? “Uh, yeah . . . well . . . there’s some black on Eleanor’s mom’s side.” “Waaaaaaaaaaaaaaah! My baaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaaack!” PCW – From whining in the ring, biting Dangerfield’s style, to infecting it with RUSH, to selling it off to the three stooges, you took the greatest federation on the planet and turned it into a laughingstock, then bankrupted it. Fans – That Dean kid still marks out for you, doesn’t he? The Universal Title – Oops. That was me. You’re the one whose cowardice killed it. Hmmm. Let’s see here. Surely there’s more. Oh right. Angelo Deville – In your dreams. You think you made me? You helped me become the next Xavier Kannon? That’s what you called me? Bite your filthy fucking tongue. I’ll never be a hollow shell, living off the charity of my wife and rivals, clinging desperately to a renaissance I only wish I’d ushered in. I’ll never undermine or steal the accolades of others, claiming them as my own. I’ll die before I become a recycled failure. I’ve never been the next Xavier Kannon. The similarities others noted were of stardom, of dominance, not personality. And they only noted those for half a year, until it became painfully obvious to all that I’d moved well beyond your ceiling, that I was strutting on top of it, moonwalking over your head like I was dancing on your grave. You were the benchmark only until I broke it, and nobody confuses that fact but you. I picked up where you left off, carried the ball farther than you could have ever hoped, spiked it, riverdanced, and lined up to do it again. You made me? All you made me was pissed me off, grating on my last nerve with your feigned benevolence and fickle demands for credit forever after. I’ve already told you your passing of the torch was a falsehood, an attempted extinguishing of my flame, and your latest rhetorical horseshit proves that intent. I didn’t call you back to the ring last year to show you how far I’ve come, to inspire pride in a former mentor; I reacquainted you with the ring to show you what’s always been true: I defeated and moved beyond you because I’m better than you, not because you thought fit to “make” me. I also did it to test the comparison, to see if your heart truly was that of a lion, to force you to roar. I thought that if I brought you back to the jungle, that if it was truly your kingdom, you’d return for more than that silly paycheque. I thought you’d rediscover your love, your skills, your pride. Wrestling me, shaking my hand when it was over, should have inspired you to return. As the champion you professed and profess to be, you shouldn’t have had a choice. True kings don’t abdicate. Lords don’t pay mere lip service to their sovereignty. We could have stood side-by-side in PRIME, columns of prosperity supporting the monolithic masterpiece. We could have done wonderful and wondrous things, could’ve aligned and been unstoppable, beyond reproach. Or we could have continued our feud, made our matches something to cherish in the here and now instead of a hat-tip to yesteryear. We could have, but you wouldn’t. You were too obsessed with securing your pathetic “future” and cementing your past. The opportunity passed you by because you were too stupid or cowardly to seize it. You made yourself a relic, and as archaeologists often do only on their death-beds, I came to the sad conclusion that I’d spent far too long chasing after little more than a trinket. A mundane arrowhead so far removed from usefulness or importance, sentimental only because it existed before me. Maybe I was mistaken and you’re not what I was led to believe. Maybe you made dupes of us all. I’m struggling to find an argument against it, to rediscover the nobility I thought I saw as a rookie. You’ve never understood, and I’m beginning to believe you never will. The past is nothing but a foundation to develop beyond, the future an intangible hallucination. All we can touch is the present, building from the past. Take care of the now and the future will look after itself. “Make” no mistake about it . . . I’ve never confused past accomplishments with current success, and I’ll never steal from the present or future in a vain attempt to justify or laud my past. I’ll never ride the coattails of former successes. I’m constantly building, constantly plotting, living for the moment. I’ve always appreciated that no one is ever made, that it’s an ongoing process, that we’re constantly growing, forever making our own way and our own selves. You never did, and you still don’t. In clinging to the past, always trying to elevate it beyond the present, you’ve shrunk. I continue rising, you continue falling. I continue creating, you continue destroying. I live, and you . . . you died a long time ago. That’s why I’m still atop the mountain, while you’re about to get buried under an avalanche. Enjoy your grave, Christian, Xavier, whatever past you’re embracing today, as it’s all you have to look forward to now. Enjoy your static eternity, locked in a depressing nostalgic loop of your own infernal making. Yours Truly, Angelo Deville
