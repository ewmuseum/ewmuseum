Check-Up

8 August 2005

Date: August 8th, 2005 Chapter I: Benedict and the Vatican BrouhahaFriday, August 5th He sat, feet swung over the edge of the leather bench. Inhaling deeply he awaited the arrival of the physician he had been instructed to get in touch with. He had seen two specialists on behalf of ACW in previous weeks regarding his knee and the damage that had been sustained at the hands of Sars the Clown. This would be the third. It wasn’t much more than a check-up really. It was a necessary precaution considering he continued to wrestle while his knee was attempting to heal itself. A quick five minute procedure just to check the progress. Even he had noticed that the inflammation had subsided greatly, however he was still in pain from time to time. The door creaked and swung open, giving way to a rather large figure stepping through. The doctor was larger than average, standing nearly Kelly’s height, at 6’4”. The white coat he was wearing was somewhat reassuring, as was the smile he sported. Medical facilities, although he had seen his fair share, made Kelly a little uneasy. He shifted awkwardly on the leather bench as the doctor approached. Kelly motioned toward his ribs, wincing in pain slightly. Another injury suffered at the negligent hands of the wrestling industry. “Okay, Kelly,” the doctor began, pulling a chair closer to the where Kelly sat. “Relax your leg for me.” Once again Kelly inhaled deeply, letting his leg fall limp. “The swelling’s gone down,” Kelly piped up. “That’s good. Yeah, it says on the sheet here that your knee was pretty inflamed. But if it’s started to go down, then that’s definitely a good sign,” the doctor paused, feeling the back of Flawless’ leg. “How long ago did the injury take place?” “Roughly a month ago?” Kelly replied, unsure. “Hmm…” once again the physician paused. “Well, there’s definitely some damage to the knee, but I’m sure the other doctors you’ve talked to have told you that,” they both shared a chuckle. “But from what the sheet says, your knee seems to be coming along. The inflammation isn’t as severe and the bruising has subsided.” Kelly felt a little better about the whole situation. “You aren’t out of the woods yet, down the road you probably will need surgery to correct this. I’m not going to try a guess at the timeframe, but it will be a necessity in the coming months.” Once again Flawless squirmed uneasily. This was news he had heard before, however having to listen to it again didn’t ease the impact. “You might be able to help your cause a little…” the doctor said, standing up from his seated position, “ever consider taking a bit of a break? You could always have them go in and take a look at your knee and be out for a few weeks. You never know, you might recuperate fully in that time- I’ve seen stranger things happen.” “Not an option, Doc,” Kelly said, hopping down from the bench. “I’ve got too many things to tend to beforehand.” “It’s hard to tell what sort of damage you have to the knee itself without going in and having a look. We have the X-rays, but they were taken weeks ago. Things can very well happen in that amount of time.” “Well, hopefully they’re of the good variety, then.” Kelly smiled the way of the doctor as he walked over to the rack on the wall to retrieve his coat. “I hope you won’t put this off for too, too long.” The doctor said with a hint of concern in his voice. “It can only do you good.” “Doc, come on, I’m perfect…” Kelly said with a wink, “my knee ought to be able to heal it self, don’t you figure?” The doctor smiled, “maybe with the grace of God.” “I don’t need Hoyt Williams touching my knee. Not after the fact he’s probably touched more than his fair share of little boys… wait, is he Catholic.” Again, the two shared a chuckle. “I’m not sure if Hoyt Williams in Catholic, Kelly,” the doctor began, pausing. At this point Kelly had already opened the door and was preparing to exit. “But I am,” the doctor finished, leaving a lump in the throat of God’s Gift to Women. “Well, you aren’t a Priest. I’m sure your hands are clean… I doubt you’ve lubed your penis with Holy water and touched yourself thinking of all those little boys in your congregation.” And Kelly Flawless crossed the line. The doctor smiled one of those smiles that you only smile when your message is to the tune of, “if you don’t remove yourself from my office within the next few moments I’m going to castrate you and beat you to death with a cane.” Perfection exited the premises rather quickly, heading for the hills before the doctor decided that he wanted to follow through with his threat. You know them Catholics, what with their strong convictions and their excessively large tempers. The way in which they elect a new Pope is more like a fist fight then a democratic process. See, they line all the potential Popes up in a circle, give each of ‘em a knife and let ‘em loose. It was about a hundred or so years after they came up with this that they realized they had made a horrible error. Cardiac arrest and anxiety attacks remove more of the potential Popes from competition as opposed to the hand of another potential Pope. But, that’s what happens when you gather a bunch of old Polish bastards (and one black guy, not to mention) to fight that barely have the energy to stand any longer, let alone stab another man to death. Although they are all fiery competitors with a temper the size of Manhattan all but one die off. Thus, the new Pope is elected. Besides, with a name like Benedict it seems a given that he’d be one crazy motherfucker. Chapter II: DelayedFriday, August 5th – 4:28 pm He checked his watch… Emphasized sigh. They should have boarded nearly thirty minutes prior, and, yet, still no real word from airport officials regarding the status of their flight. There he sat, his luggage beside him, his feet rested on a seat adjacent to him, in a gate located at the very end of the Domestic Terminal at O’Hare International Airport… he hated Chicago. He had decided to try to catch a bit of an early flight to Salt Lake City for this week’s edition of ReVolution, however, at this rate he believed he would be late. Exaggeration. Kelly was famous for it… famous for being the dramatic pretty boy. His blonde hair was tied back neatly in a ponytail as his head sank into the back of seat. He yawned. Understandably bored, understandably frustrated. 4:54 pm “Okay girls,” he said sarcastically to whom it may have concerned, “lets board this thing, already.” His head snapped to the side as a voice came over the P.A. system informing the hundred and seventy-some passengers at the gate about the current situation with the flight. “Those destined for Salt Lake City, your flight has been delayed…” the woman’s voice informed them. “Thanks, Tips,” Kelly said, understandably frustrated. “Be patient, we will begin boarding in roughly twenty minutes- thank you for flying United.” “Thank you for flying United,” he began, mimicking the woman, “I swear to God this is the last time I fly United. I’ll fly Southwest, or Delta- who cares if their peanuts suck?” Chapter III: Sunday, August 6th After a lengthy delay Kelly didn’t touch down in Utah until after midnight on Saturday morning. A long, well-deserved sleep saw him awake at nearly one in the afternoon on Saturday. He had come to Utah earlier than most of the PRIME superstars in the hopes of grabbing a little bit of relaxation in the southwest. Aside from that, his parents called the city home, and a little visit with your folks never did anybody harm. It was a breezy, humid Utah morning, but that didn’t seem to stop the father son duo from hitting the links for a morning of golf. Both extremely competitive and both not half bad at the hobby sport. They had made the trek up the Country Club, where Kelly’s dad was a member. They were nearing the end of the back nine. Both had bombed their drives, and had relatively simple approach shots. Kelly had 100 or so yards remaining with a clear view of the front of the green pin placement. Meanwhile, his father had rough 125 yards to the pin, however he was positioned on the edge of the rough with a little bit more of a difficult shot. A nine iron should do the trick he figured. He removed it from his bag and approached the ball. Kelly sat in the golf cart about fifteen yards from the ball, awaiting his father’s second shot. Both their rounds were going pretty well. Kelly was three over through seven, his father was five over on the day. Decent scores when you take into consideration the par 72, Championship layout of the course. He readied himself for the shot, visualizing the ball landing pretty much in the cup. He drew the club and back and swung through, connecting nicely, and following through with a pose. The ball climbed high into the Utah air, clearing the tops of the trees adjacent to the green. Kelly’s eyes watched his father’s ball land on the green, some fifteen or twenty feet beyond the flag stick, and suck back, with the support of the slope leaving him with what looked like an eight or ten foot put for birdie. “That’ll do just fine,” his father said, quite satisfied with his approach. “Nice shot,” Kelly said quietly, nodding. Kelly hit the accelerator and the cart putted along forward toward Kelly’s ball that was more toward the cent of the fairway, but also off to the right a tad. “About a hundred yards, you figure?” His father questioned curiously. “Yeah, around there,” Kelly said, removing his pitching wedge from his bag on the back of the cart. “Hopefully I get the same type of luck you got, playing it off the slope.” Kelly smiled at his father who acknowledged the joke, knowing Kelly thought that it was a nice shot. Kelly stood over the ball, visualizing a shot much the same as his father just hit. He brought the club back, then bringing it down nicely through the zone. The club face contacted the ball squarely. The white dimpled sphere took off like a bat out of hell, approaching the treetops just as his father’s did. “Ah,” Kelly said with a groan. “I picked it to clean.” Kelly’s father looked on, checking to see where his son’s ball would come down. “Long.” Kelly said out of frustration as the ball impacted the surface of the green some twenty-five feet beyond the hole, rolling toward the back edge of the green. “It’s playable, but it’ll be a tough putt,” his father said, informing his son of his current situation. Kelly looked the way of his dad as he slid his pitching wedge back into his golf bag, and smiled. They hadn’t been able to spend too much time together in recent years, thanks in part to Kelly’s career. And the time they did spend together often was not that which was cherished, but this… this was nice. Because, to Kelly his father was much more than somebody he referred to as dad. He was also a friend.
