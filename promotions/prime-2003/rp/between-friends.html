Date: Today We open to a blank screen. And by blank screen, we mean your screen, because it's not like this is in filming or anything. So yeah, it's a blank screen until the screen lightens a little at black, with a row of seats and two heads off to the right. And then the words 'Because Sometimes Script Just Works Better' appear on the screen. Emi: Why are we doing this again? Adri: Well, Van said that it seemed like a good idea at the time. Emi: Two things wrong with that. First, being that he would say that he had a good idea. Second, if he thought it was, why isn't he doing this? Adri: Because it's only two hours to the RP deadline and he needs to get his sleep. Emi: And we're here to prove what? Adri: That breaking kayfabe is funnier when it's in-character. Emi: Yeah... you have fun with that. Vangellus's bodyguard, who loathes her job (not that she ever does it, yet still gets paid), stands up to leave, but Adrienne Diamond tugs on her arm to make her sit down. Adri: You're suffering through this with me. Ms. Teshigahara throws her arms into the air, though more subdued a motion than it seems. Emi: The things I do for love. On the screen, it goes blank again as the narrator starts talking. 'Not in this case, unfortunately. Nothing can make crap like this ‘better’. Seriously. The highest point my writing has reached is the level of toilet humor. I’m worse than Seinfeld, for Christ’s sake!' Emi's silhouette is all that can be seen, same as the woman sitting next to her. She turns her head toward Oxios's manager, not that PRIME gets to see that much because she stays behind the scenes, like a manager should. Emi: Who's this speaking? Adri: I think it's the same guy that Van said that he saw speeding away from the Halton Arena for the last RPW show, the license plate reading '2PARKS4U', after leaving the tag team belts in the trash. He kept the Mid-South title, taking it to The Squared Circle. Emi: I don't remember that. Adri: His short program was otherwise forgettable. 'Wait, no. Scratch that. I’m still better than Seinfeld, but not yet as good as Dead Like Me. Somewhere above Arrested Development, if only for the fact my camera doesn’t appear to be operated by an epileptic, but below Family Guy. Right beside Schindler’s List. 'And somewhere in the world, a fuck of a lot of people turn over in their graves.' Emi: I suppose if I watched any sort of television or movies outside of this, I'd know what he was talking about. Adri: Don't mind this, it's the usual rambling that he does to get himself in the mood for production. Before he can actually get to writing a script for any Forsaken production, he'll just type some random nonsense. It's like how art teachers will tell you that when you first sit down to your canvas, it's so pristine and pure that you don't want to be inspired to breach it. So to go ahead and make some errant mark, anything, on the page to break that existing perfection before you go about creating something. Emi: That was almost poetic. Adri: I know, right? 'Now, this is where I’d normally delve into a Forsaken adventure, probably giving you a brief synopsis of what led up to Clyde interjecting with some completely retarded comment, and thusly hilarity does ensue. But not this time. No, this time it’s different. This time…it’s personal.' Adri: See? Emi: Guess you're right. 'Actually, it isn’t. But I’ve decided my writing doesn’t seem more like a blog than a roleplay as it is, so I’d like to touch on a subject that is very near and dear to my heart: Canine Homosexuality, or ‘CH’ for short.' Emi: What's a roleplay? Adri: It's what we're in right now. Emi: Excuse me? Adri: Well, I won't go into this much because then it might become a ripoff of Nova's Tropical Turmoil roleplay, but suffice it to say that we're just actors playing a part in a grand play that serves as our life. Like episode 13 (or was it 14) of the anime 'Big O'. It was the first episode of Season 2. Emi: And I don't really care that much. All I know is that the beginning seems to be an excuse for this 'Parks' person to tell us that his god has a bigger dick than yours. Adri: Hey, that's not right. That's just this writer's fleeting and pointless reference to George Carlin. Sky: Sorry, moving on. 'DID YOU KNOW? '…in the wild, one out of every eight dogs is gay?…there are more gay dogs per square mile than there are people?…being bitten by a gay dog can make you gay too?…he’s not just cleaning himself? To learn more about CH, please visit www.mydogisgay.com. Knowledge is power!' Adri: ... Emi: ... Adri: I apologize. Emi: You'd better, for having to have just lived through this. You SO owe me for this. Adri: I'm sure you'll think of something. Emi: Oh yeah. And it won't just be simple sex. You've got to experience the same pain I'm going through now. 'And now back to our previously scheduled program. 'Walkins: DICKWRINKLE! 'What can I say? I’ve found a Dr. Curiosity-like formula that no one else has seemed to notice yet, and I’m planning to abuse it for all it’s worth. Sure, I may not get anywhere with this particular formula, considering it’d be one of those accidental spells that always go horribly wrong or smell particularly foul if it were in Harry Potter, but at least this way I don’t have to try. Because trying is for losers.' Adri: That make sense? Emi: Who's Doctor Curiosity? Adri: He's a character with heat. That's why it's so funny. Emi: I don't follow. Adri: Well, you see, Doctor Curiosity won GTT4, he's been a champion in fWo... Generally, people like hearing about him, good or bad. When people hear about Forsaken, they either conjure the thoughts of that vampire movie, or that one David Draiman song. Emi: Draiman sucks. Adri: He's not so bad if you get past the unnecessary moodiness. Emi: Like the lead singer of Coldplay? He'd be easier to listen to if he just realized that he's got nothing to be down about and perked the hell up in his songs. Adri: Exactly. 'Now please step aside as I claim my titles and smile while doing so. Because somehow I can find pride in winning off a no-show. '…You know…in case something bad happened to the computers of Sky and Shad…that kept them from writing for a week…not that I’d know if anything did…ahem… Adri: Tsk, tsk... There's an art to the break of kayfabe that he's lacking here. It's too base. Too vulgar almost. There's no surprise to it. You can see it coming from a mile away. Emi: I repeat, why are we doing this? I see no point to it. Adri: Honestly, it's just to inflate the length of this roleplay. Emi: And I'm still confused as to what a roleplay is. Adri: It's what should be happening right now. Instead, we're tricking the reader into focusing on this when the real story doesn't start until another two paragraphs down, after the line break. Emi: GOTCHA! And the scene fades away as Vangellus Oxios runs in and thrusts his pelvis at the camera, as he has been known to do. “What are these?” he asked, holding the strange papers. Scott held sheets of thick, coarse paper in his hands, looking at the strange writing that covered them. The calligraphy was ornate and flowing, and to his eye, he couldn't make heads or tails of it. “We got them here at home,” his mother said, worry creasing her face. She lifted the envelopes where Scott had dropped them, written in the same flowing hand, but devoid of postage. All that was written was Scott's name on the front. “All I can guess is that someone stuffed them into our mail box.” Scott looked at her quizzically. “So, what are they?” “They're threats, Scott.” His heart went cold. In his hands, he held threats against himself and his family. The paper felt cold in his hands, alien and devious. He looked back down at them, his eyes hardening. But his mind raced. Who would send threats to his family? Who could have found their home? “I... I don't...” he stammered. “I don't know who sent these,” he said, his throat thick. “Who would want to threaten us?” “They're from the cult,” his mother answered quietly. “The Cult of the Poison Tree.” Now Scott remembered. He remembered a maniacal cult leader that he'd faced when they purchased a compound in Pike County. He remembered the man getting his face and screaming at him. He remembered the man insulting Lacey. And he remembered the fight. No, he hadn't started it for once. The man had insulted Lacey and Scott had stepped between them. Then he had been attacked. That was the only way to put it. The man swung at him and Scott had swung back. The brawl that had erupted had been all over the local news, but they couldn't catch what he had seen there in the middle of it. Scott's fists had swung, leveling wave after wave of the crazed devotees that kept coming. Fortunately, he'd been supported by other folk there that didn't cotton the idea of this compound. But why were they threatening him? “They had gotten busted for pills. They were running prescription pills.” Scott looked at the foreign writing. “Is that in here? Is that why?” “I suppose it is,” his mother answered, sitting down carefully on the old couch that had always been in their living room. “There are statements about you being responsible for having their leader bound. Something like that.” He began to pace worriedly. He couldn't protect them; he was on the road too much. “Have you told the police?” he asked her. “Yes,” she replied, “but they can't really do anything more than send a squad car up here once or twice a night.” Scott's guts churned with an icy dread. Here he stood, having fought a barrage of them, but he felt helpless. He didn't know what to do. He stood there, his feet feeling rooted to the floor. The concern showed on his face, washing it with a long look of confusion and pain. “What are we going to do?” he asked. “Scott, about the only thing we can do is get some dogs and a gun,” she said, shrugging her shoulders. “I don't know how well that will work, but it's all I know to do.” Scott nodded numbly. “We still have that old service pistol, right?” She paused. Scott had deliberately avoided mentioning his father, as he always did. “Yes, the Beretta. I suppose I need to practice with it.” “Make sure Lacey does, too,” he said, his voice distant. “Just in case.” “Of course, Scott,” she said, her heart aching. The last thing her son needed was another reason to worry. Yet it had been hand-delivered to them. “I think we could do with a dog or two,” she said gently. “Yeah,” he said. “Mean dog or something.” He took a few shuffling steps and let the letters drop from his fingers on the mahagony coffee table. “I'll let you all pick one you want, y'know, comfortable with and stuff. Just make sure it'll defend you guys.” She looked at him. He was a massive man, a wrestling champion, a football record holder. He was strong, he was brave, he was an excellent fighter by all accounts. But he wasn't Superman. He wanted to be, for them anyway. For his family, for Alyssa, for his partner Vangellus, he wanted to be Superman for them. But he just wasn't able. “Wait just a second,” Scott said, a slow smile washing over his face. “I got me an idea.” He shoved a massive hand into the hip pocket of his jeans and extracted that little device he hated so much. He flipped his cell phone open and punched a few buttons. “About damn time this thing was useful.” The phone signalled that it was ringing, waiting for a pickup on the other line. At least he got the right number of digits out this time. As Vangellus was about to take his swing at the fourth hole at Dretzka Golf Course in Milwaukee, Wisconsin, the cellular phone in his pocket began the drum beat to Gwen Stefani's Holla Back Girl (screw you, it's still a catchy tune). Michael Jordan was there with him, taking a break in between getting his ass kicked at pool by Matthew Perry, turned up his nose as though he had any right to be upset about someone calling him on the course when the same thing happened to him in Space Jam. 'Hold on, Jordo,' Oxios told his playing partner, even though Michael Jordan hated that nickname. Fishing for the phone in his pocket, Vangellus brushed past his keys, a condom, and his lucky rabbit's ear (and no one can tell him that he's wrong for going for the opposite end of the rabbit) before getting to the insanely small phone. Van doesn't do well with small technological gadgets. He accidentally swallowed his iPod Nano in a successful attempt to prove that it's perhaps too small. Bringing it up to his ear, he greeted the man on the phone as he would anyone. 'Yeah, um, is this Van?' Scott asked hesitantly. He still hated cell phones, and suffice to say, he wasn't really comfortable using them. But this? This was important. Really, Van never checks the number of the caller, so he didn't know who was on the other end of the line. 'This is WVOX Radio, and you're on the air with the Van Man, the Whole Freaking SHO; Vangellus Oxios. What's on your mind, playa?' Off to the side, Jordan mouthed the words, 'You're not black.' Oxios returned by mouthing, 'Half black, boyeeeeeeeeeeeee!' On the other end, Scott was relieved to hear Vangellus, even though his tag team partner always answers his phone. Unlike other times, he needed to hear Oxios's voice right now. 'Hey Van,' the manbeast started with an uncharacteristically-low voice. 'You've got a minute, right? I need to talk with you about something.' Vangellus nodded, removing his white golf glove, his stupid golf hat, and putting his 3 iron back into his bag. Jordan gestured that he was still waiting for them to finish their game. 'Yeah, hold on just a second, Scotty.' Turning to Michael, Oxios said, 'Go on ahead. We both know that you could get a 3 hour headstart and I'll still catch up to you before the 12th hole.' 'Jackass,' Jordan mumbled while shaking his head. But then he turned to the camera and said, 'But the only thing keeping me from putting my foot up his ass is the fact that he's wearing Hanes underwear.' Vangellus scans the surrounding landscape, all 360 degrees. Nothing but the two of them. 'Who are you talking to?' Jordan slumps his head, chin to chest. 'Okay Scott,' Oxios devoted his attention to his partner. 'What's up? 'Listen,' Scott started, 'I've got a major problem. Do you remember that cult I got into a fistfight with?' Oxios scratched his chin. 'Depending on what you and I count as a cult, you might have to be more specific.' He was thinking back that instance with the Teamsters. And then again with the Shriners. And then again with that group of Hare Krishna at the airport in to Vancouver. Scott sighed. 'The Poison Tree, remember? Guy got in my sister's face? I yelled at him, he tried to clock me, I ended up fighting apparently the entire membership of the damn group.' Vangellus nodded his head. Now he remembered them. Poor souls didn't watch television. Never knew what they were getting into. That's why cults need to join the rest of the world in the 21st century. But even without television, come on. Look at Scott. Normal people don't pick fights with him. Maybe they were an assisted-suicide cult. Or something. 'Oh, right. You got into a property dispute or something. How are those guys? Many insurance claims after you went all Berserker Rage on them?' Because Oxios's favorite superhero is Wolverine. And not the pansy-ass Hugh Jackman variety. Scott stood there a second. He hadn't even considered THAT might be an option. Great. 'No,' he said slowly. 'They've been hand delivering death threats to my mom's house.' He stopped, letting the cold silence hang in the air. Vangellus just blinked. 'And they've been walking away under their own power?' Scott shook his shaggy head, an instinctive motion thanks to the phone. 'I didn't know about it until tonight. But I gotta leave tomorrow for the next show. I honestly don't know what to do, Van.' 'I'm sure you've considered the usual options. Guns, dogs, the like.' Matthews nodded. By this time, he's walked away from Lacey. He's out on the porch now, scanning for any other Poison Tree cultists. Were they watching the house right now? Staking the place out, waiting for him to leave so that they could terrorize his family even more? 'I dare you to show your faces when I'm at home,' Scott thought as he nodded to Van's question. More directly, he replied, 'Yeah.' 'Well, a single gun isn't much of a deterrent against a whole cult of people who were willing to throw themselves at you like they had any chance of succeeding. And dogs need time to grow attached to you to be worth anything as guards; you have to train attack dogs, you don't just buy them.' Matthews leaned against the wall to the side of the door. It opened outward, but he was on the side opposite the hinges so that he wouldn't have to guard his face if it swung open. Not that he was much going to let Lacey or Alyssa or anyone leave this house without him with them. Though at the same time that would leave the others home alone. A big risk either way. The true feeling of helplessness that enveloped him. 'How do you know this?' Scott wondered. Oxios shrugged. 'I got my moments of clarity just like every other busta, yo.' In the distance, Jordan shouts, 'Not black!' Van covers the phone with his hand and calls back, 'Shut the hell up!' 'So what other options are you considering?' he asked his tag team partner, his co-champion friend. Matthews ran a hand through his thick mane. 'I don't know. The police can't do much unless there's an actual attack. But I can't let that happen. It'll be too late. I want... I want to be here. To protect my family. But I still have to work, and that'll keep me away from them. I can't ask them to go into hiding because they all have their own lives that they need to live, they can't come on the road with me because they're not up for that. But what can I do?' 'Have you considered a bodyguard?' Oxios suggested. Scott Matthews knew of Emi Teshigahara. Oxios's bodyguard. He never saw her, since she didn't come to the shows. But Vangellus spoke highly of her (unbeknownst to him, that was an irony in and of itself). 'I can't afford a bodyguard here. And who could I trust?' Oxios thought a moment. 'You can have mine.' 'That Emi girl? Vangellus nodded himself. 'Yeah. She's not really doing much of anything for me, but that's just because everybody loves me. But given your case, I think I can loan her to you.' 'Is one going to be enough? Even I needed a little help from the townspeople.' 'Hm... If that's the case, I can call in a favor from someone else. Believe me, between these two, any cult or small army would have difficulty.' 'This isn't putting you out in any way?' Vangellus Oxios. Tag Team champion. Popular jokester of PRIME. Can't take anything seriously. And this is the man who said the following. 'Scott. You're my partner. I owe you a lot for sticking with me, and for helping me get to where I am in PRIME. And no matter what you might think, you're decent to hang out with. We're a partnership in the ring, but outside the ring as well. If you need help, I'm supposed to provide it. That's what partners are for. That's what friends do.' And that's when Scott's eyes began to tear up.
