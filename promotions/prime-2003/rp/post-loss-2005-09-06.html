Post Loss

6 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/6/2005 You don't see it often, but sometimes there's a football team that just surprises you with its heart, tenacity and sheer determination. In this case, though, it was the fifth and sixth graders of the Fed's Creek Pirates. They were practicing today, running their hearts out, trying their best. But a big part of it was that they were trying to cheer up their coach. Scott Matthews stood on the sidelines, half-heartedly cheering them on. “Good hustle, boys,” he said, shouting out to them as they ran down the field and back. But hard as they pounded their cleated feet down the ragged grass field, they just couldn't seem to brighten the mood of the man they called Coach Rampage. The big man almost had a brooding air to him, his hair perpetually hanging in his face and his eyes almost dull with the lack of fire that usually burned behind them. This wasn't the big, powerful Coach Rampage that these boys knew and rallied to. It was almost as if the life had been drained from him. In truth, that's how Scott felt. It wasn't losing that bothered him so much. But the simple fact that he and Van had won the titles, had been champions for all of about twenty minutes... and they had lost them because he shot his mouth off in the heat of the moment. He just couldn't have kept his mouth shut, could he? “Alright, boys,” he shouted to his little intrepid band. “You guys did good today. Good practice. You go on home and get some rest and I'll see you boys next week, okay?” “Coach Rampage?” came one small voice. Scott turned his head to see Lawrence standing at his elbow. The little spitfire had tackled the hell out of a kid that'd been held back a year to have an edge in sports. Not only tackled, but knocked the absolute stuffing out of him. “Coach Rampage, what's wrong?” Scott turned his head away from the direct question out of the little fifth grader. Leave it to the children to cut straight to the point, to cut straight to exactly what he didn't want to deal with. But has he turned his head, he saw the wave of small eyes gazing back at him, all mirroring the question. “Boys,” he said, his voice husky, “I know you think a lot of me. You kids give your all because I ask you to. But when ya get right down to it, I ain't perfect. I'm far from it. Really, boys, I'm upset because I screwed up this past weekend.” “Are you talking about your pay per view?” one of the boys asked. Big Floyd, their center, was the source of the question. The big kid had been the first to really latch onto Scott as the coach and his enthusiasm had seemed to infect the rest of the team. Scott reached out and patted the center's shoulderpads. “Yeah, I went and lost those tag belts right after my partner and I had won them. I mean, winning is nice, right?” The wave of nodded heads gave him all he wanted. “Let's say you boys were to win league championship and then some team you already beat came along and talked you into a game right after that big game. And they won and took your trophy. That's what I'm dealing with.” “Coach,” Lawrence spoke back up, “we don't mind.” Scott looked at the collection of boys. Sure, they were a collection of fifth and sixth graders, but they believed in him. “Next week, boys,” he managed to get out. The fact that they still believed in him almost made him sick. Why would they support someone that had made such a mistake? * * * “Hey, Jimmy,” Scott called. “Bring another one.” Without a word, the barkeep pulled another beer from the cooler and handed it to the big man sitting across the bar from him. Scott sat there a minute, holding the bottle in his big fist before latching the bottlecap in his teeth and pulling it off. With a big breath, he spat the cap across the bar and into the trashcan. “Damn, Scott,” Jimmy said, “I really wish you wouldn't do that. Just makes my ass tighten like my wife beggin' for money.” Scott gave him a look. “Give me a shot of Everclear, too.” Without a word, the paunchy bartender poured another dollop of the clear liquid into Scott's shot glass. This had been the fourth... fifth? Hell, Scott couldn't remember how many shots this would make. Jimmy knew it was number eight, but when Scott Matthews came in to drink, you didn't get in his way. “Hey son, give some whiskey, would ya?” chimed in a new voice. Scott turned his head, blinking and raising a big hand to push his hair out of his face. Well holy... “How ya doin' there, son?” Ron White said to him, taking the proffered glass of amber liquid. “Mind if I sit here?” “Not at all, Mr. White,” Scott said, waving at the empty stool. “Better watch it, though. Jimmy's too damn cheap to buy new stools so sometimes one of these sonsabitches'll put you on yourr ass.” Scott took a long pull off his beer. “What's a big such'n'such like you doin' in Pikeville, anyway?” “Oh, come on, son. They booked me for that big auditorium you people have downtown. Mountain Arts Center or something?” The comedian took a sip of his drink and smiled. “One of the best crowds I've ever worked, though.” “Hell, where are my manners,” Scott said, extending his big paw. “Scott Matthews.” “Son,” Ron replied, “I knew who you were when I walked in. That's why I asked if you minded me sittin' here instead of just ploppin' my big ass down.” “Wait, what?” Scott asked. “I may have a bit in me...” Scott paused a moment and tossed his shot of Everclear straight back. “But I'm not drunk enough to be imaging stuff.” Ron looked at Scott's glass appreciatively. “Scott, I've seen ya on TV, I saw you wrestling way back in that Rush or whatever. If I'd known you were such a good drinker, I'd have tried to catch up with you at some point.” “Wait, wait, wait,” Scott said, waving a big hand, “you've watched me? Okay, Ron, funny, you got me there.” Ron killed off the rest of his drink and set the glass back on the bar. “I usually don't watch wrestlin'. I mean, big guys in underwear grabbin' each other? Those are manly men, right there.” The comedian tapped the lip of his glass for a refill. “But I saw you whoopin' some of them boys on there, and that looked like a real good old boy goin' to town.” Scott took his freshly-filled class of Everclear and knocked it flat back. “Yeah, I could use some buckin' up right now.” “What's goin' on?” White asked him. He watched with his eyebrows steadily climbing as Scott killed off another shot of Everclear. “Yeah, well, I doubt you watched the pay per view, but my partner and I won the tag titles. Then I let a couple of dicks get under my skin and have another match, so we were tag champs for all of a long shit.” “Hell, son, you're beatin' yourself up over that?” White took a moment to sip at his fresh whiskey glass. “Listen, kid, you made one mistake and you lost something at the end of the night you didn't have at the beginning? It's like getting drunk in Vegas, endin' up married and having it annulled in the morning. It's just a good story or another notch on your belt.” “Ron,” Scott said, rubbing at his forehead, “you don't get a marriage annulled because you mouthed off to the wrong person. You don't wanna keep a marriage from being drunk, I wanted to keep those titles.” “Well, son, I'm thinking that you can either sit here and drink yourself blind about it or you can drink and celebrate the fact that you won the titles.” Ron lifted the glass and took another drink. Scott mulled the idea over. It was a point of view that wasn't predominant on his mind. It was really more the former, which was what he normally did. But here he was, drinking with Ron White and the guy was a fan of his. Scott took his most recent beer and drained it. He was about to reply to Ron when a man came staggering up. “Ron White, you sonofabitch,” he slurred a bit. “That's nothing new,” the comedian replied. “What's up, partner?” “I was the guy that said Garth Brooks was the greatest entertainer ever,” the drunk said, waving a bottle at White. “You been makin' fun of me on TV about it.” “Yeah, okay,” came the response, “you're famous now, what're you pissed about?” That's when the bottle came streaking overhead and straight into an iron grip. Scott shook his head. “I may have had more than a couple, but boy, he and I were talkin' and you're being plain rude.” The other hand swung and cracked Scott in the jaw. Not very hard, mind you, but hard enough. Scott, grabbed the drunk by his belt buckle and throw him over the nearest table. “How's a good fight sound, Ron?” Ron White smiled back. “You'd be surprised what the Tater can do.” Scott turned around to see the man getting back up and swinging a chair. Unwavering, Scott stepped into it, raising his left arm. The rickety chair smashed around his bulging arm and his right hand took the man by the shirtfront. With another heave, Scott threw him straight into the side of the bar. A crash brought his head around, and he saw White dropping the neck of a bottle while there was a man melting down to the floor. Ron smiled at Scott and shook his head. With a shrug, Scott turned around to see someone break a pool cue over the drunk's head. The whole bar erupted and with a roar, Scott threw himself into the fray. * * * Scott drove his Jeep, pedal down and engine roaring. Blood ran down his face from the split in his eyebrow and a grin creased his cheeks. Beside him, the comedian drained the glass he'd appropriated from the bar and tossed it out the window. “Damn, Scott,” he said with a big grin, “you're a damn good scrapper. Tell you what, next time you're near any of my bookings, hit the bars in the hotels and we'll catch up.” Scott smiled again. A good fight, making a friend... he felt better after this past loss than he had in a long, long time.
