and thank-you for being my god

21 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 21 nov 08 The clouds rolled over the landscape with precise irony before they let go their contents and rained down on the crowd gathered around the hole dug out of God’s green earth. They cast their shadow upon the earth, as if they were creating some mood lighting for my father’s funeral. The crowd of people hugged one another, or stood alone, or stared into the distance. The theme for their attire was black for all but one man. For me. I stand by the hole, peering into the abyss in which my father would spend the rest of eternity physically, wearing a leopard print shirt, black leather pants and our EWA Intercontinental Championship belt. My crying eyes shielded from sight of those around me by my notorious wrap around neon green shades. Paparazzi hide in trees and cling to spires of overdone effigies to those who have passed by before. Fleetingly snapping images of me standing by my father’s ceremonious adieu. "I tell you the truth, the Son can do nothing by himself; he can do only what he see his Father doing, because whatever the Father does the Son also does,” the priest proclaims to those in mourning. Biting my lip is the only thing that can grant me any feeling in my body. Numbness overwhelms me and I fear that I might not be able to make my father proud by being the man I know he’s brought me up to be. I fear I may drop to my knees crying with my whole soul, like a kid who has grazed his knee. Pride, for my father’s teachings, is all that stands between the unceremonious wailing and me. “For the Father loves the Son and shows him all he does. Yes, to your amazement he will show him even greater things than these. For just as the Father raises the dead and gives them life, even so the Son gives life to whom he is pleased to give it…” My Aunt Marianne rubs the small of my back as if it may give me some relief but there is nothing on this earth that could ease the pain that resonates in my soul. Blood slowly warms my chin as it rolls down from my biting my lip. It mixes with some stray tears, which have made their path down my cheek and under my lip. Together they roll for freedom down my neck, disappearing into my shirt. Could tell by the looks of my father’s family me wearing my wrestling costume did not constitute a sign of respect to them. But what they knew and what was truth were always clearly different things. It was a symbol for all the hard work, blood, sweat and tears my father and I had put in to make me the wrestler I was today. Never gifted with the natural talents, but I had been gifted with tremendous heart. The heart of my father. A man stronger than ten armies with the courage of ten more. I had been blessed with the heart of my father. “I tell you the truth, whoever hears my word and believe him who sent me has eternal life and will not be condemned; he has crossed over from death to life.” Standing at attention with my eyes dead ahead, thinking of the times we’d shared together made me feel all the more weaker. The happier the memory the more the strength in my knees seemed to fail me. The words which the priest delivered were like arrows piercing my heart with pure truth. My father lived for me and I my father. "I tell you the truth, a time is coming and has now come when the dead will hear the voice of the Son of God and those who hear will live. For as the Father has life in himself, so he has granted the Son to have life in himself,” the priests said with finality. He peered across to me, nodding at me as if it were my queue and I drew on deep breath and drew even deeper for courage to assist me. Cleared my throat and spoke to the mass who had gathered to say their goodbyes to my father. “A lot of people once told me I was the spitting image of my old man. A lot of kids growing up see this as a bad thing, being compared to their father’s, I however see this as a monumental compliment and am forever grateful that if I could be one eighth the person my father is…” I paused for a moment, not realising my choice of words which seemed to echo in my head. ‘Is’ is the present tense. Unfortunately, when speaking of my dad, there were no longer a present tense. “…was I would be a great man myself. Together we worked hard for dreams which we both shared. Goals that we’d both made. Things that we both wanted and despite whatever which was thrown our way together, the Shepherds were a strong unit and we could destroy entire armies with our hearts.” I pause a moment, just to maintain my composure. This was probably the hardest thing that I had ever done in my entire life. The hardest and proudest. All at once. “My father had a tremendous heart which was open and loving and there would be nobody here that could disagree with that or may God himself strike them down where they stand. He was kind. Warm. Funny. And most of all, sincere with his kindness and his love. “Here lay the man who makes me what I am today and from this day forth I plan to honour his name, his memory and him.” I could feel that lump, which had so frequented my life at this time, forming once again in my throat and it was difficult to slide the words around it so I might finish my proclamation of my father’s greatness. “Dad, thank-you for being there for me. I miss you so much already. And I’ll love you forever more. “Thank God you are my father. And thank-you for being my God.” Step back into line and the emotion finally overcomes me. My knees give out and I crash down onto them, burying my face into my hands and howling into the hole in the ground. Slowly, a few of my uncles and cousins lower my dad into the ground. My Aunt Marianne rubs my back in time with her own cries. I can only stare as my father disappears into the hollow below. Take a handful of dirt and rise to my feet, Aunt Marianne clutches my elbow as if it helped me gain my dignity and strength to be able to stand. I toss the dirt on top of the casket below and in my mind I say good-bye to my father. My father was dead. It had finally sank in. - | s h e p | - BANG! “It is hereby ruled that one hundred percent custody of Adam August Shepherd be granted to Miss April Jane Monday. No visitation rights are permitted to Brock Aardman. It is by order of this court that this is my final ruling without any argument.” BANG! His gavel came down again with tremendous force upon his desk as my fatherhood had been distinguished by a man in a black cloak with a tiny wooden hammer. As if on cue, Adam began to cry within his mother’s arms. She also wept. She looked across at me and I could feel nothing. There was no pain. There was no joy. No nothing. I could not feel. All I could do was sit there and think back to the time my own father was put into the ground. It seemed fitting for this exact moment. The people rise and began to leave the courtroom. August Monday wraps his arm around his sister’s shoulder and he guides her toward the exit, telling her he would meet her outside. “I’ll send ya photos, Brock,” he promises. “I told her she wasn’t doin’ the right thing, man, but she jus’ wouldn’t listen to me. You know that right?” I cannot speak. “Me and you. Wednesday. Some beers. Don’t shut me out, Brock. I’m yer only window to Adam, right?” I nod. It’s all I can muster. Feel his enormous hand clap against my shoulder in consolation as the room empties. “Thank God you are my father. And thank-you for being my God,” I whisper to the emptiness. My son has gone away.
