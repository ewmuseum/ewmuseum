Rainy Dayz #0 - Rescuer

February 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 2-20, 2007 DAY: February 2nd, 2007 CUE: "Rainy Dayz" by Raekwon ---- It's raining. He's changing. My man is going insane. Insane… Los freakin' Angeles. City of Angels. Stan Hepton marched down the street at night, the glare from streetlights reflecting off his polished dome. His trenchcoat hung halfway down his shins, giving him the look of a pale-topped shadow. His eyes were directed straight ahead into the darkness of the California night. Heavy boots thudded on the sidewalk, his pace seeming much more intent than that of the cars that zoomed by on the streets. Through the fabric of the inner lining of his coat, he fingered the butt of the pistol holstered against his hip. Stan shouldn't even be here. He wasn't LAPD. He knew that the LAPD wouldn't get involved in this, though. Easton had apparently known this, too, because he'd told Stan to do this personally. That was another thing: now he was taking orders from his bait? Granted, Easton had guaranteed Stan that this girl was involved in the case, but that didn't help. Stan was here, now, and he had a job to do. Stan stopped as he heard a distressed whimper. Cocking an eyebrow, he glanced over his shoulder. A woman stood there in a pitiful state: her dress was ripped and torn, pale, bruised legs flashing through the tatters, her face contorted with blonde hair splayed all over. Stan slowly turned around. He recognized the face. His fingers closed around a small slip of paper in his pocket and he pulled it out. The detective inspected the picture and then looked back up at the woman. "You!" he cried. The woman's eyes shot open and she let out a shriek, turning about. "Fuck, no! Stop!" Dammit! She'd started running already. Stan noticed that she was barefoot and silently admired her for persevering. She might even be faster than he was. The bald detective sprinted after the woman, hands pumping. The picture slipped from his fingers, blowing backwards in the wind. In a few days, someone would find that image and squeeze one out to it. The chase took them down the sidewalk and around a corner, the distance between them slowly widening. Stan slapped at his coat, trying to force it behind his legs and stop the leather from getting in the way. It wasn't going to aid him too much, but he needed every little bit he could get. Cars honked at him and there were a few shouts, which was far more caring than Stan had ever thought would come from a city with this reputation. He knew how to ignore that shit, though. "Wait!" Stan hollered. She didn't comply; Stan had to fight the urge to pull out his gun and shoot. He didn't need to injure her, or at least, he wasn't supposed to. Easton would get mad. On second thought, he wasn't exactly sure why he cared. Half in spite, Stan drew his black .45, forming his left hand as if it was a second gun with two fingers. "I'll shoot!" Nothing; this chick just kept running. He had to slow her down. Warning shot, Stan cautioned himself, extending his arm, pointing the gun forward. Warning shot, warning shot, warning shot… the rapid explosion and whistle of the gunshot startled the night air. She let out a shriek, her head whipping around to verify that Hepton was the one shooting before darting around a corner. Stan cursed and took the same turn, sliding his gun back into the holster. The woman was huddled up against the far wall of the dead end alley, her hands up to stay Hepton. "Please don't hurt me," she sobbed. "I haven't done anything!" Stan turned around, looking to see if anybody was around, before walking towards her. Obscene graffiti stained the brick walls on all three sides. "I'm not here to hurt you," Stan explained slowly. His thick voice didn't aid his case any, he expected, but he couldn't help that. "You're Kelly Hill?" She watched him for a while silently before nodding her head. Her arms relaxed and she clutched at her knees. "I'm Detective Stan Hepton. I was sent here by an Easton Hall. Know him?" "Yes," she whimpered; there was a spot of hope in her voice. "I do." "Good. It'd complicate things if you didn't." Stan sighed, reaching into his breast pocket and lifting out a pack of Marlboros. "Smoke?" She shook her head. "You look like you need it." She shook her head again. Stan shrugged and slipped a cigarette between his lips. He replaced the pack of cigarettes and fished out his lighter. "Hello, Mr. Hepton," came a gravelly voice from behind Stan. The lighter was poised to light the cigarette, but no flame came up. Intuition told the detective that a gun was pointed to the back of his head; it was important to be cool. Kelly burst out in tears, wailing into the crook of her arm. CUE: "Swordsmen" by GZA "Hey," Stan said conversationally. "I'm sure you won't mind putting your hands up for me." Stan sighed and slowly lifted his arms into the air. He closed his eyes, silently counting. Once he reached three, he spun on the ball of his right foot, turning around what he presumed was the outside of the man's arm – thankfully, he was right – and slammed the blade edge of his hand into the newcomer's neck. The man stumbled against the wall, bracing himself with his forearm. The man was tall, dressed entirely in black robes; Stan could only tell which hand he held the gun in because of where he had struck his assailant. The man's hood fell away from his face a little, revealing a heavily lined, ruddy face underneath. Black eyes stared out at Stan angrily. Hepton lashed out with a kick to the breadbasket, but he felt an arm wrap around his ankle and he found himself thrown over; he managed to catch himself in a roll and come up. Twisting around, Hepton pulled out his gun and aimed it down the alley, but all he saw was Kelly. The stiffness of an elbow rammed down against his neck unexpectedly, throwing the bald detective to all fours. Searing red pain surged through his body and he had to squeeze one eye shut, trying to handle the pain and see at the same time. On pure instinct, he shot his foot backwards and felt it impact bone. Hepton rolled forward and came to his feet, twisting around. The robed man was crouched back a bit. Impulse drove Stan Hepton forward and he swung his arm with an overhand pistol whip, but his forearm collided with his opponent's. Hepton snatched a handful of jet black hair and slammed his knee into the man's side repeatedly, both men grunting with each impact. Hepton heard a clatter on the ground, but he knew that he himself hadn't dropped his gun. That split second reflection ended with a set of four knuckles slamming into his stomach. Hepton staggered backwards and the robed man assailed him with palm strikes and chops, setting the detective's chest on fire. Hepton's grip on his pistol slackened a bit, but he hooked his finger around the trigger guard. His eyes drifted closed for half a split-second, but when he opened, he saw the robed man's fist surging up at him. The dragon punch sent Hepton sailing through the air until his back slammed up against the wall. He groaned as he began to slide down, but just as his toes touched the ground, the wrinkled, iron grip of the robed man caught him about the throat, lifting him back up into the air. Hepton's lazy gaze met his assailant's deadly stare. "You're putting yourself in danger, Mr. Hepton," the man said gravely. "The girl is mine. You hear me?" Kelly's whimpers indicated she, at least, heard. "Ghaack!" Hepton choked. The man sneered. "Do not involve yourself in business between myself and Easton Hall. That boy will—AUGH!" The explosive crack that accompanied the shadowy man's shout seemed to cancel any further words. A rip in those robes was evident and blood ran freely from the wound. Hepton slid down, crouched against the wall. Slowly, he lifted his arm, aiming the gun at the man. The robed man's eyes widened and he dove away just as Hepton fired. Slowly, Stan dragged himself to his feet. The shadowy man reached towards his gun, but Hepton fired again; the man yanked his hand backwards rapidly. Hepton walked forward and the robed man pulled himself off the ground, scrambling backwards. Two more gunshots snapped through the air, but Hepton's aim was lazy. Kelly's sobs were the only things that accompanied Hepton now. He looked upwards at the night sky and felt droplets of rain splashing on his face. DAY: February 4th, 2007 CUE: "K Sera Sera" by Justin Warfield ---- Easton sat up as the phone in his hotel rang. The sheets fell off of his body to his waist. He rubbed his face a bit and rolled over, reaching towards the lamp and clicking it on, spreading light. He pulled the receiver off of the base and pressed it against his ear. "Hello?" he grumbled. "Mr. Hall?" came the male voice from the other end, thick with an Indian accent. "You've got me. What's up?" "A call came through for you." Easton frowned and took a deep breath. No use getting nutso about this. "And you couldn't wait until morning, could you?" "Your employer put this through as urgent," the man said. "From a… Stan Hepton?" Easton gritted his teeth now. Well, he really didn't want to be woken for that jackass, but he couldn't help it. He sat up a little straighter, rubbing his forehead with the heel of his palm. "Play it." "Certainly." There was a short pause before the message started up. "Easton, I found your bitch," came Hepton's voice. "Met this guy, I think you described him as the guy you suspected as the killer? He got away. We're back in Baltimore now. You'd better quit that tour quick, understand me? We've got to find this guy. Besides, you're the one who's supposed to be taking the falls, not me." Easton held the phone against his ear for a while longer. So she was safe. At the same time, if his mentor had made himself known… what did that mean? Kelly could still be in danger. No, no. Kelly was still in danger. Easton set the phone down again, but he didn't pretend like he'd get back to sleep DAY: February 20th, 2007 CUE: "Rainy Dayz" by Raekwon ---- Red rimmed Easton's eyes and bags hung underneath them. He couldn't help it. Kelly's being in danger hadn't let him sleep. He couldn't trust Hepton, and even though he pretty much had to right now, he didn't feel good about it. He didn't feel good about any of this. He pushed himself up off of the concrete on the hotel roof again, continuing his exercise. All of this had put him in quite a spot considering his match coming up on ReV 120. He had to put up a good effort, he knew that. People were looking at him like he might do something. He hadn't managed to capture the Extreme Title, but that didn't mean eyes weren't on him. Just because he had to take the slow road didn't mean they didn't expect him to climb the mountain eventually. The only person in this match that he hadn't beaten was Jay Phoenix, but that didn't mean he couldn't. Easton would do it. The former Ember hadn't met anything like Easton Hall and he wasn't prepared. As for the other two, they would watch him, but it wouldn't help. It never helped. Easton was always ready to bring his A game, to continue his winning ways. In a field of four, you had to push yourself until you couldn't go any more. You had to find yourself, find the will, the resolve to win. None of them would give themselves up easily. But if any of them – Karina Wolfenden, Bastille, Jay Phoenix, Stan Hepton, or Makhpyia Sapa – thought that he would go down easily, they were sadly mistaken. Droplets of rain began a slow pitter-patter on his bare back as he continued to strain his muscles with push-ups. Gettin' through those rainy dayz... all lyrics from "Rainy Dayz" by Raekwon
