Date: Timeless Benjamin Youngblood’s steps wavered around his basement workbench, plumes of cigarette smoke filtering across the wrinkles of his face as he stared down at the ovolo crown molding that had rested above his bedroom door in the family home in downtown Winnipeg. The piece was made from mountain ash and tapered upward, its juts smoothed at the edges and its body drenched in varnish. It was the only architectural hold over from the old haunt, brought to the new home in Bexar County for its sentimental value. After his wife gave birth, he would pull the piece down from the wall and etch the first name of the newborn member of his family. Candace, Brandon, Chris, Lindsay, and Thomas. The basement had been his escape, the only nook of the house he could call his own, and as such, he wanted to fill it with the things that made him happy. Stacks of Amish wood were stacked off to the side, the boards needing a few months before they’d be dry and straight enough to work with. A catalog’s worth of Kraftsman power tools were lined up in bunches along the concrete walls, all plugged into blinking surge protected outlets. He’d bought PVC pipe, networking it in a system of vents that could be pulled open and attached it all to a large scale vacuum cleaner so he could suck up sawdust and metal shavings as he worked. His little sanctuary wasn’t perfect; it had been years since he’d set everything up and he hardly had used any of it. His migraine headaches had been getting worse. Craft boxes from his wife were starting to crop around the edges. The only thing that brought him down here anymore was the barstool and the ash tray. He scratched his stubble beard and took long drags of his cigarette as he looked at the molding, hesitating in his movement because he swore the needle of his etching tool was following his movements. He knew how he felt, knew what he came down here to do. But something was holding him back. He massaged his temples and snuffed his cigarette out in the ash tray, groaning before pounding the workbench. He coughed, breathing in wheezes because his throat was raw.
