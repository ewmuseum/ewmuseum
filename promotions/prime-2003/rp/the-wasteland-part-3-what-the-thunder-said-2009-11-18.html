The Wasteland: Part 3 - What The Thunder Said

18 November 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 18, 2009 (Note: I don't know if it's necessary for me to state it or not, but there's a lot of allusions towards Eliot's "The Wasteland" here, if that wasn't clear from the title, especially in the last section.) The Policeman "Son of a bitch..." Detective Randall Cooper stood over his desk in the early morning of what was supposed to be his day off, staring at the sheets of paper littered across its surface, trying to make sense of it all. He had been unable to sleep the night before, another all-nighter in what was becoming a series of them. He had developed a reputation over the years for his cool-headed, objective approach to his work, but then he had never exactly come across a case like this before. He lifted his paper coffee cup halfway to his lips before, bloodshot eyes narrowing, he realized it was empty. His fist closed around it, crushing it, flinging it across the room at the garbage can, where it careened off the edge, bounced off the wall and came to a rest, twisted and limp, lying inches from the door. "Son of a bitch!" He raised his hand to massage his eyelids, taking a deep breath. The pressure was coming down from all directions, the higher ups, the newspapers, the victims' families. Seven bodies in under a month and no leads save for a single suspect, Fernando de la Torre, who had disappeared off the face of the earth. It was all fucked. Chasing a ghost, grasping at threads that frayed and unraveled at the touch. It could be de la Torre, sure, but it could just as easily be someone else, there could be seven different killers, for he knew. It was easy to pin it on the man with the history, but with so little evidence that there might as well be none, his sleep-deprived brain was beginning to question everything. He needed results and quick, before this blew up into a national scandal. Flipping through the papers on his desk, he soon found the report from the man's son's initial questioning. Diego Foster. He had paid the boy a visit a few weeks ago, but their first conversation had not gone as well as he'd hoped. Thinking about it now, a frown came to his face. The kid had been stubborn, surely he knew more than he was letting on. Cooper had been patient at the time, willing to let the kid off the hook, but three bodies and zero leads later, he began to think it was time to apply a little more pressure. Cooper thought of himself as a just man, but if he had to play a little hardball, with lives hanging in the balance, then so be it. You couldn't get blood from a stone, that was true, but getting blood with a stone, well, now that was another thing entirely. One Last Show "Dude, fuck that weak sauce. You've got to come with us to this show tomorrow night." Diego blinks his eyes, turning his gaze from Wally to Jimbo, then back to Wally again. When his friends gave him a call earlier, asking him if they could drop by, hang out for a while, he had thought nothing of it. Drink a few beers, smoke a little weed, it seemed like just another normal Saturday night. God knows he needed to relax after the week of interviews and press conferences his manager, Alyson, had been putting him through. Looking now at Wally, who is staring at him expectantly, hands on his hips, Diego feels the beginning of a headache coming on. "I told you, man, I can't do it. I'm supposed to go the PRIME show with Alyson on Wednesday, do some promo work for the Jewel in the Crown Tournament. I can't be in two places at once." Wally opens his mouth, finger already pointing at Diego's chest in condescension, only to be cut off by Jimbo. "I can appreciate that. Really, I can. Your career's finally taking off, you've got your shot at the big time. Get to cut a promo in Albuquerque, probably gonna be a lot of DFW fans there, cheering you on" the big man says, his bulldog face smiling with a tinge of sadness. "Can't say I'm not a bit jealous. But hey, you're definitely more deserving than me." "Aw, come on, Jim," Diego says, eyes softening as he pats his friend on the shoulder. "You'll get your shot someday. You're a funny guy. It's just a matter of time, really." "The thing is, we got a chance to do this match next Wednesday over in Phoenix, and the thing is, well, it's a six-man tag, you see. Me and Wally, that's two. We need one more or it's no good." "Yeah, but I'm sure you guys can find someone else, can't you? Mike, Big Ben, anyone else from the DFW roster? Why does it have to be me?" "Mike's working some shows out in Texas this month, and if you think I'm spending two days in a car with Big Ben you're out of your fucking mind," Wally says, face souring at the thought. "It's you or no one, D. So what's it gonna be?" Closing his eyes, hands on his head, Diego falls back onto his couch with a groan. "Look, I just can't do it, guys. Alyson'll have my head if I don't show." "Who cares what that bitch thinks?" Wally snaps. "You were just going on about how annoying she's been. She's all talk anyway. You don't owe her anything." Placing his eighth empty beer bottle down on the table, Jimbo stands up, swaying slightly before steadying himself. "Care if I use your bathroom, D?" "Go ahead," Diego says, eyes still closed. "Just don't piss on the seat again." "Ha ha. Right-o." Wally follows Jimbo to the rear of the apartment with his eyes, and at the sound of the door clicking shut he leaps over to couch next to Diego, grabbing him by the collar of his shirt. Diego's eyes snap open in surprise, a sudden anger appearing in them that make the skinny Wally release his hold on his friend's shirt and slink back a few inches. "What the fuck are you doing?" The words bring Wally back to himself. He leans in, speaking in a hissing whisper. "Look man, Jimbo's too proud to say it, but he really needs the money from this match." Diego hisses back. "What, you're trying to guilt trip me now? Jimbo can get paid just the same no matter who you get to anchor your team." "You don't get it," Wally shakes his head. "Nobody in Phoenix knows who me and Jimbo are. They're gonna pay us peanuts. If we can bring Mr. PRIME Superstar in, on the other hand..." "On the other hand what?" Wally sticks up two fingers in the air. "Two? Two hundred?" "Thousand." "....Shit." "A third of that will cover Jimbo's rent for the month, let him relax, take things easy. If you're not in, we'll be lucky to pull a fraction of that." Diego opens his mouth to say something, then closes it again. "You've got no obligations to PRIME. They haven't signed you to a contract yet. You're just wrestling on a match by match basis." Wally says, more animated, sensing blood. "Do it for Jimbo, man. He needs this." Diego sighs. "Alright, count me in. Just this one time, though." he says, raising a single finger. The sound of the toilet flushing is followed seconds later by an emerging Jimbo, who's still readjusting his belt and zipping up his crotch. Wally opens another beer, raising it in the air in Jim's direction. "Well you can go home and pack your bags, Jimbo. We're going to Phoenix." Diego was glad to see Jimbo smile sincerely as he came in to clink bottles with his friends, but though he smiled back at him, he couldn't shake the feeling that he was making the wrong decision. DF-Dubbya!!! DF-Dubbya!!! Going into week three of DFW's temporary suspension of operations, rumors began to surface about the new owners. Most of the wrestlers were still in the dark regarding the future of the promotion but apparently someone, somewhere had heard from someone else, whose girlfriend knew a guy that had a connection who worked in the mail room of the probate court, who had overheard or else come across some documentation or correspondence or, well, something official concerning the transfer of company ownership to the late "Cowboy" Jack McKenzie's next of kin. I'm sure you know how these things tend to happen. Information gets out, spreads around, nothing stays secret for long. Mrs. Marjorie Grady was McKenzie's sister, three years older and even fatter than old Jack had been, if there were any truth to the rumors. One of the old hands said he remembered seeing her at an old DFW Christmas party Jack had thrown in the early days of the promotion, oozing through the hallways in Jabba the Hutt-like fashion, but he had been heavy into hallucinogens at the time, so he couldn't be sure. So the way the story went, apparently the entirety of McKenzie's assets, DFW included, were willed to his sister, what with him having no other relatives save a detested ex-wife and a disowned homosexual son. Now Marjorie, a women with few interests outside of writing letters of complaint to network television stations and non-competitive eating, had no idea how to even begin going about managing an independent professional wrestling federation, let alone any desire to learn. However her son, Jackson, an old-school PCW/OSW fan from back in the day, recently unemployed, seemed to be warm to the idea. Latching on to this piece of information like a boa constrictor, Big Ben Harris went to work. Ben spent a lot of time on the DFW message boards. Some might call it an inordinate amount of time. What started as an innocent interest akin to googling one's name, he began to search the forums for mentions of himself. He was disappointed to see that such posts were far and few between, and the few he came across were not exactly positive, harping on his sloppy offense, his unrealistic reactions and his over-all awkwardness in the ring. The ones that didn't concern themselves with calling him a homosexual, that is. Unable to let such things pass, Ben felt obligated to defend himself. Making a fake account, he began to reply to all the posts attacking him, even those that were months old, dragging them out from the dredges of the forum. Most of the other posters assumed he was a troll, mocking him, saying that he was really Big Ben in disguise. They meant this as a joke, of course, not knowing how spot-on they actually were. In time Ben realized how suspicious his posting habits were and began to make alternate accounts in an effort to make it seem that he had a supportive fan-base. He even attempted to mask these transparent attempts with accounts that had usernames like GetBENt or BenhasAIDS, which he used to run down the posts he made on his pro-Ben accounts, in hopes that he could make it seem like he was a hot topic. The replies to some of these threads would run into the triple digits, most of it being no more than Ben talking with himself. In fact, over 10% of the 176 registered accounts on the forums belonged to Ben (including a good half dozen dormant accounts he had forgotten the password to), and if one bothered to check, they would see that Ben's posting alone accounted for over 80% of forum activity. Sometimes he would dish some insider information, things like future booking plans or locker room gossip, and wait eagerly for someone to respond. This information about a possible new owner fit the bill exactly, and he was excited because it had been a couple weeks of nothing happening, just waiting for some news. The traffic on the site was not exactly heavy, so it was not unusual for him to find himself waiting a long time for a response. He would click around on his bookmarked pages for a minute or so before coming back to the DFW boards, frowning to see that no one had yet responded. He would get up and go to the bathroom, come back and refresh the page, drumming his fingers impatiently on the mouse, seeing no response. Refresh, refresh, refresh. Eating Spam out of the can, drinking grape Fanta straight from the two liter bottle, staying up until three in the morning, staring at the screen, eyes tired and bloodshot, just waiting in anticipation. Refresh, refresh, refresh. And so on, into the night. On the Road Diego sat leaning back in his seat, staring at the lack of scenery out the side window as he attempted to somehow ignore the hip-hop music blaring from Wally's speakers. In line with a predominant trend of his generation's nouveau-middle class, Wally claimed to love rap music, enamored with the genre's fixation on his three favorite things in the world: money, cash, and hoes. (This even though though every last one of his friends was white, even though he seemed extremely uneasy about stepping into the ring with any of the black talent that had been on the DFW roster.) The CD currently bumping through the the subwoofers that Wally bought "second-hand" in a 7-11 parking lot seemed to Diego as particularly cliché. He could only listen to so many tracks about getting paid in a row before his brain completely shut it out, especially with all the talk of money problems still fresh in his mind. With Jimbo sleeping his hangover off in the backseat and Wally busy incorrectly mouthing the lyrics along with the song, Diego allowed his mind to wander. Part of him felt guilty at skipping out on the PRIME show that night. Or to be more accurate, he felt that he should be feeling guilty. But the more honest, real feeling was one of relief. The past week, running around with Alyson, doing photo shoots, interviews, shaking hands with fans outside house show events, it had been really fucking exhausting. It was certainly nothing like he imagined it would be. Fun didn't even come into it. There was none of the laid-back jocularity that characterized the backstage at the DFW Arena. Everyone was so professional, cold and impersonal. It would be one thing if Alyson showed the least bit of warmth to him at all, just a hint of a flirtation yet to come, maybe that could give him something to look forward to throughout the day, but he had yet to notice even a spark of affection. Yeah, she smiled and laughed at him, but it was always very patronizing, she was always talking down to him like she knew everything about the business. Which she didn't. She was as new to all this spotlight stuff as he was, though she didn't let on to that in the beginning. Just because she'd been working for three years in some office building, pushing paperwork around for PRIME, doesn't mean she knew any more about the wrestling business than he did. She was just as desperate to get ahead as he was, maybe even more. He regretted allowing her to attach herself to him, but he had to admit, had always had a soft spot for a pretty face. Not that she was even that great looking. She had a nice face, dressed in a sexy, yet professional business matter, had that long, curly hair that smelled so good... ok, ok, he thought, she was pretty good-looking. He was beginning to wonder what she would be like in bed when the sound of his cell phone ringing interrupted his train of thought. "Who is it, D?" Wally asked, unable to mind his own business, as usual. Looking at the number on the caller ID. "Alyson." Wally rolled his eyes. "Turn it off." Diego stared at the name on the caller ID, weighing his options. He figured he owed her at least an explanation. She'd turn up at the show by herself if he didn't, expecting him to meet her there. And if he didn't....woo boy, there was going to be a shitstorm. Then again, he didn't feel like arguing with her right now. Or ever. He clicked the power button on the phone, turning it off. If she really wanted to hang onto him, he figured, she'd find some way in her heart to forgive him. "Right on," Wally said, nodding his head as he watched Diego put away his phone. "I still say I should be your manager." "Yeah, but I want the fans to like me." "Whatever," he said, turning his head away, nostrils flaring. He'd forgotten the slight a moment later. "Anyway, hey, what do you think about stopping for the day, checking into a motel? I've wrestled for this fed before. The arena's like fifteen minutes away." "I don't care. You know I'm not picky." "I know a good motel coming up here. The best part about it? It's got a bar next door." "Are you sure that's the best ide..." "What's this I'm hearing about a bar?" Jimbo asked, stifling a yawn as he sat up stretching in the back seat. "I was just telling D that we're gonna stay at this motel with a sweet bar next door to it." "Sounds lovely." That uncomfortable feeling had returned to nestle in Diego's gut. Considering Jim's behavior as of late, this really didn't seem to be the best idea. Yet he couldn't just come out and say that. The situation required tact. "Should we really be blowing our windfall before we even earn it?" "Sorry D, my god is Bacchus and it's only right that I tithe to him a tenth of my earnings," Jimbo said, drawing a laugh from Wally. "Besides, this place is cheap as chips. Nothing to worry about," Wally said, pulling into the parking lot of the motel. It being two against one, Diego could only shrug. * * * But Diego was right to worry about his friend. Over the course of a long summer Jimbo's drinking had intensified, gradually and then drastically. Where before his outbursts had been rare and troubling, now they were commonplace and ever more easily overlooked. And he was beginning to look more haggard, dark bags taking up permanent residence beneath his eyes, cheeks sunken and pock-marked, one would be tempted to call him syphilitic, if not for the undeniably real note of despair in his voice whenever he bemoaned his involuntary chastity, which seemed to be quite often, regardless of audience. Listening to him complain about his lack of sex life, the other two kindergarten teachers at his school, both women, nodded sympathetically, even though they talked about how much he weirded them out behind his back. They felt Jim was nice enough guy, he could be really funny when not on this self-pity jag, but they preferred to think of him as a nonsexual being. Masturbating in his apartment at night, muttering their names to himself in a drunken slur, Jim found himself unable to respond in kind. Things finally came to head one day in late October. Jimbo had been staying out later and later, until one day he woke up, still drunk, in an alley on the side of a bar, shirt soiled from laying against the dumpster for who knows how long since last call. Having no idea what time it was, his watch likely stolen the night before, he took a series of buses to his school only to arrive two and a half hours late. With this incident, the other teachers could no longer turn a blind eye to his abnormal behavior and unkempt appearance. Delicate questioning of the students yielded damning testimony. "He fell down during the Hokey Pokey." "I was standin outside tha baffroom and it sounded like he was wrestlin' wit' an alligator in there." "His breath smells like Grandpa." This was enough for the school board to form an emergency performance review panel. Looking to minimize any potential conflict, they offered him the rest of the school year on special leave at half his normal salary, after which he was expected to quietly walk away. They told him not ungently that he should use the time to get his life together, but they also made it clear that if he tried to fight them on this they would drag his name through the proverbial mud, make sure he came out of the fracas completely unemployable. So Jimbo followed their advice, at least the part about walking away without a fight. He had done little to curtail his drinking habit, and in fact, not having a job to wake up for in the morning had a way of only making things worse. * * * Five hours later it took both Wally and Diego to carry Jimbo back to the hotel room. At first they were willing to let him walk under his own power, but a mere five minutes into this experiment and it was proven to be a tmistake. Looking at the large red blotch on Jimbo's cheek, already discolored, Diego predicted a particularly nasty bruise would surface by the morning. (The next day at the arena Diego caught Wally bragging backstage that the bruise had resulted from a bar fight the three had been in, while a couple of old shooters looking on skeptically. When questioned, he raised his hands innocently, claiming he was only trying to bolster their "rep" in a new territory.) Wally excused himself at the first possible moment to return to the single room he had demanded for himself elsewhere in the motel, leaving Diego alone with Jimbo in a room with two twin beds. Jimbo laid out on one, Diego stretched himself out on the other, feeling sorry for the big guy. In a perverse inverse relationship that eluded Diego's understanding, the less fun Jimbo became, the more time he felt obliged to spend with him, until more often than not he found himself babysitting his incoherent friend at the bar when he would rather be relaxing at home by himself, smoking a doob and watching TV. If he thought about it enough, he imagined that this obligation had to have come from watching his mother undergo a similar disintegration. But if it was true that it stemmed from the heavy burden of experience, he could not understand how he could watch so calmly, a dispassionate bystander, as Jim fell prey to the same inner demons that devoured Diego's mother, leaving their jagged teeth marks all along his broken childhood. He bore the memory of these scars, but had these self-same scars rendered him insensate, severing irreparably the cords of his emotional machinery? He was surprised to hear Jimbo muttering to himself. Turning to face him, he saw his friend sitting up in his bed, wild eyes roaming the room, fixing on nothing particular. He pushed himself off the headboard, using the momentum to propel himself to his feet and stagger halfway across the room. This can not end well, Diego thought, and sure enough, just when he seemed to be in the clear, Jimbo stumbled to his knees and there was vomit everywhere, mashed into the carpet, speckled on the walls like a Jackson Pollack painting. Feeling sorry for the cleaning lady tomorrow morning, Diego helped Jimbo to the bathroom and started cleaning him off, pausing momentarily to allow him to throw up again. A damp towel cleaned off most of it, but a proper shower would have to wait until morning. By the time Diego returned his friend to his bed, he was muttering to himself again. "You guys....ooo....you guys are a couple of cunts." Not knowing whether he was joking or not, Diego forced a laugh. "Yeah, why do you say that, Jimbo?" "I know you and Wally're always looking down on me. Poor Jimbo, fat Jimbo, ugly Jimbo. Can't wrestle, can't keep a job. A useless drunk." "Come on, I've never said any of that that. Not to you and definitely not behind your back." "You don't gotta say it. I can see it in your eyes," he says, eyes wide open, frantic. And then a second later, a calmness descends. "But I'll tell you one thing. You're no better than me. Me and my booze, you and your marijuana....we're all running scared from the same thing." "What? I'm not running away from anything." "Yes you are, the same as me. It's life that's got us on the run. Despair that's got us by the balls. We'll do anything to get away from it." "You should go back to sleep, Jimbo. You look tired." "You and me, D. Two of a kind. Two peas in a pod. Two peas in a...in a Pequod. Where's your white whale gotten off to, Mr. Ahab...." Thirty seconds later Jimbo's snores were echoing throughout the room. Frowning, Diego sat back on his own bed, thinking about what Jimbo had said. He may have thought that they were the same, and though there may even have been a grain of truth in it, Diego knew better. He knew all about escapism, and whatever Jimbo was doing, he wasn't escaping anything – he was running right into it, where the menace welcomed him with open arms, closing around him, drawing him in tightly. Too far down this road and there would be no escape left, save the one that waits for everyone at the end. Diego thought again about saying something, sitting down and talking things out with Jimbo before it was too late. Maybe the next day when they were both rested and more reasonable would be best. However, the urgency of this thought had mostly faded by morning, and Diego again succumbed to that easiest of all cowardices, the sin of omission. The car had come to a stop and he heard the opposite door open and shut again. Blinking his eyes, he tried to focus his thoughts. While the sickness from earlier seemed to have passed, he felt far from calm, his heart beating rapidly as he stared out the front window at the nothingness in front of him. It was night and judging from the landscape, the car was stopped somewhere in the Sonoran Desert. Diego heard sounds coming from behind him, some rustling in the back of the car. Taking a deep breath, then another, he opened the door and stepped outside. His suspicions were confirmed as he saw the black devil from earlier bent over, reaching into the trunk of the car. Diego was about to call out, but seeing a bound and blindfolded form being yanked from the from the car to the ground shocked him into silence. The devil prodded his prisoner forward. The man had his head-shaved, was wearing a sleeveless white shirt and long baggy blue cargo pants. Diego recognized him as the man who had given him the weed earlier. Recalling the man's sly wink, he shuddered. Seeing the devil was not willing to wait, Diego fell into step behind him, following as he marched his prisoner off into the desert. They walked for some minutes, Diego holding his tongue, resisting the urge to cry out, until he could not hold himself back anymore. "What the fuck is going on here?!" But there was no answer. The only sound was thunderstorm gathering overhead, looming ominous, foreboding, flashing down with a sinister crack. DA! And on into the desert, minutes, hours, Diego had no idea. His legs were sore, numbed, but on he walked. If he stopped he risked getting left behind, lost in the barren wastes, so he stumbled after, gasping for air, grasping for meaning. Following, following, sometimes thinking he was hallucinating. Sometimes seeing a mysterious figure in brown keeping step beside the devil and the prisoner, then blinking to see that three had become two. Finally the devil stopped.
