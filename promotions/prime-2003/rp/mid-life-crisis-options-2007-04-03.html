Mid Life Crisis - 'Options'

3 April 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/4/07 ‘I’m really glad that you performed the way you did Jack. It was truly impressive.’ You know, it feels good to hear that from your girlfriend. Really good. ‘Thanks babe. How goes the work on the gym?’ I tried to ask about her but the words wouldn’t come. Instead, pleasantries about the project were all I could muster. ‘Fine. It looks like it’ll be done by the end of the month. Will you be here to see its opening?’ The audible sigh of Jessica let me know that she too had tried, attempted to make the connection. So far and so far away. ‘I’ll try. But you never know how things are going to turn out. Listen, I have to go because I’ve got some business to take care of ok? I’ll talk to you soon though.’ ‘Sure Jack. I’ll let you get back to work and pass on any of the scouting that’s happening if any turns up alright?’ ‘Perfect. Can’t be opening a wrestling gym without prospects after all.’ ‘Nope. Anyway, goodbye Jack.’ ‘Goodbye.’ Click. *** ‘So give me the deal Barney, you know that what you’re saying is utter bullcrap and you know it. Give it to me straight like the old days.’ I was busy eating the core of an apple, spitting a pip every now and then to a clanging effect as they rolled around the inside of the metal can that sat nearby. Barney was stuffing his face typically, this time with the prime rib steak that he had carefully selected from the diner menu, but it looked like every other rib steak to me. Apparently Jimbob’s (oh yes, that was the full name of the place), was renowned for this steak the world over. People flocked to it like no other diner that was situated in the region of Nevada and I had no clue why. It was just like any diner you might see on television, right down to the trim red leather and the hokey fifties outfits for the style. I wouldn’t be surprised if that on some of the days they wear roller skates too. All in all, it was just like any general diner and apart from its renown, had no exciting feature about it whatsoever. We’d stopped for some coffee and a bit to eat before we headed into Reno itself and signed in to our hotels. Currently, Barney was mulling over the special steak and getting it ready to enter his mouth so that his palette could be saturated and engorged with flavour. ‘I am giving it to you straight Jack. Dave has some big plans for you and some others and that he’s still trying to finalise the details before he involves any of the parties. He has to get the go ahead from the main committee in order to get anything green lighted. You know that as well as I do.’ He cut a sliver of the steak and held it up to the eye so that it slightly glinted in the sunlight bearing through the windows. The grease dripped painfully from the meat, a glob of honey goodness to the fat man. His tongue licked his lips full of saliva and looked at me once again. ‘Are you sure that you won’t try some? You’ll never understand what I’m trying to get across if you don’t have a bite.’ ‘I’m sure Barney. Besides, I’m trying to get on a regime to get the definition and power back into the muscles. I don’t want to look jacked just yet but toning and defining the muscles would help for the image. Letting it sag like some of the older guys I know wouldn’t help sell my case for the future would it?’ Barney shrugged. ‘I suppose. Then again, I don’t need to have a physique for my expertise. So I get to enjoy life.’ ‘There’s more to life than food Barney.’ ‘And you would know how?’ I went to reply but the answer I could give felt hollow and insincere. He was right and his approach was valid. Food for him was his addiction, just like wrestling was mine. I clucked my tongue not to give in though. ‘Irrespective of our debilitations Barney, that still doesn’t mean I don’t enjoy life. I got to see the sights of San Diego, a place I hadn’t been to for a long time and I was able to appreciate the changes about the place. Not many people who visit as tourists can say that.’ ‘You know all that you said to me was that you enjoyed watching erosion. That doesn’t seem like living to me.’ ‘I…’ ‘Just forget it for now. You see this piece of prime rib? It’s a famous piece of prime rib. People come from around the world to sample the delicacy of Jimbob’s finest. They can’t explain it, they can’t describe the compulsion, they just come because they’re fascinated by the oddity of it. You may think that I come here because of my love of food - but it’s more than that. This steak, gives me something that I can’t find anywhere else.’ I could only be amused by Barney, staring at the sliver of steak placed on his fork expertly, turning it over in the sunlight like he was still cooking the choice cut. He didn’t let my smirk stop him though. ‘This steak, makes me feel part of the culture and the industry of where it came from. It isn’t just a simple food to others. They really do care and are proud that people come from all over the world. Maybe that’s why they display the signs outside so prominently. So when I eat this, I’m appreciating the culture, no matter how basic it might be to some, of those who care.’ He plopped the steak right in his mouth and gave it a long chew before slowly swallowing it. His eyes looked at me then. ‘So can you say that when you looked at the changes that had been invoked for whatever reasons, be it natural, man made or otherwise, did any of it really connect you to the place? Did they really give a response inside you other than interest?’ The long pause let me hear the bustle of the diner about us, crammed full of people that were all, and I mean all, chomping down on the famous prime rib. Not a table was without it and all the different demographics were represented. ‘I suppose…I suppose I just thought it was interesting. I guess I didn’t really sense a connection.’ Barney nodded knowingly. ‘That’s ok Jack, it doesn’t mean you can’t establish a connection with things like that but it does bring up a valid point and something you can work on.’ ‘Oh yeah?’ ‘Yeah. When you’re in the middle of a match and things are running smoothly, the engine is humming, the gears are clicking into place and the mood is just right, tell me this: how do you feel?’ ‘Exhilarated, ecstatic and in control. Powerful, I’ve got a sense of achievement and pride about the entire affair.’ ‘Exactly, as you should. Now tell me this: how do the fans react?’ Again, a long silence amidst the crowded diner. This time no random conversations or select noises could be overhead because the rush of silence in my head was enough to warrant attention. The words came out bitterly and with regret but truthfully and that was the main part. ‘I don’t know.’ ‘Exactly. Now, if you want to reach that top, you’ve got to learn how to connect with them for the desired effect. Nobody is going to care how good you are if you can’t get them on board, for better or worse. You need them to care about what you’re doing, whether they’re booing or cheering you. You of all people should understand this after being in the business this long. It isn’t how proficient you are with your craft that matters…’ He chomped down on another bit of steak. ‘…It’s how good it tastes to those who are the judges.’ *** With our short stop (and lesson for the day) over with, we returned to the road, me driving all the way into Reno as my passenger snoozed in the back of the car. He probably could’ve been awake the whole time and I wouldn’t have heard him because many things were rolling through my head after the mentor had given his pupil a lesson he wouldn’t forget. Despite all the years in this business, Barney had never given me that kind of talk before. It wasn’t out of character at all for him to do something like that and it seemed perfectly within his sphere of influence to concoct and master such a tale, it was more the timing that he was giving it to me that bothered my mind. If I had always had trouble with such a thing, why hadn’t he told me before when we were trolling around in the indies? Was it perhaps that he didn’t understand the concept then or he refused to tell it to me in case he got left behind? Both were plausible and good reasons for him to keep such information close to his heart. You’ve got to make yourself look useful after all in wrestling, because if you ain’t doing anything worth noting, then why would you be there in the first place? Perhaps this was Barney’s reward for standing strong beside him for so long and finally I could reap the benefits of his knowledge. Or maybe it wasn’t worth it before now for me to know because it truly wouldn’t have an effect on the crowd. Maybe he knew it was only a big time talent. Maybe…or maybe two hundred other things. I couldn’t make head nor tail of it and I wasn’t about to try and figure it out while I was trying to concentrate on the road. With the windows firmly up in place, sand blowing about occasionally from the wind and the sun beginning to set on the town of Reno, I pulled the car we’d rented into the non-descript motel that Barney had prescribed for us both. Nobody could claim that we weren’t making good money in this business here in PRIME, however that didn’t mean we didn’t have expense too. Rental Car? Paid for by myself and Barney Gas? Myself and Barney Hotel? Chalk that one up to us again Food? Check Toiletries? Same again Injury? Ditto In fact, I was hard pressed to think of something that the company did pay for. Unless there was some hidden expense that we weren’t aware, then I’ll be damned if we didn’t pay for everything. Now add that all up over the month and subtract it from your pay check and I’m sure that it doesn’t look nearly as impressive to anyone who has a desk job. ‘Barney.’ A snort was heard from the backseat, followed by loud farting. ‘Huh?’ ‘We’re here.’ ‘Really? And here I was thinking we were miles out. That must have only been a half hour or so yeah?’ ‘Three hours and twenty minutes.’ ‘Really?’ ‘Really.’ *** The non-descript hotel wasn’t really much to speak of and the amount of time I planned on staying there was limited to sleeping and eating when I had to. As such, I didn’t waste any time of settling into my room and going to bed. The cruel noise of a hard knock on the door was too much for me at eight in the morning. I grumbled and mumbled, slowly crawling out of bed and putting some pants on before opening the door. Even without my trusty wrestling boots I stood a good six foot five so there was no doubt that I still looked intimidating and cranky. Especially with my sleepy face on. ‘Morning Jack.’ Oh great, Dave Price. The slick college boy wafted through the doorway and another person followed closely behind him, immediately inspecting the mess of the room, looking me over once and twice, cooing and then hurriedly pulling a notebook out of his pocket and scrabbling notes onto the paper. Price looked about for a brief moment but was more interested in hearing what this man had to say. ‘Who the…’ ‘Shush Jack, this is Dante, your new image consultant.’ ‘My what?’ ‘Image consultant. You remember from the dossier, page thirty three?’ ‘I’ve only gotten to page twelve so far!’ Price clucked his tongue and straightened the designer tie he was wearing despite the immense heat outside. The sun was already beating down onto the path outside and it reverberated into the motel room, starting to heat the place up like a sauna. Oh what fun this was. ‘Well, you’ve got to catch up because we have got big plans for you today. I hope you didn’t have anything in mind for the day?’ ‘Well I was thinking…’ ‘Don’t be joking. There’s no time for you to think other than to focus on what you’re good at and that’s wrestling. Dante here is going to be the first in a line of hangers on that are going to help and create a new look for the Bull. Speaking of which, I’m not sure that I like the nickname anymore. It’s too…rustic.’ My head was already in my hands, rubbing the salt that were clearly in my wounds right now. He was like a boy kicking a puppy with the way he was blatantly disregarding everything that was going on. I swore that he could’ve been my agent the way he was blabbering on so I asked the question. ‘I didn’t hire you as my agent did I?’ ‘Of course not, why would you think that?’ ‘Well, perhaps it’s the fact that you’ve invaded my privacy with a man I’ve never met before in my life who is currently scrutinising every inch of my body…some parts a little bit too much!’ Instinctively, I pulled a pillow from the bed to cover my crotch as the man cooed once again. Price gave the kind of noise that you do when you’re not really listening other than to make sure you’re dismissing something that isn’t important to them. ‘Relax Jack, this is a new century and a new time. We’ve got to get you up to speed and your denim jeans and boots are not the look that is going to sell you to the fans. Dante, what do you think?’ The man that was called Dante swirled his way up to me, licking his finger and tracing it down my body almost inconsequentially. I batted it away before he could get near my crotch, still wary of his lustful eyes that seemed to be undressing me readily. He smooched at me - I’m not kidding he smooched at me - and then turned back to Dave, ignoring me completely. ‘He has potential, and such delicious fire that can be brought out with the right colours and choices. I’m thinking that he wears all black in the ring?’ ‘How did you know? Yes, yes he does.’ What a con man that college boy was. ‘Well…I think we could get away keeping that the same for now. But as for day to day wear…there needs to be some serious alterations.’ ‘How quickly can it be changed?’ ‘Not now, you’ll need to give me a week or two to finalise and stylise his new look but it shouldn’t be more than two weeks.’ Price clasped his hands together with glee. ‘Excellent. Thank you Dante, I think you better leave now.’ With a swish of his fingers, he twirled out of the room and left just the primadonna booker and myself to talk. ‘You know, you could’ve warned me about this last night.’ ‘And spoil the fun of surprising you? No Jack, it was better this way. Besides, Dante works best when we’re in our natural states. There’s no clothing to clog up his creativity that way. Now, there are other things that I’ve got to go through with you.’ ‘Like what?’ ‘Things that are far bigger and further reaching than mere image recognition. I want you to promise something to me.’ ‘What?’ ‘Promise that you’ll look good for your triple threat match at ReVolution.’ I looked at him quizzically. ‘What difference does it make? It’s not like I’m going to go out there and lose on purpose.’ ‘Of course not, but I just don’t want you to be seen to be inferior to Lavelle. That could…damage the plans laid out for the future ok?’ I still had no fucking clue what was going on. ‘Alright. It’s just a triple threat match anyway.’ ‘You haven’t heard the stipulation then?’ ‘No.’ ‘Ah right. Well, since you impressed so much against that communist, they’ve decided to make it a contender’s match. A belt around the waist always makes things easier to get across to the committee you know.’ He paused and headed towards the door. When he reached it, he turned behind it, cradling the hinge with his hands. ‘Now, please get dressed and be ready as quick as you can. I’ve got to go over the marketing, the branding and the eventual plans that we’ve got for you. I want you on board with what I’ve got, then I can go to the committee and push it. If I’ve got your agreement, especially with the victory that you’ve assured me, then all will be well in PRIME land for you Jack.’ ‘I…’ ‘No more words. Just get ready.’ Oh my head was spinning. *** ‘That’s great news Jack, I knew that things would start to turn around. I told you that you were impressive last week.’ ‘Thanks Jess.’ A long, long pause. ‘Well, what are you doing for the rest of the day?’ ‘I don’t know, probably get some groceries, look over logistics for the building of the new house, things like that. What about you Jack?’ ‘Apparently I’ve got to go over marketing plans, image consultation, the proposed angle Dave wants to pitch to the committee and then maybe I might have a bit of a rest and some exercise. Nothing too strenuous you know?’ The chuckle I tried to give after it was half-hearted and lacking in vigour. Jessica replied with the same empty laugh and we both sighed at the end of it. ‘Well, I guess I better leave you to it.’ ‘Yeah…I guess. I love you.’ ‘I know Jack. I know.’ Click. *** Time went pretty quickly as Dave had all manner of blueprints and diagrams to try and wow me with. Frankly I hadn’t a clue what was going on so the effect was a little minimised by it. After all was said and done about it though I did know one thing. I was impressed. There were so many ways I could go with myself in the next month or two and it excited me to know that that was going to be possible. I had the potential to break out and finally reap the reward of my hard toil. As Dave kept repeating to me over and over though: you’ve got to keep winning. Got to keep winning, pretty simple mantra but one that I had to hold close to my heart and make sure that I followed through. There is no disrespect meant to my two opponents coming up but clearly there is only one person that is going to be hungry for more and that’s going to be me. The two of them still have time on their side and still have the chance to turn around and do something else if they wanted to. Me, I’m a wrestler. And I need this.
