An Unlikely Alliance

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: After ReV 230 The sun poured in off the water, warm and bright and not uncommon for the tail end of another Indian Summer in New England. Boats, anchored solidly and tied sturdily to the docks, bobbed in the waters of Hobson's Wharf, and the parking lot adjacent to Becky's Diner had been filled with cars since the wee hours of the morning. It seemed like another typical Monday for the greasy spoon diner that had been serving the patrons of Portland, Maine since 1991, with the exception of a new client that none of the regulars, or waitstaff, had seen before. A short, thin woman in sunglasses and business attire sat in a booth near the rear of the restaurant, a carafe of coffee to her right and papers in front and to her left. She wore the sunglasses to shield her eyes from the sunlight and also in the hopes that nobody would recognize her. Lisa Tyler was not above local dives such as these, but after a raucous night of ReVolution she didn't need any further distractions. It felt nice to have some semblance of power again, although it came at the cost of Devin Shakur's sudden unraveling following his loss to Tony Gamble. She knew she should be happy; after all, both she and Blaine Blair were unceremoniously stripped of power to allow for Shakur to run rampant over PRIME. Prometheus was content to see what the Golden Ticket holder would do with himself, despite both Lisa's and Blaine's attacks on Devin's character and their pleas to not let him assume the control he wanted. Their words fell on deaf ears. Whatever it was—a ratings ploy, a structural shake-up, a game for the Board's amusement—it appeared that the tides were turning in Lisa's favor once again. Now, if only she could get Blaine back in the saddle without Shakur overriding her... The din in the restaurant reached a crescendo as the door opened and another unexpected, yet more familiar, guest crossed through the threshold from the outside. Tall, athletic, clad in workout gear and her own pair of sunglasses, she pauses to scan the floor and then make her way towards Lisa. Patrons reach for their phones but take pause when the woman presses her index finger to her lips, asking them for both quiet and their cooperation. Who would deny a Queen such a simple request? Lindsay Troy plops down across from Lisa and tosses her sunglasses onto the table. She pulls the elastic from her pony-tail, shakes out her curls, and then re-affixes it into a much neater coif. Lisa checks her watch and scowls. "You're late." "I know. Didn't bother to set the alarm when we got back from the show and slept through the front desk's wake-up call." "And where's your merry band of men?" "That's cute," Lindsay smirked. "One's on a business call, the other went back to sleep after I told him I was 'going for a run.' I'll let you figure out which is which." "At this hour, it shouldn't be too hard to figure out." Lisa scowled. "I distinctly remember asking last night for both you and Dan to come this morning." "What he's doing is more important. Besides, whatever you have to say I can always tell him myself." Lindsay turned over her upside-down coffee mug, placed it on the saucer, and reached for the carafe of coffee. Lisa slapped her hand away. "Get your own. Your punishment for holding me up." "My niece says that sharing is caring." "Well, your niece isn't the one who's going to be signing your paychecks again, is she." Lindsay leaned against the back of booth and folded her arms in her lap. "Alright then. I'll ask one of Portland's finest salt-of-the-earth types to bring me some, if you're going to be territorial about it." "My time and my coffee aren't things to be wasted." To make her point, Lisa poured herself another cup and took a sip. "This brew better not be as weak as that excuse." Lindsay waved her hand in the air and a waitress strode over. "May I get some coffee, please?" "Regular or decaf, hun." "Regular, please. Just a cup. I don't need a big thing like Miss Crankypants on the other side of the table." "You betcha." The waitress winked, then departed. "Touche, Troy," The smug look on her counterpart's face made Lisa shake her head. "Now that Shakur gave me back the power he took from me all those months ago, I decided to do a little research into some production aspects of Colossus. Do you know what I found?" "Yet another amazing pay-per-view put on by the roster." "That, yes. But more specifically: glitches." "Glitches." "You don't even phrase that like it's a question." "I didn't think I needed to." "I find it curious that there were some security footage hiccups, and that they happened to have occurred around the approximate time of a cage being lowered during a particular match in the evening." "That is quite...intriguing." "Not as intriguing as the cameras in the hallway by your locker room going dark three-quarters of the way through Devin's match and coming back up right before Killean walks into your locker room." The waitress returned with Troy's coffee and is thanked for it. Lindsay reached for the plastic sugar holder, took two packets, and sprinkled them into her drink. She pointed to the cream and Lisa slid a bowl of ice-chilled pouches toward her. "Apparently I am to assume that you're coming to me with this information because you happen to think I had something to do with Von Kelsig being laid out and the cage trapping Daniels to prevent him from helping Devin out." Lisa cracked a sly grin. "Something like that." "I see." Troy paused and slowly poured two creams into her coffee. "Do you know why I played those games with Sloboda, Devin, and Matt?" "Everyone knows by now your reasons for targeting Ward." "They know one reason. The other reason has to do with the other two." Lindsay stirred the liquid until it turned from deep black to light brown. "Matt and Devin are both Hall of Famers and both former Universal Champions. Add Sloboda in there and you've got three former Universal Champions—the only ones who were still on the roster during that time between Culture Shock and UltraViolence." "Hm." "Three is the elusive magic number of the most Universal Titles one person has held in this company. I sent the messages. Painted the bullseyes. It was my intent that in beating all three, I would get myself on the path to getting Universal Title number three. Of course, we know Shakur fucked that up for me at UltraViolence. I may have beaten both Boda and Strife later on, but somehow a tag match doesn't do either of those singles losses justice. I beat Matt, and that friendship was broken. I cracked Shakur across the face at 227, but that wasn't enough. I bided my time. When Devin thought he had the upper hand on Gamble with that cage match at Colossus, I knew what opportunity I had to take." Lisa nodded. "He's been crumbling ever since." "Shakur's been a cancer to this company for far longer than has been necessary. Gamble finally remembering what made him a serious player all those years ago did nothing but good. I intend to make sure those fissures in Devin's facade keep growing. The man who's never tapped is going to break, completely. By the end of this year, God willing, he'll be locked up in a looney bin somewhere. I don't have to take all the credit, though." "You're hinting at something." The corners of Troy's mouth lift up to her cheeks, and the small smile that helped serve as her reply is shrewd. "When I started calling you Mini-Me, I didn't mean it as an insult. You've got some of your power back, but you can only do so much without being overridden. I'm willing to take on the physical side of this eradication. Dan and Tyler would assist, of course, if needed. You don't need to really ask Tyler twice to drive his fist through Devin's face. The three of us, backing you up? Nigh unstoppable." Lisa took a moment to consider this, bringing her coffee cup to her lips twice and closing her eyes. When she finally placed it back on its saucer, her green eyes locked square on Troy's hazel ones. "We get Shakur gone, and I'll see that you're squarely on the path to a chance at number three." Lindsay holds her coffee mug up for a toast. "That's a foolproof plan if I've ever heard one."
