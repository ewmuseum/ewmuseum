Sound and Fury

27 December 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/27/08 "How bad is it?" Her voice came tiny and strained from his LG Shine (the new upgrade was a Christmas gift from Tsonda) with just the tiniest hint of Canadian accent pushing its way through. A few months back home could do that to a girl. "It's nothing. Just a flesh wound." Relatively speaking. Tyler Rayne had certainly seen worse injuries in his years. The knife slash was long (about six inches) but not terribly deep. "What happened, anyway?" He winced. Dried blood held fast to the bandage he'd hastily applied the night before. Everything had melded together in the night. Removing the bandage was as good as opening the wound again. And almost as painful. "You know how it goes. Guy steps into a bar, starts asking questions about vampires, people come at him with knives." "Oh. Yeah. Right. That happens to me all the time." "I knew you'd understand." He smiled though she couldn't see it. The phone sat on the counter next to a pack of gauze and some new bandages. The wound cried drops of crimson. He dabbed at it with a cotton ball. Peroxide. It stung. "So did you find him?" "No." "I'm sorry." "Me too." The gauze should keep the bandage from sticking this time. A much more proper dressing now that he was thinking clearly. Unfortunately, there was no chance of the wound healing in the next twenty-four hours. He'd be rolling into the finals of the Jewel in the Crown tournament with a slight handicap. "Didn't you call me for a reason, though? You know, non vampire related." "Right. I, uh... I got a package in the mail the other day." "What, that some sort of new euphamism the kids are using these days? Look, I'm happy you got some ass, kid, but it hardly merits a phone call." "Very funny. No, really. I thought it was a Christmas present, but when I opened the box... it was just full of sand." His fist came down hard against the counter. The phone hopped from the counter and tumbled down into the sink. He fumbled to recover it. "Any ideas who it might be?" "You mean do I have any idea who'd have want or reason to fuck with me to this degree? Like, who has motive to want to get at me? Only, I dunno, everyone I've ever met." "Point." "Yeah. Look, kid, I'm sorry. You keep getting dragged into-" "Forget it, Ty. You know I don't-" "But I do." "You've got the Jewel in the Crown finals tomorrow, right?" "Sudden switch of topics..." "Ty..." "Yeah. Flyer in the qualifiers. Then the finals." "You sure you're up for it? I mean, with the knife thing and all..." "Hey. It's me. I'll be fine." "That's what you always say." "And I'm always right." "You'd like to think. Recorded your sound bites yet?" "Shit." "I'll take that as a no." "No." "Then get your ass off the phone and go do it." "Giving me orders now, are you?" "Someone's gotta keep on your ass. Otherwise, you'd never get anything done." "Yeah, yeah. Your parents gonna let you watch the show?" "I think I'll be able to sneak out to a bar. Long as you promise to make it worth my while." "Promise." "Great. Then I'll call you tomorrow." "Later, kid." "Ty?" "Yeah?" "Good luck." "Thanks, B." "Every time." The line went silent. Tyler lost a handful of moments staring into the mirror. The scars upon his body. The memories of war. He sighed, knowing damn well he'd have another on his hands come Sunday. King of Kings. The Jewel in the Crown. It wouldn't be easy. Nothing ever was these days. He walked out to the living room, I Am Legend playing for the thousandth time on HBO. A small recorder rested on the glass coffee table next to his plane ticket for Miami. Tyler took the recorder and walked into the kitchen to prepare some tea. The general rule of thumb in PRIME was that, the day before a Pay-Per-View, the featured talent would gather in a sound booth and rattle off a handful of snippets for the promo videos that always opened the show. This was usually done in the morning, with about five or ten minutes scheduled between each PRIMEate's booth session. This schedule, however, never really worked for Tyler Rayne. The idea of getting up before noon, especially for something as ridiculous as a couple sound bites... well, it just wasn't going to happen. Plus, it wasn't likely that PRIME's Golden Boy could walk in and out of a sound booth in less than ten minutes. Talking ranked right at the top of the list (along with fucking and fighting) as one of his preferred activiities. And like the other two... sometimes it was impossible to get him to stop. So an alternative plan had been inacted for Rayne (and others) who couldn't fit a 10 a.m. appointment into their busy schedule (of hangovers). He simply recorded what he wanted to say and sent it in at his leisure. The tech crew would then listen to his rant and mine the most appropriate nuggets for their promo package. "There's one question people always ask me. Moreso than any other question. Every time someone recognizes me, and they're not trying to kill me... it's always the same question. What's Lindsay Troy like in bed?" He laughs and places the tea kettle up on the stove. A little heat for the burner before he leaves the kitchen. "Every time. Never fails. There's another question, though. One people ask almost as often as if Uni spits or swallows. People always wanna know how I keep doing it. After UltraViolence, after the most intense or extreme match PRIME has seen in years... after a match that effectively ended Wade Elliott's career... I showed up at the next ReVolution. I showed up to wrestle. People asked me why. No one would have faulted me for taking a few weeks off to heal. After the Dual Halo, when I battled through the entire PRIME roster to come out as the lone survivor... and showed up at ReVolution the next week to wrestle. People asked me how I was even standing. Why I wouldn't just take the week off. The ReVolution after Colossus... well, that time I think people were just being nice. We all know Sunshine was a pushover." He pauses in the hallway, not to admire the movie posters, as usual. No, this time he's admiring the shiny gold straps on the other side of the hall. The OCW World Heavyweight Championship. His first major American title. "It's all I've ever known. Fighting. It's all I've ever been good at. I don't know how to stop. I don't want to stop. Seven years now. Seven years I've been working here in the States. Seven years I've been jumping from one promotion to another. One federation closes, another sends an invitation. Seven years I've walked in and out of arenas all across this country. Every promotion I've ever stepped foot in... every federation that ever took to hire me... I've always claimed their top prize. Their biggest title. Their Holy Grail." He moves on down the hall. Remembering. Reliving. "None of those titles were ever handed to me. I didn't marry the boss's daughter or charm the pants off some cock sucking General Manager. No. I walked out to that ring every week and I earned it. I can tell you I'm better. I can talk all day long about my credentials. That only goes so far. The talk only means so much. I'm the only one who can back it up. I'm the only one who can prove it. I'm the only one who has proven it." The last belt on the wall. The PRIME 5-Star Title. "Every week. Every night. All leading up to this. Tell me somethin', boys. Where the fuck were you when I was out there defending this? When I was out there beating the next big thing? Wade Elliott. Dusk. Captain Justice. Crucifix. Cozen. Every single one of them was supposed to have a banner year. Every single one of them was christened as the next impact player in PRIME. Every single one of them fell to me. And while they were falling... where the fuck were you?" "I don't know where you were, Mr. Snow, but as sure as shit know where you weren't. You weren't here. You weren't in PRIME. I couldn't give a shit less where you were or what you were doing. I know you weren't here. I know you weren't competing. I know you weren't proving week in and week out that you were the best PRIME has to offer. You may have been something a long time ago, Snow... but whatever crown you once carried has long been lost." "Flyer... you finally beat me. It took you almost all year, but you finally did it. The other, what?, two or three times we faced off throughout the year... not so impressive. I think I've put you on your ass more than anyone else I've faced in PRIME. One night. One night out of this entire year, you were better than me. Where were you all those other nights? Where were you when I was out defending the most coveted prize in PRIME? Yeah. That's right. You were vying for the right to carry the titles of a division that barely exists. When Brandon Youngblood pussed out and ran like the coward he is, I took the 5-Star Title and elevated to heights no one had thought possible. I made that belt as good, if not better, than the one Tink's carrying around now. What were you doing? You were murking around in a bullshit division, with bullshit titles, competing against bullshit contenders. You didn't do a gods damned thing with those belts. You didn't do a gods damned thing with the tag division. You were content to keep yourself throned as king of an empty kingdom." "Rhett, kid... I'll give you credit. You've probably done more than both of these assholes combined. I've watched you scratch and claw and fight your way up for the better part of this year. I've watched you struggle to get to where you are now. I've watched you earn your spot in this tournament. Kudo to you. Problem is, kid... you just barely made it. That's the only thing you've managed to do this year. You just made it to these semis. Thus far... that's your claim to fame. It won't be enough to claim the throne, kid. Sorry to say... but you just don't have it. Not yet." He steps outside. The sand is warm between his toes. The sun shines brightly down upon him. "People ask why I do what I do. How I do it. I walk out to that ring every week because I have to. Because it's what I know. It's what I do. It's what I am. Every ReVolution. Every Pay-Per-View. Every title defense. All of it was preparation for this evening. For this event. The Jewel in the Crown. I don't care how good you claim to be, Jason Snow. I don't care who you beat, Flyer. And I sure as fuck don't care how much you want it, Mr. Locke. None of you... none of you... have done anything near what I have done. None of you are Tyler Rayne. You haven't spent an entire year proving that you're the best PRIME has to offer. Night in. Night out. You haven't spent an entire year defeating the best competition PRIME has to offer. Night in. Night out. You haven't done shit compared to me." "So bring your legacies. Bring your championships. Bring your desire. It won't amount to fuck. I've been through battles. I've been through wars. I have weathered everything PRIME could throw at me... and I have come out on top. I have always walked away. When Nitz Donnelly and Wade Elliott and Devin Shakur couldn't get back up to keep on fighting... I did. I will always get back up. I will always keep fighting. It's what I do. It's what I've been doing. It's all I've ever done. So here's your Wesley Gibson moment, kids. I am destiny. I am fate. I am the next Jewel in the Crown. I've been the jewel of this company all year long. I've been the shining highlight of PRIME for twelve gods damned months. What the fuck have you done lately?" The final line makes him smile. The tea kettle in the kitchen begins to whistle. His thumb moves the button on the recorded, switching it off. He looks out to the ocean. The beautiful ocean. Stretching onward forever. He turns to enter his house. To make a pot of tea. To send his tirade. To rest before the battles to come. His beachfront home roars like an angry dragon. Awakened and furious, it belches fire from deep within. The heat is intense. Licking at his eyebrows. It singes the edges of his fresh bandage. A force so powerful it lifts him from his feet. Throws him across the beach. He lands hard. Out of breath. Glass rains down around him. New bandages would be needed soon. Plaster and plastic and the flaming pieces of something he'd once called a home drop from the sky. Tiny, impromptu mortars formed from the wreckage of his former home. The roar of the flames is the only thing louder than the ringing in his ears. Blood mixes with sand and flame and glass and twisted steel. He pushes himelf from the beach. To his knees. Shocked. Breathing heavy. Tyler Rayne stares blankly at the fire as it burns what remains of his California home.
