Tick. Tock.

24 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/24/05 One hour and forty-two minutes. One hour and forty-two minutes in the Dual Halo…Hell, some NASCAR drivers couldn’t last that long in their “sport,” let alone in the confines of a mammoth cage where thirty-five professional brawlers duke it out to the death. The task was daunting. And Emilo fell short. But he is ashamed of nothing. Losing a half-pint of blood, breaking three ribs, bruising four others, receiving a mild concussion, and then the final dash of salt to the wound: out-witted by the speedy, much-fresher Karina Wolfenden for the loss –it was all worth it. Every crunched bone, every bead of sweat, every drop of blood…Emilo wouldn’t trade it in for the world. For two weeks later, the skin has scared over and his pride has healed...yet, the feeling of triumph remains. Emilio overcame great obstacles and vicious odds to make it from one bell of the Halo to other. He set a new record in the most vicious of PRIME matches, one that will probably remain unbroken for years. And along the way, the respect and admiration of the fans. For he’ll need their blessings where he picks up the big ball that Boda left, now the champion of the cause of ...independence? Yes, for it appears his days of the Enlightenment are over. Emilio is sick and tired of being the poster boy of club who seems to meet every other week, tardy, and always in disarray. Fear not, for if the Dark Age thinks they’ve won a sweeping victory, they’re wrong. Dead wrong. The likes of Killean Sirrajin, Ozric Mortimer, and Tony Rolo routed them time and time again in the Dual Halo. Just because one steroid-using, cocky flesh-bag jumped ship doesn’t mean it sinks. It is just that Emilio will no longer be the captain. The ocean is a much bigger place and there are many more whales to hunt. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Emilio set in the chair, tirelessly wriggling about. He tried to make himself comfortable by concentrating on the soft tapping as the round-face attendant rapped her fingers anxiously on the edge of the desk. He set patiently, watching the clock. Tick. Tick. Tick. Every now and again he’d peer down and cross-reference his findings with his watch. “9:28.” The silver Rolex was a well-deserved treat be bought himself after a good showing at “The Great American Nightmare.” He used the sleeve of his shirt to wipe away the gathering dust from the treasure, wanting the two hands to be seen clearly as they made their way in a fixed circular motion around the roman numerals. It was always fascinating how pro-wrestlers made up for their broken bones and broken promises by such trinkets. And now Emilio knew and felt why. He let out a shallow sigh, burying his tired head deep in his arms. His throbbing head was in a vice, his brains crushed by the weight of over-tiredness. He looked up at the clock: “9:39.” He’d been waiting patiently for over an hour… The irony danced through the room. The hospital clock was a full minute ahead of Emilio’s. Were the most professional of all professionals, the physician, guilty of wanting to start that coffee break a few seconds early? Emilio couldn’t help but the chuckle. In a field where a matter of seconds meant life or death, where time was the only thing you could rely on, the ridiculousness of having it read wrong was unbearable. He had set his own watch by the satellite. “9:46.” And why was it more dangerous, he pondered, waiting to be diagnosed with an ailment than the affliction itself? Five minutes went by. Fifteen – Twenty. Would the waiting ever end? Would time ever slow down? Go backward? For it would be nice if , on some occasions, timewent backward. But alas, it never did. And never would… Funny story – A man came in with a black eye. The receptionists set him down and fetched a nurse. The nurse came out and fixed him up, scolding him on the dangers of fighting. It was “10:01.” The man seemed to be a regular customer. It seemed, much to Emilio’s amusement, that he was a clown working at a circus. Yet again he had made another child cry, and yet again another father threw a punch. It happened every week. Isn’t it funny how some people, proverbially punched in the same spot every week, never quit? Don’t they get the picture? Don’t they ever quit? Emilio sighed, unfolding last month’s “TIME Magazine.” Iraq this, Microsoft that, Social Security is going down the drain…hurray. Emilio threw down the magazine and crossed his folded hands over his belly. Get a little nap. “10:17.” The next customer was a hockey player. A feisty teenage boy with a sarcastic mother, rolling her eyes at every one of her dimwitted sons’ comments. “No sir,” said the doctor, “a minor concussion isn’t that minor, son. Now I know you want to play in the big game…” “Heh.” The boy shook his head. A quick argument ensued. His mother tried to remain neutral. What a bitch. Doesn’t she love her son? Doesn’t she understand the consequences? “I’m sorry, Jeff, I’m just telling you the truth. You won’t get my signature, or that of any other doctor for that matter. That’s just the way it is.” But the way it was didn’t seem to effect the way the boy thought it should be. He walked out, planning on playing the next game. “10:29.” Emilio yawned, tired as hell. He was finally ready to drift off to sleep when- “Mr. Rage!” Sigh. The doctor came out from the back with a clipboard in hand, a smile on his face. “How are you?” “Fine, thank you. Fine.” “Indeed, you are,” the doctor started, peering down at his clipboard. “It seems the CAT scans, X-Rays all seem to confirm that you’re healing up rather quickly…Surprisingly quickly.” He looked down at Emilio with a smile. An almost suspicious smile. Emilio shrugged. “Well, it’s been ten days.” “Yeah, but what a match!” “You a fan?” The doctor narrowed his eyes. “I make sure I know as much about my patients as possible, that’s all…” “Ah…” “Anyway, my advice to you is take the next week or two lightly, and soon enough you’ll be back and ready to go.” “Full speed ahead?” “No doubt.” A moment later, as Emilio left the hospital, heading toward his car, he stopped to peer down at his watch, “10:57.” There it was. He had been there for one hour and forty two minutes…A new “waiting for your X-Ray results” record? Doubtfully. But at least he had lasted that long. And learned a thing or two. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ So the time has come to move on. The Dark Age will never be forgiven, nor forgotten either. But PRIME is loaded with an abundance of fine talent, and there’s no doubt in Emilio’s mind that he’ll find a new niche to thrive in. Everyone needs a hero. Coming second place in the Dual Halo, setting a new PRIME record, is heroic. Goodbye Enlightenment. Emilio will find a new damsel in distress.
