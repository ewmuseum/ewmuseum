Family Blood

30 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10-30-08 Blood. Coursing through my veins, flowing and bursting through my pores, a volcanic eruption overtaking my skin, blood poured out of me. My cheeks flushed red. My hands palm in a clench fist, fingernails digging into my own skin as drops of blood mark my trail. The soles of my shoes smack the pavement like a masturbating teenager. Flap flap flap. My heart pounds through my rib cage, mimicking an asthmatic reaction in my chest. As I turn around a corner, I stumble and fall, rolling into a table. Priceless Ming vases and sculptured masterpieces crash all over my pre-pubescent forehead. SCRAMBLE! Back to my feet from all fours. Rather not be an animal right now. There’s time for that later. The carpets yank from their roots as I plant and push myself forward, bunching behind me as I sprint. Rudely smashing into a bellhop, I bounce off his ping pong paddle like self and continue on my way. No regard for the help, especially when they’re not extremely helpful. Another corner. Hit it strong. Pivot. Rush. Race. I’d never run this fast before in my life, and to be honest, I never have since. And as I come across my destination, I feel this oddly warm liquid forming at the foot of my toes, consuming my foot as it diffuses over these stiff carpets. It makes a squishing sound as my toes clench up. My voice cracks in my throat as I try to speak. For the first and last time, I remain speechless. My brother wraps his arm around my shoulder, I quickly tense up. Police officers begin to tape off the area with that disgustingly yellow colored tape. It’s hard to tell where the tape begins and these sepia tone walls end. I look down, and see the remains of my mother, slowly flowing underneath the arch of my heel. A tear falls and mixes with her, and for a moment, I smile. Her and I are one again. And yet, the last memory I have of my mother is that puddle of blood, which disturbingly looked nothing like her. April 24th, 1980, 12:42 AM. ** My definition of family has changed quite a few times over my years on this earthly plane of existence. When I was younger, everything I knew on the concept of family revolved around blood. My bloodline. Immediate blood siblings, my grandparents. Mom, dad, mixing their "blood" together to create mine. As I grew, I looked up to my older brother. When I found out he was the same blood type as me, I felt that strengthened our bond. I didn’t understand the, let’s call it, the supernatural nature of kith and kin. As I grew older, I realized that family was self-definable, much like most of the English language, malleable and construable to one’s own desires, limitations, and necessities. Family became those closest to me: my friends, my workers, and my collegues. Over time, the definition of family metamorphosed. Expanded and contracted to its current state. Family. Devotion. Loyalty. Trust. Love. Very hard to find all of this in one place. ** After my parents died we moved in with my grandmother. She was aging by the day and losing bits of her sanity in seesaw equilibrium. It reminded me of the death of my mother, except she lost little pieces of her brain over time, instead of blowing it all out of her cranium at once. Little pieces of sponge…. Blood… Squeezing between my toes... I still won’t do dishes. "Jack?" Her emerald green eyes looks up from her clipboard, as she handedly jots down notes without looking down. Shit. Had I said all that out loud? "Remember, anything you say here will stay between us. Yes?" She clicks her pen frantically for a few moments, before returning to her notes. A cool breeze picks up through the open window to the side of her doctorates that hang on peeling flower patterned wallpaper. I notice her fluttering skirt flirt, revealing a little too much of her creamy thighs, flaring in the wind. I hesitate. Normally, I rant and rave during these weekly psychiatric, quote unquote, sessions. (Of course, I call these "interviews"). I would go off on why the Pillsbury dough boy deserves to be decapitated with a flaming hand saw, or how the penny is the root of all evil because it steals people’s identities from them. Just non-sensical gibberish, spoken simply to bide my time and survive until the next week. But each week I speak my mind to Clarissa, she gets closer and closer to getting inside it. After three years of sessions, Clarissa Cambridge and I are about as close as two people can be, in what would be considered, to her at least, a business relationship. I sigh. Clarissa looks up and catches me looking a bit too low at her billowing skirt. Quickly, I look skyward, blushing. "I would think back to that moment, that..." I was being uncharacteristically open today. "It’d shape my life for the next six years, y’know? And even I don’t know... is it affecting me today? Is it affecting me now?" I shrug, leaning back on Clarissa’s clichéd psychiatric couch. "I mean, I defer to you and you’re eight year debt inducing degree to give me the foresight and emotional distance to make that judgment." I smile, place a toothpick into my mouth and chomp down. "I’ll leave that to the professionals who have deemed me crazy. " I sit up, as Clarissa sharply inhales at this comment. I raise my hands in a shrugging manner. "Well, not out and out crazy, apparently crazy is a medical and psychological term that relates to them giving up." "And I’ll never give up on you Jack." Clarissa smiles as she leans in. I catch a quick peek at her bustline, and quickly return her stare. "I’m always here for you." "Is that what family is?" I quip, disregarding the toothpick as quickly as I began chewing it. "Because I’ve been trying to figure out what I think family is. Y’know? I mean, Webster’s has a pretty clear definition on it but still," I sigh, leaning back on the couch with my hands outstretched. "I’m always curious to hear what a layman has to say about such matters." I smile; it’s one of those snarky smiles. One I hope she’ll grow to love. Clarissa bites down on the tip of her pen. "These sessions aren’t about me Jack." I roll my eyes immediately. "But I’ll indulge." I narrow my eyes, listening intently. Clarissa takes a deep breath in, her chest heaving, as she begins. "To me, yeah. My family has always been there for me. They helped me get through my adolescence, they helped me gain my doctorates and they had faith in me when no one else would. But ultimately, none of that matters." My eyebrows cock. "It’s... not about what people do, actions don’t make people family Jack. It’s all about emotions. It’s about, who you love. And we can’t decide who it is we love Jack. We can’t just go walking down the street and go, oh, you there, in purple. I think I love you now. Let’s go love each other." "I’ve tried it. It works more than you think." I smirk at my own joke. Of course, I haven’t tried it, and Clarissa crosses her arms. She sees right through me. I quickly relent. "Okay. Okay." "Why we love the ones we do, I don’t know. Chemistry? Biology? Genetics? I like to disregard the science of nature and look to the intangibles. The faith of love, if you will, prevails above all else. Love isn’t God... but God is love. Find love, and find your faith." "You and that Christian heebie-jeebukus? Me?" I quickly "kip up," leaping to my feet. "I believe in hard facts. Science. The science of time. Which, in fact, is why I’m heading over to this door over here. Seems like our time is up." Her door screeches that annoying screech that makes me want to kill a homeless man. I turn to leave. "Jack?!" Clarissa shouts out, and I can do nothing but oblige her. "Wh... What’s your definition of family?" She blushes slightly, looking up at me with those bulbous bright emeralds... "Me?" I say, kicking the door to keep it from closing. "... Blood." Her eyes narrow. She instinctually crosses her arms over her chest to brace herself. "Blood... is family." And with that, I quickly exit, stage left. October 30th, 2005 ** My older brother was six years my senior. He did most of my child rearing, when he himself was still a child. I owe the world to my brother, and yet, I can’t help but feel cheated. When my brother turned 18, I don’t know if it was the stress of, well, me, or if it was just his passionate dreams overtaking his priorities… Jake left home and pursued his career in professional wrestling. When I turned 18, I followed suit. Actually, when I turned 16, I began to follow, training illegally in Southern California. One day, I heard a rumor that he went to Mexico, and so I immediately hopped a spicy fruit vendor’s van and crossed the border. I spent four years of my life wrestling in Mexico and Japan, China and Brazil, desperately searching for blood kin, wanting nothing more for us to just be together again. I was in a serious relationship with Kate coming out of high school, and I ended it with her just because there was a chance I could see my brother again. Perhaps I got so invested in wrestling because, well, I’m so invested in him? So I trained. I progressed. I wanted to be ready in case I ever found him. I wanted to make him proud. And if I found him on the opposite side of those time-honored four corners, I’ll want a taste of revenge for all those years of abandonment. I mean, we coulda been having like, so many beers by now! I’m not a perfect individual, I’m pretty sure my shrink can attest to that, but I wouldn’t give up on someone. Hell, Kate calls me bi-daily telling me to stay away. You think I’m gonna listen? Maybe with a court order. ** It was a cool October evening. With the rain delayed World Series, and PRIME’s recent schedule change of Wednesdays to Fridays, I’ve been given a chance to catch the rest of the fifth game of the World Series. And they would not disappoint. Hurling myself into the air in a form of extreme joy I hadn’t felt since my Wedding Day, I rushed over to Tony Davis and hug him. We begin to jump up and down like pre-pubescent school girls, shrieking in joy as Ryan Howard tackles both Brad Lidge and Carlos Ruiz. We look each other in the eyes, an awkward moment develops, and we slowly back off. I rub the back of my head as Tony picks out his Nintendo DS. I smile. Time to break the ice. "Y’know. I was starting to hate baseball." I shrug. Tony laughs. "Starting?" Davis plops himself down on a leather couch. "I think all baseball games should be three innings. I bet more people would watch 'em." Ignoring Tony, I quickly scan the room. Boxes are everywhere, lining up the walls. I guess we can’t hesitate to unpack anymore. There’s no World Series excuse that’s justifiable. This house had a musty odor that will take some getting used to. I really wish I came along with Dusk when he went house hunting. Of course, it’s hard to hunt for a house when you’re being hunted yourself. "Hey guys?!" Calling out from the other room was our good friend and housemate, Dusk. He wore some basketball shorts and a sleeveless shirt. His knee brace was hidden behind the wall he leaned over. "Which one of you retards sent a package here?" His face glowers a burning red. He was not our good friend tonight. I narrow my eyes, staring at Tony who had a tendency to order bobble head dolls through eBay. Tony has already busied himself with his Nintendo DS. If this had been a precious bobble head, he would have noticed in no time. Which, I’ll admit, is strange. No use even speaking to him, he’s too preoccupied. So, I shrug, and make my way over to the door. Dusk takes a towel out of his bag and plops it over his shoulder. I sigh, and take a look through the peep hole. I squint. I rub my eyes. I squint again. And I do the trademark Renner confused "blink." "… Jake?!" My eyes go wide… it’s… my brother? I quickly press my back against the door, low enough so he can’t see me through the peep hole. I don’t even realize that you can’t see in reverse, like one of those one way mirrors. Still, let’s be safe. Dusk continues to stand there, arms folded across his chest. "Who the fuck is that?" He says, pointing to the door in an authoritative fashion. "I told you, fucking lay low." "Hey!" Mary-Lynn hops down the stairs, two at a time. Perky, a skip in her step… fucking makeup?! "Is he here?" She says, finishing the touches on the curls of her hair in a mirror hung on the staircase. She tosses the curling iron onto a dresser and walks to the door. She passes by the coldest stare that Dusk has ever given her. He grabs her by her elbow, and she looks up at him startled. Even I am a bit at the roughness of it. "We’re gonna have a talk, yeah?" Dusk, annoyed, opens the door. Jake, on the other side, begins to hold something up, but Dusk shoves him aside and begins to jog. Jake recovers himself, and adjusts his makeshift clip on tie. He turns to the door, and I just can’t do anything but… Punch him in his fucking teeth. " The hell?!" Jake shouts, recovering after I sent him reeling. "You’re dead motherfuh-" I step up into his face. "Why exactly are you my mail man?" Jake’s jaw drops, and his breath shortens. "… Jack…" He takes a deep inhale and looks to the side. He was truthfully dressed as a UPS truck operator, but the specifics were of no concern to me. "Uh… No…" He begins to sweat. "I’m not your mailman. I mean, I am a mailman, I’m just not your mail man." I look down. He’s holding… "Why are you holding flowers?" "I’m pretty sure I should leave." Jake says, turning on a dime. "Jake?" My ears burn. Jake looks over his shoulders at the emerging Mary-Lynn. Now I turn on a dime, mimicking my brother moments after he does something. Great, I’m 8 years old again. "I’m ready." Jake smiles, as Mary-Lynn passes me without another look. My eyes do that sort of half moon shaped crying thing, and I begin to scratch my forehead. I can hear them fade out in the distance. Jake mentions something about just getting off work, as he opens the car door for Mary-Lynn. As she gets in, I stare at her all the way until the car slowly revs to ignition, and screeches out from the curb, into the evening horizon. I stand there, slack jawed. My brother. Alive. Here. And he’s… dating my pupil?!? "You gotta be shittin’ me." October 29th, 2008
