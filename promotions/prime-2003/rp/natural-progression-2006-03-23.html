Natural Progression

23 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/23/06 Sometimes in life, people are subject to something that is called natural progression. It can be confused with many things that control our beings. Such as a person who thinks they have stumbled upon a great idea, only to discover that it was born of necessity. That is not natural progression, but rather a single moment of brilliance that enhanced their lives monumentally. Natural progression is the type of activity that happens when a person gradually learns over time that they are changing. Not always for the best, mind you, but that they are indeed changing somehow to fit. Why they need to fit? Well that varies in any number of explanations. Perhaps they needed to find that they are capable of living independently of their parents, they find that they need to leave their job, or, as is the case here, they find that something inside of the very core of the being is changing. Imagine if you will that a person had known only one lifestyle in their entire existence. Imagine that it was suddenly ripped from them, and that the world suddenly began to spin into a shape and place they could never have imagined before. What happens next, whether that person lives, they die, they go broke, they get rich; whatever they do for the next period of their lives is natural progression as long as they are learning to forward themselves. What, it may be asked, is the point of knowing any of this? The more we watch the world around us spin, and see ourselves as small speckles of individual gains and losses, the more we learn about this natural progression and how it shapes everything. The prison rookie who serves his 15 years and leaves a hardened old man, that is a great example. The brand new draftee to the NFL who will lead his team to a Superbowl in ten years? This is another great example. So it is that Seek and Destroy find themselves in the middle of a whirlwind life. The very core of their hearts existed to rock. Anyone who has met them, seen them, or heard them play would know that. Hence, as one who might be paying attention to this little addendum to their story might be deducing at this very moment, the duo was in the middle of a most confusing natural progression. The offices of Tom Connol, Agent: “Okay guys, we’ve been through this before, and if you want to keep on playing your music, you have to go through this stint. You know full well that you can’t legally hold any sanctioned shows in the United States anymore.” The voice was that of Seek and Destroys agent, Tom Connol. He was a middle aged man who had made his way in life from nothing. He knew a lot about law, money, and most importantly, getting his clients great deals. Jason and Eric sat across his nicely polished oaken desk, which was adorned with family pictures, a phone, and several pads of paper. Technically speaking, they were there to try and find a clause to escape from the Metallica clause in their contract. Seek and Destroy were a great band, but not wrestlers and they had to come to that realization sooner or later. Perhaps The Dual Halo was the moment that they made that connection. Eric leaned forward and pushed a pen around with his index finger. The red and black shocks of hair flicked off his shoulder and down around the right side of his face. He shook his head in displeasure. “So basically, we are screwed?” Tom had previously leaned back in his chair. The older man, who had represented his clients well for nearly three years after former manager Jerry Burnside had retired to pursue a career in sitting on a beach nodded solemnly. Eric groaned and hung his head a bit, wondering why he had ever tried to upstage the heavy metal icons. It was a great moment though, and anyone who was there knew it. Maybe it was the defining moment in the careers of Jason, Eric, and the road band, who knows. As the discussion previous to this portion of the story would indicate, maybe natural progression would dictate an even greater level of success. For now however, the two rock stars were quite bummed with the prospect of wrestling again. “Guys, can’t you just do these shows? You only have a two year contract, and then if all goe…” “Two years Tom? I was already put in a hospital once, and Jason has broken two basses. Shit, you can’t get the big guy to shut up for at least three days after that. He just sits there and rocks back and forth talking in Spanish about taquitos or something.” Jason rolls his eyes at the obvious comment to hide the fact that Evans was scared. He had good reason to be scared too. These two weren’t prepared for the rigors of being thrown about by a person of the magnitude of Baltasar. Not to mention hit by croquet mallets, or falling from the top of constructions that were supposedly new and improved. Tom flicked a couple pages of the contract, which he had laying in front of him, and he looked down briefly. After adjusting his bifocals a bit, he looked back up and shrugged. The tatters of Jason’s favorite Cliff Burton shirt, which he refused to take off, hung on the big man. “Eric…Jam, there’s simply nothing you can do. Mr. Pham was more than generous with the contract we were offered, to be blunt. I expected a lot less physical risk though, to be honest.” Eric lets out a sarcastic half laugh. “Jeez, you think Tom? Who knew wrestling was real?” There is a brief silence, followed by Jason holding a finger up. Eric looks over his shoulder and then back to Tom. The agent waits for Jason to speak up, and finally the mild mannered giant does so. “What if we just learned to wrestle?” Eric laughs a little, and Tom turns a concerned look toward his client. Sure, Jason had a novel idea, and he was generally the less impetuous of the two, but could he really be serious? There was a well documented history of music industry stars failing miserably in the ring. Do we even have to mention The Insane Clown Posse? “Jam, that’s just stupid. These dudes, they’ve been doing it all their lives. That would be as stupid as you teaching someone like Mario Lemieux how to play bass.” Jam scratches his huge forehead briefly. “Dude, don’t you mean Ignatius Lisieux?” Eric shrugs. “I don’t know man, the point is that the dude doesn’t know jack shit about the bass, and you probably aren’t going to be able to teach him overnight. We simply aren’t going to learn about toe holds and sleepers and what the hell a Tiger Driver or a Hurrikinrana is.” Tom reaches up and rubs one side of his face in frustration. The whole point of the guys joining a wrestling gig was so they could play shows legally and not be sued. They were being put into harms way though, which was never part of the original agreement. The agent looked over a couple more pages while Eric and Jason argued about the exact nature of a cross face chicken wing maneuver. Tom finally found what he was looking for, and just in time too, as Eric was trying to apply a Guatemalan death knock to Jam’s skull. “Guys.” Jam screams something about porcupines and grabs a pen from the desk, he digs it into Eric’s arm and the smaller man yelps a bit and lets go. “GUYS.” Eric tries to head butt the bigger man, and he staggers back while Jam smiles. Suddenly a foot shoots up and kicks Jason square in the crown jewels. Jason cups his sack and falls back into the chair, groaning in a deep voice that belied his abnormally high pitched singing voice. “GUYS!” Finally, Tom caught their attention. The agent pointed a finger like a scolding parent at Jason, who was eyeing up the snow globe on his desk, perhaps as a future weapon. Eric sees Jason looking at the globe, and he begins to search the room for a countermeasure already. Tom looks back down and begins to read off a portion of the contract to them. “According to this document, if either of you is put into a potentially life threat…” Jason mutters across the two foot span to Eric while the agent talks about the contract. “You better watch it amigo. I got one fist of iron and the other of steel.” Eric scoffs. “Yeah? Well I killed a man in Reno, just to watch him die.” Tom continues his reading of article seven, clause fourteen, subtitle seventy two and all that legal mumbo jumbo as the two shot verbal jabs back and forth. Eric and Jam were close, almost like brothers, perhaps too much like them at times, as Tom was experiencing. “Oh yeah…well….well…I’m wanted dead or alive.” Eric shakes his head. What a goober. “Bon Jovi? I come at you with the man in black, and you bring Bon Fucking Jovi? Please. You want it Jam? Well you got it. I come from the land with the ice and snow, from the midnight sun where the hot springs blow.” Jam was hurt, he had invoked Zeppelin, and that was just a low blow. Tom rambled something that caught both of their interests though, and they both perked forward immediately. “And furthermore, if such acts should be committed, then this agreement between parties shall be considered null and void, releasing Seek and Destroy from obligations to continue fulfillment.” Eric reaches a hand out and grabs Tom’s sleeve on his sport coat. The agent looks at the excited rockers hand, then back at his face. Evans’ face mirrored that of an underage groupie glimpsing her first look at The Cock of Rock in concert. “So wait, you are saying we can get out of this?” Tom nodded. “Call the press man, we can go home and sleep easy tonight instead of having to go into a match with another bunch of mongoloids in grape strangling tights.” Tom reaches for the phone, but stops right before he gets there. He can see the look in Jason’s eyes as he grabs the receiver, and he cocks his head intuitively. The agent is an important man in the relationship of any public and its figure, so he knew when his clients were uneasy about something. “Jason, you want to say something?” Jam cleared his throat and shrugged. “I dunno man, I was just thinking. Maybe we can learn to wrestle. I guess…” He pauses, drawing a look from Eric now as well. “I guess I kinda liked being a wrestler.” Evans leaned back in his chair and looked to his left, on the ground. He wasn’t looking at anything in particular, just lost in deep thought. Here they were with a way out of the show. They were pretty good fighters, both of them, but they had already been beaten a few times already, and the sting of defeat didn’t compare to the throb of de-boot in de-face. Jam nudged Eric with his hand. “Dude, think about it. We can still do this bro; we got to look at the facts. Maybe we can learn to be wrestlers, and still play a rocking show. Then it’s like we win both ways.” “Electric” Eric Evans looked up to Tom, and nodded his head. The agent pulled his hand back from the phone and leaned back in his chair. The first baby steps of the journey from being a rock band to becoming true wrestlers were imprinted into the ground at this moment. Sometimes in life, people are subject to a thing called natural progression. The unpredictable nature and subtle motion that occurs when it happens may make it almost impossible to pinpoint when it starts. One thing is certain though, the strides we all take upon our short paths in life, the interests we develop, and the relationships we grow into, and the very way we live our entire lives has always been, and always will be dictated by our natural progression.
