The Last Straw

12 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/12/08 “Every man wants to wear the crown. Your desire to do so is no different. It isn’t special. It isn’t greedy. It isn’t egotistical or absurd. To want the pinnacle of success is human nature. To need that success is something else entirely.” - Dr. Eric Trowbridge ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Fellas “Ten bucks says you bust and go back to 48.” “No shit, Jason.” Dave Vashaun eyed up the board and fired away. “I haven’t won a game of 301 in years.” “That’s because you suck at darts!” Popping a french fry, smothered in cheese and sour cream, into his mouth, Hank Lewis chimed in from his seat at the bar. “Hank, do me a favor and choke on those fries.” “Hehehe.” Jason grinned and watched on as his old high school buddy fired off his three darts. “Shit!” “Bust!” Jason pumped a fist in the air as Dave headed for the board, retrieving his darts in frustration. “So what’s up with your brother? Why isn’t he joinin’ us tonight?” “He’s got a date.” Dave plucked the last dart from the board and stepped to the side. “I hooked him up with Elle.” “That chick you share a cubicle with?” “Yeah.” Dave picked up his beer and took a drink. “That’s the one.” “No shit? When’s the last time Kaiser went on a blind date? Hell, for that matter… has he been on any sort of date since he…” “No. This is the first.” “Well,” Hank once again interjected himself into the conversation. Looking down the bottom of his beer, he chuckled. “ought to be interesting.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Elle The cubes clanged, crashing into one another, before careening off into the sides of the glass. As Kaiser Vashaun stirred his drink with a straw, he glanced around the restaurant, studying the occupants of each table. It was crowded, but not loud. His ears nearly buzzed in the relative silence of the whispered chatter that filled the room. Kaiser raised the straw from his soda. With each passing moment, he became more convinced that this whole evening was a mistake. And as he became more convinced he’d made a terrible error in judgment, his anger grew. Straw in hand, Kaiser poked around a bit before, out of pure frustration, plunging it into a solitary cube. He watched through the condensation on the side of his drink as the straw speared the block of ice, driving it downward, pinning it to the bottom of the glass. And he held it there. Again, he turned his attention to his surroundings. There wasn’t a single syllable he could differentiate from any other around him, and he liked that, comforted, just slightly, by the fact that his words would be afforded the same privacy. Another indistinguishable conversation. “You better let that thing up for air.” “Huh?” Kaiser’s head snapped towards the soft sound that filled his ears. Standing by the table side was a brunette who’s appearance made him uneasy. She was not drop-dead-gorgeous, nor was she unpleasant to look towards. There was not a single feature of hers that grabbed his attention, and yet she was not plain. Her attractive and enigmatic nature confused him as much as her words. “That ice cube… it looks like you’re trying to drown it.” “Oh.” He turned away from the woman, something about the way she had smiled causing him to do the same. Glancing at his soda, he released his grip on the straw and the ice he had entrapped quickly sprung free, floating to the top. “Much better.” She stepped around the table and took a seat opposite Kaiser, extending her hand for greeting. “I’m Ellie.” “Ellie?” Kaiser shook her hand. “Dave always refers to you as ‘Elle’.” “Most of my co-workers call me that… as does pretty much everyone else I know, for that matter. I’d say I prefer ‘Ellie’, but I’ve gotten so used to everyone calling me ‘Elle’. I guess two syllables is a mouthful.” They each shared an abrupt laugh. “So… do you go on a lot of blind dates set-up by your siblings?” “Not very often.” Kaiser paused as he unrolled his silverware, letting it spill from the napkin. “Well… never. I mean… ya know… until now.” “Heh. You sound nervous.” “L’il bit.” “Aren’t you a professional wrestler? I mean, you’re on TV every week. Just seems kinda funny that you’d be nervous.” Kaiser shifted in his seat, and slowly began picking at his napkin, tearing it into tiny pieces. “Yeah, I dunno. It’s strange I guess.” “No, it’s kinda cute. Just surprised me. I figured you’d be the same boisterous personality I’ve heard about people tuning in to watch on television.” “I’m a pretty different guy away from the cameras.” “So when you’re home… you don’t like to roll around in your underwear with sweaty guys?” “I…” Kaiser paused for a moment, unable to form a worthwhile response. When he saw Elle’s eyebrow rise in comical form, he found the right reply. “…well, occasionally on the weekends.” “Haha. Dave said you had a deadpan delivery.” “So… how much exactly has my brother told you about me?” Elle took a quick breath. “He’s told me the last eighteen months haven’t been kind to you, but that you’re seeing a therapist and making strides to move on.” “And you still agreed to a date with me?” “Yeah, because he also said that you are a truly caring individual and a good guy. And I trust Dave’s word. I’ve worked with him for the last two years and know he’s honest when he speaks.” Elle smiled as she took a drink from her water. “Besides, if dinner doesn’t go well… no big deal. I get a free meal out of it, and thanks to the magic of TiVo, I don’t even have to miss Bones.” Kaiser laughed and, for the first time since unfolding it, placed his napkin down. With his right hand, he brushed the pile of torn pieces in front of him to the side. “You’re every bit as funny as Dave said. He swears it makes the day go a hundred times faster at the office.” “It does.” She grinned. “Speaking of a day at the office. Tell me about your job. It has to be infinitely more exciting than the nine-to-five I’m stuck in.” “Naw. It’s not really all that special. Besides, my shrink says I shouldn’t talk too much wrestling.” “Really?” “Yeah. He says to take his word for it and not mix work and relationships.” “Well, I’m sure he knows what’s best.” “Yup. At least… he better. A hundred bucks an hour seems a little steep for bad advice.” “Hahaha.” “I’m hungry. You ready to order, Ellie?” Kaiser had just picked up the menu when he caught himself, too late to punch the mute button. Peering over the laminated edge, he apologized. “Sorry. Elle.” “No. It’s ok.” She grinned, as she picked up her menu and began to survey the entrées. “I kinda like it.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The Fellas The night was dying early… typical for a Wednesday. Most of the tables at the pub were empty. A song belted out from the old jukebox, picked out by someone who wasn’t even around to finally hear it playing. Other than a few of the regulars, the only thing that hung around was the stale smell of spilled beer, and the haze from the iron lungs who’d indulged as much of their habit as possible, before the inevitable smoking ban found its way to Mississippi. “You’re worse at Nine Ball than you are darts. Christ, I didn’t think that was possible.” Jason shook his head as he placed the cues back on the rack. “I played a real-man’s sport. Football, baby. I’m sorry I can’t compete at your fun little hobbies.” “Please, you haven’t played football since high school. And even then, you were a mediocre outside linebacker.” “Wrong. Can I get a Coke?”” Dave took a seat at the bar and waved down the bartender, then turned his attention back to Jason. “I was a mediocre inside linebacker.” “Same shit.” “Whatever.” “Kaiser gonna be here next week? That guy can shoot pool. He knows what he’s doin’.” “Yeah. Unless things with Elle go really well. Then… who knows.” “How are things goin’ for your brother, anyways?” “Good. He’s…” “What’d I miss while I was in the shitter?” Hank came trudging across the pub, still in the process of zipping up his fly. “Dave was just talkin’ about Kaiser.” Jason nodded towards Vashaun. “Yeah. Things are goin’ real well for Kaiser professionally.” With his Coke delivered, Dave took a sip and let out an exaggerated sound of satisfaction. “He’s a champ and all. Gotta big tournament coming up. I think seein’ the psychologist has really helped. Dr. Trowbridge seems to be gettin’ Kaiser’s head right.” “Good to hear.” “Yup. I’m hoping my little bro likes Elle. God willing, the date tonight wasn’t entirely awkward, and they’ll be a second. I think he could use a pick-me-up on the romantic front.” “Wait…” Hank spun on his barstool “did you say Dr. Trowbridge?” “Geez. I think you’ve had enough booze, Hank. Talk about a delayed reaction.” “I ain’t drunk. I just wasn’t sure that’s what I heard. You say Kaiser’s seeing Trowbridge?” “Yeah. Why?” “I dunno, Dave.” Hank scratched the top of his head. “But, I don’t think that’s right.” “I’m positive that’s who Kaiser told me he was seeing.” “My l’il sis is a grad student in the Psyc department at UM. She worked with a Dr. Eric Trowbridge last year. But I remember her telling me that he retired at the end of 07.” “What?” Dave leaned forward, resting on the bar. His face contorted into a mix of confusion and exhaustion. It was late, he was bit drunk, and it was taking him a moment to absorb what he was hearing. “You sure, Hank?” “Positive, man. I remember Kylie telling me. She really liked the guy and was disappointed when he retired. I guess he was having some health problems or somethin’.” “What the hell?” Kaiser’s older brother started fumbling with the straw in his coke. “I dunno what he is or isn’t doin, Dave. But, I can sure as shit tell you Kaiser ain’t seein’ Dr. Trowbridge.” Dave swirled the straw around in his fingers. “Sonofabitch.” With a sudden, forceful jolt, he stabbed the straw into a block of ice, driving it downward, pinning it to the bottom of the glass. And he held it there.
