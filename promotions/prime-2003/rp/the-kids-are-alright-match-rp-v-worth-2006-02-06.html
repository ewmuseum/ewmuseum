The Kids Are Alright (Match RP - v Worth)

6 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 06/02/06 The Kids Are Alright.Written: 6th February, 2006. 'I wanna be better than oxygen,So you can breathe when you're drowning and weak in the knees. I wanna speak louder than Ritalin, For all the children who think that they've got a disease.' - Willy Mason. I School. The best days of your life. Heh. 'Hey, you seen?' It served to shame, to embarrass, to warn... but its true intention was to destroy someone's life. An eye for an eye. To disgust family, invoke abandonment from friends, and at its most potent, to force them far, far away. To make them not only feel regret, but a burning frustration with it that would cauterise whatever infected wound of the soul their vile actions has crept out from. Scrawled in lipstick, whatever pen was at hand, or in one case the blood seeping from a shaking finger, was a list of 8 names. 3 triumphantly, yet ominously, crossed out. The names by themselves packed little punch, ushered in no thoughts of sleaze, loathing, and vulgarity. Karl Newman could have been anyone. He could have kissed his bed-ridden Mother goodbye every morning, grabbed his brown paper-bag lunch, and ran the gauntlet of bullies to school every morning. Sitting there, attentively, he could have been handed back his 5th straight A on the latest history assignment, while his finely tuned mind focussed only on his upcoming toughest challenge ever, at Chess Club. Karl Newman could have been captain of any sports team, any society. Karl Newman could have been someone people pretended to know, just so they could leach some of his reputation by association. Karl Newman could have volunteered an hour every day after school to the local animal shelter, and broke down in tears when they finally had to put Rover down. Karl Newman could have been, and indeed was, the 8th name. Still fresh, dropping with accusation. Karl Newman could have been the next President of the United States. Karl Newman could have been the last ever mentally-ill inmate to receive the death penalty. Karl Newman could have cured cancer. Karl Newman could have died when the HIV he contracted from an out-of-town businessman turned to full-blown AIDS. Karl Newman could have seen the stars. Karl Newman could have watched a star implode though an overdose. But as the 8th name on the list, Karl Newman was nothing more, and nothing less, than the 7 above him. The exclusive, infamous group whose names lived under the one banner. The banner scratched into the wood of the cubicle, inked, and burned in as a permanent reminder. The banner that acted as judge and jury. Rapist. Karl Newman was scum. 'Oh my God!' some exclaimed upon seeing his name added to the wall of shame. 'I can't believe I flirted with him in geography last week. Eww. EWW.' 'I heard she SO made it up,' others scoffed, adding their own little comments in lipstick. 'Like he'd ever look at her.' 'Wow!' others gasped, holding their heart. 'I dated him for, like, 3 weeks last summer. I feel like one of those women who, like, married a serial killer and go on Oprah.' Others? Well... 'Ugh...' Having finally hit the sack at a little after 4:30 that morning, by midday, Karina Wolfenden was about ready to find somewhere comfy to curl up. Cupped hands of near-scalding water were splashed up onto dry skin and aching eyes, trying to refresh them enough to last just a few more hours. She'd probably feel even worse if she hadn't chosen to start the day at just before 10. 'I envy home-schoolers,' she yawned, almost popping her jaw out. After rubbing baggy, bloodshot eyes, Karina retrieved a couple of scrappy-looking pieces of A4, covered each side by line after line of gibberish. Studying the bizarre language she'd created about 15 minutes earlier, Karina gave it her nod of approval, before casually tossing the pages into the sink. 'Ooops,' escaped with exaggerated innocence. After rescuing the sodden pages, ink having run together, she shook them under the hand dryer, crisping the paper up. After a minute or so, Karina took a step back, admiring her handiwork. With the ink having spread through the damp paper, there was no way of knowing if what was there before consisted of Shakespeare or Japanese. 'And that's what happens when you accidentally stand your bag in a deep puddle,' was delivered with a sly grin as she tucked the loose pages back into her rucksack. 'I can't believe they keep buying this shit.' Rolling a tie off her wrist, Wolfenden pulled her blonde and green hair back into a loose ponytail, before squirming her shoulders through the rucksack straps. 'I heard that he was arrested for that at his last school,' another chipped in with, everyone keen to offer up their words of wisdom. 'And my friend's brother’s girlfriend said he's moved school, like 500 times in the last few years. Now we know why.' With a roll of the eyes that would later go on to typify her wrestling persona, the K-Wolf slipped back out into the corridor. Immediately, the noise left her grinding teeth, a heavy head being a remnant of the night before. Home-schooling was seriously starting to become her Mecca. '-d she was all, like, stop... but you KNOW that shit means yeah. Makes'em think they're still a good girl.' Ah, Karl. As eye after eye shot him an evil look, Karl Newman was oblivious to it all. Surrounded by automated friends, there to nod and offer up the occasional 'YEAH!' and High-5, he cared only for talking himself up. 'Don't think I'll call her though,' he chuckled, deleting her number from his phone for all to see. 'Way too needy.' Despite her throbbing head, the words were amplified in the soon-to-be K-Wolf's ear as she walked past, looking out the corners of narrowed eyes at them. Just the sight of him made Wolfenden want to vomit... although that feeling was there long before his name was on the wall. He even looked slippery to the touch, unable to smile without a smirk. 'Maybe I need to find myself something to play with now she's gone,' he sleazily announced to his followers, eyes seedily watching Karina as she passed. 'Maybe bat a few leagues below me, for once. Get me a charity case who'll be grateful to for what I give her.' Catching Karina's glare for just a split second, he almost taunted her by blowing a kiss her way, causing another bout of laughter from the Hyenas that surrounded him. 'Just remember...' Kari told herself under her breath, as many had already told her. 'Just count to ten.' 'Yeah,' it continued, his lecherous gaze almost making Wolfenden's skin creep. 'I bet a freak like her’s into some wild shit.' Coming to a halt, Karina exhaled through her nostrils, the last of her resolve being expelled. 'Oh, who am I kidding.' Turning back to look at the crowd, Wolfenden saw their attention had already drifted onto just about every other female who dared walk the corridor around them. Whistles and heckles echoed down the hallway, while passing faces soured with every crude suggestion. But while others hurried, rapidly clicking heels down the tiles, Karina's sneakers paced slowly towards the source of the antagonism. 'Oh, I would hit that...' Karl grinned, licking his lips at another passing object. 'Funny,' he then heard whispered in his ear, 'I was thinking the same thing.' II 'Look,' Lawrence Wolfenden tried to appeal, holding arms out at his sides in an open gesture. 'Kids will be kids, and sometimes they have to let off steam.' With every convincing, charismatic word, his pale blue eyes told the on looking Karina that she shouldn't rely on those words when it came to appealing her punishment. Having been dragged away from the office, Lawrence was clad in what summed-up the true meaning of a power suit, the cut flawless, amplifying an already dominant posture. 'Um, with all due respect, Mr Wolfenden, I have to disagree,' the councillor responded. 'It seemed like Karina was the only one who got to let off steam. And, if we're being honest, I don't think we can accept breaking a fellow student's nose, or kicking him in the... in the delicate regions... as being a valid form of stress relief.' Having not been filled in on the exact details, Laurence’s probing glare narrowed on Kari, who gave off an apathetic shrug of the shoulders, propelling her deeper into the seat. A reddening face just managed to hold back the thunder erupting under the surface as Lawrence looked back to the councillor, then the principal who flanked his left shoulder. Leaning forward assertively in his chair, Lawrence blanked the councillor, appealing straight to the top. 'Well, I'm sure that she was provoked,' Lawrence tried to rubber stamp right onto the principal's psyche. 'So if you just talk to a few people who saw what happened, then we can just wipe this off the slate and get on with our lives.' Lawrence Wolfenden wasn't used to facing disagreement. Usually his word was gospel, and met with a plethora of grovelling nods and sycophantic praise. Needless to say, the lack of this reaction didn't do much to lighten his stormy mood. 'I'm afraid it's not that simple, Mr Wolfenden,' was the disagreement put up in his path. 'There has been talk of pressing charges against Karina, and her complete lack of remorse, and refusal to apologise, leaves our hands tied.' Drawing in a stressed breath through his nostrils, the Wolfenden patriarch looked ready to explode as he turned back to his daughter. Not even needing to say a word, his stare raised her diaphragm, pushing air out over vocal chords to force the explanation out. 'He's rapist scum who needed a punch in the fa-' she went to explain her actions - however unapologetic - with. 'Karina,' the councillor instantly cut her off with. 'We've told you once about making an unfounded, and very serious allegation against Mr Newman. The school will not open itself to the repercussions of having such vicious rumours in circulation.' With suspicion warping his features, Lawrence looked back to the councillor as his daughter rolled her eyes at once again having her attempts to justify her actions derailed by a technicality. 'Fine,' she huffed, 'he was scum who needed a punch in the face, since nobody else was going to do anything.' 'Do you see what we're having to deal with?' the principal then asked Lawrence, giving the soon-to-be K-Wolf a definite sense of 'I'm still here, y'know?'. 'We have yet to receive a single complaint of any sort against Mr Newman, but your daughter seems to think he's done things deserving of her own brand of vigilante justice.' Refusing to just sit politely back and let the conversations be fired back and forth across her, Karina butted back in. 'Duh,' she dismissively grunted. 'All the guys want to be him, and, well, not like he's done anything to make the girls scared of him, is it?' Despite often being infuriated by her disobedience, there was a spark in Karina that Lawrence couldn't help but admire. Letting her outburst go, he looked to her accusers, prompting them into defence. 'This is hardly the first time though, is it, Karina?' the principal, tired of banging his head against a brick wall, declared. 'If you were a straight A student with nothing on your record, then it might be a little different. But so far this year you're scraping a 65% in Physics... and that's just the percentage of papers you've submitted, not the grades. Your Maths and History grades have fallen since Christmas... and every week I have teachers commenting that your head is in the clouds rather than in the books.' It didn't exactly make the reading Lawrence expected of a Wolfenden, and the spark he'd admired was quickly given an extinguishing look of disappointment. 'You have approximately 6 1/2 days of unexcused absence this year,' the list continued with. 'You were removed from the Lacrosse team due to violent conduct, and just before Christmas, you somehow managed to spark a near riot in the cafeteria.' 'They trampled a downed player, and I didn't start the cafet-' was hurriedly reeled off, before being slashed off as the words left her lips. 'Enough,' Lawrence decreed, having heard enough. More than enough. Enough for a few generations of Wolfendens, rather than just his only daughter. 'Go wait outside, Karina.' 'Oh, c’mo-' Karina went to appeal, before remembering exactly whose will she was up against. Dragging herself out from the chair in frustrated, laboured fashion, she swiped her belongings and stormed out of the room, never one to be fond of dealing with consequences. As the world would go on to find out, she was all about the actions. Dropping back into his seat, Lawrence despaired behind the granite business facade he kept raised at all times. Both his children had the same upbringing, none was favoured, they'd go to the same holiday locations every year, involve each pair of grandparents equally. There were no upheavals, no family dramas... yet, still, they'd turned out so different. There had to be a reason, even if his denial smothered it. News like this travelled. Fast. Nobody would care what award Max had received, only what trouble Karina was in. 'There have been some huge breakthroughs in behavioural science in the last few years,' the councillor revealed, surprised that the words weren't met with the expected look of disdain by Lawrence. 'I'd be happy to refer you to a good specialist who might be able to make a diagnosis.' Every part of Lawrence screamed at him to say no thanks. 'Okay,' he sighed, as if admitting embarrassing defeat. 'Let's try that.' III After an evening of arguing her heart out, Karina's throat was too sore to say another word. Having fought her corner until shouts could only wheeze out the words, she was left spent, exhausted both physically and mentally. By candle light, she feverishly scribbled what her body was no longer capable of expelling audibly, left hand rubbing dry, tired eyes as the right poured out her thoughts. Jabbing the pen's nib onto the page, the soon-to-be K-Wolf signed off, before licking the tip of her index finger, then using it to extinguish the flame. Expelling a yawn that was forced out by her entire body, Kari's head went light for a few moments after, before she dragged herself back up, then collapsed down onto the bed. Shoving her bag to the floor, spilling out books and folders, the young Wolfenden retreated under the covers, leaving the moonlight that crept in through the window to highlight her outpouring. Parents + Information = massive levels of suck. They really just need to stick with what they actually know... like Golf, caring about what other people think, and polishing off a bottle of wine every night while lecturing how you need to be responsible with alcohol. Supposedly, they've now discovered why I haven't scored an A all year in any subject I don't actually care about. If there weren't about 50,000 more interesting things to be doing, then maybe I'd pay attention in class. Forcing a pill down my throat every morning isn't exactly going to make me chant 'RA-RA-RA! for Calculus!'. Seriously, parents should be banned from internet search engines. And computers should just explode if they ever log on to any medical sites. Funny how they'll drop in 100 thinly veiled checks to make sure a kid isn't doing drugs, then try to answer any problems that crop up with a few pills. Anyone who doesn't see that's retarded needs some serious medication. Until last week, I was just someone with a short attention span who wasn't fulfilling her potential. Now I'm supposed to be some genius who's only hampered by some medical condition that only people I've never met before can notice? Yeah, that's sane. Hey, I'm fine, I'm doing okay, I'm happy, I think this world is a pretty cool place which I'm looking forward to living in for the next however many years... please, pump me full of drugs so I can forget about that and spend more time doing Modern History assignments. And supposedly my vast frustrations at all this under-achieving has given me 'anger issues'. Yeah, it can't possibly be because some people, just... really need to be punched in the face? People wouldn't have anger issues if there was actually some kind of justice lurking about anywhere. So many people get to walk about and generaly make life suck for everyone else. But, hey, it would actually require effort, or someone who cared, to stop all that crap. Would just be a whole lot easier to wait until someone does the world a favour, and then suspend them for 2 weeks. They fail to see that - odds are - if someone gets punched in the face, it wasn't for smiling too much, or being way too much of a nice guy. So, since I'm pretty much 50/50 on whether you've found this and started reading yet, I'll throw a little message here for you... saves me going through the brain-grating task of saying it in person. I'm alright. I'm not a straight A student, I don't enjoy school, and every now and then I lose my temper. I'm not on drugs, I'm not pregnant, I'm not in a gang, I'm not suicidal, and I'm not one of the people who actually NEEDS help and medication, but just gets ignored. Stop reacting like having personality, character, and opinions is a dangerous thing that needs to be treated as a chemical imbalance. Because when you try and jump on the OCD, ADHD, WHATever bandwagon, you're leading us towards the Village of the Damned in every school. And that movie SUCKED. Don't stifle creativity or artistry, in whatever form it develops. And maybe, just maybe, if you tried to deal with those who're just bad people, rather than a little lazy or eccentric, then the world would be a WAY better place. I need sleep. And no, I don't need a pill for it. - K.
