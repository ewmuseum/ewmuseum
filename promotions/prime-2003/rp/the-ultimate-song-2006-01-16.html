The Ultimate Song

16 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/16/06 The scene opens to the sound of a full band. The camera zooms around the room to show Jason Bass sitting in the tour bus of Seek and Destroy. He is dressed in baggy jeans and a “Ride The Lightning” shirt, his massive locks hanging free since they possess far too much THUNDER! To be tamed by a tie. In his lap is a black gloss finished five string bass, his fingers nimbly striking the thick strings. The camera jumps over to an unknown individual behind a set of electronic drums bashing away on his kit, then to yet another unknown man who is playing a red Gibson SG, riffing on it. Finally the shot ends on Eric Evans holding a silver Stratocaster, bending the strings as a lead line squeals out like a teenage girl on Prom Night. The rocking tune continues, all four members of the band rocking out. The music suddenly cuts off as “The Electric One” quits playing. He stands up and places his Strat on a nearby guitar stand which causes the fall out of the rest of the music. The rhythm guitarist and drummer look to “The Muther Plucker” who stands and puts his Ibanez bass on another stand. He looks to his partner. Jason: Dude, what gives? We had a groove going on there! Eric turns back Jason from looking out the window. He looks up at the 6’9” bassist and sighs angrily. He runs a hand through his long hair and looks away for a moment. Eric: Bro…can you believe what happened to us last week? We debuted on national television and got to give all the Destroyers out there a glimpse of what we’re capable of. And we bombed. Jason: That match was crooked from the get go, man! They bent the rules just enough to get the advantage and did you see that finish?! WHAT REFEREE ALLOWS A DOUBLE SUBMISSION?! It was a crooked match, brother. Don’t worry, we’ll do a better job this time. Jason placed a large hand on Evans’ shoulder and nodded to him. Eric looked back up and nodded with the same vigor. Jason: But right now, we have business to attend to. We’re still trying to write the ultimate rock song and bitching about spilt milk won’t do that. Do you think Page and Plant wrote “Stairway” by sitting around complaining? Eric: They wrote that song after getting high, Jason. Jason: That doesn’t matter. Come on, pick up the axe and let’s hit it. That was a killer solo you had going on. Eric looked back to his silver Strat and shook his head. He looked back to Jason: Eric: Not right now. Maybe we got rusty or something, but we need to get that out of our systems. We’re going to a gym. Hit the iron and practice some of our moves. You in? Jason nodded and walked over, kicking his amp lightly which shut it off. He looked to the rest of the live band and waved a hand dismissing them. After they left Jason headed to the front of the bus to tell the driver they were leaving. While he was gone Eric started on the gym bags. As he stuffed some clothes into a bag he reached up into an overhead compartment that belonged to Jam and something fell out as he pulled a second bag out. A “clack” was heard as a cassette tap hit the floor. He picked up the tape and his eyes went wide. Eric: WHAT. THE. HELL? Just then Jam walked back into the room. He looked at Evans curiously and then saw the cassette in his hand. He backed away slowly, throwing up his hands. Jason: Dude, I can explain that. The best rockers admit they get their inspiration from all kinds of music. That’s the ONLY reason I have that. Eric was still in shock. He looked down at the label on the tape and then back to Jason. Eric: Huey Lewis and The News? What are you learning from them? Jason: Uhm…..uhh…… Eric: Never mind, just pack your bag. The scene re-opens inside a gym. Jason Bass lies back on a bench, holding a rather heavy looking dumbbell. Behind him, Eric Evans plays the role of “Spotter.” Sweat flows profusely and easily from Jason’s forehead as he lifts the heavy weight up and down, up and down. The muscles in his arms bulge outward, almost rippling as he exercises, giving a hint to the strength this man possesses at his disposal. Eric: That’s 190, just ten more, Jam. Come on, man! The bell comes up again; Jason is obviously straining to get it up for the last ten lifts. When Eric says “200” he grabs onto it and helps Jason lift it up, setting it back into its place. Jason takes deep breaths, gasping as he lies back still. Eric helps him sit up and then tosses him a bottle of water. Jason: Jesus, dude….that was intense. He rubs his sore biceps as he drinks the cold water. He stretches his arms a few times and then stands up. The tag team partners head towards a boxing ring. Jason rolls in under the bottom rope as Eric climbs up onto the apron and steps between the ropes. Eric does a few stretches and then looks over to Jam. Eric: I think our main priority is to clean up some of our tag maneuvers. We need to work on “The Move.” Jason: You don’t mean…. Eric: Yes, I do. We got shown up BAD last week. We need to be ready this week. Vice and Gamble won’t have any idea what’s coming for them when we initiate “The Move.” Let’s do it. Jason smiles, gleaming whites are shown from behind his framed face. He shakes his head a few times to get some locks out of the way and then heads to the center of the ring. Jason: Things will definitely go to eleven this week.
