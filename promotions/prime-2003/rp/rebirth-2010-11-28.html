Rebirth

28 November 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/28/2010 A blistering cold sweeps through the air early on the morning of November Tenth. A fog rising from the sea makes visibility nearly impossible. No one enjoys spending time in weather such as this, but for some it is not a choice. Although it is no earlier than four in the morning a steady stream of men make their way down to the dockyards. No joy is seen on any of these men’s faces. In fact no emotion of any kind is seen. The faces are leathery and worn down from years of work. A glimpse of despair, of not wanting to be here can be seem on some of the younger men, but for most they have accepted that this is their life, and now they just don’t care. Four massive cargo ships fill the docks, blocking out the view of the city behind them. The stream of men making their way towards the ships stops for a moment in front of four tables, then breaks into smaller streams as the men disperse towards one of the ships waiting to be unloaded. “HOOOOONNNNNNNNNNNKKKKKKKK” The loud horn signals that it is now 4:30, and the work day has officially begun. As the crowd dissipates and the men separate into their work stations, four large cranes start up and start loading crates onto the docks. As soon as the first crate touches down on the docks the men fall into a pattern; once again showcasing the monotony of their existence. Two men take packages out of the crate and load them onto a handtruck, where once it is loaded to capacity it is taken away and another man steps forward with an empty one. The hand truck is taken to a truck, where it is then unloaded by another two men. This process is repeated over and over, and as time passes the men seem to turn into robots performing the same task over and over again. Three of the cargo ships fall into this process without hesitation, but the fourth, and largest of these stations, is at a standstill. A man waits with a hand truck, but no boxes are loaded onto it. The men seem not to care seeing this as a temporary reprieve from an otherwise hard and tedious day, but the foreman doesn’t have the same attitude as his workers do. “What’s the holdup?” “No hold up on our end boss, the loader isn’t here.” “God damn, every fucking day we have this conversation, get up there and do it. I’m done waiting for him.” “Not my job.” “You don’t get up there and do it, you won’t have a job.” “We’ll get it done you know that.” The foreman just shakes his head, and walks away. However even with the threat of losing his job the worker just stands there and waits. As the other three stations steadily unload their cargo the fourth one still sits idly. At around five thirty, an hour into the work day a hulking figure makes his way through the fog, and towards the fourth work station. “HEY” The foreman walks angrily towards the behemoth, but as if not hearing him the man continues to walk towards the cargo bay. “HEY!!!HEY!!!GET THE FUCK OUT OF HERE!!! Still no response, but the monster seems to have the support of his fellow workers as he gets a couple of handshakes, and fistbumps on his way to the dock. “GET DOWN HERE!!!That’s it, you’re done. I am sick and tired of you coming here when you please, and today is the last straw.” “Maciej, if it was up to me I would not be here at 5:30 in the morning.” “Well, now you don’t have to be Walczak, you’re gone.” Getting a clear look at the Monster’s face could this be THE Tom Walczak? But why would he be wasting time in the docks? Why would a former World Champion be doing manual labor so early in the morning? “Anything else?” “No, that’s it Walczak, I am sick of your shit, you can go.” “Alright, than I’m going to get to work, if there is nothing else.” Walczak turns away from the man and begins to load packages onto the empty hand truck. The foreman just looks at Walczak in stunned silence, but seems to accept the fact that he isn’t going home, and turns and walks away from the loading station. Walczak shows that it doesn’t matter that he came to work late. He works at a feverish pace more than doubling the speed of the other work stations, and making it necessary for more empty hand trucks to be diverted to his station. While the loaders at the other stations seem to tire, Walczak just gets stronger, and at 7:30, Walczak’s station catches up to the others despite starting over an hour later. At 10:30, an hour before the lunch bell is supposed to sound Walczak loads the last of the packages onto the hand truck. He steps down off of the dock, and makes his way towards a lunch vendor just setting up for the coming rush. As Walczak steps up to order his lunch, Maciej makes his way out of his nearby office, and does a double take from Walczak, to his loading dock, and then back to Walczak. “What are you doing?” “I’m ordering some golonka, kielbasa, and grochowka. Do you want anything?” “No, not literally what are you doing, but why aren’t you working?” “We have this conversation on an almost weekly basis Maciej. I am done unloading. The trucks to load the ships back up don’t get here for almost another three hours. I can do nothing else until they get here, so I am going to sit here, relax, and eat my lunch. I know that management isn’t supposed to fraternize with us lowly workers, but you are more than welcome to join me.” “You are a piece of work Walczak. I don’t know why you give me so much shit. You know that none of the workers at your station respect me. I can’t say a word to them that they take seriously because of the junk that you pull.” “I don’t know what you are talking about. I come here every day, I never call out, and I get my work done at an incredible pace. If I was working anywhere else, I would have been promoted months ago, but not here. Why is that Maciej? Is it because I want to sleep an hour later each day? Is it because you want to make an example of someone? Or is it simply that you don’t like me?” “How in the hell do you turn this around back on me? YOU are the one that are breaking the rules. YOU are the one who constantly bends or breaks the rules. YOU are the one who shows me no respect. Yet you still think that I’m the bad guy. You should be happy that I let you keep your job.” “Thanks for that Maciej. I am so grateful that you let me keep this minimum wage job, so I can survive my menial existence. You have never worked a hard day in your life. You just sit here, on a power trip, and get pissed off when someone stands up to you.” “What do you mean I never worked a day in my life? I am here earlier than anyone, and I am the last to leave, and….” “Take it easy Maciej, I said that you never worked a HARD day in your life. I am not the reason that the men don’t respect you. Everyone here knows that your father in law owns this company. You did not even finish high school, yet you lord over the men, and let everyone of them know that you think that you are better than them. That is why they hate you.” A look of pure anger crosses the face of Maciej, and he turns and walks away from Walczak not saying another word. Walczak picks his lunch up from the vendor, and makes his way to an open bench to eat his lunch. __________________________________________________________ As 3:30 nears the majority of workers are still working, loading up the cargo ships, but Walczak is done, and without saying a word to Maciej, he makes his way away from the docks. “Walczak, get back here…” Walczak is not deaf, but chooses to pay no attention to the words Maciej is yelling towards him. He knows that the threats spewing forth from his boss’ mouth are empty ones. He knows that he will come back to work tomorrow, and nothing will change. No matter how many times Walczak tries to get Maciej to change the man doesn’t get it. Walczak knows that he can earn a modest living travelling the country making appearances, or if he were more driven, opening up a gym to impart his knowledge and skills to others. However, if Walczak did this, then he would not be able to look at himself in the mirror. His life would be worthless, devoid of any meaning. He chooses to grind out a meager style here at the dockyards because this is where Poland was born. For Maciej, this job is nothing more than a way to appease his father-in-law, and trying to make up for a life of bullying and degradation in school. For Walczak, this job is so much more. In September 1980, when Walczak was still a little boy, these shipyards were home to the first series of demonstrations against Communist Russia. The hope seen in the men taking part in these demonstrations was something that would change Walczak forever. For many people in Poland, the demonstrations were the beginning of a way out, they wanted to take part in the finer things in life, but under Communism these things were impossible. For Walczak, these demonstrations meant so much more. Money, land, material possessions, these thoughts did not register with Walczak, but freedom, and pride these were things that Walczak cared for, and he could see those same things amongst these men. While it has barely been 30 years since Solidarnosc was first started, it seems to have left the minds of those still living in Poland, and those born in Poland during this time don’t seem to know that it even occurred. Former members of this great union, once revered, are now pushed to the back pages of history. They are no more than side show attractions at county festivals, or a temporary idol for politicians looking to build support for an election run. Walczak is here, back where it first began, to try and remind these people of their proud history. The people working in the dockyards presently are not different from those who worked here thirty years ago. Maciej is only 25 years old, to him the dangers that these men went through means nothing, and no matter how much Walczak tries to make him see what he is a part of, it just doesn’t resonate. Day in, day out Walczak tries to make something of the rather pathetic lives of those who surround him, but each day that passes Walczak loses more and more hope. Nearly two years ago when Walczak first woke up to embark on this journey, he looked at himself in the mirror, and smiled for the first time in years. In that smile he saw what he thought would be immediate success. He saw throngs of men, gathering at the dockyards, once again looking forward to the bright future of the Polish people. He saw a complete reinvention of the government, and eventually Poland becoming a player on the international circuit. Now, these once vivid dreams are nothing more than glimmers of hope in the back of Walczak’s mind. Each day that passes Walczak falls into a routine, and becomes more like the men he is trying to save. “Yo, wait up for me.” Walczak is roused from his thoughts by one of his coworkers. As he turns to see where the voice is coming from, he sees a short, slovenly man trying to run towards Walczak but dragging his club foot behind him. The man hangs on to his baseball cap as he makes his way towards Walczak. As he reaches Walczak, he keels over grabbing his sides, and as he tries to catch his breath, he wipes a mixture of sweat and saliva off his face. The sight of this pathetic little man causes Walczak to break out into a deep belly laugh. “What, the hell are you doing Krzysztof?” “I…I…I just wanted to see if you wanted to grab a beer.” “What else would I do after work?” Walczak places his massive arm around his friend almost engulfing him in the process, as they make their way out of the dockyards, and towards their favorite bar. __________________________________________________________ “What’s going on fellas?” “Not much Marek, can we get a round here?” “Sure thing.” Walczak and Krzysztof make their way towards the bar, and this being a Wednesday afternoon during November, they are its only inhabitants, outside of the token drunk guy passed out at the far end of the bar. As they take their seats, Marek puts two large glasses of Okocim in front of them. “So anything new in life guys?” “Same shit, Marek.” “So, no life changing revolution taking place down at the docks?” “I don’t know why I keep letting you mock me Marek. “ “Because I keep feeding you beers, and not charging you a dime.” Walczak raises his glass towards Marek, and then towards Krzysztof before taking a sizable swig of the beer. “I’ll get you another one, just promise me you’ll pace yourself.” “Aww, you’re the best.” Marek gives Walczak the finger as he goes to fill up another glass of beer. Walczak looks at Krzysztof and sees the man struggling to finish the beer in front of him. “I’ll never figure out why you drink beer if you hate the shit so much.” “What do you mean? I love this stuff.” Krzysztof takes another swig of his beer, and once again grimaces, not giving much support to his argument that he loves beer. “You’re an odd little guy Krzysztof.” “What do you mean?” “I mean, that for nearly two years you have followed me down to this bar every day, drank beer, and just sat quietly as I let off some steam. You never speak to women, you never dance, and you never interact with anyone else.” “Yeah, well I just am glad for all you’ve done for me.” “What do you mean what I’ve done for you? I’m not gay if that what you’re getting at, I mean I support your lifestyle, but I just don’t go that way.” “NO!NO!NO! I am not gay Walczak, it’s just that, well that first day, when you asked me to come out with you for a beer, I finally felt like one of the cool kids.” “What the fuck are you talking about? You are nearly forty years old there are no cool kids. You are starting to freak me out.” “You gotta understand man. I have always been the outcast. I was never allowed to play sports, due to my club foot. Since I was disfigured the kids made fun of me, on top of that I didn’t get good grades, so I didn’t get into college. Hell, if my uncle hadn’t worked at the docks all his life I wouldn’t have got this job, and who knows where the hell I would be. When you came around I felt like someone accepted me.” As the words were leaving Krzysztof’s mouth Walczak’s first instinct was to laugh, but then he realized that this man represented what he was working towards. He wanted to bring hope back to the country that he loved, and if he had done so to only one man that the past two years had not been a waste. “Well, I am glad that I could be some help to someone, but this conversation is getting a little to gay for me, so let’s just take it in a different direction.” “Sure thing.” With Walczak not wanting to talk about Krzysztof’s life, and Krzysztof not being much for thinking up topics for conversation, the dialogue comes to a lull. Both men, continue to sip their beers, without talking until Walczak gets up and heads off to the bathroom. “You want to put yourself through another one of these Krys?” “Yeah, why the hell not.” “Here you go buddy, don’t enjoy it too much.” “Hey, Marek, can I ask you something?” “That’s what I’m here for.” “Well, whenever I come here with Walczak the conversation always comes to a standstill whenever we come to a topic concerning anyone’s past. I mean it’s not like his life isn’t known to the world, but for whatever reason whenever I ask him about it he just clams up.” “The guy has been through some shit, and he just doesn’t want to talk about it. Once in a while he gets really shitfaced, and something might come out, but the guy really doesn’t dwell on the past.” “I am not asking the guy to pour his heart out to me, it’s just that for two years I have been coming down here with him at least twice a week, and all I know about the guy is that he likes soccer, and his favorite color is blue. What kind of friendship is that?” “It’s not a friendship Krys, that’s what you have to realize. I mean you’re a nice guy, but you have to realize something about Walczak, the guy doesn’t have, want, or need any friends.” Krzysztof nods, acknowledging that he understands, but is still not fully satisfied with the answer. He returns to his beer, and moments later Walczak returns from the bathroom. “Alright Marek, I am going to call it a night. It doesn’t seem to be a great night for drinking.” “I never thought that I would hear those words coming out of your mouth.” Walczak shrugs sarcastically as he leaves a tip for Marek and makes his way out of the bar. Krzysztof attempts to finish his beer and awkwardly runs towards Walczak before he gets too far away. “Wait up I’ll split a cab back home with you.” “No man, it’s alright, I feel like walking back tonight.” “Alright, I’ll walk with you.” “HAHA, you can barely move, just take the cab home, I’ll even pay your fare.” “No seriously, it’s good for my foot if I walk once in a while.” “Suit yourself.” Walczak makes his way back towards his apartment with Krzysztof trying to keep pace behind him. Krzysztof begins to open his mouth a couple of times looking to start a conversation, but each time he stops himself and falls a few more steps behind Walczak. “Walczak slow down.” “Krys, I told you to take a cab home. I want to get home and go to bed, I am not going to pause every five minutes and wait for you to catch up to me.” “No, seriously wait…” Walczak turns to see Krzysztof stumble for a few steps and then crumble to the ground. Walczak dashes toward his fallen friend. As he gets there he sees that Krzysztof isn’t breathing. He immediately takes out his cell phone and dials for an ambulance. “I NEED AN AMBULANCE!!!! I THINK MY FRIEND JUST HAD A HEART ATTACK!!!!” Walczak tries to help Krzysztof regain consciousness as he waits for the ambulance arrive, but lacking any medical background he is almost useless. No more than two minutes pass and the ambulance arrives. “Quick, he isn’t breathing.” “It’s okay sir we’ll take it from here.” Walczak moves away to let the EMT’S do their job. As the EMT’S load Krzysztof into the ambulance, a blank look comes across the face of Walczak. The last three days had been pretty much a drunken haze for Walczak. He did not go to work, and really never left his apartment unless it was to pick up more beer. All Walczak has done is drink and wait by the phone to hear news about Krzysztof. Maciej once again had said that Walczak was without a job, but he was not worried about that. __________________________________________________________ “RING…RING…” Being right next to the phone Walczak picks it up on the second ring, but he fears the worst. “Hello, is this Mr. Walczak?” “Yes, who is this?” “This is Doctor Sobotko from the hospital, you were listed as the contact for Krzysztof Niedzwiedki, is that correct?” “Yes, is he doing alright?” “Well, he regained consciousness, and has some semblance of speech. He asked for us to contact you.” “What can I do?” “He asked for you to come down. To be honest I would rather him not receive any visitors yet, but he insisted on you coming down.” “Alright, I’ll be there soon.” __________________________________________________________ “Excuse me I’m looking for Krzysztof Niedzwiedzki?” “And you are?” “Tom Walczak, Dr. Sobotko called me.” “He is in room 118.” Walczak makes his way down the hospital corridor towards Krzysztof’s room. Hospitals have always made Walczak uneasy for some reason, so he is constantly looking over his shoulders and making his way at a slower pace than usual. As he gets to the room he pauses for a second to gather his thoughts, unsure of how to approach the situation. “Hey there buddy.” “Aw Walczak, I’m so happy you came.” “Don’t give it another thought. How are you doing?” “Couldn’t be better.” Krzysztof struggles to prop himself up, grimacing until he finally settles in. The man looks at least ten years older, and has lost a significant amount of weight. His eyes are sullen, and an unshaven face gives him a rather slovenly appearance. The sight of Krzysztof makes Walczak uneasy, and as he pulls up a chair next to the bed he can barely look the man in the face. “To be honest man, I thought you were a goner. I could swear that you weren’t breathing .” “I wasn’t breathing, I went through the whole light at the end of the tunnel, life flashing before my eyes thing, and I had almost no physical response until last night, the doctor said my recovery was rather unexpected, and a borderline miracle.” “Well at least you’re back, and feeling better. I am sure that you will be back waddling around the place in not time.” “I doubt it. This thing has really shaken me up. I work all day for piss poor wages. I go home to a rat infested apartment, and spend all my money taking care of my sick mother. I really have nothing to live for.” “Don’t say that shit. There is always something to live for. How do you think your mother would feel if you weren’t there to take care of her, and what about your friends. There is always something to live for.” “Ha, my mother can’t even remember my name, and half the time when I walk in the room she takes her bedpan and throws it at me, thinking I’m a robber or some shit. And my friends, what friends? The only person who talks to me at work is you, and I have no social circle to speak of. My typical Friday night is going out for a drink with you and the guys, waiting until you find some girl to go home with, and then I go home alone.” “That’s just because you don’t believe in yourself enough. You make too many excuses for yourself, you think you are too much of a loser, and you don’t do anything about it.” “I don’t need any sort of rah rah speech right now. Especially from a guy who sulks and broods all day long, and never does anything with his life even though there is so much he can do.” “You’re right.” “What? No fight from the great Wrestling Champion? No more witty comebacks or life lessons? Each day at work you get up in people’s faces, and preach to them on what is wrong with this country, this world, and now you’re right is the best you can come up with?” “What is it that you want? One second you are bitching at me for trying to raise your spirits, and the next you are yelling at me that I am giving up on the speech. Maybe this heart attack really fucked up your brain because you are contradicting yourself left and right.” “Do you not get it? How am I supposed to take anything you say seriously, when I don’t even know who you are? Are you Tom Walczak, Heavyweight Champion of the World? Are you Tom Walczak, Upstart Revolutionary? Who are you?” “I am none of those things. I am a fuckup who throws away all his talent, I am a man who dreams big, but when things get tough I give up. At the first sign of hardship I wilt.” Silence fills the room as Walczak turns away from Krzysztof. After a few moments Walczak gets up to leave the room. “Wait, don’t go yet.” “Why?” “Why are you the way that you are?” “And what exactly is the way I am?” “You know, brooding, sullen, mad at the entire world. You have a vendetta against humanity, but you have nothing to be sad about.” “You’ve heard the stories. I had a rough childhood, I grew up in an orphanage, no one wanted me. It’s not a secret.” “That’s not what I’m talking about. Now, I didn’t know you until two years ago, but when I used to watch you on TV, you were different. Yeah, you were mad and angry, but you seemed to be living for something. Now, you are mad and angry and you seem dead inside. What changed?” “That’s crazy I am the same person I was two years ago, just with a new sense in life. I feel like I need to make the people of Poland proud of their heritage, and the only thing I regret is that I wasted all those years wrestling, and not putting my entire focus on Poland.” “That is ridiculous. You did more for Poland when you were wrestling than you could ever do for them working on the dockyards. What Poland needs is heroes, not dockworkers we have plenty of those.” “I did nothing for Poland when I was wrestling, I could do…” “Would you stop trying to convince yourself that you could make a difference here in Poland where no one sees you?” “I’m not trying to convince myself that I can make a difference here. I am just trying to give myself a reason to get out of bed in the morning, one that doesn’t involve wrestling.” “But why? Why would you…” “BECAUSE, I CAN’T DO IT ANYMORE!!!” Walczak’s face reddens, and his breathing becomes more labored as the words leave his mouth. He does all he can to stop himself from tearing up, and all Krzysztof can do is stare at his friend with a look of stunned amazement. “Of course you can.” “No, I can’t. Do you know how long it’s been since I have done anything of note in wrestling?” “Two years.” “No, not two years. Eight years. It has been eight years that I have done anything. Eight years ago I was on top of the world, I was the OSW World Champion, and I have never been prouder than I was back then. I held that title for just over a week, and when I lost it I felt like all the life was taken out of me.” “That’s no reason to quit. You just have to work harder, you can still…” “You don’t think I tried. After I tasted the pinnacle of my profession, I knew that I wouldn’t be happy until I got back there. After I lost, I swore to myself that I wouldn’t quit until I was once again World Champion, but no matter what I did I failed. I was back in the gym the night I lost, working on new moves, trying to build my up my skills, and fixing all the holes I thought that I had in my game. Then when I would go out, and stand in the middle of the ring, I would forget everything. I was nothing more than a brute walking around the ring trying to outmuscle my opponents en route to victory. No technique, no tricks, nothing.” “I’m sure that you can refocus, and work towards becoming the best again.” “I can’t put myself through that anymore. I want to get as far away from that world as possible. I have to make myself believe that I can accomplish something working in some bullshit job, because I can’t do anything else. I have no higher education, and the only job I have ever held is something I can no longer bear to do. When I left two years ago I thought that maybe I would tour Poland signing autographs, and meeting fans, but every time I did all they wanted to know was why I left, and I couldn’t deal with that anymore. I couldn’t deal with people judging me.” “I thought that you didn’t care what people thought. I thought you were this big tough guy, who just didn’t give a shit.” “So did I.” Walczak is left nearly speechless, looking down at the floor not being able to face his friend. He gets up to leave, and this time Krzysztof says nothing, but as Walczak gets to the door he stops. “I didn’t want to give up. I wanted to keep going but a man can only take so much failure. Loss after loss, the boos of the fans, the look of disappointment on the faces of those you want to make happy. Toward the end I just phoned it in. I went out there, but it was just to pick up a paycheck. I went into the ring knowing that I was going to lose, and I just didn’t care. I was miserable when I woke up every morning, and I just couldn’t do that anymore, so I came back.” “Why did you stay here though? I understand coming back for a couple of months, getting your mind in the right place, but staying away for two years. There is just no sense to that. “ “I can’t face the prospect of failing again.” “Who says that you are going to fail? We both know that you don’t want to work on the docks. Why don’t you do what you were meant to do?” Walczak stares at his friend unsure what to say, but no words come to his mouth. This time as he turns towards the door he leaves. __________________________________________________________ Three weeks have passed since Krzysztof had last seen Walczak. Lying in that hospital bed he had hoped that Walczak would once again visit him, but he never came back. Krzysztof was never a man to get mad or upset, but he felt disrespected by Walczak, a man who he thought was his friend. So when he was released from the hospital he wanted to get some answers for what had happened. He still had his hospital bracelet on as he knocked on Walczak’s apartment door. KNOCK….KNOCK….KNOCK… “Come In.” As Krzysztof enters the apartment he sees an almost barren space with boxes stacked up to the ceiling. He sees Walczak packing up another box, and is confused as to what is happening. “So, is that what you have been up to these last three weeks? Getting ready to run away again.” “Haha, not at all my man, not at all.” “Then what are you doing?” “I’m going back.” “You’re what?” “I’m going back, and I have you to thank for it. You made me realize that I am good at one thing in life, and that for two years I have been running away from the possibility of failure. Well, I am done running. I am back.” “I don’t know what to say.” “You don’t have to say anything. I owe you for this, and trust me I will repay you.” For two years Walczak tricked himself into thinking he could make a difference in the lives of the people he cares for by just being around them, but in these last three weeks he has realized that that is impossible, and failure or success he has to be out there, and that is what he is going to do.
