The Next Trek

14 November 2005

Date: 11/14/05 April 3rd, 2005. The Fleet Center. Boston Massachusetts. ~ The Foreboding. ...The ropes have been wrapped in tight threads of glistening spooled barbed wire, and a row of M-80 explosives are interspersed between the ominous blades, connected to a series of detonators on the side of the ring apron. Two car battery packs are connected by jump-starter cables to metal poles sticking out of all four ring corners where the turnbuckle padding has been removed. ~ The Commentary. ...In an unusually focused and dire tone the voice of Richard Parkers says, “two fine careers, Nick, and I wouldn't be a bit surprised if at least one of them ends tonight! ~ The Slaughter. Close-range explosions. Flesh ripped tenaciously apart by the glistening fangs of barbed wire spools. Electric shocks that drop them to their knees while they quiver and foam at the mouth. ...Emilio reaches up with his hand to feel a pool of blood pour down his face. A large chunk of his left cheek, about a circular half-inch in diameter, is dangling off his bloodied face as a crimson pool of liquid sacrifice runs down his shoulders. ~ The Bitter End. A Pentafix from hell. ...Vampir runs across the ring with Emilio slumped idly over his broad shoulders. But the Dark Lord doesn't stop halfway as he usually would. The fans rise to their feet as he instead continues across the ring, lifts Emilio above his head, and without regard for human life or decency, powerbombs Emilio onto the top rope! KA-BOOM~! The top rope literally gives way under the weight of Emilio’s body in conjunction with the massive explosion, snapping in half, as Emilio's lifelessly body falls to the concrete floor at ringside. ~ The Victory. The ever-excitable voice of Nick Stuart yells out in un-compromised astonishment, “somehow, some way the PRIMEal Rage Champion is back in this thing! And the Fleet Center is going INSANE!!! ...With his absolute last breath, Emilio catches his second wind and staggers wobbly to his feet. He tenderly limps over to the fallen carcass of Nosferatu and gives it a sharp kick...With the utmost respect and care, Emilio reaches down and begins to hoist the big man back to his base, using every last ounce of strength and internal fortitude, before leaning Vampir’s prone body up against the ring apron. Then, after giving the Dark Lord a quick and sloppy middle-finger, blood running down his forehead, his gut, his hands, his back, Emilio pulls Vamp back, kicks him in the gut, and plants him with an arena-shaking ROAR... ~ The End. “This match is over!,” says Nick Stuart, frantically. “It’s over, but Nosferatu is hoisting Emilio up to the top of the PRIME-A-TRON! God, someone stop this!” ...Both commentators dive out of their seats as Vampir launches himself fifteen-feet in the air from the top of the PRIME-A-TRON, powerbombing Emilio into unholy oblivion...The Dark Lord has overshot his mark unfortunately and Emilio, instead of crashing through the commentator's table - as Vampir does - lands with his small of the back on the edge of the stage before stumbling backward to another fifteen foot drop... End feed ~ He kissed her discreetly. Holding her gently with one of his massive arms, he engaged with her in a prolonged hug. From father to daughter. As he tenderly rubbed the small of her back, being careful not to press to firmly against her smaller, delicate frame, the tears welled up in his eyes. Finally, he let go. The warmth precipitated his heart. “I...I...” Emilio stammered, unable to find the words. “I know,” Sylvia smiled, planting him on the cheek with a quick peck. Emilio smiled. He reached up and rubbed the remaining tears from his cheek as his only daughter boarded the ramp to the ominous plane. As he mournfully parted for the second time with his sole genetic heir, it occurred to him that he had never felt pain on such a level. The bile in his stomach seemed to bubble up and fill his heart with acid; He had the tight knot in his stomach on many occasions, but never as intense as this. Was this what love is, he thought, this intense sensitivity to pain and sorrow? Because he always assumed it would be a much better emotion to share with someone than hate; warm and fuzzy. But never could he have imagined what kind of feelings it would evoke when the two parted their ways, if only temporarily. Maybe the first time they parted their ways he would have felt the same pain. But there was no first time. For fifteen long years, he never knew that he had a daughter. He never would have imagined that Maria, his deceased lover, was pregnant and gave birth in merely months before her untimely death at the hands of the men Emilio vowed to destroy. But that’s a different story for a different day. For now, all Emilio could help wonder was if it would hurt less if he knew her better, felt more genuinely connected....could be sure that she loved him too, and would therefore come back for certain. But if there’s one thing that Emilio had learned over the years it was that the only thing that is certain is uncertainty. And you can be sure that he was uncertain at this moment. Ironic, huh? He had lost her once and he was bound and determined not to do so again. So as he stood there solemnly, a number of thoughts passed threw his tumultuous brain. The first of which, of course, was sadness. The second, anger. He mournfully squeezed his fists together upon realization that he had no control and would have to let go. His hand was forced by the powers that be -and aye, they be great-and her leaving- it really wasn’t even his decision to make. Finally, mustering up his remaining courage, “I love you!” he called after her, rather over-dramatically. But it seemed to be drowned out by the roar of the jet engines. “I love you too,” however, came the magical response. A tingle of joy shot down Emilio’s spine. He forced himself to cut a smile. For It had been a long and gruesome summer. The last several months had been filled with so many twists and turns that it would take an eternity for Emilio to get them all categorized and stored away in his head, let alone broken down and taken in by his flailing system. Information overload. It’s like reading a long novel but not taking the time to stop and analyze it as you went along. You may have all the information, but can you out together a coherent argument for, say, a book report? Sure, Emilio was laid up. And sure, he had time on his hands. But he spent most of this not reminiscing about the days of old, but training and preparing himself physically and mentally for his return. It was a long, arduous journey. But one worth taking. And the rest of the time he spent dealing with the issues as they came up, one at a time, on a day-to-day basis. The only way you can, you know. From his sudden departure from pro-wrestling, to the ongoing ordeals with his family, to his one-night return where he lost the Global Title to Karina Wolfenden, and all back again. The summer seemed like a whirl of thoughts bouncing randomly off the walls of his empty, decaying skull now. In other words, they were dead and he was ready to move forward. So now, here he was. A proud man. A proud father. But a hurt man as well. After letting out a deep breath Emilio opened his car door and climbed quietly in, eyes still on the runway as the plane began to take off, and with it his hopes and dreams. Oh, he had wanted her to stay. He had this debate with himself many, many times. But logic and reason eventually overwhelmed his lesser-being and he came up with the unmistakable conclusion that he just had to let go. For now. He could keep her safe, he thought. As long as he kept her on the down-low. As long as those who were out to hurt him didn’t know about her. In the end, however, forced by necessity to reassume himself into the world of professional wrestling, he thought it wise not to let his daughter get in the thick of it. And who was he kidding? There were so many factors involved, and each and every one had their own unique threat to not only his being, but her’s as well. Personal politics enough had been a red flag for concern. On many occasion had he already feared losing his daughter. Whether it was to his scheming father in Nicaragua, or his extra-legal business associated from Japan, Emilio had been put in some rather awkward scenarios in the last year to say the least. The very least. And Mr. Ragerio, the fine Central American cocaine profiteer, he certainly knew of Sylvia: his own grand daughter, of course. And Big Ben and the Japanese Mafia...no doubt. No doubt. And on one occasion both parties showed interest in taking her. Emilio could not let her be put in that situation again. Not now, not ever. Angrily, he slammed his fists against his steering wheel, inadvertently blowing the horn in the process. If the last few days and weeks leading up to the departure had not bothered him, as it happened, as it unfolded before him, so did his pent-up emotions. And maybe, just maybe he could protect her from Ben. And father? Well, he was never as much a threat as a hindrance- a big one, yes, but nonetheless not a particularly dangerous man...At least Emilio thought. But he couldn’t be quite sure. Rumor has it that the old man was starting to go a little blank upstairs, that all the screws were no longer there. Finally, the years were catching up with him: the drinking, the cocaine, the obsessive partying....the enslavement of coke lab workers, civil war, riots, and starvation...politics in general. Personal and public politics. They were enough to drive this man mad. On occasion, rumor has is these days that he is known to act out in rather...peculiar ways. But then again, Emilio never saw concern to fear him....That is, until you register an offspring into the equation. But Tyler Nelson. Nay. This was a different breed of man; a different greed of man. He was known to play an entirely different ball game where the rules, and sometimes even those who call the rules, could be changed on the turn of a dime. Not like warfare. Not like the do-or-die scenarios Emilio once lived out where things were drawn out so clear and simple: either pull the trigger faster than them, or die trying. And Emilio was good at what he did. Too good. With all his pulls and friends in government, it may yet be years before the U.S. grants him citizenship. If ever. Because his reputation with the Sandinistas was so good, they were thoroughly convinced that he was a potential threat. Oh, no “potential” about it, if any man crossed him -including the government- he was a threat. A threat indeed. But from here on out, he planned, in theory at least, to remain on the right side of the law. As previously noted, however, Nelson had a certain way about himself, a certain way of manipulating the circumstances around people and events that sometimes had the effect of backing people into a corner. And a cornered Emilio Rage was a threat not only to himself but to everyone around him. Therefore, Sylvia had to go. Too risky. Too unsafe. And with all the external factors weighing in heavily on his mind as well, Emilio had finally broken down. Swallowing his heart which bled so intensely for his daughter -I just got to know her! he charged himself with- he consolidated his emotional resources and opted to take the much safer and wiser path: send her away to live with her relatives in Florida. His in-laws, to be precise. After Maria had been murdered at the hands of the Contras, her brother picked up the remaining pieces of the family tree and some of the fellow villagers, and somehow successfully applied for political asylum in the U.S. Letting out a sigh, Emilio looked into the pink cloud-covered sky as the sun set behind the sloping desert hills and imagined himself with Maria back home in Nicaragua sipping iced cola in the hazy afternoon heat. ...But as he blinked his eyes, the dream vanished, and the only thing he saw was the tangible object of the plane as it darted across the open sky and out of sight. Sighing, he fastened his seat belt and started up his car. ~ And now, days after his premature return to the ring, Emilio had surpassed regret mode and was on the attack. For a new and vile threat had come to PRIME. It was not so much the thought of destroying PRIME that had Emilio so worried -although he would be deeply saddened. It was the fact that this man, this greedy shell of a main, posed a direct threat upon not merely his career as a professional wrestler, Emilio’s lively-hood as well. For these days wrestling was all that he knew. And all that -save Sylvia- he loved and cherished. He could no longer be a soldier- that was out of the question. So this was his forum, his conduit for releasing those pent up frustrations. The ones you can either bottle up and let boil inside you, or leave in the ring when you split your opponent’s head open and nail him for the three-count. But even the perceived attack on possibly the sport itself wasn’t enough to get an ailing Emilio to laceup the boots at a critical time in the healing of his injuries. For the doctors said a mere month difference in time could make in the difference in whether he made an all-out recovery and lived his career to the fullest, or whether he re-injured his healing internal organs and risked serious injury, or death. A man needs a liver, ya know. No, not even that could drive him to such extremes. Only one thing, one factor, could pull Emilio back into the fray so fast: her. It was all bout her. He would do anything for her. Anything.He had known his daughter so little up until the last couple months, and yet he felt as if he knew her all his life. And without professional wrestling, there would be no money. And without money he could not pay for the necessities that his beautiful young daughter deserved: a nice house, decent food, proper medical care, a safe environment to live in, a real education. Yes, he had considered downgrading to your typical American nine-to-five job. He could handle construction. Maybe even become a bouncer or a security guard. You know, apply his talents. He was certainly more than qualified. But none of these would give him the financial opportunities to support his daughter to the extent that he wanted to. Only professional wrestling -or so it occurred to him, at least- offered him this stability. And therefore, none of the others would suffice. And leaving the country certainly wasn’t an option. He had grown too comfortable to the America way of life, and while at times he missed the vast forests and stomping grounds of his youth, he knew the economic opportunity there to be next to none. The grass may not have been greener on the other side of the fence, but at least up here there was plenty of placed to graze. No, no other place was fit. And neither was any other course of action. He had to do what had to be done. So it was decided rather suddenly that he would stay. And that he would fight. And the sooner PRIME was rid of this particularly insidious evil, the sooner his daughter could come home to him. ~ Sooner than later, he thought, as he sat back relaxed on his couch. “Would you care for another cup?” Emilio asked with a smile, putting his own empty coffee mug gently down on the PRIME coaster. “Maybe with a shot of something?” “Yeah...that’d be great. Whisky,” said his guest, gazing at him from across the small wooden coffee table in the center of Emilio’s small living room. “Very well.” Emilio poured the cup and the shot and passed it to the eagerly awaiting hands of his contemporary. He sat back quickly though, his own ears waiting even more eagerly for the knowledge possessed by the man before him. “So, let’s get down to business...” His associate nodded approvingly, leaning forward in his seat to converse on the delicate matter. “So,” began Emilio, “you say you’ve known Nelson for years?” ~
