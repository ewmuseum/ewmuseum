Fatherhood - Chapter 12 - Learning from the Past

5 February 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: February 5, 2009 At high school sporting events, Kenneth Dawkins was that parent. He was the outspoken, loud mouth, obnoxious parent; the one who turned what should have been an exciting interscholastic event into a situation that most people wanted to end immediately. Kenneth always made sure that he screamed loud enough so that his son would hear him, loud and clear, without a problem. “BRYAN, YOU’RE DOING IT WRONG! HOOK THE OTHER ARM!” “YOU’RE TOO HIGH, BRYAN…TOO HIGH!” “DON’T CHEAT TO ONE SIDE, BRYAN!” His instructions were endless. Had he been the actual coach of the wrestling team, this wouldn’t have been a problem; it would have been his job. But just being a parent and spectator, the massive amount of screamed instructions were completely unnecessary. “OKAY NOW, GET HIM IN SIDE CONTROL!” “GO FOR THE LEG HOOK, GO FOR IT!” “COME ON, BRYAN, GO FOR THE TAKEDOWN!” The only thing that could have made Kenneth’s tirades any worse would have been if he would verbally abuse other members of the team. Luckily for the entire Dawkins family, he didn’t. Unluckily for Bryan, he made up for it with more than enough criticism for his son. “WAY TO GO, BRYAN…YOU DIDN’T LISTEN AND YOU LOST POINTS!” “I TOLD YOU HE’D DO THAT, BRYAN!” “WHY DON’T YOU EVER LISTEN! GOD!” The criticism didn’t stop after the matches, either. The drives from meets to home were worse. “Bryan, if you don’t listen to me, you’re going to be a failure.” “This is never going to work if you don’t listen to your father. I have all of the experience, and you don’t. So just listen to me, and you’ll go places.” “Son, you need to pay attention when I talk to you and teach you these things, or else that’s just going to give your opponents one more weakness to attack, not that you don’t have enough already.” Now all of this didn’t mean that Kenneth didn’t love his son. That would be a complete lie, because Kenneth Dawkins saw big things for Bryan, but felt the uncontrollable need to push his son along. In Kenneth’s mind, the extra help could do nothing but increase Bryan’s chances for success. Interestingly enough, although the other families of the wrestlers weren’t crazy about Kenneth and his outbursts, Dawkins succeeded through all of the criticism and made it to the Hawaii State Championship Finals all four years of his high school career, and actually won the title in his Junior and Senior years. Unfortunately, this simply made Kenneth’s ego grow that much more. When the time came for Dawkins to decide where his next step in life would take him, Kenneth was very adamant about what he believed Bryan’s decision should have been. “You want to go and wrestle at Hawaii? And then what? You gonna go and wrestle in the Olympics or something? Yeah right, Bryan, be realistic. You’ve been lucky during your entire high school career, and you’ll be facing the best wrestlers in the country once you step up on that national stage. You’ll make a complete fool of yourself, and I won’t be there to bail you out like I have so many times before.” Instead, Kenneth had other ideas for his son. “Come to HCW. They’ve already seen you perform, and they know what you’re made of. You’ve got a great pedigree.” The way Kenneth said the last sentence made Bryan sick to the pit of his stomach. His father was so full of himself and believed that every bit of success that Bryan achieved was solely because of his own contributions to his son, not Bryan’s abilities alone. “You’ll get shot right up to the top of the pile if you come work with the guys here at home. You’re well known in the circuit and being close to home can’t hurt. Plus, your old man here would be able to see you through your whole career! Wouldn’t that be great? Quite a father-son combo, if you ask me!” When it came down to it, Bryan chose HCW over the University of Hawaii’s full scholarship, much to the delight of Kenneth. He gave up four to five years of absolutely free schooling for a job that wouldn’t come anywhere near meeting his financial goals, and would give him no backup in case physical damage would keep him from doing his job for the rest of his life. At least wrestling at school, he always had an education to fall back on. Add that to the fact that Dawkins would actually be sharper in the ring with four or five years of amateur wrestling under his belt, and Bryan’s choice to go to HCW wasn’t a good one. Bryan debuted in HCW in mid-June, two weeks after he graduated high school. With HCW legend Kenneth backing his own son up, expectations were incredibly high for the second generation hometown superstar, and the pressure was completely on Bryan to perform to an enormously high level. Unfortunately, the tongue-lashings that plagued the father-son relationship at wrestling meets in high school only resumed when he stepped into the squared circle. And sadly, they only got worse. After half of a year with the company, he amassed only three wins. His losses were over five times that. Bryan’s overwhelming lack of success pushed Kenneth over the edge. Finally, after a show and another Dawkins loss, Kenneth blew up. It also ended up being Bryan’s last night in HCW. He walked out without saying a word to anyone, aside from his father, and by the end of the week, he was on a plane to England to compete in a fledgling company, High Risk Wrestling. Kenneth called dozens of times, attempting to scare his son into coming back to the hometown company. Ironically, HRW closed a month after its’ debut, and Dawkins was stranded in England with no work. Finally, he flew back to the States, and found work performing in various traveling independent organizations, many times even competing under a mask to conceal his identity and stay out of the public eye so that he could keep his skills sharp while looking for full-time, well-paying work. Week-in and week-out, Bryan was ignoring phone calls from his father while going out and putting on shows for gymnasiums filled with maybe half a thousand people, pulling in two hundred bucks a night, at most. Was it a glamorous lifestyle? Not the least bit. Did it get him by, and help him get to where he is today? Absolutely. But don’t ask Kenneth Dawkins about that. * * * THURSDAY FEBRUARY 5, 2009 PORTLAND MARIOTT CITY CENTER ROOM 414 8:41 AM Bryan laid passed out in the oh-so-soft bed of the Portland Mariott City Center, “home” for PRIME superstars for the past couple of days. Although terribly comfy, with thick mattresses, fluffy pillows, and absolutely huge beds, the room wasn’t quite home sweet home. Honestly though, at this point, after the couple of weeks that the Flyin’ Hawaiian had endured, the bed felt amazing. Directly after ReVolution the previous night, Dawkins checked into his room and quite simply passed out. Sure, he wasn’t booked in a match, but his activity had been up greatly in the past few weeks. Dawkins wasn’t complaining however. The only thing that was gonna get him by was sleep. And sleep he would get. However, around 9 AM, the aggravating sound of Dawkins’ cell phone rang throughout the room. Surprisingly, Dawkins managed to ignore it the first time it rang, and continued in his deep sleep. Actually, the Flyin’ Hawaiian barely flinched. But five minutes later… …it rang again. This time, Dawkins stirred a little bit and reached blindly on the nightstand for his cell. After a few moments of bumbling around with various things on the stand, Dawkins finally grabbed hold of the vibrating noise-making object and opened it without looking. “Yeah?” “Bryan?” The voice on the other end was familiar. Too familiar. “Yeah…dad?” It was almost like Dawkins woke up instantly. “Yes Bryan, it’s me.” Dawkins was now awake, but not terribly thrilled. The conversation was choppy and terribly awkward. “What’cha need, pops? I was kinda in the middle of somethin’.” It was Bryan’s usual escape strategy. Come up with excuses, make them seem important, and get away from contact with Kenneth at all costs. Thanksgiving was the first time in almost a year that they had seen each other face-to-face, and it just resulted in an argument about Bryan’s direction in life and how it would affect his son. Bryan wasn’t about to let it get that far this morning. “Oh, sorry…I guess you did have a long night. I don’t wanna keep you long, so I’ll get right to it. Your mother and I talked to Pam and Mike, and we convinced them that you weren’t really ready to take care of Nicholas on the road. We’ve come to the conclusion that we’re going to take charge here and keep you from making a mistake that could ruin everyone’s lives here. Next time you come home, we’ll talk more about this. But I’ll let you get back to your important stuff.” “But dad…” “But nothing, Bryan…this is the decision we came to, and you’re not changing it. We’ll talk about it next time you come home. Goodbye.” Before Bryan could utter another word, Kenneth had hung up, and the conversation was over. A moment later, the same phone he had been talking into was lying on the other side of the room in pieces. Tears began flowing from the young Hawaiian’s eyes, and sleep seemed impossible now. Kenneth was going to take away the one joy in his life aside from his job, and there was seemingly nothing that Bryan could do to change that. However, Bryan realized one thing. He needed to learn… …from his father’s mistakes.
