Ante up and survive. *** Brandon Youngblood stood there, looking at the deep red sunset looming on the horizon. His body was pressed against the haggard wooden fence, the kind that you might see in the old west with a post and two large pieces of wood, one near the ground and one near the top of the four foot pike in the ground. He stood there in a pair of baggy yellow shorts that extended just below the knees, a pair of black Oakley sunglasses, and a bag of ice over his right shoulder, held in place by a white dressing. He dug his toes into the sand as he looked on over the horizon to his home, his face being let go for a little while. He hadn't shaven in days, and had grown stubble all over his face in the form of a budding beard and a moustache. Small beads of sweat appeared all over his sleek frame as he just peered out, looking at everything before him. It was as if the world itself was giving itself up to him and him only. If only he was so lucky. 'Old School' Dave Gibson proved that point mere days before. He made good on his claims that Youngblood would merely be a stepping stone for his launch to success. And no matter what excuse Brandon made about how his arm giving out, ultimately cost him the match, he was still very much at fault for his performance. Youngblood had a mixed bag within PRIME. He was heralded as a can't miss talent, and seemed to make good on that claim by running Joshua Taro Freedom, a PRIME mainstay, out of the promotion and into wrestling oblivion by making him tap out to the Gridlock in his PRIME debut. He had Jeff Garvin, a man he respected in his corner as a tag team partner for a PRIME Tag Team Championship Tournament. And much of the internet wrestling community thought that the dream team of the Original and the Greatest Wrestler Alive would take the titles with ease. However, in-fighting stopped the tSC and Action! dream teamers from ever getting on the same page. At the time, Los Diablos upset them and gave the two the fire necessary to begin their war. Their feud has gone across the wrestling world now, going from one promotion to another. At first it was about Championships, but then it became a personal war when Brandon's wife was attacked by the callous Garvin. Since then, Brandon has made it known that he will not stop hunting Jeff Garvin down until he truly makes him pay for what he did. The team of Karina Wolfenden and Tessa Windsor would go on to win the tag team titles, but the internet wrestling community cried foul, especially when Windsor proved to be a lemon in perhaps the best wrestling organization in the world. Brandon Youngblood went on to the Dual Halo match at Culture Shock, and bleed his guts out. While giving the fans an image to forever remember him by, Brandon Youngblood passed out to the Soprano from a debuting Angelo Deville. The blood loss and fatigue, as well as the concept of a future war with the Original within another wrestling promotion, played roles in the elimination of Youngblood. The instant the official called for the elimination, it caused a domino effect on the budding career of the Only Diamond In The Sport. Karina Wolfenden went on to win the Dual Halo match, but afterwards received a crushing spinebuster from a dazed Youngblood. The belief was that Brandon was not even giving a traditional three hand raise to signify a pass out, and that it was a short cut to an inevitable and accurate conclusion. Youngblood could not stop from alienating many PRIME and Wolfenden fans with that one swift spinebuster. The building blocks were laid down for Peter Vetra to come in and make a name for himself. Youngblood felt that the issue of the Dual Halo was controversial, and called for a review to be set up for the incident. But no such review would take place, and Youngblood watched his pleas fall on deaf ears as Wolfenden choose her wish. To face Boda for the Universal Championship. Youngblood was infuriated, and the cerebral Vetra took advantage. Youngblood completely overlooked the man, deeming him to be so far below him that he had no right to face him. But Vetra persisted, and was rewarded by a weak mind game attempt from Brandon. Youngblood baited a hook with his most treasured friend. His love, Melissa Garvin. Vetra took the bait, but snapped the line, swimming away cleanly as Youngblood could only watch, mortified. Vetra took his then girlfriend and held her hostage, completely screwing with Youngblood’s head. And then, with the stipulation to either get her back or lose her forever, Youngblood faced Vetra. And lost. Completely destroyed and willing to do anything, Youngblood was given a choice. If he were to leave PRIME forever, then Vetra would give him his girl back. Youngblood did so without question. And the exodus had begun. Brandon Youngblood had left PRIME without making so much as a whisper, his potential never being fully realized. The chilling feeling on his right shoulder soothed the throbbing pain shooting through his arm. Beads of perspiration formed on the outside of the ice bag, dribbling off and down to the back of the Greatest Wrestler Alive. The sky was turning to the night, the dusting of a full moon just above the setting sun. The sun itself had turned a deep crimson, as if it was ready to burn out forever and plague the world with never-ending night. Youngblood became twisted. Bitter. He went to his home in the Mojave Desert and dedicated himself to not only returning to pay Vetra back, but to crushing everyone in PRIME who stood idly during his plight. He formulated calculating reasons as to why he hated those he once respected. He did not want to just destroy Vetra, but destroy the roster of PRIME. While marriage seemed to take his mind off his ideals momentarily, he was simply waiting. He contacted PRIME about returning, stating that he never signed his walking papers and that he was still under contract. And so he came back, beating Vetra within an inch of his life and crippling his chances to get the Alias Championship he so desperately wanted. He came back with a calculated strike, much like his hero, 'the Wolf' Mike Randalls would. But the more he thought about it, the more he pushed aside his war with Vetra. He felt a power over him in costing him his prized possession at that moment that he did not need to pursue him much further. He saw himself as the only man in the organization who was willing to stand up and fight for what was right. Revenge is a powerful feeling, but it was not within the nature of Youngblood to want to destroy an entire promotion. He understood that the wrestlers within PRIME would simply find other homes. The sense was that the PRIME roster did not help him because they hated him. He was wrong. They were just indifferent. Youngblood fashioned himself as a warrior, and man who gave everything he had to pursue this art. Like a Shaolin Monk, he spent years toiling to perfect himself. In his mind, he was seeing his abilities grow without anyone to truly oppose him. Nobody had a warrior's mentality, instead selling themselves short with their own egotism or through other ways. His motives had somewhat changed. There was no honor in defeating people who were not like him. There was no honor in making those with flimsy wills break. Thus, he began to seek out those who would challenge him. His philosophical nemesis, Karina Wolfenden, was the first person on his list. And quickly, the two faced off in an epic encounter. But Karina showed Brandon that he was not the only warrior in the world of PRIME. The Negasonic Lupine was very much his equal, if not his better when it came down to it. Unlike himself, she had no doubt. She just did as she pleased and did not care about the thoughts of others. Brandon could never be that care free. That effortless. And that is why he respects her more than life itself, even if he would never admit it to anyone else. With Angelo Deville, it was about will. Deville's arrogance is both his blessing and his curse. But the Devil's Don has never submitted in his illustrious career. Brandon wanted to show the world that this man's bark was worse than his bite. To make Deville accept defeat, to make him tap and submit to the will of another would destroy the ego of the Deville. And while, Brandon failed, he is resolute in making it happen. Deville took to taking on broken down legends and recovering athletes. But someday, the two will meet in the ring. And that next time, Youngblood will be more then ready to force the Devil's Don to tap out. But his current enemy, his current rival, was the 5-Star Champion. Nova represented all that was, in Brandon's mind, wrong with professional wrestling. He was a mega talent who openly did drugs. Youngblood himself had been a recovering alcoholic, and the lesson he learned from the pain of that drove him mad. What he saw in Nova was a man who could face him, but needed to change to be a true threat. To reach his full potential. And that was what he forced him to do. If he wanted to put the boots to Brandon, he was going to need to have to legitimately focus in on him and only him. No being sidetracked. It was a calculated decision to come for Nova, to discredit one of the men who was against the Straight Edge Revolution. CM Punk would be proud. Brandon was resolute in the fact that he would take the 5-Star Championship just days before Colossus II, but he was starting to have huge doubts. The Dave Gibson contest was supposed to be a tune up. Nothing more. Nothing less. But in the end, a stubborn Gibson had hurt Youngblood in ways that would cost him in his biggest match ever. And that is not even mentioning that Youngblood was supposed to be up and coming while Gibson was supposed to be over the hill. But the supposedly over the hill wily veteran was more than a match for the aggressive Youngblood. Part of it might have been glossing over the true ability of Gibson. And the other possibility was that he was too sloppy at this stage of his career to be a legendary figure in the sport. He grabbed at the ice pack on his right shoulder, letting the cold perspiration run on his hand. He looked at the hand for a second as the star dusted sky and the powdery full moon shone through. And then he looked at the sky. Everything he ever wanted and needed was just days away from either being achieved or snatched from him. He had a bum shoulder. And as the sky sparkled with vigor, little did he know another large scale bomb of self doubt was mere hours away from detonation. *** The instant he woke up, a stabbing pain emanated from his shoulder. It was a dull throb, the sort that form a pit in your stomach. It hurts beyond hurt but you can not really do anything about it. Brandon tried to sleep on it, but could not get into a comfortable enough position to keep his slumber. So, eventually, he just decided to get up and do something. He went to the freezer and grabbed an ice pack, quickly putting it against his shoulder and then leaving the kitchen for the spacious living room. The living room was in a corner of the home, and the walls to the outside instead were glass. There were shades within the glass panes, so one could somewhat stop the flow of sunlight into the room. There was a tan carpet on the floor, and there was a plush tan recliner near a large flat screen HDTV that sat against the wall, hung like a picture. Beneath it was a VCR and a DVD player, along with a slim line Playstation 2 with the red light on it that were hooked up to the television. In a pitch black night, it would probably be the only thing someone could see, but with the full moon cascading white light down on the Mojave, that was just not the case. The room also had a large couch against one of the glass walls, itself tan like the carpet. The ceiling was painted white and was pop corned, a solitary ceiling fan humming, accentuating the air conditioning coming from the vents all over the room. There were throw pillows on the sides of the coach. On the two ends of the coach were end table, each with a white glass lamp and coasters. There was also an end table by the recliner. On the end table were a trio of remote controllers, each for their corresponding systems. On the table was a white dressing wrap as well. Brandon turned on the TV, dressed only in a pair of gray sweats. The light emanating from the television was enough for him to look at himself and wrap his arm up. And as the infomercials about potato peelers and get-rich-quick schemes played before him, he could only think about the throbbing pain in his arm. He'd taken a whole load of pain killers to soften the blow, but it was not really doing anything. And as infomercials became actual programming, the star dusted night sky evaporated to an orange hue as the sun made its ascent. Sun poured through the windows as Brandon stared blankly at the television screen. He finally got up the nerve to turn on the VCR and play the tape. The tape. Him falling out of the ring, trying to absorb the blow with his arm, and watching it not do a damn thing. He watched on as Gibson attacked relentlessly at the arm, making sure to attack with everything he had. And finally, Gibson pinned him and beat him. He stared at the television screen, not in anger, but with a stone gaze. It was like he was looking through what was going on, the images too fresh in his brain to actually register as if they had actually happened. Dave Gibson was not Nova. Nova had not reached his prime. Gibson had. And now, his arm felt all the worse. The perspiration beads trickling down his back, annoying him to the point of madness. He was uncomfortable, a steady headache coming on as the blood pumped through his body at tremendous force. Inside, he was a full scale riot, yelling and screaming for some sort of justice. Some sort of release. But the exterior was just a somber stare. His eyes bloodshot, he rewound the tape and viewed it again. And again. And again. No longer was he staring right through the television screen. He was absorbed into it. The light taking hold of his soul and making him its' slave. And he watched in quiet mortification as his body crashed to the ground. He was being shown over and over that he was not immortal. Not bulletproof. But a flesh and blood man who can get hurt. The realization was like hearing an airplane engine pin. It's the sort of lost innocence that you feel when you grow into a man. Brandon was very mortal, with feelings and opinions and weaknesses. His will? Iron. But his body was not that strong. Over time, he would break down, and his will and soul would be trapped in a depleted vessel. Even now, his own mortality had never quite sank in. He was supposed to be this wrestling machine, a man who could go forever without stop and continue pounding away. Even when he lost blood and even when he could feel his own tendons and ligaments being torn slowly and expertly by submission experts, his will was stronger than his body. His body was the weakness. A far away voice began to beckon to him. The voice, ever so faint, somewhat snapped him back into this plane of existence. Back from the glow of the television light. It was a soft and comforting voice that eased his troubled mind and mended his broken shoulder. 'Brandon...I got somethin' to tell you...and you ain't gonna like it.' Brandon cranked his head over slowly, looking at his wife who was standing there in only in a pair of gray sweats and a black sports bra. Her hair was mess. She slowly used her hands at her eyes clawing and rubbing away the painful green gobs of sleep that rested on her eyelids. Brandon tried his best to smile, but really could not get the muscles to work. It would have been wrong. He let her continue, wanting her to cut to the chase. 'You've been up how long Brandon?' She asked. And Brandon gave no response, instead turning to the alluring glow. She was putting this off. And you could not blame her. If anyone understood the relationship, they too would try to put off saying these dreadful words as long as possible to Brandon Youngblood. It was a simple fact that no matter what, the news he was about to hear would shatter him, likely spreading him into a thousand pieces. Not from being crushed, but because he would throw himself into such a tantrum that he would break like a glass that was thrown as hard as possible against a wall. And then, suddenly, a circle of about twenty candles flickered to life, all simultaneously. The light allowed for the appearance of the sand beneath the candles to be seen. The candles themselves were trapped within a glass casing, without a way to open them. And within the circle sat Brandon Youngblood. His head was bowed, his black dojo panted legs crossed indian style as he sat there, his hands hanging ominously in prayer. On his head was a long mop of black hair, and as he brought his head up ever so slowly, his face was covered in facial hair. A beard and moustache, all black, hung from his face. But the more shocking realization was the appearance of a facial tattoo, one that looked prehistoric. The edges were sharp, and it made him look like a sort of human wolf. On his arms were Samoan warrior tattoos, his muscle lean and tight. To his side, a samurai sword, the handle black leather with gems encrusted within it. The blade was curved, and looked sleek and sharp. If one was to use it, they could slice a man in two without them knowing for several seconds what had just happened. He was a warrior. He slowly opened his eyes, and the orbs burned a brilliant crimson shade that glowed. He smiled a sickening smile, one that would freeze a mortal man in fear.
