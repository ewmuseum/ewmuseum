Gettysburg (Chapter 2 of "Battlefields")

7 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/7/09 "Goddamn summer sessions! Like I really need the extra work!" Samantha Wade was cursing the air as she dipped into the 112th Ave. subway station. In her eternal struggle to get tenure with the Columbia University history department, she was horned into teaching two separate summer seminars - one for undergrads, one for rising grad students - and to be quite frank, it was driving her off the wall. I didn't spend four years on a doctoral thesis to spend my life having overprivileged Ivy Leaguers fall asleep to the sound of my voice. I swear, if I catch Jeremy Clarke dozing off one more time, I'll- She was stopped mid-thought, having walked smack-dab into a graffiti-encrusted cement wall next to the stairs. Samantha took the jarring shift nonchalantly and, fully aware she'd just made a complete ass of herself in the middle of a crowded subway station, turned around to head for the platform like nothing had happened. Which would be the exact moment she snagged her backpack on a loose piece of metal jutting out from the wall. Almost instantaneously, the canvas bag was sheared in two, sending several dozen exams on the Cuban Missile Crisis plummeting to a floor she'd prefer to touch only with surgical gloves and with a healthy, industrial strength vat of hand cleanser to jump in immediately afterwards. Sam looked up at the flickering fluorescent light above her, shaking her head as she fruitlessly tried to appeal to whatever elemental power was screwing with her day so royally and completely. With a quick sigh she dropped to her knees and began pawing around the unique bacterial cocktail of the New York subway to collect her papers. "Anything else you want to do to me today?" she inquired to the ether. All of a sudden, like an answer to her prayers, a hand came from out of nowhere, bearing several of her recently lost Blue Books. Finally with something to grin about, Sam took the papers, then shoveled the final few into her arms before realizing she had nowhere to put this infernal pile. Luckily, this mysterious hand seemed to have thought of everything, because it was just a moment before he proffered a plastic grocery bag. Relieved, Samantha scooped the papers into the bag and turned her head up for a look at her mysterious Good Samaritan. She wasn't quite prepared for exactly how far she'd have to crane her neck. He was tall, probably a good foot taller than her 5-foot-5, with messy dark brown hair and a lean, stamped out face. If she had to venture an age, she would have pegged him at a year or too older than her 31, but it was just a guess. There was something inscrutable about this very large, very mysterious man that she couldn't quite pin down. Judging from the kind but sardonic smirk on his face, he might have been only in his early 20s, but something in his eyes belied that. "You just going to stand there, or did you have a train to catch?" The words pulled Sam out of Observation Mode. It took her a moment to re-train her lips to actually form words after the sheer disaster of the last several minutes. "The way my day's going, it'll be late. Thanks, by the way." "Not a problem. Always available to help a damsel in distress." If anyone else had said it, Samantha Wade would've laughed out loud. But, there was something to this total stranger that made her believe, even through the light sarcasm, that he was sincere. "Damsel in distress, was I?" "Hey, I'm not the one who just walked headlong into a wall." "Don't knock it until you try it, pal." The stranger smiled wistfully. "Trust me, I have. Walking into walls, losing papers, it doesn't seem like today's your lucky day." Samantha rolled her eyes. If this guy only knew... "You could say something like that." "Luckily," he interjected. "I know a perfect solution for that." "And what would that be, Mr. Mysterious Stranger?" "Come have a drink with me," he noticed her hesitation and pulled back for a moment. "Don't worry, I won't bite." Sam's eyebrows rose to the point where they nearly collided with the red hair atop her forehead. "I don't normally let random subway guys buy me drinks, you know. Besides, I don't even know your name." "Best get that one out of the way, then," he said. "Name's Troy. Troy Douglas." "Samantha Wade. Call me Sam." "Well then," he said, "now that the pleasantries are out of the way, how 'bout that drink? You look like need one." "Why the hell not? The grades of the future bright minds of America can wait a couple hours." Troy smiled as Sam walked ahead, the plastic bag full of exams bobbing beside her. He didn't know why he'd offered the invitation, it certainly wasn't in his nature to buy drinks for random subway girls, either. Still, the way he was able to banter with this woman before he even knew her name was surprising. He hadn't been able to strike up that kind of a conversation since ... well, since a time he really wasn't up to thinking about right now. Then, behind Troy, a voice only he could hear came from a face only he could see. "Bad idea, boss." Troy ignored it - for now - and kept on walking towards the train. ********** "I still can't believe you're a professional wrestler." Troy smiled, picking up his near-empty bottle of Sam Adams and swirling the dregs around for a moment. "Hey, don't knock it until you try it." Sam chuckled briefly, recalling her words from just 90 minutes earlier. "I'll leave that to the big, strapping men like you, if you don't mind." "Not a problem." "Still, you don't seem to fit the mold." "There's a mold now, is there?" "Well, you're ..." "Not a brain-dead, steroid-pumped, testostorone-fueled slab of beef jerky with a barely functioning central nervous system?" Sam nearly spit out her Screwdriver in a fit of laughter, but after a few seconds and some coughing, managed to maintain most of her compsoure. "Um, not quite the language I would've used, but, spot on." Troy nodded and smiled wanly. It's not like he wasn't used to people's perceptions of what he did for a living by now. It'd been nearly 10 years, of course. "Don't worry, I'm used to the stereotypes, and trust me, I've met more than a few guys who fit that mold to a T." "And you?" She was leading him on, trying to get him to reveal some of his life. Unlike with most strangers, with Sam, he seemed to be willing to open up. "Me? Wrestling was never option one. Played a year in the NFL until I hurt my back, then I worked in TV for a little while until, well, a very long, very strange chain of chance encounters ended up with me rolling around with sweaty, half-naked men once a week on national TV." "Now that's a story I absolutely need to hear. But..." "But what?" She stood up, still smiling. "Kinda need to use the ladies' room first." "Ahh. Hurry back." "Hey, I'm aflutter with curiosity." Troy watched as the slight redhead picked her way through the crowded bar towards the restrooms, then returned to what was left of his beer. But, when he looked back to where Sam had been sitting just seconds before, there he was. Had it been a few weeks ago, Troy would have shouted in panic, knocked several glasses off the table and backed out of the bar in sheer fright. Now, all he did was flinch slightly and move on like nothing had happened. "This won't work, you know." Troy pretended to be extremely interested in the last few drops of his beer. "You can't just ignore me." "Watch me," Troy said, his voice low but clipped and forceful. He went back to scanning the room, casually trying to do anything else but confront the apparition in the same familiar black shirt and slacks plopped invisibly down in the seat next to him. The phantom only cracked a grim smile and kept up the conversation. "Chief, you haven't gotten laid in SIX AND A HALF YEARS." "Who says I'm trying to get laid?" "Maybe you don't think so, but your unconscious does. And he's looking at you right now, buddy." "Well," he hesitated for a moment, "I'm not." "Afraid the equipment doesn't work any more?" "And now with the insults." "How long until you tell her about Lauren?" Troy shuddered. Even in his hallucinatory state, that name still hadn't come across in any of his conversations. "How long until you tell her about me?" Troy set his beer back onto the table and looked back into the crowd. She was coming back. For a moment, Troy thought about telling off his tormentor, but he knew there was no need. Like always, he'd be gone when she got back. Well, not gone, but it wasn't like he'd be blocking her from sitting down. Should I really being doing this? For once, Troy Douglas decided to lay back and leave things to chance as he waved Samantha Wade back to the table. He wasn't sure what road he was heading on, but, for the first time in a long time, he felt ready to try. Hallucinations or not. --TO BE CONTINUED--
