Defaming

28 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/28/05 *CLICK* On goes the television as the opening for NBC’s Dateline plays across the screen. With a slow fade-in, the 48 Hours logo rotates into view. “This is 48 Hours. Tonight: 'Wrestling has re-emerged as a powerful force in American popular culture. At the forefront resides a company known as PRIME. But how acceptable are the wrestlers that many children are idolizing? The truth may shock you. While in the past, many wrestlers have presented characters that were violent, cruel and vicious, none of them have been as true to life as one Scott Matthews.” The view changes to a still image of Scott in the ring, leaning over the ropes with an expression of pure anger crossing his face. His arms are tense and his grip on the ropes shows white knuckles peeking out from the cut fingers of his gloves. “Scott Matthews, also known as simply Rampage, is quite possibly the best example of this ‘art’ imitating life. Presented as an unstoppable monster in the ring, Matthews executes vicious moves with unrestrained force and untrained brutality.” The shot changes to short clips of Scott in dingy gyms and halls, wrestling on the small, independent shows that were his bread and butter before signing with Rush Pro Wrestling. “It was on everyday wrestlers that were simply trying to carve their niche that this beast practiced a craft of pain and injury. Known for his staggering blows and decapitating charging attacks, known as lariats or clotheslines, Matthews made it a habit of hurting people that he got in the ring with.” Cut to a shot of a particularly nasty clothesline. The victim cuts an impressive flip on impact, looking quite impressive to those that know better. To a first-time viewer, though, it looks like Scott is doing his best to rip the man in half, and very nearly succeeding. “But what made this man into being such a beast? What would turn any human being into a walking monster?” An aerial view of a small mountain town occupies the screen. Again, to a knowing eye, this is very much NOT the Pikeville that Scott grew up in, but most of the country is quite unaware of any well-to-do places in the Kentucky mountains. “In the small town of Pikeville, Kentucky, Scott came of age in a home that one would think would be a nuturing environment. A loving mother, a veteran father and a younger sister would hardly be what one would think to give rise to such a frightening individual. They did find a way to channel their son’s aggressive energy, putting him on the football teams from grade school to high school.” New view, but basically of stock high school football. Nothing differentiates any one from another. Then a switch to Scott’s old football coach. He sits behind the same desk he’s always had, smiling as he stares off into his memories. “So Scott was quite aggressive on your team?” an off-camera voice asks him. “Oh hell yes,” the coach replied. “He was my assassin, my monster. I’d put him on the defensive line and he’d just take off around the end of the line and go in to plain kill that quarterback. He was hard to stop; once he got rolling, he’d just shrug off people trying to stop him until he hit that QB. I felt sorry for those poor boys the way Scott laid into them. I mean, we’re talking about a guy that got so pumped he put a dent in the door of the lockerroom with a headbutt.” View changes yet again, showing a darkened view of the door, overlaid with the image from earlier of Scott’s flip-inducing clothesline, frozen in mid-impact. “So what kind of person is this?” he continues in the voice-over. “An assassin that was sent to injure quaterbacks in high school football games? Yet he was given free reign to do so, and no one objected. And who would try to stop someone that wouldn’t feel any pain from headbutting a piece of solid steel? Now he’s on television in front of millions, idolized by children for his unbridled rage.” Cut to the host, illuminated only by one overhead light that casts a myriad of shadows around him. “But where did such a resivior of anger come from? The excuse would be that of his father, so we were led to believe by PRIME officials. The senior Mr. Matthews died in an work-related incident, which apparently only worsened Scott’s already-terrifying anger. So what did he do? Instead of picking up and trying to support his family, he took off in his father’s Jeep and headed out to start wrestling on small shows across the country, leaving a wake of broken bodies behind him.” Studio view of a man in a neckbrace sitting across from the host. His voice continues in narration. “James Combs, known under the ring name of Jimmy del Marco, was one of the victims of Matthews’ vicious attacks.” The man in the collar tries to nod. “Yeah, I remember. The guy hit like a freight train. It was scary to think about, and the crowd only cheered him on. It was like pouring gas on a bonfire.” Switch back to the host. “So PRIME eventually signed this beast of a man. Standing at an intimidated six and a half feet and weighing in at three hundred plus pounds, he attacks opponents with the frightening football tackle that has been dubbed the RAGE.” Short clips of Scott’s more violent RAGEs, such as his double spear on both members of Civil Disobidience and wiping out Karina Wolfenden from old RPW footage. “No one,” the narrator continued, “is safe from his violence. Opponents, women, even his own tag team partner...” The next shot is a split-screen of Scott and Vangellus Oxios holding the tag titles and the singular instance when Van ended up on the bad end of the RAGE. “PRIME officials teamed Scott up with one of their tag wrestlers in an attempt to cool his fiery temper. Despite their best efforts and even winning their tag team championship, even this partner was another victim to be knocked down.” Another shot, stock footage of amber liquid pouring into a shot glass. The narration goes on: “Imagine how badly this could be when alcohol gets involved. Several incidents of a drunken Matthews instigating brawls in various bars and clubs have been reported. These incidents have been violent to the point that PRIME has banned alcohol from all of their events, in the off-chance that this monster might get his hands on it.” Another change, to a still of Scott roaring on a PRIME entrance ramp. “PRIME has had to institute other rules as well, not allowing Scott Matthews to travel without a security escort or even walk backstage without a contingent of guards carrying tranquilizer darts and tazers. And yet, week after week, they allow him to march out on television and celebrate his angry, dangerous behavior.” *CLICK* The camera changes. Scrolling across the bottom of the CNN ticker is the simple line: 'Wrestler named Rampage implicated in attacking religious retreat.' *CLICK* 'Oh my God! I know, did you see it!' exclaimed one of four women sitting around the table. 'Yes, I did! He just punched the women like it was nothing!' another responded. 'It's completely unacceptable,' Barbara Walters interjected. 'It's just just abhorrent that they would allow a maniac like that be loose. It's horrible, not only that, but he's encouraged to do it week in and week out!' * * * Scott pulled into the parking lot. He was tired. He was strung out. There wasn't anyway he was going to get a ticket back from Lexington to Pikeville, and while the three hour drive was paltry compare to marathon sessions he'd run in the past... he was exhausted. He hadn't slept well, he couldn't sleep on the plane and everyone was eyeing him the entire trip. So he pulled in at the little diner, halfway between the house and the airport, like he had many times before. Nothing big. But in his head, all day, had been a song. He'd heard 'Turn the Page' by Seger once in the day and it had played over and over again in his head. Why were people staring at him? Why were they moving away? This had never happened before... He pushed the door in, making his way inside to sit down and enjoy a meal, something hot with meat... Every eye in the place was on him.Well you walk into a restaurant,strung out from the roadAnd you feel the eyes upon youas you're shakin' off the coldYou pretend it doesn't bother youbut you just want to explode.Scott sat down at one of the tables and returned every gaze at him. His eyes were hard as stone, staring down every person in the place that looked at him. As soon as his eyes came to them, they'd jerk away violently, frightened. He heard the television mention something that drew his attention and looked up. “PRIME has had to institute other rules as well, not allowing Scott Matthews to travel without a security escort or even walk backstage without a contingent of guards carrying tranquilizer darts and tazers. And yet, week after week, they allow him to march out on television and celebrate his angry, dangerous behavior.” Scott's eyes widened. What had brought this on? The television turned to a man seated in a chair, his long hair carefully pulled back from his face. His jaw was covered with a very well-groomed beard and he spoke very articulately and softly. 'No, this monster of a man attacked our religious retreat. We had purchased land in order to have a getaway from the members of our congregation and this man simply marched up and attacked us. After that debacle, we didn't even get to keep the land we bought.' The image switched to stills of Scott fighting members of the Cult of the Poison tree, and that's when it hit him. The man he'd just seen was the cult leader that had struck him first. Each image was carefully chosen to show Scott in a wordless roar, sometimes his hand around someone's neck, sometimes striking a woman trying to slash his eyes. But regardless of the validity... Scott stood up angrily and turned on his heel. Hell with it. Hell with it all.
