What Used to Burn (UltraViolence 2008, singles match vs. Tyler Rayne for the Five Star Title)

21 May 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 21st, 2008 Click, CLICK "I'd be puttin' yer hands on top've that counter, were I you." Little Rock, Arkansas September, 1993 The posh fellow behind the white marble counter-top stopped, his lotion-tempered and manicured hand frozen next to the small red security button that would rouse the police department and send them running to Little Rock's Embassy Suite. He lifted his hands and set them on the counter in defeat. "Smart move." Horace shifted the toothpick in his teeth to the right side of his grinning mouth, silver six shooter trained on the Embassy receptionist. Wade stood at the glass revolving doors, .357 Magnum in hand as he stood watch. "A bold move robbing an establishment like ours," said the man behind the counter, presenting an out-of-place aura of comfort with his professional blue suit. "Sure is, but I ain't the worryin' type. Fill 'er up, son." Horace tossed a white cotton sack onto the counter. The receptionist's blue eyes lifted in surprise, then turned an incredulous gaze to the rough looking man with the gun. "Playing cops and robbers, are we?" Horace smiled, only to flick his gun horizontally a few times, urging the receptionist on. He obliged, taking the sack and opening a gray lock box behind the counter, placing rubber-banded stacks of cash into the bag. Horace took a moment to glance around the building, ensuring that the three cameras, now blasted to pieces, were the only ones in vision. Moments later Horace was grabbing the bag of cash and taking casual backward steps toward the doors. "Don'tcha worry, now! I'm sure yer comp'ny's got yer money insured. Just think've us as modern day Robin Hoods!" Horace spun and stepped out the door, held by the rugged arm of Wade, who turned to throw in his two cents before leaving with his partner in crime. "Yeah, 'xcept it ain't goin' to the poor." * * * * * * * Oklahoma City 24 Hours Later "Put em' up, boys!" Three whiskeys clinked together in the dim light of a dingy tavern. The trio of Wade, Horace and Cliff sat at a small wooden table, jovial in a normally depressing atmosphere. The old bartender behind the counter shook his head as the three drained their liquor, thunking their glasses down with obnoxious laughter. "Here's to our streak've success!" said Horace, lifting a bottle of Budweiser into the air, on the verge of slurring his words. "Heres' to Wade!" chimed Cliff, his bear paw wrapping around a beer of his own, raising it to Horace's. "I dunno if yer that good at what ya do or if you're just a lucky charm, but ever since you started runnin' with us, thing's have been damn smooth!" "Good things come in threes," Wade replied, taking a swig of brew before drawing his forearm across his mouth to remove drops of beer from his goatee. "I'll drink to that," said Horace. They shared a chuckle. Wade removed his red John Deere baseball hat to run a hand over his short brown hair. As his gaze shifted upward, his keen blue eyes spotted a flash of vibrant red on the other end of the bar. He sat transfixed at the entrancing body of a red-headed girl as she finished the rest of a Rolling Rock. She carried herself toward the exit, popping a cigarette into her mouth before pushing through the ratty swinging door. "I'm goin' out fer a smoke," said Wade suddenly, the smile fading from his face. He finished his beer and tossed on his cap before pushing himself to his feet and heading for the door. "Hey Wade..." The grip from Horace's hand on his forearm stopped him. Elliott looked downward to his comrade, who looked back with knowing eyes. "Careful chasin' them legs. Go have yer fun, but keep it in yer head that we ain't stickin' 'round much longer. We head out tomorrow." Wade pulled his arm away and continued toward the exit. "And Wade..." He stopped. "Wear a rubber. Never know what kind've dirty pussy you'll run into 'round here." Wade didn't reply. He pushed through the door as Horace and Cliff chuckled at their table, stepping into the warm Oklahoma night. The sounds of traffic filled the air as he stepped into the tavern's parking lot, the pavement's light film of sand crunching under his work boots. The redhead stood on the other side of the door, leaning against the wooden building, wavy hair falling across her shoulders and a cigarette hanging from her red lips. "Evenin'," said Wade, tipping the brim of his ball cap with one hand and popping a cigarette into his mouth with the other. "Sure is," she replied sarcastically, streaming smoke out into the air. The retort caught him off guard, eliciting an eyebrow lift. He brushed it off, and lit the cigarette with his silver Zippo. "This yer gen'ral place've business?" She laughed, looking toward the ground as she shook her head. "Yeah, but it usually brings sharper conversation. Try the shoddy pick-up lines on some other bimbo." Wade let smoke billow through his goatee at the sharp remark, eyes transfixed on the rude redhead as she took another drag. "Well excuse the fuck me..." he replied, turning away. "...not like I was lookin' to take lip from some ornery cunt anyhow." The "c" word caught her attention. Her head snapped to the left, mouth agape as the future Bad Dog pulled from his Marlboro Red. "What did you just say?" she asked in disbelief, turning her body toward him. "Ya heard me," he answered, staring through the silhouettes of a row of trees lining a highway. She stared in awe for a moment, slowly shaking her head back an forth. Oddly enough, a small smile eventually crept across her mouth. "What's your name, cowboy?" "Wade. Would it be too fuckin' rude've me to ask the same?" "Jenny." "Well. Good to fuckin' meet ya." An awkward silence. Wade's cigarette slowly disappeared. Jenny flicked the butt of hers away, swiping her fingers on her overalls before taking a few steps toward the young Elliott. "Live around here?" "Just passin' through." "Really." She took a step closer. Wade turned his head, looking down at her bright blue orbs. "Sorry for bein' a bitch, I'm just not used to the no-bullshit kind of guys..." she cooed, resting a slender hand onto his broad shoulder. "It's...refreshing." "That a fact?" he said, mesmerized. "Yeah," she said, her left hand drifting down his back until finding the grip of his .357 resting under his belt. "Packin' heat, Wade?" she said lustfully. "You'll have to find out." Her tongue started drawing across her teeth, maintaining the stare as her eyes approached his back right pocket. "You'd be smart to stop right there, darlin'." He snatched her other wrist. The faux-lust dropped from her face as she quickly pulled the wallet from his pants and threw her kneecap into his groin. He released his grip and bent over in pain, the shot making him groan and knocking the wind out of him. She bolted, running along the building. Despite a terrible pain in his man-parts, Wade made a surprisingly quick chase, managing to get his big hand on her shoulder before she could sprint off very far. He wrenched her back, spun her ninety degrees and pushed her up against the tavern wall, quickly pinning her wrists to the building. She kicked, teeth grit with ferocity as he pushed up close to prevent her legs from doing any more damage. As feisty as she was, the raw brawn of the soon Southern Sparkplug held her in place as they breathed fire at each other. "Fuckin' bastard..." she snapped, out of breath, angry eyes narrowed. Wade bared fangs of his own, hot air shooting through his nostrils. She snapped her teeth at him. He managed to pull away, only to move in closer. Another attempt. Another narrow escape, moving closer still. A kiss, without resistance. * * * * * * * They shared the heavy breathing of post-fornication, their naked bodies a tangled mess in the back of Wade's red Ford Ranger, windows steamed over in an empty parking lot. "So," he huffed. "What's yer real name?" She chuckled breathlessly. "Becky." * * * * * * * "Yer makin' this an' awful pain in the ass." She kissed him deeply, straddled over him on a small blue couch in her modest apartment. "Looks like you're havin' a good time to me," was her reply, muffled through the kiss. "I ain't kiddin'," said Wade, gripping her shoulders and pulling away. "Just how long do you 'xpect me to stick around? Shit, Horace an' Cliff are ready to leave me fuckin' stranded!" She smiled, sitting up straight. "Oh, you're not leavin'." She snatched his red hat from his head, pulling it over her wavy red hair before standing up and strutting out of the room, hips swaying left and right. "Yeah? An' why's that?" "Because..." she started, disappearing through a doorway. "...a man can't leave without his hat." * * * * * * * "I ain't stayin'. I can't." "Then I'm coming with you." "You'd better think about it long an' fuckin' hard. You come with us an' life ain't gonna be no farm house. Always movin', lots've runnin'. Won't know what it's like to sleep easy." "You promise?" * * * * * * * "Becky...Becky." "Huh?" Somewhere between Milwaukee and Chicago Present Day Becky snapped out of her daze. Horace was facing her from the front seat of their Dodge Caravan, a genuinely concerned look on his face as they cruised along a highway, heading south. "I asked if you were gonna keep be able to yer head straight with all this," he said. The memories faded from her eyes. She took a moment to ponder the question, clearly directed at the long ago romance. Her gaze visibly darkened, flooding with memories less cherished as she snapped to attention. "Yeah. Snuff the fuckin' bastard."
