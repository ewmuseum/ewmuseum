The Long Ride Home

27 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/27/2006 Shawn sat there, in coach, taking it easy. It was a trip home, a chance to get back to his own bed, and he couldn't stop his thoughts from tumbling. He accepted the resulting insomnia that often came from things rattling around in his head as routine, almost. It wasn't easy for him to rest, things always shifting, changing, analyzing and thinking. There wasn't much that could slow it down, but Nicole definitely could. She was six inches shorter than his own six foot, three inch frame, but where he was layers of lean muscle, she was slim beauty. She was the balanced, incredible posture of a ballerina. He remembered meeting her four years ago, when she was playing the role of Meg in the Phantom of the Opera on Broadway. The wind had been cold and biting, whistling through the air while he and his friend Andrew had stood outside. His leather jacket had provided enough armor to cut the wind around him. The show had been very good, to Shawn's surprise. Having read the book, and hating it, he hadn't had great expectations. But then again, he wasn't entirely paying attention to the show. It was the beautiful girl in the ridiculous blond wig playing the role of Meg that had kept his attention from the third row. Andrew had known her, having gone to Juliard with her. He was an actor, and the man that had actually gotten Shawn into the grappling arts like Brazilian Jujitsu. Andrew had an affinity for Brazilian culture and the martial arts within. But that night, after the show and sitting in a TGI Friday's in Time Square, over an overpriced medium rare steak and a chicken caeser salad, they talked until they were run out into the street. She was fascinated by his stories of the state police and training FBI members, and he by her tales of performance before large crowds and holding them in the palm of her hand. She could sing and dance, he could... well, he could fight. Yet, out of that, there was some connection. It was her last show in New York, she was tired of the city, and he was enthralled by her. They dated for about a year and got married. It was her that really kept him going. She gave him that dazzling smile every time he came home, every time he got done teaching a class, every time she saw him, practically. Sure, they had their fights, but those were to be expected. Plus, they got to make up afterward. Shawn had not had much in the way of experience as a lover, only a little in college, and apparently neither had Nicole, a remarkable feat, it seemed. But he overheard her on the phone with a friend of hers. Her exact words have been: “It's like making love with a warrior. You h ave no idea how fierce he can be!” God he wanted to be home. But it was nothing he could do about it now. So he turned his mind elsewhere. He was looking at fighting three men at the Great American Nightmare. He was looking at Hessian, Vangellus Oxios and Sonny Silver. And so, in his mind, he turned the three of them over and over again, analyzing what he knew of them, considering what he thought of them, and deciding how to approach each one. He took Oxios first, perhaps the person he knew the least about. Oxios was an interesting case, given the file that Shawn kept in his carry-on bag. The man known as the Whole Freaking SHO had a physical deformity of a skull. It sounded like a rather crass statement, but it was what it was. His skull was an inch thick. So, from what Shawn could tell, he'd have to not hit Van in the head, instead aiming more for the body. The recordings of matches that Shawn could find seemed to indicate that shots to the face would affect the high-flyer, but that wasn't the most productive route to go. Shawn considered everything he could. Vangellus was a high-flyer. He was a competent striker. He'd spent time working with Scott Matthews, the wrestler that was still sequestered in Japan due to the controversy that he'd heard about with the millennium cult, called the Cult of the Poison Tree. He'd heard about that from working with some of the FBI recruits that had been sent to him for hand-to-hand training. So, Oxios had the ability to work on the mat from his time in Japan. As a former kickboxer, he knew how to handle himself in a striking situation. Shawn decided that not only was keeping to the body the best course of action, but it set up Oxios for the weakpoint in the man's armor. His neck still proved to be the weak point in his ring game. By working the body, Shawn would be able to get in close, latch on and throw Oxios with one of those suplexes he had in his repertoire and drop him on his neck. Shawn resisted the urge to default to a particular suplex, instead trying to keep his mind open so that when he got the chance to use one, he didn't pass it up in hopes of nailing the ideal. The ideal never happened in a fight, so you took what you could get. Besides, by working on the body, that robbed Oxios of the core control and leaping ability that would allow him to fly so well. Shawn didn't particularly want to deal with a high flyer that he couldn't keep locked down. Besides, it would irritate the hell out of Sonny Silver, which was a great benefit. Sonny Silver was a man Shawn had known for many years. In fact, ever since Shawn had really started on the stage he currently occupied. Silver had been promoted into Old School Fighting, and immediately picked a fight with Shawn, and they'd been at each other's throats ever since. Shawn couldn't help but finger a scar on his shoulder from one of their first encounters. Sonny had pushed a metal shelf in the back of an arena on top of him, leaving a deep, narrow slash on his shoulder. It didn't slow them down that night, not at all. But it had required stitches, which was an incredible irritant. But since those first days, Silver had improved. His ringstyle had altered a great deal in the past few years. Instead of being the idiot bumbling goof he had started out as, Silver had taken to adopting a more offensive style, a harder-hitting style. One that Shawn would continually irritate Silver by calling it an attempt to ape Shawn's fighting style. He knew better, though, largely because Silver was a wrestler first and foremost, and his striking step up his moves, as opposed to Shawn who just beat the crap out of people and then followed with whatever move seemed to be particularly fitting for the situation. Beyond that, Silver was quite successful. Sure, between the two of them, Shawn might have a win or two up on Silver, but when you're splitting ten matches with one being a no-contest, that's not saying a whole lot. That also didn't include any tag matches or matches involving more than just the two of them like the fight at Great American Nightmare would. They were very evenly matched, but while Shawn took time off to make sure injuries healed and spend time with Nicole, Silver had gone out and picked up at least one world title. That grated on Shawn, knowing that Silver had procured such a symbol of excellence while Shawn had nothing more prestigious to his name than a posthumous OSF title and possession of the retired RPW MidSouth title. So what was the best way to approach Silver? Probably the same way he always had: looking for every opening possible. Stewart and Silver were like old training partners. They had to figure out new moves, new tricks, new counters every time one of them faced the other. Silver had the advantage of being a wrestler and being better at transitions from one move to the next. Shawn had the advantage of striking harder than anyone on the roster, with more precision, with more focus, with more pain than anyone else on the roster. But not more brutality. No, as much as Shawn could claim, even his demolition of Nick Brandish didn't quite match up to the brutality of the career of Hessian. Hessian had done a great deal to earn his reputation as a destructive, frightful presence and a hallmark of brutality. Shawn knew from watching the OSW Hardcore division and even facing Hessian on the last pay per view that the big man in the match was no slouch. But as it seemed to be breaking down, Shawn would be happier to let Oxios drive Silver crazy with his high-flying antics and go after Hessian himself. Sure, in that last encounter in OSW, Shawn's heart hadn't been in the fight. Shawn was unhappy with OSW, with the dissolution of OSF, with his treatment and so on. But in RPW, it had been his oyster, and he knew, had the company lasted through the Magnum Wolf Invitational, that he would have had Hessian at his mercy. Something that he knew, now, looking back at any compilation of recordings and match footage, was that he could beat Hessian. It simply came down to the correct joint manipulation and striking. Beating Hessian would be like cutting down a tree. Hits had to be precise and pressure had to be applied at the correct points. In fact, with the match being what it was, there might even be other people working at the largest threat in the ring. Shawn smiled to himself, refraining from calling Hessian the biggest threat. Slightly egotistical as it might be, Shawn thought of himself as the 'biggest' threat in the match. But perception meant a great deal, and Hessian had the height, weight and reach advantage in this matchup. A soft jolt ran through the plane and Shawn opened eyes he hadn't realized he'd closed. Maybe he had drifted off, or perhaps he was just lost in his own thoughts. Either way, the trip was over. The plane made a gentle roll to the terminal and the King of the Cage, in all his height, extracted himself from the cramped coach seating. He popped the overhead compartment, pulled his bag down and put his carry-on around his shoulder. He headed out the front of the plane, shuffling along with the small number of passengers on this late-night flight into Bluegrass Airport. As he stepped out into the terminal, a pair of deep brown eyes locked on his. Nicole smiled at him, in her sexy jeans and hooded sweatshirt outfit, a dazzling smile that made the rest of the world seem unimportant. God he wanted to be home.
