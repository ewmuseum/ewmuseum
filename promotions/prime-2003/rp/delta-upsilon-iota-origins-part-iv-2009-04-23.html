Delta Upsilon Iota: Origins (Part IV)

23 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 23rd, 2009 Gibson’s Bar and Steakhouse Chicago, Illinois July 5th, 2003 “So did you really bring me all the way up here just to impress me with a nice lakeside view and some surf and turf? Because if so, you severely misread the old scouting report; lobster ain’t exactly my style and the only view I really enjoy is up a sorority skirt. Sorry, old timer, but the days of wining and dining blue chip talent have probably changed a little since the PCW suits took you out to the Coliseum to throw a few tridents and see a couple Christians get eaten.” “Watch yourself, boy. Maybe I ain’t nearly as old or easy going as you might think I am.” Duke Williams washes down a generous hunk of rare prime rib with half a glass of red wine, before flashing Korver a mischievous grin. “But just between you and me, the chariot races were really something else.” Duke’s faded Levi jeans and white western shirt are complemented nicely by his matching black cowboy boots and hat. Grabbing his bottle of merlot off of the table, the Malice Man quickly refills his glass, before offering some to Korver as well. “No thanks. Surprising as it may be coming from a twenty one year old college student, I’m not really a wine aficionado.” Lifting his bottle of Bud Light back to his lips, Korver empties it and sends it back to the table in rapid succession. “If slack ass doesn’t plan on getting around to our table but once every half an hour, he could at least give me a heads up to aid in the drink ordering process. Hell, I might just have to double up next round. That is, of course though, entirely up to my host.” “Fire away. (points to his wine bottle) I’m drinking the house stuff anyway. I’ve always preferred quantity over quality myself.” “Now that sounds eerily reminiscent of my sex life. Although the last time I checked, two fives still make a ten.” “You’re preaching to the choir, hoss.” Catching his server’s attention, Korver points to his empty receptacle and holds up two finders, sending the man scurrying away to the bar. Wearing khaki cargo shorts, pristinely white K-Swiss tennis shoes and a black polo shirt, Korver sits across from the Malice Man at a window side table overlooking beautiful Lake Michigan. Although the former Primetime Championship Wrestling uber-star draws the occasional glance from educated passersby, the two have gone largely unnoticed since they arrived a few minutes after two o’clock. Now, almost an hour later, they have not even begun to address the elephant that, as of this moment, still has not yet entered the room. “Sorry we didn’t get out there for the Cubbies game. I think you might have really found yourself out there with the Bleacher Bums.” “But instead I still find myself sitting here with only you at a table for three. And this is supposed to be your big pitch?” “My big pitch hasn’t arrived yet. You’ll know when he does, trust me.” “I trusted you enough to miss the annual Fourth of July Jell-O wrestling summer spectacular. Now I’m just waiting to be impressed.” As Duke looks up from his plate, his eyes catch something, something that immediately brings a proud smile to his face. With a nod of his head, he lets Korver in on the change of scenery. “Impressed yet?” Korver swivels around in his seat, directing his attention to the front of the restaurant, which has just been almost wholly engulfed by a six foot eight inch, three hundred plus pound behemoth. “Who the fuck is that monster?” “That, my friend, would be the third member of our party.” “Alright Duke, I’m on board. We’ll work out the details of this little arrangement in due time. But if you can deliver on your other promises like you have on this one, I may have just found myself a new career.” “Music to my tired, old ears, Mr. Korver.” “You know it’s funny, because this whole time I was kind of holding back. And it had nothing to do with you, I guess it was just my own misconception of wrestling. Or should I say, wrasslin’. That’s the part that really worried me, just thinking of spending half the year working in auditoriums with no air conditioning in front of a bunch of overall-wearing, tobacco-chewing, cousin-fucking rednecks makes me nauseous. As Korver finishes his mini-diatribe, a shadow suddenly passes over him, a deep clearing of the throat signaling the new arrival to the table. “Mighty pleased to make ‘yer acquaintance. ‘Reckon you must be my new pardner. The name’s Hank. Hank Cobb.” With that, he reaches his over sized hand down to the table and slowly raises the empty beer bottle to his mouth, depositing a mouthful of tobacco spit, before placing it back on the table in front of Korver. “Sorry my cousin couldn’t make it.”
