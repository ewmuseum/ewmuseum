Do I Really Have to Kill Him?

15 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/15/05 “Just what the hell is a ‘Nicaraguan Death Match’ anyhow?” asks the unmistakable, twangy voice of George W. Bush, President of the United States of America. “Beats the hell out of me,” replies Tyler Nelson. “But anything that comes from Nicaragua can’t be all that bad.” The two men are sharing a golf cart, casually cruising down the fairway of an exclusive country club. It’s a beautiful day, probably about seventy five degrees, with sunshine and barely a cloud in the sky. The President wears a red, white, and blue colored shirt, ever the patriot, and red khaki pants. He drives the cart with one hand on the wheel and the other holding onto the hand grip on the roof of the cart. He’s leaning forward in his seat, scanning from side to side in search of something. Sitting next to him, feet kicked up on the ‘hood’ of the cart and arms folded comfortably across his chest, is fellow Texan and major campaign contributor (among other things), Tyler Nelson. The CEO of Greed, INC wears a white designer polo shirt, black golf slacks, and a white sun visor. A pair of dark, aviator-style sunglasses shields his eyes from the rays of sun that bathe the landscape. The President seems to be growing frustrated as he turns the cart around and heads back in the direction they just came from. “Where in the hell is that damn ball?” he wonders aloud. “How can you lose a white ball in green grass?!” Tyler does a quick count on his fingers. “Must be fairly easy, because this is the twelfth time today and we’re only on the fifth hole.” Bush just chuckles. “I’ve never been too good at this stupid game. I wish covering up a misplaced nuke or a CIA leak was as easy as losing a little Goddamn ball.” Behind them, a secret service agent appears from the bushes and drops a ball in the rough, with a good lye and angle to the green, of course. “Your ball is up here, Mr. President!” he shouts before disappearing into the foliage once again. Tyler just shakes his head slowly, a smirk on his face, as George W. whips the cart around again. They drive up to where the secret service agent placed the ball and G.W. stomps on the carts brake, making sure it engages before getting up from his seat. The little monkey of a man adjusts himself before reaching for a club. “So about this little Nicaraguan problem you have,” The President says as he pulls a five iron from his bag. “I have her cleaning toilets and washing linens at the house,” explains Tyler. George W. Bush strides over and takes a few practice swings next to his golf ball. “I’m not talking about that bratty girl, Ty. I’m talking about the father and that match he swindled you into.” Tyler waves his hand at the President and brings his feet down from the hood of the cart, resituating himself in the seat. “He didn’t swindle me into anything, George,” says Tyler. “You know me better than that.” The President takes a swing at the ball and makes contact, but sends the ball screaming along the ground to the right and into a cluster of trees. “Mother fucker!” shouts our President, bringing a smile to Tyler’s face. “How many is that on this hole so far?” inquires Nelson, ribbing the already frustrated President. G.W. drives the club back into his golf bag and plops down in the driver’s seat of the cart, slamming his foot on the gas pedal causing both of their heads to snap back slightly. “Seems to me…” starts the President as he swerves to the right toward where his ball went, forcing Tyler to slide into him. “Christ, George!” The President continues, ignoring Tyler’s outburst as he focuses to look for his ball. “…You need to figure out just what you’ve gotten yourself into with this ‘Death Match’ thingy. I mean, what’s to say that he’s not going to have some militant Nicaraguan anti-capitalism group there popping shots at you with guns while you’re trying to teach that illegal a lesson?” George W. looks at Nelson with a dead serious expression. “It could happen. That’s how we interrogate prisoners over in Iraq, and we might have gotten some pointers from those Central American Contra’s all those years back. They’re some greasy bastards, I’ll tell ya.” The President pulls up to his ball and stops the cart a few feet behind it. He gets out and takes a look at his shot while Tyler gets up as well and heads toward a group of bushes. “What do you suggest I do then?” asks Tyler over his shoulder as he unzips his pants and begins to fertilize. Suddenly a loud “HEY!” is heard from the bushes as a secret service agent jumps out from his secure position, shaking his dripping wet leg in disgust. Tyler doesn’t give it a second thought as he continues to relieve himself. The President hacks away again, sending a worm burner toward the green but only about another one hundred fifty feet. Another profanity, another slamming of the club into the bag. “I don’t know,” sighs the President as he slides onto the seat, grabbing a sip from his bottle of water. “Why don’t you go down there and find out what the hell a ‘Nicaraguan Death Match’ is?” Tyler gives a few final shakes and then adjusts before zipping his pants back up. He turns back toward the cart and slides his sunglasses down to the tip of his nose. “Now why would I do a crazy thing like that? If Emilio Rage is any indication of the kind of filth inhabiting that country then I surely don’t want to step foot in that God forsaken place.” Tyler shudders a bit thinking of the horrors he could encounter in that third world country. “It would take me a week just to get the stench off of me.” Nelson takes his place in the passenger seat and points up toward the green where his ball rests ten feet away from the cup. “Am I ever going to get to play my second shot?” Tyler isn’t that good, folks….it’s a par three. “Look, I’ll loan you a detachment of Delta’s and some CIA support and you can head down there and find out what the deal is. If there’s one thing I know it’s to be prepared and have all the proper intelligence.” Tyler looks over at George, who looks at Tyler, and both men share a laugh at that one. “Well, I guess it couldn’t hurt to go down there and see what this is all about,” Tyler says reluctantly. “Besides, I’m up to date on all my immunizations.” ----------------------------------------------------------- Managua, Nicaragua….what a dump! I can’t believe there are still places like this that exist. It’s like this used to be hell, but the devil couldn’t even stand it so he left. The whole city is one great big ruin from an earthquake over thirty years ago. THIRTY YEARS AGO!! They just decided, ‘Oh darn, the earthquake tore everything down. Oh well, we’ll just leave all the big stone chunks lying around and go build a tin shed somewhere else.’ No one’s had a broom for thirty years?! There’s filth on top of filth for Christ’s sake! Typical lazy Mexicans. No wonder the unemployment rate is fifty percent. I’m starting to get a better idea of how Rage got how he is….he came from a whole country of losers. I can’t believe I’m here. I’m SOOO above this. My back yard is more developed and technologically advanced than this shit hole. I’m gonna have to take a bath in Purel when I get home. And burn all the clothes I wear down here. And see my therapist. Yeah, I’m long overdue for a session with Monica. Mental note: Book double sessions with Monica for the next month. ----------------------------------------------------------- Tyler Nelson walks through the Managua International Airport, looking extremely out of place in his expensive navy blue Armani suit and black shoes. The people around him, most of them probably homeless and looking for a handout, gawk and stare as Tyler quickly passes them by, not even taking time to look down his nose at them. Children rush up to him with little trinkets in their hands hoping to sell them, but Tyler just brushes them aside and then wipes his hand on his slacks. The floor of the airport is supposed to be some sort of tile but you’d never know due to the thick coating of dirt blanketing it. The poverty here is sickening to most foreigners who visit, and Tyler Nelson is no exception. “This place makes me sick,” he mutters to himself as he brushes away another young child wearing a dirty t-shirt and shorts with no shoes. “Where are those gu…ah, there they are.” The CEO cranes his neck as he spots a group of men standing near the exit of the airport. It’s a group of four men, all dressed in some color combination of khaki pants and short sleeved polo. Each has a crew cut and wears dark aviator sunglasses, giving off that military feel. One of the men extends his hand as Tyler arrives at the group. “Mr. Nelson, I presume?” He continues after a quick handshake and nod of affirmation from Tyler. “My name is Mr. Smith. These men are Mr. Johnson, Mr. Jones, and Mr. Jackson.” Mr. Smith points to each man in succession, who each give the same affirmative nod when their name is spoken. “By order of the President of the United States, we are to be at your disposal for the duration of your stay here in Nicaragua.” “I see. And what kind of credentials do you have?” asks Tyler. “If I told you, I’d have to kill you.” Tyler laughs. The soldiers don’t. “Heh heh….well..um…alrighty then.” “Suffice to say that we’ve all done some sort of operations work.” The business-like and matter-of-factness in Mr. Smith’s voice is typical military. “We understand that you have particular interest in Nicaraguan Death Match’s, so we’ve done some preliminary recon.” The four men turn in unison and begin to walk out the door. “Hey….guys,” says Tyler. “What about my luggage?” “We acquired your personal belongings before they went through customs,” says Mr. Jackson. “It’s safer that way. You never know when the local militia might pack a bomb away in your suitcase.” “Jesus Christ!” Tyler hustles up and follows the group of four closely as they exit the airport. “What in the hell did George get me into?” Then men walk toward an old, beat up, four wheel drive vehicle with dents and graffiti tattooing the body. The tan and rust color combination has no rhyme or reason, except for that it used to be white, and all of the windows are busted out. The four soldiers begin to pile into the vehicle as Tyler watches in horror. “If you think I’m going to be seen in that thing…” He points to the old Ford Bronco, his upper lip curled in disgust. The four men silently turn around and look at Tyler with stone cold serious faces, none of them saying a single word. A chill momentarily runs down Tyler’s spine as he suddenly gets the impression that he’s about an inch away from losing his life. He musters up the guts and pinches his eyes shut as he gingerly climbs into the rust bucket. He barely sits down before Mr. Smith stomps on the gas pedal, snapping his head back hard against the seat. Tyler frowns and rubs the back of his head. It’s a hot, humid night in Managua. The thick tropical air makes you sweat just standing there. Five men in a beat up Ford Bronco pull up to an old warehouse on the outskirts of town, where there seems to be some sort of event taking place judging by the noise coming from inside. As they pile out of the vehicle, each wearing a tight black t-shirt, dark green and black camouflaged pants, and black leather boots, we see that the group consists of Tyler Nelson, Mr. Smith, Mr. Johnson, Mr. Jones, and Mr. Jackson. The CEO of Greed, INC takes a moment to look over his wardrobe once again. “Hmm….I make this look good,” he oozes as he compliments himself. “Our sources tell us that this is one of the places where Nicaraguan Death Matches are held,” begins Mr. Jones. “Basically what we have here is an underground fighting ring where people try to earn money either by fighting or by betting on the fights. Fairly simplistic in design.” Tyler pauses in reflection. “So it’s like ‘Fight Club’? I hated that movie.” “Let’s go inside, sir,” says Mr. Jackson. “I think you’ll get a better idea then.” The five men enter the warehouse. The place is devoid of any type of actual warehouse fare….just an empty, dimly lit building save for the five hundred or so rabid fans, dressed basically in rags, surrounding a large steel cage-type structure. The cage is made of metal strips, about two inches wide, giving it a medieval feel. The top of the cage is curved inward, presumably to prevent escape over its walls. Tyler and his military escorts take position on top of some crates behind the crowd to better observe the action in the cage. Two men are beating the hell out of each other, much to the delight of the crowd. The noise from the yelling and screaming fans is almost deafening. Tyler leans over to yell in Mr. Smith’s ear. “This doesn’t look so bad!
