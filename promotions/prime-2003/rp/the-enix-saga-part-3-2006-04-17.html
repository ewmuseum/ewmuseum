The Enix Saga (Part 3)

17 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 17th, 2006 Post-ReVolution 90… The two sort-of-half-brothers stood on a hectic sidewalk in Japan’s business district, watching the thousands of people scurry by, faces blending together in the crowd. Because, they’re Asian, you see. And, all Asians look alike, don’tcha know? Clyde’s gaze locked on to a balloon off in the distance that just so happened to be floating towards them, the twinkle in his eyes betraying the look of disappointment on his face, while his voice trailed off into a disbelieving whisper. Walkins: We lost again, James. I can’t believe it. Farwell: I can. Maybe if you weren’t such a dick, and didn’t only think about that title of yours, we could possibly win a match sometime this year. The last time I pinned someone, Damien Cruz was still doing jack-shit. Walkins: Funny, because I just pinned yo’ momma last night! James just stood there with his mouth slightly agape, a look of pure hurt on his face. Clyde, on the other hand, forgot entirely about the balloon and instead focused his full attention on dickery of the superest kind. Farwell: My mom’s dead, you asshole. Holding his open hands up in defense, Clyde let a low whistle escape from his lips, eyebrows raised in disbelief. Walkins: Did not see that one coming. One eyebrow on James’ forehead lifted slightly, his mouth closing as the hurt turned to quasi-anger. Farwell: I’ve only lived with your family since I was five because of it. Not like you wouldn’t know by now, what with all the times you’ve made that joke already, and all the times I’ve beaten you with an oar for ten minutes straight for repeating it. Walkins: I…don’t see where this is going. But, I did see yo’ momma last night. Clyde paused for dramatic effect, grinning like a fool as James clenched his teeth and began scanning the streets of Japan for some sort of oar with which to beat the moron. Walkins: In the nude! Bam! A pelvic thrust accompanied the ‘punch line’, with Clyde holding his arms out in the air at each side. James, meanwhile, began conversing with a passing merchant. Farwell: Japan is big on fishing, right? Awaiting a response from the overly agreeable Asian, James turned back to Clyde for a second, barely catching a glimpse of the Universal Champion-slash-Stupidest Man Alive running full tilt in a vain attempt at catching the floating balloon that earlier stole his attention. James started after him, but a familiar face blocked his path, the dancing body attached to it bouncing from one foot to the other. Farwell: Outta my way, you unknown…cat…thing… Farwell shook his head, unable to muster the words needed to describe this abomination unto the Lord. Meanwhile, the Path Blocker 3000 crapped out a fortune for him. “Water splashes happy turtles. Dolphins cry, but cannot smile.” James stood for a moment, confounded as ever. Farwell: That’s stupid, Unknown Cat-Thing. Even stupider than that commercial I was in. Cut to a group of people roasting marshmallows around a campfire, with each of them also holding a glass of Bailey’s. Suddenly, the words Sense of PANIC come on the screen, as one of the girls dips her flaming marshmallow right in Farwell’s drink. With the rest of them laughing and doing the same, James just looks down at his glass in disgusted fashion. Farwell: I…I’m sorry…I can’t drink this… After five or so seconds, James tilts the glass at a precarious angle, allowing its contents to empty out over the sand around him. Back to James and Cait Si-…the Unknown Cat-Thing. Farwell: Goddamn Unknown Cat-Thing and your goddamn stupid fortunes… Unknown Cat-Thing: Why do you keep calling me that? My name isn’t Unknown Cat-Thing, it’s Cait Si- Farwell: NO! Don’t say it! I can’t afford another lawsuit. Unknown Cat-Thing: Why? What’s so terrible about my name? I love my name. Farwell: Listen, you heaping helping of useless…I’ll give you a new name; one just like the other. But, whatever you do, you cannot mention your original name ever again, nor can you reference the title or contents of our magical adventure, and even just having you in the same sentence as me is skating on thin ice. The Unknown Cat-Thing didn’t seem to fully comprehend everything, and, rather than agree to the vague conditions, he bounced from one foot to the other, struggling to understand. James sighed, for he knew he was basically dealing with a somehow-more-annoying variation of Clyde. Unknown Cat-Thing: Why can’t things just stay how they were? My old name wasn’t hurting anyone, and our adventure was only meant to bring smiles to the faces of those around us. Why has all that been taken from me?! Farwell: Because, people couldn’t figure out it was a super-long parody of a super-long game, making the idea ha-ha-larious and impervious to criticism, as well as the law. In the end, we got sued by Square twice, and again a month later by some guy named Sean. James stared far off into the distance, his eyes seeing something no one else could. Remembering something…no one else would… Farwell: That’s the real reason we had to leave Japan… A hazy glimpse of the past overtook the conversation between old adventure chums, with James looking up in the air, a noncommittal expression upon his face, leaving the Unknown Cat-Thing with a bewildered expression on his. Just before Culture Shock… Fear sat in a lump at the back of his throat, threatening to choke the very life out of him before it ever really had a chance to begin. At twenty-three years old, he was hardly a child, but there was so much in life that he had left to experience. He couldn’t even say he had yet fallen in love. This wasn’t how it was meant to end; he should’ve died an old man, surrounded by those he loved, rather than in the dingy basement of some abandoned building. He prayed for mercy, though the tears streaming down his face told him no mercy would be afforded on this day. God had no place here. Light from the outside world illuminated the darkness within the building, and he squinted to protect his eyes from the sudden flash of brightness. How long had he been trapped down here, anyway? Ten days? Twenty? Weakened by hunger and thirst, and craving the sweet embrace of familiar darkness, he sat, bound to a wooden chair in the middle of the room, mind crippled by dementia. “Think he’s been down there long enough, boss? Think he’ll talk?” To the left of a large white man stood possibly the blackest man anyone had ever seen, muscles bulging beneath an open tactical vest, with leather pants matching the biker boots on his size-16 feet; all in black, of course. His gloved hands pulled the sunglasses from his face, bald head reflecting the shine from the sun; he remained devoid of emotion while speaking, his chiseled jaw grinding his teeth together as the three entered at the same time. “No way to tell right off the bat, I’d say. Seems to me we might have to let someone do a little cuttin’.” At six-foot-six, the black man was by no means a small man, but the white man beside him stood as a near giant. The seven-footer may not have had the physique of his compatriot, but his mere presence undoubtedly struck fear into the hearts of others much faster; his long hair a jet black, with eyes as dark as night, and the typical clothing you’d expect someone raised in the South to wear. When it came to talking, he spoke with a country tone as naturally as he ever had, and sometimes it put folks off, causing them to underestimate his wit and intelligence in not just conversation, but also the fine art of persuasive intimidation. Something about the way he looked at a person could send the strongest of men running in fear, almost as if he held power over a man, crushing his will at leisure. He was their decided leader, and it had always been that way, for the others knew not to question his authority. “Ain’t that right, Carver?” His voice shook the smallest of the three out of his daydreams. As white as a ghost, one could mistake the man known as Carver for being what you might call a retard. And, if you’re one of them, shame on you. Autistic, his parents abandoned him as a child, leaving him to grow up in orphanages, where sexual abuse was more than rampant. Now, in some cases of autism, the child shows a natural talent for something. Such was not the case for Carver. Years passed by, and they abused him more, knowing he couldn’t quite understand what they were doing to him; knowing he couldn’t tell anyone, even if he did know. Then, one day, he found his special talent. They discovered the body of a young boy three days after he went missing, jammed inside an air vent, skin sliced into pieces and bones broken to fit inside the duct. Or, at least that’s what they forced themselves to believe, not willing to allow the possibility that this child - no matter how vile he was - had been forced to endure such a torture while still alive. Carver smiled for the first time in his life that first night, and again the next. He smiled now, too, sharpening the blades in his hands against each other, licking his lips and sniffing the air. Tasting his fear. The man trembled, sweat dripping off his face, mixing with the tears and saliva forming a puddle at his feet. Carver began to breathe rapidly. “Now, now, dear boy. Don’t be gettin’ yourself all riled up just yet.” The tall man turned his head and spit on the floor beside him. “We need to have ourselves a little chat, first.” “Why are you doing this to me?!” The young man in the chair cried, struggling to free himself. “I haven’t done anything wrong!” His head slumped forward, girlish cries and gurgles escaping his lips. The lone black guy in the room (he’s gonna get killed first, I just know it) lunged forward, pulling the man’s head back up, shouting in his face. “You know damn well what you did!” He reached into his back pocket and removed a few pieces of white paper stapled together, waving it in the guy’s face. “You’ve violated Square Enix’s TOS, bitch! You’re gonna pay!” The young man recoiled in fear, then wrinkled his brow in confusion. “Wait…you guys brought me here about that? All I did was write a fucking FFVII fan-fic, and you locked me in the goddamn basement for a week?! I’m calling my lawyer...this is bullshit.” He waited a second, then moved his head forward when nothing happened. “Untie me, you jackasses!” Crack. The black guy shook off his right hand, while the young man in the chair slumped to the side and spit out a gob of blood on the floor. “Carver, you take care of him. Make it last a while, too.” The first to walk out of the building, the black man put his sunglasses back on as he once again entered the light of day, followed closely by their leader, as screams of pain soon came after. “Think giving him a few hours will do?” “Think it will do just fine.” The two men turned, the leader entering the passenger side, and the black man getting in to drive their Squaremobile back to headquarters, where they would learn of their next mission. Cati Shit: Wait…how do you know all that happened, when you weren’t even there? And, what’d you do to my name? It’s awful! Farwell: It’s called setting the mood, you moving pile of defective genetic material. Fuck you, and fuck Sean! To be concluded…eventually…
