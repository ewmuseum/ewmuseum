Fear Malachi. Don't Fear Winters.

8 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 8, 2007 Fear can be easily read in a person. Not only can it be read by your facial features and the way you hold yourself, but most importantly it can be read in the eyes. It’s a certain something that people give off as if there very soul is being sucked out of them. Many people scoff at the idea that the eyes are the gateway to your soul, but honestly it’s the truth. The eyes can’t lie. Every other part of your body can. You can spew lies, you can fake anybody out with the way you conduct yourself, but the eyes never lie. They’re incapable of doing so. It’s the amygdale that allows us to perceive fear. Our brain tells us to look at the eyes of a person and the brain does the rest for us. Whether it is out of anxiety, terror, or fright, the brain just knows. The boy was frightened. It was a dead give away. His lips were stretched horizontally so that they looked thinner then elderly women who have had years of cosmetic surgery and botox on their faces. His bushy brown eyebrows were drawn together almost as if he was trying to imitate Peter Gallagher. But, it was his eyes that told it all. His soft blue eyes were widened and dilated. That much was certain as I looked at his eyes, and he was scared for a variety of reasons. One of them being the fact that he was surrounded by several large men. The second reason was the fact that he wasn’t with his mother. The boy couldn’t be older than six and at that age, separation from the mother would cause terror to set in a fashion that would scar the boy for life. The third reason? Well, it was New Jersey after all. You figure it out. I had to do something. With my brown wavy hair tied up into a ponytail, I was unidentifiable to most people. They looked at me, standing there in a pair of khaki pants and a black shirt with a black overcoat, and it didn’t immediately hit them who I was. Down the road, they would recognize me in their mind’s eye and it would hit them like a sack of bricks. These guys had not the slightest clue as to who I was. Yet, I hadn’t the slightest clue as to what the situation was. Why were these men with this boy? Was it something that I should even involve myself in? All of these questions continued to pound inside of my head. Yet, it was the fear of the boy that worried me the most. I wasn’t well aware of the situation, but it was my job as savior to handle the situation. The boy was my only link to the woman with the eyes that were familiar. I had to find her. Apparently, I also had to save the boy while I was at it. As I stood there, the last of the pack walked past me. I had counted four of them. They looked to be rather large Italian men though they were all shorter then me. Just bulkier. I wondered if I could take them. I was God, but I wasn’t unstoppable. I had learned that much thus far. “Hey…” the words escaped out of my mouth without me even thinking about it. The last guy in the group stopped and looked back at me with beady little brown eyes. If stares could cut through a person, then I would be lying there in the middle of the street bleeding to death. The man looked rather upset that I had even spoken to him as if he was God. Unfortunately for him, he was looking at God. The rest of the group, noticing what was going on, stopped and turned back towards me as well. The boy just stood there, the fear getting worse by the second. The boy didn’t want to be with these men. He shouldn’t have to be. I had to see to it that he wasn’t frightened by these men any longer. “You want somethin’?” the man said back to me, trying to intimidate me with his low bass voice. It wasn’t working as I just looked at him in the eyes before walking up to him. He didn’t dare budge. If he showed the slightest bit of fear, his boys wouldn’t only chew him up, but he would be giving a rather large tell to me. He couldn’t afford to do any such thing. Instead, he stood his ground though he saw he was easily six inches shorter than me. To a boy, he was frightening. To me, he looked like dessert. “Why’s the boy frightened?” I asked him sternly. The man, realizing that he didn’t want to waste his time on me, started to turn from me. Instead of letting go so easily, I grabbed his shoulder and cocked him back towards me. I knew what this would do. It would upset him enough to take a swing at me. I telegraphed it coming from a mile away as I ducked with plenty of time to spare before slamming my fist into his stomach. I could hear the air expel from his mouth before I decked him right in the face with a right hook that I felt up through my arm. Not pain, but success. The man’s body slowly slumped to the ground as I looked at his other three buddies. They were cut from the same rug as him. They were fighters. Hired bodyguards. They hadn’t stepped into a fight with somebody the likes of me. The next closest guy to me, just a few feet to the left of me started to reach into his jacket. I was not interested in turning this into a blood bath. Instead, I wanted this to end quickly. Acting purely out of instinct, I slammed by elbow right into the jaw of the man. You could hear the bones breaking across the street as he went down in a world of hurt. The boy just looked on as if I was a superhero here to rescue him. With each moment now, I could see the fear leaving his body as he could feel his freedom coming. As I had slammed my elbow into the guy’s glass jaw, the two other guys decided to take me out before it was too late. They were able to sneak up on me before I had a chance to react. The smaller of the two men came up behind me and locked me into a full nelson. I struggled to the best of my abilities, but the little fucker had it on tight. Before I knew it, he had turned me towards his buddy who was ready to slam his fist into my gut with such intensity. The pain rocked me hard, but I had to stay focused. I looked at the little boy who just seconds ago had thought his freedom was coming. I couldn’t let him down. Not now. Before I could even react, the guy using me as a punching bag had nailed another body shot on me. This guy looked to be the fighter of the group. Obviously he had grown up as a boxer. Thankfully, I had grown up as God. Without a moment’s hesitation, I cocked my head back right into the other guy involved who was holding me in the full nelson. My head slammed back hard enough to break his nose. I could feel the blood gushing out of his nose as he released the hold on me. All it took me was a second as I dropped low to the ground and nailed him with a leg sweep that resulted in his head slamming into the cold concrete. He would live unfortunately. I was down to one guy left who was still in a boxing stance. He tried to hit me with a right cross, but I wasn’t having any of it. I needed him to be conscious and able to listen as well as talk. I grabbed his fist and tossed him down to the ground. My eyes caught a fleeting look at the boy as if the bonds had finally been broken on him. I couldn’t lose focus though as I turned my attention back to the final bodyguard and immediately pressed my knee up against his throat. “You get to be relatively pain free because I need you to relay a message to your boss here. You tell him I want a meeting with him. He can find me at the Hampton’s Inn, room 400. Any of you think about trying to ambush me and I will leave dead bodies. Do you understand?” I asked him knowing he wouldn’t respond to me. He knew the message. He would give it to his boss. Then, I would be able to reunite this family while getting my questions answered. As I looked into his eyes, I knew he wouldn’t get up once I got off of him. I spared him. He owed me one. I pulled myself up off the man and looked at the boy. He was still a little taken aback as he finally realized that he wasn’t entirely free as he didn’t know this man either. Yet, I had looked out for him. The boy understood that much. He just didn’t know why. I slowly walked up to him and kneeled before him. “My name is Malachi. I know your mom,” I told him, slightly lying, but for the most part it was the truth. I had met his mom, but we really never got a chance to talk. Yet, I felt a long talk coming with her. Those eyes. They were like something else. “Want to come with me? We’ll be seeing her soon,” I told him and he just nodded. I smiled at home, hoping to assuage his fears. It was no use. He had been damaged psychologically. The least I could was get him back with his mother. I didn’t know what to do with him so I just started walking. A group had started to arrive and look at the carnage I had caused. I looked all of them in the eyes, putting the fear of God in them. They would freeze momentarily, giving me a chance to get out of here with the boy while getting a good start on the police. The men though would be gone before the police ever arrived. They would see to that. I just didn’t know who they were quite yet. Time would tell though. Time would tell me more than I had ever imagined. ---- An excerpt from the upcoming book, The World Through The Eyes of God, penned by Malachi. To be released in Winter 2007.
