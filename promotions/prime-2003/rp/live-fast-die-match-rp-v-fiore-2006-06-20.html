Live Fast, Die (Match RP - v Fiore)

20 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 20/06/06 Live Fast, Die.Written: 20th June, 2006. 'You burn like a bouncing cigarette on the road,All sparks... will burn out... in the end.' - Editors. I Sunday, 21st December 2042. The day I, Karina Marie Wolfenden, will die. It said so on the internet... so it must be true. Sibling rivalry naturally lead to checking my brother's 'best before' date, and, true to form, he'll be knocking around for 22 years after my ashes are scattered. Better grades, far less nagging about the direction of his life, most likely a larger slice of inheritance, and now he gets to trump me at living. As if there was anything else left he could get one up on me at. Refusing to accept the defeat, I'd switched my mode from pessimistic to optimistic. The page 'could not be displayed'. Computers really are getting smarter. Fast-forward. It's Tuesday morning. In the world beneath my window, the morning rush has long passed, and carried the rumbustious din away on its frantic footsteps. But as the walking workforce and morning commuters pass over the horizon, the sun rises to shine a little light upon their day. Already it's too muggy to go back to sleep, even with what remains of the sheets left crumpled around my ankles. A driver outside decides the solution to all his problems is to thump down on his horn. I wish him all manner of gruesome deaths, involving lava pits, eternal festering damnation, and Orson. Like a good girl, I've gotten my 8 hours sleep. My reactions should be sharper, and my hand-eye coordination unaffected. My body has its own freshly brewed take on biological caffeine ready to pour, perking me up. All this will reduce the chances of being involved in a fatigue-induced accident, dry, tired eyes glued shut by sleep at the wheel. I mentally add a couple of extra years for that. Now I'm only twenty behind. Despite the balcony doors opening themselves up to the morning, there's barely enough breeze to ripple the flimsy curtains, and the sweet odour of petroleum lingers in the air... like the chemical after-taste of tap-water after going on an Evian-purge. Through the doors, cloud hangs low, insulating, smothering the city like a pillow over the face. I knock the sleep-bonus years back off for the pollutants that I wallow in as a city dweller. Suddenly stretching out, I feel the mild-headrush. A rush free of the sour ash remnants of the first cigarette of the day that I haven't inhaled since I was 17. I knock another year off for all the stifled, throaty coughs, and putrid mouthwashing I endured for those retarded teenage months. Thanks, peer pressure. Let the side down, why don'tcha. Clocking the digital display at my bedside, my sleep registered at nearly 10 hours. Fuckleshucks. I knock off a good three years for the increased risk of diabetes that regularly lazing for over 9 hours a night brings. It's too early for me to keep keeping count. Standing, I'm suddenly anchored by the throbbing anvil that has become my head. Things were a lot more soothing when horizontal. But while it's my pounding skull that's playing reluctant host to my hangover, it's my liver that's suffering in silence. The recommended weekly intake of units is another rule made to be broken... a fate which I pray to whichever Gods will have me won't happen to my dialysis machine should I knock back a few too many for the kidneys, and fail my renal mid-term. I slash another 5 from my life expectancy, and start to wonder if making plans for next week was a little optimistic. But, on this biological tax return, there are more add-backs. The crisp, cold, syrupy Vodka was a quiet little child, never causing anyone's head any harm until a bottle of red wine corrupted him. A glass or two is said to be good for the heart. 6 must be akin to a defibrillator. Score another 2 for the long-life team. Minimalist tastes give me an uninterrupted path to stagger along as I gravitate towards the kitchen. Breakfast comes in vibrant liquid form. I tip my head back, and pour a few glasses worth of orange juice down my neck. The benefits go beyond saving on washing up... especially glasses that are prone to streaks. My immune system receives its late-morning wake-up call as folate seeps into my system, silent, yet diligently guarding me against Alzheimer’s. The anti-oxidants wage atomic war within me, going shock-and-awe against cancer and heart-disease. Cold and flu viruses break rank and flee as the Vitamin C brigade gallop into their cavalry charge through my blood vessels, supported by Vitamin B6 which keeps the white blood cell production line free from sabotage. As they rout the enemies within, Potassium and Magnesium dilate the arteries around them, reducing the pressure put on their plasma carrier. Wisely, Sodium beats a hasty retreat in the face of such might. All that from a carton, gulped down by a woman too lazy to put the coffee-maker on, or pour a bowl of cereal. I think that's all worth another five years of my life. So long as I replace the empty carton that's tossed over my shoulder and into the bin. Reacting to the sudden life my awakening has given the apartment, Betsy hops up onto the counter, then struts her sleek, feline swagger down towards me. Despite the tickling of her tail as it brushes my nose, I add another 2 years of relieved stress by pandering to her selfish demands for attention. Pets taking after owners. Owners taking after pets. With an attention span to rival even mine expired, she hops down, while I begin to medicate. Where others have salt, sugar, or a spice-rack, I have a row of bottles and cartons. A pill for every occasion. Alpha Lipoic Acid begins the proceedings, regulating blood sugar, and in turn, reversing the signs of ageing. Although odds are the sip of water that ushers it down does a better job of the latter. Calcium supplements follow, fortifying my hasty defences against osteoporosis that took damage during a stubborn vegan phase in my early teens. Thus far, they've warded off shin-splints... sadly they do little for muscle tears and ligament strains. Vitamin D kicks it into overdrive. Hemp oil lubricates the circulation, while Devil's Claw does the same for my joints. Siberian Ginseng prepares me for the day, aiding the release of natural energy, loosening muscles before I even begin stretching. Unwinding, unlocking me. The glass of water rounds the routine off. Good for the complexion. As each pill drops down into my stomach, a ping racks up another year on the life clock. And it's still less hassle than keeping an optimistic outlook. 5 minutes later, my skeleton rattles as I descend stair after stair, the bounce of my sneakers' sole negated by the speed at which I pound each step, refusing to allow gravity to simply cradle me down to the ground. Slipping earphones in, Overseer help jack-up my pulse as hit a new low of lame in listening to my own entrance theme. When they drop, they go ballistic, you know? Despite the mass of supplements flooding my blood stream, this is what makes me feel like I'm pushing the boundaries of my life to stretching point. I'm born to run. At a natural state in kinesis. Thumping heart-beat purges cholesterol, burns fat in an inferno of intent, and puts strained muscles through tough-love, making them stronger, faster, better. With the perspiration being forced out through my pores, worries of depression and mental illness are washed clean. But, sometimes, too many worries can be shed. Even through the pumping techno that fuels me, I hear the furious car horn lead in from the screech of tyres. My threading the needle of the traffic showing that it's not best to plot such a path in the blink of an eye... and only looking one way. Holding up an accepting palm, I make off again as my window is about to slam shut. One leap forward, one shimmy back. We'll net it out at about 8 years. It doesn't take me long to cancel out my tendency for running out into the middle of busy traffic. Something most children learn is beneficial at an early age. Around me, CCTV cameras could give me multi-angled playback of my run, while a visible police presence makes us all feel that little more snug when we tuck ourselves in at night. Being a white, middle-class (not to mention, well-insured) woman in a recognised 'good, safe' area works wonders for the life expectancy. A satisfying cross is pencilled through 'reckless around fast-moving things that will kill you'. Above me, the sun has made its last grasp at the sky's pinnacle, and drags itself to its rightful spot above us all. The cloud that crowded and coddled us before has been burned away before any of us realised, leaving a sun so fierce that blue sky could easily be imagined dripping down onto the sidewalk, molten. Despite being all transitory, never still, I can't run through the ferocity of its rays, as the sweat almost begins to boil dry off my skin. Never one to bother with sunscreen, despite fair skin, I goad the ultraviolet rays into taking their best shot. Mild sunburn damages the cells near my skin enough to divert blood flow, reddening like a Lobster dropped in the pot. All the my efforts in keeping a youthful complexion begin to crackle away under the harsh glare of the photo-aging heavenly body. Factor in (much later than I should be thinking about which factor to apply, admittedly) the likelihood that I'd drag my heels in having any mole checked out, and you can wind the expiry-date clock back 5 years. Verging on collapse as I stumble through the doors to my building, my left knee is once again feeling as if it's offered sanctuary to every sharp, burning thing within the same postal district. My right shoulder, which has been tightening up since I took a bad landing, can't muster enough discomfort to drown it out even a little, while the heavy strapping on my wrist couldn't manage the impossible and stop its rattling discomfort as I reached full-stride. I take the elevator back up, barely able to stand as my body harshly slaps me around for pushing it too far, once again. With teeth grinding and body tense, unable to wind down, my breaths are short and sharp, rather than the long, deep heaves I need to stave off lactic acid. Staggering through my apartment's door, I make a beeline for the couch, tumbling over its back as my joints snap into place, nothing left to unlock them. Right where they were yesterday, and the day before, and the day before that, sits a bottle of painkillers... in the shadow of a glass of water filled just before I set off. With little of the gusto with which I went about my earlier routine, a shaking hand drops the pill onto my tongue, where it almost tumbles down onto and over my bottom lip. A gulp of spilling water washes it, and its neighbour in the bottle, down before they have a chance to make another attempted escape. In a little while, my pain will ease, as it always does when I need to shortcut my body's self-defence mechanisms. As the agony subsides, it's countered by a rise in the risk of a stroke, or other serious vascular problems... all but wiping out the benefits of exercise. The asset equalling the liability. As soon as my body can take the strain, and not collapse in on itself like a beached whale, I hobble to the shower, where I douse muscles and joints with water just hovering below scalding point. Stiffness melts, and I become pliable once more. Chemically perked, basking in blissful, medicated ignorance, I fling my bag over what an hour ago was my sore shoulder, and head off towards my day job. My ticket is economy. 34 inches of leg room. Increased chance of deep vein thrombosis, a blood clot conceived in the leg, fatally born in the lungs. I'm meant to die on Sunday, 21st December 2042. But, given the complexities of life, I won't be marking it down in my diary. Not that I'm complaining about the lack of predictability... after all, the fun is in not knowing.
