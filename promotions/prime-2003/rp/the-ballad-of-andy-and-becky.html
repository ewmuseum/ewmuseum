Date: 4/16/98, 6/14/04, 2/4/08 OOC: Part 1 of 4 connected but not sucessive roleplays. ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- *The Ballad of Andy and Becky* ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- If you just walked away What could I really say? Would it matter anyway? Would it change how you feel? I am the mess you chose The closet you can not close The devil in you I suppose Cause the wounds never heal But everything changes If I could Turn back the years If you could Learn to forgive me Then I could learn to feel... ==================================================== April 16th, 1998 As long as Andrew Rossi lives, he'll never forget this day. He might get old and senile and forget where he lives and even his own name, but he'll never forget what happened on April 16th, nineteen ninety-eight. It's the day he met Becky. The love of his life...the only girl he'd ever marry and have it actually mean something. He was on his way to the gym with his old buddy Carlo Santini, currently a professional bodybuilder. Carlo was driving. Carlo: Whoa, you see that girl over there? Andrew was looking at his now archaic cell-phone, not paying much attention. It's funny how things like this happen on accident, isn't it? It's like you blink...and it's gone. Think of how many times bullshit winds up distracting you. Who knows what could have happened if you'd had your mind focused on what really matters? Rossi: What's that? SB asked him without much thought. He had a new high score in snake! Carlo shook his head and pointed out Rossi's window to a girl sitting at a small outdoor cafe, reading the days newspaper. She had a small cup of espresso on her plate, and her legs were crossed. Carlo: Her! Right there! Rossi rolled his eys, flipped his phone closed, and glanced out his window. He was instantly mesmerized. Becky wasn't "hot". Hot's not the word you use to describe the woman you love. She was beautiful. Breath-taking. All the words they use in the movies rolled into one. She was perfect. He couldn't stop looking at her eyes; two small pools of clear blue ocean water. They locked eyes for just a moment, and he quickly turned away and flipped his phone back open to pretend her was doing something else. Carlo: What the fuck are you doin'? Carlo nearly shouted it at him. Rossi: Drive around again and drop me off, Horse. Horse was Carlo's nickname in high school, but Andrew was the only one of their buddies who still called him that. He STILL calls him Horse today, actually. Carlo: No way! I saw her first! Rossi: Just do it man. Carlo: Ah, fuck it. Are you still gonna come to the gym later? Rossi: I'll let ya know. Carlo made a sharp turn at the next light, almost taking out a bike messenger. He made another quick right and turned to look at Rossi Carlo: You think the Yanks'll make it back to the Series this year? Rossi: We got Jeter, don't we? Carlo nods. He fidgets a little bit. Carlo: Y'know, if she don't like ya... Rossi: Just do me a favor, Carlo. You owe me. Carlo: Bullshit! Rossi: Janie Hostler? Carlo: Bah vonguole! All right, this is your stop ya bastard. Carlo screechd to a halt at the end of the street, and Rossi nearly jumped out of the car before he parked it. Carlo: Good luck, Casanova. Rossi: (calling over his shoulder) Won't need it, Horse! Andrew was running at this point, pushing past people in his way. He stopped and moved aside politely in front of a mother pushing her child in a stroller, and then took off running again. He came upon the cafe, but he didn't see her. In fact, he didn't see anyone. He looked to his right and saw a sign that nearly broke his heart. CLOSED Dejected, he just shook his head, slumped his shoulders forward, and dug into his pockets for his phone. Then, he heard a voice from behind... Voice: You lookin' for me? Rossi slowly turned around, and smiled. It was her! Rossi: What makes you think that? Maybe I'm just really hungry? Becky: I saw you looking at me..what happened to your friend? Rossi: Who, Carlo? He crashed into a tree! That's why I came here, so you could help me? He said with that smile he's so well known for, and she knew it was a joke. But Rossi would be damned if he still wasn't nervous as hell - and he never gets gets nervous. Something about her made him comfortable and anxious at the same time. She started walking over to him, and he could feel the sweat rolling down the back of his neck. Becky: Have I seen you before? Rossi: Uh..you go to the wrestling shows in the city? Becky: No, can't say I do. Isn't that just an excuse for a bunch of sweaty guys to touch each other? Rossi: Whoa whoa whoa, missy. You callin' me gay? Becky: That's funny...I didn't say "gay". That explains a lot, though. She turned to walk away, and Rossi started after her. Rossi: Hey, let me take you out tomorrow night! Becky turned back around, and looked into SB's eyes. Becky: I've heard stories about guys like you before. You know, the macho pro wrestler guys. Rossi: Well, maybe you heard I'm gonna be a big ol' star one day, huh? You have seen me at a show, haven't you? Becky: I took my brother to one. He wanted the WWF, but I didn't know the difference. I don't think he did either, he's only seven. Rossi: So...who told you guys like me are no good? Not that I'm the guy you're thinkin' of. He flashes a smile, but Becky doesn't return it. Becky: My uncle was a professional wrestler. Rossi: No shit? Becky smiles and furrows her brow. Becky: Yeah - he told me never to fall in love with one Rossi: Yeah? Why's that? Becky: Cause he's always on the road, and he'll break your heart if you let him. Rossi: That's all right, I'd take ya with me everywhere I went! Besides, I'd never break your heart, Cupcake. He and Becky shared a smile. She giggled a little bit. Becky: We'll see...meet me here at eight tomorrow. Rossi: Where's here? Becky pulled a pen from her purse, and scribbled down her number and the name of a fancy French restuarant on the back of a business card. Becky: Here. She gave him the card, and SB carefully tucked it away in his pocket like he just found the golden ticket. It took all he had to keep from snatching it in anticipation A car pulls up beside them beeps the horn twice. Becky: Oh, that's my sister. I'll see you tomorrow, right? Rossi: Yeah, tomorrow. She waved back at him as she got in the car. Andrew stood there for a long while after it pulled away, just thinking to himself. He didn't even call Carlo for a ride - he walked home that afternoon. ==================================================== Wouldn't it be nice if we could wake up In the morning when the day is new? And after having spent the day together Hold each other close the whole night through Happy times together we've been spending I wish that every kiss was never-ending Wouldn't it be nice? ==================================================== June 14th, 2004 - Andrew Rossi's NYC apartment Becky was lying there on the bed, still wrapped up her dreams. Andrew Rossi looked up at the clock from his seat at the edge of the bed. 8:00 AM. 'Pretty soon it's time to get up, get out, and get to work', he thought to himself. 'From nine 'til five he'll drive that God damn delivery truck, and from six to ten it's on to Angelo's'. He had big dreams, big goals, and a big enough heart to get all of it...but he knew it would take a whole hell of a lot of work. Some nights he didn't get home until after Becky was sleeping. Weekends he had local shows, and if he got really lucky he could get booked out of state. He was winning a lot, but not enough to get noticed - yet. Becky hated it. Over the years, she came to realize her uncle was right. I mean, come on! They'd been engaged three years! THREE! Who stays engaged that long? NOBODY. It's not like either of their families couldn't afford the wedding, and SB was only working that stupid delivery job so he didn't have to work for his father. She hardly ever saw him, and she couldn't imagine raising a family all on her own...so why was she still with him? Because she just loved that goofy, lovable, Italian bastard. He didn't know it, and probably still doesn't, but she left him twice and both times turned around and made it back to the city before he got home from the gym. She just couldn't walk away from the last six years of her life, as tough as they'd been on both of them. The fights got real bad sometimes, and the promises got broken as often as they were made. SB's temper had gotten better, she'd admitted to herself, but damnit if he didn't still have one short fuse everytime she mentioned giving up wrestling and going into work for his father. 'I'd rather die than work for that bastard!', he screamed at her. He grabbed her by the wrist once...and then put his whole right arm through the wall on his way out. That was the longest they stayed broken up, those two weeks in between him losing it and coming back home. They've been through hell and back, and Andrew knew it. It made them stronger. It made them closer. It made the love they shared that much more passionate, but it was killing them at the same time. He knew he had to get the hell out of this apartment and prove everybody wrong - even himself. That's been his curse ever since; he's always trying to prove himself to everyone. Andrew turned around to look at Becky, a warm smile curling up on his face. His eyes welled up a little...'She's the sweetest thing on this Earth', he told himself. And she was. She was good for him. She kept him focused, kept him balanced. And she backed him up as fiercely as they fought and she loved him more than he ever understood. Sometimes he got jealous because he didn't know how to love her as much as she loved him. But he did, even if he didn't realize it. He was a good man, he just had some flaws like any other twenty-four year old kid. She was the only one who could ever make him see that for himself. He needed her, and she needed him. She let out of a soft sigh as she slept, and Andrew brushed her blonde hair away from her face and kissed her gently on the forehead, careful not to wake her before she had to get up for work an hour and a half from now. He eased his way off the bed, slipped on his shoes, and pulled a brown UPS hat over his head. A bagel popped up out of the toaster, and he grabbed it on his way out the door... ==================================================== Blackbird singing in the dead of night Take these broken wings and learn to fly All your life, you were only waiting For this moment for this moment to arrive Blackbird singing in the dead of night Take these sunken eyes and learn to see All your life, you were only waiting For this moment to be free... ==================================================== February 4th, 2008 Miggy Harper's "Talk of the Town" was well known in Staten Island as kind of a shithole. OK, it is a shithole. The patronage was mostly forty-something businessmen who hated their jobs, middle-aged biker chicks, and neighborhood kids who just turned twenty-one, many of whom wound up sleeping with the aforementioned biker skanks. Everyone's gotta learn their lesson the hard way, apparently. Andrew Rossi was in town for the night, in between A1E and EPW shows. His birthday was the following week, and he decided to stop in for a drink. He didn't look so good, to be honest. If you saw him there, you'd have probably called him a cab. But you wouldn't be drinking in a dump like the TotT, right? Of course not. As Tracy Chapman's "Fast Car" played on the jukebox, Rossi sunk another Heineken Light, his new beer of choice at the time. He was ten in...or was it twelve? Nah, it must've been ten, right? The bartender kept cleaning the counter and Andrew had lost count of his empties. A man walked in and sat beside him. Rossi didn't give the guy a second look, but he'd never seen him before. Man: What are you drinking, friend? The man had an English accent. Rossi didn't like him already. Rossi: I'm good on drinks. Man: Fine with me. Barkeep, a Gordon and tonic on the rocks. Bartender: Sorry, we don't have any Gordon's. How about some nice American gin? Man (smiling): They make those? That'll be fine, sir. The bartender laughed and poured the drink. He slid it to the Englishman, and as he reached out with his left hand to grab it Andrew saw the wedding band on his ring finger. Rossi: You married? The Englishman took a sip, breathed in sharply at the strong kick, and nodded. Man: Twenty-five years. Rossi nodded slowly, and took a big swig of his beer. Rossi: Yeah...I was supposed to get married once. Work got in the way... Man (laughing): Well, consider yourself one of the lucky ones. Rossi: Yeah, right. Andrew took a long, hard look at the ring on the man's finger. Becky's bright, blue eyes flashed in front of him before he passed out face first on the bar.
