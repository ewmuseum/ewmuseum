Pulaski Day.....

2 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/02/05 This is a special day for Walczak. This is the one day a year that he has no previous engagements, and no matter what the situation he does not miss this day. It is none other than Pulaski Day. It is a celebration of Polish heritage and the great Polish General, Kazmierz Pulaski. Each year Walczak makes sure that he is in New York to watch the Pulaski Day Parade, and to join all other proud Polish people in celebrating who they are. The parade begins at St. Patrick’s Cathedral in Manhattan, and Walczak arrives there with plenty of time before the parade starts. He is not dressed in his normal street clothes but is instead dressed in all red and white. He has a red do-rag, a white t-shirt with a Polish flag printed on the back, red sweat-pants, and white Timberland boots all to represent his home country. There is nothing better than this day. It is a celebration of my people. A celebration of all that is good in the world, and a day that we show the world that we are not to be forgotten. Walczak walks towards the entrance of the Cathedral. The cathedral is nearly empty and Walczak gets a good seat for the opening ceremonies. This year Walczak is looking forward to the opening ceremonies even more than the previous years. That is because this year the ceremonies will be run by Lech Walesa, the man who started the uprising known as Solidarnosc. This movement allowed Poland to free itself from the grasps of Communist Russia, and set a precedent for all other countries that followed. He is a personal hero of Walczak’s and has inspired Walczak throughout his life. I cannot wait to see the great Lech Walesa speak. This man turned the fortunes of Poland around and made them into a country that it’s citizens are proud of. I hope to one day bring as much joy and pride to the homeland as he has. If the United States had a leader that was half the man that Walesa was than they would not be facing the problems they have in the past couple of years. Slowly the cathedral starts to fill up as more and more people file through the doors. Polish flags are seen throughout the cathedral, and tension builds as the crowd awaits the arrival of Walesa. Suddenly a loud boom erupts in the cathedral and Walesa makes his way down the aisle. We see the look on the face of Walczak, and it is as if he is a fifteen year old girl at a Justin Timberlake concert. He screams along with the rest of the crowd as Walesa waves to all people present. Walesa finally reaches the front of the cathedral and motions his hand for the crowd to silence. “Jak my sie mamy dziszaj?” His translator tries to speak amidst the noise that erupted after his first words. “How are we today?” It feels as if he is actually speaking to me. I did not believe that this day would ever come. My hero of so many years is literally feet away from me. The Joy is clearly visible on Walczak’s face, and he eagerly pays attention to the words that Walesa says. “Many people have forgotten exactly who the Polish people are. Especially in this country. This holiday celebrates a man, General Kazmierz Pulaski, who played an integral part in helping the American people free themselves from the tyrannical hold of the English empire. However, it is only acknowledged by the Polish people themselves, and not by the people who benefited the most from his help. To me that is a sign of blatant disrespect, but they will continue to do it until the Polish people take more pride in who they are.” That is what I have been trying to say for years now. I hope that now everyone in here will take Lech’s words to heart and follow what he has said. “St. Patrick’s day is a day that is celebrated by people throughout the country whether they are Irish or not. That is because of how proud the Irish are of themselves. I hope that some day the Polish people will show this much pride in their country and that Pulaski Day will be a day unmatched by even St. Patrick’s day.” The crowd goes crazy as Walesa finishes his short speech. He is escorted out by security, and makes his way to his float in the parade. The Cathedral starts to file out as people take spots along the street to watch the parade. Walczak starts jogging down the street and finds a spot about twenty blocks in that is open. He gets into his spot but is bumped by someone. “Hey man I was here first.” “There was no one here when I arrived and there is still plenty of room for both of us.” “With you here there is no room for anyone you big fucking prick.” “Are you Polish?” The man looks puzzled at Walczak’s question, and Walczak looks relatively calm for the situation. “Yeah why else would I fucking be here?” “Well, then why can’t you compromise a little bit of your comfort to help out a countrymen of yours?” “I have been here all morning holding this spot. I went to get a cup of coffee, and then you walk in here thinking you can get it. I even had a chair here that you stood directly in front of so now I can’t see shit.” “I’ll let you stand in front, and then you will have plenty of room and will be able to see.” “Buddy, I don’t think you realize that this was my spot.” “You know what I don’t want to put up with this today.” I cannot believe that I am getting this type of shit from a fellow Pole. This is a day in which we are supposed to celebrate our heritage. Walesa was right these people are not proud of their heritage. They just come out here and pretend that they support the greatest country in the world, but they are just sellouts. They would not leave America even if they were guaranteed a life like this in Poland. They have grown numb to the Polish culture, and I am sure that if you ask most of them what they are they say American. Walczak looks more depressed than he had at the start of the day, and he has slowed in his step as he searches for a new spot to watch the parade at. He finally stops as he sees the parade coming behind him. This parade is meaningless if none of the people here follow any of the ideas that the day is supposed to stand for. They could walk around all day with Flags waving in the air, chanting the national anthem, and celebrating the heritage, but if they go home and do nothing to change other peoples views of Polish people than coming here today was a waste of time. The parade makes its way towards Walczak, and the first group of people marching is young boys and girls in their Krakowianki. This is the future of our culture, and we must instill in them a belief of Pride. They must have a true sense of what it is to be Polish. I cannot even count the number of times that I have been called a dumb Polak, or a stupid shit just because of my heritage. This is all because of the impression that Americans have of us. Never did I meet a person and they said “Oh, you’re Polish you must be smart.” However, most of the time that is true. Walczak pounds his fist on a nearby mailbox, and causes most of the people around him to look. He doesn’t acknowledge their looks, and continues to watch the parade. The parade continues just as it does every year. There are floats with well known Polish people in the area, and some Polish businesses, but then at the end of the parade Walesa comes down the street to a roar of cheers from the car. There is a man that really gets it. He knows what it means to be proud of your culture. If a hero such as himself cannot change the feelings of Polish people than who can. Even the people that are in the Home Country would jump at a chance to come to America. They do not realize that they can stay in their country and build it up. If we just stand together than we can make our country into a force, both political and military. I yearn for the day that I can head back to my place of birth, but I know that I can do more for Poland here right now. If they have a hero that is known on a world scale than it will give them hope, and that is all that I want to do right now, I want to give them hope. Walczak looks up at Walesa one last time as he passes, and smiles in admiration. As his float disappears into the distance Walczak makes his way to a local Polish restaurant. A couple of minutes later he arrives at Kuchnia Polska. There is a young good looking hostess waiting to sit people in the front of the restaurant. “Would you like to dine in or is this for pick-up sir?” “Dine-in.” “Right this way.” “Thank you.” “A waiter will be right with you.” The hostess leaves and Walczak waits for some service. This restaurant is supposed to be a landmark for Polish heritage, but its people do not even enter it. On my walk over here nearly every restaurant was packed with people from the parade. The McDonalds even had people on line waiting out the door, but here it is nearly empty. They come to support the belief of Poland, but they do not support their own making a living in this country. “Hi my name is Michal and I will be your waiter today. Is there anything I can get you to drink?” Walczak is caught off guard and shakes the table as he is startled by the sudden appearance of the young man. “Umm…yeah can I start of with a pint of Zywiec and two orders of pierogi z kapusta. Also can I have two orders of schabowy.” “Sure, is that all?” “For now yes.” Walczak is quiet and relatively motionless and he waits for his food. He watches as the parade and all its participants disperse, and the day turns into a normal New York City day. You see it was all for nothing. These people come here for an hour or two of there lives and they think that it is enough. Now they all go home and do nothing to change anything. When they go home people will still think that Polish people are stupid, and that they can’t accomplish anything. However, that means nothing to these people… “Excuse me.” Walczak is once again interrupted in deep thought, and this time is rather angry because of the thoughts that were just running through his mind. “WHAT DO YOU WANT?” Walczak turns around screaming at the man who he assumes to be the waiter but he is wrong. The man who he yells at is none other than his idol Lech Walesa. “Sorry I did not mean to startle you.” “Uhhh….Welll….I….It should be me that is apologizing I thought you were someone else.” “No need. I know how it is to be constantly bothered in public places.” “If you don’t mind me asking what brings you here to my table?” “Well, I am a big fan.” “YOU!?!?!?!? A big fan of me? I went to your speech today and I was mesmerized. It is an honor to be in your presence, and than for you to say you are a big fan, words escape me.” “I like the message that you are spreading. While it is in a much more forceful manner than I deliver my message I believe that we are both trying to stress the same point.” “I model my message after you. I have read all of your transcripts many times in search of inspiration and they have yet to fail me.” “You flatter me with your words. I hope that one day there will be more people who are proud to be Polish, but I sometimes lose hope, but in you I always see that hope restored.” “Well, I lose hope all the time, and recently it has been happening a lot more often. I came here today to celebrate my culture, but I saw a culture that was diminished from years past.” “I know what you are saying, but these people are just looking for more strong leaders keep spreading the word and they will have no choice but to follow.” “How will they follow if they fight with their own kind.” “What do you mean?” “I was standing there today watching the parade, and there was a man who wanted to fight me over a spot. Something so little so insignificant, and yet he wanted to fight a countryman for it. To me it is a horrible thought that the few of us that we have in this country fight against each other for small things, but do not join up and fight for a cause that will help us all.” Walesa lets out a small laugh as he sips on his water and once again looks at Walczak. “Do you know how many times that happened to me?” “Impossible. You had the support of the entire country. If you didn’t than Poland may still be under communist rule.” “It was years and years before I had support from anyone. When I first started putting together Solidarnosc I received support from no one. My best friends told me that I was wasting my time. However, when they saw that it was working they joined and never looked back.” “What are they going to see from me though? I wrestle for a living, and to them I signify the Polish stereotype. They want nothing more from this country. They are satisfied with just getting by. I want more for our people I want respect. I want people to walk by and look at the Polish flag with pride instead of walking by and not looking at the Polish flag at all.” “I know the feeling. You feel no matter what you do no one will listen, and that if anyone does it will prove futile in the end. I am going to be honest this feeling will last a long time, and only when people finally believe will it go away. However, if you just keep talking and talking repeating the same message over and over again it will get stuck in their head. It’s like listening to a song that gets stuck in your head. Once it is in their head they will dwell upon it and think about it. That is when they will start to follow your ideas.” “I don’t know if I have the patience. I want people to change now.” “Solidarnosc did not rise up overnight. It was a timely process, and anything meaningful always is. You have a choice you can give up now, and chances are it won’t affect what you do the rest of your life, or you can go through with your message, you can get Polish people to be proud, and you will be remembered as a hero of the people.” “I don’t know. I want to keep preaching, but there are only so many times you can get knocked down before you realize that it’s just not worth it anymore. I can stand in the ring and take shot after shot and not fall down, but when people keep repeating “You Stupid Polak” There is a time when I just want to end it all.” “I feel confident that you will make the right choice. As they say great minds think alike, and as I stated we have the same ideas.” The two of them both laugh at the comment. The waiter arrives with their food. He places the food in front of them, and starts to walk away, but Walczak stops him one more time. “Excuse me?” “Yes.” “Quick question. Are you Polish?” “Yeah, my parents own the restaurant. I was born in Lodz.” “Oh ok just wanted to know.” “Sure.” The boy walks away as Walczak looks at Walesa with disbelief. “The boy was born in Poland, and he didn’t even notice you. You were the most important figure in the history of Poland, and he didn’t give you a second glance.” “I did not say it was going to be easy, but it will get done.” Walczak just shakes his head in disbelief “I just hope that one day the words of our national anthem reign true “Jescze Polska Nie Zginela…”
