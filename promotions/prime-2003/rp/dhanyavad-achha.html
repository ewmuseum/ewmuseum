Dhanyavad Achha

Date: last Saturday You squint, partially for the sun as it rises over the Yuba Bharati Kriranga and partially for the news. Choosing not to take the Advil this morning now seems like a poor idea; this headache will not go away on its own. As your friend, the day shift custodian's assistant, bubbles over at the excitement of Danny Ferguson's pending return to your hotel, you can't help but rub your temples and advise caution. Maybe he, who'll mainly be cleaning the suite each morning, can find some level of fun in a celebrity appearance. But as the "executive assistant" to the suite level, you know better. And you remember last time. You'd seen celebrities at the Hyatt Regency Kolkata before, and they had made some ridiculous requests, but Ferguson took the cake when he came through like a sandstorm in 2004. The rider is still taped to the wall of the break room. Priority access to the bar's beer and liquor. Close the gym every day between 3 and 5 for his workout. Redirect the satellite dish atop the building so he can get E!: Entertainment Television in his room. The staff was never quite the same after accommodating Ferguson and his 25-person entourage. It was a three day stop for the Hard Money international release tour. You wept when it was over. Despite what you tell your family, an upscale hotel in Calcutta is not where you intended to be when you left to travel the world. Unfortunately, a degree in hospitality management only gets you so far in the Far East, and when debt added up, this is where you landed - an English-speaking, high-class pillar in the middle of one of the poorest areas of the modern world. Most locals would forfeit a family member of five years of their life for a job at the Regency, with reliable housing, board and pay. You, on the other hand, have trouble waking up every morning. So rattles and hums the American spirit. Your friend the janitor gleefully relays the latest news, a blurb from USAToday.com's Entertainment section: All the countries on PRIME's world tour, India included, had declined to comply with the US's request that Danny be deported and returned home for relevant incarceration. They had all, in their own language, informed the US that they had no intention of wasting resources on the capture and processing of a man who should have never actually been captured and processed to begin with. Oh, but they had much to learn from America, who decried the lack of teamwork but were also unwilling to press the issue that much. They had asked for faithfulness from the world many times during the last several months, and a runaway celebrity was not worth of cashing those chips. In return, American spokespeople sniped at their reluctant friendlies, citing that they were more interested in the economic boom of having a star in their midst than in doing "what was right." The countries, India included, made no efforts to refute or loan credit to such a remark. I can't believe he's going to stay here, your friend the janitor says. You're quick to advise him that it's a choice of convenience, not preference. The hotel is one decently-aimed spitball from the Salt Lake City Stadium, more commonly known as the Yuba Bharati Kriranga. In a few days, the rest of PRIME's staff and roster will be arriving, taking up the rest of the suites and a sizeable chunk of the rooms. For now, though, it will just be Ferguson and his two-man posse, a considerable and welcome downgrade from the accompaniment he brought with him last time. When one's truest enemy in the entire country is one's homeland embassy, it seems his respite can be found in the American corporate franchise, the long arms of social dichotomy opening up for him in this time of need. After all, the Hyatt Calcutta is designed to look just like the one in Chicago, with the only difference being the skin color of the homeless people you pass on your way in the door. While the two of you continue to ready the suite for his arrival, your find your work ethic sapped considerably upon learning the identity of your next client. The previous visit, recalled fresh like you were only a day removed, muddles your motivation. You wonder if you are adequately prepared for the experience again. The abuse, the ridicule, the degradation; it was all a bit too much for one hour. And all you asked was if he wanted a copy of the paper in the mornings. When he left, you and several others - well, those who hadn't quit or been let go since - on the support staff refused to ever watch another Danny Ferguson movie, or even to urinate on him if he were to find himself on fire. Your friend the janitor assures you that he is different; that, although he still behaves the same, he has more noble intentions in mind. That he's one of the "good guys." You don't know or care know any of this, and dismiss it summarily. You've never been a fan of professional wrestling and his since-developed link with that mindless trash certainly does him no favors. You haven't watched PRIME and you don't believe he can be good by any definition. In fact, you don't watch television at all these days. That collection of seedy detective novels you got on eBay are all the entertainment you need. You find it a lot easier to believe that Ferguson has spent the last few months in jail than to believe that he suffered from a changing heart. Hours later, you make the final touches and prepare to greet your unfortunate guest - unfortunate for you, that is, although surely he's not ecstatic about traveling to India early and using the hotel room as his base of operations. When the door finally swings open, you momentarily lose your breath, wasting a half-day's worth of mental preparation to the last-second fear of a man like this demanding your job on a platter at the slightest slip-up. Right away, you can see that he's different. The attire is the same kind of custom-tailored, straight-out-of-Us-Weekly fare that draws the eyes of anyone in the room. The look, however...it seems off. Not just from his norm, although it had been long enough that your perception of Ferguson himself might be a little skewed. No, he just doesn't even look like a star. This job, if nothing else, has served as an excellent character study in the concept of celebrity - too bad you decided against that sociology minor way back when. There's a certain aloof carelessness that exudes from people who are either famous or think themselves to be famous. By being perceptively above most of the world, you also find yourself blocked off from them, maybe even protected. One of the luckiest parts of fame, you think, one of the things that truly makes their lives as easy as people think, is that they don't have to be aware of the world around them, the wars and poverty and the like. They don't have to read the newspaper or watch CNN or be general citizens of the world. They just get to be famous. But that's what's so odd about Ferguson: He doesn't look like that at all. The "celebrity" sheen just isn't there. It starts, you think, with the hair: Unkempt, but not meticulously unkempt. No gel or mousse...it stays in place from repeated hands being run through it. The facial hair is grown slightly past the "sexy male movie star" stubble that Brad Pitt & co. are known far. Maybe it's for lack of a personal stylist while he's out of the states, but the look in his eyes makes you think it's more than that. He's not aloof, he's...distant. His main security man, who seems, for all intents and purposes, to be the entire continent of Africa in a pinstriped blazer, handles all of your questions, even those that had been specifically aimed at the man himself. This, you'd recall later, was you first clue, as Danny's reputation seemed to be that of handling his own talking. Hollywood fellatio artists (the entertainment media, not Vine Street hookers) call it charisma; you know it more commonly as "being a dick." But neither applies here, as Ferguson drifts past you and sits in a sofa chair facing the window and the view of the decades-old stadium a futbol field away. He gave you a courtesy nod on the way there, but at the time you took it more as a "good thing you got out of my way" for stepping aside as they blew into the room. While discussing particulars with the well-dressed titan on Ferguson's payroll, you try to steal fleeting glances at the star. (No doubt, since they'll never remember you, it will be brushed off as starstruckedness, which probably isn't a real word but one they'll use regardless. The thought of being mischaracterized stings you, but the tension and the terror prevent any motions to dispel the myth.) He remains quiet, even contemplative, an adjective that would seem a cardinal sin for a man on his line of work. When the industry seems to demand attention-deficit disorder and a flashbang thought process that rarely drifts from who to screw and who to screw over, genuine moments of deep thought should be so rare that you'd think them prohibited. You've seen it countless times from this corner penthouse overlooking The World As No One Cares To Know It. But here's Danny Ferguson, the consensus points leader for Worst Guest In Hyatt Regency Kolkata history, daring to take his mind out for a walk. He's barely even in the room with you. Having never been to prison yourself nor known someone with a history, you can only attribute this to Danny's unexpected winter vacation, the one your friend the janitor mentioned to you earlier. It seems fair - what WOULD happen when a short thinker is left to bake for a long time? When the hours are no longer divided in jagged zig-zag patterns to fit everything in, the recreation being nearly as demanding as the work, would a sudden luxury of focus be the evolutional progression? More importantly, if you suddenly find yourself with a wealth of time in which to just think, what would you even think about? You wish you could stay longer and prod further into this new development, a virtual senior thesis project for your celebrity character study, but with no more discussion to be had with the mammoth guard and no response from his employer, you feel the need to excuse yourself before your perception as fanboy becomes a little more serious and a little more deserving of problem solving. You couldn't help but be encouraged in a job where no news is celebrated as good news. Curse as you might at the fallacies and incongruities of the famous mind, you always came back on the next day for the next session. If anything, your longevity can be blamed on a need for consistency and security (not usually the things people go abroad for, but you've questioned your decision-making enough for one day). As crazy as the actors and singers and politicians were, at least you could bank on them being crazy. At least you were ready for it, preparing each day to enter the hand-drawn world in which they seem to live. Yet here, without any cameras on, this raging hellstorm of a man who had personified the bombast that came with his profession, was calm and soft-spoken. Although the magazines in your lobby would go so far as to declare him a mere caricature of the young, rich and spoiled, he appeared before you as human, and little more. Maybe last time was a bad day, you tell yourself. Then again, maybe today is just a good day. You turn to go, and he calls out for you to wait. Your limbs tense and your scalp sweats as he crosses the room. Nightmares of last time and the virtual mask of spittle that covered your face after his ultimate tirade flash through your head. Underestimation in your profession is a cardinal sin, and he is about to serve as a harsh reminder. Except...he doesn't. When he reaches you, he extends his hand, making no effort to hide the bill in his palm. You shake, and he smiles, still not quite grasping the concept of warmth but enough to inform you that the tip isn't an accident. Then he speaks two words to you, a stark contrast to your previous memory of Danny Ferguson in every conceivable way. "Dhanyavad achha." Thank you. Goodbye. You would go on to share this story with as many people as would listen, but no one cared. They want to hear about the wacky requests, the conniption fits and the bitchfests. You’re not sure if anyone would quite understand how truly remarkable it is to be unremarkable. Ordinary people probably won’t. Still, though, you were impressed. Your friend the janitor said that Ferguson’s new movie comes out in about a month. Maybe you’ll give it a try.
