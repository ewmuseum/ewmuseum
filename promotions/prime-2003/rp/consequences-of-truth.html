Consequences of Truth

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Shortly After DDT "So let me get this straight..." It had started off so well. Or at least it seemed to start off so well in the mind on Danny Ferguson. After announcing to the studio his intention to return to Hard Money 2, the long-shelved sequel to his break-out film, it seemed like he was on his way to getting the Hollywood career back on track. He had waived off the painfully-necessary script revisions that had been a deal-breaker during the last round of negotiations. He had gone back to the studio and told them he was ready. And now...well, let's let him finish the recap: "...now you tell me you're going to replace me with Ricky Schroder?" "He prefers Rick." "I'll keep that in mind. Seriously, Chuck, Ricky fucking goddamned Schroder?" Across the table, Chuck, the untitled and forgettable gentleman playing the role of 'studio spokesman' in the afternoon's program, fidgets uncomfortably, not so much fearful of Danny's reactions as really wanting to get this over with so he can catch lunch on the Strip. "He's got name value. He was on 24." "Funny, 'cause he's had that same haircut for the past 24 years." "That's not actually funny, Danny. That haircut-" "What can I say, I'm not a writer," Danny jabs back, stepping on the studio response. "Then again, neither are the apes who wrote HM2, and I'm still willing to make it." "Oh, were you doing us a favor?" Chuck's teeth come out as he snaps back. Sure, he was just a studio suit, but it was a nice-ass suit, and it was paid for by tough decisions and risky business moves. By other people. But still. "That movie was collecting dust in some back corner of your vault before I came back. You're saying you would've made it with Ricky instead of me the whole time?" "Not the whole time, no. That was a...recent decision." "Exactly. So why did I come in here, expecting to talk about the contract, and get blindsided by the news that I'm being removed from a movie I resurrected?" Chuck taps his pen on the corner of the desk incessantly. It didn't take a minor degree in psychology to read his body language and know he had an answer he wasn't ready to share yet. Fidgeting a little more, he struggles for the generic answer like a boy trying to break up with his girl. "Danny, I didn't want to have to do this..." "...to my face." He shifts uncomfortably in his chair, agreeing by not disagreeing. "We normally go through the manager on these kinds of negotiations, but with your situation..." "My situation being that I don't have a manager." "...yes. You know, I'm not trying to bullshit you-" "Then don't." He locks eyes with Ferguson and his jaw tightens momentarily. The gaze wanders off for a second, long enough to build up courage without facing anyone, and then he looks back. "Fine. You know as well as I do that the flick sells itself when we can put "World Heavyweight Champion" on the poster." Danny's eyelids fall and he sits back slowly, taking that glancing blow with the kind of aplomb that would make basically anyone who ever knew him proud. Despite a break in his voice thanks to the momentary fear for his health, Chuck continues the explanation, powering through it like a champ. Or like a guy who's really craving a burrito right now. "When we first started negotiations, that's what we thought we were getting. Now '24' has more recognition than 'the guy who punched that NFL player.'" "I just put out the number one flick in the country earlier this summer," Ferguson offers, monotone and passively incredulous. "Oh, I'm sorry, is your name Seth Rogen? Can you deliver the 12-28 market? Don't get me wrong, Danny, that was a great fucking movie, but I'm not trying to win a Golden Globe. We're trying to make money." "Ricky Schroder will make you money?" "Ricky Schroder won't cost us money." Danny casts his eyes downward, breathing regularly to let his red vision fade away. Even when his eyes clear, they remain on the floor. "So this is a salary thing." "Danny, I'm not saying we couldn't make it work. But even if SAG turns a blind eye to it, then we have to convince the trades why we're paying you significantly under market value." "Significantly?" "I know what the studio is comfortable paying its lead. Hard times or not, Danny, when the Reporter starts asking why you're making as much for the whole shoot as you used to make in a week, are you really going to say it's a passion project?" With a hand running through his hair - saying fuck-all to the 35 minutes of gelling it had undergone that morning - Danny pauses to choose his words carefully. All the time spent in PRIME - and around Lindsay Troy in particular - had made him more prone to shooting off at the mouth. Back in the 'grown up world' as he regarded it, people had longer memories and thinner skin. He had to choose language carefully. "What I don't understand, Chuck," he grumbled, "is why you have moved so quickly to produce a sequel without its star. I mean, I've killed projects in the past by asking too much, but we didn't even reach that point here, I mean, I-" "That's not exactly accurate," Chuck says over him. Danny pauses, mid-sentence, and cocks his head sideways. "You're mad that I asked to meet at one instead of noon?" "No, I'm talking about the star label. THAT's not exactly accurate." "C, not to ride my own dick here, but we just had a discussion about market value, so regardless of what you think my status is-" Chuck interrupts again. "No, Danny...we've decided to tie the franchise to Christy." Danny's eyes and nostrils pulse wide at the sentence, and he slumps in his chair just slightly, incredulous to the point of speechlessness. Well, momentarily to the point of speechlessness. "That chick couldn't get through a scene without cue cards! They had to rewrite every scene so she used only one-syllable words!" "Test audiences preferred the shorter version." "What?" "Nothing. Look, the studio really feels like Christy can be the next Heidi Montag." "Who the fuck was the first Heidi Montag?" Chuck smooths out a wrinkle in his coat, knowing full well that his air of boredom was only antagonizing the action star, but also knowing full well what the studio had decided and knowing full well that the lunch rush was going to be dead by the time he got out of here and there was like, only a 70% chance of him 'accidentally' bumping into some major Hollywood player whilst getting his Tex-Mex on. This needed to be over like, 20 minutes ago. "Unfortunately, our position stays the same. You come to us with a fancy gold belt and the franchise rolls on with you. Hell, we might even get that rewrite." Danny's eyes lock fiercely on the studio-by-proxy as he continues to speak. "But if you come to us as a guy looking for a job after navigating one of the most public career collapses in recent memory, then you're no more worthwhile to us than Ricky fucking goddamned Schroder's 24-year-old bangs. Now if you don't mind, I've got a 1:30..." Without moving from his desk, he signals that the meeting is abruptly over. Ferguson stands, more in shock than anything else, his mind committed elsewhere. He had his crossroads moment during the Roulette match. Short of a visit from Mr. Destiny, he wasn't going to get a chance to reconsider his choice, to weigh his own personal goals and downright needs against his respect for and friendship with Troy. She would've understood. Everyone would have understood. But instead, he made his choice and no explanations were deemed necessary. Except those that were owed to studio talking heads who felt they were sold a false bill of goods. The bigger concern should have been that the belt was that important in the first place. It was one thing for it to stand as an unfulfilled personal goal - or even for the public and the media to question why he returned to Hollywood without it. But now Danny had reached a professional nadir where studios wanted the belt to offer the deal. This was a term suited for a professional wrestler, not an actor. Try as he might to insist on remaining the latter, the career arc had made him - very publicly - the former. This was a cause for concern. So naturally it was time for Danny to go out and do something to change this percepti- "It's Universal." "Huh?" "Universal Champion. Not World Heavyweight Champion." "Well you're not either right know, so why does it matter to me?" "Plans change in wrestling, Chuck. Things that should happen sometimes don't. It's a volatile industry." "So is film." Chuck gives one more look at Danny in the doorway and delivers the backhand with all the remorse he can deliver - a slight facial shrug and a purse of the lips. Then he pushes his glassesback up his nose and straightens some papers on his desk, trying to look busy so Ferguson doesn't press the issue further. Letting out a sigh to stymie his vision of hurdling the desk and ruining Chuck's newish suit, Danny closes his eyes and lets the door shut behind him.
