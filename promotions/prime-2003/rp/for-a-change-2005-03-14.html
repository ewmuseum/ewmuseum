For A Change

14 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/14/2005 Video Feed. What materializes after the disappearance of the logo is a plain white wall. The scene is quite still and silent. “I thought today we could use visual aides.” Practically strutting in front of the camera is the Scientific Apparatus. He also has nice hair. His name is Tyler Lopez and he is wearing a sparkling all white suit. He has a pointer stick with him. The kind your teacher used to have. Lopez: Hello again from the show that never ends. The series of revolving doors that is promo world. My name is Tyler and I have a stick. Lopez nods at the camera, and then looks longingly at the screen. Lopez: Before I get down to the pointless and ridiculous business of making fun of other grown men, I’d like to show this brief little video clip. This is for sure the touching part of the show. Another nod from the Apparatus, and the video presentation starts rolling. The puppy dog that was frozen on the screen, a Yellow Lab, starts moving. It is running in a field. It is running free. A little boy catches up to it and the puppy stops. The little boy pets the puppy. Lopez makes a motion and the scene freezes. His pointer stick is right on the puppy’s head where the boy is caressing it. Lopez: Oh, Nova. This is what I want to do to you. I just want to pet you on the head and give you the good treats that look like bacon on the commercial. You’re such a cute little honest man. But you know what, you’re right. So many times the young guns, I won’t name any names… Lopez stares at the camera for a second, and everyone in the world knows who he is talking about. Lopez: But they just shoot their mouth off for the sake of shooting it off. Nova doesn’t want to waste our time and I have to respect that. We are more talented, more seasoned, and so on, and so on. What is there to say really other than, “we’ll see what happens.” Was that so hard Kano? It applies in your case too. You can save the time you are spending doing these promos, and maybe learn an instrument. You’re far too smart for all of this, aren’t you? Of course, never mind that it only took a couple sentences from me to turn you all against each other. The standard line about, “Even Nova knows that Jon Kano is full of crap!”, and so on, and so on. It’s okay Nova. At least you’re a semi-smart puppet. I’m spoiling this little secret now because I don’t need mind games to beat you, but I just wanted to show you how easy it really is. Come up with your own secret handshake. On the video clip, the dog licks the boy’s hand. It goes blank for a second after that. Within moments it is stuck on Mr. Shane Wethers. He is frozen; Tyler points the stick at his face. Lopez: This is one of my opponents for this week, and he’s a funny man. I mean, sarcastic and everything! It’s great. Let’s have another clip. This one seemingly a bit more relevant. The image starts moving, and Shane Wethers begins to speak. This is from his last promo. “He’s Tyler from the block with a tiny microscopic… career!” Lopez clears his throat, and he freezes again. Lopez: Now, Mr. Wethers, where to start here. First off you haven’t listened to me so you really have no idea who I am other than stories that were passed down the PRIME knowledge tree. And second of all, I only ask my opponents why they are special if they go out of their way to say that they are. If they can’t back it up after I ask this, as you already admitted that you couldn’t do, then I’m not sure what the point is of saying it in the first place. No need to try to change the subject now, Mr. Wethers. What was it you said? That my career is microscopic? Do you see what I have to put up with every week, people? I’m not even really arguing points here, I’m just sitting back, looking at these kindergarten contradictions and laughing my ass off. You are thinking too hard over there, or perhaps not hard enough. Did you goof up your notes, Mr. Wethers? We can’t possibly have a real discussion if you are going to be this silly. Take a deep breath, and calm down. Perhaps to set an example, Lopez takes a deep breath himself. Lopez: But I’ll throw you a bone, and we can talk about the “issues”, if you’d like. The scene behind Tyler now is Mr. Lopez ranting when he was claiming to be America‘s Champion. Tyler looks like Uncle Sam. He is dressed like America. Lopez: Ah, politics. For someone who hasn’t been paying attention to PRIME in the last few years, you certainly like to make a lot of generalizations. Do you think that anyone in the council cared about Tyler Lopez when I walked in these doors? I have earned the respect and fear of my peers. I’m not good because we have political influence around here; I have political influence around here because we are good. I have no corporate backing. It’s not going to be until I brake every single record that they keep records for that important lips were strategically placed on our superior asses, that the “powers that be“ will back Lopez. Just because someone cheats doesn’t mean they can’t outwrestle you too, remember that. It’s best that you learn this lesson young. It’s best that you stop gliding through town, thinking that you have a right to even smirk in my direction. I invented the smirk around here, you laughable little @#%$. The screen behind Tyler returns to the PRIME logo, as somewhere someone prepares more video clips. Lopez: Oh, and for the record, if you’re trying NOT to look like a hapless rookie…don’t ask men who have done what we are doing tons and tons of times if they have any motivation. You see I have no giants to defeat. Will my legacy really change one way or the other depending on this match? No, probably not, but that’s not the point. What competitors do is win. I will never get tired of winning, and if I do, it will be the last you’ll see of Tyler Lopez. Winning is what I am here to do. Often the valiant heroes are the ones that talk about competitive fire, but we have it too. It is an indescribable feeling, and I certainly am not surprised that you don’t really understand. I will go out there, and I will show this entire goddamn place that we are larger than any of these people. The people may hate me, but I assure you that they like it a lot more than if they were forced to hate a little pussy like Tony Rolo, or Nova. I am what makes this place go, one way or the other. Lopez motions with the pointing stick, and our next video is getting set to roll. Lopez: Before I go, I thought I’d address the legend Tony Rolo. I’ve wanted to make this point for such a long time but the opportunity just hasn’t presented itself. Mr. Lopez, who is looking more and more like a schoolteacher today, looks back at the screen. The clip that plays is a rather simple one. A quite mentally handi-capped person, runs into a wall at full velocity and falls over. This looks pretty painful. The man backs up and does it again, and again, and again. Lopez talks over this. Tyler: You’re very proud of your reputation Tony, I know this for a fact. But what I come before you today to say is…what’s so special about you? Lopez glances at the screen and grimaces as the man takes another horrid impact. Lopez: I mean, really. You’ve won some matches and the “crazy” fans that don’t wear shirts to the arena probably chant your name because ninety percent of wrestling fans like two things: people bleeding, and tits. You fulfill one of these needs. I’ve said this to a few people Tony…but have you become so proud of how many times you’ve gotten up that you’ve forgotten just how many times you’ve been knocked down? There is a difference between being resilient, and just plain sucking ass. One day you’re going to leap from the top turnbuckle and you’ll die and everyone will be like, “What a shame. Hey, pass the salt.” You probably could have been something, you know that? The sad thing is, in a match with no rules, I’d put you down for the count just the same. Lopez shakes his head. Lopez: My nicknames are a bit clearer. I’d suggest you look them up, boys. You could look educated for a change. Smirk. Wink. Fade. Minutes later. There are many parking lots in Anahiem, and it is raining on all of them right now. Walking across this parking lot right here are a couple of men. One of them makes his living in front of the camera, the other far behind it. Tyler Lopez and his agent, Leroy Harvey . Mr. Lopez is dressed much as he just was. Leroy, who is a few inches shorter than Tyler and a few pounds lighter, is dressed a bit more casually. They are about the same age: mid to late twenties. They are both under umbrellas. The agent’s life is spent complimenting Lopez and thinking of ways for the both of them to make more money. They are America. Some would call this an empty existence for a young man as bright as Leroy Harvey, and they’d probably be right. Leroy: That was a fine job. The salt line…I mean that was just fantastic. Lopez: Yeah, I thought so too. Where they are walking is towards a limo. Lopez: So you guys picked her up? Leroy: She wants to go to lunch. Lopez: Okay. Arriving at the limo, Leroy opens the door for Tyler and he scoots into the backseat. Before Leroy leaves in his own direction, he puts his head inside the large vehicle and issues a parting statement. Leroy: Two things. Don’t forget about the autograph-signing thing I got ya. Tyler rolls his eyes. Leroy: Anyway, the other thing…The guy called from Toronto. No luck yet. See ya Tyler. Lopez: Yeah, later man. And the door closes. End.
