Ensemble - Episode 1

22 June 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Friday, June 22nd, 2007 “No problem, baby.” Reaching back and accepting the cash is a smiling cab driver, who is sure to get a touch of the pale, soft skin on his customer’s hand as she slides out of the black and yellow Cadillac Deville cab. Snapping his head to the rearview mirror, he counting his money in the reflection, “You call me anytime, Snow White.” As he is sitting still, waiting for the next patron with no car, he put the money in his pocket and set his other arm on the door. His eyes were brown, his skin was too, and so was his shirt. His hair was like a giant light bulb - except it was a blonde afro. He begins to run his pick through it, fluffing and styling it; just the way he likes it. Just above his lip was a half-ass mustache he thought was fly, too. His hair begins to look the way he wants, and he set his tiny Rastafarian cap on top. “Perfe-“ In the middle of his delightful self-compliment, a big man gets into the cab, shaking it and rocking it every which way. The driver turns around. “Welcome to the Cabillac Deville, where- Oh Hell no!” The driver spins in his seat, trying to lock the doors, somehow hoping that is going to get rid of the beast getting in his back seat. He frantically spins back around, pointing to the door. “Oh shit, man! I know who you are. Get the fuck out of my cab! There is no way in…I can’t…shit, man. Shit.” With that, the door was already shut and the big man was sitting in the back. The big man, however, was not just ordinary big man. I mean, the driver knew who he was when he got in the car. This was Mike Munson. Also known as; Big Bear. Munson Monsoon. PRIME wrestler. “What is this man? You ain’t got no jets or helicopters or nothin'?” Saying that with his head in his hand, the driver’s tone was clearly irate and he wanted this get over with quick. He let out a deep breath as nothing happened. He turned back to Big Bear. “Okay, look man: where you goin?” “S’cuse me brother? Oh, sorry. I was admirin’ your groovy leather seats back here. Looks like I picked a winner this time! Ain’t that right!?” Big Bear now has his arms extended all across the backseat, smiling and looking giddy as a school girl. His arms did reach all the way across, seeing as how he is six inches short of seven feet tall. “I’m goin’ to the Pit.” “Man, what in the Hell you goin to the Pit fo on a Friday night?” Staring in the rear view is the driver, who locks eyes with a dumbfounded Big Bear. His expression goes from unsure to emphatic. “Well, I’m in this, like, big time match. Biggest match of my life, really. It’s the PRIME 5-Star Challenge. It’s between myself and five other wrestlers, giving the person with the most wins at the end – presumably me – a 5-Star Title shot at Colossus IV in Seattle.” Big Bear and Cabby share a brief moment of silence. “That’s not until Wednesday though. LET’S GO GET SOME ASS!” His eyes light up and so does his voice as the bass-filled boom of ASS echoes off the car’s walls. “TO THE PIT!” “Maaaaaan,” starts in the very-skilled-in-that-department-if-ya-don’t-mind-me-sayin’ cab driver. “You don’t go to the Pit for booty. You go to the parties, playa. This is a college town,” the driver turns around, squaring up Big Bear. “Everybody gets laid.” The words sound like sweet, sweet birdsong to Big Bear’s ears. He points straight ahead. “TO THE PARTIES!” Just when Big Bear thought it was time to study the human anatomy, the guest lecturer – Mr. Cabby – was waving his arms slightly. He looks like he has one more thing to explain. “Whoa whoa, now, Boo Boo. That’s all the way on the other side of town.” The driver faces forward, away from the anxious giant in the back seat. “Might be a lot of gas. Could get expensive…” “WHAT!?” Big Bear’s head was next to Cabby’s blonde bulb-head. Cabby nods, sticking out his bottom lip and raises his palms to the sky; obviously implying he didn’t say anything. “Hah! Got ya man.” Then something falls into Cabby’s lap. As he looks down, his eyes explode; holding up a roll of bills – more like FAT FUCKIN’ CASH ROLL. He turns around, still staring at the money as his mouth hangs open. “How…” His lip quivers as he finally looks to Big Bear, who is staring out the window and scratching his armpit. He finally notices Cabby, drooling all over the newfound fortune. “W-w-what’s…H-h-how m-much?” “I’m gonna be straight with you, shooter.” Big Bear leaned forward, giving a nice Bill Clinton fist gesture to sell his most dire needs. “I’m going to need you all night, broseph. You and I are going to paint this town in panties! Can I count on you?” “All night?! I don’ know ‘bout that shit…” Big Bear’s hand had a good grip on the collar of Cabby’s shirt. The air from Munson’s nostrils even hurt Cabby. “Okay, what the fuck!” Big Bear grins, sitting back in the seat and getting comfortable. His attire was not exactly party-esque; a pair of sweat-shorts, flip flops, and a blue, button down, short-sleeve shirt. Okay, so maybe it is college party attire. Cabby’s looks in the rearview. “So what’s your name, my man? I’ve seen you around, just not too familiar with the business, you dig?” Big Bear adjusts his legs and nods his head to the beat of the tune on the radio. “Big Bear,” he blurts out. “Munson Monsoon.” “Hahaa!” Cabby chuckles out loud a bit. “That yo real name, playa?” “You askin for an ass kickin?” Big Bear was staring in the rear view as Cabby tried to avoid all eye contact. He wasn’t laughing anymore, either. Suddenly, Big Bear shoots to the left side of the car. “Is that Steve Alford!?” “Oh yeah,” Cabby says, pulling the car over to the curb alongside the new New Mexico men’s basketball coach, who is wearing that bright red blazer. Big Bear moves all the way over, making space for the college basketball legend. He opens the door, stepping in before he notices Big Bear. “Mista A, how we doin’ tonight?” “’Lit the fuck up, Bulby! Oh-“ Alford blankly stares at Big Bear, who shares the same awkward stare. “Umm… hello there. I’m Ste-“ “Steve Alford,” Big Bear interjects and then proceeds in taking a massive breath into his lungs. “Indiana University, 1984 to 1987. 1986 All-American, three time All-Big Ten, four times Team-MVP. 89% from the free-throw line is 4th best in NCAA history.” Big Bear bends over, taking in large amounts of oxygen to compensate for his inconceivable knowledge of Steve Alford. “Hi.” “…” Alford, who dons the expression of a drunk bastard, stares at Big Bear and then snaps his head toward Cabby. Cabby grins, raising his palms to the sky. Alford looks back at Big Bear. “Hey buddy, I’m Steve-fucking-Alford.” The words are slurred as Alford shoves his finger into his own chest. “I’ll do my own introducshinzzz. Bulby, you know the place. Chop chop.” With that, Cabby pulls away from the curb and drives down the lit up streets of Albuquerque. Big Bear sits back up, smiling at Steve, who is flicking off babies on the sidewalk. Cabby turns around, noticing the Lobo coach offending minors and mother alike. “Oh Mista A, you one funny mothafucka.” Cabby laughs out loud as Big Bear yanks Alford back by his red jacket. He shoves his fist into Alford’s face twelve times. Make that thirteen. Cabby jerks the wheel with every flesh thud he hears, swerving all over and screaming there entire time: “What the fuck you doin’, man!? That’s Mista Alfo’d! Get yo jive ass- JESUS!” Big Bear let Alford go as the car’s brakes squeal. All three men in the car are still, kind of taking in what sits before them. Big Bear opens his door, slowly stepping out and rests his arms on the top of Cabby’s sweet ride. Standing in front of the spun-sideways cab, arms crossed and mugs cold, stands a group of thugs circling a young, cute brunette and her little puppy. These are the kind of thugs that have run these streets of west Albuquerque for eight years. They all carry their trademark Swiffer WetJets. Not the ordinary cleaner though, these ones have one additional feature; the ends are sharpened down to a point. The leader, wearing a Burger King crown, steps forward. “Please give me your cab.” “Oh shit, the Swiffs!” Cabby cries, locking the doors. Big Bear stands outside still and grins. Steve Alford is giving them all the bird, by the way, which doesn’t seem to be helping the situation. “Swiffers?” says Big Bear, squishing his face together in confusion. “To clean up all the blood! Get ‘em Swiffs!” With a lunge of his right arm, the leader orders his gang upon the car. One made the mistake of getting close to Big Bear, though, and caught and elbow in the eyeball. The rest stop in their tacks, turning back to the leader. “You can’t elbow! This isn’t UFC, here. It’s the Swiffs. We shank you, you die. We get your shit. Do it right or-“ Big Bear slammed his elbow in the throat of the Swiff leader, sending him to his knees and clutching his throat, practicing a new, escalated breathing routine. Big Bear proceeded to snap his WetJet in two. He hates cleaning products. Especially when chumps use them as weapons to beat up puppies and damsels. After about three minutes, each Swiff comes to the realization that their leader sucks, once again reinforcing the fact that when kids from Albuquerque West High’s chess team try and start a gang, they will fail and probably get hurt. Dusting the scum off his hands, Big Bear casually and calmly assists the young woman to her feet after a job well done. “Look at my man, whoopin’ ass and makin’ moves without even breakin’ a sweat. C’mon sweetheart, hop on in. We’ll get ya home.” Cabby steps around the front of the car, opening the front passenger door as Steve Alford crawled out of the bushes zipping up his fly. “I only got to the ‘V’, but it’s in cursive,” slurs Alford, swinging his index finger toward the St. Michael’s Catholic Cathedral. With that, the New Mexico hoops general goes stumbling into the front seat, slumping down into the soft leather with a big sigh. Big Bear opens up the back door for the young lady and her Pomeranian dog, while Cabby was searching the bodies for cash, jewelry, and drugs. “So what’s your name darlin’?” chimes Cabby, turning backward as he backs up the Cabillac. “This big buffalo is ‘Big Bear’ Munson Monsoon, one of the feature names in the wrestlin’ world. And to my right is Mr. Indiana, Steve Alford. The new head coach for the Lobos.” “I’m Dorothy. I’m from Sante Fe but I’m on my way to Seattle to model for this lingerie magazine. And this is Frodo. Say ‘Hi!’ Frodo!” The girl was waving the dog’s paws on her lap, patting the dog’s stomach. Each man’s eyes suddenly found themselves on Dorothy and Frodo, speechless on her career path. “Just so happens we’re on our way to Seattle, too.” Cabby says, looking straight ahead but kind of tilting his head back to make himself heard. Big Bear turns, shocked, and looks at Cabby. “We are?” Big Bear raises an eyebrow. “Well sure, daddyo. I mean, you ain’t got no helicopters or jets yet, do ya?” Big Bear’s head shakes no, knowing full well he has just about nothing to get there on. “That’s right, then. We are goin’ to Seattle.” “Except me, asshole.” Steve Alford turns in his seat, pointing his finger inches away from Cabby’s nostril. “I gotta get to the Pit.” “You don’t go to the Pit for ass, Coach!” yells out Big Bear, gripping Alford’s shoulder and tossing him forehead first into the dashboard. “Gotta go to the parties!” “Not for ass, you dimwit. I’m gonna sleep in my office tonight so my wife doesn’t smell the two whore’s stink on me. That and the booze probably won’t go over too well with Misses A.” Alford waves his fingers around, twirling them in the air at the fact his life sucks other than being rich-as-hell for watching basketball games. “Yeah man, I remember that one time when we pulled in at 6am, three bottles of bourbon deep. Never seen a woman shoot like that, not in my lifetime.” Cabby shakes his head, remembering back to the near-death incident with Mrs. Alford. He looks in the rearview. “So the Pit Wednesday, and then on to Seattle. Deal?” “You’re gonna stop each time I have a show? Each place along the way, I'll see if I can get you guys in.” Big Bear’s head looks around checking for interested parties. “I think I can do that,” says Cabby, leaning back and grinning. “No problem, playa.” The Cabillac speeds down the road, headed into the late Albuquerque night. Looking out the window, Big Bear feels a tap on his shoulder. Dorothy peers into his eyes with her soft, hazel eyes. “You know, I appreciate the help back there.” Dorothy whispers, smiling kindly and resting her hand on Big Bear’s big paw. “Thank you.” Turning away from her gentle gesture, Big Bear stares out the window at the stars in the dark night sky. “I do what it takes.” “What it takes?” Dorothy, looking a bit lost, shakes her head in misunderstanding. “To make this world a better place,” he says, looking back into her eyes. “For people like you. Like Cabby. Like Frodo, there. I’m a wrestler, yeah; but that’s not gonna stop me from helping out people who need it. I just get to show all the PRIMEates around the world how to do the right thing, televised beating or not.” Big Bear notices a twinkle in Dorothy’s eye as she looks up at him, smiling. He looks forward, noticing Cabby making a right turn. Soon, he sees a giant gathering in front of him. Looking up to the top of the house, he sees where he is. A keg party. “Well,” Cabby says, looking back at Big Bear. He notices Steve Alford is out cold in the passenger seat, and shakes his head in amusement. “We gonna do this thing?” Jerking the handle open, Big Bear launches the door opens and steps out of the car. With a giant smile spread across his face, Big Bear pumps his arms into the air. “WHERE CAN I GET A CUP!?” As he yells, Dorothy steps out of the car and notices a large rush of 18-to-22 year old males charging her way. They are all letting out that typical “Ohhhhhhh!” you might expect at a kegger at 1am on a Friday night. “Holy shit! You’re Big Bear Monsoon! Dude, this guy once jumped off a 4-story parking garage,” explains one UNM student, clearly labeled by his Lobos hat. “It was sick!” “I saw the Chainz and Gamble match. You got fucked dude! No lube. Straight fucked!” another Lobo shouts to Munson. Big Bear grins and high fives a bunch of people, even girls are getting in on it now. He looks down and nods with the dude talking with him. One kid hands him a cup, which is empty seconds later as the giant man slams it down. “Yooo Big Bear! Want a bong rip?” One student offers, holding up his bong. Cabby grabs it and pulls one. “Dude, totally!” The two share a hand slap as Cabby tells a crowd of students that Steve Alford is in the passenger seat. A mad rush goes to the Cabillac, dragging their new savior out of the car and crowd surfing his lifeless body into the house, somewhere out of sight. Cabby drifts back, standing next to Dorothy, whose arms are crossed as she shakes her head in dismay. “When’s his match?” she asks, watching Big Bear bounce off with a group of people to get more brew. “Five days,” Cabby says, reassuring Dorothy, who locks eyes with him, not so sure about the time frame. After she didn’t break the stare, Cabby lets out a nervous breath. “Wh-what? It’s Friday night, baby. You think he’s gonna be partying for five days? Come on…”
