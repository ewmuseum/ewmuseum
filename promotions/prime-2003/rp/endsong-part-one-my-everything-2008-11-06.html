Date: 06 November 2008 Endsong : Part One – My everything - - - - - I will live today, As though it is my last day on earth, Oh, all the plans I have would not matter, If I could not spend this last day with you. I will live today, As though it is my last breath of air, My sunshine, My last meal and my last drink of water. I will live, As though I have read somewhere that The world is coming to an end. I will live today, As though I heard you say, Without me in this world, Your life would be meaningless. I will live today, As though from afar, I heard you call my name, and say, I will never forsake you, Nor will I leave you. I will live today, As though you are my anchor of strength, The pillar of my life, The will of my every being. I will live, I will live, Until it is time for us to part. I will live again; We will meet again, in the afterlife There we will always reside. ~ anon. - - - - - Hawaii Only a few weeks ago, looking back, my life had been so different; so much simpler. Back before I got subpoenaed and forced to go back to a life that I had thought that I had left behind; a life of battle for a baying crown – a modern day gladiatorial arena with the vox populous replaced by the baying masses of PRIME fans … but I am leaping ahead, that is still to come. I am talking about what was, not what will be. Back then all I had to think about was what the Hawaiian weather was going to be like and, dependent on that, what to wear as I explored the beautiful islands. A simple t-shirt and shorts when the weather was good and, to be honest, the same thing when it wasn’t so good; something that I had learned fast was that when you are in paradise even the rain is wonderful … and considering the Hell that I had lived through over the previous eighteen months I wasn’t going to let anything rain on my parade. Not even the rain. The first time I had been caught out in a downpour I had been walking through the lush and empty Countryside, basking in the solitude as if it had been created just for me. I had thrown a small prayer of thanks up to Father Sky – just in case he had given this day to me – when the first drop of rain hit me squarely in the eye. Before I could blink, before I could breathe, I was drenched as a Spring storm came out of nowhere. All I could was stand there, head thrown back, hair hanging in drenched streams across my body, and for the first time in what seemed to be a lifetime … I laughed. I relished the feel of the sudden storm as it rained down on me, Nature’s own cleansing touch; I felt tears leave my eyes only to join with their cousin’s from the Sky and I realized that they weren’t tears of fear, anger or hurt – these were something new, something different. They were tears of release. Everything that I had been holding inside myself for so long – too long – was being let loose and, just like the downpour that surrounded me, it felt good; so good. A single peal of thunder rang out across the land and, as suddenly as it had started, the rain stopped. The clouds parted and a haze rose from the ground as the Sun started to warm the land once more. I stared at the sky, rapt and awed, and felt almost reborn. The thoughts that haunted me were still there and no doubt there would be nights where I awoke, screaming and terrified … I knew that … even if there were no outwards scars to show the wounds that I bore but today – here and now – I felt renewed; reborn. I heard his footstep approach me from behind just as I could smell him, the scent that I had come to know so well. I had been brought up in the ways of my People by my grandfather and there was nothing on this World that I couldn’t track, I couldn’t hunt so, no matter how softly he tried to walk, how gently he reached out to run his finger down my back, I had known that he was there before his skin touched mine. His fingers ran up my arm, tracing the contour of my muscles and then, gently, he wrapped his arms around my chest, bring me close to him so that he could put his cheek to mine and – his breath warm against my ear – whisper a single, simple word. “Finally.” - - - - - Detroit “It didn’t have to be like that, you know!” Rick James fought hard to control his voice, to shelter the emotions that raged within him and try to remain calm; in control. He didn’t want the figure that huddled on the bed in front of him to hear the anger laced fear that threatened to overwhelm him; he didn’t want him to recognise the anguish that was being held down inside his chest; he didn’t want him to know just how hurt, how lost, how abandoned he was feeling. He fought as hard as he had fought for anything else in his life, simply so that his best friend, his one, true friend, Jay Phoenix, wouldn’t have anything else to burden him. As the words left his mouth, however, he tasted the salt tang of tears as the streamed down his face and realised that the battle had been lost. “You didn’t have to run away,” he sobbed the words through clenched teeth, “we would have helped you through it … I would have helped you through it!” A sting of pain shot through Rick’s hands as he stared at the silent and pathetic figure in front of him – a shell of the man he had known. Looking down he saw that a small trickle of blood was seeping out between his fingers and as he gently opened his hands, unclenching them, he saw that he had been holding them so tight that he had pierced his palms with his own nails. A long time ago, his mind wandered, Father John would have compared them to stigmata but that was when Rick still believed in God – when he still believed in anything; now he knew them for what they were, self-inflicted wounds that would heal in a matter of hours. Looking at the man in front of him he couldn’t help wondering, as another quiet sob escaped his throat, if the same thing could be said; would he heal? “Jay,” Rick whispered, moving forwards and reaching out a hand towards the shaking man, “let me help you. Please.” “You want to help me?” Rick had to lean forwards and strain to hear the words. Jay’s face remained buried in the covers of the bed, his shoulders shaking, but at least he responded. Finally. Sitting down, gently, on the side of the bed Rick waited patiently, hoping that his friend would continue. When he did, when the words finally came out of a throat that was raw with tears, Rick’s World spun and he hoped – prayed – that he had hear them wrong. He knew though, that he hadn’t. They rang in his ears no matter how hard he tried to pretend he hadn’t heard them. “You want to help me? Then kill me. Please!” - - - - - Hawaii While my home, no matter where I am, where I was, would always be Arizona I had found somewhere that suited me perfectly in Hawaii; Arizona was my birthplace, and the one place I could always go back to no matter what ‘they’ said, but Hawaii called to me in my dreams and, in my soul, it got an answer in return and I know that, no matter what may come and what may change, Hawaii will always be the place that gave me back my life. The place that brought me hopes out of despairs, love out of friendship. I think that I will always wonder if Rick knew that when he suggested that we go there to give me the time to recover that my doctor had advised. Could he have known what Hawaii would do for me, what it would give back to me … what it would give to us? If he did know that then he was surely gifted by the Creator with foresight and, if he didn’t, then the man needs to open up a casino because luck like that could make him a rich man indeed. Of course on the day that I stepped foot on Hawaii’s soil – or to be exact the smaller island of Maui’s soil – that isn’t the way that I felt. After enough hours traveling – by land and air - to actually class it in days instead, and with my mental state being described as ‘fragile’ if one were diplomatic and generous … and ‘nuts’ if one weren’t … the sight of Kahului Airport didn’t exacly fill me with any great spiritual revelation or succour. Anything that I felt at that time, anything that the Islands may have been tryig to tell me, was hidden behind a fog of exhaustion and medication. All I saw was an ungly grey terminal, buidlding work going on around it, and agrey sky heavy with clouds. “So,” I spat bitterly towards Rick as he descended the steps of the small jet behind me, “this is your great plan, is it? This is where you are going to keep your promise to make things better?” I saw the flash of pain in Rick’s eyes as my words stung him; I don’t recall if I felt any pleasure at the sight and, if I did, I know that I didn’t feel any guilt at that moment. Ever since he had tracked me down in Detroit only two weeks previously I had been punishing him at every opportunity. I had told him that I hated him; that he was worthless; I had spat every argument that we had ever had back in his face; I had brought up every memroy that I knew would hurt him, embaraas him – I had laughed as I brought up the fact that he has pissed his pants when we were kids because some bullies had scared him and that I had had to step in and save him from a beating. My grandfather had told me once, when I was young, that sticks and stones would hurt me but that words never would. When I looked in Rick’s eyes, however, at my words, I knew that he had been wrong. The scars from a physical wound would heal, the pain forgotten wuickly; the memory of a deliberate verbal attack however, from someone close to your heart … that was a different thing, a deeper and more hurtful wound. No matter what I said however, no matter what I did – and I did more than I care to recollect or admit to myself – he never left my side. Not once. I suppose that the fact that I had asked him to kill me – or at least walk away and let me kill myself – had something to do with that. I am not proud of what I said, I am not proud of what I did. I am proud of him though. So proud. He laid his heart and soul on the line for me that day – and continues to do so, of course – and I did my best to push him away. All I did was pull him closer; I owe him my life. I owe him everything. - - - - - Detroit Rick James wasn’t a small man by any means. He stood just over five foot ten, he ate well and worked out often. He would never win a Mister Universe title but he knew that when he looked in a mirror a fit, strong man looked back. Compared to his best frined, Jay Phoenix, of course, he looked the the legenday weakling on the beach that Tony Atlas had promised to help; when you devoted your life to fitness, to wrestling and to excellence, of course, you had a body to match that. Years of martial arts, aerobic training and countless hours on the mats and in the ring had given Phoenix a body that many men were jealous of and reactions that impressed even the most jaded ‘fans’ who lamented that wrestling wasn’t real. The fact that his slap not only got past Phoenix’s reflexes to connect swuare acorss his left cheek – the sound of skin on skin echoing harshly around the hotel room – but also knocked him back across the bed surprised Rick. For a split second – that lasted a lifetime – neither man moved, neither man spoke. Four harsh red welts quickly began to raise on Phoenix’s cheek as Rick stood, nausea running through him, watching. His hand rose again but, this time, it was to cover his mouth as shock settled over him at what he had just done. Then the words – Jay’s words – came rushing back to him, ringing through his ears as if they were being spoken again. Then kill me … kill me … kill me … kill me … Running fingers down the welts on his face, his eyes still clouded but pain obvious there too, Jay Phoenix used his free arm to push himself back to a vertical base, sitting on the edge of the bed as he made to stand up fully. The shove, both of Rick’s palms flat against his chest, caught him by surprise and he was pushed back down again. As he stared up at the man in front of him – the man that he had known for nearly all of his life and the one person who he could call ‘friend’ and now that it was completely true – he saw a stranger. Rick’s face was pale and taut, his lips pressed tightly together with a trickle of blood seeping from one corner where a tooth had bitten deeply into the flesh of one. Tears ran freely down his cheeks as he stood, body shaking, directly in front of the seated man. “Don’t you dare,” Rick spat out, saliva flying, “don’t you dare move, don’t you speak, and don’t you do anything!” “…but …” “I said don’t speak!” Rick pointed at Jay’s face, his hand visibly shaking as his voice cracked. “I spent nearly two years looking for you, not knowing if you were dead or alive and then – finally – I found you again. You were broken, you were battered, you were hurt but you were alive and I found you … and what did you do? What did you do!?” “I …” “You left me,” Rick whispered, half to himself, “you ran away and you left me!” “You don’t understand, Rick,” Jay breathed, brokenly, “after what I went through … after Ember … after the hospital, I had to, I had to …” His voice trailed out as his head fell forwards. His long auburn hair, knotted and tangled, fell forwards to curtain his face but it couldn’t mask the shaking of his shoulders or the sounds that he tried to swallow as he sobbed. Looking down, tears falling heavily, Rick reached out a hand towards Jay’s shoulder but paused – stopping himself a fraction of an inch before he made contact – visibly but silently warring with himself as anger and compassion competed for dominance. “You had to what, Jay?” Rick asked, his voice devoid of anger; drained of all emotion. “I didn’t mean to …” ”You never meant to do anything, did you?” Rick laughed, without humour, “nothing was ever your fault was it?” “It wasn’t me, Rick, you know that, “Jay breathed without looking up. “It was Ember.” “I know what Ember was, Jay, I know what Ember did to you and through you,” Rick acknowledged, “but it was you who ran away, it was you who choose to run away!” “I wasn’t strong enough Rick,” Jay sobbed out, “I wasn’t strong enough to fight Ember, I wasn’t strong enough to fight the doctors who convinced me to stay in that place, that ‘hospital’ even though there was nothing wrong with me …” “Nothing wrong?” Rick gasped out. “I know that they tried to convince you that you had had a psychotic break Jay, when we both know that wasn’t the truth, but even though Ember was gone – even though the possession was broken – there was definitely something wrong!” “… what?” “You were gone for two years, Jay – he used you, he violated you and he nearly broke you! You don’t just recover from that overnight!” “ … but …” “ … and you don’t do it alone!” “I thought that it was better that way,” Jay stated simply from behind his shroud of hair, “I thought that if anything happened – if he came back …” “That isn’t going to happen, Jay!” Rick’s voice shook with chock and fear, his words ringing out as much to convince himself as to convince Jay, “I was there, remember, I saw him die!” "Shaman?" Rick James looked around the grey landscape, turning full circle as he tried to figure out what had just happened. He remembered talking to Jay’s grandfather, promising to do whatever it took to help … to save … Jay’s life and then there was a moment of dazzling white followed by jet blackness, and then … … this. Everywhere he looked there was nothing. Literally. Dull grey dust seemed to coat the ground below his feet and a grey sky was filled with grey, lacklustre, clouds. The horizon was bare and empty and deep within himself Rick felt empty. He felt lost. He felt despair. The Shaman’s words, as they ran out of the hospital into the grounds, came back to him. "I can send you into the Dreaming, Rick, I can send you into Jay’s dream itself. You will be inside his mind, you will see what he sees, and you will feel what he feels." Looking around him, seeing and feeling the utter desolation, Rick felt hot tears sting his eyes as he realised that it had worked. He was inside his best friend’s mind. … and it was filled with nothing but loss, pain and despair. "Well, well, well, fancy meeting you here!" Rick spun around and came face to face with the black mask of Ember. He took a step backwards in uncontrolled shock and then paused, forcing himself to stand still despite the shaking … the fear … that had overtaken his body. For, tightly grasped in Ember’s hand, like nothing more than a rag doll, hung a limp and naked Jay Phoenix. Rick knew that it wasn’t completely real, that Ember was nothing more than a spirit that didn’t know enough to stay dead but he also knew that it wasn’t completely a dream either. "Being in the Dreaming is not like having a dream, Rick," The Shaman had warned him, "if you get hurt there – if you get killed there – it can be fatal." "Let him go, Kuthanin," Rick pleaded, trying to keep his voice strong – and failing, "please, give him back to me." "Give him back to you," Kuthanin … the man known as Ember … mocked, "You sound like a love-lorn girl, Ricky!" Rick’s face flushed in embarrassment but he squared his shoulders and refused to step back. "Oh my," laughed Ember, "that is it isn’t it? All these years watching from a distance and I never noticed and I know that my dear ‘brother’ here didn’t either! You love him, don’t you? Not like a brother, not like a friend … you actually love him, don’t you?!" Ember shook the lifeless form of Phoenix, which elicited a whimper from both him and Rick at the same time. "Well as sweet as that is, you sick faggot," Ember growled, "it doesn’t matter anymore anyway. I don’t know what is going on out there but I can feel myself getting stronger and Jay here getting even weaker than he always was. It looks like I win … I finally win!" With a shriek of despair Rick threw himself at Ember, hoping to catch him by surprise, hoping to knock him off his feet and force him to drop Phoenix perhaps. Whatever it was that he hoped he failed. With barely any effort Ember simple grabbed him, single handed, out of the air and held him aloft by his throat. Rick’s eyes started to bulge, his tongue protruding, as Ember slowly started to squeeze his throat and stop the air from flowing. "Two birds with one stone," Ember laughed, "not only do I get to watch my brother die just as he watched me die all those years ago but I get to take his little ‘love’ out at the same time! That is precious … that is simply …" "… NOT GOING TO HAPPEN!" Ember’s eyes gaped as a naked arm suddenly reached up and enfolded his own hand within its grasp. Looking down he saw a barely conscious Jay Phoenix trying to stand up on his own, barely having enough strength to do so, but – somehow – having enough strength to force Ember’s hand away from Rick’s throat. Inch by inch, moment by moment, the battle between them waged but Rick found first that he was able to catch a breath, and then was able to move. With a burst of effort he broke free from Ember’s clutches and crumpled in the sand as he watched Ember suddenly bring both hands up to choke Phoenix; Jay’s hands, at the same time, closed around Ember’s own throat. Catching his breath Rick could do nothing but watch in despair as Jay’s knees started to buckle, as his arms started to waver. Ember crowed in delight and pushed the advantage, forcing Jay down to the grey dust. "Jay," whimpered Rick, almost inaudibly, "don’t leave me, please" Even though he couldn’t have heard Rick’s whisper Jay suddenly glanced his way, his piercing green eyes moist with tears and with a shriek of effort he suddenly forced himself back to his feet, bending Ember backwards and then finally into the dust himself. His naked body glistened with sweat, every muscle corded with tension, as he strained with every fibre of his being to defeat the man … the thing … that had stolen his life for two years. Before Rick’s eyes he saw Ember’s form waver, becoming more and more insubstantial, until finally Jay collapsed to the ground, his hands clasped around nothing more than a thin layer of latex. Ember’s mask. Rick rushed to his side, pulling him up into his arms and crushing him against his chest. "Jay," he almost screamed, "you did it, you won!!" Tears filled Rick’s eyes and Jay’s body blurred in front of him. With a frantic cry Rick realised that Jay’s breathing was becoming shallow, was becoming laboured, and he crushed his body against his own as if he could hold onto him. "Rick," Jay whispered, his voice little more than a breath, "I’m sorry … I lov …" Rick suddenly realised that he was holding nothing, that he was alone in the fields of grey and he cried out in despair … … and woke up lying on the grass outside the hopsital. “That’s just it though, Rick!” Jay whispered, giving voice to his fear, as he brought Rick back from his terror filled memories, “he was never alive to begin with; there is nothing to stop him coming back and taking me again … and through me he can hurt those around me, the people that I lo …” As Jay’s voice trailed off Rick’s hand finally finished its journey and he ran it gently through the other man’s hair brushing it back and tilting his head backwards so that he was forced to look up and lock gazes. A rainbow haze filled both men’s visions as tears obstructed them. Dropping down to his knees Rick brought himself nearly level with the huddled figure and leant in close. “You still can’t say it, can you?” Rick whispered, smiling. “That is ok, you know, you don’t have to; there is plenty of time for you to realise the two things that are important now, the only two things that matter. The first is that you are not alone, you don’t have to – you won’t – ever face anything on your own again, including Ember … if he shows his face inside your head, or mine, I will kill him!” Jay couldn’t help the small smile that came to his lips as he heard his friend say that, knowing how much Ember had scared him previously. “Thank you,” he said earnestly, knowing that Rick truly believed that he would be able to do it. “… you said there were two things, though, what is the second?” “That’s is the most important thing, and the most simple …you are the pillar of my life, Jay, my everything …” Rick breathed, his hand holding his friend’s face steady as his bright blue eyes locked with Jay’s emerald green ones. “… I love you.”
