Stepping into the Role (Culture Shock RP for Tag Match)

7 March 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7/3/07 ‘Cut!’ How did I get here? I’m standing in front of maybe twenty Asian people (I stopped asking where they were from about twenty minutes ago when their raised eyebrows at being called Chinese didn’t go so well), rooted to the spot as I read lines off of an autocue for some commercial. It looks like it’s for some kind of shampoo but it could be a cream for genital warts for all I know. The one concession to the whole affair is that the director is American, clearly trying to cling to some past glory. He told me the name of whatever film he’d made at least three times by now, but damned if I cared to remember. How did I end up standing outside, with foam being poured all over me for some pathetic Asian commercial? At what point did I stop being a wrestler and become everyone’s favourite gay icon? This whole thing was bullshit. I didn’t really have a choice in the matter, at least that’s what Blaine Blair had stammered over the telephone. “We…we…we need to h-ave a repre…represe…representative for PRIME there and you’re in the area…,” he’d said in unconvincing fashion. Thanks a bunch, thanks a real bunch. So while some of the other wrestlers either get to prepare for their match in solitude or do press meetings that might actually, you know mean something, I get stuck with a fucking advertisement? Bull. Shit. ‘Alright people…’ I don’t know why, but our hopeful director seemed to start every sentence in English, trail off, then ramble into his piecemeal foreign language to get across what was going on. Pretty soon he’d be over to me, to give some sort of encouragement like I was a twelve year old boy and we’d do the take over. Wash, rinse and repeat came to mind. ‘Alright Jack, just got to keep it simple, nothing too strenuous or complicated, just take the whole thing easy alright? There’s no pressure, just remember like you’re in the ring and it’s time to have fun you know? Thatta boy.’ He patted me on the shoulder, only barely though due to his lack of height. I swear though that if he could, he would have finished the patronising conversation with his palm tapping on my head. It sure felt like he may as well have been. I shouldn’t be here. I shouldn’t be doing this stupid, irrelevant piece of crap advertisement. That should be left to the retards who vainly think that they can make it in Hollywood. It should be left to those who have their self-inflated ego to a nice ballooned proportion. The kind that could pop at any moment due to their own self-importance looking to share the spoils. I didn’t need to be standing around in some random locality of China holding a bottle of what could be poison. This was…insulting to say the least. As soon as this was over I was going to hurt someone from PRIME for this misadventure. ‘Everybody ready?’ The plucky director was signalling everyone back into the centre of the ravine that we were shooting in. To add to the fact that the foam was going to be flowing all over me, it was coming down from a slow gentle slope in a ravine. He’d referenced it being some sort of cleansing ritual to link to the shampoo, or so he said. The mere fact it was a lot of foamy white stuff…well I had my reservations as to what kind of imagery he was trying to convey. Seriously, how the fuck did I get here? ‘Action!’ *** Later on, (having eventually cleaned my ears out) I headed back to the hotel where the majority of the wrestlers and ring crew were staying. It was near enough to the arena, so much so that I’d taken the liberty of heading into the arena for a look. The ramp way was in the middle of being constructed and over in one corner, just out of sight of anyone who would be in the crowd on the first night was the beginnings of the structure of the Dual Halo. It was a true monstrosity and not having been in such close contact to it before I was intrigued. I headed over and admired the destructive beauty of it, the sheer steel climbing up into the heavens and the overall feel of the structure gave off its potential to make and break names within its confines. It reminded me of a similar structure of wrestling from my former company, that of the Triangles beast. There was no real comparison to make though as the Halo dwarfed Triangles easily. It was a behemoth. I had a final look at the Halo before I turned my attention back to the ring for the first night of Culture Shock. There was a slight mist coming in over the Olympic stadium as I reached the ring and slid in. It was descending ever so slowly and added to the already mystical aura of the stadium. As I stood in the centre of the arena, the edges of the spectator area was being clouded in obscurity, hiding itself from plain view thanks to the mist. As it hung low and reached my forehead I could feel the damp dew moistening my brow. It truly was a sight to behold, as the stadium became like a relic of the past, shrouded in mystery and waiting to be explored. In just a few short days the stadium would be packed to the rooftops with screaming fans, fans who were happy to see the full cohort of PRIME gracing their presence. For sure they were going to boo certain people but that was part of the job. The main thing about it was that there would be some sort of reaction to begin with. No reaction was a dead reaction. I knew for certain that it would not be one that I was going to get but standing here in the centre it presented a daunting visage of hundreds upon hundreds of people booing and wishing harm upon your name. Normally I relished such a thought…but now it felt different. I thought then upon those whom I was to be fighting on the night, almost instantly and maybe, it was because of them that it was happening like this now. Of the two, arguably Ferguson was the more controversial and the more likely of the two to be destined for greatness in the company. He was the one that, whenever he finally sorted out his own troubles with an old enemy of mine, would certainly rise to the top of the company and make his claim. For now though, he was stuck paying his dues and restoring faith within the company. As for Troy…I’ll make no bones about it, she was being held back because she was a woman. The front office of this company would for sure put on the brave face about providing equal opportunity, probably even going so far as to using Lindsay as an example of such gender equality, but anyone in this business knows that she’s being held back. It’s a simple fact: people don’t pay to see women beating men, no matter how fair it might be, they just don’t want to see it because it isn’t in the psyche of the wrestling fan. The one thing they had in common though was their misfit status within the company. Both were being held back for different reasons but they were both undeniable stars in the grand scheme of things. So what do you do with people like that? Well, you milk them for all their worth. You can’t not have them on the show because they had name value, but you can’t push them higher up because of the reasons already stated. So, you milk them for all their worth with the younger talent on the company payroll. If they win, then you find out all you need to know about the hot young prospect. If they don’t, well it doesn’t hurt their name value too much because they aren’t expected to travel any higher. It was then that it hit me. In the middle of a shrouded arena, an arena that would pack itself soon enough, I found clarity and the light in a conversation I had had only a couple of hours ago. *** I was standing in the lobby of the hotel, my head spinning with lots of words that I didn’t want to spew forth but probably would before it was said and done. To say the commercial thing had been a disaster was a slight understatement. One of the booking committee had been kind enough to grace my presence after they found out the foul mood I was in from the advertising gig I’d been thrust into. Currently he was randomly spouting out some buzz words about economics and crap like that, stuff I didn’t need to know. ‘…so you see Jack it’s all about find the market equilibrium. You get me?’ He looked hopefully at me, a stupid looking grin on his face. I couldn’t believe that the booking committee had dissolved into straight out of college idiots but there you go. I calmly ignored his rant and attempted to ask him the question that I had been pressing him with for the fourth time. ‘I get you but you still haven’t answered my question. Why me? Why have I become the one to do all the promotional crap that none of the others higher up get to refuse with their ten-foot barge pole? Why do I get stuck with this crap?’ He frowned. ‘Do you honestly want my answer Jack?’ ‘Why would I be asking if I didn’t want it?’ The long sigh he made let me know it wasn’t going to be good. ‘You’re what we call a misfit journeyman. That’s what you’re known by the committee and until you can prove to us that you’re anything different…well you’ll be doing this for your stay in the company until that happens.’ He looked at me square in the eyes and shrugged his shoulders. He didn’t bother saying goodbye as he knew I wasn’t going to answer him. He simply walked away from the hotel lobby and back up to his room. *** Misfit journeyman, that’s what he’d called me and right now I honestly couldn’t disagree with him. You see, I was just like Troy and Ferguson; I had the name value from my recent success, but they couldn’t let me get anywhere for fear of my injuries and most likely the fact that I was pushing forty in a company who’s main talent were all around the mid to late twenties range. It had only struck me now of the comparison but it fit. That was what had disquieted me though. I shouldn’t be this kind of stereotype that they were forcing me into. I was better than that. I felt the feeling in my stomach as it welled up in me and I struggled to keep it at bay. Now I realised why they liked me with Suleimon. It wasn’t because we were entertaining or a tight knit group. It was because they were learning about him from our interactions. In this match that was going to take place, the only person they cared about really was the Captain. He was the young prospect with the mouth to put him places that he shouldn’t be normally. They didn’t care about Troy, or Ferguson or me. This was a stepping stone for another personal project they had amongst the company. And I felt sick to my stomach about it. The arena had lost its lustre now. The mist had drifted away and all that was left was a silent, black and dead stadium. Now, it was a warrior’s tomb. Hanging my head low, I made the trek back to the hotel, silently considering where I was to go from here. *** One of the only highlights that I had to look forward to for this trip was that I would be able to meet up with a very old friend, someone whom I hadn’t seen in person for well over ten years. In contrast to the plucky director that I’d encountered, this man was no stranger to the countrymen’s language that he shared his home with now. For the last decade or so of Victor Jacen’s life, he had spent it trawling through the cultural tradition of the Asian wrestling market. In contrast to the hustle and bustle of the work rate that I had suffered through and trudge for a good part of the decade, Victor was respected and honoured amongst the Japanese specifically as one of the few gaijin who had stuck it out. He had spent the last decade being paid well, treated well and generally didn’t have much to complain about. He like me, however was seen as that journeyman, never truly the star but always will have the name value no matter where he went. We met in the lobby and I instantly recognised him, largely because he hadn’t changed. He was an inch or two shorter than me, stocky and brutish. The toning of his muscle had lessened and you could see that it was more of a relaxed belly now. Truly he was enjoying the culture and its perks. His bald head shined brightly from the lights and the strong blue eyes beamed with pleasantness. He gripped my hand firmly and our eyes met with solid respect. ‘Brother, its is good to see you again!’ ‘Well Victor, you know me, I’ll never refuse an invitation from you and your extravagant dinners.’ I smiled and we headed for the exit, me following the lead of Victor to wherever he was going to bring me. We hailed a taxi and made our way to some very lavishly decorated looking restaurant. The name escaped my limited, feeble attempt at the language so I left it to my host to show the way. Time passed as we swapped stories over the delicacies that we were devouring with relish. Hearty times, bad times, and all the other times in between were thought of and offered up, but eventually the food and alcohol began to wear thin as the night wore on. Victor put his hand over the table onto my shoulder and looked at me straight on. ‘Jack, what’s bothering you? I may not see you much these days but I know that there’s something up with the way you’re listlessly passing through the conversation. You’re the listener in this and I’ve never so easily been able to talk my way around you.’ He had me right, but it was that proclamation from the booker earlier in the day that was bothering me. ‘Well…I was talking to some of the higher ups…and they’ve told me basically that I’m a journeyman.’ Surprisingly, Victor laughed out loud and slapped me on the cheek. My brow furrowed and I looked at him harshly. ‘You stupid man. Jack, they think you’re over the hill and are looking to cash in…and you’re believing it. That’s not the kind of person that I knew who’d be quick to temper and anger. Don’t you reckon you should be thinking of ways to prove them wrong?’ ‘I guess…’ ‘You guess nothing! You have the power within you, you’ve just got to use it right.’ ‘What do you mean?’ ‘Neither of us are kidding ourselves in that we’re getting on in years Jack, but you’re throwing yourself around that ring like you’ve got nothing to lose. Let’s face it, you have lots to lose. You’ve just got to tailor your style now. Approach it in the right way and you’ll be able to get places. And don’t forget that you pick your spots, you’ve got the experience. Don’t be stupid Jack, you’re pushing forty, you should know how to outwork these people by now.’ He was right. Victor generally was always right, I just never told him. But he was definitely right. The fact that I didn’t question or argue his point and simply nodded meant a lot. Victor smiled and we continued with dinner. As we left that evening Victor turned to me and told me to remember what he said. I wouldn’t forget because I was finally starting to believe those words. I could still have what I wanted, I could still achieve many things, it was just that I needed to adapt and survive. I’ve done it before, so why can’t I do it again? It was time to slow down a bit and reassess the situation. With my knees heavily strapped and body full of cuts and bruises…it was time to slow down. It starts at Culture Shock. Funny, how the name is so appropriate.
