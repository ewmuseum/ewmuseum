The Chameleon

9 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/9/08 “If you are going to play, decide on three things at the start: the rules of the game, the stakes, and the quitting time.” -Chinese Proverb September 8th, 2008 7:33 P.M. Jagged metal teeth dug into my hands, as I sat motionless on the curb. Handcuffs behind my back strangled my wrists, making tan skin an infuriated pink. Red and blue flashing lights spun in the dusky sky, shattering what would’ve otherwise been a beautiful sunset. “If you don’t start cooperating,” the cop closest to me scolded, “we’re gonna have to bring you in to the station.” His shoulders were as broad as a doorway and he pushed out his badge-adorned chest at me. “Cooperating with what? I’ve told you fifteen times that this is my mother’s house!” For half an hour, I’d been sitting like a common criminal, ordered not to move from my cozy curbside seat. And the parrot in the uniform did nothing but play bad cop, trying to usher me into a confession. “Sir,” he laughed softly, condescension packed tightly into that one word, “I find that very hard to believe.” “Believe it, piggy,” I snorted, patience worn down to a nub. Shedding the mask of dutiful public servant, he let a vile grin spread across his face. I watched–he certainly made sure I saw–as he brought his hand to his “baton,” a euphemism for what I knew was an instrument of fuck-you-upedness. The cop looked around, figuring out whether there were any witnesses around. “Listen up, you chink faggot,” the officer whispered, kneeling down and putting his face right in front of mine. “Keep giving me excuses, and I’ll beat your ass until you’re pissing black and blue.” The smile remained plastered on his face, reminding me that I was captive of his whims. This wouldn’t be a professional wrestler against a cop; it would be an unarmed, defenseless civilian against a trained operative of the law. “What’s your badge number, Officer…” I strained my eyes in the dim light to read the name on his shiny golden lapel. “…Bliss?” “Eat shit.” “I’ll pass. But I would like a badge number to give to the city council, when I tell them how the San Diego Police Department hands out racial epithets like free fuckin’ Tootsie Rolls.” Officer Bliss shook his head at me, villainous smile slightly faded. Without another word, he stepped back towards his patrol car, making some radio calls that I couldn’t decipher. I was jonesing. Irritable as a motherfucker and my spine felt like it was being gnawed on. In the cool onset of night, I couldn’t stop sweating, heat rippling across my chest and forehead. My attempt to get off Tylox had lasted less than eight hours of waking time. /~/~/~/~/~/ September 8th, 2008 10:11 A.M. “So I think I’m ready to start treatment,” I announced confidently into the mouthpiece. “I’ve got the Colossus monkey off my back, and I think it’s kickin’ time for this habit.” It wasn’t just the SDSU gym shorts that were making me feel comfortable, nor the plump armchair I was sitting in. It was the voice I expected from the other side of the phone. “You think?” Dr. DiPendo sounded sufficiently less excited than I felt about transitioning out of an addict’s life. No more waking up, choking on my own sweat, needing pills in the middle of a sleep cycle. No more hidden pills, stuffed discretely into a sandwich or a drink to avoid the explanations. No more of this iciness with DiPendo. It would all be on the table. “Yeah, I wanna be done with it. I’ve been thinking a lot about this, and I gotta do it.” Since that beautiful moment in the sun, in Charlotte, I’d been thinking about its cost. The whole thing had been about redemption, but what had been redeemed? I felt great about etching my name into that immutable granite slab of history, but was I any different from the man who’d tortured Johnny Noble two years ago? Really? “Why now? You realized that the sun doesn’t shine any brighter on the top of the mountain?” “But it does, Doc.” Did I want to end this ridiculous reliance on chemical pain relief? Yes, but I wasn’t willing to feel guilty about where it had gotten me. “I just don’t like being a slave.” “Chandler, you have no business being in a wrestling ring right now. Physically, you’re as worn down as a man twice your age. Your back could literally explode with any one of the dozen impacts you take on a weekly basis.” He wasn’t telling me anything I didn’t know. When the first opinion hadn’t been to my liking, I’d sought a second. When the second wasn’t good enough, I found a doctor unscrupulous enough to prescribe me pain meds. And lots of them. “I know, Doc. I’ve gotten the lectures. But surgery just isn’t an option. The job is just as bad as the Tylox, maybe worse. I told myself that if I beat Shakur, I’d leave it all behind. Spent two weeks off of TV, playing with the idea of hanging up the boots.” I hadn’t told anyone about those two weeks. I’d spent them helping my mother with various chores around the house, just relaxing for once. Even though Alzheimer’s was beginning its siege on her body, she seemed happy, especially to have me around. And with what little time we had left…I was trading sex stories with Rayne. For that, I was inundated with guilt. “And?” “Something came up.” “It always does,” he replied, voice sharp without him having to change his tone one bit. “Not like that. These fuckers from SCCW came onto our show and they did some callous shit. And now, I’m supposed to be the figurehead and I can’t just turn my back on–” “You don’t have to be so defensive, Chandler.” He paused briefly, and I could just see him pulling off his glasses to pinch his forehead. It was his Holmes-ian manner of focusing his thoughts. “You haven’t taken that scrip today, have you?” “No,” I admitted, words falling numbly from my mouth. “You think that you can stop this by yourself?” “Plenty of people have kicked worse habits than this.” “Were they facing the pressure of being the face behind a multimillion dollar company? Not to even mention the recent stress of your father’s departure and your mother’s illness. I mean, you’re not even giving yourself a fighting chance, Chandler.” It didn’t feel half-assed, but when he said it like that, it was impossible to feel like I was giving this my full attention. I’d simply woken up, felt relatively good, and assumed that today would be the first day of the rest of my life. I damn sure wasn’t going to submit to Twelve Step Christian Propaganda. “I’ll come talk to you if I start fiending or something. Isn’t that what you do? Protect people from themselves?” DiPendo sighed, a sign that he felt ignored. It was weird how well I could understand a man whose job it was to be indecipherable. He was supposed to be inhuman in his diagnosis, removed from my world of problems and Tylox and Colossus and Ettore Calvino. I looked down at my watch. I’d been seeing the good doctor for over a year. It was now one of the handful of relationships in my life that had made it that long. “If you want me to fight your impulses for you, fine. But there’s only way I can suggest doing that, and you’re not going to like it.” “Try me.” “You need, at the very least, to take a couple weeks and step away from this lifestyle. I know a place up near Napa, it’s gorgeous. There’s a wonderful staff and a bunch of great doctors who really truly understand addiction. Most of them have suffered through some kind of their own. Drugs. Alcohol. Sex. You name it, someone there in a white coat has beaten it.” “You want…” I stammered, and the next piece of the sentence came out all jumbled. “…I should go to rehab?!” “I think that gravity of the situation demands that type of special attention, yes. It doesn’t make sense to try this if you’re not willing to sacrifice for it.” There it was. The “answer” to my problems was to give up my life, give up everything I’d worked for, and say “fuck it, the drugs are better than me.” “You know that I can’t do that.” “I think that you won’t do that. But I hope that you can search inside yourself and trust me when I say it’s the only way.” “What about weekends? What if I spent weekends there? I’ll ditch PRIME road shows. Tell them it’s about my mom.” Usually, just the mention of her would’ve stacked melancholy on top of everything else, but I was flustered, unable to drum up the right emotion for now. “And, for the record, I don’t think there’s any way that you should be keeping this from your employers. It’s a damned miracle that they haven’t found out already.” It was. I was careful about never bringing a scrip bottle to shows, packing Tylox in plastic bags that I shoved in my pocket or left in my car. After how they’d crucified Killean over mere allegations, I knew that I might never be forgiven for full-blown addiction. I would be vilified. My Colossus win would be devalued, maybe even stripped. There would be a humiliating piss test waiting for me every week. “They don’t need to know. They think that, after Culture Shock, I got myself a crack team of holistic doctors. And it’s gonna stay that way until long after I’m retired. Don’t worry, though. This conversation will definitely make it into my memoirs.” “Dammit, Chandler!” DiPendo remained unafraid of raising his voice at me. I thought back on Colossus, on whether it’d been a good idea to tell the Doc about my problem at all. All that came of it was bitching, further alienation, and further reliance on Tylox when I was having issues. Without the familiar shrink to fall back on, the panacea in my life had quickly become a couple Tylox and gin. Puts you right to sleep, and goddamn if the dream world doesn’t have fewer problems than the real one. “You’re throwing away a life for a career!” It seemed like a long time before I had anything to respond with. “Doc, you might be right. Probably are, from what I know about ya. But I can’t just walk away like that. Some people, sure. But not me. When I go, I’m going out with a twenty-one-gun salute. A fucking blaze of glory.” “You’re poisoning yourself, Chandler.” “We always want what we can’t have, eh Doc?” I tried to thaw the ice between us, but my laughter begat only long breaths from the other side of the phone. “Goodbye, Chandler.” I’d known the man for a little over a year. And now, it appeared our relationship would go the way of so many before it. /~/~/~/~/~/ September 8th, 2008 7:52 P.M. By the time I looked up and saw him, I’d been sitting alone for almost fifteen minutes. Officer Bliss was busy on his radio, probably ascertaining some actual information instead of guessing that I was lying. But my phone call, to the one man I knew would come, hadn’t been wasted. Not Gil Lavenstein. The last thing I wanted was my lawyer here to talk my ear off about just how moronic my actions had been. Not Tyler Rayne. He was busy keeping his own house in order, now that he carried Desade’s big red bullseye on the back of his head. Not Doc DiPendo. “Jesus, bruh, you been sneaking into houses now?” It brought a weak smile to my face to see the youngster. He was still sweaty and his gym clothes suggested that his workout had been interrupted by, well, me. “Kid, it’s not what you think. That’s my mom’s house.” Dawkins took a long look at the house, as if it would verify my response. He was in San Diego with Nikkie to see a doctor, but it looked like he was keeping to a strict regimen. I actually took the time out to feel a pang of pride, that my protégé was so…determined. If only I’d been that dedicated at twenty-two, I might’ve been happily retired by now. “That pissy cop over there thinks you were tryin’ to sneak in.” He gave me a strange sideways glance. “Ya do know there’s a door, right?” “I forgot my keys,” I said, which was the truth. “And I forgot my jacket inside.” Which was not. Inside the house were the last remnants of this week’s scrip. The pharmacy was wary about filling more than one a week, and I remembered that a couple pills were still sitting by the guest bed. I’d left them there one night, when I slept off a long day of chores under my mother’s roof. I’d gotten arrested trying to find those pills when a noisy neighbor called the cops. Officer Bliss had found me trying to lower myself out of the window. It was pathetic, really. And I prayed that Dawkins, in his eternal trust of people, extended me the same. “Bruh, if this is some kinda perverse lesson, I totally don’t get it.” I tried to laugh it off. This is how it would go now. Pretending like nothing was wrong, even when I felt my entire body quaking in anticipation of Tylox. “The lesson,” I chuckled, “is to not get caught.” Beneath the grin, I wanted to tell Dawkins the truth. Maybe even hoped that he noticed my dilated pupils. My labored breath. But what would Bryan Dawkins, good guy extraordinaire, know about addiction? No, he wasn’t going to save me from myself. And neither was anyone else.
