Courage

26 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9-26-05 'No matter how brilliant a man may be, he will never engender confidence in his subordinates and associates if he lacks simple honesty and moral courage.'-- J. Lawton Collins Courage is the quality that enables us to face our dangers, and deepest fears through confidence and resolution. It’s that little man deep down in your body called your confidence telling you what’s right and what’s wrong. It comes straight from the heart and is in control of the daily operations in your whole body. Courage isn’t distinct in many but fear exists in everyone. While those who know what they fear can overcome it with confidence, being brave doesn't mean there's no fear within. The rosy glow of evening light centered around the golden sun. The birds flew swiftly into the sunset searching desperately for a warmer home, far away from the coming of the North’s freezing winter. Standing leaning against a dirty black Crown Victoria stood Chris Collins ever so lazily. He was certainly dressed for the weather, wearing a turtle neck sweater neatly stitched by Ralph Lauren, blue jean pants, wheat timberland boots, and a beanie wore tightly over the curly brown locks in his head. Chris lifted cuffed hands up to his lips and blew into them for heat. “It’s really cold out here.” He could see his breath within the air blowing once more for the pleasure of himself. He lifted himself from the car, pulled the beanie further down his head over his thick eye brows, and bent down slightly looking into a side mirror from the car. At once he noticed his rosy cheeks, placing his hands and massaging them in circles. “What are you doing this for anyway?” Chris asked. “This car is nothing but a piece of junk.” From under the car a figure slid, back against a skateboard. Before you could make out a face, two green mittens grasped around the body of the car and pulled from underneath a head. Right away, from the thick glasses, and goofy looking face you could tell that it was Donald. “Look.” Donald said. “I’ve had this car ever since I was sixteen. It has always been a pleasure to me, never let me down. The least I could do is give to it the respect it deserves.” Chris frowned. “Respect? It’s a car, Donald.” He lifted himself from the mirror and returned to his leaning position. This time, placing the back of his right foot, into the body’s frame. “My great grandpa gave me this car.” “So.” “He died two years ago.” “Yeah, I know.” Chris told him. “God bless his soul too. But don’t you think you’re getting a little ridiculous with this car? You’ve had it for over ten years.” Donald shook his head before sliding back underneath the car with the use of his hands. “We all can’t just go out and buy a car anytime we want like you, Chris.” Donald said with a tad bit of jealousy. “You don’t always have to rub your wealth in my face, you know?” “Rub my wealth in your face?” Collins questioned. “When have I ever done that? I was simply questioning your future with this car. It’s not safe; I’m just worried about you. That’s all.” Donald sighed. You could not only hear him but you could also see his breath coming like smoke from underneath the car. “I’m sorry.” He confessed. “I’m just so sick and tired of putting money and hard work into this car. What happened to the good ole days? I remember when this baby used to run like a dream.” Donald once again slid from underneath the car. He leaned forward and removed himself from the skateboard, kicking it into the grass in frustration. He brushed the dirt from his blue sweat pants off of him with both hands and zipped his hooded coat up to the top of his neck. “Don’t worry about it.” Chris tried to calm him. “Good things come to those who wait. Besides, it isn’t that bad.” “Maybe you’re right.” Donald admitted. “It gets me from point A to point B. That’s all that really matters.” “Exactly,” Chris agreed. “Let’s take your mind off of it for a while and warm up in the house with a nice hot mug of Hot Chocolate.” “Hot chocolate?” Donald asked. “It isn’t that damn cold out here.” Chris nodded his head. “It really is.” “Whatever.” Donald shrugged. The two made their way towards the house. Chris leading as usual. When they reached the door, Chris kicked his boots off to the side, twisted the knob and slightly pushed it until it squeaked open. The fresh smell of pine delighted his nostrils with its forest smell. “I love the smell of your house.” He told Donald. “Thanks.” Donald shrugged. “I guess.” Donald took a step forward into his house after Chris. He too removed his shoes and placed them to the side in a neat fashion. You could tell by the neatness of his house and the cleanliness of his personality that he was just a tidy guy. Maybe too much of a neat freak. He pulled Chris’ boots into the house and lined them up against the wall, checking for dirt first of course. Before walking into the kitchen he pulled the door closed behind him. “Where’s the mix?” Chris yelled from the kitchen. “In the cabinet above the microwave,” Donald answered. “Oh.” Chris turned the faucet on, and from the dishwasher pulled two mugs. He filled them both halfway with water and placed them into the microwave on two minutes. He then grabbed from a drawer two spoons, placing them on a napkin on the table. “So, how has life been treating you?” Donald tried to strike up a minor conversation. “Okay I guess.” Chris replied, anxious to change the topic of the conversation. “I noticed that you’re involved in a battle royal or something in PRIME?” Donald asked. “What’s all that about?” “Nothing, really.” Chris lied. In fact it was probably the biggest match of his PRIME career. “The last three survivors of the battle reenter the Jewel in the Crown tournament in the second round. Nothing too spectacular I guess.” “Huh?” Donald asked confused. “Nothing too spectacular? This is exactly what you need to jump start your career in PRIME. No offense, but you’re not really doing as well as you should right now.” From the microwave came three beeps cuing the finish of the water. Collins noticed this right away and upon the sound reached forward, pushing the button to release the door. “It’s like you don’t ever care about wrestling anymore.” Donald continued. “You used to love it. You gave it your all… and now it’s like you don’t even try.” “How do you figure?” “Simple. You come from a long line of federations and in most of them you’ve rose to the top in no time.” He took a pause. “Here in PRIME you’re struggling.” “I’m not struggling, it’s just that…” He couldn’t go any further. “You’re right. I’m struggling. But it’s too late.” He opened two packets of Hot Chocolate mix and poured one of each into a separate mug. He carefully walked the mugs to the table, placed the spoons inside and took a seat opposite of Donald. “It’s never too late.” “No, you just don’t understand. I just don’t have it anymore.” “You’ll always have it. Until the day you die.” Donald told him. He pulled his mug of cocoa towards him taking a small sip, burning his tongue. “It’s how you use it that matters.” “I don’t understand.” “You’re just scared that’s all. You’ll get over it.” “Scared? Me? You’re crazy. I’ve fought tougher men.” Donald cut him off. “Exactly, it’s not the people I’m referring to. You’re letting this silly sickness get into your way.” “It’s more like a disease.” “It’s just sickle cell. You’ll survive.” Donald continued. “Besides you’ve had it all this time without knowing and you’ve done just fine. Just don’t think about it.” “I can die any day now Donald.” Chris told him. “It’s not that simple.” “My point exactly. A great man once told me to live each day to the fullest because tomorrow is not always guaranteed.” Chris laughed. “Whoever told you that is a moron.” “Well,” Donald paused. “You must be the moron because it was you.” “I told you that crap?” “Yeah!” Donald said excitedly. “You’ve always given me advice and I’ve always took it into consideration. Now it’s your turn.” Donald took another sip from his mug. “Go out there and be brave. If you really want it that bad, it’ll come to you.” Chris thought for a minute before nodding his head in agreement. “You’re right.” “Of course I’m right.” Chris lifted his mug into the air and urged for Donald to do so as well. He moved his hand in closer allowing their glasses to meet and almost break with a clinging noise. “Here’s to a new beginning in PRIME,” said Donald. “A new beginning,” Chris agreed. fin.
