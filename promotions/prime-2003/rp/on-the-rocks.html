On the Rocks

Date: The year from hell It looked a good seventy… probably eighty feet to the river below. Scooting along the edge of the bridge, Kaiser repositioned himself, wanting to make sure his target beneath was the pile of rocks and broken up concrete, and not that of the slowly flowing water. If he was going to do this, he was going to do it a hundred percent. He took a few deep breaths, let his mind wonder to thoughts of a year gone terribly wrong, and then… with a simple push… removed himself from the ledge. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Winter. 2007 “Calm down, Anth. If you don’t stop bouncing in your fucking chair, you’re gonna look like you’ve got some sort of hyperactive disorder when she gets here. Not the best first impression to make, ya know?” Kaiser grabbed a breadstick from the basket in the center of the table and slammed its tip into the marinara sauce, letting half of it fall with a ‘glop’ on the table before the bread reached his mouth. “Sorry, man. It’s just that… you talk about Michelle a lot, and you always make her out to sound like a real amazing girl.” “My little sister means a lot to me.” Kaiser swiped a napkin from the chrome holder and cleaned up his marinara mess. “Close family and all that. We’re the type that makes up holidays so that there’s a reason to get the gang together for a cookout or something. Always been that way.” “I came from the type of family who ate dinner on trays in front of the TV, and if anyone dared try and make pleasant conversation over someone’s favorite show… death threats were often made.” “Well, if you hit it off with Michelle, be prepared for more family get-togethers than you can stomach.” The two friends sat for a few moments, making quick work of the breadsticks and soda that had been brought to them a couple minutes earlier. Anthony Barker pulled at his napkin, like a nervous fourteen year old on his first date, while Kaiser stared off aimlessly at the large clock in the middle of the pizzeria. It was set to run at twenty-times normal speed, and so, as the second hand whirled around at a ridiculous pace, the clock kept its twisted time. “She’s late.” Anthony caught his buddy staring at the clock and chimed in with his typical humor. “By an hour and half.” “Funny. Michelle and her friends kinda just come and go. They’re rarely anywhere at a set time. Besides, you make it sound like you’ve got some big hot date. All she’s doing is taking a few minutes to pull away from her friends long enough to say ‘hi’ before they settle in to their Friday night ritual.” As if on cue, the door to Armand’s swung open and a group of late-teen/early twenty something ladies came laughing into the building. They waved to Roberto, the large laid-back cook who always worked the second-shift on Fridays, before making their way over to a large round table towards the front of the restaurant. “That’s them.” “Is that a question?” Kaiser cocked an eyebrow towards Anthony. “No. But this is… which one’s her?” Kaiser just laughed and threw a hand up in the air, waving it back and forth once to catch his sister’s attention. Halfway across the floor, in response, a smiling attractive brunette whispered something to her friends and excused herself from the table. “Oh…Jesus.” “Might want to pick up that jaw.” Kaiser grabbed an empty chair from the table next to him and pulled it up to their own. “Hey, sis!” “Hey, Bub!” Michelle took a seat across from the two boys, the smile still firmly etched on her face. “Bub?” Anthony turned with a laugh and shot Kaiser a ‘you kidding me?’ look. “I couldn’t say ‘Kaiser’ when I was a baby, so I called him ‘Bub’. Just kinda stuck with me.” She extended her hand across the table. “I’m Michelle. You must be Anthony.” “Yeah.” Tony awkwardly shook her hand. “That’s me.” “It’s a pleasure to meet you! Kaiser speaks pretty highly of you.” “Likewise.” “So you two went to college together up in Illinois?” “Yeah. We ended up roomed together in the dorms freshman year and had a blast. Rented an apartment sophomore year. Almost didn’t make it to our junior year… and the rest is history.” “Anth actually has a cousin who grew up in Jackson.” Kaiser inserted himself in the conversation. “Went to school at Wingfield High. So he had been to the area a few times growing up.” “Your brother used that as a selling point when he convinced me to move down here a few months ago and join him at the JFD.” “Don’t they have fires to fight in Chicago?” With a quick laugh, Michelle followed up her own question. “Must have been some really good times in college!” “Heh… yeah. What can I say. Trying to recapture the glory days!” “You’re not still bonging beers, are you?” “No.” Anthony hesitated for a moment. “Was that the right answer?” “Haha. Yeah. That was the right answer.” “You two don’t mind me over here.” Kaiser folded his arms across his chest and pretended to feel left out. “Aww, he’s gonna start pouting.” Michelle reached across the table and gave Kaiser a playful pat on the cheek. “I’ve got to get back to the girls, anyway. I’ll let you two boys continue your date. It was a pleasure to meet you Anthony. Look forward to seein’ ya again!” “Same here.” Anthony said goodbye with another handshake. “Love ya, bub!” Michelle gave her brother a quick hug around the neck.” “Take care, Michelle.” With a final polite smile, Michelle took off across the floor, heading back to her table with a youthful hop in her step. “Geez, Kaiser… she’s gorgeous.” Anthony leaned back in his chair and put his hands behind his head. “I think I’m in love.” “In just three minutes, huh? That’s impressive.” “Hey, I can’t help it, man. That girl has just got something about her. Can’t help it if I know what I like!” “Hahaha… easy Anth.” Kaiser gave his pal a friendly shove in the shoulder. “That’s my baby sister you’re talking about.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ He wasn’t sure exactly how fast he was falling. He’d read somewhere that a human’s terminal velocity was about 120mph, and that the rate of acceleration was about 32 feet per second. As he fell through air, Kaiser realized that his mind must have been working at an even greater pace than his descent to even have time to process the thought. At less than a hundred feet, he had just a couple seconds before he’d be on the rocks. Like a fine glass of Scotch. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Spring 2007 “That fucking building is outta control!” Pulling his helmet and oxygen mask from his face, the man collapsed at the feet of his Fire Chief, clutching the asphalt with his fingers. “Are all the civilians out? What’s our count?!” Chief Devon House looked around frantically for any of his men who could answer his question. “As far as we can tell, all accounted for.” Another firefighter chimed in, police officer by his side. “There’s a couple families not out here in the parking lot, but neighbors say they’re on vacation. And one single… guy who works third shift.” “Are we sure?” “We’re sure, Chief. Unless all the neighbors are wrong about those vacations.” Things were getting ugly fast. The apartment complex had burst into flames at three in the morning, which had its advantages and disadvantages for the Jackson Fire Department. On the up side, at three in the morning, most everyone was home asleep… so there was little confusion trying to figure out who was trapped in the building as opposed to who was just out for that afternoon at work, school, whatever. Then there was the downside. At three o’clock in the morning… most everyone was home asleep. That left a lot of disoriented, terrified people to evacuate. And with this particular blaze, raging out of control, that meant a heavy responsibility for the firefighters. “Ok.” The Chief took a heavy breath and rubbed his jaw. The apartment complex had been swept. The neighbors were vouching for the few not in their apartments. He had to take it or leave it. “That building could come down any second. If we’ve got it clear, I want all my men out of there.” “We’re short two, sir.” “Two?” Chief House spun on his heels in the direction of the voice. “Who do we have still in there?” “Kaiser ain’t here, sir.” “Who the hell is with him?” There was a pause from those around the chief, the sort of pause that told him several people had the answer, but no one wanted to give it. “…it’s Anthony, sir.” “Shit.” The chief’s response was far more subdued than it would have been had the silence not already given him his answer. “Who in God’s name let that happen? Huh?! After everything that’s gone down in the last couple months... who the FUCK let them go in there together?!” “Things were crazy. We just…” It was a justification that didn’t need to be finished, and it didn’t get a chance. Halfway through the firefighter’s frantic speech, a figure emerged from the blasting orange glow that illuminated the main entrance to the building. With a stumbled step, Kaiser Vashaun pulled his gear from his face. “Kaiser!” One of the men rushed towards his brother in uniform. Another followed two steps behind, each one putting a shoulder under Kaiser’s arm to help him away from the intense heat of the blaze. Back away from the hell, standing under the blinking lights of fire trucks and cop cars, one of the members of the JFD turned to Chief House. “Where the hell is Anth?” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The rocks suddenly seemed much larger. Much closer. And then they were in his teeth. Kaiser felt their cold jagged surface all over his body. Against his left ear, he heard the sound of the river rolling passed, the occasional splash of water smacking against the rocks… much as he had. Suddenly, he noticed a shadow, tall in form, sweep over him. A hand reached down in front of his face, and Kaiser took the offer climbing back to his feet. Before he looked to see the figure in front of him, Kaiser took a hand and brushed the small bits of dirt and gravel from his shirt and pants. Then, with a slow tilt of the head, he looked up. “You again.” “Yup. Me again.” “Ya know, for a guy who recently passed away, I’ve been seeing a lot of you lately, Dad.” “What do you expect? When I saw this crazy-ass leap from a bridge, I figured I had to meet you down here at the bottom and see what this whole thing was about.” “Gathering information.” “I see. So this was some sort of ‘lesson learned’ type deal. This little stunt was a sorta self-examination?” “I guess you could say that.” Kaiser looked up at the bridge where his journey had begun. It looked like a long ways from that vantage point too. “And what did you gather from this? No matter how hard you fall, you’ll always get back up?” “Not quite. Little deep for me.” “That no matter what… you’ll always land on your feet?” “Hah. Dad, you had to peel me off that slab of concrete and rock.” Kaiser turned and started to head up the bank towards the street, and his lone parked car, far above. “So, then what the hell did you even do it for?” Kaiser’s father folded his arms across his large chest as he watched his boy head up the steep grassy slope. “Just to see if I was so far gone that I’d actually jump.” Kaiser reached the top of the bank and hopped in his car. Firing up the engine, he pulled away from the curb and took the first right, turning onto the bridge. This time, to use it for its intended purpose.
