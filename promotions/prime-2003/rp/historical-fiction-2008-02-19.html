Date: 2/19/08 Fate leads him who follows it, and drags him who resists. -Plutarch September 22nd, 2005 1:43 P.M. I walked into the meeting with soaring hopes. All that was left before putting quill to paper was a meeting with PRIME’s talent relations and booking staff. They wanted to speak creatively, a euphemism I barely understood at the time. What they should have explained was that they indeed to tell me exactly how best I could be their puppet. But I had no idea. So I strolled into the conspicuously small office of Rob Smiley, assistant vice president of talent relations. The man demanded no respect, from the paltry office to his diminutive frame. But this wasn’t a podunk New Jersey scumbucket feeder fed; it was effing PRIME wrestling, all capital letters thankyouverymuch. I didn’t want anymore backstage enemies, so I played along right from the get-go. “Chandler, how the hell are ya, my man?” He barely grasped my hand before he pointed to the only other chair in his room, as if I had been planning to sit on his filing cabinet. “Please, have a seat.” I felt overdressed in Gucci duds; Rob was just wearing a yellow Ignatius Lisieux t-shirt and blue track pants. But again, and I emphasize this, I just wanted to assimilate as quietly as possible. I had a seat. “Rob, I’m great, honestly.” And I was. Healthy, eager to get back in a ring after stinging rejection from so many other federations. The age thing seemed to be a factor everywhere. You just don’t make stars out of thirtysomethings with limited ring experience, they said. Well, I had worked on my mic skills and pulled out a few more aerial tricks in my repertoire. But the no’s kept coming until finally, PRIME called back. I remember thinking that my call would come from that raspy old cowboy, Taun Phaum. No such luck. A chippy little persistent guy named Brian who worked at the front desk was all I got for celebratory semantics: he called me, told me they needed one more meeting, and that, oh, I forgot to mention, you’re hired. “I mean, I’m so ready to get back in that ring. I think that I’ve got an assload to offer on the mic, too. I’ve been working with a-” “Slow down, Chan.” Rob was boisterous even in his diplomacy. He put a hand on his chin, ready to explain some deep intricacies of the business to me. “Let’s hold off on all that chatter for a second. Give me three adjectives to describe the product you want to be on TV.” I laughed. Ya know, because I thought he was joking. His smile didn’t fade, but he just kept those beady eyes trained on me, expecting something. When it hit me that Rob’s joke wasn’t one, I scrambled for answers. At that moment, my Sidekick buzzed in my pocket; the perfect storm of unprofessionalism descended on me. “I’m so sorry, Rob!” I flicked at the device in my pocket, grinning cheerily as I returned to the matter at hand. He, too, was grinning. “I guess my three adjectives would be: loved, hated, and feared.” It wasn’t your typical answer, which I figured was good for a guy with brain dead clientele. “Whoa there, man. How on earth you gonna do all that? The lower levels of this company are pretty black-and-white. Let’s go with: competitive, fresh, and intriguing.” Not so slowly, I was souring on this meeting. Why choose three bland, ambiguous descriptors? Was this some sort of human relations test? “We don’t want fans confused over whether to cheer or boo. It hurts the overall product,” he confided in a whisper. Rob seemed like one of those guys who likes to make every conversation seem like the sharing of a nuclear-scale secret. “I guess,” I lied, “that makes pretty good sense.” Did the big boys of wrestling really play this way? My thoughts whirled. I reached down to play with the cuff of my shirt. “Do you think we might get to talking about my on-air development?” Trying to ditch the pretense of caring, I shifted subjects while keeping my eyes down because I knew that Rob was still smiling at me. “We’ve actually got something pretty big lined up, Chandler. I’m gonna be honest with you.” With the sparkle of uninitiated excitement in my eyes, I got to blurting out preemptive thanks. “Jesus, I mean…well, I mean thanks obviously. I expected to pay my dues like anyone else but I know I can bring stuff to the table that no one else can.” Dreams of Colossus danced in my head. Big stages, bigger bumps, and, oh, the endorsement money. I was probably mirroring Rob’s goofy facial expression as I pulled myself together. “I know some of the guys around here have special gimmicks and whatnot, but I’m pretty sure I can go out there just as myself and really show the people something.” Rob’s hand fluttered before landing back on his chin. “Chandler, we’ve already spent a lot of money on demographic studies, market tests, things like that.” His tone was almost scolding. Had my suggestion offended him? Was he agreeing with me? This whole new business world had me spinning. “And we are pretty sure that our concept won’t just show the people something.” He paused, licking his lips for no reason other than to create pause. “It’ll blow them the hell away.” Now, at this point, my Johnson was perking up like nobody’s business. Dreams that I had thought would take years to cultivate were within my grasp. And Rob freakin’ Smiley was literally explaining just how great my life was about to get. I ran my fingers over the sweat on my palms, trying not to look overjoyed. “Sounds like your people have really got something.” He gave a hard nod, his head threatening to topple off and leave Ignatius Lisieux staring me in the face. “Have you ever heard of lucha libre?” “Yeah, totally.” I hadn’t. “I’m a big fan, actually. If I had to say, it’s one of my favorite things about sports entertainment today.” Rob’s face contorted, becoming very small, but he wasn’t deterred. “Lucha libre has a long tradition in this country and Mexico. So what we want to do…” He rumbled around in his small desk for something, his elbows nearly hitting the back wall as he navigated the clutter. “…like this!” He thrust a picture into my face with such force that I nearly fell out of my chair. The picture slipped to the floor face-down, forcing me to pick it up before I could see anything. “What do you think?” I looked at the picture, then looked back up at Rob through tremendously confused lenses. “Rob, this is a guy in a mask.” “You damn skippy!” He put up his hand for a high-five, which I weakly returned, looking back down at the picture. It was, indeed, a guy in a mask. Specifically, his mask had an eagle motif, with eye and mouth holes. Just as problematically, he was wearing a Speedo. “That’s Halcón! He was one of most promising juvy talents.” I severely noted the use of the past tense. “Was?” “Well, ya see, the guy behind the mask ran into some serious legal problems. So we had to ‘injure’ the character for a couple months before we could find someone else to put behind the mask. You, Chandler, that’s who we wanna put behind the mask! You’re the right build and everything. Isn’t that great news?” “Yes?” My voice got away from me, betraying serious doubts. Rob, thankfully, was so content with himself that he wouldn’t have noticed an earthquake. “The internet boards have been calling for this guy’s debut in PRIME for a long time. They think he’s been ‘injured’ just so we can call him up to the big leagues.” He reached awkwardly all the way across the desk, so far that he had to stand up, to nudge my shoulder. “Turns out they were right!” “I’m sorry,” I shook my head as I started, “but I don’t understand. Not that I’m opposed at all, but why would you put me in a mask. Isn’t my real identity a selling point?” Rob’s responding laugh answered my question well enough. “Wrestling isn’t about identity, Chandler. Nobody will ever feel strongly one way or another for Chandler Tsonda. But Halcón is an imaginary character; he can be a great hero or uncanny villain!” The chance of Rob creaming his pants had risen to ninety-three percent. “It’s a golden ticket, man!” I slumped into my chair, unwilling to go along with the idea but similarly uninterested in making waves in this first meeting. “Rob,” I wondered, “have you ever had any success with this type of idea?” Maybe there was some precedent for this type of thing. I hoped against hope, knowing next to nothing about national wrestling federations. “Oh sure. We just signed a guy to do the same thing. You two are gonna put on some insane as h e double hockey sticks stuff! And when it all ends in a couple months, you’re gonna go mask for mask at Colossus next year.” A marquee match at Colossus. The dream of disillusioned kids everywhere and it could be mine. But in my stomach, if not my heart, I knew the truth. I wasn’t going to hide my face and let fictional Halcón take all the glory. Hell or high water, I pledged to make Chandler Tsonda a star, in that moment. “I’m sorry Rob,” I shouted, pretending to realize something of great importance. “I just remembered that I need to pick up my mother’s medication and the pharmacy closes in twenty minutes. Is there any way…” I let myself drift, knowing Rob would finish the sentence for me. “No problemo. You wanna reschedule this for tomorrow?” I nodded and Rob filled in a conspicuously blank schedule with my name and the same time. “Good stuff, man.” “Tomorrow, I might wanna take things in a new direction,” I confessed. In these vague terms, I hoped to set a precedent, but Rob didn’t seem to notice. He was busy writing PRIME in block letters on the top of his planner. Rising from my seat, I couldn’t help but fulfill my curiosity. “Rob, I gotta ask.” He looked up, eager to help scratch the itch. “Who’s this other guy who wants to sit behind a mask until Colossus?” Acting again like he was Robert Hansen, he looked around his office to make sure the no one had apparated through the wall. Content that no one else was in the room, he cupped his hands around his mouth, then whispered to me. “Alright, don’t tell anybody.” I nodded as he grinned, extremely happy not only to have a secret but to let it slip. “They say this guy’s the next big thing, but he’s fighting the mask idea. Says he wants to make his own name. What a freakin’ idiot, right?” Clumsily laughing, I gulped and thought of my own fate. “The guy’s name is…uhh…let me look here…” This was clearly just another dramatic pause, as nothing on the desk pertained to much except takeout food. “Here we go,” he said, looking down at a Domino’s pamphlet. “His name is Daniel Ferguson. Wrestled under a mask for a long time but nobody knew it was him. I guess you’ll meet him sooner or later.” A kindred spirit, I thought to myself.
