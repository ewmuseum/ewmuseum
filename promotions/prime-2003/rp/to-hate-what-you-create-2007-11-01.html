To hate what you create

1 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served as recovered; withheld details are marked. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-01-07 Two oh four AM. He knew it was true because it read clear in the corner of his computer screen, and the black alarm clock across from his bed validated it. They were machines that could be altered to tell a different time, most assuredly. However, in this situation, he felt that there was no running away from the truth. Every second that ticked off the clock came with a crushing crescendo of wasted time, like a hammer colliding forcefully with a block of wood. The sound enveloped the surroundings, giving a sharp shock shooting through the ears of those nearby. Or would have, had anyone been nearby. Ironically enough, he was alone in his dorm room, something that was strangely unusual. [name withheld] was one year his senior, but seemed to be three years his junior when it came to his study habits. Then again, Aaron Dillon didn't exactly know what he needed to do study wise. He was whittling away his last few credits before earning his Bachelors in History Education. Aaron envied him many nights because he could sit around playing his Playstation 2 while he had to hammer away at blocks of texts. There was a yell outside, distinctly male. He didn't know who it was, just that it pissed him off because it forced him to lose his train of thought. It was like nobody was taking this work seriously but him. He had a distinct deadline he had to meet, and every word he typed in this roleplay was one that would be better spent on his pair of papers he needed to get done. It was Hell week in more ways than one. The week had began with The Inferno, his professor for his Introduction to Western Civilization class made commentary about spinning downward into the maw of Hell with each passing Canto. This notion rang much more true earlier that day. He'd spent all of Wednesday morning without sleep, working on his first paper and studying for a Structures of the English language class of which he was sure he was going to have to take again. There was coughing in the lobby of Weller Hall. It was two seventeen. He needed to begin his research for his paper on the subject of Walt Whitman's Leaves of Grass. A group of people were talking very loudly about wanting to smoke a bowl of weed. It upset him. Getting back his thought, he'd spent all of Wednesday in bed. Well, not all of it. Just the afternoon to night. He had called his friends in Alabama earlier in the day, wishing them a happy Samhain through their answering machine. His roommate had returned and explained to him why there was all the commotion outside. Apparently, Austin called the night 'Amateur Night'. His reasoning was that those that usually party can hold their booze, while those who need an excuse to get out found themselves getting totally trashed and loud. Aaron would have loved downing shot after shot of Jack Daniels at one of the local bars, but his studying came first. Besides, his wallet was empty save a credit card. He was finding it more and more difficult to piece together his ideas in a cogent manner. Oncoming deadlines were the bane of his existence now, his procrastinating spirit draining the will from him like a parasite. It was ironic that for nearly a half hour, he had spent his time writing a PRIME roleplay rather than do what he needed to do. To be honest, Aaron did not know what exactly he was going to do with his match against Winters, all of which was over the 5-Star Championship. The studying was draining him to the point where he found it difficult to muster the sort of energy to write something actually entertaining. He just wanted to get his work done. He had other writing projects at the same time, and was dealing constantly with a growing sense of lessened self worth in the circles of internet wrestling. But filling that gap was scholastic faith. His professor for later in the day, Doctor [name withheld], had put it to him that he was writing at a successful graduate level already, which moved him tremendously. But rather than write his paper for [name withheld]'s Research Writing course, he was busy dancing around this subject of what to write for PRIME. In reality, the context was interesting. He knew he was hurting himself by writing, but he could not stop. No matter how much he hated it. It was why he could never stay away from the hobby for any substantial amount of time. It was why he was always stuck writing at the last second, skating by the skin of his teeth each instance. He had heard from many that his writing was solid, that he was capable of being published, but there was a sort of revelation that what he was writing and how he was doing it would not exactly translate to his future goals. He'd heard from others that he was boring, and he could not disagree. His roommate was sleeping now, the light from Aaron's desk lamp keeping the room from being too dark. He'd shed his glasses and started chewing into the roleplay, determined to hammer a large chunk of it within the hour so he would have time to do some research, followed by writing the paper. He needed to get this one completely done, because it was due in less than twelve hours. "If you hate this so much, why the fuck you keep doin' it?" The voice was not outwardly spoken, but it was in Aaron's head. It was the voice of Brandon Youngblood. He could tell because it had a grating sound that drove a harpoon of sickness into his stomach whenever it came up. It was like grinding metal. He was not insane, or at least he didn't think he was. Sometimes, Youngblood would surface up at random times when Dillon was alone. He'd look through a mirror and cut Brandon Youngblood promos in a stream of consciousness manner that rarely made any sense. It was all too fitting. Thinking about Youngblood's voice brought up his weathered image. It was an interesting dichotomy, as Brandon was supposed to look like a worn man in the roleplays while still having a powerful authority in appearance. It moved with his mood, depending on how he felt at that given moment about his creation. Often times he felt nothing but hatred towards Brandon Youngblood. "Why do you hate me? You made me this way?" "You made yourself this way." Aaron's creative sense lied to Youngblood. They all shared the same brain, so both sides knew it was a lie. The next couple seconds passed by uneasily. It was two forty two. "I'm not the one who has radically changed myself. That's on you." Youngblood was very much different than he had been. Now, he was mirroring his inner ugliness, to show some form of justice in Dillon's mind. Brandon, at first, was a pretty boy wrestler who was like an unwrinkled brain. And as time passed, Aaron's renditions of his character radically shifted towards whatever he strictly desired. In about four years, Brandon Youngblood had gone from a pretty boy to a grizzled bastard. "Why did you do that to me Aaron?" He stopped for a moment. He was starting to feel the story leaving him. He was growing disinterested, so he instead reached towards his binder and copy of Leaves of Grass. It was the eighteen fifty five edition. He had been struggling with his notions as to what to write about even after he pinned it in his mind. At the same time, he was unsure if he could make a solid paper out of Whitman's questioning of 'but'. "Getting bored with my again, are yah?" Aaron did not dignify it with a response. His roommate shifted on his bed. Soon, Austin would be making annoying lip smacking noises in his sleep. Aaron knew he had put things off for far too long, and decided to shift attentions. But as always, he never could manage to stay focused. Fifteen minutes later, that voice was gnawing away at his psyche, peppering his tired brain with more questions. It was the first time Brandon actually had them, as usually he would sit in the recesses of Aaron's mind and grown stagnate like a puddle of water untouched for an extended period of time. The creeping mildew penetrated Brandon's imperfections and blade scars with a withered quality that transitioned back and forth whenever Aaron saw fit to mold him. Youngblood, in essence, was silly putty. A sculpting clay that never dried, but stayed transient, for at a moment's notice his creator could fundamentally change him without batting an eye. "Am I every going to stay one way? Or am I always gonna be in a state of flux?" It was still the first page of his essay, and in the half hour it had taken him to get that far he had a trio of disconnected thoughts and a block quote from A Song of Occupations. In thinking about what he was glossing over, he wondered if those PTC bastards who yammered on and on about what it takes to be a good writer could even remotely pick up upon the layers within something as obvious as Walt Whitman poetry. He told himself time and again that the work he was doing here transcended the base judging commentary one gets by examining the text and understanding how it fundamentally works. He'd heard through others how his peers loved names like Stephen King. The hack. It twisted his stomach into knots, from time to time, to think that he would pour himself so emotionally into a piece, only to be slapped aside for his usage of terminology. As if his prose needed to be stripped to the bare bones construct necessary to convey meaning to the unwashed masses. "You're not answering me Aaron. You're going on one of your rants. And you'll just end up feelin' shitty afterwards." And that was outward egotism. Always was. He was obliterating the sacred fourth wall because it was something he had wanted to do for over seven years. And only now did he have the confidence to do so. Or perhaps it was the fact that he didn't care anymore how he was received. He was not about to lick boot and change who he was just to get someplace. At the same time, he did not know what he was trying to achieve in all of this, and whether or not someplace was a universal construct of a self made mirage. "Stop looking at word counts you fuckin' bitch!" Brandon's roar snapped him out of his own lull, forcing him to face the biting reality that he was screwed. His ambition always got the better of him. And if one day that did not get him, it would be his laziness. Time was ticking by, and Walt Whitman was fading away from importance. Brandon Youngblood and his quest for a third 5-Star Championship were what was coming to mind. He knew how he felt about that. "You're not real. Ergo, you change when I think it is necessary." "Like a chameleon?" "No. More like the clay analogy that I presented above. You're not camouflaging yourself to escape me, because I know where you are." "So you're like God?" "No. You're aware of my existence. However, by making this non canon, I can write you totally different next time." He didn't say next week because he was not crazy. He wasn't about to demand matches every week, because he knew that doing that earlier in the year completely fucked him up to the point where he laid out in ash, his spirit fizzling out. "So you aren't going to keep me one way...are you?" The ellipses worked as a pause in Aaron's writing. It was a major tell, which probably carried over to other forms of creative writing save those when he had to keep a shroud over his identity. Aaron thought he had answered the question well enough to satisfy Brandon. What made the whole thing ridiculous was the fact that Brandon was connected to Aaron's identity and brain, so he KNEW all these answers to his questions before hand. But if he was to do that, there would not be anything worth writing about. "I told you Brandon. You change when I want to change you." "You're a poor writer Aaron. Using change twice in the same sentence." "What the fuck do you know about english? I made you to be a goddamn meat head who was good at numbers." "I don't know what constitutes good writing Aaron. But then again, I know I was supposed to be a stock broker from DC who did this as a passion thing. And there was gonna be a love story and all that lovey dovey bullshit." "I was barely eighteen at the time." "No. You were going on nineteen. I think." Aaron's mind sighed for him. On the exterior, he was too busy staring at the screen in front of him, going through his usually tedious processes as far as roleplaying is concerned. Word checks would be followed up by spell checking. If he didn't like a term, he'd throw it into dictionary dot com and come up with something a little bit more colorful. In doing so, he tried to open up his vernacular in ways, to try and adopt new words. If he was going to get blasted for using words that were too large for the common PTCer to warp around their brain, he was at least going to have a working understanding of how the word worked. He knew that last sentence was a stereotype, understanding that he might get into trouble with all the right people just even uttering the thought. The shot was disguised as an in character work though. That was his story if someone was to approach him about it. He did another word check and then looked downward at the page number. At this point, he would usually get away from what he was writing and simply do something else. But there was no time for that. There was no time to be doing something like this, because it was a sick joke. He was putting a goddamn internet wrestling roleplay that was rambling on and on over his college papers. His future was not resting on this one piece of creative writing, rather it rested upon his craft. Something he loved very much. Something he was feeling like he was getting better and better at. "So, Brandon, you know this is all window dressing. Right?" Brandon didn't respond because, for this moment, he was not about to humor the writer in Dillon. Whether it was intentional or because Aaron was so disinterested in the cat and mouse game erupting between the two was something he wanted to leave ambiguous, but he failed at that as he went into preaching mode. "I'll be frank with you Brandon. You know how most of this shit is going to go down. You know that I am rushing this and that I am most likely going to earn a loss for it. And you know why I change you on a whim. So why are we skirting around the issue?" The words that came from Brandon actually surprised him, because Aaron was so out his mind tired. "Why do you hate me?" He struggled to codify his reasoning. It was not love that Aaron Dillon felt for Brandon Youngblood. Maybe, at a past time, he would not have twists Brandon's branches to the point where he was a disgusting abomination. Aaron struggled to know when that would be, as all the characters he had ever written always ended up going down in proverbial flaws. "You short change me and you skirt around on me Aaron." "I short change myself writing your myopic bullshit!" A pause. "So you hate me because I'm not what you want me to be?" Aaron did not respond to that, because he did not really have the answer for that question at the moment. "But you created me this way Aaron. You made me this wife beating, drug abusing motherfucker who you teased redemption to. But I stopped believing in it a long time ago." "And why is that?" "Because I know all that is going to happen is me...gettin' my ass in a situation that...if you found yourself in...you'd probably off yourself." Aaron didn't respond again, but Brandon was ready to go. "Or maybe that's how I am going to go out. I know I ain't walking off into no goddamn sunset. You're gonna leave me broken and crippled." "Or dead." "You won't kill me. I'll kill myself. That is how you'll play it off though. That you had no other choice. It's fucked up." "What is?" "Are you gonna kill your first born child?" "Fuck no." "Then why are you gonna punish me? Why you always hurting me? What...are you so emotionally distant from yourself that you can not given me the fuckin' identity I need?" He was looking at the roleplay, and all he could see was dialogue. He knew that, from past commentary, that Matt was not a big fan of strictly speech driven roleplays. "I'm gonna have a lot of blood on my hands...ain't I?" "Yep." "And when you're done with me, you'll toss me in the garbage like a used condom." "Pretty much." "I don't like that." "You don't have much of a choice." "It's not my fault, Aaron. It's not my fault you wait until the absolute last minute to do these roleplays. It's not my fault that you are unfocused and staying away from writing for the sake of writing." "Can I be honest with you?" "Yeah, sure." "The reason I hate you is because you mirror me. You mirror all my failings as a writer. From that damned hand job angle with Cimon all the way down to getting fired for writing long ass soap operas in PRIME. I've failed you by failing myself." "You can always change that. You were going to write a series." "But you know how most of that goes Brandon." "Actually...I don't. Mostly because you ain't written it yet." "So, you going to be pissed when I lose you the match against Winters?" "I'd be lying if I said I wouldn't" Where was I again? Oh right...Whitman. "All the way down to a shitty ending?" Yes Aaron. All the way down to a shitty ending.
