Gypsy George

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Timeless Atascosa County was only thirty some odd miles away from San Antonio, but it may as well have been in another state; lumps of tallgrass belied the arid trappings, vast stretches of land cut into valleys with limestone high rises that had been sandblasted to a reflective shine. The law was stretched thin and the water even thinner. This place belonged to the lobos. Before Brandon had ever seen wrestling on television, there was the carnival that popped up every so often in the scorched terracotta thickets of the Rio Grande. The troupe looked like they’d come straight off of Astley inspired lithographs, filled with colorful attire and dapper exaggerations. There were the usual roller coasters and spinning rides sprinkled about the fairground, all of which promised to make even those with iron stomachs weak at the knees. Along with them were tents doubling as booths, banana tooth carnies haggling passersby to win stuffed animals and tickets just by hitting a bull’s-eye with a baseball or with a water pistol. The sweltering heat had long since reached its climax point, but as the lights from above shined down upon the crowd, Brandon couldn’t feel the difference; Texas summer felt like the surface of the sun to him, and even though he sweated in sheets, there was little that could be done to cool him down. His fingers teased his bulb of cotton candy, the threads melting and sticking to his oily fingertips. Clapping his fingers into the heels of his hands only spread the stickiness until eventually he couldn’t stand it. The same was true for his lips, thanks to the large bites he snapped from the top. Trying to tongue the sugar wrapped around his lips was useless. Up to that point in his life, he’d never been to a carnival. He’d only been in Texas a few months, the family moving there because his father had taken a job offer. Or maybe it was the need to get out of Winnipeg after what had happened to him and his younger brother Chris and to his father and to the man with spider leg fingers and popsicles who lived in the boarded up house down the street. But the Youngblood children were now left to their own designs, usually taking the local bus to a water park in the suburbs. Brandon had gotten to this place thanks to a nap that had been stretched out longer than he had intended, his aquafloral swim trunks and ‘shark attack’ shirt only serving to make him look like he was one of the cartoon caricatures thumping about the grounds. Even back then, he was frugal with his money; weeks of Lincoln allowances had built up in his wallet, giving him plenty to have fun with at the water park. But the carnival was a different animal, bringing out not only his competitive fire, but his dare devil demeanor; creaking rusty brackets on The Screamer were noticeable with each steel tremble, but once he went down the slope, all the sparks and loose jostling bolts didn’t seem to matter. He’d been riding too many rides, and the fact was he couldn’t throw a baseball all too well. It was time for something different. A crowd was filtering to the center of the fairground, to the big top tent that had begun buzzing with life somewhere between the fourth trip on the Mad Octopus and his eating of cotton candy. “Come one, come all!” a man on stilts standing two stories tall with red checkerboard pants and black suspenders roared into a speakerphone, his free hand flailing to maintain a grip on his top hat. “Come see the Man Mountain Gypsy George! All it costs is eight tickets to get in! Just eight more to get in the ring with him! Test your mettle—earn one hundred dollars by being the first in Texas to beat the Man Mountain!” Brandon shoved his hands into his trunks, fighting through the webbing to pull out some of the tickets that he’d earned over the course of the day. The line shoved him along, and as he thought about what was inside the tent’s canvas confines, his imagination went wild. He envisioned a giant of muscle, his face scarred beyond recognition and a long wiry beard that shuddered with each roar from his massive lungs. In his mind, he was a barbarian, a Viking, a monster who was afraid of nothing. He tried peeking inside the eroded holes of the burlap entrance, his shoulder jerked to the side as he teetered near the edge. The hand belonged to a woman colored porcelain, a top hat with peacock feathers fastened just above the brim resting on top of her raven black hair. Her ruby sequin vest shimmered through the second hand smoke from inside the tent. “Eight tickets, sonny boy.” She squealed, chewing on her vowels as she frantically motioned for him to hand his tickets over with her gloved fingers. Hers was a face of foreign and dark beauty, too young in appearance to be fully formed. The kiss of black lipstick licked up towards her apple cheeks, a makeshift Glasgow smile curling as she rapidly blinked her eyes. Choking on the thick haze, he handed them over, nodding his head as the line behind groaned in impatience. “Is that...is that enough?” “Sure thing, kiddo,” she said, looking away to help who was next. He pushed through the burlap, a cloud of smoke funneling through the open space at the apex of the tent. The inside was standing room only now, the chairs all filled to capacity. It was all pointed to a wrestling ring, a small sliver of separation from the barricade to the ring apron. Five people were lined up just outside the ring, held at bay with clowns who busied themselves making balloon animals for children who clumped together near the corners of the squared circle. He rushed forward, trying to get a better view of what was going to happen. As he neared the closest corner, the lights dimmed, the skip of boots growing louder as he scurried forward. Suddenly, he was shoved out of the way, his sticky hands planting on the dirt floor, tobacco juice and gum collecting on the webbing of his fingers. Pushing himself up, he rubbed his hands on his trunks, glancing forward to see who’d knocked him over. A spotlight shined overtop of the culprit, her hands reaching into her pants and producing a long oak cane that has been curled forward at the tip. “Welcome ladies and gentlemen, boy and girls, but hopefully not children of all ages,” she bellowed, her Glasgow smile seeming all the more apt. “I’m the ringmaster of this twisted circus, but it is my solemn pledge that any person who can take down my giant will earn themselves one hundred dollars. Five of your peers have dared test their valor in a contest of epic proportions, against a man who stands above all else.” The pitch of the crowds booing stopped her momentarily, her fingers tightening around her microphone. Parting other children with his elbows, Brandon wrapped his fingers around the guardrail, his eyes having trouble focusing because of the bright white light shining down on the ringmaster. “A man whose fingers tickle the clouds. A man who feasts on the strength of those who challenge him. I bring to you, ladies and gentlemen, the eighteenth wonder of the world. I bring to you...Gypsy! George!” The spotlight redirected its gaze towards a tapered canvas door to the northeast just before it exploded forward, a murmur shooting through the crowd as a pair of clowns struggled while jerking on slackless metal chains. Brandon’s heart raced as he stood on his tiptoes, struggling to see what was coming out. The crowd gasped as Gypsy George peeked his neck through the entrance, a few short steps closely following before he stood at his full height. Brandon’s mouth hung open, the nearly eight foot obsidian giant lurching forward as the clowns tried to maneuver him toward the ring. “To beat him, you [i]must[/i] pin him,” the ringmaster shouted as the majestic creature broke loose of his chains with one violent upward wrench. “You have five minutes to pin him.” Gypsy George was nothing like Brandon had envisioned; while he stood taller than any one he’d ever seen and his shoulders were broad as a bull, his limbs were sinewy and lithe. He looked awkward and pained as he moved. The only thing he wore was a pair of black sweatpants that were stretched to their limits and unable to cover his bony legs. Thickly weaved dreadlocks framed his ape shaped face. Each smile showed broken and missing teeth. His chest and back were covered in rippled and bubbled scar tissue. This wasn’t a mountain; it was a mangy husk. A wall of sound surrounded all corners as the giant stepped over the top rope, rolling his shoulders as he paced back and forth on hobbled steps. The first man in the ring wore a ten galloon hat and a shiny belt buckle shaped like the Lone Star State. He carried an air of arrogance. The bell rang. The cowboy charged Gypsy George. He threw a wild punch towards his chest. The blow connected, but the giant grabbed him by the wrist and yanked him forward. Palming his head, he shoved him backward. The cowboy’s hat darted into the crowd. The fans booed. One threw an apple that hit Gypsy George in the temple. Momentarily stunned, the giant wildly craned his head back and forth. The rowdy carnival goers seemed to know the nature of this beast. They teased him. They cheered on their hero. Snarling, the giant pivoted towards the cowboy. He wrapped his hands around his neck. With a single powerful roar, he tossed him out of the ring. The cowboy landed on his shoulder blade. Brandon could hear the snap. He could hear the anguished screams from mere inches away. The bell rang. The four others who wanted to challenge the beast became timid. The ringmaster chuckled into her microphone. Minutes passed, help reaching the cowboy and escorting him away. He babbled about his hat, tears streaming down his face as his arm limply swayed about with each dragging step. The next man to challenge was wide at the waist and wore a pair of snake skin boots. He rubbed the sweat pouring down his jowls with his hands, ambling around the Man Mountain. The bell rang. “Kill him! You can do it!” “Beat that nigger!” “Get him!” The pulse of the crowd was wild, their arms shaking the barricade. They threatened to spill over, to show this freak some south Texas justice. Gypsy George smiled. He laid down. Perplexed, the fat man looked around. The ringmaster jumped on the ring apron. Her gloves struggled to grab at his collar. He flopped forward, diving on top of the giant. She couldn’t reach him in time. It was too late. The bell rang. The crowd roared. Petrified, the ringmaster watched the glob of a man pump his fists in the air in triumph. Her eyes were daggers. She watched as the beast slinked away and back behind the curtain. Brandon watched as well. ---- “Five dollars.” It hadn’t been ten minutes since the pinfall, but the maelstrom had gone their separate ways. Even so, that didn’t mean a carnie wasn’t willing to do whatever it took to earn a few extra dollars. “Five dollars for what?” Brandon asked as his eyes lingered towards Sideshow building just a few feet away. “To meet Gypsy George, kid,” the man said, his hands fishing in his pockets. He wore a long beard and slicked back hair, all of which were gray. His squinting eyes couldn’t be trusted. “Just five dollars.” Brandon wasn’t sure about any of it. He didn’t have time to think; the man pulled him inside the tent, away from the eyes of others. The back end of the tent wasn’t like the front; it was cluttered with boxes, faintly lit with candles. The man yanked him by his wrist, tugging him along. Brandon tried to yell for help. The man lowered himself, doing his best to shush him. “Keep quiet. Just keep quiet.” A loud belch lingered in the air from the distance. Rounding the boxes, Brandon caught a glimpse of the Man Mountain. He tried digging his heads into the sod, tried to pivot away, but it didn’t work. The man kept pulling. Suddenly, Brandon wished he was at the water park. Gypsy George was in a cage that was far too small to comfortably contain him, forcing him to hunch over. His arm snaked between the bars, churning the lever to a phonograph that rested on top of a wooden end table. “How you doin’ Hubert?” he asked, his voice hoarse and raspy. “It isn’t time for that,” the carnie told him, letting go of Brandon’s wrist as old time jazz music started petering through the brass cylinder of the old relic. “I’ll try to keep her as long as I can.” The carnie rushed off, leaving the giant and child to be alone. They stared at each other for seconds, perplexed by the nature of the other. Gypsy George’s eyes were yellow in pigment, seemingly glowing through the faint light. “The meaning of life is a development of your paramental metabolism.” Brandon tried to steady his breathing. “His name is Django Reinhardt,” the giant buzzed, his arms motioning to the phonograph. “I liked him ever since I was a boy. He reminds me of where I grew up. He’s a gypsy. Are you a gypsy?” Youngblood shook his head no. Gypsy George chuckled, his throat catching soon after. When he coughed, he spat up blood. “Don’t worry kid, I don’t bite. Not unless the crystals tell me to.” “The crystals?” Brandon croaked, his legs carrying him forward. “Oh yes,” the giant nodded, reaching between the bars and pulling in a pair of oddly shaped shale bricks. He laid them down near his feet, pride beginning to take hold of his voice as he raised his arms. “These are crystals. They are as we understood in ancient Egypt. A means to speak...to communicate with the spirit world.” His upturned hands were caked with rudimentary scrawlings and blotches of astral plane maps dug in by knife point. “It is essential to develop and understand what we’re doing. That’s where these come in. I need you to put your hands on these.” “Why?” “You put your hands on the crystals, I put my hands over yours, and Ausare speaks to me. He knows all, and he is fair to all. I need to know if I can owe you the favor.” The gyspy giant spoke in a manner Brandon couldn’t understand, but he nodded his head anyways. “What favor?” “I wear a ring of scars,” the Man Mountain explained, his hands running across his chest. “Can’t let bad people do favors for me because it taints my soul, you see. You are part and parcel with the company you keep. And while I need to get out of here, I need to make sure you’re on the real. I need your help to escape, to let me free. But I got to listen to the crystals. It’s just the way it is.” Reaching forward, Brandon took the shale into his hands, the giant pressing his hands over top. He could feel the wounds on his hands through the gentle rub, the gypsy carrying a low hum as he closed his eyes. His breathing became erratic the more he focused, stress lines throbbing across his forehead. Guttural chokes caught in his throat. Sweaty palms were followed by heavy breathing. Each jolt and stagger made Brandon fearful, each squeeze making him want to release his grip. An intense breathless howl punched out of Gypsy George’s lips. His eyes shot wide open. The record began to skip. Brandon let go, digging his hands into his pockets. Gypsy George threw himself away from the child, shuddering as he pointed towards him. “Get away from me. Get away from me! [b]Get away![/b]” Startled by the sudden shift, Youngblood backpedaled, his skin breaking out in goose flesh as he watched the giant wrench away from his gaze. “You son of a bitch!” the ringmaster roared as she parted the through the door and stabbed her way towards the rattling cage of the Man Mountain, her cane prodding his ribs. Brandon dove behind a pair of boxes, peering towards the pair over the edge. “One hundred dollars! You think that money comes easy?” “The crystals—” The edge of her cane snapped across the crown of his skull. “This is the fifth stop I’ve had to pay yokel trash!” The next strike struck the opened gash. Splinters cut new scars in ribbon succession. “If you don’t beat them, you don’t eat.” She paced around the cage, rubbing her lipstick off on the cuff of her vest. “You think I like doing this to you?” she asked, sudden tears flooding down her cheeks as she reached inside the cage, cradling his head. Planting a kiss on the back of his oily dreadlocks, she rubbed his shoulders, his body twitching in shock. “You got to do your job, okay? You got to do your job. You understand, don’t you? You understand? Listen to me and not those crystals.” She was the thing that hurt him the most, the person who’d scarred his body beyond recognition. “If you’re going to shoot me,” the giant moaned, “then shoot me. That way, we all go home tonight.” Brandon watched the scene from the shadows, losing track of time. The bus had come and gone, but that didn’t matter anymore. All that mattered was the getting Gypsy George out of his cage. ---- The crowd filtered away as the carnival closed for the evening, the workers slinking into their usual groups. They chain smoked and drank whiskey like they’d been doing all day, the only difference being their staggered silence and the chirps of crickets. Tomorrow was sure to bring out even more people. In the heart of the carnival, Gypsy George snored uncomfortably, his head bleeding from the shot he’d taken from the fulcrum of the ringmaster’s cane. It wasn’t anything he wasn’t used to. He’d lived on the edge his entire life, his body prominently displaying his toughness through his scars. And while many might not consider him a true Man Mountain, all one needed to do was ask the cowboy he’d flung from the ring. All they needed to do was touch his broken collarbone to know just how dangerous this beast really was. Brandon tiptoed out of hiding, rubbing sleep out of his eyes with the heels of his hands. He needed to stay alert, needed to help this poor man out. The babbling about staying away didn’t matter; no man deserved to be treated so poorly, no matter how freakish in stature. He reached inside the cage, pulling out one of the shale bricks. The lock was large, but the edges of the loop and brace were corroded. There was only one way to do this without a key. Planting his feet firmly on the ground, Brandon gritted his teeth, slamming the edge of the shale against the body of the lock. The first strike caused Gypsy George to flinch, his hands gently swatting at his face. The second strike brought sparks like flint hitting stone. The third was followed by a fourth which was followed by a fifth. Each successive blow reverberated up his forearms. There wasn’t much keeping the lock in place. The sixth blow broke the lock free, the metal flopping to the ground. The hinge of the cage door squealed as he flicked it loose. Gypsy George began blinking his eyes. The cage door creaked open, Youngblood’s hand pressing against the giant’s thigh. The touch jolted him awake, hisses transforming to shakes. “You’re free,” Brandon beamed, his smile glistening in the flickering candle light. “I helped you. You don’t have to get beaten anymore.” There were no words that could bring Gypsy George back to reality, his limbs folding into the fetal position. One touch was all it took. One touch from a small child was all it took to rend this freak of nature to shreds. Perplexed, Brandon stared into the cage, trying his best to urge the giant on, to make him fight for himself despite the pain and sickness piercing his entire body. The first thing he’d seen from the giant was a powerful roar, his body breaking free of his chains. Now, before Brandon Youngblood, the man who tickled the clouds could only quiver and choke on his fear. He watched the giant die.
