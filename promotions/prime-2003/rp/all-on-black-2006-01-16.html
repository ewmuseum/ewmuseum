All On Black

16 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1-16-06 Atlantic City, New Jersey. No city in the United States has as much of a visual discrepancy between the rich and the poor. Other than the casinos, the entire city looks as if it is degrading. Decomposing. Wasting away. The warehouses, homes, even the Dairy Queens and Waffle Houses…everything is falling apart. Well, except the casinos, that is. They thrive, they grow, and they threaten to overwhelm everything that surrounds them. They’re the great overreaching, enveloping masses. It’s not a virus. Its growth spreads unchecked, threatening to choke the life out of us. It is the death of hope for so many. Wasted. Useless. It is a tumor, on the spine of an imperfect state. Brilliant. * * * * * So Kyle Lamen simply sat, flanked by two men in very, very dark suits. Even being as expert as Kyle was in combat, he was intimidated by these two. They said nothing. They moved not at all. They were as focused as anyone he’d ever seen, eyes hidden behind dark Armani sunglasses. He wondered if they meditated. In front of him was a middle-aged Latino woman in the uniform of the Trump Taj Mahal. She smiled, disarmingly. Probably the best the casino had to offer. Her face was pockmarked, but her smile was charming. All around them were the cameras of the casino. A pit boss with gray hair and the stink of cheap cigars passed once in a while, pacing…was it nervously? The high-roller’s room for roulette, off the main concourse was something this pit boss stalked. It was his private space on the Serengeti. And if Kyle dropped his gaze just a few inches, the roulette table. The big wheel and checkerboard pattern. “The player may now bet.” The Latino woman never stopped smiling. Kyle took his right hand that shook slightly, and pushed his stack of chips onto the square marked with a single black blot. “All on black, Pilar.” Kyle tried to be like the statue-men that sat next to him. He tried to meditate. Pilar spun the roulette wheel. Kyle muttered to himself, as if a mantra. “All on black.” * * * * * The idea is to take something terrible, and make it work for you. That’s the entire point of things, since Kyle was told by a skinny doctor that he only had a year to live. Take something terrible, and turn it into something wonderful, huh? He saw the card for ReVolution 87. Booked against Chet Worth, the mercenary. It clicked. And with that, Kyle punched his train ticket. Red Bank to Atlantic City. He was going to use the city for something great. He knew that luck wasn’t the issue. Luck is a strange god, and one he had no faith in. You create your own luck. Kyle knew that he didn’t have cancer because he drew the wrong card from the deck. It was because he’d done things in his life that led him down the path he did. He didn’t get cancer from luck, it was from the steroid cocktail he took back in CCW. Or from his family history. Or from the surgery back there in PCW. Or from Danny Ferguson’s Box Office Bomb, or half a dozen other wrestling injuries. And on the same token, he was going to Atlantic City not because he felt good luck. He did it because he knew that what he was doing wasn’t just for himself. A lot of it was for himself. For his soul, for his body, and for his conscience. But he was also doing this for any number of other people that this could help. He was going to take money from those who had too much, and give it to those who needed it. If what he was doing was illegal, he’d have called himself a Robin Hood. Granted, it didn’t work out in the first ten minutes of Empire Records…but hey, this is real life, not a movie. * * * * * Pilar’s eyes were shining in surprise. The pit boss had stopped his pacing and stared in abject horror over Kyle’s shoulder. Kyle felt the two security guards tense, but only slightly. “The player doubles his money!” Kyle didn’t want to smile. This isn’t exactly what he came here for. He just simply nodded, while his insides roiled and crashed like the icy sea outside the casino. Ah, Atlantic City in January…cold and commercial as ever. Kyle figured that Chet would probably like it here. Cold and commercial. Pilar handled the chips with aplomb, doubling Kyle’s stack, and using her stick to push the chips off the board and towards Kyle. He shook his head, his blue hair bobbing. He pushed the chips back to where they rested before Pilar had moved them. Except this time, the stack was twice as big as it was the previous time. The dealer did nothing else, other than stare, mouth agape. Kyle whispered half to himself, and half to the people watching around him. “All on black.” * * * * * Casinos sell hope. And they make a killing on it. There’s a new American dream, and everyone knows it. Instead of achieving their desires through hard work, toil…the “protestant work ethic” that the country was founded on…it’s now about winning the lottery. Winning American Idol. The sweepstakes. Indeed, I’m sure that’s how people on the outside look at the Dual Halo coming up at Culture Shock. It could be more about when you get your draw, than if you are really the best in the bunch. Then again, if you talk to most of the people in that ring, who’ve been in a Dual Halo, or been in the business for a while, they’d tell you that that same hard work and toil is why people win matches…be it the small ones where you jerk the curtain on ReVolution, or the big ones like the Halo. Last year, a lot of people were good. But Karina won because she, on that night, was the best. She wanted it the most. If you want something very, very badly, you don’t sign your name on a sweepstakes list. You go out and get it. And this is why, in a few week’s time, Kyle Lamen will win the Dual Halo. Because his “unique situation” has put him in the position where he will no longer trust to hope, or prayers, or wishing. After all, that has failed him. It’s not like he didn’t do all those things after being diagnosed with cancer. But they didn’t make him well. So Kyle went to Atlantic City, to do something that WILL make him well. He’s going to raise enough money to donate a million dollars to the American Cancer Society. Sure, first he’s going to try and do it the easy way. He’ll put his entire savings on black, and try to run his “luck” as far as he can. A hundred twenty thousand dollars, riding on black for as long as it can. Four times it turns up black, and he’s got his million. And if that doesn’t work, he’ll try something else. Charity events. Donations of time and energy. Begging. Panhandling to celebrities. Maybe he’d even try to get some money out of Danny, in exchange for a beating, or retirement... Ferguson. Goddamned Danny Ferguson. * * * * * “The player takes it again! Mr. Lamen wins nearly half a million dollars!” Now the pit boss is sweating, and so is Kyle. Pilar glows, assured of her huge tip. One of the security guards cracks his neck, menacingly. He’s trying to scare off this kid’s run of luck. Kyle, is either too stupid to be scared (a distinct possibility), or too stubborn. He gives his surroundings a quick glance…not really taking anything in, his head racing too quickly. “Um, yeah. I think I’m still going.” Pilar looks at him in utter disbelief. “Yeah, I’m sure. I’m still going. I told myself I’d stop at a million. Not at half a million. If half a million dollars would cure cancer, you’d think they’d have fixed it by now.” Everyone around, the pit boss…Pilar…the thugs, they just stare at Kyle, who just stares at the silver ball where it lays on number 33. “Yep. Okay then.” He pushes the chips back onto their blot. “All on black.” * * * * * For once, he’s got a plan. He’s not going to just lay down and take what’s coming to him. I mean, if Kyle Lamen were to do that, he’d be a hypocrite. He’s not going to just sit back and let the disease take him. He’s not going to let Danny Ferguson and his “A-List” continue to make a fool out of him. He’s going to go out and take what he can, to give what he can before his life ends. He’s going to get revenge on the Face-Eater. He’s going to put Danny Ferguson in his place. He’s going to take one last shot at PRIME glory. He could rehab, save his days. Save himself from a war he can’t win against three of PRIME’s best talents. Tsonda. Face-Eater. Ferguson. But he’s not going to. He'll beat the odds. He’s putting it all on black. * * * * * “Mr. Lamen is a millionaire.” Kyle shook his head in response. “No, I’m not. This money isn’t mine. It’s someone else’s. The American Cancer Society.” If you asked Kyle today, how he was feeling then, he’d tell you he had no idea. There was a point that he completely blacked out during. This is precisely what happened during this time: “Please, no one say anything for one minute. I beg you.” The people watching, which had grown to include the floor manager, obliged. Their silence was deafening. He said three words. “One more time.” Then five more. “One million. All on black.” * * * * * There’s so many things you can do with a million dollars. For people like Vangelus Olsig, for Tyler Nelson, who’ve been around money their entire lives…it doesn’t mean the same thing it does to a kid from Red Bank. Money does strange things to people. It makes Chet Worth do, well, whatever you want. It makes Tyler Nelson move like a panther in the business world. It’s changed Danny Ferguson, at least from who he was as El Spiffy! And maybe, it could be used to save Kyle, or Timmy, the kid from ReV 86…or someone else. Later this year, as his life reached its end, Kyle would think about this day...and about his former personal assistant, Sarah. He'd think about her money micro-management, and how she would have physcially prevented him from going anywhere near Atlantic City, or his savings account. These would be some of the only instances where he was glad she wasn't around anymore. * * * * * The little silver ball spins around. It bounces around, swirls. It feels like he feels. Jumping, jerking, slightly out of control. The wheel slows down, and Kyle and the ball, comes to rest. He looks at the silver ball, and a slow smile crawls across his face. All on black. * * * * * “One of these days / It’s gonna catch up to you / Throwing looks like those around / And one of these nights / I promise to you / I’ll soon be sleeping sound / As soon as I leave town.” – Alkaline Trio “All On Black”
