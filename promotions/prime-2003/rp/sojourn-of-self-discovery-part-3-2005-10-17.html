Sojourn of Self-Discovery, Part 3

17 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/17/2005 Applebee's isn't usually regarded as the paramount of celebratory eating... if you happen to live in a place like New York. In Pikeville, though, it's a special outing. Scott wasn't exactly happy, mind you. He had lost the Intense Title bout, he was out of the Jewel in the Crown tournament, so he wasn't in the best mood, but he was having dinner with his girl and an old friend. He stepped up to the bar, waving the barkeep over. Several of the people near him grumbled at someone large shoulder up... then took a look at him. Recognition waved over their faces and they all seemed to have a desire to be at the other end of the bar. Scott glared at the folks moving away; could you possibly work at being more insulting? Scott shook his head and waved at the barkeep again. The man was hesitant to approach, licking his lips and looking around nervously. “Uh, can I help you sir?” “Yeah, give me a tequila shooter, a strawberry daiquiri and a Black and Blue.” Scott reached into his hip pocket for his wallet, but the man on the other side of the polished bar shook his head. “I don't know how to do a Black and Blue, sir,” he sputtered quickly. “Hell you don't,” Scott replied. “I've gotten one here before.” “Oh...” he trailed off. “Let me... let me check real quick.” “Screw it,” Scott said. “Vodka on the rocks.” He stood there, watching this sweating bartender and wondering what exactly was going on. But, as it stood, he didn't care. He didn't want to sit around, hoping the waiter would show up to take their drink orders, as their service had already been slow enough. He pulled a twenty out of his wallet and laid it on the bar. The man came back with three glasses sitting on a tray. Scott scooped it up and waited, even more irritated, on his change. Threading his way through the restaurant with a tray at his size shouldn't be easy, but people were almost eager to get out of his way. Those that weren't only needed a hard glare... and Scott really didn't understand what was going on. It was nice to have people not getting in his way, but he was seeing people almost dive out of his way. He finally got to his table with Alyssa and none other than Gabriel Brown. They were off in the back corner, hidden away from the majority of the establishment. “Can't believe this,” Scott muttered, setting the tray down. Each of them took their own drink and he haphazardly tossed the empty tray onto an empty table. “The service in this place has gone to absolute suck.” “Yeah, well,” Gabe muttered into his drink, “never know what's going to happen.” He knew what was going on, but didn't really want to get into it. “So, big guy, I heard there was something of a blowup last week.” “Yeah,” Scott muttered, sipping his Vodka. “I lost to Sirrajin and kind of tore up all of backstage.” “The management went and instituted a bunch of rules,” Alyssa added in. “Wait, what?” Gabe said, his head jerking upward. “Like, in general or just for you?” “Just for him,” Alyssa answered. Scott didn't really want to talk about it. “Wow,” he replied. “All the places we went, they never put rules on you like that.” “We never worked in the bigtime together, Gabe,” Scott muttered back. “So listen,” Gabe said, quickly changing the subject, “what did you want to talk to me about?” Scott nodded, shaking his golden mane. “Yeah, here's the thing.” He sighed and pushed his glass away. “I've been trying to figure out... y'know, who I am. What exactly am I? I mean, I've gone around and asked people and stuff. Alyssa here says I'm this big, caring teddy bear and such. My old football coach... he used words like assassin, monster, beast and so on.” Gabe just sat there, searching his friend's face. His expression wasn't one of concern or fear, but of interest. He'd always thought of Scott as one person he knew that seemed to have it all together. He sipped at his shooter. “This has really been bugging you, hasn't it? I mean, for a while now?” “For the last couple of weeks,” the big man affirmed. “I just... I don't know, Gabe.” 'Scotty,” he said, leaning back in his chair, “look at it this way, alright? These people see the side you're presenting to them.” “But they don't fit together,” he replied. “How can a monster be a...” he trailed off, looking at Alyssa, “a monster?” “It's because you're not a monster,” Alyssa replied, slipping her arm through his. “Oh, now honey..” he started. “No,” Gabe interrupted, “she's right.” Pause. Scott sat there, looking at Gabe. “Say what?” he finally managed. “Scott, you aren't a monster. Look, we're sitting here, enjoying some drinks in each other's company and you haven't flipped the table over yet, even though the service is sucking bigtime.” Gabe smiled at him, one of those million dollar grins he busts out occasionally. “Okay...” Scott answered. “So do you care to put this stuff together?” “I think I've got it,” the smaller man replied. “You were a monster in tag teaming, in football and when someone threatened people close to you. But with those people, you were caring and all that mushy crap. I think it makes perfect sense.” “I'm... really not getting it,” Scott said. Gabe and Alyssa both smiled at him. “I think I know where he's coming from,” she said, rubbing her man's shoulder. “Really, big guy,” the former Invincible One said, “it ain't rocket science. Look, it's basically this: you're a really loyal guy. You were crazy in football because you didn't want to let your team down, and you were in a position to do something about it. You're a great tag guy because you work like crazy not to fail your partner. You worked so hard with me that I never considered that you let me down. If you can get Van out of his... own little world, he'd probably say the same.” Scott sat there, blinking in something of a dumbfounded haze. It was like Gabe had taken the broken pieces that had been floating in his head and clicked them together without a problem. While he'd been looking at this like a Rubix Cube, Gabriel had sorted it out like a four-piece puzzle. “I... that makes a lot of sense,” he muttered out. “I never thought of that.” “You probably wouldn't have, honestly,” Gabe replied. “It's because you aren't looking at yourself from the outside,” Alyssa smiled at him. “People looking at you can see a lot more than you can looking at yourself.” “Besides, self-examination usually turns into depression,” Gabe grinned at him. “I sure hope that by people looking at me, you mean folks that know me,” Scott replied. “What are you talking about?” Alyssa asked, alarmed. “Folks lately... they act like they're afraid of me...” “They don't know you,” Alyssa said, kissing his arm. “No,” he growled, “they sure as hell don't.”
