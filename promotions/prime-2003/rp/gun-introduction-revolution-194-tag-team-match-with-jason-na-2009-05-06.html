Gun. Introduction. (ReVolution 194, Tag Team Match with Jason Natas vs. Troy Douglas & David Noble)

6 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 6th, 2009 It takes a few days to get used to. You spend a few nights waking up every couple hours, sweating hot, heart thumping out of control. But it will start to fizzle out. Your stints of unconsciousness get longer, your mind doesn\'t wander back as often. And then one day you find that you didn\'t wake up during the night at all. Your mind is clear, and you feel as though you just dropped a four-hundred pound backpack off your shoulders. That\'s what freedom feels like. * * * * * * * Gun. Introduction * * * * * * * Somewhere in Iowa His steely blue eyes popped open. The back of his head rested on his big paws. He took a deep breath through his nostrils, blinking away the slumber. Wade sat up slowly, stretching his long arms before pulling up on the lever to the side of his seat, bringing the backrest to an upright position. He rubbed his palms against his eye sockets and cracked his neck, heaving a satisfied sigh. As if the Bad Dog didn\'t have enough trouble sleeping anyway, the recent events of PRIME certainly hadn\'t helped. When you\'re abandoned by everything you know; your dog, the people you care for, your own god-damn imagination, it makes the nights even more restless, you find yourself deeper and deeper into the bottle, and with an extra pack of cigarettes in your lungs. It carries on for a little while. The stress taking hold of the times you want to be most calm. But on this morning, Wade had dropped that backpack of stress, and for a man very set in his ways, the world all of a sudden seemed very, very new. He turned the key to his black Chevrolet Silverado, \"Black Betty\" as he had named it, filling the parking lot with the bellow of its engine. He rolled down his window, lighting a cigarette and hanging his arm out into the warm Iowa air. He nodded with contention as he pulled out of the road-side rest stop and onto the asphalt, pushing through the gears while the rockin\' sounds of Lynyrd Skynyrd faded off in the distance. * * * * * * * Wade drew his forearm across his mouth, wiping away droplets of coffee from his mustache. There were few scents the \'Bama Bruiser enjoyed more than the combination of a smoldering Marlboro Red and a steaming black coffee, the perfect blend of hot, bitter liquid and burning carcinogens to hone the senses. He pushed a dirty, egg-yolk stained plate to the side of the rustic wooden table he sat at. The diner was fairly quiet. A few old couples enjoying breakfast, an irritated blond waitress dressed in a green uniform and chewing bubble gum poured coffee into white mugs, placing them on a brown tray. The diner sat on the side of the road somewhere outside Omaha. He couldn\'t remember the name of the place. It hardly mattered. Wade had long since zoned the unhappy waitress and senior citizens out. His focus lay in a torn, wrinkled map, spread out in front of him at his table. Its a wonder that he could even read the thing. It had creases, literally, everywhere. Corners were shredded, frayed and torn. The words that were supposed to mark states and capitols were completely illegible. Coffee stains, whiskey stains, cigarette burns, and God knows what else gave the map more character than it needed, and that\'s before all the customizations. Every color of Sharpy, every design of pen, and every number of pencil littered the parchment, notes written here and there, jagged lines marking expeditions traveling in every possible direction The fact that it was a map of the United States would be lost to anyone else. But to Wade Elliott, this map was his autobiography. This was his ritual. It had no set schedule, but once every month or so, the Blue Collar Brawler would sit down, pull out his map, retrieve a writing utensil of any kind, and plot the next few weeks of travel. Before PRIME, this is was quite haphazard, but since the signing of his contract it had become much easier. Where the map once revealed only uncertainty it now yielded destinations, but luckily, how he made it to those locations was still up to him. In this case, two letters, \"UV,\" sat next to the little star marking Minneapolis in the state of Minnesota, where a jagged, light blue line that started in Nevada came to a stop. From those two letters he had started a new line, his medium a wide carpenter\'s pencil. His calloused fingers guided the pencil east, back down through Iowa, into Illinois, across India, down toward Kentucky, up... ...down. The pencil took on a mind of its own. Wade\'s eyes went out of focus as the tip of the writing instrument drifted toward Tennessee. Tennessee. The Southern Sparkplug\'s eyes squinted hard. He jolted upright, eyes snapping toward the wall, as if staring through it. In truth he was staring into the back of his own mind, the gears spinning like crazy. He exhaled heavily, lips curling inward. \"Sherman,\" he muttered, his deep, gravely voice pushing the name into the air. Lazily placing his near-finished cigarette between his lips, the Bad Dog slowly reached behind himself, his hand carefully running across the black handle of the .357 magnum known as \"Alice\" in his belt. He sucked in deep on his cigarette, and in quick, deliberate fashion, extinguished it on the state of Tennessee with a quiet \"thud.\" * * * * * * * Somewhere in Tennessee It echoed in his head, reverberated off the walls of his skull, like some colossal beast had taken a heavy step in the distance. Ellis Sherman\'s head snapped upward, beady brown eyes wide behind a pair of small, round bifocals. His jaw hung slightly, revealing tiny, yellowed teeth. \"No...\" his meek, aged voice whispered. He dropped his small screwdriver on a cluttered workbench, pushing himself back and standing from his stool. He shambled his hunched, stocky frame across the concrete floor of a dark, ill-lit shop, pushing a wooden door open and stepping into the threshold, staring out to an overcast Tennessee sky. He cupped a wrinkly hand over his mouth, dragging it down a wiry salt-and-pepper mustache. \"No no no no no...\" he whispered, the volume of his voice diminishing with each \"no.\" He turned back to his shop, a panicked look on his face. His eyes drew across the dozens and dozens of rifles and pistols of every size, shape and model, hanging from the walls and resting on shelves. He reached up to grab a dangling string that acted as the room\'s light switch. He quickly yanked it downward, hiding the weaponry in darkness, and slammed the door shut. \"No...\"
