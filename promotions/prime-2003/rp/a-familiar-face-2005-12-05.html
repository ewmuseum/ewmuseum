A Familiar Face

5 December 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/05/05 I have never made but one prayer to God, a very short one: ‘O Lord make my enemies ridiculous.’ And God granted it.-Voltaire Do you think people are born into greatness? I do. No, not think that people are; I know that I am. Yes, people are born into greatness and yes, I am one of them-that’s my final answer. Napoleon was born to be a great leader; otherwise why would God have made him such a pocket-sized little bundle of hatred? The same theory applies directly to Spud Webb. The first time I stepped into a PRIME ring, I felled a giant. His name was Hoyt Williams and call it what you want, but left the ring with a “W.” I’m the only person in a while to beat Hoyt one-on-one. Last week, it was Angelo Deville. He made his triumphant return to the squared circle in PRIME, only to find that the big bad 2GVC kid from PWC was all grown up; in fact, they were sharing a victory. Instead of Deville alone at the top, I was standing right next to him, grinning like a motherfucker. In between those two victories were two losses. Yes, Chris Collins was in both the matches, but let’s be reasonable adults about this: Fuck Chris Collins. No, the only common link between the two losses was Adam Dick. The Illustrious Face-Eater. What an awful, awful kid. I mean, really, he’s not a face or a heel? Does anyone like him? Put your hand down, Parks, we don’t care. Yeah, that’s what I thought. Not that I’m bitter about the fact that he ruined my chance to be Assistant General Manager, thus tossing the not-very-cool Asians out of power and placing the extremely cool one right into the driver’s seat. *phone rings* Ack, hold on a sec. Yes. Okay. Yes, I’m aware it’s “Assistant TO The General Manager.” Okay, won’t happen again. I just had to take that; they were trying to get me to buy a subscription to VIBE or something. Crazy black guys with their rap magazines. But where was I? Oh right, I’m not bitter at all that Face-Eater ruined one of my best career prospects in years, not at all. I’m certainly not gonna take out my rage on him in the ring on Wednesday, beating him until the ring is a big red carpet. Nope, not at all. Because I have lots of options. Options are a good thing. Options ensure that, after two horrid losses to Face-Eater or his team, one is not consigned to being a Pulse Division lackey *cough* everySpanishrostermembernotnamedRage *cough*. I’ve got options and I will now choose to take a very, very stupid one. * * * * I knocked on the door the house. I’d been here once or twice before, but now that everything was changed around, it creeped the shit out of me. It was like visiting that guy you knew in high school, the loudmouth guy who was good at everything and unworthy of all the hot girls he got, only to find that he’d become a strict fundamentalist Muslim. Well, it wasn’t really like that, because in this case I hadn’t known this guy before he was the Muslim and he wasn’t actually a Muslim, but god dammit we use extended analogies in this household, so suck it up. He opened the door and there I stared into the eyes of a PCW Intercontinental Champion who was grinning like an idiot, wearing a purple bathrobe. Rush Mason had clearly let himself go since he’d left the wrestling world, but he was still a colossal moron. “Oh hey there Tsonda baby, how you doin?” While ignoring the irony of two characters from the same handler just happening to know one another, I ignored the sarcastic come-ons he always made. Guy wasn’t funny in PCW and he wasn’t funny now. Unfortunately, Fergs wasn’t available and I wasn’t exactly opening myself up to fireside chats in PRIME with my behavior. Obviously, that was fine with me but I had a wrestling question. “I don’t have the time nor the energy to put up with niceties at the moment.” I think I said this every single time I ever saw the guy, but it never managed to work. He was still huge, at least 250, but he wasn’t as jacked as he had been in his hey day, if you could call what he had done at any point a hey day. “I called the witch doctor, he told me what to do,” I chimed. He gave a slight smile, but none of the deep-throated laugh that made him so popular backstage. For having been a face for a large part of the ol’ career, he wasn’t so bad; he was, however, irritating when he knew you wanted something from him. “Nice little singing voice you’ve got there, chinky. How can I be of service?” I was more on the defensive then he was; just a habit of being around guys much bigger then me, I guess. “Aren’t you going to invite me in?” Did I really expect to be invited in? Not really. I was testing him as much as he was testing me and both of us knew it. “Umm…if I say no, you’re not going to throw one of those tantrums that you gays are prone to, are you?” Gay jokes, Asian jokes, he was really running the gamut from tacky all the way to classless. There was a reason he’d been mired in the Extreme Division (in other words: Hardcore Curtain-Jerkers) in PCW and I’ll give you a little hint: it wasn’t bad writing. The guy was corny and immature like never seen before in the “people not named Hoyt” realm. Yes, it’s a pot shot, but HEY I BEAT HIM DID I TELL YOU THAT I BEAT HIM DID YOU HEAR DIDJA DIDJA DIDJA? “Before we start a racial joke contest, I have a semi-serious question, asshole.” It was like dealing with an infant, but such is the price of only having one even pseudo-friend who’s available and not off taking calls from Chuck Norris and Taye Diggs about starring in big Hollywood productions. Love you tons, Danny; call me. “So if I’m brewing up a nice arch-rival, a real nemesis of a guy, how do I go about beating him?” “You came to ask me a wrestling question? Bleh. I was hoping for something juicy like how do I attract more black women or you know, that Voodoo stuff sounds really interesting, how do I get involved?” Right, I forgot to mention that Rush had become the fucking high priest of Voodoo or something. Talk about taking a gimmick too far. “No, I’m sorry, I can’t join your cult, but I want to know how you’d go about beating someone if they’ve been a serious thorn in your side? And I want to beat him; I don’t want to cheat.” Pause. “Okay, I do want to cheat, but I won’t.” Pause. “Okay, fine I’ll cheat you asshole, but I want him to feel like he just got his ass handed to him fair and square.” I hated, and continue to hate, Face-Eater for being momentarily, nay, instantaneously better then me. But before he’d had the low-IQ buffer of Chris Collins et al. to hang on. I wanted a win and I wanted Face-Eater to get a big old satchel full of ownage. “Is he much bigger then you?” Come to think of it, no. Face-Eater was your average 5’9ish American male. He wasn’t any bigger then me, but still the obsession with beating him now that things were one-on-one raged. “No, he’s fucking…gah, no he’s like the same size as me.” Like some sort of wise sensei, Mason looked at me pensively, sizing me up. He stroked his goatee, slightly scraggly and uneven, leaning towards the left side of his chin. “Well, grasshopper, it’s all in your mind then.” This was not news to me. “Yeah, I came here because you’re supposed to tell me exactly what in my head is screwing me up. You’re supposed to tell me that if I balance my id and my superego, everything will be a-okay and I’ll beat Face-Eater to a ‘Tropicana would be envious’ pulp!” Why the hell had I even come here? This was my problem, not Rush Mason’s. What could he possibly do to help me in PRIME? We weren’t even the same generation of wrestler. “Well fuck, man, I don’t know what you should do.” Great, this trip had all been for naught. I began to storm off, pissed off at Mason for wasting my time. I had a lonely drive coming up and if Rush weren’t very much larger then me and mostly not that awful, I would’ve sucker punched him a long time ago. “Fuck you.” Cold, curt, and to the point. I had things to do and I wasn’t going to- “When he’s in your head, though, he wins every single time. If you really believe he’s that good, then he’ll whip you stupid, scrawny Vietnamese ass.” That was something. I turned and looked at the former Intercontinental Champion, who was grinning like an idiot. I nodded to him, then walked towards the car. There was much to think about before Wednesday; most importantly, when was I going to be able to stop going into these damn ReV matches without this must-win mentality? The answer was never and I knew it.
