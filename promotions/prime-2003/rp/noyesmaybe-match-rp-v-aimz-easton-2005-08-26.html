Date: 26/08/05 Noyesmaybe.Written: 26th August, 2005. 'And oh if you stay,I'll chase the rain-blown fields away.We'll shine like the morning, and sin in the sun,Oh, if you stay.We'll be the wild ones, running with the dogs today.' - Suede. I - The Junction. 'Marry me.' Where Angelo Deville's mind was methodical and calculating, Karina's was always impulsive and erratic. Seeing Karina down on one knee, possibly the only woman on the planet he didn't want at his feet, Angelo struggled to fathom the question, let alone the answer. He almost blurted out, 'why?' The first second that passed seemed to contain a hundred beats of Karina's fluttering heart, while the second let the blood stagnate between slowed pulses as she began to fear the worst. Had she made the biggest mistake of her life... or maybe, just maybe, taken the leap of faith needed to secure the life she wanted, past the persona, to her real dreams. Another second passed, and another hundred possibilities raced through her thoughts. Angelo's lips parted slightly, teasing that a reply was about to be formed, but only a torn sigh was released... while Kari's mind had already skipped on far past the moment. II - No. Sometimes, the fall hurts just as much as the landing. The bone-jarring impact against the surface can knock you out cold, numb you with shock, or just plain put you out of your misery. But the fall? That's the cruellest of tortures. You can either look up, longingly, tearfully at the height you've fallen from, safe, fertile... or you can submissively look down at the ground, closing your eyes and bracing for the impact. Magnum Wolf had been falling for almost a year, and the bottom wasn't merciful enough to rise and greet him. 'Yeah,' he wildly spat down his cell phone, 'well tell him to go fuck himself. In fact, get him to google me, then google his stupid fucking promotion and see him out-hit about 5 million to one. Fucking moron, I'd sell out whatever town hall he runs shows from 50 times over.' Those behind him in the queue politely tried to look away, while one aghast young mother ear-muffed her mischievously giggling child with her palms. With his free hand, Wolf rummaged deep in the pockets of his skin-tight jeans, the lack of competition and exercise leaving the stitching looking strained as tips fishing for change. 'Can I help you, sir?' the young, Indian man behind the fortress-like counter enquired politely, eyes managing to keep away from the suspect sight of Magnum's pocket rummage. 'Yeah,' Magz scoffed, slapping the phone shut before dropping it into his shirt pocket, 'gimme your cheapest Bourbon.' Looking mildly self-conscious, a rarity for him, Wolf stroked his moustache and shuffled his feet as the bottle was wrapped in brown paper. 'And, um, that one... bottom row, at the end.' With bloodshot, rapidly ageing eyes, Wolf gestured to the magazine rack behind the clerks, where enough silicone wss on show to float a Hippopotamus. Eyes scanned towards the end of the rack, the publications following an increasingly vulgar trend, until it hit border-line illegal. 'This o-' 'Yes,' Wolf stated dryly, suddenly feeling a judgemental pair of eyes burn through the straight fringe of his mullet. 'What?' The elderly woman behind him mumbled and spluttered, praying for his soul, which was already too far past salvation for him to care anymore. Dropping a couple of notes on the counter, Wolf used all but his last few coins to make up the amount showing on the register, before swiping his purchases and leaving without any pleasantries. 'Have a good day!' the clerk called after him, stifling laughter. 'Fucking toolbox,' Wolf grumbled as he barged through the doors, and into the untainted glare of the baking sun overhead. It had been raining all night, and the already muggy deluge was coloured a diarrhoea brown by the grime of the city streets. People walked around even the shallowest of puddles, fearing that even the merest of lose stitches would let all manner of filth into their shoes. As the unrelenting heat only rose with the hour, the puddles festered and steamed, unleashing a rotten, bodily stink throughout the claustrophobic streets. With his face souring, Magnum wafted his magazine in front of his nose, not even daring to open his mouth and let the vomit-inducing stink wither his taste buds. As he wandered past the travel agency, Wolf had to stiffen his neck, almost wrenching it to the side to avoid the temptation to look. During the glory days of his career, he'd travelled the world twice over, working arenas where he couldn't even begin to imagine where the crowd stopped behind the baking, blinding lights. He'd be paid amounts even he was embarrassed to take just for showing up and doing his thing on the stick. People hated him, it was his life's curse, which he milked so hard that it became a blessing. By all rights, such a despicable weasel of a human being would have been a pariah, but as part of an industry where the line between person and character grew increasingly blurred, it made Magnum Wolf a star. It was all going so, so well until he crossed paths with a young woman who was his equal in all aspects, from stubbornness to arrogance, from petty vindictiveness to stand-up fighting. Thinking that he was untouchable, Magnum underestimated her. Every sneaky whisper was drowned out by an accusing cry, every cheap shot trumped with a knock-out blow. He was the villain, she was the heroine. While he was discarded as a miserable human being, she was triumphed as wrestling's new darling. Every spotlight on her pushed Magnum further into the shadows, eclipsed by her rising star. Karina Wolfenden ruined his life. With a sigh carrying so much depression that it nearly turned him inside-out, Wolf scuffed his already worn soles, dragging himself away from the temptation of futile dreams, and into the spray of a passing delivery truck as it hurtled down the street. Before he could even take a step back, the opaque, vile water had splashed a wave up his body, between lips and through fabric. 'Mother FUCKER!' he roared after the vehicle, the words growing in a mixture of anguish and rage as he heard the Bourbon bottle shatter against the curb. As the liquor seeped through the brown paper and into the gutter, Wolf's fists clenched so hard that his un-trimmed nails pierced the skin, while grinding teeth nearly warranted a spewing of enamel dust. He drank to forget, and the crunch of broken glass underfoot meant that he'd have to suffer the indignity of his own memories for another day. Having the putrid stench embedded in his clothes left Magnum gagging the whole way home, not even his bushy moustache able to filter out the odour. With squelching steps he dragged himself up the stairwell, not even bothering to see if the permanently out of order elevator had been fixed. The sharp hit of ripening urine left Wolf wanting to tear his own lungs out as his steaming body reached the 10th floor. It was then, even through the choking fumes that exuded from his body, that he smelt her. A sweet, natural fragrance so saccharine that he wanted to vomit. It was instinct, firmly embedded in his psyche as a self-defence mechanism, like the tablets that leave alcoholics doubled-over, emptying themselves through their mouths if they sip so much as a light beer. The air seemed to strengthen, forcing the stale, decaying smells from the stairwell. After all, she was above 'his' air. 'No,' blurted out, devoid of his usual scathing inflection, as honest and personal word as he'd ever spoken. 'Whatever it is, no' His heart was doing its best to smash through the prison of his ribs, and make a dash for it... and for once, it wasn't because the only exercise he was getting lately was down to the local convenience store. His tongue was sticking to the roof of his mouth, where the scant amount of moisture left acted as an adhesive between the arid surfaces. She'd destroyed his career, and was now so close he could taste the vindictiveness. 'Wrong answer,' the all-too-familiar voice corrected him, forcefulness riding above the nonchalant delivery. He hadn't even turned to where her voice emanated. Resting his eyes upon her would be more than what was left of his ego could take. She was the reason no promoters would return his calls, and those that did piled on even more cruelty with almost degrading amounts being offered. His name was mud, just like the putrid stains that blotted his shirt. 'Come to gloat?' Magnum questioned, words forced through a down-trodden scowl. Their personalities were born to conflict. There was no common ground, no resolution. Nothing was complimentary, allowing only clashing. 'I need you,' she then spoke with stark, bare honesty, words delivered so dry that no emotion whatsoever could survive upon them. Normally, Wolf had more wise-cracks and witty comebacks that most peoples' tempers could handle. A silver-tongue often ended in a bloody lip. But those words had him bogged down in silence, not even waiting to think of a snarky retort, as he suddenly knew not a word. He hadn't breathed, and his heart slowed down as if he were hibernating, trapping himself in the moment. 'Deville,' she added, knowing how that one name was entwined through the life of Magnum. Wolf's eyes closed, while the breath he'd held in until lungs yearned for fresh oxygen was finally exhaled. 'I don't want any part of this, any of it, anymore. I don't want you being there every time I turn around, and I don't want Deville's smirking face always leaning over my shoulder. You people are... you people are severely fucked up. I may not have much, but at least I have a little bit of fucking sanity, especially since I left you lot behind.' Ask most retired wrestlers, and they'll tell stories of their glory days on the road, anecdotes from wild nights at hotels, and embarrassing tales of long road trips. They'd fondly recalled the places they'd visited, the matches that had helped make them stars, and what fans would do just to get an autograph. Ask Magnum Wolf, and he'd walk away. Push the matter and he'd tell you to fuck off. He'd walked the darker path through the business, becoming embroiled in sinister twists that would be far fetched for even the most soap opera-ish of TV writers. Some would say that wrestling is their life, and for Magnum, they'd fused in a way that nearly ripped him in two when he tried to separate them. How he envied the marks, safe behind their infallible barrier of kayfabe. He fucking wished it was all a matter of plot twists and gimmicks. 'I want Deville brought down,' danced with shivering steps up Magnum's spine. 'You might just be the one person on this planet that he's nervous about. I don't even care for the details, but you know things about him that he wouldn't even let others think... or even think about thinking.' 'You know what?' Wolf questioned the rusty numbers on his door, still not turning to her. 'I think I'll pass, but thanks, nice to see that after all this time you still want me to kill myself.' You'd never guess it from seeing her on television. Karina Wolfenden, the K-Wolf, the extreme sports loving adrenaline junkie that captured the hearts of wrestling fans. Butter wouldn't melt, and some even looked up to her as a role model. Wolf knew more than he ever wanted to, about too many things. 'It looks like you've managed to do something even worse,' her words mocked his surroundings with. And why not? This was beyond the bottom of the barrel... it was wallowing in the gunge left over, and allowed to go mouldy. 'One day,' Magnum growled, knowing that if he looked at her, the false, angelic smile she could muster upon demand would leave him wanting to punch himself, 'people will find out who you really are.' 'You looking forward to waking up tomorrow morning?' she then jabbed at him with, landing a stinger. 'Got much planned? Any work coming up? Maybe a date? A nice little walk in the park, soaking up the sun and smelling the flowers before breaking into song and proclaiming that the hills are alive with the sound of music? Because, yeah, it just kinda looks like you've got another day without showering planned, with a damp porn mag for company.' Swallowing, Wolf almost choked himself on phlegm, nerves barely allowing his throat to constrict. 'You do realise that I'm only alive because Deville is that fucking cruel, right?' dry words hissed as nervous fingers smoothed out his bushy, verging on comedy moustache, awaiting a reply that quickly revealed it wasn't forthcoming. 'What did he do to the precious little Karina then?' As much as the scent of her around him sickened Magnum, there was arousal in his disgust. 'He said no to me.' If it's true that you only hurt the one you love, Magnum and Karina made Romeo and Juliet looks like a short, romance-less fling. They'd held one another's lives hostage for so long that one was bound to feel Stockholm syndrome creeping in. But while Wolf had to accept that Karina ended up holding him in the palm of her hand... somewhere, deep down past his revulsion, he wished she'd have taken something else in her hand. Karina didn't even have to prompt an answer verbally, only narrow her eyes, making the focus on Wolf so intense that the words were squeezed out. 'Yes...' III - Yes. '...yes,' Karina Wolfenden directed, almost getting flashbacks to Lemmings as her actions and words prevented the numerous deliverymen from bumping in to one another, or tumbling down the stairs. 'That needs to go in the bedroom at the end of the 3rd floor landing, oh, but leave the lamp, that's for the study at the bottom of the rear spiral staircase.' Really, she should have been taking the weight off her feet, as a healthy 7 pounds, plus its comfy home for the past 8 months, had begun to make her ankles swell. As the sun crept into its 10 o'clock spot, Karina could see why she'd refused to accept anywhere but here. A series of old farm buildings, restored for nearly two whole years to create a dream home, had somehow managed to more than satisfy all of her picky tastes. Walls of white made the buildings appear vast, while the sunlight that flooded in from the east cast all the right shadows and highlights. 'That needs to hang from the second floor's landing,' the orders continued, 'and please try not to drop coffee on that, it was reupholstered last week.' She wasn't always as domineering as this, well, at least not anymore. Age had mellowed Karina, and the years had calmed her. Once a free spirit, adventurous side spreading her younger years across the globe, she'd stumbled upon the most unlikely of anchors to tie her down. With the aches and pains no longer bringing the same sense of pride that they once did, Karina turned to her artistic side. Her fiancé has 'secured' her a job as an exhibition co-ordinator at the nearest gallery, and the moment she never thought would come finally arrived... she'd found a normal job that didn't bring overbearing frustration to the forefront. To put it simply, Karina Wolfenden was... normal. 'Oh, actually,' she continued to orchestrate, 'that looks better in the light down here.' Deville had given her a blank cheque to decorate what was to be their family home... giving in to what he knew would be the inevitable. Stepping back towards the entrance, Karina took a good look at what was hers. Scraping her natural, brunette's mane back into a ponytail, Karina's rosy lips seemed to drift naturally into a smile, having finally grown used to the muscles over the past few years. Familiar taps approached behind her, the soles of extortionate dress shoes against the floorboards. She felt the fingers just manage to lock around her, before being entwined by her own, and then a stubbled chin resting down upon her shoulder. Sharing the same vision, it was like looking into their futures, both imagining the years to come as they finally settled down into family life. 'Well?' IV - Maybe... '...well?' her fragile voice almost pleaded for an answer with as she looked upwards to Angelo, out the tops of wide, hopeful eyes. She'd prepared herself for the no, thoughts racing down every available path, and was equally ready to pounce up at the first hint of a yes, eager wrap herself around Angelo, knowing that all their troubles were left behind. But, in her world of black and white, she'd neglected the grey. 'I...' Deville stuttered, for the first time his words not rolling off the sliver tongue with infallible confidence. 'I don't know...'
