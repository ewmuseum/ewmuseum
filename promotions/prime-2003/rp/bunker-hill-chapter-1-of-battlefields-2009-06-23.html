Bunker Hill (Chapter 1 of "Battlefields")

23 June 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 23 In so many ways, Philadelphia was were it all started. Sure, Troy would always be a Greensboro boy at heart, but this, the City of Brotherly Love, was the site of so many seminal moments in his life. Where his pro football career began, then ended in a terrifying instant. Where he pulled himself back onto his own two feet - literally - and took the first steps towards recovery. Where he met Jason Reed, the man who took the chance on turning him into a professional wrestler. Those moments, those montages flashing in his mind's eye, were a decade or more in his rear view mirror as he returned to his adopted home this time around. He was in the back of a barely-working old cab, stuck in rush hour traffic on I-95 by the Walt Whitman Bridge, waiting unpatiently to pull up to his hotel. And, to all the world, the only thing joining Troy Douglas in the back seat was a rapidly accumulating film of dust and grime on the upholstery. Of course, Troy knew different. He was there. He was always there now. The nameless phantom who first haunted his dreams, then stared back at Troy every time he looked into a mirror had become a walking, talking nuisance every waking moment, gnawing at Troy's increasingly fragile psyche with every sarcastic barb, every cryptic missive, every pointed look with those beady, sunken eyes. Tired, Troy rubbed his eyes, doing everything he could not to stare straight at the apparition in the seat to his right. "You know, you should probably be wearing that seatbelt, chief. This guy doesn't exactly seem like the poster boy for Driver's Ed." Looking away, Troy tried to stare out the window into the blinking midday sun reflecting on the Delaware River. It was business as usual by now, ignoring his own burgeoning insanity just so he didn't have to deal with these damn hallucinations for another moment. It wasn't exactly working. "Silent treatment again, huh? Yeeeeaah ... THAT'S really been working like a charm, hasn't it? C'mon, boss, I'm dying for some good banter." If the cab driver had turned around at that very moment, he would've seen Troy Douglas directing a positively murderous glare at the dusty vinyl seat next to him before angrily shrugging his shoulders and pulling a tattered copy of today's Daily News off the seat and beginning to defiantly leaf through the pages. The cab finally ducked of the highway and onto the urban maze of intersecting streets that was Center City, but even as Troy attempted to use his reading as a shield against his own subconscious, he was failing in a spectacular fashion. "Anything interesting?" Troy pulled the newspaper up even further, burying his nose in the fold. "Aww, was it something I said?" The newspaper rose again, forming a paper tent around Troy's face. "C'mon, champ, it's not like --" That was it. The last straw. Damn the consequences, he was going to confront this head-on. "For the love of fucking God, will you ever SHUT UP? Months now, every fucking second of every fucking day, you're in my ear, drilling away at my brain like your trying to tunnel through a goddamn mountain! Just leave me ALONE! I CAN'T TAKE IT ANYMORE!" He breath was shallow and quick now, his fists clenched, head shaking, veins nearly exploding from his brow, heart racing like an over-caffeinated hummingbird. "This is MY LIFE! I don't care if your a part of my mind or not, you're not supposed to be here. You want to hurt me? You're hurting me, alright? You're breaking me to tiny bits with every fucking word! And why? Why? Why won't you just let me be? Why can't you just let me be HAPPY?" Troy was shaking so hard now that he threatened to bust right through the frame of the car and drop right onto the middle of Market Street. If the driver was disturbed at all, the disinterested look on his face as he maneuvered through traffic like a salmon heading upstream didn't indicate it. In a city like this, he'd probably seen far stranger. That wasn't helping Troy Douglas. As he shook like a madman, the face he saw just inches from his did little more than flash a sardonic, evil little smirk. "Told you before and I'll tell you again, boss. You can make me go away ANY time you want. All I need are the magic words." "Then get the living, breathing fuck out of here!" "BZZZZZT! Wrong answer!" Exasperated, Troy threw his arms backwards, sending dust flying through the cramped cab as he slumped into his seat. He didn't get a chance to sit for long, though, as it was only a moment before the cab peeled to the left and came to a screeching halt in front of the Doubletree Hotel housing the PRIME roving caravan for the week. "That'll be 17 bucks," came the gruff, emotionless voice of the driver. Troy dug through his wallet for a 20 stuffing it into the driver's hand and nodding, giving him the universal signal to keep the change. The trunk popped open, Troy yanked his bulky suitcase and duffel bag out, and not two seconds after he slammed the trunk shut, the yellow cab was gone, reintroduced into the cornucopia that was Center City at rush hour. Troy turned around to head into the hotel, but was headed off by yet another pearl of wisdom. "He seemed nice." Five blocks away, Andre Bell scanned the sidewalks for new fares. Only two hours left on his shift, and after his last passenger, it was one that couldn't end soon enough. Weirdo.
