The Rain

1 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8-1-05 06.21.03. Japan. It's June. It's Osaka. It must be raining. Tsunami season is just getting started, and each day the skies open up and douse the swarms of humanity that occupy Japan. The salarymen are starting to trickle back from work...at least the ones that don't work late into the night are. The housewives are finishing errands. The high-school students have mismatched pieces of their uniforms...a pair of requisition slacks but also a Black Label Society t-shirt. The girls are pulling their skirts higher. Some are popping and locking over in an alley, faces shining with the rainwater and sweat. Everywhere, the rain slips over their umbrellas, catches in their clothes, their hair, their shoes. Their briefcases and backpacks. The rain washes them clean. The young white man is not clean, though. He wears a rain jacket instead of carrying an umbrella. He is the only one, in the sea of bodies, without shelter or umbrella. If the strangeness of the man walking with umbrella was not enough, he was a young, handsome American, with dyed-blue hair matted about hanging soggy about his forehead. He drew the eyes and attention of all who saw him, but his green eyes were dark, and he paid them no mind. He entered the bicycle garden, the gleaming silver collection of various bicycles that grows around Japanese train stations. Only a few of the bicycles that arrived in the morning had disappeared already, with so few businesspeople returning from work as of yet, but there was a bustle in the garden as the early crowd began to pick their way through, looking for their means of transportation home. The young man weaved through the twisted metal sparkling in the rain, reflecting the gray light of the overcast afternoon. He touched nothing, neither body nor bicycle, and despite how wet he looked, it felt as if the rain did not touch him as well. He could not even see the falling rain, for it would not look him in the eye either. At Nanba station, he stepped on the escalator that led up to the traintracks. He stood patiently on the right side of the escalator, then noticed that everyone else was standing on the left. He took the half-a-step that he could to his left, and waited...and sure enough, more speed-inclined people started filing up the escalator to the now-clear right. The young man moved back to the left, and strode up the moving stairs. To the right, there are a number of machines that resemble ATM's. The young man remembered his friend's directions, and slid a 1000 yen note into the machine, pressed a button with barely legible kanji on it, and the machine spat a rail ticket into his hand. The young man did not truly understand the process he had just performed, but was content with the result. He made his way to the train that read 'Kobe' in red LED lights. The speakers overhead spoke in lilting tones, alternating between Japanese and English. As he stood in line behind a matron with wrinkly eyes and a salaryman reading an issue of Shonen Jump, the young man thought about the trains in New Jersey, how they always ran late, and how grown men would never read comic books in public. In front of him, the train to Kobe pulled up, precisely on time, and he could not supress a grin. Swiftly, a line of humanity, almost akin to ants marching, filed into the car. The young man sat down in one of the last available seats on the bench, but stood back up when he saw that the matronly woman ahead of him in line earlier was without a seat. He stepped aside, and she sat down. 'Arigatoo gozaimasu,' she thanked him. The young man shook his head, and responded with one of the few words of Japanese he knew. 'Iie.' Meaning 'No.' The woman took this as an acceptable answer and smiled, which caused the young man to blush slightly. He bowed his head quickly, awkwardly, then let his eyes settle on a poster on the wall. The walls were decorated with block letters of kanji, eye-popping colors, and beautiful faces. The most beautiful face of all belonged to a young woman in a poster that the young man saw on that train. In English, it read 'Miho Kurosaki - Super STAR!' He skin was pale and flawless, her smile like dawn breaking, and her eyes like the last flashes of dying stars. The young man simply saw the poster once, and lost himself in the young woman's doe eyes. He knew that he was doomed to love a woman he'd never know, never meet. When it happened in Japan, it was called Idol Worship, and the otaku, the fanboys would obssess over the pop idol singers and models. They would love as much as they could without ever getting any nearer than the fortieth row at a concert, without ever being in the same city as them. In his head he called it pathetic, but refused to find his way out of the poster's gaze. 08.01.05. New Fucking Jersey. The apartment was almost empty, and gray light only filtered in through the picture windows that looked out onto Broad Street, the central drag in 'downtown' Red Bank. 'Downtown' in quotations, due to the fact that it's suburbia, and 'downtown' consists of only a few blocks of three-story buildings, a Hilton, and a textile factory-turned-mall called The Galleria. No lights were actually on in the apartment, save the one above where Kyle Lamen sat at a cheap IKEA dinner table (The Collingswood Collection. Ninety-nine, ninety-nine.) looking blankly at a stack of white forms. 'Seriously, I can't frickin' do this right now.' Kyle says, as he gets up from the table, and storms into another room, away from the table and paperwork. He wears a white shirt with the sleeves torn off and a Ramones logo on the front, and black baggy jeans with chains on them for look, not any discernible reason...but he's gone, disappeared into his bedroom. Out of the kitchen comes Sarah McIntyre, Kyle's personal assistant/handler. She's young, barely over the age of twenty-two, and she wears the type of 'power suit' one might expect in the wrestling industry. Hair tied up, thick-rimmed black glasses, black jacket over a white blouse...all standard, but her skirt barely reaches mid-thigh, before revealing black fishnet stockings and three-inch stilletto heels. She looks like someone who couldn't decide whether to emulate Aimz, or OSW's old president Jane Reagan, which is honestly not too far from the truth. 'You fucker. Would you give me thirty consecutive minutes of your time, for once in your goddamned life!? We don't get this paperwork filled out, you don't wrestle on Wednesday. That's the LAW!' Exasperated, the curses fly out of Sarah's gorgeous ruby lips like vultures out of the Vatican. Her eyes, a darker green than Kyle's are filled with anxiety and anger. 'You don't understand a damn thing about what I go through on a day-to-day basis to keep you organized, do you?!' Sarah yelled into Kyle's bedroom. Kyle shut the door on her. 'Knock if you want to speak with me. Have some respect for a man's house!' he called through the closed door. Almost as soon as he said this, Sarah knocked loudly on the door. Kyle swung it open and looked her in the face beatifically. 'Thank you for choosing to handle this like a professional. What can I-' SMACK! While Kyle had been talking, Sarah had taken off one of her black stilletto pumps, and now hit Kyle solidly in the head with it. 'You've got ten minutes to goof off. Then back to your paperwork, got it!?' Kyle left the door to his room open, and simply walked away from her, and Sarah followed him into his bedrooom. While the rest of the apartment was virtually empty, unpacked boxes, posters, and a pretty sizable Fender amplifier cluttered up his room substantially. The posters on the wall were of Kubrick's 'A Clockwork Orange', Red Bank local Kevin Smith's 'Clerks', a couple of tour posters from the NYC hardcore band H2O, big-ass Clash poster of the cover of London Calling, then several cutesy posters of J-pop idol Miho Kurosaki. Sarah looked at these posters, one of Miho in a bikini getting out of a pool, another of her bundled up in a cardigan, and scoffed loudly. 'Jesus, Kyle. Grow up. You know, as an F-list celebrity, you DO have access to real women occasionally, right? I mean, at least one's who don't know you.' Sarah scanned the room again, briefly and walked out of the room. Soon, Kyle followed her, carrying his big Fender amp in one hand, and a hard guitar case in the other. He set the amp down by the table, laid the guitar case on the floor, and popped it open. Inside the case, lay his most prized possession. A hand-built 1991 Fender Stratocaster Custom guitar, done in jet black with custom pickups, a redone ash fingerboard with the words 'Six-String Samurai' done in gold filigree underneath the strings, and a super-thin jet pickguard. Spread about the jet black of the guitar's base were very carefully painted pink cherry blossoms, looking as if they were tossed about in a windstorm. But, the strangest thing about the guitar is that, on the back end of the guitar, there was a black cloth-wrapped handle, sticking out approximately seven inches. Sarah, new to her job as Kyle's PA, asked the obvious question. 'Hey, Kyle...what's the handle for?' Kyle smiled, and held up one finger as if to say 'wait and see.' He flipped the guitar over deftly, and along the back, three black leather straps held a katana in place, attached to the back of the guitar. The scabbard for the sword was done in the exact same fashion as the guitar, right down to the pink cherry blossoms carefully painted on it. He very slowly and deliberately pulled the sword out of it's scabbard, and the perfect silver of the metal gleamed even in what little light there was in Kyle's apartment. Kyle, very gently, caressed the edge with the ring finger on his right hand, drawing a little blood. 'Holy shit, Kyle. That thing's real?' Sarah exclaimed. 'You got that in Japan? How'd they let you take it on the plane when you moved back here?' Kyle just grinned. 'They had to, Sarah. I made the sword myself.' Sarah looked at him, eyes askew. 'No way.' Kyle just shrugged. 'Fine, don't believe me. I've still got nine minutes, right?' Sarah nodded, as Kyle returned the sword to it's scabbard, then pulling the black strap of the guitar over his shoulder. He quickly pulled the cord from the back of the amplifier and attached it to a wall outlet, then produced a cable for the guitar to connect to the amplifier and plugged that in as well. Sarah checked her watch. 'Eight minutes, Kyle.' Kyle nodded, pulled a guitar pick out of his pocket, and clicked the amplifier into life. The hum helped his mind clear. Kyle may call himself an ex-Jew and a sort-of-Buddhist, but in reality, music was his religion. He let the pick in his hand move according to it's own volition, and what came out were eight minutes of rollicking power chords, tricky finger progressions, and a few off-notes and mistakes. But for all eight minutes, be it punk standards (he did about a minute's worth of 'God Save The Queen'), his own music (the chunky beats of Kyle's current favorite song with 'American Ninja', called 'Phoenix City Lights'), or even just whatever sounded right (the opening to the Stone's 'Paint It Black' into the bridge from Slapstick's 'Cheat To Win' into the verse from Springsteen's 'Born To Run' complete with Kyle whistling the sax part, into the Pixies 'Debaser' chorus)...he looked happy. Content. Alive. So much so that when his eight minutes were up, Sarah even gave him an additional twenty seconds to put the finishing touches on Digger's 'I Want My Hat Back'. As he gently put the guitar/sword back in it's case, unplugged the wires and such, Sarah talked to fill the sudden silences. 'You know, Kyle...if you wrestled like you played the guitar...' Kyle finished her thought for her. 'I'd never lose?' Sarah smiled. 'Exactly.' Kyle just shook his head. 'Not a chance. I screwed up over a dozen times just jamming out on songs I know by heart. Wrestling's not the same thing. I screw up ONCE in a match, and I could be out of luck. Do you think Ellis Easton wouldn't take the opportunity to shove The Knife In The Back into me if I screw up once? Do you think I wouldn't get hit by a Guernica Driver if I lost my attention for a spell?' Kyle slammed the case shut. 'Unless you're a perfectionist, you don't become a champion in wrestling. You have to be perfect to succeed in a place like PRIME. This week, I have to beat thirty-five people. Thirty-fucking-five. And in TWO matches, no less. One false move, and I'm out, done...and you don't get a second chance to make a first impression, Sarah.' He picked up the case, and the amp, and returned them to his room. Undaunted, Sarah still called to him. 'To be technical, didn't you make your first impression at Colossus?' she smirked. Kyle returned to the room, eyes flashing. 'You're missing the point.' Sarah nodded and smiled glibly, doing her best impersonation of Kyle on his better days. 'I don't think anyone thinks I can do it,' Kyle said, 'But I think I can win both of my matches on ReVolution.' Sarah's eyes lit with worry. 'Uh, Kyle...no offense, but there is NO way that's happening. I mean, Karina Wolfenden? Dave Gibson? Ellie Easton? You're dead, man. MAYBE you could pull it out over the Diablos and Vetcetera or whatever if you ride Zameer like an elephant, but otherwise, you're totally fucked.' Kyle scratched at his nose absently. 'It's ELLIS Easton. And there's always a chance, y'know?' Sarah just shook her head. 'No Kyle, there's no chance. There's the same chance that a snowball has in the sun. There's the same chance that I'll wake up tomorrow with you in your bed. No chance whatsoever. You said yourself that you'd have to be perfect.' Kyle went back into his bedroom, and came back with a burlap sack. He turned it upside-down over the IKEA table where Sarah was sitting, and sharp scraps of metal fell out, clattering loudly and some sticking directly into the wood of the table. Sarah jumped back with surprise, nearly tipping her chair over. Kyle leaned over her. 'If I screwed up just once during the thousands of steps when trying to make a katana, something like this happened. It took me nine months to do it, but finally, I was able to be perfect. If I could forge that sword, in the other room, if I could create something that's going to last, a legacy...then I can win both my matches at ReVolution.' Sarah doesn't smile, doesn't frown, doesn't do anything. Her face betrays nothing that she feels. Kyle sits down in one of the chairs around the kitchen table, and starts back to work on the paperwork, insurance, contract information, the like. Neither person says anything in the gray light of the afternoon. They simply wait for the silence that surrounds them to disappear again, into the rain. 06.21.03. Japan. 'How...did you know...come here?' an older Japanese man with crooked teeth and badly broken hands asked. He barely left the door open past a crack, to look at the silly-looking American with the strange blue hair standing on his doorstep. The young man ran a hand through his wet hair. He dug around in one of his pant pockets, before pulling out a heavy piece of paper and handing it to the man. The young man knew that the piece of paper was a letter from a friend in Japanese, saying that Kyle would train this older man's son in the art of professional wrestling, in exchange for learning how to forge a traditional Japanese katana. The old man started to read, as Kyle looked around. The old man in the house on the outskirts of Kobe was one of the last surviving practicioners of shinsakuto, the art of traditional Japanese swordmaking. Over the course of the next nine months, Kyle imagined learning the process... Dousing the hot metal in barrels full of river water. Folding the 'tamahagane', the 'jewel steel' thousands of times to offer flexibility and strength. Painting the metal with a special clay so that when fired, the sharp edge with become harder, and stretch further, curving the blade. 'Why?' asked the old man. He cocked his head to the side, and Kyle knew that due to the old man's less than perfect understanding of the English language, he wouldn't really hear his answer, so he needn't bother speaking freely. He did anyway. 'Because I'm tired of fucking up. My life has been a series of mistakes, and wasted potential. I disappeared from not one, not two, but three different wrestling companies. I've severely injured someone due to my own ngeligence in the ring. And to be perfectly honest, I don't know when my time will run out, and that this nerve damage to my right arm will become so severe that I'll never be able to move it again.' The young man's eyes welled up, and took on a pleading expression. 'All I can do is forge a legacy for myself...something that when I'm dead and gone, I can leave something behind that shows that I was here, and I didn't always fuck it up. I want to build something for myself, be it a sword, a wrestling match, anything, that can speak for itself. I want to create a flash of lightning for the moment...is that too much to ask?' He fell to his knees on the older man's doorstep, with a small tatami mat in front of him, and looked at the couple of pairs of shoes sitting on it, waiting patiently. The old man tucked the letter into his pocket, and motioned for the young American to enter his house. He turned and retreated into the interior of his home, leaving the young man on the outside of the house, with one foot in the door. The rest of the journey is up to the young man. Fate can conspire to take you so far, but be it in wrestling, in swords, or in life, the rest of the effort is dependent solely on your own will to succeed. The handsome American takes a moment to step back, out of the shelter of the old man's doorway. He pulls the jacket over his head, off his body, before tossing it aside. The hair gel that was sticky and matted atop his head dissolves slowly in the cascading rain. His eyes are washed clear, the redness that had built from days of jet lag and uncomfortable sleep becomes whiter. The sweat of walking and travel disappears. The guilt that traveled with him, over the ocean to Japan, and has followed him on his travels, drips slowly off his back, and into the small puddles that form around his feet, in the garden in front of the old man's house. The water from above coats him. It covers him. It baptizes him. It washes him clean. And it is perfect.
