Can't Get Right

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: A week before Colossus ~Sunday Night~ August, 22nd Matt rolled his cell phone in the palm of his hand, flipping it side over side, like a deck of cards. Without even realizing it, he began to chew at his bottom lip, tearing through layers of flesh till he finally drew blood. “Have you called her yet?” From somewhere in the house, he heard Mary inquire about the phone call he had promised her he’d make, and he wiped away the blood with the tip of his finger before answering the disembodied voice of his wife. “Just about to.” “Good. Cuz you were a complete ass, and you need to apologize.” “Yeah...” his response probably wasn’t loud enough to be heard. Looking down at his phone, Matt went to his contacts list and pressed his finger against the touch-screen, smearing blood across its surface as he scrolled down to the ‘L’s He found her name and selected it, pressed ‘Send’, and waited. It rang several times, maybe four or five before going over to voicemail, and Matt took a deep, loud breath to gather himself before the ‘beep’. “Hey Lindz, this is Matt…Ward. I just wanted…” He pulled the phone away from his ear and made a disgusted face. “Jesus Christ, she knows who I am.” Tapping around the screen, Matt pushed a button or two until he heard an automated message. If you are satisfied with this message, please press ‘1’. If you would like to re-record your message, press ‘2’ and wait for the beep. If you would like to erase this message and end the call, press ‘3’. He pressed the number ‘two’ key and tried again. “Hey Lindz, it’s me. I thought that I should probably call and apologize for my actions as of late. For the past few months, the last three or four weeks in particular. I’ve acted inappropriately… we both probably have… and I want to apologize for that, even if I didn’t really start this mess. I mean, I tried to patch things up, talk things through, even though I wasn’t really sure what was going on. God forbid a guy grow sick of trying to do the right thing only to get the cold shoulder in return. I suppose I just should have kept playing nice, sent flowers, taken you out for a beer after one of the shows and confessed what a doofus I am and how guys are stupid. Then again, I guess the problem goes way back. I suppose I shoulda just smiled and told you how great and hunky Rayne was when you told me you were fucking him, or wished you well and reminded ya to use protection with trash like that. But I committed this terrible, horrific crime called ‘being honest’ instead of just feigning excitement and throwing you two a party or som…” The message cut off, filling its allotted space, and this time Matt was automatically prompted by the familiar voice recording. If you are satisfied with this message, please press ‘1’. If you would like to re-record your message, press ‘2’ and wait for… “Ok, ‘nough of that.” Comforted by his venting and feeling at least moderately more capable of sincere apology, Matt pressed the ‘2’ key and tried to relax. He heard the beep and injected his voice with a shot of optimism. “Hey Lindz! It’s Matt. Listen, I wanted to call and say I’m sorry. Was hoping I could get ahold of you before we head to San Diego. Give me a call back when you get this so I can apologize for… for, ya know… ReV and… all that stuff. All sorts of things.” For the third time, Matt tugged the phone away from his ear as he cursed, and for the third time, he was prompted with a series of options. If you are satisfied with this message, please… This time, his thumb darted for the ‘3’, and as he officially ended his embarrassing attempt at a call, he tossed his phone onto the couch, watching it be swallowed by the crevices between the cushions. “Did you talk to her?” Mary Ward walked into the living room, drying a dinner plate with a small towel. “No. I was fumbling all over everything I said. I need to do this right. Figure I owe her that much. Besides, it went over to voicemail, and leaving a message probably isn’t the best way to apologize.” “Good point.” “So, I’ll try ‘er again tomorrow afternoon when I get back from Mom and Dad’s.” “Ok.” Mary bundled up the towel in her right hand and threw it at her husband, smiling as it thudded against his chest and fell to his lap. “Well if you’re not gonna deal with that now, then get your butt off the couch and come help me unload the dishwasher.” “Yes, ma’am” Matt pushed himself up from the sofa, leaving his phone lost in the folds, and he followed a step behind his wife, hopeful that a good night’s rest would help him make a proper apology. ~Monday Afternoon~ August, 23rd “I don’t understand?” “The corrective surgery didn’t work. The tear from the initial operation is still there and they can’t close the wound.” “I thought this procedure was supposed to have a ninety percent success rate?” Matt folded his hands together and let them fall against his parent’s dining room table. “Are you telling me he lucked into that ten percent?” “Afraid so.” “Jesus, Mom. The guy can’t catch a break.” Chris Ward took a seat next to her son at the table and put a hand on his back, comforting him just as she might have done twenty years earlier. “It’s been a bad run of luck.” “No shit.” Matt shook his head as his eyes briefly darted upward. “And things had been lookin’ up. Mary and I were working through some stuff. Had a great birthday on Thursday. Colossus is just around the corner and I’d been looking forward to the whole week leading up to the big event. I had a good talk with dad a few days ago, said he was feelin’ better.” “You know your father, he’s never gonna let on that anything is wrong. Certainly won’t let anyone ever know that he’s in pain.” “Stubborn.” “So are his boys.” Chris smiled and forced a laugh. “But look at it this way, things can only get better.” “Easy to say when you’re bottomed out.” “But it’s true.” “No it ain’t. Nothing’s goin’ right. Every time we try to turn things around, stay positive, we pull ourselves up and then just hit bottom again. It’s one step forward and two steps back.” “All you can do is try.” “Get tired of that.” Matt let his eyes wander till they locked on the clock in the corner of the microwave. “I gotta get goin’, Mom. Early flight out to the west coast tomorrow and I don’t have anything packed.” “I figured.” “I still can’t believe Dad didn’t say anything the other day.” “He didn’t want to ruin the afternoon. That conversation meant a lot to him. Said he hadn’t laughed that much in ages.” “I know. But you can’t fake a grin and keep hidin’ the truth.” He shoved back his chair and stood, cracking his spine as he straightened. “Might as well tell it like it is when you get the chance.” ~Monday Evening~ August, 23rd Matt slid his phone from the pocket of his jeans and pulled up his call history. The top entry under ‘dialed’ read her name, and he selected the call, then pressed the ‘Send’ key. It took about thirty seconds for the phone to stop ringing and the voicemail greeting to play. When it finished, he began. “It’s me… “Wanted to say I’m sorry. For a lot of things. I’m not in good shape right now, don’t really know where my head’s at or when that’s gonna change. And that’s about as honest as I can put it.” He paused for maybe half a second. “At this point, I just hope no one gets hurt too bad on Sunday… "...but I’m not makin’ any promises.” When the last word fell from his lips, Matt slowly lowered the phone from his ear and touched the screen, triggering a voice. If you are satisfied with this message… He pressed ‘1’ and shoved the phone back in his pocket.
