Stop Signs & Perceptions

16 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/16/08 “We all see the world through different colored windows” -Some Grateful Dead poster hanging in a hippy store at the mall * * * The (Angry) Driver It hissed like some pissed off pit viper, and a puff of smoke billowed from under the hood. He wasn’t an auto mechanic, but he didn’t need a grease covered jumpsuit to diagnose his car as totaled. Completely fucking totaled. “Ok, sir… from the beginning… what happened?” The police officer on the scene flipped open a pad of paper and prepared to jot down some halfhearted notes. “That fucking cocksucker ruined my BMW 3 series!” “Sir, please watch the language. How did the accident happen?” “I was sitting there, waiting to turn left out of the parking lot of the dealership. I looked to my right…nothing. I looked to my left and saw a car coming my way, but he was a bit up the road and was going to have to brake at the 4-way stop anyways. So I started to pull out… except the jackass didn’t stop… ran right through the fucking stop sign and HIT ME!” “I’m not going to ask you again, sir. Watch the language.” The cop had seen this sort of guy before. Most people involved in an accident were either too shaken up to say much of anything or they wouldn’t shut up, screaming every sort of profanity known to man. “Sorry, it’s just… that’s a BMW. Brand new! I was just driving the thing off the car lot! That car…” The man turned and pointed to the scrap metal on wheels that had just recently been his new toy, “…costs more than this fool probably makes in a year. The big, bald fu…” “Ok.” The officer cut him off. “So, you were turning left out of the BMW dealership, and the gentlemen driving the pick-up truck ran through the 4-way stop up the road and smashed your front end just as you were pulling out across the lane here. Do I have that right?” “Yeah.” “Ok.” With his pen, he pointed across the street at a woman standing by a Dodge Neon. “That young lady over there says that she witnessed the accident from her car, so I’m going to speak with her, and I’m sure we’ll verify your story and be able to square this thing away.” “That idiot better have some damn good insurance!” The officer sighed and headed across the street. The ambulance had just arrived to check everyone out, but all parties looked to be fine physically. With any luck, the woman would give her statement, the medics would clear everyone, and he wouldn’t have to deal with any more paperwork than necessary. It was his perception of things. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The perception of Kaiser Vashaun in PRIME was not entirely favorable. To be honest, he was widely perceived as an asshole, too arrogant for his own good. From the first day he had walked in the door, he had rubbed many the wrong way. From the egotistical, overly artsy ‘debut hype’ promos, to the cocky and dismissive interviews… Kaiser Vashaun had been pompous since day one. His loud ways had been frowned upon. The general consensus was that he should wait patiently, and once he had slowly climbed the ladder, shout from the top. Yelling and thumping the chest from the middle rung… it was perceived to be uncouth. Perhaps winning the Intense Title had been a good thing for Kaiser. It would be a ‘tough as nails’ humbling experience. The perception of the Intense division in PRIME was not entirely favorable, either. It was a bloody, often barbaric world filled with gimmicked and garbage matches, populated by psychos happy to leap off a twenty foot ladder or swing a 2x4 wrapped in barbwire at another man’s skull, hitting horrific homeruns like the Rays at Fenway. The marriage of Kaiser’s cockiness to the mid-card grittiness of the Intense division was perfect. That was the idea, anyway. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The (star-struck) Witness “Ma’am. Just want to thank you for helping us out here this afternoon.” “Not a problem, officer.” “Ok, if you could just tell me what you saw go down, we’ll be able to letcha on your way.” “Sure. From what I saw, the guy driving the BMW just pulled out in front of the man in the pick-up truck for no reason.” “I see.” The officer made a note, dotting his eyes violently with the tip of his pen. “And the gentleman in the pick-up… did you see if he came to a stop there at the intersection?” “The 4-way stop? Yeah, I saw his brake lights come on and everything.” “So you would find the gentleman driving the BMW to be at fault?” “Based on everything I saw… absolutely.” As the cop jotted down a few more notes, the woman looked over her left shoulder towards the man who had been driving the pick-up. “Great. Alright, if you’ll hang tight, ma’am, I’ll be right back with you in just a couple moments.” The cop lowered his head and, with heavy steps, trudged off towards the third party. As the officer turned and walked away, the woman made eye contact with the pick-up driver. She saw him look back her way and her cheeks changed a light shade of red. Laughing to herself, she smiled and winked at the tall, dark and handsome pro-wrestler. It was her perception of things. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Kaiser’s perception could be quite different. He didn’t view the Intense title as the same violent, weapon-driven, barbaric trophy that so many others did. To him, the intensity was a badge of honor to be worn through fierce competition and immense amounts of success. His talent was to be the weapon of his choice, and if that meant bucking every tradition and spitting in the face of the accepted norm, he was more than willing to do so. People had thought the Intense Title would humble Kaiser. Some went as far as to say they believed it would bring him down to its raw and real level. Instead, The Next in Line had decided to raise the level of the Intense Title to that which he believed he belonged in the first place. And now, he fought to redefine intense… to make it something as esteemed as he viewed himself. For Kaiser Vashaun, there was no ladder to climb, no glass ceiling to shatter through. He did not believe in paying his dues. His own thoughts were that talent was payment enough, and winning was the only requirement to sit on the throne. And he had won. Won his debut. Won the Intense Title. Won against SCCW… when no other PRIME employee could. In his mind, he’d already punched his ticket to the top. The notion that he was an arrogant asshole… he wouldn’t argue with that. But the perception of Kaiser Vashaun as anything less than the top dog in PRIME… that he couldn’t stand for. He perceived himself to be the next in line… that his time had arrived. And sometimes, a lone individual’s perception could force a reality. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The (B-list Celebrity) Guy in the Pick-Up Just what he needed. Hadn’t been paying a damn bit of attention, and now, three days before The Great American Nightmare, PRIME’s reigning Intense Champion was about to be in a world of shit. All for flying right past a little red octagon. Kaiser watched as the officer headed his way. Behind him, the woman standing next to the Dodge Neon stared at him, smiled and winked. Smiled and winked? He didn't give it much thought, though he did find it a bit unsettling. “Ok, sir,” The cop stopped a couple feet in front of Kaiser, “hopefully we can wrap this whole deal up. I’ve talked to the gentleman driving the BMW and, while he claims that you tore right through that 4-way stop and plowed straight into him, the woman who witnessed the accident says that you did, in fact, stop and that the other guy over here just pulled out right in front of you. So if you can just confirm what she told…” “She said he was at fault?” “Yeah, why? Are you honestly gonna tell me otherwise?” “So it’s her word along mine… against the other guy? I mean… if I say he’s at fault, and he says I’m at fault… she agrees with me, and he gets sited?” “Yeah. That’s how it goes. Unless that young lady over there was lying. And I don’t see why she’d do that" The cop looked up at Kaiser, then back to the woman, and finally back to Kaiser. "Would she have reason to do that?” “N…” and then he thought about the smile and wink. She had told the officer her story, turned his way, smiled and winked. Before he could stop himself, Kaiser started thinking aloud. “She knows me.” “Beg pardon, sir? You two know each other?” “No.” Kaiser snapped back to reality. “I've honestly never seen her before in my life.” "Ok... so, you wanna tell me your side of this story or not?" "Yeah." Kaiser looked past the officer, saw the woman still standing and smiling, and he couldn't keep himself from joining her in a grin. "Pretty much just what the lady said... that crazy fool just pulled out right in front of me." It was his reality.
