Date: REV 134 Blog of AWESOMENESS Entry 2 "The Hottest Shit Out There." What’s up subjects? It’s Eli “Soo Sly” VanNess, reporting to you LIVE from the webernets for your seconds installment of “theUPstarts Blog of AWESOMENESS~!”. If any of you loyal subjects saw Revolution last week, you inevitably saw myself and Felix get our asses handed to us by those two-timing, tight-wearing, butt-loving (I’m almost 100% on that one) superheroes. The match was going pretty well and for a second there I was convinced we were going to win. It wasn’t in the cards, subjects, what can I say? We’ll get ‘em next week. Now, back to topic, I’m positive that you guys come to this blog to take a step into both mine and Felix’s life, so I’m going to give you the play by play of what we’ve been up to lately. When we walked through the curtain after being defeated by Sound and The Fury, we weren’t exactly sure what direction we were going to take ourselves in. Felix was pretty down on himself for being the one that got pinned (thank God, it wasn’t me) and I was just generally depressed. We hopped in my run down Toyota Corolla (200,000 miles and still running, bitches) and shot uptown to do what anyone in our position would do. Drink ourselves stupid. We arrived at “Tom’s Shanty” a secluded bar off of the strip. Felix and I figured that winners or losers that we were sort of famous now. Television = Fame, people. Upon entering the overpowering smell of beer, sweat, sex, and ass stung my nostrils. We headed to the bar and were received by the friendly bar man. He was about all of five feet and had to have weighed at least two hundred pounds (when I say this guy was a chode of a man, I mean it). His shirt reeked of a combination of ass and tobacco, and his loose red suspenders did little to keep his pants up. With a swift scratch of his crotch and un-wedging of his boxers he extended his hand to Felix. “Uh, hey” Felix said, attempting to avoid the impending handshake, “what’s the strongest shit you have back there?” The bartender was not to be denied his friendly handshake and he forcefully grasped Felix’s hand and gave it a tight squeeze. Felix choked back vomit. “On a side note,” I said, now feeling really, really (really) dirty, “is there a bathroom somewhere in here?” The squat bartender released his gross death grip on Felix’s hand and motioned to a remote corner of the bar. The hallway that led to the bathroom was narrow, to say the least and the walls had all sorts of “interesting” (to once again, say the least) stains on them. I did my best to ignore the brown and cream colored splotches on the wall, but it was too much for Felix. He shot to the end of the hallway and darted into the bathroom. I kept my casual walk going, and tried to seem inconspicuous to the hardened “bar folk” (I believe that’s the politically correct term for drunks and whores). I pushed open the door and was greeted by a whole new array of smells. I’d go into detail, but just thinking about the make up of the bar bathroom is making me taste puke in my mouth. Felix was already crouched over a brown toilet (it was originally white, but well…I’ll let your imaginations fill in how it became brown, subjects) hugging the thing as if it were his long lost brother. He stayed locked in that position for a solid five minutes before the contents of his stomach emptied into the bottom of the toilet. I was not amused. “All this over a stupid handshake, man? You are one fucking germophobe.” Felix glared back at me as if I was being completely unreasonable. “Did you see what he did right before he shook my hand? Gave his fucking nuts and ass a rub. I don’t know about you, but I say no to second-hand ball grabbing. That’s fucked up.” I looked back at him unapologetically. I can’t say that I was mad at him for losing the match, because I already figured that there would be many-a-time when I would be the cause of a loss. It’s to be expected. Felix lurched again and emptied the remainder of his stomach fluids into the toiled. I turned and washed my hands, just incase the bartender felt the need to somehow clench my hand in his vice like grip. Felix straightened himself and moved me out of the way to wash his hands. He dipped his hands into the clear pool of water and splashed it in his face. He succeeded in both washing his face and mine, because the direction of his splash was very inaccurate. It took me a solid thirty seconds to get the water out of my eye and blink my contact back into place. We exited the bathroom and went back to the bar to see if the bartender had accomplished anything in our absence (mainly making our drinks strong enough to kill a bear). As we strode up the bar, the dirty man standing behind it began to chuckle. He slid two drinks to the edge of the stand. “Two drinks strong enough to kill a bear. You guys could use it, after the beating you took from those two superheroes.” “THEY’RE NOT FUCKING SUPER HEROES, THEY’RE MEN DRESSED LIKE SUPER HEROES! IF I DRESSED LIKE YOU WOULD I BE A DIRTY OLD FUCKED UP BARTENDER THAT’S GOING NOWHERE IN LIFE? NO! I’D JUST BE A MAN DRESSED LIKE A DIRTY OLD BARTENDER WHO’S GOING NOWHERE IN LIFE. LEARN THE FUCKING DIFFERENCE!” I think it would be safe to say that I overreacted just a tad. The old man took two steps backwards and retreated to his “office” behind the bar (I put the quotes over “office” because it looked to be another bathroom with an extra chair and something that resembled an ancient laptop). Now that everyone in the bar was staring directly at us, we took our seats and began sipping on the drinks, which like the bartender, reminded me of ass. The drinks resembled salt water when something oily was thrown into it. You know, greasy, reflective look the water gets when a potato chip or something falls in? That’s what these drinks looked like. They were jet black, making the ice inside invisible. The only reason I knew that there was actually ice in my drink was that I damn near choked on one when trying to take a MANLY GUZZLE. Felix and I finished our drinks and immediately stood up and left. There were too many drunks and whores (ahem, “bar folk”) staring at us. We flew out the door and made a quick left, nearly bumping into an old woman who was puttering down the sidewalk. We made a break for the street and hailed a taxi, because by then neither of us were in any condition to drive. We flung ourselves into the ragged backseat of the first yellow taxi that stopped and asked him to drive us to the nearest hotel. He nodded and took off. Believe me when I say that this driver was either just as drunk as the both of us, or blind in one eye because he was flying all over the place. There were TWO occasions, TWO, when I thought that we were going to steamroll someone right into the pavement. Halfway through the drive the man guiding the vehicle spun around and began speaking in some undistinguishable accent. “Oh helo, two you the up sharts. Shooper hurro kick you ass on the telervision.” “Holy fuck is there anyone that doesn’t watch this shit?” “Welp, PRIME is the hoetest shit oot there.” “Please pull over, we’ll walk the rest of the way.” The driver pulled over and we rolled from the car and began stumbling our way down the sidewalk. We had no idea where we were going but we figured that since there were a lot of people just milling about, there must be a hotel somewhere. After about five minutes of walking we were tired and asked for directions. We thought the answer to our prayers came in a suave looking business man clad in a suit with slicked black hair and thinly rimmed reading glasses. He was immersed in a conversation on his blue tooth phone. “Excuse me, sir, can you tell me or my friend how to get to the nearest hotel?” The business man spun around to answer us but closed his mouth as if he were very surprised. He continued with his conversation. “You’re never going to believe who just asked me for fucking directions! No, not Sound and The Fury, those other two guys who they beat on earlier tonight. Yeah, they look a lot bigger on tv.” You’ve got to be shitting me. I thought people really didn’t watch pro-wrestling anymore. I guess I was fucking wrong. He spun back around and continued his conversation. The last words I caught were: “Hell yeah, man, I mean PRIME is the hottest shit out there right now.” We continued our alcohol laden mission to find a hotel and thought we were finally rescued when we came to a Holiday Inn. “YES!” Felix screamed, “Holiday Inn is the bombast.” This one, however, didn’t seem as good as Felix remembered. It was housed in a dilapidated building that lacked both lights and locks on the doors. However it did have a friendly doorman that was willing to show us to our room. Our doorman, who we later learned was named Hank, was about six feet tall but looked horribly malnourished. His arms were akin to the thickness of hot dogs and his eyes were so far into the sockets that he looked like a skeleton stole a pair of eyeballs and attempted to duct tape them into his skull. He led us in silence down the corridor until he reached our room. “Room 704” he said as his wheeled around, “holy shit.” “What?” “You two are the guys who just lost to – ” I cut him the fuck off. “SINCE WHEN DID ANYONE START WATCHING WRESTLING AGAIN. FUCK OFF DOORMAN!” He shrugged and turned around and left us there standing in the hallway. Before he swung around the corner he turned and said: “Well what do you mean when did people start watching wrestling, it’s PRIME it’s the -” I cut his ass off again. “We know, we fucking know. The hottest shit out there.” The doorman shook his head and rounded the corner, leaving us to sleep off our booze and plan out how we would redeem ourselves in our next match.
