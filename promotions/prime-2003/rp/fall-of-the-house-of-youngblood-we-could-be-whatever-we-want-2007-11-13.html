Fall of the House of Youngblood: We could be whatever we want to be [JitC Round 1]

13 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-13-07 I noticed today, in picking up a novel, that falsehoods trace back to the very texts in which we pick up. I've been in libraries and have smelt intelligence. It stinks like aged paper. The bindings on some of the older style books trap the smell when the middle portion is ripped away from the side of the book. Intelligence feels like a book, it's thick sheets of paper feeling coated as you press your finger tips against them, peeling back page upon page as you delve deeper into what the words mean. It's slightly yellow, meaning that what is being presented is not always pure or universal, but blurred. There is a quality to this that is unexplainable to the new age of thinkers, but professors and casual collectors try to impart the wisdom of the books as if they were vinyl records, like there was a purity in these given forms. It was a new printing of William Faulkner's The Sound and the Fury, which stayed true on the page but was different in presentation. The cover was a thicker sheet of stock paper, the binding flat rather than being bowed. I read page after page, trying to work through the different perspective shifts, but had a great deal of difficulty getting absorbed into what was going on. It wasn't that I knew what was going to happen, nor was it the ranting writing style Faulkner implements. Rather, it was the feel of what I was reading in the physical form. My senses told me that this was not an intelligently written novel because it had been stamped out of a factory, lacking the soul of an older printing of the book. I brought it to my nose before I laid it down for good, and it smelled not of aged wisdom, but of glue. I'm left with these lingering thoughts in my head, causing a divide with what we as Youngblood's are and what we could have been. My family has seen better days. Or at least we believed them to be under our own veil of ignorance. To be frank, I hate to reminisce just for the simple fact of doing it. I think the romanticism of the past, while often a novel concept, is inherently flawed because it does not focus in on the reality of daily life. Things are constantly blurry, the muddy middle ground muddling the picture to a fascinating twist of sterile harshness. I remember, though, from time to time of a period in our lives when things at least seemed like they would go on the right track. I believe it might be a bit of useless trivia for the rest of the world, but Brandon wasn't always the deformed manifestation of all his flaws. They were there, mind you, especially near the time when dad disowned him for the whole wrestling kick, but I always looked at it like those were fixable. Now, I see, those cracks on personality have only gotten worse, like an untreated sculpture that is crumbling at it's foundation. Perhaps that is a bit too negative, but he let his angry strike down the woman of his dreams, and for nearly a year tortured himself in solitude over the whole thing. He was barely eighteen back then, and I followed with him just a few years behind him. If I remember right, I was just going into high school. I played my role as the happy go lucky little lass of the family, wearing my cute pink ribbons with a deeper sense of self loathing for being a fraud. I felt dumb, so I acted out as if I was. But Brandon saw through me. For all his faults, Brandon has that way with people. To strip them to their bare component parts and break them down to what they truly are. And for that, he's distant. I think that's why he is constantly on the move, because one place can never house him completely. I was reading a novel, The Catcher in the Rye, which I had to hide in a lock box that I had under my bed, and was trying to describe exactly what I was reading. I rode along with Brandon during the day, beating back the Texas heat on a rural farm that stretched as far as the eye can see. He bailed hay all day long, working most of it without a shirt on, his movements mechanical. He didn't talk, nor did he take but sips of water. He wore his hair short, in a crew cut back then, and he looked a lot like my father with a large jawline and wide nose. He was attractive back then, which is odd for me to say because I'm his sister, but he had no shortage of relationships which would last a few weeks before ending abruptly. When asked about them, he wouldn't say a word beyond a stock "Things didn't work out". I never believed it. I think the thought of conformity was a challenging notion, given birth by the oppressive nature of the house we grew up in. It was certainly that way with me as I grew older. I remember the skyline as the sun set. It was a beautifully hazy mixture of violet and orange. Brandon looked towards me as he left, his arms hanging loosely at his sides. I had been laying on my stomach inside the truck bed, my flip flops dangling on my toes as I read page after page of the bitter story presented by Holden Caulfield. Brandon caught me doing the anti-air head thing in reading something beyond a Cosmo magazine, and almost instantly I felt embarrassed. My embarrassment found an outlet as I looked at my brother who was still without his shirt. "Brandon...I'm your sister. Not your fuckin' girlfriend. So how's about you put on a shirt?" He just shook his head at me, jabbing his thumb to the passenger seat. I held the book close to my chest as I hopped off the tailgate, closing it off. Dad had gotten Brandon this old beat up Chevy that had a scarred up bed and dents all over. It was ten years old, and still needed some work. It got him from place to place, but that was about it. I opened the door, taking a seat and buckling up. Brandon joined me inside the truck, not buckling his seat belt and taking a little sip from a canteen as he turned the key. The pistons fired up, screaming a loud roar that escaped through the exhaust pipes. "I'm gonna drive around for a bit." He didn't ask me if it was okay because it wouldn't have mattered. Besides, I knew what he meant by driving around. It was all good in my book. We drove just past the cornfield on a long dirt road until we found a patch of grassy flatland. Brandon turned off the road gently, driving out in the open space until he was comfortable with the distance away from being seen. Parking the truck, he took the key out and got out, pulling the seat forward to find a little black toolbox with a padlock on it. He looked through his keys, trying a trio that looked similar until he popped it open with the third one. He pulled out a plastic bag with five joints he had already rolled up. He stared at me with a smirk, and for a moment I forgave him for his stench. The sun was tucked away, enveloping the skyline in a perfect blackness with bright white stars dotted in random places save for the constellations. He lifted me up, giving me a boost by cupping his hands low enough so I could put a bare foot onto them. I stepped onto the hood truck, sitting down slowly as Brandon joined me by my side. He left the bag between us, pulling out a pair of roaches. He let me pick which one I wanted first, and while the gesture was sweet, it really didn't matter. Lighting up, we both just stared blankly into the night, the only thing we could hear being our breathing. There was a risk in all this, and I got a charge from it. "How was your day working?" Brandon just put his hands behind his head, easing into a more comfortable position. The minutes ticked by, the silence causing me to become unsettled. I looked over towards him, and all I noticed was that we was staring there, not in this world but in another. "Why do you let yourself be a pawn Lindz? Why do you let 'em treat you like you're dumb?" I was caught off guard by the statement that broke our distance, but it filled me with a warm sense of self worth. I couldn't help but flash a smile, but as I did my brain forced me back into my role as the dimwitted younger sister who's head was filled with air. "I bought this book Brandon, and it smells like glue. Why is that?" Brandon no sold me. "You're better than this. You always have been." I took another hit, a dull pain filling my insides. "I know. We all have our roles to play in life, right B?" "Only if you let them define you." "You've always treated me different Brandon. Why is that?" "Because you have potential. We all do. But we need to seize our moments. Seize our fuckin' time when we have the chance. Or something. I dunno. You wanna listen to some tunes or something?" Just hearing that come from someone else made me feel warm all over. It is sentimental, yes, but the fact is that it was something I needed. I wanted to feel good about myself, about my station in life. To be truthful, times were bad for me inside my head at the time, but that's wounds that haven't healed. I don't like talking about what happened then. Looking back, that little blurb of discussion with my older brother made me think we had our whole lives ahead of us to do with as we wish. It actually changed my life, and probably is the very reason I am still here. And I have to live with the fact that I privately rejected him to fit in with the rest of my kin. While he tries, there is an inherent bitterness to his reactions towards everyone, save me. And that is only some of the time. Like him, I have expectations for myself. And while our family name falls into ruin, I guess all we have to stand on is ourselves, for the institution we have partaken in for so long has made us the flawed creatures we are today. But like Brandon told me, I try to seize my moments, to be the best that I can be. Because, if we approach it like that, maybe a Youngblood can seize their reality and do something valuable with their life. Maybe become a king, or queen, rather than the idle holder of a flawed crown that magnifies disappointment and failed potential of our heritage. For while we look like a normal family on the surface, we're full of falsehoods. We play our roles, albeit poorly now. We've tasted the bitterness of loss, and without a head, our body runs around like a chicken right off the chopping block. I just hope that when we are nothing but ash, that we smell like it. That way, there would be proof of our existence in our essence, not that we were completely fake and stamped out like we were out of a mold.
