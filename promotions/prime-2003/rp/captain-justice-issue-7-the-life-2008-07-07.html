Captain Justice, Issue #7: The Life

7 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 7-7-08 I did it. ReVolution 165 was my first actual competition in several months, that minute blip on the radar with Xavier Kannon notwithstanding. Tony Rolo proceeded to run his mouth as always and go on with “little dicks” this and “you suck big dicks” that and where did it get him? Another notch on the now-superfluous Silver win column. I should’ve felt good inside. All the right ingredients for Humble Pie were there: unjustly treated my protégé like garbage, talked some shit, talked some more shit, and when it was all said and done, Tony Rolo saw the blur of my sole connecting square with his misshapen head before I pinned his narrow ass. It should’ve been notice to the wrestling world that even when I’m NOT killing my body day in and day out, I AM, always have been and always WILL BE a force that WILL fuck you up if you provoke me. I fought on behalf of my charge who was insulted by his placement on the RoloVolution card. Want to know what I was met with when I walked back to that locker room? Soren greeting me with the news that his girlfriend got a promotion. Whoopity-doo. Kid, that’s great. REAL great. From the stories you’ve espoused on our many plane trips, I’m positive she busted her ass off to get what she wanted, but when I just walked to the back, putting my ASS out on the line because you felt wronged by somebody, the first words that should’ve come out of your mouth should’ve been “Thank you, SIR.” Oh, to have that kind of naivety again. He may have been my student for going on two-and-a-half years now, but he still behaves like he just fell off the turnip truck. If you’re involved in this business and your head isn’t in the game when you’re in that arena, somebody that IS looking out for #1 is going to either dump you on your head or choke you out right quick. Especially aforementioned bodies that currently harbor grudges because you allegedly had a hand in tampering with their piss tests. He’s getting too complacent around me and his surroundings and it’s becoming very bothersome. I’m comfortable enough to admit I love him like my own son, but Soren Knox is in need of being taught a life lesson. Badly. ~ ~ ~ The gravelly-voiced, glum tones of Tom Waits exploded through the speakers of the blood-red Chevy Avalanche, the horns trumpeting in tune with the beating hearts of Sonny Silver and Soren Knox as they raced down the open road. Far and away from the hustle and bustle of civilization heading across Interstate 10 on the way to New Orleans for ReVolution 165. It was a road Sonny traveled several times over from his days in The Squared Circle keeping closer to the Southwest side of the United States; one he knew from an old wrestling friend-turned-law-enforcement who warned him in advance of where the speed traps where so far out. The end result? Going eighty-five at dusk, the sky painted a beautiful shades of pink and orange as the final beams of sun slowly faded over the vast horizon. Heavy-duty tires kicking up rocks as he navigates the beast masquerading as a vehicle down an empty stretch of Interstate. Wanting to test how badly his new rental car’s engine could go, one pedal made a mere purr transform into a full-blown roar as his foot rammed down on the pedal and cut through sky. “WHOA, WHOA, WHOA!” was Soren’s contribution as Sonny continued to snarl in tune with the sounds of the Chevy’s engine. Holding onto the Jesus bar for dear like it was the last chopper out of Saigon, “Soren,” Sonny replied with a wry grin as he rolled down his window and allowed a wintry breeze to blow against his face. “The great Winston Churchill said it best… stop being a goddamn baby and live a little!” “Uh, trying! Riding down the road like a maniac? Not helping!” Letting his protégé’s pleas for normalcy fall to the wayside, Sonny couldn’t help but smile to himself as peered upward. The side of the sky devoid of sunlight was already showing him a sea of diamonds swirling around in a sea of black. Moments like these were rare treasures to Sonny. Even man expending much energy into hating his fellow man needed a break. He sighed to himself when he glanced at Soren and remembered the purpose for bringing him out here. “All right, fine,” Sonny decried the entire time he began tampering with the gas. “Besides… we need to talk shop, kiddo.” Easing off the gas pedal, Sonny opted to press down on the brakes, letting the pressure placed on the engine die down. Now going with the flow at a respectable 60 miles an hour, Soren couldn’t help but be alarmed at how quickly the fire within both teacher and vehicle died down; eerily in synch. “Troy Douglas and the Upstarts. What do you know about any of them?” Asked Sonny, the question armed with a hidden cache of concern for whatever Soren’s answer may be. “The Human Headlock Machine, right?” he chuckled at his own bad joke, but ceased immediately upon inspection of the humorless expression adoring Silver’s face. “Uh… yeah. Intense Champion. The Upstarts… weren’t they frat boys? Or was that DUI?” “DUI are the frat boys. The Upstarts were some retards around for a cup of coffee last year. Me and Shakur smacked them around once in a tag team match, I think.” “Pfft, no sweat,” the voice behind Captain Justice boasted proudly. “Troy Douglas isn’t going to know what the hell hit him when he’s got two-hundred eighty pounds barreling into his chest. I plan on stopping the Upstarts in their tracks. I don’t plan on wasting all the momentum I’ve gained from battling Tyler Rayne up and down the Greater 48 on some punks looking to make names for themselves.” “All well and good, my friend… but you do know you’re teaming with the Princes, right?” The boastful look? Gone. In its place? A dour façade. “Simon and his flying monkey?” “The very same.” “Joy,” Soren answered with a Hurricane Katrina-sized torrent of sarcasm. “You know, I hear a lot all the time about how good workers get screwed… admit it, I pissed off CP Cantrell.” “Well,” Sonny responded, eyes fixated on the road. “We did kind of have a hand in tampering with Killean Sirrajin’s drug test. If you believe otherwise, I’ve got a Baby Dusk I can sell you back in Seattle. It cries and moans just like the real thing. But, anyway… the family shit. Keep it to yourselves. You want another shot at Tyler Rayne down the line, you need to start getting some more wins under your belt to get back into contention.” “Yeah…” his voice trailed off as he touched a hand to the very same throat that had its air robbed thanks to a Tyler Rayne Triangle Choke that cost him his very first shot at championship gold. “But enough about that. What about Killean?” “Pfft. Killean’s a mental midget. By the time he knows what’s going on, we’ll have busted his ass and he’ll be no problem for us.” “I know, right?” Another nervous chortle was met with silence. Whipping out his cell phone to check for texts, disappointed appearance crossed his face. “Shit, I still haven’t heard back from Rachel. She’s been shopping with some twink named Ethan. I need to give her a call.” Sonny turned his head. “You need to focus on the task at hand, that’s what you need to do. Worry about next week and keep your thoughts on how you’re going to carry the Princes to a win.” Settling into his seat, Soren Knox continued to gaze out the passenger window, staring off into nothing. “I’m sure I can do it, Son. Don’t worry. It’ll be yet another situation in a long line of many where I carry his narrow ass. It’s just like old times. Like this time when he and I were in our teens. He kept trying to tag along with all my friends, right? Before he started off with this dapper “holier-than-thou” no-sell routine, he was a needy little bitch always wanting attention.” “Soren…” “…Anyway, me and some friends waited for him one day after school because we were tired of his tagalong crap…” “Soren…” “…We ganged up on him and duct taped his ass to the basketball hoop in nothing but his boxers…” “SOREN…” “…It was awesome, man, you should’ve seen it…” “SOREN!" Sonny’s fist greeted the upholstery, nearly shaking the Sirius radio mounted on the dash out of its screws while making Soren jump in the process. “You’ve been getting sloppy, Soren. And I’m telling you this for your own good, but right now, your performances have been pretty stop-and-go. That kind of shit doesn’t play here in the big leagues. Besides… name something you’ve done recently.” “Tyler Rayne. Something you’ve never done.” “Beat him, but he beat you right back after you gassed out AFTER he got through facing Logic and Simply Beautiful.” “Xavier Kannon.” “Great, you beat a guy whose entire arsenal consists of “jump, rinse, repeat. And how about when you dropped the ball against your first main event match with Lindsay Troy and Tony Gamble.” “Oh, Jesus,” Soren trailed off, eyes locked onto his trainer. “YOU’RE gonna lecture ME on losing to Lindsay Troy? That’s rich. That’s like Dusk giving advice on winning world championships…” THAT DID IT. The brakes SLAMMED viciously, nearly jettisoning both occupants of the vehicle through the glass were it not for their seatbelts. The truck now resting awkwardly against the shoulder of the empty road, Silver ran a hand through his mane. Forcing himself to look at his ward straight on, he let his next words rest in the back of his throat before he shot them from his lips. “You listen here and you listen GOOD, you little shit!” His lips curled inward, the scowl enough to startle his charge. “I have beaten NAMES. Names you aren’t worthy of tying the boots of. I have given you EVERYTHING, Soren. EVERYTHING. Do you understand me? I could’ve picked anybody for your role. Out of the hundreds of students that have graduated the Silver Lining, YOU were the one with promise and YOU were the one with the fire. Where’d it go, Soren? Where’d that fire go?” Soren buried a face in his palm against the glass. “You know, what is your OBSESSION with wins and losses, Sonny? Quite frankly, I don’t give a shit. I’ve been wrestling because I love to. Because I know I’m good at it and because it makes me happy. The fact that I’m getting a little W every now and again means nothing to me because it IS nothing to me. No matter what, I’m going to look back on this six-man tag and shrug my shoulders because nobody’s gonna remember it one, two, five years from now.” “Winning is EVERYTHING, Knox. It’s EVERYTHING. You sit there and think all is well because you run with me. News flash: I’m a fuckhead. I’m THE Fuckhead. I’m not the most well-liked person in the locker room. If you’re under my stead and you aren’t doing something to make yourself more noticeable around these parts, then you’re nothing. They’ll treat you like nothing and before you know it, you’ll be doing thirty-minute chain-wrestling sequences where your only fans will be seeing you through grainy DVDs.” Sinking back into his seat, visibly defeated for the moment, he allows Sonny to continue pouring his emotions. “That’s WHY I had Killean Sirrajin’s drug test tampered with. He wasn’t going to bat an eye your way, despite how much you idolize him. He needed a catch to be drawn to us… more importantly, to you. It’s not fair, but that’s LIFE. That’s THIS life where you do what you gotta do to put the ball in your court. You going to slam dunk that motherfucker or are you going to pull another 5-Star Title match and choke?” “Shut up,” was all Soren could retort with, quietly. Sonny ignored him. “No. When I’m talking, you better goddamn listen, Soren Knox.” An odd silence was all Soren could put in. Sonny nodded, menacingly. “Besides. Your brother? That Simon Knox guy? HE’S the kind of guy I’m looking for.” Perking his head up, Soren shot up from his funk. “Yeah,” his trainer continued. “He’s got the STAR look. Takes no shit from anybody. THAT’S the kind of protégé I’m looking for. Good looks, great ring presence, great performer…” “Shut it…” He eyed Sonny with grinding teeth, but it wasn’t enough to shut the valve of words Sonny poured out. “Yup… I might have made a mistake with you… your brother’s better for the job, anyw-” He doesn’t get to finish his sentence as an open palm greets the former Universal Champion across his face, sending a gob of spit splattering against the windshield. Soren’s hand remained firmly in the slapping position, but raised a quizzical eyebrow when the response he was expecting – that of retaliation – was replaced by laughter. “What the fuck’s so funny?” Soren inquired, nonplussed. “By jove,” came Sonny’s instant reaction as he patted a hand on Soren’s shoulder. “I think he’s found it!” ~ ~ ~ I did it. I saw a glimpse of it, albeit, for a solitary moment; the fire that burned deep within Soren Knox when he first came to me in a grungy t-shirt and jeans, begging for a spot in my school and wanting to not be just any wrestler. The best. EVER. He trained so hard, to masochistic levels at times. Even when his gargantuan physique gave out on him, he’d keep beating on himself like there was no tomorrow even after everyone else departed for the day. He scraped skin off his bare knuckles and pummeled his back to the point where I thought his spine would resemble an MS victim. Over time, he grew to just love the sport for what it was rather than for his personal gain. It’s an admirable attitude, but on this level of competition, you need to decide what’s good for business and you’ll make decisions you’ll regret for the rest of your life. Some you’ll like, some you won’t, but if you show hesitation, even for JUST a moment, another shark not conflicted by emotion will feast on you just because they can. Soren, I truly am sorry. I’m sorry that I’m not the person you want me to be. I’m sorry that I deliberately gave you that money to get your life started just to make you feel you were indebted to me for life. I’m sorry that ultimately, I’m using you for my own personal gain rather than being in it solely for what will undoubtedly be your meteoric rise to stardom. I’m sorry I continue to tug your heartstrings, knowing that you’re not in full control of your emotions and that your soul is still malleable. But put me back in that hotel room, arm broken and not a soul to talk to in the world besides you begging for more time to train? Put me back in that situation, having everything to gain and nothing to lose? I’d hand you that check all over again. This business is my life. And I’d gladly choose life over death any day.
