Letting Go And Hanging On (Colossus IV, vs. Sonny Silver, Uni Title Defense)

Date: Various August 8, 2007 Unfamiliar Territory. Early morning Like most late July/early August mornings in Cambridge, Massachusetts, the day dawned sunny and hazy, the promise of near-unbearable humidity later in the day evident in the air. Even the slight breeze coming off the Charles River did little to dissipate the growing mugginess, instead pushing the warmness over the Boston suburb and letting it hang overhead. On Grozier Road, college students ran along the tree-lined sidewalks, no "real job" to go to and nowhere else to be except out exercising before it got too hot. Front doors slowly opened to reveal either pajama-clad inhabitants out to get the morning paper or business-attired working worlders heading to their cars to drive themselves, or be driven, into Boston. All seemed to be the 7:30AM status quo until a line of moving trucks slowly rumbled down the street, breaking up the peace and quiet of the high-end neighborhood with squeaky brakes and puffs of exhaust from their tailpipes. This unusual Wednesday morning activity brought more people out of their homes to watch the trucks line up, one by one, up along the street in front of number forty. The house's SOLD sign swayed proudly in-between two tiny shrubs in front of the house, bringing even more attention to itself than in previous days. The house hadn't been on the market that long, the neighbors had recalled in conversations with one another over swanky dinner parties in early July, before the sign was changed from FOR SALE to its present status in what seemed to be only a matter of days. Nevermind that none of them had even seen the new homeowner yet, with socialite wives keeping watch through silk curtains in-between manicures, pedicures, tanning sessions, facials and hair appointments for themselves and their teacup-sized dogs. Quite an intriguing start to the day, indeed. Across the street, a small group of people gathered to watch the movers exit their vehicles, noticing for the first time the array of different kinds of trucks parked on the street: Three from M-Geough, a local furniture company, two from Precision Movers with Florida license plates and three separate car transport trucks; not the big rigs but smaller, private ones. The men milled around their trucks, talking and laughing, while one pulled out a cell phone to presumably dial the occupant inside. "Have you ever seen so many moving vans?" Constance McKenney, a financial advisor in her early forties, inquired. She was standing on her front porch dressed in a cognac-colored suit, briefcase in hand, while her neighbors slowly trickled to her. "The Jameson's had six when they moved in two years ago, but that was only because their bratty kids needed to take everything with them," sniffed Maura Logan, a do-little socialite whose hair and makeup were always perfectly done by 7:00am every day. Her bubble-gum pink velour track suit could blind unprotected eyes with its brightness, which made Constance glad she had sunglasses on. "I hope these people don't have any kids. That's the last thing this neighborhood needs: more little banshees screaming their heads off at 6am." "Oh come on, Maura, the Jamesons' kids aren't so bad," Marshall McKenney said with a laugh, then checked his watch. "Gah, I'm going to be late for my 8:30 at this rate. I need to remind my assistant not to schedule meetings right at the start of the day. You girls behave yourselves and give me all the juicy details later." He kissed his wife on the cheek, then headed to his BMW. "He doesn't think they're bad because he sleeps like a rock, right through the alarm most of the time," Constance shook her head with a wry smile. "I wonder what these people will be like." "Here's hoping it's a bachelor with a nice stomach," sighed Samantha Collins. "All of the cute ones are over on Highland, and they're never outside when you want to sneak a peek." The women all giggled; even Constance, the only married one of the three. They continued to watch and speculate about the age, gender and occupation of the new resident when Constance's teenage son Nolan slunk behind his mom and leaned against the inside of the door. "You're all wrong about the new one." Constance, Maura and Samantha turned to look at Nolan, who was brushing bagel crumbs off the front of his rumpled t-shirt. "Do you know who's going to be living there, Nolan?" Constance asked. "Yeah. I saw her when I was coming in from walking the dog last night." "Ahhh, another female," Maura said, now regarding the house with a territorial air. "She must have money." "Yeah, you could say that." Constance studied her son's face, watching his lips turn upwards in a knowing smile. "Who is it, dear?" Across the street, the front door opened and a Doberman trotted out onto the front porch, sitting obediently while his owner stepped out behind him, then loped down the stairs and around the side of the house at the owner's command. The ladies across the street gawked at the exceptionally tall woman walking down the stairs and striding along the front path to greet the movers. "It's Lindsay Troy," was Nolan's only reply. ~**~**~**~**~ June 19, 2007 Casa di Troy: Tampa, Florida. Early evening. The luggage met the polished wood with a CRASH as Lindsay let her body slide down the front door to the floor, the weight of unseen burdens expediting her descent. She was home again, except this house didn't quite feel like home anymore; ironic considering she spent nearly her entire life living inside its walls. Now, it seemed foreign to her: a place she knew she owned but felt uncomfortable in once inside, not unlike a stranger. Her stomach churned at the thought of its emptiness, with no one to come home to now except her long-time security staff and her dogs. Her mother left first, her life involuntarily claimed by a brain aneurysm while in childbirth with Alaina, then her father in a plane crash six years later. Alaina moved to Houston before her marriage, taking the Radicomm company headquarters with her. The men Troy's dated have long since gone, some hating her first before she got the chance to, and now with Melton added to the list, Lindsay wondered exactly what was keeping her rooted to a house so full of heartache and bad memories. The good times felt like they happened a century ago and she wasn't sure if she was up to making new ones anymore, at least not in this palace that her father had built all those years ago. She raised her hand upward, flicking the hallway light on and then no-look feeling along the wall to the intercom embedded in it. Her finger fell on the right button, pressed it and waited for Frank Macera to pick up the phone at the security station near the front gate. "What's up, kid?" "Frank, I need a chat." Her mouth felt like her dentist stuffed gauze between her cheeks and gums. There was a pause. "Alright, let me call Johnny on the radio to cover here. Give me ten." "I'll be in the kitchen." ------------- Frank choked on his water, bringing his glass down away from his mouth and managing to swallow before coughing violently. Lindsay watched him with lifted eyes, her chin resting on top of her hands, which were lying flat on the smooth marble top of the kitchen island. "And you decided this...when?" he asked after finding his voice again. "Ten minutes ago." "You come home after a month on the road and you decide you don't want to live here anymore?" Lindsay nodded, then lifted her body up when Ciro, one of her three Dobermans, half-climbed up on her chair to lay his head in her lap. She scritched him behind his ears and he huffed. "Frank, every time I've come back home since January I've felt completely hollow, like I don't belong here anymore. I've lived in this house for twenty-two years; I shouldn't be feeling this way. This is my home, and now it feels like a cheap motel I wouldn't be caught dead staying in." "Lin, if this is because of the separation from Joey..." "It's not...it's not just that," she sighed, then looked down at the counter top. "It's Mom and Dad and Jaime and Marcus and Matthias. It's Sonny. It's everything that's happened that's has been stacked one on top of the other with Joey being the peak of this gigantic mountain of shit." Frank placed his forearms on the marble, then put his weight on them, listening closely while the woman who thought of him as an uncle, despite no blood relation, continued on with her waterfall of concerns. "Alaina's moved on because she wanted to, for a new life with Dan in a new home with a new family," Lindsay said, now petting Ciro's head. "She's never felt the way I do because she's never had these kinds of things happen to her. I need to move on. I have to, or else all my demons are going to keep bombarding me every time I walk through the front door, no matter how hard I try to push them away. Everything good that's happened keeps getting pushed to the edge and blocked out. I can't keep living like this, Frank." "No, I suppose you can't," he frowned, then placed his weathered hand on top of hers. "Do you have any idea where you want to go?" "Not New York, that's for sure. The city's not big enough for me and Melton. And there are too many Yankees fans there anyway." Her blunt reasoning made Frank laugh,a deep chortle rising from the depths of his slight beer belly. "Well, I can't think of a better reason than that." Lindsay smiled, then stood up from her seat, causing Ciro to jump down to all fours. "Wherever I end up, it'll be a fresh start. I think that's the most important thing here." "Those demons may follow you, Lin. You may never get rid of them." "They may. But I think this is a good start on the road to exorcism." ~**~**~**~**~ July 27, 2007 Oystercatchers: Tampa, Florida. Night. "I'd like to make a toast," Brent DiPetrillo flattened his tie against his shirt as he stood up from the table, lightly tinning his dinner fork against his wine glass to call his family's attention to him, "To my beautiful wife Elise, who's been my anchor for over thirty years of marriage, who's weathered the storms by my side, never drifted away for clearer waters and who doesn't look a day over forty-five." "Oh what, you couldn't say twenty-five?" "Hey, let's not press your luck here, babe." Everyone laughed and clapped as Brent sat back down, leaning over to give Elise a kiss on the mouth. "You two girls will be wishing you look as good as her when you get to be fifty-eight." "Brent! Stop! It's bad enough you made cheesy nautical references in front of half the restaurant." "Listen, I stayed away from saying you're my true North. But I meant every word." Lindsay and Alaina smiled at their aunt and uncle, who were still very much in love with each other after all these years. Next to Alaina, Dan Ryan squeezed his wife's hand underneath the table before everyone dug into their dinners. There were very few occasions that they all could get together, with Dan and Lindsay on the road nearly year-round, but the times they shared were always special, no matter how grand or low-key in nature. "Before we start getting too involved with our food, I was hoping to say a little something of my own," Lindsay said, putting her fork down and taking a sip of water. All eyes reverted to her as she looked down at her plate to compose her thoughts. "We're gathered here tonight to celebrate Elise's birthday and I don't want to take away from the celebration, but since it's a rare occasion where we're under the same roof, let alone sharing a meal, I wanted to tell everyone all at once instead of individually face-to-face or over the phone." "Lin, I swear, if you even tell us you're getting back with that dipshit..." "I know, Brent, you'll toss me out of the family and remove me from the Christmas card list," Troy said, wryly. "It's not about that." She took a deep breath and just came out with it. "I'm moving, out of Tampa. Out of Florida, actually." Alaina's fork clattered onto her plate, crystal blue eyes widening in shock as she looked at her sister. Brent and Elise looked surprised and even Dan, whose general demeanor was usually stoic, looked a bit taken aback. Silence befell the table and it was a long moment before anyone could find the right words. "But....WHY?!" exclaimed Alaina, not bothering to check her emotions in the presence of family and strangers. "I need to, Lanni. I can't stay in that house anymore. It's too hard now." "What's too hard? I don't understand." "Alaina, calm down..." "Lindsay, you can't just sell that house without telling me first! Daddy built it! I can't believe this..." "Hold on a second," Lindsay motioned with her hands, attempting to assuage her sister and prevent a large public freak-out. "First of all, I'm not selling it. I could never sell it, just for that reason. I want that house to stay in the family and I don't want strangers living in it. But after everything that's happened, both lately and in the past, I feel like a stranger going to a place that feels alien and unknown. I don't like feeling that way in a place I've called home for so long." "But you, we've, lived there for years. Now all of a sudden you're packing up and leaving?" "How many good years have I had in that house, Lanni? Really think about it. There was the three I had with mom and dad before you were born, but I was so young that I can barely even remember, and ever since Dad died there's been a veil of sadness hanging over the place. A lot of the time we don't know it's there, sure, but it doesn't mean it's just disappeared. I've had happy times in that house but overall there's a lot of sorrow and baggage that won't go away, may never go away, some of which I've brought on myself. It doesn't feel like home anymore and I need to go somewhere where I can have that feeling again." "She has a point, Alaina," Elise said, reaching over the table and squeezing Lindsay's and Alaina's hands. "You two haven't had the easiest of lives, despite being born into a life that would have been easy if not for unfortunate circumstances." "Lanni, I'm not doing this because the divorce means I'm on my own again. I've felt this way for a long time, but I never left because I thought that my life would balance itself out. I don't think it's going to as long as I still call that house home. I need something fresh, new scenery, new experiences." Alaina nodded, softly. "So you're not selling the house." "No, and I may stay there in the winter if I can't take the snow." "Snow?" "Yeah," Lindsay smiled. "I'm moving up to Cambridge, outside of Boston. I closed on the house last Saturday. Move-in date's the 8th." "Guess the location makes sense, Sox Fan," Dan interjected with a smirk. "Don't hate just because the 'Stros are terrible this season," Lindsay retorted. "Oh, I would never..." "I'm happy for you, Lin," Brent said, raising his glass again. "This will be good for you. When's your last night here?" "The 29th. I'm doing one more big training session with Tiffany tomorrow, then I have some things to do around the house on Sunday, then Monday I leave for Seattle. Colossus Press Week." "I think I may have to join you tomorrow, then." Now all eyes were on Brent. "Brent," Elise started, "you can't. Your stroke..." "Oh fuck the stroke...sorry, sweetheart, but I need to say it," he apologized as Elise winced at his language. "I trained this girl for ten years before she ventured off into the wrestling world and there's no way I'm going to miss being a part of her last hard workout before facing that ceffo Silver. You remember that time he came into the dojo with that Rory Hayes kid? A smug prick that one," Brent smirked at Lindsay, "I may have had a stroke, but I can still break cinderblocks as hard and big as his head." "It'll be just like old times then, DP," she grinned. "You know I can't stop you once you get something in your head," Elise groaned. "But enough with this macho, smash-kick talk. Lin, spill all the details about the house. I want to hear everything..." ~**~**~**~**~ July 29, 2007 Basement of Casa di Troy: Tampa, Florida. The last night. "I've been waiting for this night for quite some time, Sonny." The basement of Lindsay Troy's Tampa home was notorious for being a wrestling "dungeon" of sorts, akin to "The Dungeon" in Calgary with much more modern amenities. A circuit of weight training machines lined along one wall, free weights along another, wall-to-wall mirrors wrapped around the expansive space and, in the center, a regulation-sized wrestling ring. Surround-sound speakers played "Whatever" by Godsmack on repeat, a tune familiar to both Lindsay Troy and Sonny Silver, having been their tag team theme music for as long as they were together. Troy stood tall in the ring, decked in her full fighting attire, with the PRIME Universal Title strapped around her waist. Long sheets covered the mirrors along the back, so as not to project the cameraman's reflection while filming. With a heated glare and a frosty smile, her hazel orbs penetrated through the camera lens and her lips worked their condescending magic. "Throughout your little escapades as Mister Silver, Rogue Agent to Mister Silver, Fuck You Flunky to Sonny Silver, Broken Record, I've kept, for the most part, generally quiet as far as your antics are concerned. Quite a feat for me, you know. Outside of the usually ribbings and truth-sayings, I've pretty much let you run your mouth about me, knowing full well when the time was right...when it counted the most, I'd have my full say. "Lucky for me, you've given me so much to work with. "I suppose anyone with common sense and an Internet connection can find their own Way-Back Machine and go back in time to a few years past, when you and I first crossed paths in that Disney-run conglomerate-slash-wrestling fed, but I won't bore the general public with all the details as to just how much I carried your near-dead weight ass. The history is important, as you know, but I'll spare reminding everyone that it was my idea to team up, my reasonings behind it, my idea for the name and the theme music and the targets, et cetera et cetera et cetera. "Like most things in life, the woman did all the work. What did you have to do? Get on a mic, scream at people, and occasionally kick someone in the head really hard. "Wow. You're some pig, Wilbur. Sommmmme pig." Troy smirks now, putting her hands on her hips. "You've done everything possible to get me to notice you as something other than an opportunistic leech, including bringing my family up to get under my skin. You think that's some novel concept in today's day and age? I've built up an immunity to it over the years after the first thousand people employed that tactic to rile me up but deep down, you're a softy for your little girl, as much as you try to hide it. You brought up my father as casually as you would the color of the sky or the dinner specials at a restaurant, but there's one thing my father never did. "He never willingly abandoned Alaina and I. "A dead man is a better man than you. "You walked away to restart your career, a choice I'm sad to say you'll be regretting once you walk out of Seattle, the town you once called home but now casually refer to as an afterthought, empty-fucking-handed. Your so-called homecoming will not turn out to be so happy. "You said your ego's gone, but your ego never left. And what an absolute blow it's going to take next Sunday in front of fifty thousand-plus strong when I make you realize that keeping your enemies too close is a bigger mistake than making an enemy out of a friend. "You crave what I have, but you haven't suffered enough for your yearnings to be rewarded. You're here as the number one contender because of me. Not because you deserve it. Not because you worked super hard to get here. But because I broke your hand and made you shoot your load over it. I kept silent while you publicly stewed, I revealed in your anger while aloofly ignoring your pleas, but never once drowning you out. I waited. And it paid off when I wanted it to. "Don't think I saved you from Blaine's axe just to give the people what they want. I saved you to remind you that where your control has wavered, mine has always been in check. "I'm not going to be a 'one and done' champion. "You knocked me down, but you let me get back up. You'll be lamenting that until the end of days, Sonny Gabriel Silver. I'll be seeing to it. "You should not start what you can't finish, partner. "That's what my daddy always said." BLACK.
