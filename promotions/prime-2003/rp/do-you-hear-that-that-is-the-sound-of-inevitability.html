Date: Events occur 4/20/08 and 10/06/06 Everything that has a beginning, has an end. Sometimes, you end with that beginning. Other times, you begin with that end. This time is somewhere in the middle. ---- The bullets tore through the plaster, sending stony shards through the lobby. The security force was a crack team, armed with M-16 machine guns and chest armor, and there were more of them than the two invaders in black trenchcoats. That didn't mean they stood a chance. The guards fired a relentless stream of automatic weapons fire at the pair, and the lobby was quickly filled with a smoke borne of shattered and crumbling pillars. The man hunkered down low behind one of the pillars, face serenely calm as he tossed aside two empty weapons and waited for his opportunity. When he stood, the man's shots were relentless, and their aim was next to perfect. Men in darkly colored helmets fell as he charged, a submachine gun in each hand. The spent bullet casings littered the hard tile floor. One guard across the lobby tried to draw a bead on the man as he ran, pumping his shotgun fearlessly. His eyes told a story. Just give me one shot, you bastard. The man's partner saw the shotgun-toting soldier, and she sprung into action. One hard, high kick sent the gun into the air, spinning end over end. It fell into her grasp. She fired a shot into the soldier's back, then two more at nearby guards before they saw her and she had to retreat behind the crumbling ceramic of a pillar. Beautifully worked marble shattered around the man as he pressed relentlessly forward, not caring when the magazines of his weapons clicked empty. A nearby guard's fallen weapon caught his sight, black against the green of the floor. He leapt forward, turning a one-handed cartwheel to avoid another hail of gunfire. He came up with the gun-- And then the world went black. ---- The woman with the forged signature of highlighted brown hair looked away from the darkened television set, lower lip jutting forward into a pout. "I was watching that!" Cozen folded her arms over her not-prodigious chest, a legal-sized pad of yellow paper lying cross-wise in her lap. "The Matrix is a great movie. Bang, bang, the real world should be like that." They were in the back seat of a stretch limousine, wending their way through the streets of downtown Chicago; a small television set built into the console now gone dark thanks in large part to the remote control in the hands of the woman sitting over the PRIME Universal Champion's shoulder. "The real world," Siena van der Wal said. "Is not the Matrix." "That's an ironic comment," Andreas said, perched nearby with the Chicago Sun Times open. "You know, considering the content of the movie." "I don't know why you're all stressed out," Cozen said, picking up the well-chewed nub of a #2 pencil and going back to what passed for her journal. "It's not like we're going to meet Tinkerbell." "You will kind of wish we were, I think," Siena commented. "I'm rather certain she will, actually," Andreas put in, not looking up from a fascinating story on the upcoming South Dakota primaries. "I was trying not to freak her out by overstating it." "She's drawing a castle, dearheart," Andreas chided gently. "I doubt you could manage that feat." "You never know." ---- This requires some explanation, perhaps. But first, an interlude. ---- The Year of Our Lord, 2006. Granite Peak is a very unimpressive name for the highest point in the state of Montana and the seventh tallest peak in the continental United States (ninth in the whole country -- damn you, Alaska and Hawaii), but it is considered one of the most difficult in the United States to ascend due to inclement weather, the skill it takes to climb, and difficulty in finding a route up to the summit, and thus, it was the last of the state highpoints to be climbed. Two travelers, bundled from head to toe in thick winter gear, picked their way up to the base camp at the Froze-to-Death Plateau, breath coming in heavy, damp plumes as the exertion turned their faces red. The gentleman wore dark blue, the woman bright red. Both wore knit caps (hers had a jaunty ball atop it). Their faces were covered by white masks to protect them from the cold, while their eyes were shielded by thick shades to stave off the glare. He favored snowshoes, while she picked her way along with ski poles. "This is..." the woman gasped for air. "This is the dumbest idea she's had in years." "You say that every time," the man responded. "That's because it's true." There are no pathways from the ground up to the Froze-to-Death Plateau, and the wind gusts along Granite Peak make it unsafe for helicopters to drop off passengers. So if you want to come to the Plateau (or, heaven forbid, go beyond it), be prepared to, as they say, pack a lunch. Actually, be prepared to pack several. But that's why he chose it to hide out here. ---- May 20, 2008 "Cross your legs. You're a lady." "Fuck that, lovely, it's not like we're taking her to dinner with the Queen of bloody England." "Says you." "You're stressing out for nothing. I've met with her before, and it was entirely no-stress. Completely laid back." "That's because you have a penis, Andy." Cozen sat in a high-backed chair, dressed in a deep scarlet dress, not slinky so much as it was girlish. Lindsay Troy's hair was bound up and plaited behind her in a braid, held in place by a black-and-white, oblong octagon of a thing. A long table separated the van der Wals from their mandated charge. "One of you should decide whether I should worry or not," Cozen said, not looking up as she picked at her milky white leggings. "Because I always lean towards not worrying. Momma always said I'd get crow's feet, chop 'em off and put 'em in the soup." "You'll be fine," Andreas van der Wal said reassuringly. "If you listen to every word I tell you," Siena van der Wal added. "You two seriously need to read the same book," Cozen scolded. "We'll worry about getting you on the same page sometime soon." Andreas sighed, directing a warm smile at, were size to be put out of the equation, might be considered a preteen version of Lindsay Troy, especially if she'd come from an affluent family. "Given the amount of home strife I'd have to go through if we didn't listen to Ms. Siena, I suggest you be at least slightly concerned." "Okay, then," Siena smiled, and it was not a pleasant thing. "Remember to sit up straight, cross your feet at the ankle, and never -- and I mean never look her in the eyes. She hates that." "Why?" "I never asked," Siena said. "No one has," Andreas emphasized. The softest honking comes from... somewhere not in this room, muffled by walls in between walls. "Our ride's here." Andreas bent down for his leather attaché case. "Did you get the white stretch or the black one?" Siena asked as she turned to follow the blond man out of the room. "Or -- ooh! -- the Hummer!" "I went with the ordinary black." "Aww." "I figured she would dislike our drawing any attention to it. I almost considered a town car." "I would stay here -- where's Cozen?" Andreas stuck his head around into the room, smiling brightly. "Come, dear. We have punch and pie." "Punch and pie?" Andreas nodded. "And a DVD player in the car." That was all it took for the Faceless Fighter to skip out of the room, braid flaring out behind her. The movie she chose was, of course, The Matrix. ---- The man's name was Niles Gregory, or at least one of them was. For many years, Niles did many bad things for many bad people -- he unearthed secrets folks would rather leave buried, he ransacked houses looking for that one scrap of paper that turned a whispered rumor into an inescapable truth... he'd even turned kidnapped family members against their loved ones. That all changed one day when Niles became just as vulnerable as the people he'd mercilessly targeted. Perhaps learning of his daughter made him like the Grinch at the end of the Seuss cartoon, and his heart grew three sizes that day. Perhaps his ego simply couldn't tolerate being victimized by the same tactics his employer paid him to use against the common folk. Whatever the case, the day Niles learned his seed had, indeed, sprouted was the last day Niles Gregory did bad things for a living. What followed might have changed if the dark-haired girl in question was actually his child, of course. ---- As they drove through the Windy City, the PRIME Universal Champion made notes in a new legal pad, wrapped in a green cardboard cover with "The Docket" imprinted in the lower right hand corner. XOXO, COZIE ---- In the movies -- like, say, in The Matrix -- everything important happens up high in a building. After all, it's the glamorous companies that occupy the top of a skyscraper, the ones that can afford the high-rent office space with spectacular views. So it could be somewhat of a surprise that the elevator Cozen's riding in alongside the van der Wals dings to a halt after cresting only fourteen of the building's thirty-four stories. Get used to those little quirks. The trio made their way down the hall, stopping before a frosted glass window. "Elisabeth Quinones, DC PA," it read. "I don't think I need a chiropractor, Tyler," Cozen objected. "At least not until I get to practice my wicked contortionist skills. Of which I have none." "It's more of a... special kind of chiropractor," Siena suggested. "More of a metaphysical one," added Andreas. Within, the office was small -- little more than a nook, really -- with a huddle of mismatched chairs (brightly colored plastics, fresh from the assembly line, alongside darkly stained woods, pockmarked and chipped by years of use) surrounding a sliding glass window. A dark-haired woman, lustrous strands of tar bound up into a silver clasp shaped like the Caduceus of Hermes, filed her nails obliviously on the other side of the window. Andreas made his way to the counter, knocking gently on the glass. The tar-headed girl started, yanking the white cord of an earbud headphone from her ear. "Andy!" she whined-slash-exclaimed. "How are you? You scared me!" But she was still behind the glass, which masked every third word or so -- it was truly thicker glass than any chiropractor had any right to have. "Watch this," Siena murmured to Cozen as the receptionist tugged open the glass window. "He's like magic with a penis." The dark-haired girl wore a short, snug white lab coat, her nametag reading, "Nurse Maggie." Dimples formed on her cheeks in her smile. "Let's try that again," she said ashamedly. "Hi, Andy." "Maggie," Andreas said. "I love it when she's in town, just so I can come see you here." Maggie rested her elbows on the countertop. "If I believed you, you know I'd make sure to arrange things like this week more often." "Ah, if only we had the ability to control our schedules. Our girl would be competing in Barbados all the time. One of the people in the federation lives there, so he'd get the hometown pop." "Barbados is totally awesome, yeah. Like... this one time, we went, and I got to meet the bobsled team! Sooooo awesome." "That's..." Andreas shakes his head. "Nevermind. The Doc's in?" "For you, Andy?" Her smile was brilliant; her upper arms did their part in collecting and framing. "For you, I bet she's always in." "So that means yes?" "She's in the piano room." Cozen's whisper was hushed as she tugged on Siena's shirtsleeve. "Why does a chiropractor have a piano room?" "Because she wants it." The answer was delivered matter-of-factly, as if it was obvious. "Excellent." Andreas signaled with a hand to Siena and the Faceless Fighter, who moved just on the outskirts of Nurse Maggie's sight. "I'm supposed to show you all inside in just a few -- " The interior door closed with a soft click. "Hey! You're going to get me in trouble!" Andreas laid a hand atop Maggie's with That smile in full effect. "It's okay," he assured her. "She likes Siena." "No, she doesn't." "Well. No, she doesn't." ---- Most doctor's offices are similarly designed in a sort of "spoke" system, a series of consultation rooms surrounding a central area that is one-half nurse's station and one half doctor's room. Dr. Elisabeth Quinones' office was not dissimilar to this setup, although the office was too small to have more than three rooms for her to see patients in, and one whole wall had been knocked out, combining two of the rooms into one larger room. It was from the larger room that the soft tinkling of a baby grand piano emanated from, and it was to that room that Siena van der Wal led the woman called Cozen. The music wafting their way was quite pleasant, actually; high-pitched and melodic, though it followed no appreciable pattern. The door to the room was closed, a plastic red pennant flipped outward. Siena glanced up at the plastic, pulling one burgundy lip in between her teeth. "What does it mean?" Cozen asked. "Red means stop. Blood. Fire. Courage." "It means she doesn't wish to be disturbed." Siena's voice was quiet. "Oh." Cozen's shoulders slumped, her posture going from straight line to gentle arc. She dug the toe of her patent leather Mary Janes into the ground. When she looked up, the Troyslash had split her features. "Well, fuck that." Young Trozen bumped upon the door. Within, the room was faintly barren, the wallpaper a grungy shade of grayish green. Pressed up against the wall was a glossy black piano, propped open and being played expertly by the woman seated on the bench. Long fingers wrapped in black leather played so smoothly across the ivories, they might be said to be "dancing". The woman was dressed head to toe in white, an expensive and flattering pantsuit with the barest hint of black around the lapels of the suitjacket. Well-worn, flat-heeled boots depressed bronzed pedals as her fingers sped up, flying along the whites and blacks. The hair that fell in front of the woman's face did not deter her, or obscure her sight of her fingers -- but this was because her eyes were closed. It was like a statue played the piano in a chiropractor's office in downtown Chicago -- and that was not even the weirdest thing this woman had done that day. Fans of professional wrestling who dared watch other networks than fX to get their fix knew this woman -- and they hated her. But Cozen didn't watch anything on television other than great movies like The Matrix, so she had never seen Alexandra Eloise Pierce before. Oh, this was going to be good. ---- But first, another interlude. ---- 2006 The moment that a hike crests onto a plateau is, when that hike carries one partway up a mountain the size of Granite Peak, absolutely glorious. The sun is up, the earth is flat, and the strain in one's legs is gone.
