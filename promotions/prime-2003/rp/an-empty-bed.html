An Empty Bed

Date: Sometime pre-Culture Shock I hate this shit. Ice cubes clinked together as the crystal tumbler was lifted to her lips again and Mother Russia's finest vodka passed through on a slippery slope down her esophagus. Too many sips had fallen to their demise this way tonight. Odd, for anyone who knew Lindsay Troy on a personal level knows that she's not even a "vodka person" to begin with, even when the liquor runs smoother than an Alfa Romeo on a straight stretch of pavement. Call it laziness, her not wanting to sample any other spirit from the hotel room's bar, or call it indifference, her not caring that the first bottle she grabbed was something that would normally make her face twist in disgust. On this night, Troy just wanted to drown in something other than the murky quicksand in the swamp of her actions. She couldn't quite tell what she hated more: the drink, or her reason for drinking. As her eyes flicked over to the beside clock to see how long this bout of insomnia had lasted (3:00AM so, two and a half hours now), they were forced to pass over hardly-rumpled sheets turned down against the mattress, and done so angrily. Sheets not coiled tightly into knots, or kicked asunder; not flicked with sweat or curled against bodies in a protective cocoon against the chill of the late-winter air that the room's heater wouldn't totally dissipate, the expensive nightly cost to call it a temporary residence notwithstanding. No, Troy was this bed's lone occupant, like so many other ones before it, across countless cities and numerous countries. But, unlike all of those other beds in all of those other cities in those various countries, Lindsay Troy no longer wanted to be the only one inside it. The Queen no longer wanted self-imposed loneliness. As much as she fought against it, for as long as she had, she was finally starting to get comfortable with the idea of Tyler Rayne as a constant companion. Despite him being obvious trouble, with a penchant for bringing more harm to himself and others than he did good, and despite his knack for pissing anyone and everyone off without much thought to the contrary, their up-and-down "relationship", for lack of a better word, had finally gotten to a place where they had both felt there was something deeper lurking below the complicated surface. Of course, it all went to shit when she, again, refused to choose sides in a battle where all parties were wrong and none of them were willing to admit it. Being Switzerland had worked about as well for the Queen as choosing sides and, ultimately, being on the losing end of them: from FU to Desade, to Ferguson and Wade, her track record for making a choice and sticking to it was less than stellar. It could be said, though, that her track record for success when being an island, ensconced in loneliness, unto herself was more than impressive. It was downright scary. Her lips upturned slightly then, and her eyes returned to her drink still in hand. If she was to be shunned by her closest associates, and left to return to an empty bed in a cold and unforgiving climate, she would find solace in keeping warm by all of the history that preceded her. The Queen's greatest accomplishments always came when the big, bad world turned its back on her. And that's not something the Next in Line would soon be forgetting.
