Half the Fun

Date: Last week Wednesday, Oct. 12 “What do you MEAN, I don’t have a car?” The obsequious little douchenozzle behind the desk wrinkles his nose. “I’m sorry, sir, our computer isn’t showing anything.” “Can you check again?” The douchenozzle rolls his eyes as if you’ve asked him to turn water into wine or—this is just your guess—give an accurate description of sexual intercourse with a woman. He persuades to cut a little tap solo on the keyboard that lasts for several minutes. Tappity-tap-tap-space-tap-tappity-tappity-tap-tap-tappity-backspacebackspacebackspace-tap-tappity-tappity-tap-backspacebackspace-enter-tappity-tap-tappity-tap-space-tap Christ, hastily written roleplays contrived perilously close to a deadline don’t take this long. Another sigh from the obsequious douchenozzle. “I’m sorry, sir, I’ve checked three times. We simply don’t have a car available for you, Mr… Gibson.” “Sonofabitch!” To your dismay, the obsequious douchenozzle barely budges at your proclaimed profanity. The little old lady behind you in the line, however, lets out a rather appropriate gasp. “There, there, Margaret…” her wizened husband says. You can almost hear her throttling the strap of her pocketbook in an attempt to quelch the profanity. Is she muttering? You’re not sure. Maybe she’s damning you to hell. Wouldn’t be the first time. “Look,” you say. “I don’t know what sort of computer error you’ve made, but I need a car and I need one now. I have to be at work in…” the faux leather band of your Timex is starting to fray “20 minutes, and I need a rental car to get there.” “I’m sorry, sir, we’re simply out of rental cars.” “But I had a reservation!” “I understand, but we’re out of cars.” “What’s the damn point of a reservation if…” You wisely trail off before going on another profanity-filled tirade or—worse—putting yourself in jeopardy of a lawsuit from Jerry Seinfeld (and you KNOW that guy’s got good lawyers) for riffing on old material in an unauthorized fashion. “Sir, we need to deal with the next customer.” “I, I just—Can you at least give me the number for a cab company?” The obsequious douchenozzle’s upper lip curls ever so subtly, as if you just let a fart or asked him to pump his own gas. “I’m afraid you’ll need to call information, sir.” Rolling your eyes, you pull out your cell phone and punch in the digits, prefixing it with the Kansas City area code. A surly—but thankfully competent—fellow answers and says a car will be there in the next five minutes. Time to do a quick inventory, though. You plunk down on one of the chairs and start rifling through your bag. Somewhere at the point where you’re holding up a gig in one hand and a pair of brass knuckles in the other, you realize three sets of eyes—one weaselly, two likely unfocused without the aid of those corrective lenses—are staring at you. You look up, give a half-smile and a shrug and go rooting back through the bag. There. Everything’s accounted for. Of course, you checked your bag three times before you left—and once after picking it up at baggage claim (hard to check a pair of knucks). But you’re always terrified you’lll get to a show, open the bag and find you’ve only remembered one boot, or your trunks have magically turned pink during the ride in from the airport, or some other nonsensical mishap. HONK HONK! “Your… cab is here, Mr. Gibson.” You grab your bag and start for the door. “And enjoy your shift at Foot Locker.” You wheel. “IT’S A GODDAMN REFEREE SHIRT!” This time the old lady can’t repress a “Mercy me!” “I’m sure it is, sir,” obsequious douchenozzle says. “Enjoy your shift.” You’d like to strap the little punk in a Douchenozzle, but you just don’t have time. **** People see you at home and they’re jealous. Even though you’re not sure why. Despite the rampant abuse of steroids, drugs and alcohol, they’re jealous. Despite the fact that multiple wrestlers from past and present die each and every year, they covet. They call you a star who has it all. You call them dumbasses. They don’t see the real you. They don’t see what you go through. They don’t see you wake up at 6 AM and make two connections to hit the show in Canada that night, then go to IHOP afterwards because there’s no point in checking into a hotel for two hours before heading right back to the airport to go home. Some folks measure their lives in coffee spoons. You measure yours in rental car contracts and Marriott points. Not the REAL Marriott mind you, just a neverending series of Fairfield Inns. Tom Walczak thinks he’s had it rough? He thinks prison is hard? Try navigating your way to an arena with a rental car map that is written in Spanish for some reason. He’s got no prayer.
