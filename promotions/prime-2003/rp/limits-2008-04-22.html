Limits

22 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: April 22, 2008 Ed took a sip from the thermos cup and choked down the cold coffee. He’d been driving his cab all night, well past the end of his shift, for this one fare. Ed had made sure to call his wife, just to let her know not to wait up for him. The way things were going, it was going to be a long, but profitable evening. The meter read one hundred and eighty dollars, and just kept climbing. Hot or cold, as long as he had coffee in his system and gas in his tank, Ed was willing to drive as far as his fare wanted him to. He glanced up at the rear view mirror and took a look at his passenger. The guy’s head was wrapped in bandages like some kind of mummy. He said he wanted to go to the hospital, and when he gave Ed the address he wasn’t fazed when he was told how much it was going to cost him. "Just drive," the guy said and handed him a fifty. "You’ll get the rest when we get to the hospital." That was all Ed needed to hear. He adjusted the balance for the radio, feeding more juice to the speakers in the front to drown out mummy guy’s mumbling. He’d been doing that for the past couple of hours, and it was starting to get on Ed’s nerves. Still, money was money. In the backseat, Crucifix rested his tired head against the window. With bloodshot eyes, he looked out into the darkness. It was far too overcast to make out the moon or stars. Even the occasional overhead lighting on the freeway seemed to struggle against the hungry night that seemed to devour any light it could reach. It was a fight David Cross could relate to. "I’m sorry," he said. Alex Creed opened his eyes and looked over at the side of Cross’ bandaged head. "For what?" he asked. David sighed. "I let my mind get the best of me." Alex shrugged and nodded. "Kinda doing it right now too, aren’t you? What with me being a hallucination and all." "I guess." Creed continued. "But yeah, you did go to a bad place, mentally speaking. I swear, the stuff that went through your head - and as a figment of your imagination, I’m quite aware of what goes through your head. But I think Tyler Rayne should get a little credit for taking you down for the long three." Cross chuckled weakly, still staring out into the night. "That was a close match though." An imaginary hand playfully punched Crucifix in the shoulder. "The man’s riding a tsunami of momentum," Alex said. "Besides, you can’t just jump into the deep end of the pool and hope to stay above water right off the bat. You haven’t wrestled for how many years?" Crucifix fidgeted in his seat, his eyes focusing on the glass rather than what was beyond it. He could make out the ghostly reflection of his own concealed face, the remains of which burned with embarrassment under the gauze. "Still earned that shot at Dual Halo," he muttered defensively. The tone was enough to tell Alex it was time to let the matter drop. Almost. "Seriously dude, I wasn’t banking on you winning the belt your first time out. No offense." "Oh none taken, I think." Ed looked in the rear view mirror and turned down the radio. "Scuse me?" he asked. "You say something?" "No," Crucifix said with a tired sigh. He let his head roll back and he stared at the roof of the taxi. Even with the sparse illumination from the dashboard and the ever-changing meter, he could see the constellation of cigarette burns etched into the vinyl covering. He heard the music in the front of the taxi get louder and assumed Ed had gone back to his own private world of rock and roll. Private worlds are nice, Crucifix thought to himself. That’s where he’d spent the better part of the past week. He remained in his room, deep in meditation, hoping to regain control of himself. For Cross, it was like an addict - one day at a time. The whole matter with Alex had tested him, and like an alcoholic reaching for the bottle Crucifix had let himself sip from the cup of madness. And just like an alcoholic, he liked it. A lot. "I gotta tell you," David’s hallucination said. "I’m blown away at how much you think of me. As far as imaginary friends go, I’m looking all right." Alex looked down at his bare, muscular, glistening chest. He wiped a finger across his shiny skin and looked at his fingertip with a hint of disapproval. "Not sure about the baby oil though." David looked over at Alex, then quickly turned back to the window. "That’s just, uh, well I fell asleep at the hotel and I guess I left the TV on. Sparticus, y’know." "I see." Alex wiped his finger on the seat in front of him. "I, uh, I feel kind of unclean now. You might not want to bring this up when you see me in person." "What’s that?" Ed asked over his shoulder. Crucifix didn’t say anything. He closed his eyes and hung his head, hoping that sleep would take him until he reached the hospital. It had been a hard week; performing his zazen to help regain his focus had been like steering a sailboat through a hurricane. The effort exhausted him both physically and mentally, and if it hadn’t been for the message Marilyn had left for him he would probably be sleeping. Maybe. Of course, Wang’s impeccable message-taking skills made it hard to discern the urgency that Marilyn needed him. A note slid under his door that read "M - Hospital" left enough to the imagination that David decided he should make the trip. So, tired and hungry after only having eaten twice in the entire week, Cross dragged himself into a cab. Judging by his present half-naked company, he was going to need some serious recovery time when this visit was over. "Penny for your thoughts, chief?" Alex asked. It was an effort even to think the words. But since Alex Creed wasn’t really in the taxi with him, David decided that thought was easier than speech. "You know I can’t afford to lose you, right?" he said inside his head. "I’m short on friends and I’d prefer the ones I have left to stay on this side of the ground." Cross managed to open his eyes and saw it was getting darker inside the cab. Either that, or Alex was getting harder to see. "You won’t lose me," he told David. "We Creeds are hard to keep down." "Blessing and a curse, right?" An image of Alex’s father with a sawed-off shotgun cradled in his arm, looking much like the Terminator he was, flashed across David’s mind. Alex caught the visual. "Something like that." Another mental image caught the young man’s attention. "Hey Dave? How am I doing? You know, in my coma." Crucifix felt his lead-curtain eyelids slam shut. "Well I don’t want to upset you or anything –" "Uh, you’re talking to a figment of your imagination." "You’ve lost quite a bit of weight." "... how much are we talking about here?" "Twenty, maybe thirty pounds." Alex bolted upright in his seat. "Thirty pounds?! Man, slip a protein shake in my IV tube or something!" Crucifix didn’t move; the gravity inside the taxi seemed to have doubled. His arms, his legs, his entire body seemed to be pressing down into the cushioned seat. "Probably not a good idea. You’re already starting to look pretty smooth." The anxiousness was easy to detect in Creed’s voice. "Wait, you mean the abs?" "Alex you’re flat. Zero definition." Even with his eyes closed, Crucifix could see Alex looking almost frantic. "Okay, get some of those electrical pads – you know, those electric ab flexor ones – and slap ‘em on me. Just because I’m in a coma doesn’t mean I have to get all flabby. Are you keeping my curtains open during the day?" "Doesn’t help. You’re on the shady side of the building." David could see Alex holding his breath until he finally said, "I’m sorry, but your tan has faded." It took a moment, but Alex managed to find his voice after his jaw had dropped open. "Okay okay, that’s easy to fix," he said quickly. "Pick up some tanning lamps. Extension cords. Maybe a power bar so you can plug them in along with the ab pads." David kept needling his friend. "You’re being kinda superficial aren’t you? I think the more important issue is your health, not your tan or your hair. The sigh told Cross that his message had gotten though. Alex leaned back in his seat. "Technically, you’re the one who’s thinking I’m superficial. But I guess you’re right. I’m just being... wait, what about my hair?" "Your what?" David said with a mental smile. "Hair. You said my health was more important than my tan or my hair. What’s wrong with my hair?" Alex was seated sideways and stared at Crucifix with a pleading look in his eyes. "Give it to me straight - do I have terminal bed head?" "Well the good news is that you don’t have to worry about bed head. The bad news is you’ll never have to worry about it ever again." Cross was so exhausted, he was giddy. "It’s gone Alex. Your hair fell out." He hard Alex’s voice crack. "It - it what?" "The virus. It made your hair fall out." That was all the imaginary Creed could take. He flopped back in his seat and hugged his chest, slowly rocking back and forth. "I feel ill." "You are ill. That’s the point. There is a silver lining to all of this." "I’ll save a fortune on shampoo?" Was the taxi still moving? David wondered. Was he still in the taxi? "I’ve got two students from England. They’re twins." He saw Creed smile a mischievous grin. "Twins you say? Well I guess one of us might as well be enjoying themselves." "It’s a brother and sister. Plus, I don’t get involved with my students." Alex raised an eyebrow. "Are you sure? If the news has taught me anything, it’s that teacher/student relationships is all the creepy rage these days." "I’m sure it is." "I don’t see how this is a silver lining." It was David’s turn to apply a healthy dose of grinning. "They’ve got money. They’re willing to pay whatever I want to ask for me to train them. You’re going to get better now." Alex’s imaginary eyes lit up. "The abs?" "Yes, you’ll be crunching your way back to that disgusting eight pack in no time." "The tan?" "Borderline melanoma. Congratulations." "The hair?" "Well, two out of three ain’t bad." He had no idea how long he’d been sleeping, but when David opened his eyes it felt as if it wasn’t nearly enough. He looked at the mirror in the front of the cab and saw the driver looking back at him. "The hospital’s just up ahead," he announced. "You slept like the dead for the past two hours." "I needed it," Crucifix groaned. Alex leaned across him and looked out the window. Ah, still hallucinating. Clearly he needed more rest. "Guess we’re here," Alex told him. "If you talk to me, try to avoid mentioning the hair." -==- It was all he could do to keep from buckling at the knees when the elevator first started moving. When the elevator finally stopped and the door opened, David remained propped up against the back wall with his eyes closed. He took a deep breath before stepping forward, but his eyes snapped open when he heard the shouting. "Go ahead! Call security! I’ll shove this cane right up their ass!" Now the voice belonged to Marilyn Creed, that much David knew. The words were, well, probably not all that surprising considering she was a Creed after all. If she was anything like her older brother, David had either missed the colorful language or she was still just warming up. He could see her by Alex’s room, walking cane in hand - a gift from her brother (the reason for needing the cane, not the cane itself). She was waving said cane at a linebacker of a nurse, whose bullet-shaped torso was wedged between Marilyn and a man with a very fine suit and stylish salt and pepper hair. The man didn’t seem too concerned by the threats from the red-faced Creed swinging her cane, missing him by inches. He avoided her swings, but his expression never changed from one of borderline boredom. Threats didn’t seem to be a big deal to him, whoever he was. The gravity that had been trying to pull Crucifix down to the center of the earth seemed to release its hold on him. David was almost floating down the hall rather than briskly jogging. As he got closer, he could see Marilyn’s red cheeks had tears running down them. Still swinging wildly with watery eyes, Marilyn tried to whack the man in the suit after a lunge over the nurse’s shoulder. He deftly stepped out of the way while the wall in the nurse’s outfit grabbed Marilyn by the waist and carried her to the far wall. As soon as she was gingerly put down, David tried to get her attention. "Marilyn? Mar what’s wrong?" For a second, Marilyn didn’t see him. "Get back here you little cocksuc -" she shouted at the man. The anger in her face softened when she noticed David standing off to the side. Nurse Bullet stepped aside, still keeping herself between Marilyn and the Suit. Marilyn hobbled over to David and hugged him. "Your leg acting up?" Cross asked. Marilyn didn’t hear the question. Instead she gasped as tears started to resume flowing, "It’s Alex. He’s... he’s gone." She pulled herself away and shot a murderous glare at the frowning nurse and the indifferent suit. "These bastards weren’t even going to tell me!" Gone. Alex was gone. Gravity had found David once again, and it felt like it had brought friends. His legs buckled, but he caught himself before he fell to the floor. His stomach felt like it had continued on its way to the center of the Earth, along with all the blood in his body. He felt heavy, empty, and very cold. "W-what? When?" Marilyn wiped her eyes with the palm of her hand. "They won’t tell me," she said through her teeth, tightening her grip on the cane’s shaft. Crucifix couldn’t believe it. "They won’t – a man died and they wouldn’t even tell the next of kin?" Marilyn’s eyes widened and her mouth opened into a large O when her head quickly turned to David. "God no, he’s not dead! He’s just not here anymore!" A barrage of realization hammered the exhausted Crucifix, crushing his cerebral cortex under the heel of this new information. His head was swimming, and he stumbled two steps to the side. His shoulder hit the wall and Nurse Bullet stepped forward to steady him. "I know that’s better, him not being dead," Cross said. "But I’m not really seeing how he isn’t here anymore. Did he wake up?" "Talk to the lawyer," Marilyn spat, as if speaking the word lawyer was like wiping one’s tongue with a used strip of toilet paper. "Hollinsworth." The fog of confusion started to clear. David looked around. "Lawyer?" The man in the suit, Hollinsworth, had wisely took advantage of the distraction and was halfway to the elevator before Cross even noticed he wasn’t there anymore. "Hey you!" David shouted at the back of the suit. He wasn’t going to stop. In fact, he started doing the very opposite of stopping and broke into a run when the elevator doors opened to let a young couple out. "Hold the elevator!" he shouted. He stuck out his hand, twisted his body sideways, and managed to slip between the closing doors. He hit the back wall as the doors closed behind him, and quickly turned around when he felt the elevator moving down to the lobby. Hollinsworth adjusted his tie and ran a hand through his hair when the doors opened in the lobby. He managed one step forward before taking three back, with an assist via Crucifix’s hand around his throat. The doors started to close again and a doctor tried to slip inside at the last minute. With his hand still around the lawyer’s throat, Crucifix’s left foot snapped backward and stopped inches from the doctor’s surprised face. "This one’s full," he said to the doctor while staring at Hollinsworth. "Take the next one." Moments later, the elevator was moving and the two men were alone. David was close enough to the lawyer’s face that the man could almost taste his foul breath. "Where’s Alex?" he demanded quietly. Hollinsworth’s horrified expression started to dissolve into one of curiosity. He looked at the bandages, the broken teeth, the wild eyes. "Are you David Cross?" he asked. Crucifix let go of the man’s neck, leaving behind red marks that wouldn’t fade anytime soon. "That’s me." The lawyer exhaled sharply, ran a hand through his salt and pepper hair again, and reached into his coat. He didn’t notice David’s left hand opening, his fingers stiffening, ready to bury themselves in the man’s eyes if he pulled out anything that even resembled something dangerous. Instead of a gun or a knife, he pulled out a roll of blue paper and handed it to Cross. "Then this is for you." "What’s this?" David hit the Emergency Stop button, bringing the elevator to a shuddering standstill. He opened the roll and read the heading on the first piece of paper. "A restraining order?" he asked in disbelief. Hollinsworth had now fully regained his composure. "Yes. You are to remain five hundred feet away from both my client and Alex Creed. Alex has been removed from this facility," he didn’t try to mask his disgust, "and relocated for his own safety. During our evaluation we discovered that he was being poisoned during his stay here. That was why he was ill. That was why he wasn’t recovering." "Poisoned?" David couldn’t believe it. How did no one pick up on that? How did no one detect it? "Then why the restraining order against me? You think I was poisoning him? The lawyer pulled out the emergency stop button and the elevator resumed motion. "We don’t know who it was. Hence the reason for his relocation, and the restraining order. No one is allowed near Alex or my client." The elevator door opened and Hollinsworth stepped out. "Now if you’ll excuse me." David remained inside, trying to read the pages and failing to understand a single word. His brain was like cold oatmeal. He was almost surprised when a thought managed to jump into his head. "Hey! Lawyer!" Crucifix shouted to the man walking toward the nearby stairwell. "Who the Hell is your client, anyway? Hollinsworth didn’t shout. He simply spoke loud enough to be heard clearly. "My client is Alex’s father – Victor Creid." God... no... It was all too much. His body, now his mind, had been driven past their limits. David collapsed to the floor of the elevator before the doors could finish closing.
