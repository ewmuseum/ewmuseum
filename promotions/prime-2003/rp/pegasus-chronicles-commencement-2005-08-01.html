Pegasus Chronicles – Commencement

1 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 1st, 2005 Prologue. It’s custom for stars to be lost in the shuffle. Diamonds appear from the ruff a lot. Yet, in this day in age, for one to appear out of the darkness and step into a spotlight, that’s unheard of. However, it’s happened… It’s been three months since this man has seen a ring. A near fatal injury put him on the shelf in Global Wrestling. His appearance for his last televised appearance was a loss he’d never forget. Losing not only his championship, but also a shade of hope. Doctors gave him the word that his shoulder was dislocated, and his collarbone had been snapped. Tears flowed from the warrior’s eyes, as they stated, “You could wrestle, but… theirs chance of being paralyzed if it breaks again.” With that type of risk being involved, speculation was at large. Could a former PTC Global Champion, a multiple time World Champion, one of the largest rising stars in the industry today, still be able to compete? It was a doubt that was answered with one name…Story Begins. ”PRIME.” A name echoed in the media’s face, like it was a prophet of God himself. It was a certainty that if this name were used then you would be looked on with hawk’s eyes. PRIME was the future of the wrestling industry. In two years, it’d gone from a run of the mill average company, to spotlights and fame. Anyone who was anybody, wanted to be there… and there was no difference when it came to this certain individual. ”Ahhhh… what the hell, it’s… WHAT!? 12:41 AM!” A rustling of the covers turns into a dash for the bathroom, as the six foot six inch man plays catch up on his late start of the day. A glow of light shines from the bathroom into the murky hotel room, illuminating the room in an outcast. Two lamps, a bed, a television set, and a coffee table are all that sit in the room. Beside the bed lays a large black bag with wheels on it, and a grip for pulling. Suddenly a showering of nails noise is heard, but then is recognized as the water running in the bathroom. The man walks from the bathroom to the bed again, this time sitting down and grabbing the phone near the lamp. Dialing a number he brings the phone to his face and waits as it rings. Ringggggg. Rinnggggg. Ringgggg. Click. ”Hello, room service, how may we help you?” answers a gentle feminine voice. ”Ah… I need a taxi, and quick,” demands the man trying to be pleasant about it. ”Okay sir. Your name?” asks the room service woman. ”… Jesse Fristik,” replies Jesse as he shows impatience in his voice. ”Thank you. Your taxi will be here shortly. Shall I call you when so?” the voice replies and waits for an answer. ”No, I’ll be waiting in the lobby for it!” nearly yells Jesse as he gets ready to yell. ”Okay. Have a nice da---.” the voice says but is cut off. Click. The phone is hung up, as Jesse dashes back to the bathroom. Boxers are dropped, and then a sliding noise is heard, as he enters the shower. Five minutes later, the shower door slides open, a towel is grabbed, and he walks out to the bed. Sitting down he looks at the black bag on the floor and decides to unzip it. Pulling out a black shirt, blue jeans, a pair of socks, and some boxer briefs, he then zips it up. Just as he goes to get dressed he stops… ”Ahhhh damn,” he whispers in pain. Grasping his right shoulder with his left hand he rotates it, trying to get feeling going through, and finally it does as he lets go and takes a deep breathe. ”Please… don’t let that bother me tomorrow night,” Jesse whispers as if he was praying. Shaking his head from the thought, he wipes the droplets of water from his forehead and then gets dress. Grabbing the black bag by the pulley bar, he hurries out of the hotel room and down the hallway. *** *** ****** *** ****** *** *** Inside the taxi, Jesse sits looking out the window. His eyes gazing off at the many lights that Memphis has. His body is fixated with the seat, as he begins to day dream about what is to come, and what was, for this warrior. Ah… I can feel it already. The ropes touching my back as I bounce off them. The turnbuckle pads as I lean on them before each match. The ring canvas that will be my butchering board before each win or be my burial ground before every loss. The lights that make us larger than life men in this Broadway we call wrestling. Oh, but most important of them all… the fans. How I so dearly miss the fans. Their chants still echo in my head, as I left the Global ring that night. Now… there will be new fans, chanting in a new arena, under a new company. Never has something new been so inviting to me. It’s almost surreal… if it wasn’t for the injury. I know I can survive the pain, but I… don’t know if I can overcome the risk factor. I can’t risk being paralyzed. If that happened, my life would be nothing. I know nothing else but the ring. That squared circle is my horseshoe to life. I’ve hit so many ringers I’ve lost count. If only… I knew I could forget I had this. Maybe I should have waited until it was fully healed. Doctor’s said two more months, but like usual, I’m stubborn and say I can go ahead. Ah, oh well. It’s now or never. This was my chance to make an impact, and I wasn’t going to pass it up. Not for anything… Finally mustering the strength to smile, Jesse turns his head to his bag and reaches inside it. Pulling out a picture, he brings it nearer the window for some light. A slight sigh of relief is left, as he looks down at the picture of what is a young girl, no older than five or six. Shaking his head of all doubts, he tucks the picture into his pants pocket and continues looking out the window with only high hopes on mind. Loss of Strength. Screeching to a stop in front of a local pub, the taxi door opens up, and Jesse steps out onto the sidewalk after handing the driver a twenty. ”Keep the change…” Jesse ensures as the driver goes to find the change, but stops. Nodding, Jesse waves to the driver and then walks across the street to a blinking neon red sign that says “Power House Open 24/7.” It was a gym, and inside it was nearly empty, with the exception of a staff member whom wore a grungy body suit that had grease stains, and one or two members who were doing cardio workouts in the far left corner. Putting his bag down, Jesse takes off his shirt, and stretches. After a few minutes of stretching he finally feels all right, and takes a seat on the workout bench where a one hundred and ninety-five pound bench is set up. Laying back he sniffs the musty air of the gym, inhaling before he gets ready to bench. Placing his thumbs on the smooth spots of the bar, he then grips the bar, and cracks his neck. Tightening his shoulders, his arms become solid and he pushes the bar up off the set up, clearing the first step of benching. Exhaling, he descends the bar, letting it touch his chest, before pushing it back up and inhaling again. Smiling he drops the bar on the rack, and sits up. Going to the side of the bench set, he picks up two more twenty-five pound weights and places them on the bench, raising his total to two hundred and forty-five pounds. Breathing out as he lays back down on the bench, his body shivers once as he rolls his eyes and stretches his arms and shoulders. Laying down he replicates his set up from before, placing the thumbs where they belong, and then raising the bar, bringing it down to his chest… Pushing up, he holds his breathe, and his face begins to go red. His right arm begins to shake, and he stops midway through the bench. Exhaling he tries to get a boost of energy from letting the air out. Suddenly he lets out a deep breathe and a groan, before shooting the bar up and dropping it on the rack. Sitting up slowly, he lets his feet touch the floor, and he puts his palms on his knees. Looking down, his right hand is still shaking, along with his whole arm. Clenching his fists, he closes his eyes until the shakes ceases. Exhaling in aggravation, ”Damn… This can’t be happening.” In less than an hour, his focus has already been side tracked, and his mind is already doubting his full potential. Can this warrior overcome adversity in his first match in three months? Can he shoot back from a life threatening injury and prove to the world he has more in him? Or is he fighting an uphill battle that can’t be won?
