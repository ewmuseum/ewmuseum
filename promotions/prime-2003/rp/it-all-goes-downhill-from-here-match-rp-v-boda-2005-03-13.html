Date: 13/03/05 It All Goes Downhill From Here...Written: 13th March, 2005. 'Why should you know better by now,When you’re old enough not to? I wish I knew better by now,When I’m old enough not to.' - Beth Orton. I - Mood : Relaxed, Going On Intrigued. Despite the northern hemisphere's spring holding back the fierce sun that would bake Marrakech dry over the following months, the crowded souk almost steamed. Traders, locals, and tourists mingled amongst one another, the busting enclosure of commerce amplifying their body heat, leaving the frantically bartering traders' foreheads beaded with sweat. Infusing into the heady heat were the pungent aromas of their produce... the subtle, almost bitter waft of turmeric blended with liquorice hint of star anise. Prices were yelled back and forth, with hand exclamations lucky not to have taken someone's eye out. Strolling almost obliviously through the market was Karina Wolfenden, her usually pale, freckled skin adopting a tanned effect as the glorious, natural light reflected off the sun-baked earth that formed the Djemaa-el-Fna. With a floaty white skirt skipping gracefully off the dusty floor, and with an embroidered peasant top respectfully leaving only her hands exposed, she appeared to be soaking in the scene just as much as she was observing it. Startling lilac hair was restrained by a simple headscarf, leaving only eyes of burning emerald to heighten her plain features. Serene amid the commercial chaos, Karina's hips snaked and spun to guide her tall, almost awkward-at-times body through the shoppers. This was her preparation, the indulgence of senses and relaxation of mind she felt was needed before stepping into the ring with Chris Sloboda. Karina was already as fit as her body was ever going to let her be, so days in the gym were hardly worth it. Her performance in the ring wasn't dictated by training or discipline, it relied on her mood. Brimming with confidence, relaxed and content, she'd fly into opponents with a courageous heart, getting back up from whatever they could hit her with. But if she felt stressed, if her head was down, pulled into one of her sulky moods, then her brimming furnace of fiery fight was quenched. Eyelids crept down over shimmering irises as her head dropped back, feeling the subtle warmth of the sun tickle her nose and cheeks, providing solar energy for the soul. 'Here, here, over here!' stallholders enticed, their French carrying a heavy accent, far from sliding off the tongue. A hint of a smile had been set across the K-Wolf's pale pink lips ever since her flight landed. Morocco was one of the places that never failed to amaze her. A land of stark contrasts, from the snow-capped Atlas mountains, down to the golden beaches that mirrored those of the French Riviera from across the other side of the Mediterranean. Bustling cities, stacking people on top of one another, lay north of the barren Sahara, where the scarce life could trek for days, weeks, without laying an eye on one another. It had everything she could want... relaxation, adventure, sun, character, culture, history, fragrance. Paradise for an escapist such as Karina Wolfenden. 'Hmmmmm,' she pondered, scanning the stalls either side of her, full of original and bizarre creations. Hand-carved bracelets, rustic jewellery that shunned generic shine for earthy shades of bronze, and necklaces adorned with all manner of obscure gems and minerals were there to be bartered for. But she was waiting for something to really catch her perusing eye. 'Heh.' Merchants held out handfuls of their wares to her, trying to tempt the westerner to shell out some on her money, charming her in gravely compliments. Each time, she politely declined, gesturing that she had no money... much to their disappointment, and in one case, apparent heartbreak. Then, just as she'd reached the outermost extremities of the market, where the smells of ground spice were lost in the open air, her eyes finally locked their intrigued gaze upon something in the distance. Eyes narrowed, and she felt herself pulled towards the stall, riding the waves of local shoppers that seemed to carry Karina to her desired shore. 'Spec-tacular...' she marvelled with hushed admiration. The stench of warm leather clogged her sinuses as Karina's lips slowly parted in glee. Running her fingers along a braided, brown leather belt, she then cupped the buckle in her palm, reading off the words engraved into the mottled silver. 'Universal being...' If it weren't for the frustrating confines of gravity, and the restrictive requirement of oxygen, the belt's slogan would sum up Karina perfectly. Since she'd flown the stuffy, asphyxiating confines of the family home, her own apartment had been treated as a hostel. Her fridge had nothing but long-life milk and bottled juice, while her electricity bills were scarcely anything above the standing charge. Her passport read like an Olympic opening ceremony, while her vocabulary spanned 9 languages, 4 fluent and accentless. Just staying in the States for an extended spell felt like being stuck at home on a Friday night, while her senses had learned to recognise regions by their smell and taste. Drop her anywhere on the planet, and she'd probably act like an organic compass, able to pinpoint her location by the crispness of the air, or the chalky aftertaste of the mountain stream. 'How much,' she reservedly enquired in flawless French, her nonchalant exterior hopefully keeping down the price. Having learned the art of haggling, Karina tucked her pride away for a brief moment and adopted Bambi-eyes. Holding her hands behind her back for effect, she then tilted her head to the side, looking far from the wily and cynical creature many knew her as. 'Forty Dirhams,' he almost gargled back in his throaty tone. Barely five dollars, less than Karina would spend on a coffee most of the time, but half the fun of shopping here was in the haggling. Lips pursed together as she pondered the price, breathlessly sighing in an uber-rare show of her seductive side. 'But for a beautiful lady such as yourself,' he pondered, 'thirty.' 'I bet you use that line on all the girls, you old charmer,' she smiled back, wanting to see if she could drop him down just that little more. Reaching her right hand up, she freed a few stands of lilac hair and twiddled them around her finger, all the time looking out the tops of her feline eyes at the stall-holder. 'Twenty five?' Holding both hands to his heart, the man playfully exclaimed that her hard bargaining was killing him, before conceding to her price. 'The Universe is now yours to take,' he prophesised, handing over the belt symbolically before tucking the proceeds into his pouch. 'I don't know if it's ready for me yet,' she remarked, waving to the man thankfully before being absorbed back into the crowds. The instruments of the buskers seemed to use the market itself as an amplifier, the bass thumping up through the dusty floor, the percussion resonating through the hanging jewellery, with the layered vocals of the traders forming a soothing choir. Its sound drifted from Karina's ears as she weaved her way out of the market, being given a peculiar eye by a Donkey being herded the opposite way. Out in the open, the sun seemed more vibrant, free of the hazy hue that always descended upon the market. Looking down at her purchase, Kari narrowed her eyes protectively as the sun reflected off the buckle. 'Universal, hmmm.' If she was to get PRIME's own version, it wouldn't cost her a penny... just every bit of fight she had to give. II - Mood : Content... This Is How I NEED To Live. Stood atop a peak in the Atlas mountains, looking down on the harsh terrains that surrounded the snow-capped extremities, Karina Wolfenden felt like she'd just returned to the spawning ground. The air, untainted by industry, seemed to refresh her lungs, while the light breeze washing over her face brought colour to the K-Wolf's lightly freckled cheeks. She could stand there for hours, just breathing, eyes closed, knowing each inhalation would purge the toxins of modern, Western life from her. While some would choose the stuffy confines of a gym, its air overloaded with the face-souring aroma of stale sweat, Karina could feel about a hundred times fitter by climbing towards the sky. Rather than looking back at her perspiring face and damp, clingy clothing, she could look down the peak, to where rock patterns formed a mosaic all the way to the horizon, broken up only by prickly pear plantations that defied the conditions. Her skin could be aroused by the glorious sun, controlled like a thermostat by the early spring, far preferable to harsh, synthetic lights. If Boda were standing at the foot of the peak, the K-Wolf would have flung herself whole-heartedly at him, forming a snowball around her spinning body until she crashed into the domineering Universal Champion. Hopping up, she spun her board 90 degrees, freeing it to follow the contours of the peak. That first feeling of inertia, washing up over her body from the feet up, always raised the hairs on the back of her neck. The slicing of board edge through snow sounded like the perfect symphony, conducted by rapid twists of her hips. Building up speed, her crystallised breathe trailed behind her like an exhaust fume as she navigated the slope, expelling fresh, virgin snow with every turn. Her speed was evident as twin braids of lilac hair were ravaged by the air being sucked into her slipstream, while the insulating clothing was pushed tight against the front of her flat body. This was the feeling she felt walking down the aisle, the sense of anticipation massaging her shoulders and back, while the chants of the fans were mimicked by the board cutting swathes through the snow. And it was only the build-up. Able to see the camouflaged terrain of the blanket white snow, Karina locked onto the first natural jump that appeared, breaking off like a Jet Fighter detaching from its wingman to begin her approach. The constant jolts of the rarely-used slope would have ravaged the knees of most, grinding cartilage against bone, but Kari's legs acted as shock absorbers, leaving her with a smooth ride. Heartbeat increased, and the sounds of Graham Coxon blasting through her earphones seemed to be raised by a few notches as her senses heightened. Taking a deep breath, she readied her body, letting her path be decreed by the jump's entrance, allowing the natural path of the mountain to be her guide. Fists clenched, eyes narrowed, and lips pursed. Shoulders twisted, knees crouched, and toes curled. Dropping down into the steepest part of the slope, she crossed the threshold, knowing that there was no backing out of the jump... at least not if she wanted to walk away from it. Feeling the lip of the jump underfoot, the K-Wolf thrust herself along with it, flinging her nubile body into the crisp, Atlas air. Unwinding her body like a tightly-wound elastic band, Karina spun mesmerisingly, rotating along just about every axis imaginable. With wide open eyes, she watched striking blue and breath-taking white blend into one, almost losing track of where her body was in flight. Senses adjusting to the barrage, Karina got her bearing, and knew she was set for a safe touch-down... such an anti-climax. If she'd settled for something lower, then she wouldn't be set to battle Boda for the PRIME Universal Title. If she'd have aimed lower, her match on Wednesday wouldn't be the main event. If she'd have aimed lower, PRIME fans wouldn't be predicting her as 'one kick away' from becoming PRIME's #1. Anything less would be betraying the competitive side that fuelled her. Forcing her body to comply, to defy the laws of physics, she launched into a final spin, fingers grabbing the board like a comfort blanket as she waited for the much welcomed horizon to drop into her line of sight. 'C'mon, c'mon,' she urged, almost feeling the snow approaching fast. Then, just as the divide between blue and white burned itself into her focussed retina, Kari released her board, feeling it slap down invitingly against the snow... before it was expelled by a bump upon landing, ripping the control away from Wolfenden. Sucking in air, she felt herself lunge forward as her board had its breaks unwittingly applied, before her face was introduced to the snow she craved so much. As if she were crowd surfing, Karina's body rose from the snow on waves, tossed along only to be caught again on another rise. Going limp, she just closed her eyes and endured the ride, sucking up the hits that would bruise, but feeling nothing that would do any damage to her resilient body. After all, she'd gone though the same routine ever since she first became addicted to the rush, and grew to accept the consequences. At first, panicked trips to the hospital accompanied a tumble, before she'd learned to disguise any limps or discomfort. As crash landings became second nature, all Karina had to do was hide the bruises, and as her body fully developed, they barely hung around long enough for anyone to notice. With her momentum chiselled away by the slope, Karina's body rolled to a halt, almost motionless bar a rapid convulsion of her upper body. Laughter. Embedded in the snow, clothing highlighted by brushings of white, the aching K-Wolf burst into laughter as the rush refused to subside. Staring up at the sun, with the snow making the back of her neck go numb, she just remained in her chilly bedding, not wanting to try again until the feeling deserted her. The mountain, her most fierce of rivals, had never managed to beat her. Had never managed to keep her down, or drive her off. At ReVolution 58, she'd throw herself at another mountain... an intimidating, heavy-set Czech mountain that stood atop all the others with spiked, blonde peaks. And she wasn't about to let one beat her yet. But with endorphins almost lifting her out of the snow, and her pale face flushed red with the feeling trying to force its way up through the surface, her life threatened to mimic the slope. When she was that high, that brimming with energy, its all to easy to crash back down to earth. III - Mood : I Need Something To Hit That ISN'T A Wall.
