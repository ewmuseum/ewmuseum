To Whom it May Concern

7 October 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/7/08 With a sigh that made no sound, only moved his chest, he placed the pen on his desk and picked up the piece of paper he'd hunched over for the last two hours. Mr. & Mrs. Barker: I wish I could say that it came as a surprise to me when the notice of a civil suit, filed by you... against me, arrived in my mailbox. Unfortunately, it was something I had expected since the final day of the criminal trial, when a jury of my peers found me innocent of all crimes and failed to give you the closure you had been desparately seeking. Recently, my father passed away, as I'm sure you heard (or read about in the newspaper). While he will surely be missed by the many lives he touched, his absence has, and will continue to have, a much larger impact on my life. My father and I were always very close, making the months since his departure very difficult and empty. And while, by no means, am I insinuating that the loss of a father is equal or comparable to the loss of a son... I want you to understand that your emotions and sorrows are not completely lost on me. It must strike you as odd, perhaps even repulsive, to listen to the man you belive responsible for the death of your first-born child talk of sympathy and understanding. Perhaps, all is made worse by my refusal to stand up and say that I am innocent of the crime against Anthony (other than the original "not guilty" plea entered at my lawyer's advice). Based on the civil suit you have filed, it is obvious you still believe me guilty, and though I fear an acceptable ending to this entire situation is not possible, if even some semblance of closure can be attained by my proclamation, then I ------ "Knock knock!" Kaiser's head snapped, like a rubberband pulled beyond its limits, and honed in on the sound of the voice. Already halfway in the front door, Dave Vashaun passed on the formal invitation to enter. "What's that, bro?" "Ehh," Kaiser looked down at the letter in his hand,focusing for a moment on the point where he had been interrupted, "just some offer for a new credit card." "Like anybody needs more of those. They must send a dozen of them things to me every damn month." "Yeah." Kaiser folded up the paper and dropped it in the wire-frame trashcan beside his desk. "Tell me about it."
