Like Lemmings With Nothing to Lose

3 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/10/2006 Now As soon as the lights cut out, they were promptly replaced by the much dimmer back-up lighting and consequently the building’s sprinkler system spluttered into life. Jonathan Winters sighed and wiped away the sweat from his eyes -- with the sprinkler system active it was a pointless action, but of course, he didn‘t realise that until afterwards. Another workout ruined. He silently complained. I knew something like this was going to happen. I fucking knew it... He’d told Stakiri as much an hour ago when the warning signals were first ignited, but his employer had always been stubborn. Even more so than Winters, and that was saying something. He threw his sweat towel on the seat of the rowing machine and made for the door. The many civilians who worked in Stakiri Corp Towers were already rushing toward the emergency exits like lemmings with nothing to lose and they were the smart one’s. Winters headed upstream, against the current of the river -- if one were so inclined to apply a metaphor -- barging through people and revelling in the fear deeply etched within their eyes. Winters had always appreciated the effect fear had on the human body, though he never truly understood why. He figured it had to be just another remnant of a long lost memory brought with him from the previous life he never knew. Conversely, it wasn’t outside the realms of possibility that it was just one of the many side effects that came working closely with Cornelius Stakiri -- evil has a tendency to rub off on a man. Winters was beginning to enjoy this; manhandling people with impressive effects. However, he was forced to check himself when a few of the unfortunate employee’s were hurled into the corridor walls with a sickening thud -- he always had found it difficult to gauge his own strength. He felt a little sorry for them, as they slumped to the floor, either out cold or too hurt to stand, and are promptly crushed under the stampede -- but he wasn’t sorry enough to go back and help them. Whatever was going down, was going down in or around Stakiri and that had to be his first priority. After all, his employer was the only one with the key to his past locked inside his stubborn skull and Winters couldn’t allow that key to be lost, no matter the consequences. Winters pushed through the last of the civilians and quickly sped away to the other side of the to floor, toward Stakiri’s office. Just as he rounded the corner and was finally in sight of the reception area, the back-up lights cut out, leaving nothing but darkness. Now, there could be no mistake; this was no accident, it was an attack. I fucking knew it... Then, his ears pricked up to the sound movement ahead and some more to his left. More civilians? Maybe, Stakiri? Or attackers? Fuck... He knew that making noise would inevitably give away his position and automatically put him at a distinct disadvantage against whoever the fuck was attacking the building, if the attack actually was in the direct vicinity, but there was no other choice. Besides, these guys have already lost, they just don’t know it yet. Who ever these fucks think they are, I don’t particularly care. They’re efficient enough to cut both primary and secondary power, but whoop-de-fucking-do. Who couldn’t do that? I’m Jonathan-fucking-Winters. They don’t stand a chance... “Stakiri?” He said, allowing the question to linger in the air for the longest time. The only response he got was a muffled shout from directly a head and he had to assume it was Stakiri. If there were any more civilians in here, they’d be crying out for help by now. He’d fucked up... Motherfucker! They’d got there first and Stakiri was already incapacitated. Where’s Aeryn? No. He shut the thoughts down before they had time to grow into any further detail. There was no time to be thinking with his dick, besides Aeryn was more than able to look after herself. Winters turned to his left and anticipated an attack, but his senses were too slowly to comprehend the thing moving at him at a phenomenal pace. He needed to be ready for anything, but the truth of it was, he wasn’t ready and he was granted a few brief seconds before losing consciousness, to feel the thing slam into him with all the force of a locomotive train, definitely causing some kind of internal damage. Darkness took him - there’s a lot of that going around lately. Ten Minutes Ago Harder. Faster. Longer. The sweat poured off Jonathan Winters face as though it was a splendorous waterfall and he couldn’t quite feel his legs anymore. Sounds like the making of a great porn film, right? I wish? He flicked the off switch on the treadmill and slumped down upon the elegant metal bench that prominently took root by the side of the workout apparatus and dabbed at his face with his small sweat towel. He unscrewed the top of his water bottle and took a long, satisfying gulp of the liquid. “Pushing yourself a little hard, aren’t ya?” Winters looked up to see a well-built guy just finishing up on the rowing machine. He recognised the man as another one of Stakiri’s unofficial operatives. If memory served him right, Winters had worked with him a couple of weeks back, taking down a young upstart gambling syndicate, headed by a Russian guy who had delusions of capability. Winters didn’t catch the guys name. Must’ve been good though, Winters didn’t even hear him enter the room, but then his mind had been elsewhere. The well-built guy realised that Winters had no intention of answering him back and stood from the rowing machine, shaking his head. They probably think he does it because they somehow think Winters believes himself to be better than them, they probably think that his ego won’t allow him to stoop to their levels and actually talk with them. They’re wrong of course, not about Winters think he’s better than them -- he is -- but that’s not the reason why he doesn’t talk to them. He doesn’t talk to them because he doesn’t have to. Why waste time and energy making meaningless small talk to people you hardly even knew? It didn’t make sense to him, but then he’d never tried to see the sense in it. The guy did have a point though. He was pushing himself hard, too hard though? He wasn’t so sure about that. Pushing himself that extra mile is what got him to where he is today -- into a lucrative contract with PRIME, a shot at Tony Gamble’s Internet Title and then the Internet Title itself. Everyone had seen what happened when he hadn’t pushed himself; everyone had seen him have the title stolen from him by a crazed, and extremely jealous, Tony Gamble; everybody had seen Adam win the title off him a few weeks after that. Now, he’d been booked in a four way dance to determine the number one contender for the PRIME Five Star title and he had no intention of letting himself down this time and allowing the smell of glory to leave his nose. Tony Gamble? I’ve beaten this fool on two separate occasions for the Internet title. They do say three’s a charm, but ultimately, Tony Gamble sucks. It’s never going to happen; he’ll never defeat me and I still have a beating with his name on it for stealing my damned title like a fucking thief. Fucker. Lindsay Troy? Mediocre at best. A member of a below average tag team that split up due to it’s incapability to function as unit or to succeed. Vangelus Olsig? PRIME legend, perhaps, but what has he done lately? Opinions were opinions, but Winters’ opponents were in this match for a reason. The management have deemed them eligible to challenge for the Five Star title and therefore his opinions become meaningless. He had to treat each of them as a real threat and judging by his recent form within the ring, he needed a lot of training before he was comfortable stepping in a ring with real threats. Winters stood, tentatively testing whether he had any feeling in his legs now and, more importantly, whether they would be able to carry his weight. He stood and his legs held true, despite giving him a twinge here and there. He’d sat down too long and therefore the next part of the workout would be painful as he forced his muscles back in line. He welcomed the pain. He’d just approached the rowing machine and had wiped the seat over in order to wipe away the other guys sweat, when the lights cut out. “Fuck.” He sighed and wiped away the sweat from his forehead and eyes. One Hour Ago. “I don’t care who he says he is,” Stakiri bellowed. He was in a bad mood and he decided to take it out on the receptionist. Winters suspected she enjoyed being verbally abused for a living; there could be no other real reason she hadn’t quit already, like the so many who came before. “I don’t fuckin’ want to speak with him. I have enough problems to deal with as it is!” Stakiri had left Winters in charge of the clean up crew’s in his absence, but with the extra training he’d undertaken as of late, his self titled gangster hunt had taken a back burner. The other guys in the crew were too incompetent to deal with the problems on their own and with this new crew that’d moved into town and set up shop last week, everyone was on edge. Winters could tell Stakiri was pissed at him, but he didn’t really give a fuck. Wrestling came first and Stakiri knew that, hell, Winters had even thought he’d accepted it -- at least to some degree. Losing fifty thousand dollars a week from the protection racket from the area in which this new crew had adopted had changed his mind quickly. It didn’t seem like a lot of money for Stakiri to be worried about, but he’d told Winters in confidence that his sources had suggested that they’d already begun to speculate about gambling, drug running and prostitution. The wheels of industry were turning. Besides, Stakiri couldn’t allow it on principle. The principle being, if he allowed these morons to do it, a hundred more would move in and expect the same opportunities. Fuckers... “But sir,” the receptionist began. Stakiri had put her on the intercom so everyone in the room could hear. “They say it’s within your best interest to talk with them.” Winters ran his hand over his face. Stakiri’s face turned a dangerous shade of red and he expected to see a spontaneous combustion of Illustrious Face-Eater proportions. “WHAT THE FUCK COULD THEY POSSIBLY HAVE TO INTEREST ME!” He yelled. The receptionist on the other side of the line squealed, but apparently quickly composed herself. “He’s mentioning your ‘new gang problem’ and something about Oregon,” she told him. With that Stakiri’s eyes widened. There was a genetics organisation from Oregon that had threatened some of Stakiri’s legitimate business claims. Last month, he flew out there to sort it out and while he was gone, the gangs moved in like cockroaches. “Go on,” Stakiri told her, a lot calmer now, but still shaking with anger. “Put them through.” “Mr. Stakiri,” the voice boomed. There was an unmistakable jovial tone to his voice, which seemed somehow misplaced. “I trust you have this conversation on loud speaker?” “Of course,” Stakiri confirmed, rolling his eyes. “Good,” the jovial voice replied. “I want your two little freaks to hear this too.” Stakiri glared up at Aeryn and Winters, grim faced. The others in the room, Stakiri’s lawyer Richard Moss and his guy on the streets, Miguel echoed his action. What the fuck...? “Fuck you,” Winters told him. The man on the other end of the line laughed heartily. “You mean they don’t know?” “They know,” Stakiri responded, his anger quickly rising to the surface once more. But Stakiri wasn’t lying. Winters and Aeryn did know about their genetics being altered, everyone in the room did, they just didn’t know how he knew. “How do you know?” “We have our ways,” the voice informed. He was playing with Stakiri, that much was obvious. “You mentioned Oregon before...” Stakiri pushed. The man on the other end of the line chuckled. “Right to the point, eh? I like that. I can see why you got so far.” The man’s voice lost it’s jovial edge, like Winters knew it would and the use of past tense in the last part of the sentence didn’t escape him either. He don’t like this and he could tell by the tension in the room, the sentiment is a shared one. “Last month, you went to sabotage your competition in Oregon, you remember that?” Fuck... “I have no idea what in the hell you’re talking about,” Stakiri lied. Winters had no idea what Stakiri expected to achieve by the lie. Whoever the hell was on the other end of the line already knew everything there was to know, that much was obvious to everyone. “You don’t?” There’s a sarcastic chuckle, but that‘s stopped abruptly enough. Winters suspected the guy was a lunatic, but that was neither here nor there. “Well we certainly do. Before, I’m embarrassed to say that we were a little...unprepared for your actions. You use illegal means to do business and a whole lot more besides I’d wager.” He allowed the sentence to linger for a minute. “I didn’t quite know the extent of your involvement, but that has been remedied and now we have all the information we will ever need.” “I don’t have time for your delusions,” Stakiri replied, keeping the facade up to the very end. “You’ve obviously got the wrong end of whatever stick you were looking for and I suggest you just go and find the right one.” “Cornelius, Cornelius, Cornelius.” His voice regained more of it’s false modesty. He was playing with Stakiri now and Winters knew then more than ever this was going to get ugly -- real ugly. “Was that a threat?” “Take it how you like.” Winters could see the Stakiri getting angrier by the minute, but he could also see a good number of strategies already percolating about what to do next. “Very well,” the man conceded. “Just know that you will pay for actions and it will be sooner rather than later. I’ll be seeing you, Cornelius. I’ll allow you to guess when.” “Very good,” Stakiri answered. The line went dead and Stakiri glanced around the room pointedly. “What the fuck?” Aeryn muttered, voicing everyone else’s silent bewilderment. “First of all I want to know just how this fool knows so much about our organisation and what we do,” Stakiri began. Miguel, Stakiri’s main guy on the streets, nodded, taking the task on enthusiastically. Winters suspected that guy would use any excuse to hit the streets swinging and Stakiri had just handed him a damned good reason for doing it. Blood would be flowing by lunch time. Stakiri then focused his attention on Aeryn. “I want to know everything, and I mean everything, you unearthed during your little excursion to Oregon. I want names, faces and locations.” Aeryn nodded. “Of course.” Winters shook his head. He didn’t like this one bit and what’s more, Stakiri could tell. “Winters,” He said, “you disapprove?” “No,” Winters replied, the thoughtful expression prominently fixed upon his face. “I just think we should beef up security.” “What?” Stakiri asked. “You were actually intimidated by this fool?” “Intimidated? No,” Winters told him. “Convinced? Definitely. This guy has been planning this for a while, you really think he’s going to show his hand this early without knowing he can get to you whenever he feels like it. He’s coming and he’s coming today.” Stakiri rolled his eyes and Miguel chuckled. “You are intimidated.” “Whatever,” Winters replied. He didn’t really give a flying fuck what they thought of him, his concerns were valid ones and they all knew it, no matter what kind of front they chose to put up. “Just remember I said this.” “Winters go home and get some sleep,” Stakiri ordered. Winters wasn’t his slave and Stakiri would do well to remember it. “You sound like you could use it. The rest of you know what has to be done.” Winters shook his head and left the office. This was going to come back and bite Stakiri in the ass, he just knew it. Now. Turns out Winters wasn’t ready for anything and neither was Stakiri. Winters awoke, not to darkness, but to light. Whatever had attacked him was long gone, there was nothing in the immediate area moving and definitely no sign of life within his peripheral vision. Winters sat up and couldn’t help a groan slip out. He felt as though he’d just been mowed down by Tchu or something. Whatever had hit him had done a real number and he clutched at his ribs instinctively as he rose to his feet. He heard a groan emanating from Stakiri’s office and as quickly as his body would allow, he headed in that direction. Stakiri was slumped in his now blood-stained, opulent leather chair, cradling his head with his right hand. Aeryn was slumped against the wall to his right that had a vicious looking dent in it and blood poured down her face from the open wound at the top of her forehead. However, more suspicious was the tape recorded delicately placed directly in the centre of the desk. What the fuck is that? Winters thought, regarding the device incredulously. “What the fuck?” Stakiri spluttered. He already had the tape recorder in hand before Winters could try to stop him. He hit the play button. “Cornelius,” it greeted. The voice was a lot clearer now, but there could be no doubt in Winters mind that it was the same man as before. “It has begun.” Fuck...
