Date: ReV #87 Sanctum Salubritas They sat around the table. The brick-walled room was dark. They didn't use electricity here. Only red candles lining the walls served to light this area, just barely. A round conference table with seven chairs: four to one side, three on the other. The three men in black suits only blended in further with the darkness. Only the older man in the middle with the spanish accent, Vidago, would speak. The others were there for 'the look'. They entered from the door opposite the throne, upon which a murky figure in a dark red suit sat quietly, observing. From the far wall, adjacent, the group of four entered. They were wearing gray cloaks within the sanctum, but were not so identical as the black-haired caucasian 'suits' across from them. One was small, only 5'5' and barely scraping 130; Ross Colbert Falkson, a mouse of a man. He sat on the left. James Richards as on the far right, average-sized man, nothing striking about him. In the middle right was a massive man named Jordan Giassi, easily 350 pounds, if not more. The robes did not betray his size or frame; he was quite muscular. Finally, to the middle left, the somewhat-portly Indian man Deska Yelemenchi. And beyond them, the mysterious Daniel Baubier, the leader of the Cult of the Poison Tree. No one saw him close-up, aside from James Richards. Vidago: Good of you to make accomodations so swiftly after this call. Jordan Giassi was 10 feet away from Vidago because of the length of the table, but still leaned in closer to intimidate through body language. Giassi: Who are you to make any calls to us? We don't have to report to you until the job is done. At the nod of Vidago, the man to his left slides a folder forward on the table. Given the amount of paper inside, it's quite 'weighty' and easily crosses the distance to Falkson, who opens and reads it. Vidago: The job is done. Falkson: What is this? The calm air of Vidago as he sat arms-crossed, not even looking in their direction, irked James Richards. Vidago: You are soon to be indicted by the Justice Department. Our contact has informed us that it should be only a matter of days before they move on-- The impulsive Deska interjected. Yelemenchi: We can fix this. Vidago: Poor fool... there is nothing to fix. Nothing you can fix. We shall have no connection to a group so sloppy. Giassi: You need us just as-- Vidago. I will thank you not to interrupt me again. The Unit does not need you. As you may see in that dossier, we agree with the government that you are 'small potatoes' at best. You served a purpose, however you are not the only ones suited to that task. Any street gang will serve for distribution. Most likely they would be more discreet about their affairs. Falkson: We haven't told anyone outside about the trafficking. Vidago: I'm talking about this 'Scott Matthews.' Why the devil is he so important to you that you set yourselves into national attention to destroy his reputation? Yelemenchi: He insulted our leader and his glorious organization. Giassi: An enemy of the Poison Tree. He must be broken, and eliminated. The man on Vidago's right shook his head. Vidago: No one man is worth this. You have violated of the terms of our contract. Have you noticed that this Matthews character has been missing from his home, along with his family? They have been placed into protective custody to testify at the trial Mr. Baubier. Daniel Baubier was a rock in this meeting. James Richards sat quietly, paying close attention to what was going on but without a word. Jordan Giassi pounded on the table with his fist. Giassi: Bastard! At a sigh, Vidago rose from his seat. Vidago: And of course, this does not even touch upon the even more appalling discover that you've been selling bleached aspirin and selling Perfection for your own coffers. This is unforgivable. Our current contract is terminated. Your incompetence is to blame for restructuring costs which will set us back at least two weeks. However, I am a businessman first and foremost. At the proper compensation, I can give you something that you want, while serving The Unit's benefit as well. Yelemenchi: I don't trust you. Vidago: Trust is not an issue at this. You will take my offer, I know it. Yelemenchi: What could you give us? Vidago: For the location of Scott Matthews, that you might rectify your many ignominious defeats at his hands? You will pay whatever I ask... Falkner: And how much would this cost? The black suit turned away from the table, stepping out of the room last. He didn't bother to look at them when he gave his answer to close the conversation. Vidago: Everything you have.... Philedelphia, Pennsylvania As luck would have it, I did manage to get in touch with Skylar Green. Seems that he didn't go off home after that show. He and Shawn Stewart are traveling together on the road. Two guys, one Explorer (hey, they're big guys). I missed my ride out. Richard Parker is an asshole when it comes to travel plans. So there I was, getting a ride from The Replacements. I could hardly contain my excitement. The car ride from New York to Philidelphia is a few hours. More than enough time for me to slip in a passing mention about managing them. Of course, if they stuck around PRIME, I'd probably have to stop using Vander Jung as my name. Though it is really something of an assumed name. Nobody would hire a guy named Beka Jung. Might use my middle name, Tristan. That's a little better. But so far, nothing. Seems that these are two of the quietest guys you'd ever be in a car with. Shawn Stewart was only listening to 90s rock the whole time. Green slept nearly the whole way through. When I did try to speak about their matches, they were... well, it wasn't dismissive. But they just didn't think about them. Both pretty much said the same thing. 'It's in the past now.' Are they serious? They both fall off the map and return as a tag team, something nobody would expect, and then just up and outclass the very guys they're replacing. And they're so nonchalant about it. I asked about the upcoming match against The A-List. Green only managed 'Whatever comes, we'll deal with it.' At least Stewart was a little more vocal on that subject. Didn't like their attitude, or their style, or what they'd done to Kyle Lamen after that tag match. I've gotta figure out how I can get some real attention out of these two. I can't just be a road scout anymore. I want to be noticed. Where the action is. If I can manage someone, I'll get free camera time until I can bulk back up for my return to wrestling after years away. Doing it with these replacements would be even better. They're watched because they're established. And people want to enjoy them while they're around. Doesn't hurt that they make a good team. Being attached to them... that'll only serve my interests faster. Sure, they probably won't be around long enough to do anything substantial. But while they're here, I can get what I can from them. His Other Kismet Alyssa sat curled in the corner of the small bathroom, her body shaking with uncontrollable sobs. Her face was a mass of red with tears dripping from her chin in an unending flow. Her sobbing cries were unintelligible; they were just unrestrained, unadulterated cries of grief. Her body was covered in slick, dark blood, from her neck down all over her body. Her hands were clenched together around the engagement ring that Scott had given her not so long ago, framing it in the delicate grip that was covered in the blood of the man that had given it. Van had fallen to a sitting position on the floor, his face blanched and his knuckles bloody. His normally-bright eyes were veiled with an uncomprehending dullness. He had.... he had mastered his car as well as any man could and gotten them there as quickly as they could. He'd hammered down the door as strongly as he was able. But in the end, there in the bathtub, was their friend, Scott Matthews, dead. His body was covered with blood from the waist down, which still dripped off his still form. The holes he'd slashed into his forearms bled no more, with a heart no longer beating. He had already been growing cold when they had found him. But he had gone from them. The liquor bottles were on the bed in the hotel room. The mirror was shattered. And Scott was dead. On the wall, he'd had the presence to mark his last thoughts in his own vitality. His unsteady finger had left two messages in the runny crimson on the tile of the bathroom. One was to each of them. “Van, you were my best friend.” “I'll miss you, Aly.” They both sat there until the police arrived, having been alerted by the front desk clerk. They were picked up by Scott's mother. Even though she was clearly hurting, the iron matriarch didn't let it show until they had gotten back to the house, but once there.... The house that he'd grown up in kept Alyssa, Cathy and Lacey in tears throughout the night. Van simply sat in the living room, alone, in stunned silence. Unknown Location *Ring* *Ring* What? Damn, these guys are cheapskates. Green chalks it up to 'Family.' Stewart just isn't a big spender. They got separate rooms, they like their privacy. Really, I think Green was going off to sleep some more. Has to be one of the laziest wrestlers I've seen. He slept throughout the show last week, slept in the car... Right now now he's probably snoring in his room, pillow saturated with drool. Shawn Stewart didn't say much. But odds ten-to-one that he's looking in the mirror right now and wanking to the image of himself. He strikes me as that kind of guy. The way he moves in the ring you can just tell that there's this air of superiority he's got around him. He tries to back it up, but still... lighten the hell up a bit. I got my own room. Nightstand at the side of the bed with wall-mounted lamps on either side of the room. Bathroom had a toilet, shower, and sink, but otherwise wasn't anything to write home about. I'm under the impression that whoever does the narration for this place hasn't even once stayed in a Motel 6. If he did, the slogan wouldn't be 'We'll leave the light on for you,' it'd be 'Eh, we've got beds.' My day (yes, it's still only about 1 pm) will consist of some jogging around the mile. We're on the outskirts of town off of the interstate. Lighter traffic out this way. I can hear myself think, and I'm only slightly choked by smog. I keep up the jogging for cardiovascular fitness. If I'm going to get back into the ring, I've gotta be able to go without resting for too long. Don't wanna be like one of those new created characters in Smackdown vs. RAW 2006. Until you get you stamina stats up, your character is about ready to pass out after only four body slams. I've got a long-standing membership to World Gym. Should be able to find one around here, but I can always pay the temp fee for a smaller one. I'll do that after an afternoon cocktail. This stuff is amazing. I don't know what it is, but it works. And that's just what this steroid is, and helps you achieve. The guy who sold it to me... Rosco Falkson, I think, he identified himself as. Windsor, Ontario, Canada Adrienne Diamond lay in bed. Emi was out at her exercises. Meanwhile, Diamond, who was gaining a few pounds, resolved to get up 'sometime' and do some jogging. 'Sometime,' though, meant 'someday,' and that was always at least a day away. Richard Hargrove wired the phones, at her request, in order to make such calls. He was in on it, too. Teshigahara Emi and Vangellus Oxios were in the dark, though. Adrienne didn't want her fiance to know what was going on with her. She truly loved Emi and wanted to keep her. But Emi's strong sense of justice would mean that, at the very least, the relationship would be broken off if word of her activities spread. As for Vangellus, it was for his own inability to keep secrets that he'd be left in the dark. He was useful to The Unit in that he provided an easy medium for their money-laundering. His paychecks went in to finance them, unbeknownst to him. Oxios merely knew that he was making 'investments' that Adri coordinated for him. Meanwhile, the money made from Perfection, their recreational drugs 'Elysium,' and the so-far experimental 'Achilles' all went into a slush fund laundered through Oxios's mysterious (even to him) Van World Order. Someone had to wonder where his money was coming from. But no one could figure it out, the very few who'd bothered to find it. It was brilliant of her to set this up. Who in the world would suspect that a professional wrestler would be unwittingly used to assist in the machinations and business goals of drug traffickers? Only its masterminds. A stroke of genius disguised as 'pay-per-view cashouts.' The perfect crime. His Other Kismet The funeral was a somber event, private in the extreme, as Scott was buried in the small hillside cemetery that belonged to the church that Cathy was a member of. Scott's grave was dug in the middle of a grove of willow trees with the money from his last pay per view payoff. His casket was of solid steel and he was buried in his typical attire: boots, jeans with a buttondown shirt and his hair caringly and meticulously brushed back. Everyone sat on the folding chairs in the overcast morning, looking at the casket sitting over the grave. Van was dressed well, in somber grays. Alyssa wore a full-length, long-sleeved dress. She still wept openly, but in soft, exhausted sobs. The preacher sadly closed his Bible and lowered his head. Everyone there was quiet, wallowing in their grief of the big man that wanted nothing more to take care of the people that he loved so dearly. And in the wind, a soft whisper of a mournful melody blew through the willow grove. Alyssa rode away from the funeral in the front passenger seat of Scott's old Jeep. Lacey had quietly assumed the role of possessor of the vehicle, and everyone had silently assented that it would have accorded with Scott's wishes. She watched the mountains of eastern Kentucky roll by, the land she'd come to love with him. And in those few moments, she knew she wouldn't be seeing them again. She thanked Cathy and Lacey, hugging them both, and loaded her little rental car with everything she'd brought to visit for the holidays. With a tearful farewell, she drove away, leaving behind the mountains that had towered around them. It was a long, lonely drive back to the apartment that she and Scott had moved into. She walked in and dropped her bag next to the door. She kicked the door shut behind her, hurriedly, as she felt the all-to-familiar lump rising in her throat. With the door safely shut, she dropped to her knees, curling into the fetal position and wept again. Everything that could have been, the life that they were planning to have together, it was all gone because people didn't know him and hated him. And he was still taken away from her. In the course of the evening, she had not been able to do anything. Trying to watch TV had only resulted in her reflexive action to lean on his broad chest. Making something to eat was her trying to decide which of his favorites to surprise him with. She tried to take a shower and shut it off as soon as she looked down into the tub. With a final effort, she had crawled into bed, but desperately cried out for the massive arms that used to hold her so close and comfort her so well. She cried for her lover that had made her forsake her hedonistic ways for just him. And all of it, in her heart, was just entirely too much. It was in this moment that she understood why Scott would drink so much when he was depressed. He always told her it was so he could just forget about some of the worse parts of his life. With a deep breath, she pushed herself up and out of the bed. Her left hand still fingered the engagement ring she couldn't bear the thought of taking off. But as she made her unsteady way into their kitchen, her resolve grew. She threw open the cabinet and grabbed the bottle of Southern Comfort from the shelf. Alyssa wasn't a big girl; standing at about five and a quarter and weighing in at a buck fifteen, she couldn't even dream to keep up with Scott's feats of alcoholic prowess. He could finish off a bottle of whiskey by himself. She couldn't handle more than a few shots. She lay back down on the bed and slid the engagement ring off her finger and clenched it in the palm of her hand. With strength brought from desperation, she tore the top from the bottle and took a deep drink. It burned all the way down her throat, causing her to cough and sputter harshly. But the burn occupied her mind and the warmth spread over her. With a grim nod, she tipped the bottle upward again. She'd forget for the night, or die trying. Alyssa Sanders was found a day later when she wouldn't return Cathy Matthews' phone calls. In a state of reasonable concern, Cathy and Lacey went to Lexington to check on her. With the spare key that they'd left at the house, the two of them made their way in to find Alyssa lying on the bed, an empty whiskey bottle on the floor and her engagement ring clutched in her hand. A picture of her and Scott in an embrace lay next to her on the bed. She had left no note, but the message was clear. Scott's eyes shot open, his body covered in a cold sweat. He drew in a quivering breath. He was in the safehouse, hidden away in the witness protection in order to keep his family safe. He knew that now, but disassociating that from the vivid dream... What had happened was different from what he had dreamed, but the result had seemed so real to him. He had seen the grief from a disembodied point of view and had watched every agonizing moment as Alyssa had drank herself to death. That memory made him reach for her, but she was not on the other side of the bed. Instead, her head was laying on his chest, her body shaking with open sobs. “You,” she started, then faltered. “You... you talked in your sleep. You said you were sorry you had died. You were telling me to stop.” Scott wrapped his arms around her and buried his face in her soft, raven hair. “I'm so sorry, Aly,” he whispered, tears falling down his cheeks. “I didn't know...” Cincinnati, Ohio Even though it was 50 degrees that morning, James Picot walked to work. He only lived a few blocks from the office. It was a nice, fair-weather day. His step had a noticeable spring. It would be a light work day, he could tell. Not many cases pending on the docket for this calendar day, surprisingly. He'd slip out early and attend to his other plans. The park in front of the building glistened from the dew upon the grass. The building got larger, he approached from the front. The face of the building looked fun, for once. The early morning light hitting it covered up the weathering, the stripped paint. The courthouse looked, for once, in all its grandeur, like a respectable house of adjudication. Those stairs, normally a sight of intimidation, were now a happy little jaunt. Life was good. Great, even. He could already see Sarah's smiling face. She always got there an hour ahead of him, ready to work. His coffee would be ready for him, schedule queued, work organized neatly in folders on his desk as she always did for him. Scary how they thought so much alike. That's why they'd been friends for so long. The breeze hit his face. He could swear that there was the hint of French Vanilla wafting into his nostrils. Sarah's morning delight. Traffic was light for this time of morning, when there should've been a rush. The sun was unusually bright being this early. Birds flitted at ground level, robins for him to catch on eye as he made his way happily to the only job he could ever see himself in. Then the bolt of thunder. This morning had been just for him. The last that James Picot would ever see. The shot was clean, direct; straight through the middle of the forehead. The ejection burst ensured that he would be relatively clean, though the sidewalk was another matter. James Picot. 29 years old. Victim of the Cult of the Poison Tree.
