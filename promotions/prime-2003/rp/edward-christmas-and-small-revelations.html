Date: Different Ones. Essentially, when picking a pin-and-tumbler lock the most important (and possibly most difficult) part of the process is to remain calm. Locksmiths and lock picking enthusiasts, such as Edward Christmas, will attest to the fact that it can be one of the more trying, frustrating experiences an amateur can endure. This, however, was never a problem for Ed, who was a consummate professional when it came to bottling up frustrations and picking locks alike. Inside of a pin-and-tumbler lock dwells a cylinder that decides its fate. Pins embed themselves into the cylinder, holding it in place when locked, preventing it from rotating. A correct key will open the lock by moving particularly obtruding pins from the cylinder, allowing it to rotate. When someone doesn't have a key for a particular lock, be it due to negligence or trespassing, a different set of tools are required. Edward had spent many days of his life crafting his apparatus. To successfully pick a lock, you need torque and tension. Christmas had both of those at his belt in plenty, all of the picks and wrenches glinting even in the darkness of 2:30 am. Though his pretty, shining utensils did not hold a candle to the beauty of the deadbolt itself, Ed had noticed. Cold, mechanical. Logical. Edward had always admired locks. They were simple. A lock didn't know anything of good and evil, of wrong and right. It knew of worthiness and nothing more. Locks held no friends nor secrets for eternity. As soon as a deserving adversary crossed a lock's path, it revealed its skeletons. Truth be told, Ed had always modeled himself after a lock. Cold, mechanical. Logical. Were he to have an opportunity to look back on this night, he would relish the fact he seemed to embody those qualities. The dexterity Ed handled his tools with was admirable to say the least. It happened in a flash. Sliding a small, tension wrench with his left hand in the very bottom of the keyhole, he turned his hand to the right mocking the actions a key would make. While applying consistent pressure with that tool, his right hand placed a pick diligently into the top of the keyhole and began raking it across the pins that held the cylinder. He could feel the pins on the inside and was partially sure he'd worked with this very kind of lock before. His hands didn't shake. They hadn't in years. The pins reached sheer alignment and the door unlocked all in a matter of seven seconds. Christmas ran a pale hand through his red hair. Everything about the house he chose was quaint and whimsical. Even with an exoskeleton of dark brick and mortar, the dwelling was made bright and alive by glistening windchimes and potted flowers hanging from the front porch awning. Edward twisted the gleaming, gold doorknob. Despite his best efforts at silence, the old door creaked as he unsealed it from its place in the doorway. click Ed flipped the switch of his small, blue flashlight. click Ed cocked the hammer of his small, black pistol. He drew deep breaths and took short steps around the house. His heart was battering his sternum and pummeling his eardrums. This worried the normally calm and collected Edward Christmas, who took a brief moment to gather himself. "Cold, mechanical. Logical," he whispered, likely inaudibly over his own beating heart. On this night, he knew where his victims were. Their house was practically his own. With the intelligence he'd gathered, he knew every location of every possible hazard. Assiduous and with intensity gathering in his gut, gun held in front of him with two hands, Edward Christmas stepped into the master bedroom. Show time. Edward flipped the light on. "Carrot," Nine muttered, asleep and unwilling to wake up. "Go back to bed." Edward stepped towards the queen-sized bed. "Carrot, come on," Nine rolled over, cutting himself off when he saw the gun barrel and the wielder. Nine rubbed his eyes in disbelief, "Officer Porter?" "Shut the fuck up." bang ************************************* NINE HOURS EARLIER He wore the tin badge as well as anyone. Quiet confidence excreted from Edward's pores even as he examined himself in the sideview mirror of the Homerville Police model Crown Victoria. Aviator sunglasses bedecked his freckled face. Curly, red hair kept short and military uniform. Opening the door and stepping out, he couldn't help but breathe in the fresh, Georgia evening air. "Ah," it seemed to quinch an imaginary thirst of sorts, that deep inhale of it. "Such a beautiful day." His official police boots seemed to stomp whenever they touched the ground, heavy and burdening his feet as much as they seemed to guard them. Though not quite used to the uniform, Christmas could see himself as a cop in another life. Edward's fist rapped the door softly. A small girl called from behind the door, "Who is it?" "Homerville Police, just checking in." After a moment the door opened from the inside. A bearded man in grey sweatpants and a white t-shirt opened the door, an orange haired, little girl beside him. "Yessir?" "Hi. I'm Officer Charles Porter," Edward began his deceit. "Homerville Police. We heard of little Carrot's unique situation and Id personally like to present you with some ways that I and the community of Homerville, can help. Mind if I come in?" Christmas flashed a brilliant smile. The bearded resident stood silent for a moment, scratching his chin. " Not many people know about Carrot's "unique situation," Officer. Who told you?" "Why, I could never tell you that, Mister. In good faith, I've told too much already." Edward feigned embarassment, "To be honest, Mr. Nine, heh-- I broke protocol by even coming here. This aint exactly authorized." With a slight hesitation, Nine agreed. "Sure. I'm Nine, but I guess you already knew that. Come on in." "Splended," replied Edward. "Wonderful." ************************************* "Okay man. Okay. Officer, calm down. What's going on?" Edward fired another shot in the air. "Are you fucking stupid? Did I not just tell you to shut the fuck up? Yet here you are, running your god damn mouth. You must not be afraid of much. That, or you're a stupid fucking idiot." Nine wisely neglected to retort. "You learn quick," Ed noted. "Quick enough for me to know that you just aren't afraid of much, anyway. That right there though, see, that presents a serious conflict between you and I. I need to know two things from you, motherfucker. Not only that, I need to obtain two things from you as well. You keep that stupid mouth of yours shut and you might just keep your brains inside of your skull." Perhaps reality set in on Nine during Edward's speech. Salty tears welled up in his eyes. "So," Christmas continued. "What I need from you, Mr. Nine, is the combination to that safe back there." "...What else do you need?" Edward Christmas grinned in such a way that Nine knew the answer before it ever crossed his teeth. It was an answer that agonized the numerologist and tore his heart into pieces. Christmas even chuckled, "I'm gonna need the girl, Nine. I'm going to need Carrot." ************************************* THREE WEEKS EARLIER The dingy motel room of The Scottish Inn served as Edward Christmas' meeting place. Cockroaches, alive and dead, littered the carpet along with empty, plastic cups and other pieces of trash. Even the lightbulb, dim and near death, braced impact after impact from flying insects attacking it for its warmth. A table covered with cigarettes, car magazines and newspaper sat between the two men. The two of them had discussed their plans briefly on the phone. The deal was simple: One man does another's dirty work and gets paid handsomely for it. "So, Mike Collins is it?" Carrot's Uncle smiled, extending his hand for a shake, "That's the one. How are you doing bro?" Christmas did not oblige his potential employer with a shake of the hand or a smile. Business was business. "I'm doing well. I need you to tell me what you want." Mike laughed, "The guy I got your number from said you were the real deal. But to call out of nowhere and you bail me out of trouble with a pesky officer trying to haul my ass to jail? Fuck yes, I called the right guy. You iced that fucker. What's his name? Fuckin' Porter?" "Mike." "Yeah?" "...Tell me what the fuck it is you want already." Shaky hands caused the half-smoked cigarette to tremble between Mike's index and middle finger as he spoke. "There's this motherfucker in Homerville. Sixty miles south of here. He's got my niece, Carrot... and I want her back. I'm willing to pay any price, man. Any price you could possibly imagine." "Twenty-five grand." In a knee jerk reaction, the cigarette flew from Mike Collins' lips as he spat in disbelief. "TWENTY-FIVE GRAND!?!" Edward wasn't in the mood for playing games, "Look. I don't know what the fuck you think it is I'm doing here, but I'm not gonna help a sick fucking bastard like you obtain your niece just so you can molest her without payment." Mike interrupted, "I didn't molest her." It was something that Collins still could not admit to outloud, nor would he ever be able to. It brought almost as much shame as it did delight to him, his sick tendencies. Christmas shook his head, "Whatever. Twenty-Five Grand. Take it or leave it." Collins rubbed his bald head, "Twenty-two." "Agreed," this time Ed smiled and extended his hand. "You shake my hand, I expect full compensation." Mike laughed, "You'll get your money when you bring me my niece." ************************************* Denial pressed firmly against Nine's brain. "You can't have her." "Last time I checked," Ed stated calmly. "I'm the one with the goddamn gun in my hand. So let's have a little less arguing and a little more doing-whatever-the-fuck-I-say, alright?" Nine chose to forgo further argument. Ed began, "Now, I know she's scared shitless in her bedroom after all the racket my gun made. Probably under her little bed, crying her little bitty eyes out just like you're trying to stop yourself from doing. So we're gonna let her wonder what's going on a little bit longer while you take me to that backroom and open up that god damn safe." Nine shook his head in disbelief, "You don't know what you're getting into." Christmas snorted, "No. But I know that you've got a big ass safe and you don't believe in guns. So forgive me, fucker, but you've peaked my curiosity." Nine's tears could no longer be held back. Sliding down his face in unrelenting trails, they fell from his eyes to his chin before taking a great, suicidal dive to the ground below him. Though it had been a while since he'd felt this kind of pain; a while since he'd felt this kind of heartbroken sense of helplessness, ever again would've been too soon. "Get the fuck up," Edward demanded, motioning the gun in an upward fashion. "Take me to the safe." Nine stood up from the bed, finding the barrel of a pistol placed firmly against the back of his cranium. Powerlessness crept all over his body, like spiders over someone who's taken in too much Angel Dust. His skin was crawling with weakness. "Walk." Nine did as he was told. Guided by Edward's flashlight shining outward from behind him, Nine led him down the hallway and into the furthest room down it. He flipped the lightswitch. "9, 11, 22, 63, 45," the upset numerologist said the combination just loud enough for his potential gunman to hear. Christmas walked to the safe. It was taller than him by several inches. With an ear placed near the lock, he carefully guided the turndial through each number Nine said outloud. Soon, he heard the click of the lock disengaging. Edward swung the door open. He would've said something about what treasure he found immediately, but... "You fucked up!" He fucked up. As Edward turned to answer, the shimmer of a kitchen knife grasped tightly in Nine's hand caught his eye. "Don't fucking move a muscle, you bastard!" Nine was breathing hard, the subject of murder being tossed around hard in his mind. Edward sat in a stunned silence, "Oh. Where'd you get that?" "It was sitting on the table right here by the safe. You must be a real fucking amateur, putting the combination in yourself instead of having me do it. You don't watch movies, you fucking prick?" "I don't mean the knife." Nine glanced up from his moment of gloat to the open safe and the bricks and bricks of brown powder lining it. "Oh, that. That's end's meet." ************************************* "How is one to live a moral and compassionate existence when one is fully aware of the blood, the horror inherent in life, when one finds darkness not only in one's culture but within oneself?" - Barry Lopez ************************************* It had been a day and a half since Edward Christmas had been tied up to a chair and gagged in the Nine household. He noticed that Nine went through his due dilligence to shield Carrot from all of his actions. She had hid under the bed, as Edward guessed. She hid there until long after Edward had submitted to being tied up, to avoid being stabbed to death right then and there. He also noticed the safe full of heroin was moved out, brick by brick, by twenty or so workers who all seemed to know Nine on a fairly personal level with their brief dialogue and small talk. To be fair, he'd tried to shoot the bastard. "Oh, that. That's end's meet." Edward lifted the gun quickly and aimed it at Nine's forehead. bang, bang, bang Ed wondered how he had missed all three shots. The rare, even impossible blunders at point blank range made him consider fate, destiny, karma and God. All of those thoughts zoomed through his mind at a frenzied pace as two of the workers lifted him up along with the chair he was tied to and others placed plastic on the floor beneath him. "I missed?" Nine's eyes were wide and fear-stricken. His face had narrowly avoided three bullets and he didn't even move. "...You missed..." Edward sat for just a moment, pulling the trigger to his empty gun over and over. "What next?" As men covered the walls with the very same kind of plastic sheets, taping it from ceiling to floor, he cried outward with sound. No real words escaping from the ballgag in his mouth. He thought of Mike Collins, the bastard who got him into all of this. Of Charles Porter, the Officer he murdered and assumed the identity of for one job. He was going to Hell... he knew it. Edward shook and screamed and fought to try and tear away from the chair. There was no budging. His screams became frantic, erratic gasps for air as he watched people shuffle slowly out of the room. Almost everyone of them turned and looked at Christmas before they made their respective exits. He met eyes with Nine once more. "What next?" Nine didn't answer for over a minute, his heart still lightyears ahead of him after being shot at like that. Finally, he responded. "I think... you already know the answer to that." He had fought hard in the moment following those words, Edward had. He even narrowly missed Nine's face with the handle of his pistol. Another near miss that would've changed the course of history. Nine did not waste time chattering with his newfound prisoner. He filed out of the room along with everyone else, save two men. Edward Christmas and a particular hispanic fellow, holding the gun that Ed had held only 36 hours prior.. The spaniard was short, overweight and rather hairy. His clothes were plain: blue jeans, black t-shirt. Though none of that mattered to Ed, who cried outward as the spanish man shut the door behind them. "Nine is a good man," offered the spaniard. Edward couldn't stop his frenetic pace of breathing and sobbing. The spanish man lifted up Edward's pistol. "When you try and hurt a good man, a Pythagorean, like Nine, people like me... we get offended." Edward knew the end was near, but it was approaching too fast. Through the gag in his mouth his pleas seemed futile. The sweat pouring down his face displayed to the spanish man that he was in a state of panic. "pleasedonkillmepleasedonkillmepleaseDON'TKILLME!" "Your gun. I found some bullets for it." The spaniard ignored Edward, who could only cry now. The Lord's Prayer ran through Ed's mind over and over and over. "please...please...please...please...please..." "If you feel like you can't handle seeing it coming," The Spaniard smiled at the pathetic being he perceived before him, "Perhaps you should close your eyes." Edward Christmas closed his eyes. His hands shook violently. They hadn't in years. AM I GOING TO HELL? PLEASE GOD. PLEASE GOD. Finally, he responded. "I think... you already know the answer to that." The Spaniard cocked the hammer. Cold, mechanical. Logical. "I hope you've made peace."
