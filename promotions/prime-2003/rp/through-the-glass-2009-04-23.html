Through The Glass

23 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/23/09 The sound of the glass bottle gliding across the wooden surface makes unto itself an echo. An echoing that sounds through all the space that surrounds the bottle, and through all the silence which paints itself to the scene like a dash of red passion delight on a canvass. Funny how we give ourselves to the silence of this room yet it’s not a complete silence, but just a silence that blocks every thing out besides the spinning bottle. Question mark should placed at the end of this sentence when we ask ourselves how and why are we paying attention to the bottle? The hand that spins the bottle becomes the answer. We, here in this room, pay attention because the hand of John Lyptica spins it around and around. He hasn’t uttered a word to us, he hasn’t glanced at us, he didn’t bother to recognize our even being here, but instead he seems it necessary to leave us only with the same noise over and over. We scribble on a piece of paper every twitch of John’s hand and every spin around the table before excusing ourselves. Chalk it up to a wasted afternoon because we got absolutely nothing for the website profile. The sound of the crew driving away in their Ford lends to the scene a new sound. The scene melts away like a drug is burning inside of it as John stops the bottle and lifts his head up. Not showing any emotion, he moves close to the ground and grabs a half empty bottle of beer. Pushing it to his lips he begins to drink as the morning pushes along. John continues to walk with the beer in his hand, and seems to be headed down the hall. He pushes the door open. A poor man’s eyes would be considered a rich man’s bank account if he could only see what John is looking at right now. Lovely, beautiful, curvy Scarlett laying without anything on across the bed. A wrinkle in the velvet sheets block a complete view of her breasts while her leg is in the air stretched out, and the other leg comes underneath making the view of her from behind blocked as well. But, it doesn’t affect the opinion of the masses because it’s still obvious she is beautiful. Scarlett moans a bit before stirring upward. Pushing the full velvet covers up against her body, she covers as she awakes and sees John at the foot of the bed. She reaches her arms out to embrace him, but John just stands there taking swigs of his beer. Scarlett pulls her arms back and sighs as she slides out of bed. She slips on her robe, pulls her hair back into a ponytail, and then falls back down into the bed. Scarlett: I was thinking. Instead of a white dress…I could wear a velvet one. John: It’s just one ceremony Scarlett. Wear whatever you want. It’ll only last 30 minutes anyway. Scarlett: You convinced me to leave Vegas and my good client list to move down here in California, and chase your wrestling dream. All with the bait of getting married. So it better last longer then 30 minutes. John: I think the dream is starting to become reality. I’m a little disappointed in the booking decision of PRIME, but at least I got signed. Scarlett: Who are you competing against? John: A guy who calls himself Meat. Never met the man, but I heard they call him Meat because he sucked so much of it during his brief stint as a homosexual. Apparently he got confused during his teen days. Scarlett: Haven’t we all been there? John: No Scarlett, we haven’t. If I would’ve been like Meat, my commander would’ve broken my nose every opportunity he got. That’s why men like me were shooting guns off in the desert and in the jungle while men like Meat can’t even survive on a game of Call of Duty. This match will be easy… Scarlett. Aren’t you the least bit scared? You’re an MMA fighter, not a wrestler. John: They told me I probably wouldn’t make it in the special forces because I wasn’t good in high tense situations. I was the best on my force. I was told I couldn’t make it in MMA, and I rocked Vegas. This is just step 3. Scarlett never has been one to buy much into John’s ego nor expect him to succeed to the degree John does. She is more practical, more down to reality, but in a way that is what makes her the weaker of the two. She has no great accomplishment because she’s scared to jump, she tells no great stories cause she’s afraid to speak in public, and she has a hard time pushing herself in when the subway is rolling down the tracks; she hates the darkness of a tunnel. She has to see the light in order to feel safe or she will just stay where she is at. The only risk she has taken in her life was moving with John to California. John doesn’t care if he sounds egotistical, sounds like he’s a firm subscriber to wishful thanking, and he sure doesn’t care if someone thinks he will fail or not. He isn’t afraid of any one in MMA or on the PRIME roster. Why should he be? He wasn’t afraid when ten men were in front of him spraying bullets through the jungle. John, matter win or lose at Revolution, is subscribed to win. Scarlett gets her a cup of black coffee and discusses with John on why he didn’t give an interview for the crew after previously agreeing to it. John simply explains he didn’t like the way they looked when he saw them. One of them had a crooked nose and he hates crooked noses. John is picky like this and is one of the main reasons why the only people he communicates with on a consistent basis is Scarlett and Lace, his best friend from back in the Airborne days. Wrapping themselves in hand and hand, Scarlett and John pass back flirts and kisses as the clock ticks by slowly. John, kissing Scarlett one last time, begins to get ready for his drive to the stadium as he is being picked up by Lace whom he never leaves without. Scarlett pushing her face into her velvet robe disappears into another room. The room now standing alone without any Lace or velvet, gives itself back to the spinning bottle which is spun around quickly by a passing John. The bottle rests at the edge of the table where it catches the reflection of John staring back at the bottle. John, seemingly losing his focus on the here and now, stares deeply at the bottle like before when the crew was here. The strong force of John’s hands engulfing his face takes John back a step or two. He falls to his feet while still looking at the bottle, and for this moment his eyes open as wide as the red sea did for the Jews. John seems lost and fades out without any lace or velvet to keep him company from the memory in the glass. [Flashback: Enemy Outpost 2 Years Ago] He was yellin’ something, but I couldn’t quite make it out. His body was shaking quite a bit and by the spot that became noticeable on the front of his pants, I’d say he lost control of his body functions. He was a cocky man when he was far off in a distance waving his Toys R Us rifle in the air. But now, he’s standing here toe-to-toe with an Airborne Ranger whom has a gun pressed tightly into his forehead and suddenly he isn’t cocky anymore. I cocked the gun back as my gaze was fixed on this man’s scared face. I whispered to the man that in three seconds I am going to put the finishing touch on his life by pulling the trigger. I counted each second by slowly as tears began to form on his face. I slowly said three and he closed his eyes. I pulled the trigger and splattered his brains out all over the grass on which we stood. His brains looked like overcooked eggs. He was the last man that we had to take down at that outpost….the others had surrendered and were tied up. There were about four men standing around me holding their mouths in distaste. One stepped forward and commented on the appearance of his brains. He said I fried his brains like I was freakin’ human skillet. They would never call me John again. In fact from that instant I heard, "Skillet, go to the hostages. Keep eye." The quickening footsteps of John heralded past the scared prisoners. Each thump of the boot into the mud struck fear into one of them, and if it wasn’t the thumping it was the rattling of his pistol. Carrying a semi-automatic weapon in his hand, he made sure that it pointed at each one of their faces as he walked by. The other soldiers were soon to join John in the watch of the prisoners. Soldier 1: Skillet looks like you survived your last mission. You still going home? John: Yeah the ranch has been too lonely for too long. Soldier 2: You going home to Scarlett? She still waiting for you? John: As always…. [A deep, hard slap goes against John’s face at this moment] John: Who the hell just slapped me? Soldier 1: No one slapped you. Soldier 2: No one slapped you. At this moment a bright streak of light crosses through the air. A helicopter appears out of nowhere and begins to lower to the ground in front of John. John pulls his pistol out and has it in his left hand while the AK is waiting in the other. The door of the helicopter swings open and John begins to lose some of his focus. Dropping to one knee, John lets go of all his weapons as a man in a long white jacket stands before him. Calling out his name causes John to look up slightly. The tall, sophisticated gentleman before him is reaching out his hand. John is scared…the other soldiers aren’t there any more and neither are the prisoners. It’s just this man and him alone on the battlefield. John climbs up to his feet and looks at the man with his staggering focus. Doctor: John, come back to me. This isn’t real. John: I’m going home to Scarlett and Lace. Doctor: We’ve talked about this before John. They don’t exist. You make them up so you have someone to save you, someone to communicate with you. John (reaching his hand out to the Doctor): It’s as real as it gets for me. [Present Day Reality: The Hardwood Floor] John gets his focus back as his eye catches the glimpse of the bottle once more. He notices the bottle being picked up by the man before him, Dr. Richard Langford. Langford walks toward John slowly with bottle in his hand. Laying the bottle by John, he spins it. John zones in on the bottle once more. Langford: Finding you passed out on the floor is not what I wanted to see John. I think you should come back to the hospital for awhile with me. We were making so much progress. Langford: You need to realize John.. Scarlett is something you make up when you feel you need someone to comfort you or save you. Lace is something you make up when you feel you need a friend. It’s time to wake up to reality John and get yourself acquainted with real people. Langford stops the bottle. He slaps his hands together and the sounds rips through John’s head. John snaps back and looks into the cold, blue eyes of Dr. Langford. Seeing the reached out hand of the Doctor, John grabs it and for the first time in quite awhile is pulled to his feet. John: You’re always trying to take away what means the most to me. I will not allow that… I have a debut to involve myself in later tonight. I have a girl to marry and a friend to shake his hand. Your not going to destroy it again for me. Langford: Calm down John. I’m here for the better you. Scarlett isn’t here and neither is Lace. Now what is real is this crazy notion to wrestle. That I will not allow. These gentlemen before me will assist in bringing you back to Griffin. It’s what’s best for you John…. John’s eyes begin to dart around the room. He screams for Scarlett, but no answer. John pushes himself against the wall as the gentlemen approach him. They both grab one of his arms and ask for him to come along nicely. John twists ones arm and pushes him to the floor while the other one is taking down quickly, and has his nose broken. Langford asking John to calm down backs up. Scarlett comes out of her room and grabs John…. John: She’s always here Doc when I’m scared the most. What’s fake about that? Hearing the spinning of a bottle again, John glances over his shoulder. Not knowing who spun the bottle, John looks on with mystery in his eye as does Scarlett as well as Langford. The three look on as the reflections of John’s problems and imaginations play out through the glass.
