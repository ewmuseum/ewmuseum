Pain and regrets (PPV)

14 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 14th May I’m going to tell you a little story, stop me if you’ve heard it. It’s a story about life and the twists and turns that life presents to you. It’s a story about a man who was always the underdog, a man who was always fighting against the odds. I’m not asking you to feel sorry for him; I’m simply asking you to listen to his story. I It was Christmas when I first found out, I remember it like it was yesterday. Snow had begun to fall, settling gingerly on the ground and on the trees, turning the world into a picture perfect Christmas setting. The wood on the fire crackled as I sat in front of it, gazing into the flames that danced their hypnotic dance, my hands out stretched, the heat lapping at my fingers. The Christmas tree stood proud in the corner of the room; a number of presents stacked high underneath it. Things had not been fantastic as of late but mum had always made an effort whenever it was Christmas. The one time of the year when everything should come together, all differences should be put aside for the thought of family. I turned my head and focused my attention upon James who was sat silently in front of the television, you could almost feel his retinas burning from sitting too close, but it kept him entertained at least. My brother had been getting himself into a lot of trouble at school recently; apparently he had not been paying attention in classes. Yet mum and dad didn’t seem to care. They had been arguing a lot recently, James and myself tried to ignore it but sometimes late at night it woke us, we couldn’t hear much but we definitely heard it. James had told me that it was because they didn’t love me anymore. I knew he was lying; he always did things like that. Don’t get me wrong it hurt and a part of me always wondered if they were yelling about me. Mum made no qualms about talking about how tough things were since I came along, how money was tight and things were going to get even tighter. I didn’t know what she meant by that; I was going to find out soon enough. Do you know how hard it is to be told that you caused problems, things that you had absolutely no choice in. I didn’t ask to be born; god knows that I tried to take my own life on a number of occasions. My head darted as Dad fell in through the living room door, the smell of cheap perfume being cancelled out by the smell of stale ale on his breath. He had been drinking again, it was Christmas Eve and he had been out drinking. “Don’t sit too close to that fucking screen.” He shouted loudly, his voice slurring at the seemingly simple sentence. James didn’t even turn around to look at him and simply shuffled backwards a few feet, his eyes still locked on the screen. He slumped down onto the sofa as I continued to watch him, sighing extremely quietly, I was only young, seven years old but I still knew that something was drastically wrong. Mum had entered the living room from the kitchen, the smell of turkey wafted in as she entered, attacking my nasal cavities, my stomach twisting with hunger just at the smell of it. She looked at my dad with a look that I have never forgotten. It wasn’t quite horror on her face, it was something less a harsh realization. “Steve, you were at the doctors eight hours ago, where the hell have you been.” She paused for a moment as the smell of drink and smoke hit her. “Have you been drinking?” She folded her arms, the way that I had seen a number of times before, this was about as angry as she got. She never lost her temper not really, “Yes I have been drinking and do you know why?” My father slurred, raising himself up to his feet, swaying backwards and forwards pointing an intimidating finger at my mother, my attention was fully focused on him now, incase he did something, incase he hit her. “No Steve, not here, not in front of the kids. It’s Christmas. You can’t ruin it for them.” My mums voice was desperate, pleading with him, had I not been watching the events unfold, I would have imagined her to be on her knees as she spoke. “Why not? They’re going to have to live with it. Kids you should know your father is going to die,” I remember the words being shouted, they still echo around my head at times, the nights when I don’t sleep, lying in bed with a cold sweat. I wanted to cry, but the shock was far too much at the time. I was only young, the concept of death something new and scary to me. Unable to comprehend the idea that one day, someone just wouldn’t be there anymore, little more than a memory on the world’s rich tapestry. “I have cancer Sarah…they’ve given me five months.” II Five months, it’s not a long time. The world seemed to spin that much quicker back then. James had begun to rebel even further; he had not handled the news of our father’s illness very well at all. Mum didn’t seem to react at first, dismissing it as something that only happened in the movies, that she would wake up one morning and everything would be fine. As the weeks progressed though, dad grew weaker and weaker, he lost his hair during the chemotherapy, he lost weight drastically, his face almost skeletal, and as much as I hate to say it towards the end he wasn’t my father anymore. Not the one I like to remember. And me? Well I was the man of the house now, ten years old and the man of the house, I took care of James where possible and tried my best with mum, giving her cuddles when I saw her crying, making cups of tea for her, it was the little things, just trying to ease the pain for her. It eased my own pain too; I had to be doing something, to stop my mind wondering to the dreadful inevitability that one morning I could wake up and for him to be dead. I cried a lot during those five months, mostly when I was alone. I guess I didn’t want anyone to see, even though they would understand totally. It just wasn’t the thing to do. I had tried to keep the emotions in check, letting them build and fester deep within me. I suppose in retrospect it’s not surprising I turned out this way. A man with the self-confidence of a lentil, who refused to let people close to him, for fear that one day he will lose them. May 5th a day which rings in my mind forever. Father had taken a turn for the worse and had been in hospital for the past two weeks; it was the final run in. The doctors had said that he could stay at home, so he was in comfortable surroundings, a home atmosphere for the last few days, he had refused however, not wanting to put us through the horror that would be finding his body. We were all sat in the family room; a white blind shielded us from the spring sunshine outside, the warmth being blocked out, the room feeling eerily cold. Mum was sat huddled in a corner her hands wrapped around a cup of tea, clasping at it frantically, literally something to hold on to. She was a far cry from the woman that I used to know, her vibrant life had been drained out of her, the stress and emotion becoming too much. She was pale now, her skin had become dull and lifeless, and her hair seemed to have become a coarse shade of gray as well. James had not spoken for about a week, a constant look of anger on his face. Then there was me, sat silently, my fingers intertwining with each other. It was a family divided, through events, which we had no possible way of coping with, no real answers, no real hope. The door opened slowly, I remember my heart sinking immediately, with just a simple open of a door. It wasn’t so much it opening as the speed. I can almost see the doctor on the other side sighing deeply and taking a deep intake of breath to prepare himself to deliver the news. He stepped into the room, a middle aged gentleman with gray hair beginning to protrude through his once golden locks. He closed the door silently behind him, holding a clipboard tightly against his chest. “I’m sorry.” He continued to talk, explaining what had happened in depth, my brain began to try and understand it. Everything seemed to stop, for a moment the world stood still. I lurched over and vomited over the floor, my stomach twisted and contorted as he spoke. Mum broke down in floods of tears, in years to come she would say that in someway it was a relief, that his suffering was finally over and the grieving could finally begin. It was at that point for the first time in my life I asked. Why? What the hell is the point if it all ends in death? Ten years old and doubting my existence already. It didn’t really bode well for me did it? I had already begun to turn my back on the world that would prove to hurt me and as James ran over to mum and hugged her tightly, his eyes streaming tears, I simply formed a fist and clenched it tightly. III “Earth to earth, ashes to ashes, dust to dust.” The voice of the reverend spoke over the sniffles that were coming from the bodies around the grave. I stood in a plush black suit, my head lowered as I stared down into the grave, the coffin resting six feet under ground. I glanced over to the headstone, which read Sharon Edwards, beloved mother who will be missed forever. It had been eight years since the death of my father and things had been progressing along, as life invariably does. But life always throws you a curve ball when you least expect it, just when things seem to be getting back to normal something happened once again. “Ever feel like life is out to get you John?” The voice interrupted my thoughts as I turned my head to face my brother James. His teenage years had straightened him out so much, he was vying for a place in college even though he was only seventeen, and everything was going right for him. His face now though was a face of distain, totally gutted. In essence all they had was each other. I bowed my head again slightly; did I ever feel like life was out to get me? How did I explain t him that every single day I doubted my existence, doubted the point of it all how everything was a waste of time, how everything led to horrible and tragic pain. “Every single day James, every single day.” After that day I swore to myself that I would be alone forever. The pain of someone leaving me had become too much. Mum’s death had left me and James orphans, old enough to take care of ourselves, but still alone all the same. The drunk driver that hit her had been given a paltry four years in jail. That son of a bitch had better pray that I never meet him; I would kill him plain and simple.. I couldn’t deal with that pain again. As the first shovel of dirt was tossed into her grave a single solitary tear fell from my eyes and landed on the soil below, quickly being consumed, never to be seen again. IV The train journey had been long and arduous; the child sat opposite me had decided that a four-hour journey would be broken up best with bouts of screaming. The sun had been beaming through the window for the entire journey, I could already feel the side of my face burning, it always happened. I stretched my arms out and groaned loudly, my back clicked readily as I did so, causing me to wince slightly, before reclining once again into my seat. I was still baring the injuries that had been inflicted on me during my bout with Weed and Easton, a number of stitches in my face and my back was shot. Medically I probably shouldn’t be traveling, let alone competing, but this was my last chance, there was no-way I was going to let it slip, regardless of the pain barrier I would have to go through, I knew that the match was going to hurt and drain me. I had told Louise to stay at home for this one, I didn’t want her to be there should we lose, the trip to California would certainly have been more entertaining if she was here though. It felt as though my entire life had been leading up to this moment, as always it was going down to the wire. There were no second chances this time; there were no time for mistakes. I realized that the child opposite me was staring at me, following his eyes gaze I notice he was looking at my arms, the scars, which covered them. He was only young; he had probably not seen anything of the sort before, yet I was not in the mood for the questions, which I could already see, forming on his lips. I hissed at him, his startled face twisted and contorted before he started screaming again, he had been doing it the entire journey, no one would think anything of it. Journeys like this always seemed to take forever, when you are waiting for something it takes an eternity to come. “The next stop is central station San Francisco and we will be arriving in a few minutes, will all passengers departing, please make sure to take all luggage with them.” I sighed deeply and rose to my feet, pulling my kit bag out of the overhead compartment and made my way to the door. It was then that the thought entered my mind, the thought to just stay on the train and not show up. Who would notice if I wasn’t there? Would anybody care at all? The thought of failing yet another of life’s trails and tribulations, of facing utmost disappointment, to have something handed to you, before it is snatched away in an instant, giving you a taste of the world you long for. I turned my attention once more to the crying child, my mind no longer thinking thoughts of annoyance or hatred, but it began to wonder. I didn’t know anything about this child, his life experiences, his whole life was ahead of him and the struggles that he would have to face would be hard on him, but he wouldn’t give up. At the end of the day it’s all about survival. I’ve been through much worse than losing a job and survived. I understand why I doubt myself, why I feel that there are no options left, but if I lose at Overkill then I’ve simply lost a job; there are things a lot more precious in this life. The train pulled into the station; the creaking of breaks grinding against the wheels, pulling the train to a slow and comfortable stop. Taking one last look back at the crying child, who was now being comforted by his mother I smiled to the world in general and nodded. As I stepped off the train, the midday sun hit my pale skin, warming my softly causing a type of aura about me. I reached into my pocket and pulled out my sunglasses, sliding them onto the tip of my nose. ”Right.” I said to no one in particular as I took a solid stride forwards, flinging my bag over my shoulder. ”Lets go get me a contract.” Fin.
