By Matt Mills Tyler Rayne has had a controversial role in PRIME since his debut almost a year ago, (inciting the infamous “Penis Reference” scandal). His quick wit and penchant for cursing have made him both a hit with fans and an absolute nightmare for the FCC. During his tumultuous tenure here in PRIME, Rayne has peppered his appearances with gratuitous female nudity, copious amounts of unadulterated bloodshed, and more alcohol than a Kennedy family reunion. This most unpredictable of PRIMEates has backed his antics, however, with solid in-ring performances. He is the current 5-Star Champion and shocked the wrestling world a few short months ago with his ultimate victory in the Dual Halo. He’s been toe-to-toe with some of PRIME’s true greats, including Hoyt Williams, Tony Gamble, and Dusk. But all of the antics and accolades have been overshadowed recently by the most talked about act in recent PRIME history. In an unexpected, and what many are calling an uncalled for, attack, Tyler Rayne ran down Wade Elliott with his car. Just as the Southern Sparkplug was arriving to the Bradley Center last week for ReVolution 160, a car driven by the 5-Star Champion slammed into the back of Elliott’s truck and a vicious beating ensued. (The full video can be seen here.) While Wade Elliott did recover enough to get some retribution before night’s end (see video here) many are asking if Rayne’s initial attack went too far. Some, like former Universal Champion Lindsay Troy, made their feelings known at ReV 160 (see video here). Most of the locker room has kept quiet on the situation, preferring to focus on their match-ups for UltraViolence, though there was one crown-wearing communist who had this to say. "Rayne laying the HAMMER down! Beatdown city, baby. Holla at ya boy." It is possible, though unconfirmed, that Devin Shakur was inebriated at the time of comment. Again, there is a short lull before the page redirects, this time opening a new video window. ********** The room is well lit and sparsely furnished. There is a generic wood desk at the forefront of the camera. It is accompanied by an equally generic black office chair. The sound of his boots upon the floor echoes in an otherwise silent and empty room. He approaches from behind the desk, dressed, as usual, in a pair of jeans and a black t-shirt. He pulls the chair from the desk and takes a seat, leaning back so as to prop his boots up on the desk. Tyler Rayne adjusts his ever-present jacket to a comforting position before turning his attention to the camera. “Word on the street is that PRIME wants an explanation. The fans. The employees. Particularly, my employers. Everyone wants to know what I was thinking. What in the world would possess me to run a car into Wade’s truck? The answers, my friends, are out there. Those who know me well should not be surprised. Those who know Wade well should not be surprised. And yet, here we are… and everyone seems surprised. Seems to think I owe an explanation. That… will come in time. For now, however, I’d like to start this off with a little story. The story of the Indian Boy and the Snake.” The 5-Star Champion snaps his fingers, causing an immediate haze to blur the view. His own visage begins to waver, as if he is nothing more than a mirage. An illusion created by the shimmering heat of a blazing highway. The waves and colors begin to blend, melding into an incomprehensible kaleidoscope. As the blurred and shimmering images begin to twist and spin, they also clear, sifting and settling into a new image. An image of great, sweeping plains. Huts and wigwams set about the plains, creating a village. And there, just on the outskirts of the village, a young Native American boy setting out on a great adventure, eyes steeled against the monolithic mountain looming in the distance. “Long ago, in a Native American village in the North, it was tradition for young boys to set upon a vision quest at the age of thirteen. The boys would be sent into the wild. Alone with nothing but their own wits. In this particular village, it was tradition that each boy would set out to climb the great mountain in the East. At the top of the mountain, he would find his vision, and thus, become a man.” The young Native American boy pushes his way through the brush. He pauses to take a final look back at his village. Perhaps the last he’ll ever see of it. With a heavy sigh, he marches forward, out of the brush and into a deep valley of brown grass. “This boy was no different from those who had come before him. Or those that would come after. His task was the same. His resolve was the same. He marched for two days, undeterred. Sleeping in piles of leaves and grass. Building small fires to keep himself from freezing at night. The days, while harsh, were uneventful. Until, on the third day, the boy stumbled upon a snake.” The boy wields a thick tree branch in his right hand, the end sharpened to a point, forming something of a makeshift spear. The end of that spear is pointed down directly at the coiled, writhing form of a rattlesnake rising cautiously from the grass. “Please, the snake said, I mean you no harm.” “I do not trust you, the boy said. You are a snake. You will bite me.” “No, the snake replied. I am old and dying. I barely have the strength to move along the grass any more. I could never fight against one such as yourself. You come from the village, do you not?” “Yes, the boy said shortly.” “And you are on your vision quest, the snake stated. Are you not?” “Yes, answered the boy again.” “Then I have a request, said the snake.” “Speak quickly, the boy replied. I know that you snakes are both liars and deceivers. I must get on with my journey.” “Of course, said the snake. See, I am old and frail. In my youth, I used to climb to the top of the mountain and watch the sunset. It is my only wish to see the sunset from the top of the mountain one more time before I die. However, I am unable to make the climb on my own. I would ask, young boy, that you take me up the mountain with you. Help me fulfill my dying wish.” “How am I to trust you? the boy asked. You are a snake. A fiend.” “I am old, the snake answered. I have only this one last request before I die. Take me with you, and I can help you upon your journey.” The boy hesitated just a moment, pondering upon the snake’s request. If the snake could be trusted, which was doubtful, it would certainly be handy to have such an ally at his side. And the snake did look frail… “I will help you, the boy finally conceded. If you agree to help me as well.” And thus an agreement was made. The boy cautiously picked up the snake, still unsure of its trustworthiness, and placed the snake around his shoulders. “The snake rested there peacefully for the rest of the day. It was another three days march to the base of the mountain. The boy ran into very little trouble, as many animals made it habit to avoid the proximity of snakes. The snake lived up to his word, showing the boy secret paths and hidden accesses within the mountain that made his climb much easier. It took them four tiring days to scale the mountain, but with the snake’s wits and the boy’s youthful energy, the ascent was much more effortless than any the village had heard tale of before.” At the top of the mountain, the boy warmed his hands over a small fire. The snake is curled in the dirt some distance away, eyes glued to the blazing orange sky and the heavy lull of a setting sun. With a satisfied smile, the snake closed his eyes and fell asleep, his dying wish fulfilled. “That very night, the boy had his vision. The next morning, the boy animatedly explained his vision to the snake, who listened patiently, but then suggested they begin their descent. Going down the mountain was easier than coming up. A full week later, the boy and the snake returned to the exact spot where they had met.” “Here you are, the boy said when they arrived at the spot. Back home.” “Thank you, said the snake. You have proven to be a great friend and a generous human being.” “You are welcome, said the boy. And you, too, have been a good friend. I will tell great stories to my tribe of your courage and wisdom.” The boy set the snake upon the ground once again. The exact same spot in which he was found. The snake curled his serpentine body back around, perhaps to say a final goodbye to the young boy. Instead, without warning or hesitation, the snake lunged forward, sinking his venomous fangs deep into the boy’s chest. The boy swatted the snake away, staggering back. He clutched at the bleeding holes in his chest. He fell to a knee, eyes glazing white. And as the poison takes its hold on the boy, so does it take hold of this scene. The edges of the plains begin to whiten. The image of the snake begins to blur. Everything is fading. “Why? the boy asked. Why did you bite me after all we’ve been through?” The boy sucks his last breath. The screen turns white. And as he dies, the boy hears the snake’s unforgiving answer. “You knew what I was when you picked me up.” The bright, white screen begins to darken. The image comes into focus once again. Tyler Rayne standing behind the desk, now topless. His chiseled, ravaged body on display for all to see. He spreads his arms to the side, looking down at the multitude of scars that mar his torso. “Wade Elliott, let me introduce you to a friend of mine. Tyler Mother Fuckin’ Rayne. More than a dozen Hardcore championships to his credit. At least three World Tag Team Championships. Four World Heavyweight Title runs. The current PRIME 5-Star Champion. The 2008 PRIME Dual Halo Winner. The baddest, meanest, most dangerous cunt you will ever step foot in the ring with. I haven’t just beaten my opponents, Wade. I’ve retired them. I have ended more careers than you’ve held championships. I have sold out more arenas than you’ve downed bottles of Jack. I have a reputation, Wade. A reputation for being brutal. Violent. Merciless. The PRIME officials handed me a contract because they knew who I was. Knew what I was capable of.” The 5-Star Champion runs a finger over one of the larger, nastier looking scars across his chest. His eyes fall to different spots of his body, memory reliving the pain and agony that brought this creature to life. “I’ve never tried to hide what I am, Wade. I’ve never made a single attempt to be something I’m not. All you have to do is look, Wade, cast your eyes upon me… and you will know everything you ever need. What I’ve done. Where I’ve been. What tortures I survived to get there. Everything you need to know is right. Fucking. Here. Unmistakable. Undeniable.” His palms fall flat against the desk. His eyes glower at the camera, burning with an intense hostility. His jaw set in grim determination. His resolve unwavering. “You knew what I was when you picked this fight, Country. Don’t act so surprised that I broke out the venom. You asked. You received. What happens at UltraViolence is not on my hands. It’s on yours, kid. Yours and yours alone.” The screen shifts to static, then settles again. The camera is dying. Another jaunt of static, and the screen fades to black. The camera is dead. But as it dies, we hear the snake’s unforgiving answer. “You knew what I was when you picked me up.” <end feed>
