Inside the Shallow Bay

7 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/7/04 Date: 10/7/04------------------------------------------------------- He stared out the window, as if he was simply an owner looking within a glass bowl. Looking at his prized gold fish as they swam in different directions. All perfect in their own little way, yet still different. Still real. As if the imperfections somehow became perfections in this, the beautiful and cruel world we live in. New York City was busy hustling and bustling on this early morning, with people trying to make their way to whatever locale they were aiming at getting to. People bundled up to brave the cold harsh mistress that was the end of autumn. Some wore earthly tones, showing that they were more level than most. Others wore extravagant colors, showing that they were less rational and spoke more with their hearts. The sky was overcast with grey wispy clouds, seeming to dull the day to a fault. It was sometimes hard to stop feeling the gapping hole within yourself pulsate on a day like today. The streets were full, and the snow cover was minimal. Most of the snow left upon the Earth was tainted thanks to the different chemical compounds used to break down sheets of ice. And you can not forget the stained snow thanks to the dirty nature of a large scale city. The sounds of horns and people could be heard only somewhat thanks to the glass shield protecting those inside from outside. The diner was somewhat crowded. Business people grabbing a quick bite here was a normal routine. And what is life but a series of monotone routines brought together for a greater meaning? They gulped down their coffee and hoped their prestine suit wouldn't get stained. Some younger people were just hanging out here, again part of a routine. But it was of little consequence what everyone was doing. This place was soulless. It didn't matter one way or another. That is why he stared. Stared out into the great expansive void because nobody cared. This was not a place where everyone knew your name. Those places exist only in small towns and sitcoms. And it was this very unminigated feeling of nonexistence that poured like blood through his veins. He stared, cigarette dangling off his lips. It was in need of a good flick, thanks to the burned up ash the stayed there. His hair was wild an unkempt. His face spoke of the inability to sleep. Sagging eyelids and stubble adorned his face. His hands were within the confines of his powder blue hoodie. His jeans were dirty, with a glaring ketchup stain upon the thigh of his right pant leg. His white sneakers were dirty. He felt dirty. He did not care. He hated her. It hits you. Like a bullet tearing through the soft tissues of your brain when you decided living wasn't the right thing to do anymore. It is not enough to simply get a simple side swipe. That would be too simple. Too naïve of a notion. A side swipe happens unexpectedly. Completely out of your range of vision. That makes it an uncontrollable variable. You can not avoid something you can not possibly see or expect. It is the way the world works. If you could, you'd fix all the quirky little surprises that caught you off guard in a wrong light. You would work a little harder at your job to ensure continued employment. You'd circle the date of your wedding anniversary to avoid the loud altercation you and your significant other would have as a result of your forgetfulness . It was the seventh of October. A few days after Tuesday Night Wrestling had taken over Cincinnati. There was almost a week standing between now and the third match of the Best Of Seven series between Brandon Youngblood and rival Alias. Truth be told the relationship shared by Brandon and Christoper Declan Sheffield was that of friendship outside of the squared circle. But once the lights on the camera went on, the mood changed. The two men would become good and evil, day and night, ying and yang. Chris had been there and done it all in wrestling. He was a multiple company champion, and was also a multiple company franchise wrestler. Brandon was just getting his first tastes of big time wrestling. His year was not filled with the accolades of his friend, but his achievements and abilities showed the wrestling audience that they were looking at a new power player in the industry. The first match had been a complete war, having gone over twenty five minutes. The saying goes that you hurt the ones you love, well it works both ways. Chris barely survived the match mentally thanks to wear and tear from his cross country to get to Fans Wrestling Organization and All-Star Championship Wrestling cards. Brandon might have lost via submission, but the toil taken was on the shoulders of the his opponent. The second match played off that, the physically and emotionally spent Alias being unable to defeat the seemingly fresh Youngblood. The story of the series was to be that of fatigue, and how you can overcome a young lion waiting to pounce at your place on the mountain top when you showed the slightest hint of weakness. Of coming back for impossible odds. The mindset was simple. Keep yourself focused on the upcoming match. Don't think about outside influences. Just keep your eyes firmly looking forward and your feet planted the same direction. However, again, life is never that simplistic. Whenever you seem to want something, it never comes, and the opposite rears back and clocks you upside the chin with a hard right. Such was the case for Brandon. Just before he was about to hit home plate with his girlfriend, just when it seemed his personal life had finally taken a look up, she happened. 'I didn't know that you-' She never finished. 'Shut up.' She never finished thanks to a sharp retort. Brandon paid her no mind. Her was his sister, Lindsay Youngblood. And for him, it was a very necessary response. After all, his lady was now a couple hundred miles away all alone and he was stuck here, dealing with family issues that seemed to never cease long enough for life to ever be enjoyable. 'So, let me get this straight. Because mom and dad weren't going to allow you to quit college, you ran away. At nineteen, you leave the one place you know is safe and sound and take what you have from your savings and move in with some punk you met on the internet. Now you expect ME to mend a bridge that you not only burned down, but threw away the plans to so it can no be replaced.' The angry tone mimicked the shaking of the coffee cup clasped in his right hand. After taking a hearty gulp, he took a long drag off his smoke, breathing deep the cancer causing carcinogens that addicted and killed millions on a yearly basis. 'Look, he's a good guy. You should hear him play his guitar for me. It's like...I don't know...so soulful.' Her green eyes lit up as if impassioned just thinking back to the many nights she simply sat and listened. Brandon could merely roll his eyes and let off an audible groan before smashing his cigarette into the ash trey. 'How many times has he fucked you?' The tone was brutal. Honest in ways that only the dead and those without a care for the intended audience could appreciate. However, his sister was not involved in either crowd as it pertained to this style of question. 'Excuse me, but that's none of yer damn concern.' Her tone was beginning to take up more of a child on the offensive. As if she had known she had done wrong, but did not seem to accept it before those in front of her. Brandon slammed his fist into the table. 'I JUST MADE IT MY GODDAMN CONCERN SIS!' His face was flushed in anger now, as he could feel the rage only a parent could feel at this juncture. The yelling caused a momentary pause in the movement of the diner, as people started to glance over. Brandon had obviously began making somewhat of a scene, and his demeanor in the situation was anything but rational. But this dealt with blood. With family. And paint his brains across the wall, he was just as much a puppet to those emotions as was anyone else. And with her, it took on special meaning. He looked her over, looking at the sister he hadn't seen in three years or talked to in two. She looked nothing like the little wall flower he remembered. She was beautiful back then, yes, but she was proper. She was very clean cut and had a very intellectual appearance. She wore earthy tones and dressed in uniforms her entire high school life. She was drilled and hammered with religion. The girl he remembered could not stand a speck of dust laying around. She could not tolerate laziness. That was the past though. Now, before him, was a very different woman. Her hair wasn't brunette and done up in refined fashions. It was a mass of red, black, and blonde hues. Brandon could only compare the style to that of Amy Campbell, a woman he'd seen as he made his rounds through the industry. She featured a piercing through the end of her left eyebrow and Brandon could also see that she had her tongue pierced as well. Brandon could see a tribal designed tattoo on her right forearm, and on her left wrist as well. Clothing wise, she had gone with a white and pink 3/4 jersey styled shirt with a faded Suicide Girls logo on it, a three row studded pyramid bracelet on her right wrist, a pair of black jeans held up by a studded belt with white shoes. She looked punk, or modern to some. She was still beautiful, but in a different way. She was free in all the ways most could never be. All she did was slouch back arrogantly into the both and flash a sly smile. 'Well, does it count when I don't remember? Cuz that is like the make or break.' Brandon inhaled. He exhaled. He wanted to breath fire. He wanted to scour the world and burn away all the mental anguish this situation made him feel. She could tell that he was not in the least bit happy, and she felt guilty. She simply reached into her pocket, pulling a pack of Kools and slipped one up, reaching once again into her pocket to bring out her lighter. She went to light. She was startled when Brandon slapped the cigarette out of her mouth. 'Don't even start with that stuff. It WILL kill you.' He had taken to a stern voice, but not of anger. He was more in the parent role now, trying to bring sense into her head. She went to grab the cigarette, now on the other side of the table, but Brandon grabbed it and snapped it in two. 'HEY!' Brandon simply looked her in the eyes. Another smile flashed across her face as she responded. 'You know, there were a few things I didn't know about you Brand. I didn't know you were my father and I sure as fuck didn't know that you were gonna preach like a damn hypocrite.' Brandon sighed. 'Look, Lindz, I only smoke when I have to travel 'cross the country to deal with family problems. This is easily the first pack I have EVER smoked. My woman is miles and miles away, and all I want to do is go to a damn gym and lift. So, instead of playin' this game, give me the reasoning as to why I should even get face to face with my folks?' 'Brandon, you do realize that by doing what you did, that you broke up the family? You showed me and Tommy and Candace that we didn't need to be the mirror images of perfection. We could be who we wanted to be. I saw this lifestyle, this music, and I wanted it. Without you, I would have never gone after my dreams.' Another sigh. 'Look, I am not the example you need. I went into wrestling because it was easy money according to Rashard.' He tried to fool himself. She couldn't be fooled. 'Yeah, and those Randalls action figures never existed? Like you didn't ever go to Raw whenever it was in town. Like all those tapes of Hornet and Windham were just blanks? Sorry Brand, I knew about your little secret well before anyone else.' Brandon couldn't get out of this situation. 'So, yeah, part of it was a dream. But I've fallen Lindz. I got fired from Action because I wasn't gonna sell out. Right now, my future with the Circle is in the air because of the health of Harvard. We are having some trouble making ends meet. I can't take time off to take Missy out on the vacation to Greece she has always wanted. I can barely keep the gas tank full on my Hummer and I have to deal with changing time zones everywhere.' 'But you wouldn't trade it in for the world...would you?' She knew him. Knew his desires. His dreams and his wishes. 'Honestly? No. But the life I led isn't a dreamland. And you taking drugs and screwing like a damn rabbit isn't the life either. What you need to do is break it off and go home. Maybe they can still accept you it you apologize.' She gave off a sad smirk. 'Just what I want, acceptance from them.' 'Sometimes, you have to crawl back. But trust me, living with them is better than living paycheck after paycheck.' 'I don't care. I'm not leaving Kyle. For your information, he is my soulmate. We were lucky to have the flat. But even if he lived in a box, it wouldn't matter. He's still mine Brandon. I might be depressed and I might do a little pot and shit to deal with the emptiness inside, but without him, I would be dead. Wrists cut open and the whole bit. Without him, I've got nothing.' Taking care of family, it was a binary choice. Either you are there for them, or you disappear when they need you most. Brandon understood where she was coming from, somewhat. He had been there, with no one to turn to. And even if it was the wrong thing to let this...Kyle...come along for the ride, so be it. He was background noise. His sister was what mattered. 'Alright, we don't have to tell the folks anything. You saying you two need a place to crash? I'm on the road all the time, and I got my apartment in San Antonio. I'll talk to Benny, see if he can get you a job over there. It ain't gonna be much, but it's money in your pocket. If he comes, he has to get a job. You two will be paying half the rent to live in my place, and you will not screw it completely up. No drugs in there. If I find even a damn gram of pot when I come back, you're on the street. No more drinking. No more partying. You got me Lindz? I'm bending over backwards here to make sure that you have great quality of life. I'm not even making you break off your little love bullcrap. You follow my rules, and we got no beef. That a problem?' She was almost giddy. She figured she'd be knocking on her folks door and begging for forgiveness. But Brandon, he had come through for her in her darkest moment. All she did was shake her head yes. 'Good. Now, let's get your things and get the heck on out of here.'
