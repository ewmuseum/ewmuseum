Into The Abyss: Part XXII - Under Suspicion

2 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02-09-09 He rubbed his wrists, massaging the light welts around them. Placing the handcuffs back on his belt the officer that arrested him got into the car, his cheeks rosy red and his face sullen while his partner sighed and held his hands on his hips. A stern expression met him when he looked back up at Von Kelsig. "I can only apologize on behalf of the Anaheim Police Department, Mr. Von Kelsig. Mistakes like this seldom happen but in such cases it usually involves interstate communications." "Mistake?!" Von Kelsig gasped, chuckling in disbelief, "You pulled me up in front of my boss and my work colleagues and arrested me for MURDER! That's not a mistake, that's a big fuck up." He growled. The officer raised his hands, gesturing Von Kelsig to ease on the profanities. Adjusting his hat he glanced to the technician's truck they had emerged from moments before where Lisa Tyler and a legal representative of PRIME vehemently ordered the playback of the Elite Title match from Night One of Colossus, complete with time stamp. Once convinced, they left the truck while the officer now standing before Von Kelsig radioed back to the station. Word came back to him that the call from Memphis P.D. instructed the Anaheim force to request Von Kelsig fly back to Tennessee for questioning regarding the murders of Gloria and Larry DeLancy. To say he was pissed off would be an understatement. "Again, I'm sorry for the mix up. Rest assured PRIME and yourself will receive a full apology from our department." The officer continued. Von Kelsig remained stone-faced. "How is sorry going to cut it? I've got a reputation to uphold, and some bumbling idiot at a desk gets me humiliated at work in front of my peers, and not only that it's to tell me that my ex-girlfriend is dead! You have any idea how messed up that is?" He mewled. The officer sighed and backed up towards his patrol car, holding his hands up and shooting a sympathetic look at Von Kelsig. "Sir, I'm sorry. If it means anything heads will roll back at the station, but again all we can do is apologize. I'm sure the Memphis department will echo our sentiment when you fly out to meet them." said the officer, plopping into the driver's seat while Von Kelsig felt his eyes well up, "Sorry for your loss." he added before the car drove off into the night. Turning away from the policemen Von Kelsig kicked at the dirt and cussed under his breath. What the hell happened? He thought to himself, dragging his feet across the blacktop back to his rental car. Memphis called out to him, and damn it if he didn't have to answer. Getting into his car he made his way to the nearest airport, following the trail of rotten luck right back to its source... ---- Several hours later The sun crept over the horizon, throwing light through the frosted glass windows and over the body bags lying on the slabs. The morgue attendant, clad in scrubs, stood opposite Von Kelsig who was visibly tense, stiff as a statue. By his side, two men dressed smartly in black pinstripe and royal blue suits respectively. One wore a fedora and chewed on a toothpick, while the other wore bifocals and compulsively ran a hand through his slicked back hair, even though the wax held it firmly in place. The four of them stood in silence as the morgue attendant drew back the zippers on the body bags, looking up to see Von Kelsig's expression turn sour as the plastic fell away to reveal a grotesquely bludgeoned face in one and a gaunt ghoulish face contorted in a scream in the other. The giant whimpered at the sight of it, unsure of exactly what he was looking at. "That's her. That's Gloria." He stammered, recognizing her from her broken button nose and her delicate jawline. A few patches of short brown hair left on the scalp were stark white at the roots, confirming it was her. "You're sure?" asked the detective in the fedora, holding a handkerchief over his nose. "Yes. Definitely, it's her." "What about the other one?" the other detective asked, pushing his spectacles up the bridge of his nose. "I can't be sure. I don't remember what he looked like...it's too-..." Von Kelsig paused to draw breath. "It's OK. If you can't identify him the dental records should." The detective said, discarding his toothpick on the table. Frustrated, the morgue attendant picked it up and tossed it in a nearby bin before zipping the bags back up and moving them onto individual gurneys. The detectives escorted Von Kelsig out of the morgue and down the busy corridors of the hospital, and once they were outside all three breathed in the fresh air, a welcome change from the fetor of death back in the morgue. "Detective Greco and I would like to ask you a few questions down at the station, get you to fill out a few forms Mr. Von Kelsig." said the man in the black pinstripe suit, removing his hat to dust it off before placing it back on his balding head. "Sure..." Von Kelsig sighed. They made their way to the Buick parked across the street and drove off back to the station. Von Kelsig pulled his legs in and held his head at an angle to the low roof, trying to get comfortable in the confined space of the car. The whole way there he gazed out of the window at all the people on the street. Once upon a time he would have given anything to be faceless and nameless like them, blending into the crowd instead of sticking out like a giant sore thumb. The more he thought about it the easier it seemed to be to accept that he couldn't have a life like theirs. The untimely and horrendous deaths of Gloria and Larry seemed testament to just that. ---- "I'll get the papers Barnes, go on and take him to Room #8, I'll be there in a sec." Greco said as they walked through the front doors of Union Station. "Bring three coffees with you, Greco." Barnes replied. All at once heads turned in awe at the arrival of the seven foot behemoth being led in by the two detectives. Ignoring their stares, Von Kelsig studied the tiled floor as Greco led him past the front desk and through a door into a long corridor filled with numbered doors down one side. Making their way to Room #8, Detective Barnes ushered Von Kelsig inside and closed the door behind him. The room was basic, painted beige and smelling of stale oak. Directly opposite the door a two-way mirror hung over a steel table bolted to the ground, around which four chairs waited to be occupied. Von Kelsig sat directly beneath the mirror opposite Barnes, who removed his hat and jacket and hung them over the backrest. "Now we're just going to ask you a few questions to get a better understanding of the situation. You're not a suspect, you're not being investigated...we just want any information that might help us find out what happened." Barnes assured him, a sympathetic smile on his face. "I understand. I mean, I don't know how much help I can be but..." Von Kelsig trailed off as the door opened and Detective Greco entered, juggling three coffees in one hand, papers and files in the other and a tape recorder under his arm. Kicking the door shut he approached the table and lay everything down. "First of all can I ask you to fill out these forms, standard procedure. Compliance and confidentiality and so on and so forth..." said Greco, fixing his hair again before placing a coffee in front of Barnes and Von Kelsig. Finally he sat the tape recorder in the middle of the table and relaxed in his chair. Von Kelsig took a few moments to skim and sign the papers, stacking them and returning them to Detective Greco. Taking a sip of his coffee he sighed again, trying desperately to shake off the last few fibres of emotion that bound him to the cadaver he had just identified in the morgue. Pressing down the record and play buttons on the recorder, Barnes cleared his throat and leaned on the table, while Greco sat up straight and glanced at the two-way mirror. "Forensics report the time of death as being some time on the fifteenth or sixteenth of this month. Can you tell us where you were on the days in question?" "I was at Fenway Park, in Boston. Both nights. On the fifteenth I was wrestling in a match. The next night I was backstage for the whole night watching the show. Any staff member in PRIME will confirm-..." Von Kelsig paused as Barnes raised a hand. "That's fine. I just wanted to confirm your story with previous testimonies." Von Kelsig thought back to the cops in Anaheim, and realized no one had made mention of the mistake that landed him in cuffs. A soft whimper was all that came of a sudden desire to ask about it, stopped abruptly by the rolling cassette in front of him. "Now, tell me about your relationship with Miss Carmichael." Barnes asked, while Greco studied the giant across the table. Von Kelsig scratched his beard and stared vacantly at the table, his eyes shimmering in the overhead light which, he noticed, was caged behind steel mesh. A slow realization of his surroundings drowned out the sorrow, replacing it with a nervousness that clung to his spine. "I met her a few years ago when I lived in Detroit. I was running my own business and she worked for an associate." He used the term very loosely, after all his suffering could be 'associated' with Reuben Blanco, "During a period of transaction we met and kind of just fell for each other. We moved away from Detroit and travelled around for a couple of years before settling in Memphis. I mean things were great between us, we loved each other so much. Aside from differing opinions on my going back to wrestling everything was great. It was love." He shrugged. "How did she come to meet Mr. DeLancy? Why would he have been with her in your house at the time of their murder?" Barnes enquired, while Detective Greco continually glanced back and forth between the two men taking mental notes. "When we moved to Tennessee we didn't have work, we were living off of my savings. I opted to return to PRIME and Gloria looked for work in town. She bartended part time at a few places and got a job as a receptionist at a salon owned by DeLancy." "So he was her boss?" "Yeah." Barnes folded his arms and leaned in closer. He cocked his head at the intimidating presence before him, who for all his imposing features held a look of vulnerability on his face. "So that's how they met. Were they involved in a personal capacity? Out of work?" He probed. "I guess so. I'm on the road with PRIME a lot so she probably spent most of her time with friends from work." "Do you know if Miss Carmichael or Mr. DeLancy had any known enemies, anybody that might have wished harm on them?" Came the next question. Von Kelsig nearly smiled at the thought of it, and took a breath before answering. "No, nobody at all I could think of. I was never at home long enough to really get to know anyone in Gloria's circle. Barnes retrieved the files and picked out a single page. Von Kelsig could only make out typed letters and a section that looked handwritten. "I have here a sworn statement from a Ms. Donna Lynn Jamieson, a colleague and acquaintance of Miss Carmichael. Do you know her?" "I met her once, yeah." Von Kelsig replied, a lump forming in his throat. The pleasantries were slipping in favour of protocol. "According to Ms. Jamieson, Gloria and Larry became close, and I quote, 'They were spending a lot of time with each other. Gloria started as a receptionist but it was Larry's idea to train her up to become a hairdresser. He enrolled her in a college course and also took her on nights out. I met her boyfriend once, he seemed nice. I don't think he had a problem with them hanging out so much, although part of me thought he didn't know because he is a wrestler so he's away from home a lot of the time.'" Barnes read from the statement. Von Kelsig looked Barnes in the eye, trying to place all his nerves into one physical form and intimidate it. Barnes returned the glare, while Greco had some more coffee. "Is it possible to assume that perhaps your partner was having an affair with Mr. DeLancy before their death?" Anger and frustration flashed in his eyes, caught in a twitch of his eyelid that Detective Barnes noticed immediately. Even Greco thought there was something suspicious about the reaction. A soft squeaking emanating from the cassette in the recorder prompted Von Kelsig to glance down before meeting Barnes's stare once again. "Yeah...it is." Von Kelsig groaned. "Now..." Barnes continued, trying to hone in on the vital data Von Kelsig was sharing, "did you have any idea that something might have been going on?" Von Kelsig shuffled his feet beneath the table. His palms began to sweat and he sneakily stroked his beard in an attempt to dry them off. Clasping them again he bit his lip and shook his head. "Gloria was really upset about my work schedule. I never thought she'd be driven into another man's arms but I guess that's just what happened." Barnes sat back in his chair, his arms still folded. Greco removed his glasses and cleaned the lenses, while his partner flitted through the files. "So she was having an affair?" Von Kelsig shrugged. "How did you know?" Von Kelsig felt his stomach drop as he relived the moment in his head, knowing that yet again he'd have to confront it head-on. "I...walked in on them one night. I wanted to surprise Gloria before I had to prepare for the next show. I had no idea they were in the house together." "When was this?" Barnes asked, appearing laid back. His sudden relaxation prompted Von Kelsig to question his motives now. He had a sinking feeling he wasn't just being questioned any more. This stank of an interrogation. "The twelfth. I remember because I left for Boston again the next day to get ready for the pay-per-view show. That was the last I saw of either of them." He replied. Von Kelsig finished his coffee and licked his lips. His stomach gurgled from hunger but he made no complaints. "What happened when you found out?" Barnes queried, feeling as though a lead was about to jump right out at him. "I heard them before I saw them. Like I said I wanted to surprise her so I crept up to the bedroom – I didn't arrive at the house until around midnight – and I heard them...together. I didn't know what to do so I went back downstairs and went to the kitchen. About twenty minutes later they both came downstairs and he left. She walked right into the kitchen and got a hell of a shock..." Von Kelsig said, involuntarily chuckling at the thought of the look on Gloria's face when she found him in the house. Barnes's tone was much flatter now, a serious glare in his eyes. "How did you react? Were you angry" He chose his words carefully. "Sure I was. Livid. I asked her what the hell was going on, and she was so shocked at seeing me she just erupted...blaming me for not caring and trying to throw all her guilt onto me. She started hitting me and crying and going nuts and I..." "Did you hurt her?" Barnes butted in. "No," Von Kelsig said softly, blinking the welling tears in his eyes, "I never laid a finger on her. I loved her too much." His body grew hot and uncomfortable, like he wanted to shed his own skin and get away from himself. He thanked God his beard was thick enough to hide his rosy cheeks, blemished with guilt. "So what did you do?" "I flew back out to Boston that night. This all happened on the thirteenth you see...but after what happened I just shut down mentally, you know? I got a cab, went to the airport and flew back to Boston for the show. Didn't look back once. I don't even remember if I locked my door or not." Now he knew he was lying. Strangely though, he felt a new sense of power looming in his gut. The Detectives gave no indication they knew he was lying, but then again neither did he. The body in the morgue was so horribly ravaged he never even recognized the cut over her eyebrow or the abrasion across her face as his own doing. Whatever cruel twist of fate had befallen them, it had wiped the slate clean for him. Thinking back to the ride over to the station and all the people on the streets, he knew then that his purpose was never to settle down. He was to be a road warrior for life. And then Barnes narrowed his eyes at him. "Sir, were you involved in any way at all with the murders of Gloria Carmichael and Larry DeLancy?" Barnes asked, pushing the tape recorder an inch or so closer to Von Kelsig and holding his gaze over the giant. Being called 'Sir' turned the behemoth's stomach. "I already told you I was in another state, thousands of miles away. How could I have been involved? What are you implying, Detective?" Von Kelsig asked now, his tone firm with a hint of anger. "Nothing is being implied. We simply follow the facts. Now-..." "No, hold on a second here," Von Kelsig growled, "I told you all I know and I don't appreciate this line of questioning. My whereabouts on the night of their deaths can be proven by millions of people worldwide with access to a television. Unless you were planning on charging me with something we're done here." He added, pointing a finger in Barnes's face. Raising his hands, Barnes pushed away from the table and got up. Greco waited a moment before getting up, while Von Kelsig slid his chair back with a painful squeak and walked towards the door. Calmly, the two Detectives approached the door, Greco grabbing the handle while Barnes fished something out of the breast pocket of his shirt. Held between his index and middle fingers, a card was extended to Von Kelsig. "Take this. It's our card. We'll be handling this case so if you remember anything else or want an update on the case feel free to contact us at any of these numbers. Thanks for your co-operation, sir. You are free to leave." Von Kelsig snatched the card out of Barnes's hand and glanced at the details before pocketing it. Grumbling under his breath, he waited for Greco to open the door before skulking off down the corridor and out of the station. The two detectives watched him leave, a wry grin on the face of Barnes, while Greco continued to observe Von Kelsig's gait as he disappeared from view. "What do you think?" asked Barnes, nudging Greco in the side. "I don't know...his story checks out but I got this feeling..." his partner replied. "I know. I'm beginning to wonder if that mistake in Anaheim was such a mistake after all..." "There's nothing else to go on. He's the only tie to both deaths, but he wasn't there. No DNA, no fingerprints...nothing. Both bodies were clean. It's like-..." "Like a professional hit." Barnes growled, retrieving another toothpick from his pocket and proceeding to chew on it thoughtfully. With that they retreated to their office, a fresh theory in their heads while Von Kelsig disappeared into the streets amongst a crowd that he would never fit in with. Glancing back at the station his heart pounded in his chest. ...What happened to them.........what the hell is going on...
