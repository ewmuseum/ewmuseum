Date: 9/16/09 Benjamin Johnson had a luxury that most men and women don't have. He enjoyed his work. Even on nights like tonight, when he'd gone twenty minutes with "Rotten" Rick Reynolds in a Texas Bullrope match in front of a packed house of 450 in a National Guard Armory near Austin, Texas. Looking at himself in the bathroom mirror (public bathroom, more specifically. Not much resembling a locker room bathroom in the armory), he saw a familiar picture looking back at him. The Lone Star was marred with wounds of all shapes and sizes. His wrists are rope burned, bruises are all over his arms, chest, and shoulders, and dried blood clings to a cut across his forehead. He wasn't as handsome as he'd once been. Fifteen years ago, he'd entered the business, young and unscarred. Now, he was an older and wiser man, beaten down physically, mentally, and emotionally by his chosen profession. When he thought about it, though, he was luckier than most. He never had worries about getting his paychecks. His promoters were reputable, and the checks always cleared. Benjamin had avoided most major injuries, though he had sustained a broken leg and lots of stitches over the course of the years. The only thing he really had against his career was that it had never risen above this level. Sure, he'd worked out for bigger companies. He'd had a tryout or two that hadn't ever amounted to anything. Really, he'd long since accepted the fact that he'd never rise above his station in life. That, though, didn't stop him from sending out tapes every couple of months. By his reckoning, every man needed a dream. Dreams kept you young, after all. With an expert eye, Benjamin surveyed the cut on his head. It had stopped bleeding, and didn't seem to be that deep. Definitely didn't need stitches. Probably wouldn't even need a butterfly bandage. Not bad at all. Especially considering he'd been in Bullrope matches that he hadn't even been able to walk away from, let alone walk away with minimal damage. With no shower for him to go to, he considered the possibility of covering himself with Axe body spray in an effort not to kill people with his smell before he could get home and clean himself off. Before he could contribute to the destruction of the ozone layer, though, he heard his phone ring. Out of habit, he reached for it where he always kept it, in the front pocket of his gear bag. Expecting to see his home phone number appear, he was startled to see a number that he didn't recognize at all. Did he even dare hope? Flicking the phone open, he held it to his ear, and said simply "Hello, Benjamin Johnson speaking." A fifteen year career flashed through his mind at the mention of the word "PRIME." For the next thirty seconds, all was silent. After that span of time came a most unusual sight: a 6'5" chiseled specimen of a man sinking to his knees, tears streaming down his cheeks. A toilet flushed soon after, and out from a nearby stall stepped... Rick Reynolds. He looked similarly worse for wear, but still sported a goofy grin. "Heya, Benji," he smirked, "we sure gave them one Hell of a--" He saw the man -- his opponent, his friend -- crying, and quieted down as Benjamin managed to get out an "I'll be there tomorrow" before clicking the phone off. "Shit, Benji," Johnson heard, as if not even in his own body, "are you okay? Something happen?" It took several more moments for Johnson to be coherent enough to look up at the man he'd drank with after countless shows when they'd been tag team partners years back as the Texas Cowboys. "PRIME called," he managed. "They...want to hire me." "Holy fuck, man," Reynolds gasped, eyes wide. "I've got to go tell everyone!" "No, don't," Benjamin groaned. "There are fans out there..." "Shit, man, kayfabe's dead." Rick smirked at his pal. "Much as you hate to admit it. Even here in Texas, everyone's on the damned internet. They'll find out soon enough. Isn't it better to have it known here? This is your home, man. You've been in this state for fifteen years!" Benjamin thought about it for a few moments. Fifteen years ago, the thought of telling the fans ANYTHING was ludicrous. Unthinkable. But Rick was right. The wrestling world had changed, both for the better and for the worse. "Alright," he finally agreed, grudgingly. "You win. Let's go." Wiping the tears from his eyes, Benjamin pulled himself to his feet, but Rick was already out of the bathroom, bellowing in his deep voice. "HEY EVERYONE! BENJAMIN GOT THE CALL!" A group of fans on their way out of the armory stopped where they were. Ditto for a couple of the wrestlers, some of whom were signing autographs, others of whom were chatting about the evening. "He's goin' to PRIME," Reynolds continued, "and I can't think of anyone more deserving." The Lone Star stepped out of the bathroom moments later, to applause. ~~~~ Post-match phone calls to loved ones were a fact of life when you wrestled, no matter the scale. Usually, his little girl picked up the phone. Today, though, it was his son. Out of the many vociferous fans that Benjamin had, he was hard-pressed to find anyone who cheered him on more than Jacob. "Dad," the seven year old exclaimed, "how did it go tonight?" Benjamin allowed himself a tight smile. While kayfabe as a whole was dead, he drew the line at explaining that fact to his son. Growing up a huge wrestling fan himself, he remembered just how much more *enjoyment* he'd gotten out of the sport when he'd believed it was all real. "I won, but it was a tough fight," Johnson explained. "Reynolds is a tough, tough guy." "Yeah, I know," Jacob said, "I'm just glad you got revenge on him for hitting you with a chair last month." Johnson chuckled. Wrestling really was a simple thing when he thought about it. Motivations were easy: Vengeance. Justice. Desire. "Something else happened today, son. I...got the call from PRIME. I'm flying out to their headquarters tomorrow to sign the contract." Fifty miles from the armory, a young boy's eyes grew wide as saucers, and soon enough Benjamin heard his son's scream. "Dad's going to PRIME!" It was amusing, he thought to himself, just how similar his son and Rick Reynolds were in a lot of ways. Moments later, he heard the transferring of phone from son to mother. "You're not kidding about this, are you," she asked, voice equal parts chiding and excited. "When did you find out?" "Right after the match, actually," Johnson replied. "They want me to fly out tomorrow morning to sign the contract and take a bunch of promotional photos and stuff." "This...things are going to change, now," she said, unable to keep the sadness out of her voice. It was his dream, one that he'd followed tirelessly. Beyond that, he'd be able to provide for their family now in ways he couldn't even dream about beforehand. She might even be able to drop her own job if needed and spend her time at home taking care of the children. The tradeoff for all of that, of course, was that he'd be home one-tenth of the time that he was now. "They are, love, I know," Benjamin said. "I'll be home as much as I can. I know it's going to be hard...but this is everything I've ever wanted. I want to be able to send our kids to where they want to go to college, not to where we can afford for them to go to college. PRIME's one of the biggest wrestling companies on Earth. If I do well, it opens up so many other opportunities." What could she say to that? They'd spent nights imagining what would happen if he ever got The Call. How much easier it would make their lives. How much more security it would give them, especially now with the economy being what it was. But it had always been thought. Now, it was reality. "I'll be home in about an hour," Benjamin promised, "to spend the rest of the night with you and our children. My flight's at 6:00 AM." To her, the implication was clear: we'll talk about this when I get home. She sighed, softly. It probably was for the best that they have this particular talk in person. She had many worries and doubts, but she didn't want to ruin his moment with them. She couldn't imagine what must have been going through his head, to work at something for fifteen years and finally, finally achieve one's ultimate goal. "I'll see you then," she said, "and I love you." "I love you, too," Benjamin replied, and then the call was over. Of all the sacrifices a professional wrestler made for his craft, it went without saying that his family was at the top of the list. ---------------- PRIME Headquarters One day before ReVolution 204 Stepping into the huge, well appointed offices of PRIME was like going into a new world. Benjamin was a veteran of the business. He had friends who'd hit it big, so sure, he'd been backstage for shows of bigger promotions and been in awe at the machines that backed them, but never had he been quite so in awe as he was now. He'd never known quite how much went into being a wrestling superstar. At the door, he was greeted by the secretary with a hearty "Welcome aboard, Mr. Johnson," and then been sent to another part of the building to take his publicity photos. Benjamin had wrestled in traditional wrestling tights and boots for all fifteen years of his career, but never had he felt quite so...naked in the attire as he did in front of PRIME's cameras. He posed for some with his cowboy hat on, and others without it. They told him that the photos would be used for a variety of purposes: on the official website, as 8x10s to be sent out to the press, and all manner of other things. Hell, they even shot a short video of him for use in the company's matchup shots that would air on ReVolution and PPVs. There was only one thing left for him to do, and in many ways, it was the thing he was most nervous about. Benjamin had heard all the horror stories about wrestlers from the indies being given ridiculous characters once they hit the big time. At the very least, PRIME seemed willing to give him a chance to have some input. If he could just have secured a meeting with Ms. Tyler, he was convinced that the passion he could speak with would let her agree to what he wanted. Unlike many of the promoters he worked for, however, Lisa Tyler was a busy, busy woman. All he had, therefore, was an e-mail. He felt awkward as he dug into his pocket, pulling out the brand new Blackberry he'd purchased. It didn't take him long to decide that these newfangled gadgets were not meant for men with large fingers like his. After a little while, though, he managed to get the hang of it, even if he did have to correct numerous typos along the way. "Ms. Lisa Tyler As you know, much of this country is in a state of financial crisis. People are losing their jobs, having their hours cut, and seeing their savings evaporate. I've been wrestling for fifteen years, and in that time, the one thing that I've learned more than anything else is that pro wrestling is an escape for people, in both good times and bad. They tune in because they want to see action. They want to laugh with us, cry with us. I want to be myself, Benjamin Johnson. A man with a loving wife and two beautiful children who's just gotten the biggest break of his life. I want people to look at me and realize that they, too, can achieve their dreams with hard work and determination. I'm well aware that this sort of thing can turn fans against me. I'm not going to shove it down people's throats in a holier-than-thou manner. I just want to be me. Thank you for your consideration, Benjamin Johnson." With a heavy sigh, he punched the send button. Three hours later, his inbox contained the words he wanted so desperately to hear. "I like it. Let's go with it." And with that, Benjamin Johnson's final doubt had been erased. --------------------------------- Backstage at ReVolution 205 To say that his adrenaline was flying a thousand miles a minute would be a vast, vast understatement. It was fifteen minutes before he was set to do battle with Jason Cross in his ReVolution debut. His PRIME in-ring debut. The promo he'd cut on 204 had lessened his nerves somewhat. He wasn't really a promo guy by trade. Ben was at his best cutting passionate pre-or-post match interviews. But Hell, this was the big time. Promos were how you made your money. He would learn. It wasn't like he had much of a choice. PRIME or no PRIME, though, there were some traditions Benjamin Johnson was going to maintain on the eve of battle. First, he came to the arena dressed to wrestle. Like Hell was he going to be seen in a shirt and jeans like some sort of fashion model. He was a wrestler. Period. Second, he looked at a picture of his family that he kept tucked into his wallet. He kissed the picture before putting it back in the wallet, and placing the wallet back inside his duffel bag. Third was his warmup routine. Fifteen jumping jacks, fifteen situps, fifteen pushups. Just enough to get the blood flowing without tiring him out. His real warmup had been done an hour or so ago. A knock on the door took his attention just as he finished the final pushup. No matter what promotion he wrestled for, he knew what a knock meant: time to get your ass to the curtain and get ready to go. His elbowpads were adjusted quickly, and then Benjamin Johnson stepped out into the hallway, a tight look of concentration on his face as he began the walk. It had been his goal to make this entire ritual as close to what he was used to. At first, he'd despaired; after all, PRIME was a long, long away from a national guard armory in Texas. But as he approached the curtain, Johnson had come around to another way of thinking. The task itself, in fact, was pretty easy. Sure, there were thousands of people. Sure, the lights were brighter than they'd ever been. Sure, the theme song he'd used ever since he'd attained "Grizzled Indy Veteran" status, "Turn The Page" by Bob Seger, was playing a lot more loudly and clearly than he'd ever heard it. But at the end of the day, Benjamin Johnson was going out to the ring to wrestle. Something that he'd done, by his count, two thousand four hundred and seventy six times before this. After the week he'd had, this was the simple part.
