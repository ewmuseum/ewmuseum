Venice Lifestyles - S01E04 - Before Boston

May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 2009 He'd read it inside and out numerous times. He knew the number, the date and the time like it was his own birthday or another special date. He could run down it's design, the font it was printed in and the colors used without even looking at it. So then why was he looking at it non-stop? Why was he opening it and reading it like he'd never seen it before in his life? “You've been sitting there lookin' at that thing for half an hour now.” Vance said after entering the room and opening the fridge to get a beer. “What's the big deal?” “It's a very big deal Van... I h-” “Nicky hasn't been home for a pretty long time.” Interrupted Leticia who took a seat on the couch directly beside Nitz. Vance opened his beer and took a sip before turning a free desk chair around and sitting in it backwards. “Exactly.” Donnelly said, slapping the ticket against his hand. “I've looked at this plane ticket so many times. Hell, I was even the one who went to pick it up but I still can't believe in a few short days I'll be back in Boston for a visit.” “Van your chili is bubbling up. But you've gotta understand Nicky's family. He hated his brother for leaving town and the family when they needed him most and now, after his Mom got through cancer and his Dad broke his alcoholism, he was gone and hasn't come back since. They will look at him just like he looked at his brother.” Vance got up from his chair and removed the cover from the crock pot in the kitchen. He took the big wooden spoon sitting beside it and sifted through the mixture. “Haven't you talked to any of them since?” Raymes asked while grabbing a bottle of sauce from a nearby cabinet. “My sister briefly and even then it was only for a minute or two.” He shuffled in his seat as he spoke. “I basically asked how everyone was, how she was and how my friends were. Beyond that, nothing was really talked about. Although I did let her know I might be passing through the area in the coming week but she's not telling anyone else.” “Van what the hell are you putting in that stuff? I like hot but I don't wanna burn my mouth off.” The big man turned and smiled at Leticia. “Secret ingredient. If I told you, I'd have to kill you.” Raymes tipped back his beer after he spoke taking another large swig. “I haven't even spoken to Mikey. Damn, I grew up with the guy and I don't even have the decency to pick up the phone. I have become my brother.” Nitz sighed and got up from his seat and paced the room a little. “Give yourself a break Nicky. I love your family, but dysfunctional would be a pretty big understatement.” Leticia got up and grabbed her friend around the shoulders. “I'm sure they still know that you still love them.” “Who says he did in the first place?” Vance smiled and tilted the rest of his beer back as he returned to his chair. “Good point. Heh.” “You're so bad! You know you care about them.” “Yeah, but do they care about me still?” Nitz meandered towards the kitchen and grabbed his own beer from the fridge. He then made his way over to the crock pot Vance was tending to just a second before. “Touch that pot and you'll be going back to Boston in a box.” Vance didn't even have to turn around to see what Donnelly was up to. “When's this stuff gonna be ready? Reminds me of Mikey's Chowder. Haven't had a bowl of that for a long damn time.” “Chowder? You're comparing my masterpiece chili to some throw together chowder? I'm insulted.” “Vance, we grew up in New England.” Leticia pulled Nitz from the kitchen away from the crock pot of chili. “It's pretty big out there.” “If you ever tried some of Mikey's, you'd be drooling every time you thought about it. And I was asking about when the chili is ready cause I've got an early flight to catch.” Nitz stared at his watch and did the math in his head. He knew immediately that he'd be sleeping on the plane. “Thirty minutes and you'll be tasting some of the best chili you've ever had. Perfection takes time there pal.” “You certainly won't miss your flight Nicky.” Silently, he kind of wished he was. |~o~| LAX was packed. No, maybe that was a bit of an understatement. LAX was a damn zoo and Nitz wasn't doing too well mingling with the crowd. In the span of about five minutes, he had gotten up from his chair a dozen or so times to check the board. His flight was running late. Very late. He'd been in the airport for two hours. “What on earth is going on here?” He muttered under his breath. He didn't think anyone could hear him, but an innocent looking 10 year old girl overheard him. “Some guy said it's because the pilot didn't show up.” “Oh well that's just fan-fuuuuuu-...” He stopped himself, realizing that he was speaking to a child. “Fantastic.” He sat back down in his seat which was right beside the girl. “What're you going to Boston for?” The kid asked. Nitz frowned a little, unable to look at the girl in fear of showing his discomfort. “Family. Haven't seen them for a long time.” “I'm going back to my Mom's. She lives just outside of Boston.” “Who's flying with you?” “You are.” “Huh?” “My Dad dropped me off.” “You're kidding. You're flying alone?” “Sure, what's the big deal. I've done it before.” He thought for a minute about what she said. Sure, Leticia had described his family as dysfunctional. No, actually that would be an understatement according to her. But never once when he was a kid was he put in a plane by himself, made to take a cab by himself or jumped on a city bus by himself. Heck, he was hardly allowed to bike to the corner store when he was a kid without his mother watching as he glided down the sidewalk. Yet here was this little girl, sitting beside him and saying that she was flying from Los Angeles to Boston by herself. Even Nitz Donnelly has standards and he kind of drew the line at children being forced to fly alone. “This plane doesn't have assigned seats. You're sitting with me.” “And why should I trust you?” “I...” Crap, she was right. What gave him the excuse to play guardian? What made him any different, to this little girl anyways, then some sick pedophile or child kidnapper? He really didn't know how to respond. “See? This is why my Dad is sending me back to my Mom's. I'm too smart for him.” “Smart?” “Yeah.” The girl turned in her seat to explain things. “I knew when he was lying about things. I've been told I'm a rather bright person. But I could tell when he was fibbing about things like why I was getting a baby sitter or why he couldn't take me to a movie or the park. Most of the time it was because he was drinking or he was dating a lady but I still knew. I could even hear him with these women...” “Ok, maybe it's best I don't discuss this with you. I could end up in jail should someone overhear.” “True, but I'm sure you've never talked to a 10 year old about something so grown up.” “No.” “You don't know how many times I've been told that.” She said, turning to look at the flight board. There was no change in the time, but the flight status had changed. “Looks like we're getting on the plane soon and don't worry. I have pepper spray in my purse.” “Ok, then I guess you're somewhat safe.” “Yep, but the rest is up to you.” “What do you mean?” “If I'm sitting with you, you're the one I'll be spraying in the eyes.” “So why are you sitting with me then? Thought I couldn't be differentiated from the perverts and all that?” “Honestly, we've been talking for a few minutes and you haven't called me weird. People think I'm strange cause I talk like an adult.” She smiled, winning a small grin from Nitz. He also nodded. But inside he did think the girl was a bit strange. Some people twice her age could barely form a sentence, never mind have a decent conversation and an intelligent one at that. But regardless of how strange it seemed, she did make something of a silent point. His family was defined as dysfunctional, yet they both knew how to raise their kids. What they were passed the age of 18 was of their own doing, but they certainly weren't brought up bad. The nervous feeling in the pit of his stomach didn't disappear but he actually felt a tad better. Anything would be better than being 10 years old and getting tossed on a plane in Los Angeles where adults can get kidnapped and killed, not to mention children. 'Boarding for flight 376 to Boston is now beginning. Please make your way to gate 12.' People rose from their seats like robots and began their march for the gate. Nitz grabbed his bag. “What's your name kid?” “Olivia.” “Nice to meet you Olivia. I'm Nick.” “Also known as Nitz.” “How'd you know?” “My Dad used to watch wrestling all the time and he fell asleep drunk on the couch. I was practically raised by the TV as he snored on the couch.” Now he felt like hurling. “Is your mom picking you up at the airport?” “Yes.” “Good, remind me to have a quick word with her.” Not like he thought that he could actually give parenting advice, but even his standards were not as low as Olivia's parents. It certainly made him feel better about his own family, even if he still really didn't want to see them all. Especially his brother. “You could talk to her, but you might be better off talking to a wall.” “You're almost too smart for your own good.” Nitz said as he stuck out his arm for Olivia to lead the way to the gate. “I get by. I'll try not to talk your ear off for the whole flight.” “It's ok.” So much for resting on the flight before facing his family for the first time in over a year.
