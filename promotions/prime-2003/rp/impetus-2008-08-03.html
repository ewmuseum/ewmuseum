Date: August 3rd A cavalcade of surprise marched through Jason’s psyche, stretching his facial muscles as he reached into his mailbox and pulled out two white envelopes. It was seldom enough for PRIME’s Anti-Superstar to retrieve one piece of mail from the cold, grey box in the morning, but two was practically unheard of. He unceremoniously slammed the door of the cheap, aluminium mail locker sending a harsh metallic CLANK echoing through the lobby of the rundown apartment block that he called home. After turning the key in the lock, Jason turned and walked towards the exit, sliding out of a set of large, glass doors that were long overdue a clean with all the grace and elegance of a baby elephant. The sunlight beaming from the heavens provided a deceptive smokescreen for what was really quite a chilly afternoon in New York City. Jason could never quite grasp how it was possible for the sun to be shining so fiercely at such a low temperature. Of course, being Jason Natas, he hadn’t exactly put a great deal of thought into the matter, but it was something that puzzled him nonetheless. With his head bowed he focused his attentions on the two envelopes in his mitts, blindly ducking and diving through the hordes of pedestrians in an instinctive (and rather impressive) manner that only those who had lived in the Big Apple for any length of time had mastered. The first envelope wore a familiar, blue emblem that he had seen a hundred times before; the word “Potemkin” with some squiggly, frilly bullshit underneath. Jason sighed with displeasure, knowing that communication from his trio of agents could only bring about bad news. He slid his thumb in behind the seal and tore it as gently as a man of his size and disposition possible could. His mind suddenly clicked as soon as he caught glimpse of what was inside. A return air ticket from New York to Charlotte and a booking receipt from some fancy-sounding hotel with a name he couldn’t be bothered to memorise. Natas groaned audibly as he stopped as a pedestrian crossing. He had become so disillusioned with his profession lately that any reminder of his status on the PRIME roster only seemed to weigh on his mind. Colossus was just around the corner; the biggest night in PRIME’s calendar year, a real shot for a guy like Jason Natas to stand up and be counted, and The Anti-Superstar couldn’t have cared less. It’s not that he didn’t want to forge a successful career as a pro-wrestler, because he did. In fact, Jason Natas wanted nothing more than to rise above in a world full of phoney, self-obsessed egomaniacs with Hollywood good looks and egos of interplanetary proportions. He would have loved to throw a fist in the face of the Xavier Kannons and Novas of the world, but it just wasn’t clicking for him. His disdain for the showbiz side of the sport didn’t help; if Jason was made to bear witness to just one more pompous little asshole strutting down the ramp under a waterfall of pyrotechnics and fireworks he would probably scratch his own eyes out with a blunt razorblade. Jason just flat-out could not stand being in the same building as these pompous jackasses and knew that they’d all be in overdrive for Colossus, what with it being the biggest show of the year and all. The idea of not showing up had entered his mind on more than one occasion over the previous couple of days. Jason would much rather kick back in his apartment with a bottle of bourbon in a couple of packs of smokes then go out and “perform” to the masses, but he also knew that the repercussions of such a decision could prove disastrous. There was the monetary situation, first of all. Before he’d entered PRIME, Jason had been flying far too close to the poverty line than he wanted to, and if he were to give up on the sport he’d surely fall back into a familiar life of grime. Why would another promotion want to take a chance on an antisocial street fighter with a tainted wrap sheet and minimal wrestling ability? And then there was Potemkin. Jason knew that messrs Loftman, Jewel and Najera would sue him for everything he was worth at the slightest hint of a breach in contract. He’d be ruined, pure and simple. Wiping his mental slate clean, Jason slid the Potemkin envelope into the back pocket of his stonewashed jeans and focused on the second envelope. The handwriting was unfamiliar and unprofessional; the block capitals looked to Jason like they could have been written by a preschool kid. Once again he pulled the envelope open and slid the contents out. The words “Mega” and “Job” jumped out from the sheet, followed by “invite you to attend a waffle party…” That was all Jason needed to read. The mere sight of the name of his incompetent PRIME part-time allies was enough to flip his angry switch. Without a second through he crumpled the letter into a ball and tossed it over his shoulder. “Hey!” a confused bum blurted as the paper connected with his long, greasy brown hair. The homeless man picked the paper up from the concrete and, without rising from his pit of squalor, yelled “you dropped somefin’!” at Jason from a toothless mouth. “Keep it,” replied The Anti-Superstar without even looking back. Jason pulled his iPod from the recesses of his Pitchfork hooded sweatshirt and pushed the headphones into his ears as the bum rolled out the piece of paper. Not many people would be too excited about the possibility of a Mega Job hosted party, but what he found written on that A4 sheet brought a little ray of sunshine into a blighted existence. ***** “I dunno, I think I preferred their earlier stuff,” office lackey Lisa Tauer mused between mouthfuls of a painfully bland Subway “Spicy Italian” sandwich. A rogue slice of mayonnaise-lubricated lettuce slid from the end of her herb-encrusted sub roll, landing on top of a sizeable pile of crumbs that had accumulated on the table top. “They have earlier stuff?” Delila Morrell perked up before washing the taste of processed meat from her mouth with a quick blast of refreshing Sprite. “I thought they were a new group?” Dionne Resnick huffed. Discuss the ins and outs of the recorded output of Nickelback wasn’t exactly her idea of riveting conversation. It was a regular occurrence for her to spend her lunch break desperately trying to dodge the inane banter that often spewed from the mouths of her colleagues, and this afternoon was no different. “Yeah, dummy! Don’t you remember ‘How You Remind Me’?” “Oh! That was them?!” Dionne stared blankly from the window of the fast food restaurant in disguise, half-finished sandwich sitting before her. “Wait a minute,” she said, suddenly perking up. “That guy looks familiar.” Delila and Lisa suddenly tore themselves away from Chad Kroeger’s insipid musical creation and instead focused their collective gazes through the glass. “Who? I don’t see anyone,” said Lisa. “There. Look. Tall guy, blonde hair, black hoody,” Dionne said, trying to pinpoint the focus of her attention by extending a dainty hand and pointing. “I still can’t see him,” Lisa stated, squinting. “What, the guy with the tattoo on his face?” Delila asked. How the hell can she see that? Dionne thought to herself. Must be those milk bottle glasses she wears. “That’s Jason Natas,” nodded Delila as she took in a mouthful of Coca Cola. “Oh, the drug-dealing wrestler guy?” Lisa quizzed with a decisively sour facial expression. She had heard the name on television and assumed from his rough-as-sandpaper mugshot that he was bad news. “That’s right. I heard he lives just round the corner,” Delila explained. “Dangerous guy, apparently he used to be a part of some kind of illegal fight club.” Lisa nodded and listened for a comment from Dionne. It didn’t come. She turned her head and, much to her surprise, noticed that her colleague was still staring at the hulking brute outside. “Come on, Di, he can’t be that interesting.” “It’s not every day you see a sporting star walking through the neighbourhood,” Dionne began. “Plus, he’s kind of good-looking in that rugged, Clint Eastwood kind of way. I bet he’s a demon between the sheets,” she said with a mischevious glint in her eyes. “Mrs. Resnick!” an almost outraged Mrs. Morrell quipped. “Don’t forget that ring on your finger!” “Good-looking? Are you kidding me?!” Lisa asked, more disgusted than outraged. “They guy looks like he had a fight with a lawnmower for God’s sakes! Just because he hasn’t shaved in a couple of days doesn’t mea-… hey! Where the heck are you doing?” Dionne had already flung her buttress from the uncomfortable plastic chair and made a b-line for the door before either of her associates could stop her. ***** ”I’M HOG-TIED ‘N DIRTY, I’M TOUGH AND MEAN, KEEP YOUR WALL STREET SLICKERS, AND YOUR AMERICAN DREAM.” The rough, indecipherable growl of Eyehategod’s burst from his headphones, making him oblivious to the calls of the tanned brunette following him up the street. “Excuse me!” repeated Dionne Resnick at a slightly louder volume. She was finding it hard to keep up with Jason Natas’ giant strides and the high heels certainly weren’t helping. Again the words seemed to pass him by. It didn’t surprise her though; with the volume that this guy was “listening” to that obnoxious heavy metal filth a plane could crash into the building beside him and he wouldn’t bat an eyelid. There was only one thing for it. She reached upwards and tapped Jason Natas on the shoulder. Instinctively the big New Yorker spun around on the heel of his foot and adopted a defensive stance; usually when someone tapped Jason Natas on the shoulder it ended with that person being on the receiving end of a teeth-loosening right hook, but not this time. Jason adjusted his stance accordingly when he saw that it was a woman who stood before him. He pulled the earphones from his lugs without pausing the thoroughly antisocial New Orleans sludge onslaught. Natas could tell from the look in her warm, hazel eyes that she was more than a little bit intimidated by him, so he made the extra effort to be as accommodating as he could. “Yeah?” he quizzed in his rough, hoarse speaking voice. It was the warmest greeting he’d exchanged in a long time, even though it wasn’t exactly friendly. “Hey, umm,” she began, tripping over her own words. “Are you that Jason Natas guy?” “That’s the rumour,” he responded, his eyes slanting with suspicion. Usually when a stranger approached him in the street they wanted to kick up some shit or call him out, but the twentysomething before him didn’t look capable of such an act. She was attractive, very photogenic. Jason could tell from her formal attire that she probably worked in a boring job that she loathed, probably some kind of office slave monkey. Her Caucasian skin was tanned – probably artificially – and toned, but not to the point where she lost her womanly figure. Jason cast his vision down passed her slightly longer than shoulder-length hair, passed her thin eyebrows and round, smiling face towards the more interesting parts of her anatomy. Dionne Resnick certainly wasn’t Scarlett Johansson, but her natural curves were still more than enough to drive the average man into a frenzy. “Well, sorry to trouble you,” she began, cautious, as if treading on eggshells, “but my kids are big fans.” She was lying between her teeth, but it seemed to be working. “Any chance of an autograph? It’d make their day…” Jason wasn’t quite sure if he believed the “big fans” part, but he shrugged his shoulders anyway. What harm could it possible do? “Sure.” She reached into the pocket of her black jacket and conveniently pulled a pen and a business card, handing them to Jason. “Here you go,” she smiled nervously, still quite intimidated the by the reputation of the man standing before her. The Anti-Superstar merely scrawled his signature on the back of the card. It was only the second or third time anyone had asked him for his insignia, and his general knowledge of autograph signing etiquette was poor. Whereas the likes of Lindsay Troy and Bryan Dawkins may ask for the name of the kids and scrawl down some kind of witty slogan, Jason Natas kept it simple. “Thanks!” she faux-beamed as she took the card back. Jason nodded and went to turn away, but her words stopped him before he could get anywhere. “Wait! You forgot something!” she blurted, desperate to stop this moment from escaping her forever. “Huh?” Jason quizzed as he ceased the process of putting the headphones back into his earlobe. “What’s that?” “Your phone number.” Jason was bowled over. Even after four months worth of global exposure as a part of the PRIME roster, Jason still couldn’t believe how damn easy it was to pick up women when you’re a professional wrestler. Before it seemed he couldn’t score in a brothel, but now he rarely found it hard to find somebody to share his bed with. He knew that it had absolutely nothing to do with him, of course. After all, Jason Natas looked as rough as a badger’s arsehole with his piercings, tattoos, poor hygiene and terrible dress sense. No, it was the money they were after. Ring rats, the lot of them, obsessed with the idea of shaking up with a “celebrity” for the night. Jason Natas didn’t give a fuck about that; a score was a score as far as he was concerned. Carefully he wrote his digits on the card and handed it back to the brunette, who grinned again. “I’ll call you, tough guy.” He watched her tight form as she slid away across the street. A smile curled up the right hand side of his mouth, before he finally turned and headed for the nearest bus stop. ***** “Jason, you CANNOT fuck this up!” the suit-clad Mr. Loftman almost shouted from the other side of the glass-topped desk. “It’s the biggest night of PRIME’s year, the biggest night of your LIFE! You know what that means? You show up, beat the living daylights out of those three dickwads, and leave. Simple.” Huffing, Jason slid down in the comfortable leather chair. Usually when he found himself in the office of his agents he felt overcome by the sheer level of abhorrence he felt for his trio of city slicking benefactors, but this time was different. Instead of feeling infuriated he felt tired, worn out, like he didn’t care. “Hello?! Anybody there?” the smarmy prick at the other side of the desk asked. Mr Loftman could tell that Jason Natas wasn’t listening to him, and it was pissing him off. “Right. Roll into Charlotte, squash DUI, kill Jimmy Bonafide, got it.” Loftman sighed, frustrated at the indifference that clung onto Jason’s words. He knew better than to try and stir interest in the man, though; Jason was far too thick-skulled for that. “Oh shit,” Loftman said, taken aback by the time displayed on his silver Rolex. “I gotta get out of here.” He stood from his chair, grabbed the scattered documents that lay before him and haphazardly crammed them into his open briefcase. Jason was relieved, thankful that the meeting was seemingly over and he’d soon be free from the shackles of his agent. “Just remember, Jase; DO. NOT. FUCK. THIS. UP. Comprende?” Natas only nodded as he rose from his chair. “Good, because if I find out you’ve ‘missed’ your flight tomorrow morning… well, I don’t think you’ll be too pleased with the outcome.” Jason sneered. Honestly, he thought, what kind of a threat does this fuckin’ cunt think he poses to me? The Anti-Superstar watched Loftman hastily depart the room. The agent could make all the threats that he wanted, but Jason had already made his mind up. He wasn’t going to Colossus, not after the glorified hour-long lecture that he’d just received. The man he knew only as Mr. Loftman had gone all high-and-mighty on Jason, telling him that his lifestyle was unacceptable and that his attitude towards his employers needed to change. Well fuck you, Loftman. Jason wasn’t going to fly to Charlotte; he was going to miss the biggest night of the year just to spit the blonde-haired prick. Suddenly a buzzing in his jeans interrupted Jason’s train of thought. He pulled the offending object – his dated cell phone – from his pocket as an unfamiliar number flashed across the screen. He brought up the text message. ”hey, remember me from earlier? u signed an autograph for my kids? anyway i was just wonderin if u wanted to go out for a drink sumtime? i’m free tomorrow night. – dionne xoxoxox” Jason smiled. He definitely wasn’t flying to Charlotte tomorrow. ***** Daylight poured into Jason Natas’ cramped bedroom two mornings later, awakening the grizzled Anti-Superstar from a near sleep-free night. Turning his head to the left, Jason noticed that the cute brunette beside him was still sound asleep, bed sheets pulled halfway down her torso. Not a bad thing to wake up to, Jason thought as he swung his tree trunk legs over the side of his bed. He stood upright and stretched out tired muscles, before pausing to pull a discarded pair of jeans over his lower body. After a yawn he grabbed his cell from the bedside table, the words “24 MISSED CALLS” flashing back at him accusingly. “Hmm, sixteen more than yesterday,” he mumbled. Jason knew exactly who they were from. Potemkin had been frantically trying to reach Jason after he’d missed his flight the previous morning, but he’d so far managed to block every incoming call and delete every text message. Why on Earth would he want to fly out to a city he had no interest in visiting to pursue a career he no longer had any interest in when there was a toned beauty at least five levels to good for him practically offering it to him on a plate? Complete no-brainer. Then, a noise. The sound of somebody rustling beneath coarse, over-starched sheets. Natas turned back around again as Dionne balled her fists and rubbed the sleepy from her eyes, squinting in the uncomfortable morning light. “Heeyyyy,” she said, sitting upright, making no effort whatsoever to cover her modesty. “Mornin’ sunshine,” Jason retorted, offering her a smile that revealed a mouthful of uneven yellow teeth. Jason could tell from the look on her face the words that were coming next. “Another round?” she smiled. Let it never be said that being a professional athlete is without its perks. He slid back into bed, pulling the sheets down past her belly button piercing as a muffled, polyphonic chime began to resonate from the confines of her handbag ***** “Bah,” Joseph Resnick murmured as he snapped his cell closed behind his desk. He’d been trying to get hold of his wife all morning and to no avail. It was nearly eleven ‘o clock and he hadn’t heard a peep from Dionne. He had to remind himself of the nature of her job in order to calm his nerves. She was always pretty hard to get hold of when she was working, especially when she was away on one of these last-minute business trips, but damnit, this sixth anniversary party wasn’t going to plan itself. So he sighed, leaned forward in his chair, and began to pitter-patter away at the keyboard that put the food on the Resnick family table. He’d call her back in an hour or so. ***** A shower and a quick breakfast quickly refreshed Jason after meeting the demands of Mrs. Resnick for the second time. After cleaning himself up he slumped back in the brand new grey leather couch that he’d parted coin for just a few weeks ago and let out a content sigh. I should be in Charlotte right now, he thought. But he wasn’t. Instead of making the final preparations for the final showdown with Jimmy Bonafide and Delta Upsilon Iota, Jason Natas was about to enjoy a little bit of post-coital television. Truth be told, over the course of the past twenty-four hours he’d almost forgotten about his career. The preening little dickheads and the nauseating displays of ‘showmanship’ had almost completely departed his psyche and it was certainly something that Jason Natas could’ve gotten used to. Of course, a no-show at the biggest show of the year wouldn’t look good on his track record, but Jason didn’t give a shit. It’s not like he had any desire to pursue this way of life anymore. When he signed on the dotted line with PRIME he never imagined it would end up like this. As a former street fighter, he saw it as a legal medium through which he could satisfy his hunger for violence and putting a hurt on people. If only he’d known then what he knew now of the backstage politics and showbiz bullshit, he might not have signed in the first place. Jason grabbed the remote control and tapped the power button. The large LCD screen flickered into life. ”… WELL JOHNNY, FIRST UP WE’VE GOT THE TEAM OF DUI AND JIMMY BONAFIDE TAKING ON THE UNEASY ALLIANCE OF MEGA JOB AND JASON NATAS! WHILE MANY HAVE SPECULATED…” The words that came from the speakers seemed to pass Jason by as tunnel-vision took over. There, on ESPN, where the visages of three men he truly despised. Hank Cobb. Colby Korver. Jimmy Bonafide. Alongside pictures of himself and Mega Job. As he scanned his eyes across the mugshots of the five individuals who had made his life in PRIME insufferable over the past few weeks, it all came back to Jason Natas. He couldn’t let the pricks win. He couldn’t let DUI and Jimmy Bonafide get the better of him. Those three had disregarded every warning that he had thrown at him; they’d ejected him from the arena on ReVolution last week, and Jason Natas just couldn’t let that slide. Now he could see beyond his dislike for the business in general, for there was something more important at the forefront now. Pride. He made the decision in a split-second. Jason Natas was going to North Carolina.
