Date: Today Surreptitious Alliances. The thing that hath been, it is that which shall be; and that which is done is that which shall be done: and there is no new thing under the sun. - Ecclesiastes 1:9 There is nothing that Chet Worth can do that is new. It's all been seen before, and will be seen again. The options have dried up, and all he can do is replicate some feat done by our human ancestors, in a different venue. Symbolically, he could march from Africa to Rome, taking over the world. He could rise up, put his mind to the test, and take over the wrestling world as his own, dethroning Cylde Walkins to take the Universal Championship, and cement his name as the best there is to offer at that given moment in time. He could be David, and with sling in hand, take down the powerful Goliath. It wouldn't be difficult. To overthrow Deville, or Hoyt Williams wouldn't take much doing at all. He'd simply need to stick to his guns, and not stop until they fell. He could take on the substantial wealth of Tyler Nelson, and all he stands for, crumbling his empire, and standing tall on the rubble of what used to be. Chet Worth could be the proverbial Martin Luther, leading all who follow him to eternal happiness against the status quo. He could lead people like Balthasar, Danny Ferguson, The Illustrious Face Eater and Chandler Tsonda to the very pinnacle of PRIME, and watch as through the rest of time, people speak of him with reverence and respect. For some men, greatness isn't simply something they fall into. Greatness is their birthright. Power, glory, and respect go hand in hand as they walk seamlessly though life, knowing that with each step, they will do something great. For Chet Worth, his birthright is mediocrity, and there's no shame in it. For all the people who want to be great, there needs to be a driving force behind them. Someone who will carry out their orders to a 't'. Someone who will follow them to the ends of the earth, simply because they are who they are. That was Chet Worth's calling in life. To be the one to assist another to greatness. He'd accepted that long ago, and came to PRIME for that purpose and that purpose alone. He was a lackey, and was good at it. Someone had to do it. So, could that be why he chose Kyle Lamen, when he could have attacked someone like Tchu, Nova, Tyler Nelson or Angelo Deville? Taking out Lamen would have put Worth into the favor of Danny Ferguson, a man who with his tremendous star power, and charming good looks could fill the pockets of the Worthwhile Mercenary. But, that too could have impressed someone like Hoyt Williams, who could need a man like Chet Worth to be the new cornerstone of the RISP. Tyler Nelson could use a man like Chet Worth to protect his considerable investment in PRIME. The road to Albany was long and tiring. Rather than flying to the next destination, Chet Worth and his agent Peter Maxwell chose to drive across the country to the next ReVolution. In truth, Worth was afraid to fly, having done so only once in his life, and it was an experience he'd not like to repeat. As a child, his parents didn't take many vacations. His father, Peter Worth was a steelworker in Cleveland, Ohio. His mother, Elaine was a homemaker. Rather than spend money to go somewhere for a week and spend their house payment on a hotel room, they would normally take long car ride vacations to some destination within a few hours of Cleveland. They were middle class, through and through. A few months after his thirteenth birthday, Elaine won a contest in Ladies Home Journal that won the family a vacation for four to Orlando, Florida. The family was elated, and arrived at the airport hours early. 'Mom,' Chet said. 'What's it like to fly?' Chet's dad spoke up first. 'Well, it's okay, until you have to help land the plane.' 'Land the plane?' 'Dear,' Elaine replied, her voice turning a bit on the cross side. She knew what was coming, and wanted to stop it before it got out of hand. Peter simply waved his hand at her for the interruption. 'Yeah. Like in the Flintstones. We all have to put our feet through the bottom of the plane, and stop it from slipping off the runway.' 'But...' 'Oh, and to save fuel, we might have to flap the wings.' 'I've never seen the wings of a plane flap, dad.' 'Well, that's because we live near the airport. They don't have to flap then, since they're landing.' 'Oh.' 'And one more thing, Chester,' his dad said, reaching into his pocket and pulling out a roll of quarters. 'Take these, and hold onto them. If we run into trouble, you're going to need the quarters for the oxygen masks.' 'Oxygen masks?' 'Yeah. If the cabin depressurizes, we're going to need to pay for the oxygen. There's nothing free on a plane, son.' Unbeknownst to Chet at the time, his father was a bit of a joker, playing practical jokes on his friends at the plant. His favorite joke in the hot plant was to go behind a bin with a water hose, and call frantically for one of his buddies, as if something went horribly wrong. As they rounded the corner, Peter would open the hose, and douse his buddy with it. All in good fun. But today, a thirteen year old boy was petrified as he handed his ticket to the flight attendant. 'First time?' she asked, in the usual flight attendant, sing-songy type voice, with all the warmth and genuine compassion of a wet noodle. 'Y-y-yes, ma'am.' 'Well, don't worry. We'll make sure you don't have to fly the plane,' she said with an unnoticed wink toward Chet's old man. Chester Alyoisius Steven Worth was convinced that he was going to die at the young age of thirteen, without ever being able to touch a woman's boobies. His life started flashing before his eyes, which back in 1987 wasn't in Hi-Def like it is today. The family walked onto the plane, and took their seats, Peter placing luggage into the overhead compartment. Within seconds of sitting down, Chet had his seatbelt on, not willing to take any chances, landed or otherwise. His entire body tensed as he looked around for the exits. A few moments later, a calming voice came over the intercom. Good afternoon, passengers, thank you for flying United Airlines. We're pleased to have you. Today's flight to Orlando from Cleveland, Ohio will take approximately two hours and forty-five minutes to complete, and when we land, the temperature should be in the mid seventies and sunny. At this time, if you all could observe the exit signs, and make note that the seats below you will turn into an inflatable device, in the event of a water landing. Oh, and Chet? Keep those quarters handy. The plane erupted into laughter as Peter Worth smiled widely. Chet's face turned a deep crimson, and he wanted to bury himself. He burned inside, not angry at his father, but because, once again, his father got him with one of his jokes. He was skeptical at first, as he usually was with his father. But, once he came prepared with his own roll of quarters, he really thought he was telling the truth. That's what he gets for trusting. The car stopped at the side of the road somewhere near Cheyenne, Wyoming. Peter Maxwell sat in the driver's seat, flashlight in hand, checking the map. Chet Worth sat back on the passenger's seat of the Chevrolet HHR, his eyes closed as he felt the passing of traffic shake the car. It was Saturday, and the two needed to be in Albany by Tuesday evening. The odds were not in their favor. 'We could have already been there,' said Maxwell. 'Shut up.' 'I could be in the hotel, drinking some coffee right now, warm.' 'Shut up.' 'But no. You can't fly. You hate to fly. You have to drive.' Chet opened his eyes, his face turning red once more. He pointed at his partner and frowned. 'Pete, I'm warning you. Shut the hell up.' 'It's just like driving, Chet! It's not like you've got to do anything. Just sit there. Up in the air.' 'Shut up.' 'Give it a chance! Come on!' 'No.' 'Goddamnit, Chet.' 'Don't take the Lord's name in vain.' 'Fuck you.' 'Fuck you.' 'No, fuck you.' 'No, fuck yourself. You always say how much you like pussy.' 'Yeah. Funny, asshole.' 'I know. Now shut up and drive.' 'Whatever.' Chet Worth threw his hat over his face, and pushed his seat back as Peter Maxwell took the vehicle forward at a speedy 65 miles per hour. 'I used to be someone you know.' Maxwell said, glancing over at the trying to sleep Chet Worth. 'Yeah. I know. You tell me this all the time.' 'The Alpha Solution, Peter Maxwell, they called me. I used to wrestle the greats...' 'And now you're driving me across the country. Life sure kicks you in the teeth every once in a while, huh?' '...' 'Yeah. Shut up and drive.' Maxwell sneered. 'Sure you don't want me to stop off so you can wash your hands again?' 'Shut up, Pete.' 'I mean, this is a rental car. There could be germs galore in this thing. You know what people do to these rental cars, don't you? Sometimes, people rent them, just so they can throw it to a few hookers. That way, if the car gets towed, the rental company gets it's car back, and the john's still got his car.' 'Pete...' 'Hell, you're probably sitting in a pile of hooker piss right now. How's that feel?' 'I'm going to...' 'Wash your hands fifty times with a new bar of soap? Brush your teeth ten times while you spin in a circle?' '...kill you.' 'Yeah. Right. You couldn't deal with the mess.' '...' 'Yeah. You shut up now.' 'I need to stop.' 'No, you don't. The sooner you stop all that crap, the better you'll feel.' 'I can't help it.' 'No, maybe not. But, I can. Don't worry. I won't placate your OCD.' 'Gee, thanks. And I'm paying you?' 'Ten percent.' 'Twenty percent too much.'
