No Denzel Washington?

3 May 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 03, 2007 To say that Easton had a problem with this was a vast understatement. His life was still ensnared by the spectre of his shadowy mentor and even more recently by that sadistic man known as Chainz. Adding this on top was not a good idea, not for Easton. He believed in giving back to the industry. This was just not the time. "I will not take 'no' for an answer," came the thick West African drawl from across the table. Seated across from the shaggy-haired Canadian was a dark man, his hair shaved until it was barely distinguishable from his ebon skin, insistent eyes fixed on Easton. A crimson button-down was draped over his muscled frame with a LIONS F.C. logo stitched over the left breast. The leader of the Lions Fighting Club, Antony Ozubike, was an imposing figure. Of that, there was no debate. "I won't give you 'yes,'" Easton replied. By contrast, the shorter, light-skinned Canadian – who was standing, not wishing to give Anton that much of an advantage – was dressed in a white t-shirt and a loose pair of shorts, seemingly comfortable despite the displayed annoyance on his face. "I can wait," Anton promised. Easton rolled his eyes. Apparently, he was one of those that thought muscles gave him licenee to conduct himself in whichever manner he wanted. "Just three numbers for the police," Easton warned. He gripped the back of an empty chair, staring holes into Anton. The dark-skinned fighter grunted. There was no getting out of this one. Anton knew that Easton wasn't simply a showman, and even if he wanted to try to force the matter with fists, Easton wasn't going to go down easily. Mutual assured destruction was apparently a sound strategy. "He needs to be trained," Anton reiterated. Easton sighed; he was going to get the whole spiel again. "He is a wrestler, he is not like me, I can not train him properly. I have taught him all he wants to know, but he needs to get better. You are the only one who can do this." "Bull-fuckin'-shit," Easton snapped, eyes fierce in a tired fashion. "There are tons of people who can waste their time training some kid. I'm not one of them." "It. Is. Not. A. Waste." Anton's lips were drawn thin and his eyebrows knitted together in frustration. Obviously unused to being denied. Obviously unused to dealing with Easton Hall. The grizzly Dragon laid his palms flat on the table, leaning forward insistently. "Get out of my house," he growled. "He needs your training," Anton said deliberately. "He will get it." "Get out of my house." The black warrior scooted his chair backwards and stood up, muscles knotted under his skin in a reflection of his consternation. "I will not take no for an answer, Mr. Hall," he repeated. "Get out of my house." Easton's eyes followed Anton's dark shape as he walked past him and towards the door. He turned, not wanting to let the man out of his sight. Anton didn't look back as he pulled open the door and stepped through, letting it close politely behind him. Easton glared at the door for a few more seconds before turning back around. He pushed the chair back into its place at the table and ran his fingers through his hair. Alexander Shaw. Easton knew next to nothing about this kid and he was supposed to up and train him? Easton had trained people before, it was true, and they had turned out well. Laz Kennedy, Javier Sikkan, Roanoke LaRouche; they had made some small impact on the wrestling world, each of them. But he had trained them when he was idle, when he wasn't involved in any promotion – especially not one of the stature of PRIME. Now he had a title to defend and a name to make. He didn't have time for this. He tried to push Shaw out of his brain, but what replaced it was even less savory. The image of Chainz was enough for him to decide it was time to hit the gym; he wasn't going to let the vile thought settle in his brain by being inactive. It seemed that no matter what he thought about today, it would serve as an annoyance. Perfect. *** Easton was stoop-shouldered before the red, swinging heavy bag, his hands snugly inside red bag gloves and brought up in a form like a boxer's, his shins protected by similarly-colored pads. A punch lanced out at the bag in rapid style and Easton, moving on the balls of his feet like a dancer, moved so his perspective was perpendicular to the bag and punched again. He didn't want to lose mobility at all, so he liked this sort of exercise; worked your punching power as well as keeping you agile. He knew that going into this fight with Chainz, he wasn't going to do much of his normal submission fighting, primarily because he didn't want to. It would be a safer path to victory to wrestle him as he had always done, but that wasn't on his mind. He just wanted to punch Chainz in the face until he couldn't take anymore – the idea that he could restrain himself in order to actually wrestle to his normal effect was fanciful. He slammed his elbow into the bag and followed it with a sweeping kick from the side, dancing away to meet it perpendicularly as before. The man wasn't smart, he was just sick, and that was probably what disgusted Easton the most. There was no intelligence behind his "mind games," they were just him acting out his debauched fantasies. The lack of all sophistication… it baffled the mind. Easton Hall did not like being baffled. Vows to kill. Easton had never been above giving them, yet at the same time he knew that he wasn't going to follow through on it. In a way, Easton supposed, he was displaying no more intelligence than Chainz. Both of them were brutes, almost mindless in their search for their goal, and the only difference between them was that Chainz was willing to follow through on it. It almost made Easton look… weak. No. Not weak. Easton wasn't weak. Was he? A tap landed on Easton's shoulder and he whipped around, lifting his left arm up defensively. Surrounding him and the bag on all sides were men of various heights and ethnicities in red jerseys emblazoned with the LIONS F.C. logo and all obviously in peak or near-peak physical condition. Directly in front of Easton was the intimidating black form of Anton Ozubike, trunk-like arms crossed over his chest. "I told you, Mister Hall," Anton said calmly. "I would not take no for an answer." Easton stripped off his gloves angrily, hurling them to the ground. "This is ridiculous," Easton breathed. He took a step towards the wall of Lions, intending to push his way through, but one of them stepped up and blocked his path directly. Easton locked eyes with him. "This is Shaw," Anton said in somber introduction. "I want you to train him." Alexander Shaw was Easton's height almost exactly, dirty blond hair that capped his skull and little more. He was built a bit stockier than Easton was, though. If Easton had been in the mood, he would have considered the kid a fine candidate for training. As it was, "You want training?" Easton sneered, jutting his jaw towards Shaw in a challenge. The man lifted an eyebrow, but he didn't speak. Easton shook his head, brushing past Shaw; he knew the man had purposefully not resisted, but it didn't matter much. "Follow me." The Lions parted as Easton marched past, towards a blue mat with a white circle painted on it. Having chosen the battlefield, Easton pulled off his shinguards and tossed those aside as well, flexing his feet as he made his way to the far end of the circle. Shaw assumed that this was an invitation to take a place directly opposite the Dragon and he did so, rubbing his hands together to get them ready. Both men seemed to lament the absence of chalk, but they were similarly used to having to go without it; one didn't use chalk when going out in the squared circle, after all. After a few seconds of silence that had allowed the Lions F.C. men to surround the mats, Easton took a few steps towards his prospective protegeé. "Come at me," Easton challenged, dropping to his haunches and gesturing for Shaw to come forth. The Lions F.C. fighter assumed a low stance as well, slowly moving towards Easton. Easton's eyebrows were a downward arrow, intensity oozing from every pore. This wasn't a tryout. This was a lesson. Shaw extended a hand just a tad, gauging Easton's distance, but at that moment the Canadian sprang forward. His hand closed around Shaw's wrist and he twisted under it, torquing the arm and forcing Shaw up onto his toes. Rapidly, Easton shifted his weight and tossed Alexander to the mat. He kept his hold on the arm, turning Shaw onto his face and pouncing on top of him, hammerlocking the arm and slamming his forearm down onto the back of Shaw's neck. He struggled, but Easton wasn't letting up; his weight was all on his arm, making sure that Shaw wasn't moving much at all. "Get the fuck out of here!" Easton snapped at Anton, furious eyes locking with the Nigerian's. "And don't you bring me any more kids!" Easton lifted up, releasing Shaw and brusquely striding back through the crowd of Lions. Anton's eyes watched Easton as he left, angry and obviously just barely holding back from having the Lions box Easton in once again. Easton didn't care as long as Anton prevented this from getting too messy. He wasn't going to train this kid, no way. He stooped down and picked up his gloves, calmly, slowly slipping them back onto his fists. *** "You shot on him," came Brian Jackson's voice over the phone. "That's a pretty shitty thing to do, man." "He deserved it," Easton replied. The bowl of ramen that was staring Easton in the face was cooling off, but Easton didn't care too much. He hated how cheap he was sometimes. Then again, there were more important things that required his money. He stirred the noodles around with his fork. "No, he didn't. Anton, maybe. He didn't do shit to you." "So who's side are you on, again?" "Come off it, E. It was a dick move and you know it." "Sure, sure," Easton acquiesced. He lifted some noodles up with his fork and frowned a bit. "Made me feel better, at least." "Chainz has got you worked up. No way you'd pull this kind of shit normally." "What's normal, exactly?" Easton shifted the phone from one shoulder to the other and stood up, deciding he was thirsty. "There's always some guy I hate." "Those guys you storyline hate." "Sully is kind of a dick all the time." "Yeah, and Axis? Kael St… okay, bad example. But Axis, at least." "Axis is an arrogant prick and you know it." "But you never let Axis throw you off your game, Easton. This is different." Easton didn't speak, pulling open the door of the minifridge and peering inside. Brian was right, but he had forgotten Kael Stanton. Kael had done more than throw him off his game. Kael had… "I'm putting a stop to Chainz, Brian. That's all." His hand wrapped around a bottle of water. To tell the truth, Kael had done what Axis had never wanted to do, what Suleimon never dared to. If anything else, he wasn't going to let Chainz get the same chance, because he knew if that bastard did, he would go farther than Kael had, just for the purposes of getting to Easton. To come back finally, to visit Jeanne again after so long, only to see her flayed like a piece of meat… Easton took a long drink, trying to flood the thoughts out of his mind. "I'll talk to you later, Brian." Click. *** Easton stepped out of the hotel lobby and started down the street. The heat flogged the back of his too-white Canadian neck where his mass of hair let it. God, so it was getting hot now? Easton shrugged his head and slid his sunglasses over his eyes, attempting to meld into the crowd. He was blocked by a familiar red buttondown that brought a scowl to his face. "What are you running away from?" the Nigerian's bassy voice boomed at him. "I told you no," Easton growled. "Get out of my way." Easton tried to muscle his way past, but Anton grabbed him by the shoulder and forced him backwards. "I told you I would not take that for an answer, Mister Hall," Anton snapped. "Fuck off," Easton spat. They locked eyes once again, Easton's countenance made mysterious by the shades. He was glad that he had gone on the cheap and gotten ominous black plastic. "Train the boy." People began to swarm around them unconcerned, leaving a little circle of privacy, a stone in the stream with two shipwrecked survivors. "You have a tough time with hearing, don't you?" Easton pushed past Anton once again and tried to disappear into the crowd. "What do you want!?" Anton called after him. "What do you want to know about the Chain-man?" Easton stopped in his tracks. A few people jostled him as he stood there, a frown passing over his face. How did everybody know how to push his buttons? Was he really that transparent? He felt a hand lay on his shoulder and he brushed it off angrily, turning to face Anton. "Train him. I'll deliver the Chain-man to you." "You think it's that simple, do you?" Easton sneered. "You think you can just rough him up and I'll do what you want?" Easton shook his head in disbelief. "You're as stupid as you look, Anton. I don't want your help and I'm not training your kid. Capisce?" Easton turned on his heel, marching into the crowd and leaving Anton to stare at his back. How could this guy believe he was going to be blackmailed or bought into doing what he wanted? No, Easton wasn't budging. The problem was, it was that simple. *** Evening. Time to turn in, at least for Easton. He opened the door and flicked on the light switch, throwing incandescent light over the hotel suite. His foot slid a little and he blinked, catching himself on the wall and lifting it up. A business card. Easton picked it up and walked into his room, closing the door behind him. As he slung off his duffle bag, he looked over the business card. Lions F.C. Predictable. Easton crumpled the card up and reached over to the table, picking up his cell phone; he never liked calls in the middle of a workout. He inspected it and his lips pursed thoughtfully. A voicemail. Easton tossed the crumpled cardstock into the trash as he checked his voicemail, pacing about. Finally, the computerized voice finished its introduction and let the message play. "Easton." Anton. "We know where this Chain-man is. We can take you to him or we can take care of him. If you want information, we can tell you that, too. Whatever you want. You know where you can find us. We can make a deal. You know my price." The voicemail clicked off and, in a split-second afterwards, Easton hurled the phone against the wall. This was too much. Easton was tired of this fucking insistence, this needling, like everybody could be bought. Easton wasn't going to be bought. That wasn't how he worked. Apparently, though, Anton didn't understand that. Well, Easton was going to make him understand. He stalked across the room and snatched up the card, unfurling it to see that it was the one he wanted. This was going to end. Reluctantly and a bit foolishly, Easton strode across the room to pick up his phone, hoping he hadn't just demolished the thing. *** Easton stepped out of his car into the deep night, slamming the door noisily behind him. He drew himself up to his full, if by itself unintimidating, height and strode towards the building in front of him. It was a tall, narrow affair built of red brick, looming ominous. The rooftop caught the moonlight like a silvery crown. Everything else was silent, still. There was a dark shape on the steps up to the building. Easton squinted as he approached, but he didn't see any better. He'd called ahead, though, and Anton had said he would someone would be out to talk. Easton didn't want to talk, though. He just wanted to get inside and talk to Anton himself. "Take me to An…" Easton's voice trailed off as he approached and the person sitting on the steps became clearer. The blond man lifted his head and his eyes met Hall's. Rage welled up in Easton's face and he balled his fists. Goddammit! Didn't this motherfucker have tact!? Easton lifted his fists threateningly as he made his way towards the man. "What the fuck are you doing out here?" "Anton told me to come out here," Shaw said flatly. "He said you wanted to discuss prices." Shaw began to rise, using the stone rail to help himself up. "Prices!?" Hall howled, punctuating it with a screaming punch that collided like a freight train with Shaw's jaw. The Lions F.C. fighter stumbled backwards, catching himself on the steps. "Hey!" Shaw cried, but Easton wasn't thinking. He leapt onto Alexander Shaw, pushing his head against the steps with one hand and continuing to rain punches down onto the man's head. Alexander squirmed and finally managed to get his hands under Easton's ribs, pushing him backwards; Hall gripped Shaw's hair as he was being pushed backwards and dragged the man off of the steps and onto the sidewalk. Shaw tried frantically to pick himself up, but Easton had already gotten his balance and savagely kicked Alex Shaw in the jaw. Shaw fell onto his shoulder and turned slowly, looking up at Easton as he tried to scoot away. Furiously, Easton jammed his foot down onto Shaw's chin. His green eyes blazed with anger, fists clenching and unclenching. The kid deserved it, didn't he? He had the nerve to ask about prices. Yet, he was unprepared. Once again. Easton's breathing shallowed as he studied the kid. His hands slowly unclenched. "Get up," Easton grunted, lifting up his foot. Shaw's gaze was distrustful, but he slowly got up off the ground, never letting his stare wander from the Canadian. Once he was on his feet, Easton jabbed his thumb down the street. "Run. Around the block. Don't stop until I tell you to." Shaw's blank look was more from disbelief than from misunderstanding. "Go!" Slowly, Shaw turned and began to do as Easton ordered. The discipline of this kid was impeccable. Easton found himself wondering – unwittingly, to his credit – how Anton got these kids like this. It was military-like. *** Easton slung his towel aside and took a seat on a chair, his arm slung over the back. Shaw had hit the showers, which meant that Easton had a bit of a break before he began his own workout. "Why are you training him?" Easton looked up to see Anton standing just behind him, arms folded over his chest. "Why not?" Easton shrugged. Anton's lifted eyebrow was accusation enough, but Easton didn't feel the need to answer the gesture. "And what do you want done about the Chain-man?" "Nothing. I can handle it." Easton turned around again, staring resolutely at the wall. "You are afraid of him." "I'm not afraid of him." God, if that voice in his head didn't keep insisting he was, he was going to put a bullet through it's loud mouth. "I'm just desperate to beat him." "Fear makes men desperate." "Yeah? So does anger." "Desperate enough to beat up one of my students unprovoked?" Oooh, that stung. "Desperate enough to beat you up." "Is that a challenge?" Easton sighed. "Not yet. I want to be in tip-top condition for this match. I don't intend to give Chainz the edge by letting you break my arm." Anton smirked in a self-satisfied fashion. "So you cannot be bought, but once we challenge your ethics enough, you'll change your mind." Once again, Easton sighed. He stood up and picked up his towel, turning and brushing past Anton. "Don't bring me any more kids." Easton kept walking. He could work out later. One thing he was not going to do was sit around and be abused verbally.
