The Other Shoe

1 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 1 11.01.07 - 3:58 a.m. The BlackBerry nearly buzzed off the coffee table in Danny's living room, waking him up from what had otherwise been a pretty restful night, the calm before a coming storm. He checks the caller ID and squints a little, making sure his eyes were clear. Then he punches the "answer" button and holds it to his ear. "Vin?" "Bad news, DF." "Are you drunk, Vin?" "No, but you and I should talk about that after this." "After what?" "You watching E!?" Normally that would sound like a stupid question, but since we're talking about Danny, questions become infinitely less stupid. So it just so happens that Danny was watching E! - or rather, that he'd left the television on before passing out late the prior evening. Halloween parties and such. He was still dressed in his Green Lantern costume, a relatively mean dig at the movie he had buried a few months back. As Danny's eyes focus on the high-def, he notics his own face on television. This was neither new nor relevant, but it almost always leads to something important, and this time is no different. The words under his face jumble together, but he'd already punched up the volume to get the full effect. "...says that Ferguson received as many as three shipments of the sterioid Nandrolone from Signature Pharmacy between June of 2005 and..." "Oh, goddammit." -Ω- Wednesday, Nov. 1, 2006 Pensacola, Fla. Blaine Blair gave the guard a nervous but polite smile as the gate locked shut behind them. He'd had the whole flight, the whole drive and the whole walk to gather the language necessary to deliver the news, but like a rookie waiting to step out onto the stage for the first time, he was blanking before showtime. This was the oft-discarded intern's first crack at responsibility under current PRIME CEO Chet Worth. Wringing his damp hands as the guard led him down the final strip, he declared inwardly that there's no way he could be cut out to do this kind of thing regularly. The Federal Prison Camp at Pensacola Naval Air Station was a minimum security prison, but Blaine didn't know any better, sufficiently scared and sufficiently straight before anyone thought to use one to achieve the other. It was jail, and jail is scary. End of discussion. It housed any number of lunatics and miscreants, and he would have never stepped inside if not for one particular lunatic/miscreant/none of the above. "Inmates is allowed out on the base durin' daylight, but he ain't never wan' come out," the guard offers. Feeling himself inching closer to a perfect storm of claustrophobia and hypochondria, Blaine barely acknowledges the man, replying only with a half-whimpered "Oh." They arrive at the cell and the guard ushers the intern forward with a wave of his hand, no longer interested in wasting words on the kid and at least marginally concerned that the slightest over-pronunciation could make the kid unwittingly release his bowels right there and then. They ain't payin' him enough to mop. As the guard walked away, Blaine cleared his throat, smoothed out his tie, cleared his throat again and stepped forward to the doorstep of the cell. Inside, Danny Ferguson kept his eyes to the window, watching the people working outside at the base with more contempt with envy. He knew that going outside didn't make him any closer to returning to the life he came to expect. Sensing the presence, his gaze drifted to Blaine at the door and he instantly jumped up from his bunk, no doubt giving the visitor's nerves something to scream about. "It's about goddamned time, man." he chastised, grabbing some of his belongings around the cell and stacking it into a heap. "I...I-uh, I'm sorry, I-" "Whatev," Danny steps in, the gratitude in his voice just barely offsetting the condescension. "I'll take it up with Chet later. Do I need to sign some papers or can we just walk out?" Blaine shuddered inwardly at the implications in Danny's statement, but wasn't able to cut him off at a more appropriate point. Noticing Blair's tenuous response (since the intern couldn't be even remotely opaque with his feelings if he wore a gimp mask 24/7), Danny paused and took a step back. "You're from PRIME, right?" Blaine sighed and wiped his glasses. "We've met before, Danny." "Christmas party?" "A little more recent. Like, weekly." "Ok," Ferguson acquiesced, "so what's the holdup? Chet's posturing to get me thrown in jail was great for ratings, I'm sure, but now it's time to fix things and let me go home." Another deep sigh from Blaine drew his breath into the kind of frantic panting that had derailed countless prom invitations back in the 90s. He wasn't going to let this happen again, and did his best to power through. "I've got bad news, Danny," he choked. "I'm not here to get you out." Danny stepped forward again, his face inches from the partition between them. His breath was growing short as well, albeit for entirely different reasons. "I know you're just the messenger but your memo had better include a fucking awesome reason why I am not going to be taken away from prison when your boss knows I did nothing wrong." It had been less than a month since Danny had been dragged out of ReVolution 107, kicking and screaming and under arrest for the supposed murder of the Illustrious Face-Eater. This was still a good time before Adam Dick would prove to be both undead and unzombie, but the longstanding argument for his innocence - the lack of precedence for one man causing the premeditated combustion of another remotely - remained strong enough that he could have walked if the cards fell. Unfortunately, the man holding the ace was Chet Worth, who for indiscernible reasons had refused to speak on behalf of his former employer and partner. Little did Danny know that none of the above had anything to do with that last sentence. "We got the results of your drug test back, Danny." Ferguson's breath slowed immediately and his eyes closed. He backed up and sat back on his bed, slightly crushing the items he had piled upon it seconds earlier. Running a hand through his tussled red hair, he looked up at Blaine and made eye contact for the first time. "How high were the levels?" Stunned slightly that they had skipped past defiance and straight to remorse, Blaine paused before answering. "Not astonishing. Probably forgettable and forgivable if the issue wasn't on SportsCenter every night. But they were high enough that someone could make an issue out of it." Danny nodded his head silently. Like the intern before him, he had mentally rehearsed how the day would go when PRIME finally came to his rescue (or rather, did the right thing). You can never quite be prepared for when that day arrives, though. You can either try to shoehorn the actual events into the script you have prepared, or you can roll with it. The former was unreasonable to Danny. The latter felt impossible. Struggling to find the next step, Danny remained quiet until Blaine proceeded. "This wasn't just a courtesy call, Danny. I've got an offer from Chet. You stay here, ride out a couple of months until this gets buried, and we'll have you out of here in early '07." Danny's face contorted in confused anger. "You want me to serve a prison sentence for a steroid suspension?" "He said it's the lesser of evils for everyone. We'd be better off answering questions about bureaucracy and misfiled paperwork that left you in jail than having our drug-testing program investigated." "What drug-testing program? You telling me those are cigarettes Nova's rolling?" "And your short stay will be spun into martyrdom by the west coast media, a far cry from the public flogging you'd get if a steroid story hits the tabloids in a Barry Bonds News Climate." Another hand ran through Danny's hair as he began the next chapter in an ongoing series of ruminations about the state of his personal space at the moment. As much as it looked like a move to save face for PRIME, it would do the same tenfold for Ferguson, who had just finished shooting his latest movie and who undoubtedly faced brighter days ahead without the dark cloud of performance enhancers. "This is crazy, kid." "It's not ideal, but it's the best solution in PRIME's eyes. Chet's willing to do this for you." "Chet's an asshole." "Chet's a businessman." Danny nodded, not so much agreeing as admitting that his own comment was made in spite of the situation, not of the man. After another liberal pause, he looked back at the intern. "This never comes back on me?" "If it works, Danny, it never comes back on anyone." -Ω- 11.01.07 - 11:37 a.m. Outside Danny's hotel room in Boston, a throng of photographers, reporters and discounted versions of both varieties crowd by the front door, waiting to snap a picture of the day's newest goat. It had been a long time since Danny had received this kind of media attention, but this was a steep price to pay for Hollywood relevancy, one of the most prestigious backhanded compliments a performer could receive. The door opens and Danny's twin (at least as far as attire goes) bodyguards step out, trying to push back the group as Danny himself emerges, forcing his way between the guards and down the hallways as the paparazzi give chase. "Danny, how long have you been taking steroids?" "Danny, would you attribute your wrestling career to the steroid use?" "Danny, are steroids to blame for your failing movie career?" He weathers each shot like a professional, or at least someone whose skin has been calloused from previous trips around the media carousel. He'd been through this before. Darting down the hallways to an elevator, to a car, to the arena, to anywhere but there, Danny ignores the crowd as best he can, keeping the aviators on and the eyes fixed forward. That is, until one question from the side floored him like an overhand right. "Danny, is it true that you failed a drug test in PRIME once before?" Quietly, everyone in entertainment thinks the media frenzy is no big deal until they end up staring down the barrel. And secretly, people either believe their secrets are so far buried as to never come back up, or they make peace with the truth and wait dutifully for its revelation. But in both instances, no one is ready for the moment when the closet door opens and the skeleton spills out onto the living room floor. Danny stops in his tracks, looking to see where the comment came from. Dametreyus, as skilled a secondary as you will ever find, shoves his employer forward, urging him towards the exit and trying to cover up that his man had been hurt by the parting shot. The simple hesitation was enough, though, and the reporters sensed blood in the water. "Danny, how many drug tests have you failed?" "Danny, why doesn't anyone know about this test?" "Danny, has PRIME been covering for your drug abuse?" The elevator is held open by a hotel employee and Team Ferguson piles on, with the bodyguards and hotel security taking turns shoving media back. Danny's jaw quivers as the doors slide shut. -Ω- Tuesday, Nov. 1, 2005 Denver, Colo. The Rocky Mountains, still a few years away from being the namesake of a baseball punchline but well-versed in being the namesake of a baseball joke, stretched out into the horizon beyond the resort gymnasium. Enjoying or at the very least enduring a private workout session with all the doors blocked and all the vacationing CFOs and CPAs griping to the front desk, Danny Ferguson completed another set of curls and dropped the bar. He plopped down on a bench and leaned in to his BlackBerry, which had been perched on one of the other machines and set on speaker. "It's absolute trash, Allan," he spat, following it up with an actual loogie on the rubber-mat floor. "I tear through that damn tournament and they have the fucking balls to match me up with a chick in the semis." "You played that well, though," came the crackled reply from the phone, or rather from Danny's agent Allen North, by way of the phone. "You didn't give the media or the fem-nazis any material to work with." "My biggest night so far and they hand the tournament to the bland guy in the black tights," Ferguson groans, referring to PRIME's King of Kings pay-per-view and its marquee Jewel In The Crown tournament, which had taken place a week or so prior. "I mean, that idiot's going nowhere in this sport, but he's going to be able to call himself 'champ' for the rest of his miserable career." "Speaking of going places in the sport, Danny," Allan segued masterfully, "it's been about four months. How long does this PRIME thing continue?" "How long are we looking at before Hard Money 2's script hits my hands?" "Yeah...bad news on that," Allan sighed. "Studio trashed the last draft. They want to start from scratch." Danny stood up and kicked the bench, frustrated with the call as much as the fact that he was late to his appointment in a hot tub with several female resort guests. Although it bears questioning why anyone would go to a ski resort to lounge in the hot tub when first class airfare to Jamaica is comparably priced. "Dammit, Allan!" "They're going to make it a high priority in the next couple months." "Right, 'cause haste has certainly paid off for us until now," Danny snapped back, pacing slightly around the phone and running a hand through his matted hair. He paused momentarily before lifting the curl bar up for another set. "If I don't have a project lined up, I'm going to start another cycle." A deep sigh from the agent crackled the 'Berry's monotone speaker. "If you can't pass a studio drug test, then I can't line you up for another project." "Like they haven't overlooked that before," Danny's breath became short as he started repping the bar. "Look, I'm not locked into wrestling forever. It's just something to do until we get a movie deal. When I get back into the box office I'll be bringing the entire 18-25 market with me." "Just don't get caught with a piss test." "I'm not the only fucking actor on the gas, Allan." "The people who beat their chests over this issue don't go after actors. They go after wrestlers." "Well I'm not exactly a wrestler, so I doubt that will be a problem." -Ω- 11.01.07 - 4:22 p.m. Flashbulbs popped on the steps of the TD Banknorth Garden as Danny struggles to enunciate for every TV camera, digital recorder and microphone in attendance. "I would like to stress the difference between use and abuse when it comes to drugs of this nature," he says, pausing liberally to gather himself and to go over his next set of carefully scripted words. "While I am not denying that I received shipments of an anabolic steroid, I am categorically denying any and all implications that are being drawn thereafter." For a preventative shot, it isn't particularly effective. The day to that point had been a whirlwind, and lacking the kind of support system that a man of his stature would normally enjoy - agent, manager and particularly publicist - Danny is flying blind and alone, doing the best he could to cull a statement from years of watching his peers do the same. "Danny!" comes a question from the audience. "What about the failed test in 2006?" "No comment," Danny replies, doing his best to return the issue to its more factual base. "Again, what came out today is that I had received the material. Nothing about usage, nothing about abuse, nothing that incriminates me in the fashion that I've been represented today." "Why are you taking performance-enhancers in a performance-based industry, Danny?" "Doesn't that sound like a stupid question?" Danny retorts, not thinking the answer through. "So you're saying it's a no brainer to take steroids?" "No, I'm," he stammers, frustrated but not willing to flip out and give them a 'roid rage headline the next morning. "I am asking that people please refrain from judging on something they don't know." Speaking like a man who has no grasp of Hollywood, Danny fights back his emotions and does his best to dance out there in front of the world. They had landed too many heavy blows, and he was going down fast. Back in the middle of the maelstrom for all the wrong reasons, Danny finally understood one of the altruisms of celebrity - perception is nine-tenths of the law. -Ω- Monday, Nov. 1, 2004 Los Angeles, Calif. Danny cringed as he pulled the syringe out, not so much hurt as squeamish. He'd grown accustomed to the process, but that didn't make it any easier to do, especially when twisted around to watch yourself in the bathroom mirror. The worst part was that he couldn't just get it over with after a workout when the adrenaline and testosterone kept inhibitions and better judgement in short supply. The directions called for regular injections at odd hours. He cursed the decision a routien part of the process - and wondered why it looked so easy back when he was kicking dirt around RUSH as El Spiffy!. Cleaning up any leftover mess in the bathroom, he strolled out to his kitchen and smiled at the visitor leaning on his counter. "Allan?" Up-and-coming agent Allan North perked up at the sound of his new client approaching, and he extended a hand. "It's a pleasure, Danny." "I'm glad to have you representing me." "Please, I should be thanking you," North beamed, laying it on thick. "You are my marquee client now, and I'm not going to let you down." The two sat down on a pair of couches in Danny's living room, under the watchful eye of Dametreyus Fuqueiawytas. Allan shuffles some papers as Danny rolls through his BlackBerry to check some texts. "So what kind of projects are we looking at, Al?" "I’ve got some bad news on those, Danny - the offers on the table are shit." Ferguson looked up from his phone, his brow furrowing slightly. Allan's voice skipped a little - nerves more than anything - but he persevered. "A bunch of comedies, some bit roles, a lot of stuff where it sounds like they want you just so the audience will go 'damn, that's Danny Ferguson!'" "And we don't want that?" "No, sir. I mean, look at you, man, you're not some bumblefuck child star any more." Indeed, the MTV athlete look had crept into Danny's daily style, an evolution due in no small part from the muscles he'd put on since people had seen him last. "You've been out of Hollywood and the public eye for almost five years, Danny," Allan insisted. "We have a chance to completely repackage you here." "Repackage me as what?" Allan tossed him a script. Danny looked a little confused, holding it up. "Hard Money?" "It's an action movie. Bruckheimer's in talks." "I know. Vin Diesel's in it," Danny replied. "VD's my boy." "So he'd understand that a role like this is more important for a guy who needs to relaunch a career than a guy who's banking the entire Riddick franchise." Danny flipped through the pages absently, trying to picture himself in the role. "You think I can pull this off?" "I think you're America's next action hero, Danny," Allan said. "And all we have to do to show everyone else that is to get you in this movie." After a few more pages turned, Danny ran a hand through his hair and looked up at his new agent. "I think this is going to work out well." -Ω- 11.01.07 - 6:59 p.m. "I didn't think we'd be doing this again, Danny." Blaine Blair sits behind the desk he'd been occupying at the Garden for the past week, a loaner from the on-site staff. His makeshift office - the same that would be used during Sunday's broadcast - is still in disarray and not yet ready for television. He isn't too concerned about his own appearances at the moment, but rather that of his talent. "How did it happen, Blaine?" "You tell me." "Fuck off. I mean the leak." Blair throws up his hands, annoyed. "I don't know, Danny. Chet just walked out on us, so God knows who he's been talking to. Besides, I'm not at the home office every week, I don't know who gets access to which files. Tyler Nelson insists that we keep a copy of all relevant paperwork for audits and such. Some intern could've found something...I don't know. It just happened." Blaine sighs. "And unfortunately I can't make you the same deal Chet did last year." Danny shifts nervously in his seat. He's waiting for the bad news. "It's in the public now, Danny," Blaine delivers. "There's no cover or saving face for either of us. I have to think about the company." "So what does that mean for me?" "Right now, I don't have that answer. But I can't offer you any protection or any safe haven. Now I have to put PRIME first." Danny's eyes stay locked on a corner of Blaine's desk as he listens to what the boss has to say. "Look, you have your reasons for what you did and I respect that. But understand that I have reasons for what I'm going to do, too." Remaining silent, Danny brings his eyes up to meet Blaine's. In a brief moment, the man returns to the timid intern he was just 365 days prior, but he swallows hard and perseveres, just like he did back then. "We're talking a suspension at minimum, Danny. Three months, probably. You'll get paid the base salary, but if I don't put the hammer down I'll get eaten alive." "So you're throwing me to the wolves in your stead?" "You brought this on yourself, Danny," Blaine insists, sitting forward in his desk. "Don't talk to me like you're a victim." -Ω- Saturday, Nov. 1, 2003 Zion, Ill. The hospital managed to maintain the hopelessly unsterile smell that a toilet bowl had in the moments before you puked. The smell that made your stomach churn at every whiff. Unfortunately for most of the clientele at the Midwestern Regional Medical Center, the stomach was turning long before they ever stepped inside. In a small side hallway, Dr. Mark Beck flipped through a chart next to an unmarked door. Content with his preparation, he knocked and opened in a fluid motion, clearly a skill he picked up in medical school. "Hi, how is everyone today?" Some meagerly murmured answers greeted him as he lets the door swing shut. Still flipping through the chart, he looked up and smiled. "Good, good." Proceeding to a side table and the rolling stool, he sat down and scooted over to the medical table, reading some final notes and then clicking a pen. "Ok, the nurse said all of your vitals checked out normal. In terms of general health, you've responded as well to the therapy as any patient I've ever had." Sitting on the table in a pair of faded jeans and a heavy sweatshirt to hide his frail form, Danny Ferguson smiled back at the doctor, his eyes weary and hollow. Running a pale hand over his hairless head, he nodded and fought back tears. "We're going to continue as planned - dial back the aggression a little. Your hair should start growing back in after a few months, and we'll keep watching the tumor to make sure nothing flares up. But you're already on the comeback trail, so to speak. So that's the good news." "There's bad news?" Danny's voice cracked a little at the question, sighing heavily at the prospect of not being out of the woods. "Minor," Dr. Beck replied. "Your muscles have atrophied to a considerable extent, and the tests the nurse did were a little discouraging in terms of recuperation. In other words, your strength isn't returning as fast as I'd like." He clicked the pen again and started scrawling on a piece of paper. "I'm going to prescribe something to give you a boost in that department. Nothing excessive, but it will help speed the process along. The nurse can walk you through how to administer it." The doctor looked up at Danny and smiled again. The actor looked nothing like his days on television, but things change, and he hoped that Danny would be able to come back from this. He may never go back to Hollywood, but that hardly meant his life was over. "It's going to be ok, Danny," the doctor stressed. Ferguson managed a weak smile and stared at the prescription he was handed. "Everything's going to be ok."
