Back Again For The First Time

2 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/2/07 It was late in the afternoon when she stepped in front of my camera. She walked into my eyes at once, all vibrant, shifting colors. A slim frame is just a coke habit away; a beautiful face as inorganic as the plastics holding it up So the fact that she looked like a real human immediately caught me. In her eyes, I could see her brain purring to life. Rarely did these ad girls fire off anything into their neurons beyond “make that look where it’s like your appendix just burst”. Why do you think “high-fashion” models have those cooing eyes and tangled facial expressions? But about her…heh, it really was about her. Between takes, she cautiously smiled at me. Who ever knows if those things are flirtatious, but I couldn’t take her offerings any other way. I was busy with the bustle behind this ad campaign; we were set to put her face in Times Square. Women like this were in and out of our offices every day. And despite my better instincts, I rarely treated them as anything but investments. Buy low; sell high; all that jazz My eyes caroused along the slender orchestrations of her body. She weighed me down, not allowing me to float into normal business musings. Lead weights kept my sight perched on her flimsy shoulders and long, winding legs. Like the learned taste of a familiar meal, she struck me as someone I had spent a past life with. Or maybe this one…if I could place her face. With my back turned to her as I bided my time, it was hard not to reach for the warmth of her gaze upon my back. Looking over a memo about new PR approaches, I scrambled for memories. It would have to wait until after her shoot was over. Business first…I guess you could say I learned that the hard way. Failure upon failure gets your head right. Colossus brushed her out of my mind for a moment. The big stage beckoned once again, leading me back by the hand. Don’t let anyone tell you that leaving this all behind is hard; leaving is easy. Staying away is hard. Someone else will tell you that this is what makes wrestling like women, to which I will retort: no, not really at all, that’s why one of them is grappling sweaty men and the other is too vast to explain but…not that. “Mr. Tsonda?” I spun around, having been caught unawares in my head. Raising an eyebrow, I found myself directly at eye level. When you’re under six feet, this is a risk you always take. “Omigod.” A silencing epiphany. “Is something wrong?” “Your name…is Alicia?” It was half-pronouncement, half-question. She cocked her head to the side, ignoring the rogue bangs falling into her eyes. “It is. Alicia...Berry”. Something lifted in her face, unlocked by the recognizance. “Interesting.” I could see a cache of distant confidence bubbling to the surface with her. A defense mechanism against the gaggles of equally confident males in her life, I assumed. “I didn’t think you’d remember me.” “Beautiful is memorable, what can I say?” Did she think I didn’t remember, or was that tone more of a wish? You don’t wanna be the one guy who owns a shared memory, good or bad. “I’m sure all your drunken hookups say that.” “Yeah, but most of them had to by their own drinks. Far as I remember, I both wined and dined you.” No time for the whole history lesson. I was a model, as was she, at the beginning of the decade. It will always be chic with beautiful people to get hopped up, optionally use protection, and try to create more little drool-covered beautiful people. And once, only once, that’s what had happened between us. Such is life. “I half-expected you to jump in front of the camera and steal my spotlight…or are you back with that boxing stuff?” At least there was no indifferent aloofness with this question. You’re crazy if you don’t think that I’ve caught shit from every person who’s ever been to Milan for doing the only job that’s more closet homo than male model. “Wrestling.” I couldn’t pretend like I don’t care anymore. Just like I ditched the adolescent pre-match nonchalance, I had realized last year on the road to Colossus that I needed wrestling far more than is healthy. “So then yes.” “Listen, I’d love to do this, but would you like to have dinner?” Best defense is a good offense. And deep down, who’s above their loneliness? Who has really escaped that need of community? Neither of us. “You know, get back on the wagon again together? Maybe get a little sloshed and make a sloppy toast to new beginnings?” She smiled. I remembered she was a top-notch drunk, touchy-feely and always grinning. “I’m not sure,” she wondered aloud, “it’s such a good idea.” I grinned back at her, knowing that Alicia wouldn’t start like that if she weren’t going to give in. “Hell, I don’t think you’re such a good idea. You’re my temporary employer, remnants of an old drunken mistake, and a professional wrestler.” For a second, I thought all the questions were coming. It was foolish to think that anyone hadn’t seen the news. Professional wrestlers dropping like flies, sometimes taking those close to them down the slick slope. I’d faced the music on it several times already. No, I don’t take steroids. Yes, I have Xanax in my house. But I don’t even own copies of the Bible. All of it only made me content to be in a financial situation where I didn’t need PRIME’s money. Hell, if six months from now, the government decides that we’re all mutant freaks who need to be put on the streets, fine with me. I’m so deep in so may business interests that I’ve hired and fired myself as a consultant three times today. I don’t need a bank-endorsed slip of paper with a dollar amount to validate anything. And yet… “Hell yeah. And a damn good one, babe.” * * * * * I’m tired of describing in depth how, when, and where I’m hammerfaced. From now, if I tell you it’s nighttime, just assume that I’m well on my way to a vicious hangover. Just like now. Vicious inner monologues about her are cutting me up, freezing me in times of indecision. Kiss her hand. No, don’t. She’ll want you back if you don’t try as hard. Don’t ignore her, though. Make sure you touch her hand. Don’t get all cheesy on me now. Grab her hand and hold that shit. Don’t do this business where you glance off at the first contact of skin on skin. Smile. And then all the voices grow quiet because dammit if those endorphins aren’t just out for ass. I go to throw the door closed, but my hands are otherwise occupied. I give in as she gently slides body weight up against me…then pushes, my back banging into the door to my condo as it closes. She lifts my shirt and throws it on the kitchen table. I return the favor, tossing hers onto the back of the couch. She’s as warm as I remember. She’s like that last flourish of fireworks on Independence Day, teasing a finale as the uninterrupted flashes trail on for several slow-mo minutes. And boy, she’s worth waiting all year for if you know she’s gonna be there. “When,” she clenches onto my bottom lip with her teeth, “was it?” She’s still kissing me, but her eyes are open, monitoring me. Weird. I stop. “Five years ago?” “Too long,” I blurt out, releasing the first thing that comes to mind. She returns with a small, private grin, hearing what she wants out of what she likes. For me, it’s been too long without a woman or without sex or in the dark. Just too long. She feels everything as I enter her, from my leg shaking slightly to the drop of sweat tiptoeing down my chest. What was once all passion and courteous foreplay becomes violent, choreographed intimacy. A curt moan hangs in the room. The muscles in my hands remember every molecule of her body. It’s good to be back. * * * * * I drift in and out of sleep, exhausted and naked, too fatigued to reach for the aspirin on my bed stand. Dreams shift their appearance, giving me Alicia, then delusions of grandeur about Colossus, then all the grey matter between. My head feels like a buoy bobbing softly on the top of water, light and barely even there. “You,” Alicia mumbles, half-asleep, “are amazing.” She’s panting, grabbing at her nose to try and stifle the tingle. “Not much of a human being…but infinitely better than last time. Good…” She trails off, tracing a line into my back with the tips of her guiding fingernails. I have nothing to offer back, for once. Glancing up at the clock, I can see the rest of the week unfolding in front of my eyes. So I close them and coalesce everything that’s still huffing and puffing around in my head…until it’s all just swirling black pools on the backs of my eyelids.
