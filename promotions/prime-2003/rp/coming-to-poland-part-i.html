Coming to Poland (Part I)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 18-June 20 Date: June 18, 2007 4:36 PM Location: Island Travel Agency, Bellerose, New York Walczak is seen walking down the street, he has a plain white t-shirt tucked into his jeans. His head is cleanly shaved, and his face is nicely trimmed showing off his signature goatee. He keeps a brisk pace as he makes his way towards the travel agency. The windows of the store are lined with posters showing various Carribean Islands and European locales. A bell rings as Walczak walks through the door, he is met by an eager saleswoman waiting to make yet another sale. “Hello sir, is there anything that I could help you with today?” The woman height pales in comparison to Walczak not even coming up to Walczak’s chest. She has straight blond hair coming down to her shoulders and is wearing a white blouse and a blue skirt. Walczak’s eyes open up as he clearly likes what he sees. “Yes, well, I was interested in taking a trip to Poland, and I just wanted to see what deals were offered, and if all my paperwork was up to date.” “I should be able to find something for you. If you just follow me over to my desk we’ll have a look.” Walczak follows the saleswoman to the back of the office. Her desk is cluttered with papers and pens surrounding the computer. She sits behind her desk, and Walczak uncomfortably in a rather small chair in front of the desk. “There are a couple of packages that we are currently offering, is there anything in particular you’re looking for.” “Actually before we talk about travel I just want to make sure that I don’t have to get my passport renewed, or fill out any special forms.” Walczak slides his passport across the table, as the lady picks it up with a somewhat confused look on her face. She opens the passport looking down at it. “Mr. Walczak, you do know that all the information is right here. It says that you don’t have to renew your passport for another four years, there was really no reason to ask me to look over it.” Walczak postures up in the chair trying to make himself a little more comfortable. “I know that you are not familiar with my past, and I know that my passport is nowhere near renewal. However, on numerous occasions, having the proper paperwork has not worked out for me. This time around I wanted the certification of a professional.” “I see nothing wrong with your passport, now did you want to book your flight today?” “The quicker I book this ticket, the better it is for me.” The travel agent once again turns towards her computer scrolling through the various options for travel to Poland. Walczak is now leaning back in his chair, not taking his eyes off of the travel agent. He stares at her up and down until he sees her eyes come up to look back at him. Walczak turns his eyes away, and slight reddish tinge appears on his cheeks. Walczak is somewhat embarrassed at the fact that this woman has caught him staring at her. He clears his throat, in an attempt to ease the tension. “Is there anything available?” “Which city would you like to land in?” “Gdansk, if possible.” “First class or coach?” “First class preferably.” The woman once again turns her attention towards the computer. Walczak makes sure not to make the same mistake, by staring directly into the ground. This prevents him from seeing the woman continuously gaze away from the computer and towards Walczak. “Alright Mr. Walczak I have an open ticket leaving tomorrow morning from JFK. You would have to stop over in Warsaw for a connecting flight to Gdansk, does that sound good.” “Not a problem for me.” “Now did you need anything else, a hotel or maybe a car rental?” “I have everything that I need in Gdansk, all I need is the ticket.” “The ticket comes to a total of $2,713.56, will you be paying by credit card, cash, or check?” “Visa.” “Alright, I will just run this through print your ticket, and you should be set to go.” The woman walks away and Walczak sits there almost sulking awaiting her return. Damn, I just made a complete ass of myself. There are few things in this world that rarely get me nervous or worried, but for some reason women always got my juices flowing. I just can’t interact with them, and it’s been so long since I have had a relationship of any sort with one. The travel agent makes her way back towards Walczak, and he is once again careful to avoid eye contact. This is a Walczak that we have rarely seen, he is almost afraid of the woman. It is odd that someone no more than half his size, can instill such a worried feeling of Walczak. “Here you go Mr. Walczak. Your plane ticket is printed out, and you are registered on tomorrow’s flight.” “Thank you.” Walczak grabs the ticket from the travel agents hand, and slowly gets up from his chair. He makes his way towards the door, but stops halfway there. He turns back towards the travel agent. “Sorry, but I don’t think that you ever told me your name.” “Oh, well, it’s Valerie Lo Truglio.” “Thank you for helping me out today Valerie.” “It’s what I get paid to do.” Walczak once again turns towards the door this time slightly opening it setting off the bell, but once again he turns back towards Valerie. “Listen, I’m going to be out of town for a little bit, but I was hoping that if you weren’t doing anything I could give you a call when I come back, and maybe we could get together.” Valerie twirls her long, blond, hair unsure of what to say. Walczak waits anxiously, but he soon realizes he has no chance. “I understand.” “No, no, I would love to get together when you come back.” “Great, can I get your number?” “Sure.” Valerie walks over to grab a pen and paper, and a grin of accomplishment comes across the face of Walczak. She hands the paper to Walczak and giggles as Walczak puts it away in his pocket. “I’ll give you a call when I come back.” “Looking forward to it.” Walczak finally makes his way out the door, almost strutting on his way back to his car. I didn’t expect buying a plane ticket to turn out this well. Who knows maybe things in America are turning around. Walczak steps into his Yukon Denali, and drives off down the street. This is the first time in a long time that Walczak has shown any semblance of joy in the United States. Walczak has never been a ladies man, and could not be happier with his latest conquest. Date: June 19, 2007 8:00 AM Location: John F. Kennedy International Airport “Would all passengers sitting in first class please make their way to the front we are about to begin boarding for LOT Flight 257.” The voice coming over the loudspeaker wakes Walczak from his slight slumber. The announcer repeats his message and Walczak makes his way towards the entrance. “Boarding pass.” Walczak digs into his pocket, pulling out a crumpled out boarding pass. He hands it to the employee, a short rather chubby man, with poorly combed over black hair, and thick glasses. His blue sports coat is covered with specks of dandruff, and he looks almost disgusted at Walczak’s boarding pass. He places Walczak’s boarding pass into the machine, but the machine kicks it back out immediately. “Is there a problem?” “You are supposed to keep your boarding pass in good condition, not all crumpled up. The machine doesn’t seem to be accepting it.” “Can’t you just type it in manually, I just wanna get on this flight, and relax.” “No sir, I can’t just type it in manually. I will try and straighten out, and put it through one more time.” The man slowly straightens out the ticket, and slowly places it into the computer. After a couple of seconds the computer has accepted Walczak’s boarding pass. “There we go. Enjoy your flight.” Walczak shakes his head not saying a word to the employee. He makes his way through the door, and towards the airplane. I hope that this is not a sign of things to come on this flight. How hard is that guy’s job? He takes the fucking boarding pass and he puts it through the fucking machine. It doesn’t matter now, I’m gonna relax in my seat hopefully go to bed, and wake up in… Walczak’s eyes open up as he gazes upon the scene he has just walked in on. He does a double-take of his seat number to make sure he is in the right spot. Nope he was right row 2, seat B. In the seat next to him is an all-to-eager male in his mid forties, already chatting it up with the female sitting in front of him. The man’s gray sweatpants, and soup-stained maroon long sleeve, makes one think that this man does not fly first class all the time. Behind him sit two young boys, no older than six. They jump around on their seats playing with their model airplanes, as their uninterested parents sit in the seats on the opposite side of the aisle, both talking on separate cell phones not paying any attention to anything their children do. Walczak stops a stewardess as she makes her way down the aisle. “Excuse me miss, is there a possibility that I could change my seat?” The stewardess takes a look at Walczak’s seat number, and than walks over to check her seating chart. “There are a couple of seats open in coach, but the seating chart shows that all of the first class seats are booked. If you wait until the flight takes off, and some first class passengers don’t show up, I am sure that you will be able to switch your seat.” “Thank you.” The stewardess smiles and continues on her way down the aisle. Visibly unhappy Walczak once again glances over his unfortunate situation. He takes his backpack off of his shoulder, and tries to find some free space in one of the overhead compartments. He finally finds some space about three compartments down from his seat. He turns around to head back, but now has to make his way back to his seat much like a salmon going upstream. The aisles are now flooded with passengers trying to get back to their seats in coach. Passenger after passenger hits Walczak with their bags, first the abdomen than the face, and so on. Finally a bruised up Walczak gets back to his seat, and lets out a sigh of relief as he leans back comfortably. However, just as soon as he closes his eyes there is a tap on his shoulder. “Hello there!” Walczak looks over to see that the slovenly fellow from before has finished his conversation with the person in front of him. Walczak had been hoping to avoid all conversation period on the trip, and the fact that this man has a wide smile, and a look of excitement on his face makes it even worse. Walczak closes his eyes once again hoping that the man will take a hint, but once again he is tapped on the shoulder. “You’re a big fella aren’t you?” Walczak lets out a grunt, and straightens up in his seat just as a stewardess brings over a tray of champagne and orange juice. “Would either of you gentlemen like some champagne?” Walczak grabs two glasses downing one right after the other, while the man next to him grabs a glass of orange juice. “Just orange juice for me. One glass of champagne, and I get a little lightheaded.” The man giggles oddly at his own joke, and the stewardess accompanies him as a chorus of laughter floods into Walczak’s ears. Another grunt is heard from Walczak as he shakes his head at the scene that surrounds him. “Miss could I get a glass of vodka and just keep them coming.” “Not a problem I’ll be right back.” The stewardess leaves, and Walczak realizes immediately that this once again means inevitable interaction with the man sitting next to him. “That’s quite a drink order this early in the morning. Well, at least you spoke I was beginning to think that I was going to have to sit in silence this whole trip.” “I was hoping to sit in silence this whole trip.” “HAHA!! That was quite a witty comeback. I’m sure that you will warm up to me in no time.” The stewardess comes back bringing Walczak two glasses of Vodka filled mostly with ice. She places them on Walczak’s tray and walks away smiling. Walczak sips on his vodka through a tiny red straw, looking uncomfortably through the side of his eye at the man who has yet to take his eyes off of him. “What is your reason for going to Poland, business or pleasure.” Realizing that the man is not going to get the hint, Walczak knows that engaging him in conversation will be better than just continually listening to him go on and on incessantly. “It’s my homeland, and I’m just going for a quick visit.” “That sounds like fun. I won a radio contest, and I picked Poland out of a hat. That way it took the thought out of it you know. If I made the decision on my own I would have questioned my choice day after day. Wrong. Right. Wrong. Right, and on and on and on. This way it’s kind of like fate. Like I was telling Stella, she’s the lady in front of us, it’s as if someone wants me to come to Poland. I… So much for conversation stopping this guy from talking. I just wanted to get to Poland quickly, maybe catch up on some sleep. I didn’t want to talk to some faggot for nine hours, not even having time to eat my meal, or get some drink in me. Look at this guy he must know that I’m not paying attention to him, but he’s still not stopping. The happy look, the constant moving of lips, this guy could go on for hours. “And that’s when the tip exploded and got all over my sweater. I really didn’t see it coming.” The movement of the plane brings a stop to the conversation as the man reaches to his side and buckles up. Walczak finishes off his vodka, and the stewardess comes over to take the empty glasses. Walczak puts his tray up preparing for take off. Walczak looks over at the previously chatty man only to see him with his eyes closed, and breathing in and out heavily. The sight brings a smile to the face of Walczak, and he takes advantage of the situation, closing his eyes and preparing for take off. The plane makes its way down the runway gradually building up speed until it finally gains enough to lift off the ground. Walczak begins to nod off as the plane rises into the air, only to be woken yet again, this time by cries coming from behind him. He looks to the side and sees that the man continues to breathe in and out as if nothing had happened. He glances behind him to see the two children crying hysterically. Walczak realizes that the change in atmosphere has probably caused the kids ears to pop, but when he looks over at the parents they remain almost unfazed. The only difference is that they have swapped cell phones for laptops. “Aren’t you going to do anything about your kids?” The children’s mother glances away from her laptop towards Walczak , but only momentarily, and returns back to her laptop. Walczak unbuckles himself from his seat and once again turns towards the children’s mother. “Your kids are having a fucking fit, and you’re not going to do anything about it.” “Excuse me sir, but I don’t appreciate that kind of language around my kids.” “It doesn’t matter what I say, because all sound in this entire goddamned airplane is drowned out among their crying. Now could you please get them to fucking stop crying?” The mother glares fiercely at Walczak, and closes her laptop emphatically as she makes her way over to her kids. All this time the father acts as if nothing is going on continuing to work on his laptop. The mother tries to comfort the children, but clearly has no idea what to do. Eventually the plane levels out as final altitude is reached. The children eventually stop crying as they get acclimated to their new environment, and their mother goes back to her seat with a look of accomplishment. Walczak looks next to him, and awaits being drawn into another conversation, but sees that the man must have fallen asleep during take off. He goes to recline his chair back and fall asleep himself, but is met with yet another obstacle. One of the children from behind him is now standing next to his seat. The boy seems to be the older of the two. He has a blond buzzcut, and crisp blue eyes. He sports a blue sweatshirt with a fire truck gracing the front. The boys jaw falls almost to the floor as he looks at Walczak. “Can I help you with anything?” The boy remains unresponsive, only closing his mouth to prevent any drool from falling out and landing on his sweatshirt. He finally wipes his mouth with his sleeve, and begins to speak. “Are you Polish Punisher?” “Do you think I am?” “You look like Punisher, and my brother said you isn’t, but I think that you are.” “You can tell your brother that he’s wrong because I am in fact the one and only Polish Punisher.” The boy runs off and you can here the whisperings of “I told you so” and “I knew it was him”. Then a scurrying of little feet grows closer to Walczak and now both of the boys appear next to him. “Mr. Punisher, could you please sign this for me and my brother?” “I don’t see why not. What are your names?” “Make it out to Ron and Don.” The boy hands Walczak a copy of the most recent Pro Wrestling Illustrated, with Lindsay Troy holding her newly acquired Universal Title plastered on the cover. As Walczak reads the cover he notices something that catches his eye. A tournament set to begin for a shot at the Five-Star Title. He flips through until he sees the article. Skimming through it he notices that he has been named as one of the participants. A smile comes to Walczak’s face as he hands the magazine back to the kids. “There you go.” “Thanks Punisher.” The two boys run back and talk about meeting one of their favorite wrestlers. Walczak lies back finally able to relax in the suddenly relaxed atmosphere. I guess that this flight isn’t going to be as bad as I first thought. First this douchebag next to me falls asleep, so I don’t have to amuse him by pretending I’m listening to his bullshit. Then I meet a couple of real wrestling fans, Polish wrestling fans, who really understand why I’m in this country to begin with. Granted they can’t be older than six, but you gotta get these kids young nowadays. Then to top it off I find out that I am gonna get a second chance at that Five-Star Belt. I had that pathetic excuse for a wrestler Easton Hall dominated. He was fucking done and that belt was mine until that pathetic cum guzzling sausage puppet Lisa Tyler got involved. Now I get something that rarely comes my way, a second chance. Walczak rests his head and reclines all the way back and finally dozes off after a turbulent start to his flight. Date: June 19, 2007 11:30 PM Location: Warsaw International Airport Walczak is awaken this time violently, by one of the stewardesses. He rubs his eyes and stretches his hands into the air letting out a loud yawn in the process. “Sorry I had to wake you from your slumber sir, but the plane has landed, and the gentleman next to you can’t get out. Everyone else has left and we have to clean the plane and refuel.” Walczak looks next to him and is met by the smiling man and an exuberant wave. The man begins to open his mouth, but Walczak quickly gets to his feet and makes his way back towards his bag avoiding any contact with the man. Even though he only has the one backpack, he waits until the man gets his bags and disembarks from the plane. He finally leaves the plane eagerly awaiting his first glance at his home country. He brushes past the flight attendants that remain on the plane, and makes his way out. He quickly exits the terminal, and only briefly gets a glimpse of Poland. He looks down at his watch and realizes that he is about to miss his flight to Gdansk. FUCK!!! I am gonna be late for this flight and then I’ll have to sit around here waiting till the morning. I only have a couple of days time before I have to head back to that hellhole across the Atlantic, and I can’t waste what little time I have in an airport terminal. Walczak rushes off towards the domestic gate, running over a number of bystanders to get to his gate. He arrives only to see the door closing. “WAIT!! KEEP THAT DOOR OPEN!!” The employee closing the door jumps back in shock, but Walczak’s scream does prevent the door from being closed. Walczak runs over to the desk handing the man his boarding pass. He places his hands on top of his head and tries to regain his breath. “Cutting it a little bit close aren’t we?” “Yeah, overslept.” The man places the boarding pass in the computer this time having no trouble with the machine. The man gives Walczak his stub, and he gets on the plane. Looking around he sees only four or five people scattered throughout the plane. Walczak sits in the first row, placing his backpack on the empty seat next to him. The nearest passenger is not even within shouting distance of Walczak, and again falls to sleep rather quickly. No more than forty-five minutes later Walczak is awoken by a rough landing. Looking out the window he catches the first glimpse of his hometown, Gdansk. The look upon Walczak’s face is almost giddy, like a child running down the stairs on Christmas morning. This time Walczak is the first to disembark from the plane. He makes his way out of the gate walking past the few people that wait for there friends and relatives. He walks through the automatic door, and gazes into the horizon. Finally back home. There are few places in the world that can bring me peace. I can relax here, and almost nothing could go wrong. When I am in America I feel like I am constantly looking over my shoulder. I trust no one there, and I feel like there is so little that I can accomplish. Here, here it is completely different. I treat everyone in this country as an extension of my family, and I feel that they do the same to me. “Tomasz, good luck this week. We are all rooting for you.” A man in his twenties yells to Walczak before getting into his car. Walczak waves acknowledging the man, something he would never have done in the United States. The man drives past Walczak, giving a thumbs up on his way out of the airport. These people are refreshing, they are truly respectful. Fucking Americans boo at you, they spit at you in the streets, even though you provide them with the one night a week that they can’t miss. You pour out you blood, sweat, and tears in the ring, and they still treat you like shit. That’s not the case in Poland. The first fan I meet here makes me realize that what I do is worthwhile. The only problem with coming here is the fact that I know that in a weeks time I have to go back, that I can’t stay here and enjoy myself. No worries though because in a weeks time all of my opponents inside that ring will wish that I would have decided to stay in Poland. Walczak takes a deep breath and makes his way over to the taxi stand. Walczak’s entire demeanor is different in this country over 3,000 miles away from his home in New York. The man who normally spends his days plotting on how to prove that America is the plague of the earth, has a free mind. His mind is focused on relaxation, and getting back in touch with his people, and while Walczak knows that this is only temporary he will be sure not to waste anytime while here.
