Date: ReV 211 Tokyo, Japan- 11:47 a.m. 10/16/09 She leaned against the doorframe, bare arms crossed atop modest breasts. Heat emanated from beneath the black cloth of her sports bra, radiating up into the taut muscles of her forearms. Her chest heaved a bit more than usual. A meticulously sculpted pack of abs flexed with each exhalation. Sweat dried into the cream colored skin. The perspiration did horrid things to her hair, pulled into a hasty ponytail just before she’d opened the door. Strings and strands of errant black escaped in all directions. Kairi was a lithe woman, though she worked diligently to keep from being thin. She was tone. Firm. Adhering to a strict workout regiment that even the most celebrated of professional wrestlers would have trouble keeping up with. She would know. Her beautiful, bright green eyes stared across the room at one now. “Please tell me you are not going to Thailand for a fight.” Her voice was soft. Not quiet. It carried easily across the twenty feet separating herself and the intended recipient. He had no trouble hearing the request. The perfect inflection. Her ever careful pronunciation of each word. She always spoke clearly. With purpose. Her words now were not an accusation, as they would be from the mouths of most others. There was no hint of sarcasm. No hidden meanings or agenda. She spoke softly, the words laced with genuine concern. Tyler Rayne did not respond immediately. Nor did he immediately look up from the mundane task of packing his suitcase. His hand hovered above the plain white tee he’d just pressed onto the top of the stack. Fingertips grazed across the cotton. Soft as the voice that floated across the room. Usually just as comforting. Now, though, the plea brought him to pause. He stared down at the white streak of discolored flesh across the top of his hand. A jagged shard of glass shoved through his hand… ages ago. Had to have been five years, at least. Of course he imagined it, but for just a moment he thought the scar had the faintest little glow to it. It might have squirmed a bit, too. A frown tugged at the corners of his mouth. Every scar was a reminder, but this one in particular bore a striking resemblance to the surgical remnants left six months ago. Vicious incisions on each of his legs… “Look, kid, I know you’re concerned, but…” “Concerned would be an understatement.” He sighed. “It’s just business, Kai. You know as well as I do those Bangkok brewers prefer dealing with me. Get a kick out of the brush with fame. We’ll get a better deal if I go.” Now it was her turn to measure a response. He was right, of course. There were certain markets across the world where his status (either as a male, a professional wrestler, or a scourge of the criminal underworld) won favor over her own striking beauty and unparalleled business acumen. Bangkok was the worst of these. It seemed the brewers there were enamored with all three potential incarnations of Tyler Rayne, an unfortunate prejudice she’d long reviled. However, The Katana’s Point was running desperately low on those particular ales, and it was impossible to uphold a reputation of providing premiere Asian alcohols when one did not, in fact, have such alcohols in stock. “Your legs--” “Are getting better.” “You’re still using the cane, Ty.” “Yeah, but at least I’m not in that fucking wheelchair.” He flashed her a lopsided grin. The grin, as a matter of fact. A captivating expression of roguish charm and unwavering confidence that twisted his rugged features into their most handsome and irresistible possibilities. When people spoke of love at first sight, when men bragged of leaders and heroes, when women whispered of their deepest fantasies and secret sexual conquests… it was this smile to which they referred. The twinkle in his eye. The perfect curve of his lips. The striking, but not blinding, white of his teeth. The slightest hint of a dimple in his cheek. Small intricacies and minute details that added up to the penultimate of facial expressions. There were two people within the long, storied history of Tyler Rayne’s life that were immune to the magical charm of this smile. One of them was the Japanese goddess standing across the way. The other was some haughty bitch named Lisa Tyler. “I’m serious, Ty. I can’t… I can’t lose you again.” She made no attempt to hide the pain. The edge of torment that gripped her words. Maniacal, almost. As if the thought itself were enough to drive her mad. Her face dropped along with her voice. She attempted to hide there, cheek pressed against her shoulder. He faltered for just a moment. She was the most composed person he had ever known. Unshakable. Until six months ago. Since then she’d been prone to similar episodes. Remembering how close… “You’re not going to lose me.” His palm pressed flat against the now closed suitcase. He leaned toward the nightstand, a concentrated effort not to collapse, as he reached for the rounded amber handhold of the bamboo cane. Well, at least it was designed to look like bamboo. His fingers wrapped around the golden orb, obscuring the view of the fossilized mosquito inside. It had taken quite a bit of searching, and even more zeroes from his bank account, to acquire this particular piece of Hollywood memorabilia. However, when he learned that he would indeed be in need of such an item, Tyler refused to accept anything less than John Hammond’s own walking stick. The cane dropped heavy on the floor. He leaned on it too hard. Needed it more than he would ever be willing to admit. The deep limp favored his left leg. Some had raised the question, though not within earshot of Tyler himself, whether he’d ever fully recover. The question had not been answered. It was an effort to cross the handful of yards separating them. He did so slowly, though he attempted to rush as much as his body was willing. The cane rested against the wall. She was so much shorter than him, even in this dilapidated state it wasn’t much trouble for him to wrap an arm around her shoulder. Pull her into a comforting embrace. She accepted eagerly, arms wrapping around his muscular frame. The side of her face pressed hard into his chest. A single tear dampened his black tee. “He killed you, Ty.” “Only for a little bit.” She laughed despite herself. A single, choked guffaw. He smiled and hugged her tighter. “I’ll be careful. Jules is going with me. You know she’ll keep me out of trouble. We’ll check in under a couple fake names, stick to the hotel area. Be gone two… three days tops. Nothin’ to worry about.” “With you, there is always something to worry about.” He smiled again. She knew him far too well. Bangkok, Thailand- 10:57 a.m. 10/19/2009 “That was, without a doubt, the worst breakfast I have ever had.” She spoke with absolute conviction. Her round face was set in a grimace of disappointment, dark eyes narrowed at the sulking reflection in the elevator doors. Tyler Rayne looked down at her, an old friend hired on as personal assistant now that he was not as… capable as he used to be. Her jet black hair was twisted up into a bun at the back of her head, held in place with two intricate sticks. She wore a red dress. Classic Chinese design, complete with the serpentine golden dragon. Juli Lee did not often present herself in such stereotypical fashion, but as she had commented earlier in the morning… she looked damn good when she did. The Chinese-American (former) paralegal was average in most all respects. Average height. Build. Cup size. She fit well into a mold of Asian women that had been produced for generations. A stark contrast to the usually striking women Tyler associated himself with on most occasion. In that, though, it was hard not to notice a surprising beauty in little ol’ Juli Lee. Despite her rather plain features and unremarkable nature, she did not pale in comparison to the drool inducing vixons that often accompanied him. In fact, she seemed to shine the more for the fact that she didn’t shine at all. There was a simple allure to Juli. A promise that none of the others could hope to offer. She was utterly and genuinely normal, other than the current employ, that is. Normalcy. The advantages of an average life. Something Tyler himself had never known. So very few of his friends did, either. In that, he found something astounding about her. Something to treasure. He followed the cursed glare of her eyes and caught the same reflection. Well, not exactly. First he took a moment to straighten his leather jacket. It looked just a hair off-center. Then he noticed the ridiculous pout on her face and found something else amazing about Juli. She was absolutely hilarious. Tyler burst into a fit of laughter that only served to deepen her sulking. Cheeks burned almost as red as the fabric of her dress. “Asshole.” “C’mon, Jules. The food wasn’t that bad.” “I wouldn’t know. There were too many strange people shoving their elbows in it. I never got a chance to eat.” He frowned. There had been an unexpected crowd gathered for his meal this morning. Fans, of course. He was cordial, as always. Never let it be said that Tyler Rayne didn’t enjoy time with his beloved fans. He had not, however, considered the impact this had on his assistant’s enjoyment of their complimentary meal. The elevator doors opened and he limped through. The cane thudded across the hall toward their room. Juli, though she let him leave the elevator first, quickly overtook his stride. By the time he reached the room, she had the door opened and waiting for him. She fluttered into the bathroom, a quick moment to freshen up before the last of these arduous business meetings. “What time do we have to be there again?” “Noon, Jules. We’ve got time.” He stood there in the foyer, waiting. There was no point in hobbling around the room. She’d only be a minute and he was far too slow to… Something moved. There in the corner. He was sure of it. Tyler took one staggering step forward, leaning hard on that cane, and peered into the shadows. “Tyler Rayne.” The voice was rough. Male, with a thick Asian accent. “Jules… be a doll and stay in there, would ya?” She did not answer, but he could hear her shuffling something across the counter. No doubt she had heard the voice as well. Tyler took two limping steps forward, placing himself in front of the bathroom door. He leaned to the right, shoulder against the frame. It was a relief to take the pressure off his left leg. It was also convenient that Juli could slide the small .38 into the palm of his hand without the intruder noticing. Tyler kept it there, arm curled up just at the small of his back, and waited for their unexpected guest to reveal himself. The man stepped from the shadows slowly, both hands raised in a sign of peace. His face was worn. Haggard. Creased with wrinkles and scars. He looked like old leather. His eyes were sharp, but thin. Heavy, perhaps. He wore a pinstripe suit that fit well enough, though it had probably looked better on him a decade ago. “I mean no harm. Come with… proposition.” His English could use a little work. “I’ve got a proposition. Get the fuck out of my hotel room.” “Just want to talk.” “Yeah, well, I’m not in the mood to chat with people who hide in the shadows. Generally they end up stabbing me. Or shooting me. Or both. It’s very bothersome.” “The Jade Scorpion is making move for Thailand. She want control Bangkok as well as Tokyo.” “Fail to see where that’s my problem, pal. Now, let’s go back to the part where you get the fuck out.” “We need your help, Tyler Rayne.” Tyler sighed. The man did not seem threatening… but he didn’t seem to be giving up, either. “You’re barking up the wrong tree, pal. I’m retired from the game. Got a nice little agreement with Jade. She keeps away from me and mine, and I don’t bother bringing the fucking wrath of god down on her little criminal empire. I don’t give a shit if she owns Tokyo. Or Bangkok. She can frolic naked through the streets of Beijing for all I care. I’m out. OK? This isn’t my life anymore.” “We do not know if we can stop her. She take Bangkok. Even more powerful. No one stop her.” “Again with the part where it’s not my problem.” “You trust her, Tyler Rayne? You think, when so powerful, she keep her word. Keep the truce. She will come for you, just as she comes for us now.” “You know about World War II? The Nazis invade Poland and piss off Russia. Lotta people figure if it hadn’t been for that, Hitler could have won the damn war. Instead, Russia joins the Allies and fucks their plans right to shit. So for the last time… I don’t give a shit about the rest of the world. So long as she keeps her greedy little hands off of Poland. Savvy?” “I… understand. Unfortunate we could not come to better terms.” “Just for you. Now if you don’t mind, I’m running a bit late for a meeting.” The mention of their business endeavor brought a timid little girl from her hiding place. Tyler gave one significant nod toward the door. Juli snatched the .38 from his hand and skittered out into the hall. “I trust you can find your way out the same way you came in. If you’re here when I get back, I will kill you.” “It has been pleasure, Tyler Rayne. As expected.” Tyler scoffed. “Sure, kid. Sure.” Bangkok, Thailand- 2:55 p.m. 10/19/09 “Kairi’s not going to be happy, you know.” Juli led their way down the hall once again. Tyler limped along behind at his now customary (and devastatingly slow) pace. “What’s not to be happy about? I got us an even better deal than I’d expected. Plus we met those kids at breakfast. The ones in the band. I have procured both beer and entertainment for our nightclub. You ask me, that’s one hell of a successful business trip.” She rolled her eyes. “That’s not what I was talking about.” “Yes, well, if you’re referring to ol’ Leatherface, there’s really no need to make mention of that, now is there?” She paused just in front of the door, a horrified realization crossing through her face. “You don’t think he’s still here, do you?” “Sweet Jesus, I hope not. Kai will be really pissed if I have to kill someone today.” That was not the reassuring answer she’d been hoping for. Her hand hovered above the card reader, unsure. Shaking even. Somehow that made him smile. Normal in all aspects. Even fear. He himself could use a bit of that. He wasn’t as immortal as he’d like to think. The cane was an ever present reminder of that. Juli steadied herself enough to unlock the door, but hesitated to proceed. He took the initiative and stepped through the darkness first, switching on the light with his cane. Nothing. The lights did not flip on. Tyler grunted and took a heavy step forward. Juli rushed in, afraid to get too far from her protector. She paused just inside the door, not quite closing it in case there was a need for quick escape. He waited. Watching the shadows. There didn’t appear to be any movement. No sounds save for Juli’s quickened breathing. Another step forward. Another. He limped past the bathroom, pausing after each step. Still no sign of danger. This eased him a bit, and Tyler took one more step forward. His foot stumbled over something on the floor. Something heavy. The cane came down quickly, but slipped in something slick and flew from his hand. Tyler’s foot caught beneath the object. His leg twisted, and he fell forward. Juli gasped. She rushed for the bathroom and, without thinking, flipped the light. Surprisingly enough, that one worked. She gasped again. Tyler pushed himself up to a sitting position on the floor. His legs were too weak to stand without some assistance. He looked down at the mass of unexpected weight on the floor. The object upon which he had tripped. It was a man. Or at least it used to be. Now it could be more accurately referred to as a corpse. The pinstripe suit was splattered with darkened spots. A slick puddle of blood oozed out from under him. His hand, rough and just as leathered as his face, clutched tightly to the handgun that had failed to save him. The entry wound, Tyler was guessing nine millimeter, burned through just below the shoulder blade. Probably opened a decent hole in his chest, which would account for the puddle of blood. “Strange. I don’t remember leaving this laying out.” Juli lurched once. A hand rushed to her mouth and she bolted into the bathroom. It was only a second later that she began to wretch. “Oh, come on. The joke wasn’t that bad.” A sudden sense of foreboding took him, and Tyler looked up to the hotel room door. The one Juli had left just slightly ajar for her escape. Now, a set of fingers wrapped around the edge of the door, followed very closely by the barrel of a nine millimeter. Another unexpected guest pushed quietly into the room. This one was not native. American, most likely. Tussled brown hair. He entered with a lopsided grin, though it wasn’t nearly as endearing as Tyler’s. The man’s face was too chubby. Eyes too close together. He wore a growth of stubble, couple days old. Again, though, he failed to pull off the rugged look as well as Tyler. On this brown haired man, it just looked… haggard. Unkempt. He pushed the door close with the sole of his shoe. Triumph gleamed in his eyes. “So you’re the infamous Tyler Rayne.” “That’s the rumor.” The man laughed. Genuinely. He thought he was in control. He was overconfident, of course. But in that, the response amused him. An authority figure who’d either seen too little or too much defiance in his time. Tyler was guessing the first. The Fed was still trying to work the angles. He wouldn't shoot. Yet. “Lovely to hear from you, Dawk, but I’m kinda in the middle of something. … … … What? … Tink? … You’ve gotta be fucking… … … Where are they taking him? … … You’re on your way? … Yeah. Chandler… He moved faster than expected. His legs might not be what they used to be… but his hands hadn’t lost a fraction of their speed. He didn’t even bother to remove the gun.
