The Preggo Trilogy: Sacrificing for the Greater Good.

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: CWMC Final b the past... (Three Months Ago) Ahhhh, Lake Superior. The site of Primetime Central Headquarters. Purchased from the Government in the late '90s, this former Military compound has seen much remodeling work since coming into ownership of the wrestling powerhouse. The outside still looks the same: industrial and plain with every square inch coated in concrete. The inside however has been redesigned and refurbished, taking on the appearance of any regular office building. Like all large corporations, it takes cohesive components to create success. PTC is no different. The building is sectioned off with different departments, all fulfilling their own minor purpose in the grand scheme of things. Located on the ground floor are the Entry-Level workers, all coming and going with a high turnover rate. While few of the employees are mainstays, all of them would rather bitch about movies like Spider-Man 3 and Batman Begins or whether or not Cro Cop is 'the shit' than focus on their daily routines. Those that do manage to escape mediocrity are allowed onto the second floor, restricted mostly for Employee Moderators. These people aren't much better than those lower on the ladder, because they spend just as much of their time bitching with the regular employees (except Dean; he loves anything that exists). Then, the cream of the crop--located on the top levels of the HQ compound are the Corporate Administrators. These offices are shared by a group of people who don't really do anything aside from organize Infinite Gauntlet and Unified Championship bouts then take all the credit for the success that PTC receives (can't forget Pete, that lazy bastard). Somewhere in between the second and third levels is a very important facet to Primetime Central's dichotomy, one that could be considered the former Interfed's life-force. Designated the 'Event Coordination' team, these employees stick to their corner of the building for each day's majority. Each team member plays an integral role in organizing the events and tournaments that take place in the league. When a Unified Title Match needs a promotion to host, these guys are on it. When an Infinite Gauntlet Open Invitational needs an arena booking, these guys are on it. Right now, Jay Phoenix is planning to kick off 2007's first big Tournament: the Chris Williams Memorial Cup. And these mother fuckers? They are on it. Some people are in their cubicles, on the phone, leasing booth spaces for merchandising. Others are searching for the best venue in Flagstaff, Arizona, that would be able to contain such an event. Not a single person is wasting time in the face of such a crushing deadline, with opening round match-ups scheduled to take place in less than two weeks. Well, maybe one person is wasting time, but 'waste' is such a relative term these days. "I'm sorry, but my hands are tied. There's no way I can help you out there," Betti Soboloski says in an unpopulated corner of the office. She consciously watches out for her co-workers, hopeful that no one notices her. "COME ON, CUZ!" The Illustrious Face-Eater yells, prompting Betti to shush him rather rudely. "Who got you that internship in the first place, huh? If it weren't for me, you wouldn't even HAVE this job." Betti takes immediate offense. After all, the path she took to become Assistant to the V.P. of Event Coordination was anything but a cake walk. "For your information, it was your reference that made the company decide to throw my resume at the bottom of a huge stack. I had to call in a favor from one of my University Professors..." "You can't just help me out this one time, Beej? Come on! We go far back. Remember in '86 when we kicked Lil' Ernie off of the top bunk at Lake Powell? Remember how funny that was?!" Betti chuckles at the memory but she still unable to help her cousin. "I'm sorry Adam, but I can't get you a Unified Title Match. I don't even work with the Uber Judge. I've only met him twice, and every time he speaks he makes me feel stupid by the way he talks." Facey slams his hands on the wall, causing a few workers to glance in their direction. "FUCK, BETTI. WHAT THE FUCK AM I GOING TO DO WHILE I'M WAITING FOR LAZY ASS PETE TO START UP GTT?!" "There's a Infinite Gauntlet Open Invitational coming up soon." "BO-RING. What are you guys working on now?" "Um...," she turns towards her co-workers,then back to Adam only now her face contains a worried expression. "We're working on the Williams Memorial Cup..." "Tournament! Awesome!" "...But all the seeds have been filled." "FUCK THAT SHIT. Can't you, like, erase someone's name and pencil me in?" Betti slaps Facey on the shoulder. "I'd get fired if I got caught." "So? I'll give you some of the prize money when I win." The two cousins share an expression that tells them both they're getting nowhere slow. "All of the Flight Itineraries have been sent out to the participants. They're already registered and-" "Who's all in?" Betti looks confused in response to Adam's vague question. "THE TOURNAMENT! Come on, who's in the fucking thing?" "Well," she stretches out the syllable, rifling through a stack of papers she holds close to her chest, "Off of the top of my head there's Jacob McKail, Kimbusa, Dusk..." "Gay. Gay. Gay." "...and James von Doom, Tsuyoshi Tanaka, Tsunami Oni..." "Really Gay. Gay. Gay, literally." "...Jason O'Neil, 'New Shit' Bobin-" "ARE YOU FUCKING KIDDING ME?! I SHIT ON NEW SHIT! I THOUGHT THAT MOTHER FUCKER WAS SUSPENDED FOR BEING A BITCH AND NOT PAYING THE BOUNTY HE HAD ON ME AT LAST YEAR'S GTT!" By now, Betti and Adam have forgotten that they are supposed to be quiet. A few of her co-workers have already taken note and plan to use her being unproductive to their advantage, like the office-sharks that they are. "That's ridiculous! If you want to bitch about this, you can go to the Complaint Department; it's on the first floor." "But what's the point of having a contact in the company if you can't exploit it?! Come on, Betti. Just go scratch off that fagot from FUSE's name and pencil mine in--no one will notice the difference! You might even get the key to the city for committing a public service!" Betti has grown frustrated. She starts resting her hands on her hips while shifting her weight from one leg to the other; if she knew she'd be standing this much today, she'd have never worn heels. "No, Adam. Some of these people could really benefit from this." "What? Jason O'Neil needs another chance to drop the ball?" Facey retorts. "Seriously, Adam. There are people that can use this prize money. Like Foster Nackedy? He has a wife and kid to support. And Brock Shepherd? He's got a pregnant girlfriend at home. And Adrian Bastille has a four-year-ol-" "I DON'T GIVE A FUCK ABOUT NO WIFE AND KIDS AND NO PREGNA--wait a second.... Pregnant girlfriend.....?" A fiendish smile creeps across Adam's face as his eyes roll towards nowhere in particular, a sick fantasy suddenly manifesting in his head. The moment is awkward enough that it causes Betti to become nervous. Then, Facey starts reaching into the front of his pocket. "Adam! Go to the bathroom if you're gonna-" But she notices that he's withdrawn a money clip, quickly silencing her. Unleashing a few hundred dollar bills, Facey lifts his gaze to see Betti's eyes light up. The portraits of Ben Franklin begin to mount, and Betti finds herself more and more susceptible to her cousin's "charm." After all, who isn't a sucker for money? "Betti, I want you to tell me where I can find this Preggo..." ---- Brock Shepherd is in a state of admiration as he watches his girlfriend prepare for bed. Six months pregnant and wearing nothing more than a white satin nightie, April thoroughly counts the number of times she runs the brush through her shoulder-length locks. It's a process she undertakes often, but only now has Brock stopped what he is doing to observe. With her mind staying occupied, Brock is allowed to fully appreciate the warm presence she provides. All of her idiosyncrasies and all of her mannerisms never became lost to him. If anything, the time they've spent together has only strengthened the foundation of their love. At this point, Brock can't imagine his life without April. And in a few short months, they'd be a family. Yes, three months from now they'd be moved into their new, six-figure dream home. April fell in love with it at first sight, and Brock felt compelled to make the down payment with his GCW salary. Three months from now they'd have a child to raise. Whether it is a boy or a girl, Brock doesn't and will not care. He'll love the child either way and he'll make the necessary sacrifices to become a great father. Three months from now he hopes they'll be married. And tonight is just another chance to take them one step forward. Brock knows this; the anticipation is killing him as he toys with the engagement ring in his pocket, purchased earlier this week. It isn't lavish by any means, but after the massive dent their down payment on the home put in his pocket, he reasons that the jewelry is more than suitable. Brock holds off in popping the question for the night, however, because he knows that asking at this time would do more bad than good. April is more empathetic than most women, especially in her current state. To worry her about their financial issues would be detrimental to her health at this point. Instead, Brock decides to wait until they're in a more stable situation before taking that next big step. It took the last of his credit and then-some to be able to purchase that ring. They'll have to scrape by for the next month with what they can while waiting for the next pay check to roll in. Funds are scarce and not likely to present themselves. Brock's finding it consistently hard to make the money he needs to support April in a legitimate fashion. Luckily, the Chris Williams Memorial Cup is about to get underway, with a hefty cash bounty coming to the winner of every bout. Every participant that makes it to the next round automatically gets a $10,000 check. Those that get to the Finals receive $40,000 each. The winner, when it's all said and done, receives an additional $100,000. Winning this tournament would put Brock and his future family in a great position, and he's not about to let that opportunity go. His first round opponent hasn't even been announced yet, but whether it's a big name or some schmuck will not phase him. They're just another stepping stone to him. A footnote on the path to 200 Grand. April turns away from her reflection in the mirror to glance at Brock, who lays smiling on the bed. "What?" she asks him. "Huh?" he replies, confused. "You're staring." She playfully rushes over to jump on the bed. "What're you staring at!?" "Ah! Careful, Hon." He gracefully leans forward and embraces April, his hands slowly crossing her large belly. "Answer the question!" she insists by jamming the brush handle uncomfortably in his hip. He curls to his side in defense and crawls away, jumping off the bed and standing to his feet. "Our girl is getting so big." He smiles, staring at her stomach. She pretends to be offended in the interest of being playful. "Since when do you know it's a girl?" "I can just tell." Brock says with confidence, again leaning on the bed to place a hand on April. "She's so playful when I check on her; she's daddy's little girl." She places her own hand on top of his, rubbing the back of it gently. "It is a boy, for your information!" "Oh is that so? And how do you know that, missy?" "Because. I had a dream." She speaks without the slightest hint of doubt in her voice. Just as well, she could have taken the ultrasound and seen it. "We had a son and he was beautiful." Brock can only reply with a warm smile. The ring in his pocket is sizzling, burning a hole, but he must keep it restrained. How he'd love nothing more than to give April his name, but the time isn't right. Instead, he tilts his neck to kiss April's pregnant stomach at the peak of the arc before saying softly, "See you soon, son." "Brock? You know what this means, right?" Brock doesn't know exactly what it is she's referring to. He jumps back to his feet and heads to the dresser to put on a t-shirt and replies, "Probably. Refresh my memory." "We talked about names. If it was gonna be a boy--remember?" "Yeah, I remember," he says, pulling a Tool hoodie over his head. "I want him to be named after his father. I want him to be named 'Brock'." "I know, Hon." "Now I remember when you said you didn't want there to be a Brock Junior but I think it's really important that our first born takes his father's name and we still have to decide on a middle name and I think it'd be really cute if he-" "April, Ape--Calm down! It's not good to stress yourself with things like this." After a deep breath to think it through, he finally continues, "Brock is awesome. Brock is perfect." Once again at her side by the bed, he leans over and kisses her passionately. She is left smiling, and as he pulls away she can't help but let the words "Brock Aardman, Jr." slip from her mouth. The name strikes a chord in the father-to-be. It only serves to add to the almost-overbearing sentiment that from now on, he is responsible for another life. It makes what he has to do so much harder than it needs to be... "I'll be back." "Where are you going?" "Gym. Need to be in shape for this Tournament coming up." April smiles and demands he kiss her one more time before leaving. "Don't stay out to late." She says in his ear as he pulls away. "Don't worry." He says as he makes his way out the front door. "It'll be like I never left." ---- With the hood of his black sweatshirt pulled tight over his head, Brock walks down the rainy streets with his hands covering the product he holds. The cotton on his hoodie is too thin to protect what he's carrying from water damage, so he's forced to cup it tight in his palms. Crossing State Street from where his car sits in a parking garage, the GCW signing quickly darts across the road as on-coming traffic nearly takes him out. Car horns and obscenities are abound but Brock ignores them all. Times like this, a man must keep his head clear for anything that can happen. Rounding the corner and crossing the entrance of a local Gold's Gym, Brock quickly sprints past the front door and makes his way to the back side. Sitting in the alley, under an awning that protrudes from the building, are two large and rather scary-looking body-builders. Both await Brock as he approaches quietly, expressionless faces etched on the both of them. Brock turns to make sure they're alone and that no one has followed him. Reasonably safe, he removes the package of vials from his jacket pocket and hands them to one of the body-builders. They both respond with a 'thank you' then bestow him with an envelope, containing a thick stack of bills inside. Brock nods and shoves the money into his pocket before turning back down the alley he came from. The body-builders return inside the Gym from the fire-exit, and just like that the deal is done. It's not the fact that he knows he's a better person and that he shouldn't degrade himself to such lowly acts. It isn't even the fact that he doesn't want his girlfriend to suffer for the choices he's making. The pain that Brock Shepherd feels inside is purely a result of his yet-to-be-born son, nestled inside April's stomach. For a second, Brock finds weakness in his knees and almost slips on the pavement. He falls to a single knee, and suddenly the rain begins to pour torrentially. It beats upon him harder than before, adding difficulty in getting back to both feet. "You could use some help." The voice of the stranger strikes him with a second wind, and he bursts upright to face the unknown. "Who the fuck are you?" he says to the man suddenly standing before him, who is prepared for the weather under the protection of his vinyl coat. "Someone who thinks you can utilize your time more efficiently. Hasn't anyone told you that drug-deals are so immature?" Cautiously, Brock turns to glance at the alleyways and dark corners of State Street. "You sneaking up on me?" "Nah, I don't sneak--I prepare. Allow me to introduce myself; my name is Richard Adamson. I'm simply a man who can benefit from a service you can provide. A service that will rewarded handsomely." "Nice to meet you, Dick, but what the fuck do you want?" The man smiles at Brock through the rain, extending his hand for a friendly shake. Brock is hesitant to return the gesture, but something about the moment sets him at an odd peace. Whether it's the mystery the man presents or his concern of Brock's welfare is irrelevant. All that matters is Brock takes the man's hand. "If you're looking to make money, maybe I can help out your family. You'll be able to ditch the petty steroid ring and move on to something more... desirable." "Like Ecstasy?" Brock waves his hand in dismissal. "I'm not a drug-dealer, man. This is just something to keep-" "No, I don't want you to deal any drugs. See, the substance I need is far too potent to be dealt on the street. If made available to the masses, its users would rise up in a frenzy and plunge our entire society into a hell which we could never escape." The man reaches for Brock's hand and quickly pulls him close, forcing himself in a position to whisper in his ear. "...Have you ever heard of Dimethyltryptamine?" ---- DMT. It's nothing Brock has ever heard of before, but after the long car-ride he's just taken, he doubts he'll ever forget about it. Richard Adamson, his new "employer", was sure to pass on every piece of information he happens to know about the drug, if you could call it a drug. Apparently the most potent hallucinogenic substance on the planet, Dimethyltryptamine (commonly known as DMT) has been studied and used over many different ages. The Mayans of the early Seventh Century extracted it for their rituals in which they'd speak with village elders who have passed on. Dead-Heads from the '70s found it useful in their recreational activities. To this day, scholars and professors from all over the world have examined the ramifications that DMT presents. One professor from New Mexico theorizes that it's the key to unlocking the knowledge within ourselves about our own species. Others say it is the chemical responsible for dreaming and "life-flashes" in near-death-experiences. Some even believe it is integral in the human race's communication with extraterrestrial beings. Whatever the case, it's all too weird for Brock. A drug that makes one dream that they are speaking with aliens is not something he has been dying to try. But when the money is good, the money is good. If Richard would pay him the amount he promised upon completing the job, Brock may not even have to make it to the second round of this Tournament. He still must complete the job, of course, which is partially the reason Brock and Richard have driven this far. "See," Richard Adamson explains to Brock as they pierce through the darkness, "This DMT stuff, it's in EVERYTHING. It's in you, me, little Timmy from down the street and his alcoholic mother who lets you poke her in the crotch while he watches cartoons. And not just that. You see that cactus out there?" Rich points to the darkness; Brock turns to look. The rain has since stopped but that hasn't made seeing in the dark any easier. "I don't see shit." "We're in Arizona, man; I think it's safe to assume that there'll be a fucking cactus somewhere out there." Richard snorts and wipes the snot from his face. "Anyways, even that fucking cactus in the middle of the pitch-black, yeah. Even THAT produces mother fucking DMT. It's everywhere, man! God intended for us to have this shit!" Brock can't help but become a bit nervous as Richard's enthusiasm grows. He can barely keep his hands on the wheel and often removes them while explaining things, as if to give a physical property to his words. "So why don't you just get your DMT from, y'know... everything?" "Pft." Richard scoffs. "Just because we all make it internally, doesn't mean it's as easy as eating your own excrement. Hell, it probably won't even be that potent. But, with the right concentrated dosage, you could theoretically speak to Jesus for a whole fucking day if you take it correctly. But there are only a few places a guy can find these things ripe with DDT." "And those places are?" Brock asks. Richard responds by slamming on the breaks, causing a cloud of dust to surround them. Turning off the engine and the headlights, Rich simply turns to face Brock and then points out the wind-shield. "You're in one of them." Richard jumps out of the car, leaving the confused Brock to follow. Surrounded by the nothingness of the Sonoran desert, Brock can't help but find himself in a state of confusion. "Where the fuck are we?" "Unfotunately," Ignoring the question, Richard continues his rant as Brock follows him. "Unfortunately, the most common producer of DMT in this area ain't no bigger than a balled up fist." Reaching into his jacket pocket, Richard withdraws a picture and a flashlight, turning on the bulb and shoving both in Brock's chest. After taking a moment to let his eyes adjust, Brock examines the picture closely to see... "A bullfrog?" "That ain't no regular bullfrog, son. That there is Bufo Alvarius, a.k.a. the Colorado River Toad. Its venom is deadly, but once it's dried up, it becomes the most potent DMT resin one can get without a chemistry set." "Yu... You're fucking kidding me. I thought all of that toad-licking shit was just a joke." "'Fraid not. If I were joking, why would I bring you out to the middle of the desert? Like I was saying, Unfortunately, Colorado River Toads are hard to see. Even more unfortunate is the fact that they like to come out at night. Probably the shittiest of all facts is that the best place to find these mother fuckers is right here: the Tohono O'odham Indian Reservation." Brock is still a bit hazy about what he needs to do; the details have never been fully explained. "So, wait, you want me to find one of these things?" Richard nods, shedding only a hairline of a smile. "Man, this could take all fucking night." "I've gotten luckier than that. Besides, the fuck you care for? I'm the one paying you because I'm too lazy and would rather get stoned. YOU'RE the one on easy street. All you've gotta do is find the thing and you get fifty grand." Brock still seems unsure about the entire ordeal. It seems ridiculous to go through all of this trouble and pay all of this money for what is, at the end of the day, a simple frog. Or toad. Despite the minor details, it surely seems dangerous and worth a second guess. Brock hasn't even agreed to go through with the job; he merely promised to listen to Richard's proposition based on the fact that $50,000 is at stake. One car ride later, and this stranger is shoving him into the desert with a flashlight and a Polaroid of an organic drug-factory. "Come on, man. You wanna go back to handing off steroids or are you going to make some real money?" "...How illegal is this?" "Devastatingly." From inside his jacket pocket, Richard withdraws a roll of bills. They are then tossed to Brock, who examines the funds before placing them in his pocket. "You get the rest when you find the frog." Without waiting for an answer, Richard climbs into the car. He situates himself for a night of resting by reclining the seat and turning up the radio. "If I fall asleep," he says while digging through the console in search of his marijuana pipe, "Just wake me up. I'll be chillin' here all night." Not even a word exits the mouth of Brock Shepherd, who now more than ever feels completely void of options. Opportunity is spitting right in his face. The sounds of his footsteps treading across the desert are enough of an answer to Richard, and Brock begins his trek into the great unknown with nothing more than a flashlight and picture in his hands. Moon and stars all hide behind the clouds. In time, even the dome-light of Richard Adamson's SUV fades into the darkness. Aside from the pale beam that shines from the flashlight, Brock is all alone. But in his heart, despite the emptiness, he truly believes he's doing something good. He's doing this for his family and, more importantly, for his child. If only he would have stuck with his gut. "Hey, Chief Big Cloud," Richard says into his cell-phone, "Yeah, it's Little Eater." Taking a moment to puff from his pipe while hearing what the other line has to say, he nods his head a moment before once again speaking into the receiver. "The package has been delivered. Check in his front right pocket." And without so much as a goodbye, the phone clicks shut.
