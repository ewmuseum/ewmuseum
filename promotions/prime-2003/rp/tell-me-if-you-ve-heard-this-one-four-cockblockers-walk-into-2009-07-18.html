Tell me if you've heard this one, Four Cockblockers Walk Into A Gym...

18 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/18/09 A long, black, Cadillac Escalade pulled up to the curb, the twenty six inch chrome rims seemed to sparkle under the dim glow of the street lamp. The low bump of bass was barely audible with the dark tinted windows rolled all the way up, but the four African American gentlemen in the SUV had turned it down for a reason – they didn't want the people inside to know they were coming. “You sure he's still in there?” the short stocky guy taking a hit off a blunt asked, before he let the smoke drift out from his mouth so he could breathe it in through his nose. The white tee shirt he had on made him look even darker than he really was, as hard as that is to believe. His name is Karl Bates, but his boys call him Hootie. He's been running the Screwston Soldiers street gang for the last seven months after his cousin, Sandman, got busted with enough crack in his house to fill the San Andreas fault. “Yeah, he's in there,” Krazy Day, De'Ton Walters, looked back over his shoulder at Hootie. With a black hoodie pulled up over his head, you could not make out any of his features, save for his yellowed toothy grin. “Mother fucker's always sitting up in his office trying to find the next lost soul to save. You ask me, ain't nobody around here worth saving.” “Fuck you, bitch,” Jayson Johnston, the tall lanky kid behind the wheel with the corn rowed hair, slaps Krazy Day on the shoulder. “I'm practically a choir boy compared to your crack-head ass.” “Fucking choir boy over here,” De'Ton motioned toward the one they call Dunk. “All of a sudden he wants a little Jesus in him. Did you done forget you're about to pop a cap in this fool's ass.” “Both of you mother fuckers is talking way too much shit,” Hootie interrupted the small of exchange of words before they ruined his high. “Grab the pieces out the back, then go and smoke this son of a bitch before I have Gauge take you fools out for wasting my damn time.” “Shit, I don't need a reason.” Gauge, the caramel skinned brother with the clean fade just smiled as he looked back at Hootie through the rear view mirror. “Whatever,” Dunk replied with a shrug of his shoulders. “Let's do this shit.” The four men step out of the vehicle, the low hum of the motor keeping a steady beat going for the drama getting ready to unfold. * * * * * Officer Caleb Dawson sat in the booth by himself. A cup of steaming hot coffee was in front of him on the table, and a clear glass of ice water had condensation covering it to the left of that. Having pancakes before going on his graveyard shift had become a bit of a ritual for him. Well, it was a ritual for the man that was somewhat of a mentor to him, Jack Connors. The two had done this every night before their shift for five months before Connors was killed in the line of duty three months ago. He had responded to a late night break in at the Wright Pawn and Jewelry Company on Westheimer, thinking it was another smash and grab of whatever was closest to the front windows. It wasn't. Connors had rolled up to the curb in front of the pawn shop without even thinking about surveying the surrounding area. The getaway van was parked in the alley, but the driver was cowered in the shadows keeping an ever watchful eye on the streets. When Connors stepped out of his patrol car, the lookout didn't even bother to fire a warning shot. He opened fire with a semi-automatic weapon and hit Jack with four shots to the chest before he even realized what was happening. The coroner claimed one of the first few shots had killed him, but that didn't stop someone from being sure he wouldn't be getting up again; a gunshot from a different caliber weapon had been fired to the back of his head. The move was uncalled for, and though no one had been apprehended – there wasn't even a suspect in the case – it was decided that the second shooter had a distinct hatred for law enforcement. Though, the case could be made that it was a hatred for any type of authority. Still, Dawson had grown very fond of the ritual and made sure never to break it. While he did not consider himself to be a superstitious man, he did consider himself a man of respect. This is how he paid his respect to a fallen comrade. It helped that the pancakes in this place practically melted in your mouth. “More coffee, Caleb?” Caleb turned his attention away from the cup of coffee he had cupped between both hands to find Annabelle Edwards there with the pot of coffee in her hands. “Hot and dark, just the way you like it.” She told him with a wink. Caleb wasn't certain, but the two seemed to be exchanging flirtatious comments for the last month. There was no ring on her finger, and even with the loose bun in her hair she was still a bit attractive. He had never seen without her uniform on, and once mentioned it to her as innocently as he thought he could. She still managed to twist his words around and tell him that she wasn't that type of girl. They both laughed about it. He didn't apologize for the comment, and she didn't seem to mind, but neither one of them acted upon the comment any further than that. “You know me too well, Annie.” Caleb leaned back in his chair so she could top off the cup. It did not escape his attention that the top two buttons of her blouse were unfastened, so when his eyes darted in that direction for a better view he found himself to be pleasantly surprised with the fact that her bra was a pink lace one you'd most likely find at a Frederick's of Hollywood. He always took her for a WalMart type of girl, but he was very glad he was wrong on that one. “I'm sure there's a few things I don't know about you.” “All you have to do is ask,” he told her with a grin, her mocha skinned breasts with the strawberry wrapping still fresh on his mind. “I wouldn't mind getting to know you a little better myself.” There it was, he had thrown out the line. All she had to do now was catch it. “Are you asking me out, officer?” There was the playful grin he had grown fond of over the last few months. “I-I guess so.” “You guess so?” The grin was gone, and he knew really quick that he did not give her the answer she expected. “Look, it's been a long time since I asked a girl out,” he exhaled softly. “I'm a bit rusty.” “I'm just teasing you,” she laughed. “I'd love to go out with you.” “How does tonight sound?” he asked, a bit more confident since she told him yes. “Sounds good,” was the answer she gave him. “I'm actually off tomorro-” The sounds of gunshots filled the air, not only cutting her off but taking his attention away from her as well. “Oh shit,” he jumped out of the booth and nearly stumbled over an elderly gentleman that had dropped to the ground as soon as the first shot was heard. “Call nine one one, now!” “Sam!” she screamed to the man in the kitchen, but he already had the phone in his hand. * * * * * Tyrell Dawkins was under his desk, beads of sweat on his brow as he inhaled and exhaled heavily. He wasn't sure what had caused him to look up from the paperwork on his desk, but he thanked the Lord that something had made him do it. He never would have noticed the four men in black sneaking around in the gym, making their way toward his office, until it was too late. The shots started to ring out as soon as he had slammed the door shut. He had barely been able to duck behind the desk when the first blast of the shotgun splintered a majority of the wood on the door. He started to fumble through his keys, searching for the one that unlocked the back door to his office. He wasn't sure if anyone would be waiting for him out there, but he felt that they would have opened fire if there was. “Come on out, you fucking snitch!” “You ain't getting out of this mother fucker alive, Marquis!” Marquis? Who the hell were these guys and what did they want with... Oh shit, The Sandman. Tyrell had completely forgot about Marquis agreeing to tell them when Sandman's next drop was going to be. How had they found out? It really didn't matter anymore, or it wouldn't if he didn't get the hell out of there as soon as he could. With the key in his hand, he dashed toward the door and fumbled around to get the key in the lock. “Shoot the fucking lock!” It wouldn't be long before they got through the door, but as he twisted the key he felt confident he would be out in the alley by then. BOOM! The shotgun blast rattled the walls, as he twisted the knob on the door and pushed it open. “Shit, there's another door!” A splinter of wood from the door lodged itself in his arm as another round of gunshots were fired in his direction. He stumbled against the opposite wall, but quickly regrouped before the shooters reached the door. He was running down the alley when the first person made it to the door. “There he goes!” He could feel the heat from the bullet as it whizzed past his head. He doubted they would keep missing, but he prayed that he would be able to make it around the corner of the building before they could stop running long enough to actually aim. “Get down!” The voice didn't come from behind him, but from a man that ran around the corner of the building with a gun in his hand. Tyrell saw the man point the gun in his direction, so he dropped to the ground just in time to hear the man scream again. “Freeze, police!” Another round of gunshots echoed against the walls before the police officer could open fire. “What the fuck are you doing, Dunk! That's a fucking cop!” The officer managed to squeeze a few rounds off of his weapon, but Dawkins saw the mist of blood floating through the air as the bullets ripped through the man's chest. “I got his punk ass!” “Let's get the fuck out of here!” “What the fuck did you do?” The words all came at once, and Tyrell couldn't make out who was saying it. All he knew was that the footsteps were headed in the opposite direction. His eyes, though, they were fixated on the man who had just saved his life as he fell backward onto the unforgiving asphalt. He crawled over, hoping that no one that was after him had stayed behind to make sure there would be no witnesses. It took a bit of effort to make it to the officer, and what he found when he got there made him gasp out loud. Officer Caleb Dawson was coughing up blood, a bullet wound through his throat had blood pooling up in the curve of his neck. The sound of tires screeching at the other end of the alleyway was quickly followed by the sounds of sirens approaching in the distance. The first officer would get there and call in that an officer was down and for an ambulance to be sent as soon as possible. Tyrell sat there on the ground and prayed that it would arrive in time.
