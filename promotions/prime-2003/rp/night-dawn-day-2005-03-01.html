Night - Dawn - Day

1 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/01/05 Sometimes Jim liked to make small-talk with the boys when he fixed them up. He’d ask about their families, and watch their eyes light up as they tried to explain to him how beautiful their baby girls and boys were. He’d shoot the shit about anything, just to keep guys talking so they don’t have to think about their dislocated shoulders, their broken fingers, their deep, sagging gashes still bleeding from a match forty-five minutes ago… …but Jim wasn’t striking up conversation tonight, no sir. The kid he was fixing up didn’t look like he was in the mood to chat…just staring off into space, face bunching up occasionally when Jim hit a tender spot. Jim always called ‘em like he saw ‘em…didn’t see any point in doing otherwise…and he had made up his mind as soon as he saw Nova. He could bandage the kid up real nice and send him packing… …but Jim had the distinct impression that there was something inside of him that was gonna need more than just burn salve to heal. “Never shall I forget that night…which has turned my life into one long night, seven times cursed and seven times sealed. Never shall I forget those flames which consumed my Faith forever. Never shall I forget that nocturnal silence which deprived me, for all eternity, of the desire to live. Never shall I forget those moments which murdered my God and my soul and turned my dreams to dust. Never shall I forget these things, even if I am condemned to live as long as God Himself. Never.” - Elie Wiesel, Night CHAPTER XV ... I don’t have a catchy opening line to smoothly transition into my own analysis of what happened at Culture Shock. I'm at a complete loss for words, and for those of you who know me, you know that's very much out of the ordinary. I searched my soul for the most poignant, dignified, resoundingly cunning response to your display of gross disloyalty and cowardice, and what I came up was this: FUCK YOU. Eh, to hell with it...I've never been one for subtleties, anyhoo. Big Poppa…Razor…I can’t believe that… …wait. Let me take that back. Razor, you douche-bag, I never trusted you, not when I heard you were following me to PRIME, and I DO mean following me, not when I heard you were backstage somewhere, and not when you came out and played it up like it was all water under the bridge. Never. I took it with a grain of salt, and the assumption that if anything went wrong, I had my best friend there to back me up. Again I’m shooting myself in the foot with all these damn assumptions, aren’t I? Maybe I’m being a little too hard on myself here…there should be allowances for some types of assumptions… …the fire-fighter rushing headlong into the inferno assumes that his team will follow suit. The quarterback assumes that his offensive line will do everything they can to protect him. The trapeze artist assumes that when she finishes the twirl forty or fifty feet in the air that her partner will be there, arms outstretched to make the catch. I assumed that my best friend would defend me the way I’ve always defended him. Silly me. How, BP? Why? Why now, after everything we’ve been through, why now start buying into Razor’s bullshit? And I know that’s what this is…Razor’s bullshit, self-serving, have-to-control-everything, manipulative-ass mentality infecting you. He saw you down on your luck, out of work, sans Sapphire (and I’ll get to that later, believe me), and capitalized on the perfect opportunity to do the impossible… …get one of us to believe him. Have you forgotten about Empire Wrestling already, Poppa? Have you forgotten how dumb-founded we were when Razor turned on us, accusing us, of all people, of abandoning him? Have you forgotten how YOU felt? Well, being the friend that I am, do allow me to remind you: God damn. God damn Razor Blade. He’s lost his mind. He’s lost his wife. He’s lost his friends. But he’s kept his ego in tact. And maybe his ego was the thing that was the most important to him anyway. After all, would he have cared about us if we weren’t all there, bowing down to him every time he added another win to his undefeated streak? And would he have given us up to erase those two little blemishes on his otherwise perfect record? You bet. Those aren’t my words, Poppa…those are yours, from your first match with Razor Blade in August of 2003 in Empire Wrestling. Re-read them carefully. Look at all the things you accuse Razor of…how are you any different now? You’ve become what you used to hate, you fucking fair-weather friend. Your life goes to shit…a period out of work, a girlfriend of years and years who high-tails it on you…so what? You take it out on your best friend? Jealousy is all it is, and I’m here to tell ya, it’s pathetic as hell. Sapphire left you. Shit happens. But do you deal with you? Hell no, you don’t…because that would require maybe, just maybe, taking some responsibility for what happened. No, instead of examining what went wrong with your own relationship, you decide to set about ruining mine. You really are a loser. Hypocrisy is the name of the game, Poppa…and you’ve won First Prize! Maybe you have forgotten how much of an irrational, embittered, washed-up hack Razor was and is, BP…but I haven’t. I never forget that kind of betrayal. You’ve lost the war, man. You a year ago would hock a loogie in your eye if you saw yourself today. And don’t think I’m passing it off as Razor’s master plan and you in the lead role of “pitiful victim,” here, either. YOU were the one who contacted Liza and told her to infiltrate my trust. YOU were the one who told her to fabricate sexual assault and battery charges against me. YOU were the one who ruined my family and my livelihood. You made your bed, and now you’re gonna fucking sleep in it, if I have to staple the sheets to your corpse myself. … I’ve lost the title that I wanted so badly to champion, and for second time in my four-month tenure with PRIME, my only allies have turned on me. I've got nothing. I’ve got fuckin’ NOTHING. Motel 6, Chicago, IL, a few blocks from O’Hare International Airport… Ring, ring, ring! Ring, ring, ring! Ring, ring, ring! Nova lies on one of the twin beds in the room, staring down at the generic floral pattern that most likely blankets every bed in the complex. He takes a drag from his cigarette and ashes it on the carpet. He stares over at the phone as it continues to ring. Ring, ring, ring! Ring, rin- It stops, and Nova flicks his cigarette into the garbage can. “Good,” he says aloud, “probably fuckin’ room service lookin’ to squeeze a few extra dollars out of their patrons…” Emotionally and physically exhausted, Nova reaches up and turns off the lamp that hangs above his bed. He slides under the covers and rolls over on his side, ready to let sleep wash over him. … Ring, ring, ring! Ring, ring, ring! Ring, ring, ring! “Oh, for God’s sake…” Ring, ri- Nova rolls over and yanks the phone off the console. “I don’t want any food delivered, I don’t want to order any porn, and I sure as hell don’t want a wake-up call in the morning! Are you satisfied!?” “Chris?” Time stops. It feels, to Nova, like someone has knocked the wind out of him, or set a very heavy object on top of his chest…like a baby grand piano. “Chris? Are you there?” “Ar…Ariel?” Nova manages to get out. “It’s me,” Ariel confirms, “how are you?” “I’ve been better,” Nova admits, “my God…I can’t believe I’m talking to you.” Silence on the line for a moment… “Chris, Liza contacted me,” Ariel says, her voice getting shaky, “she told me everything…I…oh, shit…I’m so sorry, Chris…I-” “Ariel, don’t…,” Nova tries to interrupt, but Ariel continues. “Your daughter wants her daddy to come home,” Ariel goes on, “and so do I…so do I.” Suburban Detroit, MI, four and a half hours, and approximately 284 miles later… Nova quietly shuts the door to the Ford Taurus he rented from Enterprise in Chicago, and leans against it, lighting a cigarette as he stares at the house he hadn’t seen in four months prior to this very moment. Amazing…I’ve dreamt about this since the divorce, but now that I’m here, it’s almost hard to go inside. It’s like I have to prepare myself or something. Nova begins walking up the driveway, duffel bag in tow. He approaches the front door, takes a few quick drags from the cig, and flicks it away. Here goes… Knock, knock. Nova waits a few seconds, and knocks again. Still no answer? He tries the knob, and to his surprise, the door opens easily. Nova’s heart pushes its way up into his throat as the living room comes fully into view… …and nothing has changed. The couches still form a square around the fireplace. The rooster lamp still provides damp light from the coffee table. The painting of Nova’s father crafted by his mother still hangs over the fireplace… …but what his eyes settle on isn’t a piece of furniture or sentimental knick-knack… …it’s his daughter, sitting upright on the rug with a doll in her hands. Nova lets the bag hit the floor and drops to his knees on the rug. His mouth hangs open and a tear runs into his beard as he watches his daughter shakily stand up and stumble a few steps over before falling into his chest, her tiny arms reaching toward his neck. Nova looks up from his daughter’s mop of curly brown hair to see his ex-wife standing in the doorway to the kitchen, her shoulder-length dark brown hair hanging over her face, almost obscuring her eyes, more beautiful in Nova’s eyes than ever before. He hoists Lulu up as he climbs to his feet. He stares at Ariel for a moment, before going to her. The sun rises over the industrial expanse of Detroit…DAWN. Nova sits in a chair on his front porch, slowly rocking forward, a cigarette dangling between the index and middle fingers of his right hand, watching bright orange creep over the horizon. The neighborhood is blanketed in a foggy gray. He waits. “Hey, it’s Nova,” Nova says into the phone. “What can I do you for, Nova?” the voice on the other end of the phone inquires politely. “Got time for a detour to Michigan?” Nova asks. “Hmmm…well, I’ll have to move a few things around on my schedule, but I may be able to clear a slot,” the voice replies, a hint of sarcasm detectable in his tone. “Good,” Nova says, a grin forming on his face. Nova puts out his cigarette as a taxi pulls up on the curb. At 6’7” and 285 lbs, the trench-coat-clad behemoth that climbs out of the backseat immediately stands out as a freak beacon in Suburbia. Throw in the clown make-up, and it’s time to get gawked at. Ozric Mortimer shuffles up the driveway and climbs the wooden steps to the front porch. Nova stops breaking a nugget up on the cover of Tom Wolfe’s The Electric Kool-Aid Acid Test and motions for Oz to pull up the other chair. The corn-shuck frame creaks loudly as Ozric takes a seat and begins rocking back and forth. Nova holds up both ends of the rolling paper, and in a few quick motions of his hands, produces a tightly-rolled joint. “Impressive,” Ozric says softly. “I’ve been doing this for a while,” Nova replies with a grin before scribbling something on the side of the spliff and extending it to Oz. “Hmm,” Ozric murmurs, scratching his chin, “eh, what the hell…don’t have my drug of choice, so why not?” He takes it from Nova, and produces his own lighter to spark it. “And that would be?” Nova inquires, raising an eyebrow. “Methamphetamines,” Ozric says, his eyes lighting up at the mention of the word as he takes a long drag and holds it in. “Fresh out of meth, I’m afraid,” Nova says with a shrug. Ozric takes another hit and holds the joint up to see what Nova scribbled on it. “Hope.” He hands it back to Nova, blowing smoke out of his nose as he clasps his hands together. “There is hope, Oz,” Nova says firmly. “Is there?” Ozric poses rhetorically, “Fuck…I dunno. The resistance is broken.” “Well,” Nova begins before taking a draw off the joint and handing it to Ozric, “can’t say I didn’t see that one coming…notice I wasn’t exactly signing up for the Enlightenment. I had a feeling it would eventually crumble.” “I didn’t,” Ozric replies bitterly, “but maybe I was just naïve to think we could fight back.” “You can,” Nova assures him. “Can we?” Ozric replies, looking Nova in the eyes, “sometimes I don’t think so. Most of the time I do…but there are still those times when I feel like it’s all fucked, anyway.” Nova stares at his unlikely ally, recognizing his inner struggle symbolized, either intentionally or unintentionally, by the half-white, half-black make-up that adorns his visage. “You say ‘you,’” Ozric points out, “still not up for facing down the demons, eh?” “My apologies,” Nova says with a shrug, “but unfortunately, I have my own problems.” “So I’ve seen,” Ozric replies with a nod before adding, “it’s hard when your friends turn on you.” “He’ll get his,” Nova spits angrily, “trust in that.” “I do,” Ozric agrees before holding the joint roach up to the light…it’s burned down past the writing. “You can give that to me, and I’ll toss it in a bowl later,” Nova says before taking it from Oz and setting it on the table. “Whew,” Ozric sighs, “thanks for the smoke. Is it too early for booze?” “Never,” Nova replies with a grin. “Good man,” Ozric says with nod before removing two Heinekens from his coat and handing one to Nova. They hold them up and clink the bottles together. “Looks like we got our backs against the wall,” Ozric says. “It would appear that way,” Nova replies. “To hope?” Ozric proposes. Nova nods. “To hope.” The sun climbs from the horizon into the sky. Night is dead, dawn is gone, and the day is here.
