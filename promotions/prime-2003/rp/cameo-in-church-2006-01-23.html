Cameo In Church

23 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1/23/06 “And now…our guest minister for the morning is the Minister Chandler Tsonda.” Those are the words that bring me to the pulpit this morning. I’m wearing a ridiculous set of full, boastful red robes that I had cooked up especially for my evangelical debut. Of course, I haven’t exactly come up through the farm system to do this. * * * * * I had been waiting for entirely too long when my connect finally showed up. I’d be referred by “some guys who know a guy” and that was enough for him. He had the goods and I’d been jonesing all week for something that only he could supply. “How much will it cost me,” I sputtered, aware of the severe illegality of my actions. Then again, I never professed to sainthood, only a clear-cut superiority over you, jag-off. Wiping at my nose (all the subtlety of a Psymon “I’m a freaking addict, do you get it?!” promo, I realize), I tried to suppress twitching fingers, but they knew as well as I did that I needed to feel the product in my hand. “For what you need? It’s gonna be a little pricey, man.” He was almost apologetic, but this was just business to him. Dealing was life and that meant it was as serious as any desk job, maybe more so. “I’ve got it covered, whatever the cost.” I needed this, for the sake of the eternal temptation…and for the fact of this promo’s existence. He looked over his shoulder nervously, before sliding me the small package. Then, as the rules state, we both walked our separate ways. In my hand was raw, uncut power. That’s right: I had just been ordained as a minister on the black market. * * * * * Taking a deep breath for clarity’s sake, I proverbially throw out the first pitch. The crowd is laughably formal. The fact that fat women in their floral print dresses (and hats with brims bigger than their entire wearer) get away with their fashion choices is one of the true mysteries of this church business, but then again, a lot of this religion garbage is rather peculiar. “Welcome, clientele, to the swankest Godly get-down to ever hit the chocolate capitol!” That certainly wakes up all the women who have just been skimming along and doing their nails and the men who have been passing the time by trying to find where in the Bible it says that masturbation is actually a sin. Eyes are wide open, persons are slack-jawed, and just like I prefer it, all eyes are on me again. “I come to you from the exotic East, where our ways are very different from our own. However, we are all connected by one common thread: hatred of the dyslexics.” Behind me, I can tell that the Reverend is about to flip his lid, but is being calmed by several of the other ministers, who attribute my statement to my status as “one of those foreign fellers.” I don’t know why they think I’m foreign...but if it’ll make my little jaunt in the church a little more fun, I’m not going to correct them. “And I stand before you because there is a great evil in our world today.” The people are absolutely rapt now; you start talking about a great evil and maybe, just maybe it means you’ll start blaming black people for problems. And hey, if they’re the problem…might as well put those buggers back into chains, right? The reverend behind me has settled down now. “That evil is the evil of the false prophet.” Well, it’s not Negro bashing, but it’ll do to start talking about false prophets. I’m already a pro at this because, let’s face it, talking in church is more or less giving an in-ring promo. You can’t tell me Jerry Falwell wouldn’t have been a sweet Paul Bearer-esque manager for some beastly wrestler. It’s a match made in Heaven (get it? Oh fuck you, that’s a great pun). “The false prophet is everywhere around you,” I say in my best drawl. Call me crazy, but religion seems best served with a Southern accent (heat about two thousand years, serves one billion). “He seeks to touch you with just a fingertip, a thimbleful of his soul of darkness. After just a momentary touch, he will rule your soul.” The feeling is strange, talking in front of a crowd without being booed. I’m not sure I like it. There’s no reason to continue; I could just end the speech now, get my applause (do they do that in church?) and walk off stage. I guess I just don’t get the whole “being a good person” schtick. “Many of these false prophets will disguise themselves as the faithful, even!” This tale of brimstone has genuine oohs and ahhs from all over. Some of the more faithful women put their flabby arms around their young children, physically shielding their spawn from the devil’s influence. As I’m thinking up my next theological gem, I get an unexpected response. “How will we know who is a false prophet?” A voice from the herd stands out above the rest, wondering out loud about my nonsense. Then again, I’m sure it’s the same blather one hears every week in the sham known as organized religion. “Homies, let’s not jump the gun.” Just stall a little more. “You see…” I start, looking out on the sheep with the inescapable knowledge that I’m the only thing in their minds. “There is a man, a wrester by the name of Hoyt Williams.” For the first time in the history of ever, there is a mild pop for Hoyt, although in church, a mild pop just means subdued happiness and dumb grins (just like the general activities of Imperium). “You may think that his brand of God is the only word, but, my friends, this is…sheer buffoonery!” I can do the whole Great Awakening rhetoric-filled fiery speech thing, too, Hoyt. “False prophet barely begins to lift the veil on hateful individual!” Behind me, the two ministers seem a bit perturbed again. However, I’m about to pull a mega-swerve on any nay-sayers. “Because, if my memory holds true, Philippians 4:13 reminds us that “I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me.” Much of the crowd nods, as if their silly little Bible needs personal reaffirmation. “Do you know what this tells us, broheims?” I take the silence to mean no. “You see, when I first stepped into the PRIME ring, I dealt the beast a mighty blow. As it is said in Smithsonian 33:12, “j/k about the whole meek inheriting the earth thing, beating the champ will seal the deal.” Confused stares from the crowd and more rustling behind me, but I don’t leave time for any doubt to sink in, immediately getting to the point. “I’ve slain this false prophet and I shall again, delivering swift justice. In nominie patrie, et filie, et spiritus sancti. Word.” I step off the pulpit and quickly hurry out of the building, with the intention of getting the hell out of Dodge once the jig is up.
