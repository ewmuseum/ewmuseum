Selling Out : Part One - 'The First Day Of The Rest Of My Life.'

March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: March, 2005 Selling Out : Part One - 'The First Day Of The Rest Of My Life.' Right, let's play a quick game of catch-up, shall we? The one with the lilac hair and abrasive attitude, that's Karina Wolfenden. Wrestler, Snowboarder, Free Runner, Sky-Diver, Base-Jumper, your all-round 'throw herself off something high' kinda gal. Not the most sociable of creatures, she's cursed with being as stubborn as a particularly set-in-his-ways Ox... a trait she inherited from her father. You'll usually find him refereed to by Kari as, 'that adulterous bastard.' So, yeah, she basically told him - in her less-than-eloquent way - to get over his 'fucking mid-life crisis' and get out of her life. One problem. He technically funded her life. Well, not 'technically' - he just plain DID. And that's where we pick things up. Independence has finally, truly been achieved... and in escaping the asphyxiating claustrophobia of her father's shadow, Karina had a stack of bills to pay, a credit card recklessly used on the assumption that it would be wiped clean every month, and rent on her overly extravagant apartment to make. All this on one of PRIME's lowest salaries, and whatever Indy bookings she can pick up. As Morpheus so succinctly put it: 'Welcome to the real world.' One week ago, almost exactly to the hour, Karina Wolfenden was approximately 12,000 feet above the apartment she was now lounging in. Almost at home so high above the world, her heartbeat was barely elevated, and not a bead of cool, petrified sweat even attempted to force itself to the surface of her lightly freckled face. Beneath her, life was being played out, with wages earned and conversations partaken in. Traffic formed the arteries, veins, and capillaries of the landscape, expelling carbon dioxide to engorge the trees and plants that formed the tissue around them. It was at times, places, like these that Karina was most comfortable. Almost detached from life itself, able to observe rather than partake. As she jumped, it felt liberating. Freed from the anchors of her feet, offering up her lithe body to be ravaged by the air it displaced, hurtling down to the ground. Without the goggles, Kari's focussed emerald eyes would have been dry-blasted, while her lips tightly pursed to combat the ripples she felt wash over her cheeks. Gravity was her greatest enemy, and although the jump was surrendering to it, allowing its restrictive grasp to yank her back down to earth, it was with the finger rather than with a handshake. Releasing the smallest canopy she could talk the jump centre staff into giving her, Karina closed her eyes, absorbing that split-second feeling of inertia throughout her body. As naturally as walking down the street, Karina made her rapid and winding descent down to the target, wishing she had more than just the 2 eyes gifted at birth to take in the sights. Known as a fearless daredevil, she saw it more as just living. So why was a woman who shocked even hard-to-please wrestling fans with death-defying moves, would jump from anywhere she had at least a 50/50 chance of surviving, and who looked on injuries as reminders to 'do things a little differently next time', nervous, hesitant about opening an envelope? Well, when it's the credit card bill that you assumed was being paid for you, and includes a 4 day break in Morocco, near $1500 of bike parts, an overly extravagant $1000 for a custom re-spray, and another pair of long distance flights - amongst others - most wouldn't exactly be tearing the envelope to shreds in unbridled eagerness. Despite the aura of worldly independence that she oozed from every flawless pore, she'd been as funded as a Gulf state in the 80's. Slumped across her kitchen table, still wearing the pyjama bottoms and vest - which seemed to overcompensate in fabric for Karina's humble chest - that she woke up in, Kari held the envelope in both hands. She had no idea what amount would await her, even though she was the one who'd run it up. It was almost a feeling of guilt working its way to the surface, reminding the K-Wolf that she barely even listened to prices before handing over the plastic. A light, staggered thud was then heard as one of her black felines - Betsy - hopped onto the table from one of its chairs, then strode over to her stressed-out owner. Plunging her head down to rub it against Kari's arm, the cat then turned, tickling the K-Wolf's face with her tail like a feather duster, nearly making her sneeze. 'Hungry?' Kari then asked after a quick splutter, holding the envelope out to the bemused feline. 'They'll buy 'my cat ate my bill', surely?' Betsy just sat, staring blankly at her owner. 'You and Psylocke can hunt for your own dinner, right?' she then enquired in jest, resting her chin on the table as her hand ran over the black feline’s sleek fur. Those watching PRIME on TV might think that given the high production values, and the usually full arenas, that all the roster would be reaping in a high salary. For the most part, they'd be right. Those who committed fully to PRIME, accepted the exclusive contract, signed over their likeness rights, and made themselves available for all manner of publicity events were well rewarded. Those who would only commit to a loose, open contract, and did nothing more than turn up every week for 2 hours at the most, weren't. Hands up who can guess which of those Kari fits into? She should have been worrying about Tsunami Oni, she should have been worrying about how she was going to afford to travel to the next couple of events... but nothing could break through the mental block of the bill she clasped between her fingers. 'If I faint,' Kari remarked to Betsy, 'don't try to wake me...' An index finger then wormed its way in under the envelope's seal, then ripped it open, leaving the cause of such anxiety there for the taking. Letting out a laboured sigh, Karina removed the bill, and set her eyes upon the balance, boldly printed in the deepest of blacks. 'Fuck,' was the only word that seemed to do her position justice. Reluctantly, she scribbled the amount from the bill onto the scrap of paper that was starkly laid before her on the table. It was the equation of her current life, what she needed to pay, attempting to be balanced against what she could earn. Maths was never Karina's thing. She was wary of anything which had a fixed answer, that couldn't be argued against no matter the passion of the objector. Cold, arbitrary numbers. No allowances for happiness, no consideration of emotion. The intimidating values would remain the same whether she cried, smiled, or belted out 'Singing In The Rain' naked across the rooftops. Well, that last one would probably slap a hefty fine into the outgoings. 'What's the going rate for a kidney,' she then mumbled to herself, coming to terms with the balancing figure. Trying to bring her debt down, even if it was just for her own sanity, Karina looked at the variables. Scrawled below her PRIME income was her appearance fee for PTC's Seven of the Best... higher than most tourney receipts, but still reflective of a pot split 14 ways. 'And to beat Oni,' she mused, checking the literature before adding the prize for 1st round victory to her income. 'Ugh, either they're selling seats at $5 a throw, or they're making a killing on this.' A rare look of stress was etched on Karina's face as her hand propped up her forehead. Lilac hair dropped over her face, creating a comforting barrier between herself and the scribbled debt for a few seconds, before she blew a breach into it, then scraped the mane behind her elven ears. 'And to win it,' the K-Wolf continued, never one to ever take wins for granted, but overruled by the situation. 'At least drops it below wrist-slashing levels.' With the debt reduced further, but not nearly enough, Karina took another numeric leap of faith, taking her optimistic Seven of the Best performance a step further. 'And to win the Global Title,' Kari sighed, knowing everything was all dependant on more factors than she wished to consider... the most unreliable being, unfortunately, herself. 'If Chris Storm wasn't bullshitting when he bragged it all around RPW...' With a trio of victories, not a single one assured in the slightest, Karina was still looking at a hefty sum with a mocking negative sign in front. It would have been even worse had she not clinched a lucrative fWo payday the night before by taking on Aimz, the most she'd ever been paid for 8 minutes work. A few more, even after the extra travel expenses, would nearly clear what she owed. But, as always, a little indiscretion added a garish black cross next to the name of Karina Wolfenden. And for that 'little indiscretion' see; getting into a backstage fight with Amy Campbell at fWo's Ruahh~! Brought in for a guest appearance, it could have been the K-Wolf's chance to add an extra weekly gig to her schedule, probably earning as much as the rest of it put together... and more. But put two polar opposites such as Kari and Aimz into a war of words, add a hurt knee for Wolfenden and a bruised cheekbone for Campbell, and you're not going to get a friendly chat. 'Looks like you couldn't handle the step up to the big leagues,' Aimz remarked backstage after their match, marking her patch with verbal territorial pissings. 'But don't worry, I'm sure all your geeky little net fans will still GUSHING for you...' Despite her less-than-diplomatic way of putting it, Aimz was right. Karina had made an error, was punished for it, failed to learn, and was knocked out cold live on fWo TV. Having to play second-fiddle to the K-Wolf in PRIME, the Red Raver couldn't hide her spiteful delight at besting Wolfenden on her turf. 'As opposed to banging one off over you?' the curt Wolfenden muttered back. Tired of being seen as eye-candy, a t-shirt seller who was over on image rather than talent, Aimz wasn't about to take any of Karina's smart-ass remarks... especially when she was a 'guest' in her house. 'Here I am trying to do you a good turn, and all I get is insulted,' Aimz sighed, her bitchy, spot-protecting side creeping through. 'Turns out you didn't really do much to impress anyone here - bar making me look really, REALLY, good - so doesn't look like I'll have the pleasure of your company here anymore.' The K-Wolf could almost picture the money emptying from her already overdrawn account. 'Must be quite a shock,' Aimz continued to push, their dislike of one another on just about every level imaginable plain for everyone to see. 'Something that you wanted, that you couldn't run and ask Daddy to buy for you.' Hmmmm, probably not the wisest of things to say. Having had to work menial, degrading jobs just to afford to get to wrestling shows when she started in the business, Aimz couldn't stomach people who had everything handed to them on a decorative plate. 6 days a week she'd worked for borderline illegal money, nearly all of which was spent just getting her to the ring, where she earned all that the promoters could stomach paying to a skinny little redhead. But despite her inner-justification, mentioning Kari's father was about as far from a good idea as you could get. 'Amy,' Kari smiled back, a mask of innocence upon her plan-featured face. 'Y'know what the last thing me and my father shared was?' The Red Raver's mismatched eyes narrowed in curiosity, before Karina's right hand jetted out, slapping Aimz on the cheek that Karina's kicks had bruised earlier. The sound of the impact alone alerted everyone in the corridor, and bodies were hurled between the two wrestlers before Aimz could fly back at her, fists clenched. 'Alright,' one of the road agents growled to the struggling Wolfenden as he pulled her through the exit, 'you're done here.' With that just another on her list of backstage misdemeanours and 'questionable' political decisions, it looked as if adding a few more bookings to her PRIME schedule was becoming less of an option. Scarily, what annoyed her more was not being able to hit Aimz with the line that was screaming to be used, 'this conversation was brought to you by the letters S, T, and D.' Clever, huh? Well, not that clever given the numbers still sat in front of the K-Wolf. Living on a budget was something entirely new to Kari... you might as well have asked her to translate the Old Testament into Hindi. Worryingly, the only expenses she had down were for rent, credit card, and bills. Cost of living was expected to be fed from any scraps left over. Leaning back in the chair, finally detaching her elbows from table, Karina looked out into her apartment. Just one, gaping space, rented empty and raw, but filled with the essence of Karina Wolfenden's life over the past 2 years. She loved everything about it... from the fact that she could crawl out of bed and grab a drink from the fridge without even having to endure to colossal inconvenience of opening a door, to the east-facing wall, made up almost entirely of windows, which made it look like she could just stride out into the sky. She wasn't going to move, so something else was going to have to give. 'Ugh,' she groaned, running palms down the sides of her face, warping her eyes as they stared at the ceiling. 'How bad could it really be, honestly?' Trying to justify it to herself, Karina slithered up from her chair, pacing over towards the sofa. 'They can manage tasteful, right?' The wrestling industry had seen nearly everything from Karina. Daredevil high-flying, skull-splitting kicks, heroic underdog wins, defeatist squash losses, giant pops from adoring fans, fierce scorn from disgruntled employers, and chants of 'KA-RI-NA!' echoing around arenas. One thing it hadn't seen was a Karina Wolfenden t-shirt. Fans would adorn their signs with pictures of Kari that they cut from any rare wrestling magazine articles that mentioned her, or that they'd printed after hunting her image down on a search engine. But never, and entirely intentionally on Karina's part, had her face been plastered over merchandise. That act of resistant privacy was about to come to an end. In a defeatist manner fitting her sacrifice, Karina collapsed down onto the sofa, then lifted her feet onto the edge, hugging her knees in an almost foetal pose... as if she needed that subconscious piece of comfort. Barely remembering the number of her employer, she slowly recalled each digit, before hovering her finger above the last. 'It pays the bills,' she whispered as her mantra, 'it pays the bills...' With a hint of defiance, she plunged her finger down onto the 9, then waited for her call to be answered. 'Thank you for calling PRIME,' a chirpy and overly, bordering on falsely, polite voice answered. 'Mr Pham's office.' Her voice peaked at the end, turning a normal statement into a question. 'Can you tell him that Karina Wolfenden needs to speak to him,' was forced from her resisting lips, voice heavy and laboured as it tried to sandbag. 'One moment,' the instantly irritating voice again spoke, sounding more like a high pitched beeping that a sound made naturally by a human. 'Now connecting you.' The aforementioned 'Mr Pham' would be none other than Taun Pham, PRIME's co-owner. To say that he and Karina Wolfenden don't get along would be less of an understatement, and more of a bare-faced lie. He's controlling, likes everything done his way, his talent arriving on time, 'team players', and for everyone to be respectful to those who sign their cheques. Karina, on the other hand, prefers a more 'non-existent' managerial style, to only do as she pleases, to turn up when she wants, and couldn't really care less about more people she shares a roster with. Needless to say, they've never skipped arm-in-arm through a Mary Poppins-eque song-and-dance routine. 'What have you done this time,' was his knee-jerk reaction, having grown used to hearing about Karina injuring herself working for other promotions rather than getting social calls. His eyes narrowed as silence greeted him. 'Look,' his short-of-patience voice rasped, 'if yo-' 'I've reconsidered about my likeness rights,' her voice then drifted gleefully into Pham's ear, setting forth the idea-machine in his head. 'They're PRIME's if you want them, put me on a t-shirt, make some of those foam fingers, whatever. Same goes for picture signings, promotional events, all that... stuff, too. ' 'This is usually the place where you make some sarcastic remark and once again quash any faith I have might left in you,' Pham's words harshly slashed. 'I wish,' was all Kari could offer up in return. Unseen to the K-Wolf, Pham's usually rigid mouth was breaking into a victorious smile. Possibly his most rebellious employee, too adored by the fans to ever punish, and too much of an asset to get rid of, was falling into line. The PRIME fans had already taken her to their hearts, her PTC exposure amplified her reputation, and the upcoming Seven of the Best would give her the chance to claim the greatest prize in wrestling that was independent from any promotion. He saw posters, he saw t-shirts, he saw extortionate photo signings, and he saw PRIME parading their Negasonic Lupine at any publicity event going. 'So,' Karina sighed, head rested on her knees, staring into nothingness. 'What's the market rate for my dignity?' Next: Selling Out : Part Two - 'They Put My Name On A T-Shirt.'
