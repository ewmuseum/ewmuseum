That’s PRIME, that’s what all the people say.

12 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/12/06 Driving in his white Cadillac fully loaded like an Irishman in a parade is your savior Hoyt Williams. He is cruising along headed to an interview with the press for Prime’s Ultra Violence pay-per-view which some people suspect will be Hoyt’s final match in the company he helped build. His emotions are somewhere between David Alan Coe’s “Fucking in the Butt” and the religious standard of “Amazing Grace”. To be honest the answer of his return is still weighing heavy on his mind as he isn’t certain his damn self. His world has been a tail spin since losing the Universal title, spending more time with his new born baby, defending the extreme title, and being named one of Time Magazines most important people. Many options exist for the champion and with the poor treatment he’s received in Nelson’s PRIME era they are becoming more and more glamorous everyday. There are a lot of things in life you often take advantage of like air or sex, both of which are no big deal unless your not getting any. Same thing with title shots and the recognition that goes with them they are a drug, a need, a want and Hoyt Williams craves it. His fix isn’t in, and the fact that two Hall of Fame member’s isn’t the main event at the fore mentioned PPV boggles his mind. It’s all Ignatius’s fault who couldn’t draw a stick figure, let along a crowd. When I was a young radio producer I worked for a hall of fame disk jockey who taught me a lot about life, radio, and the world. One of the things that stuck out to me was to use my car as a class room. So many people are stuck in their cars for over an hour a day, and what do they do? Listen to mindless music. I listen to vocabulary builder tapes and other logic audio problems. Hoyt Williams is one of those who listen to mindless music while driving. Nothing wrong with that as it does erase the mind of really everything. When you’re singing along to a great song on the radio, even the act of driving takes a back seat to the moment. Hoyt lets loose on a Sinatra song changing up a few lyrics as he sings along really getting into it. Hoyt Williams: (singing) That’s PRIME, that’s what all the people say. Before Hoyt can react his face is eating airbag and his mind is now fully aware of what is happening but is powerless to do anything about it. The impact is amazing and both cars front hoods crumble up into each other like an accordion. Hoyt’s chest hurts where the seatbelt burns into his skin holding him in his seat keeping him from being thrown through the windshield. Finally all the crunching and shattering ends as momentum ceases and nothing is left but the calm after the crash. MY MY Hoyt Williams: Holy Shit! Hoyt speaks not just to acknowledge the situation but to make sure he is still alive. When a terrible accident of any kind happens the first thing you do is ponder if you are dead or alive. It may be a silly thing to do since if you’re pondering if you’re alive, chances are you are. But then again we don’t know what death holds and most of us assume we keep a conscious and a thought pattern in death. Well I guess except for atheists but no body gives a shit what they think anyways. But those who believe go through through this actualization moment grasping the reality of where you are. Hoyt Williams: HOLY SHIT! The next thing that happens once you realize you are alive is you take inventory or what is broken and how bad you are hurt. Hoyt wiggles his toes, feet, and checks his hands. All his functions still seem to be intact but the odd thing is he feels no pain. Which is probably the main reason why we question if were dead or alive since the body should be in horrible pain. We all know it’s the adrenaline that keeps us feeling good even in terrible pain. Hoyt Williams: HOLY SHIT!!!! The final process is to remove yourself from the situation and look at the carnage from a distance figuring everything out. Hoyt gets out of his car looking at the wreck in complete amazement as both cars are unrecognizable. He is surprised to find nobody is around to witness anything that has happened on this suburban street. He pulls out his cell phone and calls 911 dropping it on the street as he watches the other man emerge from the wreck. Hoyt Williams: You ok? The man is in his thirty’s and his career is apparent as he’s dressed like Roy Orbison only with a white collar. Thee recognizable uniform of a catholic priest fresh from mass. Hoyt gets back up, walks over to his car and pulls out his keys from the ignition, he then picks up his dropped cell phone. He limps over to the trunk opening it slowly pulling out his suit case with the extreme title, and a flask of whiskey he has hidden in a secret compartment near the spear tire.
