Free Will

Date: GAN 2009 Friday, October 2 Dallas, Texas The sign atop the door of the studio flashed "ON AIR" in bright neon red. A turn of the knob convinced Troy Douglas that the door was indeed locked, so he quickly devised another, albeit extremely rudimentary, method of entry. He walked to the glass door that looked out on the hallway and rapped it a few times with his knuckles. In the studio, hunched down over his microphone and a stack of papers, Ben Castor didn't even have to look up to know who it was. He simply prattled on, nonchalantly waving his intern - a short, jumpy college sophomore - towards the door. A few seconds later, Troy slipped into the studio, and as soon as a commercial break crackled over the speakers, Ben finally rose from his customary crouch, something that, over 15 years of friendship, Troy had come to call Ben Castor's "Radio Mode." Smiling broadly, Ben spun around in his chair, pulling his noise-canceling headphones from atop his head so quickly that his normally messy dark brown hair started to look like it was trying to escape from the top of his head in every direction. "Fifteen years, and you still won't wear a hat," Troy said. Ben's bright grin rapidly changed to his far-more traditional sarcastic half-smirk. "Careful what you say, big man," he said. "Wanna spend 15 minutes getting grilled about steroids in wrestling?" "You know, you're the reason I hate talking to reporters to this day." "And people wonder how I got this good." Troy shook his head as he grabbed a chair from the other side of the studio and pulled it next to the spare mic. Ben Castor would never change. He couldn't. The Earth suddenly reversing its own rotation and breaking apart into space would be far less of a surprise. Sarcasm was bred into Ben like speed into a racehorse. It made him a damn good radio host and a hell of a lot of fun to be around, but it was an often frustrating trait for a best friend. Especially when you've only got a seven-minute station break at the top of the hour and a 90-second "SPORTSFLASH!" to get through what had the potential of being a very awkward conversation. "I didn't just come here for the interview, you know," Troy said. Ben feigned taking an arrow to the heart. "Aww, you're hurting my feelings. And here I thought you were always a fan of my hard-hitting questions and incendiary wit." "Seriously, Ben." Troy narrowed his eyes, trying desperately to get his friend to appreciate the gravity of the situation. He didn't have a lot of people he trusted for advice anymore, and while Ben Castor might not be the best man for the job, he could at least trust him farther than he could throw him. Ben Castor stands 5'9", maybe a buck-sixty. Troy Douglas could throw him a long way. "Okay, shoot." Subconsciously, Troy's right hand shot to his jeans pocket, where he felt the velvet-lined exterior of the box that was the very reason he had come to Dallas for his friend's advice before heading to Waco. "Uhh," Troy mumbled, before deciding just to get the words out before he forgot them. "Samscomingintotownandimgonnaaskhertomarryme." Ben squinted through horn-rimmed glasses. "You know, some spaces and punctuation might do you some good, buddy." "Sam's coming in to town for the show and ... I'm going to ask her to marry me." That was enough to drain Benjamin David Castor of his seemingly endless supply of snappy comebacks. He set his glasses down on the desk, blinked a few times, and stared at his friend. For his part, Troy simply tapped his fingers nervously on the desk, his eyes occasionally flipping across the room to his old roommate. "Are you serious?" Ben wasn't joking anymore. He was, at once, both genuinely intrigued and concerned at what he'd just heard. "No, I just emptied a healthy portion of my bank account at Tiffany's because the shopping bug hit me." Troy knew it was a little glib, but if anyone could accept that, it was the man staring goggle-eyed at him right now. Still, Ben Castor had just one response. "Are you SERIOUS?" Troy straightened up. If he was going to say this, he was going to say this right. "Yeah, I'm serious, Ben," he said softly. "And you've known her for how long, exactly?" "About four months." "Then no, there's nothing crazy about that," Ben said, before his eyebrows started to flare wildly, like they always did when he was about to embark on another patented Castor Rant. "Except, you know, pretty much everything. I met Sam, and she's great and all, but this is WAY too quick. You and Lauren were together basically since you were seven years old, then she dies, and you go what, six years without being in a relationship?" "Six and a half." "So, six and a half years, then you meet this woman, and four months later you've got an engagement ring in your pocket? It's too soon." Troy drew his mouth into an even line, gritting his teeth to repress the urge to slam his fist through some extremely expensive audio equipment. "You think that hasn't gone through my mind?" "Apparently not." If there was one thing Ben Castor had a knack for, it was playing Devil's Advocate. "It has," Troy grunted. "A million times, then a million times again since I bought the ring. Hell, it's just about all I think about. I know its fast, Ben. I also know it’s the right decision." Ben scratched his chin quizzically. "How's that, Oh Sage One?" "I just do." As soon as he said it, Troy Douglas knew that one wasn't going to fly. "You just do? You want my seal of approval; you're going to have to do better than that." Damn the equipment, Troy spiked his right fist off the table, sending a two-day old overnight format flying away. "I'm not here for your approval, Ben. I came to let you know what I was doing, and I hoped you'd be happy for me. "Why should I? Why should I be happy that my best friend is making a half-assed decision that could ruin his life one more time?" "You know how you feel about Alicia? How I still feel about Lauren?" "Yeah." "It's the same way with Sam. This isn't a choice, Ben. This one was made for me." Ben Castor understood. "Then save me a toast at the wedding." "Um, she kinda has to say 'yes' first." "That won't be a problem. Trust me. Just find the right moment, and it'll go from there." And just like that, the red light in the studio blinked on once again, and Ben Castor assumed his position, back on stage on the airwaves of the entire Metroplex. "Alright, we're back on the Ben Castor Show. It's a Friday drive time edition, and we're thrilled to be joined in studio by a longtime friend of the program, professional wrestling superstar Troy Douglas. Troy, good to have you back with us..." ***** Three days later New York City Samantha Wade's suitcase rattled incessantly in the seat next to her as the cab sped along the final stretch to JFK Airport. Well, as much as a cab can speed through the interminable series of traffic jams that had been her journey from just outside her Morningside Heights apartment all the way to JFK. But, Sam Wade had a flight to catch, and if she had to run onto the tarmac and hang onto the landing gear for dear life just to get on this particular 747, then so be it. She was going to Texas, and that was that. In fact, that's almost exactly what she had to say to her department head the week before. Roger Hendrickson was a brilliant historian and an even better teacher, but easy to negotiate with he was not. It took more than a little begging, pleading and cajoling just to get the 10 days of vacation that had been owed to her for nearly two years since completing her doctoral thesis. Then, she volunteered to teach two sections of the dreaded Intro to U.S. History for the next two semesters, and the venerable academic finally assented. Lousy Machiavellian bastard. Sam fidgeted and shoved the wheel of her suitcase away from her right thigh for what had to be the 50th time on this eternal cab ride. Finally, looking off into the distance, she saw the sprawling complex of JFK through the early-evening fall fog and exhaled, ignoring the overwhelming scent of fresh pine and old leather inside the cab. Almost there. At long last, the cab squealed to a halt at the US Airways gate, and Sam went through her mental checklist. Ticket? Check. Suitcase? Check? Pay the driver? Check. As she handed the gruff cabbie a neatly folded group of bills, he spoke for the first time since asking her what her destination was. "So," he said. "Where ya flyin' too." "Waco." "What the hell's in Waco?" Sam smiled as her suitcase scraped against the uneven concrete. "Everything." ***** Four hours later Troy closed the door of his rented Ford Escape with a thunk and made his way through the parking garage, alone in a maze of multi-colored automobiles, going through his mental checklist. Wallet? Right pocket. Keys? Same spot. Flowers? Clutched firmly in his right hand. Ring? ... ... Fuck. He scrambled madly, backtracking quickly towards his car as he ran his hands through every pocket and hiding place he could find. Nowhere to be found. Fuck. Again. This fully grown, 6'5", 260 pound, 33 year old man was ready to break down and cry tears of frustration right in the middle of an empty parking garage. Until finally, blessedly, his right hand brushed against the inside pocket of his tan leather jacket. Troy laughed bitterly, his mind finally beginning to settle after racing at terminal velocity for the last 30 seconds. Calmed and assured, he patted the concealed ring once again and stepped forward towards the terminal and the craziest decision he'd ever made. Not that it was even a choice, of course. ***** Her head throbbing, her eyes glazed ten times over, Samantha Wade finally felt the jet rattle to a halt. The fuselage rumbled slightly as the connection was made with the jetway, and at long last, the seatbelt light flickered away and the pilot gave the one message she'd been begging to hear. "Welcome to Waco. Thanks for flying US Airways, please exit the plane safely and enjoy your stay." Sam reached up to grab her carry-on bag from the overhead as she shook out the cobwebs, then turned into the aisle. Where she was promptly rammed in the knee by an eight-year-old holding a Nintendo DS that jabbed right into her thigh. Story of my life. Sam paused, and once the coast was clear and the kid's mother had made her sincerest apologies, she finally hauled herself off the plane. She made her way slowly through the jetway and to the terminal, where she started to scan the crowd. It didn't take long for her to find who and what she was looking for, and her mood immediately brightened at the sight of it. There weren't many others in the terminal with the ability to wave a bouquet of roses a good seven-plus feet off the ground. ***** Troy waved the flowers in the air as a signal, praying he wouldn't start flinging flora all across Terminal C. Luckily, it didn't take long before he spotted Sam's unmistakable red hair out of the disembarking mass, and he quickly picked his way through the throng towards her. Finally he reached her, and she immediately clutched her tired, dragging self onto his right side. Troy was perfectly content to escort her to a nearby row of chairs where she slumped down, relieved. "Got you flowers." She forced a smile past the exhaustion. "They're beautiful, Troy." Troy Douglas shifted nervously in his seat, gazing intermittently at her lovely hazel eyes. Every few seconds, his eyes darted towards his jacket pocket while his hand danced apprehensively on the arm rest. Sam noticed little of this posturing, as she was fighting off sleep with every breath, every heartbeat. Troy, on the other hand, was a nervous wreck. He'd built himself up for this moment since the day he'd stepped into the jewelry store and laid down the money for the ring. He was going to do this, he knew he was going to do this, and yet, as every second ticked away towards that mythical Zero Hour, new scruples arose. Was he ready for this? Was he moving too fast? Would she even say yes? The questions and doubts raced through his consciousness at Ludicrous Speed, so many thoughts in so short a time that he was scared he might overload and drop dead on the ground here at Waco International Airport. Then, he looked in her eyes one more time. Screw it. Let's do this. He sat up, and whispered in his girlfriend's ear. "Uh, Sam?" Her ears perked, her eyes fluttered open. "Yeah?" "You mind taking a look at something for me?" She smiled slightly, and he fumbled around awkwardly for a moment before finally settling on his jacket pocket. Slowly, carefully, he produced the box and flipped it open, revealing the ring. The diamonds sparkled, even in the flat fluorescent light of the terminal. That woke her up better than any alarm clock. "Samantha Wade," he said, his voice trembling slightly. "I met you in a subway, and you saved my life. You've brought me the kind of joy I haven't felt in a long, long time, and I wake up every day knowing that it's going to be better than the one before, just because you're in my life. I don't know a whole lot, but I do know that I never want that feeling to go away. Not for the rest of my life. "So, how about it? Samantha Wade, will you marry me?" For a second, there was absolute silence and stillness. If Troy looked closely enough, he could see the tears beginning to leak slowly from her eyes. After a moment, there was still silence. No more stillness. Wordlessly, she nodded, the tears already starting to flow. Troy's smile widened to a previously unthinkable degree as he slipped the ring onto her right ring finger. Perfect fit. Turns out Ben Castor was right after all.
