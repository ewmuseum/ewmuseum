Good Samaritan

26 March 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3/26/06 It's surreal, almost. Titan is standing in the back of the room, his big hands folded together. He feels horribly uncomfortable, dressed as he is. It's not like he's wearing a tuxedo; he's wearing his regular BDUs for when he's not on the job, but as it turns out... well, he just feels horribly out of place at a wedding dressed like that. He wished he'd been able to grab his suit bag, but unfortunately, the trip was quick, right after the escape from the safehouse. It's not like anyone else noticed or paid any mind. Scott stood in front of the commanding officer in his faded jeans and a black buttondown shirt he'd gotten someone to loan him. Van stood next to the big man, dressed in khakis and a collarless shirt, acting as best man. Alyssa had a veil at least, but was dressed rather similarly to Scott: jeans and a fairly nice blouse, probably borrowed from one of the wives on the base. Lacey and Cathy stood next to her, dressed in much the same manner. It was a rather plain affair, but it all seemed to fit. Scott was a rather simple man, surrounded by people that cared for him and marrying his one woman. She gazed back at him, her face a dazzling smile and tears of joy shining unshed in her eyes. Cathy and Lacey couldn't keep theirs back and dabbed at their cheeks with Kleenex. Titan stood in the back as the only invited guest. They were all set up. Scott and Alyssa had a house on the barracks, as did Cathy and Lacey and Van had an apartment to himself. They were stationed near an empty warehouse being set up with a wrestling ring and workout gear so that Scott and Van could hone their craft with some of the best in the country. For them, life would be training here all day and going home. But it wasn't their old lives and Titan couldn't do a thing to help bring that back. All he could do was help them set up here. He'd helped as best he could, but it hadn't been enough, and it gnawed at him. He'd been raised in Israel on stories like that of the Good Samaritan, always helping everyone he could as best he could... 'A man was going down from Jerusalem to Jericho, when he fell into the hands of robbers. They stripped him of his clothes, beat him and went away, leaving him half dead. 31A priest happened to be going down the same road, and when he saw the man, he passed by on the other side. 32So too, a Levite, when he came to the place and saw him, passed by on the other side. 33But a Samaritan, as he traveled, came where the man was; and when he saw him, he took pity on him. 34He went to him and bandaged his wounds, pouring on oil and wine. Then he put the man on his own donkey, took him to an inn and took care of him. 35The next day he took out two silver coins[c] and gave them to the innkeeper. 'Look after him,' he said, 'and when I return, I will reimburse you for any extra expense you may have.' -Luke, 10: 30-35 Titan rolled over in his bed in his temporary quarters on the base. The New Testament parable was rattling back and forth in his head. He couldn't help Scott and Van any more now, but he could keep them in good graces by taking up the slack their absence put in PRIME, so he thought. Was that why he was doing it? Some extension of duty? He rolled over and finally, sleep claimed him. The sun hung high in the air over the land of Israel, Jerusalem in particular. Titan strode down the street in his faded gray BDU's, passing by people that turned to stare at the giant that walked by. To him, it was just a day he had liberty from the IDF. He wanted to find some food that wasn't MRE or just plain bad and he wanted to be around some regular people. Good luck getting them to treat him like one, but that wasn't really the issue. He'd been working on interrogation duty for the last two weeks and he needed to get out in the sun to alleviate the darkness he preferred to work in. He always exploited his height in questioning, so he kept the room dark except for one light pointed at the man in question and stood far back. It's where he'd gotten the code name Shadow Mountain. But today... he felt like some pizza, a Pepsi and just sitting out in the sunshine. He was the kind of guy that couldn't be deployed, he was too big. So he was kept inside as part of the IDF Military Police. It made him feel bad, being the big, bad guy that even his own force didn't like. Oh, they respected him and his work until they had to deal with him, same as everyone else. But he'd seen his share of fighting. He was involved in a raid where he was needed to tear through entryways on the structure. Flashbangs and tear gas were deployed and he smashed the doors in. Good thing, given that they were going in after hostages and didn't want to waste time placing charges; charges would have slowed them down and might have hurt hostages if they were in the wrong spot. Titan had just ripped the doors out and moved in. Even one tango (codeword for dirty terrorist bastard) had turned to try and drop him, but he was too close. Titan had grabbed the weapon and put a .357 round between his eyes. But enough of that. He dealt with enough stress, enough anger, enough hate every day. That's when his eyes spotted it. A man stepped off the street into the pizza parlor he'd been heading for dressed in a long coat on a hot day. Titan's blood ran cold. The explosion knocked people in the street down and rocked the car parked out front. The car's windows shattered (the parlor window shattering kind of went without saying) and blew glass into the passers-by. Titan felt the flow of people push past him, streaming away from the site of the seconds-old bombing. Though a thousand fall at my side, though ten thousand are dying around me, the evil will not touch me. I will see how the wicked are punished, but I will not share it. For Jehovah is my refuge! I choose the God above all gods to shelter me. How then can evil overtake me or any plague come near? For he orders his angels to protect you wherever you go. They will steady you with their hands to keep you from stumbling against the rocks on the trail. You can safely meet a lion or step on poisonous snakes, yes, even trample them beneath your feet! -Psalms 91He moved forward. He was trained to assist in these kinds of situations and try to administer aid early on. The flow of people ceased as he stepped to the edge of the debris, glass crunching under the soles of his combat boots. He kept going, picking up the tablecloth from one of the overturned sidewalk tables. He stopped short to assess the situation... and there was little he could do. Inside, people lay scattered, pierced by shrapnel packed into the bomb or scorched by the actual blast. There, inside, lay a mother with her arms wrapped around her little daughter, motionless with their dead eyes open wide in dead terror. He shook his head; mourn later. People needed help immediately. A shriek brought his head around. One of the tables had fallen on one of the patrons outside and she screamed a curling howl. He moved up, carefully lifting the table up. She had been sitting with her back to it, apparently unhurt but for one detail: one of the plastic forks that the parlor had used had been propelled by the force of the blast right through her hand. Titan knelt next to her, ripping a length from the tablecloth. “Ma'am,” he shouted to her, “you have to look at me. I'm IDF, I'm here to help.” “My hand!” she howled, staring at the grotesque wound. Titan grabbed her by the forearm and carefully draped the plastic sheet over it. “Look at me,” he shouted to her over the general din going on around him. “You need to try and not panic. Don't look at it and wait for the EMTs to get here. When they get here, shout for them.” He turned, searching for more injured... And sat straight up in bed, his body shivering, soaked with sweat. He swung his legs over the side and pulled himself up, heading for the bathroom. The light flicks on crisply, shining a fluorescent glow on the white tile. He leans on the sink and cranks the cold water knob. With a big hand, he splashes water on his face. It wasn't supposed to be that vivid anymore. It was supposed to fade. He shuddered. Time heals all wounds... very slowly.
