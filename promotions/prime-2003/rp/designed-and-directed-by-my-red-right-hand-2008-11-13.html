Date: 13 nov 08 He's a ghost, he's a god, he's a man, he's a guru You're one microscopic cog in his catastrophic plan Designed and directed by his red right hand Nick Cave & the Bad Seeds. Samantha Gideon dangles a meat cleaver before my eyes, letting it swing like a pendulum between her thumb and forefinger. The light catches it and throws reflective prisms of light against the walls. One prism catches my eye and momentarily, I am blinded by the cleaver. Bashfully, she kneels on the end of the bed, swinging the cleaver up onto the flats of her hands like it were an offering. Like an altar boy in church bringing the priest the body of Christ himself. A sobering experience indeed being presented by a cleaver. I could feel the entire alcoholic buzz flushing from my system only to be replaced by a case of cottonmouth and doubt. I shake my head at her, not interested in the offering but she neglects to take my feelings into consideration. “Please?” “I… I can’t, Sam. It’s too dang’rous.” She seems overwhelmed by wholesome inner peace that can’t be described in words. Could be the cocaine doing it but her harmony seemed too pure to be fuelled by an artificial product. “Trust me, Brock. I want this.” Pull my knees to me chest and shake my head. Can’t bring myself to terms with doing what she’s asking me to. It just doesn’t seem right. No matter how much scotch I’d poured into my body I couldn’t overcome the idea of doing this to another human being. I couldn’t bear the idea of cutting Samantha Gideon’s hand off. - | s h e p | - Detective Renner stands in my doorway with his hands jammed in his pockets, staring around and poking his nose too far into my business. His collar is pulled up around his neck. His cheeks are red from the chill of the breeze bashing the warmth of his body. “We’re droppin’ the charges, Brock. Jus’ can’t make nothin’ stick on ya.” He throws his arms up in defeat, allowing his hands to slap against his thighs. “Coroner reckons it was self inflicted.” As he speaks, he stares into the distance, as though the facts never weighed up with his gut instinct and he couldn’t bear to look me in the eye. “Whaddayou think, ‘tective?” He shrugs. “What’s it matter?” “Matters to me.” He stifles his laugh. “Heh. Yer the last person, I swear it, who wants ta know ‘bout my opinion, CHAMP! Trust me on that one.” He spins on his heels, stepping through the door jar to the path when my voice stops him in his tracks. “You think I did it?” Not even turning to face me again, Detective Renner shakes his head and answers. “Nup. Don’t think it, Mr. Shepherd. It’s matter of fact, to me, that ya did it.” He draws a long and deep breath filled with the pain of science unbinding his resolutions to one of the most heinous crimes he’d seen in some time. But these days homicide work was more like a violent game of tag. Psychiatrists, shrinks and doctors all tie the evidence together with the angles things get cut and the way the blood spatters and the real cops, like Renner, go and try to tag them. Being a detective was null and void these days. Detective Renner knew he was just the shell of a man he was born to be. That his dream of being Sipowicz or Harry Calahan was shattered the day the white coats took over the investigations. It was all CSI and nerds cracking the crimes these days. “But lucky fer you, matter of fact don’t mean nothin’ compared to beakers and microscopes… CHAMP!” - | s h e p | - ”I WON’T!” I scream at her. Bounce off the bed and gather up my clothes, start putting them on. The party was over now. This had gone from fun to weird in about 0.5 fucken seconds. Any buzz of my victory had been completely discarded and I found myself in what felt like an ironic hall of mirrors. “Why? I WANT THIS!” “I’m not cutting your hand off, Sam. That’s just fucked up. You know what’s like to live life without a hand?” She shakes her head. “It ain’t fun, Sam. It ain’t somethin’ that should get ya wet or excite you or inspire you, Sam! You’re not thinking this through.” Finally dressed, I turn to give her my parting words. “This ain’t somethin’ I’d wish on anyone, Sam. Not even my worst enemy. This is a fucken curse and you’re wishin’ it on yerself? Maybe, just maybe, you oughta talk to someone and get this fixation in yer head sorted out coz this li’l black duck can’t help ya!” Slipping around in front of me, looking more deranged by the second, naked and clutching a meat cleaver in her hand, Sam puts her delicate hand upon my chest to halt my exit. She stares into my eyes, asking and praying for me to understand without even needing to say the words. Her body trembles as I look down at the hand on my chest. There are scars around the wrists. This has been attempted before. Attempted poorly. My eyes run up her arm and into her eyes, which keep on begging. “I’ve tried, Brock. I don’t have the strength maybe. Or the courage. I dunno. I’ve tried. And I’ve failed. I NEED someone to do this for me. I NEED this,” she pleads. “You need this?” She nods. “You need someone to cut off your hand?” “Please.” “Why me?” Her mouth slowly contorts into a smile. A gentle and warm smile, which I have never seen on her face before. Mostly everything in her body language had been some sort of sycophantic carnal lust the resonated in her soul. Wanton desire to be with what she wanted to be. Now she appeared human. “You ever been told you can’t do somethin’, Brock?” - | s h e p | - Sit at the bar swirling my Jack Daniels around in the glass, letting the cubes of ice chink as they circled. Drinking by my lonesome to give me a chance to think about the events that had transpired in the past few weeks. My memories jolted me as flashbacks of the celebration, which ended in Samantha Gideon’s death. They crawled back into my mind only to paralyse me with the fear that I might’ve actually have done it. Being let off was one thing, but the not knowing was something else entirely. Bartender mops the counter top while staring at me with all his discontent. I catch his gaze and he tries to look away, but not in time. “Think I done it, doncha?” His wiping of the counter speeds up as he tries his best to ignore my presence and my interrogation. “I’M FUCKEN TALKIN’ TO YOU, CUNT!” SMASH! I pitch the tumbler of Jack Daniels into the wall. It shatters into a million pieces as other patrons rise to their feet not knowing whether to flee or to watch. “You outta yer mind?” he asks, like nothing was happening. “I saw the way yer fucken lookin’ at me. Think I don’t notice?” He shakes his head. “I ain’t lookin’ at you anyway. Maybe you oughta head on home, boy. You had too much to drink.” Reach across the bar, my hand wraps around his neck and I drag him over the counter top, crushing his larynx in my vice like grip. My left hand had grown so strong over the years since it was constantly relied upon. “You know full well, you miserly cunt, that was my first one. How ‘bout you start explainin’ yerself to me before I start punchin’ an explanation outta ya?” His flaccid old hands paw at my wrist, trying to relieve my grip on his oxygen flow. Feel a presence coming up behind me. Toss the old guy forward so he hits the wall hard enough to fall to the floor. Spin to see an oaf of a man swinging a pool cue with all his might. Drop my hand flat down and lunge forward so my momentum seizes the pool sticks. It snaps into two pieces on the crown of my head and the oaf is startled by his weapon’s meagre showing. My left drives into his belly, then I drive his face into my knee. He falls to the ground in a pool of blood, choking on his own snot and vital fluids. Look at the rest of the bar, blood has began to trickle down my face from the pool stick’s attack. “Anybody else wanna interrupt me while I’m tryin’ to speak?” A few heads shake. Few more folks sit down and get back to their business. I get back to mine. Pull the bar keeper to his feet and push him into the wall. “I’ma ask you one more time, old man. Why was you lookin’ at me like that?” Having caught his breath it seems he caught his truth as well. Face contorts into a combination of hate and anguish. “I know how this story ends with people like you. Ya get the taste for one and then ya keep goin’. It’s sick. Lettin’ ‘em bleed out like that.” Shake my head at him. Let him go and head for the door. “You know how the story ends, huh?” He nods, with his face still twisted with hate. “Hope it has a happy ending.” Grab my coat from the hook by the door and pull it on. “I like happy endings.” - | s h e p | - “Promise ya not goin’ anywhere, Brock?” “Promise,” I tell her. Why I agreed to something like this I will never know or understand but there was a human being that was suffering in the way I was suffering. All because of a hand. On her knees, with her naked back pressed against my chest, Samantha Gideon draws a deep breath. Her left hand tightens around the handle of the cleaver which seems to have drawn upon a life of its own. She twists her head around and kisses me on my mouth before her left hand rises. She screams with raw aggression. The cleaver slices through the air and through her wrist, hitting the carpeted floor with a ker-thud. She drops the cleaver as she looks down at her wrist, which is cleanly lopped off on a 55 to 60 degree, or so, angle. Her right hand twitches a few moments as the nerves finally come to their resting place. Her body shakes and shivers as her nerves get used to the fresh air and there is blood. Oh, so much blood. More than I would ever have imagined. She turns and hugs me, kissing the nape of my neck as she gasps for air. Her body is going into shock due to the blood loss. Set her down on her back in the motel room, ever so gently. She is smiling. She rests her hands above her head as though she were nude sunbaking on a Jamaican beach with the UV rays tenderly dancing across her snow white skin. “I’m gonna call for help,” I tell her, trying to remain calm myself. Samantha is not listening. I am dialling. I am shouting down the phone. I’m hysterical. It all happens so fast but by the time I’m back to Samantha’s side the ambulance is on its way. Far too late to save her but Samantha is peaceful. Her eyes quiver as they fight the darkness, yet she is happy. She is smiling. Her hand reaches out and gently brushes my face affectionately, as if saying thanks, and Samantha Gideon accepts her darkness. Through helping her through her suffering, she guided me through mine. My red right hand.
