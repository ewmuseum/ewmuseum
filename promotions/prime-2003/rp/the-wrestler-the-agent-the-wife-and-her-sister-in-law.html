The Wrestler, the Agent, the Wife and her Sister-in-Law

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Halo-time 0 Christian Alexander Kannon-Hall “Throughout my life, throughout my career, I thought I’d felt everything. I’ve been blessed with such joy that I cried myself dry, and been left so emotionally ravaged that my eyes couldn’t weep a single tear. I’ve laughed until every surgery I’ve undergone ached, and I’ve split the skin over my knuckles more times than I can count, punching walls out of frustration. I’ve let the buzz wash over me, and I’ve been left broken. I’ve felt love almost burst my heart from my chest, and I’ve felt betrayal plunge its cold dagger into my back. But never, until now, have I felt… have I felt…” Tom Cruise won’t stop staring at me. From behind my folding table, surrounded by every relic and reminder of my wrestling career, I watch the raging torrent of fans I’d expected take the rather more modest form of a mere trickle. A fickle trickle that’s easily distracted by every booth between me and the hall’s doors. Welcome to ‘WRESTLEVENTION’. It’s turned out to be a most depressing invention, I’ll likely need an intervention, from which I‘ll likely not even attempt circumvention. From the booth next door, I overhear someone I’m pretty sure I trained with reel off a whole list of promoters and wrestlers who’ve held him down, screwed him over, and are supposedly to blame for never making him the King of the World. I WAS King of the World… and here we still remain, side by side, perceived as equals. Tom Cruise still won’t stop staring at me. And his smile has curdled my Latte. Under a banner inviting fans to ‘Come meet wrestling’s ONLY Scientologist - Xavier Kannon’ I sit, surrounded by a life-sized cardboard cut-out of Tom, a promotional poster for Battlefield Earth, numerous replica belts that make my career’s title haul look positively gaudy, and what appears to be either a hand-whisk or some sort of dianetics tool. If it’s a hand-whisk, I may just be able to force it into my ear and whisk a bloody, pulpy end to this all. With a gold pen, I go through the motions of giving a few more black and white photos my ‘XK’ signature. Somehow it preserves just a little dignity that it’s not my ‘outside’ name bleeding from the pen. Swinging my feet up onto the table, I feel the thin layer of feeble wood give under my heels, almost snapping in two. With the table sagging a few inches in the middle, a valley soon filled with pens and coffee lids, I kick back in my chair, staring up at the ceiling… still feeling Tom’s eyes on me. “This buys the new hot-tub,” I tell myself, answering the quite obvious question I’d been posing myself every ten seconds for the last hour. “This buys the new hot-tub.” In reality, my bar tab, room service bills, and a couple of movies I made sure wouldn’t appear on the joint credit-card, would probably leave me just about breaking even once I’d upgraded the convention’s ticket back to Barbados. Something blocks my light. “Eycks Kaaay?” is spread so thin by the drawl its delivered with that it almost don’t register. “Eycks Kaaay?” I swing my feet down and blink my eyes back into working order to be met by a lanky young fan, the thin, almost transparent facial hair adorning his top lip adding only days, rather than the years he’d hoped for, to his face. From top to toe he’s decked out in ‘convention-wear’ a PRIME baseball cap, a PCW t-shirt, an OSW replica belt over his shoulder, and a rolled-up copy of Chryste’s playboy edition gripped in a likely clammy left hand. The cynic in me ponders if he still has enough left for one of my ‘reasonably priced’ signed photos. “Yeah…” I reply as he already begins to fidget with impatience. I might be a little dismissive about this whole ordeal, but I’m not too proud to admit that my ego, feline-scale selfish in nature, will always unfurl itself to be stroked, to be the centre of attention. I wait for him to ask me what I really thought of Wrestler X, if it’s true that I had it out in the parking lot with Wrestler Y, if Wrestler Z really did ‘that’ thing on the road near Reno. What was my best match? What is my best memory? Do I think I’m better than him or her or it? Tell me how great I was. Tell me I’m the best. Gush. Me. Me. Me. Him: “Is it true that Angelo Deville’s dead?” My ego hisses and draws its claws, neck-hair tufting in spikes, before hopping off the couch and abandoning me. I die a little inside. “…have I felt so… redundant?” 1 Rush Mason They say you can never really quit. They say the energy of the live crowd supercharges us 5 or 6 nights a week, we become so highly tuned we can’t slow down. They say we become Formula 1 cars… we work best at 200 miles an hour, but can barely turn a corner at 30. They say nothing in the world can replace it. They say we HAVE to go back. I have no idea what they’re talking about. “I need you to do something for me,” I order down the phone. “I need you to hang up, then call back and say the EXACT opposite of what you just said? Okay? Great. Bye.” With a satisfied release of a sigh, I switch lines and call my receptionist. “Julia, hold all my calls.” At one time, I plied my trade in a 20 foot by 20 foot ring. Now, I do it across over 20,000 square feet of office space. It’s all thanks to the live crowd that supercharged some of us 5 or 6 nights a week. It’s all thanks to the fact that some of us become Formula 1 cars. Some of us need the spotlight, need the camera flashes, and need the attention. And they need an agent they trust to make it happen when they hang up their boots. I could tell the ones back when we shared locker rooms. Out of my newest 10 clients, I’d already predicted 9 of them, and had already found gigs for 7. But this one? This one I didn’t expect. “I said hold my calls,” I bark, as one finds its way through to me. “She says I’m to say ‘She needs The Greatest Man Alive’,” Julia relays, repeating it awkwardly like it were a sinister code. “And she says someone called Rashard will know what she means.” This one really I didn’t expect. “Put her through.” 2 Eleanor Kannon-Hall His head is shiny. Like, REALLY shiny. He also realises I’m staring right at it. Using it to check my lip gloss was probably the give-away. “Your head is shiny,” I enlighten him with. “Like, REALLY shiny. With shine.” “Good morning to you too, Ellie,” Rush, with his shiny head, greets me with. “Have to say that Eks Kay was the last person I expected to hear from.” “What about Elvis?” I pose. “Or a mute?” He might be a 6’5, muscle-bound, sharp-suited super-agent, but he’s not above being teased by someone small enough to crawl through the sleeve of his jacket. “I’ve gotten soft, haven’t I?” he concedes, knowing I’d always keep him on his toes. “How about, I have to say that Eks Kay was one of the last people I expected to hear from? Better?” “Much better, Rushard,” I praise like a Kindergarten teacher impressed with some colouring-in. “My name’s STILL not Rushard,” he assures me, just as he does every time I try to make the deed-poll occur by repetition alone. “I know,” I concede. “Yet.” Even though I can’t help toying with the big guy, even more so now he manages to rent more office space than I could fill with Jimmy Choos in my wildest dreams, Rushard is one of the best. Just, generally. Someone you broke into the business with who’s never had a bad word to say about you, or took a shot as soon as your back was turned, is a rarity. And he also has a REEEEEEEALY shiny head. “So, is he serious?” Rush then shrugs the playful stuff off by asking. “He actually wants back in?” “If he doesn’t, then…” I try to word in a way that makes sense, also abandoning the joking around. “Then… I’m worried about him. He left how he wanted to years ago, things were GREAT, he barely even watched TV, let alone a card, until I went back to manage Angie, and th-” “It true he’s dead?” I’m interrupted with. “I’ll only believe it when I see a grave I can spit on,” I hiss in as prickly a fashion as I can muster to let him know that it’s a sore topic. “Anyway, then Deville decided to toy with him, he did that match for a million, a-” “That was real?” Rushard again interrupts. “I thought it was just hype… but an ACTUAL million?” “After tax,” I divulge, trying to keep on topic. “He went from leaving on his own terms, at the top, to trying each time to make up for the last time, and, well, he’s all out of name value. A few years ago, you could put his name on a card and there’d be crowds outside the building the night before, just in case their ticket had the wrong date printed on it by mistake… but now? It’s just a case of who’s gonna make a name for themselves off of his.” Despite his name just having been likened to a falling star, Rushard knows there would be money in an all-guns-blazing return of Xavier Kannon. He also knows that he could take a decent cut of that money. So where’s the enthusiasm? Even his head looks a bit less shiny. Don’t get me wrong, it’s still shiny, but just a little less… well… shiny. “He really ready to go back on the road?” Rush asks, his voice hinting that the idea ran through his mind more than once before. “There’s nowhere which will pay what he’ll want that won’t have a little breach clause just sitting there ready to strip the carpets from your house if he drops out.” “He’s serio-” “Go to someone else,” is then blurted out, bumping my words out of the air. “Please, don’t make me do this, Ellie.” “Er… huh?” I ask… well, kinda, like, hint at asking. “Like, what was that?” “I know you trust me because I’m one of the guys that never, ‘scuse the French, fucked him over,” Rush explains. “And he’s crawled up the backs of quite a few people, but he’s never fucked me over, either. But… on this? Ellie, c’mon…” “What?” I yelp. “He’s gonna fuck me over,” is bluntly declared, hammering down between us and purging whatever jovial air was left out of the room. “I’m not saying he’ll mean it, or he’s planning to… but I’m gonna end up getting fucked over.” “6 years, is it?” I snipe back. “7 years you’ve known him… known us?” “I know I’m being a fucker here, El,” I explains. “No need to tell me, but you said it yourself… you got a million a couple of years ago, for about half an hour’s work. How much of that’s left? Half? Quarter? Couple of hundred thou? A month into this big contract I’m meant to negotiate, on a 8 hour drive through the swamps of Hicksville, Deliveranceland, what’s to stop him saying ‘Fuck it, I got half a mil in the bank and my own fuckin’ island in the Caribbean!’?” I was hoping he and his shiny head would ask me that. “He’s giving it away,” I almost purr out of satisfaction… kinda crazy when you think about it. “Out of that million, there’s two hundred and twenty one thousand, eight hundred and eleven dollars… rounded up… left. And he’s giving it away.” Rushard is as speechless as his head is shiny. “So?” I smugly urge. “Call me in three days,” he concedes. “If they haven’t put him in a padded cell by then, we’ll do this.” 3 Isabelle Madison Kannon I’m this good. “Oh, HECK yes!” I marvel, holding up the finished pair of tights in front of me. No, I’m really this good. “When leggings next come back in,” I whisper to my newest creations, “I might even make you for myself.” I’m Izzy Kannon, and at this moment in time, I rock… hard. Yes, my big brother might be just a tad more famous than me, but that’s only because my handiwork usually comes complete with someone else’s name and logo on. Think of me as wrestling’s fashion consultant… solid black and jeans are my mortal enemy. Tony Gamble’s blue and orange combo? Me. Yes, I made blue and orange work. Hoyt Williams’ Sistine Chapel tights? Me. Yes, I was the one that stretched God across his ’package’. Angelo Deville’s iconic black and white? Moi. Je règne. ZURRRRRRRRRR The intercom buzzes, heralding the arrival of my big brother, and I get goosebumps. That would be lame if I didn’t, as previously noted, rock. “Yep,” I answer, flushing myself with modesty to take the Christmas Eve-esque excitement from my voice. “Come on up.” Assuming he’s taking the elevator rather than the 9 flights of stairs, I hurry to create a bit of last-second pizzazz. The junk cluttering my work table is swept onto the floor to make way for the tights, which are then covered by a couple of curtains of fabric. “Hey, Iz,” Christian calls from the door as he pops his head in. “You okay? You sounded like you were about to go into a sugar-coma on the phone.” “Yeah… was a little excited,” I admit, having almost head butted the ceiling I was bouncing around so much. “Wanna see why?” “Um, okay?” he replies, looking at me as if I’m a little more crazy than normal. At this point I decide to put down the scissors that I’d somehow picked up in the panic. That makes him look a little less… well, whatever someone looks a little less like when they no longer think their wild-eyed sister is going to stab them. The wild-eyes are down to pulling an all-nighter to get these tights finished. It might actually be hard to part with them. “I know you told her not to, but, yeah, El spilled the beans,” I admit, to his total lack of surprise. “So, I present to you, the Isabelle Madison originals for your big return…” I whip the fabric away from the tights and wait for his curious expression to morph into something that screams, You’re THAT good! Not quite what I get. On the table before him are a the aforementioned tights, a stark contrast of black and white, a mirror image in themselves. Separating the stalemate of monochrome colours is a shimmering gold trim, while a lavish XK logo dominates the front of them. The iconic Deville design that I thought I could never better. I proved myself wrong when it really mattered. “Is this a joke,” he snaps, not moving from the spot. “They’re HIS colours… no… not funny.” Bastard. Shaking his head, he turns to leave, not even looking back at me. “Where the heck are you going?” I demand, grabbing the tights in a fierce fist and striding across the studio after him. “And no, they’re not a joke, they’re about 18 hours of bloody hard work is what they are… and they’re also magnificent.” “They’re HIS!” he roars back at me, and I feel my feet dig in ready for the fight. “You think after all he’s done to me, to Ellie, to Alex, that I want to go out there dressed as him? You think I want to play dress-up as Angelo?” “He. Is. DEAD!” I match the ferocity of his roar with. “And do you think I don’t know what he did? You think I’ve forgotten? I know what he did, because I had to watch him do it, knowing there was nothing I could do to stop it… and I watched him do it, all the time wearing those colours, all the time wearing the tights I made for him. But now, now he’s out of the picture finally, now you’re going back without him hanging over your head like the sword of bloody Damocles. He took so much from you, now you take his colours… and I can make them look better on you.” Tights clenched in a fist, I plunge them into his chest, taking him aback a little with the power it packs. “I…” he goes to reply, letting the tights unfurl in all their glory before him. “I… wow.” “I know,” I nod. “I’m this good.” “The gold?” he asks, running a thumb along where crimson red would have bordered Deville’s attire. “Time to be Midas again, big brother.” 4 Xavier Kannon I can hear them grumble as they approach, huddled together like penguins, forming rank against the bitter early morning chill. “They put a 6 in the evening so we didn’t have to use the one in the morning anymore, y’know?” moans Iz, words half-muffled by a long, stripy scarf that Ellie’s in the process of sharing. “Just so we’re clear,” Rush joins in, “It’s charged time and a half before 7.” The sun is yet to rise up over the concrete skyline, leaving the sky an anaemic blue, waiting for the first spark of daylight to enrich it. Trapped in the limbo between night and day, the streetlamps are firmly on the side of the Polar Bears, having switched off half an hour ago to save energy costs. The passing headlights of early commuters and taxis cut through the shadowy valley between high-rise buildings, where the streets have to wait for the sun to scale all 20 or so stories. “Thank you all for coming,” I greet the huddled masses with, anticipation warming my every extremity so I feel almost toasty. “You hid my cellphone and said I could only have it back if I came here,” Ellie grumbles. “You said you’d stop paying my rent if I didn’t show,” Izzy chimes in. “I was up at 5,” Rush feels obliged to complete the tri-fecta by revealing. “And my 7 o’clock is only a couple of blocks from here.” “The enthusiasm is most welcome, folks,” I sigh. I take another peek up at the sky, then down to my feet to see the contrast between the sidewalk cloaked in shadow and bathed in sunlight. It’s nearly time for the grand reveal. “As you know, Deville’s money is now gone,” I announce. “Every last tainted, grubby cent of it. Gone.” “Good riddance,” Ellie voices her opinion. “Still say a transfer to my account could have cleaned about $100,000 of it,” Iz snipes. Rush stays quiet. He probably thinks I took one too many chair shots to the head. “I was going to throw it off a roof, flush it down the toilet…“ I explain, keeping an eye on the light creeping towards my toes, “anything, just to get it out of my… out of our lives. But somehow I knew I had to do something in Angelo’s honour.” “Um, you’re kidding, right?” blurts out Ellie, aghast. “Flushing it down the toilet would have achieved that.” “Despite his flaws, Deville was a man of charity,” I continue, trying to keep a straight face and maintain the act. “So it’s only right that the last of his money reminds people of that, and helps spread an important message to the youth of America.” “He on any sort of medication… or twelve?” Rush asks of my two girls, thinking I’ve finally lost it. “Ladies and Gentleman,” I then declare as the sun finally breaches the city’s defences and illuminates the billboard behind me, “here’s to a true hero…” The hypnotic, powder-blue eyes of Angelo Deville stare down at us all, the most subtle of sneers showing his contempt for all mankind. Golden-blonde hair slicked back, acute cheekbones… he looks every bit the roman sculpture chiselled from marble that dominated the wrestling world. “…with special thanks to North American AIDS Awareness.” But rather than the burns and barbs that flicked with ease from his silver-tongue, a decidedly different message is stamped in bold beside his image. I DIDN’T MAKE MY BOYFRIEND WEAR A CONDOM - Angelo Deville (1978 - 2007) >=) “It’s…” Ellie whispers in awe. “Beautiful.” “Burn,” is Izzy’s contribution. “You’d better hope he’s dead…” Rush chimes in with. “Iz,” Ellie mischievously points, “photo opportunities.” While the two women in my life begin their quest to fill an entire digital camera with themselves and the billboard, I’m left standing beside Rush, just as I had at the start of my career. “So?” I urge. “You in?” There’s an awkward pause as he goes to answer, but cuts himself off, dropping his head and puffing steam out of his nose. That would be a no, then. “Are you serious?” I spit his way. “As much as I hate saying this, you… um… you fucking OWE me.” “Is that the case,” he snarls back, intrigued. “Please,” I scoff. “Who do you think looked out for you in the locker room? Who do you think stood up to shoot down the parade of awful ideas they came up with trying to find something for you to do? Me. Who do you think said you could still be something big every time another bunch of talent cuts came? Me. And look what happened when you tried to go it alone, when I tried to let you find your own way in the business… remember that? What was it called that they tossed you into? Do the words ‘Black Attack’ mean anything to you?” He stares right back at me, teeth grinding together louder than any words he could speak. “Face it, you were nothing without me,” I goad. “NOTHING. Hate to break it to you. So you might have your agency now, you might have that client list raking it in for you, the sharp suits… but if I’d have passed you over as a tag team partner you’d be assistant manager of McDonalds somewhere in Charlotte, and we both kno-” Ouch. He hasn’t let himself go. A fist smashes into my mouth, grinding my lip over my teeth and jolting my jaw back. There’s that split-second feeling like after the alarm goes off that you feel yourself slipping back to sleep. I stumble back, trying to keep my footing, while my mouth floods with the irony sweetness of blood. I hear Ellie remark behind me that we’re just doing what we do. “Ego back? Check. Can still take a punch? Check. No slush fund to fall back on? Check.” After reeling off his checklist, he outstretches a hand. “Welcome to Mason and Associates, Superstar.” “Throughout my life, throughout my career, I thought I’d felt everything. I’ve been blessed with such joy that I cried myself dry, and been left so emotionally ravaged that my eyes couldn’t weep a single tear. I’ve laughed until every surgery I’ve undergone ached, and I’ve split the skin over my knuckles more times than I can count, punching walls out of frustration. I’ve let the buzz wash over me, and I’ve been left broken. I’ve felt love almost burst my heart from my chest, and I’ve felt betrayal plunge its cold dagger into my back. But never, until now, have I felt… have I felt… alive. Have I felt free.”
