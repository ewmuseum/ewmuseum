There are no atheists in foxholes (ReV 55 RP)

30 January 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01/30/05 The snow fell gracefully, backlit and beautifully it cascaded to the ground. A thick fog crawled slowly over the surface of the earth. The natural serenity was so calming – or at least it should have been… “Where’s Sean?” I shouted with as much force as a man can shout. We were all sitting within six or seven feet of each other, but I couldn’t be sure that anyone’d heard me. “I think he’s been shot!” Came a voice from somewhere to my left. I was surprised that I even heard it. “Shot? How’d he get shot?” I yelled back at the voice. “I don’t know, he’s—oh shit! He’s bleeding! He’s bleeding everywhere!” The sentence struck me like a bolt of lightning. For a moment, an instant, a snapshot in time it was silent – even the ringing in my ears seemed to cease long enough to realize that one of my best friends would likely die here, tonight, right now. I still remember meeting Sean… “Did you win the lottery, too?” I asked. Though a sick-hearted laugh he replied, “Yeah. January 11th.” “September 26th.” We both nodded awkwardly for a moment or two, then we sat silently for a long time. We’d both just been drafted by Uncle Sam to go see the world. Of course at the moment all we were seeing was the inside of an olive drab bus that was carting us and one duffle bag each to Fort something-or-other in Texas before we were to ship off to the Korean ‘Police Action’. Police Action my ass! We all knew we were headed off to war. We’d only met each other about an hour before hand when I’d asked Sean if I could sit next to him. He obliged with a simple nod. That worried sick feeling was scrawled all over his face – I tried my best to lighten the mood. “Seen any good films recently?” Sean just shook his head, his poofy, red hair waving around subtly as he did. He licked his lower lip then bit it. He was probably thinking about his girlfriend or maybe his family. “Scared?” I probed, trying to find something that might ignite a conversation. Sean almost got defensive. “No!” he snapped back. “I am. I’ve never even shot a gun before.” I think he’d finally started to let his guard down ‘cause he answered, “Actually, I am too. My dad died in World War II. Mum tells me he was a great man.” I listened as Sean talked about his father – someone that he, obviously, held very close to his heart. Listening to him talk almost made me feel comfortable, safe. It was like when my mother used to come into my room after I’d had a nightmare and tell me a story. She’d stay with me until I wasn’t scared anymore and she’d talk softly and sweetly and make the whole world disappear. It felt like as long as she was around I would be invincible. His tales of days long gone by made me think of my own father – I can still see him waving from the curb as the bus pulled away, his other arm comforting my mother – it seemed once the news came that she wouldn’t stop crying ‘til I got home. If I survive this, maybe I’ll get to. Before either of us realized it the bus came to a halt. We both looked outside at what was certainly a military compound. Sean and I looked at each other, our eyes connected and I could tell he was as scared as me. Sure we’d only just arrived at camp - on home soil, no less. And the war would be over at any moment, we might not even get sent out before it ends... but that did little to ease our fears. We each grabbed our bag and stood up, awaiting out turn to get off the bus and launch headlong into our future, seemingly our destiny. “Hey, what’s your name?” I quizzed of my newfound amigo. “Sean. Yours?” “Conrad. Real nice to meet ya.” “Likewise.” I nodded as my turn came to file off the bus and so I did, with Sean in line shortly behind me. I stepped off the vehicle and started making my way to report in when a voice came from behind me. “Conrad!” I turned and saw Sean jogging up to me. He looked at me in all seriousness as he extended his hand for a shake, I obliged. “Conrad, promise me something.” “Sure.” “Look everyone needs friends; everyone needs someone to get their back. As long as we’re together will-- will you look out for me? If I promise to return the favor?” I nodded. With a thundering crash my serenity was shattered as a bomb landed just thirty or so yards north of my position, shooting snow and dirt and smoke, thick, dark smoke into the night sky. I scrambled from where I was over to Sean. There was blood everywhere. I fought back the tears and put on a strong front. Sean was just lying there in Todrick’s arms, not moving, and barely able to speak. I huddled in close to him. “Sean, what can I do, Sean?” “Am I gonna go home? Am I gonna see my mom again?” I couldn’t stop the tears. I tried, God knows I tried, but they just kept coming out. I was breaking my promise. What could I do? “Of course you are, Sean.” I could barely hear him over the explosions and gunfire that tore up that small field in South Korea. “My picture.” “What?” “My picture,” he repeated, “of my parents. I don't want to ruin it.” I remembered just which picture he was talking about. I went into his jacket pockets, almost frantically until I found it. But it was already covered in blood, Sean’s blood. I wiped it off with my coat sleeve and put it in his left hand. He raised it up with a smile on his face – I think it was the first time I’d seen him smile in a long while. “Conrad, te-- tell my mom I love her, okay?” “Don’t talk like that, Sean, this little wound is your ticket to freedom!” “Yeah,” Todrick piped in, “you’re going State-side! If only we were all lucky enough to get shot.” Todrick was a good man, obviously visibly shaken by this ordeal, but he was only a kid – hell, we were all only kids. But still they handed us our toys and sent us out to play. Being out here was like being flung into the pit of Hades, filled with fire and death and filth. Todrick was always the guy who lightened the mood. My poor hair! I’d just gotten back from registering and they sure don’t allow you to be shy. I was now sporting my new fatigues. I still had a duffle bag, but now it matched the bus I came in here on and it was filled with bedding, extra uniform and some basic necessities, like shoe polish. I pushed open the door to my barracks where there were already three guys unpacking so I went in and began to do the same. Out of the blue a voice broke the silence. “Dammit, that it! Everyone who comes in here is wearing the same uniform as me!” I recoiled and meekly responded, “Sorry, I—“ “You SHOULD be sorry. Now none of the boys will pay attention to me! And no one will know who bought this outfit first!” I let out a sigh of relief - at that moment I was almost too tense to laugh, but the other guys in the room had a good chuckle. “Jesus, you’re just kidding. You shouldn’t do that to a fella.” Just then, much to my pleasant surprise, Sean walked in. Todrick, of course, wouldn’t pass up the chance, “Holy hell! That’s really, really it! I’m talking to that supply sergeant right now! This is an outrage!” Sean was, of course, very confused but we all laughed again. I stepped back a couple paces and put my arm around Sean’s shoulders. “Don’t worry, man, he’s just playing around. He got me at first, too.” Todrick walked up to both of us. Muscling himself in between us and draping his arms over us both with a coy smile on his face. “You know what, guys. I don’t care what everyone else says about you both, you guys are alright!” He snapped his arms off us and walked away as the barracks broke out in laughter. Sean and I hadn’t felt this comfortable since the draft. It was nice – for a change. I could tell this guy would make a welcome edition to the pact Sean and I had made. It’s always nice to have some levity in a situation. “Hey,” I called back at him, “Hey buddy!” Todrick turned around with a smile still emblazoned across his face and called back, “Yeah?” “What’s your name, bud?” “Bud, actually! Damn, you must be psychic!” “No, seriously,” Sean retorted. “My name’s Todrick.” He said, extending a hand to Sean. “I’m Sean,” was his reply, “And this,” pointing to me, “Is my buddy, Conrad.” We all shook hands. None of us had been playing this game for very long, but already I had the good fortune of running into two quality people. Todrick instantly became one of my favorites. Sean laughed at his ‘good fortune’ for a moment before the pain from his stomach and the bullet lodged within caught up to him. There was blood everywhere. It wasn’t my first brush with death, not by a long shot, but it was the first time I’d been forced to watch a friend dying. It was harder than you might think. “Hey Todrick, d’you have the first aid kit?” “What?” “First aid kit!?” He nodded emphatically before I took Sean off his hands and let him search for it. Todrick dug through his bag while Sean started to lose it. He got real worried about meeting Jesus. “I gotta get right.” He yelled at me. “What do you mean?” “I know I’m gonna die, Conrad, it’s okay. I just need to get straight with God. I got some sins need coverin’.” Finally I clued in to what he was meaning all along. I turned and shouted in Al’s direction. “AL!!” Al pulled his nose out of the Bible, which he had only been able to read by the light of the bombs going off all around us. He hurried as fast as he could to where I was with Sean and Todrick, who was still looking for the aid kit. Al took one look at Sean and almost went hysterical. “Oh God, oh God! Look at him!” I reached out and grabbed Al by the scruff of the neck and pulled him in real close. I was so angry at the way he was going on, I really gave it to him. “Listen here, you shut the hell up in front of him or I’ll shove that Bible so far down your throat that you’ll need Jesus and his miracles to pull it out! Get a hold of yourself!” I let Al’s collar go and he fell backwards with some fear in his eyes. But he quickly sat up like a man and asked what I needed, his mind still focused, obviously, on Sean and the crazy amount of blood pouring from his stomach. “What do you need?” “Put in a fix.” “Is it that bad?” I just nodded, not wanting to actually admit what was, I knew, inevitable. Al complied and leant in close to Sean as he pulled out his Rosary with the hand that wasn’t holding the Bible already. He wasn’t a priest, but he was the closest thing we had and he’d have to do. “What do you need to confess?” Al asked meekly. “The usual, Padré,” was his response. Al made the sign of the cross and opened the Bible. He flipped around through a couple pages before stopping somewhere in Psalms, I think it was. Al started to preach, and all the rest of us just sat and listened. They say there are no atheists in foxholes, and Al made us believe. “The LORD is my shepherd; I shall not want. He maketh me to lie down in green pastures: he leadeth me beside the still waters. He restoreth my soul: he leadeth me in the paths of righteousness for his name's sake. Yea, though I walk through the valley of the shadow of death, I will fear no evil: for thou art with me; thy rod and thy staff they comfort me. Thou preparest a table before me in the presence of mine enemies: thou anointest my head with oil; my cup runneth over. Surely goodness and mercy shall follow me all the days of my life…” “…and I will dwell in the house of the LORD for ever. Amen.” “Amen!” the rest of the barracks shouted. Al had been kneeling beside his bed, praying while the rest of us were taking some well deserved time off. We had just finished Basic and we were meant to ship out tomorrow. Me and Sean and Todrick were teamed up with Al. We were all allowed to spend a couple days of R&R before we shipped out. Al looked up at us with anger in his eyes that we never took him seriously. “You guys laugh now, but Jesus will become your best friend out on that battle field!” We’d heard that one a million times before; little did we know at the time that Al was telling only the truth. The sole person that gave Al the time of day was Sean. Al stood briefly before sitting down on his bed and straightening his things. Sean walked over and sat beside him while we joked about Jesus and John and the like. “Are you a priest or what?” “No,” Al replied, “I just love Jesus. He’s the way, the truth and the life.” “If you’re not a priest, then what’s so different about you? I can see it. You almost glow. Sometimes I wanna touch you for luck.” “Sean, the only difference between the two of us is that I have Jesus. You can have him too, if you want.” Sean shook his head, “I don’t know about that-- listen, thanks for the talk, Father.” “I’m not a priest. And I’d prefer you didn’t call me that. God’s the Father.” Sean smiled, “How about Padré, then? It’s Spanish.” Al just smiled a simple smile of consent. As Sean started to walk back towards us. “Sean?” He turned around. “Jesus loves you.” Sean let out a small laugh through his smirk. He nodded and before he turned around he said… “Thanks Padré.” With that Sean closed his eyes and breathed his last. I checked for a pulse, nothing. I shook him and shook him as Todrick pulled out the aid kit, too little, too late, unfortunately. I screamed at him to wake up. I shouted until I was hoarse. Then I cried. I held him and I cried. I took his picture of his mother and put it into my own coat pocket, hoping to never forget him. “Tony’s back!” yelled Todrick. I gently lowered Sean to the ground and bellowed in Tony’s direction. “What happened” How’d they find out we were here?” Tony crouched down near the three of us and gave us as straight an answer as he knew how to give. “Well, I talked with Darryl over the hill.” “He’s the C.O. of the 45th division?” asked Al. “Yeah,” Tony began his reply, “Darryl said he’s not sure, but I think someone’s been turned. These plans weren’t even relayed over the radio! I have no idea how the Chinese and North Koreans found us out. We’re like sitting ducks in a—“ Just then Tony saw Sean. “Wha— what’s happened here?” “He’s been shot.” Todrick offered. “And!?” I just shook my head. “Fuck! Fuck! Fuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuuck! Jesus hell! Fuck you, you pinko commie bastards!” Tony was livid. “Look, let’s grab him up and get the hell out of here. I promised you guys I’d take of you didn’t I?” I nodded, “Yeah.” “In fact, what did I say when you guys first landed at the US Military base in Seoul?” Todrick started… “I’ve been in this man’s army a good while now. I was in the last big one and I’ve never lost a man. Ever. No one gets left behind. Not yet, not ever.” The man’s crisply folded uniform was deceiving. He seemed like an army brat, but he was drafted and just decided to stick with it. Not one of really knew why anyone would want to do that, but it was his decision to make. “Major?” Sean asked. “What?” “Are we all gonna stick together? Through and through.” “There are a couple rules. The first is that my platoon calls me ‘Tony’ and the second is that my platoon is my platoon until you boys go back State-side. Now, let’s get the introductions done with. You,” he said pointing at Sean, “who is everyone?” “That’s Conrad, he’s a natural leader. With good people skills and the ability to organize effectively. He’s Todrick, the local funny-man. Given the choice to laugh or cry, Todrick always comes up with the former. And that’s Al, I call him the Padré.” “And who are YOU?” I jumped in there, “That’s Sean. True blue through and through. Honest and true and he tells you it just how it is. The best man on our team.” I see, Tony said nodding. He tossed his right arm around Al. “So, why does Sean call you Padré?” “It means ‘Father’!” Sean shouted as Al simultaneously replied, “Cause I love Jesus.” Alright boys, let’s go have a drink before the action starts. We all went to wet our whistles with our newfound C.O. despite a sick, uneasy feeling in the pits of our stomachs. “That’s right,” he said grabbing up his gun, “not yet, not ever. I don’t intend to start now. Let’s get him up and get going.” Tony stood up as Todrick and I grabbed Sean’s body. Just as we were about to make a break for the battalion aid station less than a mile to the south Tony spotted Darryl bringing his platoon over to where we were. “Hold on, guys! Darryl’s bringing his guys over! Maybe they have some information for us!” We sat tight for what seemed like days; of course it was probably less than five minutes in all reality. Darryl and his crew slid down into our foxhole with us. He had four guys with him, just like Tony. There was Ryan, Chris, Joe, Darryl of course, and one guy that everyone just called Kat. None of us ever knew his real name. We all went to basic at the same time, but we never really got chummy, except Darryl and me, we saw a couple pictures together after Basic, before we hit the field, but he was a different kind of guy than me. Ryan looked down at Sean. “Oh man,” Ryan started, “Is he--?” “Yeah,” was Tony’s snap reply, “that’s why we need to move out. I gotta get this man’s body to battalion aid so it can be shipped home for a proper funeral. I made these men a promise and I vow to keep it! Care to cover us?” “Sure” replied Darryl. Tony turned around to grab the feet of his fallen comrade when suddenly a bright flash of light and the loud crack of a pistol sent Al cowering into the corner and saw me wearing my Commanding Officer’s brains. In the blink of an eye Darryl had pulled out his rifle and blasted Tony in the back of the head. I couldn’t believe it. I just sat there, paralyzed with fear – or wonderment. Like a bolt of lightning Todrick tried to make a run for battalion aid. BANG! No such luck. Ryan had other plans. The five of them stood over the two of us still left alive. “It’s you, isn’t it? You’re the traitors?” I demanded weakly of Darryl and his posse. “We prefer the term, opportunists.” Chris replied smartly. Joe laughed. Joe did! I couldn’t believe it! Of all the people who could have turned coat Joe seemed the least likely. Joe used to be in Tony’s platoon, he was transferred out, but they remaind friends up until, well at least in Tony’s mind until about one minute ago. We were all supposed to be on the same team, the same side, the same special divison known as PRIME. But this wasn't a game, this was a war and in a war there is only one victorious team - but even they are losers. After Chris’s abrupt reply the man known only as Kat raised his gun and pointed it at me. I closed my eyes and awaited the bullet; I refused to die without honor and dignity! “If you’re gonna kill me just get on with it!” But as I was shouting I heard Al whisper something under his breath, “Father, into your hands I commit my spirit.” And with that final prayer the Padré pulled the pins on two grenades, stuffed them both in his pockets and ran headlong at our new foes. It was a move Jesus would have been proud of, I’m sure. I tried to get up and jump out of the way of the explosion, but there was no opportunity, by the time I realized what was happening it was already too late. Al took out all five of the 45th division, and half of me. My left arm and leg were all but disintegrated in the blast. All in all, the whole ordeal lasted about five minutes, but it felt like only a second. Now here I am, broken, busted up and bleeding. I know I’m going to die, even though I’m the sole survivor, am I really the winner? I guess you can decide. I know the Padré’s not here to put in a fix for me. But I’m gonna try for one myself. And God, if this sticks can I please bring Tony and Todrick with me? And can you ask Sean and Al to wait for us on the way up? Thanks in advance – here goes… Our Father, who art in Heaven, hallowed be thy name, thy kingdom come, thy will be done on earth as it is in Heaven. And give us this day our daily bread. Forgive us our debts as we forgive our debtors. And lead us not into temptation but deliver us from the evil one. For yours in the kingdom and the power and the glory forever. Amen.
