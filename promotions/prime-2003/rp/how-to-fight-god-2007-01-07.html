How to Fight God.

7 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07/01/2007 “So,” the security officer began, climbing off some workout machine or other and reaching for his sweat towel. He dabbed at the sweat that had formulated upon his brow. He was a deceptively built individual, despite his relatively small stature, with shifty brown eyes that never seemed to be at ease, probing and scanning the immediate area at regular intervals. “I see that you’re fighting god this week...” Jonathan Winters lifted his head from the weights he’d been working on for the better part of the hour and simply glared at the security guard questioningly, idly wondering whether the moron intended to follow the observation up, or perhaps even reach some kind of point. But of course, nothing came. Stakiri Towers, Winters’ place of employment beyond the wrestling industry, was equipped with a full size gym with all the latest equipment on the market. It was an extravagance Stakiri’s often liked to indulge in, in his role as the self-proclaimed ‘world’s greatest employer’. The irony of it was, very few people ever made use of such a facility and most of the time, the place was totally devoid of life; just the way Winters liked it. “You heard of Malachi?” The security guard asks, unscrewing the top of his water bottle and taking a satisfying gulp. Of course I’ve fucking heard of him, you fucking moron! Winters silently chided, promptly returning his attention back to the weights. What a stupid fucking question! He had considered sharing his opinion, but that would just encourage the fucking idiot and the last thing on Winters mind right now was a conversation with the damned vegetable. “He’s that fucking lunatic who believes he’s the lord incarnate,” the security guard needlessly divulged. “Can you believe that guy? How he’s not been locked up in some kind mental institute is beyond me...” That’s not all that’s beyond you, half-wit! Winters grinded his teeth together, fiercely, hoping the pain would somehow draw his attention away from the idiot’s archaic opinions that have already been regurgitated too many times to accurately tally. Take the fucking hint and shut the fuck up! The security chuckled to himself and shook his head. “Fighting against god. One on one. I mean, what would your tactics even be?” Winters ignored him completely now, though he could feel the glare of the fucking clown-shoes burn a hole in the side of his head, as if the security guard somehow expected him to unnaturally responsive all of a sudden. Good fucking luck with that! It didn’t take most people this long to grasp the translucent concept of sheer, unbridled ignorance, but this dim-witted son of a bitch wanted to set a new world record. Fucking jism receptacle! “Ever since I heard you joined PRIME, I’ve been a big fan,” the self-proclaimed security officer told him. “I mean you’re just badass, I--” “Take your tongue outta my ass and cut the bullshit!” Winters demanded, dropping the weights to matted floor with a dull thud. Winters had hoped to squeeze as much information out of the pitiful fool as he could beforehand, but the task was proving to be more tiresome than he’d expected; he’d had enough of his bullshit and couldn’t hold on to the facade in any longer. The ‘security guard’ developed a shocked, yet puzzled, expression. “What the hell are you talking about?” “You are not a security guard,” Winters told him. He stood and idly wandered over toward his tormentor. “Hell, you probably don‘t even like wrestling...” The ‘security guard’ shook his head and chuckled that same fucking grating chuckle of a man trying too hard. Winters wasn’t impressed; he saw through this dickhead’s thinly veiled guise a long time ago. “I have no idea what the hell you’re talking about, I--” “Two weeks,” Winters interrupted him. He had no need for manners, so feeling apologetic at such sudden abruptness didn’t even enter into the equation. The ‘security guard’s’ act of shocked confusion continued. Persistent little fucker… “What?” “That’s when I first noticed you tailing me,” Winters told him. “I’d noticed you a number of times during the building that day and found it suspicious, but I didn’t know for sure until I caught you following me home that night.” Winters noted the shocked expression casually wash away. The game was up and now they both knew it. “I hate to break it to you, but you’re not very good.” “Fuck!” the ‘security guard’ cursed, making no attempt to hide his disappointment. “So,” Winters began. He was determined to get something out of this charade; one way or another. “Stakiri doesn’t trust me all of a sudden?” “I hope not,” his stalker muttered in response. “Information is being leaked to opposition and he wants to know who. He told me you were above suspicion, but wanted you followed regardless.” He paused and glared Winters directly in the eye. “I’m glad he did, I don‘t think he should trust any of you freaks.” If he expected Winters to be angered at such a trivial insult, he was sorely mistaken. Winters simply nodded a half response, contemplated the acquired information without regard for moron stood before him. “He told me you’d be like this. He said you were cold, ignorant and distant, but I think during the time I’ve been watching you, the meaning of those words have been redefined.” He paused, making sure eye contact with Winters was firmly established. “He explained it away with a few bad experiences in your life, but I don’t buy that. I think you’ve been waiting to betray Stakiri from the very beginning and he’s too blind to see it.” Winters considered his words for a second and then nodded solemnly. “You’re speculations and misguided conspiracy theories are irrelevant to both me and Stakiri.” Winters paused and glared at the fool in the eyes. “But your judgement may not be.” His watcher developed a confused expression. “What the hell is that supposed to mean?” A wry smile crept across Winters’ lips. “That’s nothing to concern yourself with,” he answered, slowly wandering toward the exit. “And to answer your previous question; to fight a god, you have to be a god.” Winters left the room, shouting over his shoulder, “Luckily, I fall into that criteria.”
