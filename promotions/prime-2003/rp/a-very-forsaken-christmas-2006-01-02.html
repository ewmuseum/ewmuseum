A Very Forsaken Christmas

2 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 2nd, 2006 For many years, people believed that a bumblebee should not physically be able to fly, even though it clearly does. They believed it could fly, simply because it didn’t know that flight was an impossible task. And that gave hope to many people that their dreams could come true, no matter how impossible things seemed. Of course, scientists had to go and disprove that theory, once again shattering the hopes and dreams of all involved. But, what if there were another bumblebee, so to speak? One that didn’t know flying was an impossible task, despite it being so. Despite everyone knowing in their hearts that this bumblebee had no hope of flying. And what if that bumblebee spread its wings and flew on the one day it was given the chance? What if it flew above the expectations of everyone watching, and gave hope to the masses once more? What if? Fly, bumblebee. Fly. The Drive On the roads, leftover snow from the previous night’s storm melts into water and trails along the gutters, the air still crisp enough to keep people in heavy jackets, but mercifully warming up enough to coax the frigid citizens from their homes. Two or three automobiles rest parked sporadically in driveways, with a single shoddily built car being the only vehicle functioning on the roads; driving that car is James Farwell, and sitting next to him is Clyde Walkins. Filling the car’s interior like a torrential flood of feces, “Silent Night” is being sung by someone that shouldn’t even be singing. Like Shania Twain. Or most of Australia. Farwell: Goddamn Christmas carols. Jesus is born and suddenly everyone goes gay. Turning the dial on the radio, much to Clyde’s dismay, James fiddles along until he finds one of those oldies rock stations that only ever play rock. No matter what the circumstances. And what does he find? “We Wish You A Merry Christmas”. Unleashing a primal grunt, James switches off the music and goes back to trying to keep warm. In front of his face, his breath becomes more and more densely structured with each puff. Did I mention the heater’s broken? Farwell: It’s colder in here than it is outside. I’d walk if I didn’t think I’d get shanked by some hobo. James peers out the passenger side window, eyeing an adult male and a young boy sitting in front of a bank, a torn blue blanket being the only thing covering them besides their filthy clothes and shattered sense of self worth. Farwell: Useless filth is what they are. That man is too lazy to get a job, even though he’s killing his kid, and yet people expect us to pity them. Take a good look, Clyde. That’s where your tax dollars are going. Clyde turns his head and peeks out the window, eyeing the pair outside of the bank. His eyes narrow as they make contact with the adult hobo, and his middle finger slowly rises into view as the red light turns green and the car pulls away. Walkins: Those damn hobosexuals won’t get a red cent out of me! James hears it, but he doesn’t bother. What’s the point in even correcting him anymore? Farwell: Anyway, enough about those…uh…‘hobosexuals’. What did that letter say? Clyde looks genuinely perplexed. Walkins: What letter? Farwell: The letter you got today. Clyde blinks twice, but says nothing. Farwell: The reason we’re driving? Nope, not registering. Farwell: From the Make-A-Wish Foundation? Walkins: The Make-A-Fish Woundation? James grips the steering wheel tighter, his knuckles quickly turning white. The look on his face shows that he’s trying to restrain his anger, and he clears his throat as he tries again. Farwell: The place that you work for, after having won that stupid Lightsaber Tournament. The place that gave you the Chocolate Wookie. The place that sends you money every month, so that we can actually eat. Clyde thinks long and hard, tapping his finger against his forehead, before finally coming up with the answer. Walkins: What is PRIME? Farwell: This isn’t Jeopardy, you dumb bastard. And you still managed to get it wrong. James slows the car down, pulling over to the side of the road. Cutting the engine completely, he takes in a deep breath, focuses for a moment, then turns to Clyde. Farwell: Let’s try this again. The Children’s Make-A-Wish Foundation sent you an urgent letter today, in lieu of the normal payment. You said we had to go somewhere, so I’ve been driving you around town for the past two hours. Just tell me what the letter said, so I can find where we need to go, and we can eat again this month. Walkins: Ohhh! The CHILDREN’S Make-A-Wish Foundation! You should’ve said so in the first place. James mutters something under his breath, possibly a curse on Clyde’s very soul. Walkins: They said we needed to go to their headquarters right away, or we won’t get paid. Something about not fulfilling contractual obligations, etcetera, etcetera. Probably your fault. So, what’s for dinner? Turning the car on, James hits the gas and peels back out onto the road, spraying a kindly old lady with gutter-sludge in the process. His knuckles still white on the steering wheel, James maneuvers the bucket of rust through the slick streets like a racecar driver. Just not Dale Earnhardt. And you can’t even say you’re offended by that. You knew it was coming, and still you continued to read. For shame. The drive on the way to the headquarters of the Children’s Make-A-Wish Foundation is a quiet one. Mostly because snow has once again begun to fall, and with it comes the obligatory drop in temperature that rapidly cools the inside of their automobil sans heater, but also because James is pissed. Pissed at Clyde for not holding up to his end of the cash cow bargain. Now, they could be looking at eking out a living on the salary of professional wrestlers. Professional wrestlers in PRIME. The monthly payment they received every month in exchange for Clyde’s representation of the Foundation could be lost simply because the little retard didn’t feel like asking for donations once a week. Without that, they’d be poor…er. Therefore, it should come as no surprise that when James pulls into the parking lot next to the building housing the Make-A-Wish Foundation headquarters, he maneuvers Clyde’s door right over a large puddle of water left from the previous snowfall. And Clyde thusly ends up ass over teakettle, thrashing around in the dirty water. Thusly, a good laugh is shared by all. Thusly. Clyde shakes himself off to the best of his ability, unable to fully remove the sights and smells that come with landing in city water, but that still doesn’t deter him from his task. He is to see the head of the Foundation, and he is to get his money, come hell or high water. Six minutes and twenty-three seconds later, Clyde stands outside the passenger door of their car, an elderly man standing frailly by his side. Farwell: Clyde, what is this? What the hell is this? Walkins: Let us in, and I’ll explain everything. I think he might already be dead. James gives a moment’s thought to the quandary at hand, and finally gives in to Clyde’s request, unlocking the doors with one of the few power features still functioning. In a hurry to help the old man into the car, Clyde swings the back door open and hits the geezer right in the gut. The force of the blow doubles him over and sends his false teeth flying over the roof of the car, and it takes all his strength to keep on his feet as he grabs hold of the open door for support. Panic-stricken, Clyde removes the dangerous variable from the equation. In this case, that would be the door. And removal would be slamming it shut. Lying in the backseat, the old man sleeps seemingly peacefully, but we all know he’s in a world of hurt right now, even in dreamland. On his face, the happy expression turns to slight pain, then growing agitation, followed by more pain as he possibly relives what just transpired not twenty minutes ago. Walkins: Look at him back there, sleeping like a little baby. Farwell: Yeah. All that screaming really wore him out. You still haven’t told me why we have possession of him, by the way. James slows the car to a stop at a red light, the high-pitched whining of the brakes calling more attention than before to the ambling wreck of a machine. Men would laugh, children would cry, and so went the circle of life. Watching more snow descend from the heavens, few other vehicles dared traverse the slick streets in search of their destination. For some, it would be their final destination. Not our heroes, though. Otherwise, what would keep you all from killing yourselves each week over sheer lack of awesomeness in the world? Nothing, that’s what. Walkins: Well, since we haven’t lived up to our contractual obligations, we’re being forced to take care of some guy’s grandfather. They can’t put him in a home because he has connections, but they said he’s a big fan of boxing…and that’s kind of like wrestling. Long story short: we’re keeping him. Farwell: …I’m going to dump the two of you off at the first homeless shelter I find. In the backseat, the old man utters a muffled whimper and recoils in pain as his dreams become more vivid. The Prelude Back at Forsaken Central (a.k.a. some house, crooked game show, won, James, etc.), Clyde and James hover over the old man as he sleeps on their couch, still fidgeting and moaning like one would assume old people do. Suddenly, and without warning, The Forsaken are taken aback by a barrage of shock and awe! as the old man jerks awake, flailing his limbs in a vain attempt to get on his feet. He calms after a few seconds of struggle, rubbing his eyes and instinctively taking his dentures from a glass of water on the table in front of him, placing them inside his mouth. A moment passes as he moves the pseudo-teeth around a bit, then licks his lips, trying to figure out what’s off about them. Walkins: Wakey, wakey, sleepyhead. Welcome back to the land of the living. Farwell: He wasn’t dead, you jackass. The old man rubs the bridge of his nose, uttering a low grunt of a sigh. His brow wrinkles as the pain takes a moment to subside, and the aching in his hands gives way as his head begins to clear. Old Man: I wish I was. Groaning again, he shuts his eyes and takes the glass of water from the table, downing it in one gulp, ‘minerals’ and all. He licks his lips as that taste returns once more, but stops when two of his teeth fall out of his head. Nervously, Clyde drops to the floor and runs his hand along the carpet, inadvertently attracting more dust and filth to the teeth than before, finally handing them back to the old man. The old guy just looks at the pair of teeth in Clyde’s hand, sighs, and brushes them back onto the floor. Old Man: That’s ok, it’s not like I need to eat… James looks at Clyde with a glare that could cut through a woman’s top and dry-hump her silly if only it wouldn’t get you ten in the pokey. He then switches to the look of a saint as he sucks up to the old guy, knowing that he’s their meal ticket. Farwell: Let me run down to the store and get something for dinner that isn’t so hard on your teeth. Clyde, entertain this gentleman while I go to the store for him. Before anyone can object, James grabs his coat and slips his shoes on as if the first person fully clothed and out the door wins a lifetime away from Clyde, quickly disappearing back into the snowy abyss. With the house now completely silent, save for the sound of Clyde and the old man breathing, the two remain in their prone positions until the old guy breaks the silence. Old Man: Since you went and broke my teeth, do you think I’d be able to have a few of my friends over? They’re no trouble, sir, and I promise we won’t make any noise. Clyde thinks it over, then comes to the same realization James did. Old man happy = money x square root of Charizard. Well, maybe James didn’t have Charizard in there, but he sure had the money. Walkins: Sure, you can have your smelly old friends over. But, I’m not changing any diapers. Clyde Walkins brown-noses for no one! Old Man: I wouldn’t worry about diapers, if I was you. By the way, do you have a basement? Walkins: We sure do, but we don’t use it anymore. There are spiders down there. Old Man: Good, good. Walkins: What, you like spiders or something? The old man pauses for a second, but decides against saying anything, instead just shaking his head as he moves to the nearby phone. A few numbers later, and he’s dialed his fill. Just in time for James to return. Farwell: Ok, I couldn’t find anything mushy that wasn’t carrot or celery-related, so I got us McDonald’s. No one actually chews that stuff, anyway. Tapping his snow-covered boots off at the entrance, James shrugs the coat from his body and makes his way into the kitchen, carrying two bags of fast food in his arms as he searches the cupboards for plates. Finding none, he sets the food down on the counter, eyeing the stack of dishes in the sink. Farwell: Sorry, we haven’t done dishes in a while. To be honest, I usually just eat right out of the pot. Walkins: And I just eat whatever I find lying around. Farwell: You sure do, Clyde. You sure do. Patting Clyde on the shoulder, James then turns back to the bags of greasy, artery-clogging food, removing the contents from their packaging. Farwell: You have a choice: Egg McMuffin or their new McSlug Burger. The old man looks like he’s about to cry in disgust, and finally makes his choice after a bit of hesitation. Old Man: I’ll take the slug. On the other side of the table, Clyde slams his fist down as James passes the burger to the old man. Walkins: I wanted that one! Farwell: Too bad. You’re getting an Egg McMuffin. Walkins: But…they’re gross! Last time I ate one, I ended up vomiting chunks of under-developed baby chicken for a solid month…what’s in that other bag? James’ eyes narrow and he looks back and forth quickly, unsure of whether or not to divulge the contents of the second bag to his cohort. Farwell: Those packets of ketchup are put out there for you to take. It isn’t a crime, you know. Clutching the bag in his arms like a child, James defensively backs away from Clyde and the old man, moving nearer to the fridge. Unfortunately for him, the overloaded bag splits at the bottom and a thousand or so tiny bags of ketchup spill from within, landing on the floor and even under his feet, exploding in an orgy of tomato and salt. Meanwhile, the doorbell rings in the background, unbeknownst to Clyde and James, who both try to salvage the ill-gotten ketchup. The old man, however, hears the doorbell loud and clear. The Club Farwell: Hey, Clyde…have you seen that old guy in the last hour? Sitting in a high-back chair, a velvet robe covers Clyde’s body, and warm black slippers his feet. The newspaper is rolled in one hand, as he plucks a corncob pipe from between his lips with the other, releasing a final little puff of smoke. Beside him, a fire roars beneath the mantle, casting shadows onto the far wall of Clyde and his chair, as well as the martini on the circular table resting to his side. Walkins: You know I haven’t. I’ve been playing Cabela’s Domestic Game Hunter since dinner. Cut to Clyde dressed up in typical safari hunting gear, a high-powered rifle strapped over his shoulder. Wandering through the alleyway behind a row of houses, Clyde slowly and carefully sneaks up to a fence, climbing up to use the adjacent tool shed as a perch. Taking aim, he has his prey locked in his sights. The view switches to look straight down the barrel of the gun, and right into Clyde’s eye, then it switches to the face of a border collie staring back at Clyde. Clyde’s face cringing as he tries to steady the rifle. The dog looking back at him. Clyde wiping sweat from his brow. The dog tilting its head. Clyde taking aim again. The dog. Clyde. The dog. Clyde’s finger starts to pull the trigger. The dog’s tongue sticks out. The screen goes white. Bang. Finally, the view changes to Clyde sitting in the same high-back chair, smoking a pipe and reading the evening paper while a fire cracks in the background. Above and behind his chair, the head of the collie is mounted to the wall, tongue dangling from its maw. The name Tricksy is intricately engraved on a silver strip of metal placed neatly below the head, presumably the former centerpiece of a collar. Clyde takes a sip of his martini. Meanwhile, a little boy opens the back door with a bowl of food in his hands, calling for his dog as he spots a white mass near the fence. Now back to The Forsaken. Farwell: Wait…you killed someone’s dog? Walkins: Aye...bagged me a sixty-four pounder. Farwell: Are you insane?! You can’t just go around slaughtering people’s pets, living under the pretense that it’s some sort of twisted game. I have half a mind t- Walkins: The old guy’s downstairs with a bunch of other old people! Trying to take the heat off himself, Clyde rats out the old man, having had the keen eye to spy on him earlier as he led his elderly friends down into the basement. The diversion works, surprisingly, and James’ focus turns from the decapitated canine to the group of old people possibly orgying it up under his feet. Farwell: Why didn’t you tell me earlier? One of them could throw out a hip and sue me! James grabs hold of Clyde’s arm, pulling him out of the chair and towards the door leading to the basement. Directly behind the two, the reverberation of the chair falling causes the ‘trophy’ to dismount the wall, soon taking up residence on the floor, its head tilted in an inquisitive manner. Pulling open the stained wooden door, the smell of Polident and Depends fills the nostrils of both men. A twisted glimpse into the future, perhaps? James and Clyde exchange glances, neither man wanting to be the first to enter into the realm of the elderly. In an unspoken agreement, they both take the first step at the same time, and the second, and more and more until they finally reach the bottom. The smells and sounds tell them they’re too late. Farwell: I can’t look, Clyde. Please, tell me they aren’t going at it like rabbits. Walkins: Only if rabbits have started going at it differently since I last checked… Peeking through his fingers, James soon drops his hands from his face as he takes in the sights before him. Old people. Fighting. For money. And a crudely constructed cage cutting anyone off from entering, with a locked gate in the middle for those lucky enough to be invited to join. Farwell: What the hell is going on here?! Trying to push through the gate, James creates enough of a commotion that the old man they brought home is forced to meander on over to them. Cantankerously, he scratches his crotch and pulls open the gate, letting them through. Farwell: What have you done to my basement? And who are you, anyway, old man? Closing the gate and checking to make sure no one saw them come in, the old man slowly turns and hobbles over to Clyde and James. Old Man: I’m Old Man Patches. Patches takes in a breath, expelling it in a stinky blast of unbrushed dentures. OMP: I live around these parts. And then he snorts in a second breath, this time hawking a loogie to his side and down. Right onto James’ shoe. James doesn’t even react for a second, and when he finally does, he simply glances down at his shoe, then back over to Clyde with a bewildered look on his face, who responds with a shrug. Walkins: What is this, anyway? Bumfights? OMP: This here is Patch Club. Walkins: What the hell’s Patch Club? OMP: First rule of Patch Club is you don’t talk about Patch Club. Second rule of Patch Club is get me a beer! Walkins: Yes, sir! With a salute, Clyde sprints right into the cage, hitting his head on one of the steel support poles maintaining the cage’s structural integrity, before falling flat onto his back. A few feet from him, James wipes his shoe off against a nearby wall, then looks at Old Man Patches, who spits on his other shoe. OMP: And take that little queer back upstairs with you, before I decide to reciprocate the favor he did for me, and remove a few of his own teeth for him. Fearful, James quickly grabs both of Clyde’s arms and starts dragging him through the gate and up the stairs, keeping an eye on Old Man Patches the entire time. Farwell: Old people must really get pissed when they don’t have their carrot mush… A little under an hour later, Clyde comes to after taking that vicious shot to the head. Holding his skull as James hands him a couple aspirin, Clyde blinks hard a few times to regain focus, choking on the pills when he tries to swallow them dry, resulting in James needing to perform the Heimlich maneuver in the middle of the living room, just as Patches comes up the stairs to get some more beer and porn. OMP: When you two Nancy-boys are done playing Deliverance, I need one of you to go to the liquor store for me. Reaching into his pocket, Patches fishes around for a bit, pulling out a handful of bills. He looks down at the money, shrugs, then stuffs it back into his pants. OMP: Get something for yourself, too. Patches disappears back down into the darkness of the basement, and when they know he’s out of earshot, James turns to Clyde. Farwell: We have to get rid of that guy and his friends. They’ll drive me mad by the end of the week. Walkins: Yeah, and he ate my dinner! The traitor must die! Farwell: Let’s keep that as the backup plan. Listen, old people like naps, right? Well, they’ve gone quite a while without one today, so if we slip something into their drinks… Walkins: They’ll die! Wonderful plan! James’ eyes narrow again as he looks at Clyde, then shakes his head. After telling Clyde not to move from where he’s standing, James grabs his coat again and heads out the door in search of liquor and pills. Of course, he’s a cheap bastard, so the liquor is really just mouthwash, and the pills were bought from a shady underworld merchant, but it’s the thought that counts. The Fight When he returns, he’s already mixed the pills and the mouthwash, and Clyde’s still standing right where he was when James left. Knees trembling, a dribble of sweat drops down Clyde’s forehead as he tries to keep standing in place, and that’s when James realized it took him three hours to find an underworld merchant shady enough to accept human blood as payment. Farwell: You can sit down now, Clyde. Upon hearing that, Clyde’s legs buckle and he crumples to the floor in a heap. James gives a noncommittal grunt and moves on to the second phase of his plan. Drugging the elderly. Without Clyde to help him, James pours the drinks into paper cups and grinds the pills into each, carrying them on a tray to the battling old people below. It takes not but a minute for them to catch wind of any sort of alcohol, and they flock to James as the fights are halted for the moment. Each of the elderly drink their fill, and when hey start to feel the effects of the pills, James assists them up the stairs and into the guest room. One after one, he takes drugged old person up into the same room, until there’s only one left. A frail old woman clutching a walker. Glued to her lapel is a tag. Hello, it reads, my name is E. Anderson. Farwell: Come along. You’ve had a long day, and you must be so tired. Placing his hand gently on her shoulder to help guide her upstairs, James is caught off guard as she lifts her walker and catches his arm between the bars, spinning it and causing him to flip onto his back. But not before catching his ribs on the edge of a stair or two. As he takes a moment to go over what just happened, the old lady is already hobbling up the stairs to make her escape. James knows he has to get to her before she passes out, but his attempt is thwarted as she locks the door behind her, sealing him in the basement. Farwell: Ah, shits. Clyde spots the old lady making a break for the front door, and just manages to dive over the couch to stop her, still out of breath from standing in place for a few hours. Walkins: James told me to kill you as a backup plan. You are The One, Mrs. Anderson. I cannot allow you to leave. Moving forward, Clyde reaches out to grab her arm, but she holds out her wrist and blinds him with the glare from her Medicare bracelet, allowing her a new path of escape. Without sight for several seconds, Clyde loses track of her and is forced to use a pair of cheap black plastic sunglasses for protection, all the while scanning the room for any signs of elderly life. Walkins: Come out, come out, wherever you are. Startling Clyde, the old lady rushes out from the closet, nearly knocking him down with her walker. Just barely sidestepping the attack, Clyde maneuvers past the screaming old biddy, causing her to land flat on the floor. Adjusting his sunglasses, Clyde then straightens his invisible tie. Walkins: You’re becoming quite a nuisance, Mrs. Anderson. Just as Clyde’s about to finish her off, though, he’s stopped by Old Man Patches, who holds a broken beer bottle at Clyde’s throat. Walkins: Go ahead, kill me. The best thing about being me is that there’s so many of me! Patches and Mrs. Anderson share a confused look, but then it’s back to the task at hand. OMP: Listen here, whippersnapper. We’re gonna have Patch Club in your basement tonight, and every other night, otherwise you’ll never see another penny out of that Foundation. Besides, I kinda like it here…you two fruits have a nice basement. And if you were to make us leave, I might just have to apply a little pressure in the right places. Walkins: …Are you coming on to me? Old Man Patches stabs him in the shoulder with the broken glass. OMP: If you know what’s good for you, you won’t tell anyone about this little operation we have going on. I’m sure a certain underground organization wouldn’t be too happy to hear one of their best moneymakers got closed up because a couple of homos couldn’t keep their mouths closed for once. Silently, James grabs the old lady from behind, placing her in a choke hold after having jimmied the lock on the basement door open. Farwell: Aha! It looks like the tables have all of a sudden turned, doesn’t it? OMP: Not really. If you kill her, I kill him, and then the Mafia kills you. Walkins: Wait, did you say Mafia? OMP: What’s it to you? Clyde looks over at James, who shakes his head and repeatedly mouths the word ‘no’, before smiling and looking back to Patches. Walkins: Is it true that a Mafia Don can never refuse a pool challenge? OMP: Well, yes…but I hardly see how that correlates with-… Walkins: Tut, tut, tut! You leave that to me. Just get that Don of yours on the phone, and tell him there’s pool to be played. I’ll be in the basement. Pushing free of Old Man Patches, Clyde walks over to the stairs and heads back down into the basement, leaving James and Patches to look confusedly at each other. The Happy Ending Mafia Don: This had better be worth my time. What do you want, kid? Standing next to a pool table set up in a hidden room of the basement, a stereotypical Mafia Don eyes Clyde Walkins as he sets the balls up in entirely the wrong manner. Walkins: I want to challenge you to a game of pool. If I win, the old geezers get out of our house for good. Mafia Don: And if you lose? Walkins: I’ll give you the deed to this house. Mafia Don: No. Walkins: And the souls of three AWC wrestlers? Mafia Don: No. Walkins: …Twenty bucks? Mafia Don: Let’s play some pool. The Don reaches across the table and begins setting the balls up in the proper position, while his cronies unzip a black leather case with a golden pool cue inside. Nervous, James leans over to Clyde as he stretches his quads for some reason. Farwell: I think we might be in a bit of trouble, Clyde. That Mafia Don looks like he knows what he’s doing. You might have bitten off more-…dammit, Clyde! Quit doing those squat thrusts for five seconds and listen to me! Walkins: Don’t worry, James. I’ve got this under control. Smiling at the Don as he finishes setting everything up and readying his cue, Clyde graciously motions for him to take first shot. Walkins: What one do we try to get in the hole again? Grinning like a man about to take candy from a baby, the Don lines up his shot and breaks, sinking a ball right off the bat. Next to Clyde, James gulps and prepares for the inevitable. Less than a minute and a half later, the Mafia Don is packed into his car, along with his cronies and the elderly. Farwell: I can’t believe you sharked a Mafia Don. We’re so dead. With big fake smiles on their faces, James and Clyde talk to each other through their teeth as they wave goodbye to the furious Don. Walkins: Just keep on waving. When the car is fully out of sight, The Forsaken feel they can breathe a little easier, but jump when they turn around and see Old Man Patches still standing in the house. Walkins: What are you doing here? I thought we killed you! OMP: You have to take me back to the Foundation, dammit! They won’t be happy to have me back there, but a deal’s a deal. I hope it was worth your life to get me out of your house. Farwell: Somehow, I think it was. C’mon, Clyde, let’s get this thing out of our house before it expires. Back at the Make-A-Wish Foundation, Clyde tries to explain why Patches is back with them, and why he should still keep getting paid. Needless to say, he’s not too convincing. After receiving a final payment, Clyde is tossed out of the building, and right into another puddle left from the snowfall they endured the previous day. Brushing himself off, he walks back to the car, where James is waiting for him with the engine idling. Farwell: How’d it go? Walkins: I got fired and they gave me some money. Farwell: Better than I expected. This might be a Merry Christmas, after all. The tone in James’ voice almost sounds hopeful. Walkins: Why do you hate Christmas, anyway? James hears the question, but doesn’t respond. Instead, he leaves his body and finds himself back to when his hatred for Christmas began. Twelve years ago at an outlet mall. Clyde dared him to sit on Santa’s lap. James did it, because back then he loved Christmas. It was his favorite time of year. But, when he sat on Santa’s lap, something changed. Something…poked him in the thigh. The photograph he never shows anyone says it all. The awkward expression on James’ face as he looks down at Santa’s lap, and the mildly horny-slash-probably a little stoned look on Santa’s. The elf taking the pictures knew what was happening, but did nothing to stop it. Walkins: James? Can you hear me? James? James is suddenly brought back to reality, as he finds himself in the comforting cold of the crapmobile once again. Walkins: James, are you…crying? Farwell: I’m not crying, dammit! I won’t give him the satisfaction! Clyde moves back a little in his seat, positioning himself further from James, who bites his lower lip and sniffles slightly Walkins: …Kay. Can we go cash my check now? His eyes affixed to the windshield, James reaches down and puts the car into reverse, pulling out of the parking spot without even looking behind him. A quick switch of the gears again, and he takes the car over the heightened part of the sidewalk and right into the gutter, dragging two of the tires along the side until he reaches an intersection. As James starts to come out of the trance, he spots the bank and pulls over to the side of the road, letting Clyde out as he turns off the engine and decides to stretch his legs a little. The cool air will do well to help him forget his troubles. What James doesn’t account for, however, is that the two hobos they saw earlier are still there, waiting to further ruin his day. Telling Clyde to go on ahead into the bank, James decides to have a word with the elder hobo. As he approaches them, they greet him kindly. Adult Hobo: Good afternoon, kind sir. Do you think you could find you way to sparing some change for a couple of hungry folk like ourselves? Farwell: Only if you could find your way to kissing my ass. Their cheery expressions now gone, the two hobos stare at James in utter disbelief. Adult Hobo: Excuse me? Farwell: You heard me. People like you are the reason people cheat on their taxes. People like you are the reason there’s so much crime and illiteracy and poverty in the world, because all you do is keep making more of it. If you had any sense in the world, you’d give this kid back to his mother and go be a goddamn hippie somewhere else, because the free ride ends here! Unexpectedly, the little one breaks out in tears. Child Hobo: This is worse than the Christmas mom died… Putting an arm around his son, the man tries to comfort him the best he can, while staring a hole through James. Adult Hobo: Shame on you. When his mother was hit by a train while trying to save a pair of sneakers tied to the tracks, I was so grief-stricken that I couldn’t work any longer, and then no one else would hire me. After all, who wants to hire the guy that married someone with an extreme shoe fetish? No one. And now me and my son live on the streets, because people like you don’t care enough to do anything. Feeling guilt for one of the only times in his life, James hangs his head and searches for the right words as his opaque soul lightens, but Clyde finds them as he returns from the bank. Walkins: Hey, kid. Santa said to tell you that your mom isn’t really dead. She’s waiting for you in my car…haha! Just kidding, she’s dead. James’ eyes go wide and he makes the throat cutting motion, trying to get Clyde to stop. Clyde clearly misreads the gesture. Walkins: She’s extremely dead? James hurries with the signal, and this time Clyde seems to get it. Walkins: We cut her head off. As the two hobos look on in shock and disbelief once more, James makes the save by performing his first gesture of goodwill in many a year. Farwell: I wasn’t too nice to you two, and since it is Christmas, and we just got paid…well, I think it might be nice if the four of us had Christmas dinner tonight. Maybe we could all find something to celebrate. Adult Hobo: Did you just say you got paid? Farwell: I did. Standing up, the adult hobo pulls out a gun and aims it at James. Adult Hobo: Hand over the cash and no one gets hurt. Child Hobo: Yeah! Hand it over! Holding a brown bag out at arm’s length, the child’s gruff voice and five o’clock shadow give away his true form. Farwell: You’re not even a kid! You’re a midget! Walkins: I hate midgets! Reacting out of instinct more than anything, Clyde pulls back and boots the midget right in his oversized head, sending him sliding across the sidewalk. Taken by surprise, the supposed older hobo lowers the gun for a moment, and a moment is all James needs to put all his skill as a wrestling technician to good use. Smack. Field goal kick, right between the uprights. Rushing to the car as the two hobos writhe on the pavement, James stops short and runs back over to them, frisking their bodies until he finds a couple wallets and a watch that catches his eye. Trying it on as he starts the car, James takes a moment to admire his new bling, before hitting the gas as Clyde counts the loot in the wallets they ‘found’. Walkins: There’s at least a hundred bucks in here. We’re rich! Farwell: Looks like it’s gonna be a Merry Christmas, after all. With thoughts in his head of Clyde punting a midget, and himself kicking a hobo right in the smelly old balls, a smile crosses James’ lips as this memory overtakes the old one, and Christmas regains its magic.
