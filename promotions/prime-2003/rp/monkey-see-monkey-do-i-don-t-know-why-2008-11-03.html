monkey see monkey do (i don't know why)

3 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 3 nov 08 “I’m mighty glad ya could take some time fer a chat. Just needed some unbiased ears, y’know?” “The fuck I’m s’posed ta do, sunshine. We’re practically fam’ly, right?” August Monday just shrugs at me, takes a long drink of the Coors Lite I bought him, holding it between his thumb and index as he drank it. He rocked the bottom of the bottle back and forth like a pendulum within his grip, just trying to be the listener he clearly was not. “I ain’t real good at this, Brock. I bin round some real bad eggs, sunshine, ain’t never heard of shit like this before.” Stifle my laugh. “Seems funny a feller like you, whose so switched on, can’t remember nothin’. Just sayin’,” August admits. Swirl the whiskey in my tumbler in a circle, just watching the ice crash against the glass walls. “I hear what yer sayin’, Augie. I’d be double takin’, too. Detective Renner seems to think I’m his man.” “Why’d they let ya out then?” “Dunno. Guess sprog ain’t enough to commit a man to a penitentiary. HA!” My humour seems lost on August. He stares dead ahead of him, trying to remain unbiased all the same. Hell of a situation to put a man in but I needed someone to listen. Not judge me. Knock back the contents of my glass to rekindle the courage burning in my belly. “I ain’t like that, Augie.” “Like what?” “I ain’t…” the words are hard to say. “I ain’t a fucken killer.” August nods. “I know that. I seen plen’y killers, Brock-O. The Asylum was full of ‘em. I don’t see it in ya. You don’t shout out ‘I’m a murderous bastard’ to me, sunshine.” “Maybe I been framed.” He shrugs at me. “Maybe you have. Dunno.” Staring into the bottom of my empty glass doesn’t seem to appropriate any logical explanation as to who would even try and do something like that to me. I notice August’s attention spinning to the television in the corner. Standing in all of his Jewish glory is Jerry Springer on a re-run of his show. He’s sporting his usual bemused look as to what he’s presenting on his program, almost always stifling a laugh as he displays to the rest of America the bottom-feeders of society. “Today on the show, we’re talking to people who desire strange things. Fetishes. Some people call them freaks. But these are people with legitimate desires for something outside of the ordinary.” August digs his elbow into my ribs, which stirs me to sit up straighter. “I fucken love, Jerry.” I just nod in reply. “Our first guest today is Raquelesha. Lets’ give her a warm welcome everybody!” Jerry calls to his audience. They begin to applaud, as does Jerry, trying to balance his cue cards and microphone whilst clapping. The crowd let out some gasps as the applause slows. Out comes a stunning black woman, she’s missing a leg at first glance, and uses a crutch to support her on her way out. As she turns to take a seat in one of the chairs, we can see she has her leg tied up behind her thigh. “That’s gotta by uncomfortable,” remarks August. I can feel my mouth gaping wide open, stunned to see what I’m seeing. Pieces of my puzzle begin to fit into place within my mind’s eye. “No fucken way!” I murmur. “Meet Raquelesha, folks. Raquelesha has the desire to become an amputee,” Jerry advises while the audience gasp in shock some more. She nods proudly from her seat. “Tha’s right, Jerreh. I don’t want my leg no mo’, aight? I has had this desire fo’ years an’ years, aight? Jus’ don’t want muh leg no mo’. Is that so much ta ask?” she boldy questions the host. Jerry shrugs as the audience all mutter amongst themselves the repercussions of wanting to be an amputee. I can’t believe my ears. “I was born wit’ two but I only ever wanted one, aight? But I can’t find no doc who’ll do the operation fo’ me, Jerreh!” Running his hand over his mouth, hiding his stifled laughter again, Jerry Springer asks his question of Raquelesha but directs it to the audience. “So you’re trying to find a doctor who will amputate your leg?” “Tha’s right, Jerreh. But there ain’t none in the country who’s gunna do it. I gotta fly out ta Thailand coz tha’s the only country I bin able to find someone who’ll do it.” August looks at me, his mouth gapes as wide as mine. “Yer not thinkin’…” He doesn’t need to finish his sentence because I’m already nodding. He finds himself nodding also. “Yeah. S’pose it’s likely.” My mind’s eye was busy putting the clues together like a well-oiled machine. “Y’know, Augie, she had an awful fixation on my hand.” “Figures a girl interested in doin’ would take to a feller like you.” “That’s what I was thinkin’,” I admit. - | s h e p | - Samantha Gideon rolls off the top of me, her chest heaving with the loss of breath for the ride we took together. Collecting my own breath I sit up, snatching the bottle of Jack from the side table, tipping it’s contents into my throat to fuel the celebration. “Holy shit,” she gasps. “You really know how to please a girl.” “Ya ain’t so bad yerself, Sammy.” She blushes and pulls a sheet across her naked body before realising it has all been seen before. Brushing aside her momentary lapse in exhibitionism, she rises to her feet without the aide of the sheet’s coverage. Taking a bottle of champagne, she necks it straight from the bottle before moving across to her handbag, bending down to remove some of its contents. Divert my gaze to give her some privacy and decency, by not staring into her breakfast. “You want some?” she asks. Between her thumb and forefinger, at eye level, is a clear ziplock bag with a white powder inside of it. “Whoa! Is that what I think it is?” “You think it’s coke?” “Somethin’ like that. I don’t think I’m interested.” Her mouth turns down at the sides with a melodramatic pout. “C’mon, it’ll be fun. A little bit of Dutch courage can’t hurt nobody.” “’Cept you don’t have to piss in a cup twice a week, Sam. I do. Let’s not and say we did, right?” She shakes her head, tipping some of the contents out of the bag onto the bench, taking a razor from her other hand to begin cutting it up. “And I thought you were a champion,” she probes. “And here I was thinkin’ peer pressure went out in high school. No thanks, Sammy. No blow for ol’ Brock.” She shrugs. “Suit yaself, Brock.” Shifting it into lines with a credit card then she rolls a fifty-dollar bill into a tube. Tucks one end into her left nostril and chases the line from tail to top. Throws her head back, taking an unladylike sniff to pull the powder into where ever it needs to go to find the euphoria. Staggering back toward the bed, as the cocaine took itself to that special place, Samantha began to swing her hips, pushing her hair up on top of her head with her hands cupping the back of her neck. Gyrating her groin, her hands slowly descend to her crotch and I felt a lump in my throat forming. “Oh wow. Coke just makes me wanna FUCK SO HARD!” Choke out a laugh. “You really know how to get a feller throbbin’, Sammy. Whaddaya say we have drink for a bit while my nuts refuel, huh?” Again, the melodramatic pout. Piling herself onto the foot of the bed and rolling onto her back, spread eagle, she sighs heavily. “So… you just wanna chat or somethin’?” “It’d be nice, I guess. Since we bearly know one another.” She sits up, as if she just had an idea. Her gaze wanders to take a glimpse at my missing right hand. “Let’s trade secrets!” she gasps profoundly. “Secrets? I’m an open book, Sammy. There’s no secrets to Brock Shepherd. I’m a simple man with simple pleasures. Fuckin’, drinkin’ and fightin’. That makes me happy.” Her finger rolls along her lip as if it were helping her find the words she needed to say. Gulps down a deep breath and takes a longer look at my missing hand. “Wanna hear my secret?” - | s h e p | - “Brock, s’pose this bitch was nuttier than elephant shit an’ went an’ done this herself like. How you gunna prove it?” August asks. He is now onto the Jack Daniels also, having come to the same revelations on the situation as me, trying to stomach the idea of it all. I down the last of my glass and put it on the counter, nodding to the barkeeper to top it once more. “Fucked if I know, Augie.” “You can’t prove it ya better learn to like the taste of dick,” he jokes, chuckling to himself. I can’t help but laugh at the situation. “Things keep comin’ back to me, Augie. Like in the movies. Piece by piece, ya know?” He nods. “Yeah. I know what you mean. Same thing happened to me after Ellen died,” he says, with an awkward pause to accompany his words. Sigh heavily, resting my face in my palm, trying to find some answers within myself as to what happened. Hear the door to the bar open but pay it no attention. “You’re ruining our image,” says the familiar voice. Jody Monroe takes up a seat beside me, resting her own face in her own palms as her elbows supports her head on the bar. “Just Wrestling Champion massacres whore! Fuck! This is not a good situation, Brock. First Crippen gets dragged through the system with a murder wrap. Now you! What is this? The curse of the Just Wrestling Championship?” She nods at the barkeeper and points to my drink, raising two fingers before noticing that August has finished his drink also. Her digits change from two raised to three. August nods at her good willed gesture. “Is it too much to ask for a regular champion? Is it?” she asks. I wasn’t quite sure if it were rhetorical or not, so I shrug nonetheless. Knock back this JD in one fell swoop, putting the glass back on the bar. Exhale my exhaustion of thinking through the situation. It had kept me up for countless nights now and I was no closer to the truth than the extreme epiphany brought on by the foolhardiness of common Americans and their fantasies on Springer. “I dunno if I can get you out of this, Brock. I can pull strings but damned if I know how to get you out of this,” she admits. August nods and raises his glass. “Amen to that, sister. The boy is fucked!” She turns her attention to Monday. “You’re August Monday, right? Have you thought about a tour with Ju-” “You’re singing songs to the deaf, darlin’. Ain’t interested.” Jody Monroe’s chest drops again as she sips her glass of Jack, cringing at his bitterness. “If only I could go back there,” I mutter under my breath. Monroe’s ears prick up at my external thought process. “Go where?” “Back to the Motel 6. Just to see if it jogs anything. Like a memory or somethin’.” “Does that shit work?” asks August. Shrug at him. “Dunno. Seems to work fine in the movies.” August strokes his beard and looks to his watch, then back at me, then his watch again. “Leave it with me. We’ll getcha some memories back, Brock-O. Leave it to the Raging fucken Fear.” My near brother-in-law takes his leave, bidding Jody Monroe adieu and leaving us to make our own conversation. “Think he can help?” she asks. Shrug at her. “He can’t hurt nothing. Right?”
