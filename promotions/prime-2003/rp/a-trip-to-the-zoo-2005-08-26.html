A trip to the Zoo

26 August 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 26th August 2005 The whispers of the public were once again a familiar sound in the ear of Jason Jameson. The looks and glances from everyone he passed were part and parcel of his days once more. In his words, “it feels so damn good to be back…” Walking through the Honolulu Zoo, Jason could feel pairs of eyes watching him. He could hear himself being recognised by the other visitors around him and He knew that everyone was talking about him. The reactions were expected by Jason. Not because of some over inflated arrogant ego that most P.W.A. graduates possessed but from a sense of relief. He needed the reactions to justify his return. He needed the reactions to appease his doubting minds. He needed to be a success to spite his mother. Mrs. Susan Jameson was not best pleased about the grand return of The Star to the wrestling ring. The single parent, deserted years previous by the father of her only child, had nursed her son after the disaster that his PCW career had been, and before that his attempts at NTICW and DWF. She had watched as he swayed on the brink of existence deciding on his own mortality. She had helped him through, helped him process his thoughts, and set Jason up with a successful shoe shop in Toronto. Susan had rescued her son, so the morning she found him stewing over a letter from PRIME, she had only one mindset; keeping her son out of a wrestling ring. As Susan opened the letter, she realised that Jason had been accepted to the Ideal 4 competition. The Primetime Wrestling Academy still held an eminence with fed-heads around the world for producing skilled wrestlers. Of course, Susan didn’t care. She was keeping Jason out of the i4 competition. And she had managed it. Jason was lead to believe he hadn’t been accepted and he was okay with that, knowing it was a long shot for a failed PCWer to make it into PRIME, but seeing as Jon Kano made it, Jason fancied a go. Three months later and Susan came clean. She told her son the truth, sighting that she was protecting him from the wrestling world. There and then, it that single sentence that she said, everything between the pair was lost. The bond that the mother and son had shared for the previous twenty years when Jason’s father walked out was gone. There and then, Susan Jameson lost her only son. “Hey…” The Missing Ideal was yanked from his daydream as he was approached by a family of three; a dad and his two children. “…are you from PRIME?” asked the father as he rested a hand on a shoulder of each of his children, shepherding them towards the professional wrestler. The man was about forty, with slightly balding brown hair that was classically swept to the side. He was wearing a green polo shirt with khaki shorts and had to squint at Jason as the sun was glaring into his face. Turning away from the gorilla enclosure, Jason was back in real-time. He was no longer playing back THAT conversation in his head. Looking at the family a smile grew across his face. Finally some verification of his return to wrestling; finally his return would be justified by the public response. Jason nodded with a smile as the threesome stepped closer. The boy and the girl that the father brought had very differing expressions about meeting the superstar. “This is my son, Justin…” smiled the father as he encouragingly pushed his little boy forward. Justin was wearing a sky blue t-shirt and matching shorts as he clutched a small notepad and pen t his chest. Upon his father’s gentle push, the young boy who was no more than four or five stepped forward and extended the paper and pen at Jameson. Smiling Jason dropped onto the balls of his feet and met the five year old at eye-level. Two years out of the ring and in the wilderness had taught him to cherish any moments with the fans. Gratefully, Jason received the notepad and the pen and flipped the book open. Resting the pad on his knee Jason scrawled on the pad, writing a simple message to little Justin and then adding his signature. Standing back up Jason was introduced to the girl. “This is my daughter, Rachel…” Rachel, your typical Goth fourteen year old wearing loads of clothes even in the blistering sun, received the same reassuring push from her father but she seemed to treat it with distain as she totally no-sold the introduction instead directing her attention towards the gorillas. “Hello Rachel…” beamed Jason, trying to make the most of a quickly deteriorating situation. Even the hello was no-sold by this Hulk Hogan of conversation as she twisted her head back further making sure that Jason had no chance of eye contact. One got that sense these actions were more in spite of her wonderful father rather than any distain for Jason. “Isn’t wrestling fake?” mumbled the teenager after which her father pleaded with her to behave. Jason continued to smile, not sure of where to go with the enthralling conversation. He glanced up at Rachel’s dad who as yet had not introduced himself, to see that he was focusing on his daughter. After the awkward silence that followed, Rachel’s dad made his apologies and the family were gone. Rachel feeling pleased with herself that she had shown up her father and Justin clutching his new autograph close to his chest. Jason rested his hand on the railing and watched them leave. Wrestling was a cut-throat business and if he had to hospitalise a tag team to get into a federation so be it, but he always would have time for the fans. Jason Jameson in the ring and Jason Jameson out were completely different, but where they matched… They are Ideal.
