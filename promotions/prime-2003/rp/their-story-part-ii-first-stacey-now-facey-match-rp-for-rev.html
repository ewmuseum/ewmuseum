Date: Tomorrow His Story: My Home is Your Home… but not by choice ~Saturday, January 21st, 2006. 2:19P.M~ Coincidence doesn’t exist. It’s nothing more than a myth… like Bigfoot, The Loch Ness Monster, true happiness. All there is to the idea of ‘coincidence’ is blurry photos, blurrier tales, and misconstrued information. Coincidence is just a way for people afraid of opening their eyes and facing facts to eviscerate the truth and replace its guts with laughs and fluff. The simplest way to put it… coincidence is essentially bullshit. “Bullshit. That’s a load of shit and you know it, Scotty.” Scotty didn’t seem to be hearing the profanities that erupted from his brother’s mouth like the rinse of a beday toilet (get it… no….?... okay). Instead, the blonde headed younger Ward brother was entranced by the way the ketchup rolled out from underneath the bun of his hamburger. “Check it out, bro” Scotty spun the burger in his hands “its dripping out evenly all the way around! It’s all systametrical.” “Symmetrical.” “No, it’s like that all the way around. Look…” Scotty shoved the burger towards his brother’s face, but Matt blocked the motion, pushing away the sandwich. Scotty shrugged his shoulders and took a big bite, ruining the systametricalness. “Anyway, I don’t think it’s bullshit. Stacey said she had family that lived in the area and that she just came to stay with them cuz things are kinda rough for her right now. Why is that so hard to believe?” “Because as soon as she showed up in town, she was at my door. Not to mention the fact that she’s called here four or five times since she showed up.” “And you keep on ignoring her.” “I have too. She’s basically stalking me. You think it’s a coincidence that she dumps a baby on Killean’s doorstep, apparently leaves him a note about how sorry she feels for me after he beat the shit out of me with a baseball bat… and then about a week later she’s at my door, live and in person?” “Ok, you win. It’s not coincidence. She wants in your pants.” Scotty took another bite from his burger, practically unhinging his jaw as if some sort of snake species engulfing its unfortunate prey. “Even if that’s the case” the words barely broke free, somehow managing to drill their way through the mass of saliva, bread, and slaughtered cow “what’s the big deal? You call her back and explain things to her. End of story.” “It’s never that easy with Stacey. And to be perfectly honest, I’m not sure she’s emotionally stable. You know Stacey has had some problems in the past, battled with depression, maybe mania. I’m not really sure what all she’s dealt with.” “Wait, you mean to tell me you’ve got a Ph.D. in psychology and you have no idea what her diagnosis was?” “She was never my patient, Scotty. Rule number one in the code of ethics for a psychologist… never provide therapy to anyone with which you have any sort of prior relationship. Add to that the fact that I was never privileged to her file, and I can only guess based on what she told me.” “And I’m guessing that’s probably not the most reliable information in the world, seeing as she was all crazy and stuff.” “She’s not crazy… well… I dunno.” Matt stood up and headed for the fridge. He had passed on lunch. Between the lingering effects of the concussion and the severely bruised rib he had suffered after a rather painful landing in the loss to Clyde, he had lost most of his appetite. Still, Matt hoped that perhaps a cold drink would wash away the foul taste in his mouth. “The point is, I really don’t need Stacey hanging around here or calling every other day when I’m trying to work things out with Mary.” He reached into the fridge and grabbed a pop. “Work things out? Have you even thought about reaching for the telephone and calling your wife? Have you even thought about fixing things with her enough to cause you to pick up the car keys and at least flirt with the idea of driving over to her parents house to see her?” “I think she needs her time and space… otherwise, why’d she leave in the first place?” Matt popped open the can with one hand and dropped it on the table, its contents splashing out in a small radius around the drink, as he slumped back into seat across from his brother. “Besides, lately… my work has been eating up all my time and energy.” “Ohhh, that’s right. I forgot your time recently had been completely consumed by winning the Universal title as quick as anyone could imagine, and then losing it even quicker.” “It’s more than that, Scotty. The entire PRIME organization has some… ridiculous bias against me.” He had to pause for a drink, the taste was growing even worse. “Everything I said on ReVolution a week and half ago was true. And further proof lies in this bullshit random tag event they’re throwing together for ReVolution this week. Am I really supposed to believe that it was just luck of the draw that left me teamed with the one prick in this fed who can’t stand me, and a guy I’ve never heard of?” “Of course. Must be a conspiracy, bro. I mean…sticking you with Facey and Collins, two guys you are clearly a league above…” “I never said I was better than Collins. I said I have no idea who the guy really even is. Never even crossed paths with the guy. Maybe I could wipe the floor with him, maybe he could wipe the floor with me. It’d be nice to know one way or the other before being forced to team with the guy.” “So you’re pissed cuz you have to team with a guy you don’t like and a guy you don’t know you don’t like?” Scotty finished off his burger, not seeming to fully follow, or fully care. “Facey isn’t just a guy I don’t like. He’s an annoying punk who weighs about a hundred pounds soaking wet, and for all his self-promotion about being Super Fuckin Duper… he’s probably gonna go cost us the damn match at ReVolution. Lisieux will probably eat him for breakfast. Hell Noble or Arson might break him in half for that matter. Look, the reason I’m pissed is because I shouldn’t even be a part of any six man tag gimmicked event that’s just prostituting the promotion of Culture Shock. What I should be doing, is climbing into the ring with Clyde and winning the Universal Championship for the second time, since PRIME seemed so intent on doing everything they could to stack the odds against me the first time.” With each word that parted his lips, it was clear that Matt was building towards an explosion that would typically be reserved for the ring. Success and rage were inversely proportional. As success had collapsed all around Matt, his anger had steadily risen, leaving it towering above the remains of happiness that laid at his feet. The violence of each syllable filled the air with a sting that was wearing thin on his brother. “You know what, in all this talk about screw jobs and bias and wrestling eating up all your time… you were right about one thing. That Universal title is devouring you.” Scotty nodded his head towards his brother as if to add some sort of emphasis. It was emphasis his brother didn’t want. “Save that shit for Socrates. I don’t wanna hear it.” It seemed tension was filling the air, or some kind of cliché of that nature, and for a moment it looked like blood would be shed between brothers, but a knock at the door saved the kitchen from a crimson mess. Matt stood from the table and headed into the living room, almost forgetting his younger brother. When he opened the door, he expected Stacey and braced himself for the worst. Oddly, that wasn’t enough. “Hey, fatty… you’ve got cable right?” There in the doorway stood The Illustrious Face-Eater himself, complete with a small suitcase in one hand and a pillow in the other. “What the hell is going on?!” “Slumber party! I’m moving in for the week.” Facey slipped right past Matt and into the living room, where he threw his belongings down on the couch as if he’d lived there his whole life. “Someone’s gotta make sure you stay focused for our match at ReVolution. Rumor has it your wife dumped you and I don’t need you going all emo on me in the ring Wednesday night cuz you lost you’re poor little title and cuz you’re poor little wife took the kid and left you all alone.” Just as Facey finishes the sentence, Scotty walks into the living room. “WhOA! You went gay already! Jesus she just left you!. Ohhh… I get it… she left you cuz you’re a huge qu…” “That’s my brother, Scotty.” Matt points towards his younger sibling, who just nods his head. “Don’t see the resemblance. Did you guys have a milkman in your neighborhood growing up?” Facey crashes on the couch beside his belongings. “Nice couch. Pretty comfortable. You ought to get at least a decent amount of sleep on it. I even brought you a pillow.” “This is ridiculous. I’m gonna go for a walk. When I get back, I want you out of my house, Dick!” Tchu grabbed his coat from the closet near the front door and left his own home, slamming the door shut behind him. “He’ll be back! They aaaalllwaaays come back.” “Of course he will.” Scotty scratched his head in confusion, “he specifically said he would be back.” “Told ya.” Facey started to grab for the remote, then paused. “Wait! Even better idea. Where’s this douchebag keep his telephone?” * * * Her Story: She Wished it Would have Been a Telemarketer ~Saturday, January 21st, 2006. 2:31P.M~ “Is it just me, or has television just gone down the toilet in the last few years?” He shoved the remote towards the screen, clicking the ‘Power’ button as he did so, thankfully bringing darkness to the misery that had filled the TV set just moments earlier. “Pretty much, Papa.” Mary didn’t look up, instead keeping her eyes locked on Georgie. The mother and daughter had been playing on the floor for the last half an hour, and to be honest, she couldn’t even say what her father had been watching that so disgusted him. Still, their tastes were quite similar and she figured it couldn’t have been any good. “The newspaper is more entertaining than anything Hollywood is churning out these days.” “You just like news. You want to know about the important things going on in the world. The real stuff. And unfortunately, the closest television gets is reality TV… actually… reality TV is probably father from reality than anything on TV.” “Amen to that. Damn MTV generation.” Just as Papa was about to go into a mini-rant, the phone rang. He walked over to the small corner table in the living room and looked at the caller I.D. “Says its from, Matthew.” Immediately, Mary jumped off the floor and ran over to the phone. As she looked down at the I.D. as if she didn’t believe her father, Papa went over and picked up Georgie and said something along the lines of, “you come with Grandpa. We need to leave mommy alone to talk with daddy.” As the two left the room, Mary answered the call, just before the machine could kick in. “Hello.” What she heard on the other end was not her husband’s voice. Immediately, before she could even ask who was calling, she felt the nervous lump that had formed in her throat vanish with disappointment. As uneasy as she may have been at what she would say to Matthew, or more specifically, what she would say to him… she wanted desperately for the opportunity to hear it. “Who is this? Why are you calling from Matthew’s house?...... huh? Mr. Super what?...... Georgie…… I’ve been waiting for him to call……. I’m going to go…….bye.” She hung up the phone with a puzzled look on her face. Just a minute earlier, she had been filled with what she thought was hope, though she couldn’t be sure. It had been so long since anything other than emptiness had consumed her that it was entirely possible she was confusing hope with anger or paranoia or any number of things. Still, she thought it was hope. But hearing some stranger ramble on about how she needed to patch things up with her husband left her feeling only confused and disgusted. Those two feelings she remembered for sure. She’d spent months confused, deprived of her memory, and more recently, upon its return, she’d found plenty to be disgusted with. “That was short.” Her father’s words raced across the room and filled her ears, snapping her back to her surroundings. “What did he have to say.” “It wasn’t Matthew.” “What? Then who was it?” “I dunno. Just some guy who said I should call Matthew, not wait for him to call me.” “Well,” Papa sat down on the couch with the baby, and bounced her gingerly up and down on his right knee. “maybe he was right.” * * * His…errr… Facey’s Story: Uuggghhh… ~Saturday, January 21st, 2006. 2:31P.M~ “Dude, c’mon… put the phone down.” Scotty pleaded with Facey, but the man otherwise known as a dick wouldn’t listen. “Where is Mary staying?” “With her parents, why?” Just as Scotty finishes answering, and conveniently a split second too late, it occurs to him why. He slapped himself on the forehead. “Excellent!” Facey examined the list of numbers on a little card near the phone. “Ahah! Speed dial #3!” The unwanted guest pushed a couple buttons and lifted the contraption to his ear. For a moment, he sat, saying nothing, looking completely focused, then suddenly, he stomped his foot and slammed his fist on the table. “Damn it! No one is answering. Are her parents asleep already? I mean… I know senior citizens go to bed early, but gimme a brea…” before he can finish the sentence, Scotty walks over and turns the setting to ‘speaker phone’. “If you’re going to do this. I’m going to listen.” Scotty grabbed the receiver and put it back in its place. “Fine. Have it your way, shrinknuts.” “What?” Scotty’s confusion is followed by a loud ring that echoes through the speakers. But it’s a short ring, as someone picks up on the other end. “Hello?” “Dude, its Mary!” Scotty whispers as quietly as he can, letting Facey know that it is in fact his desired target on the other end of the line. “Hellooooo Mary!” Facey lets an excited greeting blast free from his always moving mouth. ”Who is this? Why are you calling from Matthew’s house?” “This is The Illustrious Face-Eater. Adam Dick! Mr. Super Fuckin Duper!” Even though Mary isn’t around to see it, and Scotty could care less, Facey throws his arms in the air, cheering for himself. ”Mr. Super what?” “Super Fu… nevermind” Facey lets his arms fall back to his side. “It’s not important. What’s important is that I have to team up with your husband this week for a match, and I can’t have him costing me a victory because he’s moping around the ring all depressed that he hasn’t seen his wife or daughter Georgia…” ”Georgie” “Sorry, whatever. The point is, I don’t want this bummed out shell of a man dragging me down with him. You two really need to sit down and work things out! Come to an agreement! Kiss and make up! What’s taking so long, huh?” ”I’ve been waiting for him to call…” “Whaaa?!!?! The 1950s was sooooo last month. You do know its ok for girls to call guys nowadays, right? Pick up the phone, darlin’, and give your husband a call so you can get over this whole Days Of Our Lives nonsense!” Scotty shook his head and rolled his eyes, unable to fully believe what was going on. ”I’m going to go” “Bad phone plan?” ”bye” “Bye-B…” the second syllable never completed as Mary hung up the phone before Facey could finish the farwell….errr… farewell. Farwell, farewell… they’re both depressing “I can’t believe you just did that.” Scotty walked back and forth, afraid that his brother was going to kill him for letting Facey use the phone… especially to call Mary. “Matthew’s gonna kill me for letting you use the phone… especially to call Mary!” “Meh, he’ll get over it. What do you have that’s good to eat?” Facey stepped into the kitchen and started rummaging through the cupboards. “Besides, it’s for the best. Your brother needs to get his head in the game, I don’t want him costing me the match this week.” “That’s funny, he was saying the same thing about you earlier.” “What? WHAT?! He’s worried about me losing the match for us? Pleeeaaase. I’ll carry Collins’ ass and his too! Besides I’m not the one who lost my championship as soon as I’d practically won the damn thing. You’re brother is terrible. Guy can’t even win a match on his own, and he’s worried about me costing him a loss?” “Matthew is 9-1. He’s only lost one match since he joined PRIME.” “You’re just defending him cuz he’s your brother.” Facey grabbed a jar of peanut butter and threw it at Scotty, who just barely managed to duck the flying food. “Its true. Just one loss.” “To Clyde Walkins! He lost the Universal title, so that counts double. And he lost it to Clyde Walkins… Clyde Walkins! So that counts quadruple. Lets see… one loss x 2 x 4 = …. Something around ten! You’re brother is 9-10. That’s a losing record! He’s a loooooser!” Scotty was about to try and form some comeback, probably something along the lines of… “well, he kicked your ass a couple times already!” or “he’s got a better record than you do in PRIME!” or “don’t throw peanut butter at me, tool!”… but he didn’t have to stand up for his brother. Matt had returned home. “I smell lame” the words thundered into the kitchen from the living room, “He must not have left yet.” Matt finished the sentence just as he stepped into the kitchen to find his brother standing in annoyance and Dick tearing through the cabinets. “Don’t keep any faces in there. Sorry.” “Haha, funny guy. You’re brother tells me you’re afraid I’m going to cost us the match at ReVolution.” “I might have said something along those lines. Cant say I’m at all happy about having to team up with you and Collins.” “You should be grateful, shitwipe. Leaning on two other guys is probably your best shot at getting back on a win streak.” “Why haven’t you left?” Matt looked pained as he said the words. “Jesus, Nelson probably paid you to come bang on my door, just to piss me off…” “Save the ‘woe is me’ conspiracy theory for someone who doesn’t hate you. And for the love of God, cheer up before the party, ok?” “What party?” Scotty and Matthew said the words in unison. “THE party!” So much for clarification. Still, as unclear as ‘the party’ might have been, one thing was very clear. It was going to be a long week. A long, crazy, facey kind of week.
