She's Hearing Voices (Match RP - v Cain)

Date: 09/10/05 She's Hearing VoicesWritten: 9th October, 2005. 'She's falling down the stairs,She's tearing out her hair.She's screaming my name, in the night,To keep her warm.' - Bloc Party. I - Withdrawal Symptoms. 'You asleep?' The words positively sparkled through the hazy world surrounding Karina Wolfenden. It was the voice she'd been playing over and over on her answering machine, month-old messages becoming a vocal loop of distant hope. As many free PRIME DVD's as she could blag from her employers had taken up home in her PS2, scene-select gravitating straight towards the in-rings of Angelo Deville. He didn't speak words, he exuded them. Sliver tongue flicking off barbed put-downs, comedy so dry it bordered on arid. 'Hey, you,' followed soothingly as a bemused, distant grunt escaped the muffling covers. Kooky, quirky, and admittedly a little left of the middle she admittedly was, but Karina Wolfenden wasn't crazy. She knew Angelo wasn't there, or at least her rational side did, which was thankfully overbearing. 'There are so many things I wan-...' the words paused on, a rare moment when the voice of Angelo Deville wasn't entirely sure of what to say. 'So many things I need to say to you, Kari.' The words were coming from under the covers, not beyond them. Hugging a pillow tightly to her curled-up body, Karina just waited for them to cease, to fade back out into whatever oblivion the Crimson Angel had hurled himself. Teeth ground, trying to drown them out, while eyes screwed tightly shut, letting her focus instead on the swirls of colour hovering in and out of the blackness. 'I haven't been the most honest of people in my life,' her subconscious breached every defence that Kari could erect to deliver. 'To my word, yes. But as far as my intentions went, no. My life has been one long gamble, and there's only so many times you can go all in, before you're forced to leave the table. I left without a trace because... well, if I stayed, it wouldn't be me they came for.' No matter how tightly Kari's eyes screwed up, they couldn't stifle her tear glands, as a drop of salty sorrow dropped from her cheekbone to the sheets. 'As much as you were my savoir, in some respects, you were also my weakness... my Achilles’ heel,' continued on as Karina began to shiver under the covers. 'I was left with a choice, maybe the first real one I'd ever had to make. Stay and risk losing you, or leave and risk you losing yourself.' Despite the grinding teeth, tightly shut eyes, defensive thoughts, and comfort hugging, part of Karina wanted to throw the covers off of her and spring back into his arms. She missed the way he'd stay in front of the mirror until every last hair was in place, the way his eyebrows arched despite his steely expressions, even the infuriating habit of folding everything crisply, ready for the next morning's routine. What would he miss about her, she wondered. 'You're stronger than that, Kari,' the void left by Angelo assured, 'why do you think it was you I wanted? I needed an equal, maybe even my better. If I was here, you'd probably land one on me, so why be any different in my absence. Everything I had to leave behind is yours. Use that to remember me, not these voices, these fears, these hopes... these dreams.' Just as she'd pulled the covers further over herself, fortifying, the voice detached. 'Kari.' From beyond the duvet, through her ear rather than from her brain, the voice of Angelo Deville stated her name... two syllables lavishly delivered with regal inflections, made to sound the most special syllables ever muttered by man or angel. Hurling the covers off, Kari shot up, eyes wide open in hope of once again settling of the secret smile of Angelo, free of smugness, free of a sinister smirk... as Semisonic sang, the one he saved only for her. Reality's mocking arrow then shot through her heart, while any warmth her thoughts brought was extinguished by the early morning chill that crept icily in through the open windows. 'Did I..?' her unsure side couldn't help but wonder, trying to swim through a murky, hazy sea of alcohol to rescue the memory of the night before. Rationality lectured the K-Wolf, drilling it into her that she decided to leave the windows open the night before, needing the fresh, crisp 1am air to battle the sickly feeling that excesses had burdened her with. Nodding along, she let reason think it had got through... assuring that side of her that she wasn't cracking. But in her deepest thoughts, under the radar of sense and reason, Karina knew what she had to do. As the cold sweat from her brow soaked into the pillow she hugged to her, the K-Wolf sucked in a deep breath, swelling herself to combat the shivering. She couldn't carry on not knowing, accepting disembodied voices from her inner-fears, her subconscious, to act as methadone for his addictive, opiate personality. Whether he wanted it, or not. Whether it put her at risk, or not. Whether it was fair on him, or not. She had to find Angelo Deville. II - An Angel Gone To Hell. As far as match preparation went, it pretty much fucking sucked. The 'catch a flight to Barbados and get stuck in a near-monsoon' method stood little chance of catching on. Wisely, there wasn't a cab driver on the roads, which had rapidly become a network of rivers with their own tides and currents. The choice of suede sneakers was looking a poor one, as cream was darkened by the overflowing water, and feet squelched with every step. Accompanying the pummelling onslaught of the tropical downpour was a temperature that hadn't dropped below 30 degrees all day, leaving the K-Wolf steaming under her sodden clothing as the hour-long trek worked her muscles hard. As she reached the seclusion of Merlin Bay, the dense, lush foliage acted as a shelter, only for the occasional weighed-down leave to finally give, sending a heavy drop down 10 feet onto Kari's nose. By the time she reached the Kannon-Hall's home, her sodden clothes were almost dragging along behind her as every fibre had soaked up as much rain water as its structure could house. Each step up towards the door caused a gurgling in her sneakers, while a rapid shake of the head expelled a fine spray from Kari's drenched mane across the Kannon's porch. *Knock, knock!* 'Don't tell me you forgot your keys agai-' the thoroughly domesticated Eleanor Kannon-Hall went to moan at her returning husband, only for surprise to slice off the end of her words. Before Karina could do anything more than scrape the drenched pink fringe from her face, the scowling Eleanor made her feelings clear. 'You're not welcome here.' Just being within a few feet of one another seemed to make the humid air between Karina and Eleanor crackle with electricity... the prelude to an eruption. To Eleanor, the K-Wolf was nothing above a spoilt brat, who'd refused to grow out of her teens, and whose only reaction to not getting what she wanted was to stamp her feet and throw a fit. And she wasn't afraid to tell her so. Having nearly been bankrupted when her husband's wrestling venture - RPW- began to crumble under its own expectant weight, she'd seen the good side of the business at its most pure. Guys with families to feed had agreed to forfeit their wages week after week, old friends from promotions past had agreed to appear for free if it would sell a few more tickets, and even those with the reputation for being the most ruthless and egotistical came up to then Kannons to ask, 'what can we do to help?' And then there was Karina. Run-in after run-in with management over backstage etiquette and booking freedom clouded over the blue-skies that the rest of the roster helped bring about, before she then upped and quit under a rumble of thunder. 'Look,' the K-Wolf tried to inject some quick-fire reason into the conversation with as her right foot shot forward to block the slamming door. 'I know that we do-' Having less than no time for Wolfenden, Eleanor was always going to seethe at the sight of the woman, drenched at her door, unannounced. 'As part of the security procedure, I have to ask you officially, kindly to leave my property,' Eleanor then bluntly stated, words carrying a weight far beyond her petite, 105-pound frame. 'Now, if you refuse to leave, I press the little button here, and watch them drag you off... which I'm not ashamed to say I'd rather enjoy.' The sight of Karina soaked through to the skin brought not even a hint of sympathy to Eleanor's icy surface, but Kari knew that the name on her lips would hook its talons deep into Mrs Kannon-Hall. 'It's about Angelo,' Kari hit her with, removing her foot from the door's path... which Eleanor instead chose not to slam. 'What about him?' the wife of Xavier Kannon tried her best to sound nonchalant in asking. 'Not that I should really care, given all he's pu-' 'But you do care,' Kari snappily replied, having seen the reaction in Eleanor's pale blue, Bambi-like eyes. 'I don't know what all the ties are between you, Christian, and Angelo... and I'm guessing I don't want to, but there's something that ties you three together.' It was a back story that Karina never plucked up the nerve, or fostered the desire, to explore. Some questions are best left unanswered, and then there are some it's best not to even ask. But for things involving Devilles and Kannons, it was for the best if you didn't ask yourself the question: should I ask the question? 'He's been missing for over a month,' Karina went on to divulge, while Eleanor simply crossed her arms, low across her torso. 'Nobody has seen him, heard from him, or has any idea where he is. His homes are empty but not for sale, everything he used to have his fingers in are just going about their business without him... he's... he's just removed himself totally from m-... his life.' The primal, vindictive side of Eleanor couldn't help but force a smile into being at Kari's apparent distress. 'What an effect you have on men, sweetie,' was the catty response that Eleanor couldn't resist clawing Karina with. Soaked through to the skin, worried she was losing her mind, and with nearly a whole day until her return flight, Kari lacked the will to score any hits back. 'Maybe I owed you a free shot,' Kari absorbed it with, refusing to be distracted by Ellie's remarks. 'But no matter what little insults you want to throw my way, there was always something between you and Angelo... I knew it, and Christian isn't stupid enough to have been oblivious to it, either. You might have just been the one person that he'd tell this to... I don't know, maybe ask you to go with him.' Eleanor's arms uncurled from across her bare midriff, hands planting on hips as lips pursed, 'I'm a happily married woman,' Ellie stated in her defence, eyes narrowing at the accusation. 'I have a ring on my finger, and a husband in my bed every night... so you do not want to be following that line, sweetie.' 'So you're telling me, in all honesty, that you haven't heard a word from him,' Kari's potent words interrogated, eyes ploughing the emphasis deep into Eleanor's. 'Not a call, not a letter, not an e-mail?' 'No,' she fired off instantly, choosing that moment to look past Karina and check on the weather. Kari felt the instinct in her reply, and could clearly see that her stare was repelling that of Eleanor's. 'Look me in the eyes and tell me that's true,' the suspicious K-Wolf demanded, sliding her right foot discreetly into the path of the door once more. 'Angelo Deville has not said a word to me since advising me oh-so-politely that he no longer required my services,' Eleanor seemed to resent saying, not too keen on reminding herself of the sense of degradation felt by the public PRIME firing. 'And if you want my advice, sweetie, the world is a better place without him.' Glossed lips grew their smile back, and eyes regained their sparkle as she saw Karina's head drop in defeat. 'Oh, he was all sweetness and light around you, I'm sure,' Eleanor went on to advise, while Karina struggled to find even a word to mount Angelo's hasty defence. 'But there's a reason he always had locked doors and drawers, a reason he had so many people on the payroll to watch his back, and a pretty sodding good reason why he has police files that make War and Peace look like a few bullet points... even if nothing stuck.' In his absence, Karina wondered if her shattering thoughts were filtering out only the good memories to keep, discarding the suspicion, the mistrust, the worry. 'So, keep telling yourself that he was a misunderstood rogue who'd found a human side through you, darling,' the patronising deluge continued. 'But for a man who called himself the Crimson Angel, he was a work of nobody but the Devil. And even Satan himself would have been ashamed to have spawned such an evil piece of... language I'd rather not use.' With Kari having retreated behind her fringe, Eleanor looked down to where the K-Wolf's foot was still blocking the door, having decided the unexpected visitor and her questions had outstayed their welcome. 'Now, kindly leave my property,' the cold cattiness continued with. 'And if you know what's good for you, fly back home, and thank... whoever it is odd people like you pray to, that he's out of your life.' III - The Usual Suspect. Hopping up the stairs towards her apartment, Karina was presented with sudden question. 'Did I forget to lock the door?' Clearing the final flight in two leaps, Kari sprinted down the slippery hall and flung herself through the door, only to be stopped in her tracks by the sight which presented itself. Her apartment looked like a bomb had gone off, and then someone decided to run a herd of cattle through it. Furniture was toppled, smashed to pieces, and thrown across the expanse of her spacious apartment, while anything once adorning a wall had been ripped down, and stamped through. But it was beside the slashed couch, broken coffee table, and ripped-up pile of papers, than she saw a bloody, broken body struggling for breath. 'He told you not to look for him,' was forced from his mouth, gargling with blood, and followed by a gasping wheeze. Bounding over broken and up-turned furniture, it didn't even occur to Karina that the voice was all too familiar, but previously one she ran from, rather than rushed toward. Skidding down onto the rug beside him, it slid her across the floorboards, settling beside the man who left her eyes jammed open. Magnum Wolf. Bruises were already swelling his face, marbling broken skin with infesting purple and tender brown. Both lips were split, but their blood was lost in the mix, collecting with that of broken nose, split eyebrow, and gashed cheek. Out-of-style hair and bushy moustache were already caked in the blood, having hardened and deepened in colour in the time he'd spent down. 'What the hell are you doing here?' Kari exclaimed, letting instinct take over as she grabbed Magnum by the collar of his already torn black shirt. Shaking him, Kari rested a knee on his chest. 'Who sent you? Way are you still in m-' A violent coughing fit left a shaking Kari releasing Wolf, a split-second before he awkwardly rolled onto his side, letting forth a stream of red-streaked vomit, before he almost choked on what he couldn't expel. Still left stunned in the headlights, Karina just knelt beside the man who was at one time her bitterest of foes, watching him heave and cough, every motion causing him agony. 'He told you not to look for him,' Wolf repeated, as if it was his duty that must be fulfilled. 'And you ignored him...' From the shadows, Betsy and Psylocke, Kari's black feline companions, watched on, wide-eyed and still startled, holding the truth of what happened within their un-comprehending memories. 'He? Who? Deville? When?' the K-Wolf reeled off in quick succession, sliding away from Wolf, distraught, looking around the remains of her apartment. 'I haven't even heard from him, so how the fuck am I meant to know what he's said? Magnum?' Planting blood-soaked hands on the corner of what remained of Kari's coffee table, Wolf struggled to get to his feet, shrugging off any timid approach by Karina to help. With face betraying the agony his body was in, and torn mouth scowling, Wolf pushed her away, stumbling across the room until he could anchor his wounded frame to the back of a chair. 'You know when he told you,' Wolf hissed, before his body was ravaged by another dry heave. 'It's just easier to ignore it.' Karina's thoughts hadn't even had a chance to begin catching up, and she remained knelt on the floor, looking around the wreckage. 'Where the hell are you going? You need a doctor, you're...' 'No longer needed,' Magnum finished the sentence truthfully for her as he limped towards the door, grabbing on to anything he could to avoid slipping on the blood that dripped from his broken body. 'This puts me even with him, so... finally... I'm free. Free of him, free of you... free from all this shit.' 'What the fuck are you talking about?' Karina demanded to know, shooting up from the blood-stained rug and gesturing to all around her. 'Who did this... who did this to you? Why?' 'He's gone,' Wolf stated in parting, tucking hands into pockets as he skulked away. 'Take it as the blessing I have.' As he heard the door tap shut behind him, Wolf hobbled down the stairs, waiting to hear of Kari was following. Turning in the stairwell, Wolf took a look back, only to see the door pushed harshly shut, leaving him to drop back against the wall, straightening his hunched over body, 'Heh,' he gargled, before dredging up a mixture of blood and phlegm, which were promptly spat down the gap between staircases. 'Heh... heh...' It was the favour that cleaned his slate. Removing hands from his pockets, Magnum licked torn knuckles, needing the mild anti-bacterial effect of his saliva to cleanse the wounds before he could properly tend to them. After wiping clean the ring that adorned his right index finger, Wolf then tenderly felt around his torn left cheek, almost able to feel the dent it left. 'Heh,' Wolf chuckled, before wincing and holding his side. 'Top that, Durden.'
