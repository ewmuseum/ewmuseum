DUAL HALO 3

Date: THE FUTURE The faint hiss of the releasing gas ushers him back to life, or at least closer to life than he had been for quite a while. The world slowly fades from deep black to navy blue and then settles on a medium gray before all the lines and contours begin to focus. “Ferg? Ferg, can you hear me?” The voice is initially cold and tinny, reverberating in his head as sounds become words. It’s akin to that moment when Charlie Brown hit puberty and all the French-horn adult voices started sounding like actual people. I don’t know if that moment ever happened, but you have to imagine that’s what went down. Color is introduced back into his world as the hissing slowly dies off. He lifts his arm for the first time; it feels like it’s made of steel. Cracking his neck and shaking his joints like one of those stone statues coming to life in some kick-ass mummy/sci-fi/fantasy movie, he looks down at the arm, and finds that it is, in fact, made of steel. Or something close. “Master Ferg?” He looks up at the source of the voice. As the man in the lab coat shifts into focus, it becomes increasingly obvious that he’s looking at the man through a screen. Almost instantly, a blur of sensory monitors, radar blips and other readings and headings are displayed…along with 01/01/2000, 12:01 A.M. in the bottom right corner. Still can’t figure out how to turn that fucking thing off. He smacks himself in the side of the head to shut the date/time widget off, but all he gets is a resounding CLANG sound. More confused than ever, he steps forward out of the chamber and his feet find solid ground in front of the man in the lab coat. Who is like, six inches taller than he is. “Hrm,” the man says. “Hrm?” he asks, the question punctuated by the annoying blip of white noise that comes before and after walkie-talkie transmissions. He shakes his head at the sound, thinking it to be a phase, much like his very crowded Heads-Up-Display. “Well, I just,” the doctor starts, before trailing off. “I thought you were going to be bigger.” “Excuse me?” “You know,” the doc says. “Everyone hears the stories about how the Master Ferg is this big powerful cyborg motherfucker who can save the world. You don’t seem like the type to be saving the world.” “And you don’t seem like the type to casually drop the word ‘motherfucker’ in sentences.” The coat taps his pencil against a clipboard, which he has been holding this whole time so don’t ask me where it came from. “I guess we’re both defying convention, then.” He turns to find a mirror, but the reflection in a nearby window panel does the trick: “What the…hell?” “That’s kind of what I said,” the doctor confirms. “But hell, the name on the tin said ‘Master Ferg’, so I guess you’re him.” Ferg puffs his chest out a little at the realization that he is, in face, a badass cyborg. “Shit yeah, I’m him, son,” he boasts. “Who else could it be?” “Well, like I said, someone taller.” “…” “…” “Who the hell are you?” “It’s mostly irrelevant, because once you leave this room, you’ll never see me again.” “Thank God for that.” “There’s no need to be spiteful, champ. World saviors don’t tend to hold grudges. Now let’s get you hooked up with the A.I.” Master Ferg stares at the doctor for a few seconds, the visor masking his spite. “You calling me dumb?” The coat looks up at him. “No, but it sounds like I missed a great opportunity.” He pushes a couple buttons on a console next to the chamber that Ferg was in. After some whirring and flickering, the glowing blue hologram form of a shapely woman in a skintight dot-matrix-looking jumpsuit appears before them. Master Ferg cocks his head to the side. “…Troy?” The hologram looks at Ferg, then immediately notices her form fitting attire. “Oh, fuck this.” “Thank God you’re here,” Ferg says. “I thought I was going crazy.” “Yeah, you must be crazy if you think I’m going to roll out in this Michelle-Pfeiffer-as-Catwoman number.” Ferg shrugs. “I don’t write the code, ma’am.” He looks up at the doctor, who is trying to check out Troy’s ass. She spins around quickly and wishes she had tangible properties so she could slap the taste out of his mouth. “Sorry,” the doc says. “As the Cortana representative you’re contractually obligated to wear that.” “Why am I fucking Cortana?” The doc shrugs. “I don’t write the roleplay, ma’am.” She spins back to Ferg, whose visor, like a pair of reflective shades, can neither confirm nor deny that he was trying to check her out as well. “Why am I fucking Cortana?” she repeats. “Well, you’re not at the moment,” Ferg says. “But since I’m behind the wheel here, I could arrange something…” “Funny,” she says, her inflection reminding him that it’s actually not funny. “This is because I’m the only workable female character, isn’t it.” “No, it’s because you are be de facto voice of reason, and because we have a completely healthy nonsexual relationship that defies conventional gender roles.” She looks at him, fuming inwardly, but unable to properly reply to what was a pretty good point. “You read that somewhere, didn’t you?” “It scrolled on my thingy,” he says, gesturing toward the visor. “Whatever,” she pouts. “I’m a hologram, do I even have a gender?” “Years of ‘she-male’ jokes and you still leave yourself open for that one?” With that, the Troy hologram vanishes, and Ferg sighs. “Troy?” “I’ll meet you in the flight deck, dickhead,” her voice echoes in his head. Before he can ask how to find the flight deck. a bright blue arrow appears on his already-crowded display. He spins around a little, trying to get his bearings, then starts walking towards it. “That must be awkward,” the doctor says in parting. “It’s no big deal. I say stupid shit all the time, she’ll forgive me with a little begging.” “No, I mean that your AI is taller than you.” “Dude…chill.” Master Ferg steps onto the deck, where a small army of other folks are standing. In the middle of them, the Troy AI – positioned in such a way as to prevent any men in the room from standing behind her – taps a foot against the ground. “Took you long enough.” “I had to keep doubling back on where I had previously come from, because that’s the only way to get anywhere in this damn place.” “Right,” she says. “Well, have a look at what you’re up against.” He peers out past the deck and into space, where amidst the planets, an ominous structure sits. Two giant rings, giant and menacing, drift between the stars. “Oh my God,” Master Ferg says under his breath. “…it’s a Dual Halo.” Troy nods. “Yup. And you’ve got to get in there and stop it from destroying everything.” She nods to a screen and two images come up. One is Chandler Tsonda with a long neck, sitting in a floating chair. The other is a bunch of small insect-like creatures wearing what looks like individual luchador masks with marijuana leaves printed on them. “The Prophet of Tsonda commands the Halo, and he has to be killed. I’d say more, but we have to glaze over this part so you can finish writing this on time.” “Fair enough.” “The prophet has released a swarming, unavoidable menace known as The Face. If it gets ahold of you, it turns you into a fucked-up caricature of yourself thanks to complicated and over-cooked storylines.” “Jesus.” “I know,” Troy says. “This is gonna be tough, so we enlisted some help.” She gestures to the men standing to Master Ferg’s right. One is a gruesome alien with ancient armor and no discernible genitalia. “The Code-arbiter,” Troy continues, “is an alien intended to be your right hand man.” Ferg looks at the alien. “Your characters haven’t been active for weeks.” “But this is a video game parody – and a weak one, I might add – and I’m black,” the Code-arbiter replies. “Huzzah for stereotypes.” Troy gestures to the other two, dressed in similar armor to Master Ferg, but with some color differences. One of them is smoking through the vent on his helmet, but the other is less distinct. “Are you sure you can do that?” Master Ferg asked the smoking one. “Ya sure y’can mind yer own fuckin’ business?” the soldier replies, his drawl punctuated by puffs of smoke. Before Ferg replies, the other man steps in. “DUDE,” he says in a thick New England accent, “WHEN ARE WE GONNA FUCKING FUCK THESE FUCKING MOTHERFUCKERS UP1?!” “Tone,” the Code-arbiter implores, rubbing his temples…if he has temples… “FUCK YOU YOU FUCKING FOUR-MOUTHED HUNCHBACK MOTHERFUCKER, I’M READY TO KILL. I WAS PLAYING EARLIER AGAINST THESE STUPID PUNKASSES AND I FUCKIN’ NO-SCOPED THIS BITCH OUT OF A GHOST AND STOLE IT AND GOT A DOUBLE-KILL SPLATTER.” “Well,” the Code-arbiter says, “glad to hear you put some thought into it.” Master Ferg looks back at Troy. “This is all I got.” “We had parodies worked up for other guys do, but we ran out of time.” “I don’t know if I can do it,” Master Ferg says. He turns toward the Dual Halo again, watching it float in silence. “What, why?” Troy asks. “There is one who can beat me,” Ferg says, “one whose powers exceed my own, one who-“ “Tchu’s not in it this year.” “Thank fucking God,” he says, slamming a fist down on a desk hard enough to make some people jump. He puts a hand on his head and breathes a sigh of relief. “Guy beat you, like, twice, didn’he?” asks the smoking soldier. “I don’t want to talk about it.” “You can do this, Danny,” Troy says. “You can finish this fight.” “You’re right,” Master Ferg says, his revelation expedited in the interest of time. There was one, and it kicked a lot of ass, but it got destroyed. And then there was a second one, but it got kinda jumbled because the point-of-view kept changing, and so it was still kinda cool, but not as cool as the first one. This one, though…this one is the one I can finally claim as my own.” He looks out at the Dual Halo, triumphant, ready for the battle ahead. “By the way, you know there were Dual Halos before you came around, right?” “Yeah, but no one cares about those.” “You’re a douche.”
