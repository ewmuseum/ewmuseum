Faith

19 September 2005

Date: September 19th, 2005 “Reason is our soul's left hand, Faith her right.” - John Donne *** September 13th, 2005 Often it’s those with faith in their hearts that face the most scrutiny in their day-to-day lives. It’s ironic really. Those closest to God- to the creator- are the one that receive the most heat. Whether it stems from others’ ignorance or not is something that isn’t up for debate. It seems odd that the entity that they have entrusted their souls to wouldn’t step in however and cease the harassment and the – for the lack of a better word – bullying. But that doesn’t seem to be what faith is about. Those who have faith are not immune to troubles and hardships. They’re just as vulnerable as one who sees God as the enemy. That is why faith is such a miraculous thing. It shows just how strong human courage and resolve truly are. It’s astonishing to think that an entity that does not show its face can have such an overwhelming influence on the lives of its followers. However, as strong as this bond may appear between human resolve and divinity, it seems that the threads can be cut with as much as a large wind gust and a few days of precipitation. He kicked aside another tattered chunk of wood that once held together the master bedroom of his home. Hurricane Katrina hadn’t been kind to Alex Ross. The wind and the rain barreled in like a bat out of hell, and- as they say- the rest was history. A twenty-five foot plus tree had crashed through their living room, leaving little left intact. The water damage from the rain and the proceeding flooding left little to the imagination as to what kind of impact a storm of that magnitude could have. The entire Gulf Coast found itself devastated. Billions of dollars worth of damage… nothing compared to the loss of life, of course. Building destroyed, economies decimated, family lives torn to pieces like paper through a shredder. Alex was speechless- what could he say? He was standing at the foot of his once bed, a six-foot tree limb lay across it. The mattress looked like a waterlogged sponge. The headboard of the bed snapped in two and splintering. The water dripping down the walls that had somehow managed to survive the storm eerily resembled teardrops. The roof of the home had almost been blown entirely off. They had found their front door at the end of their driveway, along with a few window shutters and cat that had unfortunately not survived the storm. The door had not killed it, but it seemed to be sheltering the carcass, which reeked of death thanks to the warm late-summer sun. How could God create something this awesome? It was proof that no matter how powerful man gets he is still no match for Mother Nature. Perhaps it was God’s way of keeping man in check. A warning, suggesting that regardless of how far we advance we will always be many steps behind in the big picture. It’s a depressing theory. One that could potentially hold true, but who am I to speculate? Alex took a last look at his once bedroom and exited back toward the hallway. Rubbing his hand along the doorframe which was somehow still standing he sighed. As he walked down the hallway toward the kitchen, he looked to his right. The badly damaged, once off white, structure was now littered with mold and holes. He ran his hands through his hair- the stress had taken its toll on his body. He looked through broken glass and the two water-stained pictures that still hung in place, a little skewed albeit. One, a picture of him and his wife Lacey, the other, a shot of his baby boy Connor. He took both pictures down and carefully pushed the glass out of the frames. He tucked them under his arm and continued toward the kitchen where his wife stood, holding their son. He had lost so much in the storm but at least in the end he had them… his family. *** He laid his head atop the cold hotel pillow. This was to be his bedroom from now until their house could be rebuilt. As he shut his eyes briefly, he felt a wash of anger come over him. Not because he was to be living in a hotel for the next few weeks… not because he had lost nearly all of his possessions in the hurricane… and not because his life was in ruins. Rather, because he had let it happen to him. The situation itself didn’t enrage Alex, it was the fact that the more who swore to watch over him had created something so incredible that it had almost destroyed him. Alex sneered. Why? He thought to himself. He wanted to scream it out but his wife Lacey was lying next to him- fast asleep. I put my life… my soul in your hands. And this is what I receive… Alex could feel his eyes well up with moisture- tears… of sadness- of anger. You swore to protect me. You said if I gave you my faith that you would watch over me and through the thick and the thin see to it that I’m lead to salvation. He opened his eyes and sighed. This is far from salvation. My family… our lives. It’s gone. Everything. Our home is destroyed; our lives are ruined. Maybe I don’t see the big picture, but I had a different idea in regards to salvation. Alex turned his head over on the pillow looking over to see if Lacey was actually asleep. He hesitated for a few seconds before returning to his former position- he sighed. Bringing his arms out from under the blankets he flopped his wrist down beside his head. He noticed the white bracelet he’d been sporting for the lesser part of a year. Engraved in it was the word ‘FAITH’. Up until I graduated from Texas I didn’t actually have faith, you know? I’m sure you do. You created that big mother- that big storm so I’m sure you can hear me now. It wasn’t until recently that I actually started believing what they were telling me. They being my parents and everybody at my church. Up until I was about 22 I was just paying lip service. Shame bled through Alex’s thoughts. And then I began to understand it. He paused, taking a deep breath. They weren’t pressing religion on me because following you was the best way to go. They were pressing it on me because it truly is the only way to go. Or was anyway. I’m not too sure anymore. I’m conflicted now. I’d ask you to show yourself to me, or give me some sort of a sign, but I’m not going to play dumb. Our faith in you is far beyond blind. Again he paused. I don’t understand… a good soldier will never leave another man behind, yet our Lord decided to not only leave his children for dead but created the reason they perished. Your followers- your soldiers… those with faith, are out there in that mess tonight. Countless are dead, countless are wounded. Their lives ruined, and for what? To show you are almighty? To brandish your sword of influence? You’re killing your children… people who have entrusted their souls to you on a whim. Good fathers don’t send their children off to die. They protect them, they watch over them… so I truly do not know what you are anymore. *** The only sound in the air was the breathing of both Alex and his wife Lacey. They lay side by side on the Best Western bed. The moonlight streaming in through the window revealed a crib on Lacey’s side of the bed. Their son, Connor, was fast asleep. The situation was not ideal for either of the two parents, but it was the best they could manage at this point. The hotel was chock-full of guests. The majority of which were victims of Hurricane Katrina… their homes decimated- uninhabitable. They are shared that common bond, no matter which income bracket, race, or religion they were from that’s what they all shared. That vulnerability… that weakness. The clock beside the bed read 3:43 am. Alex had dozed many hours prior… his arm hanging off of the side of the bed, his FAITH bracelet, taken off of his wrist, placed on the dresser beside him. It seemed as though the faith that the American Poster Child once had was disappearing rapidly, however, unlike Alex’s theory, he wasn’t one to leave one of his children behind. *** “We all dream; we do not understand our dreams, yet we act as if nothing strange goes on in our sleep minds, strange at least by comparison with the logical, purposeful doings of our minds when we are awake.” - Erich Fromm There are some things Science just cannot explain. Some things that we cannot comprehend as rational human beings. But maybe that’s how it was intended. It’s sort of boring- yet fulfilling- having a rational explanation for everything, isn’t it? Wouldn’t it be neat if everything happened because it happened? And it was beyond us? That’s one of the problems with human development and progression. We can now explain why the majority of things happen the way they do. The one freedom an incarcerated man has is the ability to dream. This may be able to be explained by Science but dreaming is one of the most beautiful and complex aspects of man. Men have predicted the future through dreams… they have relived the past. Amazing things happen in dreams… some of which cannot be explained. But that is one of the miraculous and enthralling things about mankind: some things do just happen because they happen, and some things Science just can not touch. The room was white. In it sat a chair and two plasma television screens suspended as if from nowhere. On the white leather chair sat Alex Ross. Elbows rested on his knees, he was wearing the same clothing that he had gone to sleep in the previous night. He wasn’t quite sure what was happening, he stared intently at the plasma screens. Nothing was displayed on them but they appeared to be buzzing ever so slightly. Suddenly the screens flicked on. On the left, a scenic view of the sky was portrayed. White clouds danced about as the sunlight beamed down through the sky. On the right was a depiction of what was clearly Hell. Smoldering ashes being strewn about by a light breeze with fire cackling beneath the surface. The sound of the flames filled the room as Alex diverted his attention from one to the other, and then back again. “Do you know what this is, Alex?” A voice said from behind him. The American Poster Child didn’t have to turn; it was almost as if he was half-expecting the voice to accompany the pictures on the screens. He sighed. “This is a dream.” The voice chuckled a little… Alex was correct, after all. “This isn’t real… this is just something my subconscious has conjured up in an attempt to- to… I don’t know what it’s trying to do, exactly. But I know this isn’t anything special. It’s not divine intervention to my sudden lack of faith.” Again the voice chuckled a little. “Pretty set in your newfound ways, aren’t you?” The voice asked. “Actually, they’re my old ‘ways’… my newfound way was that thing you call faith.” His demeanor had changed considerably as opposed to the usual Alex Ross. His anger was getting the best of him; no longer was he polite and respectful. “You’ve always been a little blind, Alex.” The American Poster Child awaited the voice’s reasoning behind that last remark. “I’ve watched you go to church for the past twenty-five years now, and you’re correct, it wasn’t up until recently that you began to get it slightly.” “I understand it completely.” Alex responded. Yet again the voice chuckled. “Alex, you’re still very blind to the whole topic. You’re a very innocent person, my friend… it’s one of the reasons why you are who you are. It’s why you’re so ethical. Another reason you’re so ethical is because deep down I know you truly want to be believe, and you do. You just don’t fully comprehend what it is you’re trying to believe in. But that’s alright… most don’t.” Alex didn’t say a word, he took a deep breath, realizing that there could potentially be more to this than something his subconscious conjured up. “I’ve come to you tonight not to bless your ears of a speech about divinity and following the right path. Alex, at some point in your life, whether it be tonight or fifty years from now, you will realize that having faith isn’t necessarily about me protecting you from mortal existences such as hurricanes or other men. It’s not about this life at all. It’s about what you’ll experience after you step out of this shell and step into one of those two windows,” he said, referencing to the two screens. “I can hope that you’ll make the decision to go toward the left, but if you choose the right I will support you. It’s what I do.” “I’m going to leave you now, and you’re going to wake up in a few hours and whether you remember this or not is fully your choice… it’s up to you which window you enter.” Alex continued to sit on the chair, still as could be. The screens froze, one showing a picture of the sky, one a picture of the ashes. The white room sat, suspended in time, as Alex continued to sleep within the confines of the hotel bedroom, his FAITH bracelet still atop the table beside the bed. Outside the window, the sun had began to rise, unveiling a very clear sky… not a cloud in sight. *** September 8th, 2005 It’s quite primitive, really. Throw the ball; catch the ball; run the ball. To simplify it even further: score more points than your opponent and you win. They say the game of football is a game of wits, played one down at a time; played one pass at a time; played one inch at a time… sort of like life. A clash of brawn, grit, and intelligence. Much like wrestling. With one exception. In football you have teammates. Teammates to shoulder the blame alongside you. Teammates to relish in the glory alongside you. Like the game itself it all boils down to a single thing. In this case it’s accountability. Mistakes are shared by the entire team, as are successes. You may kick the game-winning field goal, but it is the team that is victorious- not you. And then there’s wrestling. Mistakes are not shared by a team, and the blame falls squarely on your shoulders when you stumble. To the same extent however, victories are that much sweeter because they are accomplished solely by you. You are accountable for one person in this world: yourself. And trust me, there is nothing worse than letting down the one you are accountable for. Wrestling, like football, is a game played one inch at a time, one move at a time. A game of psychology, strength, and determination. A game in which success is sweet… and failure is bitter. A game in which you find yourself accountable for the one who it hurts the most to disappoint. I understand you don’t want to let that one person down, Danny… but the fact of the matter is this: neither do I. Failure is quite the bitter pill… one which I intend not to swallow.
