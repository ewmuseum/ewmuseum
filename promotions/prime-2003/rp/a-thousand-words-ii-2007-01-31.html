A Thousand Words: II

31 January 2007

Date: 1/31/07 CLICK I told my therapist about the dream I had the other night. The one with the talking picture and the warnings and the blah, blah, blah. Dear God, if anything, that shit was surreal. The next day as I was heading toward the arena for the Georgia Dome show, it was all I could think about. I’ve never been one to obsess about dreams – at least the ones you have in your sleep. But this one was like one of those cries for help; some internal struggle thing, I guess. I dwelled on it all day. I spoke to Chet Worth about it before working the show, he passed it off as stress and told me I could get away from the shows if I needed to. Naturally, I wasn’t going to sit out on an opportunity to travel the world during these shows, so I told him it was nothing and went about my day. Oddly, I didn’t think much of it anymore after the show. It was great performing one last time before embarking on this new journey. I’ve been to Japan and Puerto Rico several times in the past for several different shows, but getting to be a banner figure for a full-fledged World Tour has been something I’ve sought to do for a long time and now I’m doing it. You know, back to the dream for a second. I want to think it’s a sign or something. Something’s wrong and I need to fix it, though? Bullshit. I’ve done nothing wrong. Even in my dreams, Eme’s pointing the finger at me for her crap that she just needs to get over. I’m living my life the way I want to and she just can’t accept it. I’m gonna be the best and she’s not gonna talk me out of it no matter how she pleads. Dreams… that’s all they are. Chemically induced hallucinations presented by your subconscious to occupy the mind while the body is dormant. The end. Besides, they can only let you escape for so long. Because when you open those peepers in the morning, reality hits you. Boring, crappy-ass, bringer-downer, want-out-of-it-but-you-just-can’t-find-a-way-out reality. And the reality of the situation? I feel like I’ve had something taken away from me. Like, I was robbed. Something of mine – personal freedom in this case – had been taken away from me. I know when and how this happened, but the why part is still so foreign to me. It’s really shitty of me to say this, but everyday, there’s this dark part of me that wishes I never slept with Eme in Chicago that weekend. None of this shit would’ve happened if I’d stuck to my abstinence pledge while I was on the road. I used to be able to travel from show to show without fear. Without all the extra frequent flyer miles. Nobody to leave behind to focus on my career and that worked for me so well. I love my family and it breaks my heart to say this, but being with Eme doesn’t feel like I want to do it; it feels like a chore that you just have to put off until you muster the energy to go through the appropriate motions. Am I in a place in my career where I’m happy? Oh, you bet your ass I am. I have GOLD. ACTUAL gold around my waist that I won without any screwjobs, hidden tricks, interference, or anything. I was poised to go over and I did it. Personal life? Maybe not so much. But I’m not about to stop now. These problems can happen any fucking time and just as easily, they can be fixed any fucking time. But for right now, my reality is that I’m going to keep doing what I’m doing and there isn’t a single force on this Earth that’s going to stop me from doing as such. …………………………………….- -……………………………………. Rio De Janeiro, Brazil January 16th, 2007 Rio De Janeiro was a bustling source of life in the Winter heat. (That whole hemisphere thing, people.) On one of the many, many street corners, people were going about their day. It wasn’t like your crazy-as-shit towns like New York, Seattle, Chicago, blah blah blah where everybody and their mother were a bunch of rude assholes caring only for themselves in their ventures to jump from place to place. Here, the culture was a bit more refined. People were a bit nicer and more serene as they went about their business. The tourists were appearing by the dozens and in some placed, looked even to outnumber the locals. On one such corner, Sonny Silver himself was taking in the sights. He’d just gotten back from witnessing the splendor that was Cristo Redentor (Christ The Redeemer), a massive figuring depicting none other than the Lord’s child Himself. Silver wasn’t a religious man since his early childhood and didn’t really find a place for it in his life now, but it was still quite a sight to behold. "…Fucking cab…" After a low mutter, he reached into the pocket of his jeans and obtained a Butterfinger bar. Tearing it open with sharp teeth, he started to snack on the tasty distraction from the fact that the cab was running a bit late. He had to get to the fucking show in an hour and it wasn’t going to happen this quickly. Finally, he spotted the cab rounding the corner. He waved his hand into the path of the speeding vehicle, making the driver force himself to slow down. Taking a rolling luggage bag of his belongings behind him, he puts the stuff in the trunk, keeps his backpack close to him, then hops into the back as he readied himself for his first big show overseas. "Maracana Stadium, por fa vor," he told the cabbie as he shut the door behind him. Soon, he would be off for his next show. In the insanity that was traffic, he figured it was going to be a while before he got to the arena, so a small nap was in order. Truth be told, he hadn’t slept so well since that night in Georgia with the screwed-up dream. A quick power nap would recharge his energy (hopefully, anyhow) and soon, he’d be at the arena ready to open up a can of whoop-ass against whoever the fuck they put against him. Sonny tilted his Seahawks ball cap over his eyes to shade himself from the annoyance of natural light, then let himself drift off on the agonizingly slow ride to his destination. CLINK. CLINK. CLINK. CLINK. Oh, FUCK. Not again. Not here. Sonny jerked back to life and gazed around the cab. He couldn’t be hallucinating. That was a stupid dream. "The fuck?" "My apologies, good sir," The cabbie spoke up as he pointed to the hanging picture on the rear-view mirror of the vehicle. A young girl – no older than ten, perhaps – was seen in a field next to what appeared to be her mother. "Mi familia. This cab is, how you Americans say, piece of crap. The picture strikes against windshield". Sonny looked at the picture and nodded. "Gotcha. Mis apologias." And that was that as he turned his head to the side, tilted his cap back down and sought to find the slumber he so desperately needed. Moments passed before something awoke him from his attempt at effectively napping. "Sonny?" He jolted again, looking around for the sound of the voice. "Sonny? You’re Sonny Silver, si?" The cabbie again. Sonny let out a sigh of relief that he wasn’t flipping out again and nodded affirmatively. "I am. Wrestling fan, I take it?" The cabbie laughed and pointed in the rear-view mirror again, acknowledging his passenger. "Si, si. My family enjoys American wrestling. Real cool sport." "Yeah, it is," he replied with a hint of self-importance in his voice. PRIME had been real good to him so far and he was proud of the work he’d put into it. Nothing more was said between the two as the cabbie continued to focus on the road while Sonny continued to drift off into the Land of Nod. No more being paranoid. He wasn’t going to let his home life fuck with his progress in his working life. They were two separate entities and they would STAY that way. "FUCKING RIGHT IT’S AWESOME!" Once again, Sonny came to from his nap to look up in the seat ahead. "Excuse me?" Sonny asked the driver. "I say nothing, sir," he told him as he turned the corner. He shook off whatever he head and tried to resume his nap. At this point they were a quarter of the way to the Maracana Stadium and they were going to tear that shit down. Sonny smiled at the thought of performing overseas for the first time since the Fans Wrestling Organization and let those notions carry him off. "Don’t listen to that bitch, Sonny." Sonny snapped up and looked around the cab. Shit. It was happening. AGAIN. More voices. He was losing it. He just had to be. The first thing he did was flip through his wallet and to gaze on his pictures. Photos from vacations and family get-togethers past were looked at, but none of them seemed to speak to him. Hearing far-off voices was a sure sign that something funny was wrong with the brain, but he tried to rationalize whatever had been going on due to a lack of sleep. "Come on," he whispered so as not to get the attention of the driver. "What are you doing here?" "Open up your backpack, Sonshine! I promise I won’t bite!" His eyes narrowed and his lips pursed as his mind wandered. There were no pictures in his bag. Just some clothes, a yet-to-be-used disposable camera, some other basic knick-knacks, The Intense Ti… Shit. Was it THAT? The voice, which was not unlike his own, continued to taunt him with cries of "open the bag." Silver shook his head and rapidly downed the bottle of Aquafina he’d stored in the much smaller pocket on the side. This was getting to be too much. With apprehension setting in, Sonny unzipped the little black backpack and came face-to-face with the prize he’d acquired in the midst of battling the vile Sun Tzu. The Intense Title. This was a true victory to him and a personal one at that. It was a symbol that Sonny was doing something right for the first time in his long, yet entirely vacant career. Silver glanced over the light bouncing off the metal nameplate that displayed it for all to see. "I know! That’s my shine!" "AHHHHHHHHH!" Silver jumped in his seat, catching the attention of the driver who glanced in the rear-view mirror. "Sir, everything okay?" He let the belt fall into his lap and took in a few deep breaths. Panic was setting in and who knows what else would set in… some foreign disease he couldn’t pronounce, maybe? All Sonny did was take out a handkerchief from his bag (yes, people still had those) and wiped the sweat slowly oozing from his forehead. "Yeah, I’m fine." That was all he got out before he gazed at the shine of the title once more. He gazed up once to make sure that the driver was focusing on what he was doing. If this… THING… was communicating with him, the last thing he needed to do was look crazy. In a hushed whisper, he spoke to the implausible talking title. "What are you?" "Well, Son, when I got wind of that stupid talking picture you had in your wallet, I felt I needed to say something. Think of me as a representation of what you want. You’re a star, kid. Nothing bad’s gonna happen to you. You’ve got it all, man. You got money, you got talent, you got fame. Everything that you’ve sought to gain in your career, you’ve got it now. You finally have something to call your own. A shining beacon of the success you hold and it’s here, telling you everything’s gonna be okay. Plus, you’re rich. You could, like, buy bodyguards and shit so nothing bad does happen to you." Every word the title spoke was hitting the right notes to Sonny’s ears. Although, of course, he had to get a little desensitized to a talking piece of tin, but once that feeling settled, he let himself relax and put the entire scenario out of his head. "I seriously doubt talking to inanimate objects makes me ‘okay’ by your definition… but… you’re right. Eme should just be happy for me. I get it now. Everything’s going to be good." As the trip to the arena was at its end, the driver couldn’t help but catch a glimpse of the slightly disorientated man in the backseat whispering to himself. It distracted him for all of a moment, but shrugged it off. Some sort of pre-show ritual or something, perhaps. He wasn’t pulling out a sniper rifle or any of that so his day was all gravy. He got to meet a star and that was good enough for him. All the while, Silver spoke to himself in a quiet tone that permeated his mind with serenity. Everything’s gonna be good… Everything’s gonna be good… Everything’s gonna be good… …………………………………….- -……………………………………. Honolulu, Hawaii – The Silver/Ke’ala Household Present Day Another in a seemingly endless line of near-sleepless nights had concluded at the sound of the mechanical alarm whirring to life on the nightstand. As a hand reached out to find the source of the racket and thoroughly punish it for awakening the body from their slumber, it finally stopped when a small picture came tumbling off the nightstand, striking the floor with a dull thud. Once the alarm finally stopped, Eme Ke’ala used the tried and true pick-and-flick motion to dispose of some unwanted eye crust. Making her way down to the floor, Eme grabbed the portrait – that of her husband – let a little huff of air escape, then placed it back on the stand. She used a few heavy sighs to remind herself that she was awake and would have to leave for work in an hour. Once again, she would be forced to face yet another day running on only about three to four hours of sleep and the thought perturbed her. The dreams were getting a lot more surreal over time to the point where she thought a Stephen King novel was going on in her head. The first thing she did to face the brand new day was her morning ritual of checking her phone for messages. To keep from waking the baby, that nifty little ringer got turned off at night and she would return the messages the next day. A nice little "good night" from her husband across the world would get her to trudge through another day until he finally returned home. "NO NEW MESSAGES." "Crap…" How long had it been? Three, maybe four days now? He’d promised to call and she hadn’t heard from Sonny in days. Sure, she could sit silently and stew over her husband’s absent-mindedness or whatever the hell was going on in his head, but for right now the only thing she could do was go through the motions. Stewing wasn’t going to go to work for her, call the babysitter, or pay the goddamn bills. That WOULD be nice though. She threw off the bedsheets and rolled out of bed, clad only in a black tank top and jean shorts, and headed towards the bathroom to slap some cold H20 on her face to get her awake and ready to go. The droplets sent a slight shiver traveling through her body, but the little jolts peppering her felt just right. Eme would be ready to venture out into another day at the office and prepare the last of the presentation work for that big Preston case she and her team had been working on. If everything went through, the chance of that big promotion she’d been gunning for in the last year would finally pay off. Eme went over to the armoire and searched through her belongings until she found that lovely red blouse and black skirt she’d been dying to try on since she purchased them at Macy’s the day prior. Feeling a little giddy at the thought of looking better than that bitch, Janice in Accounting, she laid them out on the bed and headed in for her shower while Gaby remained asleep in the crib. "Eme, you’ve gotta help me. I can’t do it alone." Were it possible for a young Hawaiian girl, her face felt like it was turning ghostly white. It couldn’t be happening again. She was awake. Why did he come back now? Facing her troubles for what had to be the sixth or seventh time in the last couple of weeks, Eme peered out of the bathroom to glance around the room until her gaze became fixated on the voice’s source. The same place she’d been hearing it from all this time. "Eme, I’m only going to get hurt if you don’t help me." She frantically ran her hands through her hair and let out a soft sob. This just wasn’t happening. Not again. Why was this voice calling out to her? She knew that she and Sonny were having their troubles, but they’d always fought threw them all and grew stronger. What was it begging for? Help him out how? All the questions raced through her head, but only one would escape from her mouth. "From what?" "From myself." Uneasily, she made her way back onto the bed, setting aside her clothes so she could have a seat. What the hell was going on in that head of hers? She should be halfway through a warm shower prior to getting ready for the day ahead and instead, she found herself conversing with what appeared to be a talking portrait of her husband, the professional wrestler. Looking around the room, she struggled to come to terms with what was going on. She’d tried to get through to her husband. Give up this business and be a family. But no matter how much she cried or how much she pleaded for him to stay, his resolve remained impregnable. "I don’t know how I can help," she pleaded. "He won’t listen to anything I say, it’s… it’s like his family is just a distraction to him. "Trust me when I say this, Eme. It’s killing me inside. I want to tell you what’s going to happen to me, but I can’t. You’ve got to figure out a way to bring me back home before I do something I will ultimately regret." Eme wiped an errant tear off her face, alll the while trying to wrap her brain around this. "You won’t even help yourself, Sonny. HOW?! How can I help you when you’re the most stubborn jackass on the planet?!" How pathetic was this? Venting to no one in particular. No answer she wanted; no answer of any kind was coming from that portrait. Like all the other dreams before it, there was no one left in the room with the exception of Eme and the sounds of her crying daughter. Soon, she’d jolt from her nightmare. Then… Another day begins. …………………………………….- -……………………………………. Royal Ramblas Hotel – Barcelona, Spain The Day Before The Show Well, the company has a whole lot of fucking faith in you, don’t they? The first official title defense was against two men – both undefeated thus far in their PRIME tenures – who were both like yourself. Two very different paths, but three rising stars in the wrestling world. No idea how the match was even going to go until you got there, but going out and giving it your all was just about all you could do. "165." CLAP. "166." CLAP. "167." CLAP. "168." CLAP. In the serenity of his own hotel room just one day before the next big show on PRIME’s World Tour, Sonny’s head was finally in the mindset he was looking for: not dwelling on some shit he can’t fix, but deailng with the stuff that he CAN do something about. He was getting himself mentally ready for what had to be his biggest challenge yet. He’d only heard in some circles about this Sloan character. Nothing good, but hey. It brought in the ratings. Sonny was down with that. And the other cat, Garbage Bag Johnny. That was… something. But he was highly successful in AWC. For every one punch that Chainz’s big ass was going to throw, he would need to hit him with about five or six. He wasn’t Karina. He had a bigger frame than her and had more experience with hitting things to jelly. He had to take him down. As for the hobo… maybe toss a quarter his way? "169." CLAP. "170." CLAP. "171." CLAP. "172." CLAP. To warm himself up, three hundred push-ups in between claps at the apex of the push always geared him up. The sweat trickled off his forehead and his wifebeater became drenched in the persperation he released. In order to win this, he’d have to be ready for anything. "You know, they’re going to take you away from me." It’d been a couple weeks, but that Intense Title was still conversing with him and that was a fact that perturbed Sonny at first. But as time went on… strange as it sounded… the belt was right. He would lose it if he didn’t gear himself properly. He no longer thought of it as something that was making him go insane. No. His mental faculties were perfect. "Keep going, Son. If you don’t, you’re gonna choke. Do you really want to be known as a choker? Those kind of people live to be ridiculed, Son." …Aside from that. He gazed at the belt once, but kept pushing through his imaginary voice’s constant barrage of antagonism. Sonny knew what needed to be done. "197." CLAP. "198." CLAP. "199." CLAP. "200!" CLAP. This title wasn’t leaving his side. Not yet. It would leave under his terms and his terms only. As long as his mind was sharp and his body fit to go, he would perservere in the face of two of AWC’s golden children. "You know," he grunted in between reps. "It’s incredibly rude to speak when I’m busy. You nearly made me lose count." "Sorry. But it’s true. You know it, I know it, the American people know it, and the people on this World Tour you’re so fond of know it." "Perhaps… is there a reason, however, why you only quote silly things like this?" "Don’t ask me. I’m just the part of your subconscious that wants what’s best for you and your career." In his head, Sonny continued to count the number of successful push-ups he’d completed and was down the home stretch of 2 and a quarter out of 300 at this juncture. As the strenuous exercise proceed, the plans began to forumlate. There were gonna be two men attacking from either side. One was more unpredictable than the other and the other was more violent than he. Which, by proxy, also made him unpredictable. Crap. This wasn’t getting him anywhere. No matter what his gameplan was, the incentive remained the same. Now the target was on his back and it wasn’t going anywhere. Only thing to do for now? Keep counting. "Sonny…" He glanced up, but the voice that presented itself was not of the one telling him to succeed. This one, he’d also heard before. Sonny stopped his push-ups and snapped up to his feet, using a towel to dry himself of his odious sweat. Sighing heavily to himself, he reached into his wallet and from there, a picture of his wife and daughter was procured. A curious glance made its way over to the title belt, but it was no longer telling him anything. The picture, however… "Sonny… It’s not too late to end this. Finish up this last show and be with me. Be with us." Sonny draped the towel over his shoulders to remove some leftover sweat from his forehead, then rubbed a hand through his beard. "You know I can’t do that, Eme. If I stop now, everything that I’ve worked for goes in the toilet. And if that happens, then how are we going to live? I can’t go back to being some faceless nobody! Can’t you understand where I’m coming from? All my hard work is paying off for both me and you AND Gabrielle!" "At the cost of your own soul…?" For the first time since this entire debate between normalcy and surrealism, Sonny found himself laughing at the claim of the voice. "Soul? Soul?" He stood up and screamed at the photograph. "YOU KNOW THAT’S A LOAD OF CRAP! You don’t even believe in things like that! You’re really just scraping the bottom of the barrel on this one, eh? Little Eme doesn’t get exactly what she wants for once in her life. You don’t see with the eyes that I do, you nagging shrew. I’m seeing long-term. I become famous, you get all the money and Eme gets into the best schools because of the funds I get from this job. It works out for everybody, so why can’t you drill that into your skull? "No it doesn’t. We still don’t have a husband OR a father in this house! Save yourself or you’re going to get hurt." "What aren’t you telling me? Get hurt HOW?!" But once again, no pertinent answer, let alone an answer of any kind, would be received on this day. He simply let himself fall backwards onto the bed, letting his body rest after another in a long line of distressful episodes. Reality was getting to him. Real bad. But it's not like there was really anything that he could do, it seemed. He cast his gaze between title and portrait, then nodded to himself. He sat up, taking both in hand and tossed them back into his bag, zipped the thing up, heaved it into the closet and slammed the door shut with a loud "BANG!" No longer did he want to hear from anybody. In the back of his mind, it piqued his curiosity a little bit. What the force had been alluding to. But how was he hurting himself? Sonny was doing everything he needed to be doing and that was enough for him. He glanced up from his daze and shrugged to no one in particular. The next course of action was simple enough. "One." CLAP. "Two." CLAP. He did the always useful Silver Method of dealing with things: Sonny buried himself in his work and tuned out everything else around him.
