Some Good Ol' Fashion Procrastinated Bullshit

undated

Date: 1-8-06 Just in Time!!! I woke up in the middle of a cold sweat the other day. I was in the middle of a vivid dream that my mental claws were desperately latching on to remember. Usually, upon waking from dreams, their loosely contingent plot lines shattered into puzzle piece details without compatible, fitting edges. This particular dream had something or other to do with the rotation of the earth and its orbit around the sun. I remember thinking, “If only there was some way to measure and segment the amount of time that passes in the duration of one orbit!” I was crushed when I remembered that there was already a device that split time into segments. The last thing I needed was a lawsuit slapped on my ass for copyright infringement, so I sat in my favorite chair- a gray padded number that had a few rips and tears in the aged upholstery, but you’d never know just from the way it swiveled. I called it my festering chair because I’d sit in it whenever I became enraged so that I could let the anger boil and ferment inside of me. Once the anger had sufficient time to steam, I had processed a bouillon cube of thoughts into a simmering broth. Spite washed over me as I imagined that miserable bastard- that miserable bastard who invented the calendar before I could think of it. Who the hell was he to decide when the year should start? According to his arbitrary time frame, the New Year had just begun. Everyone around me was awash in self-absorbed babble about changing their lives. People were still bobbling slippery, hot potato resolutions about eating healthy or losing wait or quitting smoking; all bullshit endeavors soon to be forgotten. I usually strayed away from resolutions. I hated vegetables. I never trusted food that grew out of a patch of dirt. I preferred processed food- food made by robotic machines. Robots never make mistakes. They know what’s good for you. And even if I wanted to get rid of one of my many vices, willpower was never one of the suits I wore. This year would be different, though. I resolved to find that son of a bitch who invented the calendar and kill him unless he handed over the patent rights. Then, I could change the beginning of the year to a less arbitrary date. I’d even make the years shorter; thus prolonging people’s lives. When people start living for one hundred fifty to two hundred years minimum, humanity will have me to thank. I’ll win Nobel Peace Prizes, drive around in one of them topless cars, and get married to a beach full of naked Swedes. In fact, it would be a disservice to mankind if I didn’t kill this calendar making fascist. It’d be un-American, god damn it! First, though, I had to discover the inventor of the calendar. The first resource I turned to was the resource I always turned to first- a resource that goes overlooked more often than not in this information age- my own mind. For one, it’s always sitting right there inside of my skull. And even if it’s not always the most accurate source of information, it’s free from corporate bias. The encyclopedia business is more black market corrupt than you’d think. Besides, you can’t spell encyclopedia without cyclop, the singular form of Cyclops. And everyone knows that nothing good ever came from a giant monster with one eye. Anyway, I started to add everything up in my thinking chair; a different chair completely than my festering chair. This chair had no back, and it was more of a mineral stool than a chair by appearance alone. I sat on it naked with my one leg resting across the other’s thing and my balled fist parked beneath my beard tufted chin. This, as I had read, was the ideal position for thinking. I figured that an endeavor such as segmenting the earth’s orbit into individual rotations required the precise calculating skills of some kind of mathematician, but I couldn’t just go hunting down every lanky pimple colony in glasses and hiked up trousers. It would be inefficient despite how enjoyable it might be. As much as I fancied the notion of punching out anyone smarter and weaker than myself, I just didn’t have the time for that. “I’ll put that on my to-do list,” I noted to myself in a dull mumble from over my chin fisted, sleeping jaw. It occurred to me that this half-baked inventor must be quite narcissistic to decide when the year should stop and start regardless of how detrimental it may be to the rest of his species. He was a lax murderer, that cocky shit, and I knew his weakness had to have been his ego. Calendar is such a random, rootless word. It couldn’t have come from the Romans. They were too organized. It had to have been a last name from Germanic origins. They’ve been trying to ruin shit for everyone since the Visigoths. Don’t even get me started on those god damned Visigoths. So it had been decided, then, that the product was named after its creator. Calendar was no longer just an object forced upon mankind to measure time in grueling, life-defying increments. Rather, it was a name that didn’t appear in the local directories. Without the drive to scour the nation before I absolutely had to, I decided to take a walk to visit the local authorities. Normally, I stayed away from the authorities on account of that I was typically drunk, and the cops don’t take you seriously if you’re drunk. But this time, I had made important resolutions that I swore to myself I’d never break. So I walked there, albeit rather slowly, so I could smoke away this hangover. I didn’t care what the coppers would say anyway. I breathed deeply and evenly and I psyched myself up having conversations with imaginary policemen so that I was prepared for what they’d say and have the most professional answers. I’d walk right into the station and state my case. “There’s a criminal abounds by the name of something or other Calendar. Let’s just call him Mr. Calendar.” I knew he was male because females are bad at math and thus incapable of complex division problems. “I’m completely sober, officer. If you don’t believe me, you can check my vitals.” You see, I wasn’t afraid in the slightest to bleed for this cause. I had the resolve of a martyr no matter how many bodily fluids it would cost me. I saw a skinny dweeb with glasses on the way. I didn’t have time to stop and punch him in the face, but I spooked him by lunging at him and growling. I had an unparalleled lunge where I’d land squarely on both feet in front of my victim. It was important to land both feet at the same time because if you lunge with one foot first, the victim can expect the other foot coming later and brace himself for the full intensity of the lunge. The dweeb backed up several feet and exhaled a slightly exasperated pip before speeding up and away from me. I chuckled to myself. “What a coward! What an invertebrate prick!” I should’ve tripped him up and spat on him, but I was in a hurry. I was always prone to procrastination, and if I tarried any longer, I knew that my mission would end futilely. I wouldn’t let that happen this time, so I trudged onward towards the police station. My hair tangled into my mouth as I walked, and I tongued to spit it out. “God damned wind.” I always hated the wind. It always got in my way. I kept on walking. I knew the police station was close. I had been there on a paddy wagon several times before, and I just had to retrace my steps backwards from the times I had to stumble home after being released. They released me sometimes, when they knew I could make it home alright. I wasn’t always that drunk, you know. Sometimes I could make it home just by the smell of things and the position of the stars. I had instincts like a canine, but I procrastinated too damn much. And I was almost there, too, when I looked to my right at the fenced in graveyard. There was an ornate headstone off in the distance, and I could tell from outside the fence that it had been carved with more care than the other stones. There were stone angels adorning the top, playing trumpets, wrapped in sashes beside a very curvaceous cross that stemmed from the center like a star ornament. “Who was buried there?” I wondered to myself. It must be someone important with some money to spend on a headstone. I vowed that I’d never need a big headstone; just something with my name on it and a few words of warning to keep children away. They were always dancing over everything. They had no respect. I looked down the sidewalk a bit, and I still saw the police station in the distance, and I could smell the coffee and donuts, and I could hear the thick city accents accusing me of being a drunk and telling me to get out of the police station. It’d happened once before when I went in to ask if I could borrow some of their firearms. I’d heard a knocking sound outside of my apartment, and I could have sworn it was robbers. I could’ve beaten them away with a mop if I had one, but I figured a gun would do me just as well. You’re always a bigger man if people think you’re going to kill them. But there I was, on the other side of the fence, and something kept drawing me to the grave. Maybe it was the sheer size of the thing. It towered in comparability to the mausoleum, and there were just so many god damned angels protecting the damn thing. Who the fuck needed so many angels? It was enough to make a man do foolish things. So I tried to scale the fence, though my lack of balance really hindered me, and my unlaced sneakers barely fit in between the bars. I was able to pull myself up, though, like some sort of clumsy animal. In fact, I scratched myself quite well on the pointed knobs- those pestering steel stalagmites that poked out from the top. My belly was red for days and quite sore beneath the stomach hair that made wolverine quilts around my gut. Still, I pressed on, wishing I had cleats to tear my way through the craggy, leaf-strewn ground that some people held sacred. It felt like regular ground to me, albeit unnecessarily craggy. I wish the land was all flat. I was sick of all these uneven soil business. It made walking a surprise, and I didn’t like surprises; especially when I was walking about. The grave I approached only grew slightly larger as I came near, and I read the executive carvings on the headstone. It had a quote in Latin or something, which I thought was uncharacteristic; no more than a flimsy attempt to sound official. And then I got to the name. Calvin Endar. It took me a while to shorten the full first name back to its abridged form and then combine the two names- the first and the last- to piece together the puzzle. What did it for me was the years that the man had lived. He had lived a while, I don’t remember when, but the years were his own. Calendar. He had created these years and lived to their exact specifications as if he had wished to just so he could die faster and show off his big god damned grave with all the bloody angels all over it. The shit was dead, and there was nothing I could do about it except urinate on his grave and hope the piss seeped through the dirt and soiled his bones. So I did. But as I did, I came to realize that despite the size of his tomb and the number of angels surrounding it, I felt a bit bad for him. There were no roses at his grave site like there were roses on even the smallest of stones. There weren’t even carnations. So he had invented the calendar, but he had been so busy in his life with all of the calculations that he had never known love or born an heir. The patent had died with him and was probably now public domain. There wasn’t a damned thing I could do about it but finish pissing and wipe the last leaks of my junk onto his headstone. I developed a rash days later, but more importantly, I did what I always do when I start to do something but find out that it’s harder than I imagined it would be. I gave up. I broke my resolution. “Fuck them,” I said as I tried to climb the fence again. “Let ‘em all die in seventy five years and see if I give a shit.” Hell, they could’ve all lived longer if it wasn’t for the arrogance of their creator.
