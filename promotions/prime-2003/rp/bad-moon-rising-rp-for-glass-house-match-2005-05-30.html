Bad Moon Rising (RP for Glass House Match)

30 May 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/30/05 clink, clink, clink It's one of the greatest sounds in the world. The sound of ice cubes hitting the bottom of a glass. But even more euphoric is the sound bourbon makes when it splashes into that same glass. Even when it's being poured by the very unsteady burn scarred hand of the Trashcan Man. His hand was unsteady for any number of reasons. He was definately shook up by the letter he had received at the last ReVolution. The sheer disappointment he had felt when the referee had lowered his and and raised Nick Brocken's hand in victory instead certainly had its part to play. But the main reason was the Trashcan Man, after two years of sobriety, was good and drunk. He weaved in from his kitchen, sliding a little in his sock clad feet, big toe sticking out of the hole in his left one. Settling himself in a recliner, he took an appreciative sip of the Jack Daniels in his glass and set it down on the table beside him before he leaned back and gazed into the flickering flames of his fireplace. Why does he have a fire going in late May? When tempatures even at this late hour were a very comfortable 58 degrees? If you have to ask, you still don't know the Trashcan Man yet. You see, one of the defining characteristics of this fan favorite is his pyromania. The publicists in the wrestling federations that he's been in have glossed over that fact, not wanting the bad press of having someone with a real psychosis on their roster. But the fact remains that the Trashcan Man is fascinated with fire. Fascination isn't strong enough of a word, really. The kindling of a flame is damn near orgasmic for him. It's also a sort of purification ritual for him. When things are really bad, when confusion is at its strongest, he will open the battered silver Zippo he stole from a guard at the Evanston clinic, that hellhole he had been trapped in for many years. He'll start to light a cigarette, then the longing will overcome him and he'll place his hand into the fire, welcoming its searing touch. Some people would say it's like cutting, and it does have its similarities. However, those poor souls drag those razors across their skins to bleed out their pain and suffering are indulging in a form of suicide. Some of them do eventually take that last step and truly open their veins, ending their miserable existences. This isn't the case for the Trashcan Man's self immolation. It has... deeper roots. Roots from his early childhood... ____________________________________________________ 'Michael, where are you, you ungrateful wretch?!' Young Michael was in fact in a tree. An apple tree, to be precise. He was laying on his shirtless stomach on a branch, legs dangling underneath him as he allowed the welts from his latest punishment to be eased by the light spring breeze that blew through the leaves. Wiping the tears from his eyes, he stayed quiet. He knew that the punishment he would receive for hiding from Father Callabryn would be even worse than the ten straps he had recieved across his back from the slender cane for not knowing his scriptures word for word, but he didn't care. He just needed some time alone, some time to heal. Unfortunately, he wouldn't have that time. The ten year old boy trembled as he heard footsteps crunching on the leaves underneath his tree. 'Devil child, how dare you hide from me? Get down from there this instant.' Sniffling back a sob, Michael slowly made his way down the tree, scraping his hands on the bark as he slid down the trunk, landing at the feet of the stern, emaciated old man standing before him in the black clothes and white collar of a Catholic priest. 'So boy, what do you have to say for yourself?' demanded the priest, hands on his hips. 'I'm sorry, Father Callabryn,' replied the boy. Michael had learned at an early age that it was ALWAYS Father Callabryn, never just Father. 'Sorry isn't good enough, boy. Explain your actions!' 'I... I... I just... It hurt so bad...' stammered Michael. Father Callabryn shook his head, before reaching out and grabbing a hunk of Michael's bowl cut hair. 'Of course it hurt bad, you were terribly neglectful. It's that DEMON you have inside you. Apparently I can't beat it out of you, stronger measures will have to be taken.' With that, his bony hands still gripping the hank of hair, he strode off at a brisk pace, Michael sobbing as he ran along. He had to take two steps for every one of the priest's to not have a bald spot snatched out of his head. Yet another lesson the boy had learned. Father Callabryn dragged him into the small rickety church the two of them lived in and threw him to the floor. 'Stay there!' he commanded before striding off. Michael, knowing that running wouldn't help now, huddled in a corner and wept. Why won't you listen to me, Mikey? He's just going to hurt you some more. Just wait till he falls asleep tonight and... The voice had started in his head over a year ago. It suggested... horrible things. It was proof that Father Callabryn was right, he DID have a demon inside of him. He prayed and prayed every night for God to cast out the demon within him, that he'd be good and read his scriptures and not have lustful thoughts, but God never answered. Just the voice, laughing at him. 'Leave me alone,' muttered Michael under his breath. 'You're filthy and dirty and evil. Father Callabryn told me so. You make me tainted by being in me. Why can't you just leave?' Because I AM you, Michael. Listen to me... The voice in his head was interrupted by Father Callabryn walking in again, holding an lit candle. 'Now boy, by the grace of God, we will drive the evil out of you. Give me your hand.' Michael's eyes widened as he shook his head, backing up against the wall. He gasped as he felt the recent welts on his back scrape against the roughhewn wooden wall behind him. The priest sighed and knelt down by the boy. 'Michael... this is something we have to do. You do feel the evil inside you, don't you?' The boy sobbed, but he nodded. Almost lovingly, the priest continued. 'You do want it out of you, don't you? To be a GOOD boy?' He cried a little harder, but he nodded again. 'Then give me your hand, Michael. I'll help you. And God will as well, if you just open up and let Him into your heart. Now give me your hand.' Michael swallowed heavily, then held out his hand, trembling with fear. The priest grabbed his wrist, knobby knuckles turning white with the force of his grip. 'Say the Lord's Prayer, boy, allow God's grace in you and we'll drive this demon out together...' 'Our Father, who art in Heaven, hallowed be thy...' The prayer was cut off by a scream as the priest held his hand in the fire. 'Pray, boy! Pray! You have to pray to God for the strength! You're unclean, you must be purified!' Crying, Michael continued... 'Hallowed be thy name...' His voice raised to a pain feel screech as he felt his flesh burn in the candle's flame. ____________________________________________________ The Trashcan Man sighed as the memory washed over him. The candle became a daily ritual for almost a year as the priest continued to try to drive the demon out. Until one day when He finally rose up in young Michael's head and took over the body. He locked Callabryn in his bedroom and burned the church down around him. It was the first time his second personality had taken over, and Michael paid the consequences for his darker side's actions for years afterwards, being thrust into various mental institutions before he had been sent to the Evanston Clinic, where he had been under the tender ministrations of Dr. Lovecraft. Ministrations that were funded and designed by Alan Kriegman. The Trashcan Man had freed himself from Evanston, again from the use of fire. This time however, his dark side hadn't taken over and forced him to do it. Trashy had done it himself. He never investigated the incident, never discovered how many people had died in the fire, and he didn't want to know. Isn't it amazing how we try to hide the hard truths, even from ourselves? Besides, it hadn't been the first time someone had died from his actions, now was it? After escaping Evanston, the Trashcan Man had found himself with a bigger challenge, even more painful than the drug therapy, the electroshock treatments, the surgery to have the chip implanted in his head. He had to live in the outside world. The Trashcan Man had never had any friends. Father Callabryn never let him play with any children, never let him even see them. When the priest would hold services, Michael was locked away in a closet, told to keep quiet under threat of punishment. The one time Michael asked why, he was beaten so bloody he passed out. After he was awakened, Father Callabryn explained that he didn't want the evil in Michael to taint anyone else. Only Father Callabryn himself had the faith in God required to keep himself free of the demon's influence. And it's not like there's much of a chance to make friends in mental institutions. So Trashy slowly made his way through the world, finding someone who needed cheap labor and wouldn't ask questions. He'd work long hours and go to sleep in a cheap ramshackle apartment. He tried to learn about the world around him by reading anything he could get his hands on. He'd watch every movie that came into the theatre nearby, he'd buy used cassette tapes and listen to them on the old stereo he had picked up in a pawn shop. He'd watch TV constantly, immersing himself into pop culture, trying to make sense of the social miasma that was the outside world. He learned a lot, but he still had trouble connecting to people. He just couldn't fit in. The fires didn't help either. Sure, he'd make sure no one would get hurt, but it's hard to make friends when all you have to talk about is what you watched on Saturday Night Live last night after you had set that grocery store ablaze. But he did discover the wonders of junk food. ESPECIALLY soda. Caffeine and sugar kept his nerves jumping. He had never had anything like that before. Candy and ice cream went down his gullet, as well as potato chips and pizza. A luxury had been long denied him, and he gorged. He was still strong and muscular from the back breaking labor he endured every day, but he gained weight from all the junk food he devoured. Trashy ran a hand down his flat stomach as he took another sip of his Jack Daniels. As he tried to figure out why he continued to lose, he had undergone a regimen of exercise and controlling his diet. And it showed. He was down to 255 pounds, probably in the best shape of his life. Not that it helped. He still has only won one match in PRIME since his arrival. Nowhere near the success he had found in PCW when he first arrived. Wrestling had become his savior. After discovering it on TV, he had a tryout in a small indy fed called the CWF. A little podunk operation with more budget than talent, and that's saying something. The Trashcan Man's toughness and fearlessness had quickly made him one of the biggest names there. After all, how does taking a powerbomb compare to night after night of 1000 volts being channeled through your skull? His tolerance for punishment had more than compensated for his lack of skill. He didn't like the atmosphere though, and left shortly after joining. He had watched the newest national federation on TV, Primetime Championship Wrestling, and he decided to send them a tape. Just on a whim. And they answered within a week. He met with a man known as the Phantom Booker who had been impressed by the sheer amount of punishment the Trashcan Man could take. He had also been impressed by the Trashcan Man not questioning his wearing of a stocking over his head like every other member on his roster had when he had first met him. Trashy, in a moment of candor, laid out as much of his history as he remembered at the time, and the Phantom Booker helped him out, using his connections to get Trashy a false identity so he could pay his taxes and start a bank account. He still got a chuckle every now and then at cashing his checks that were made out to T. C. Mann. PCW was a godsend for the Trashcan Man. It was just coming off it's first Pay Per View, and it was hungry for success. At the beginning, there was little of the backstage politics that infested the business as a whole. Everyone was there to make PCW the biggest thing the industry had ever seen. So they welcomed new talent with open arms. Even freaky outcasts like the Trashcan Man. Unfortunately, on the roster was a man that the Trashcan Man SHOULD have remembered, if he hadn't been brainwashed not to. Alan Kriegman. He thought he had put Kriegman away for good, working with Charles Isaac Anderson to put him behind bars. He thought that his 'Master' was out of his life. And now this letter shook him to the core. He glanced down at the letter sitting on the table beside him and picked it up. He knew it by heart from constant readings, but like a moth drawn to the light, he started reading it again... ---------- Dear Michael, It has been with great enthusiasm that I have followed the course of your independence over the past two years. Though my incarceration is indeed bothersome (surely you can commiserate), I am not completely blind to the world beyond this prison’s walls. I know that you spent several happy months in Old School Wrestling upon my imprisonment. You had new adventures ahead of you and you were surrounded by new friends; Surely, in that Indian summer of your new life, the world was yours for the taking. Even your defeats and sorrows had a certain sweetness beneath them. After all, were they not the bitter fruits of your own freedom? A happy time for you, indeed. I believe the federation closed for business a short time later, didn’t it? You dropped into obscurity at that point, partially to recover from the brutality of your final confrontation with dear old Clyde, but mostly because the only home you’d ever really known was no more. A few years earlier you had stumbled into Primetime Championship Wrestling thinking it would keep you off the street for a few more months, not imagining that in the Phantom Booker’s backwater beginnings you would find a place in the world. People like Ed, Howard Porter, Ethan “Meanstreak” Giroux, Ivan Stanislav, Doug Troy, Clyde – and later (and you would probably say most importantly) a certain glittery heroine by the name of Tempest - became your version of a family, friends and enemies alike. ------- Tempest... So filled with life, so energetic and bubbly. So wonderful. So in love was the Trashcan Man with her that it hurt. He took a long drink, ignoring the cold ice against his teeth as he tried to kill that pain. Why did he never tell her how he felt about her? Why didn't he try to take her from Ivan Stanislav? Because he was scared. That's right, the Trashcan Man, world reknowned for his fearlessness, was scared. Scared of a little five foot nothing slip of a purple haired goddess who unknowingly held his heart in her glittery little hand. He was scared of the cruel laughter he knew would come from her perfect lips stabbing him with the icy dagger of rejection. Trashy never was a ladies man. Hell, he was still a virgin. Well, with girls. When he was 12, one year into his confinement in the New Hampshire state facility for mentally disturbed juveniles, he was raped by a prison guard. It's not something he liked to think about. However, it was the first time that Michael, his dark side, and the scripture agreed. An eye for an eye. A few weeks days the incident, he had found a way to work his way loose from the straps that constrained him to his bed for his weekly medical checkup. He had snatched the syringe filled with God knew what chemical cocktail they had been injecting it with and slammed it into the guard's left eye. He still sometimes reveled in the feeling of the eyeball popping and squirting juices in his face as he pressed down the plunger, injecting whatever it contained directly into the bleeding eye socket of the bastard that had hurt him and humiliated him so. The guard died. Michael was horrified at first, but his dark side had talked to him, and convinced him that he had done nothing wrong, the filth had deserved it. So many things have left their scars on his psyche. And though he loved Tempest, the thought of her rejecting him had filled with him with a fear he hadn't felt since those days under Father Callabryn. So he let her go. Lighting a cigarette, he continued to read. --------- But the lie you imposed upon yourself did not last forever. OSW faded away, and with it, your new life. Tempest and Ivan dissolved into Russia. Clyde found peace in oblivion. Charles Isaac Anderson returned to the shadows. Jeremy Howard, Kremmen, Jon Hull, Xavier Kannon, your old friends in Asylum: All gone. And let us not forget your best friend, Ed. With the fall of the Ron Meischker’s great empire you had nothing to do, no one to see, and nowhere to go. You had nothing left. The Indian summer of your freedom had finally yielded to the harsh winter of the real world. So it was that like you’ve done so many other times throughout your life, you vanished. I would very much like to know what became of you in the year between your disappearance and reemergence in PRIME. Was it the presence of old, familiar faces that drew you there, Michael? Even if it was only the likes of Chris Sloboda, Jon Kano, and Hessian? Is the bottom of the PCW/OSW barrel close enough to give you a taste of the (man, they’ll pass you by) glory days? I wish I could inquire as to whether or not this letter finds you well, but I’m afraid that I already know the answer to that question. Is it true what I’ve heard? Did you, my adopted progeny, really fall to the son of Duke Williams? Are you really teaming with Jon Kano? Am I being fed terrible, terrible lies by my sources, or are you indeed toiling away at the very bottom of the card while “Hardknocks” Chris Allen himself dominates the main event? There was a time not very long ago when these men were the punchlines of your jokes. There was a time not very long ago, in the Renaissance of the industry, that together we held one of its greatest promotions in our grip. Under my leadership the Think Tank wielded complete dominion over Old School Wrestling. You recognized me as Master, but you were far more than my right hand. You were my deadly sword. You were what held my enemies at bay, what removed all obstacles in our path to greatness. And I was usurped only when you were turned against me. It doesn’t have to be this way, Michael. Together, there was nothing we could not accomplish. There were none who could stand in our way. Reunited, PRIME would be ours for the taking. We could build it up to the heights of greatness reached only by your former home, and then surpass them! And we could bring them all back, Michael! Your old family! Meanstreak, Clyde, Howard Porter, Kremmen, Frisco, Kid Wonder, Xavier Kannon, Doug Troy, Jon Hull, Asylum, Ivan Stanislav…even Tempest! All of them. If we build it, they will come. A reunion unlike anything ever seen before! Under our dominion, the new PRIME would lead the way into a new golden age. The industry would usher in a second, greater Renaissance, and ruling it all would be us! I as the Master, and you as my sword. As father and son. Or is the “independence” Tempest preached to you everything she promised it would be? Think, Michael. The world has been cruel to you, so very cruel. All your friends, even your purple-haired liberator”, have abandoned you. You’re jerking the curtain in an obscure theater whose greatest performers would hardly have been a match for the plebians you and I easily triumphed over every week. And of course, you’re losing yourself once again. Are you prepared to agree, or is this clipping from Tuesday’s edition of the Chicago Tribune necessary evidence? Homeless War Hero Rescued From Fire by Masked Man.By Mick Green, Tribune Staff Reporter5/24/06 64 year-old John Spencer, a homeless medal of honor-winner, is in stable condition after being rescued from a fire on Mendscape St. that consumed the entire abandoned apartment building he was squatting in. It is believed to have started sometime around 3 AM, Monday morning. His rescue, however, was not by the fire department, who did not arrive in time to save the building. An unidentified witness who reported the blaze also observed that Spencer being carried out of the flames over the shoulders of a masked man. The story was later confirmed by a fully conscious Spencer from his hospital bed: “I woke up and the room was full of smoke. Couldn’t hardly breathe. I was pretty drunk. I got up and stumbled around for a second til’ I took a pretty bad spill and wound up breaking my crutch arm, so I figured I was [in trouble] and I start calling for help. Next thing I know this guy kicks a hole in the door, then reaches in and opens it. He was wearing these orange rubber gloves and a cardboard mask with a smiley face on it. Says something about God being dead, then grabs me and jumps out the second story window. I asked him about the mask. He said it was Halloween. Called me a Snickers bar.” The condemned building had been the site of a police raid on a group of drug dealers earlier that day. Fire officials believe the fire was a work of arson, possibly started by another of the building’s previous inhabitants in an attempt to destroy evidence. The masked rescuer is believed to have been another squatter and not the source of the blaze. “This fire was started by someone who knew what he or she was doing,” Deputy Fire Chief Saul Goodman explained. “The empty gas tank we found is a strong indication that whoever did this wanted to see the place burn. This smiley-face guy wouldn’t have saved Spencer if he was a dealer trying to erase the crime scene. He’s a hero, in my book.” Police have not yet released a statement, but an inside source on the department relays a general feeling of agreement among the investigators with Goodman’s theory. Spencer’s “Smiley” will probably not be named a suspect. John Spencer served in the Vietnam war as private first class in the 25th infantry division from 1969 to 1973, when during the last month of his tour he and four squad mates were ambushed by a pair of Viet Cong snipers. Spencer, who took a bullet to the leg, was the only soldier able to fight back. He successfully killed the two attackers and then dragged his seriously wounded lieutenant, the only other survivor, three miles back to camp despite his leg injury. His leg was later amputated above the knee because of the damage done to it both by the bullet and his rescue efforts. The lieutenant died of his wound hours afterward. Upon his return home Spencer was awarded the Congressional Medal of Honor, but due to unexplained reasons was living on the street within a year. Starting fires, Trash? Losing control to that gibbering fool Lachesis? The reinforcements made to your mind at Evanston and through the Chip are beginning to unravel. You’re beginning to unravel. How long do you think you can last like this? How soon until He decides to make his move? You know of whom I speak. If Lachesis (or “Mr. Smiley Face” as you call him) has the freedom to move about your mind as he wishes, even taking control when he wants, then imagine what could happen when He finally breaks free. There will be nothing left of you, the Smilin’ Dude, or anyone else in that head of yours when he finishes. He will seize control of your mind and utterly destroy everything you have ever been. Your body will be His vessel, but you, whether your name is Michael Kriegman or the Trashcan Man, will be gone forever. I can help you. I can save you. I can free you from Him once and for all, and never again will you have to burn. With my help you can be complete, your fullest potential realized. I can make you whole. If you let me. Alan Kriegman -------- How? How did know the things he knew? How did he know that the Trashcan Man was unravelling? 'Anyone else in his head'... how could he possibly know about the splinter personality forming? Alan Kriegman knew more about the inner workings of the Trashcan Man's mind than any human alive. Possibly even more than Trashy himself. Draining his drink, he groggily rose to his feet. He stumbled forward, almost falling until he raised his hand to catch the wall. The hand that was carrying the empty glass. It took almost a full minute of blearily staring at the bloody handprint on the wall to figure out that he had smashed the glass, gouging his hand in the process. He opened his hand and stared at the glass shard sticking out of it. Weaving on his feet, he put his shoulder against the wall as he concentrated and pulled the shard out of his hand. Stumbling to the kitchen, he ran it under the tap in the sink, watching the blood and water swirl around in the white porcelain basin before going down the drain. He grabbed a clean dishcloth and wrapped it around his hand tightly, realizing through his drunken haze that he probably didn't want to bleed to death. Although death wasn't that bad of an option if He was going to eventually take over. Reaching into his pocket, the Trashcan Man pulled out his wallet. Its battered leather barely contained the morass of business cards and receipts the Trashcan Man accumulated. He started pulling out the rubbish of the past few years, casting aside. 'I know it'sh in here shomewhere' he slurred as he continued his search. With a exclamation of discovery, he found what he was looking for. A key. Grasping it in his good hand, he made his way through his home and into his bedroom. He looked up at a framed poster of PCW Alliances, his first Pay Per View. Glancing down through the match list, he reached with his cut hand, the white dishcloth turning red from the blood seeping into it, and touched the glass over the listing of his match. Tables, Ladders, And Chairs match for the PCW Extreme Title!Boda(c) vs 'Homicidal' Anthony Cay vs Trash Can Man! Smearing a red streak of blood on the glass, he drunkenly read it to himself. Reaching up, he pulled the framed poster off and leaned it against the wall beside him, exposing the keypad locked safe behind it. 'What'sh the combination again?' he muttered as he reached up with his left hand. Concentrating mightly, he manages to put in the correct sequence of numbers on the third try. Pulling on the lever, he opens the safe and takes out the steel lockbox inside. Cradling it to his chest, he stumbles to his bed and sits down heavily. Inside this box was something important. Something that could be his destruction. Or his salvation. His hand shaking, he inserted the key and turned it with an audible click. Lifting the lid of the box he reached in pulled out a small black plastic device. It had a few white buttons and a dial on it. It was the controlling device to the mind control chip in his head. Gingerly holding device, he made his way to the patio. Staring out over the Chicago skyline, he gazed at the thousands of lights of the Windy City. His gaze went up to stare at the crescent moon casting its light over the view before him. He had to win this match this week. He had to prove to Kriegman that he didn't need him. He had to prove to himself that he didn't need Kriegman. The alternative was just too much to bear.
