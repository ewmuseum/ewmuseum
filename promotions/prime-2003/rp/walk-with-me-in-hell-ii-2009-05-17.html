Walk With Me In Hell (II)

17 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 17th May “Fate is for those to weak to determine their own destiny.” - Kamran Hamid * * * * * His tongue felt as though it was wrapped in sandpaper as he shifted it around a mouth drier than a bag of old bones. The only moisture he count find came from sucking hard on the spittle-soaked rag that gagged his mouth, and every swallow was so painful that he may as well have swallowed a handful of shrapnel. He couldn’t tell how long he’d been conscious for, but it wasn’t long. From the spluttering purr of what sounded like an engine and the constant, uncomfortable sense of forward motion he knew he was in-transit. It was only when he tried to stretch his legs and they would go no further that he realised he was in the trunk of a car. Bound, gagged, and with no room to move, Aidan began to panic. He bashed his feet violently against the wall and tried to do the same with his hands but the unseen shackles around his wrists put halt to that. The claws of desperation dug into his inner cortex as he struggled inside his pitch-black prison. Kicking until his leg muscles ached and screaming until his croaky voice gave up on him, Aidan soon ran out of strength. There was precious little oxygen left in the trunk and the heat was becoming too much to bear. Physical exertion was futile and would only hasten his descent to unconsciousness, and the last thing he wanted was to black-out again. As the air became thinner, it dawned on Aidan that he was struggling to breath. Frantically moving his mouth towards a gap in the hood, he breathed-in heavily but soon recoiled in a fit of coughs and splutters. All he’d done was ingest tiny threads of cloth from the sodden rag between his teeth and that didn’t bode well for his arid throat and lungs. Aidan dropped his head and gave in. It was no use. He could feel himself drifting… He could feel himself… He could feel… He could… Wait! A sudden shudder to the left and the squeal of old brake pads. Loose gravel crunched under the vehicle’s weight. The engine died. They’d stopped. He dug deep, Aidan; driven onwards by a sudden surge of adrenaline and a glimmer of hope. The few seconds it took for the driver to pull open the trunk passed at a glacial pace, but soon his damp, clammy skin was caressed soothingly by the cool night air. With grunt his captor grabbed him by the arm and hauled him from the car. The brute dragged him out the trunk and dropped him down to the gravel. Aidan landed facedown; sharp chunks of cold stone dug into his cheek and immediately he felt the chilling sensation of surface water seeping into his scruffy fatigues, though the fresh air travelling down his windpipe superseded his discomfort. Aidan heard heavy footsteps pace around the outside of the car, before the figure came back around and unceremoniously slammed the trunk. Suddenly a rough, calloused hand wrapped itself around his forearm and hauled him to his feet. Warm air from his assailant’s nostrils kissed the back of his neck as a gruff voiced grunted for him to “fuckin’ hurry up.” * * * * * “Relax, guys. I’m sure he’ll get here any minute,” said Nathaniel Loftman as he nervously rolled up his jacket’s sleeve and glanced at his expensive silver wristwatch. “It’s not like the kid to be late.” “Better be. Gone to a lotta trouble for this kid – boss ain’t gon’ be best pleased if he don’t show,” snarled one of Cordell Abraham’s bulldog lieutenants. Standing inside the stuffy boxing club, Loftman felt his body temperature begin to soar. Beads of perspiration began to build on his forehead and a prickly heat slowly crept up his spine as he stood nervously with the New York crime boss’s goons. He was the one who’d brought Aidan Chase into the circle in the first place, and if Chase didn’t turn up he knew it wouldn’t just be Aidan’s head that was for the chopping block. There were three of them; all clad in rough outfits of denim and leather that Loftman supposed made them feel big and intimidating. Not that they needed it, of course. Big ugly bastards, all of them; built like brick outhouses and with weathered skin bearing the scars of their chosen profession. Each stood at least two inches taller than Loftman, with the African-American leader of the pack towering above the group at somewhere between six-five and six-seven. “Fuck this shit, man,” the stockier man with the shaved blonde hair. He shook his head and glanced Loftman in the eye with menace. “Call the motherfucker again.” “I just did that two minutes ago,” Nathaniel protested. “Man, I don’t give a fuck,” the thug barked, striding menacingly towards him. “Try again. Fuck waitin’ another half-hour for this prick.” Realising that another protest would probably result in a black eye, Loftman bit his tongue and mumbled something as he begrudgingly pulled out his cell phone. Hitting the redial button and pulling the cell to his ear, Nathaniel prayed for an answer. * * * * * Thunk! The steel door closed hard and heavy shorly after Aidan had been kicked through the door. It obscured the polyphonic ditty chiming from his back pocket, but not for very long. When he heard it, Aidan's captor reached down into the back pocket of his jeans and yanked it out. The next thing Aidan heard was the sound of the plastic device skittering across a concrete surface, and he felt the lump in his throat grow larger as another avenue of hope evaporated. With another grunt his captor yanked him upwards and he felt the sharp edges of the plastic bind around his wrists dig into his skin. As he was shuffled forward, Aidan's nostrils began to pick up traces of gasoline in the air; a smell that grew stronger with every step. Eventually he felt two big paws clamp down on his shoulders, compelling him to stop. He tried to say something but the gag prevented anything other than a couple of incoherent mumbles escape his mouth. "Shut up," his assailant snapped and then, much to Aidan's surprise, dug his fingers began the makeshift blindfold and yanked it from his head. Chase winced in pain as his captor took a few hairs with him, and his eyes stung as the first sign of light he'd seen in hours burned the back of his retinas. Slowly his vision returned. Through squinted eyelids he saw that his surroundings were grim and unwelcoming. Walls of concrete and corrigated iron and with a floor splattered with a myriad of oilslicks and gas leaks, the building was clearly some kind of mechanical servicing area. Dying weeds sprung up from cracks in the floor and the workbenches were abandoned and without fixtures, leading Aidan to believe that had it been a long time since the place had been used for it's intended purpose. "Gerrin' there." He barely had time to react as his captor planted a size thirteen boot against his backside and pushed him downards. Aidan tried to push his hands out to break his fall but again his shackles restricted him, so when he fell face-first into the conrete pit the results were less than pleasant. He felt the whole left side of his face going numb as he groaned and rolled over onto his back. The dirty bulb hung almost directly above him and shined painful light into his weary eyes, so he bowed his head and hoped that it would go away. "Gettin' comfortable?" that damned voice mocked him, only this time it sounded a little more distant. It was then that he realised his vision had turned to him. Snapping his head upwards as fast as he could, he saw the face of his enemy for the first time. Standing above the pit, looming over him like a grave spirit, was Jason Natas. Immediately Aidan's heart started to pound with such intensity that he thought it might burst through his ribs. He pushed his hands together and tried to pull his wrists from their plastic shackles but to no avail. The PRIME grappler noted his struggle. "Ain't no use doin' that, kid. Gonna get yerself cut up is all." As much as he loathed to believe it, Natas was right. He admitted defeat in his struggle as warmth began to emanate from his grazed, bloody cheek. He looked up. The Anti-Superstar was crouched at the edge of the six foot deep pit, staring at Chase with a blank facial expression. Again he tried to mumble through the gag. "Fuck's sake, would've thought ya'd worked that one out by now," Jason said, shaking his head. Aidan clenched his fists. Bit down hard on the rag. Jason Natas was the sole reason his life had spiralled out of control in the first place, and now, once again, he found himself at the ruffian's mercy. It had been little over two months ago in a Manhattan back alley that they'd first "met". Aidan was on his way to a glittering wrestling career and a lucrative PRIME contract until Jason had caught wind of the deal he'd brokered with Nathaniel Loftman's Potemkin agency. Just like that, in one quick, violent swoop, everything he'd ever worked for disappered before his very eyes. The knock-on effect was devastating. He lost his girlfriend, he lost his self-respect, he lost his dignity. Aidan Chase had nothing left. He torched Jason Natas' apartment in a reckless, spur of the moment retribution act, and went crawling back to Mr. Loftman, knowing his connections with Cordell Abraham's organisation of thugs could protect him from Jason's terrible wrath. Or so he thought. "Don't do such a good job 'a keepin' yerself inconspicuous, kid," Natas grunted as he rose to his feet. "Should know better than t' turn int' them roads that don't have no streetlights. All it takes is one blow t' the head..." From the back pocket of his ragged, wet jeans, Jason pulled a small - yet heavy - blackjack. He swung the truncheon's ring around his index finger a couple of times, before tossing it down into the pit. It landed by Chase's feet, coming to rest in a small pool of motor oil. "Ain't the biggest mistake ya've ever made though, no. That was when decided it'd be a good idea t'be pourin' kerosene through my letterbox..." Again Aidan's tried a failed verbal protest, and again Jason Natas brushed it off. "Thought you'd 'a learned yer lesson back in the alleyway. Figured you'd 'a learned then that I ain't a man to fuck with..." he stopped pacing back and forth. Suddenly he leapt downwards, joining Chase in the pit. Instinctively Aidan recoiled, but found himself helpless to back away from the oncoming wrestler. Kneeling on one knee and pulling Aidan's chin up, Jason ran his thumb across a small, pink lump at the bottom of Chase's neck. "See that pretty little scar ain't even gone away yet." Jason stood up. "Guess ya thought ya'd got away with it, eh? Figured ya were safe with Lofty an' those fuckin' low-rent Godfather extras from the boxin' club, huh?" With a wheeze Jason hauled his large frame out of the pit. He walked slowly around the outside. "Guess again, boyo. Got a few ideas 'a my own." Walking outside Aidan's field of vision, Jason suddenly flicked a switch. Aidan was startled by a sudden burst of mechanical noise above his head. It echoed around the garage's cavernous recesses and the mechanical oscillations sounded only a few feet from his head. He scampered away from the corner and looked up. That's when he saw the cement mixer. * * * * * "WHADDAYA MEAN 'NO FUCKIN' ANSWER?!" the irritable blonde thug roared. Nathaniel sighed and parted his lips to speak, but before he could utter a word he found himself pinned up against the wall by a man he wouldn't look twice at in the street. "Could'a been spendin' a nice relaxin' evenin' at home tonight if it wasn't for you an' this whiny little sack of shit cryin' to Cordell!" "WHOA! Steve, easy!" the African-American leader of the pack stepped forward, pushing his forearm down across his cohort's arms and forcing him to break his grip. Loftman doubled-over, catching his breath as the underboss spoke. "Hurtin' this guy ain't gonna get us nowhere, man. Be cool." "'Be cool'?! I've been 'bein' cool' since eight thirty, Lex, and this cunt still ain't shown himself!" "Maybe he's driving or something?" Loftman wheezed. He was well aware of how lame and desperate his excuse sounded. "He's probably on his way here right now." "Since when does that stop a guy from answerin' his phone?" Steve snarled. Loftman gritted his teeth and looked at the raging thug with nothing but complete and utter contempt in his eyes. "He'll call back when he sees the missed calls. Trust me," he said, struggling to maintain his cool. "Fuck that! I'm goin' home," Steve announced. He turned to try and walk away, but from Lex's brick wall of a chest in his way. "Fuck no you ain't," the deep-voiced leader said. "You know what happened to the last guy that walked out on a job..." Steve felt red anger bubbling behind his skin and a vein pounding on his forehead. The last thing he wanted to do was to have to wait another minute in the presence of the slimy suit-wearing scumball, but he knew he didn't have much choice in the matter. Shaking his head he slowly turned around and kicked a heavy bag in frustration. "Wasting our time, man. Fuck we doing here, exactly?" "Waiting," Lex said with stern authority that Steve knew better than to question. Cordell's lieutenant turned to Loftman. "Your boy's got fifteen minutes. After that... well, Jason Natas ain't gon' be the only one we go huntin' for tonight." * * * * * “MmmmfffhmmmMMMM! MMHHMFMFFMMFF!” Aidan's protests grew louder and louder as his fate approached him. The cement mixer's barrel revolved above the pit with menace. From it's size he estimated that there was at least enough concrete in there to fill the the pit two-thirds of the way up, and with Jason watching guard, he wouldn't stand a chance. “Know somethin', boyo? Gettin' tired a hearin' that shit come out yer mouth,” Jason said, shaking his head. He reached behind him and suddenly pulled a knife from his pocket, flipping the blade open with the press of a button. As Jason jumped back down into the pit, Aidan tried desperately to evade his captor. But he was stuck. In a prison of concrete, with a knife-wielding Jason Natas leaving over him, Aidan closed his eyes and let the angels take him. He squeezed his facial muscles together and felt his tear-ducts swell, but the moment never came. Violently he bashed his feet into the ground and tried to tell Jason to “DO IT!”... … but the only thing that got cut when Jason Natas knelt down was the rag around Aidan Chase's mouth. Immediately Aidan lurched forward and coughed; his throat was still as rough as hell and drier than it ever had been thanks to the cotton gag soaking up all the moisture. He fell to his side and breathed-in lungfuls of gas fumes as Jason tossed the sodden rag over his shoulder. Chase looked up and tried to speak, but he couldn't find the words. “Would've thought ya'd have more t'say after bein' silenced for a couple hours. Ain't complainin', tho-” “Fuck you,” Aidan croaked. His voice was that of a man with a sandstone throat. “Heh,” Jason smirked. “Be watchin' that tongue 'a yours if I were you, boyo,” he pulled the knife up again, pointing it towards Aidan's outh. “Ain't exactly in any position t'be makin' threats now, are ya?” “Fuck you.” Chase repeated himself. Flaring his nostrils, he suddenly lunged forward towards The Anti-Superstar. It was difficult though; without the use of his hands, Chase only succeeded in flinging himself into the concrete floor. Jason dodged the awkwardly lunge with a quick backstep and chuckled as his captive lay helpless on the ground. “I'll fuckin' kill you man, I swear!” Chase growled without any thought for what he was saying or the ridiculousness of his words, given the context. This time Jason summoned a hearty chuckle from the deepest recesses of his blackened lungs. Slowly rising to his feet and producing a packet of Marlboro Reds, Jason slid one between his dry, cracked lips and lit it up, being mindful not to drop hot ash in the oil puddle beneath him. Without a word he climbed out of the hole again and walked over towards the cement mixer. He didn't look down at Chase as he grabbed hold of the handle. “Wait! What are you doing?!” Aidan yelled, his eyes wider than the Grand Canyon. “Finishin' the job,” Jason nodded. “Ain't got 'nuff patience to sit here an' listen to your fuckin' shit all night, boyo. No offence.” The big New Yorker motioned as if he was going to pull the lever and bury Chase under a tsunami of sloppy, grey cement. “NO! WAIT!” Aidan shrieked. Ear-piercing, like a banshee. “Don't do it! PLEASE!” Jason relaxed his grip on the lever. “One good reason why I shouldn't.” “I'm begging you!” Chase crawled hopelessly towards Jason and the cement mixer, ignoring the request. “PLEASE MAN!” He let his hand slide down the lever, Jason; but he didn't let it the metal handle leave his grasp entirely. Pointing his free hand at Chase and exhaling noxious cigarette smoke through his noise, Jason scowled. “Convince me.” “I'm sorry man. Please!” Aidan's back fell against the side of his concrete prison. “I didn't mean to do it, I swear! It was... it was an...” “... an 'accident'?” “Yes! Yes! It was an accident!” he enthused. “You GOTTA believe me!” Desperation was rife in Aidan Chase's distressed tones and his red eyes were practically bulging from his skull. A tear rolled down his right cheek, washing some of the dirt away and leaving a thin, glistening trail in it's wake. He was at his wit's end. This was it. All he had left. Jason Natas rolled his head to the side, shrugging his shoulders. He let go of the lever and Aidan felt a small wave of relief wash over him as The Anti-Superstar turned his back for a moment. It was at this point that Aidan felt the small, rectangular bump under his jeans. With Jason no longer facing him and uttering something in that cigarette-ravaged south Bronx accent, Aidan managed to fiddle and fidget, shifting his bound hands around until the came in contact with the object. The rough, plastic surface was something that he recognised instantly. It was the cell. Fingering at the device and flipping it open, Aidan prayed that it was still working after Jason had flung it across the room. Awkwardly he fumbled at the keys, blindly trying to compose a text message through memorised keystrokes. “Please man, please...” Aidan repeated, more preoccupied with issuing an SOS SMS than anything else. “Ya put forward a convincing case, cunto. Can't deny that,” Jason said, swinging around on his heel and pointing his cigarette at Aidan. He stepped forward. “Trouble is, I ain't so sure I can believe ya. Desperate men'll say anything t'get outta situations like this if they know what's comin' to 'em. Trust me, I know...” As Natas stared at him Aidan bashed his fingers against the keypad more discreetly, praying that he wouldn't notice. “I swear on my Mother's life! I'll do anything man, PLEASE!” Jason stepped forward once again. He raised his eyebrows, rolled his eyes from side-to-side. Aidan could see he was pondering his options. “You... you believe me?” Aidan said. Faint hints of hope began to break through his cracked, wavering voice as his own face stretched with surprise. “You're going to let me go...” He maintained the same stoic facial expression for a couple of seconds, Jason. “Fuck no. Rot in hell.” Jason Natas stretched forward and yanked hard on the lever. The cement mixer's barrel began to roll forward as Aidan smashed his thumb into the phone's “SEND” button. * * * * * A sudden buzz in Nathaniel Loftman's pocket dragged his attention away from the bickering gangsters debating his fate. He pulled up his phone and slid it open. New text message. Loftman brought it up on his screen. “NATATS.” That's what it said. “Natats...” Loftman mouthed. It didn't take long for it to click. “Natas... FUCK!” his roar was enough to garner the gang's attention. “Shit! SHIT!” “This better be from your fuckin' boy...” Lex said as he strode across the gym. Reaching his hand out, he snatched the cell phone from Loftman's grasp and brought it into the light, examining the text message. “Fuck is 'natats'?” “It's a typo...” Loftman swallowed hard. “Jason's got him.” The realisation swept across Lex's face like a freight train. The man who'd came to the group seeking “protection” from Jason Natas had fallen into the hands of a common enemy. Lex knew exactly what to do. He turned around and made for the office. “Where are you going?!” barked Loftman. “Aidan's usin' one of our phones, right?” “Why does that mat-...” He stopped at the door, turning his head. “... because we can track it.” * * * * * His first day at school. The first time he got to take the stabilisers off his bike. Swinging from the school climbing frame on a warm Summer afternoon. His tenth birthday. His first kiss. The time Dad dressed up as Santa Claus and his beard fell off. Prom. High-school graduation. His first match on the indy scene. His first date with Amanda. The night he told her he was going to sign a PRIME contract. Everything came to him in a blur. A flash of constant motion. A lightning-quick burst of good times and great memories. With the bat of an eyelid it was over, and Aidan Chase was left with nought to console him but his fate. He pushed himself as far into the corner as he could and closed his eyes, praying for a miracle. The contents of the cement mixer hit him quickly. It was cold – a lot colder than he thought it'd be – and it soaked through his already-damp clothes on contact. It hit the ground with an almighty crrrrrasssshhhh and the flow seemed almost endless. Curled into a cocoon, Aidan pushed his feet against the wet concrete, trying in vein to push it away. But something was wrong. It was too easy, the way he'd moved his legs. He slapped a hand against the floor and plunged it through four inches of ice-cold surface water. Water. Not concrete. The final remnants of fluid dribbled from the edge of the lowered cement mixer and Aidan opened his eyes. He was sat in a harmless pool of H2O, and Jason Natas was laughing at him. But he didn't care. Natas could have his amusement. All that Aidan felt was immense relief. He thought that Jason was going to bury him alive and fill his lungs with thick, grey cement, but he hadn't. He was alive, and he thanked a God he'd never believed in for having mercy on his soul and giving him a second chance. Eventually he composed himself. Looked up. “Y-you...” was all that he could muster. “Shut the fuck up, boyo,” Jason responded as he hopped down into the water and hauled the trembling Chase to his feet. Pushing the burly former wrestler against the wall, Jason pulled out his knife and cut the tie around his wrists. For the first time in what seemed like an eternity, Aidan Chase let his arms swing free. He brought his wrists up to the light and examined them. The coarse plastic had chaffed hard against his skin and traces of blood were dotted around his wrists' circumference, but there was nothing that wouldn't heal in a couple of weeks. Turning to face Jason, Aidan wished for the strength to charge forward and take The Anti-Superstar out but found nothing. He was defenceless; drained physically and mentally, terrified after being taken out of the game by the PRIME brawler. But alive. Jason sat himself on the edge of the pit and glared at Chase. “That was yer final fuckin' warnin', hear me?” he barked. Aidan swallowed hard. Nodded. The gravity of the situation was beginning to become apparent again. “Next time you an' I run into each other, results ain't gon' be so pretty. So clean yer fuckin' ears out, boyo, 'cause I ain't repeatin' myself.” From the pocket of his leather jacket Jason pulled a small, brown envelope adorned with hand-writing that looked like a chicken's footprints. “There's a key to a motel room in New Jersey in this envelope an' a bus ticket t'get ya there. Go t'the room. In the safety deposit there's a one-way plane ticket outta the country. Go there, stay there, an' never show yer ugly fuckin' face 'round my neck 'a the woods again, 'less ya wanna wind-up in a pine box b'fore ya hit thirty.” Tempting Aidan forward by holding the envelope out to him, Jason snatched it away as the man who burned down his apartment dropped to his knees. Standing up and beckoning for Aidan to climb out of the pit, Jason watched him struggle back to his feet and pull his hefty body up onto the floor. He was soaked from head-to-toe, his hair dripping wet. For a man of his size he was having terrible trouble performing even the simplest of acts and made climbing to his feet look as difficult as breaking into The Royal Mint. Still, Aidan got up. He looked Jason straight in his cold, dead eyes and started to utter to the word “but”... “Not a fuckin' word. Close that fuckin' trap 'a yours 'fore I cut yer tongue out.” Aidan nodded. It would take hours before he'd get over the shock and fully comprehend the implications of his forced relocation. For now, he was at Jason's mercy. The ball was in The Anti-Superstar's court, and Aidan dared not blink as he reached out for the envelope. A sigh of relief was breathed as his wet hand clamped onto it. Phew. Just as he looked to pulled the paper towards him, Jason suddenly grabs him by the wrist and pulls him close. Jason slowly pushes his forehead against Aidan's. The look in his eye tells Chase that he must never return. Natas quickly let go and pushed open the heavy steel door. He took hold of Aidan's shoulder and threw him out the door with such force that Chase almost fell to the floor. In the evening rain, Aidan Chase turned to face Jason Natas for the last time as he backed away. He'd been let off the hook spectacularly, and in some sick, twisted way he wanted to thank The Anti-Superstar. All he got in return from Jason was a “fuck off, then,” and a dirty look. And so Jason Natas watched Aidan Chase turn and flee, as fast as his bruised legs would carry him, into the blackest of nights. He wondered whether he'd live to regret not killing the kid as he vanished from eyesight. The scare tactic had worked without question and he'd effectively petrified Chase into compliance, but the boy did have the balls to torch his apartment... But Jason didn't have long to ponder. There were headlights coming up the road. * * * * * “That's his car!” Loftman shouted, pointing wildly towards the bashed white automobile sitting in front of the lonely garage. “There! Stop!” The Mercedes slid to a halt on the damp surface and four of it's five doors immediately swung open. Lex pulled his shotgun from under his seat and lead the charge towards the open door. Forgoing military precision, the four men poured through the door like water through a funnel. They cast they frenzied gaze across the large, single-room building but nothing stirred, not even a cockroach. Sweating, Lex looked around carefully. His colleagues searched cupboards that weren't big enough to house one man (let alone two) as Nathaniel Loftman slid into the room in a panic. “Aidan! AIDAN!” he bawled at the top of the lungs. His voice reverberated around the building's concrete shell but the response never came. “Steve, check the car. Marlon, check out back. Make sure this fuck don't get away,” Lex said as his troops nodded and made for the door. Loftman ran his hands through his dishevelled blonde locks and dropped to his knees. He pulled his jacket from around his shoulders and tossed it to the floor in frustration as his greatest failure became apparent. Aidan was gone, and Lord knows what Jason had done to him. It was then that Nathaniel spotted it. Small and grey, rectangular in shape. Bobbing face-up on the surface of the pool of water in the mechanic's pit. Aidan Chase's cell phone. * * * * * Loftman's roar was so loud that Jason Natas could hear clearly from one hundred yards away. Stood in the bushes, tried and out of breath, Jason stopped in his tracks and turned to face the garage. Through the foliage he thought he could make out the silhouettes of two figures; one searching the perimeter and another ransacking his abandoned car. He knew exactly who they were, and he knew exactly who'd sent them. Jason may have driven Aidan Chase from his life, but knew then that a far more dangerous entity now threatened him.
