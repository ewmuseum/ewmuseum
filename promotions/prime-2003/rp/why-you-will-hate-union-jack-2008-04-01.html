Why you will HATE Union Jack

1 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 01 April 2008 His name is Jack Wilberforce and the only word that can truly describe him is, fantastic. No if’s, no but’s -- he’s the man. Everything he touches turns to gold, every girl his eyes callously latch upon immediately fall head over heels in love with him and everything he attempts, he accomplishes with casual ease. But that isn’t why you’re going to hate him. You’re going to hate him because in addition to all of his talents, attributes and charms, the bastard is also richer than the bloody Queen. And what’s more, the jammy bastard didn’t even earn a single penny of his fortune. Like all idiotic rich people whose only accomplishment in life is to be rich (see: Paris Hilton/Nicole Richie/The Prince of Wales), he was born into his considerable fortune. He will tell anyone who asks that he is born of aristocratic blood; he’ll claim his long dead mother to be the second cousin of the Duchess of York and most won’t even bother to check these details or to even pose a doubt over such a notion. But be fair; Jack is very much an idiot and he’s frustratingly rich, who in this wide, wide world isn’t going to believe he’s truly of royal descent? The truth of the matter is, his father made the vast family fortune as a mere tie salesman. By the age of twenty-five Jack’s father, Jeffrey Wilberforce, was selling ties on the street corner, just by Victoria Street Railway station, making a sideline in scalping concert tickets with a rather suspect man called Leroy at the Manchester Evening News Arena. By the age of twenty-seven, Jeffrey caught a lucky break and made enough money to open a small stall in the high street rather promiscuously entitled “Tie Me Up!” (most people entered the establishment believing it to be some kind of kinky sex shop. They were so embarrassed, most of them bought something to save face). By the age of thirty he had a chain of shops throughout the United Kingdom. By the age of forty he was dead and his ungrateful son had already cashed in on his fortune. Jack promptly sold his father’s tie empire only week after Jeffrey died in a freak badger related incident the likes of which have never been seen before or since and that was two days before the funeral, which, unfortunately for his family, Jack was unwilling to attend. Such a tale of arrogance, denial, betrayal and selfishness paints a rather quaint picture of the man known to the world today as Union Jack, but you would be an idiot to let any of this deceive you. Despite his many, many flaws he is a very capable man and what he lacks in skill, he more than makes up for in luck; anybody who has ever succeeded in this world will no doubt tell you - if asked - that this is the perfect combination to have. As previously alluded to, he has excelled at everything he’s ever attempted and that certainly does not start and end with the rather colourful world of wrestling. With a focus of conquering the all known barriers of class, thereby breaking into the realm of aristocracy, Jack’s first interest naturally led him to the so-called ‘gentlman’s sport’, Polo. Charging around atop of horses in an arena of sport and competition as if they were medieval jousters desperately trying garner the affections of their king. However, Polo is too civilised a sport to warrant such needlessly violent connotations and perhaps this is why Jack got bored of it so very easily. That’s when he turned to fencing, the sport what Jack would letter describe, a tad chauvinistically and perhaps entirely bitterly, as “The ancient art of a sword fighting duel, only womanised…” When he vacated this venture for pastures new, he remained undefeated at a professional level of the sport, much to the chagrin of the true blue blood aristocracy and successful men of industry who had rejected him as one of their peers. Then, one day, whilst drinking Irn Bru and fooling around with his girlfriend of the hour on the sofa, Jack noticed the world of professional wrestling and for the very first time gave the profession some serious thought. He’d been bereft of a hobby for a number of months (besides chasing girls and punching homeless people in the face, of course) and was getting the need to occupy his time and his mind without productive, yet entertaining and there was nothing more entertaining to him that extreme violence and inflicting pain. The wrestling industry was something of a step backwards from his pursuit of forcing his way into the blue blood of aristocracy and the elite of the upper-class fools, but by that time, he was more than a little disillusioned with the world of fine wines and expensive caviar. He promptly studied the industry and quickly became engrossed to the point of having a library in the east wing of his estate entirely dedicated to wrestling DVD’s from all over the world, past, present and very much the future. It wasn’t long after that, he decided that it was to be his next conquered hobby. But that is not to say he doesn’t respect the business. Quite the opposite, he respects so much he spends valuable time learning its secrets, mastering its tricks and most of all playing dirty. True enough, someday I have no doubts that he will conquer the wrestling industry, but nobody ever said he was going to do without vigorously cheating and being a complete cock vest along the way. This is Jack Wilberforce. This is Union Jack. This is the future of PRIME. Bow down to your Sovereign. From the journals of Richard Whittingham III (dated three days before making his PRIME on camera debut) ADDITIONAL: The SHITHOUSE legally changed my name to ‘Jeeves’ through the courts!
