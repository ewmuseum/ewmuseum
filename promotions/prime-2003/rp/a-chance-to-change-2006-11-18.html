A chance to change

18 November 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 18th 2006 Yeah. It’s him again. The man known to PRIME as N. Why the letter N, only he knows. Why the campaign for fair play in PRIME, though, is far more obvious. Once again, the man seats himself at a desk, identity concealed by the Hallowe’en mask upon his face, no trace of hair hanging loose from the wide-brimmed black hat and his build disguised by the cloak hanging over his body. Not even a trace of skin shown, the last bit of it covered up by black leather gloves. And behind him? A large screen, depicting in digital eternity, the image of Hoyt Williams striking out with his hidden weapon from Revolution 111. "Fans… observers… journalists… lend me your ears. For what you see right now, behind me is a vision of a possible future. It was a few years ago that nobody knew of Hoyt Williams. A veritable unknown in this sport and for a particular time, nothing more than a shadow of The Malice Man, his own father. Yet at some point, the same could be said of Duke himself - a commentator sitting behind a desk very much like this one, spending more time outside the ring than in. But Duke was not one who hid behind the microphone when the bell rang and his name was called. Nor did he shamefully hide behind a concealed weapon… and while he was perfectly willing to send his opponents Whiskey Bent And Hell Bound, it would seem that in the case of his son, the bottle pours VERY far from the shot glass." At the end of the sentence, he rose to his feet, slamming a fist down onto the desk. "But this is only one possible future. Thanks to his cheating, Hoyt will now advance to the next round of the Jewel In The Crown. If allowed to continue, he will no doubt either pull the wool over the eyes of his next opponent, unless HE cheats better than Hoyt. And for this, he will be awarded a hastily-reconstructed trophy, one kept under lock and key as though nothing had ever happened. So I leave it to more trustworthy people to do something about this. In that very match, there was a referee unable to detect the checkmate, commentators who refuse to point it out and cameramen who saw it as it happened. But I know what I saw and heard. I know and so did you." The camera, presumably operated by someone aiding the so-called terrorist, slowly closes in on the bone-white face. "What you saw was a man making a mockery of the sport and what you heard was a three count given unfairly to him. And what I heard… was a thousand, then two thousand, then four thousand, then sixteen thousand… and the numbers will carry on… on their feet, booing NOT because of simple choice of their most popular wrestler, but because he obviously lacked the things that this sport can call the very foundation it was build upon. Strength. Speed. Skill. Talent. Because ultimately, that’s what you call came to see… isn’t it? But the are security guards and barriers, as well as plain old common sense telling you that rushing to the ring to prevent such a victory is pointless. In the end, your interference would be robbing the cheated man of his own chance to prove he has the gifts to win on his own. But there is one thing you can all do. And that… is this." Opening the drawer of his desk, N pulls out a plastic clear bag and raises it. Beneath the shiny surface, an assortment of black cloth, folded and fresh, sits behind a mask just like his - the hollow-looking red eyes and almost caricatured grinning face leave no distinguishing mark as to who the wearer might be. And all too soon, it become apparent why N would be holding such an item aloft. "Beginning next week, these masks and costumes, whether Chet Worth likes it or not, will be distributed among the audience. So if you too believe that what you just saw in the ring was not just a man being cheated of his opportunity to show his abilities, but yourself being cheated of the money you earned and spent on a ticket or Pay-Per-View to show your support and appreciation of this company, then show everyone around you; the wrestlers, the other members of the crowd, the cameras, ANYONE who is watching you are just like me and yearn for a better match… Until next time." The broadcast ends with the now-familiar stylised N, surrounded by four borders representing a wrestling ring. It seemed N has designs on not only the Jewel In The Crown, but PRIME as a whole, if not the entire wrestling community itself. Only time would tell if the fans, like him, felt the same way about the direction the sport was going. And if they did… how would Chet respond to seeing N’s face replacing those of all the fans who kept his company running?
