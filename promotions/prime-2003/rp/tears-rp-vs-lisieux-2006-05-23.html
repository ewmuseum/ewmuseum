Date: 5/23/06 Trying hardto beat RichTard -me May Twenty-something, 2006… Morning Kicked back on a sofa in a hotel room, in front of a TV serving as nothing more than background noise, can be an ideal situation for thinking. But lately, his thinking had come in jolted, disjointed and hazy form. He wasn’t sure how long it had been since he had discovered that Killean had paid a visit to the home of his in-laws, specifically to talk with his wife. He couldn’t remember how many hours he’d sat around the house, letting the thought seep into his brain and marinade his mind. He couldn’t remember what day it had been that he’d bumped into Anne at the store…didn’t know what today was. And the only thing that could survive in the void between two such ambiguous endpoints… was confusion. That’s what the past however many days had been…confusing. His first reaction had been one of anger, prompting Matt to promise Anne that he would pummel Killean. When he had returned home from the store, however, his second wave of contemplation had been one of disbelief, causing a guttural laugh to erupt from his insides. It had been a rather sudden change. Still fuming as he had walked into his house, he had slammed the door shut behind him with such great force, that it bounced right back open, not catching in the latch as it was intended to. Immediately, his rage had carried him in quick, precise steps towards the refrigerator to tuck away the ground beef he’d just purchased for safe keeping. As the door swung away and the frigid air had escaped, it seemed to cool his thoughts and instantly, as he tossed the beef into an empty spot on the middle shelf, the absurdity of the situation had ignited in his mind. For some reason, Killean had thought it a good idea to show up at the door and greet Mary with a ‘hello’ just two months after beating her husband savagely up and down the corridors at a ReVolution… something Mary had witnessed first hand. And yet, it had occurred to Killean that a trip to visit her as she stayed with her parents was the right kinda move to make. That thought alone had been enough to cause Matt to lean back against the fridge door he had half-heartedly swung shut, closing it mostly with the weight of his frame as a heavy chuckle took control. Anne had always been a feisty, overtly opinionated individual. She was the type of person who spoke what was on her mind and didn’t pull any punches… literally, or so it seemed. Even now, as he sunk into the cushioning of the sofa in his hotel room, listening to the hum of the television mix with the bustle of life outside his window, his mind played out the scenario, how it might have gone down, in its own humorous version. Anne walked into the house, and dropped a sack of groceries on the countertop. What she saw when she turned to joke with her sister about having to do all the shopping, she was greeted by the sight of a very unwelcome guest. Across from her sister at the dining room table, sat Killean Sirrajin… the bastard that had attacked her brother-in-law in a fashion that had churned her stomach as she watched it unfold on television. ”Who let that son of a bitch into our home?!” Mary tried to say something in defense of Killean’s presence, but Anne was in no way interested in the specifics. ”You better find your way out the front door and find it fast. I’m about two seconds from beating you the way you did my brother-in-law! Not one to risk being considered a liar, Anne moved immediately towards Killean to make good on her promise in two seconds, if need be the case. Too stubborn and cocky to let some ‘bitch’ put him in his place, The Supreme Machine just held his spot at the table. Fine, your choice, asshole. With one quick, powerful movement, Anne drew her right hand back, then whipped it forward in a motion that blurred its surroundings. Her palm cracked across his left cheek, stinging as much in the form of physical pain as in embarrassment. But, the momentum of her slap didn’t stop with sting. It carried enough force to send Killean toppling over backwards in his chair, causing him to crash face first on the carpet, leaving a red burn on his right cheek to match the cherry colored palm print on his left. By the time his mind finished playing its version of the scene, he could feel the moisture following the contours of his cheeks. The image of the massive warrior flipping over backwards in his chair from the force of a slap from all one-hundred and thirty pounds of Anne… it was enough to make him cry. They had formed in the corner of his eyes from the absurd events of a February afternoon. They were born of humor. They were tears of laughter. * * * May Twenty-something, 2006… Noon Mary stretched to the left, then to the right, making sure that she was ready to go for her daily jog. She adjusted the iPod strapped to her left arm and set it to the ‘Hard Rock’ play list, then hopped down off the porch. She hated to jog without music…in fact, once, having misplaced her iPod, she just decided to stay home and do a crossword puzzle. Without music, she simply couldn’t stand doing something she otherwise enjoyed. It wasn’t just that it helped her keep a steady rhythm… it helped, at least in theory, to keep her mind from wandering while she jogged. And lately, preventing her mind from wandering had been a nearly impossible feat. It was her hope, that the music would at least delay the onset of rapid fire thoughts. Mary turned to her right and slowly morphed her walk to a slow jog, then to a full-fledged pace by the time she reached the intersection. Debating which way she wanted to go on this particular day, she decided to round the corner and head towards downtown. For the first ten, maybe fifteen minutes of her jog… she wasn’t sure, she knew only that it was three full songs, that’s how she kept time when running… Mary was able to stay focused and soak in the sounds of the music. The focus, however, had no signs of permanence, and after three songs, her mind slowly began to bounce around. Initially, she thought of a series of mundane things. Georgie had been particularly cranky this morning. Her baby girl had only finally fallen asleep five minutes before Mary started her jog. Mary’s father offered to watch his granddaughter, but Mary hated leaving him alone with a screaming baby. Oh well, they say that really cranky one’s are a sign of intelligence. They know when something’s not right, and they know screaming can fix it. What a little problem solver she is. Georgie’s antics reminded Mary that she needed to buy more baby food for Georgie. They were also almost out of diapers. This spawned the thought that she needed to get more money out of the ATM, which led her to the thought that her next check from the publisher should be deposited into her account soon. Baby food to pay check, Mary thought with a smile. Matthew had always made fun of her thought pattern. One day in the summer after their high school graduation, while driving in his car, she had erupted in elated fashion out of the blue. “Hey I’m taking a love seat to college!” The jump from the end of their previous conversation to her exclamations about the love seat had made perfect sense in her mind, even if she couldn’t remember how she had made the connection. Matthew had since called her occasional off the wall remarks “love seat comments.” He’s right, you do have an unusual thought pattern. Mary shook her head, No, you are not going to think about him this entire jog too. As she neared the edge of the neighborhood, Mary saw a V-Rod roar down the street past her. She hadn’t ridden a motorcycle in years, not since before she and Matthew got married. It had in fact, been her ex-fiancé Rob, who made her give it up. “I won’t have any wife of mine driving around on one of those things!” Thank God I left that man. What an ass. It hadn’t been his chauvinism that had been the deciding factor however. It had been the giant bruise under her right eye, something she had “earned” after Rob’s “hard day of work.” There were many things Mary could tolerate, abuse was not one of them. Even if that abuse is inflicted not upon her, but himself? Mary shook her head. If Matthew wants to kill himself, he can go right on ahead. It occurred to her, that she had once again found herself on the subject of her husband. From motorcycles, to Rob, to Matthew. She knew she didn’t mean it, didn’t mean that he could go ahead and kill himself if he wanted to, but it was the only way that she felt that she could cope with it. The thing of it was, she knew where the anger came from. She jogged largely in part to work out her frustrations. In college, she had also boxed some. It was all with the proper protective gear, but she still knew the satisfaction of knocking someone out. Especially if that someone had it coming. Unconsciously, her right eyebrow went up. Anne had stopped to see her earlier in the day. They had talked for about five minutes before Mary knew Anne had done something wrong. It took a few minutes of finessing before Anne told her she’d seen Matthew. Mary shrugged. Frankly, she felt Killean had it coming. She’d been very impressed by their initial conversation. It wasn’t until after he left that she heard what he’d called Anne. Mary knew that Anne had been out of line, but she took serious offense to people insulting her family. I can call my sister whatever I like, but if you even hint at it, I’ll knock your head off. Turns out that she wouldn’t have to, her husband would take care of it. The funny thing of it was, initially, Mary didn’t want Matthew to know about the visit, because she didn’t want him to hurt Killean. Later, after she found out about the exchange Sirrajin had with Anne, she wasn’t so concerned with Killean’s well being. As for Matthew, she just wanted to hear the sound of his voice, even if he was calling simply because of what had happened with Killean. She cared little about the intentions behind the call, she just wanted to hear his voice. And not when he was talking someone down on TV, she wanted to hear the voice he used just for her. Mary shook her head. She absentmindedly looked both ways before jogging across the street. Her feet were just carrying her now; she wasn’t paying any attention to where they were leading. She just couldn’t understand Matthew. Why doesn’t he just call? She took in a deep breath, then breathed out slowly. Why don’t you call him? She knew the answer. She’d picked up the phone to call him at least ten times. Each time she had hung up quickly. Once, she actually let it ring, but she hung up before anyone answered. Mary had hoped that if Matthew saw her parents number on the Caller ID, that he’d call back. He didn’t. Maybe he’s too angry. Maybe he’s too proud. Maybe he doesn’t care anymore. Maybe he’s thinking about getting a divorce. Maybe he’s seeing someone new, someone younger and prettier. Maybe … the truth was that she didn’t know. She didn’t know what was going on in his mind. She barely knew what was going on in her own. It was clear she was confused, and hurting, and frustrated and the truth was, that there was a part of her that just wanted to cry… but she didn’t. There were no tears. Mary didn’t cry. She just ran. May Twenty-something, 2006… Afternoon The air was warm and even tasted of salt, though the view from the balcony of his hotel room offered no sight of the ocean. From four stories above the earth, he watched the life below him unfold with cynicism. An older man rode his bicycle along the walk, wincing in what was probably arthritic pain, too stubborn to hang up the hobby and resign himself to a relaxing recliner in front of a TV. Too proud for his own damn good. The thought was fleeting, pushed from his mind by the shield of subconscious that wouldn’t allow him to suffocate in the rather apparent hypocrisy. A young couple, twenty was pushing it, were tucked under the shade of large tree. The boy pushed his back against bark, bracing himself against the base of the trunk. The young girl sat between his legs, leaning her weight into him, turning her head the slightest bit and burying her face in his shoulder. With a smile, the boy leaned down and kissed her on the top of the head, both of them laughing as a few strands of her black hair caught in his mouth. On the balcony, Matt leaned forward, bracing his weight by wrapping his palms firmly around the pristine white railing. While he watched the teenagers laugh and love, he thought back to the first time he had told Mary that he loved her. In a similar setting, at a similar age, the two had sought relief from the burn of Ohio Julys in the shade of a large tree… an Oak, as he recalled it. The exact type of tree may have been uncertain, but the rest of that evening was quite clear in his mind, despite the years that had past, threatening to fade its color. July 2nd, 1996 Matthew grabbed Mary by the hand and pulled her towards the oak tree in the back corner of the park. They had been in search of a cool and quiet afternoon to shatter the high speed blur that had sent the summer rushing by. Finding the quietness they had desired was relatively easy. Without the pool, without the basketball courts, without the new, state of the art playground equipment, Moat’s Park was a wasteland to the jubilant youth. They preferred all the glitz and glamour of Fountain Park on the north side of town. Moat’s park offered only a couple of old tennis courts and the chance to enjoy Mother Nature, but at 93 degrees, that enjoyment could only truly be obtained from the sanctuary of shade. The strength of his tug pulled her off balance, causing her to fall towards him, sending them both tumbling to the ground. As they crashed, they both laughed and blamed one another for spill. He scooted himself across the grass, warm to the touch even in the shade, and pushed his back against the trunk of the tree. She followed suit, wiggling between his legs, leaning her back into his chest, pulling his arms around her, as if grabbing restraints and locking herself in for a rollercoaster ride. “This is a little better.” Matthew playfully squeezed her with his arms. “Whatcha think?” “I think its almost ninety degrees and that’s in the shade. It’s been ridiculously hot this past week! Eighty degree… I mean… that’s alright. But ninety? That sucks. Just makes you sweaty and smelly.” She scrunched up her nose and narrowed her eyes to mimic the universal reaction to such unpleasant odors, and though he couldn’t see her face, he knew exactly what she was doing. “Are you saying I smell?! Oh yeah… well… you smell!” “Shuddup! You know I didn’t mean that, dork. I was just sayin’… ninety is too hot. That’s too warm to really be able to go outdoors and enjoy, ya know?” She tilted her head backwards hoping to his eyes showing comprehension. “Well, its supposed to cool off after the Fourth. So, shouldn’t be more than another couple days of this.” For a moment he paused, realizing that something he had said triggered another thought. Once he figured out what it was, he spoke again. “Speaking of the Fourth, you want to go to Fountain Park and watch the fireworks? My family is going down, like always… just thought you might like to come along, keep each other company.” “I can’t. Already promised Kenzie that I’d go to Columbus with her to watch the fireworks there. Her and I haven’t really hung out at all this summer, so when she told me she was headin’ over to Columbus to catch the fireworks with a couple of her roomies from college… I felt like I’d be a real lousy friend if I said ‘no thanks’.” Again, she tilted her gaze backward, this time though, it was what she was hoping not to see in his eyes… disappointment. No such luck. “It’s ok. No big deal.” His words said that he was fine, but she knew better than that. If his eyes weren’t enough, the tone of his voice sounded like it was wedged somewhere between heartbroken and angry. And he probably had a right to be. A month and a half earlier, when they had finished up finals week at college, a conversation with Matt had made a random turn towards Independence Day, and though no formal plans had been set, she had made it clear how much she would like to head to the park with Matthew and his family to watch the fireworks. But as the summer passed and Mary found herself spending next to no time with Kenzie, a childhood friend, she just couldn’t bring herself to decline the invitation to Columbus. “I’m sorry” was all she said. Don’t worry about it, Mary. It’s nothin’ major.” His frustration sounded through, but he was determined not to let it guilt her. “Yes it is, Matt. You must hate me.” “No, I don’t. Mary, you know I don’t hate you.” “I know” She turned her head a bit and buried it in his shoulder “You know I love you, right?” The comment caused her to immediately pull her head from off his shoulder, despite the mere fraction of a second it had been there. “Matt, you do not.” “Yes I do. “No you don’t. How could you even know that? We’ve never even kissed.” “I don’t have to kiss you to know that I love you.” “Yes you do.” She let it be at that, and rested her head back against his shoulder. For a couple minutes, they just sat in silence. Mary quickly forgot about the exchange, focusing her attention on blocking out the heat. Matt let the sun mix with his frustration, and together they burned him. What right did she have to tell him how he felt? Wasn’t the closeness they shared enough to make it clear? Her dismissal churned his insides with its simple nature. “Shit!” Mary jumped up suddenly and looked at her watch. It’s 4:05! I promised Mom I’d be home by four so we could get an early dinner and catch a five o’clock movie at the mall! I gotta go, babe!” She turned and started to jog across the lawn towards her house. “I’ll give you a call tomorrow!” As she shrunk into the horizon, his emotions got the best of him. There, under the shade of a large oak tree, in a barren park, he cried. They were tears of anger * * * May Twenty-something, 2006… Evening A simple ‘ding’ signified the elevator’s arrival to the seventh floor. Matthew stepped off, hands clutched in fists, the phrase “Seven Twenty-Six” grinding through his clenched teeth. The earlier flashback to the afternoon from his college years had left a bad taste in his mouth, worse than even the salt. For over an hour, it had swirled in his mind, playing back over and over. And, as the anger from that past afternoon seeped into the current evening… the thoughts of Mary led him to recall Killean’s visit. This time, it didn’t amuse him at all. What it did do, however, was send him to the front desk in order to acquire the room number of his nemesis, who he knew was staying in the same hotel for ReVolution 94. Now, possessing the information he had desired, he marched down the elegant corridors of the seventh floor, not knowing exactly what he planned to do once he knocked on Killean’s door. Still, the uncertainty did nothing to slow his steps, each one placed with precision. Around a corner, Matt found ‘726’ on his right, two doors from the end of the hall. Instintively, he slammed his fist against the door three times. From the other side, he could hear the rustling of someone moving across the room. The words, “just a sec” faintly forced their way through the heavy door. As soon as he heard the handle turn, Matt readied himself, and when the door opened… he pounced. For all he knew, he could have been assaulting a stranger. There was no hesitation to assure that it was in fact Killean who opened the door. As soon as he saw the light from the window in the room, Matt charged forward. Had it been Charity who opened the door, it would have been her being shoved violently across the floor. But it had been Killean who greeted his arrival, and it was Killean who found himself being driven back, the collar of his shirt firmly in the grasp of Matt’s hands. The two men came to a sudden stop, courtesy of the wall on the far side of the room. “Stay away from my wife.” He said it in an eerily subdued tone that gave no impression of the anger that had carried him to Killean’s room. “Stay… away.” Killean still seemed to be in a state of stock as the words escaped the mouth of his former friend. Finely, his senses fused with reality again, and Killean shoved his hands up between Matt’s arms, pushing them off of his chest. “What the hell’s your problem, man?” Killean straightened his collar, smoothing out his shirt while giving Matt a quizzical look. “Two months ago… you know what I’m talking about. Don’t play fuckin stupid. You know damn well what I’m talking about!” Again, Matt reached up and grabbed Killean’s shirt, slamming him forcefully against the wall. “I just stopped by to apologize for what she witnessed and try to make amends.” “You don’t have any business showing up at my house to talk to my wife!” Matt pulled Killean away from the wall, only so he could shove him right back against the cool surface. This time, Killean had had enough. Again, he shot his arms up between Matt’s grasp, but this time, he grabbed two fistfuls of shirt himself, turning and driving his fellow employee into a mirror on the adjacent wall. Several large cracks crawled across the surface of the mirror. “It wasn’t your house… and you’ve got to be some sort of husband for her to qualify as your wife!” Killean’s words were filled with vile hatred, but he quickly took a deep breath and released his grasp, perhaps from sympathy, not wanting to have another violent incident to apologize for to Mary. Killean’s grip relaxed and Tchu slid away from the wall, several pieces of the mirror, held in place only by the pressure of his torso, fell to the ground, cutting into the carpet. “How I handle things with Mary is between her and I.” “C’mon, Matt. There’s nothing between you and Mary. There hasn’t been for months.” “It’s complicated.” It was discomforting how civil each man’s tone had become. “Not all that much. What’s really the matter, Matt… the fact that I went and apologized to your wife… or the that its been five months since you screwed things up and you haven’t done even that much?” Matt closed his eyes, not wanting to search for the answer to that question. He ran his fingers through his hair, then did so again, this time pulling with more force as they slid back. “Look…” Killean headed across the room, stepping over the broken fragments of mirror, making his way towards the doorway. “You want to hate me for taking up Nelson’s offer… fine. You want to hate me for what goes down between us when we step between those ropes… go ahead. You want to hate me for the Underground… that’s fair. But don’t come at me like some sort of bitter idiot because I tried to apologize to your wife for what had happened between old friends. Hate yourself for how things are between you and your wife.” Killean stepped out of the room, then looked back. “I’m supposed to meet Charity for dinner at the restaurant across the street. Go ahead and trash the room if you want… PRIME will pick up the bill.” He disappeared down the hallway, leaving Tchu alone in the damaged room. For a moment, he let the words of his former friend bounce around inside his skull. Then, everything that had been said between the two, everything that had been said between he and Mary… everything that hadn’t been said… it all infested his insides. As his head sunk downwards, taking his gaze with it, he saw several small pieces of himself reflecting back at him. It was only a fractured glimpse, but in one shard of mirror, he saw several drops of moisture fall from the corner of an eye. They were from the past ten years. They were tears of sorrow and anger and everything.
