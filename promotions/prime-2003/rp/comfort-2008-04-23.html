Comfort

23 April 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/23/08 "The time will come when it will disgust you to look in the mirror." -Rose F. Kennedy April 21st, 2008 10:59 A.M. You spar. You just do. It’s one of those things that makes Sports Entertainers cringe, but sets the veins of Wrestlers alight, blood dashing happily to fingers and toes. For anybody who joined PRIME because it’s fun to rub shoulders with Nova or because they saw the number of chicks outside Tyler Rayne’s locker room, sparring doesn’t mean anything. But for someone like me, someone who wants to win the big matches more than he wants to live past middle age…well, you spar. Sitting on the floor, I admired the fighters on the walls. Posters adorned with inveterate names like Wrecking Ball Ingalls covered every surface, decades of dust removing not a speck of the menace in those eyes. I stretched silently, reaching for my left foot with my right hand. A week off, no matter the circumstances, had allowed me to hit the reset button and come to. That, and the bottle of Tylox tucked sheepishly under spare clothes in my gym bag. "Chan," a voice called, "you gonna stretch until the weekend or are we gonna fuckin’ hit something?" It was the proprietor of the gym, Arnie Baylor. Arnie was a barrel-chested towhead from the coast, tightly packing as much California affect as possible into carved, stout features. His claim to fame, as anyone who had ever walked through the door could tell you, was a near-miss at representing the United States in the 1988 Olympic Games in Seoul. With regularity, the story would end on a sour note, Arnie grimacing and reassuring his listener that, "Man, if I hadn’t missed my flight to qualifying, I woulda put Ray Mercer under six feet of Korean mud." "Arnie," I shot back, "you sure you don’t wanna check your blood pressure before we hop in there? Beating Johnny Noble was one thing, but if I don’t know that ‘The Geriatric Slayer’ works well on a t-shirt." In truth, Arnie was only forty-six, but years don’t treat heavyweight boxers quite the same as the rest of us. His shoulders sagged with the weight of carrying two massive, artillery arms. But when he laughed–now he was laughing loudly–his great tidal wave of a chest pumped up and out, demonstrating just how fearsome he must have once been. I started the compulsive process of taping my wrists. It was in a gym like this, years before, that I had learned the sacred art of small-scale body fixes. I couldn’t help thinking about my new fix, and wondering what the leather-faced regulars would vilify me for an inability to cope with the pain. I imagined that I might incur an extra round of insults, more copper-tongued jeers. But secretly, would my bottle of "get out of pain free" cards invite quiet grumblings? I would rather find a new gym than be cast off with the guys who beat their wives and the hardcore drug users. The gym is not a moral authority, but de facto separation exists between the good guys and the bad guys. The faces and the heels. "Hey limpwrist, hurry the hell up!" Arnie was inside the ropes, shadowboxing and waiting for me. "So you can break my wrist in front of all these people and go shooting off your mouth about how you should be the one in PRIME?" Like any red-blooded man who spends time physically improving himself, Arnie was proud. Being able to hang with the (relatively) young bucks kept him from complete middle-aged melancholy. "Not a chance, Baylor." I wrapped my wrist too quickly, threading crooked, snaking black lines. I hopped up off my ass and caught a potent whiff of gym funk. This place was a 1930’s relic, an old do-it-all dancehall/speakeasy/casino. At some point, the money stopped coming in and the walls started crumbling. In its waning years, someone had the idea to fix one or two walls and let people beat the hell out of each other within. And here we were, years later, a thriving (if pungent) gym. "You ready to get knocked off your arthritis meds?" I hopped over the top rope, chest surging with the rush that precedes any fight. Arnie picked up his big Thai pad to chest level. "Do your worst, princess." And I did, swiping a left hook into the pad and then spinning with a right elbow. Each movement was accompanied by a grunt, as Arnie pushed back with the large foam-covered pad. Carefully placed blows met their match, the knowledgeable resistance of my pseudo-sensei. In a moment that seemed to be outside of invincibility, I jumped and spun one hundred eighty degrees for a crescent kick, but something stalled. The gears crunched and I dropped out of the air, falling awkwardly onto my side. I didn’t even get off a shriek. "Chan, Jesus, you alright?" I huffed in response, my back still heaving at my air supply. Hiccups of breath made me sound so weak, so unfit to be back in the ring. "Chan, say somethin’!" Arnie had watched Culture Shock and everything since. He saw my reticence to go flying, but he probably figured it was more about a reassessment of ring style than injury. After all, wouldn’t you switch things up if tried-and-true had come up short in the big time? Finally able to take a breath, I slid backwards on my ass towards the ropes. Arnie just watched, like a spectator at the Coliseum, wondering if he was watching a wild animal in its throes of death. I propped my back against the ropes, finally letting out a scratchy groan. "Fuck, get…some ice," I urged. "The fuck just happened there, Mr. Jewel in the Crown?" Arnie no longer wore his trademark smirk, despite the playful tone. "I pulled my back, man." That didn’t seem to answer his question. His eyes stayed trained on me, in this sad state. "Not a big deal." "Horseshit." It was a harsh word, digging and cutting. When Arnie got in your face, you listened. That was one of the gym’s cardinal rules. "The fuck’s the matter with you, comin’ into my gym all beat up and not tellin’ anyone?’ He shook his head. Another stone-clad rule disobeyed, the star pupil failing miserably in his apprenticeship. "I swear, this is the first-" "Chan, you’re makin’ yourself look like an ass." " I wanna fuckin’ spar, Arnie. You’re gonna turn away an old friend because my back’s held together with popsicle sticks and Elmer’s?" I hated to play off of what had always been unwavering support from Arnie, but I needed a proving ground. I couldn’t risk a PRIME employee seeing me in such a bad way. "You know that I can’t have you here, in good conscience." My heart curdled for a second. "It’s a pain on you and me." I wanted to revert to the old self-involved Chandler and accuse Arnie of knowing nothing about pain. But he was almost twenty years ahead of me on the curve. "There are plenty of people taking a flyer on me right now. You’d just be another accomplice; don’t flatter yourself." The voice I heard out of my mouth was serpentine and cold; maybe Arnie could see the shame I was carrying. "Maybe," he started, before a long pause, "…one of those people should give a fuck about what you to do to yourself." Someone else had to be my own self-sustaining voice of reason, an irony lost on both of us at the time. "I don’t, so why should they?" In another life, I would have broken down to Arnie and explained how in trying to regain honor and a sense of pride, I was losing myself to a fierce competitive drive. Kinda like how everyone reveres Michael Jordan as this deity of basketball, when the truth was really that he was a prick. To be so over-the-top competitive separates you from some of that humanity. I was Darwin’s pet, man returned to primordial pursuits (you can imagine early hominids in perfect hammerlocks over scraps of mastodon). "Chan, I’ve see your tough times. I’m tellin’ you, whatever you think makes this suicide worth it–you’re wrong. I’ve wanted that before, to let everything else implode for a real shot at bein’ something." Arnie knew what he was talking about; I might as well have been looking at my own worst-case future. "Arnie, I don’t need your patronizing. Let me train so I can do what I was meant to do." What I meant to do: finally win the Universal Title and be the best, rather then talk about being the best. Even that scumbag Silver had proved himself to be the best at one time. The almost-Olympic boxer took a long look at me. Someday, I would tell him that this couldn’t happen in the ring. The Tylox brought back all my ring skills, all the thing that I would never have been able to do if I were in constant pain. But Thursday through Tuesday would be torture for me; this is what he had to weigh on his soul. "Fine, but you’re not gonna flop all over my ring. Only stay in here if you can hang." Two more Tylox on Monday. It wasn’t addiction; it was getting up to stay functional. And it was easier than Arnie kicking my ass out of his gym. I jogged over to my bag and picked up the bottle, my own shadow keeping the secret. If I had thought there was another way, I would have tried to find it. I promise I would have.
