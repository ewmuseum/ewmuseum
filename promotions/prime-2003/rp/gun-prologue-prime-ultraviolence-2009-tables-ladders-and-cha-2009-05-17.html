Gun. Prologue. (PRIME UltraViolence 2009, Tables, Ladders and Chairs Match vs. Killean Sirrajin)

17 May 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: May 17th, 2009 He trembled. His long, thin fingers shook as they lifted from his work desk. His bottom lip quivered under his black mustache, and his beady brown eyes started to glaze over, staring straight ahead, unable to lay eyes on what he had created. “Ellis?” His eyes snapped to his left at the quiet, concerned tone of the woman standing in the door. A short, modest woman in a blue dress, white apron wrapped around her waist. She leaned against the room's ratty door jamb, blue eyes wide, yet calm with concern for her husband. “Are you alright, Ellis?” she asked, a southern Kentucky twang in her diction. A pause. He was able to control himself a moment, closing his mouth to swallow while his fingers removed his circular bifocals, still twitching. “I fear...” he started, stopping to retrieve lost breath. “...I fear I have created something terrible.” His wife bit her lip, and turned her gaze to his messy work table. Among the clutter of small gears, springs, tiny screws and levers, sat two revolvers. Silver bodies shining bright against the dullness of the room. Grips so black they may as well have been forged from a shadow. Remarkable specimens, without flaw. Yet, so very, very wrong.. And as Ellis Sherman's wife drifted toward his seat and took his hand, he realized that there was no atonement for such a sin, and that the only fate awaiting him was that of a burning eternity. * * * * * * * Gun. Prologue * * * * * * * ”A gun ain't just a hunk've metal that makes a loud noise an' puts a hole in somethin' on the other side. When a gun gits made in a factory, or by some feller in a shop, they ain't makin' a weapon. They ain't makin' somethin' that can put the lights out on some other cunt. They're makin' a gateway. A way out. An' I ain't talkin' 'bout some kind've stress relief or any pansy shit like that. It ain't a way out fer the sonnuva bitch holdin' it, it's a way out fer somethin' a whole helluva lot worse. They're holdin' a gateway to Hell itself. Anyone whose shot a gun can tell ya 'bout a small rush they git when they pull the trigger, once the 'bang' hits their ears. Changes from person to person. Fer some, it makes em' smile, makes their heart pump. Fer some, it makes em' angry, makes their blood boil. Fer others, it makes em' scared, chills their bones. Whatever it is, it's the same god damn thing. That emotion ya feel, every single god-damn time ya pull the trigger, is a piece've the fuckin' Devil comin' out. I fuckin' love it. But not every gun'll bring 'im out like that. Every one's different, all depends on how its made, where its made, all sorts've shit. Shootin' paint cans with a B.B. gun ain't the same as blastin' holes in walls with a twelve gauge. Poppin' some cunt in the dome with a sniper rifle from a mile away ain't the same as sprayin' a crowd with a couple've Uzis. They come in all shapes an' fuckin' sizes, an' they come real nasty. But none've em' are so fuckin' bad, so god-damn terrible, as the Twins. That story starts with Ellis Sherman, the best god-damn gun-smith to walk the face of the Earth. Anyone who tells ya otherwise don't know their ass from their fuckin' elbow, an you can tell em' Ol' Wade said so. The little bastard takes pieces've steel an' turns em' into perfection. One day he made the Twins, an' unleashed Hell on Earth in the form've two identical .357 magnum revolvers. I've got one've em'. Let me tell ya 'bout it.” * * * * * * * “Who the fuck's Ellis Sherman?” Jefferson City, Missouri April, 1996 Alice Dawson cocked an eyebrow and shook her head as Wade Elliott set his heavy work boots on top of her desk, sand and dirt sprinkling from the treads. “He's a gun-smith,” she replied. “The best, they say.” “Yeah? Who's they?” asked Wade, incredulous and uninterested, steely blue eyes looking downward as he lit a Marlboro Red with a silver Zippo. “Anybody who knows anything about the market,” she said, smoothing out her black dress pants as she sat down in a black leather desk chair. She was a haggard woman. Middle age, but with features that would tell you otherwise. Tall, thin, you could even say attractive if you could look past her rough demeanor. She turned a pair of beady black eyes to the man across her desk. Rough incarnate. “Believe me,” she continued, a heavily smoke influenced rasp of a voice escaping her thin lips. “He's the best. Makes all sorts of guns, sells em' for a lot've stacks of bills, a helluva lot more than anybody ought to spend, but this is America n' all.” “Good ol' American Dream,” said Wade, spewing smoke through his bushy brown goatee. “Make a pile've dollars an' cents so yer free t'spend as much as ya like.” “That's the truth,” she said, running her spindling fingers through thin, black hair. “So what's the story?” asked Elliott, ashing on the floor and locking in a poker face. “Word on the street is that Mr. Sherman has went and made something...extraordinary. I'm not sure what, but those in the know are buzzing. Whatever it is, I want it.” “Never took ya fer one to pack heat, Alice,” grunted Wade. “Funny, but that's why I hire assholes like you, and I'm not the ornamental type. Something like this will be worth a lot of cash, so needless to say, I'd like to be the one to sell it.” Elliott heaved in another drag, blasting streams of toxins through his nose. “So what's the problem?” he asked, his already deep, gravely voice dropping a level. “He doesn't just up and sell something like that to a guy off the street,” she sighed, stressed. “Or even a big time collector. He's a fidgety little bastard, tucks those fancy projects away from greedy hands.” “Hands like yers.” “Hands like mine. Hands that make the money that end up in your filthy paws.” Wade spit on the floor. “He lives in Tennessee,” she continued, standing and striding toward a nearby window in the small, tidy office. “I'm not sure exactly where, but I'm sure that sniffer of yers can find out easy enough.” “I ain't gon' be happy if I end up on a god-damn goose chase, Dawson.” “Do your job, Wade,” she snapped, head whipping back to the seated roughneck. “I don't care how many geese you have to chase.” Wade's tongue flipped back in forth in his mouth as he shared a stare-down with his employer. He stubbed out his cigarette and removed his boots from her desk, standing slowly and nodding his head. “Be seein' ya then,” he said, swinging the office door open and slamming it shut with his exit. Alice kept her eyes on the door a few moments after he left, then turned back to her window, looking down to the parking lot to see her large, Alabama born employee trudging toward his ramshackle Ford Ranger, hopping in and roaring off toward the road. “Bastard,” she muttered, returning to her desk. * * * * * * * “Sticking out like a sore thumb” wouldn't come close to explain Wade's appearance at the Allure Nightclub in Louisville, Kentucky. He was more like Giraffe walking through a Wendy's drive-through and ordering a spicy chicken sandwich. He scowled at his situation. The music, some weird techno dance stuff, stung his eardrums. He could hardly hear himself think between that and the constant conversation, more like yelling, attempting to rise above the heavy bass and only making it worse. He tapped his thumb impatiently against the shiny black bar, until the very young, very blonde, very attractive bartender finally made her way to Wade's attention. She smiled, leaning in to hear his order. “Watcha need?!” she asked loudly, cute and bubbly. “Can I get a whiskey?!” Wade yelled, hoping to make his order clear by leaning close to her ear. “Jack Daniels?!” she asked to confirm. “Sure, that'll be fine!” he hollered. “Jack and Coke comin' up!” she shouted with a wink. “No! No!” he yelled, stopping her before she can make the beverage. “Just whiskey! Straight!” “Like, by itself?!” she asked, head tilting to the side. “Yeah, just...” Wade stopped, and decided to take the easy route. “Nevermind! Gimme a Rollin' Rock!” She nodded and bounced away, leaving Wade to shake his head and run a frustrated hand through his short, un-kept brown hair. He nodded as she returned, sliding the cold green bottle to his big hands, and took a long, long swig. “Well, you certainly came dressed the part,” came a voice to Elliott's left. Wade turned his head to see average looking fellow, clad in a basic black shirt with jeans, dirty blonde hair pulled back in a pony-tail, a small perma-smirk on a round face. “Fergot to shine my boots.” “That must be it,” laughed the man, taking a seat. The wave of a finger attracted the bartender's attention. “Dirty martini, three olives,” he asked with a wink. She returned the favor, and quickly poured the posh beverage. Wade shook his head and swilled from his Rolling Rock while the man spun in his barstool, resting his elbows against the bar to survey the crowd. “Got the info I asked fer?” Wade asked, setting his bottle down. “What's the rush, cowboy?” “I ain't in the mood fer shootin' back an' forth, Ted.” Ted nodded, sipping from his glass. “Fine, fine,” he said, sucking in air through his teeth to chase after the vodka's burn. “I got you an address.” He passed a folded piece of paper under the bar and into Wade's fingers discreetly. “An' ya sure this is the right spot?” asked Wade, quite serious. “That's the place. You know anything about this Sherman guy?” “A lil' bit. Gun-smith, apparently his hardware fetches a pretty penny.” “That's about it,” Ted confirmed. “Gonna do some fetching?” “Man's gotta make a livin'.” Ted nodded, then leaned in, his smirk fading to a more serious expression. “No need to run in guns blazin', Wade,” he said. “This Sherman guy's a meek little fella. Won't give ya any trouble.” “Fer his sake I hope yer right,” Elliott replied, finishing his beer and setting on the counter. He stood, retrieving his wallet and tossing out a ten dollar bill. “Thanks Ted, I owe ya one.” “Just don't do anything stupid,” he said. There wasn't a reply. He watched as Wade passed through the crowd like Moses through the Red Sea, offering as many nasty looks as he received. Teddy smirked, and ordered another drink. * * * * * * * Knoxville, Tennessee The remains of Wade's cigarette exploded against the pavement, a miniature firework in the late evening. He savored the burn in his lungs as he leaned against his truck, big arms folded across his chest. A small white house with a smaller, ramshackle shed-like addition lay in the cross-hairs of his eyes. He unfolded the piece of paper that Teddy gave him, then double checked the number on the mailbox. He nodded to himself, then released a cloud of smoke into the air. He strode toward the home, crushing the cigarette butt as he crossed the street. * * * * * * * “These need to be hidden away,” said Ellis Sherman to his wife, frantic and nervous as he bumbled around his shop. “Ellis, you're scaring me,” she said, folding her arms as if a cold chill had overtaken her. “These will not be sold,” he continued, pushing various odds and ends around on shelves, searching quickly. “They will be no gift, no object of profit. Their existence is known only to us, and it will stay that way.” She bit her lip, staring at the pair of .357 magnums on his work bench. She never had, and never would truly understand what was so frightful about his creation, but even should could tell that he was correct. Just laying eyes on them made her blood run cold. Ellis finally pulled a pair of wooden boxes from a top shelf and waddled back to his work bench. “They must be separated,” he said, taking one of the revolvers and placing it inside the box, closing the top and locking it with a small key lock. “To keep them together is to unleash Hell itself.” He brought the box to his wife, placing it in her reluctant hands. “Take this inside, I'll lock up the other and we'll decide where to bring them tomo...” Knock knock. Ellis froze, wide eyes turned toward the door. A moment passed. Two more knocks, each separate and deliberate. KNOCK. KNOCK. “Mr. Sherman? Ya've got a customer waitin' outside!” came a loud, gravely voice through the door. Ellis turned to his wife. “Go. Go!” he whispered, shooing her with his fingers. She nodded quickly, turning and running into the house, closing the door quietly behind. ”C'mon now, Ellis. I'll huff an' I'll puff an' I'll kick this god-damn door in!” Sherman's head whipped left and right, unsure of what to do. He jumped to his stool at his work bench, grabbing up random gadgets, pretending to be hard at work. “Ah, um, I'm sorry, I'm closed!” he shouted, eyes turned downward. BOOM. The force of the kick snapped the iron locking pin to the door, swinging the door open violently and smashing it against the wall. Ellis shook with fear at the ruckus, a large, rugged-framed silhouette standing in the doorway. “That ain't what we call 'customer service,' Ellis.” Wade stepped into the light nonchalantly, peering left and right at the mess that was Ellis Sherman's workshop. He drew his gaze back to center, to the small, petrified man at the bench. “Heard ya made somethin' real special, Ellis,” said Wade, stepping closer, pulling a pack of Marlboros from his pocket and popping a fresh one into his mouth. “Was wonderin' if I could take a look.” “W...well, if you're going to be so...forward...” stammered Sherman. ”...I suppose I could sh...show you some of my w...wares...” He stood, moving to his shelves and leafing through them. “What were you looking for? Rifles? Shotguns...” “How 'bout ya shut yer fuckin' mouth 'fore somethin' bad happens, Sherman.” Ellis did just that, the knuckles of his wiry fingers going white as they gripped a shelf. Wade started moving things around on shelves, poking his head into random boxes and piles of who knows what. “Ya made somethin' that's been stirrin' up the ol' shit-pile, Ellis,” Wade said. “I ain't here to do nothin' bad, but you can bet yer ass that I ain't afraid to if I don't leave here with what I want.” Sherman gulped with fear. “Wha...what is it that you want?” “See, yer the one's who's s'posed to tell me.” “I...I don't know what you're talking about.” Elliott rolled his eyes, curling his lips inward and breathing in a frustrated breath through his nose. “Ellis, yer makin' me lean toward doin' that bad thing I was talkin' 'bout.” “PLEASE! I..don't know what you want!” “S'pose yer arm bendin' at a funny angle will jog yer fuckin' memory!” roared Wade, taking a terrifying stomp forward. “NO! PLEASE!” begged Ellis, hands crossed above his head as a last ditch, useless defense. But Wade did stop. Sherman's beady black eyes were slammed shut, his body shaking as it waited for the pain to come. It never did however, and after many moments he opened one eye to see the large invader transfixed on his work bench. Wade's head tilted, locked on a round, silvery object poking from under a piece of sheet metal. He gently reached out, pushing the piece of metal to the side. His sharp blue eyes widened, mouth drifting agape. He might as well have discovered the Holy Grail. The .357 magnum resting on the work bench was a beacon of light amidst the piles of junk. A large revolver, flawless. A pitch black handle fading into the blinding shine of a silver body. His pupils followed a simple, yet elegant black cursive inlay. It read: I come into a region where is nothing that can give light. “Well now, Ellis, whatta ya have here...” Wade whispered. Elliott took the grip in his hand. It was heavy, yet alarmingly comfortable. More an extension of the fingers than a tool in his paw. A sudden burst of emotions shot from the weapon and through his arm. Hatred, respect, fear, power, aggression, and awe. He needed it. “Looks like I found what I was lookin' fer...” “NO!” bellowed Ellis, rushing the much larger Wade. The fire that ignited in Elliott's eyes was horrific. Through grit teeth he swung his left hand, the back of his knuckles clocking Sherman in the jaw and sending him spiraling to the cement floor, a pair of yellowed teeth clacking under the table. “Back the FUCK up!” barked Wade, a deep, unearthly growl joining his voice. Ellis clutched at his face, spitting up blood as Wade heaved, fire and brimstone fuming from his nostrils. “I'm takin' this,” he huffed, shoving the pistol in the back of his ragged jeans. He pulled out a silver Zippo and lit the cigarette that still hung from his lip, spewing smoke into the ceiling. “Pleasure doin' business with ya.” Sherman could only groan as Elliott thudded away, slamming the wooden door shut. And as the sound of Wade's Ford drifted away, the gun-smith, Ellis Sherman, could only weep. * * * * * * * Jefferson City, Missouri KNOCK. KNOCK. “Come in,” said Alice Dawson, closing a notebook and pushing a pencil away. The smell of whiskey and cigarettes filled the room as Wade entered. He wore a rare smirk on his face as he closed the door behind him. “Well?” she asked, simply. “Mission accomplished,” he said with a sinister tone. “Fantastic, let's have a look.” He ripped the magnum from behind his belt, tight in his grip as he rested the bead between Alice's eyes. She paused. “Enough fucking around, Elliott. Give me the gun.” “Here's a twist fer ya: no.” She squinted her eyes, glaring at the man in the doorway. He lowered the gun, and opened the chamber, spinning it, letting the ominous buzz fill her ears. “He made 'imself one helluva weapon, Alice,” started Wade, flicking his wrist and snapping the chamber back into place. “Feels mighty good in my grip.” “You're makin' a big fuckin' mistake, Elliott,” she hissed, hands planted on her desk. “Oh I am?” he replied, feigning confusion. “Last I checked I was the one holdin' the big fuckin' .357 in my paw.” “You listen to me, Wade. If you don't hand that gun over, you're not only a dead man, you're a fucking idiot!” “No, you listen to me you fuckin' cunt!” barked Wade, the hell-fire rising in his eyes. “I'm keepin' this piece, and yer gonna reach into that lock-box ya got tucked away in yer desk, put every fuckin' cent in my hand, an' we're both gonna go on our fuckin' merry. How's that sound?” “Go FUCK yourself!” she screamed. In a flash she snatched a pistol hiding under her desk, whipping it out with pure intent to drop the backstabber across the room. But the sound of a .357 magnum and its corresponding bullet putting a hole in your head tends to throw a wrench in your plans. Alice Dawson crumpled like a pile of dirty laundry as she hit the floor, a brain-matter and skull-fragment littered display left on the wall behind her. Smoke drifted from the magnum's barrel, the man behind it scowling angrily. He then lowered the gun, clicked on the safety and returned it to the back of his jeans. Wade walked behind the desk without so much as a glance at the nearly headless corpse on the floor, reaching into a drawer and retrieving a gray lock-box. “Sorry darlin',” he said, closing the drawer. “But I feel like me an' this here magnum are gon' have a successful career t'gether.” He stood, and headed for the door. “Maybe I'll name it after ya.” * * * * * * * ”That's the long an' short've it. Weren't til' 'round six months later I heard this gun had a twin. I might've been inclined to find it, but I was on the other side've the god-damn country at the time. But now, as it turns out, PRIME's headin' back towards the way of Tennessee after UltraViolence, so I think it's time fer me to pay another visit to ol' Ellis Sherman an' find Alice's twin. I can hear it callin'. It wants to be with its other half, in my other hand. So I'm answerin' the call. I'm comin' fer it. I want it. I want it bad.”
