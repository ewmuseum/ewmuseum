My Mind, on Other Things.

12 October 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 12/10/2006 You could cut the tension in the room with a machete, but only if the blade was sharp enough. It’d been a bad week for Stakiri and the rest of the crew and I couldn’t help the smug grin from creeping along my face every now and then. God, I just loved being right. I told him that it’d happen, I warned him over and over. He dismissed me as a mild annoyance. Serves him right, I guess. Ever since the attack on the Stakiri Building last week, they’d seen nothing of their enemies. No more attacks, no messages, no important power plays his territory, nothing and this had made Stakiri more worried than ever. Direct confrontation was his preferred way of doing things and the head games whoever the hell was behind this was playing, was beginning to irritate him. Everyone could tell. It was getting late now and the moonlight from the clear night sky danced in through Stakiri’s wide office windows. Aside from the obvious effects of fatigue, it was just another reminder that most normal people were curled up their comfortable beds around about now. Just the very thought of it persuaded me to glance over toward Aeryn, sat on the brown leather sofa in the corner of the room. My timing was impeccable, as I looked over, she’d just embarked upon a rather impressive stretch, her arms extended wide and her breasts pointed toward me, temptingly. It took all I had just to look away and to pretend to be concentrating upon my illustrious employer once again. Out of the corner of my eye, I noticed Miguel, Stakiri’s man on the streets, yawn uncontrollably and what’s more, Stakiri noticed it too. I closed my eyes and shook my head, already predicting ahead of time what was about to transpire. “Oh, I’m sorry,” Stakiri started, his eyes burning holes in Miguel’s forehead. “Am I boring you?” I tried my hardest to contain a smile as Miguel squirmed in his seat, uncomfortably. He acted like he was so cool most of the time; he had the shades, the slick Armani suits and enough gold to make Mr T cry and it looked so strange to see the fear take over him. Miguel lowered his head, glaring at the carpet intently, embarrassed that other people had to see him like this. “I’m sorry, boss,” Miguel apologised. I took opportunity of Stakiri’s momentary lack of focus, to take another glimpse of Aeryn. I saw that she was smiling too. We thought alike, always had. “Now then,” Stakiri began, his attention now firmly fixed upon the entire room. “Any updates?” Multiple sighs echoed around the room. It was getting late and they all wanted to go home, after hours of being forced to wait around for Stakiri to finish another damned board meeting. Richard Moss, Stakiri’s lawyer, and Aeryn were the main culprits, but this time, Stakiri didn’t react. Instead, he glared around the office intensely, allowing the tension to envelope them all once again. I had no idea just what in the hell he hoped to accomplish, but then I really didn’t care. I had bigger, more important, things on my mind. Primarily, it involved PRIME. “Richard,” Stakiri began. His lawyer perked up upon the mention of his name and adjusted his spectacles accordingly. “In light of our recent war of words with our friend from Oregon, has there been any legal actions against us recently that could cause suspicion?” It had been a busy week for me at last weeks ReV. For starters, I was booked in the main event against Lindsay Troy, Vangelus Olsig and that damned thief Tony Gamble. The latter of those names, had tried to get friendly with everyone involved in the match beforehand, promising them the world, that they’d work together and defeat the rest, just for his own selfish needs. That sneaky little bastard. The man has no honour, but I suspect most had realised that long ago. It was my first ever main event booking within the organisation and, despite Gamble’s ineffective meddling, I did myself proud, even if I do say so myself. “Well, there have been a few attempts to initiate contact and attempt to establish a rapport with the Stakiri Corporation,” Richard Moss replied. I’m only half listening -- if at all. “But those were by separate organisations entirely. It’s quite possibly they may all be umbrella companies for that bio-engineering company in Oregon, but finding that information may take some time and considerable resources and we’re only working on a hunch.” Stakiri nodded. “Get on with the digging anyway. We need all the information we can get on these bastards, even if it does turn out to be redundant by the time we uncover it.” Regardless of the little details as to precisely how I won the match, I did one and I am the number one contender for the PRIME 5 Star title already. Yes, Chandler Tsonda did interfere in the match, at its very death, taking out both Gamble and Olsig, but that was well out of my control and therefore, I really shouldn‘t be held accountable. But I probably will be. Bad press and Jonathan Winters come hand in hand and I don’t doubt that the wrestling dirt sheet’s will undoubtedly concoct some kind of bullshit that will link me to Tsonda in some way. These fuckers have nothing better to do. Regardless of the publicity and all the other crap surrounding it, I have to say I’m mildly curious as to why I was spared during the assault. Perhaps those three guys have something going on that I’m not aware of. Interesting... “Also, run your findings past Aeryn,” Stakiri ordered. “I had her working there for a few months. She didn’t close enough to the source to finger the fucker responsible for this, but some of those suspected umbrella organisations may be familiar with her. Refresh my memory, what was the bio-engineering company called again?” “Bio Designs,” Aeryn answered, removing her jet black hair from her eyes. Damn, she’s hot. “But, from what I saw, this place wasn’t big enough to be a hub for these umbrella organisations.” Richard nodded and then voiced the conclusion everyone in the office had already come to, “But that of course could be one of those umbrella organisations itself.” Stakiri nodded in response and my mind drifted away from the conversation yet again. This time, on that weirdo Adam and his strange fixation on me. The damned fool beat me a couple of weeks back for my damned PRIME Internet title and since that time, he’s been following me around like a lost puppy. By this stage of my career, I fully expected to have a stalker of some kind, but I kind of expected it to be a girl, not some weird guy out of his league within the wrestling industry. I shudder a little and slowly fade back in on the conversation -- I haven’t missed much. “What about the streets?” Stakiri questioned. There wasn’t as much anger in his voice, compared to the last time he spoke to Miguel, but it was damned close. “As you’re well aware, a number of gangs, old and new, made a play for territory whilst you were busy in Oregon last month,” Miguel explained. Winters remembered it well. He’d been assigned to bust a few heads and bury a few bodies during that month. “One gang has been a little more resistant than the others. Always finding new guys to fill the ranks once we knock ‘em down to size and have always been well armed no matter how much business we take from them.” “That means substantial financial backing,” Stakiri interjected, stealing Miguel’s thunder. “Precisely,” Miguel confirmed. “It might be worth looking at these guys more closely for the time being. Getting confirmation on the inside and stem the problem from that angle. Seems the better option.” I drift out again. This conversation is getting real old, real fast and besides, my mind is on more...complicated matters. Charity -- I heard she’s giving it away. It was certainly an unexpected turned of events that I really didn’t see coming. Especially after that backstage run in we had a couple of weeks back. I thought we got off on the wrong foot, but for some reason she wants to work with me and I’m not sure how I feel about that. On the one hand, she is an opportunistic whore who, while her boyfriend, husband and additional fuck buddy are away, will play. So she could be deadly serious about this whole thing. On the other hand, I really pissed her off a few weeks ago. I’m a very paranoid man, I admit, but it doesn’t take Sherlock Holmes to figure out where this one could be going. I could have a Sirrajin’s boot size firmly tattooed on my head, by the end of the week. If that is the case, at least I can prepare myself for it, but I have no preparations in mind to deal with Aeryn, when she confronts me about this whole damned thing. By now, Aeryn would know. She watched every show I’m involved in religiously and so that means she’s already well aware that Charity has a very reliable reputation for being a international slut. What does Aeryn think of with me by forging an alliance with her? Does she think it’s for business or pleasure or both? The whole idea seemed good at the time, but now I’m not so sure. Stakiri nodded. “Good.” He glared at Aeryn. “Can you handle that?” “Sure thing,” she said. “Winters,” Stakiri added, “you’re her back up.” As soon as my name’s mention I nod and then continue with my ponderings. Despite what the others in the room may think, they maintain dominance of whatever half-assed scheme Stakiri was getting them all involved in this week. I enter this week’s ReV with mixed feelings. On the one hand, I have my shot at Tsonda for PRIME 5 Star title. On the other hand, however, I’ll have to deal with Charity and make sure throughout it all, she doesn’t just decide to stab me in the back for my troubles. Great, just great. “You all know what to do,” Stakiri told everyone. “Get to it.” I was the first one out of there. I could feel myself falling asleep throughout the entire thing; not because of exhaustion, more because of the boredom. I failed to see what any of this had to do with me anymore and then, of course, I remembered my assignment for the week. Another fucking thing to worry about in the coming months. “So,” Aeryn initiated. Turned out, she was right behind me leaving the office. Saved me the trouble of waiting for her by the elevator, I guess. “Were you going to tell me?” I played dumb. “About what?” She rolls her eyes and sighed in my direction. I never knew a sigh could be used as a form of attack. Now I’d been education. “You know what about,” She told me. “The Charity thing. What’s going on there?” I sighed. I have no answer for her. “I thought it was a good idea at the time.” “What? Aligning you’re self with a slut?” She asked. She wasn’t angry, just playful; or at least I hoped that was the case. “Getting someone to watch my back,” I answered. “You know how many people I’ve pissed off in my time?” Aeryn rolls her eyes and grins -- ah, she is being playful. “Yeah, I‘m one of ‘em.” “Well,” I said. I almost smile too -- almost. “All in a day’s work.” She shakes her head and I take it as a sign that she wants to move the conversation on. “So, I guess you’re covering my behind next week then, huh?” I was right. God loved being right!. “Yeah,” I responded. “I’m watching it right now as a matter of fact.” She chuckled playfully and gave me a dig in the arm. It hurt considerably, but I act tough and try not to let on. Then, we go back to her place. You’re not coming.
