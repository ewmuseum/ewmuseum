Judgment: History

1 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11/1/07 I was getting into a routine, seeking out Dr. DiPendo’s advice before my biggest trials. This feeling of belonging struck me as odd; my home was a literal pile of ash. Maybe I was vulnerable, looking for somewhere to relate to. Maybe, in trying to self-liberate from psychological clog…I had just become dependent on DiPendo. His secretary recognized me, even gave me a cute smile brimming with optimism. I felt bad, mentally reducing her while sizing her up. Her thighs were hidden, perfect things; her breasts embellished by an expensive WonderBra; and her face sculpted poorly, a bit misshapen but cheery enough to make up for it. The new Chandler Tsonda wanted to think of her as more than just a vessel of sexuality. No saint, I thought to myself, as I imagined her excited lips on mine. "Good morning, Sophia." I winked at her, in that way of being nice by flirting and seeing what comes of it. Her embarrassed smile betrayed nothing. "I need one of my personal emergency sessions with the Doc." Glancing down at her work, she was all business again. Or was she avoiding my gaze? "Mr. Tsonda," she started, "Doctor DiPendo is booked all day today." Her eyes danced around my efforts to meet up with them. Something was rotten in Denmark. "It’s an emergency". I stated this flatly, hoping to convey the necessity of my request. But Sophia was having none of this, now repelling me with her stiff body language and pretending to fiddle with papers that did not have my answer. "Sophia." There was no more questioning in my voice. I needed what I needed. "There are extenuating circumstances here, Mr. Tsonda. Doctor DiPendo simply cannot shift things around today for you." "For who, then?!" I didn’t intend to roar as loudly at her, but I couldn’t waste time feeling bad as she shrunk down into her chair. It was a moment I would wish to have back. Turning towards the closed door of DiPendo’s office, I thought I was seizing life by the balls. The sensitivity of the door surprised me; I expected more of a fight to get into the office. DiPendo and his douchebag client immediately turned their heads towards me, at which point I realized I hadn’t correctly planned out my soliloquy. The guy on the couch was mortified. His eyes bulged out of their sockets with anger and embarrassment, but he was waiting for DiPendo to make the first move. "Doc, I-" "Get out." The man’s voice was implicitly authoritative, so he didn’t need to scream and shout. It takes a stronger person than I to remain stoically calm in the face of the daily irrational behavior of humans. DiPendo’s peaceful demeanor had stopped taking me by surprise, but I thought this, of all things, would get a rise out of the man. "Step out of my personal office, Mr. Tsonda." "Who are you calling Mr. Tsonda?" He had the nerve, but didn’t need to sell his frustration to anybody. He was ballsy enough to completely depersonalize our relationship, but collected enough not to throw a shit fit. DiPendo doesn’t thrive on the boisterous displays of emotion like yours truly; just one of the ways we’re quite the odd couple. "Doc, please." I was waiting for a sigh, followed by the acceptance of my childish ways and a sermon on finding and crushing my demons. That was our situation: he the oft-abused sage, I the calamitous prodigal son. In DiPendo’s eyes, my failings made me infallible. Or so I had expected. "Please what? Please don’t charge you with trespassing? Please don’t blacklist you with every psychiatric group in San Diego? Please show a little common sense, maybe. Or please, act like the mature adult you so often fail to be." There was a tone to his voice, but no added volume. "Please, man. I’m sorry. I want your help; haven’t I changed?!" In these desperate times, I could lay my visible emotion out for him and show him the immediacy of my needs. "The world is Chandler Tsonda’s oyster. It always has been and I can’t change that." Those disappointed eyes. Pupils dilated, embarrassed for me in my current state. I was embarrassed for me. A lone survivor from the other side of the world, born in that bloody quagmire, unable to cope with average human weakness. Where was my Vietnamese mother’s son? Was this redemptive attitude leeching my soul? I couldn’t pretend to be a good guy any longer like this, leaning on DiPendo or anyone else. "Forget it." I started out of his office, completely and utter fed up with things as they were. The door slammed closed loudly behind me. And then I just leaned until I fell, slumping up against the wall. There I was, in the middle of the office lobby, Sophia’s eyes sneaking judicial glances at me. You may wonder why a man loses himself in these flights a couple of nights before a hugely important obstacle. This upcoming match wasn’t even supposed to be a cathartic release of emotion, just a test of strength and will. And here I was, all sorts of twisted with nobody’s help. You may wonder and it all starts with history. * * * * * * * * As all melodramatic scenes require, it was raining. Not cats and dogs, but the angriest, fiercest tears the angels could conjure up. And as all my life’s personal melodrama requires, I was tipsy. I had no business being up. The rain’s frantic percussion was the ideal soundtrack for the moment. It was one of those nights. Those nights after the bar where you go home alone that you really get introspective. Drinks get the cogs spinning upstairs and things snowball from there. You start with every girl you met at the bar, scrutinizing the ins and outs of why you’re there scrutinizing the ins and outs and not fucking her. Then you move on to girlfriends you wish you were still with, coming up with reasons upon reasons of why she’s better off without you. You even flip through girlfriends you know would never have worked, just for pity’s sake. Somewhere in here, you break your BlackBerry because none of your drunk texts bring anything to fruition. There we were: the glass of cherry brandy, the skeleton of a PDA, and myself. We were ready to call it a night, but a knock on the door jolted me. Lesser storytellers would call this knock ominous, but it’s expected that no one is waking me up at 3:56 A.M. to tell me I won Publishers Clearing House. No need to front like this is good news. I hoped it was the wrong door so I could rip into someone, but then retire to sleep. An actual houseguest at this time would only result in me apologizing multiple times the next morning. On that note, I opened the door. The mess in front of me appeared to be a human. The crinkled fleece hoody hid the person’s entire torso. Even after a cordial unzip, the face didn’t strike me as familiar. "Chandler?" Apparently, I struck the hooded apparition as familiar. In my mildly intoxicated state, I tried very hard to think about remembering this in the morning. "Yep." I failed at making my response sound like a question. "Who are...do you want," I stammered, expertly failing to express both trains of thought. "Who, huh?" "Listen." "Not until you tell," I spit. I was only able to vocalize certain, fragmented pieces of thoughts. I hoped this wasn’t my long-lost brother or something because we weren’t getting off to a great start. "My name’s Anthony, but who I am doesn’t matter. Who you are does. I work for Ettore Calvino." Coffee won’t do it. Showers won’t do it. But the name of that coward sobered me up instantly. With the gloss lifting from my eyes, I gritted my teeth but said nothing. Expecting bad news hadn’t prepared me for this, whatever this was. Anthony looked like he was in a hurry, but also saw my immediate change in demeanor. He was choosing his words with caution. "Mr. Calvino needs to see you," he announced. "Hastily." Things were always meticulously organized within the Calvino cult. What contact I had shared with my father had been terse and emotionless. Like pulling off a Band-Aid. "He knows where I live. Tell him to stop sending cronies to San Diego." "Let me try this again," Anthony said, clearing his throat. "Ettore Calvino is a very busy man-" "Noted. Why don’t you ask him how "very busy" he was during my childhood? He’s not a capo to me, he’s a failure of a father." Anger and booze blur my memory, but I remember standing there, completely unafraid of a man who many people probably fear. "I don’t do the clichéd mob thing." Anthony cracked his knuckles loudly. "Mr. Tsonda, there is some urgency attached to this business. The quicker you realize that Mr. Calvino is trying to look out for you too, the sooner I can stop bothering you." I started to see my father in front of me. I didn’t have details to hate, just his image. I wanted to know his nervous tics, his deepest fear, his favorite color. All so I could hate him with more than just myself. And what could he possibly need? A year and a half ago, I left his house, certain that we’d said our final farewells. The next time I had expected to see him was at a closed casket funeral, he the victim of some unnamed Mafioso violence. "Anthony, I can’t go out there right now. Is Calvino still based out of Boston?" The underling nodded, sensing my acquiescence. "Tell him he can come find me. I’ve got some serious business of my own to take care of first." "That whole wrestling deal? Heh, my kids love it." The stock character came to life, a glint in his eyes. I nodded soundlessly as Anthony withdrew from the doorway, still testing my with his eyes. "Good luck with all that. Don’t be hard to find after your show." I remembered telling Titan St. James the same thing just a month ago. But now I couldn’t think about him, the Great American Nightmare, any of it. I’d reinvented myself as a wrestler who overthought these things and things were all coming full circle. My father. My inability to focus pre-match. It was ironic, in the way that causes you to fall apart outside a psychiatrist’s office. Anthony was strolling down the hallway, disappearing but leaving a hard imprint of who he represented. I would dream of Ettore Calvino, of Vietnam, of my mother, of violent orange sunrises halfway across the world. And soon, I would have to go settle things. More things, at least. I thought immediately of seeing Dr. DiPendo. Now I actually needed him. I needed this to work. I had to hope for something.
