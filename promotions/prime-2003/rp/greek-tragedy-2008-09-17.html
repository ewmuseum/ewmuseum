Greek Tragedy

17 September 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 17th, 2008 On the edge of his bed, with his size eighteen feet set firmly on the ground and his reddened face almost completely covered by his gigantic hands, lies Hank Cobb. Ordinarily the walking embodiment of good natured, southern ease, the doppelganger before us bares little resemblance to the native North Carolinian who debuted in PRIME over six months ago in the legendary Dual Halo, save for the obvious physical similarities. Upon closer examination, Cobb’s reddened face cannot be traced back to a rollicking overindulgence in his brew of choice, Bud Heavy. Nor is it the remnants of a hearty belly laugh in response to his partner Colby Korver’s irreverent, and usually offensive, comedic stylings. It is instead the result of an emotion in complete contrast to Cobb’s very essence…unadulterated grief. For it is not sweat that pours through the cracks between Cobb’s fingers, but the salty liquid most commonly known as tears. And not tears of joy nor of triumph, but instead remnants of old, forgotten wounds that in actuality never fully healed. Although a laundry list of missed opportunities both in and out of the squared circle may very well have left behind a very palpable and distasteful memory in the mind of Cobb, it is not the origin of this crippling emotional torment he is now struggling to endure. This tragedy is far removed from the squared circle that Cobb now calls home, yet infinitely more personal. In the world of professional sports, the tragedy of untimely death is always magnified by the intense scrutiny of the public eye. And on the heels of this examination often comes both unwanted and unseemly details, thanks largely in part to an around-the-clock media culture that often neglects the existing facts in favor of a created story. But this story is a little different. This story is as simple as it is saddening. This is the story of Skip Prosser. They first met during Cobb’s freshman year at Wake Forest, which had just introduced Prosser as its new head men’s basketball coach. Only seventeen years old, Cobb was finding it increasingly difficult to adjust to the rigors of college academics, while at the same time balancing his duties as an offensive lineman on the football team. After each grueling practice, Cobb would amble leisurely back across the freshman quad, from the football facilities to his dorm room in the Collins Residence Hall. Thanks to Wake Forest’s rigorous admission standards, Cobb’s gargantuan frame dwarfed his fellow classmates, although he was always quick to exchange pleasantries with his warm Southern drawl. On this day, however, he had just endured an abortion of a practice, during which he repeatedly found himself on the losing end of his match up with the starting right defensive end. Completely unfamiliar with such athletic failings, having dominated the high school competition as a four-year starter, Cobb found himself wandering aimlessly across the picturesque campus as the sun began to meander toward the western horizon. So preoccupied was Cobb with worry and misgivings that he even went so far as to impolitely ignore the greetings of several passersby, an oversight whose mere publication would have sent his poor mother to an early grave. There was one particular individual, however, who did not pass by him so covertly. For as Cobb began to descend the stone steps that separate the upper quad from its lower counterpart, he ran smack dab into a man of far lesser size, but far greater stature. And as the native Pennsylvanian dusted himself off, Cobb struggled to stammer out an apology. "I…I…I’m really sorry Coach Prosser. We just finished up practice and I’m fixin’ to grab some supper and then get to my studyin’. I can’t tell you how embarrassed I am. I really should have…" "Son, just because I wasn’t smart enough to get in here doesn’t mean I can’t tell when an athlete’s had a bad practice. So do me and everyone else around here a favor, for the rest of today get football out of your mind and just worry about watching where you’re going. You have only have twenty-four hours left to wait for something that you’ll have twenty-four years to remember. Take it from me, if you continue to improve, you’ll have a chance to get better." As Cobb responded to the coach’s renowned wit with a huge grin, Prosser continued on his way, having done what he had always done best, positively impact the lives of the students around him. Although it was not the last meeting between Coach ‘Skip’ Prosser and Hank Cobb, as they would enjoy many more during his freshman year at Wake, it was this first encounter that always remained most poignant to Cobb. And then, on July 26th, 2007, Coach Prosser collapsed in his office around noon after jogging on the track adjacent to his office on Wake Forest's campus. A staff member found him unresponsive around 12:45pm and medical personnel performed CPR and used a defibrillator in efforts to revive Prosser. Although he was rushed to Wake Forest University Baptist Medical Center, it was for naught, as he was pronounced dead about an hour later from an apparent ‘sudden massive heart attack.’ The man that once quelled a young Hank Cobb’s fears with but a few simple words was no more. But his memory will live on forever. So as Cobb mourns, wholly stricken with grief at the passing of a man who meant more to those he touched than he ever truly knew, he turns his gaze back to the papers at his feet, a personal letter published in a local newspaper in the days following Prosser’s death, written by one of his players. As he reaches the open letter’s end, Cobb finds himself once again sobbing uncontrollably for this great man. This was a man who taught him, though not his teacher, and coached him, though never his coach. This was a man who would take great pride in the steps that Hank Cobb has taken in his wrestling career. But one gigantic step looms large on the horizon. Cataclysm. For each fed’s champion, it is an opportunity to stake their claim as the top wrestler on the planet today. For the main eventers, it is about taking the next step toward championship gold. For the feds involved, it is about brand dominance and superiority. For the fans, it is the realization of a dream thought impossible, now just days away from occurrence. But for Hank Cobb, it is far more simple. He will be there to fight.
