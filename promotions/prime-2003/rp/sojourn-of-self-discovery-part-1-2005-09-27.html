Sojourn of Self-Discovery, Part 1

27 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 9/27/05 “I never really wanted to end up like that,” Scott said, his face downcast and eyes closed. “It was just one of those damn little twists of life that ain't no fun,” he muttered softly. “But if it hadn't all happened, I wouldn't'a met you, would I?” A slight smile cracks his stoic lips. “I reckon not.” The big man sat there, toying with a bottle of vodka that used to be full. He'd taken care of that not terribly long ago, but the bottle was still sitting there. “No,” he goes on in his husky whisper. “No, things have turned out really good, no question about that. But... sometimes I can't help but wonder what could'a happened...” “See, I was supposed to be set for life. I was supposed to take that big record I'd run up for being a defensive lineman and all. I was going to go to college, get known as a monster for tackles and sacks and all. I had that all figured out, best as I could, which ain't really sayin' much.” The big man shuffled the hotel chair carefully. He knew from experience that if he wasn't careful, it would fall apart under his weight. He gently set a big forearm on the table and sighed. “No, it was going to translate into a big ol' NFL career, bein' known as the new monster on the field, the guy the pocket was supposed to stop but couldn't. I was going to be a beast out there.” Scott didn't let himself cry when people were around, save one person. And even then... he couldn't stop a tear from running down his cheek. “It was gonna be a good life. I was gonna have a lotta money, big house, fancy cars that I'd always wanted.” He set the bottle on the floor, his hair hanging down over his face again, but he didn't care. “I really hated my dad. He was an angry, miserable sumbitch that never could be happy with me. Oh, Lacey, he loved her. He loved Mom. But that damn sumbitch just couldn't ever be happy with me. I dunno if he wanted me to go into the Army like he did or not, he wouldn't ever say. But by hell, nothin' I ever did was enough.” He sat straight up, almost defensively. “But I ain't blamin' me being screwed up on him. I ain't gonna let him that kind of satisfaction, still screwing me up years after he's gone. No, I just hated him and worked hard to prove him wrong. I wanted to take everything he wanted and shove it up his ass. My problems come from me: in all that angry stuff, I never did learn who the hell I was. I still never have.” It was an admission he'd never made before to anyone, not even to himself. It shook him, deep down, to realize and say out loud that he didn't know who he was. “That's what still screws with my head. I was going to be something big, but now... now I don't even know who the hell I am.” He looked down and cracked another slight grin. “I know I'm buzzed, though.” “Damn stuff is one of the few things I've got. I can get stuff out with this junk, I can get away from it for a while, even a few hours is all I need. But it's about killed me. Back in the day, back in RPW, I about drank myself right out of this mess I'm still in. But I got clear of that, didn't worry about it much anymore after that. I mean, I about died so what difference did any of that shit make? It didn't.” With a wave of a massive paw, he knocked the bottle over. “Here's the kicker. I hate that shit. I hate how it burns, how it screws with my head, makes me slower and dumber. I hate how it gets me into trouble. I hate it all, but I keep going back to it because I don't know any other way to deal with it all.” “I can still blame that on my old man, if I want.” He rubbed at his eyes; they were feeling raw and grainy, but he still wasn't tired. Hell with it. “The thing that just makes me crazy is that the world I could've had just had to end when my old man died. It couldn't be that it was a choice I made, or something happening to Mom or anything. No, it still had to be about that sumbitch.” With a burst of anger, Scott gripped his hair and pulled, bringing several small groups of golden mane from the mess that covered his skull. “Couldn't just let me live, could he? That fucker. I just wanted to be me. He changed all that, and I went to go work in that lumber yard to support Mom and Lacey.” His eyes lifted, looking like pure murder gazing forward. “I hated it there, too. It was the same thing, all day, ever day. I would move logs around because I was the only one that could without some damn machine or another. I could just grab one of those things and heave. It'd land somewhere in that direction, but the important part was that it wasn't where it had been. So my job was just having nothing where I was standing. It made me want to kill something, and the brawls that those guys had about gave me the chance. They'd have someone pick a mark and circle up so you couldn't run and let the guy jump his mark. So one day they came after me for some damn reason and I broke the guy's face in. I smashed his nose, I busted up his sinuses, all that good stuff. By the time I was done, he was bleeding out of everywhere.” “Yeah, they fired me for that shit. Nevermind that the kid picked the fight with me.” A derisive snort gently sounded from his nostrils. “So I took up wrestling with I think about five days' worth of training under my belt. Hell, I don't think I've gotten any more since then. I'd just go crazy and wail on someone and hope I'd win.” He eased in the chair, this time it actively groaned out in protest. He was trying to be careful, he really was. “So I set out for a life on the damn road that I'm sure used up more years of my life than I wanted to give it. Two years of driving around, sleeping in the Jeep every damn night, eatin' peanut butter for three meals a day and pickin' up the front end of that Jeep to keep strong. I spent a fuckin' Christmas in some shithole between Savannah and Atlanta because I had a show on the 26th and there was no way I could make it home. So I sat there in that diner for four or five hours that day, sippin' coffee and trying my best not to cry and rip the table off the floor.” He threw a contempt-filled glare at the bottle he'd knocked over. “You wanna know where I spent the rest of the day? I spent it at that waitress's shitty apartment, drinking whiskey and muttering about how life sucked. She was a hot girl that had run off from home and got stuck working the ass-end of the job in some crappy truckstop diner where she met other losers like me. Difference was that we both knew it sucked. So we dranked and screwed all night long. Merry Christmas.” He pulled the table closer and leaned on it a little more heavily. “So I'm traveling all over the damn place when I finally get ahold of an old friend of mine. Ol' Gabe, friend of mine growing up, and he wanted to make a tag team in a new fed that was getting off the ground. So we did this comedy bit with me as the monster backup and him as the outright idiot. But it wasn't enough. RPW was paying once a week and I needed to send more home, so I was doing RPW one night a week and other southern territories the other six nights a week. Keep the Sabbath? If I'm going to Hell, that might as well be the reason, though Lord knows there are plenty of other sins.” Scott couldn't quite get his gaze to focus. “So anyway, that's where I met Alyssa. Life... it changed a little. She had money, lots of it. Hell, she still does. She insisted that I start sleeping in a bed in a hotel and that she'd pay for it. I couldn't talk her out of it, and frankly I didn't want to. Sex in the Jeep wasn't something I'd ever done and wasn't eager for it. But those first few nights... I was layin' there, wide awake because I'd forgotten how to sleep in a bed. But she insisted, she still does...” He looked over his shoulder where his beautiful girlfriend lay asleep. The sheets lay in a clinging spread, emphasizing the naked curves beneath. Scott smiled. It had gone from animal attraction, which almost wasn't a joke, to something more real now... he didn't bother naming it. “She... she's the only person in this whole business that I'd regret if my life had been different. If my life hadn't gotten screwed to hell by my old man... I wonder if I'd still have met her. She's sharp, good creative consultant and all... I hope I would've. She makes this damn life on the road more bearable and I almost never drink with her around. 'Cept after she goes to sleep. She knows it in the morning, but she don't say anything.” He turned around. “I beat myself up after Tropical Turmoil. I blew the win that should have been defining and turned it into a monumental fuckup.” He blew out a tired sigh, rubbing again at his eyes. “I never expected Van to end up being such a good partner. If you can get past all that crazy garbage he does... well, if you can get past that, you can find a guy in there that really cares about people. And I failed him. Everyone expected it to be the other way around: he's the idiot, right? He'll fail Rampage because Rampage is only failed when his partners can't hold their own. No, I blew it. So I'm saying this because someone's got to hear it.” He lifted his hand from the table and pointed. “It's not that I want pity. This week I have a chance to redeem myself to my partner and to the world. And I don't give one good damn if you think I shouldn't have it. I've worked hard to earn every damn thing that will eventually come my way. These belts? They're gonna belong to us and we're going to have earned them. Me? It's only the beginning for me... but I'm not going to get the rest of what I earned. Not until I learn who the hell you are.” With that, Scott gave his reflection in the hotel room window a last look and pulled the curtains shut.
