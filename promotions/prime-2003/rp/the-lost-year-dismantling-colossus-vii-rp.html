Date: 2009 and 2010 August 2010 The wooden blades of the ceiling fan spun overhead, working in conjunction with the central air conditioning system to cool the large expanse of Lindsay Troy's master bedroom. Rain pelted the windows outside, another torrent in a stretch of rainy days that reminded her more of the end of fall rather than the end of summer. It was somewhere in the single digits of early morning, but Lindsay didn't bother to check to see what time it actually was. Instead, she had been watching the fan for what felt like forever, unable to get back to sleep despite turning over to either side and clearing her mind from any thoughts that lurked there She rolled her head to the side and looked at Tyler Rayne, sleeping face-down on the pillow and snoring lightly. An idea crossed her mind to nudge him awake, to wrap his arms around her body to spoon her close—this usually worked when insomnia claimed her and prevented her eyes from closing in sleep. Tonight, she didn't want to disturb him. Their flight to San Diego was early and their press docket was full the minute they stepped off the plane. One of them should be well rested, she thought, so she left him alone to remain in slumber. Lindsay sat up, tossed the sheet away from her body and slipped out of bed. She didn't bother with slippers or a robe and instead pattered barefoot across the hardwood floor to the door. She stepped over the sleeping Ciro and glided into the hallway. A couple steps toward the stairs and she found her self standing in front of a closed door on the opposite side from her bedroom. Her hand wrapped around the doorknob and twisted it open. A creak was stifled by easing the door open slowly. Light from the streetlamps outside poured into the room, hitting various pieces of furniture and casting shadows across the walls and floor. An overstuffed chair was pushed into one corner, near a window, and two dressers were against perpendicular walls. In another corner stood a crib, made from white birch to match the dressers. A baby mobile hung overhead, unmoving. Her gaze hesitated on the crib, then lowered to her toes. ~**~**~**~ December 2009 "So, did you go see the doctor again? Do you want to know ahead of time what you're going to have?" Alaina asked as she stood over an open drawer and rummaged around for a corkscrew. "Where is this goddamn thing?" "I got it, I got it, go sit down!" Alaina shooed Lindsay away, who laughed at her younger sister's attempt to not let her do anything in her own home. A business meeting with Regan Communications Group found her in town and, naturally, staying with her sister. "You didn't answer my question." "We go next week. I was supposed to go on Thursday but Dr. Nicolosi's mother just had hip replacement surgery back in Hartford, so I said I could wait until she got back from helping her. Tyler's got a match this week anyway, but he'll be with me for the appointment." "I don't think I could wait if I was in your shoes again. All between her legs. ~**~**~**~ December 2009 A week and a half later It came in from the Harbor, ominous and foreboding and swift in its descent. Following the wind currents, it snaked around the coastline, licked at the edges of civilization and poured over the Charles River into Cambridge. Mid-December's gloom was an unforgiven mistress and one that Massachusetts residents had long been acquainted with. This was only the beginning of the cold season and much worse was in store for those who chose to make their lives here. Northeast winters were known for their endurance and often teased an extended stay well into April. No snow squalls or biting cold was in the forecast for today: just overwhelming gray and the occasional spittle of rain. Alaina navigated the black SUV through the mist, halogen headlights serving as twin beacons to guide the car back to the sanctuary of home. It was early morning and the roads were quiet, not yet packed with the commuters vying like madmen to get into the heart of Boston for work, school or play. She kept to the speed limit, did not race yellow lights to red, and stole a glance over her shoulder to her sister lying across the middle row of seats. "We're almost to your place," she stated quietly as she directed the car into a right hand turn. The blinker clicked back into position and silence returned to the cabin. Lindsay's gaze were trained on the center console of the car and she didn't look up when Alaina addressed her. Her hazel eyes, known for their mischievous glint, were dull and devoid of life. Her brown, naturally curly hair was matted flat against the back of her head and her skin had taken on an ashen pallor underneath the blanket that was wrapped around her body. They made a left turn into the driveway just as rain dotted the windshield. Alaina put the car in park, cut the engine and twisted her body around. "Dan said he was going to pick up Chelsea so she could take the dogs out while we got you settled. Tyler should be here soon too, he was right behind us. Do you want to wait until they get here, or do you want to go in?" Lindsay lifted her eyes to look at Alaina and then craned her head toward the house. She began to sit up, grimacing from discomfort. "Alright, just...just stay there. I can help. Just give me a second..." Alaina unfastened her seat belt, grabbed the keys from the ignition and jumped out of the car. She opened the back door and helped her sister to a sitting position. "Slowly now, Lindz. We're in no rush here." Lindsay stepped outside and leaned against the driver's door as Alaina grabbed her purse and an umbrella that was wedged in the seat back pocket.. She watched her sister with an uncharacteristically demure expression, then lifted her eyes to the heavens. "Was it supposed to rain?" "I don't know...here," Alaina opened up the umbrella with a pop and held it over Lindsayr's head. "Let's get inside before it turns into something heavier than just drizzle." The two slowly walked around the front of the car, through a side opening in the hedges and up the front steps of the porch. Inside the house, three distinct barks sounded the alert that company was here. Alaina paused to flip through the keychain until she located the correct key. "What's the alarm code again?" "830920," Lindsay replied, flatly. "OK. Let me go in first so I can turn off the alarm and get the dogs calmed down." Alaina inserted the key into the locks and stepped through the threshold. The door immediately shut as the beeping of buttons and the barks of the dogs interrupted the once quiet morning. It didn't take long for the dogs to be silenced and for Alaina to return to collect her sibling. "Ready?" Lindsay wordlessly, and carefully, stepped inside. The house felt foreign—a country where she didn't belong. Almost immediately, Ciro, Isis and Angus loped over to greet their master. She managed something resembling a smile as she patted each dog's head, but did nor said no more. "Let's get you over to the couch so you can lie down." "Alaina..." Lindsay started to protest, albeit weakly. "Lindz, don't start, please. Just let me help you, alright?" She could have protested. She could have asserted that she was no thing that needed coddling. She could have put her weary foot down. But Lindsay did none of those things. Instead, she allowed her sister to lead her over to the sprawling leather sofa. The dogs followed behind. "Just let me sit up for a few minutes, Lanni. All I've been doing is lying down." "Well...I guess that's alright," Alaina mused, then decided. "I'm going to start bringing some of the stuff from the back of the truck inside. I opened a can of Coke for you, since the doctor said you might still get nauseous." "Thanks, sis." Alaina kissed Lindsay's forehead and provided a gentle hug. "I love you. I'll be right back." Ciro, Isis and Angus eyed Lindsay with confused and concerned eyes. She stroked each of their heads again and when she got to Angus, he started to whine and placed his head in her lap. His nose brushed against the flatness of her stomach, and she absentmindedly placed her hand over where her bump should have been. Tears cascaded down her cheeks and streamed down her neck. Some dotted the front of her shirt. She slid from the couch to the floor and curled into a fetal position, wailing in anguish. The dogs dropped down next to her and offered their presence for comfort. ~**~**~**~ August 2010 Warm fingers floated along the small of her back and the pinpricks of stubble rested against the flesh of her shoulder and side of her neck. The presence of Tyler Rayne behind her, body pressed lightly against her back, elicited a soft sigh from her throat. She leaned back and let him hold her. "You should be sleeping," Tyler mumbled, disoriented and drowsy from sleep. "We've gotta be at the airport in a few hours." "Woke up. Couldn't get back to sleep." Lindsay turned her head slightly, her eyes fell on the tip of his nose and the slight part of his lips. Her hand reached across her stomach and grazed the top of his, which nestled itself against her hip. "I want to take the crib apart." There was a pause. Tyler struggled to have the statement process in his brain through the hazy fog of dreamland's remnants. "Right now?" "Right now," she repeated. The weight of Tyler's chin lifted off her. "Like you said, we've got a few hours before we need to be at Logan." "I'm like half asleep, but I could think of all sorts of other ways to fill the time. Much more fun ways. Naked ways." She smirked and squeezed his hand. "It's been a little over eight months since we lost the baby, Ty. I couldn't even bring myself to look at the crib for the longest time, never mind this door after Dan moved it out of our room." "I know. It was hard for me, too." "When I thought I was OK to look in here, I felt such an overwhelming sadness at what was supposed to be that I couldn't stand to linger. Being back out on the road's been a welcome distraction, even though it's come at the cost of a severed friendship with Matt." "Fuck him. Piece of shit." "Right now, yeah, he is. Maybe I am, too. I can't be understanding all the time. Neither can he." She stopped for a moment and looked back into what was to be their son's nursery. A sanctuary. A safe-haven for growth and nurturing and milestones. "Point is, despite all the shit that's gone down since last year, for the first time I'm starting to feel a little peace. I want to move forward. I'm ready to." Tyler sighed. He pulled at Lindsay's waist and encouraged her to turn around to look at him. When she did, he took her face in his hands and kissed her lightly. His thumbs ran along her cheeks as she returned his intimate gesture in kind. "I'll see if I can find a screwdriver or wrench or something." He padded down the stairs, leaving Lindsay standing in the threshold of a broken future. She walked into the baby's room and stopped in front of the crib. Her fingers glided over the wood and she looked down at the folded baby blanket at the foot of the cradle. "Baby steps," she whispered into the darkness.
