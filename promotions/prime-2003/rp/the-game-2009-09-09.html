The Game

9 September 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 09/09/09 - - - - - When there are so many divergent paths to choose from, so many different choices that could be made, so many options, so many possibilities … how do you know which is the right one? If one false step can lead to a fall, if one wrong move can bring the whole pack of cards … so precariously balanced … down in a jumble of debris, if one misjudgement can change the plans that have been laid out … then how do you do anything at all? Simple. You just do. You see it is something so often overlooked in life, but a small truth nonetheless; the only thing to fear is fear itself. If you have two paths in front of you and you do not know which one to take then just your foot onto one or the other, it doesn’t matter which, and march boldly onwards. All paths lead somewhere after all so you will get somewhere; that has to be better than sitting on your backside worrying about left or right. If you have two choices to make, then make one. You will either be right or wrong, after all, so what is the worst that can actually happen? Oh yeah, I already said it … you can be wrong. Big deal. If you don’t make any choice at all then you are definitely wrong! Reach into your pocket and take out the first coin that you find, flip it high in the air and then watch it fall … let fate make the decision for you, if you can’t make it yourself, but let it be made! Who cares if you don’t make the right one, after all who is there to tell you what is right and what is wrong … do something, do anything. If you fall, pick your sorry ass up again and keep on stepping, keep on climbing, keep on moving … fighting … bleeding … sweating … crying … striving. Keep on! If the cards fall, shuffle the damn things and build that house again. If your plans fail then start them all over. Whatever you do make a decision! Make a choice. Take a path. Gamble. - - - - - “Remind me again how you talked me into this?” With his long auburn hair tightly tied back into a long plait that fell over his left shoulder to cascade down across his chest almost to his belt line, Jay Phoenix’s face was a mask of barely controlled panic as he stared around the opulence that surrounded him. The intensity of his gaze - eyes piercingly bright jade - seeming almost enough to cause spontaneous combustion as frown lines marred the surface of his otherwise perfectly bronzed skin. The figures that filled the room, and caught his gaze, moved from small gathering to small gathering, like leaves blown on the Autumn wind, with no meaning, no reason to their chosen path other than the hope that they would be seen to been seen, that they would mingle with the right people, that – finally - all the jaw-achingly false smiles and empty would somehow be worthwhile. A drone of chatter and hum filled the air as small talk babbled forth from the macabre social dancers, all of whom were busy in ensuring that they were the centre of attention, that they were in the spotlight, that they were noticed. All except one; all except for the one man who, in a room filled with faces, appeared to be alone in a crowd. So used to being the centre of attention, so used to the spotlight, and so used to being noticed wherever he went Jay Phoenix was the one person who simply wanted to fade away but was unable to. "As I recall you didn’t need much convincing," came the happy sounding reply as Rick James draped an arm over Jay’s shoulder, a large smile on his face as he took in the crowd in front of him. “And as I recall, when you agreed to come here, we were in the middle of something much more fun than talking anyway …” “Rick!” Jay spluttered, staring around him in shock. “Shush – people can hear you!” “So what, Jay?” Rick laughed as he pulled his lover further into the room. “Its not like we were doing anything illegal.” Reaching out, Rick grabbed two glasses of champagne from a passing waiter’s tray, nodding his thanks and he moved, effortlessly, through the throng of bodies. “Well,” he stated, turning around to stare into Jay’s eyes. “Not illegal in all States, anyway.” Rubbing his hand through his shaggy blond hair, his tanned face looking like a youthful surfer instead of the twenty-eight year old former journalist, and now freelance writer – stroke - defacto ‘manager’ in the mad, mad world of wrestling that he seemed to have become, Rick realised that the joke had fallen flat. He leant forward, slightly, as he took a deep breath. Looking around the room at the people who, obliviously, still kept up the never-ending chase of popularity, Rick gave a small shake of his head as he reached out to squeeze Jay’s hand. “I’m sorry, Jay, really. I know that you are still uncomfortable with all of this …” “Not all of it,” Jay exclaimed holding tight to Rick’s hand as he stared directly into his eyes. “It is all still pretty new, yes, and I find it uncomfortable that people find my … our … private lives so interesting, but I am NOT uncomfortable with being with you.” “Good,” Rick said, leaning forwards and softly kissing his partner. “Because I am not sure that I could keep my hands of you all night!” “All night?” Jay asked, smiling despite himself. “You couldn’t even wait five minutes before you gave people something to look at!” “Let them look,” Rick stated, glancing around to grin mischievously at the people who were blatantly staring at the couple. “It’s not like they haven’t heard the rumours.” “Rumours are one thing,” Jay pointed out as he pulled Rick away from the immediate crowd, and further into the building. “But I think that you just confirmed Perez’ last blog about the ‘shocking’ secret I have supposedly been keeping.” “Bitch,” Rick laughed. “He actually called me your dirty, little secret.” “Well, to be fair, he didn’t mention me by name,” Jay said. “Or you for that matter.” “How many other ‘Flamers’ would he be talking about in PRIME?” “You’d be surprised,” Jay mused. “I mean have you seen how Snow looks at me?” “What?” Rick choked on a sip of champagne. “Really – has he been checking you out?!” “No, doofus,” Jay grinned. “I just wanted to see your reaction.” “Though, thinking about it, he does talk about you a lot!” “Rick,” Jay leant forwards, head touching Rick’s. “I know that you love me but that doesn’t mean that every guy is after me, you know.” “Well he better not, you’re mine!” “Excuse me, sirs, would you care to take your seats?” The calm voice interrupted the pair and, as they turned to face the speaker, saw another of the waiters standing discreetly to one side. With a nod of his head he indicated that they should follow him and, as they were led through the people, they arrived at a table covered with green baize. The waiter indicated two vacant chairs – the other six being already filled – and as Jay and Rick took their places he moved back through the crowd. “And now the night begins,” Rick stared into the profile of his friend, noticing the intensity in his expression and reached out to lay a reassuring hand on his shoulder. “Hey, are you ok?” “Not really, Rick,” Jay whispered, watching as the woman across the table from him started to shuffle a pack of cards. “Maybe I should have told you this before agreeing to the charity event, but I don’t know how to play poker!” - - - - - The pile of chips in the middle of the table was bigger than it had reached at any point previously that evening. Jay looked down at his cards, for the fourth time in as many seconds, and then glanced up at his one remaining opponent. The obese and sweating man, dressed in an ill-fighting blue suit, his thinning hair swept over his shining cranium, may or may not have stared back at him. The mirrored sunglasses simply showed Jay’s own face so it was hard to tell. “Your bet, sir.” Nodding his understanding at the female croupier Jay stared at the pile of chips again. Eight people had started the game, about four hours earlier, and now there was just the two of them left. Rick had folded only the hand before, and Jay knew that the luck that had got him to this point – or the bad luck that had got rid of the more experienced players – was about to run out. You could only win so many hands by other people having worse ones. The fact that the fat man – Charlie or Carl, he couldn’t remember which – hadn’t even looked at his cards in the last five minutes, but had still bet most of his remaining chips worried Jay. As did the fact that all he had in his hand was a whole load of nothing with only their suit in common. That, and the hope that a miracle would still happen. The river – as Jay had learned the open cards on the table were called a few hours ago as Rick whispered quick hints to him – showed the Queen of Hearts, the Jack of Hearts, the Queen of Spades and the eight of Hearts. Puling his own cards up, again, to his face he stared at the colours and numbers on them. Ace of Hearts, six of Clubs, seven of Diamonds and the King of Hearts. Even his rudimentary knowledge – or lack thereof – of poker led him to believe that he held what amounted to a big hand of crap with close to a million dollars at stake. Nearly two hundred and fifty thousand dollars of that money was his – something that Rick hadn’t told him until after the game had started – and while he knew that it would be donated to charity he also knew that Carl’s … or was it Charlie’s? … charity of choice was simply a front for a much more ‘organised’ business than it was for anything worthwhile. So he knew that, if he lost, the money – his own money – would be wasted. Staring at the fat man, laying his cards face down on the table again, he pushed all of his chips into the centre of the table. The croupier looked at Jay, then the chips, then the other player who, with a small smile, nodded his head and pushed all of his won chips into the middle. A buzz of anticipation played out in the faces and voices of the people who were still watching the game. With a small movement Rick stepped closer to Jay and rested his hand on his shoulder as the dealer dealt the final card. The fat man – Charlie, Jay suddenly remembered – laughed as Jay slumped back in his chair as the card hit the green, velvet material. Throwing his cards across the table he reached out and started to sweep the chips towards him, chubby fingers grasping, as the two of Spades, the three of Clubs, the Queen of Diamonds and the Queen of Clubs were revealed. “Four of a kind,” Rick whispered, “Four frikkin’ Queens!” “I know,” Jay breathed softly, and then started to laugh. “Thank the Gods for that!” Throwing his own cards, face up, across the table they landed beside the flop card that the croupier had dealt – the ten of Hearts. The fat man paused, glasses slipping down his nose, and stared – bug eyed – at the cards. At the Royal flush. The chips slipped through his fingers as he rocked back in his seat; reaching out, Jay grabbed Rick by the hands and pulled him close, kissing him deeply. “People are looking,” Rick said, when he managed to get some air. “Yeah,” Jay replied. “I know.” - - - - - As they walked, hand in hand, towards the car – knowing that close to one million dollars was being sent to UNICEF – Rick suddenly stopped, turning to face Jay with a quizzical look on his face. “There is something I don’t get.” “What?” “You said that you didn’t know how to play poker.” “That’s right, I don’t.” “But you out-played Charlie Parker. You beat the unbeatable.” “Yup.” “… Jay?” “Yes?” “You didn’t know, did you?!” “About what?” “About the cards – about his hand – about the odds of you getting the last heart?” “Nope. Not a clue.” “All that money, though, what were you thinking?” “Simple. After everything that we have been through, after every choice we have had to make … when there was only two choices, two paths – victory or defeat - I did the only sensible thing.” “What - you guessed; you gambled?” “No. I won.” - - - - - If you must play, decide upon three things at the start: the rules of the game, the stakes, and the quitting time. ~Chinese Proverb
