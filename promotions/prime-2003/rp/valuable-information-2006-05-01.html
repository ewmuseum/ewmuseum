Date: 5/1/06 May 2006 From time to time in a person’s life, things slide carelessly off the surface of the tongue, extending into the atmosphere and infesting ears that were never meant to hear those things. Sometimes, that things, that knowledge, spills into the possession of the wrong types of person long after the spiller forgets the knowledge isn’t meant for anyone to obtain. Sometimes, say… two months after the fact… a gal just forgets she promised her sister she’d keep her mouth shut around the brother-in-law. On occasion, those slips come between lovers, in the comfort of bed, tucked under the sheets that casually hug the contours of their bodies. Other times, those slips will come in corporate offices, where public perception ends and the grotesque truths of obsessed businessmen float to the surface, like dead, stinking fish. And, from time to time… it just happens in the middle of a grocery store. Matt reached through the frigid air that swirled around the meat department in the back of supermarket. As was typically the case, all the good ground beef was in the back of the open cooler, buried behind dozens of pounds of already browning, rotting raw flesh. Finally, laying crookedly across a package of bacon, he found a pound of ground beef that had been cut and packaged that day, looking healthy… as healthy as something dead and shredded can look anyways. Content with his choice, he headed back towards the front of the store towards the check-out lanes. Along the way, he passed the dairy aisle… choosing it instead of cutting through the cereal aisle in hopes of avoiding a crowd of eight year olds screaming at their mothers that they wanted marshmallows in their cereal. Luckily, his decision paid off. Not only were the overly hyper children absent from the dairy aisle, but the typical hustle and bustle of the store was scarcely present at the time in the cool world of milk and cheese and eggs. Only two other people populated the aisle… an older gentleman struggling through hampered eyesight to read the expiration dates on packs of shredded sharp chedder, and a woman, bent over in an inadvertently seductive fashion, searching for a gallon of some certain type of milk. Matt thought she looked like she had a good figure. She looked to be about his age. She was in great shape… Suddenly, the woman found whatever particular gallon of milk she was looking for and mad her selection, standing up, taking a step back, bumping right into him. …She was careless. “Ohh! I’m so, sorry.” She turned to apologize face to face. She was his sister-in-law. “Anne?” For some reason, he said it in puzzled fashion, even though he was looking right into her eyes and could clearly see it was her. “Matt!” Anne threw her arms around his neck, giving him a rather enthusiastic hug, and nearly flinging the gallon of milk down the aisle towards the old man in the process. “I haven’t seen you in like… “Not since before Christmas.” He gave her the answer she was searching for, as he cautiously wrapped his arms around her waist, returning the hug. Apparently, it seemed, Anne was a bit more sympathetic to his situation than her sister was, which wouldn’t have been quite so ironic if that sister wouldn’t have been his wife. “Yeah. Too damn long, that’s for sure.” She released her grip and stepped back and sensing some uneasiness in the air, flipped the subject to something else. “I see things are crazy as ever in PRIME.” “It’s the world of professional wrestling. Crazy is about all it knows how to be.” He gave her a small smile as she laughed and switched the gallon of milk to her left hand, her right arm tiring from the weight. “But yeah… its about what you’d expect. A guy in a mask and cape running around talking shit to everyone. A dude with blue hair and a guitar. A greedy piece of trash running the company like the weasel he is. The usual.” Again she laughed, seeming to really enjoy the random encounter, and he had to admit, it was nice to see that despite how rocky he had made things with Mary, Anne still enjoyed his company and didn’t hold his faults against him. “Its great that you guys got that new deal with the FX network! I’m sure that was a big deal for everyone in PRIME.” “Yeah, I think we were all happy to see it happen. Nelson probably more so than everyone else… but sure, its gonna benefit us all.” “Benefits me too. Now I don’t have to worry about subscriptions to the Playboy channel just to watch a wrestling show. I love watching ReVolution… but the naked chicks just aren’t my thing.” “You’ve been watching ReV?” “And ordering the PPVs.” “Even since… well… ya know?” He reached back and rubbed his neck, feeling instantly a little worn from the mention of the problems between he and Mary. “Of course.” Anne gave him a soft smile. “Matt, I know things are rough between you and Mary right now. And I really wish you’d get your ass over to Mom and Dad’s house to work on making things right… but I don’t fault you for wanting to wrestle. You like what you do, and you’re good at it. And I think its entertaining. I understand where my baby sister is coming from… but I don’t blame her and I don’t blame you. You guys will work it out… hopefully soon… but meanwhile, I’m gonna keep enjoying ReVolution each week.” “Good to hear.” He meant it… though it probably didn’t sound that way. “Glad to know you’re still watching.” “Damn straight. Just waiting for you to finally get that asshole, Killean in the ring. I can’t believe after what he did to you back in December that he had the nerve to stop by and talk to Mary like nothing was going on, and then go out there just a few days later and attack you during the Halo…” “Wait… did you say he stopped by to see Mary?” There was a sudden look of panic on Anne’s face, as she realized she’d let slip a fact she’d promised to keep quiet. But quickly, that look vanished in favor of a sympathetic gaze, as she abandoned any desire to try a cover up. “Yeah. Back in February… just before Culture Shock. I stopped by Mom and Dad’s to drop off some groceries I’d picked up for them while I was out, and he was sitting, talking with Mary. He claimed he had come to apologize for what he did to you, and how that affected her...but I didn’t buy it. I snapped… hit him, not as hard as I should have… and told him to get out.” “Shit.” It was all he said, then she jumped right back on top of the conversation. “I promised Mary I wouldn’t say anything to you about it. I wanted to. I thought you deserved to know… but Mary seemed really upset about the idea, so I promised her I wouldn’t tell you. I’m sorry, Matt.” “Don’t be. It just slipped. You didn’t mean to say anything.” Now it was his turn to be comforting and sympathetic. He gently placed a hand on her shoulder. “Thanks for being honest about it and not just trying to brush it off.” “Yeah” was what she said, but it was meant as “you’re welcome”. For a moment, they both stood in the middle of the aisle, making a customer wheel their cart around them. Finally, Anne shook off the heavy feeling and reignited the conversation with the same energy she’d possessed just a few minutes earlier. “So,what are you going to do about Killean?” “I’m gonna hit him… a lot harder than you did.” “Good to hear”
