What dreams may come

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Colossus VI A massive grey stone and glass building. The dark night sky, covered in a thick layer of low clouds. A digital screen, jutting out along thick metal girders, displaying a message that scrolls along in bright neon letters … “PRIME – COLOSSUS - SOLD OUT!!!” A crowd of people milling around the entrance to the arena. Congregating at the ticket desk. Baying and begging for tickets that aren’t there. Vain hope evident that they still may get a chance at seeing history in the making. One common thread is that all the people have an air of excitement about them, and air of anticipation, mixed with a palpable sense of disappointment in not getting tickets. They are all the same. Faceless and legion. Except one. A low wall at one side. A solitary man sits and, even surrounded by all the people, one man among many, he is alone. Jay Phoenix. Wearing a long black leather coat that is pulled tight around himself, arms wrapped across his chest. Features somewhat covered by the high collar that is pulled up around his throat … from the elements themselves, the low temperature evident by the regular pale mist that flows from his mouth with each breath, moving up and out like some phantasmal tendril, misting with the combined breaths of the other people in the only communion that Phoenix seems willing to share at this moment. It is obvious that even though he is close physically to the other people, he is miles away mentally; his eyes looking through the people, through the neon sign, through the building itself. Hair tied back and hidden under the coat. A black bandana covers the rest. It is hard to recognise him without stopping, looking close, something that the people are not doing. With a sigh, his attention seems to come back to here and now. He focuses. He scans the crowd. Listens to the excited conversation that fills the night. People talk, their voices mingle, but only sound is there – words have no meaning. Phoenix shakes his head, slightly. A small smile on his face. He stands up. Looks up into the dark night sky. Raises both arms up, slowly, until they are perpendicular to the ground. Spread wide, wing-like, held aloft and still. He stares intently. Breathing stops. His whole concentration put into that which he is doing. Waiting. The loose tendrils of hair, that dangle from under the bandanna, start to float and dance. A gentle breeze, in the still night air, moves more and more violently around him. The covering is ripped from his head; his hair weaves a mad gyration around his face. The whirlwind builds. Focussed on Phoenix himself, the small crowd of people at the building talk on oblivious to everything, mere metres away. Ignored. All sound ceases. The roar of the wind, which would be expected from such a display, silent. At the edge of perception, like a half-remembered dream … a sibilant voice, little more than a breath in the air itself is heard. Picked up, repeated, by first a second voice, then a third, and finally a fourth. Follow. … follow … … follow … … follow. The wind stops. Devoid of conscious volition Phoenix walks away from the arena. Into the night. Follows. He walks until he is clear of the crowd. He moves along sidewalk of a garishly lit shopping precinct. Walking ever onward. A dark, gaping expanse of alleyway. A dirt and grime encrusted hand, reaching out, battered cup held tight. A face almost completely covered with wild, matted hair. The skin, where visible, coated with years of grime. A torn and battered woollen hat. Bloodshot eyes peer up. A slurred voice. “Hey buddy, sphare shome change?” Phoenix walks by. Not ignoring so much as not even realising that he is there at all. As he continues on his way, abuse follows. “ Well screw you Mr High and Mighty …” A well dressed woman. Grey trouser suit. Greying brown hair tied back in a tight and prim looking bun. Walking out of a Starbucks, juggling a briefcase in one hand with a cup of steaming coffee in the other. She tries to use her high-heel enclosed foot to prop open the door. She exits the premises and, as she steps foot onto the sidewalk, Phoenix … uncaring … jars her, spilling the copious amount of coffee across her chest. She shrieks, shock more than pain. “Hey you moron, why don’t you watch were you are going, or get a God damn guide dog to do the job for you, your stupid hippy!?!?!” She gesticulates angrily, holding out the now half empty cup off coffee as if in accusation. He stops. Slowly turns around. Stares blankly at the woman who, realising that she is all but alone on the darkened street, backs off. Reaches into his pocket. Pulls out a crumpled wad of money. Pushes it into the cup, causing more coffee to be spilled, this time across his exposed hand, steam rising. He remains oblivious. A monotonous voice, looking through the woman. “Sure buddy, here you go, now make sure to buy something to eat.” Turns on his heel. Continues to walk along the street. Leaves a very confused woman staring at his back. Turns a corner. A large church. The large church. Familiar. The steps to the church are clean and well kept. Pristine. The spire at the top casts a long shadow across the ground. Across Phoenix. Splitting his face in dark and light. He stares, at the building, for a few seconds. Waiting. A breeze picks up. Dust devils forming from the leaves on the sidewalk. Flapping and circling they dace around the Church, never touching it. He pulls his jacket close around him. Keeps walking. He turns the corner around the church. Follows the path of the breeze. The streetlights become less regular until it is just the illumination from the full moon and the stars that supply the light. It is enough. A metal gate set into the stone wall. He stops beside it. Reaches out. A squeal of old metal on older stone as he walks through. Gravestones and flowers everywhere. Memorials to those who have passed; gifts from those still here. He walks through a meandering path of graves. He reaches a large, unique marker; a winged angel in black marble. It is here that he stops, kneels down. Head sinking into his chest, his breathing suddenly laboured until, with a gasp as if from a body held too long under-water, is suddenly free to breathe once more. The roses in his hand, petals red as blood, are dropped to the ground. Eyes stare ahead, focussing on the marble, focussing on the writing, focussing on the words, focussing on the name … His own scream woke him and, clawing at the sweat-drenched sheets that enveloped his body, he sat up straight. Reaching for the lamp on the cabinet beside his bed his hand struggled to remain steady as he pushed the button; shadows fled before the light as his breath refused to calm. “Jay,” Rick shouted out, running into the room, eyes wide. “What’s wrong? What is it?” “I couldn’t see it, Rick,” Jay gasped, breathless. “I don’t know who it will be!”
