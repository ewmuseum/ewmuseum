[Warsong] Fear

Date: ReV200 Light rain gently tapped at the thick window, drops caressing the glass in rivulets, as Rick James watched the small commuter jet taxi to a stop. The single runway, that served Flagstaff Pulliman Airport well, glistened in the late afternoon sunlight, steam rising to meet the falling rain in a juxtaposition of elements. His head pressed against the glass, cooling his forehead, as his breath formed a mist of snowflake patterns just under his eyes. The butterflies in his stomach vied for position as his emotions warred between anxiety and excitement. It had been two days since he last saw Jay, two days where every minute felt like a lifetime and the ache of separation was a palpable and real pain in every part of his body; now, as he was about to be reunited with the man he had loved longer than he had admitted, he couldn’t help but worry about him. The last phone call had ensured that. While known as easy going and carefree to the outside World, to the fans who thought that – because they saw him on the TVs in their homes – they knew him, the reality of Jay Phoenix was very different. Rick had known him for the majority of his life. First as children with nothing in common but the fact that they also had nothing in common with the other children on the reservation, then as adolescents forming a bond that would only deepen over time, until, finally – with a passion built from crisis – they became soul mates. Lovers. Still he knew that, while he may know the man known as Jay Phoenix better than possibly anyone else on Earth, he hardly knew him at all. Restrained. Stoic. Slow to anger. Quick to forgive. These were just some of the things that Rick knew for sure about Jay, things that he had allowed himself to share with the World. The deeper emotions, the hopes, fears and dreams shared late at night as their bodies warmed the sheets wrapped around them, were things that he was coming to know, slowly but surely. So, to hear the voice of that man, cracking with emotion – hurt, yes, anger, most assuredly but under them, deeper down and while still hidden starting to appear through the edges, fear and despair. The noises of the PRIME arena were still audible in the background when Jay had phoned Rick, to let him know that he was on his way home, and it had been the caller I.D. that had let him know who was calling. The voice had sounded so different. Flat. Clipped. Beaten. All because of one person. Jason Natas. Rick sighed, hand reaching up subconsciously to trace the healing wound on the side of his head. He didn’t know Jason Natas well but, what he did know of the man, was more than enough. He had dealt with his sort before. Bullies with an ego so big that it was obviously compensating for something else that was lacking. As the only white kid on a reservation Rick had always been the outsider. Had always been an easy target. It was how he had met Jay, of course. Three or four of the usual suspects, older kids on the reservation who were there because it was their life, because they had nowhere else to be, rather than like him. His father had chosen to move there, to practice medicine and make a difference by helping people, and Rick had been pulled along for the ride. And the taunts, torments and beatings of course. Three months of abuse had felt like three years. Each and everyday brought something new as the kids thought of different ways to amuse themselves at his expense. Then came the day when it all ended; it all began. Another Native kid had turned up in the classroom, someone that Rick hadn’t seen before. He was slightly taller than the other kids, slightly bigger too in a way that hinted of the muscled build that was to come a few years later. Thinking back Rick could admitted that he had, even then knew that Jay was attractive but, back then, he didn’t know how to rationalise the feelings and thoughts that he was having. So, instead, he simply put it down to a mild case of hero worship at the sight of this calm and composed boy who seemed to dominate the room without saying a word. During the day he found out that Jay had been travelling with his grandfather, coming to terms with the death of his parents, and that no-one in the class really knew what to say, or how to treat him. Rick was separate from the others because of his difference, because of his culture and skin colour. Jay was separate because he had gone through something that none of the others had and, by his subtle actions, through choice. He made it obvious that he didn’t want, or need, any of his peers to talk to him, to console him or pick up the friendships that had been there before. In three months too much had changed. A lifetime had passed between them. He didn’t want, or need, the companionship of the other kids but when they targeted Rick, as they did every day, outside the school he found himself standing over the fallen white boy and shielding him with his own body. It only took one punch, to the larger of the bullies, and the fight was over. He wasn’t even breathing heavily but in one second, he stopped what had been going on for three months. That day formed a bond that he hadn’t asked for, that he hadn’t wanted but, over time, he grew to cherish the friendship and bond that was there with Rick. Years later that bond would become something more, something different, when he realised just how much he loved his boyhood friend. How much they loved each other. Rick sighed, watching the small figures leaving the plane down on the asphalt below. He knew bullies, intimately, and he knew that Natas was the worst of the worst. He hadn’t targeted Rick because of anything that he had done, or said. He hadn’t targeted Rick because he knew anything about him. He did it because he could; because it was a way to get at the man that Rick suddenly recognised below. His heart skipped a beat as he took in the loping stride of the man in black below. He didn’t need to see his face to know him. He could have picked him out of a darkened room simply by the way that he smelled, or by the touch of his hair. He knew him. As he watched him disappear from sight he knew, just as he had known by the tone of voice on the phone, that something was wrong. As the stream of figures began to appear in the arrivals area of the airport Rick sighed, squaring his shoulders and wiping at his eyes. He glanced back at the window, checking out the smile that he had forced onto his face, before turning back to the crowd. His eyes passed over everyone who passed, filing away small details of each of them in a brain that worked like a computer in that regard; Jay had once teasingly called him an idiot savant, gently mocking the remarkable gift that allowed him to take in, filter and store details that he wasn’t even aware that he was seeing. The kiss afterwards had taken the sting out of the not-quite-barbed comment. He wasn’t aware of it happened but, as the last figure came through the long corridor, his pupils dilated and his breathing and heart rate increased. Every single detail – the hint of stubble on a normally closely shaved face, the tangle of hair that had fallen loose of the braid at the back, eyes that were just starting to become bloodshot, the bruise that was forming at the junction of the ear and jaw – was taken in as he ran forwards and pulled the man into an embrace that could, on someone lighter, weaker, have caused damage. As it was it still caused a slight wince of pain. “Unless you are actually trying to hurt me,” Jay said, breathlessly, “you might want to let up a bit.” “Sorry,” Rick smiled, stepping back but not letting go completely. “I’ve just missed you so much.” “It was only a couple of days, Rick,” Jay stated, softly. “A couple of days that we didn’t need to be apart for, though!” “We talked about that,” Jay sighed. “After what Natas did ... after what happened, I didn’t want you at the arena.” “I would’ve been fine …” “You weren’t last time.” Jay reached up, tracing the wound on Rick’s head as he himself had one minutes before hand. “You can’t always me the knight in shining armour, Jay,” Rick reached up and took Jay’s hand in his own, squeezing it reassuringly before bringing it to his face and kissing it gently. “You can’t always protect me, you know.” “I can try …” “Let’s not talk about it now, Jay,” he smiled through another kiss to the hand. “Let’s not argue about it.” “Sorry, it’s just that …” Jay’s words were cut off as Rick leant in and kissed him, slowly and softly, on the mouth. Time stopped and space ceased to exist. It was just Rick and Jay. Just the two heartbeats, pressed close together, and the kiss. “… fags.” With a jolt Rick and Jay were brought back to reality as the word was snarled close to them. Jay stepped bac, abruptly, pulling away from Rick before reaching out to steady him as he nearly stumbled. Glancing around he saw the family moving through the airport, the teenage son staring back at them with a sneer on his face. As Rick stared in shock Jay moved forwards, half-running, before stopping in front of the group, pulling the parents up short. “Don’t you teach your kid any manners?” he barked. “I’m sorry …” The man stuttered, confused, as his wife grabbed his arm in shock. The teenage boy opened and closed his mouth, a fish struggling to breathe out of water, as Jay reached out and poked him in the chest. The last of the family unit, a girl slightly younger than he brother, rushed away from them with her father following, shouting at he to come back. “This little brat just insulted me and my …” he trailed off, catching Rick’s eyes as he approached the gathering too. “Your what?” the woman asked, her voice shaking. “Listen’ mister, I don’t know what you think just happened but we are just here on vacation and don’t want any trouble with your sort.” “My SORT?” Phoenix bit off through clenched teeth, his attention moved from the boy to the mother. “… and just what ‘sort’ is that?” “Why, I don’t know,” she said, looking between Jay and Rick as he reached out to place a calming hand on the taller man’s shoulder. “We were just warned that tourists are sometimes targeted by locals …” “He ain’t a local, mam,” the boy snorted. “He’s one of those poofs.” “You little …” Jay’s words, as loud as they were, were drowned out by the sharp slap of flesh on flesh. The mother’s hand connected with her son’s face, sending his head jerking to one side. “Duncan Bradford Taylor!” she shouted. “You take that back right now.” “… but he is …” “I said take it back, right now,” she repeated. “You apologise to this man, and you do it now!” “… but mam, he was kissing him …” “I don’t care who he was kissing,” she leant in close, pulling the boy’s face around until he was forced to look at Jay and Rick. “It isn’t for you or me to say who is right or wrong for people to love, but it is for me to say what is right or wrong for you to say. Now unless you want your ass tanned when we get to the hotel you apologise to this man right now!” “It’s ok,” Jay said, shaken. “Don’t worry …” “I’m sorry, mister,” the mother said, coldly. “But I don’t recall asking you how best I should bring up my son. Now, Duncan, what do you say?” “… sorry …” “I don’t think that he heard you.” “I heard him, honestly …” Jay tried to say, but was interrupted. “As I said,” she repeated, staring darkly at the boy, “I don’t think that he heard you. Apologise, and do it properly.” “I’m sorry, Mister,” the boy said, voice cracking. “Erm … that’s ok, I suppose,” Jay said, his anger evaporating as the absurdity of the situation hit him. Mollified, the mother let go of her son, sending him off to find his sister, and stood staring directly at Jay’s face. “I’m sorry too, you know.” “You don’t need to …” “I already told you that I didn’t recall asking you what was best to do,” she said, with a small smile. “My brother, his uncle, moved to San Francisco a few years back. He got tired of the intolerance he was put through back at home, in Dakota, because he was gay. Duncan doesn’t know, we haven’t told him yet, but I won’t have him treating a stranger that way. Not because of that. It isn’t right.” “He doesn’t know any better.” Jay stated, softly. “That ain’t no excuse,” she said. “Ignorance ain’t no excuse for hatred. Difference ain’t an excuse for bullying. My brother moved thousands of miles, away from his home and family, to find a place where he could be himself. Be with the person he loved.” She looked between Rick and Jay, eyes soft. “Looks like you two have that as well.” Reaching out Jay took Rick’s hand, squeezing it. “We do.” “Then cherish it,” she said, tears falling slowly down her cheeks. “Cherish each other.” “Are you ok?” Jay asked, reaching out to touch her arm. She pulled back; wiping the tears away sharply as her son and daughter appeared on their way back. “His uncle,” she said, nodding at her son. “My brother. He was killed a few months back. Beaten up outside a club by a gang of kids while waiting for his boyfriend to finish work. He moved all that way just so that he could live his life without fear but it still found him; the fear, the intolerance, it killed him.” “Gods,” Phoenix breathed. “I’m so sorry …” “Don’t be,” she stated, simply. “Don’t be sorry, don’t be afraid. Just be whatever it is that you want to be.” Without looking back she grabbed he kids by their hands and trailed them away. With a sigh Jay turned to Rick, seeing the tears held back by willpower in his eyes. Reaching out he placed a hand gently on his cheek as he leant in close to him. “I was just so afraid, Rick,” he said, breathing the words into Rick’s ear. “When I saw you lying there, bleeding, I realised that I couldn’t stop it – there was nothing that I wouldn’t have done to protect you, to keep you safe, but it wasn’t enough. He hurt you, Rick, and there was nothing that I could do …” “He didn’t hurt me, Jay,” Rick answered, softly. “He hurt us. He went after me to get to you – he went after us!” “It’s my life, Rick,” Jay stated. “I knew what I was getting into when I went back to the business.” “And so did I, Jay.” Rick’s voice was firm. “I have been with you since your first match, remember, I have been in this business as long as you have.” “It isn’t the same, Rick. You don’t need to get hurt like that …” “And neither do you!” Rick stepped back, holding Jay’s face between both hands as he stared directly into his eyes. “At least not alone. We are together, Jay, so it is time to start remembering that.” “What do you mean?” “I mean that, next week,” Rick nodded. “We face this together. We stop being afraid!”
