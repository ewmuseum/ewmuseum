Date: 06-10-09 The Bullhorn Bar and Grill Waco, TX 6th October, 2009 7:27pm Another day, another town, he thought as he sank another shot of Bourbon. Tapping the empty glass against the bar he savoured the burning sensation crawling down his throat and let it soothe his worries, numbing them under a drunken haze. Alerted by the knocking, the bartender shifted past one of his waitresses and collected the bottle on his way over to Von Kelsig. "Same again?" Von Kelsig nodded, "Make it a double...and throw in a beer." he growled. His eyes watered slightly as the remnants of the shot left a bitter taste in his mouth. Hunched over the bar, he sat patiently with his arms folded while his drinks were served up. Extending a tenner between his index and middle fingers he let the barman pick the bill out of his hand and sat for a moment staring at the fizzing bubbles frothing around the neck of his beer. The time on the State-shaped clock behind the bar tocked past seven-thirty. He'd been in Waco three days already, and had spent the last 72 hours pumping iron at the gym. He hadn't seen any other PRIMEates around town, and wondered if that was due to his early arrival. It was routine for him to arrive at the next venue a day or two before the show, maybe meet up with Sloan or anybody that would keep his company but without realizing it he had left ReVolution 208 in Oklahoma and travelled straight to Texas with no detour. He had nowhere else to go, no one to see. The thought of his destiny being tied to this damn career haunted him as he took a refreshing gulp of beer and let out a belch. There had been no word whatsoever from those detectives about the double homicide, and weren't the crushing loneliness of life on the road a constant reminder of what could have been with Gloria, he would have forgotten the whole episode. Lifting the bottle back to his lips he tilted his head back to take another drink and suddenly paused in mid-swig. Lowering his beer he looked over his shoulder in time to see the figure quickly glance away. Thinking nothing of it he regarded his beer for a moment before putting it down in favour of the shot. As the sour warmth washed over him once more he felt a pair of eyes burning a hole in the back of his head and quickly turned to meet the gaze of the man watching him. This time he made eye contact, but only for a split second. The gentleman was seated by himself next to the door, with the same pint in front of him as when Von Kelsig first noticed him three-quarters of an hour earlier when he walked into the bar. His gaunt face wore tiredness like a second skin, but the giant couldn't have known why he tamed such fatigue. Anyone looking that exhausted should be laid up in bed for a while. That, or sparking up another joint. Von Kelsig kept his eye on the tired man, quietly daring him to stare up once more. He had lived a life being stared at and talked about, mostly in awe, but always behind his back. This guy seemed no different, perhaps a nervous loner who happened to enjoy wrestling, or maybe he was just another envious soul that wished he had been built in such a powerful image as Von Kelsig. He could tell by the way the man vacantly gawked at the television in the corner of the bar that he was still in his field of vision and perhaps still the focus of it. He knew the giant had clocked him, and nervously he reached for his beer and took copious gulps of it. Narrowing his eyes, Von Kelsig sneered at the man before turning back to the bar and taking another drink. Seeing the giant's back turned the tired man quickly finished his pint and got up, taking care to let the clicking of the latch be the only sign of his departure. On hearing it Von Kelsig glanced to the table at which the man was seated and then at the exit. As he shifted back around on his stool a feeling of déjà vu suddenly came over him. Something seemed off. He couldn't quite place it, but the fact that someone would walk out just for being noticed definitely warranted suspicion. The strange familiarity of it unsettled him, so much so he couldn't help but linger on it. He had frequented more bars, pubs, taverns, clubs, holes, dives and squats in the past couple of months than he could remember visiting in several years, including the one he owned. When the past became too much to bear a hard session always numbed the pain. But he had been to so many recently, why did the image of this one man resonate so strongly with him? In retrospect he recognized the man's features. He was sure he'd seen him before somewhere but from where, and why? Before he could delve much further into the mystery, a figure brushed past him and took a seat at the next stool. Taking little notice, he rolled the empty beer bottle around in his hands and tried to concentrate, but the bustling atmosphere coupled with a mounting inebriation made it difficult to focus. "Penny for yer thoughts?" Her voice was soft yet firm, and the thick Texan slant on "you" reminded him of Wade Elliott, even if the 'Bama Bruiser was an Alabamian. Reluctantly he looked up. She would have been easy on the eye weren't her gentle features weathered by a tough living. She was clad in ripped denim jeans and a black wifebeater. Stealing a glimpse of her body he noticed she was short and stocky, framed not in fat but thick muscle that emphasized her curves. Meeting her gaze he smiled politely as she nudged him. Her long, thick curly hair was held at bay by a confederate bandana tied around her forehead, the biggest clue that she was a raging tomboy. "What's'a matter, cat got yer tongue?" She asked behind a ballsy smile. "Something like that." Furrowing her brow, she noticed the empty shot glass next to the bottle of Lone Star. A shrill whistle pierced the air as she motioned the bartender over to them. "Double up the Viking an' gimme a pitcher, Waylon." She ordered, emphatically slamming a fist onto the mahogany. "Comin' right up Luanne." Waylon replied, already angling the jug under the tap. As his beers and shots were placed in front of him Von Kelsig felt slightly awkward as Luanne grabbed the jug in both hands and held it to her lips, condensation dripping off onto her bust as she greedily guzzled down her drink. A loud burp followed as she took a much needed breath and set the pitcher back down on the bar. "Now ya owe me. Howdy, I'm Luanne." She smiled again, extending a hand. "Hi," He began, taking her masculine hand in his own massive paw, "I'm..." "Angelus Von Motherfuckin' Kelsig, a.k.a. Hessian. Largest athlete in the industry today and two-time PTC Champion." Von Kelsig's jaw slackened with surprise. In response Luanne giggled a husky, tobacco-ravaged giggle. Lifting one of the shots he raised it and clinked the glass against her pitcher. "Cheers." They toasted one another, and Von Kelsig immediately followed up the first shot with another. He did his best not to pucker his lips at the bitterness, while Luanne continued guzzling from her jug. He was a little unsure about her, merely because she was the first woman to approach him in a very long time. He had it on good authority that his presence in social gatherings intimidated most women who couldn't see past the giant imposing frame, the scars and the beard. "That's some body you've got, you a body builder or something?" He asked, ignoring his troubling doubts and politely reciprocating her kindness. "Got it in one hoss!" She grinned, flexing a bicep, "State Champ three years runnin'. Got me second place at the Nationals last year, shoulda won it overall but tha judges figured a dickless 'roid monkey deserved it more." "Say what?" Von Kelsig said with a chuckle. "Oh, you should've seen this chick. She was huge! I didn't think nothin' of it 'til rumours started flyin' around 'bout the same time as the finals. Nobody never proved nothin' though so Queen Kong went on to win the competition. I got a lotta support from some of the other girls though fer workin' like a bitch to beat 'er. Fuck it, there's always next year!" Again Luanne raised her pitcher with a whoop and took another drink. Von Kelsig couldn't help but smile as he started on the first of his beers. Her obvious fun-loving charisma separated her from that category he was automatically depositing women into, that being the "Slut bitch" category started off by Gloria. "So...tell me a bit about the man behind the beard." Luanne continued after a short pause, shooting a doe-eyed gaze at Von Kelsig. He felt a warmth was over his face and quickly realized it wasn't the alcohol. He was blushing. Meanwhile, outside the establishment the tired man was sitting across the street in his parked up Pontiac, a cell phone plastered to his ear.
