Gods and Monsters

27 January 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 27, 2009 The speck appeared far off in the distance. A tiny, blue blip across a vast field of stretching white. Tyler Rayne’s left eyebrow twitched. The spasm occurred eerily close to the location of his newest scar. Superheated glass, propelled by the explosion of his California beach house, had torn a deep gash through his flesh. Though now healed, the wound would forever be remembered as a pale discoloration slashing up through the hairs of his eyebrow. That was a month ago. A week after the California incident Pierce Lavelle called. Tyler’s loft in Manhattan was torched in a still unexplained bit of arson. That was the catalyst. The hangman dropping out the gallows floor. Next was Seattle. Then Chicago. Miami. Indianapolis. Las Vegas. Myrtle Beach. Denver. Lincoln, Nebraska. Gardiner, Montana. A half dozen others. Every property Tyler owned within the continental United States toasted in the span of two weeks. If your quarry goes to ground, leave no ground to go to. A predator was lurking somewhere in the dark corners of Tyler’s life. An overenthusiastic shadow player lacking the true skill of the hunt. Tyler Rayne had been running for most of his life. He had learned long ago that the best way to stay one step ahead was to simply keep moving. He traveled all over the world with the biggest wrestling promotion in the US. He had almost two dozen residential properties stretched across the country. Not to say anything of his international properties. Safe havens, every one. Each week was a new city. A different state. Maybe even an entirely different country. Constantly moving. Constantly staying one step ahead of the threat reaching up from behind. This hunter lacked the necessary skill to subdue his prey. The hunter could not keep up with Tyler Rayne. Until two weeks ago. What this menace lacked in skill it made up for with efficient ruthlessness. One by one his havens were taken away. A surgically executed series of strikes that left Tyler Rayne with nowhere else to run. His options had become severely limited. In this limitation, the hunter would find his victory. There were only so many places Tyler had left to turn. Less than a handful of viable options. These, too, would be eliminated, of that Tyler had little doubt. Slowly. Perhaps more strategically. Inevitably, though, every haven would be burned. He would be left with nothing. Alone in an open field. A lamb awaiting slaughter. The soft crunch of compacted snow alerted him to the approach. The bright blue jacket served as a beacon in contrast to the muted sheen of snow. The only speck of color for miles. Tyler watched as the downtrodden traveler powered against the elements. Rubber boots took hulking steps forward, pushing and smashing their way through six inches of snow and ice. The traveler’s face was covered in both a black ski mask and the glowing blue hood of the jacket. His eyes betrayed an escalating uncertainty, despite the confidence with which he strode slowly forward. Thick, wool-lined gloves clutched tightly around the white plastic handle of his red Thermos cooler. The very same insulated container he used to store lunches for work. Loyalty pushed him forward through the harsh terrain of the Great North. Fealty and hope unwittingly passed this weary traveler beneath the snow covered branches of another enormous evergreen. From one of those branches Tyler Rayne stood. Tyler’s foot found nothing but air when he stepped away from the perch. Gravity grabbed fast, pulling his two hundred pound frame toward the cold field below. He made no attempt to stop the descent. He only turned, arms stretched upward, to watch the blue coat’s determined hike. Gravity pulled him down. Tyler’s fingers wrapped against frozen lengths of wood. Gravity could no longer claim dominion. His direction shifted forward. The body propelled with extra force by his downward momentum, now converted to this new push. Energy cannot be created nor destroyed. Only transferred. The things you’ll learn in junior high science. The tree shook violently at the unexpected weight. Snow fell in sheets from the vibrating branches of a twenty foot pine. The traveler looked up to investigate the commotion. He could see nothing but powdery white. Wouldn’t have seen the rugged soles of Tyler’s boots rushing forward. Even if he had, what could have been done to stop the rush of contact? The boots smashed solidly against the traveler’s solar plexus. Energy transferred from Tyler to the chest hidden beneath that blue coat. The impact knocked him off his own feet. The grip on his lunchbox loosened and failed. The traveler fell, aching and lacking breath, into the drifts of snow. The disturbed collection of flakes collapsed upon him. Tyler, too, sank deep within the winter’s blanket. Not nearly enough to stop him, though. Tyler’s own gloved hand was wrapped around the plastic handle before he’d even absorbed the impact of the fall. His arm swung out from the side with a carefully calculated speed. The traveler sat up exactly as expected. When expected. The cooler exploded with devastating force against his jaw. Ten plus pounds of fluid contents swilled and tumbled with the motion. The cooler turned as its contents shifted. Plastic rolled across the traveler’s face. Teeth jostled loose. His bottom jaw cracked beneath the combined might of Tyler Rayne and the shifting weight of the cooler. The container burst as well. It had never been intended to withstand such impact. Seven pouches of human blood released free into the cold air. Scattered and lost within the drifts. Had the traveler noticed, he would have been terribly distraught. Unfortunately, all he knew now was pulsing red and blinding pain. His attacker loomed above. The shadow was imposing. Frightening as it lowered toward him. There was a sudden stinging pain in his neck. Flaming hot blood coursed through his veins. His vision cleared. The mind-numbing pain relented. For the moment. The traveler looked up into the eyes of his attacker. Colder than anything he’d ever known. Buried six inches deep in snow. Lost in the frozen wastes of Canada. Those eyes were colder still. His brain sent the command to his spine. There should have been a shiver. A physical representation of the chilling stone that settled in his gut. His body did not respond. Nothing below his neck would respond. A glint caught his eye. The sun reflected off the needle point of a syringe. "Don’t worry, Mr. Stevens. It won’t kill you. It’s a paralyzing agent. Prevents your brain from sending signals to the rest of your body. You won’t be able to move for a bit. Feel? Sure. Move? Not so much." The voice was devoid of all emotion. Though the inflection was perfect… it sounded so alien. Foreign. There was no anchor to the voice. Nothing that tied it to humanity. To feeling. "Who… who … who are.." Words were almost impossible. Tyler offered an amused smile at the attempt, though. Such futility. He busied himself with removing the leather wrap from his jacket. Unrolled it on the blanket of snow. "I’m going to ask you questions, Mr. Stevens. You are going to answer them. I believe that you have information vital to my survival. Information I wish to learn. First though, as a show of good faith, I will answer your question. We met, you and I, five nights ago at a club in Detroit. The Coven. I bumped into you. You took exception. There were some choice words exchanged. Nothing physical. You left the bar in your drunken stupor. I left with your wallet. Your name is Malcolm Stevens. You work in a blood clinic in one of the seedier neighborhoods of Detroit. You live in the basement of a rundown slum apartment by choice. You spend your earnings on books and DVDs centered around the exploits of vampires. You have an obsession, Mr. Stevens. You’ve been going to The Coven every Friday night for the last three years. Hoping against hope the rumors were true. Five days ago was the very first time you were privileged to meet the owner. A man by the name of Se7yn." Two items are removed from the leather wrapping. A cigar cutter and a mini-torch. The first Tyler admires as he continues his tale. The second he sets on the traveler’s chest. "I didn’t know for sure what the two of you spoke of until now. Understand that I am not fucking around, Mr. Stevens. I cannot impress that upon you enough." Malcolm Stevens no longer has control of his own body. When Tyler Rayne lifts the traveler’s right hand and removes the glove… there is nothing to be done about it. When he slides the cigar cutter down around Malcolm’s middle finger… there is nothing but anticipation of the inevitable. The cut is quick and clean. So much so that the pain doesn’t even register until a second and a half later. The scream is unimaginable. Inhuman. Too bad there isn’t a soul within miles to hear him. The cigar cutter is set aside. The mini-torch is ignited. Tyler cauterizes the wound. "Oh God… Oh God… Oh God… Oh God…" "That’ll be enough of that, Mr. Stevens." "Oh God… Oh God… Oh God… Oh God…" "I said enough." "Oh God… Oh God… Oh God… Oh God…" "ENOUGH! I am your god now you miserable little shit. I need to know one thing. How did Se7yn find all of my safe houses? No one knew. Not even Katt. How did he find them?" "I… I… I don’t…" He doesn’t even know where the knife came from. Malcolm only knows that his abdomen is suddenly split open. He only knows the searing pain of the gash. The harsh bite of the cold rushing into the wound. He knows Hell. He knows pain unimaginable. He knows the touch of Tyler Rayne’s fingers on his inner organs. "AAAAAAAAARRRRRRRRRRRRRAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAGGGGGGGHHHHHHHHH!!!!!!!!!" "I assume you don’t know the story of Prometheus, Mr. Stevens. You dropped out of high school your sophomore year. Prometheus was a Titan. He stole fire from the gods of Olympus and gifted it to man. As a fitting punishment, Zeus ordered that Prometheus be chained to a boulder. Every day a giant eagle would peck at his flesh. Eat his liver. Every night Prometheus would grow a new liver. New skin. He would be healed… and the next day the eagle would feast again." Tyler’s fingers jabbed deeper into the wound. "AAAAAAAAARRRRRRRRRRRRRAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAGGGGGGGHHHHHHHHH!!!!!!!!!" "You would be absolutely amazed to learn how true that story can be. My patience runs thin." "I heard him… he said… something about the Nine. … I think." "The Son of Nine." Tyler wasn’t entirely surprised to learn of the connection. It made a certain amount of sense. After all, the Nine’s ninjas were the ones who had jumped him back at The Katana’s Point. The night they’d taken Drake. Where was the connection, though? How did the Son of Nine know any more about Tyler’s houses than… "The kid." It was the gods damned kid. "Fuck." He should have known. Should have figured it out sooner. It was so… obvious. Tyler stood. He didn’t even bother packing up his supplies. Just stood and tromped away. "You can’t… you can’t just leave me here!" "I’ve learned what I needed to, Mr. Stevens. Though, as a token of my appreciation… one final lesson. Learn to believe. Have faith. You came out here because you believe Se7yn is everything he claims to be. You trudged through this barren waste because he promised you immortality. You came here to worship a monster you’ve placed as god. So have faith in your god, Mr. Stevens. Believe in him. If he is what he claims to be, he will smell the blood on the snow. He will come. And he will find you." "If… if he’s not? What if… what if he doesn’t… doesn’t come?" "Then the wolves will."
