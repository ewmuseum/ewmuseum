PRIME — Roleplay

Many welcomes (The Real Black Angel)

4 October 2004
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Until now, I have remained seperate to the PTC community, bar the occasional lurk and glance through a few shows. The reason I say hello now is because I have a pair of RPs that I consider my finest writing thus far, and I would like to see how those outside PTC view them. Obviously, these won't have full significance if you havn't been following PRIME, but it would be hypocritical for me to blame you fine people for that, wouldn't it? Enjoy... The judges stare down at the young man from behind a tall curved wall of wood, their faces completely emotionless, their eyes hard, completely unmoving. Past the blinking of the eyes, one might think the five near-identical men mannequins, made to stare down at the space below them with the gaze of hawks. In that space, a man is seated on a chair in an otherwise empty space, head down, long blonde hair hiding face. “We have decided the crime of the accused.” The middle judge’s voice, deep and resonant. The man looks up at the speaker, his face just as stony, his blue eyes just as cold, as those above him. “The court deems the accused a traitor against himself.” The man does not blink. There is nothing odd about this statement to him. “You are the one who claims to be one with the Tao…” The judge on the far right speaks up. “Balance” The one sitting right of the lead judge. “Serenity” The far left. “Peace” The close left. “Calm” All four at once. “These were your mind.” The apparent leader speaks again. “But we have watched you. We have seen every single action make a mockery of your own self. In your careful considerations, these thoughts you still hold help maintain your knowledgeable balance, you are finding, in every shadowy corner of your mind, these doubts and imperfections that cause your self to crumble to dust.” “Chaos” “Confusion” “Disorder” “Anger” “These are your mind.” The man below the judges lets this wash over him, still refusing to show emotion. There is no denial or rage. He just stares… There is a silence. The judges await the accused to fill the void. “I may be guilty of some of what you claim…” The hall-filling sound of all five at once. “You are.” “… But I am only human. There is no destruction of what I am. I have stayed true.” “The stories tell otherwise.” “The stories?” Still he has not shown any hint of worry. “Stories of others. Those around you who have seen you break, and have paid the price for your actions. You cannot be trusted to know yourself. Only others may know you for what you are.” “That much is true.” “First witness… the samurai.” From the darkness darknes?, a figure emerges. Clad in elaborate leather and metal armour, head covered by an ornamental helmet with the face of a demon. “Speak of the accussed’s crime.” The samurai mask seemed to nod. Then the tone came, distant, wavering… “I pledged myself to honour. I was determined. I was strong. But my honour was scarred. My duty, as a samurai, was to end my life. But this man… he took into his lies. Told me he was my ally. Told me I did not have to commit my duty. And I believed him. But in reality, he was selfish, and manipulating me.” On the floor, the accused face is contorting with confusion and the slightest hint of worry. “All he wanted was the reward. He wanted the honour of killing me himself.” “I would never do such a thing.” The mask turns to face him. “The Blade in my back says different.” Slowly, like a clockwork man, the man in samurai armour spins around. And there it is. A bloodied Katana, stuck into the small of his back. The accused takes a moment to take it in before jerking backwards, shaken by the horrific sight. “You denied me my honourable death so you could stand over my corpse and take the honour for yourself.” “No.” “You told me you were another samurai. One of honour. And maybe you think that. But you were too greedy to serve.” “No.” “Your glory was more important than everything I am.” “No.” “You killed me.” “No!” An outburst of Anger. The left judge laughs, high above him. “Anger? That is not in the nature you claim to have preserved.” The Accused looks up, even now trying to suppress the anger in his eyes. “These are lies.” “They are all truths. The fact that you do not know this shows how far you have gone” The head-filling voice of the centre judge. “You sacrificed another so you may rise. That is pride, selfishness and deception.” The accused shakes his head, but says no more. What point is there? nothing can live and speak with a sword in the back… what was that? He’d heard it before. Like his own, but it was detached, somehow separate, like - “The second witness – the man of two faces.” The second figure… a man dressed in dull brown, covering his whole body, diverting all attention to his face. A mask, like the cork masks worn in ancient Greek theatre, ones created to show a single a single emotion without any doubt. The upturned eyes, the upward curve of a thickly lined moth – a happy face. “You are my friend. My ally. You give me help, and I shall help you. You trust me. Do you remember me?” The accused nods. The mask hides his face, but the voice and stance are somebody he knows… his identity just below consciousness. “ Yes… I think I do. “You see? I can be trusted. Trust…” The accused blinks. “Snivelling fool.” A harsh mask, eyes like pointed daggers, mouth pulled into a sneer. “Don’t you see? I have gotten what I want. You have served me well. Very well indeed. If I can stand there behind you and allow you to bring me success, then it is no bother to me to listen to your ramblings a mask cannot just change and act like I actually give a damn about you and your instabilities then it’s a low price to pay. But make no mistake, as soon as you fail me, you are gone. Just like you have to those who trusted you, you will be stabbed in the back.” “I believe that speaks for itself” the near right judge. “You are taken in by this man. Despite your own duplicity, you are still open to the lies and tricks of others. To us, this does not suggest one who is a wise observer. It speaks of gullibility, of foolishness.” The accused stands, unable to keep that hint of anger and despair off his face. “I am weak because there are those who will deceive me?!” “You are weak because you do not see it.” The accused again is defeated. Before again taking hs seat, he shoots dagger eyes of his own into the mask. He does not fully know this man, but “I trusted you.” He knows this much. The mask does not change, but the done of his voice is beyond doubt a smirk. “Of course. I would not have it any other way.” “The third witness – the jester.” Indeed, the figure that emerges from the shadows this time seems to be on the verge of dancing a jig with every movement, and is clothed in the red and yellow outfit of a medieval era court jester, complete with bells on his hat that jingle when he moves. “Speak of the accused’s crimes. The jester giggled with a high, cracking, warbling voice, like a Hyena’s laugh. “Yer honours, I am just a humble maker of mischief, born to entertain the people throughout my days. And that can be a tough job, particularly after happy hour at the local tavern!” Another madcap giggle. Just your humble joker. But I’ve done a teeny bit of swordplay in my time and the accused, well, he fancies himself as a burly fighting man himself. So once or twice, so he’s thrown down the gauntlet to me a couple of times, and I may be only a humble court jester, but whom I to deny the whim of a man as “honourable” as the accused. Well, to jump to the point your honours, I’ve actually beaten this man! Me! The jester trounces the fighter! I’ll tell ya this much your honours, that’s funnier than any of my jokes! “Is not this interesting?” booms the voice of the head judge “How many times has the accused challenged you, jester?” “How many cow’s tails does it take to make a rope to the moon, your honour? Quite a few. And let me say that apart from one of two occasions, both in a manner more dubious than a lady of the night’s affections, there are no walls I’ve bested this fighter.” “I would not sink to cheating to defeat some fool!” The accused exclaims. “I have there is only blackness… honour.” Where was that voice coming from? And what was it trying to tell him? “The samurai has already debunked that claim, accused. Be silent.” The words of one of the lesser judges cause the accused to lapse back into silence. “This is very interesting, is it not? Not only do sink to repeatedly challenging a man who we would presume is so far beneath a man of your perceived status. And you are defeated. The accused speaks up “I was forced to face this man. It was not my choice.” “And yet still you are defeated.” “I am not superhuman. The jester has surprising skill.” “But you are supposedly a fighter - you should defeat even a skilful amateur easily. The fact that you cannot shows that you are weak. It is that simple.” “I shall fight this man again. And I shall win.” The judge simply smiles “That shows your denial. You cannot accept that, a man supposed to be your inferior is in fact your superior. If you keep fighting, you will win. But there is no honour in denial.” “Final witness – the Lord” The accused looks towards the previous witnesses. He looks ahead – and there is the lord, a red-haired figure in a golden crown, standing before him. His voice is deep and head-filling, like the head judges’, but richer. “I am the Lord. I have become the Lord by earning the Love of the people, and proving myself greatest in combat against the previous Lord. But there are those that wish to deny me my crown. The accused is one of them.” “I have no need of your titles.” “Yet you hunger for it so. You wish to defeat me in combat and take all my glory for yourself. The root is, that despite your fake wisdom, you still want to be like me. You still wish to be the one who is adored and respected. That is the reason for your failings. That is why you stab the back of the samurai. That is why you trust the two faced man. You want the title of Lord so badly. But you shall not have it. For you are weak.” “I am not weak.” the accused snaps back. “You are confused and foolish.” “No I am not! “You are pathetic and greedy…” “NO!” The Lord gives him only a pitiful glance “And you are in denial.” “And that is the final piece of evidence. Despite your attempts to be close to balance and serenity, you have been swayed by the same petty rewards as the mortals below you. They have taken over your head and clouded your all-important judgement. That is the foundation of all your flaws and chaos. The time for decision has arrived. We deem you, the Black Angel…” All four spoke at once “Innocent.” Black Angel looks up, hope in his eyes. “Innocent?” “How could it be otherwise? The Black Angel is the perfect side, the flawless side. He, in himself cannot be corrupted. What is wrong is the persona hidden behind the mask has leaked.” “What are you talking about?” The judge smirks “Is your wisdom so far gone you cannot even remember that the Black Angel is your creation? A division of you. He was created to be perfect, so as he was created as a distinct set of values, anything breaking those is wrong. The accused looked down. He knew what was coming next. “The Black Angel cannot accept any responsibility. There is only one man who has the guilt here. And that is you… Aaron Fletcher. The court finds you… All at once. “Guilty.” What does it mean? It means horror. It’s the worst thing possible. Why? What is it? The judges. They laugh. Cold laughter. Cruel laughter. It fills his head yet again. They’re amused by his horror. The breaking of Aaron Fletcher… The witnesses laugh. The samurai. His mask falls off… to show not an oriental face, but a Caucasian one. With a beard, and an Ankh tattooed on the throat. Adam. Laughing at him. Laughing at the weaknesses of Aaron Fletcher. “No…” The Greek mask clatters to the floor. This face is an oriental man. Young, vibrant. Joshua Taro Freedom. His last ally. Laughing at how easily he is fooled. “No.” The jester hat falls away. Joey Troy. Laughing. Laughing as always. But not with joy or amusement. With malice. Laughing at the devoted Aaron Fletcher and how easily he is toppled. And how easily he shall fall again. “No!” And the crown is placed down. Tony Rolo. The untouchable one. Laughing at this pathetic man’s visions of grandeur. Laughing. There is only laughing, the whole universe is laughing nothingleftbutlaughingnothingnothinglaughingatmela ughingatmyweakness The faces expand, fill everything this can’t be happening, until there is nothing but their faces laughing. Everything is laughing at him. This can’t happen Aaron Fletcher falls to his knees and clutches his head, sobbing at the nothing. “Nononono this isn’t real nononono this is… a dream! nononononono |It’s all a dream! nonononononono… It’s a… --- “-Dream” He knew where he was before he could even see. Another nightmare. Images drifting around a head… nothing. How horrible it had been to watch himself fail, to know things were wrong but only be able to command himself to wake too late. He drifts back to the soft bed below him, his mind full of pink fog, slipping back into sleep. But those images will not go away. They keep stirring around, every detail filling his thoughts. It is said that sometimes our dreams can show us thoughts that are bottled up. If that was true… he staggered out of bed. The cold water splashed into his face, a wake-up call to every nerve. It had been a dream about weaknesses. Those in his past turning his every action into a flaw and making him weak. All the strengths he had we’re being picked apart, made out as lies – no, that wasn’t right, he thought as he looked at his haunted eyes in the mirror. They weren’t his strengths. They weren’t Aaron Fletcher’s strengths. They were the Black Angel’s strengths, and it was Aaron who was making them decay. That was the thrust of the dream, wasn’t it. That’s how he was “a traitor to himself.” Selfishness. Foolishness. Weakness. These were the things that Aaron Fletcher contaminated the perfection of the Black Angel with. But how could such things be true? He hadn’t “killed” the honourable Adam’s career for his own glories – it was so Adam could have a worthy exit. The fact that the number one contendership was on the line had been a coincidence. And Freedom – under the mask, there lurked a trustworthy man – had he not worn a mask, that showed the arrogant, selfish side was a façade? That was what he told himself. But if dreams are formed of our inner thoughts, of things that can only emerge when we cannot guard against them, then those judgements where what he secretly thought – knew, even. It was mind bending. Was his last match against Adam, despite all that he told himself, for his own purposes? Did he really feel that Joshua would abandon him? He’d spent so long saying that, despite his foolish nature, Joey Troy was a skilled athelete, his equal, and there was no shame in conceding defeat, particularly not when you had defeated him in return. But it was true he sometimes thought of Troy as a Jester. Did he really know that all his devotion was worth nothing, only making him an inferior to an unfocused amateur? And did he know he was below Rolo’s level? Aaron knew he was too impulsive before he entered PRIME. That was why the Black Angel existed – to provide the balance in Fletcher’s raging storms of emotion. And for a time, it had worked perfectly. But he was not perfect. Eventually, Black Angel was forced by circumstances to question his actions. That was the beginning – to question yourself was to destroy certainty so central to Angel. A crack had appeared in the mask. And things seep through the cracks – in this case, Fletcher had slipped into Angel. Fletcher was a “good kid”, passionate about wrestling. It was him that lusted for the title, him that trusted, him that told himself that any opponent he lost to was just as good as him… He came out of the bathroom, and came to sit by two objects – a thin white mask, and the PRIME tag title. This coming Revolution, he would face the Jester, in the most important match he had fought yet in PRIME – here, the prize he won from Adam was cemented, and he would be not below the Lord, but level – challenging him. All by beating Joey Troy. One he had faced so often, and so often lost to. But he could defeat Troy back, and the golden glitter before him was proof. He had pinned Troy ‘s shoulders to the mat for three seconds, that was all. But mostly, the success had been down to a lightning fast reaction and calculated thought that had saved Joshua Taro Freedom. A perfect move. Most Black Angel like. So the Black Angel could defeat Troy. According to the judge, the Black Angel was a perfect side. So if the Black Angel came out on Revolution – not Aaron Fletcher, then Joey Troy would fall. But wait – had Black Angel not fallen to Troy right at the start of his PRIME career – when the Black Angel was still pure? Had Aaron Fletcher contaminated the Black Angel from the start? Had this contamination been the reason why belts had been beyond him, and only appeared to him when a match was won by Black Angel’s perfect sharpness? Is that why it had taken him so long – from one King of Kings to the next to reach the summit of PRIME? Interesting. That would mean that at Revolution, it would be Black Angel in his true form – for the first time ever – that fought Troy. And if an impure Angel had gotten so far, then surely the true power would be enough to crush all before him. At Revolution, the match would be, in the eyes of the fans who could only see with sight, Black Angel vs. Joey Troy. But it would be Aaron Fletcher who would face a challenge - to don this mask and let it consume him, to let the true Black Angel enter him. Then – victory. You cannot deny yourself – Aaron Fletcher could not escape the verdict of weakness Aaron Fletcher’s mind had passed. But perhaps the Black Angel could. Fade to Black.
