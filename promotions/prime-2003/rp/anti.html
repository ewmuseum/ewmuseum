Anti

8 July 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 8th There's a bluebird in my heart that Wants to get out But I pour whiskey on him and inhale Cigarette smoke And the whores and the bartenders And the grocery clerks Never know that He's In there. - Excerpt from Charles Bukowski’s Bluebird. You are Jason Natas. Your are PRIME’s Anti-Superstar. A man whom happy-go-lucky babyfaces no better than to cross. A man with a mean-streak so wide it makes the Grand Canyon seem like a pothole. A man who’s unrivalled ferocity and stiffness makes him a foe that not many men relish facing. A man who fucking hates his job. You hadn’t realised it until last week, until Troy Douglas ended your quest for the Intense Title in a fraction of a second, but now there is little doubt in your mind that there is no future for you in professional wrestling. ReVolution 165 was supposed to be your night. It was supposed to be your big chance to show the world that you’re more than just another face in the crowd, more than just another jobber to the stars. It was supposed to be the night where you finally broke the hex and, for the first time in your young professional career, made everyone stand up and take notice. It didn’t happen. You were soundly beaten by a competitor who outclassed you in just about every department, and you were the only person in the world that didn’t see it coming. Nobody expected Jason Natas to usurp Troy Douglas in Tennessee except you. You thought, that having met him several times in the ring already, that you’d be able to learn from your mistakes and finally pick up that ever-elusive singles match victory. You were wrong. Jimmy Bonafide shouldn’t be as strong an opponent as Douglas, but he’ll still put up a fight nonetheless. If you’re able to channel your frustrations, you should be able to put him to sleep with relative ease, given your disdain for the PosterBoy. Despite your technical shortcomings you are not a complete bust, and if you can put your anger and tenacity to good use you could yet become a force in this business. But you won’t. And you know why, don’t you? You just don’t care anymore. You don’t care that you posses what is perhaps the current worst singles match record in PRIME. You don’t care that the fans don’t give a flying fuck about you. You don’t care that your opponents show you no respect, either on-air or off-camera. The fire that once burned within has been all but extinguished. After UltraViolence rolled around, you were filled with enough piss and vinegar to sink a battleship. You were going to use your frustration at your stop/start wrestling career as a weapon, you were going to show everybody that you’re much more than Bryan Dawkins’ personal bitch. Were. Now the thought of being a mere blip on anyone’s radar doesn’t piss you off anymore. You’ve lost the one thing that had kept you bobbing along in this industry, and now you’re quite happy to sit out the remaining months of your contract, collect a nice, fat paycheque while “mailing it in” night after night. The mere thought of continuing in this sport sickens you. Just thinking about spending another night in an arena full of the preening, egomaniacal pretty boy pricks you share the show with makes you skin crawl. You clearly underestimated just how odious the average professional wrestler is in real life when you agreed to join PRIME in the first place. In fact, as you look back over a life of grime, it seems like becoming a professional wrestler is near enough the only regret you let your mind accommodate. ~~~ “I don’t get it, buddy, I really don’t,” Jason observed wearily as the barkeep he knew only as “Rusty” drizzled one measure of bourbon over the top of a couple of fresh ice cubes. “You’re on national television every week, you have any idea how many kids would kill to be involved with something like that?” “Yeah, well, think I’m gonna quit this shit anyway, had enough ‘a…” Natas began, but his intoxicated tones soon tailed off into nothing as he found himself unable to finish his sentence. Rusty slid the small glass over to Jase, who instinctively reached down into his jeans to grab his wallet and tossed the barkeeper a couple of crumpled dollar bills. Jason accepted the drink with a polite and soon felt the familiar burn of the liquor as it hit the back of his throat. As he swallowed, Jason took a look around the bar he had been frequenting for the past couple of nights. It was almost dead, save for a couple of down-and-outs propped up at the other end of the bar. Good, Jason thought, just how I like it; nice an’ quiet. He knew he’d probably have to move on in a couple of nights anyway; once the locals caught wind that a PRIME wrestler had made the place his public house of choice they’d be all over it like flies to dog shit. Another occupational hazard of the professional wrestler. Without a second thought The Anti-Superstar tipped the remaining contents of the glass down his throat. “Gimme another.” “Sorry, pal, I think you’ve had enough tonight,” retorted Rusty much to Jason’s surprise. He observed the barman taking a somewhat defensive stance, his arms folded. As he felt a sneer contort across his features, Jason considered biting back at the barman for a split second. That thought soon left him, however. Whereas just a couple of weeks ago he probably would’ve jumped down the poor guy’s throat at his last comment he found his desire for confrontation – much like his desire to cultivate a career as a wrestler – was dead and gone. He accepted his fate quietly and moved out into a brisk Bronx evening. Jason glanced at his watch – it was already gone 1am and he was due to travel out for ReVolution 166 the following morning. He should probably hit the sack already… Fuck it, Jason thought, what harm’s another couple ‘a drinks gonna do? ~~~ You dealt with your problems last night the same way you always did; by dousing them with alcohol, and as usual it’s got you absolutely nowhere. It’sa nice little placebo, sure, but what was it going to achieve in the long-run? You sure as hell won’t be able to answer that question by glaring at the bottom of a bottle, anyway. When you dragged yourself home and found yourself face-to-face with a mirror, you couldn’t believe how low this whole thing had left you feeling. Usually you’re the kind of guy to take things like this in his stride, but it seems like your failure, thus far, as a wrestler has really rattled your cage. You’re doomed to a sport that you can no longer tolerate until you contract expires, and those eight months are going to feel like an eternity. Potemkin aren’t going to give a shit about what you’ve been thinking recently. You’re just a product to them, nothing more. Just a neat little way for three fucks with far too much money to dip their toe in an industry that is mostly foreign to them. Signing on with these corporate jerk-offs was another mistake, you know that. For as long as you’re in this industry you’ll be nothing more than their puppet; the slightest breach and they’re going to sue you for all that you’ve got. Part of you wants to put in the effort for their sake; you know that they’ve taken a big risk dragging a street urchin like you from the gutter and giving you this golden opportunity. But all sympathetic thoughts soon evaporate when you realise that they’re the ones that brought you down to this new level in the first place. All you ever wanted to do was crack a few skulls on a national level, and now these idiots wanted to turn you into some kind of sports entertaining pantomime act. Well, fuck them. Your name is Jason Natas. And in your current mindset, you’ll never amount to anything in this business.
