The Characters Of Battle (Chapter III)

7 May 2009

Date: 5/7/9 The Characters Of Battle (Chapter III) A hundred and some odd years ago, the world was storied to be flat, and nowadays, scholars say it\'s more of an oval. The oval is the circle\'s rectangle, and you can look at ovals and say they\'re ovals and say the same thing about rectangles, but there\'s only a fine point that divides the two insomuch that you can watch a shape become another shape, but only as it approaches and then again where it passes. There\'s a blind spot in the eye of the storm, and it makes sense. Once you\'re thrown in, it spins you around, knocks the guff from your craw and spits you out, six ways from sunday to only god knows where or nowhere at all. The fact of the matter, as a matter of fact, is that things venture in with a force great enough to penetrate a cyclone, said force and cyclone each self-perpetuating. Still, all lines pointed in towards the bottom of the funnel, the refuse, in this case, builds up, pushes out and layers on top of itself. Thus, the interwoven fabric of circles and squares is a fabric that weaves other sheets atop itself and stitched through, quilting to a block where the shapes of the subcutaneous hems and seams appear only whence cut. If an arbitrary area of this block is broken down into its intertwining sheets, the stitchwork may be cohesively patterned, but the sheets, when combined, are done so in a manner that the misalignment of threads create a more complex spiderwebbing of veins that form cloaked shapes: circles made of squares and vice versa and amalgamations betwixt them. Within those veins float vesicules, and when interlocked, these circulate through not only the loops and pacings of their own shapes, but freely, also, from sheet to sheet. The outlines stay roughly the same while their compositions transfuse. Multidimensionally speaking, the outlines are their own free-floating entities within a larger vascular system. Thus, like a single sheet of figurative fabric woven with concentric squares and circles expanding along both axes in infinite and retaliative enclosure that checkerboards out across time and space, the massive figure amasses through its own fabrication with equally fluid arrangements surfacing, definable only through estimations projecting a theoretical whole. Some say it\'s the eye cast inward. *** I looked up at the sky, and it looked like it was caving in. I could have been waiting for a bus. I couldn\'t remember for certain, but I was sitting on a bench. It was where a distraught man with moldy, green teeth had once stumbled across the street to relay to me that his glasses had been stolen, and it was where I had once waited for a bus before. Logically, it seemed, I was there waiting for a bus, but when the possibility arose that I may also have been waiting for a distraught man, I suddenly wasn\'t so sure. It definitely wasn\'t a pleasant thing to be waiting for, I surmised, but neither was a bus. Whatever I was waiting for, it wasn\'t going to be pleasant. I felt around in my pocket, where I had kept a knife from the kitchen. I wasn\'t wearing any glasses. I never wore glasses. Perhaps mine had been stolen. Like most areas where you can\'t tell whether or not you were waiting for a bus, this area was no different. It invited danger. I kept on the lookout for a man wearing two pairs of glasses as a sure sign of danger, but it was no use. With his eyesight augmented, he\'d see me well before I saw him. A bus stopped in front of me and all sorts of people began filtering out of it, and I studied each one of them carefully. Most of them walked along in one direction or the other, some bumbled about more than others. One man stood and opened an umbrella. It made me realize that it was raining. \"Excuse me, sir. Do you have the time?\" he presumed. \"The time for what?\" I drizzled, wrought with beguile by the man under the belled shape of his umbrella. \"The time that it is right now.\" \"Can\'t say I have any time at all,\" I clumsily tongued. \"Well, do you know if the other bus has passed yet?\" he pointed, firming his lips against a frown, to the crossing street. I shrugged and looked to my right, reflexing to the will of his pointing. I almost turned into a man, who had snuck up on the bench and was now sitting beside me. In appearance and demeanor, he was made all of parts I didn\'t have, like an assemblage of my cavities. I stared, taken aback at how juxtaposed this all felt; how rare it was that a man who was anything, though not everything, that I was not. His polarity concerned me. The first man had disappeared, perhaps under the unnecessarily large bell of his umbrella, and that, too, had gone -- off in either direction, though when surveyed, turned up in neither. The other man sat, appearing unaffected by our polarities, perhaps due to our polarities themselves. Something wasn\'t adding up. \"First thing\'s first,\" he said, and I watched him look straight forward. \"I\'m not here to nickel and dime you, you see? I\'m not really here. I\'m imaginary.\" \"Huh. Really?\" It was all I could manage. I panned my gaze slowly away, and I felt his eyes turn towards me as I looked forward. I turned back, only to find him looking ahead as he was before, not a muscle displaced. \"Second,\" he continued. \"There\'s a man watching you from so far away that you can\'t see him.\" I stood up and looked around, to no avail, imagining that every mundane flourish was the settling dust from a missed event; imagining that I was imagining it all. I had lost track of how many days it had been since I had slept. For as long as my eyes were open, there was far too much I hadn\'t yet seen. *** In the intertwining of fabrics of circles and squares, it is established that there are circles and squares stitched into each individual sheet, but on any bisecting plane across sheets, the lines that cross are the same lines, and the points on those lines are shared across multiple shapes. A line is a line is a line is a line. The lines that make up one shape are no different than the lines that make up another. There are things that are shapes and there are things that are not shapes, and the lines and shapes which, when rearranged, total the same sums regardless of construction at any constant point in time. Still, shapes are at ends with each other, to almost no point at all in the grand scheme of things. At the very root, there are still circles and squares, which only appear to be moving, removing, then reappearing, even with no end game in sight, and they don\'t like each other. As to why, there really isn\'t much of a reasoning beyond it being how it is, and maybe coping with a lack of reason is reason enough. There\'d be nothing else to do, otherwise, and the appearance of moving was enough to keep things from shutting down completely. It\'s a method of propulsion; of continuance. Momentum is all an illusion. Movement takes constant, inefficient work, which must be done much too quickly to produce direction. Any direction will do over none at all to fend off the fossilization of shapes. Over stillness; redundancy. *** \"How do you know you\'re imaginary?\" I asked. \"How do you know you\'re real?\" he responded with a better question; one that I couldn\'t answer. \"I asked you first.\" \"Look, I\'m being courteous here,\" he replied, and I tried to look him in the eyes, but his head just rotated like I was turning him along a swivel. \"Most imaginations will never just tell you they\'re imaginary outright. They make you go along and do very real things, as a consequence, and then when it\'s too late, you find out that everything you did was at the whim of something that never existed in the first place.\" \"Then why\'d you go out and tell me?\" I questioned. \"I can\'t believe it\'s all a matter of courtesy.\" \"You could say our interests align.\" \"Real or imaginary interests?\" my brow arched. \"Both, in a sense.\" he mystically proposed. \"How do you mean?\" \"Things are real, concrete. Interests are imaginary,\" he explained, and I wasn\'t quite sold. \"You can have interests in real things, though.\" \"Oh yeah?\" I was getting tired, looking for an exit. \"I haven\'t slept in a while. You\'re going to have to be less abstract.\" \"Alright,\" he flatly revealed. \"Money. And I know you want to know the point of it all.\" \"I suppose.\" \"So, how much is it worth to you?\" \"Probably not too much,\" I checked my pockets, more so to fill the gaps than play accountant. \"How about a cigarette, then.\" I grumbled and reached into my pocket. I handed one over and lit another for myself. I imagine he pretended to smoke it. \"I guess I could\'ve saved myself a cigarette if I imagined you didn\'t smoke, huh?\" I contemplated, unable to tap into the power of my own creation. \"I said I was imaginary, not that you were the one imagining me.\" I turned, and he was looking right at me. \"The point is, just because something\'s imaginary doesn\'t mean it\'s not right. It just means it\'s not there.\" He grinned between pretend drags. \"That what you were looking to hear?\" \"Not now that I\'ve heard it,\" I sighed. \"I guess it cost me a cigarette either way.\" \"Thanks for the cigarette, by the way.\" \"You know. You\'re a real son of a bitch.\" Another bus came by, and I decided I was getting on it. It cost me the cigarette I was smoking, but at least I knew what I was getting.
