"Listen Son...It's Just..."

6 November 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 6, 2008 Phillip Velasco. Growing up, his rags to riches story inspired me greatly. His background suffered the same hang-ups as mine, which always led me to believe that if he could trade the grimy confides of professional wrestling for a corporate position, sitting behind a desk making six figures a year...then I could do the same. The Wrestling industry had become an unbearable unburden to me. The politics, the schedules, the high expectations and minimum pay...it all lost it's glamour. I never really felt as if I was achieving anything. Sure there was the Hall of Fame, and the toppling of Doctor Curiosity...but I never felt as if I actually deserved those feats. The Hall of Fame, to me, felt like nothing more than a consolation prize for sticking with PRIME throughout it's rough periods. It was never something I, Vangelus Dante Olsig, felt I truly deserved. And Doctor Curiosity...well, I just counted myself lucky enough to catch him on the only bad day he's ever had. Professional Wrestling didn't have anything to offer me outside of a reflection of just how big a failure I truly was. I wanted out. My heart desired a better means of living. If anyone could grant me the desires of my heart, it was Phillip Velasco. Two weeks on constant internal warfare ensued, but I finally managed to complete the application. I knew I was qualified for the position...over-qualified even. The tasks it entailed...excellent processing skills, accounting skills, and general knowledge of the way corporations ran...I knew it all like the back of my hand. Phillip knew that. He was the one that taught me it all. Confidence? I guess you could say I had that. "Your qualifications, ummm...wow. They're just...yeah..." That confidence, however, slowly began to shred as I watched Phillip twit nervously. It wasn't often that I bore witness to such an occurrence as Phil wasn't a timid guy. He had no reason to be. He was sitting on top of the world. Yet, as I sat across that desk, watching him fiddle his thumbs, my spirit couldn't help but sense uneasiness. "The uncertainty of that statement is troubling, to say the least." "Oh, no...no need to be troubled, it's just..." In an act of desperation, I attempted to restore the comfort level that we'd once shared. "Phil. It's me. Vangelus. Please...just tell me, bro...what is it?" "Well, fine. I'll be straight with you. You're certainly qualified for the job. That ship has long sailed. But there are a few minor...kinks...yeah, kinks...that are standing firmly between me granting you the trademark Fist-Pound of acceptance...y'know what I mean, kid?" This was starting to get frustrating. There was an underlying motive that Phil was dancing around. "No. No I don't. I haven't 'known what you mean' since you said hello ten minutes ago." "Heh...yeah. Right. Ummm...I guess you can it's akin to the outline of our universe. You have Pluto on the right, and then there's, ugh...let's see...there's Jupiter on the left...and right in the middle there's the big, illuminated sphere we call the su-..." "Layman terms, Phil...please." "Heh...right. I was never good with analogies, was I?" Ceasing to respond, I provided him a look that told the entire story. Get on with it. "Okay, here's the skinny..." Out of nowhere, Phil erupted in an enormous fit of laughter which led me to believe that he'd engaged in a fair share of acting classes recently, as it was pretty evident that the tickling was far from genuine. "PWAHAHAHA!! Get it? The Skinny? Lord KNOWS I haven't been near Skinny since the 8th grade, huh? Hehehe...heh...h-..." I gave him the look again. He cleared his throat, sweating a bit more profusely now than before. It was starting to become mighty clear to me as to what was going on here. "Phil, I'm starting to believe that either someone has placed a bounty over your life, or the most likely candidate, since your life isn't exactly what I'd call 'Bounty-worthy'...I didn't get the job. Now tell me, am I hot or cold?" "Listen, son...it's just..." As he took a deep breath and leaned forward, those four words began to resonate inside of me. Memories began to flow like an emerald mountain...memories of the last time I'd heard those words 'Listen Son'. Memories. Flashbacks. I thought they'd ceased. The memories. The flashbacks. The hurt. But as soon as those words escaped Phil's mouth, they all came rushing forth like a mighty wind, dragging me to a place in my past I'd willed never to return to. A place of rejection. A place of hurt. A place of Betrayal. A place of hidden hatred. ------------------------ I was eight years old, living in the most uncertain time in my life. It was times like these that tested the validity of my soul. I was on fleet from Haiti, seeking to escape the sure death that had befallen my Mother and Father and was headed my way. My journey, which included bizzare travels with the most unlikely of sources, led me to the states where I wondered the streets aimlessly, seeking food and shelter from the ever shifting weather. One day it was hot, the next day it was cold. One day it was windy, the next day it was storming. Yet, in a miracle of mercy, a beautiful lady by the name of Marsha Springate stumbled upon my barren soul. In a gracious effort, her heart screamed for me. A little boy wandering the streets...alone...hungry. It led her to tears. He tears led her to action. He action led me to residence inside of the Springate home. I'd never experienced anything of the like. This place was huge. Multiple bedrooms. Multiple bathrooms. Cars all over the yard. High quality food, the likes of which my taste buds had been virgin to prior. Clean clothes. Toys. It was different. The people didn't even look like me. But it felt like Heaven. Heaven on earth. They had two children who weren't much older than I. One was 9, the other 11. They were getting ready to start school. Marsha asked me if I wanted to attend school as well. Having never experienced a formal education before, I excitedly responded with a yes. The first day of school was, to me, the equivalent of the first game of the World Series. It was a dream road that I was traveling, which, I felt, would lead me to a grand prize. It didn't. It led me to experience something far worse. At the conclusion of the school day, I walked inside of the home to see both her and her husband, Richard, standing there waiting with great joy. "Hey there, Dante...how as your first day of sc-..." Suddenly, her jaw dropped as she bore witness to the shamble which was my new shirt. It was ripped to pieces, attributing the scar on my face and the dry blood that covered my palms. "Oh my...WHAT HAPPENED TO YOU!?!" Uncontrollably, I began to sob. I'd been jumped. I didn't even know why. "Some kids...they jumped me. I couldn't fight them all off." Richard gazed on with the most damning of looks. Marsha, on the other hand, was frantic...near the point of breakdown, even. I could feel her heart in her emotions towards me. "Oh my Go-...okay. Okay, honey, just...just go to your room and clean yourself up. We'll take care of this." The smell of blood lifted into my nostrils as I turned to walk away. Before I could fully get away, however, Richard kneeled down beside me, taking me in his arms in order to show his support. "I'm so sorry this happened to you, little man, but it won't happen again, you hear? I'm going to make sure of it. Okay? That sound good?" I nodded. Richard always seemed far more distant than Marsha, but I still felt as though he cared. "Alright, buddy." He smiled, patted me on the shoulder, and sent me on my way. Their care lessened the pain. ------------------------ Having forgotten to inform them on how the rest of my day went, I raced back to the living room only to discover that they'd escaped to their bedroom. Figuring no harm would be done, I raced to the bedroom, set to knock on the closed door. Something stopped me though. The sound of yelling and screaming. I didn't have to press my ear to the door...it was loud enough that I could hear it clearly. It scared me. "You know why they did this, don't you?" "Of course I know, Richard, and it pisses me off to no end. What are we going to do about this?" "We? WE? YOU, Marsha, are the one who got us into this mess. We were doing perfectly fine until YOU up and decided one day that you wanted to be a SAINT and bring some helpless child from God knows where into our home. The Kids were doing great, we were doing great, everything was great, great, GREAT! But your overzealous desire to fix what wasn't even broken changed that. Now our kids have to attend school with FEAR because they're folks are the ones that brought that little dark-skinned RUNT into this neighborhood." My heart sunk. I couldn't believe what I was hearing. Suddenly, I felt worthless... A feeling that I'd come to experience time and time again in subsequent years. "Don't you dare, Richard...Don't you DARE!" "Marsha, grow up. You hear what they're calling him. A Darkie. A Muslim. A Colored. A Leecher. An Ingrate. A Terrorist. A Co-..." As I heard the names, my hurt slowly transitioned into hatred. Hatred into embarrassment. Embarrassment...right back to worthlessness. "RICHARD, STOP! I DON'T WANT TO HEAR IT!" "WELL I HAVE TO HEAR IT EVERYDAY, MARSH! Sitting at home all through the week, you don't know what it's like to have to walk unto my job and have all of the other guys, who once admired and respected you, look down upon you because of the PLAGUE you've sown into this community." "Is that what this is about? Your bruised ego? He is not a plague. He is a human being...a CHILD who was crying out for help. He had no place to go. No parents, no food, no clothes, no shelter...and what? What did you want me to do? Just turn my back to him?" "There are homes and orphanages where he could've easily fit in. Why did you have to bring him here? Eating our steaks, wearing our nice clothes...He doesn't deserve that stuff. He has a place where he belongs, and it is NOT HERE!" "You really don't have a heart, do you Richard? What if our parents would have said the same about us when we were failing to make ends meet? What if they deemed us unworthy, and therefore refused to help us? Then you wouldn't have the steaks, nor the clothes, nor the cars...you would be in the EXACT same situation as Dante!" "You're comparing Apples and Oranges, Marsha. Those were our parents. They shared the same blood as we did. They shared the same lineage as we did. And most importantly, they shared the same skin color as we did." "And that's what this ultimately boils down to, isn't it? You don't like him because he's a Native American." "He's a Native, Marsha. That boy has no American in him?" "But he has blood in him. And he has hunger in him. And he has thirst in him. And he has breath in him. You know what all that leads me to believe, Richard? That he's a functioning human being JUST like you and me. Can't you become color blind long enough to see that?" "Marsha, I'm going to say it one more time and we're going to be done here. He. Doesn't. Belong. Here. And you know what else? As the man of this house, I am laying down the law..." A brief moment of silence ensued. "The boy has to go." "No....NOOO! I am not sending that poor innocent child back out on those cold streets. He will get sick. He will starve. He will DIE!" "GOOD! Because that's all this family has done since he's arrived here! We've died! Our children's safety has died! Their comfort has died! My position at my job has died! Our stature within this community has died! And if he doesn't leave now, which he will, then we won't just die hypothetically...we'll be KILLED! These people? They're not going to stand for him being in this community any more. If we don't get rid of him, THEY will...and they'll take us with him. The ingrate has got to g-..." The next thing I heard was a slap. "I TOLD YOU NOT TO CALL HIM THA-...UGH!" Now, banging. He was pinning her up against the wall. "LOOK MARSHA! I'M NOT PLAYING GAMES HERE, ALRIGHT! WE'RE PACKING HIS THINGS AND SHIPPING HIM BACK ACROSS THE SEA WHERE HE CAME FROM TOMMORROW! Those ingrates birthed this kid...let them deal with him." "You are a Racist." It was the first time I'd heard that word. It automatically took rest in my spirit, disturbing me greatly. Even at such a tender age, I knew it wasn't anything good, and judging by the context it'd been used...I knew it was something I wouldn't be able to change. "No, Marsha...I'm a realist. Now go pack his things." "I'm not packing a a single CLOTH! He is our adopted son and we WILL treat him as such." "So you're not going to abide by my command voluntarily?" "Not. At. All." I smiled. Marsha really loved me. She cared about me. She wasn't going to let anything happen to me. I comforted myself in that knowledge. "Fine...Don't pack a cloth, then. Let him stay. We'll see how it turns out." As I heard his footsteps approaching, I took off in a sprint. There was nothing to worry about. Richard may not have liked me... But Marsha loved me... And I was safe in her arms. ------------------------ The second day of school had come and gone, quite better than the previous. I didn't get jumped this time, but I couldn't help but feel isolation from the rest of the kids. All throughout the day, that word...'racist'...I kept hearing it over and over again. It troubled me. But Marsha's defense of me subdued that troubling. I walked in the door. The first thing I notice? My belongings...all packed up. There wasn't much, but the little that there was had all been suited up. Were we getting ready to go on a trip? "Wha-...what's going on?" I asked. "Dante, come here." She gave me a warm, firm hug. I immediately sensed that something was wrong. Very wrong. She released the hug and looked me square in the eye. "You trust us, right?" As I nodded, I couldn't help but notice a large gash on the side of her face. The thumping and smacking that I'd heard the day before suddenly came to mind. He did it to her. I knew he did. "We're doing what's best for you. What you went through yesterday is only going to continue if you stay here. We love you and we simply do not want that to continue happening. Do you understand?" I nodded yes, but in reality, I didn't understand at all. She knew that. "No you don't..." Tears began to rush down her face with little regard. My heart plummeted. "Marsha..." Richard called out in an effort to get her to regain her composure. It was useless...so he took over. He placed his cold hand on my shoulder.
