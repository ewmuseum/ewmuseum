Date: 2/21/08-2/22/08 Previously in the Devin Shakur story... A man had his dream of serenity shattered by untimely death... After six years in prison, Devin Shakur stood at the bedroom doorway of his first wife, Kimberly Crawford, in tears. Her life cut short by his father brutally murdering her. The father continued to punish the son... Anthony Shakur hired a Braintrust to ruin Devin's life even further, but Devin called upon his adopted family, the Smith's, to execute all three members. The son cast the final judgment upon his father... Devin slashed Anthony's throat with a piece of glass and left him for dead. Old detectives with an ax to grind made their presence felt... The next generation of Connors, Larry and Steven, fueled by the rage of their ancestor’s murder at the hands of the Smith crew, coincidentally picked up the case of Anthony Shakur. They immediately found a link between Devin and the Smith crew. A botched interrogation brought questions upon Larry and Steven... Devin called the bluff of Larry and Steven when they made claims of video evidence that showed Devin leaving the crime scene of his father's murder. Devin stormed out of the interrogation room. Larry and Steven were both thrown off the case and Larry was reassigned to Robbery Division. But the detectives refused to give in... Rachel Smith assassinated the two people who presented Devin as a suspect to the detectives. Larry and Steven confirmed beyond a shadow of a doubt that the Smith clan partook in the murders. Only to be at a stalemate when some old friends arrived on the scene... Devin was about to be murdered by Larry and Steven until the leader of the Smith clan, Christian Daniels, previously in exile, made a surprise return to hold the boys off. For six months both sides have been dormant... ...But things are about to heat up… February 21st, 2009 Pullen Park 5:30 PM Cynthia Connors clutches the coiled swing chains with her petite fingers. Rocking back and forth with the assistance of her father's hand pushing at her back, the four year old with curly blonde hair that everybody envies feels like she is flying. Every Saturday she takes her father to the park and plays all day. Larry gets jostled awake at 9 in the morning and thinks going to the park is one of the most painful experiences of his week. He works five grueling twelve hour days and by Friday night is exhausted. On the drives over, he wonders why his wife Maria doesn't do this once in a while. After eight hours of watching the joy of his life, his Lollipop, giggle and smile exuberantly, Saturday is the best day of his week. He doesn't have to deal with anybody calling and interrupting him because nobody other than Maria knows his private number. He is able to make sandcastles, get ice cream, ride the merry go round, throw food to ducks in the pond, and push her on the swing. As the sun sets over the trees and turns the clouds a light shade of orange, father and daughter bring their fun filled day to a close. All of the other children are clearing out of the park, making the moment much more serene for the duo. “I wanna touch the sun, Daddy!” Cynthia shouts. Larry gives her the big shove and allows Cynthia to have the thrill of swinging on her own. He moves around to the side and watches her push herself to keep moving, one arm reaching out to try and grab a hold of the sun. “You almost got it that time, Lollipop.” Larry says, crossing his arms and smiling. Cynthia's momentum slows down and she produces a pout that lets her father know he needs to get behind her and push one more time. Putting his hands up in a defensive manner, Larry walks around and positions himself, bringing Cynthia almost over his head. Unable to contain his joy, Larry readies himself. “Hang on tight, because we're going to get that sun this time.” “I know we're gonna do it, Daddy.” Cynthia replies, looking back and giving a smile that melts his heart. Extending his right foot forward, Larry exerts all of the energy in his lanky arms to get Cynthia one more shot at the sun. She swats at the massive object with her left hand, once again missing. “I messed up on that one, Lollipop. I'm sorry.” Larry bashfully says, hanging his head in shame. “You need a time out, Daddy!” Cynthia quips back, pointing an authoritative finger at her father. Larry walks away, doing his best to fool his daughter into believing that he is really heading over to the sandbox for a timeout. “Come back Daddy.” Dropping the act, Larry rushes back over and picks his daughter up out of the swing, giving her a big hug. He locks eyes with her and becomes lost in the moment. She's only four years old. She is so innocent. She doesn't know the first thing about what her father goes through on a daily basis. For that, he is a happy man. Larry puts Cynthia back on the swing and wraps his arms around the chains, looking down at her. She puts her finger on his nose and then on her own nose. “I tell you what Lollipop, since you didn't get to touch the sun, I'll make it up to you by taking you to Baskin Robbins in a while and giving you a big scoop of ice cream! How's that sound?” Cynthia's face lights up. “Two scoops! Two scoops!” Larry pretends to contemplate, putting a finger underneath his chin. “Your mother probably wouldn't approve of that much...But she's not around. Alright, two scoops it is.” “Yay! You’re the best, Daddy.” “No, Lollipop, you are the best.” The two share another embrace, one that Larry wishes would last forever. He never wants to let her go, never wants to let her out of his sight. But when he hears a vibration in his pocket, he thinks a response that he hopes his daughter never says. Breaking the embrace, Larry looks into his pocket and sees 'Home' on the screen. If Maria calls him during such important time, then the matter must be paramount. He puts a finger on Cynthia's nose and undoes the phone flap. “Be right back, Lollipop, don't go anywhere or you won't get any ice cream.” “I won't go no where.” Larry walks away from the swing and over by the trees, putting the phone to his ear. “Hey honey, what's going on?” The tone of Maria's voice is indicative that the call is essential. “Honey, work called you again and your boss said you need to stop prying into the Shakur matter...” Larry steps far enough away so Cynthia doesn't pick up any of the language being used. He glances over a few times to ensure that she hasn't moved from her assigned position. Cynthia sways back and forth without doing much, deciding on what flavor of ice cream she wants, chocolate or strawberry? Almost like clockwork, a new individual sits down next to her on the swing. Standing next to him is a small child about Cynthia's age. The father instructs his son to go over and play on the merry go round, keeping a watchful eye on him while the child rushes over to swing with another kid. The father's muscular arms dwarf the chains and his adult body has to wedge itself into the child size seat. Easily able to rock himself back and forth with powerful legs, he decides to give Cynthia a friendly boost. His massive arm wraps around her swing and he effortlessly pushes Cynthia back to the highest point Larry had her at moments ago. Larry takes another look at the scene and sees the happiness on Cynthia's face. She's not frightened by who she sees, so Larry keeps a lazy eye on the swing. “Hello Lollipop,” The abrasive voice of Devin Shakur says, looking over at Cynthia. He cracks a friendly smile and playfully looks into Cynthia's green eyes. Cynthia responds with glee, not being privy to the maxim of never talking to the strangers, “Hello!” Devin calmly takes a look over his right shoulder at Larry, completely oblivious to who is sitting inches away from his daughter. He cracks a smile and Cynthia inquisitively follows Devin's eyes. “That's my Daddy! He works with the po-lice.” “I know your Daddy, Cynthia. In fact I work with him.” “Are you a cop too?” Devin's pause is premeditated. He anticipated this question and was pondering on the walk over how to answer it precisely. He doesn't want to scare the child because one scream from her will send Larry into a whirlwind of panic. “Not necessarily, but I have known him for quite a while. In fact, I knew your grandpapa Mike and great grandpapa Daniel. They were cops too. Has he ever told you about them?” Cynthia shakes her head no. Devin makes a mental note to one day expose Cynthia to two of the most dishonorable men he's ever come into contact with. “Well they were cops, just like Daddy. He tells me that you don't want to be a cop though. You wanna become a doctor!” Shakur says, heightening the pitch of his voice. “Yep, I'm going to be the bestest doctor there ever was.” Cynthia replies with an unabashed smile. “And I'll bet that after you fix up all their boo-boos, because you'll be such a smart doctor, you are gonna give all of them a big lollipop.” “Yep, lollipops make everything better.” “I'll bet they do. In fact...” Shakur reaches into one of his long dress pockets and retrieves a giant cherry lollipop. Christian made sure that they stopped off at the nearest gas station and got one since they are Cynthia's favorite. “I have a lollipop here for a special little girl. Now where might I find her?” Cynthia screams, “Me! Me! ME!” Devin pretends to scan around the park while Cynthia bounces out of swing trying to grab his attention. Finally after surveying in every possible direction, he looks down at the excited Cynthia, arms extended to receive her treat. “Well I can't find a girl around more special than you, so I guess you win.” Devin hands her the goody and watches her devour the wrapping. For a moment, she forgets that he is there, too much enthralled in her prize to care about the man that gave it to her. After spending twenty seconds sucking the cherry out of the square, she looks back up at Devin, who is still smiling at her. “You know Cynthia you look just like your Daddy. Has anybody ever told you that?” Cynthia's jaw almost drops to the sand. She seems appalled. “I don't look like Daddy! He's old. I'm never going to get that old.” Even in the midst of pretending to adore the child, Devin can't help but laugh at her convoluted logic. He sometimes misses the ability to think like a child without the repercussions or backlash. Nobody will judge a child because they find the talk to be charming and cute. Devin reaches his hand forward and removes a strand of curly blonde hair from her face. “I really mean that, Cynthia. You might not see it, but you are just like your father. I can see it in the face, the eyes...” The naïve ignorance The stupidity Larry hangs up the phone and turns around, taking a good look at the “father's” face, stunned at the sight he sees. “CYNTHIA! GET AWAY FROM HIM!” With determination usually reserved for capturing criminals, Larry rushes over and takes his daughter off the swing, removing the lollipop from her mouth. She cries out, clearly enjoying the treat and Devin's friendly company. Larry brings her over to the sandbox and puts her down next to a large column of sand. He instructs her to continue building while he goes back over and talks to Devin. Cynthia asks if she can have her lollipop back and Larry shakes his head no, causing Cynthia to shout in anger. It takes Larry pointing his finger at Cynthia and ordering her to play with the sandcastle before her cries quell and he is able to walk back over to Devin. The rage in his eyes is unparalleled. If Devin was in fear of being captured, he would run for the hills right now. Larry Connors has a look to kill. But Devin knows Larry won't lay a hand on him, not in front of his Lollipop. The detective gets right over Devin. His hands are shaking, the veins clearly showing. Devin can almost hear his heart beating and Larry is trying his best not to scream. He doesn't want to leave a bad impression on his daughter, even though explaining this situation when they get home will be tough. With gritted teeth Larry blurts out, “What the fuck are you doing here, Shakur?” Looking over at Cynthia, Devin notices that she has been instilled with the fear of God and is not looking at the adults. He lackadaisically turns his attention back to Larry, a smirk etched wide on his face. “Fuck you Larry. You aren't the boss of me. I can do whatever I want whenever I want so long as I don't break the law.” The arrogance of Devin's tone makes Larry even more furious. He glances back over at his daughter and then crouches down in the face of Devin. “You sick son of a bitch. You don't have the right to come over and speak to my daughter. You are still under investigation for murder.” Devin shifts his leg around and stands up to his full six feet two inches. With a whistle, Devin summons the child that Larry presumed was his son over to his side. Devin reaches into his pocket and pulls out a 50 dollar bill, sending the kid on his way, his job fulfilled. He's now eye to eye with Larry and their images couldn't be more opposite. Larry knows that he's lost this round, dropped his guard, and the results could have been fatal. Devin could have snapped his little girl's neck without her even hearing his approach and there would have been nothing he could do to stop it. But Devin didn't. The smug look on his face shows that he knows exactly what he's doing. He's in complete control of the situation. The angst in Larry's face only makes Devin's grin stretch that much further. He's taking great enjoyment in watching Larry suffer. “Stop trying to scare me, Larry. We both know that none of us are under investigation anymore since you got tossed to robbery. Your interrogation ruined any chance you had of catching us. Our camp has been quiet for too long.” Devin says, leaning up against the swing post. Larry's facial expression changes to one more horrific. Something in the shadows leaves him with an immense feeling of discomfort. His stomach turns, hands slightly trembling. Devin licks his lips but even he knows that his words aren't having such a significant impact on Larry. From behind Devin, Christian Daniels menacingly walks into the scene, causing Larry to take a protective step back toward his daughter. For the first time in a long time he's scared, staring down the two vipers before him. They are the reason for his misery and now they have him trapped in a corner. Hand to hand combat would be useless against Devin and Christian, both well disciplined fighters in multiple styles. One shot from either puts most men to the ground. Impending darkness continues to clear the park, and not many men are going to be willing to cross these two, even to help the unarmed Larry and Cynthia. Christian continues where Devin left off, “But now the game is back on. Just keep this in mind…What we did today was simply a warning. The next time we come near your little Lollipop, we ain't gonna to be offerin' treats.” Christian puts a finger across Devin's neck and slowly slides the digit across the entire length of his throat. The two walk away, leaving a vivid impression on Larry. He quickly rushes over to the sandbox, lifts Cynthia from the ground, and exits the park. He needs to head back to his sanctuary where he feels safer. February 21st, 2009 7 PM Larry Connors Residence “Those sons of bitches!” Larry Connors resists the urge to topple his four foot dresser drawer. He's pacing the master bedroom back and forth with his angry wife, Maria, staring at him from a respectable distance. Larry is only 25 years old and doesn't have the amount of patience his elder brother Steven has. Ever since childhood, he's shown a volatile temper and can explode at the drop of a hat. An hour and fifteen minutes after the encounter, Larry is still seething, adrenaline pumping, arms shaking, face the darkest shade of red humanly possible. He wants to tear every strand of hair out of his head since he can't strike anything. His anger management coach forbade that when seeing anything that made him tense up with rage. Maria puts her long brunette hair into a ponytail and furiously walks over to her husband, throwing her arms out to her sides. “You let him get that close to our daughter? What is wrong with you, Larry?” Larry looks over at his wife, giving her an annoyed stare and tilting his head to the side. He can't resist anymore and throws his fist forward, toppling a book shelf and sending the books down onto the ground. “I didn't think he'd fucking show up, Maria! He brought a kid with him!” Maria presents a baffled look, leaning down to pick up the books. She's having a hard time believing this but knows her husband is no liar. “He brought a kid...to the park...just so he could fuck with you?” “Yes, and paid the little bastard 50 dollars right in front of my face. I didn't know Maria. You really think I'm going to willingly put my daughter in that kind of danger?” Maria's anger rescinds. Once she rises to her feet from rearranging the books, she brushes the hair from her face and looks at her husband's disheveled expression. He's clearly traumatized over the event and she knows that an additional conflict is not going to help the situation. She walks over and places her arms around his waist, putting her head against his back. “I'm sorry, honey, I really am. I know you wouldn't put Cynthia in danger, no matter what. You are a great father.” Larry breaks free from her grip, turning around and looking his wife in the eyes. Those cold eyes are what made Mike Connors believe that his son could make it in the police business, the look of a man possessed. The drive of someone who can't stop until justice has been served. The thought of Devin Shakur touching his little girl sends shivers up and down his spine. “He put his hands on our girl! He touched her hair! He was inches away from her! The man could have done unspeakable things to her without a care in the fucking world! How am I supposed to understand anything else?” “They are a bunch of assholes, Larry. We both know that. I'm just as mad as you are, and God forbid I ever get my hands around their pathetic necks. We misjudged them. But Cynthia's not going to remember this in a few days and they didn't hurt her.” Larry looks over, almost on the verge of tears, “They said...That next time won't be a warning...” The rage subsides from Larry's body and Maria reaches out to hold him once more. He shoves her away and leans against the wall, sinking down toward the floor and wrapping his arms around his knees. Maria gently goes down to the floor with him and finally embraces him, locking Larry in her arms. They have been husband and wife ever since high school graduation and she's not letting him go now. Even though this is the first instance where their daughter has been compromised, she can't let their conflicting parental styles clash right now. That isn't going to solve any problems. She takes Larry's head and holds him tight while tears snake from his eyes. Kissing the top of his head, she allows him the necessary space to vent. “They were that close, Maria...They were that close.” Larry says, coughing and wiping the tears from his eyes. He's finally somewhere where he doesn't have to worry about what people think of his overemotional behavior. Although, he prays that Cynthia doesn't see her father, her hero, in this condition. “But they didn't hurt her. I've known you long enough and you've told me that it takes a true idiot to perform a crime in the middle of the day with tons of witnesses around. They are evil bastards but they aren't stupid.” Larry seems to ignore her comment, too caught up in the “What ifs...” “What if they took her...All it takes is a second. I would have never seen her again.” Larry's arms reach around to Maria's back and he cinches himself into her embrace. He almost lived every parent's worst nightmare. “...Never got to hold her in my arms. Never got to tuck her in and kiss her good night. I couldn't live with myself after that, Maria, I just couldn't.” Larry's body weight and loss of emotional control drives them to the ground. Maria stays underneath, unflinching, rubbing his back. “They aren't going to take her from us, Larry.” Maria says, her voice protruding confidence. “You aren't going to let that happen. Steve isn't going to let that happen...I'm not going to let it happen. Together we are stronger than they ever will be.” His head rises from her breast and she wipes away the stream of tears which have reached his lips. Larry has a furious headache and can't speak without his head throbbing. “They...Took both my father and grandfather away in the same week and both my uncles. They don't care who they hurt...Nobody is off limits to them.” Maria strokes Larry's hair and forces him back into her arms. He's not ready to be released yet. The wailing continues but she doesn't care. She is only concerned about his well being and emotional recovery right now. She's been through this many times so she knows how to soothe him. Larry needs to let it all out now so he can be at his best when he takes the Smith family and Devin Shakur down. She knows that he can do it. “You know that I've supported you through everything that you've done. I don't care what you have to do, baby, just do it. I'm going to always be here for you. Steve will be too. Cynthia will never leave you.” Maria releases her grip from Larry and allows him to rise to a seated position, furiously wiping the tears away from his face. She reaches over, placing a comforting hand on his shoulder, and he reaches over to place a kiss on her lips. He knew there was a reason that he decided to take the leap of faith after high school. “I just...” Larry stops, trying to repress new tears, “...Don't want to end up like my Dad or Paps.” “Trust me.” Maria says, tilting Larry's head so that he looks her in the face. She doesn't have an ounce of regret in her words. “You won't.” A faint pitter patter is heard at the door and both parents look over at their daughter's arrival. They give her the biggest smiles imaginable. Cynthia curiously asks, a little scared, “Are you guys fighting?” The parents throw each other an incredulous look before staring back at their treasure. “No sweetie,” Larry says, motioning her over with one of his lanky arms. “We weren't fighting. We were just talking. Now come here and give me a hug, Lollipop.” Cynthia rushes over and jumps into her father’s arms. She clutches his neck tighter than ever before and he makes sure that he doesn't let go for a whole minute. Larry will do what he has to do to protect his family. At any cost. February 22nd, 2009 11 AM Shakur Residence Devin Shakur and Christian Daniels clink their beer bottles together and slouch down on the leather couch behind them. They feel like they are on top of the world. “I thought motherfucker was gonna shit a brick when he saw you sittin' over there with Cynthia, man.” Christian states, extending his feet out onto the glass table and throwing his head back to down the rest of his beer. Devin laughs and slaps Christian on the shoulder. “Dude, he wanted to kill me so bad. I swear he's going to try and come by here later to drive by us.” Unfazed, Christian shrugs his shoulders, “Let him, only gonna speed up the process, kid is in robbery for two or three more months. He ain't got no legit resources from the pigs. He ain't got shit. He's doin' this shit on his own with that dipshit brother of his, Dev, same bullshit his pops and dad tried to do on us. Look where that got 'em.” “I don't know what was worse,” Devin replies, “That he reacted the way he did or that his daughter didn't scream. He hasn't said anything to her about us. I mean I just walked right up there and she was happy as a clam.” Christian smirks, reaches over to the coffee table and retrieves another beer, popping the top with his hands. “We gotta remember, before we off that little bitch, to tell her how bad her family really is...Hate somebody not knowin' the truth before they die, y'know?” “I hear you, man. Though seriously,” Devin says, straightening up and firmly planting his feet onto the lion designed carpet. “What do you think he's going to do next?” Christian looks over at Devin with a befuddled look on his face and shakes his head, patting him on the shoulder while slouching further down on the couch, his feet almost off the glass table. “You worryin' too much, boy. Let him do whatever he wants, ain't gonna matter. We always come out on top. He don't got what it takes to compete with us, bottom line.” A knock on the front door sends Christian's right arm up in dismissal, leaving Devin to rise from the couch and walk over to open the door. Standing before him is a UPS man, nervously holding a package in his hand, glancing over his shoulder. Devin looks around the corner to see what could be worrying the delivery boy so much, and laughs after discovering the reason, two Doberman pinschers barely being contained by their posts. “Yeah, you should have brought a steak. Those dogs don't like anybody just driving through here. So what do you got for me?” The delivery boy replies, “Don't know, doesn't even have a return address, although it was express delivery so I assume it's important.” Shakur ponders, taking the package from the kid and lifting the box up to his ear, shaking it around back and forth to deduce what the contents are. After a moment, a perplexed look manifests on Devin's face and he gives a shrug of the shoulders, “Definitely not anything harmful.” He reaches into his pocket, retrieves a 100 dollar tip for the delivery boy and sends him on his way, ignoring whatever the kid has planned for a thank you. Christian barely looks away from the DVRd episode of Criminal Minds, while Devin throws the package up and down. “Da fuck is it?” “No idea, has to be something small because it's barely moving around in there.” “Well open the damn thing'n get your ass back over here. Only like two minutes before the show starts up again.” Devin reaches into his pocket and flips open a large seven inch knife, cutting at the rough taping off the package. His hand reaches down and pulls out a small envelope. Devin nonchalantly flings the package box over his corner and rips open the envelope with the same knife, forgoing any kind of safety measure. The knife goes back into his pocket and Devin looks down at the contents. A stack of pictures The first one makes Devin freeze in place, unable to move. The other pictures in the set drop to the floor. Christian instantly rises from his slouched position, moving over to his brother and staring down at the photos. Not much can make Christian Daniels' mouth stay open like a mailbox without a hinge, but this picture can. The date on the bottom right corner reads 12/4/2002, Devin Shakur's release date from prison. The picture itself is brutal and one that Devin never thought his eyes would never have to see again. Instantaneously, the memories of that painful day flood into his head, unable to be restrained. 12/4/2002 Beverly Hills, California Inside a taxi cab, Devin Shakur eagerly anticipates seeing his wife, Kimberly Crawford, for the first time outside the parameters of a prison cell. He's waited for six years to get a fresh start on life and Kimberly has given it to him. Their pen pal relationship from prison developed into a full on relationship and eventually marriage. Today, Devin gets to touch his wife, tell her that he loves her, and whisper in hear that he'll be with her forever without a guard peering through the door to watch them. However, when Devin rounds the corner, the scene he comes upon is the complete antithesis of what he expected. Yellow crime scene tape surrounds the entire perimeter of the home and police flood the front yard, crime scene technicians going in and out of the giant Beverly Hills mansion. “Stop the car! Stop the car!” Devin screams to the cabbie, flinging him a 20 dollar bill and rushing out of the vehicle, forgetting the red roses he bought. Devin bolts ahead maneuvering underneath the yellow tape and running straight for the house. The police officers rush over and derail his attempts of getting inside. He has to show a picture of their wedding from inside the prison to prove his legitimacy as her husband before they feel content to release him. Once he gets inside, he shouts her name repeatedly. “KIMBERLY! KIMBERLY! WHERE ARE YOU?” Devin scans the living room and finds no sight of his beloved. Upon a complete scan of the first floor, his eye catches something that he missed the first time, a drop of crimson on the first stair. Hopping up three stairs at a time, Devin shoves a crime scene technician out of the way and makes the worst right turn of his life. Blood, blood, and more blood, he sees red everywhere he looks. On the carpet. Mirrors. Bedposts. Door. Sheets. Nightstand. Every muscle in his body wants to turn away, run from the house, get back in the taxi, and drive away. He knows what is waiting on the bed and doesn't want to see it. Already on the verge of a volcanic emotional explosion, this would send him over the edge. But something draws his eyes upward. The killer didn't even give her the dignity of covering her body. Her face is battered and bruised from the severe beating. A phone cord rests next to her head, used to take the last breaths from her body. Devin stands in silence, staring at the corpse. He is in complete shock. Who would want to do something so vile to such a beautiful woman, one who wanted to give him a chance at redemption? Unable to contain himself any longer, Devin covers his face with his hands and drops down to his knees. His face sinks into the floor and a range of emotions spill from his body. He cries. He vomits. He screams. He slams his head into the wall. Christian picks up the rest of the pictures, organizing them into a stack and flipping through them. One shows the strangulation marks on her neck from the phone cord. The second shows a close up on her swollen and bloody face moments after her death. The last one is a shot of Devin sitting on the front porch steps, head clutched into his hands, arms around knees, bawling like a four year old who just lost his dog. He just lost the love of his life, his soul mate. Christian turns over the last picture and sees the note on the back. He summons Devin, shaken and on the verge of tears, to read the inscription. “Come after my family again. Trust me, I can dig deeper you sick fuckers.” Love, Larry Devin's eyes hang down, unable to fight the pain any longer. Christian's fists tighten and destroy all but one of the pictures. Christian had Larry Connors coined as a man who would resort to physical violence to protect his family, not incite the beast, which is exactly what he's done. Christian looks over at Devin, his body leaning against the door, legs unable to support the rest of his 214 pound frame. He takes a hold of Devin by the shirt and walks him over to the recliner. Christian isn't the one to give comforting advice or tell somebody that they will be alright. Instead, he leans down and pulls Devin's chin up, looking him dead in the eyes. “That motherfucker is gonna die, you bank on it.” Even though he's rocking back and forth in the chair, clutching the lone remaining picture, soon his sorrow will turn into rage and that's exactly why Christian leaves him alone. The mammoth biker knows that Larry is emotional, but would he really do something so bold and not expect swift retaliation? The Smith crew is the worst of the worst, unafraid of anybody, and he shows them pictures of Devin's first wife moments after she died? It will be the worst mistake he's ever made. Christian reaches into his pocket and pulls out a cellular phone, hitting one button before putting the device up to his ear. “Mike, pull the gang together ASAP, we're gonna have a meeting, fuckin' Connors done gone too far. Somebody's gonna pay.” Christian hangs up the phone and leaves Devin to look at his first love, sprawled out on her bed, dead at the hands of his own father because he never wanted Devin to have any kind of happiness. February 22nd, 2009 3 PM Shakur Residence In a dimly lit basement with only a single light hanging down from the ceiling are Michael Blaze, Jason Hardy, and Devin Shakur. Christian is pacing back and forth, holding the crumpled pictures behind his back. He's already given Michael and Jason the 411 on what happened and their faces are a clear indication of their thoughts. They are sickened. Devin is still transfixed by the horror of what was supposed to be the happiest day of his life.
