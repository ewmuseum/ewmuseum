The Sons of Caliban

3 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/03/06 'Omaúiyaúa úiñhañ anawaðopþañ kta ye.Maúiöazo úiñhañ owaúaüniðe kta ye.Aopemaúiye úiñhañ uñmasöe kta ye.' “Tell me, and I will listen.Show me, and I will understand.Involve me, and I will learn.”-Lakota Proverb 'I want to tell you a story' Damien turned and looked at his friend, who was searching through his memories for the opening lines. A light went off, and he began. “There was this little boy who would sit on the hill everyday, watching the eagles fly across the sky. He watched carefully as they streaked across the sky and tore the animals from the desert floor with a flick of their mighty talons. He watched with great curiosity as they floated along the horizon stroking their wings maybe once the whole time. Or sometimes, when the air seemed thick and hot, they stood still in the air high above him, barely moving at all. He was so jealous of their flight that he decided to demand them to teach him how to fly.” James Willis smiled with pride as he told the story. It was one that he had been told a thousand times as a child, and one that he knew had been passed to him as a relic of a quickly fading world. James told the story as it had been told to him, changing it in the ways he felt he should as his father had done, and as it had been expected that he should. Damien, without affect, listened intently. “So the boy went up to the highest hill he could find, and waited for the eagles to come to him, and teach him how to fly with him. The eagles flew high above, paying no mind to the little boy on the hillside. So again, on the next day, and many days after, the boy sat on the hill, and waited for the birds to teach him their secrets. But none ever came to him. Frustrated, the boy had an idea. He gathered some leaves, and made for himself great, wide, beautiful wings like they had. He then took a stone, and carved himself a long, sharp beak as the eagles had. He ran up the hill early the next morning and once again waited.” The two sat on the porch of James’s home. It was late, and they had decided to have a couple of beers, and enjoy the crisp Montana air before turning in for the night. James looked up at the pitch black sky, seeing the birds soaring overhead in the scorching sun. Damien looked up as well, and he saw them as well. “Now the boy sat on that hill, showing off his beautiful feathers, and singing through his stone beak. Still, the eagles gave him no more attention than they did any day before. Angry at the birds for being so selfish, the boy began to curse at the birds, he began hurling rocks at those that came close enough, and he scared away the creatures that the eagles hunted so skillfully. The eagles scattered and retreated, and the boy was left alone on the hill, satisfied with his handiwork. Then, suddenly, there was a loud clap of thunder, and the boy looked up to see The Great Eagle coming down from the sky. With a great joy, the Boy spread his wings, and the Great Eagle swooped down and lifted the boy up into his claws and high into the air. Soaring above the earth, the wind in his face, the world swimming far below him, the boy finally got his wish. Up in the sky, the boy was untouchable, and soon all of his friends would envy him. Then, the Great Eagle released him from his talons, and the boy began to fall to the earth. He flapped his leafy wings, but they simply tore apart the faster he flapped. He tried to cry out, but his stone beak muffled his cries. The boy fell for a very long time until he crashed to the earth and died.” James smiled despite the morbidity of the story, and cracked a fresh beer as a final punctuation. “Can you dig it, Damien?” The Latin Assassin knew that there were no easy answers, so he let James pull it all together. “Yeah, I dig it. So what does it all mean?” “It means that if you’re going to piss off the Great Eagle, you should have more than leafy feathers and a stone beak to save your ass. It means that when you ask for the spotlight in PRIME, that you’re going to get it. That’s what it means.” Damien nodded, and looked at his friend, who had left the ring behind him a long time ago. And, like so many other things, James had a great story to tell about the day that he did. If James was nothing else, he was a great story-teller. James was a man Damien had met and become friends with during his time with the Southwest All-Pro Wrestling Company. James was fast, strong, fearless, and always on the brink of breaking through. But the shot never came. James had been wrestling with the company for seven years before Damien showed up, and had missed every boat that would have gotten him out. Somehow, when the bigger companies came looking for new talent, James had always been given the night off. Many of James' peers knew what was going on, but James was very trusting, and assumed it was just bad luck. Freddy Golden was an old friend, and always cursed James' rotten luck just as loudly as James did. Even as James brought in sold-out crowds, gotten the SAPW name buzzing, and kept Freddy Golden in the black, Freddy cursed loud and often. James was three-quarters Sioux Indian, so he was quickly given the ring name of Jimmy Strong Crow, along with completely inaccurate head dress, war paint, and a leather loincloth. James accepted his role without much resistance because he realized that if you have to explain why something is offensive, you can pretty much assume it will fall upon deaf ears. So, with little more than a shrug and a hungry daughter to feed, he took what was handed to him and paid the bills. Damien and Jimmy wrestled together until the day Jimmy hit the numbers. The story wasn't as funny as James tells it to be, but Damien often repeats it best he can. As the story goes, Freddy had come up short in paying the guys for the third week in a row, and the hundredth in total. Jimmy, having grown tired of wrestling for free, stormed into Freddy Golden's office, and demanded to be paid. Freddy emptied his pockets on the desk to show that there was no money to be demanded, and told James to take whatever was there and to pack his shit and go home. James had been replaced by a new meal ticket in Damien, so Freddy was willing to send the boy packing. Refusing to be fired without severance, took the seven dollars and fourteen cents, the crumpled lotto ticket, and a pen with a woman that got naked when you tipped the pen upside down, and stormed out. As luck would have it, the lotto ticket was worth seven millions dollars, and Jimmy was not legally required to give a penny of it to Freddy Golden. Freddy, as the story goes on, tried to cash in on his new meal ticket, but Damien Cruz had already been scouted and plucked up by the Wrestling League of America, an indie league that toured the country and paid a lot better. Since that day, James, his wife, and their two daughters had settled into the retired life. Debra Willis still taught at the local reservation High School, and James did computer repair to stay busy. Their older daughter, June, was eight, and had already begun to show signs of her father's bold curiosity and her mother's gentle patience. They also had a newborn daughter, Opal, was born on her father’s thirty-seventh birthday. James had an amazing wife, two beautiful daughters, a home that he had designed and helped build, and a life that allowed him to follow whatever whim he so chose. In a lot of ways, Damien knew that James deserved every blessing he had. He also knew that James had a lot of living to make up for, having given so much of it to the Business. Debra came on onto the porch holding Opal, and June followed behind her with a plate of chocolate cheesecake. There was a message written for Damien in strawberry icing, and Damien blushed when he read that it said ‘Good Luck, Damien!’ “June wrote it herself, Damien. And if you see that little bit of red by the corner; Opal wrote that.” Damien laughed, and thanked them both. The girls were then hugged, kissed, and sent off to bed. Debra joined the two men, and helped serve the cheesecake. Damien passed on a second serving of chocolate cheesecake with regret. His friend, who no longer had to worry about his 'fighting weight', cut a third slice for himself, tempting Damien. “I’ll tell you, I haven't seen my toes in over a year, and it feels great.' Jimmy was exaggerating, but not by much. His wife came up behind him, patting his growing potbelly. “More cushion for the pushin’, Baby.” he boasted, pulling her down onto his lap for a kiss. Debra hit her husband playfully, and then collected the dishes. “You owe me dishes duty this week.” James agreed half-heartedly, and the two men were left to their own devices again. “I wanted to thank you for letting me crash here. I am on my way to Washington in the morning, and couldn’t bear another night in a hotel.” James waved off Damien’s thanks, reminding him that he was always welcome. “Your family is awesome, Jimmy. I really can’t thank you enough.” James huffed, “Cruz, cut it out. I told you, it’s nothing. Now let’s stop ignoring the elephant in the room. What about Worth?” Damien sighed, allowing himself to face the reality for the first time since getting the card in the mail. He went from obscurity to facing the Halo winner in a span of two weeks. He was starting to feel a bit of vertigo. “What about him? He’s a force of nature. I’m not sure I know what to think at this point. I actually don’t think about it much. I never have. Right now, he’s a name on a piece of paper. I think I’ll really get a grip on my game plan when my music hits and I part the curtains, you know what I mean?” James nodded, and began to roll a cigarette. “I have a ritual. As I’m sitting in the dressing room, or in the bathroom, in some cases, I tape my hands. I started doing it after I broke my thumb back in Cielo, and have done it superstitiously ever since. Now I know why a lot of guys tape their hands, and I do it for those reasons too. But mostly, it’s a moment of Zen for me. On the ride to the venue, I’m listening to music, or doing interviews before hand. After I get prepped I’m standing by the entryway listening to the crowds, or listening to the TV and watching the matches before mine. In the seconds between my intro and the closing bell I’m surrounded by a wall of sounds, sights, and adrenaline. But there is this time when I’m by myself, getting ready to face it all. It’s that moment that I sit in silence, listening to the sound of the tape being pulled across my fist. I listen to the sound of my own breathing. I think about nothing, I just watch as the white tape stretches along my hands, and takes shape. And as the layers of tape go on, it’s like those ceremonies that the Samurai held in which layers of armor were going on. Each strip is put in place, and it’s like string of thoughts comes with it. After the first layer, I feel my body relax, and my muscles feel heavy, wet, and deeply attached to my skeleton. Then, with the second layer, they come to life. Every cell pulls tight against every other cell, and I feel like I am an organic whole. And it keeps going that way, like a giant machine being turned on section by section. Nerves get charged as tendons grow stiff and my eyes grow wide and responsive. I begin to hear, and see, and taste, and feel, and recognize the minutia of my surroundings. I begin to feel my body in a very intimate way. It’s surreal, but it’s also a clarity that I could not begin to describe. You know, even as I talk about it, I feel like I’m barely scratching at the surface of what I’m trying to describe. I just know that I am preparing my body for something very important. Even when it’s a dark match against a no-name, and everyone is expecting a squash, I follow my thoughts to that same place. I take nothing for granted.” The smoke streamed from Jimmy’s nostrils, and he seemed to be in his own head. He was listening to Damien, staring off into the darkness, hearing everything that The Latin Assassin was saying, and rolling the words around in his mouth. His understanding was palpable. “So what do you feel when you lose?” he asked. Damien could feel a coppery sting in the back of his throat as the sensation came back to him like a reflex. “I feel disconnected. I literally feel like my nerves have been burned at their tips. After the physical shock wears away, and that is usually very quickly, I feel like a disjointed mass of scar tissue, broken clockwork, and faulty wiring. I replay the match in my head, scrutinizing every mistake, recognizing every missed opportunity, cursing every time I had the match in my grasp and let it slide away. I think that my sanity is most at stake in that period in which I am trying to figure out why I failed to win. If the day ever comes in which I lose touch with myself, it will be in those times. I take losing very hard, and I guess that’s why I fight the way that I do. It feels like a basal drive. When I lose, or when I feel like I’m losing control of the match, I feel like a drowning man. I get rabid sometimes. I try to posture as this stoic, calculation, unshakable fighter, but in the back of my head, I’m clutching at whatever I can to regain equilibrium. Most times, I find the foothold, and get to go my way. But other times…” Damien stopped, noticing that his friend has begun to smile widely. “What? Are you laughing at me?” James shook his head, and took a long pull from his cigarette before crushing it out. “I am just starting to remember what it felt like to be where you are right now.” The man stood up, and Damien rose with him. “This Chet Worth guy is dangerous. He’s dangerous because he’s smart enough to take you seriously. From what I’ve seen, he takes everyone who steps between those ropes seriously. He’s not there to put on a good show; he’s there to snuff you out and collect his pay. If you think you can go to that ring with a game plan, he’ll just throw a curveball at you and have you chasing your own tail trying to stick to it. He’s a fucking bruiser. He wants to prove to the whole company that you are riding a lucky streak right into a brick wall. So don’t let him. If he wants a brawl, give him a bloodbath. If he decides he wants a wrestling match, put on a clinic. And so help me, if he plays it stiff, you break him in half. The second the ref gives you an inch of leeway, you take a fucking mile. The time for navel-gazing is long over. Now you didn’t ask for my input, but dammit if you’re gonna get it. I’m not your father, I’m not your coach, and I am not telling you things you don’t already know. Damien, I have watched you do things in that ring that amaze me. I have also watched you stumble and piss yourself because you get lost in your own head. PRIME is not for the weak, it’s not for the careful, and it’s not a place where effort is enough. Like it or not, you’re out of your element. But I get the feeling you like it that way. So do what you have to do to win this one.” Damien Cruz, although not surprised, was disarmed by the man’s almost desperate interest in what came next for him. “I appreciate your concern, Jimmy. And I appreciate your hospitality, your kindness, and your advice.” James offered Damien a handshake, which he accepted. With nothing to gain or lose, James gave him the last of the wisdom he had gathered after twenty years in the ring. “PRIME is a ripe fruit waiting for you to smash it open, Cruz. I have said that since day one. Now if you don’t see it for what it is, you’ll miss a great opportunity. But keep in mind that the moment to declare your intentions; you’ll have a huge bull’s eye on your chest. There are a lot of petty, vindictive, envious motherfuckers in this business. There are men- and you’ve seen it a million times- who will break your arm just to stop you from competing long enough to take your spot on the roster. There are a hundred guys who have no other talent but fucking it up for those who do. Guys like Freddy Golden who would rather earn a few hundred bucks off your sweat that let you earn the hundreds of thousands that you are worth someplace else. And there are guys like Chet Worth who don’t respect the sport, the lifestyle, or the sacrifices men like you and me make, but will smile wide for the cameras, and shit on everything we have broken bones and promises to uphold. Damien, you cannot let men like him eclipse men like us.” The Last Son of Lucha Libre remained silent, and James accepted it. The man placed a hand on Damien’s shoulder, and went off to bed. Damien, whose main purpose in coming there was a god night’s sleep in a welcoming bed, fell asleep on the porch chair under the stars, and woke up just as the sun rose over the Montana desert. As the plane took off, Damien looked down and watched as the Montana desert dropped away. It then hit him what the story really meant. He smiled, closed his eyes, and hoped that Chet Worth was ready to fly.
