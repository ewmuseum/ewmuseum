Good Help Is Hard To Find And Even Harder To Keep, Especially When You Fire Them

6 July 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: July 6th, 2009 Oh, what he would do for a nice, stretch Hummer limo. With a stocked wet bar, and a couple stacked women in the back? Heaven. Hell, even a chauffeured Town Car would do. At least he could stretch his legs out a bit instead of being crammed into that backseat like a sardine. The thoughts of longing race through his mind like streetcars as dust from the gravel road fills the inside of the taxi, irritating both the eyes and lungs. He pulls the neatly tucked and folded handkerchief from the front breast pocket of his grey suit jacket, quickly covering his mouth with the silky white material. Tyler Nelson coughs several times into the kerchief, a disgusted scowl on his face. His eyes squint and blink rapidly, tear ducts springing into action to try and wash the dust particles out. Still covering his mouth with one hand, he uses the other to pound on the Plexiglas partition between the front and back seats. “HEY!” the King of Greed shouts. “What the hell’s with all this dust? The windows are rolled up for Christ’s sake!” The cab driver, who actually could be mistaken for an ordinary, average guy if he weren’t driving a cab, waves a hand in front of his face as he tries to see the road through all the dust inside the vehicle. Satisfied with his visibility, he points a finger toward the floor. “The floorboard is rusted out,” the cabbie explains. “We’ll be lucky if we don’t drop right the fuck out onto the road. I’ve told the company a million times, but they won’t budge on giving me a new car. They say it’s not in the budget.” “I just had this suit cleaned,” says Tyler, pulling on the jacket’s lapel. “Do you realize how much that costs?” Nelson looks at the cab drivers attire, consisting of a black t-shirt and blue jeans. “Obviously not.” The Greediest Player in the Game does his best to fight off the dust that has infiltrated the vehicle as he looks out the window at the countryside. Everything looks the same as they pass row after row of corn. It’s almost enough to make you dizzy if you stare too long. He just shakes his head in amazement. How could anyone actually want to live out here? Prison seems like a better destination, assuming it’s one of those minimum security prisons that could probably double as a country club if they ever decided to charge admission. There aren’t very many reason’s for Tyler Nelson to even fly over the state of Iowa, let alone take a taxi into the middle of nowhere. But good help is hard to find and even harder to keep, especially when you fire them. This isn’t a time for pettiness, though. Whatever he did wrong, however heinous the indiscretion, Tyler will just have to dig deep and forgive him. The King of Greed is in a high stakes game of chess with a certain inferior woman who shall remain nameless *coughLisaTylercough*, and every king needs pawns to win. Don’t they? The cab begins to slowly decelerate and the dust trail left behind the car begins to catch up with it, engulfing it in a white cloud. After it dissipates we can see a road that leads to a farmhouse on the left hand side. The cab turns up the drive toward the house, passing a mailbox with a single word spelled out in black and silver reflective stickers. SIMMONS The farmhouse is quaint, if you want to put a word on it. It’s not big, just a one and a half story with a porch that wraps around from the front to the right side. The white paint is faded and peeling in some spots, and the shingles covering the roof are a weathered grey with a few tabs randomly missing. Parked in front of the house is a red late model Ford F-150 pick-up with a weed sprayer hooked up to the trailer hitch. A line of tall, mature trees runs along the west side of the property to block the high winds that can kick up from time to time. As the taxi comes to a stop in front of the house, the Greediest Player in the Game surveys the scene with contempt. Farmers are at the lower end of the totem pole as far as he’s concerned, unless you’re talking about the large agri-business conglomerates that get millions in subsidies from the government and swindle all the small farms. They know how to do business. Nelson taps on the partition to get the drivers attention. “Wait here,” he orders. “And be ready in case something goes down. I don’t know how this is going to go, so we might have to make a quick getaway.” The driver flashes a puzzled look at Tyler, but nods his head in compliance. The former CEO of Greed, INC opens the door and steps out of the cab, brushing the dust off his suit with his hands as he shakes his head in dismay. Satisfied that he’s not going to get it any cleaner, he buttons the jacket and heads toward the front door of the house. He casts a wary eye from side to side, looking out for the Children of the Corn. They had to be out there somewhere, didn’t they? He reaches the porch and raps on the screen door several times. Soon an elderly woman appears, her slight frame, tattered house coat, and long, stringy white hair telling a lot about her advanced age. She’s probably the oldest woman alive, Tyler reckons in his head as he forces a smile. “What can I do for you, sonny?” the woman asks, her shrill voice crackling like a witch on Halloween. The woman casts a keen eye on Tyler, looking him up and down. “I’m looking for Simmons. Does he live here?” queries Tyler as he looks past the woman into the house. The woman continues to eyeball Tyler, the wheels turning in her head. Finally a light bulb goes off. “I know who you are.” Tyler’s ego swells. He’s popular even in the boonies. “You do, huh? For ten bucks I’ll sign an autograph for you.” The car salesman-like grin on his face quickly disappears as the woman shoves the screen door open and kicks Tyler square in the shin. “OWW!! What gives, lady?” he whines as he recoils, a slight grimace on his face. The woman’s eyes are ablaze with anger as she points a crooked, accusatory finger at Tyler. “You’re the one who treated my grandson so bad for all those years! I oughta bend you over my knee and give you the spanking that your parents never gave you!” The woman advances on the King of Greed, albeit very slowly, her bare feet shuffling across the wooden porch. Tyler retreats to the top step, thrusting his hands out in front of him in an effort to get the woman to stop. “Look, I don’t know what the hell you’re rambling on about. Did you forget to take your meds, or is this what drinking well water does to you?” Suddenly a figure appears in the doorway behind the woman. “Grandma, what are you doing?” “Jesus, Simmons!” Tyler exclaims. “Can you put a leash on the old bag already?” Stepping out of the house and gently grabbing his grandmother by the shoulder is Mr. Simmons, the former head of security for Greed, INC., and more importantly, Tyler Nelson. He’s dressed in a flannel shirt and bib overalls, a far cry from his standard issue black suit and sunglasses. Simmons sports a full beard, and the hair on his head is rather shaggy and unkempt. “It’s okay, grandma,” Simmons assures her. “Let me talk to Mr. Nelson.” Grandma Simmons glares at Tyler, who curls his lip and childishly glares right back. She takes one more swipe at Tyler’s shin before retiring back into the house. Mr. Simmons turns to face Tyler, a surprised look on his face. “I didn’t think I’d ever see you again, let alone all the way out here.” Simmons scratches at his beard for a moment. “What are you doing out here, anyway? I assumed that when you fired me the last time that was it.” Tyler makes sure that Simmons’ battle-ax grandma has definitely disappeared before climbing back onto the porch next to Simmons. He gathers himself up and returns that fake smile to his face. “I just wanted to see how my favorite security guard was doing,” he lies. “It’s been such a long time.” Simmons nods his head in agreement. “That is has. Last I heard you were having some financial trouble. Did you get all of that squared away?” “Who is he, Geraldo Rivera?” Tyler thinks to himself. “No hill for a climber,” he responds. “I got a new gig back at PRIME.” “Running PRIME again? I thought you sold it.” “Not exactly, but I’m working on it.” Simmons pulls the screen door open and motions for Tyler enter the house. “Come on in, I’ll get us some lemonade.” “Are you gonna lock that lunatic up?” asks Tyler skeptically. Simmons smiles sheepishly. “Don’t worry about grandma, it’s ‘bout time for her nap anyhow.” The two men enter the house, with Nelson keeping a sharp lookout for Grandma Simmons. Simmons leads Tyler through the living room toward the kitchen. He doesn’t notice Tyler stopping to look at the pictures hanging on the wall. There are dozens of pictures and magazine covers in frames, all of them featuring Tyler Nelson. There are 8x10 glossies of Tyler from as far back as PCW. Also featured are plenty of wrestling magazine covers, all of them with Tyler Nelson gracing the page. Tyler smiles as he relives some of the memories in his head, from the Media takeover of PCW at Shanghei’d in China to winning the PCW World Championship and on to his days in FSW, OSW, and PRIME. “Ah, there you are. I thought I lost you.” Simmons walks over and hands Tyler a glass of cold lemonade. Tyler takes the glass and points at the wall. “I didn’t know I was your idol, Simmons. I’m flattered.” He takes a long drink of the lemonade… “Those are granny’s pictures.” …and almost chokes on it. “What the…*cough*…is she some type of stalker or something?” Simmons chuckles a bit, walking over to the wall and pointing to all of the various pictures. “Well, she couldn’t find many pictures of just me, so she had to pick these.” Tyler squints his eyes and follows Simmons’ finger as he points to himself in each one of the photos and magazine covers on the wall. He’s almost impossible to make out in many of them, either standing far in the background or even laid out on the ground in some of them. But he’s there nonetheless. The King of Greed, a little put off that the wall was in fact not a tribute to him, furrows his brow slightly. “I guess it’s time to get down to business, because I’m not sure how long I can stand being here. I’d like you to come and work for me again, as my bodyguard.” Simmons’ expression is unchanged. He takes a sip from his glass of lemonade, his eyes fixed on Tyler. “Hello?! Earth to Simmons!” Tyler quips. “Are you going to accept or what? I mean, I came all the way out here to offer you the chance to work for me again, and you’re gonna slow-roll me? I’m not going to beg, so let’s have it. Yes or no?” “No thanks,” Simmons says calmly. “Wha..what?” Tyler stammers, obviously shocked. Who turns down Tyler Nelson? Simmons walks back toward the porch, pushing the screen door open as he takes another sip of his lemonade. “I like it here, Mr. Nelson. Things are nice and calm, and I don’t get beat up every week.” Tyler sets his glass down on a table and follows Simmons out to the porch. “Nice and calm? With that banshee running around?” asks Tyler, alluding to Grandma Simmons. “How can you possibly want to stay here with that? I’d think that you’d be packed up and ready to go, just waiting for someone to give you a reason to leave.” Simmons looks out over his land, with the rows of corn stretching up from the ground. “You’ve never had any family to look after you, or to look after for that matter. You just don’t understand.” “I don’t understand?” Tyler’s feathers are getting slightly ruffled now. “What I don’t understand is how you can stand there and turn me down after all I’ve done for you. You may have taken one or two ass-kickings in the past, but you were always well compensated for it. And what about all the women you pulled while you were with me? You remember that freak down in Miami that wanted you to put a saddle on her? Doesn’t that count for something?” “She wanted to put a saddle on me,” Simmons corrects. “And you did compensate me plenty, and paid for all my hospital bills, too. I wouldn’t have anything I have today if it wasn’t for you. But that life is in the past for me. I have things to take care of here.” “Plowing the back forty is more exciting than working for me?” “Less stressful, that’s for sure.” Tyler puts his hands on his hips and sighs. What’s it going to take to get what he wants this time? If he had money he could just try to buy him off, but that’s obviously not an option. “Simmons,” Tyler says in a noticeably less conflictive and more serious tone. “I’ve got something big going on in PRIME, and I need someone with me I can trust. I need someone who’s been to war with me, who I know will take a bullet…or two. I need my old lackey back to make this thing work.” The former bodyguard shoots Tyler a frown, but he knows how his old boss works. It’s not easy for him to ask for help, and although the words may not be eloquent Simmons understands the underlying meaning. “Tell you what,” Simmons says. “I’ll come and help you for a few weeks. My cousin Shirley can come and stay with grandma for a while.” Tyler’s face lights up with accomplishment. “Go ahead and tell that taxi he can leave. I’ll get us to the airport after I pack and get a few things squared away.” Simmons starts back in the house, but Tyler stops him just as he grabs the handle on the screen door. “Say…can you float me a few bucks for the cab? I forgot my wallet.” That was a lie, as his wallet was indeed in his back pocket. It’s just that it was empty. Simmons shakes his head, a slight smile on his face. “I’ll take care of it.” The bodyguard makes his way down to the cab as Tyler watches on. The look of content on the King of Greed’s face is almost sickening. Things have gone quite well for him the last week or so. He managed to perform a hostile takeover of PRIME that was ridiculously successful. Lisa Tyler had to be hearing the footsteps of a man on a mission by now, if she ever made it back from her trip to Nicaragua. Now he had Mr. Simmons back to act as a buffer between himself and anyone who would attempt to harm him. Simmons always did make a good human shield. The pieces are all falling into place for Tyler Nelson. Falling into place, inDEED.
