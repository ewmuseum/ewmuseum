The Rise and Fall of the Insidious Fourth Reich. (Match RP, Culture Shock)

11 February 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 02/11/05 Pearl Harbor, Hawaii. On Sunday, December 7, 1941, Japanese Imperial forces launched a pre-emptive, sneak attack the United States of America. President Franklin Delano Roosevelt called this a “day that will live in infamy.” But at the Sapporo Dome, in Hokkaido, Japan, one member of PRIME will make their day of “infamy” synonymous with “victory.” On February 13th, 2005...infamy will be relived in the form of PRIME’s own imperial invasion of Japan. An empire of professional wrestling greatness. Culture Shock will send shockwaves through the ranks of professional wrestling, the very likes of which have never been seen before. Careers will be shattered and bodies will be left in tatters. While some will bring home gold, others will be brought home... In caskets. Emilio Rage realizes this, and he is prepared. Very prepared. He realizes that the stakes are high and that it will not just be about the Dark Age verses the noble Enlightenment. For when good and evil collide in front of millions and millions of people on a program, televised around the entire industrial world, gray engulfs everything. At this point, there will be no balance. The equilibrium of black and white will disappear in an ocean of hate leaving nothing but the “in between.” The morality of right and wrong, the perceptions of hot and cold, and indeed, even the notion of victory and defeat...tossed out the window. All. Utterly. Shattered. Coming off a sour defeat in PTC, Emilio cannot stomach another shallow loss. It hurts to fall from the ladder, and that much more the nearer and nearer you are to the top. It is just that simple. Come hell or highwater -although probably both....and then some- Emilio Rage will be there. He will find it in his heart to overcome the despotic notions of having been cheated. He will arrive in Japan fresher than last year’s electronics. Faster than last year’s Mitsubishi...well, okay, maybe a little faster. And deadlier than last year’s tsunami. Pack your bags, folks. Emilio is coming home to Japan. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ August 10, 2042. Mortimer Memorial International Museum, San Francisco. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ The clouds parted along the peripheral of the valley, shifting silently out to sea. The orange school bus hovered gently over the shoreline, zipping side to side as childish faces peered out the windows and the waves crashed gently into the sand. A small, rounded face stuck to the electro-glass barrier and peered out at the serene environment. No longer was real glass needed, magnetic field barriers having been readily accessible to the pubic for the past decade. Curious, the brown-haired child squished his plump face against the barrier, peering out behind them as the bus picked up speed. He watched intently with a tinkle in his blue eyes as a flock of seagulls drifted precariously nearby before swooping back into the air in a fascinating, spiraling whirl. “Ah,” he sighed, wishing he could be back at his home in Maine where the water was cool and darker. But he couldn’t help but smile to himself as the golden Californian sun rose heroically over the renovated Golden Gate bridge. The behemoth served only as eye-candy now, of no further practical use. All that was left was an historical museum lined with purposely placed relics of the old, combustion-chamber, fossil-fuel world. “Cars,” they were called. The word ran easily off the tongue. These, however, were the very same vehicles that had once polluted the earth. And as the bus zipped by, parallel to the great monument, Luke couldn’t help but smile with the deep dimples on the side of his lips penetrating into his warm cheeks. He wondered why people would live that way. Enjoying himself now, he began to forget of home. Besides, he thought, looking down as white walls of water broke across the humbled, jagged rocks, life was not too bad. The waves made the sandy beaches, he knew, by carving small particles off from the face of the seemingly un-penetrable stone cliffs. Slowly, century by century, creating the beauty before him. Luke knew that in the cosmic scheme of things his time away from mom would appear insignificant. Why, he thought, reprimanding himself solemnly, if this were the year 2000, it’d probably take several hours to get back home. An exhausting trip compared to the forty-nine-point-one-four minutes they’d made their trip in...Relax. Enjoy yourself. But as the hover-craft pulled into the docking point, rising over a wavy green hillside above the ocean line, Luke’s heart sank into his chest. A looming white building stood before him. Encompassed in an enormous white marble frame, supported by dozens of Greek Revival-esque pillars, was the museum he had dread visiting. The Mortimer International Museum. The bus slowed down, teetering wearily on the edge of rounded, cement clearing in the grass. All around the grounds of the museum, lining the perimeter of its lawn, there were fountains of marble angels. The small statues were cutely spitting water into a deep granite pool, much to amusement of Luke’s fellow children on board the bus. The bus released its gravi-metric traction field and came to a slow and steady stop. “I hate history...” As impressive as the site was -the tall, elaborate buildings, the smooth blue fountains, the tree sculptures, and the towering brown monuments, sculptures of various historical figures- Luke couldn’t help but feel a heavy load of acidic apprehension in the pit of his stomach. Whenever he was around such historical places, he always felt as if he were some small, non-recognizable, and utterly faceless part of the giant universe; A mere speck in the night sky. This caused a terrible, rotting feeling. And combined with the long, drawn out lectures given daily by the boring, white bearded men at school, it appeared as if there were a conspiracy to absolutely bore him to death. On his gravestone would read the dates “? - ?, Killed by the hand of History...but he’s not good with dates.” Ironically, if he had to compare himself to greatness someday, it’d be a shame that after simply not listening, he wouldn’t have a basis of comparison anyway. So he might as well remain that speck in the night sky. Living for a few years, and then burning out into non-existence. The various pods opened up at once. A moment later, children poured out of the craft like soldier ants, quicky scurrying across the green grass. “Hey!” Luke turned in time to see his friend Harley lash out, throwing him down to the grass. Luke couldn’t help but smile now, rolling back and forth over the ground as Ms. Wellington was too occupied with dozens of other pesky kids to notice their rough-housing. Ever since his brother Mat had set off to the University on Olympus, Mars, Luke took much satisfaction on rolling around on the floor of his bedroom with his best friend, Harley. Granted, howver, his ten-year-old companion wasn’t nearly the match that Mat was. A moment later, Luke had Hurley pinned to the ground, twitching and wriggling to get free. “G...Get away from me,” Hurley shouted out but not without a hint of playfulness. Luke just let out a laugh. Unfortunately, Ms. Wellington had gotten whiff of the mischief. “Mr. Ragerio,” she scornfully asked, “would you like to spend the rest of the trip in the crystalization chamber doing virtual studies?” She stood with her hands on her hips, questioning Luke from the front of the line. Luke bowed his head. “No ma’am.” “Well then please take your spot in line! We’re heading in,” she patiently explained to one of her obviously dumber students, “to see the E. Monument...” The young fifth-grade teacher trailed off before adding, “And Mr. Symington?” “Yes...?,” Harley called back in a sweet, innocent voice and a twinkle in his eye. Ms. Wellington couldn’t help but laugh. “Oh, you rascal...in line with you, too!” Harley gave his friend a soft punch on the shoulder as they ran to catch up with the group. Luke turned and rolled his eyes. “Teacher’s pet...” he shot. But Harley shrugged it off with a nervous laugh. He wasn’t known for being very popular in the class and certainly didn’t want to lose the person he considered to be his only friend. Luke, too, was the butt of many classroom jokes, being a very large kid for his age. Rumor had it that he stayed back three times in the first grade. Luke almost dreaded waking up every morning. “Okay children,” Ms. Wellington called out, “please find your note-partners as we’ll soon be heading into the Ragerio Library.” “Aw!” the students collectively called out in a chorus of dissatisfaction. But the persistent teacher just peered back and scornined them with her intent eyes. “Now children! Do you realize the trouble we went through to make this wonderful opportunity available to you? Why, when I was a little girl we had to sell candy bars just to take a second-class Trans-Atlantic Bullet through the ocean to India! Can you imagine?” she said, her feathers obviously ruffled. “No,” Luke piped up in the back. “But I can imagine being bored today...” he laughingly proclaimed. He then added, sweetly, “Ms. Wellington.” He gave a slight bow. The kids around him began to chuckle as Ms. Wellington focused her eyes, sending him a poignant reprimand. Luke took a step back, frightened. Rumor had it that Ms. Wellington used to be in the Psych-Core during the Eurideum Wars before becoming a teacher... Laughable. But nonetheless, the easily-scared child swallowed hard. Ms. Wellington simply shook her head in disapproval. “Now as I said, everyone line up. And oh!” she called out as the kids formed a somewhat crooked excuse for a line, packing in like sardines along the marble walkway leading up to the structure of the library. “Don’t forget your library cards...” “Whoops,” Luke joked, giving Harley a playful shrug. His small friend smiled back. “This is the only way you’ll get access to virtua-history chambers, and without them you might as well go...” But before she could finish, Luke turned away and jokingly made his way back toward the bus. This drew in another round of wanted laughter. “Class clown, eh?” mumbled the fat shuttle pilot as he gave Luke a stern look. “Not really, but judging your occupation, I’m bettin’ you were...” “What’s that?” asked the pilot, cupping his ear. “What, did you go def in the war?” “Huh?” Luke sighed as he turned to head back into line. But by now, the other children had already started piling their way into the building. The mischievous boy ran to catch up. A moment later he found himself peering up at a mammoth structure. A long rectangular building, ten stories tall and about five times as long. A mammoth gray statue of some Hispanic man guarded a marble white archway which led to the entrance. He held one of those old-fashioned, combustion-powered “bullet” guns, slunk heavily over his shoulder. As anti-historical as Luke was, as he walked by this statue, cautiously taking a moment to stop and admire its smooth figure, a chill went down his spine. The cold, dark eyes seemed to be staring off into space at some unknown and dark enemy. His masculine frame was adorned with odd, ancient clothing over a tired-looking body. Luke quickly slipped by. “There you are, mister man!” cried Ms. Wellington, quickly grabbing Luke by the arm. She always seemed to pop up at the most random moments, a proverbial jack-in-the-box to Luke’s disgruntled taste. “Mr. Ragerio, what in the word has gotten into you? Have you lost your mind? This is a school trip and I’ll not have one of my students getting lost in San Franciso. Could you imagine?” No, in fact, he couldn’t. But he could certainly imagine getting this academic leech peeling off from his free-spirited back. The young teacher quickly pulled herself together. As quick tempered as she was, she was just as sporadically forgiving. “Just listen here,” she instructed as she got down on her knees and wiped a soft hand across Luke’s dark face. “We’re here to learn, not to embarrass ourselves, okay?” Luke gave her a quick nod. Releasing a quick sigh, his teacher than turned and motioned him in the direction of a lone older lady, sitting by a virtua-computer at an exhibit on “21st Century Films.” “That nice young lady over there will get you set up...” I need to be set up with a ticket home, he thought dismally. If you had to jump through all these boring hoops to achieve the “sugar and spice” status, Luke decided he would rather be a hellion. “Yeah, sure,” said Luke, batting his eyes in a manner that suggested he simply didn’t have it in him to put up a fight. “Whatever.” Ms. Wellington quickly got to her feet, shooting him another one of her looks. It never ceased to amaze her students how her twinkly eyes could flip to those of Adolf Hitler at the drop of an accidental pencil, in class, and elsewhere. “Sometimes I just don’t understand you,” she said, folding her arms angrily over her chest. “Now you’re one of the brightest young men I’ve ever come across...Certainly, as Mr. Malone has told me, a great athlete as well...But what is it with you and history that just doesn’t seem to connect? Why, I never!” She then began tapping her feet in a manner Luke had only seen when she was ready to issue demerits. Luke swallowed hard. He couldn’t help but feel like an ant under the critical eye of his flustered-cheek teacher. All he could manage was a simple shrug. It had pained him constantly throughout his brief school career that everyone, including, it seemed, the teachers themselves, thought of him as your stereotypical jock. Sure, the gym teacher Mr. Malone and him had a good working relationship...But he never took free rides. “So,” she started as her pupil, with his head elsewhere, drew back a blank stare, “that’s it?” “Uh...Yeah,” said Luke, throwing off another jerk of his shoulders. “I...uh...well I just don’t care...I guess...” he started as he trailed off, seeing that this wasn’t the answer she was looking for. But after a moment of intense apprehension, Ms. Wellington’s face softened and her anger was replaced by a look of sincere pity. “Well, Luke...maybe....maybe today you’ll learn something,” she offered, forcing a smile. “I mean, you are the biggest boy in the class...therefore, your brain must be so too!” she joked while giving him a playful nudge. At this, Luke could only shake his head. His cheeks went red as he realized this was the generically inspired speech that special-ed teachers give to their small groups of students after they were done being reprimanded for fighting over marbles and such. How irritably degrading to one’s sense of intelligence. “Yeah...riiight...” “Well, in any case,” Ms. Wellington shot back as she gently took Luke by the arm. “Just pull out the library card, and let’s see if we can’t at least learn something today, now shall we?” “We shall,” responded Luke un-optimistically as he reached into hiscoat pocket to pull out his library card...and felt nothing. “Shall...not...” he said emphatically as he took a defensive step back. Wellington shot him another look as he bowed his head in shame. For a moment, Ms. Wellington said nothing. No words passed between the nagging spirit of the teacher and the thickheaded student. Luke just turned and watched a group of other children from another school run past talking excitedly amongst themselves. Nearby, the sounds of explosions rang out into the air, no doubt from an exhibit on one of the four famous “World Wars.” But Luke, he realized, ironically, couldn’t tell which one... Thick tears began to form in the corner of his eyes as Ms. Wellington simply shook her head. “Ah well...lunch break is in an hour. Until then,” she added, pointing back toward the entrance, “I guess you’ll just have to wait on the bus.”So there it was. His heart broken, young Ragerio prepared to head back to the bus. Yet again would he return home to mommy empty handed -empty headed, rather- with his tail tucked firmly between his oversized legs. He’d have to explain once again that he simply couldn’t grasp the concept. Didn’t understand the “stupid, God damn question!” That if two plus ‘X’ equaled four, it’s not what would ‘X’ equal, but simply, who cares? ...was his question. But the sad part was that people did care. His mother cared. He cared. He knew there was more between the block on his shoulders than a processing plant for football plays. But how to fill it with the all-empowering knowledge....how to learn how to learn, was beyond him. Without saying a word, Luke turned to exit the mammoth museum, feeling just as lonely as the animal eyes he peered into as he made his way outside, the documentary on some extinct species called “dolphins” playing above his head on the museum ceiling. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ He didn’t notice the strange man at first. A small, hump-backed hermit hobbled along the grounds of the museum, feeling the soft grass with his cane. His aim seemed to be as concentrated and directed as the attention span Luke had in class. He bumped into small statues and tripped across small monuments as he slowly made his way toward Luke. Luke had almost made it all the way back to the bus when the man suddenly turned and said, in a surprisingly youthful voice, “why so sullen, boy?” Sullen? Luke thought. That’s like understating the massiveness of the universe, or the tastiness of warm apple pie. Luke turned to face the man, raising a brow. The man had a short, stocky frame and looked as if he were quite strong as young man. But after living a full life, he now he walked with a limp, carrying a wrinkled face on his stooped shoulders, as well as a head, stereotypically covered with matted gray hair. He had deep green eyes and spoke with a slight jerk in his tone, wavering from high low pitches. Most notable of all, he wore a continuous smile. Luke couldn’t help but be fascinated by the odd figure. “Ah, nothin’,” Luke answered, the question suddenly dawning on him. Again, he bowed his solemn head in an almost ritualistic manner. For some strange reason the man gave Luke a wink. The every catalyst he needed to start out the day. Luke could feel the cool blood boil up under skin, gushing life back into his wary system. He opened his mouth and began to blat out all that was on his mind, respective of the odd feeling of the moment that had overtaken him. “It’s just that I hate this...this boring history crap and school stuff I have to put up with every day, and I now I’m stuck waiting in the bus until...”Luke felt he could go on and on. But the man shot him a certain look, and the boy quietly shut his mouth. “Did you say history,? the old man asked with a twinkle in his eye. “And ‘boring’ in the same sentence?” Luke store back, puzzled. There was something familiar about the intensity in the man’s tone and he felt, for once, like not spewing out all the bullshit he’d constantly fed to the open mouths of his teachers, principal, family...etc. “Well,” he replied, modestly, “it’s not that I don’t like history, it’s just that I...well...yeah, I hate it!” he said, stamping his foot hard on the ground. I mean, really, it’s such a dry subject...” His voice trailed off as he saw that the man seemed outwardly disappointed. Luke stood back, waiting for the man to respond. But he remained there, still. Silent. “Sir? Er...um...” Luke inquired... Finally, without saying a word, the man made a one-eighty and began marching in the direction of the museum. He turned and motioned Luke to follow him. “But,” Luke called out as he chased after the surprisingly fast old man. It was of no use though. He had no choice but to follow, compelled by his inner-instincts. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ A moment later, Luke found himself standing in what would have once been called a “library.” Scattered, dusty piled of old bundles of paper, printed with printed “ink”, lay strewn about, covering table, chairs, and stools. As tattered and clattered as the closed quarters was, Luke felt at ease. An old brick fireplace added an illuminated glow to the surroundings, painting wavering shadows across piles of old papers. The air smelled of a dusty attic...yet, without the dust. It had the feeling of a familiar place, hidden away in time...but very well preserved. Some un-nameable aroma came forth from a kettle over the fireplace, sweetly carrying its scent to Luke’s curious nostrils. “So,” the old man inquired as he took a seat on an old, dusty chair, “why were you being sent back?” Luke snapped out of his trance and peered back over at the man. “Huh?” The man blinked back at him, saying nothing. “Oh, yeah...” Luke began, biting his lip. “Ah, nothin’ bad...I...uh...just forgot my, pfft, library card.”he explained, rather annoyed. The old man just nodded his head complacently, staring off into space. “Well, who says you need a library card to learn history anyway?” “No one,” Luke laughed, “though she sent me back to the bus anyway...But I honestly didn’t lose it on purpose...” “I believe you, son,” the old man replied as he took a gentle sip from an old fashioned coffee mug. Luke squinted over toward the mug and saw that it read “Disney World,” and was imprinted with one of those old-fashioned, twentieth-century logos. The old man caught his look and smiled. “I thought you said you didn’t like history?” “I don’t,” started Luke, as he defensively held up his hands. “I just like the looks of the cup...that’s all.” “That’s all, huh?” the man asked without expecting a real answer. He set back in his old chair let out a slow, shallow sigh before putting down the mug with a shaky hand. The long, protracted bones seemed to stick out of the end of his fleshy, wrinkled hands as he tenderly reached over and pulled up one of those “book” relics off rom the table, all the while sporting a wide grin on his graying face. “Well, what if I told ya,” the man started with fire in his eyes, “that I could give you a better history lesson than...than all those damn fools,” he added, shaking his fist toward the ceiling where the first floor of the museum was. “And that without the help of them there fancy-shmancy machines, I could really make it come to life?” Okay. Great. Luke had a fun time. He got to speak with a cool -although slightly demented- old man, and shared a few good stories, as well as his thoughts on public education. But the time to leave was now, ripe for the picking when the seemingly innocent geezer brought up the no-no subject... “Ha! Yeah right...” Luke retorted as he rolled his eyes. After a quiet pause, however, he realized finally that the senile old man was frowning. Great. Now his feelings were hurt. Luke didn’t want to hurt his feelings. So he stepped toward the old man, forcing a look of fascination to come over his face. “Ah...well...what do you mean?” is all he could muster as a question, trying to entertain his tired soul. The man smiled back and gave the kid a wink. “Well son, unlike those fools above me, I lived through the damn mess, so if it wouldn’t bother you too much, if I told ya some of my stories.” He said this in a confident, a-matter-of-fact tone, although Luke had the impression he didn’t mean it. He couldn’t mean it. History was boring. Plain and simple. “Why,” said the man while he banged his cane on the floor, shaking the boards, “I bet you’d learn more from me than you would in them damn schools any day. Huh?” This time, Luke couldn’t help but light up with a genuine smile, shaking his head. “Well...I mean, I have lunch in an hour. But I’m sure a few stories wouldn’t hurt...” he added, smiling. The old man shook his head disagreeably. “I mean, I’d be honored.” said Luke, trying to make up for his failure. “Ohh! Time...bah! Time is a pigment of our imagination, an anchor of our soul. And if you could for one moment -just one moment- cast that linear, stupid perspective aside, you could see the universe as it is...” Unsure of what the old man meant, Luke simply shrugged as he took a seat opposite from the man. “So do you want to learn somethin’ or not, youngin?” “Heh! If you really believe all that mumbo-jumbo crap you just spewed,” Luke started, wanting to offend the man to the point of maybe forcing him away, “than yeah, screw the bus, I’m in!” Surprisingly, the old man smiled back at him with a long crooked grin. The fire lit in his eyes. “Well then, where was I? Ah yes...” Luke leaned back in his uncomfortable old-fashion seat, under the impression that it’d be a long, long day... Indeed. It was. _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _I. DARKNESS RISES _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ _ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~Spring, 2007 A.D.~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Earth was home to one of God’s most dysfunctional creatures. Man. For centuries he had polluted the earth with his vile concoctions, ideologies, religions. Their masterful machines, each one dubbed as the next evolutionary step in “progress,” ran at the expensive other species, and even at the expense of other men. For his own convenience, man subjected nature to his will. I say “he” because for the last thousand years they have organized themselves into patriarchal societies.But for thousands of years before the time of Christ, the Western mind had been formed in the minds of tribal leaders, spreading their propaganda like wildfire across central Europe and Asia. The age of the Greeks rose and fell, as did the Romans, as did the Chinese before them. And until the age called “The Enlightenment” came, it seemed that this imperial state of mind would continue to sink its roots into the dark heart of man, penetrating their souls for their bloodlust for land, gold, and battle...until there was nothing left. Then came the discovery of the New World. In an age where logic and reason began to finally prevail over nationalism, religion, and greed, the New World, born of The Enlightenment, was seen as an oracle of hope in a realm of despair. These settlers would cast their anchors just as they’d cast aside their petty notions of monarchy and “empire,” tossing them into the sea forever. Or did they? For as the New World was conquered, so were its one-time friendly inhabitants, the remnants of early human culture. These anachronistic “beasts,” had to be wiped out. And so they were. Three million of them were slaughtered like pigs. Treaties were signed and broken, cultures that were consistent with the laws of nature were now replaced with those of an angry, cultish, monotheistic being called “Yaweh,” or “God.” Those who didn’t conform were either murdered -by law, or illegally- or silently assassinated with biological warfare, exposing the “savages” to diseases such as “smallpox,” and other European-rooted ills that were harmful to their Native systems. Then slaves were brought in to do the bidding of the new masters, supposedly rooted in the cause of “democratic revolution.” Old empires were replaced my new, more malign institutions, under the guise of a “republican” agenda. On the inside, however, they were just a mutated, more sophisticated version of the empires of old. They directed the frustration of their own abused subjects through the ballot box as opposed to bloody revolution. This left just enough of the masses complacent so that the system could stay in place, rarely, if ever, to be questioned. And it happened simultaneously, all over the world. When Parliament finally stole the reigns from the royal family in Great Britain, the empire continued to occupy Scotland and Ireland, and colonized India. France colonized Vietnam, and the United States colonized almost all of Latin America. And when new and dangerous empires, just as insidious, came into form, the world clashed through two infamously brutal and bloody wars. Czarist Russia, Hitler’s Germany, and the Soviet Union all collapsed, failed copycats of the empires around them. It was at this time in history that a certain Vampir Nosferatu, Master of All Darkness, came onto the scene... He saw that his time had approached. He saw that while the world was engulfed in bloody, imperial wars, nationalistic institutions vying to subjugate the third world masses, the time to strike was then. Feeble humanity was ripe for the pickings. He saw that while all the bloodshed continued, gullible humanity continued to falsely believe that they were, in fact, logical, and that the era of Enlightenment was still upon them. To these poor, pitiful souls, weaknesses became strength and ignorance became a virtue. So the Dark Master made his move... ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ So by the spring of 2007 his plans were already in motion. A mysterious and seemingly un-curable virus swept its way across Europe in the early winter of 2005,. The first steps taken down the path to dominations were enacted, but not by His own hands. Rather, humans themselves took part in their own destruction. While they should have been preparing and working together in preparation of the oncoming Dark Age, they instead continued their petty national wars. In the ensuing years, rogue nations broke off from their colonial parents, rebelling at the injustices of their subjugation. The empires fought back. Not since the end of World War Two had the geopolitical map of the world been so greatly adjusted. Terrorist cells spread as interim governments collapsed, giving way to dictatorships. Right and Left-wing extremists took advantage of the polarizing situation and sought to enact their own petty agendas. Take the U.S., for example. After an unsuccessful invasion into Iran, in which the U.S. only managed to instill more heat into the flames of its angry masses, giving the fundamentalists a free ticket to power, the American empire began to fall. And before long, it was brought to its knees. After Egypt led the charge of rogue Middle Eastern countries, taking Israel by storm, the U.S. counter-front was forced to quickly withdraw from the region, leaving many of its military necessities behind. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~The Persian Gulf. Early 2006.~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ A group of scared soldiers stood huddled together, shivering in the cold desert night. Suddenly, a shot rang out, drawing their attention down a winding dirt road. The moans and echoes of countless dying souls filled the air with their melodic, deathly music. A bare-bellied, pregnant woman ran out from nowhere, suddenly appearing on the scene with a bundle wrapped in her arms. 'Bomb!' one of the men yelled. They raised their machine guns and fired, sending fiery sparks out to cut through the thick, black night whilst bullets ripped through her flesh. She fell to her knees as a spotlight shone down on her body. Blood ran in a thick, steady stream from her mouth. She dropped the package and died. A soldier moved in and kicked the package... A dead baby rolled out, head ridden with bullet holes. 'Shit!' cried the soldier as he dropped back in fright. 'Oh my God! God what have we done,' he shouted, dropping his weapon. He fell to the ground as his buddies moved in around him. “Shh. Dude, it's okay. We all...' began his buddy with tears welling up in his eyes. But the crying moans of his friend drowned him out. 'It's all good, Ross, we...we all make mis...' He never finished. His brains littered the side of the street as mortar crashed into their position, sending shards of metal in every direction and tore through unprotected flesh like butter. An entire platoon wiped out. The yellowish, inhuman eyes of an insurgent looked on from the safety of a decommissioned tank, rocket launcher in hand. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ At home, civil discontent slowly evolved into outright revolution. After the Supreme Nationalist Peacemakers (S.N.P.) came into power, via the ballot box, led by the tactful Dani Furher, the government began to dismantle itself, and, as the old proverb goes, the revolution ate its own tail. After several months of Marshall law, with the military taking control of the urban populace, Furher used the public’s discontent to his advantage. After a successful coup d’ ta, the S.N.P. had taken the executive branch by force. From behind his desk at the White House, Furher had the control to blackmail the divided Congress into submission.
