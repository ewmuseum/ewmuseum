Dusk and Dawn?

31 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/31/05 Humiliation. Regret aside, it is the most painful emotion in the repertoire of the human spirit. For six months, Emilio played daddy to Peter Vetra.. Every time the uppity smidgeon of a competitor stepped into the ring with the Alias Champ, Emilio didn’t just wrestle him, he humiliated him. But on the ReVolution before Ultra Violence, the very card preceding the biggest night of Emilio’s career, the reverse was true. On that night, Emilio was not on the top of his game. A week of torturous reflection and self-ostracizing left him open for attack. And the wolves seized on the opportunity. If Vetra’s patented Underestimation didn’t knock a few screws lose, a few proverbial joints out of place, the Pentafix certainly did. Vampir Nosferatu, leader of the Dark Age, and Dark Demigod of the wrestling world, unfurled his banner of chaos on ReVolution 59. Assisted by the insidious Tiamat, the omnipotent harbinger of hatred unleashed his demons on an unsuspecting Emilio Rage. And the Alias Champ, although he retained his title, paid the ultimate price. His dignity. Touche, Vampir. Touche. Nosferatu capitalized on Emilio’s lack of focus and blind-sided him with the biggest embarrassment of his career. A loss to Peter Vetra. An unmistakable stain on his vibrant ladder-climbing story in PRIME. To top it off, the Pentafix to the outside nearly shattered Emilio’s spirit and his bones. When Emilio hit that cement with an appropriate thud, for a moment, he couldn’t feel his legs. If his body had just contorted a few degrees in either direction, who knows if he’d be walking today. With the PTC Global Title match right between that night of misery and the approaching Ultra Violence in his hometown of Boston, Massachusetts, Emilio had his plate full, and most of the ensuing meal tasted sour. Pub grievances. “You don’t think I was gonna kick out of that move!?” Emilio interrogated, slamming his bottle of Boone’s Farm down on the top of the bar, spilling the cheap wine everywhere. Maya let out a sigh, using a towel to wipe the red stain at her shirt. “Well I was, buddy! I was.” Emilio trailed off, feeling the ache rise like the phoenix in his flesh. Another shard of pain shot through his lower back. Quietly, he released the bottle, spilling wine everywhere. His training partner and friend, Mike Lornitzo, grabbed the bottle and tossed it aside. “Come on, hone,” said Maya, biting her bottom lip, “we’re going home.” “Wait,” Emilio said, struggling to shake her off. He turned and looked back at his friend, Mike. “And I wasn’t gonna lose that match, either,” he informed, drunkenly, “and you remember that.” With a cocky laugh, he took his finger and jammed it into Mike’s chest. Mike slowly turned his gaze, looking up to face Maya. Maya shook her head apologetically. “Yeah, I thought so,” Emilio said, collapsing to the table. “Come on...” Maya said, tugging on her boyfriend’s collar. With Mike’s help, she lifted Emilio to his feet and directed him toward the bar, dodging between tables where drinking patrons watched on with pity as a wrestling hero and local celebrity was shamefully dragged out of the bar. The mood of things. “What were you thinking!?” Rather than give her a proper response, Emilio turned his head on the pillow and burped. Perhaps a night of drinking was not what he needed. A night on the town, said Mike, would be good. But what’s good and golden can only turn to led when the alchemist is in his lowest hour. Instead of the others bringing his spirits up, Emilio only lowered his friends to the lowest common denominator: himself. The night was ruined. He was exhausted. Training for the Global Title match was painful enough, but to lose to Peter Vetra was almost unfathomable. The interview afterward, compliments of a local new channel when he stepped off the plane at the Boston airport, was tantalizingly brutal. The media simply ripped him apart. “Mr. Ragerio, after losing to Peter Vetra, who many would have one-time considered toward the bottom end of the roster, how do you expect to defeat Vampir Nosferatu?” “Is it true that you’ll be competing in a Japanese Death Match? And right after your Global Title match?” “Are you afraid?” “So, Vampir Nosferatu is undefeated in singles competition. Your thoughts.” “My thoughts? Get the fuck out of my way...” He manually parted a whole through the crowd with his briefcase, and by accident toppled a young reporter along the way. The Post and the Inquirer had a field day with that pic... What a week. Maya jumped out of bed angrily, her feet landing with a thud on the hard floor. She ripped the sheet off the bed and drug it across the floor, heading toward the living room. He’d been acting like this for days. A fool. A drunkenly malign, cocky, arrogant son of a bitch. A complete, utter fool. Maybe, then, it was time to spill the beans...Give him a wake-up call. With the news she just heard from Mike, she knew, however, she couldn’t tell him now. While it may have had its magic effect and lifted him from the shallow depths of self-pity, it was a risk she wouldn’t gamble tonight. For all intense and purposes, it could break him in this weak condition. “But honey,” Emilio began in a futile protest. “But you made a total ass of yourself! And it wasn’t just tonight, hone,” said Maya, softening her tone. “You’ve been...you’ve been this way for days. And well,” she added, taking a deep breath, “maybe you just need some time alone.” With that, she exited the bedroom and slammed the door shut. Emilio let out a groan. He lay, eyes wide open, in the quiet dark solitude of his room, staring up at the black ceiling. He couldn’t sleep. Instead, he immersed himself in the isolated cathedral of his mind, giving in just as the toller struck the bell. Did he really? [TWO WEEKS AGO] Patch pulled the trigger. His body jerked suddenly as he toppled down on the couch... Exhausted. Video games. Ever since he was a child, they had been his salvation. They served as his antidote to the vile trials and tribulations of everyday life. When he accidentally poisoned his hamster with kerosine, “Super Mario Bros.” When he broke up with Sheela after the junior prom, “Ninja Gaiden.” And now, unable to let the words flow from his quivering lips as Sheri towered over him with her hands on her hips -“What!? [LAST WEEK] Officer Starr sat quietly at his desk. A box of stale, half-eaten donuts set on one side of his computer, an old cup of Dunkin Donuts coffee on the other. He leaned back in his chair and folded his arms behind his back. His darkened eyes, drained from a lack of sleep, looked idly around the police station. Officers moved to and fro, filing reports, laughing gently, and talking business under the bright lights. Starr reached up and pulled down the shades. Slowly, his eyelids began to close over his wondering eyes. He let out a yawn and leaned further back in his seat. His new assignment had been one of great importance to the mayor. It involved the protection of a local celebrity and Chief Brunson saw Starr as the only field agent capable of taking on the task. Though Starr felt honored -his buddies dubbed him “Star Starr”- he couldn’t help but think he bit off more than he could chew. All the international relations, drug money, laundering...he felt like an ant trying to prevent a boulder from rolling off a cliff. Already, he’d had to make a few gutsy decisions, too. The federal government, the mafia, they all had an interest in this case. It was hard enough to keep the feds off his back, let alone his own teammates. Junior deputy of this operation, Michaels, was an old friend of Starr’s, and he felt bad withholding information. But some of it, well, it just had to be done. Before he could engage in his slumber, he was awakened by a quick beep. The glow from his computer intensified, spitting out a message in front of him. He read the report, rubbed his eyes, and then read it again. The text didn’t change. He grabbed for his coat, knocking over the coffee with a sharp elbow. “Shit.” He fixed his tie, threw on the coat, and ran out of his office. “Wait ‘til they get a load of this...” He didn’t have much time. Harboring mystery. [PRESENT DAY] The two weeks had gone by fast and without much incident. From the living room, a long pair of arms held Sylvia tight, pulling her into his tall frame with a gun held on the squirming girl’s head. “Hello, my name is Ben,” the Asian man started with a smile. What? Emilio’s entire frame shook. The decisive words gouged at his body, forcing him to reel back in surprise. He was stunned. A daughter. Amelia’s daughter. His daughter. It was almost too much to take. “Yeah,” he said, pausing, “yeah, I’ll call you back. Uh huh. Thanks.” He hung up the receiver.
