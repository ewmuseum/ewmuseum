Visions (ReV 81 Roleplay)

15 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 11-15-05 “I dream my painting and paint my dream.”~ Vincent Van Gogh Visions are mental images produced by the imagination. We all have them, for if we didn’t, no one would succeed in life. They provide us with a path of what we hope of the future, and once we foresee that path we follow hoping to cherish our visions. Our visions are the children of our souls, and if we have the courage to pursue them, all of our dreams will come true. ~~~ Amber Dalton lay nestled in bed; between the sheets, hands tucked underneath her head, as she stared blankly up at the glass ceiling. She listened to the heavy breathing of her two-year old son, Santana, and began to think about his father, the man she hadn’t seen in so long. It was the month of morality for her, a tradition she had continued since she was sixteen, so the examination took her to no surprise. Like most Sunday nights, the San Diego, California sky glistened beautifully with stars, as the sun had just begun to disappear over the horizon. In a daze, she didn’t notice that the moon had risen overhead until its light illuminated the room with a divine glow. She raised her hand up to her face and gently caressed it, reminding herself of what he used to do. In fact, if it wasn’t for him, she probably wouldn’t have been there in the first place. A huge argument over another woman, and suspicion, ceased a divorce and torn a once happy family in two. She would never admit it, but deep down inside, beyond his late night creeping, and a few crumbled phone numbers in his pockets, she loved Carson Collinson, or as we know him, Chris Collins. More importantly, she felt that she needed him. From across the room the door slowly squeaked open snapping her back into reality and returning her to the woman she created and hid all her emotions behind. The light from the hallway crept through the cracks of the door and blinded her like it did each morning. The doorknob swung and bounced off the wall denting it and created a noise loud enough to wake a bear from hibernation. Amber jumped in shock and looked over her shoulder at the crib to the right of the room, relieved that Santana was still asleep. “Oops sorry, I didn’t know he was sleep.” Her sister, Rhonda whispered through loose lips as she continued to emerge through the darkness. “Don’t worry about it.” Amber replied. “It’s my fault. I should have been got that thing fixed.” She pulled the sheets from over her body and threw them to the bottom of the bed. Rhonda, still crept, until she finally took a seat beside the woman who she had shared her home with. “Did I wake you?” Rhonda asked frantically out of breath diving into her sister’s arms and connecting with a huge. Amber shook her head. “What are you doing here in the dark?” “Just, thinking.” “Thinking huh?” She scratched her head and tried to figure out what the matter was. “Don’t tell me it’s that time of the month again?” Rhonda could see right through her sister. She knew what she was thinking ever since they were kids. That’s what being a big sister was all about. She took Amber’s hands into her own and looked deeply into her eyes. She could feel the hurt and pain she had been going through by glaring at the tears that formed just below her lashes. Amber began to whimper and gently pulled away. “No.” She sniffed. “No, it isn’t that. I’m just tired that’s all.” Rhonda nodded her head and smiled. “I hope so.” Using her two index fingers, Rhonda wiped away the tears, and accidental clumps of Amber’s dried up mascara from the bottom of her eyes, just where the tears had made their path down her dry face. She reached over and snatched a few Kleenex tissues from its box and handed them to her little sister. “Now dry yourself off and come down stairs. I have someone here who wants to meet you.” “But…” “No buts about it.” “I’m a mess. Just look at me.” Rhonda tilted her head to both sides and a smile grew on her face. “You’re beautiful. Now throw something on, I’ll meet you downstairs.” ~~~ It had been almost an hour since her sister Rhonda had announced that she had a surprise for Amber downstairs, but she was in no rush to find out what it was. Rhonda always did things like this to make Amber feel better, but in the end her attempt to make things better always whirl winded into only making things worse. It didn’t even feel right to her. She and Chris had been divorced for almost six months now but she still loved him. She didn’t want to meet another man, because she didn’t want to fall back in love. Huh… love who needs love? She looked into the mirror, eyes wide open and hated the person she saw. This wasn’t her… this couldn’t of been her. This person was a liar, a cheater, and the thick contents of make-up covered just who she really was. It bottled up all her emotions, and brought an ache to her heart. She didn’t want to be that person anymore. She wanted to start over. She pulled the gun shaped faucet handle towards her body as steaming hot water spewed from the device. She took another look into the mirror and cupped her hands into a bowel underneath the pouring water. Its steam condensed into vapor and dew formed against the mirror fogging it instantaneously. She caught as much of it as she could and bent down, splashing the water into her face. In the act of doing this her brown wavy hair dangled to the sides of her face, damp at all ends. She pulled the handle back to its normal position, wiped a circle in the midst of the mirror with the palm of her hand and looked back at her reflection. Distraught at her irregularity and the sight of make-up everywhere, she grew frustrated and became depressed. She chafed the make-up with both hands into the pores of her brown skin and slapped her hair back behind her head. With hesitation Amber pulled the cabinet holding the mirror she so ever gazed into open. Shelves upon shelves of bottles all stood lined color coordinated for easy access. Amber reached for a yellow and white bottle, Zoloft, the anti-depression pills she had been taking for the past three weeks, noticing what it was off hand. Her hands began to shake as she grasped the top of the Zoloft bottle with strength that seemed unknown to her. She placed it on the counter of the sink and twisted the top to the right popping it off and sending pills to whisk in every direction. She could hear the pills rattle against the base of the sink as they tumbled down the drain helplessly. Amber dropped to her knees. “…Shit,” she uttered through a cry. Amber consolidated the remaining pills back into the bottle leaving two in the palms of her hands. She placed the open bottle back atop of the sink and catapulted the two pills, one after another without the use of water down her throat and into her system. She swallowed spit and held her throat in profound pain feeling like she had just eaten a brick. She rubbed the temple of her head in circles with a few fingers -- this usually calmed her down-- and recollected herself from the cold slightly damp floor. “Why am I doing this to myself?” Barefoot she walked uncomfortably across the tile of the bathroom floor pushing open the door which led to her room. Ever since she was a child, she always wanted her own bathroom and now she finally had that. She walked towards the crib where Santana had been laying and picked him up. Santana stuck his tongue out through his chubby cheeks and slowly opened his eyes only to close them readjusting. Amber cradled him underneath her arms and rocked him slowly back and forth. She pulled his blanket tightly over his body swaddling him like the infant that he was. She softly sang him a lullaby. “Rock-a-bye baby, in the tree top, When the wind blows, the cradle will rock,When the bough breaks, the cradle will fall,And down will come baby, cradle and all.” From the soft and comforting harmony of her voice, you could tell that she was an actor trying to make a breakthrough into Hollywood. Amber kissed Santana on the forehead and hugged him one last time before returning him to rest. “I’ll miss you…” She whispered. She twist and twirled back around heading for the bathroom. When she reached, she walked back inside, turned off the light and closed the door behind her. ~~~ Rhonda looked down at her watch for about the third time in the hour. She grew concerned, wondering just what was taking her sister so long. She leaned forward and rested her long head in the tips of her fingers. A figure sat across from her, clad in a black and blue pin striped suit. A hat with a feather sat across the top of his head, and the dim light made it almost impossible to see who he was. “Where is she?” The man grew impatient. There was something about him. The tone of his voice articulated with familiarity, but it was much too early to have any suspicions. “I don’t know,” Rhonda replied. “You think she’s alright? Should I go and check on her?” “No, no, no.” Rhonda assured. “Let me go and talk to her. I am her big sister you know?” She insisted. He lifted his hand and cued for her to sit down. “I pretty sure she’s alright. Let’s give her a few more minutes.” Rhonda nodded and took a seat. Her glass of wine sparkled on the wooden table, bubbling, and waiting for her to drink from it. She reached for it and lifted the glass to her lips ingesting as much of the wine as she could in one shot. She grew a little woozy. “So, how’s life?” She asked of the man. He coughed clearing his throat. “It’s alright I guess.” He told her. “But it could be better. Hey do you mind if I turn on this light? It’s pretty dark in here?” “Sure.” You could see his hand rise above his head in the darkness as he reached for the beaded string underneath the lamp shade. As quick as you could snap your fingers he pulled the string down towards the earth and in an instant the room was set aglow. It was Chris Collins. “Now, that’s a lot better.” Chris exclaimed with excitement feeling a bit refreshed. “Back to what I was saying. It seems like my life is finally heading into the right direction. This ReVolution I’m up for the Pulse Championship against the likes of Chandler Tsonda and the Illustrious Face Eater.” Rhonda nodded and replied tipsily with question. “That’s good?” “Damn right, and I have a good chance at taking home the title as well.” “How, so?” Rhonda asked sounding as interested as humanly possible. “Well, for one I’ve already beaten Adam Dick a.k.a. Facey in Core Wrestling for the Classic Championship, it should be no problem doing it again the second time around.” “And, Tsonda?” Rhonda asked. “I don’t know. Mentally thanks to the visions I’ve been having I think I’m there.” “Glad to see everything in your little world is falling into place.” Chris readjusted himself on the couch taking a breath of fresh air, simply ignoring Rhonda’s attempt at insulting him. He never liked her before, but he was doing his best to bite his tongue. “That’s why I’m here. I’ve been doing some deep thinking and I finally realize that I love her and only want to be with her. I know I’ve cheated on her before, but hopefully she’ll forgive me and take me back. I visualize the two of us raising Santana and continuing our family.” Rhonda shook her head in disproval. She began to pour herself another drink but refused knowing her limit. “It’s a little too late for that.” She told him. “You haven’t been in Santana’s life for two years and now you’re just trying to waltz your way back into it?” Chris sighed. “Those have been the worse two years of my life.” He explained. “I’m nothing without them. I know I’ve done wrong in my past but I have… NEED a second chance.” Rhonda continued to shake her head. She rose from the couch and walked over to the bottom of the stairs leaning on the wooden rail of the staircase. “I don’t know. You’ve hurt my sister one too many times and I don’t know if I’m ready to see her like that again.” “But you have to understand.” He tried to persuade. “I love her.” Rhonda grunted. “Pfft… That’s what all men say, few really mean it.” “I’m one of the few. What can I do to prove it to you?” “I’m not the one you have to prove anything to, Chris.” Rhonda reminded. “Even if I did say yeah, she’s still the one who has to make the final decision. “I guess you do have a point.” The two sat in utter silence. Rhonda looked up the stairs leaning forward trying to hear what was going on above them. It seemed like nothing. She started towards the couch where she had previously sat, but her silence was broken by the crying of the baby, Santana. “I’m going to go and check on them.” She told Chris. “I’m going with you,” He demanded. ~~~ The room was dark, empty and beyond the crying too silent. The door to the bathroom stood halfway open. Chris and Rhonda scurried into the room as quick as they could; looking for both Amber and Santana, making sure that everything was alright. Santana’s whimpering made it easy for them to find him in the darkness. Chris reached forward and pulled the son that he hadn’t seen or even cared for in two years up to his body, hugging him tightly not wanting to let go. “Shh…” He told the little man. “Daddy’s here now. Everything’s going to be alright.” Rhonda began to panic. “Amber!” She yelled across the room. “Amber! Where are you?” There was no reply. Rhonda, familiar with the room leapt over to the wall and flicked the light on. She could hear some sort of gargle coming from the bathroom and knew that her sister had to be in there. “Amber is that you?” She asked not knowing what was to come. “Chris I think she’s in there.” “Let’s find out.” He told her. They both emerged towards the bathroom and opened the door with ease sending it bouncing off the wall. “Amber! Amber where are you?” Rhonda cried out not being able to see a thing. Chris reached, one hand holding the baby who had continued to cry, and the other for the light switch at the side on the wall. …CLICK… Went the light as it flickered on. What they saw was too much to explain in words. On the floor lay Amber. Eyes rolled to the back of her head, the bottle which had once contained the pills now scattered across the floor in one hand, and white foam oozing from her mouth. “AMBER!” Rhonda screeched. “AMBER!” But it seemed too late. Chris tried to calm Rhonda pulling her away from her little sister. He handed her the baby and kneeled down to the woman that he loved. Tears had formed in the pits of his eyes and he lifted her head from the floor. “…Amber.” He cried out. “Amber. Speak to me honey. I love you. I’m sorry. I’m sorry.” He tried to apologize. But it was too late. Chris placed his fingertips gently on her neck awaiting a pulse but there was nothing. The woman he had once loved and the mother of his son, Amber Natalyah Dalton was dead.
