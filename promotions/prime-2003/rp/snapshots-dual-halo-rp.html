Date: Sixteen Years Los Angeles, California Saturday, March 8th, 2008 "It is with the deepest and my most sincere regrets, that I hereby tender my resignation with PRIME effective immediately." Pen to paper. Paper to pen. His glaring red eyes, filled with sadness and contempt, holds the pen gently in the crook of his thumb. He reads the words out loud in his own mind and hates how they sound, how they feel, but most importantly, what they mean. Craig's right hand remains steady; the pen just sitting there, wondering to itself if it's done for the evening. It's the left hand though that would cause any normal person to worry. It sits there trembling uncontrollably with no end in sight. A tingling sensation runs up and down his arm as he desperately tries to make sense of it all. For the past hour, he's grabbed at his arm, hoping to relax the nerve, but it's a crystal clear sign to him that he's losing everything by the minute. His brain is fighting against him and it's winning with ease. The pressure continues to build in his mind as he closes his eyes and let's his head just hang there for a second. Giving up the only constant in your life, the only thing that you know is unsettling, nerve racking, and painful. So much has changed around him, his life, marriage, a daughter, and death. However, each time something drastic happened in his life, he was able to turn to wrestling. Being in that ring, he knew it would help him figure everything out in his life. Except for this. For nearly two months now, he'd been living with Multiple Sclerosis, if living's the right word. The days were becoming increasingly shorter for him. The pain borderline unmanageable. Bottles and syringes of different drugs littered his bathroom, hidden from the naked eye, and all they did was set his nerves on fire or cause him extreme fatigue or nausea. The dosage on each drug had been increased significantly because of his will and desire to continue to compete night in and out in that ring he loved so much. Was love supposed to hurt this much? He was almost certain that it wasn't. Life had left him no other choice, not with the crossroads he's faced with now. The decision had been made for him. Now, he only had to follow through with it and not chicken out like he wanted to do so badly. No, he tells himself. This is it. Eighteen years later, this is it. If only it wasn't, everything would be so much simpler. With the Dual Halo coming up so fast, he wouldn't be able to have that chance at becoming great, becoming iconic. It eats away at him, the reality settling in and giving him no other option but to give up. A problem for a man who's never given up before. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ They say "they never really miss you til you dead or you gone" So on that note I'm leaving after the song ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Boston, Massachusetts March 3rd, 1992 "What the hell're you doin' here, kid?" Don't do it to yourself. Don't make my same mistakes!" Frank yells at Craig who just stands there and takes it all. The words simmer for a minute in Craig's mind before it hits him. "The heart attack...," his voice trails off as a light bulb clicks on in his mind. "Heart disease. When I found out, I gave up the sport. Then, a few years later, I died of a heart attack. I gave up the one thing that I was certain would kill me faster, and even though I did it, I still died from it. MS doesn't go away, Craig. You will die one day. Not wrestling, not standing in that ring and competing won't make it just go disappear all of a sudden. Trust me, I know from experience. Take my advice here, boy," Frank says, revealing even more of himself to Craig. The words ring true in his ears as he looks back at the piece of paper with his writing on it. "It is with the deepest and my most sincere regrets, that I hereby tender my resignation with PRIME effective immediately." He picks it up in his hand and just looks at it before looking at Frank again. "You want that defining moment? That cage, that's going to be your defining moment. Don't you let it slip past you. Don't give up on it before you've really gone at it," Frank says and Craig's eyes fill with tears that he somehow manages to keep at bay. The paper in his hand crumples as he closes his fingers around it. He closes his eyes and can see and feel the fatigue and pain in his mind. Instead of letting it linger there, he does what he's always done; push it to the side. He can feel his whole body shaking, distraught from the entire encounter. "Frank...," Craig says with his eyes closed, but he doesn't hear a response. As he opens his eyes he finds that Frank isn't there anymore. No longer can he keep the tears at bay as he feels his body just let go. His knees grow weak as he stumbles to the nearby wall and slowly slides down the wall to the floor. Everything he'd bottled up for the past sixteen years was pouring out of him. He could only manage to say one final thing. "I'm sorry."
