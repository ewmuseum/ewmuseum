Date: August 8th, 2005 In winning the Star Wars CW Lightsaber Battle™, Clyde found himself in a position too good to pass up. He was going to defraud a charitable organization for his own personal gain. When the call from the Children’s Make-A-Wish Foundation came, Clyde was wary about accepting their offer. The moment they mentioned there would be cash and candy involved, however, he agreed to meet with them that afternoon. The following is not just an account of what happened on that day, but what happened during the entire five days between then and when they arrived at Salt Lake City. Enjoy. Or…die. I “For the last time, Clyde,” Farwell said as the two men sat in their parked rental car, “I’m not going to let you commit fraud. Now go into that Make-A-Wish thingy and tell them you can’t be their spokesman.” “Why can’t I be?” Clyde whined, puffing up his chest and getting ready for a tantrum. “They called me…because of my mad skillz, yo! It ain’t my fault all the honeys want to wax my jimmy-stick and get their stank all up on my hang-down.” Farwell sighed to himself. “That’s it, I’m canceling MTV when we get home.” Clyde simply crossed his arms and pouted further. “Das cold, yo. I thought you was my peep.” “What in the name of hell does your turning black have to do with marshmallow birds?” James questioned, narrowing his eyes as he stared at Clyde. “Anyways, get in there and tell them you’re not allowed.” “B-b-but…” Clyde started up, giving the puppy dog eyes. “They offered me a lot of money.” Farwell’s right eyebrow shot up. “How much money? I mean…it doesn’t matter. You can’t just go about committing fraudulent acts whenever you feel like it.” Clyde’s tone became straightforward and dead serious. “They offered me a hundred-thousand and all the candy I could carry. I’ll give you half and we’ll live like kings.” James was a little taken aback by what Clyde said, and took a few moments to let it all sink in, as his thoughts turned to what the future may hold for him if they were to take the money. Cut to Farwell sitting on a plush white couch with a hoard of women surrounding him. Decked out in a dreadlock wig and 80s-style clothes, James puts his arms around the two closest women as they lean into him. “I’m Rich James, bitch!” Back to The Forsaken, as the sound of Farwell’s laughter fades away. “Clyde,” he started, coming out of the daydream, “remember what I said about always being honest, as well as never taking money from strangers?” Clyde nodded. “Forget it all. Everything.” James leaned over and opened Clyde’s door. “Now go out there and milk them for all they’re worth!” Grinning widely, Walkins burst from the car. Well, he tried to, anyway. After choking himself out on the seatbelt, Clyde unbuckled it and fell out the open door, causing James to shake his head in disappointment. But Clyde was soon smiling once more at the thought of what lay before him, and as he slammed the car door shut he found himself wondering where he had left his Chocolate Wookie. As James pulled out of the parking lot and began singing Ted DiBiase’s theme music under his breath, he soon came to a red light and slowly checked his rearview mirror after coming to a complete stop. And there he saw it. Buckled up safely, Clyde’s Chocolate Wookie sat in the middle of the back seat, staring straight into Farwell’s eyes through the mirror. James shuddered as he felt a chill run down his spine. When the hell did Clyde put that there? James wondered silently, unable to take his eyes off the Wookie, even as the light turned to green and horns began sounding behind him. Finally snapping out of the trance, Farwell made a mental note not to look into the rearview mirror ever again. And he didn’t. Only when James had parked the cheap rental car in the hospital’s underground parking lot did he look back to check up on the Wookie. The seatbelt was undone. “That’s it, I’m outta here,” James said, opening the car door before hastily exiting the vehicle. Not bothering to lock it, Farwell shut the door and prepared himself for a visit with an old friend. Meanwhile, Clyde was being given the grand tour of the Children’s Make-A-Wish Foundation headquarters. “And this,” the executive showing him around said, as she raised her arm to point to a blue door, “is where the magic happens. Inside this very room are the dreams and wishes of thousands of terminally ill children, and it’s our goal to see that they come true.” “You mean you’ll grant any wish in the world?” Clyde asked, an idea to further defraud the charity developing in his vastly inferior brain. “Oh, of course,” she responded, smiling. “We try to make the last hours of our patients their best ones. In fact, we even keep a list of past wishes so children can make easier choices as to what they want their last memory to be.” After being handed part of the list, Clyde scanned over it, noting names and numbers for each one. But one such name caught his eye, for under the number 00137 was the word ‘Clown’ with the rest of the words around it blacked out. “What’s this one do?” he asked inquisitively. “We…don’t get many clown visits anymore. Not since the last one,” she said solemnly, taking the list back from Clyde’s hands. Cut to a smiling young boy sitting on a hospital bed, waiting for his wish to come true as the door to the room slowly opens and in walks Ronald McDonald. Hanging his brown overcoat and hat on the back of the door as it closes, he turns towards the kid, taking off his belt as he does. “I’ve had a hard day at work, so it’s gonna be rough.” And then nothing but darkness as he flicks off the light switch. Shuffling the papers back into her briefcase, the executive looked back at Clyde, who was humming the tune to Gilligan’s Island, before questioning him. “So have we come to an agreement, Mr. Walkins?” Clyde took a second to think about it, before responding with, “SHOW ME THE MISAPPROPRIATED MONEY!” A snap of the fingers later, as well as a contract signing, and Clyde was in possession of two hulking bags of cash, conveniently marked with the dollar sign right on them in fluorescent green. After he left the building, Walkins looked around the parking lot but couldn’t see James anywhere, and so decided to get back to the hotel on foot. Five seconds after turning the corner, Clyde spotted a dark alley. “Ooh! A dark alley!” he gushed. See? Running into the alley with a moronic grin on his face, Clyde was soon assaulted by a group of gang members and homeless people. Taking a urine-soaked rag to the face, Clyde shrieked and dropped the bags of money, as the attacking group moved in on him for the kill. As he felt punches and kicks strike him with an animalistic fury that can only come from living on the streets or listening to a Good Charlotte CD, Clyde accepted his fate, giving praise to the Chocolate Wookie for making his death a swift one. But just as he felt the life slip from his body, he was rescued. Looking up to see the person who saved his life, Clyde could only make out the silhouette of a man with graceful flowing locks against the setting sun in the background. “J-Jesus…is that you?” Clyde asked, climbing to his knees. “Close,” the voice responded, as the figure moved forward to assist Clyde in standing. “I’m Shawn Michaels. Pleased to meet you.” Oh my God, Clyde thought to himself in a panic, this is the guy whose copyright I always infringe upon. Ok, Clyde, be cool. He doesn’t know who you are. Just be cool. “I stole your moves,” Clyde blurted out. “E-Excuse me?” Shawn asked quickly, raising an eyebrow. “I mean…I loved your last match. It was a real classic.” Michaels laughed to himself. “Yeah…yeah, it was. They always are.” And then an awkward pause ensued. “So…” Clyde said, looking around before picking up the bags of money. “Thanks for saving me and everything, Mr. Michaels.” “Please,” Shawn said, raising his hands. “Call me Jesus. My mother calls me Mr. Michaels.” “But, you just said-…” “Wait! My Son-Of-God Sense is tingling! I must take my leave,” Shawn said quickly, cutting off Clyde. Ripping his sweatshirt in half to reveal a black t-shirt with the words ‘God Milk’ in white lettering underneath, Shawn ‘Jesus’ Michaels took off out of view, leaving Clyde alone with his money amidst a pile of dead bodies. “Hmm,” he mused to himself as he stepped over a few corpses and out of the alley, “seems like a nice guy.” As the sun began to further set in the background, Clyde wondered where James could possibly be. Coming out of the hospital with a solemn look on his face, Farwell made his way to the underground parking lot, finally finding the rental car after several long minutes of searching. Opening the door, James plunked down into the drivers seat, turning as he noticed something out of the corner of his eye. Sitting in the passenger seat, buckled up, was the Chocolate Wookie. Normally that would have been enough to freak out anyone, but the fact that blood coated the entire seat and door cinched it. Following the trail of blood with his eyes, James swallowed hard as he saw the cadaver of some thug in a ski mask with a screwdriver in his hand slumped over partially into the car. Noticing no blood on the Wookie, save for on a small brown stick that resembled a club in his right hand - which James knew wasn’t there before - Farwell leaned over the seat and slowly pushed the body out of the car, pulling the door shut afterwards. Quickly moving away from the Wookie, James kept an eye on it, then quickly realized the blood on the club was gone. Almost to the point of tears, James knew what had to be done. When he pulled up to the carwash, Farwell rolled down all the windows, then stepped out, preparing to let it go through on its own. Not being a complete idiot, however, James quickly leaned back in and removed the Chocolate Wookie, not wanting to chance angering it by drowning it in hot soapy water. Five minutes later and he was driving back to pick up Clyde, everything in the car making a sloshing noise each time James moved. Keeping an eye on the Wookie the entire time, Farwell was actually relieved when he found Clyde sitting on the street corner being propositioned by elderly men. Walkins clutched the bags of money to his chest and ran through a sea of groping old men, finally reaching the car. Not questioning the Wookie in the front seat or the general wetness of the vehicle itself, Clyde yelled for James to hit the gas, and they quickly disappeared into the night. II James didn’t bring up what happened with the Wookie. He didn’t want to frighten Clyde, but he also didn’t want to risk saying anything about the Wookie within earshot of it. There would be an investigation, but the car rental place could deal with that. The Forsaken had already taken off on the next leg of their adventure in a new car from a new dealership, and James wasn’t about to let himself take the heat for a crime some evil candy committed. “We have to take a slight detour,” Farwell said casually, not even turning to Clyde or the Wookie seated on his lap. “I went to see my uncle in the hospital yesterday. The doctors say he doesn’t have long, and seeing as how I’m his only living relative, he’s decided to leave his estate to me. All we have to do is sign some papers at the house and it’s all ours.” Clyde didn’t respond. He couldn’t respond. Worried, James looked over and saw Clyde with his eyes shut, tongue hanging out of his mouth. A slight glow seemed to emanate from the Wookie. “Clyde? Clyde?” James shook his shoulder, and instantly Clyde sprung to life. Meanwhile, Farwell tried to shake off the impending heart attack as he took the exit that would lead them to his uncle’s estate. “Where are we going?” Clyde asked after several seconds, as he yawned and stretched himself awake. “Disneyland?” James sighed to himself. It was going to be a long couple of days. III “Templeton, is that you?” an elderly woman’s voice asked. Opening his eyes groggily, Clyde turned slowly to look out the window of their rental car, instantly noticing the faces of two old people pressed up against the glass. “It is!” the old man next to the woman cried out. “It’s our little Templeton! He’s come back to see us, ma!” Clyde slowly sunk down in his seat. He didn’t want to be Templeton. No one wanted to be Templeton. But for some strange reason, these two old people thought he was just that person. “Who the hell’s Templeton?” James asked wearily, stepping out of the car. “Our boy,” the two cried out together. “Our little boy’s all grown up! Templeton, come give your parents a hug!” Clyde quickly locked his door. Looking for an out, he lifted the Wookie to eye level. “Your name isn’t Templeton, is it?” The Wookie remained motionless. “Didn’t think so.” Clyde hung his head in disappointment. “Why do I have to be Templeton?” The clawing at the door caused Clyde to snap back to the reality at hand. Moving away from their peering faces, he yelled for James to help him, but Farwell was already unloading a few of their suitcases from the trunk of the rental car. “Don’t worry, they’ll tire themselves out. Templeton.” And then James was back in the house. An hour-and-a-half later, Clyde walked through the front door, lipstick smeared all over his forehead and cheeks. The Chocolate Wookie under one arm, Walkins slammed the door shut and locked it, gasping for air as Farwell came up behind him. “Finally got away, eh?” James asked, smiling as Clyde turned to reveal the lipstick smudges on his face. “Only by the grace of the Chocolate Wookie,” Clyde said, bowing his head as he did so. “I should sue them for emotional distress. They kept me locked in a car for over an hour, in ninety-degree heat!” “It’s seventy-five-degrees out, and I saw about eight chances for you to escape in the first ten minutes alone,” James paused for a moment. “Besides, it’s not like you have the best luck when it comes to frivolous lawsuits.” Cut to a courtroom, with Clyde and James standing at the plaintiff bench. “The case of Walkins versus God shall now begin,” the judge says, looking to the defendant. “Where is the defendant, anyway?” Clyde and James snicker. “I guess if he doesn’t show up, I win by default,” Clyde says, proud of his master plan. Just then, the door to the courtroom is pushed open, as God and a team of twelve high-priced lawyers enter the room. Moving to his respective bench, God places his briefcase on the table in front of him, cracking it open. “Let’s do this,” he says, as his team of lawyers encircle him. God turns to Clyde, then looks up at the judge. “Sorry for my lateness. I’d have been early, but somebody slashed my tires.” Turning back to Clyde, God’s eyes narrow as Walkins nervously bites his lower lip, and Farwell just accepts defeat. Back to The Forsaken. “I guess you’re right,” Clyde sighed, petting the Chocolate Wookie. “It’s just that hearing the name weighs heavily on my soul…” “Don’t worry, Templeton,” James grinned, “we only have to stay the night, and by this time tomorrow we’ll be back on the road.” Clyde hoped James was right. He didn’t know how much more Templeton he could handle. His soul already weighed three pounds as it was. IV Turns out, he could handle a whole lot of it. All the rest of the day, and through the entire night, the old couple walked around the house, eerily moaning the name ‘Templeton’ like two otherworldly specters back from the dead, with the sole intent of making your life a living hell just because they can. “I don’t know how much more I can take,” Clyde groaned, while James played Solitaire to pass the time until the executor of his uncle’s will arrived. “It’s always Templeton-this, or Templeton-that…it’s driving me crazy.” “That Chocolate Wookie’s driving you crazy,” Farwell muttered after noticing the Wookie was nowhere in sight. “Where is that thing, anyway?” Clyde shrugged. “What am I, my Wookie’s keeper?” Almost on cue, a shrill shriek echoed through the air, followed by the sound of a sharp object slashing through some form of matter. Clyde and James just looked at each other. “I guess we know where it is now,” Farwell said quietly, putting the cards back into the deck before moving towards his uncle’s old rifle cabinet. “Don’t be silly,” Clyde responded. “A Wookie’s scream is defined by several interesting characteristics. One of which, oddly enough, is a small, dangling-” “No! No more!” James’ exclamation was one of horrified disgust, as he put up his hands in surrender. Letting out a heavy sigh, James grabbed the first gun he saw, made sure to load it, then began walking in the direction of the scream. Coming to one of the empty rooms, Farwell slowly pushed the door open with the muzzle of the gun, steadying the rifle as he walked into the room. Sitting in the middle of the floor was the Chocolate Wookie. And nothing else. “That’s it, I’m outta here,” Farwell said for the second time during their adventure, dropping the gun and walking away as Clyde ran in the room to scoop up the Wookie. An hour later, the executor of the will arrived for the finalizations, as James learned his uncle had passed away around the same time he heard the scream. Could it have just been some strange ghostly goodbye, or something more? As they left, Clyde breathed a sigh of relief when the old couple didn’t come by again to call him Templeton and sexually accost him. Leaning back in his seat, he closed his eyes and smiled. The Wookie simply began to glow again as Farwell kept his eyes on the road. V They had finally arrived at their destination, with a few days to spare. Clyde and James were a hundred-thousand-dollars richer between the two of them, Farwell had just inherited a semi-mansion, and the Templeton incident was all but forgotten. James just couldn’t shake out the images left in his mind by the Chocolate Wookie. There was something not quite right about that thing, and the longer he let Clyde keep it, the longer the two of them were in danger. It was at a McDonald’s restroom - where so many illegitimate children were fathered - that James finally got Clyde alone. “We have to ditch the Wookie,” Farwell said bluntly, catching Walkins off guard. “But I can’t do that to him,” Clyde responded quickly, in a whiney tone. “He’s my best friend in the whole wide world.” “I thought I was your best friend,” James said, then quickly corrected himself. “It doesn’t matter. The thing is pure evil, and it needs to go. I think it killed some guy who tried to steal our car…and then those two old people went missing after we heard that scream. We could be next, Clyde.” “Wait, who would want to steal our car?” “Some punk kid with little to no sense of value. That was back at the hospital,” James checked behind him for any sign of the Wookie. “I found him halfway in the car with blood everywhere. And the Wookie was in the front seat, when it was in the back before.” “So it kills people who try to do us wrong, what’s the big deal?” Clyde said nonchalantly, patting James on the shoulder. “I think you just have a case of Wookie-envy.” “Wookie-envy?” James raised an eyebrow. “Fine, if you want to keep that thing, go right ahead. But don’t come crying to me when you wake up without a head. And I swear to God, if that thing kills me…I will kill you…” Clyde laughed. “How are you gonna kill me, if you’re already dead? Huh? Huh?” James just stared at him. Clyde’s laughter turned to nervous fear. “Fine, I’ll take away its Gaffi Stick then,” Clyde conceded. “Happy now?” Two hours later, The Forsaken were settled in their hotel room, Clyde having removed the Wookie’s…Gaffi Stick and thrown it out the window. “I’m gonna take a shower,” Clyde called out. “Keep an eye on the Wookie for me.” James’ blood ran cold when he heard those words. Turning to try and find the Wookie, he spotted it sitting on the kitchen counter. Where the club used to be, a fork now rested. “I hate my life…”
