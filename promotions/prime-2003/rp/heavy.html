Date: September Chicago. September 15th, 2008 “Geez man, you oughta see a shrink." “I am.” He clenched his hands, feeling plastic crack under the pressure of his fingertips. The earth beneath him struck with a jolt and as his innards were beaten like eggs in a cake mix, he fought back the pungent taste of puke. “You can ease up on those arm rests, buddy. Hard parts over.” Flying had never been one of Kaiser Vashaun’s preferred methods of travel. It had been the only true drawback to a career in professional wrestling. In the last six months, he’d survived more flights than he would have cared for in a lifetime. And this particular flight might have been the worst yet. Problems with weight-distribution and being twelfth in line for departure, with the air-conditioning off to conserve power, had led to a miserable wait. Nasty turbulence had kicked in once they’d finally gotten the plane off the ground, and… “Geez pal, I think you owe the airline a new seat.” …and loud, obnoxious passengers flanked him on each side. Not to mention that one of them smelled awful, like a big toe that had been stuck in someone’s ass. “Six hundred bucks for a coach ticket… I’d say we’re even.” Kaiser removed his death-grip from the arm rests, the plastic cracking and popping upon release. “I just don’t care much for landings.” “What’s so bad about those? Once you’re on the ground, you can’t fall outta the sky anymore.” “That’s true.” Kaiser wasn’t even sure why he was engaging the guy in conversation. As the pilot came over the speaker and began the typical ‘thanks for joining us routine, Kaiser just continued to chat, all the while trying to breathe through his mouth. “But you can plow two hundred miles an hour into a building during the landing.” “Jesus, you got problems.” “Can you maybe talk to someone else or somethin’?” “Sor-ry.” The guy held up his hands as if to back off, then rolled his eyes as he turned and said something to the person across the aisle. “Current temperature is 97 degrees. Thanks for choosing Delta Air Lines.” “97 degrees? In September?” Talking to no one in particular, Kaiser unhooked his seatbelt as the plane came to a stop. “Christ, that’s warm.” “Bout average for this time a year.” The guy to Kaiser’s right, who emitted the rancid odors that had nearly grounded the plane, chimed in with his two cents while he scratched at a rash on his arm. “Right. Hundred fucking degrees in Chicago. Average. ” As Kaiser pushed his way into the crowded aisle of the plane, he made a note to himself. Long delay on the runway. Turbulence in the air. Flanked by loud, obnoxious, smelly… and apparently…stupid passengers. No maybe about it. This had been the worst flight of his life. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Last Week – The Office of Dr. Trowbridge “What went through your mind at Colossus when you captured the Intense Championship?” “Relief.” “How so?” Dr. Trowbridge crossed his legs and leaned back in his chair, making himself comfortable. “Relieved the match was over. There for a minute, I didn’t think I was ever gonna be able to keep that guy down. I thought we were gonna keep fighting through the end of the PPV feed.” “Anything else?” “Relieved that after all the trash-talking I’d done, calling myself the uncrowned champ, that I was able to somehow pull through and not look like a jackass in front of 80,000 people. I had to come out on top. Otherwise, I don’t know what woulda happened with my career.” “What’s next for you? Professionally?” “Cataclysm. The big SCCW Vs PRIME show.” Kaiser bit at his thumbnail while he shoved the words through clenched teeth. “You have a match?” “Champion Vs Champion. LiveWire Vs Intense. Gotta match against a guy named King Blueberry." "I see." The doctor chuckled as he scribbled something on his notepad. "Why are you laughing?" "You don't find something amusing about facing a guy who's named after fruit?" "I take all opponents seriously. Especially those in possession of a shiny belt. I don't think you quite understand what it takes to be a champion in this business, doc" Kaiser leaned back in the over-stuffed leather couch and put his hands on his knees. "But yeah... his name's goofy as shit." "So then, this is a very big deal for you." "It's pretty simple. Superiority is at stake." "Ah, a sense of self-worth." "No. Fed superiority. Every last sonofabitch in PRIME." "Well," Dr. Trowbridge put down his pen and folded his arms across his chest, "that's a lot of people." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Chicago? September 15th, 2008. He didn’t remember O’Hare airport being anywhere near the MGM. Or a pyramid. “Shit.” Kaiser glanced at his watch. 3:03P.M. He was running late. The goddamn delay on the flight was gonna cause him to miss his meeting with Blaine Blair. And as CEO and a member of PRIME’s Board of Directors, being late to a meeting with Blair was like a guy being late to his own funeral. Just didn’t happen. Only today, it looked like history was going to be made. “SHIT!” Kaiser maneuvered through the crowded airport terminal like an awkward sixteen year old taking their driving exam, swerving left, swerving right, stopping suddenly, and occasionally running someone over. He was nearing the the eleventh different 'baggage claim' arrow, pointing the ninth different direction, when a burst of baldness, sticking out through a mess of red mop, caught his eye. "Debo!" Kaiser threw up his right hand in an effort to direct attention towards the random shout. "Christ." Ronald DeBrosse let his balding head sink for a moment, wondering if he could just pretend like he hadn't noticed a thing, but as he heard his name shouted again, he realized it was pointless. Stopping in his tracks, he adjusted the strap of his carry-on bag that hung from his shoulder. "Kaiser." "Hey, Debo. How's it goin?" "Good. I'm good. And for the record, you're six and a half feet tall. You don't need to raise your damn hand to stand out in a crowd." "Fair point." Kaiser looked back and forth, almost pacing in place. "Listen, I'm supposed to meet with Blair in like twenty minutes. You know a quick way across Chicago?" "What?" "My flight was running late. I need to make up time, or I'm gonna be late for my meeting with Blair. So help me out here...you're my agent... that's what you're supposed to do. Besides, I know you don't want to see one of your clients get his ass fired. That's money out of your pocket." "Have you lost your mind or something?" Debo glanced over his shoulder, looking ahead towards his gate. "Is this some sort of joke?" "Why?" "I'm not your agent anymore. In fact, I'm not a sports agent at all. I haven't had a client in months, and you know it, jackass. I don't need you rubbing it in my face that gone are the days of being my own boss. And now...now i'm stuck in some lame-ass nine-to-five job, making same-day flights back and forth across the county, wherever my boss tells me... all to schill some crappy products to corporations, so my superiors can have even more cash to waste on new cars, or new houses, or new sets of tits for their wives." "What the hell are you talking about?" Kaiser's voice rose above the hum of the crowd. "Forget it. I'm not playing this game .Something's seriously wrong with you." Debo pointed down the aisle way, towards the gates from which Kaiser had just come. "Anyways, I've got a flight to catch." And heading in the direction he'd just pointed, Ronald DeBrosse slithered his way through the mess of travelers. As he disappeared in the swarm of human life, Kaiser thought he heard the word "Dickhead" float back to his ears. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Last Week – The Office of Dr. Trowbridge "I've never been beaten. Never pinned. Never submitted. Now each week, I have to further that. Winning is like going out to the mall and holding the door open for a stranger. They walk through and nod a 'thank you. And then the stranger right behind them wants to sneak on through while you're holding open the door. And then another person, and another. Each one of 'em smiling as they step by... thinking so highly of you. But ya can't ever stop now. You just have to keep holding open the door for everyone, cuz the second you let it go and step inside yourself... then you're just the asshole who slammed the door in someone's face. Nobody remembers the two dozen people who smiled as you held it open for them." "Kaiser," The doctor removed the pad of paper from his lap and set it on the small coffee table at his feet. Leaning forward, he clasped his hands as he rested his elbows on his knees. "it seems very clear to me that you consider yourself the foundation for everything and everyone around you. In our past meetings I've heard the same sentiments about your life at home. You feel every terrible thing that has happened to your sister, to your father... your entire family... is somehow your fault. And you, and only you, can right the ship for all of them. Here, it's the same thing professionally. You've put every superstar, every member of the Board of Directors, every fan of PRIME on your back." "So?" "So that's a heavy load to carry." "I'll be sure to lift with my legs." "It's too much pressure for one man to put on himself. And it's completely unwarranted." Dr. Trowbridge sat back in his seat, hoping his own relaxed posture would calm his patient. "Kaiser, the balance of the universe doesn't rest on your shoulders. People's lives don't depend on whether or not you win some match, or a series of matches. The only thing the outcome of the contest affects... is the record book." ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ Chicago? September 15th, 2008? "What the hell?!" Standing some forty feet in front of him, off to the side of the baggage claim, Kaiser could have sworn he saw the figure of Blaine Blair. Only it was impossible. Blaine Blair was waiting at headquarters, in some elegent office with mahogany blinds. And Blaine Blair wore personally-fitted, pinstriped suits, with the neck-tie always perfect, never loosened or crooked even an eighth of an inch. So the gentlemen waiting at the baggage claim, with the stubble of a five-day beard and a cheap brown suit, minus tie, couldn't possibly have been Blair. Kaiser turned his attention away from the man, and focused on the parade of luggage that slid around the conveyor belt. As he watched and waited for his bag, he nearly forgot about Blaine's grungy twin. Until he heard his name echo through the large room. "Kaiser!" Headed his way, in full 'dressed-down, needing a shower' form, was the geeky fellow who'd rose up the management ranks right in front of everyone's eyes on PRIME's flagship show, ReVolution. "Blair?" "Yeah..." "What the hell are you doing here? I'm supposed to meet you at the Chicago headquarters in..." As Kaiser turned his back on the circling luggage, he glanced at his watch. "... eight minutes." "HA!" Blair scratched at the back of his neck. "Funny." "Why is that funny?" "Uhh... the fact that the office building in Illinois was knocked down a few months ago?" "What are you talking about? I flew out here to Chicago specifically to meet with you about the PRIME Vs SCCW supershow." "Did you notice the desert you were flying over? The hotels? The Sphinx? This isn't Chicago, Kaiser. It's McCarran International airport... in Las Vegas. And yes, we're here to meet about SCCW and PRIME, but not the Cataclysm show. That's the past. We're here to talk about a potential development deal for you." "Wait. Wait a minute." Kaiser looked around frantically. The pieces of the puzzle started to come together in his head. A hundred degrees in September might have been unheard of for Chicago, but not for the desert of Nevada. "What's going on? Cataclysm is over?" "Yeah." "What's the date today?" "September 15th, 2009? What day did you think it was?" "Holy shit..." Kaiser searched for support, made his way over to a marble pillar and leaned against it. "I missed something. What the hell happened?" "Beg pardon?" "What happened? At Cataclysm. What the fuck is going on?" "Are you serious?" Blair raised an eyebrow at his former employee. "Yeah." "Cataclysm... the big SCCW Vs PRIME show. You lost to King Blueberry in the LiveWire Vs Intense Champion match. Not what we'd hoped for. As a result, SCCW staked claim to the throne. PRIME struggled along for four or five months, then SCCW bought it out. They closed headquarters in Chicago, tore the building down, laid-off a bunch of employees... crew members, reporters, wrestlers. Set up shop out here in Vegas, where they keep PRIME going as a farm-league of sorts. Feeder fed where they bring along guys who are rough around the edges. I'm in charge of developmental talent for PRIME. Bit of a step down from Board of Directors for a billion dollar organization. Thanks, by the way." "This isn't possible." Kaiser shook his head, trying to rattle Blaine's words from his brain. "I didn't think you could lose either. Might have been alright had it been an intimidating fruit like an orange or a lime. But a frickin' blueberry?" "Limes are intimidating?" "They're sour." "What happened to everyone else?" "Everyone else?" "Cantrell. Debo..." Kaiser ran his hand along his shaven head, wishing he had hair to pull from his scalp. "C.P. is back in reality TV. He's working on Big Brother... whatever number they're on. And apparently, he pitched an idea that FOX is in love with. 'Temptation Jacuzzi'. Kinda like Temptation Island, only a more... cozy setting. And a slightly smaller budget. As for your ol' sports agent... after he let you go..." "Let me go?!" "... his reputation took a hit. He was always a small-time guy anyways. Three or four months ago, he closed the agency and now he sells composite super-spatulas and assorted kitchen appliances. Or something like that." Blair sneezed, then wiped his nose across his sleeve, streaking a bit of snot in his stubbled beard. "And you... you kinda fell off the face of the earth for a few months. Then popped back up on the indy scene in the south a few weeks ago. And that's why we're here. To see if you might be interested in a developmental deal. Just an opportunity to get back on your feet. No garunteed money. "Heh" Kaiser let out the weakest laugh, then turned and knocked his head against the marble pillar three times. Everything had just turned upside down and fallen to shit. All in the span of a couple minutes. "Did you really think this was 2008... a week before Cataclysm?" "Yeah." "And that you were in Chicago... not Sin City?" "Yeah." "Geez, Kaiser. You oughta see a shrink." Kaiser turned back toward the baggage claim, and tried to forget about the snot in Blair's beard. "I am."
