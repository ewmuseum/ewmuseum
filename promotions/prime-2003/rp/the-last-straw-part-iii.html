Date: Around the Holidays NOVEMBER, 2008 “Ya know, this place is something else.” He’d never realized it before, but the office wasn’t what he would have expected for a psychologist. Bright colors, intriguing and expensive artwork, a sleek expensive desk… those were the sort of things Kaiser Vashaun thought would best describe a therapist’s office. But, the room he sat in for ninety-minutes, twice a month, was very much the opposite. The furniture was unspectacular. The walls, which were painted a hunter green, featured just a couple quant pictures of some rock formations. Thick wood blinds were slanted partially open… but even in the middle of the day… the doctor couldn’t take notes without the aide of a lamp. “I rather like it.” “Yeah, I guess I just figured a psychologist would have things a little…brighter?” “I’ve always liked dark colors. My living room at home is painted in maroon.” “Does that speak to deeper issues?” “Who’s shrinking who here?” “Sorry, Doc.” Kaiser leaned back in the couch, cracking his knuckles as he did so. “Anyway…” “Anyway?” “Sometimes I wonder if I’m putting too much into the JitC… into PRIME altogether… like maybe I’m too focused on achieving success after success. Ya know what I’m talking about?” “All too well, Kaiser.” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ THURSDAY, DECEMBER 3rd, 2008 Kaiser parked the car in front of his apartment complex and clenched the steering wheel, twisting so hard, he practically tore the faux-leather in his grip. His brother’s truck sat in the same spot it had occupied when Kaiser had left almost three hours earlier. Though he’d known better, he’d spent the better part of the movie with his eyes closed, praying to some God he didn’t fully believe in, that Dave would be gone when he got home. No such luck. Shutting off the engine, Kaiser watched the pouring rain pelt his brother’s vehicle, pooling in the bed of the truck. It hadn’t rained this hard in longer than he could remember… coming down so fast, it was filling quicker than it could drain from the back of Dave’s pick-up. Stepping out of his sanctuary, Kaiser crossed the lot towards his brother’s truck. Making his way around to the rear of the vehicle, he flipped the latch, and opened up the floodgates. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ NOVEMBER, 2008 “C’mon, Doc. You spent two years there.” “Two and half. Eight years total.” “So then you’ve gotta have the answers. Give it to me straight. Is it unhealthy to crave that success?” ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ THURSDAY, DECEMBER 3rd, 2008 “Still here, huh?” Kaiser closed the door behind him, scuffing the souls of his shoes on the entry mat, trying to spare his living room carpet. Sitting on the sofa, Dave Vashaun clicked off the television and tossed the remote onto the coffee table. It clacked as it rolled across the wood surface, falling to the floor. “Easy on my table, dude.” “You bought it at a garage sale.” “Fair enough.” The younger Vashaun brother pulled his hoodie over his head, tossing it in the corner of the room. “I’m guessing you want to talk about how my therapy is going.” “How’d you know?” “Wasn’t hard to figure out, Dave. You’ve been askin’ about it a lot lately. And tonight, at the movies, Ellie said that…” “Elle.” “Ellie… said that you had been talking with a couple co-workers the other day about a ‘problem’ with my psychologist.” “She told you that?” David pushed himself out from the grasp of the leather couch, and folded his arms across his chest, staring his brother down. “Gimme a break, Dave. You make it sound like you’ve been betrayed. She had no idea what was going on… just heard you mention a problem with my therapy and asked me out of concern. So don’t act like some poor, scorned…” “I’m not. I was just surprised.” “Right.” “So, she knows?” “She knows.” “And? Is she ok with it?” “Why wouldn’t she be?” Kaiser trudged towards the kitchen, then stopped short, turning to face his brother. “C’mon, Dave, just go ahead and say whatever it is you want to say?” “What are you doin’, Kaiser?” Dave unfolded his arms, shoving his hands in his pockets. “Why lie about a Dr. Trowbridge? Why aren’t you taking this seriously?” “I’m taking this seriously. Just because you…” “He’s a fucking wrestler, Kaiser! Are you kidding me?! I wanted you to get help. Dad wanted… begged and pleaded with you to get help. And your idea was to go see some Neanderthal-turned-shrink?” “I sought out someone I felt could help me personally and professionally. And I think it’s pretty god damned pretentious of you to think that he’s not a ‘real’ psychologist just because he spent some time as a professional wrestler.” “Pretentious? God forbid I be pissed that my brother has spent the last nine months making up some bullshit lie about the doctor he was supposedly b…” “If I’d be up-front with you, Dave… you woulda done the same thing then that you are now. You woulda flipped your lid and told me how much of a joke the whole thing was.” “It is a fucking joke!” Dave shoved his leg forward, driving his foot into the edge of the coffee table, knocking it over. A half dozen magazines spilled to the floor, sprawling across the carpet, wide open. “You need help, Kaiser. Not advice on headlocks and the rigors of the road!” “I’m getting help.” “Real, legit help. Not this! Not whatever you’re getting. I know…” “Dave… you’ve never once walked into a burning building. You’ve never once led your sister into harm’s way. You’ve never stood trial for a thing in your life. All you know… is that I need some help getting back to my feet. You don’t have a clue beyond that.” “This wasn’t what I had in mind.” “Well, I dunno what to say, Dave. You were right. Dad was right. I needed help. And I’m getting it. But I don’t owe it to you or anyone, to get approval on what doctor I see. Or whether I go to therapy once a week, or once a month. I’m getting back on my fight, but I’m doing my way. I’m moving along at my pace, and I don’t see what’s wrong with that.” “I don’t get it, Kaiser.” Dave grabbed his jacket from the arm of the couch and marched towards the front door. “Dad passed away. Mom won’t talk to you. You won’t talk to Michelle. I was it… the last bit of family you still had, and you go and pull this shit… lie to me for the past nine months… and now you’ve got nothin’. Why? For What?” “Haven’t got that far yet.” Dave didn’t respond, slamming the front door behind him. Kaiser waited for the rattling to stop, let the ringing in his ears clear, then headed over to his coffee table and took a minute to survey the mess. ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ ~ NOVEMBER, 2008 “If my time in PRIME taught me one thing… it was that it’s a very thin line we walk. And a very different world on either side.” “How different?” Kaiser leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees and clasping his hands. "Every man wants to wear the crown. Your desire to do so is no different. It isn’t special. It isn’t greedy. It isn’t egotistical or absurd. To want the pinnacle of success is human nature. To need that success is something else entirely." “Something like inhuman?”
