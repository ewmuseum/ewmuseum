Judgment

14 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/14/07 I looked across at him and he at me. Doctor DiPendo’s glasses dipped low, threatening to hurl themselves off the bridge of his nose. His pad sat on his knee, as he looked down through thoroughly washed lenses at me. “Chandler, why would you say you’re here?” I bit my lip trying to come up with an aloof response. It’s hard to talk to shrinks. No matter how many times you hear about the tree of trust or the open line of communication, you will never get beyond the inane judgment of others. To judge is human; maybe psychiatrists get paid because they can suppress the instinct. I doubt it. “Dunno, really. Time for the yearly mental checkup, I guess. Need to make sure I’m still dangling by that last strand of sanity.” “Do you think,” he starts, folding his hands on his lap, “that you’re a fairly stable person? I mean, how does Chandler Tsonda see himself?” Every movement of his hands was cerebral and efficient. None of the regular flourish; all of his meaning swelled in his face, a proud and certain thing, at that. “Are you asking me if I think I’m a crazy guy?’ It wasn’t supposed to be a dick question. I was used to being the one talking circles around someone else. Sadly, I was up against a consummate professional bullshitter. He smiled, as certain of my questions as he was his own answers. “Most of the time, no.” He waited, smile still slipping at the edges of his mouth. “I mean, I don’t shrink heads or kill household pets. Is that what you want to hear?” I didn’t know if I was going to last five minutes in this guy’s office, why would I start spilling the good stuff? “That’s a pretty good start. I get the feeling that you’re here because you need someone to listen as much as you need whatever answers or results or whatever you call what I do.” His body language was peculiar…it was at the same time reflective and absorbent. He was taking in every modicum I was offering up, while giving away nothing himself. His poker face could wipe Daniel Negreanu’s snotty little Canadian mug off the map. But his words about needing someone to listen made me think of the A-List, once again a professional quartet. Once again four singular personalities. The target this time was different though…we were all well known enough, able to make it by without the shared name recognition. Dare I say the A-List was looking for something more metaphysical? Certainly, gold was all well and good, but life was more complex now than before. Danny’s struggling other professional life had him in spiritual knots. The Face had convinced people, albeit for only weeks, that he was a salvageable soul. Mike Wade was yet an A-List padawan. They were people, not characters. We’d given up the soulless anti-establishment institution; it hadn’t worked for Che Guevara, no matter what those trendy t-shirts tell you, so why would it for us? In this struggle for the new group identity, we’d fallen back on the old ways. Turn on everyone in sight. Suspect everyone. We all knew that the next step could very well be repeated implosion. Hell, you need only turn back your TiVo to ReV 107. And as much fun as we’d had at Colossus swerving Jack Murphy to another disappointing loss, it only continued to prove the inescapable point: history was repeating itself. “I mean, I wouldn’t say I’ve got a consistent ear twenty-four hours a day. But who does? My people are always around when I need them and I bounce all sorts of stuff off of the reliable ones. I mean, Tammie had some great ideas about a recent business proposal on the extension of major brands into Southeast Asia.” “Just because they’re not talking,” he laughed softly, “doesn’t mean they’re listening. It’s not healthy to only get professional advice on matters of the mind and heart. As much as you may enjoy these people, my job tells me that almost everyone is out for number one when it comes to a job.” His laugh flowed out and into his cadence like tributaries to a river. “Well, I’m not really as hands-on with those business interests anymore. I took some time off from…my other occupation to get them in order and now I act as overseer for a lot of different things.” “Chandler, I’m aware that you are a professional wrestler. I think you think I have some issue with this. I don’t.” He managed to sound scolding and apologetic at the same time, heaping on the pig pile of duality in this encounter. “I don’t really think of it as a job.” More true than it had once been. Wrestling no longer paid the bills. Like Danny, I was master of my own domain and thusly, no longer slave of whoever the hell owned PRIME now. But it wasn’t just that… “I mean, I work hard at it. It takes up a lot of time. I take it seriously. But it’s not a job.” “What is it?” For the first time, I heard uncertainty mixed in with his warm, bass voice. How did I plan to explain the unhealthy connection to the squared circle? “It’s a release. It’s the same reason that high school jocks join bowling teams. I need the competition, the black-and-whiteness of it. You can say one way or the other whether you’ve succeeded. I mean, business is so convoluted; making money can’t be the only measure to success–it’s too inclusive, too close to the “everybody wins” preschool mentality–but you also can’t say that only Bill Gates and Warren Buffett are ultimately the only winners. I know none of that makes sense, but it’s what it is…” He held off talking for a second, evidently to make me feel even more stupid about my answer. “Chandler, that makes total sense.” It was unnerving, the utter faith in me that I saw, growing in his features. Wasn’t his job to counteract these primal and adolescent fantasies of mine? You don’t coddle your kids by letting them win, you swat their shit like Mutombo and bully them all the way to the basket, teaching them how to lose by putting them on their ass. Mom was too concerned with my assimilation to teach me about trivial things like wins and losses. Who could blame her? We’d left a warzone for a better life in America. It was impossible to imagine any meaningful loss given the circumstances. “Dude, it just doesn’t. It’s fucking perverse.” “Don’t you feel good when you know you’ve won something? “Obviously.” “Didn’t it feel good to finally show Johnny Noble that you were better?” The surprises kept coming. Sure, he could have done a minute of internet searching to seem more credible, but something about him knew more than just the facts of the case. Sure, I could remember the incredible warmth, the glow, even as the boos rained down. I could barely stand above Johnny Noble at Colossus III, but I could hold my head high knowing that I had bested him. It was a feeling I had not felt anywhere else in life. I still didn’t peg him for a wrestling fan, but in a way, the special attention was what convinced me I was just another patient. Other people probably filled out questionnaires about their past; my responses just happened to be available in video form. “It was awesome. Best thing I’ve felt outside of really good sex.” “What’s wrong with going after something you enjoy?” “It’s infantile. Realistically, no one can even aspire to win all the matches in a three-month period, no less in a career. And it’s such a swing from win to loss with me that I’m a bona fide danger to those around me.” The last part was a lie. I didn’t care about what my post-loss explosions did to friends or enemies for the moment. I wanted DiPendo to justify why I should permit them to exist in the first place. Why I should let myself continue down the self-destructive road of being a wrestler. Why it didn’t make sense to ride off before the sunset and save myself a lot of grief. “Chandler, I don’t want you to refute this or even respond right now. I think you are a good person.” In mid-sip, I nearly deposited water all over the fancy Arabian rug. Snorting, I looked him dead in the eye and disobeyed the most recent instruction. “Doctor, you’re not going to-” “Quiet!” I was shocked into silence at how gruff his tone got. I was going to sit through a sincere evaluation of self, regardless of my own objections. Maybe that’s what you pay a hundred bucks an hour for. “You are a good person. This is not the sword on which you eventually fall, unless you walk knowingly down that road. I’ll have to ask you for some time every couple weeks so we can figure out what it all means. But it’s not inherently evil. It’s…workable.” I wasn’t surprised, but I was disappointed in hearing that my issues would be time-consuming. DiPendo could not immediately find the panacea; he didn’t know what ailed me. The man, in fairness, was doing the equivalent of hearing one of ten symptoms and grasping at a diagnosis. “Someday,” he continued in his own little vacuum, “you will figure out what type of personal salvation you’re looking for. I can tell you, though, that it’s somewhere inside that wrestling ring.” The foundation of my life swayed from side to side. His analysis pegged me as needing PRIME as much as I had thought I did. My fears and hopes, bundled in one. Damnation and salvation in a footrace to my…mind? Soul? Body? It was like watching yourself at the stake, the flames frolicking underfoot, while a tantalizingly slow rain started. Torment in uncertainty. “I figured there were no easy answers.” “Your questions were hard to ask. They should be as difficult for me to answer, or I wouldn’t be much good at this.” Silence for a long time. The man still hadn’t touched the pad that slid on his slacks. “You think that there’s a light at the end of the tunnel, though? I mean, this isn’t one of those ‘the journey is the ultimate goal’ cop-outs, right?” The doc grinned widely at me. “I think there is something, yes. Something good, waiting for you.” My head had been lowered this whole time, plumbing the depths of thought and tasting the shallow waters of shame for the first in a long time. I looked up, expecting to see a glowing halo around DiPendo’s head. “Isn’t that what you want, Chandler? Don’t you want to find, to earn what you’re looking for?” “I want it, but I’m not sure I deserve it.” The leopard couldn't change his spots because he couldn’t understand why he had them.
