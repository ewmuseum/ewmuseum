Furnace

15 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15th April “One sole desire, one passion now remains/To keep life’s fever still within his veins/Vengeance! Dire vengeance, on the wretch who cast o’er him and all he loved that ruinous blast.” – Thomas Moore. NOW. The last of the gasoline dribbled from the nozzle, splattering against the coffee table’s mahogany like a Jackson Pollock. The harsh funk of petroleum hung thick in the air, stinging the insides of his nostrils and watering his eyes. He swayed briefly; dropping the can do the floor and spreading his hand against the wall to keep from falling. The fumes were becoming unbearable; his brain feeling so inflated that he felt his skull might pop at any second; such was the intensity of the headache. He was dizzy and sweating behind his ski mask, yet still he stood in the doused apartment that would become a desert of charcoal and ash with one incendiary burst. Fire, the most destructive of elements. Something that man had learned to create aeons ago, and yet something that no man would soon tame. It was nature’s great destroyer; an unrivalled force of absolute obliteration. The perfect tool for the task at hand. Dressed in black and clad in shadows, he steadied himself and gazed through bleary eyes at the doomed home of his enemy. Swallowing back the lump of hesitation that had grown in his throat, he reached into his pocket and pulled from it a pack of matches. Gently thumbing the box open, he looked across a modest abode full of the ill-gotten gains of a stolen career. The place really wasn't all that extravagant; save for the ridiculous sound system and the obligatory LCD television it was just your typical New York studio. The walls were white and the floors were laminate. The furniture that probably looked good in the early '90's was now shoddy and dated and he thought that he might be doing the apartment's wretched occupant a favour by burning it. With glorious retribution hogging his inner cortex he stepped forward. A cracked, worn-out leather jacket lay on the couch, it's gasoline-splattered exterior twinkling in the dim moonlight. It was familiar to him. The same one he'd seen his enemy wear on television the previous week. The same one he was wearing on the night he snatched his career away. It was a fitting place to start. Smirking behind his woollen mask he struck the match. Let it dangle from his fingertips for a few seconds until the flames licked up his thumbnail, warming the skin beneath. He dropped the match and turned to run. ---- THEN. It was a downpour, yes. Mother Nature had opened the heavens with the rumble of thunder and the crack of lightning. Large droplets of rain tumbled Earthwards with great velocity, immediately creating treacherous puddles on the concrete surface. It was so swift, so sudden; blotting out the sunlight and materialising in a matter of seconds. The kind of storm usually reserved for the forests of the southern hemisphere. Aidan Chase pulled the back collar of his Wal-Mart employee’s fleece over his head and rued his luck as he ploughed a convoy of steel shopping carts through the parking lot. He’d only been on his shift for thirty minutes and already he was practically soaked to his skin. He mumbled something to the tune of “I don’t get paid enough for this shit” as he pushed the trolleys towards the big grey building across the car park. A trolley boy can never say that he has the most exciting job in the world at the best of times, but with his clothes completely sodden and his walkman bettery dead he was having a truly rotten time of it. Aidan pulled back his heavy, soaked sleeve and glanced at his watch. 11:32am. Another seven and a half hours of graft before he went back to his lonely apartment to use electricity he could barely afford in a vein attempt to keep himself occupied until the sandman came and the cycle repeated itself. Still he struggled on; clinging tightly to the fragile hope that the NY indy scene would pick up and he’d soon be swamped by a dearth of bookings. It was then that Aidan Chase realised he’d reached a new low. The straits weren’t always so dire. Little more than a month ago he was happy. Dating the most beautiful girl in the city and with a fresh contract for the country’s greatest professional wrestling organisation, Aidan Chase had every right to be living on Easy Street for years forthcoming. He’d debut in the Dual Halo and make a splash before working his way up to the top and becoming one of PRIME’s all-time greats. That was the plan. He’d be unstoppable; a man at ease with himself being paid to do the only thing he’d ever wanted from life. That dream died the day he met Jason Natas. Immediately Aidan tried to banish thoughts of PRIME’s rejuvenated Anti-Superstar. The way he’d been handled by the uncouth ruffian made him want to curl up under a rock never to emerge. He’d not been the same since Natas had twisted his arm and forced the young grappler to concede a contract that he believed to be rightfully his. Aidan couldn’t go on like this, no; the knowledge that his surrender to Natas had effectively cost him all that was dear to him was something he could cope with no longer. Jason Natas had sunk his claws in, whether the brazen Anti-Superstar knew it or not, and Aidan knew the only way to release the hex on his psyche was to strike back at the man who’d cost him a legacy. Thud. He wasn’t looking where he was going. The impact of the trolley line crashing into a lamppost sent stiff vibrations shivering through the carts and pierced the air with a metallic clang. Cursing himself, Aidan pulled the carts away when a familiar polyphonic melody began to chime in his pocket. Sighing, Aidan pulled the dated cell from his pouch and read the text message. A sudden ray of light ignited his troubled psyche. He didn’t recognise the number but the message, instructing him to be at Sal’s Diner in forty-five minutes, certainly was. That was their place. Aidan and Amanda’s. It was where they’d first met and where they’d continued to congregate throughout the years. The number was unfamiliar but it had to be her. Who else could it be? Without a care for his collection of trolleys he abandoned the convoy in the middle of the parking lot. Turning on his heel, walking briskly towards the bus stop, thinking that he would trade anything for just a moment of her time. * * * * * He risen from his slumber later than he'd intended, Jason; strolling up to the curtains all sleepy-eyed and groggy. Pulling the drapes apart and glancing out across a grey, dreary, cityscape, Jason let out a deep yawn and pushed his arms skyward, stretching just about every muscle above his waist. Soon he pulled a pair of jeans and a black tee from the wardrobe and pulled them over his rough, heavily-tattooed body. Aside from the obvious tiredness, Jason felt good. His body was full of none of the aches and pains that many of his peers moaned and groaned about constantly. But then again, Jason Natas was built from sterner stuff than the average professional wrestler. Actors masquerading as fighters, most of them. They get a little bruise on their ribs or a cut under their eye and they think they're hurting. Jason often thought it was a shame that more of them hadn't gone through the ranks as a warrior of the streets like he had. It might have done the moaning bastards some good. Moving through to the kitchen, he felt as though life was getting sweeter by the day. A fortnight prior he'd scored a massive psychological victory by pinning Bryan Dawkins, and while the previous week's main event ended with a loss at the hands of a hell-bent Tyler Rayne, but that was okay. For the first time in a short, troubled career he felt at ease with the wrestling world in general, and he was looking forward to his forthcoming clash with some big rookie he couldn't remember the name of. Especially if weapons were involved. Fixing himself a bowl of cereal and plopping himself down in front of the television, it wasn't until Jason caught glimpse of the clock on the news channel's ticker that he realised his flight to Kansas was due to take-off in an hour and a half. “Fuck.” * * * * * The burst of muggy, warm air that greeted him upon arrival at Sal's Diner was sweeter than a glass of fresh water after a week in the desert. Wiping away some of the accumulated rainwater from his brow, Aidan began scanning his eyes around the busy eatery for any sign of the woman he'd once called his life's partner. Sal's was shaped like the letter L with the sitting area curved around the long, straight bar. It was decorated in simple red and whites. Tiled floor. Cheap plastic tables. Uncomfortable chairs. Really, the only thing that endeared the place to Aidan was its sentimentality. It was buzzing, no doubt. Elderly couples enjoying a cheap lunch, hungry long distance travellers fuelling their empty stomachs, manual labourers stopping to pick up the yard's afternoon feed. It seemed that every walk of life was well-represented in the restaurant save for the one that actually mattered to Aidan. He took a second look around the place just in case he'd missed her, but still there was no sign. Then he caught glimpse of the clock on the wall and realised that he'd arrived five minutes early. Phew. He breathed a sigh of relief and spotted a spare table across the room before making his way over there. Sandwiched between a group of moaning old people and two out-of-place suit-clad office workers, Aidan dumped his soggy form on the chair and began to wait it out. Five minutes became ten. Ten became fifteen. Fifteen became twenty and he was getting tetchy. With his drenched clothes quickly drying out inside the diner's warmth, Aidan had never felt so alone. He'd been speculating Amanda's whereabouts for much longer than was healthy and he was beginning to sweat. Thinking that maybe someone had played a vindictive trick on him, he was just about to up and leave when he found a cup of white coffee slapped down on the table in front of him. He looked up to the sight of blonde-haired Superagent™ Nathaniel Loftman removing his blazer and hanging it neatly over the back of the free chair. “Sorry to keep you waiting. Traffic's like a fuckin' snail going through sludge today,” Loftman chirped, sitting down in the chair opposite Aidan Chase and swallowing a mouthful of lukewarm coffee. The blue-eyed Potemkin executive smiled as he and Chase met eyes. “How've you been, kid?” “You-...” Aidan began but his voice wavered. The picture was becoming clear. “What the fuck are you doing here?” “Whoa, now! What kinda way's that to greet an old friend, huh?!” was the response of the ever-cocky sports agent. Underneath the table Aidan clenched a frustrated fist. He'd been flummoxed. In sixty minutes he'd gone from a desperate no-hoper moping around in a supermarket car park to a man with a shot at redemption and then right back down again. It was a lot to take in. “You tricked me,” fumed Aidan between gritted teeth. “You sleazy motherf-” “Hey, back up a second there, Nelly.” Loftman raised an eyebrow. “I tricked you how, exactly?” Aidan parted his lips, ready to unleash a torrent of bile at the agent. He wanted to berate the man for leading him astray but soon found the text message streaming passed his mind's eye. There was no mention of her name, there were no x's or o's or any other sign of affection he might expect from Amanda. Hell, it was sent from a different freaking number... Aidan Chase felt like throwing up. The only person that had deceived him was himself. “I thought you were Amanda.” “Huh?” Loftman's face contorted with confusion as he took in another sip of coffee. “I don't have tits. Well, last time I checked, anyway.” Aidan was agitated. Moving his hands onto the table top, his chest heaved as he summoned a long, displeased sigh from the bottom of his lungs. “Well, what do you want?” he quizzed the man who'd first offered him the chance to realise his PRIME dream in the first place. “'Cause unless it's a big money contract, I'm not interested.” Loftman frowned. He thought about making an oh-so-witty “time of the month” joke but figured the kid would probably bolt. “What, can't one guy meet another for coffee without getting the Spanish Inquisition these days?” “Mean it, Loftman.” From the look on Chase's face Loftman could tell he was dead serious. He sighed, mentally changing his gameplan. “Alright, fine. I know what happened.” Finally it seemed as thought Aidan's anger was subsiding. His facial muscles loosened into a strange mask of shame and confusion. “Well, uhhh,” he lowered his head, staring into the milky coffee, “maybe Amanda and I weren't just to be.” Loftman asked himself who “Amanda” was. “No, Aidan. Not that.” Swallowing, Aidan looked up. For the first time he clasped hold of the cup, holding it for comfort more than anything else. His encounter with Jason Natas was still fresh in the memory and it was as if Loftman was prying the open wound with a stick of salt. “I don't know what you're talking about,” Aidan said coyly, pulling the cup up and sipping on it's bland, unsatisfying contents. “I know you know what I mean,” Loftman jabbed, his tone stern. “That wound on your throat wasn't there the last time we met. You gonna tell me it was an accident?” Nathaniel pointed towards the cigarette burn wound that poked through Aidan's shirt collar tellingly. Now no more than a centimetre in diameter, the wound had scabbed over and was beginning to peel away. Feeling exposed, Aidan grabbed his collar tightly and pulled it over the burn. He tried to avoid making eye contact with Nathaniel. “I know he got to you, Aid.” Aidan's lip quivered and his breaths wavered. All of a sudden he was back in the alleyway. Jason Natas plunged the business end of his cancer stick into his throat and dropped him to the floor under a barrage of closed fists and hooked elbows. At the mercy of the man who'd purposefully bashed into his car, Aidan dialled the Potemkin Agency's number and kissed goodbye to an opportunity that would likely never again present itself. “Whadda you know about it?” snarled Aidan defensively, snapping out of his daze. “I know that he probably beat the unholy shit out of you, anyway. At least he must have threatened to. A guy just doesn't pull out of a contract as desierable as the one we'd put on the table for you, Aidan. Don't jerk me around.” If Aidan looked up he'd see Nathaniel Loftman staring dead-on, analysing his every move as if he were under a microscope. “I changed my mind,” Aidan mumbled almost completely incoherently. “You 'changed your mind'...” Loftman shook his head. “Come on, man. You and I both know that's a sack of horse shit.” Aidan knew; oh God, he knew. But he would not let up, no; he was far too proud for that. When Aidan Chase was in second grade he promptly strolled up to the fourth-grader who'd stolen his lunch money and kicked him in the nuts. When his alcohol-poisoned Father finally lashed out at his Mother it was Aidan who struck him in the jaw and sent him packing. Aidan Chase had never let anybody treat him like a doormat in all his time on Earth, and he wasn't about to admit his greatest shame to anybody. Another mouthful of caffeine and another change of strategy from Loftman. “Look, I know how you're feeling. If I'd smacked around by some knuckle-dragging Neanderthal in a back alley I'd be feeling pretty shitty about it myself, but this is Jason Natas we're talking about, not Al Capone. You can talk to me, kid. He ain't lurking around the corner ready to jump out.” The agent paused but the interjection from Chase never came. He continued. “I can tell from just sitting here that you're pissed. The fact that you're wearing a Wal-Mart uniform speaks volumes, kid.” Aidan cocked his defeated head to the side, looking very much deflated as he slumped back in the chair. “Not much you and I can do, is there?” “That's where you're wrong.” Loftman leaned across the table, clasping his hands together and lowering his voice. “Aidan, you are not the only person in the world who wants to be rid of Jason Natas.” Aidan felt his insides swell as Nathaniel Loftman dangled the carrot in front of his nose. He inhaled heavily, knowing that he would have no solace until he had reaped sweet revenge upon Jason Natas, and there it was for the taking. He knew nought of Nathaniel Loftman's plans or the “other people” who wanted to see the PRIME grappler take a fall, but it was an attractive proposition. Loftman had worked with the man for over a year, through thick and thin. With his resources and understanding of The Anti-Superstar there was surely nobody better to aid him. He sat upright, opened his mouth to speak but he was caught in two minds. “I can help you, Aidan.” Again Aidan sighed. “Thanks...” he began, “... but no thanks.” “What?!” Loftman blurted uncontrollable. “Have you lost your marbles?” Chase had no time to waste listening to Nathaniel Loftman any longer. He rose to his feet and pulled his soggy fleece over his shoulders. Vengeance was a must, yes; but it would be done by his own hand. It was his and his alone to reap, and he'd only feel like even more of a wet blanket by roping another in to do his dirty work. “If you try doing this on your own he's only gonna hurt you again. Worse, this time.” Loftman stood up, watching Aidan storm towards the door. “You can't do this on your own!” Watch me, Aidan thought as he disappeared into the storm. * * * * * His preparation was hasty. With little time to lose Jason went into his bedroom and stuffed a couple of night's worth of clothes into a holdall hastily before jamming his wallet and his phone inside his pockets. Twelve months ago this wouldn't have happened. Jason would sooner have no-showed than gather together his belongings at such a rate, but he wasn't interested in throwing a career he'd worked hard to win back any time soon. For as long as there were posers to be bruised there'd be an Anti-Superstar in PRIME, and he'd make it to LaGuardia if it was the last thing he ever did. Slamming the door behind him with more force than intended and locking it tightly, Jason didn't realise that he'd left his leather jacket until his taxi was halfway there. * * * * * Nathaniel Loftman was right. Aidan Chase knew it. Jason Natas was a beast of a man; a man who knew nothing of morality and who's idea of diplomacy revolved around punches to the face and kicks to the gut. And he was tough, Jason; unlike many of his peers he knew how to fight. Years of slumming it in The Bronx had left him with a knockout right hook and a mean streak as wide as the Grand Canyon. If he took Jason on head-to-head Aidan had no chance. The Anti-Superstar would hurt him, cripple him, even. Maybe worse. There was no sense in rushing into a head-on collision on his own, but he would sooner accept his own demise than embrace the help of the snake Nathaniel Loftman. Physical violence was no longer an option; Aidan realised almost as soon as he'd left the diner. Something a little less direct was in order. The idea came to him instantly. Smiling as he made his way back to his empty apartment, Aidan wondered if he'd kept hold of Jason Natas's insurance details. ---- NOW. Like an antelope on the Serengeti, he ran from the blaze. The ignition was instantaneous and the apartment was awash with orange flames of devastation before he knew it. He darted from the apartment and through to the stairwell with such haste that he narrowly avoided tumbling down the stairs. Instead he clung tightly to the cold steel banister. Shaking the remnants of his petroleum haze from his head he charged down the stairs, not wanting to risk the elevator. The only things travelling faster than his body were the thoughts racing through his mind. Would anyone see him on his escape? Where would he ditch the sweaty, black threads clinging tightly to his body? Where would he go to clear his head after this glorious act of retribution? Thoughts all of his own concern and none for the plight of his adversary. He made it down through floors thirteen, twelve and eleven and soon he was halfway down. His body temperature increasing with every heavy footstep, he felt forced to pull the sweat-drenched ski mask from his clammy face and stuff it into the pocket of his bomber jacket. Like a jackhammer his heart pounded. Thump. Thump. Thump. Each repetition with such force that the next might burst through his ribcage. But still he ran. Third floor. Second. First. He made it to the ground floor eventually and ran outside into the pouring rain. The cool night air sent a chill up his spine as he bolted across the road and disappeared into a nearby alley. There, leaning forward against a wall, he caught a breather. Panting like a dog, he looked over his shoulder for the first time. On the fifteenth floor flames roared from the broken windows, crawling up the red bricks, leaving a blackened char in their wake. It brought a smile to his lips as he reached into his pocket and punched three digits into his cell phone. “Fire service, please,” Aidan breathed. He wanted to set a message to one occupant of the building, not the whole block.
