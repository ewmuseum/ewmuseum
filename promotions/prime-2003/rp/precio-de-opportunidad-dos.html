Precio De Opportunidad - Dos

Date: Continuous Precio de Oportunidad - Dos PRECIO DE OPPORTUNIDAD: DOS By Danny Ferguson Last time in the series... I/E. SHOPKEEPER I've never seen that mask before... CUT. CROWD Spif-fy! Spif-fy! Spif-fy! CUT. REGGIE DELRAY We're being followed... CUT. DRIVER: Where are our boys? PASSENGER: Quattro just stepped into the office building. Cinco is mulling about outside, trying to blend in. CUT. MAN That mask is your ticket, if you want it. All you do is put it on, and it will do the rest. CUT. 76933: Where's the kid? 52112: He knows how to pick his spots, apparently. The shot changes to just behind the two men, showing an empty sidewalk in front of them. FADE OUT. FADE IN. Reggie Delray and the bodyguards sit in the lobby of an office. Reggie fidgets nervously in his seat, as this is one of the 163 hours of the week that he's out of place. Jim Pibb reads an NFL preview magazine, one of about thirty on the coffee table. This one picks Buffalo and Arizona for the Super Bowl. Dametreyus is reading a new copy of the Martha Stewart Living magazine, connecting to the woman with whom he shares both a love of embroidery and a criminal record with at least one felony. Reggie looks at the clock, then at his watch. Both tell the same time, surprisingly enough. He grunts and pushes to his feet, shuffling for the door as his weak bladder signals the call of nature. REGGIE: I'll be right back. Neither guard looks up from their magazine as he walks out. He's not the one paying them, after all. CUT TO: INT. ALLAN'S OFFICE - DAY Danny Ferguson is sprawled out on a couch as Allan stands behind his desk. ALLAN: I assume you finished reading the 'Double Down' script. DANNY: Yeah. What a piece of garbage. ALLAN: It's going to need a rewrite or two. DANNY: Why can't we get the guy who redid the first one? ALLAN: He's asking too much. Above market value. Danny shakes his head in disgust. DANNY: Honestly, some of these people can't even spell their fucking names, and they think they should get paid more than me. ALLAN: I know, it's a travesty. (changing the subject) Anyway, I think this is a good development, because it lets us focus on other ventures. DANNY: Such as? ALLAN: Well, it seems you're starting to pick up steam with this wrestling idea, so we can stick with that, but it does give you six days out of the week off. DANNY: Hmm...you're right, that's up a bit from my usual five. ALLAN: Exactly. So I think we can occupy that abundance of free time with some short projects. DANNY: What, like commercials? ALLAN: You've got it. We have a couple people who are starting to ask about using someone like you to sell their products. Allan sits down at his desk and shuffles through some papers. Intrigued, Danny sits up and watches him. DANNY: So who is it? Some rinky-dink online poker site? A car company? ALLAN: Actually, there's one that fits in pretty well with this PRIME gig. DANNY: Oh yeah? Tapping a ring on the desk as he moves one piece of paper to the top of his pile, Allan looks up with a smile that looks more baiting than encouraging. ALLAN: How familiar are you with MetaTrim? CUT TO: INT. BATHROOM - DAY Reggie pushes into the restroom after getting lost for several minutes. Apparently there was only one restroom on the floor, and it was all the way on the other side of the building. Cursing under his breath, he pushes into a stall and shuts the door. He was never comfortable in urinals. Always thought somebody could sneak up on him. He closes the door and locks it, then proceeds to let loose for a couple of minutes, as we see nothing but the door of the stall and the stall next to it. Reggie lets loose a satisfied sigh as he finishes up. He zips up his pants and we hear him fiddling with his belt. Suddenly, a pair of hands appears on the partition between stalls. In a quick motion, the boy in the hooded sweatshirt vaults the wall into Reggie's stall. A sudden grunting yelp is heard upon contact, and then silence. CUT TO: EXT. MEXICO CITY - DAY It should be clear very early that this is not the same city we saw last time. The old arena is replaced by a more modern sportatorium. The fan base has changed, going from less families to more young males. The city has aged greatly in a few years. As a matter of fact, only one thing has remained constant in the time we've been away, and that's the name on the marquee. ¡EL SPIFFY! But now, speaking and thinking with an experience that didn't have before our last visit, we know that even the constants are mutable. VOICE The question is almost always a how. How can a man fall victim to the Evil of the mask? How is he unable to resist? How does it find a host so in tune with its own perverted intentions? We enter the new arena in a single flowing shot, swooping through the crowd to a high point, where a few young boys cling to the rafters of the upper deck, trying to get a good view. Obviously, it's perfect. In the ring, an unidentifiable wrestler is sprawled on the canvas, barely moving. The crowd is rocking, and the reason why is perched atop a nearby turnbuckle. After a quick signal to his fans, El Spiffy flies off with a twisting elbow drop, and quickly covers for a three count. The building shakes. VOICE The answer is simple, but not always easy to understand. The mask doesn't find Evil men. It makes them. El Spiffy climbs into the crowd after being handed an elaborate championship belt. The fans swarm him, all wanting to touch their hero. VOICE It finds the weak, those with an emptiness of character and spirit. With the promise of immediate success, it takes them in. These weak men are often too quick to accept the trade. Spiffy disappears under the swarm of hands and bodies as the scene fades. CUT TO: INT. LOCKER ROOM - LATER After the buzz of the night has died off, El Spiffy stands alone in the locker room. On first glance, we can see that it's the ring boy from years before; he has the same partially-obscured facial features and the kind of meek body structure that's not normally seen in a 'superstar.' One thing is quite different, though, and that's his size. Muscles, of the unnatural size and shape, have found their way onto his quiet frame. It has not been long since we last saw the boy, and you could take a fairly easy guess as to where they came from. But just so we don't leave anything to the imagination, the scene gives it all away. With one leg propped up on a chair, he slowly presses a syringe into his buttock, exposed slightly with the tights tugged down. Through the mask, we see his face twisted in a grimace while the off-white substance in the needle is squeezed out. VOICE Their second shared trait, emblematic of the personal weakness, is a preference - even a reliance - on finding a short cut, an easy route to the promised success. Spiffy quickly packs up the needle and returns it to a bag. He trades it for an unidentifiable bottle and shakes some pills into a cupped hand. Sitting down in the chair, he swallows them dry and grimaces again. Off camera, a door opens, and Spiffy tries his best to hide the nerves. MAN (O.S.) I don't know how you do it, man. EL SPIFFY What's that? The same nondescript luchador from before walks into the scene, sporting street clothes instead of his tights. He does keep the mask on, however. He smiles. MAN How you can go out there and throw yourself around like that. Get the crowd cheering because you walk the tightrope every week. El Spiffy looks at him for a second, then smiles back. He picks up his bag and zips it up, trying to give the impression that he's about to leave. EL SPIFFY The Spiffy is immortal, friend. I taunt death like it's a little brother. Even if it grows bigger and more powerful than I am, I know that it can't get the better of me. The other wrestler gives him a slight chuckle. Seeing Spiffy preparing for an exit, he turns for the door. MAN Just be careful, sir. Sometimes it's not the little brother's strength, but the big brother's weakness. El Spiffy pauses, then returns the chuckle in a subtly patronizing manner. The man doesn't notice, and after some incomprehensible pleasantries, he exits. Alone again, Spiffy stands, and immediately freezes up from pain. He stays stoic for a moment, trying to will it away with clenched fists. He turns to pick up his bag and leaves through the back of the end zone, disguising the physical agony with each step. CUT TO: INT. OFFICE BUILDING - DAY 52112 and 76933, the mysterious agents, are standing in the lobby. 52112 is calmly trying to explain his situation to the receptionist. RECEPTIONIST: I'm sorry, sir, I'm sure your friend is a tremendous actor, but Mr. North is only seeing his clients today. 52112: That's the thing, he's a prospective client. He's looking to get into some new work. RECEPTIONIST: Well, I'm sorry, but he doesn't seem to have you on the list. 52112 pauses, then leans in closer to the secretary. 52112: We were sent by Allan's brother. RECEPTIONIST: (quickly) Peter? She blushes as she realized that she had just divulged the identity of Allan's pornstar brother without any hesitation. 52112 nods affirmatively. 76933 gives her a quick wink and that Johnny-Carson-esque hip swing, and she looks away, blushing even further. RECEPTIONIST: Please just go in. 52112: Thank you ma'am. I trust you, you won't be disappointed next time you spend that night at home. She shoos him away and the two men make for the elevator before she regrets the decision and calls for security. CUT TO: INT. ELEVATOR - DAY The two men step onto the elevator with another man in a suit, who shies to the other side, away from the strangers. Ignoring his presence, they carry on conversation as usual. 76933: I don't understand why we didn't take him earlier. 52112: Violates our protocol. Right now, it's an orange alert, to use Homeland Security terms. 76933: And that would be... 52112: Be aware, but not active. It's passive contact at the moment. We're supposed to be Watchers. We only step in when it ceases to be a passive connection between the two marks. 76933: So as long as the kid's just following, we can't touch him? 52112: Bingo. But once they open a line of communication, then everything is in danger, and we become involved. Across the elevator, the other man is growing more and more concerned with the conversation. He nervously punches a button and steps out onto the next floor. 76933: What are the odds he's already there? 52112: He's a smart kid. 76933: Meaning? 52112: We need priority one tactics the second this elevator opens. 76933 breathes in deeply, then exhales as the elevator dings and slides open. CUT TO: EXT. ARENA - DAY A massive line stretches around the same sportatorium-style arena that we saw before. Fans stand with pens and photos clenched in their arms, waiting to meet the man who excites them every time he steps into the ring. El Spiffy, along with several other weekend stars, stands at the entrance to the arena, taking pictures and giving autographs to individual members of the throng. A jovial mood is in the air today, just hours from one of the biggest shows this arena has seen. The autograph session is supplemented with a huge banner advertising the show. Although people have come to see a number of the performers, it's fairly clear who the majority want to see. VOICE The shortcuts, in whatever form they come, are a product of greed, a desire to expedite the success that the mask guarantees. It could be said that the shortcoming is not in wanting more, but in a lack of satisfaction for what they already have. While Spiffy chats with the fans, giving the same sort of fake laugh we heard earlier. The shot pans away from the commotion, to a group of men holding court over the event. One is quite clearly the promoter, evidenced by his untucked polo shirt and hanging credential. Others around him are the support staff. As we approach, he's talking to a young American with a shaved head. His t-shirt and cargo pants combination stands out from the rest of the men, as do the biceps bulging underneath. PROMOTER (smiling) I don't know, Matt. Do you really want to be part of this? The pomp and circumstance? The smoke and the mirrors? The American, Matt, keeps his eyes on the crowd, but tries hard to return the smile. MATT I will do what it takes to get ahead, Jose. Every ex-athlete in America with $2000 wants to be a wrestler. I can't get my break there. I need to be a gringo, or a gaijin, or whatever else it will take to get my foot in the door. The promoter smiles and pats him on the shoulder. Behind them, a man in a charcoal suit approaches, but neither notices. JOSE Well I wish you luck, son. I hope we can give you that kind of opportunity tonight. MAN IN SUIT (from behind) Excuse me? Both men turn, but the suited visitor keeps his focus on Jose, the promoter. As we see him up close, we recognize the face. It's the same man who once removed the El Spiffy mask. It's the original El Spiffy. But of course, no one else knows that. He looks quite a bit better than the broken man who was last seen lying on a locker room bench, bloodied and worthless. His hair is slicked and he sports a neatly trimmed goatee. He smiles awkwardly at Jose. JOSE Can I help you? MAN IN SUIT You certainly can, but I don't think you're going to be happy about it. Jose gives a look to Matt, then looks over the suited man's shoulder towards his staff. Without returning eye contact to his visitor, he replies. JOSE Why's that? The man waits for Jose to look him in the eye, then shakes his head, remembering something. MAN IN SUIT I'm sorry, I haven't introduced myself. I'm El Spiffy's manager. He extends his hand to shake. The camera catches a fresh looking tattoo of a watch face on the inside of his wrist. Jose shakes loosely, still apprehensive. JOSE I didn't know Spiff had a manager. MAN IN SUIT He does. JOSE Since when? MAN IN SUIT Since I just told you. An uncomfortable silence fills the space between them as the suited man stares a hole through the promoter. To the side, Matt, the American, remains a quiet witness. MAN IN SUIT We've recently signed an agreement with an American company. Beginning next month, he will be appearing in the United States on a regular basis. JOSE (clearly caught off guard) What? MAN IN SUIT He will still be available to you, but only on a quasi-regular basis...and for an increased price. JOSE Are you screwing with me? That's bullshit. MAN IN SUIT (sighing) I anticipated that you wouldn't take us up on that offer, and that's why I've instructed El Spiffy to deliver his farewell speech to the fans at tonight's show. Jose remains speechless. In his stunned scoffing, he turns to Matt, who doesn't offer a reaction, only staring at the 'manager.' The man continues. MAN IN SUIT I'm going to insist that you not try to speak to my client, as he's been advised not to speak to anyone. Good day. The man turns on his heel and walks away in the direction he came, showing a rather heavy limp on one side. Matt steps up next to Jose again, but keeps his eye on the charcoal suit. The camera and the promoter look back towards the public appearance, and at El Spiffy goofing around with some small children to the delight of the crowd. JOSE I do believe I've just been stabbed in the back by one of my wrestlers, Matt. Matt doesn't answer, as he's still looking back at the suited man as he disappears into the arena. Without looking back, Jose chuckles uncomfortably, trying to hide his rage. JOSE Maybe we make a new star tonight, then. Maybe we make Matt Register a star. He turns and looks over his shoulder, and Matt finally breaks his gaze and returns his attention to Jose. He smirks. MATT Maybe. CUT TO: INT. OFFICE LOBBY - DAY The door to the office shuffles open, and Reggie's usual ugly pants shuffle by as Dametreyus and Jim read their magazines. They catch the wardrobe in their peripheral vision and continue reading. Reggie shuffles across the room, quieter after his trip to the restroom. A few uncomfortable moments pass before the door to Allan's office opens, and the agent and Danny appear in the doorway. ALLAN: ...so I'll make the phone calls and set up meeting for the next week or so. DANNY: Make sure I get paid, Allan. That's all I care about. ALLAN: And that's all I care about. You getting paid, I mean. And then me getting paid. But not until you do. Allan's grumbling, quiet revisions were a result of Danny's disgusted expression. Ferguson wasn't listening, as he stares across the room. DANNY: Now Reggie, I know I don't pay much attention to you, but I'm sure as FUCK you're not a Mexican. At the four-letter word, the bodyguards turn their attention away from their charge and across the room, where Reggie sits. Except it's not Reggie. Sitting, in Reggie's ugly pants and sportcoat, is a young looking Hispanic man. He sports a shadow so heavy it's about 8:15 instead of 5 o'lock, and has a Mike-Tyson-esque tattoo covering the left side of his face. Our viewers will remember him as the same young man who was following Team Ferguson earlier in the day, sans hoodie, naturally. He doesn't answer and the bodyguards move forward. The young man pounces from his seat and reaches inside his coat, causing them to stop in their tracks. YOUNG MAN I'll give you a hint. It's not a fucking nine-iron. CUT TO: INT. ARENA - NIGHT The scene is quite different from our previous visits, as the crowd is not chanting El Spiffy's name. They're not even cheering. As the famed luchador stands in the ring, a mic in his hands, the sound that echoes from the rafters...is weeping. EL SPIFFY I am truly sorry to all of you, but I am glad I got to say goodbye. Perhaps The Spiffy can come back some day. Perhaps you will see me once again. Children and women wail. Men are quiet, not even angry at the announcement they just heard. El Spiffy, long a hero in this little arena, is leaving them behind. It was as though a family member had just announced that they were leaving. No one quite knows what to say. El Spiffy senses this morose demeanor in the air, and does the only thing he can to salvage it. EL SPIFFY But your lives without El Spiffy begin tomorrow! Tonight, tonight I will give you everything I have for one last time! A cheer rips through the place as his attempt to cheer the crowd echoes. The camera stirs through the audience, and finally comes to a stop at a tunnel on the second deck, where his manager watches with an unflinching eye. EL SPIFFY I promise each and every one of you...if tonight is the last time you watch El Spiffy cheat death, I will make it memorable. A cheer rises up again, but quickly we: CUT TO: INT. EMERGENCY ROOM - LATER A stretcher blows through the set of swinging doors that lead into the emergency area, punctuating the cut. El Spiffy, his body heaving and convulsing, lies on the bed as it's frantically pushed by a pair of RNs. Behind the bed trails a delegation from the arena, including the man in the suit, some miscellaneous crew, and two wrestlers - El Spiffy's opponent for the night, and Matt, the American. RN He's having a system failure. Extreme stress on a weakened heart. Alert an on-call of a critical cardiac arrest. A third RN disappears, running off as the bed is pushed down the hallway. It suddenly hooks into a room as the group from the arena fall back to a waiting area. There, the luchador who faced El Spiffy on this fateful night is beside himself. LUCHADOR I don't know what happened! I had him in hold, and suddenly he- MAN IN SUIT Don't worry, son. It's not your fault. The man, interrupted, stops babbling for a few seconds as this man speaks. Sensing he still has the attention, the manager elaborates. MAN IN SUIT His body was under heavy strain from...the effects of the job, so to speak. We were making the move to the United States because the American style would have been easier on him, less intense. I asked him to be careful tonight. He chose not to be. Off to the side, Matt chimes in. He doesn't challenge the man, though, and rather, he sounds like he's playing to him. MATT He was giving the crowd a good match. MAN IN SUIT (giving him a side glance) He was making sure none of the hype of his American debut died off with a subpar performance. There were officials from other US companies in that crowd. The fans, the other 95% of the audience, they mean nothing. Remarkably disinterested, all things considered, the man gives a nod to both men and turns away, off to find a place where he can safely use the cell phone he had pulled from his jacket pocket. Furrowing his brow, Matt gives the other luchador a cold pat on the back and slumps into a chair. The wrestler, not feeling any better, remains standing. FADE OUT. FADE IN. INT. HOSPITAL ROOM - NIGHT El Spiffy rests in a hospital bed, a plethora of tubes and wires surrounding him. He is unconscious. His mask remains on. RN We didn't have time to remove the mask, and had some difficulty when trying to get it off later. I understand how important it is to preserve the identity. She looks up at her company, the man in the suit. MAN IN SUIT I'm sure he will thank you for it later. The RN smiles. With little more to do, she leaves the wrestler and his manager alone in the poorly lit room. He gives her a nod and a smile as she leaves. The door shuts, and he finally moves, stepping around the bed to get closer. MAN IN SUIT We all make mistakes, don't we? Obviously, El Spiffy doesn't answer. The man plucks a pair of surgical gloves out of the box above the bed. MAN IN SUIT Unfortunately, I don't think we're always ready to accept the consequences of our mistakes. He leans down under the bed, by the machinery that flanks it. With a couple of grunts and short tugs, the machines all go black. El Spiffy's body churns and heaves as his life support is cut off, but the suited man holds him down with gloved hands. MAN IN SUIT (still speaking to Spiffy) Don't worry; The hospital won't interfere. They know better. Our friends will see to that. The body stops moving, and the suited man lets up on his grip. His hands move to behind El Spiffy's head, and he begins to untie the mask. MAN IN SUIT I don't know what all the trouble is with this mask. (chuckling) This is the second time I've been able to take it off. MATT (O.S.) But now you need someone to put it on. The man looks up, not very surprised by the intrusion. That's not to say he isn't - he just doesn't show it. Matt steps forward to the side of the bed. MATT You've just made a deal to bring El Spiffy to America, and now you have no Spiffy. I need a job in America. And I think we both know that these promoters in the States don't have a clue what El Spiffy looks like. I think you know where I'm going with this. As he speaks, the man continues removing the mask. When Matt finishes, the manager holds it out to him. He takes it without hesitation. MAN IN SUIT You may want to wait until we exit the building to put that on. Matt nods. He'd planned this all too well to let it be spoiled by a nurse's assistant. The men remain quiet, standing over the drug-riddled body of the former ring boy, still looking as young as that night in the locker room on his deathbed. The camera begins to pan up and out of the room. The star of Matt Register had not been borne that night...but the star of El Spiffy remained as bright as ever. CUT TO: INT. OFFICE LOBBY - DAY The young man who had been stalking Team Ferguson keeps the bodyguards - and everyone else - at bay, courtesy of what we presume is a gun in his coat. Or rather, Reggie's coat. After securing his position, the man gives a coy smile to our star, perhaps content that he's stealing this fourth-wall spotlight. YOUNG MAN It's good to see you again, Danny. You're a hard man to follow. DANNY: Do I know you? Danny furrows his brow, trying to place the young man. Allan is trembling with fear. DANNY: What are you, some rejected writer? An extra I spat on? The crew guy whose parking lot I stole? Because if so, you're going to have to get in line to threaten me with a gun, kiddo. It's not your turn. YOUNG MAN Sorry, not a Hollywood sob story. Just a guy looking to have a conversation. DANNY: There are better ways to schedule meetings around here, even if they are less eff- YOUNG MAN Shut up. Everyone's attention is turned away as the door to the office lobby slams shut. A familiar voice echoes from off camera. 52112: Aw, come on, Danny, how can you forget that face? YOUNG MAN & DANNY (simultaneously) Juan Carlos? Keeping an eye on the guards, the young man stares at the new entrant to the room. After their dual expression of shock, Danny's surprised gaze switches from the door to the young man. The two men from earlier, 52112 and 76933, step in for good. The larger one keeps an eye on the armed young man, and the wiser one elaborates. 52112: Something like that. But I take it from Mr. Ferguson's expression that you two haven't been formally introduced. Allow me to do the honors. He produces his cell phone again and presses a single button before holding it to his ear. 52112: (into the phone) We have priority situation. Hot contact. He hangs up before getting a response and shoots his partner a look. Stepping out a bit, he opens his palms to calm everyone involved. He holds one hand out to Danny... 52112: Danny Ferguson... ...and the other out to the young man. 52112: ...meet Luis Alazar. Danny still looks confused, but the man continues. 52112: In other words, El Spiffy IV, meet El Spiffy V. Danny's eyes widen as he glances back and forth between 'Luis' and 'Juan Carlos.' Everyone is at a loss for words, which is fine by me, because we're about to... FADE OUT. TO BE CONTINUED.
