Transition

10 February 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 2/10/06 “Move, move, move!” Titan shouted, bending his massive form over the two women. He turned, blasting a few rounds out of his .357 Desert Eagle back towards the house. One of the cultists slumped over the frame of a broken window, twitching slightly. Next to him, Scott Matthews leveled the Mossberg and blasted two shells of low-spread shot. A shout of pain answered them as another offending cultist fell to the ground, clutching his face. The day had taken on a rather quiet start. Everyone got up quietly, had a light breakfast and did what they had been doing for the last week: sitting around waiting. Scott had cleared out all the nearby brush and trees in order to open up the area around the cabin. Lacey had been taking the night watch, keeping a lookout through the dark hours and Van... well, Van had been doing his best not to 'flip out' as he so eloquently put it. Everyone was under a great deal of stress and half the house wasn't dealing too well with it. Van, Lacey and Alyssa were all wrecks of nerves and fear despite the precautions that had been taken. Titan was a veteran of this sort of thing: he'd lived every day in fear for years: fear of a random explosion at the corner of the street, of a random attack in the middle of a beautiful day. Scott met the encroaching fear with a snarl and determination. He'd been harried and harassed by the Cult for long enough: he hoped they did come. Surprisingly, it was Cathy that was the calmest. The matriarch of the Matthews clan was cool and poised through the whole thing. She went about her day like nothing had changed, making sure everyone was as rested and well-fed as they could stand to be. The only outward sign she herself showed was to resume something she'd given up years ago: for the first time Scott could remember in at least fifteen years, he'd seen his mother light a cigarette, take on drag, then pinch it out and throw it away. They spent long nights in sleepless worry: Scott found Alyssa huddled in a ball next to him, her arms wrapped tightly around his chest and her head nestled on his shoulder, which was wet with tears. Van simply became jumpy, reacting to sudden noises like he was in a combat zone. Lacey had taken up night watch simply because she could not sleep at night, and so she just switched her schedule, making it her job to not sleep at night and rest during the day. For five days after word of Picot's death, the house was more of a den of silence than anything. Titan kept a careful eye and slept lightly, the way he'd learned to in the barracks all those years ago. Any out -of-place sound woke him and he'd move with a stealth that belied his size. Usually it was Lacey moving around, trying to keep watch out of a new window, but once he'd found the teenager in the middle of a panic attack, breathless and tears streaming down her face. But this morning, everyone had settled into a stark, stressed routine when the first notice arrived. Titan knew the man from the file: Jordan Giassi, the massive bodyguard of Jean Baubier, the head of the Cult of the Poison Tree. Giassi wasn't armed, but instead marched straight into the house, incidentally following a terrified Alyssa who had gone out to one of the Explorers to retrieve a book. He followed close being, keeping the door from shutting, and there in the foyer, two behemoths had collided. Scott had reacted to his mother's shout and come out of the kitchen looking like an enraged bull ready to charge. He was met head-on by the angry bear of a man and the two locked into a terrifying ballet of combat. Scott and Giassi both threw massive fists at one another and both absorbed the blows. Titan had moved into position quietly, and seen that Scott's lessons in control hadn't entirely sunk in yet. As the battle raged on, Scott's self-control slipped away. He couldn't afford to loose his temper in the face of a bigger opponent, especially one that turned out to be well-trained. Scott's hamfisted strikes bounced off the equally determined man as his own hands found the Manbeast's throat. Scott hammered away with his forearms, trying to get the beartrap for his trachea, but to not avail. Giassi smiled coldly, his icy blue eyes boring into Scott's with a sick satisfaction. “I will enjoy watching you die, infidel,” he snarled through clenched teeth. “You have manhandled the Illustrious One, and after I kill you, your family will be forfeit.” Scott's eyes widened as he redoubled his efforts, but again, it did no good at all. His strikes didn't seem to faze the man before him and he could not get those fingers loose from around his neck. Titan slipped in behind Giassi and brought down the butt of his pistol on the back of the man's neck. His grip slackened, but he spun to face this new assailant. He stopped dead, looking into the eyes of the biggest man he'd ever seen. Titan's other hand grabbed one of Giassi's and the barrel of the .357 was pressed to his temple. “Scott,” Titan said coldly, “you really need more work.” The smallest giant in the house needed, massaging his throat, but his face looked murder at the offending cultist. “He's already here, which means the rest of the group can't be that far behind. Let's dispose of him quickly.” A look of fear crossed the cultist's face, but when Scott's fist cracked into the back of his skull, he slumped bonelessly and Scott hefted the man over only shoulder. As he turned for the back door, Titan motioned for him to stop. “We have to do something,” he said, and pointed for him to continue. He continued as they walked. “He's got to be incapacitated so that he can't interfere when things get crazy.” “I will not!” he shouted, forcing his way off of Scott's shoulder. “Resilient punk, aren't you?” Titan asked, leveling his weapon. Giassi stared down the barrel of the gun and laughed. “You didn't kill me before,” he sneered, “you won't now.” “I don't need to,” Titan replied before coldly putting a .357 magnum round through the man's thigh. He fell to one knee, his mouth frozen open in a wordless cry of pain, but before he could cry for more than a moment, Scott cracked him again precisely right behind the ear with his elbow. “Leave him here,” Titan said, matter-of-factly. “The others will be here soon.” And Titan wasn't wrong. No more than half an hour later, a group of men dressed in hunter's camouflage came out of the woods, handguns held forward and searching for movement. Titan had directed the women towards the middle of the house, away from the exterior walls. He, Scott and Van had taken up positions to meet the oncoming force. As it turned out, they all approached from the front, and with a low whistle, Titan brought the other men forward. “Amateurs,” he muttered under his breath, drawing a bead on his first target. Van lined up at another window, holding the .22 rifle in his hands. Titan held the Winchester 308 to his shoulder and Scott stood just to the side of the front door with the shotgun in his hands. The big man was agitated; he'd hit the panic button for the FBI response team to come and make the save, but part of being in such a secluded location meant it took anyone a good deal of time to reach them, the panic team included. Titan stared through the boarded window, assessing the men. As he watched them move, he could tell he wasn't dealing with professionals. The moved smoothly, but had no signs of body armor and held mismatched weapons. They paused just outside of the house, waiting. He almost shook his head: you never wait outside of place you're moving on once you've left cover. He held his breath and gave the trigger a smooth pull. The shot rang out, and he could hear the women scream behind him, but his shot took the man squarely in the chest. The big round was fired into the center of mass more to see if his guess on the body armor was right or not. The man spun and fell, a gaping hole in his left pectoral, just above the heart. Van followed up, firing the small rifle. The hollowpoint .22 hit one in the shoulder and he dropped to the ground, his pistol thumping in the dead leaves outside. His cry of pain brought a smile to the giant's face. He'd scream, the panic would infect the others and this might get to be just the slightest bit easier. The men outside were definitely not professionals; when that first shot rang out, they all froze and watched the man go down. They should have started returning fire... but as was said, these men were devoted, not professional. Once Van's shot hit and the victim screamed, they cut loose, firing a ragged volley towards the structure. Titan bent down, lowering himself beneath the window and behind the reinforced wall. Van did the same, but in more of a diving motion. Titan breathed a thankful breath that he remembered to point the firearm away from himself and Scott. The shots hit the house, and thanks to the steel reinforcing built in, did not penetrate. Titan leaned up slowly to see them shooting in all manner of directions, but mainly upwards, towards the second story. He took careful aim and fired again, this time taking care to make it a belly shot. Another man stumbled backwards and crashed into the ground, howling in agony. Van squeezed off a pair of shots himself, neither one hitting, but adding to the general disorder outside. Titan saw a group gather and run for the door. He ducked back down and looked at Scott. The man met his gaze and nodded back, pumping a shell into the chamber. They slammed into the door all at once; it was too much for even the reinforced hinges to take and the door gave way. As the men looked into the open door, they saw Scott standing there, a shotgun leveled at them.... and decided discretion was the better part of valor. The initial wave of shot took one man in the side, peppering his gun-arm with a wave of 12-gauge pain that dropped his Glock to the ground. Scott took a step back and grabbed the door, lifted it upward and jammed it into the frame. As had been the plan, when the rifles ran out, it was time to leave. Van slung it on his back and headed straight towards the women. Titan did the same, but pulled his sidearm and followed. Scott brought up the rear, facing back with the shotgun leveled. He opted to blast off a round as a deterret, peppering the door with another wave of shot. The scream from the other side of the door was an answer that it had been a good idea. And that brought everything up to speed. The group ran for the Explorer parked behind the house and everyone made their way towards it. Van dove into the backseat, Alyssa and Lacey right behind him. That's when Scott and Titan looked at one another in sheer terror. Cathy, Scott's mother, was nowhere to be found. By this time, the cultists were obviously in the house, and were making their way into the kitchen, the room in the back and nearest the vehicle. That's when they spotted her, standing at the door to the kitchen, alone. Cathy Matthews was a strong woman. This whole ordeal was infuriating to her and her inability to do much was frustrating. When the attack finally came, she'd had enough. As everyone else had hustled out of the house, she had made her way into the kitchen and reached up under the gas stove. With a hard pull, she ripped the gas line free and set it atop the counter, pointing towards the room's only interior door. She took a few steps as the putrid aroma filled the room, and moved outside. Once there, she lit a cigarette carefully and took a long drag. She looked back over her shoulder to see men in camo filling the room, weapons held at the ready. She tucked the lighter away in her pocket and opened the door. “You,” she said in a steady voice, “have terrified my family, defaced my son and nearly drove him to suicide. You forced us out of our home and attacked us. I'm through with you.” And with that, she expertly flicked the cigarette butt into the kitchen. As it arched through the air, she turned and ran, moving for all she was worth towards the Explorer. The cigarette hit the countertop, scattering ashes burning embers and the room lit up as the gas ignited. The explosion carried her forward, dropping her hard on the packed dirt out back. Titan shielded his eyes against the blast, but Scott was already moving forward, charging like his old linebacker days: bent forward, head down and his arms tucked close while the shotgun dangled from the shoulder strap down his back. Titan raised his pistol to cover, but there was no movement from the house. Scott lifted his stunned mother in his arms and turned, heading for the vehicle. “Get in the back!” Titan shouted as he moved towards the drivers' seat. He twisted the key that had been left in the ignition and jammed the rear hatch button. Scott dove in, still cradling his mother in his arms and pulled the hatch shut. Titan hit the gas, screaming them away from the house in a spray of dirty and leaves. Scott limbered the Mossberg to watch for anyone following, but slumped against the spare tire, his breath coming in ragged gasps. Back at the cabin, Giassi had awoken to see the entire assault go down in failure. From his vantage, he could see the men get shot down, enter the house and the explosion from the kitchen. His eyes were wide with horror: the Blessed Order of the Pious Tree had failed, just as he had. How was that possible, when acting with the blessing of the Illustrious One? The sound of even, deliberate steps brought his head around. James Richards, the right-hand man of Baubier himself, stood there in a navy suit, looking down at him. “This is your fault,” he said quietly. “You alerted them that the group was coming by charging out yourself. I knew it was done as soon as you left. Oh, we might have killed one or two of them had we waited to hire some real professionals, but no; Baubier wouldn't wait. Neither would you.” Richards reached into his jacket and pulled out a .38 revolver in a latex-gloved hand. “You're a failure.” With one shot, Giassi was no more. Richards threw the gun at the house near one of the fallen bodies and walked away. Titan navigated the SUV up the emergency route away from the house. To call it a road would be overly generous, and was part of the reason he had requisitioned four-wheel drive vehicles. His mind raced: somehow they knew where the cabin was, somehow they had a breach in security. He knew it would happen, but he didn't know how. That's when a line of FBI Suburbans cut across his path. With all that had gone wrong, he wasn't about to take chances and lifted his sidearm, but when a flow of agents he recognized poured out of the vehicle, he took a deep breath and lowered the pistol. He pushed the door open and stepped out. He didn't holster it, though. “Report, Agent,” said the Special Agent In Charge, Agent Cortez. Titan took a deep breath and slowly began to recount the events. “We were attacked early in the morning by a high profile member of the cult, Jordan Giassi. He moved to engage in hand to hand combat. We disposed of him by a knockout blow and a .357 round to the leg. Half and hour later, we were attacked en masse by a group of nonprofessionals.” Cortez's face changed into a dour expression. “Understood. Any ideas of the breach in security?” “None, sir,” Titan breathed. “I do think, at the moment, it's safe to say that the breach would have little to no idea what will transpire from here. I recommend we use the Delta Option.” Cortez looked at the giant. “Yeah, I think we're going to have to. A helicopter is en route to take the subjects to location. We'll have them taken to location to prep for Delta.” Scott remembered sitting in the helicopter, an arm around his mother and the other around his fiancee. Both were silent, as was he. The morning was nothing any of them had ever expected. Seated across from them, Van spoke softly to Lacey, trying to calm her down. She still seemed to be in a state of horrified shock. In fact, no one had said anything until they arrived at the FBI compound in Seattle. Titan was in and out of the waiting room they were in, sometimes with papers in hand, sometimes not. He looked at them sympathetically. Finally, he came in and sat down, facing everyone. “I'm sorry, everyone,” he said softly. “This... never should have happened. In fact, there's no reason that any Cult members shouldn't be in prison, especially the leadership, after that big initial drug bust. But now, we've got to take more extreme measures after what happened.” “Where do we go now?” Cathy said, sitting up. “Japan,” came his simple response. The shock crossing everyone's face was apparent. “I know it seems extreme, but, believe it or not, it's the best option. We have forces there already, we might house you on a military base and you two gentlemen,” he said pointing at Scott and Van, “ can even train with some of the best wrestlers in Japan.” Scott nodded slowly and sat back. The big man was speechless, but his smaller partner was not. “Listen,” Van said, pulling an envelope from his back pocket, “I don't know who could use this, but it's at your discretion, okay? Nelson's going to be getting rid of Green and Stewart soon, and I hate to leave people hanging by just leaving a hole in the roster. I can't get a team in there for us again, but I do have this. It's a contract to wrestle on the PRIME roster. You find someone that can use it, use it yourself if you want to. I just don't want to leave the place more out-to-dry than I already have.” Titan opened the envelope and looked at the heading. PRIME Wrestler Contract was stamped across the top of the form. He nodded and tucked it away. That's when a soft touch turned his head. Alyssa Sanders was standing at his side. “First, I wanted to thank you for everything you've done to help us,” she said softly. “Second... do you think there might be someone here that can perform a wedding before we leave?”
