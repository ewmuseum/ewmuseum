Blood

28 June 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/28/05 Blood. It's a nice Friday night, turning Saturday. It was the end of a long, painful week in the world of wrestling. One of those kind of weeks where you just say to yourself 'damn, is any of this worth it?' Brandon Youngblood's answer obviously would be a yes. Even with everything he'd succumbed to this past week, whether it was the tap out to the Crimson Angel or the defeat at the hands of the Tennessee Technician. At the end of the day, he could only take it in stride. Deville/Youngblood I was such a great match that the two had to met again. Box office kind of stuff. There almost always is a sequel. He was still in his white singlet with the crimson trim, but it looked vastly different than it did going into the night. It was soaked in beer, soda, and Brandon's own blood. The blood letting from the Garvin ass kicking forced his red life force to cascade down his face and neck to his chest. The Y logo on the chest of the singlet was almost completely covered in blood. His hands and taped wrists seemed to be trembling. Not from anger. In the ring, wrestlers can make it through their battles because of adrenaline. It keeps the body going at full capacity. But when that adrenaline high wears off, the body crashes, and the man or woman who was cheered as millions watched slumps back into reality. For Brandon, any time the adrenaline high wore off, he felt tired. The longer the match went on, the longer he'd just sit there in the back trying to overcome his fatigue. But when he bled, the crash was supplemented with a sickness. He felt like puking his guts out, but did not have the energy to. Because of the blood letting, he'd go into shock. Thus, the shaking. But his whole body trembled. On the Youngblood scale of blood, this was in the upper range. The last time this had happened was almost back to back. Once in the Dual Halo. The other against Garvin for the Jolt Triple Crown Championship. However, that time, he did not let his body recover. He lost so much blood within those two shows that he needed a blood transfusion. He'd actually came close to killing himself just by wrestling. It was then that he decided that he had to marry his girlfriend, Melissa. Life was way too short, and the fact was that in this business, with this lifestyle, something can happen one moment that might alter your life forever. He was hunched over as he sat in his chair in the locker room area, not moving. He was quietly seething. How could he let Angelo Deville make him submit? How could he? Angelo was a great wrestler, yes, but Brandon knew that he was so close to making the Crimson Angel submit that it sickened him. Right now, all his inadequacies were jumping out to him, pounding him into submission. Every time he lost, he felt almost helpless. Like he wanted to die. Winning and losing were things that were taken very seriously in his life. It was a trait that was passed in the Youngblood gene pool. Success makes you. And in the wrestling business, winning is everything. Sure, on the street Brandon would get recognized. Sure, Brandon could hook up to the internet and see thousands of people patting him on the back for 'that great match' he had. But wins, titles, those are what define you. Brandon tried to rationalize his obsession. He wanted to be the best. He trained hard. Harder than anyone else. He'd ran everyone morning he was in his Mojave Desert abode. Running through the endless sand dunes effortlessly until the sun came up and turned ice to fire. He had done his bridge ups and his weight training twice every day he could. He'd worked with students in the just opening Gridlock Wrestling Academy, putting them in holds and stretching their bodies to the very limit. He did it not only to push them, but to push himself. And considering that students need a personal invite to attend, he was working with people he had chosen. He'd worked and wrestled with former NCAA Champions wanting to break into the pro ranks. He'd even worked with some of the most notable Mixed Martial Arts masters, and almost always on a regular basis trained with Olympic quality wrestlers in California. There were even times when he was able to work with good friend Christopher Sheffield, himself a world class wrestler, in random sparing matches. Tape study? Brandon hounded it. Every day he was not on a plane to get to a wrestling show somewhere, he was studying and training. Sun rise to sun down. Sure, he'd take time out to enjoy the company of his lovely wife, but he was busy perfecting his craft. And while he loved his job, it was still work. Others in this sport did not treat it so. And while he had destroyed the man target on his hit list, it only suited to bring about the challenge of an entire organization. Angelo Deville was almost a byproduct of the large scale battle. But Nova? Nova was different. Nova was a drug addict. A man who reveled in his pop culture and his wit. What he stood for was bad for wrestling in general. Youngblood was not a moral man, he'd known that for a long time. He did not believe in a lord or savior, just believing in the canvas and four ring posts he performed in. But the embarrassment of having a company you fight for, that you live and breath for, that you almost die for, being represented by a pot smoking Deadhead was daunting. Nova was there to market to the kids because he was accessible. But no man who does drugs should be marketed. They should be shunned. Brandon was a former alcoholic. He knew the taint. And he was ashamed of it. He had fought the demons of his past and beat them back, and finally had overcome the overwhelming feeling of guilt he had. But Nova? He seemed to be in step with his. He seemed to revel in his own disgust. For Youngblood, the ring was a sacred place. Every time he steps foot into a ring, he feels as if he is stepping into the most holy of places. And to see a man who did not respect himself enough to give all of what he had to train and fight in the squared circle? He needed to be dealt with. Taken care of. Destroyed. The agony of defeat from Jeff Garvin subsided. And while he was exhausted and his body was basically crashing through the floor, the rage that fueled him bell to bell had resurfaced. His teeth were bared as he thought about Revolution. Thinking about the sound of Angelo Deville acting as if it was over. Thinking about whatever it was Nova would respond after two weeks of getting his ass handed to him before everyone to see. His body trembled at a more rapid rate, this time in anger. He knew his upcoming match. Knew that he was going to dive headfirst into Nova and a Vangelus Olsig. He had Nova's Five Star Championship in his possession. And it would only be a matter of time before Youngblood completely broke the will of Nova and drove him out of the sport. Olsig was just a man caught in the crossfire. However, he was guilty as well, obviously on the other side of Youngblood's crusade. Two birds could be killed with one stone. Revolution. It was mere days away. A new week was coming to replace this disaster. And while the fWo was foremost on his mind, with what the Original had done and with what the fans had done, he still looked forward to what he could do to destroy two more abominations in the world of professional wrestling. And as Brandon Youngblood picked himself up to look for someone to help him with his head, it was abundantly clear that someone was going to pay. Nobody has this bad of a week without paying for it in spades. Blood. Is it worth it? You bet your ass it is.
