Date: Today The students couldn’t quite comprehend what they were looking at. Sure, most of them had seen it on TV, but this was real life. In person. For years, the man before them had been the Hardcore Isosceles Trapezoid, a professional wrestler and substitute teacher that had become an institution at Brigham Young High School in Salt Lake City. He was loved by students for his odd ways. Now, however, he stood before them with slicked back brown hair, wearing a suit. Even his name had changed. Mr. Trapezoid was now Mr. Williamson. And for Mr. Williamson, this was his last day as a substitute teacher. “It’s hard to explain, everyone,” he told the students, ten minutes before his final class ever was to end. Clearly, the wide-open mouths of the class indicated that they didn’t quite understand why the former Trapezoid was giving up teaching, and/or renouncing his identity. “But this is my decision. It is one that I came to after a great deal of thought and consideration.” The students sat quiet, math books opened on their desks. There hadn’t been much of a class today. As he looked at the class, Williamson could see the looks on their faces. Upset looks. Disappointed looks. To a degree, he understood. Most people had liked The HIT, for one reason or another. He was honest and open. Completely willing to put himself on the line for the fans enjoyment. Even during his feud with Karina, he had his supporters. Now, however, he was with The Deville. As such, he doubted that he’d be getting many cheers. He opened his mouth to speak, to try to say something to the kids who knew him by what he used to be. Mercifully, however, he was saved by the bell. To a man and woman, everyone in the class rose silently, gathered their belongings, and left. It was spooky, really. Justin knew that he had thrown the baby out with the bathwater, so to speak. He didn’t HAVE to stop teaching. It was only the hardcore wrestling that made him ill. But the teaching was part of who the HIT was, and so it had to go. The door to the classroom closed with a resounding crash, leaving Justin Williamson alone. He tugged at his collar somewhat uncomfortably. Even after a week, he wasn’t really used to the unfamiliar attire. Angelo Deville had introduced him to a life that he knew nothing about. It was a life of fancy clothes, fine restaurants, and respect. And Williamson liked it. “I’ve earned this, damnit,” he told himself, thinking back over the insane depravity he had withstood over a five year career. Barbed wire, glass, and fire had left their respective impressions all over his otherwise impressive figure, as he worked for whatever various promoters would pay him. Sure, the IWO had paid relatively well, as had PCW, and as was PRIME. But he had never been one of the top stars, never made the money that an Angelo Deville would make. That was, until he became the Deville’s partner. Suddenly, gifts came flowing out of the woodwork. The very suit he wore was a gift from Angelo, after entering an exclusive tailor shop and throwing down $500 to close the shop to all but himself and Williamson. Life was good, but it could always be better. “Those Mexican bastards are going to learn why it is…unwise to meddle in the affairs of superiors,” he said, in a somewhat condescending tone of voice. Gathering together his belongings, he walked for the door, slamming it behind him just as his students had done. The door on one chapter of his life slammed with it.
