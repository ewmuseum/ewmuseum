A flick of the wrist, a twist of fate

4 September 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: September 4 A creamy expanse of alabaster thigh lay before me like an unswept and unpopulated plain to a would-be pioneer. I stroak the smooth surface, eliciting an animalistic groan. The path of my hand leads to the nexus, a familiar and oh-so-inviting swath of red hair shaved in a delicate yet tantalizing stripe down the middle. Greedily, I bend my head and begin to partake. Smooth rounded hips buck, then jerk, then begin to undulate in rhythm to my oral ministrations. A hand wraps in my hair—back when I had enough hair to grab—as a voice alternates between groans and coos. “More,” she sighs. “More… more… more…” “More, sir?” “Whuzzat?” You look up to the friendly and plump face of the woman in a white dress shirt, black tie and black apron standing over you with a pitcher of water. “I said, would you like some more water?” “Sure.” You shift slightly to the left as the woman leans in and pours. “Where’d you just go just now?” You turn to face the source of the second female voice. The one belonging to the woman with the meticulously disheveled auburn hair, wearing a pair of black pants and a blue shirt with just enough buttons undone to leave something to the imagination. One eyebrow is cocked inquisitively as she awaits an answer. “Sorry… just thinking about something… something from the past.” You’d faced a Deville and Los Diablos, Hessian in a Chain Match and soon you’d face a chain-gang reject in a straight-up one on one match. You’d faced Timo Bolamba, Trashcan Man, Azala Zameer and Six Finger Cisco within the confines of a steel cell. You’d taken on Ivan Stanislav. Boda. Brandon Youngblood. Karina Wolfenden. Every tag team in PRIME—all at once, no less! But none of them sparked a quivering in your guts quite like your latest opponent. A blind date. Not blind, per se. Not in this age of the Internet and cell phones. Call it a date with severe cataracts and glaucoma, then. And maybe a limp. Yeah, definitely a limp.“You want any more of the nachos?” “No, you say,” taking a sip from the recently replenished water glass. “You won’t be able to last too long if you keep drinking like that.” Now that she mentioned it you did notice a certain… fulfillment at the moment. You start to stand before you realize a familiar feel of tightness. Apparently the previous trip from memory lane ran a little too deep. Forget Phase 2… while your frontal lobe was asleep at the switch, the boys in the basement appeared to take things all the way to Phase 4. “Nope! I’m fine for now…” She shrugs and motions to the waitress. “Like we should listen to you anyway…” I turn to face the row of young men standing in the ring. My ring. The squared circle I sold my flatscreen to buy. The one resting square in the middle of Dave Gibson’s School of Wrestling. “What did you say?” “You heard me,” the kid said. I see tattoos running down his arms and try to will my brain away from pondering the ink, the thought of the needle jabbing into his arms again and again, broken up only by the tattoo artist fastidiously wiping away the excess ink mixed with blood after every few pinpricks. He’s smirking from beneath an unkempt mop of sandy hair. He’s slightly built but I can see the bulges of muscles underneath. He has a wiry build. Ropey muscles. The type of kid with endurance. The type of kid who knows he’s an athlete, who’s proven it on the field of competiton—maybe in a couple of sports, maybe more. The type of kid who thinks he’s something. The type of kid who thinks he can walk into a wrestling ring, who’s watched it on TV and tried to duplicate some of the moves in his backyard with a few old mattresses and thinks it’s “nothing special.” It’s the type of kid I hate. And the type of kid I love to teach. Not because he’ll make it, but because he’ll provide such a valuable lesson for the ones who will. “I’m afraid I didn’t.” I step up until I’m in the kid’s face. He’s got a good two inches on me. Probably plays basketball. “I said, why should I spend my money to have YOU teach me how to wrestle? You’re a has-been. You got run out of Old School Wrestling because you were washed up. I watched, man. I know. Your neck’s shot. You’ll never wrestle a real match again, and everybody knows it. I want a teacher who’s got CONNECTIONS, man. Somebody who can hook me up for a shot at a major contract. What can you hook me up with? A coupon at the local liquor store?” A few snickers from elsewhere on the line. I jerk my head left and right and they cease as quickly as they appeared. “You’re right.” Murmurs up and down the line as the big talker smirks triumphantly. “You’re absolutely right. I’m done. Out of the business. Probably not worth a damn. So, I’ll tell you what kid. What’s your name again?” The kid pauses, tossing his head back so that the hair flies out of his eyes. “Terry Dakota.” Sweet Jews for Jesus, he walked IN with a ring name. That’s just begging for it. “All right, Mister … Dakota. I’ll make you a deal.” I get down on the mat, onto all fours, facing away from him. “Keep me down.” “Excuse me?” “Keep me down, if you can keep me in the hold for 30 seconds, I’ll refund your money. You can go find a school run by someone with a little more ‘credibility.’ Deal?” He shrugs and starts to lean in. I feel arms wrap around my torso, then link as hands clasp at my breastbone. Too high. This won’t take long. “Somebody wanna keep the clock?” you call out. “Yeah, right here,” a reedy voice replies. “Ready… set… go!” I feel the pressure intensify at the breastbone. I feint a twist to the left before going to the right. He stays right there with me. Impressive, he knows more than I thought he might. Too bad he doesn’t know all the fundamentals. I swing my right arm back and feel the satisfying crack of bone on bone as my elbow smashes into the side of his head. There’s a groan and the grip lessens, allowing me to wriggle free and ensnare Mr. Dakota in a go-behind. I grip his right arm and twist, wrenching it into a hammerlock and shoving him facefirst to the canvas. I twist. I bend. I twist again. With every manipulation of the limb I elicit a scream, with each yell at a higher timbre than the last. “Time to tap out.” Hearing a hand slapping against the canvas always sounds so damn sweet. I pry myself off and try not to look too satisfied as Mr. Dakota wriggles through the ropes to the floor, clutching at his right shoulder. Judging from the smell he left behind, he just might’ve shit his trunks while he was in my grip. He wouldn’t be the last. Thank God it didn’t leak, cleaning this canvas can be a bitch. I stand and face the group. “Anybody else got an opinion?” “An opinion about what?” From the look on her face you realize you must have said something. “Um… the restaurant.” Smoooooth, ace. This date’s really going great. “It’s OK, I guess. Dinner conversation could be a hell of a lot better, though.” She smirks as she says it. Sarcasm; it’s a lost art and you appreciate it so. “Sorry. Tough day at work.” “Right… what is it you do again? Your profile said you’re in entertainment.” “Yeah, that was out of date.” “So then what do you do now?” “I’m a traveling salesman.” “Any good?” “Well, let’s just say I’ve got a reputation for selling to anybody and everybody.” “Ah, so what’d you do before that?” “I helped run an entertainment company.” “Really? Cool! How’d you get that job?” A creamy expanse of alabaster thigh lay before me like an unswept and unpopulated plain to a would-be pioneer. I stroak the smooth surface, eliciting a quick giggle. A well-manicured hand slaps me away. “Shoo, fly.” I smile up into her green eyes then lean in for a kiss. Her hands wrap around my neck, and I feel a sudden swell of emotion as if a champagne bottle just uncorked from within my chest. Now, release from the kiss and lean in to her neck. Kissing, nibbling, licking as she makes some animalistic noises of appreciation. Now my head descends, and I make her make noises of a different kind. * * * * * I sprawl on my back smoking a cigarette with a satisfied smile. She’s laying on her stomach, half-wrapped in a sheet, her legs kicking idly in the air near me as she taps something out on a laptop. It was the only relationship I ever had that made sense. Scratch that—it was the only relationship I ever had, period. My name was Dave Gibson, and I was Old School. Her name… Was Jane Reagan. And she was the CEO of Old School Wrestling. Everyone else called her their boss. I called her my lover. Not a bad perk after a few months on the job. “I’ve got to hand it to you, Dave,” she said. “Hessian and Propaganda against Ronin? Genius! It should make a ton of money for us…” “I’m just surprised no one else thought of it first. Tag team wrestling’s always been able to draw big money if it’s done right.” “Some of us didn’t spend all their formative years watching wrestling every Saturday, Dave… EEK!” She shrieks as I spider my fingers across one of her bare feet in mid-kick. I can’t resist and I pounce, digging my fingers into her ribs as we turn into a thrashing twisting mass. “C’mon, I’ve got to practice my technique…” I say over her giggles and shrieks. “Can’t let the ring rust start, ya know?” I show mercy and sprawl back on the bed. She rears onto all fours, shooting me an indignant look. “That is NOT attractive!” she says, puffing an errant piece of hair out from in front of her eyes. I shrug. I know she doesn’t mean it. The twinkle in her eyes betrays her. We were nothing but employer and employee for the first few weeks—even though I did always have a thing for redheads. Yet we always were friendly. She respected me because I knew so much about wrestling at such a young age, started asking me about the old days, about all the tapes I watched. And who was I to refuse? Turning down company that stirs the boys below is ill-advised for any man. Turning down company that stirs the boys AND pays your check is flat out stupid. I guess if I was watching it in a movie, I’d have seen it coming a mile away. Hell, both of us would have. But instead… it just happened. One day, I was praising the effective storytelling in an Ivan Stanislav match. Next thing I knew, we were sharing a hotel suite at every stop on the road. The pillow talk took a turn to business right off the bat. How can we make more money? Who’s underrated? Who’s overrated? She sat there and asked me all the questions, and I answered them honestly cause that’s my way. I’d like to tell you I didn’t know what she’d do with my knowledge. I’d like to tell you that I thought she was just gathering information to bounce off the suits. Instead, I knew what she wanted. Justification. For personnel moves. For hirings. For firings. Most of all, she wanted someone to run the book—be a ghost writer, if you will. Bring in the wrestling knowledge she lacked and would never have, but—when combined with her business acumen—could make OSW bigger and better than ever. In return, doors started to open for me. The endorsement offers were just the start of it.Do you have any idea how many expense reimbursement checks I got from the office that really came from Jane’s discretionary fund? My accounts grew fat on dinners in restaurants I never stepped into and rides in cabs that I never took. On camera I was playing the part of a man who would do anything for a buck. Meanwhile behind the scenes it stopped becoming a part. I got a taste of the good life and wanted more. More money, more suits, more cars, more booze (ESPECIALLY more booze). Plus there were pills—more of them, as many as possible. Going balls-out in the ring night after night, taking crazy bumps and spears from 400pound Africans had started to take their toll. I always said I wanted to prepare for a career after wrestling. If the pain kept up like this, I’d be starting that career sooner than I thought. For now, though, I’d try to gut through it—even if it meant I would have some crappy matches. Bah, who cared what the smarks said any way? When it came to selling out, Snoop Dogg had nothing on me. I didn’t mind. I was doing what I loved—wrestling—and spending all my spare time with a woman I thought I COULD love. At least it was the closest I ever came to love, in a situation I thought feelings were mutual. I was on top of the world. There was nothing I could not do. Except one thing. * * * * * “You understand why you can’t, right, Dave?” she said after our second time of the night in a Pittsburgh hotel suite. “It’d cause too many questions. What would people say if they saw you getting a huge push? An extended run at the main event?” “That I was Triple H’s long lost brother?” A playful smack on the tricep. “I’m serious!” “I know.” Her head lies against my chest. As her red mane settles against my pec, I can feel my heart begin to pump faster. “Just a title shot. That’s all I can guarantee you. No title reigns. It’d create too many questions. We can’t have people find out about us, Dave. They can know we’re dating. They CAN’T know you’re helping me write the shows.” “I won’t tell a soul.” She draws her head up. “It’s not that you don’t deserve it.” “I know… Let’s talk about something else, OK?” “Hey, fine by me.”Your date shrugs and takes another sip of your Bud Light. No pretenses with this one, she’s perfectly happy with her cheap domestic beer and a cheeseburger. That may put some guys off. For you, it’s a bonus. “So how come you don’t work there any more?” You glance down at your lap briefly (all quiet belowdecks, sir!) and sigh. “I had some health issues.” “People are starting to talk, Dave.” She’s pacing back and forth in front of me, and I think it might be the longest the both of us have remained clothed after a show since the relationship started. Definitely a bad sign. “Stop being s’ dramatic.” I was slurring my words. I did that a lot lately. I’d been wrestling less and drinking more. Apparently, I’d gotten sloppy—or so Jane thought. Either that or she wanted to get rid of me. “I made it very clear our working relationship couldn’t come out. Now people are starting to wonder where this Power Play business is going to go. There’s no way we can keep you out of the upper card if this feud with Register becomes reality. I can’t have that, Dave. I can’t have people figure it out.” “S’ stop it then! You’re the friggin CEO! Stop whinin’ and DO somethin’—find s’mbody else t’write th’ damn shows!” She pursed her lips. “Dave… you’re a mess, OK? You need help with the drinking. Your work’s started to slip—in and out of the ring. I know what this business can do to people… and I don’t want that to happen to you.” She squats down in front of me. “I care about you, Dave.” “But do you love me?” Her eyes are starting to get watery. “Yes, Dave… I think I might.” “Think you might?” I knew it was enough… even though it really wasn’t. “OK, Jane. I’ll clean up.” You wipe desperately at the stain from the spaghetti sauce and only do a better job of rubbing it into your pair of khakis. Your date watches with a twinkle in her eye. “Drop something, Dave?” “Sonofabitch… yes…” “I should’ve known better to get Italian. If it’s red, it’s on me. Sauce, ketchup, blood…” “Blood?!?” “I’m accident prone.” “Riiiiight. Lot of risk as a salesman, eh?” “There can be.” “I’ve just got one question, though.” “Fire away.” “How do you find the time to sell stuff inbetween all those wrestling matches?” “I’m not quite sure what you want me to do, Mr. Gibson.” “I thought things were pretty clear. You’re a plastic surgeon. By all accounts, you’re a good one. I want some work done.” I had to give Jane credit. She had connections and set everything up. Last night, Power Play turned on me. The stable I built betrayed me behind its new master—Matt Register—and left me in a pool of my own blood. The crowd ate it up, especially when Matt gave me a piledriver onto his OSE title belt. We milked it for all it was worth. Referees, trainers, a stretcher job with a backboard, the works. An ambulance brought me to the hospital, but not to the emergency room. I had to get ready for surgery. As the doctor questioned my diecision, I already was starting to feel that ache. The yearning, the need. The thirst! But I had made a promise. And once Jane had explained her plan… well, it was genius. I needed a break—from the ring and the booking. I also needed to get clean… or at least she thought I did. And if I couldn’t be a champion, I knew I couldn’t stand to lose her too. So we agreed to the neck injury that wasn’t a neck injury. Read it again. My neck injury was faked. It didn’t happen. Get it? It was a sham, a ruse, a way to get the smarks off my back and some time to get sober. There was just one thing I needed. “A scar…” You run your hand across the front of your neck in a brief horizontal motion. “Right there. The type of incision you’d see from a neck surgery that went in the front, here, near the larynx.” “Mr. Gibson, that’s a dangerous procedure. It comes so close to the vocal cords and… may I ask WHY you want this done?” So I can have a chance. So I won’t drink myself to death. Mostly, so she won’t leave me. So I won’t lose it all. But I didn’t say any of it. This doctor had skill but not the best reputation. Jane had some kind of dirt on the guy; I never asked, she never shared and he did his job anyway. The operation took him less than a half hour. Prep, cut, then stitch it up to resemble the kind of scar countless wrestlers of this era have either on the front or back of their necks—the legacy of the high-impact style. I went straight from the hospital to the clinic. I haven’t had a drink since. “How long has it been then?” Almost two years. “I thought alcoholics had their sobriety counted out to the exact minute.” “The last few days were kind of a blur. One last hurrah, you know? Besides, all that matters now is that I never touch the stuff.” She nodded and took another forkful of the dessert you’d been sharing. They called it Brownie Overboard, and you figured a diabetic might lapse into a coma just by looking at it. She ate more of it than you did. Memories had hurt your appetite. “We’d better get going if we want to catch the movie,” you hear yourself say. She frowns as you stand, and the frown stays there as you hold the door open for her on the way out to the parking lot. How could she know? How could she know what had happened in your last relationship? How could she know how it ended? How could she know that you’d never fully trust another woman—another human being—ever again? ”I’m sorry, sir, but you still have an outstanding balance of…” “Look, I know, OK? I know what the balance is. It’s the same that it was when you called yesterday, and the day before that and the day before that.” “Well sir, if you’d pay your bill—” I hang up. Then I rip the cordless phone’s holder out of the wall and throw it against the floor, grinding it under my heel. I got out of rehab and she was gone. She resigned from OSW while I was laid up. Changed her phone number, moved out of her place and hadn’t left a forwarding address. How does a CEO disappear? She apparently knew how—and she’d been planning it. I should’ve known. She did such a good job bilking nickels and dimes to compensate me for my booking contributions. In the end, she took one big chunk of change—the one intended to set up my fake injury and the subsequent rehab—and used it to get the fuck out of Dodge. Meanwhile, I was left holding the bills, for me and my mom, who had fallen into bad health lately. No one wanted to trust endorsement deals to a wrestler recovering from aiddictions. The wrestling label was turn-off enough for a lot of companies. The combination was a killer. I wasn’t wrestling either, not after OSW finally shut its doors. Jane and I had done too good a job faking my injury. We’d had the doctor talk to another doctor with a less than sterling reputation—for money on the table that I thankfully didn’t owe (it’s hard to pay hush money in an installment plan)—and he drew up fake X-rays and documents indicating my deteriorating neck. It and the surgery, combined with my love of the booze and the pills, meant no wrestling company would touch me, not as an active competitor. I bounced into RPW and tSc briefly as a manager to try and make ends meet temporarily. But my mom’s health and my own efforts to start the wrestling school prevented me from devoting myself fully to either endeavor. Then one day I got a call from Amy. It turns out the Red Raver had some connections of her own—including getting the doctors on PRIME’s payroll to ignore some pre-existing health conditions. I was back. You stand in the mirror and stare at your reflection. Like you do on so many nights, you run your fingers across the scar running horizontal across your neck. One flick of a wrist by the surgeon, one incision by a scalpel. That was all it took to end your career as a wrestling superstar. It took almost two years and a lot of work by a good friend to bring you back. You wanted to thank Amy. You tried, in your own way, every single day. But you still couldn’t trust her. You loved her—in a way more truly than you ever loved Jane—but deep down you were waiting on the day she would betray you. But she’d given you a second chance, and allowed you back into PRIME under the radar. Now you had the biggest opportunity since your comeback, a shot at the Five-Star Championship. Brandon Youngblood, for all his skill and self-proclaimed prowess as a scout of the vulnerable points in men, had been duped like everyone else. He saw the scar and drew his own conclusions. Didn’t he wonder how you could bump like a crack monkey each and every week with seemingly no ill effects? The great technical wrestler made a tactical blunder once by underestimating you. It wouldn’t happen again. But he’d still work under the preconception that you were a piledriver away from a wheelchair. What would he do when he realized your neck was fine? What would he do when he realized you’d been living a lie like everyone else? He wouldn’t do anything… because your subconscious can do many things. It just can’t betray you to your enemies… no matter how much it tries to destroy you from within. The match is tomorrow. You need your sleep. Thoughts of a pair of redheads float through your dreams. They both broke your heart. You try to cling to the one that didn’t do it on purpose.
