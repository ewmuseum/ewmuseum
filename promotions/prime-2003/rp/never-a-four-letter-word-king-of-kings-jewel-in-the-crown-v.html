Date: Timeless “Never?” “Never.” “Not ever?” “What the fuck do you think never means?” ~><>…<><~ For many the mountaintop can only be spoken of as wistful metaphor, an idyllic sentiment uttered of a goal never reached, a victory to which they perpetually strive. For a choice few it is simply home. Some people feel they have a lot to prove. Others find they have nothing. Which group is better off depends entirely on whom you ask, on where they’ve been, what they’ve seen and accomplished. Those staring at empty trophy cases ache to fill them; those decorated like war veterans reflect on their moments of triumph, defining them as ultimate, as if without them their life would be a blank slate. Rare is the individual from either camp that recognizes the truly important while still clinging to the happy side of mortality. As this year rang in Angelo Deville was not among those divine chosen. He had occupied his home atop the peak for what seemed an eternity, reaching it on his first foray and looking back only to gloat. The sweetness swelled his chest like sails leading full winds, making him float wherever he went, billowing out as hot air whenever he found an audience. He was higher than any other and he embraced it like none before. The knowledge that he had nothing left to prove was his calling card, and he distributed it everywhere his lordly saunter took him. He was the interminable favourite, the sure bet, the money in the bank. Pundits proclaimed to all who would hear that he was unable to fall, incapable of stepping down. Until he retired, that is, vowing to never return. Trying to leap, to end it all in soaring style, to leave a horrific imprint on the universe so far below, he bellowed his final “Fuck you”, hoping to hear the echo as he plunged. But gravity flipped. Upside down, the mountain – the whole world – pierced his heart and crushed him. ~><>…<><~ (t3h d3v1ll3 appears on the screen inside a bank with lots of customers with moustaches and wearing hats. (Angelo goes back to his place in line and continues bitching about the weight.) ~><>…<><~ The Deville beckoned The Grin to rise, revelling in the confusion, the palm of his left hand – his slapping hand – stinging from the blow he’d just delivered. Around them thousands of voices were joined in disapproving howls, infuriated beyond their playful role by the abomination unravelling before them. Searing Gamble with his glare, no-selling the return of it, The Devil’s Don willed himself not to spit in that pale photocopy of his face. Over the years innumerable people had mimicked him, begging for his guidance, beseeching him to share his secrets, but none before had ever sought out plastic surgeons to be physically remade in his image. Intended flattery or not, it rubbed him every sort of wrong way. The only consolation was the hideous scar, a blemish Deville figured served him right. While the imitation irritated Angelo beyond forthright words, the ironic ignorance was so severe he was almost incapable of continuing the charade. Here was a man who had sought him out as an apparent student of his guile, unabashedly naming himself his heir, as if that was a way to success, as if The Deville had ever planned an inheritance other than pain. At first he thought Gamble’s intentions were as disingenuous as those of the artist formerly known as The HIT. As he stared into those beady eyes, however, he knew the deceit ran a more shallow course. Both intended from the very beginning to screw him over, but his late Disciple at least had an inkling of what he was getting into. The Grin had not a clue. Despite that, or perhaps on account of it, The Deville wanted to try a new angle. He wasn’t going to take the man under his wing, but neither would he sabotage him with lies. Instead he would tell him things he would already know if only he could put faith in his own mind and abilities, if only he could screw his head on right. Thrusting a finger into his swampy reflection, The Deville growled, “Never, under any circumstances, for anyone, lie down.” That was lesson number one, and in time Gamble would come to understand it. He thought he already did, but The Deville wasn’t talking about being a pushover in the squared circle. He was talking about life in general, about following your own path, trusting yourself above any and all others. In that, Gamble had already failed. ~><>…<><~ The smoke barely had time to roil before Nova’s stormy head thrust through, displacing it. He was pacing and chain-smoking, though the latter was merely an indication of his addiction. The former was the cause for concern, as uncharacteristic of The Rising Star as running a marathon for health benefits. His eyes were wide, almost panicked, flashing angrily. “We fucked up, man!” came his exasperated cry, and not for the first time this minute. Adrenaline still coursed through him, remaining from the beating they’d just delivered to Chet Worth, but it was the least significant cause of his trembling. “I’m never going to get my rematch now. You realize that, right?” Again The Deville paused in re-donning his suit to wave a dismissive hand, and again the gesture only enriched Nova’s anger. “We just sent the man in charge to the hospital!” Each word was bitten off with venom, reverberating sharply against the lockers. Their Fuck You comrades were gone, having hurried out as soon as possible to fill commitments beyond PRIME’s gate, leaving the once-unlikely duo alone. “We did,” The Deville agreed casually, nodding as he latched his belt. “Meaning next week someone else will be in charge.” Nova blinked. “That’s your plan?” A cutting rant raged on his tongue, but he managed to stifle it with a deep breath. “Dude,” he began again, obviously choosing his words with great care, “that’s one hell of a gamble. Even if Chet isn’t back, he’ll have put it down that I’m out of the title picture.” Smiling, Deville planted himself directly in front of his closest friend, gripping him by the shoulders and locking their eyes. “Damnit, Wyatt, relax. This isn’t you, and you should know that dropping the ball isn’t me. Whenever I gamble, rest assured the fix is in. I promise you, I have never and will never do anything to compromise your rematch, let alone your Universal victory and the ensuing reign.” A reassuring squeeze. “Never.” ~><>…<><~ Did you really think I’d stay retired? After all I’ve been through, all I’ve done, did you believe I could walk away for good? That the passion had suffered its final twitch and died? I did. With all my heart and soul I believed – I knew. When I left it was with the understanding that I’d accomplished all I needed to and more. There was no name more universally renown, no presence more sought after, respected, feared. I had absolutely nothing left to prove, and I rode off into the sunset so others could usurp my throne. It was time, it was necessary, and it was welcome. In fact, it was long overdue. Like a spirit liberated from mortal life, retirement – genuine retirement – freed me to look back over my career with an objective eye, to measure myself without the skewing shadow of a pending return, of future schemes and plans. It allowed me to look at my mistakes without petty justification, to accept them. It enabled me to embrace myself, not as some lordly legend, not as you wanted me to be, not as I wanted you to see me, but to truly discover and appreciate who and what I was. It enlightened me, revealing I’d descended into self-parody. And through that illumination I shed my constrictive skin, growing beyond. As I return – here, now – I understand the exact same things I did before. Only the knowledge is deeper, the reasoning richer and refined. I’m still sneering, shouting “Fuck you” at the top of my lungs, but the echo falls beneath my ears, your reactions beneath my anxiety. The sudden and persistent expectations had made me forget the mountain, the climb, the journey; I became my role, as defined by my impressions of your anticipation – and now it’s time to shatter that mould, to remind you how I stole into your consciousness in the first place. I myself have long since remembered. Once more I dance to my own macabre rhythm. I still hear your bleating tunes, your lamenting wails, but the meagre impression they make on my mind has no bearing on my footsteps. My path is my own and only I can walk it – only I know the direction. I heard all about the respect I’d lost, how I’d never be able to return and recover my glory, how I’d washed up dead on some distant shore. If you believe that, more power to you. Tell yourself it can never last forever, that I can never recapture the triumphant road, that I’ll never again have the view I once had. Tell yourself I’ve never won a number one contenders match, that along with clusters they’re my curse. Tell yourself I’m in the Jewel in the Crown to help elevate a successor, an heir, that I’ll never strive so high as to be crushed again. Believe whatever you will, whatever your heart desires . . . just remember that it once again affects me as much as it should. Remember that as far as I’m concerned, never means nothing.
