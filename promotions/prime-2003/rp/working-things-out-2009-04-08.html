Working Things Out

8 April 2009
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/8/09 "Every man must decide whether he will walk in the light of creative altruism or in the darkness of destructive selfishness." -Martin Luther King Jr. April 6th, 2009 6:13 P.M. "So, just so I’m all shiny on what you’re saying here, you’re big bad ‘total war on Snow’ by day, and fluffy mentor to troubled rugrats by night? That’s a hell of a double life, Tink." Ty thrust the medicine ball at me, and I caught it, covering up what would’ve otherwise been a pained groan with a loud grunt. Grunting, I’d found, was always an acceptable noise in a weight room. Any sound that could be taken as an admission of pain, though, and you started to get looks. "It’s pretty worthwhile stuff, Ty. These kids, ya know, they didn’t have some deus ex machina shit like you and me. Allan hooked it up with his friend, showed me a couple of their records. I mean, it breaks your heart, man." The man known to fans as the Underground Pimp had his back to me, and he ravaged a nearby punching bag. A fifteen-second flurry of rights and lefts concluded with a sterling uppercut that shook the base of the bag. A couple guys who thought they were the resident hardasses shot Ty a look the equivalent of which can only be expressed one way. Dayum. "A," he started, turning towards me and using his best matter-of-fact voice, "I dunno what you think you know about my childhood, but no French foofs came and plucked me out of Baja. B, didn’t that Allan North douche work for Your-Pal-Who-Must-Not-Be-Named? C." He shook his head with a hint of frustration. "Tink, there’s a lotta sob stories out there. Too many. The only way you fix other peoples’ problems is by puttin’ ‘em on your own shoulders, and I can tell you, as a casual observer of your life, that you don’t need any more goddamn problems." Rayne turned back to the bag with a spinning heel kick before he let of another round of punches. I tried to keep going with medicine ball hoists, but my back wouldn’t have it. I gave up the illusion of working out. "Fine, you had a horrifying childhood. Still, you make millions in a semi-legitimate business and people around the world know your name. Don’t tell me that you never caught a break before you became legal." Another frenzied set of punches delivered to the bag, Rayne turned back to me, sweat finally beading on his forehead. "You know that when you just sit there and watch me work out, it doesn’t do anything to quell all those homo rumors I spread about you, right?" I rolled my eyes, picking up a 35-pound free weight and starting on half-assed curls. "You didn’t answer my question. Someone stepped in and did something for you, otherwise, with that mouth of yours, somebody would’ve crippled you." "You didn’t answer my questions, so it doesn’t matter what was done for me. North, former close associate of a known douchebag, is known spoon-feeding you life lessons? And–" "It started out as a PR thing. But I really think I should do it. It’d be good for me to give something back." He shook his head again, turning back to the punching bag. Was he even paying attention? Rarely could I imagine the goings on in Ty’s head. There was something deep and dark in his psyche, something I barely understood. "What," he muttered between punches, "are you giving back? Sympathy ain’t a gift, Tink. It’s a fuckin’ crutch for people who can’t get up and get theirs." "These kids don’t even know how to walk on their own. Crutches are exactly what they need." Did I believe that or was I justifying out loud? Another round of punches absorbed by that big red cocoon, Ty didn’t turn around this time. He didn’t want me to see his face. "I’m sure these tykes have seen some shit cards dealt to them, but where do you fit into the equation, Tink? You’re a professional wrestler who drops F-bombs like they’re pennies and rarely, if ever, answers for his actions. Dr. Phil, you ain’t." "Doctor Phil’s an asshole." "Fine, that’s one thing you have in common. Touché, salesman." "Hardy har. Don’t you think that spontaneous acts of selfless good are worth anything?" I dropped the weights to the ground. The loud stinging clang caught more than a few glares from sweat-stained gym rats, and Rayne turned back to me, done with the punching bag for the day. "Tink, I do this whole back-and-forth thing with you because I’m your friend, and because I know your shrink dropped you like a bad habit. But it’s tired, man. If you really think your soul’s worth saving, how come you’re coming up with a new way to redeem yourself every two months? Christ, man, I’m starting to think you’re gettin’ all closet religious on me." "Sometimes…" I started, but thought about what Ty had said. It was true that, ever since Dr. DiPendo had refused to see me professionally, I sought profound meaning in everyday conversation. And since my most frequent everyday conversations were with Rayne, Dawkins, Allan, and my mother…each one played a role in my self-psychiatry. "…nah, fuck it." "Tink." Ty folded his arms across his chest with an irritated sigh. "Out with it, ya fuckin’ girl." "Well, I mean, you and I both, we’re closer to the end of our careers than the beginning. And what do ex-wrestlers do? The guys who were on juice make headlines when they die early, and everyone else immediately fizzles out. If I wanna use this gig to do any good in the world, it’s gotta be now." "We entertain people every week. That’s as much good as I was built to do, and I know that. In this world, either you’re a saint or you’re just a regular old guy. Difference between you and me, is that I know which one I am." "I don’t think I’m a saint. The whole reason I’m even interested in this is–" "Is what? So you can make up for some broad you hurt ten years ago? So you can repent for punching somebody who didn’t deserve it? You’re lookin’ for a clean slate, and it doesn’t exist, partner. Sooner you see that, the sooner you stop lookin’ for ‘get saved quick’ schemes." Damn the truth. Always blindsiding you like that. "I can’t decide right now." "Then why the fuck’re we talkin’ about this and not getting sauced up with strippers, like I originally suggested?" "I needed to break a sweat." More than that, I needed to hear myself think out the idea. Going to a juvenile detention center? It really wasn’t me. Didn’t make any sense. Ty was right about having enough problems that I didn’t need anyone else’s. And at the end of the day, Tsuperstar Enterprises didn’t need me for PR. But to every reason I listed off to myself, the reply was simple: it’s a good thing. And if it meant I could do one good thing, in a life of bad or (perhaps worse) inconsequential ones, that was enough. "Well, you watched me break one. That’s close enough. Time for the lovely ladies of the Amazon Club?" I didn’t know if one good thing would matter in a sea of bad choices, but the saint in me–or whatever it was, a conscience, maybe–would still have to split time with the sinner. "Why the fuck not? A cooch to the face always clears the head." "Doesn’t make a lick of sense, how that works. But I motherfuckin’ agree."
