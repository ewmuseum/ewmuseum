Schrödinger's Cat: Chapter 2

4 December 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: December 4th, 2007 His panting was rapid and labored. The sounds of footsteps were not that far behind. He knew that he had to concentrate and try to find a way out of that place or that would be his last breath. The rushed footsteps of his followers got ever closer to him. He was almost there; he could sense the end of the maze. The gun in his hand gleamed. It seemed to be calling to him to use it. The prospect of ending someone’s life was something that didn’t sound appealing to him. It was all he stood against. The survival instinct though, would do what it needed to help him survive that test. He dashed towards the corner of the hallway as the yells and ruckus of his followers were on his trail. The debris of the bullets hitting the cement walls made him block his face with his arm as he dived for cover. He fired back just to keep the men at a safe distance. Every time he hid back in the cover the wall gave him, he heard the shots ricocheting off the wall. He stood there for a second until they stopped shooting. For a few seconds, all he could hear was silence. The particular sound of reloading a gun made him jump into action. He ran from cover to cover quicker than one of his pursuers was able to reload. There were others shooting at him, but they couldn’t get him. He opened the door and collapsed outside. He had never felt as tired as he felt at that moment. There was a sudden and very sharp pain on his leg and he instinctively grabbed the spot with his hand. When he looked at his hand, he saw the blood. In no time, the two men who were following him came out the door, guns at the ready. “Well, what do we have here? It appears that this little thief got what was coming to him,” the guard said as he kicked the gun out of the man’s hand. “You thought you could escape this place without us catching you? You fool.” The guard kicked the man where the blood was flowing from the gunshot wound. Antonio was writhing in pain on the floor. He could see the guards’ sadistic smiles and he could sense the impending doom. The guns were raised and aimed at his head. Antonio closed his eyes and waited. The pain from the wound and the expectancy of death made him think back to his life. “Good bye.” The shot was loud. The second one wasn’t as loud as the first one, but as surprising. Two thuds followed the two shots. Antonio opened his eyes and saw the bodies of the two men lying on the ground besides him. “It took a lot out of not to just shoot you along with them,” Burton said as he picked Antonio up. Antonio had passed out from the pain. --- Burton sat on a chair going over some papers. The rustling of the papers made the man sleeping on the bed besides him move around until the pain from the gunshot wound woke him up. Antonio sat up on the bed with a grimace on his face. At first he was disoriented, but after a few seconds, he realized he was exactly where he didn’t want to be. “Good afternoon. I thought you were never going to get up. You get a little scratch and you are down for a day and a half. Pathetic,” Burton said without looking up at Antonio. Antonio tried to speak, but the only word he could come up with was an unintelligible croak. He coughed and tried again, this time being able to at least work around the dryness of his throat. “Did you… get… what you… needed?” he asked before catching a glimpse of a water bottle sitting in the night table besides him. He grabbed the bottle and drank a little bit. He also used some of the water to splash on his face. “At least you are good for something,” Burton said as he threw the papers on front of Antonio. Antonio looked at the file and saw the names of different people. The file labeled them as insurgents. “Who are these people?” Digital could recognize some of the faces from somewhere. Maybe he had known them in the other world, he couldn’t remember. For some reason the two universes meshed inside of his mind and he kept forgetting the people he knew. “They are part of the resistance,” Burton sighed. “You saw how this world looked when you went outside. Saying its post-apocalyptic is an understatement. You might say it’s still in its apocalyptic stage.” Burton looked through the blinds in the room to the outside. He liked to keep the blinds closed to keep some sense of hope and normalcy while inside his house. “So, what did you want with those names? Are you going to murder them?” Digital asked, fearing the response that would come. The lives of those people would end up in his conscience forever if he was an accessory to their deaths. “No,” Burton answered as he walked out of the room. Antonio looked around the room. It was dark and a sudden sense of gloom overtook him. On top of the door frame there was a plaque, he read it but he couldn’t understand it. It read ‘Os iusti meditabitur sapientiam, et lingua eius loquetur iudicium. Beatus vir qui suffert tentationem, quoniam cum probatus fuerit, accipiet coronam vitae. ’* For some reason, he felt much better than he had felt before. The pain in the leg seemed insignificant and the pain inside of him seemed to go away. --- Antonio was looking out through the window as Burton entered the room. The blinds were completely open and for the first time in years, the room was illuminated by the sun’s light. “What are you doing?” Burton asked. Antonio turned back towards Burton and shook his head. “Can’t a guy enjoy a little sunlight or am I to be in bed for the rest of my time here?” Antonio limped back to the bed. The pain from the wound was much less than it had been and walking was no longer a torture. Antonio’s mood changed somewhat with his captor’s presence. “What happened to those people? Did you hurt them?” he asked somberly. “Who do you mean?” “Don’t play the idiot with me. It doesn’t suit you,” Antonio responded. Burton sighed. “I don’t want to hurt them,” he said as he sat down. “The truth is that I am trying to rescue them. The resistance is trying to rescue them. They are my friends… they were your friends as well.” Antonio was more confused than ever. “What are you saying? What is really going on?” There was something inside Antonio that told him not to ask the questions he needed the answers to. He knew that nothing good would come from them. Nothing ever did. “The truth? Are you sure you want to hear it?” Burton asked. “Yes.” “Very well. The truth is that in this world resources are scarce. Food is almost nonexistent, except for the wealthy. We do have some patches of land that give us a few of the necessary elements to survive but it’s so sporadic that half the time, we have no idea if our harvests will work,” he put a pack of pictures in front of Antonio. “The truth is that the Antonio of this world was a friend of mine, a friend of all of us. What I told you before was a lie,” Burton admitted. “Everything?” Antonio asked as he saw the pictures of his old friends. The group, the resistance. “No. Not everything. I did kill you. I killed you because you betrayed me… you betrayed all of us. You were our leader, a man that everyone feared. That was until they got to you. You were tired of losing the people you loved and you thought that striking a deal would be the best thing to do,” Burton laughed halfheartedly. “I was never a history guy, but Michael Collins would have been proud, except that you got greedy… twenty of us were murdered because of what you did.” Antonio was out of air. He couldn’t believe what he was hearing. He knew that it really wasn’t him, that person that Burton kept describing. That person was the farthest from what Antonio really was, but that didn’t help him hide the questions that were splitting his head in half. Could he really be that weak? Could he? Most of the times when we think we know ourselves is when we least do. “I… I don’t know what to say,” Antonio said as he hung his head in defeat. “That wasn’t you, I know this. I had to make sure though. That is why I lied to you at first and why I sent you on that mission. Believe it or not, I am not fit to lead this resistance. I have come to terms with that. We need you and this time, I have a feeling that it will not end in the same way,” he said as he gave Antonio another file. “This is the date and the route the prisoner convoy will take and this one will be the supplies convoy. We need both, but we don’t have the man power.” Antonio could see and hear Burton’s fatalistic attitude and he didn’t blame him. This was a world torn by despair and conflict and it was understandable that Burton would feel the way that he does. Antonio had felt like that many times before. He realized at that moment that he had something to prove. Not to Burton or to anyone else… but himself. He needed to know that he wasn’t that person that would knowingly cause the death of his friends. “I will help you,” Antonio said as he extended his hand to Burton. Burton shook it. Nothing had felt right for a long time except for that handshake. “Are you alright to walk? I want you to meet the rest of the guys,” Burton said as he opened the door. “Sure, I think I should start putting more pressure on the leg to have it ready. Where are they?” When he went out the door, there was a collection of fifteen ragged looking men and women. Antonio could see in their eyes the determination and that certain look that only people who have suffered great losses have. He recognized some of the faces, but he just wasn’t sure. He shook hands with all of them. Some of them smiled at him as if they knew each other for a long time and others couldn’t look at him in the eyes. Antonio imagined that because of him they must have lost someone they loved. He had to set that right. “One question Burton. I was wondering… what does that sign on top of the door frame mean? I felt good for some reason when I read it.” “’The mouth of the just shall meditate wisdom, and his tongue shall speak judgment. Blessed is the man who endures temptation, for when he has been tried, he shall receive the crown of life.’” *-“Lilium” by Kumiko Noma based on biblical passages and the hymn Ave mundi spes Maria (“Hail Mary, Hope of the World”)
