Anger

3 July 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 07-03-2007 “Talk,” the man says. Across the way sits another man with dark brown eyes. He looks back at the man across from him as he can feel his throat tighten around him. He tries to swallow, but is unable to do so. His throat is so dry that it is painful for him to do so. He tries again with the same result. He doesn’t like the situation he has found himself in. In the back of his mind, he was certain that the man sitting across from him would kill him without reason. Yet, he had accepted the meeting thinking that fate would be on his side. Fate though didn’t exist in this world. His world. He looks into his eyes and finds his very soul wishing to escape. The eyes were cold and lifeless. Yet, they burned bright. Oh, how they burned so bright; like a fire dancing in a dark, empty night. “You must not speak English,” the man continues, his impatience starting to show with each passing second. “When I say talk, I mean for you to speak. Not to be a fucking mute. If I wanted a fucking mute, I’d speak to the rest of your fucking country of radical fuckheads. You, on the other hand, I want you to speak. So, get the fears of me killing you out of your head because my decision on that has already been made. The only thing left for you to do is to tell me what I want to know, and pray that the decision I made was to spare your life,” the man finishes speaking, his eyes never wavering, never moving an inch. CLICK! The man jumps back in fear, knowing that he won’t even get to speak his final words. He waits for the bullet to explode from the barrel of the gun and pierce his skull before taking a chunk of his brain with it as it exploded out the back of his head. Yet, there’s nothing. No gunshot. No piercing. No exploding. No missing pieces of his brain. He looks at the man, his red eyes still bearing down upon him, and then he sees another fire; one that isn’t from the man’s eyes, but mysteriously appears in the palm of his hand. He sits there, scared of the man sitting across from him. Then, within a moment’s notice, the fire disappears before appearing once again, preceded by another click. “Okay, I’ll talk,” he finally yells out, the fear no longer able to remain below the surface. The man sitting across from him smiles even though he can’t see it. Then, the fire extinguishes itself again, leaving only the red eyes of the man sitting across from him. “Four days ago, I received a phone call. One that told me to meet a man, that I only know as Mushka, across the street from the local grocery market. I was trained from a young age to do as you’re told, and it has continued in life with my place in the army. So, I went where I was told and waited. Hours went by and I was ready to go back home when a voice came to me. I never saw a face. Just a voice. The voice told me to give the pictures to my cousin and he would know what to do with them. He told me where I could find the pictures and I gave them to my cousin who seemingly understood what it was he was supposed to do. I never knew the pictures were…,” he tells the man before his voice finally trails off, frightened to say the words that were coming next. “You had no idea that the pictures were of my daughter is what I believe you were trying to say,” he responds. Once again, silence in the room. “So, now I have told you everything you need to kn--,” he begins to say before his words are cut off. Yet, he wasn’t interrupted by the man wanting to say something to him, but instead from the inability to talk any longer. Instead, he felt the pain as it slid across his neck. The perforated edge of the blade sliced through his skin, severing several arteries, as if it was a slice of ham. He can fool the man’s hand around his throat now as his blood slowly trickles out of his body. It is only now that he understands who it is that he is going up against. Not a man, but a man who has lost all will to live. Slowly, he can feel the darkness seeping into him before his body collides to the ground. The sickening thud of his body collapsing against the unforgiving concrete even hurts him to hear. Unfortunately, it’s the last thought he could possibly have as his heart gives out on and him and takes him away from the world. Above him, a knife is being polished. The blood from the blade then drops to the ground. A light flickers on and as it does, the face of the man polishing the blade is revealed. His red eyes give it all away as he looks at his fallen prey. Then, a knock is heard on the door and the man looks towards it, wondering who it could possibly be. “Who is it?” the man asks, dementia starting to set in. “Craig, it’s me. We’ve got a lead,” the man outside the door responds. Craig looks at the door, his eyes beginning to narrow. “On whom?” he asks back, feeling the exhaustion kick in. “Brady,” the man on the other side asks and then the exhaustion Craig had started to feel vanished. Instead, it was replaced once again with anger. ---- Hours pass. Craig doesn’t sleep though as he waits, knowing that he is far from being done on his mission. A bottle of water sits in front of him, untouched. No longer does he care about himself. He hasn’t taken care of himself in days. The hair on his face had started to grow in and at this point had covered his entire jaw. For him, it was a completely different look than the one he generally had. Yet, he frankly couldn’t be bothered. He had other things to focus on. The door leading to the room opens slowly and in walks a bald headed man who looked like he was all business. “You clean it up?” Craig asks, referring to the body in the room adjacent to this one. The man, not one for many words, just nods his head and Craig responds with silence. The days hadn’t been kind to Craig or the ones who were against him. Inside of his mind he counted the number of people he had put six feet under and realized that this was number twelve. Yet, he was certain that he would not be the last he put there. There were still plenty of people to come that would feel the fury that he was feeling inside of his soul. Word had started to get back to Santiago and that made him smile. He wanted to make sure that Santiago understood what exactly was going on, leaving no questions as to his intentions. He was going to strike the fear of God into his heart and then when he least expected it, Craig was going to rip his heart out. Then, another door opens and in walks another bald headed man. Craig was certain that all of these men were coming from Bald’R’Us, but he couldn’t find the energy to care about it. The man sits down in a steel chair directly across from Craig and drops a folder onto the unforgiving wooden table. Craig slowly takes the folder and starts to read it. “He’s on the move through China? What the hell is he doing there?” Craig asks out loud, not expecting anyone to answer him. He wanted, no, needed to meet the man who had shot him in the abdomen back in May and put him out of commission. If it hadn’t been for the fact that he had been shot, Craig would’ve been there with his daughter. She wouldn’t have had to die for his sins. Oddly enough though, Craig hadn’t been able to find any other information on Brady. He was certain that Brady wasn’t some sort of black ops agent, but just your average everyday person. So, the thought that crossed Craig’s mind wasn’t how is Brady doing this, but more of why is Santiago trying to hide Brady from the world. He had heard that Brady and Santiago were far from friends or even partners, thus what was the reasoning behind this cover up from Santiago. “Keep an eye on him,” Craig says before getting out of his chair. The man across from him nods his head. “Twenty-four hours, I want to be sitting across from him, looking into his eyes and finding out just what he knew about it all. About me, my daughter, everything. Get going,” Craig tells the man who immediately gets up out of the chair and walks away without so much as a word. This was how Craig enjoyed doing his business. He feels the concrete underneath his feet before opening a door that leads him out of the abandoned house he had been staying in for the past few days as he put his final plan together. Slowly, it was starting to fall in place and he could see the end in sight. As he felt the cold air hit his lungs he thought about his daughter. It ate at him to know that she was dead for his sins. He had done things in life that not even he could explain and she was the one that had to pay for them. It was unfair he thought because he was the one who should be paying for them. Santiago though had decided to play God and remove her from the world. Craig was intent on showing him what it truly meant to be a God. He knew when word got back to Santiago about Brady, Santiago would know that his powers were starting to diminish. All along, it was about finding out how to strike the fear of God in a man who thought he was God. Craig was about to find out how easy it was. ---- Brady kept his head down as he weaved through the traffic, wanting to make sure that he didn’t make eye contact with anyone. As he walked into the dank hotel he felt a shiver down his spine. In his right hand was a paper bag. It’s contents was pure and simple; a bottle of alcohol. As he raced towards his room, the previous bottle of alcohol starting to wear thin, he was being consumed by the thoughts that he so desperately kept trying to push away. The feeling of the cold steel gun in his hand, the echo of the gunshot as it rang throughout the arena, and the very sight of blood dripping out of Craig’s abdomen as he collapsed to the ground, shocked by it all. He reaches his door and starts to fumble with the lock on it, his nerves starting to get the best of him. Finally though, he is able to open the door and steps in, feeling as if he was finally safe. He was far from it. “Hello, Brady,” the voice whispers into his ear. A chill is sent down Brady’s spine as he instantly knows whose voice that is. The bottle of alcohol in his hand slips from his fingers as the fear terrorizes and paralyzes him. Craig’s hand reaches around and covers his mouth while his other arm wraps around Brady’s throat as he drags him across the room. Brady feels hopeless. He knows that any minute now he is no longer to be breathing, that he will be dead. Then, his body is thrown into a chair and a light comes on. As his eyes adjust to the suddenness of the light filling the room he can see a faint outline of Craig’s body. Craig casually sits down across from Brady, a smile on his face. “Brady, did you know I was coming for you? Did you fear that I was going to find you eventually?” Craig asks. Finally, Brady’s eyes adjust to the light and he can see Craig, yet it’s not the same Craig he thought he would see. The beard on his face has changed the man entirely. He looked nothing like the man he had shot, the man he had once idolized, the man he became obsessed with. It was then that he saw the damage he had done to Craig. Shooting him had lead to him losing his daughter. He knew that much. He just never realized that when that all happened, that he would crack into a million pieces and slip away from the reality that he had created for himself. “Of course you didn’t,” Craig answers for Brady who still hasn’t found the courage to respond quite yet. “You couldn’t have. You thought you were safe. You thought that Santiago could save you forever, and unfortunately, that was not the case. You should’ve known all along Brady, that I would come for you. Yet, you didn’t which interests me greatly. But, that doesn’t even interest me the most. The thing that interests me the most as I look at you is the fact that outside of tonight and the night you shot me, I have never seen your face before Brady. You never existed to me until you interjected myself into my life and shot me. So, the question I have to ask is this Brady. Why? Why did you shoot me? Was it Santiago? Was it someone else?” he asks Brady and then silence fills the room. He waits, but never gets an answer. Instead, he remains silent and resolute. Craig gets out of the chair and walks over to Brady before kneeling right in front of his face. “You will talk. You will answer all of my questions tonight, and you can get any idea out of your head that you’re walking out of here alive tonight because you’re going to suffer an unimaginable pain when I’m done with you. You can count on that,” he whispers, his tone very glottal and then he pulls out a small knife in a flash of an eye before he puts four equally long cuts along Brady’s face. The blade stings as it slashes his skin and he can feel the drops of blood dripping out of the cuts. Then, Craig walks away and heads towards the door where he kneels down to pick something up. The bottle of alcohol, Absolut Vodka. He then walks back towards Brady and opens the bottle of alcohol in mere seconds. He looks at Brady who can only wonder what is going through Craig’s demented mind. He then starts to pour the alcohol on Brady’s face who begins to yell from the pain. However, the scream is halted as Craig puts his hand over Brady’s mouth. “No, you don’t get to scream. You don’t get to yell. You don’t get to show your pain. Instead, you get to suffer in silence. Just like my daughter did,” he tells Brady who desperately tries to scream, his body needing for it to scream. Yet, without the ability to do so the pain worsens. His face feels like it is on fire as Craig takes a chug from the bottle of alcohol that had just brought him so much pain. Craig doesn’t make any attempt to wipe off the alcohol, instead sitting down once again across from Brady and watching the man make several contorted faces from the pain. “Now, Brady, answer my questions. Why did you shoot me?” he asks him and Brady looks up at him, the pain taking away from his fear and is ready to answer him. “Because you took my family away from me,” he answers in a shortness that irritates Craig to no end. Craig immediately gets up out of chair and slams his elbow into Brady’s face. Blood erupts from his nose as Craig sits right back down in his chair. “You don’t get to be short with me. You don’t get to be cute either. You shot me and because of that, my daughter is dead. Do you understand that? If my daughter had been alive, I could care less about you. I’d let you wave in the wind like a flag until I decided to visit you when you least expected it. However, seeing as how mu daughter is dead and all, I’m not quite in the best of shapes. Do you really want to piss me off?” Craig asks him and Brady only looks back at Craig before responding. “Does it matter? You’re going to kill me anyways,” he says. “Very true, I am, but I could make it quick Brady. You might think I enjoy torturing you, and while that is true to a degree, for the most part I just want answers. To me, you’re a pawn in the game that Santiago is playing with me, and it’s one that I don’t quite understand yet. You, you’re a means to an end. Your death is going to send a message to Santiago, and that message is going to be Checkmate because if I can get to you, after he so eagerly tried to hide you from me, then he knows I can get after him no matter how dark he’s gone. So, tell me, what do you mean by the fact that I took you from your family?” Craig asks of him once again. Once again, there is silence as Brady chooses his words carefully. In his mind, he decides that it’s time to just give the answers. To himself, he’s worth nothing in this world and he knows it. He wants to end the pain and suffering. “I was a gambler. I liked to take chances and my wife hated it. The short sweet answer is this. I betted on you, week after week, and week after week I kept losing. One week I’d bet against you, and I’d lose. Another week, I’d bet for you and I’d lose. Eventually, I lost my family forever. My wife walked out with my daughter and they were gone from my life just like that. It tore me up,” he begins to say before Craig just shakes his head, cutting Brady off. “You shot me over that? You tried to kill me because you couldn’t handle your business? That’s just sad and pathetic,” he tells Brady who in return shakes his head. “You don’t understand, do you? I didn’t try to kill you because you couldn’t handle your business. You don’t understand how you affect people’s lives, do you? You’re like a black hole. You suck people into you, causing these problems for yourself. I never wanted to kill you. Well, I did, but I was not going to go to any end to do it. I blamed myself. Santiago gave me a way out. You see, that guy hated you. With a passion. You had screwed him up and something tells me that he’s not the only one that you’ve fucked with, regardless if you know it or not. Even if you’re unaware of what you’re doing, it doesn’t make it right. You take from so many people that you don’t even realize it. It’s just the way you are, it’s who you are,” he tells Craig who sits there, trying to digest it all. It was almost as if it was an epiphany. He knew that he wasn’t completely innocent with Santiago. Craig was far from an innocent bystander. There was a history there with Santiago, one that he didn’t even fully understand. “You take from people and they want revenge. It’s human instinct. You, you’re going through it now. Even if you didn’t know it, you’re responsible for the fact that I lost my family. I bet on you, sure that’s my fault, but the constant variable was you. You couldn’t handle your business and thus I lost my family because of it. You did whatever you did to Santiago, knowingly or unknowingly, and he took away your daughter. Ask yourself, Craig, how many other people have you hurt? It would seem to me that your debt is far from being paid,” Brady finishes, knowing that his death is next. Craig gets up out of the chair and begins to walk towards the door. He’s sick to his stomach and can’t be here any longer. He feels the world spinning around him. He had to come to terms with his dark and twisted past, a past he couldn’t begin to understand, a past he hadn’t completely pieced together. There were still black areas when it came to his memories, but the words from Brady’s mouth made sense. It sickened him. As his hand reached the door knob, he turns towards Brady and realizes he is still tied up. Craig slowly walks over to him and cuts away the ropes that bind him. He helps Brady up who is surprised by all of this. “You’re going to let me go?” he asks Craig, wondering why he would be doing this. He looks into Craig’s eyes and the fire that is still there scares him to death. “No,” Craig says before taking the knife in his hands and jamming it straight through his throat. The look in Brady’s eyes is deafening to him. He had thought he would get a second chance in life, but Craig couldn’t give that to him. People still had to pay. People still had to suffer. “You’ll get to repair the damage to your family one day, but not at the cost of my daughter’s life,” Craig tells Brady before walking away. As he walks out of the room, he drops to his knees and throws up right there. The fire in his eyes dies away as he can barely live with himself anymore. The world was starting to become clear to him. Crystal clear. He could no longer exist in it. Soon, he wouldn't. To Be Continued.
