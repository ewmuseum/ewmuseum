Howard Harrington

Date: 5/7/09 ““Owing to the fact that all experience is a process, no point of view can ever be the last one.” -William James May 4th, 2009 3:13 P.M. The Dike is a horrible fuckin’ place. The realization is a chilly one, evident as soon as you walk through those Gothic gates at the front. I wasn’t too familiar with it yet, but I knew it was unforgettable. The lack of bars was deceiving, with double-reinforced doors instead. It was a new age prison, meaning that it couldn’t look like some kinda medieval dungeon. No, it was far more humane to vacuum-seal everyone in. It looked better on the news not to have 12-year-olds in cages. I was nervous for our meeting, and sweating it harder than I’d imagined. Dixon was on my mind. The way Barius had thrown him into solitary for \"acting up\" was definitely a way of showing his superiority. It hadn’t taken me long to realize that the guy was a lunatic. Just because you dress up a psycho in business casual and throw a couple academic degrees on the wall doesn’t change who he is. And Barius was a man obsessed with control, a wolf in sheep’s clothing. Yeah, that was a good image for him. Did I belong here? It was a question I’d asked dozens of times, but now, with the damp chill of the visitors’ area on my arms, it was the only thing on my mind. This was the place where you saw outsiders, people who came here voluntarily. The \"residents\" (Barius’s terminology was as cruel as it was condescending), the guards, the shrinks. Whether it was by mandate of the state of California or by mandate of having to keep your job, everyone showed up because someone told them to. Except for the visitors. It was a strange aesthetic, the outsiders wearing their see-through smiles and pretending not to be terrified. I was glad not to be some family member, roped into visiting a son who was undoubtedly the blemish of the family. I’d felt that way once, like my existence was just to make things tougher for my mother. That frequently crossed my mind these days. I was here, above all else, to try and even out all my wrongs. I sat the table, waiting impatiently. Barius had insisted I be on time, and being one of those \"you’re late if you’re on time, you’re on time if you’re five minutes early\" pricks, I was stuck waiting. I’d never been able to stand waiting, and the visitors’ area was designed to maximize the unease of waiting. Sitting alone at one of those tables, with nothing to do but think, was eerie. Some of the people around me cried. Hell, one or two of the inmates cried. It was still a strange thing to me, locking up cubs. It defied biological imperatives, strained mothers’ faces. Finally, led by the guard named Victor, he appeared. It’s a slow, awkward walk to these tables, especially when you’re in the blind date situation we were in. He didn’t know me, I didn’t know him. Do you wave? Do you give the head nod? Do you look down at your feet until you’re literally right at the table? Neither of us seemed to know. But once he sat down across from me, I felt some of the nervousness subside. I tried not to give too much importance to it. Despite how I’d built this up, we were just two guys shooting the shit. Mister Tsonda started to talk. I listened. May 1st, 2009 2:48 P.M. \"You got any kids, Allan?\" I was back at Tsuperstar Enterprises HQ to sign the last of the tax forms. At least that was the guise. I was due for another trip to the Dike on Monday, three days from now. And that was a terrifying premise, given the epic fail of my first trip there. Not because I was afraid of getting yelled at by a faux macho teenybopper, but because my vision of doing a good thing had been as wrong as anything can be. I wasn’t ready to help these confusing, insecure things. So I was stuck, desperately fishing for advice from those around me. Allan glanced up from his desk, puzzled look sprawled across his face. Raised eyebrows had already answered my question. \"Chandler, I know it went badly but you can’t really be coming in here looking for my child-rearing advice.\" \"The fuck do you talk about with Miscreant Junior?\" Meeting new people, plunging into trust, was not something I did easily. Throw in my well-documented aversion to kids, and I now seemed comically ill-suited to the task in front of me. But I was going to do something, anything, to make life under that rotten motherfucker, Tom Barius, more bearable. \"You could ask them why they’re in there.\" \"Nice uplifting subject matter, sure. Besides, they’ve got this bookworm asshole shrink tyrant that runs the place. I’m sure he makes ‘em talk about that.\" I paused, unsure of whether I was guilty of prying. \"You didn’t answer my first question.\" Allan was business first, and I could respect that. But right now, I needed more than him playing matador with my questions, sidestep after sidestep. \"Yeah, I got a kid, Chandler. What difference does it make, if he’s not in the Little Leagues of Criminal Endeavor?\" He was touchy about something. Allan certainly seemed like the divorce type. Maybe that was it. \"I dunno. I’m literally starting from scratch with this, and since you proposed the idea as good PR, you owe it to me to give me something.\" \"I missed the part of my employee contract that gives me dual function as legal advisor and an obligation to tell you anything you can pry out of me.\" So, I was prying. But whenever someone calls you out for prying, it’s something that, secretly, they want to talk about with someone. But they know they can’t look like they want to talk about it, especially not when they wear a totally-put-together face at work like Allan did. \"Telling me what you know about kids doesn’t require a tell-all memoir about your dirty little secrets. But if you don’t wanna talk about it, fine. We can sit here quietly, sign these papers, and make small talk about the playoffs if you want.\" He sighed, loosening the neck of his tie and unbuttoning the first button of his shirt. \"The only dirty secret in my life isn’t a secret at all. My brother has the most famous penis in the world.\" It was a hilarious juxtaposition. Allan, the quintessential businessman’s businessman. Peter, the most famous male porn star of all-time (go wither away and die, Ron Jeremy). \"What I have are private little secrets that aren’t your business or anybody else’s.\" True, but if Allan truly wasn’t ready to talk about these secrets, he would’ve have kept talking about them. \"Fair ‘nuff.\" I paused, giving Allan time to stew in his own thoughts. The pause was important. \"You see LeBron?\" \"I mean, it’s not like there’s some secret book they give parents about raising kids. Soccer moms are just as clueless about raising kids as deadbeat dads. You get insight, not knowledge.\" Bingo. Allan was rambling about whatever kid problem he had. Now, maybe you’re thinking that it was manipulative of me to wring it out of him, but like I said, people want to talk about these things. You just have to give them the right stage. \"I’ve got a daughter,\" he continued. \"She’s eleven. But I’ll be Nicholson in The Shining if it’s not the most confusing eleven years of my life. My wife says it’s all normal. How getting a hard-on for a guys who look like porcelain dolls is normal…I’ll never know. Really? Zac Efron?\" Too bad. It wasn’t helpful information, but dammit if it wasn’t quietly entertaining. Allan North was never flustered, but here he was, gesturing wildly and losing his cucumber-cool tone. \"Guess that’s not exactly the type of insight I need to figure out my clink all-stars.\" \"It’s not hard to read kids, Chandler. It’s just hard to understand them.\" \"How hard was that, Allan? Huh? Ya two-stepped around it, tried not to, and still ended up giving damn good advice. Feel that warm sensation in your chest? It’s not a coronary, homie. That’s the love in the air.\" \"I mean,\" he muttered to himself, \"he looks like he’s performing in Kabuki Theater or something. Is that makeup? Does he have some kind of facial disease?\" Read them. Don’t try to understand them. That was better than nothing. May 4th, 2009 3:16 P.M. \"Howard Harrington, right?\" \"Yeah, that’s me.\" \"Nice to meet you,\" he said, playing the cool customer \"I’m Chandler Tsonda.\" \"Yeah,\" I replied, \"I’ve heard of you. After Dix’s blow-up, there was a rumor going around that you were volunteering here. No one believed him, though. He’s got a real big mouth.\" I paused, finally feeling the weight of talking to a full-blown celebrity. \"But you already know that.\" \"Dixon’s…\" He scratched his chin diplomatically, not wanting to agree with me. It was true. Dixon was a loudmouth to make up for the fact that this place had stripped him of his balls. You come in for smacking your mom’s boyfriend around, and this place tells you you were wrong for it. Barius tells you that you were wrong for it. And since I didn’t know the whole story, I didn’t care to judge. But Dixon was a loudmouth. \"…he’s a good kid. Just frustrated.\" \"Hard not to be frustrated in here. It’s getting nice outside. About to be summer vacation.\" \"You still in school?\" \"Missed this year,\" I confessed, \"but I’ll be going back for senior year starting in September.\" \"Just here for some R&R?\" He grinned. I don’t know if it was intended to cheer me up or just because he thought it was funny. \"Only three months.\" Twelve weeks. Eighty-four days. Two thousand and sixteen hours. One hundred twenty thousand, nine hundred and sixty seconds. It hadn’t been a day before I’d given in and done the quick calculations. One of the other guys–one of the only inmates not interested in immediate intimidation or juvenile hazing–had told me that the quickest way to get out was to pass the time. That’s all I’d been doing. \"With good behavior, of course.\" Barius had given me the \"privilege\" of meeting with Tsonda for so-called good behavior, another privilege of which was watching five hours of relatively private television a week in a small room with other well-behaved inmates. Two of those hours were PRIME. It wasn’t that I still believed in the stuff, the heroes and monsters dichotomy, but it was comfort TV. The same thing on the inside that it had been on the outside. I’d only been here three weeks, but strings like this, delicate connections to the outside, were important. Good behavior, as much as I hated Barius, was the only way I’d get back out and finish school. \"You seem pretty put together.\" He pursed his lips, looking confused. He didn’t look quite so foofy in real life, although the gelled hair was suspect. At least he wasn’t wearing Italian shoes or something. \"And put together kids aren’t supposed to end up in the California penal system.\" \"Not in my opinion.\" \"No, I don’t have some sob story about innocence. I think…I’m supposed to be here.\" \"You think?\" The goofy grin was gone. He looked as sober as my lawyer had when I’d explained that I wanted to plead guilty. \"I mean, I was guilty. The judge could’ve waived any time inside, but she said a short run would insure I never did anything like that again.\" \"And…\" He rubbed his forehead with his thumb and forefinger, evidently stressed out on my behalf. \"…what was it that you were guilty of?\" \"I was the worst Best Buy employee of all-time.\" \"Kid, I hate to bring this late breaking news to you, but I’m pretty sure that’s not a crime. Who arrested you, the Better Business Bureau?\" This was the wise-cracking Tsonda I was used to from TV. When confused, he rebounded with a silver tongue and a self-assured aura. It was why people rooted for him in PRIME, and it was actually hard to argue with in person. \"Last Christmas, there were a lot of people coming in with that worried look on their face. When you work at Best Buy, you recognize the face. ‘I’m gonna buy this TV, but I don’t have a clue how I’m going to pay for it.’ I figured that Best Buy didn’t need the money as much as these people, so I started selling TV’s for the customer discount. For three days, I sold every TV for three-quarters of what it was worth.\" \"A regular Robin Hood of consumer electronics. Nice move, Howie.\" I suppressed a smile. It was always nice to hear that people approved of my crimes, especially someone famous. \"Yeah, well, when the manager found out after looking at the Christmas receipts, he went berserk. I thought the worst of it was when he fired me. But then some corporate lawyers came to my house and told me I had to pay back what I owed or they’d sue my family.\" \"Assholes.\" \"No kidding.\" \"So you gave those one of those suits a black eye and ended up in here?\" \"Nope. I told my parents what happened. And they said that what I did was the right thing to do…but that they didn’t have the money to spend on lawyer fees if our family got sued. I wasn’t eighteen yet. We’re talking almost ten thousand bucks here.\" \"Jesus, kid!\" Maybe it wasn’t a character on TV. He seemed like the exact same guy I’d watched for years. Simple: if he liked you, he was likeable. But if he didn’t immediately take a liking to you, if he wasn’t obligated to like you because you were a pathetic inmate, you were probably going to catch hell. \"You got some kind of a martyr complex to ya.\" \"Isn’t that what Lisa Tyler said to you last week?\" \"Let’s not worry about what Vice President Midol had to say last week. Keep going.\" \"Is she hot in real life?\" It was a question that I couldn’t quite ever figure out. I’d never get a better answer than right then. \"They say the devil comes in the form of a babe. And I haven’t checked if her eyes are green, but if that’s true, then she’s the frontrunner for the position.\" \"Nice.\" \"What about Lindsay Troy?\" \"You got a lotta questions, kid.\" I paused. I didn’t know if that was a bad thing, if he thought that I was prying. \"I didn’t mean to–\" \"I guess she’s good looking, if you’re into that.\" \"Awesome.\" \"So…finish the story,\" he urged, like an impatient child. \"You told your folks. What then?\" \"I turned myself in to the police. I told them what I’d done. They were ready to throw away the case, but then the douchebag lawyers from Best Buy asked some Congressman to make sure I got punished. They called me a thief of the worst kind for stealing from them during the recession. Said I was a traitor. Banned me from Best Buy for life.\" \"Greedy motherfuckers.\" \"Right? So yeah, the trial was quick. I pled guilty. The judge said she realized my good intentions, but couldn’t ignore the crime itself. Sentenced me to three months with Barius in the D. The judge told me that Barius’s ‘experience with trouble teens’ would help me turn around.\" \"Raw deal, kid.\" The corners of his mouth twisted up. Being in here even made the great Chandler Tsonda frustrated. It was contagious. \"Twenty three days down. Sixty one to go.\" I didn’t want to question this. I didn’t want to stop Tsonda from coming to the D. But the question was eating at me. \"So…what are you doing here?\" \"Wish I knew, Howie. I tell my business people that I’m doing it for the PR. I tell my mom that I’m doing it to save my soul or something. I tell Barius that I’m doing it for community service. And…I tell you that I have no idea why I’m doing it.\" \"Maybe you’re just stealing from Best Buy.\" He looked at me like I had nine heads. \"Ya know, there’s just some stuff that’s not fair out there. And you’re doing your part to make that unfairness suck a little less.\" Neither of us spoke for a moment. \"That’s…that’s a damn good point, kid. Say, what would a guy do if he wanted Barius to let him inside the cell block or whatever they call it. I feel like I’d be more help if I could talk to a group. Not that this isn’t a hoot, but ya know, not everybody is in here for some kinda steal-from-the-rich shit.\" \"You don’t wanna waste your time with genpoppers in here. Fifteen-year-old junkies, guys who can’t wait to turn eighteen so they can go federal. Some of it even depresses an eighteen-year-old, and I don’t even live in the real world.\" \"I should at least see for myself. Can’t let Barius decide what I get to do around here. Where is Mein Fuhrer anyway?\" I laughed for a couple seconds. It felt unfamiliar, but good. \"One of the genpoppers wrote a death threat or something. Barius was running around trying to figure it all out.\" \"Yeah, well, if that prick hadn’t been around last week, Dixon wouldn’t’ve flipped out like he did.\" \"You don’t know Dixon. That’s all he does.\" \"You ever talk to him?\" \"Only on pain of death.\" He looked slightly disappointed, as if he wanted to know more about Dixon, but no more questions came about the troubled Dominican. \"Say, Howie, I can’t stay long today. I’ve gotta go play nice with a doctor so he lets me wrestle this week. You think you might wanna chat again sometime? I’ll bring you some autographs and shit if I can.\" \"Yeah, sure, Mister Tsonda. But I don’t need any autographs or anything.\" I tried not to sound weak. \"Probably just get me black-and-blue’d anyway.\" \"Mmm.\" He looked back, that lame sympathetic look on his face. \"Maybe I have something that can help with that. I’ll drop it by next time.\" \"Cool.\" He got up, checking the time on the famous BlackBerry. It was still pretty surreal. \"Say, kid…\" He paused, putting a finger to his mouth. \"…thanks for the chat.\" \"Yeah,\" I said, unsure of whether I could say ‘you’re welcome’ to the celebrity in front of me. He didn’t give me time, and when I saw that he was going to shake my hand, I panicked. \"Thanks,\" I said, mimicking him. We shook hands, he nodded, and then he was gone. It was back to life in the D. Life under Barius. But even though Tsonda lived in a different world, of flashbulbs and limousines and championship belts, I didn’t mind deluding myself that we weren’t all that different, he and I. Just two guys, sitting at a table, shooting the shit.
