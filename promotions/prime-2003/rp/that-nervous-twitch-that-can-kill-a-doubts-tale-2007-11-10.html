That nervous twitch that can kill, a doubts tale

10 November 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10 November 2007 The winter storms bombarded the city once more. Joshua looked out from the empty gym and watched as his New York fellows rushing by. It was odd Joshua thought to himself, how little people prepared for such weather. New York was never a sunshine haven, yet not one of the people running by, trying in vain to stay dry, had an umbrella. They had no rain coat; these people were dressed as if the sun was a close personal friend. “Idiots.” Joshua said knowing full well nobody was there to hear it. Turning around and staring out into the empty training room Joshua began to do what all wrestlers do when alone. Thoughts, ideas and plans started to form. This was happening because Joshua, who had just signed a contact with Prime, was scheduled to take part in their Jewel in the Crown tournament. This news had pleased Joshua. His first reaction was so full of joy he hadn’t even heard the name of his round one opponent. He had simple repeated the news to his fiancée and daughter and joined in their screams of elation. Of course the news finally reached Joshua’s ecstasy ears a few moments later, and Joshua’s sigh had said it all. Hoyt Williams. Joshua remembered the man who so resembled the falsely adopted western worlds image of Jesus. That long hair that over grown bread and that annoying ability to come back from supposed death. Joshua wanted to make an impact, but not on someone who, when he had left Prime had been carving the very best to pieces. “Hoyt Williams, god damn it.” Joshua said to himself bring his focus back to the present and away from his memories. He had wandered his way through the running machines and was now resting himself on a weights bench. Sitting somewhere he hoped had been cleaned, as these spots always had overgrown men sweating on them. Outside the rain continued its onslaught on the city, but the people had finally given up and taken refuge. They had, Joshua guessed, found somewhere dry to wait it out. Joshua himself was going to do the same. This was despite the fact his home rested on the twenty fifth floor of the building across the street. Joshua wondered to himself, keeping the gym in total silence. Why? Why wait when twenty seconds of rain would lead him home. A question, he told himself, for another time. “Focus on Williams.” Joshua said, only this time aloud. The Williams made a slight echo barely loud enough to hear, and Joshua began to remember seeing Hoyt’s matches. It was as if the echo, that faint rebounding sound wave had snatched Joshua memories and was bouncing them faintly back into the forefront of his mind. Joshua could even remember the day he had been told Hoyt had signed with Prime, and how he had felt about the self named Personal Jesus. - - - - - - - - - - - - - “Who?” Joshua said looking at the man who had been speaking. “Hoyt Williams, The companies just signed him. He has some hype behind him too.” The reply came from Morris, a running who had come to hand Joshua the nights run sheet. “Wait, isn’t he that god freak?” Joshua asked. There was a clear note of amusement and contempt in his voice which he didn’t seem at all ashamed to have used. “Yeah.” Morris laughed. Apparently sharing Joshua’s view on Williams. “Our Personal Jesus.” Again Morris let out a laugh while a smile emerged on his face.” “Man, you know what I should do. Send him a couple fish and half a loaf, then invite the entire roster to his dressing room. See if he can feed us all.” Morris laughed, it hadn’t been a funny joke, but Joshua was much bigger and had for spent the last month amid a so named Blood Feud. “Or I could send him a Judas Priest album. You think he’d get the rib?” - - - - - - - That had been it; a couple of cheap, unfunny jokes were all Joshua had thought Williams had been worth. Hoyt had made a blip and then Joshua had forgotten all about the man. Until of course, he started winning. It was remarkable the effect a couple wins have on people. Joshua’s own opinion had changed drastically over the following weeks, though the two had never met before. “Is that true?” Joshua asked himself. Had they really never meet before? Joshua racked his brain, literarily smacking it a couple of times trying to remember. Had he faced Hoyt Williams in a match? Joshua found he wasn’t certain. “Surely I would have remembered.” He said again to no one, now becoming conscious of just how he must look and was thankful of the rain. Standing he tried to remember everything he could about Hoyt’s wresting style. Joshua knew he had improved almost ten fold since Hoyt would have last seen him. Yet seven days ago he had just ended, in tears, a sixteen day run of alcohol, cocaine and loose women. He was being labelled a traitor by those at his last company. He had hate mail arriving daily and his relationship was just about surviving the horror stories of those mad sixteen days. “Am I even ready?” Joshua said before he could stop himself. His nagging fears getting the better of him. Joshua hearing himself speak the words, felt as if someone had just calmly slide a knife into his back before sitting down beside him to see his reaction. “No.” He told himself and was surprised at his own sternness. “This is my chance, winning this match is to let everyone know you’re the best. That you and you alone are the greatest wrestler alive.” Joshua’s blood seemed to be pumping around his body a little fast now. “Haven’t I been telling everyone I met that’s just what I am? How can I honest say that if I even for a second doubt myself? No! I am the best; I am, to date, an uncrowned King.” Joshua had momentarily forgotten he had stood and was shocked to find himself on his feet. Joshua had allowed his self motivating speech to get the better of him. He could feel his testosterone cracking up through the gears. Joshua stared around, he wanted to use this sudden burst of energy but no one was around. He had known that, but now was the first time he had really wished there had been at lease one person near him. Joshua looked outside; New York’s weather had gone from terrible to biblical. Sensing a sign of sorts, or maybe it was mere coincidence he seemed not to care. Joshua rushed to the front doors. Took out he’s keys and then opened the doors and stepped out into the pouring rain. Turning Joshua locked the doors and marched across the street already soaked to the skin and noticing a good three inches of water was settled on the roads. Yet he was smiling, he seemed full of purpose and belief. It did indeed take Joshua twenty seconds to reach his own building. The doorman quickly opened the door. His look of concern unable to stop Joshua who rushed by straight into a waiting lift. Everything seeming to coming together like this just added to Joshua good feeling. “Not ready.” Joshua laughed wondering why he ever aloud such dangerous thoughts to entire his mind. Stepping out onto the twenty fifth floor, Joshua was handed a surprise. His fiancée was stood waiting for him. She was dressed in a heavy rain coat and carrying an umbrella. She was just about to come looking from him. Joshua dripping wet rapped his arms around her, lifted her gentle off the ground and kissed her. He tried, successfully to place his feelings of love and thankfulness for her into the kiss. As they slowly pulled apart she stumbled back a little almost euphoric with glee. “What…” She began. “I’ve been an idiot. I keep allowing these ideas take over me but I have a chance Jess. I have a chance at being Prime’s new King. Jewel in the Crown Jess. I almost allowed it to slip away without trying.” He gazed into her eyes and saw no signs of doubt in them which made him match her gleeful look. “You’ve always been a King, it’s just about time one of these stupid companies saw it.” Her words made Joshua fell instantly better. He lifted her up again and started carrying her back towards their apartment. Joshua in that instance became total unaware as to why he had been worried. Sure he knew Williams was good, great in fact. Yet Joshua had just remembered a saying he once used on his opponents to cement his great ability. “You can be good. Hell, you can be great. Just understand you can’t be Joshua Taro Freedom.”
