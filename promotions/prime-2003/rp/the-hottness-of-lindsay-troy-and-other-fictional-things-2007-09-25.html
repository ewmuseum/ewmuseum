The Hottness of Lindsay Troy and other Fictional Things...

25 September 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 25/09/2007 “Dude! Just admit it! You were pathetic!” Walter exclaimed with a chuckle. Jonathan Winters sighed, ignoring him and kept an careful eye on the building across the way. It supposedly housed Larry, a usually consistent informant Winters illustrious employer, Cornelius Stakiri, utilised on a regular basis, but lately Larry’s information had gradually become more and more infrequent and inconsistent with passing month and Stakiri had grown suitable suspicious. “There’s a reason for everything, Jonathan,” he’d told him, “and Larry‘s had better be damned good!” Now, he and Walter stood on the rooftop across the road from the informant’s crappy apartment block, in a less hospitable part of town watching on as the remnants of the summer foliage fell from the trees and drifted down to cover the street below them with a stereotypical orange/brown Autumn tint. Not that Walter had noted any such beauty… “Come on,” Walter continued, “just admit it!” Winters rubbed his face gingerly. It’d been a hell of a long three hours metaphorically caged up there with the moron and although Winters patience was usually formidable, Walter had breached it without even breaking a sweat. For most of it, he’d decided to painfully remind Winters of Dice, Dollars and Tragedy running through the card as though he booked the damned thing himself. “Admit what, Walter?” Winters asked him, never taking his eye of the target property. “That I lost the Roulette match? I would’ve thought that was obvious to all…” Walter shook his head. “You didn’t just lose the Roulette match. You got your ass pounded!” Winters shook his head. “Whatever.” “Oh come on now!” Walter said, positioning himself into Winters direct line of sight. “Don’t get all moody on me! I’m a fan, I really am!” Winters rolled his eyes. “But being the second one eliminated? Out of ten? You can‘t be happy with that?” Winters shot Walter a cautionary glance and pushed him out of the way. “I don’t want to talk about it.” Walter nodded. “Quite understandable…given the circumstances.” Winters tried to ignore him the best he could, but now the fucking idiot was just grating against his skin. The question is , Winters silently began, is it intentional or mere innocent absent of mind? “Not even a little?” Walter began anew. “I mean, you must want to vent a little. You went into the thing a favourite and came out a chump!” “Walter,” Winters began, briefly removing his eyes from the target, “read my lips. Shut. The. Fuck. Up. Cock Vest.” At first, Walter appeared to be offended but his face quickly contorted with confusion as the words were fully processed in his head. “Cock Vest? Seriously?” With a grunt, Winters turned his attention away from the imbecile. “What does that even mean ? You just made it up, didn’t you? Admit it!” Winters remained silent, but Walter sure as hell didn’t. “I know I said I was fan an’ all, but the truth is I didn’t really think you’d win anyway. I mean, you have the drive an’ all, but dude, Lindsay Troy is HOT with a capital, uhm, er, HOT!” Winters shook his head in disbelief. In-fucking-sanity. But Walter didn‘t seem to notice. “That belt looked so damned good around her waits Yoda himself couldn’t take it off her, never mind nine mere mortals!” “We’ve had a discussion about fictional characters before, Walter,” Winters told him, “ and rest assured Yoda is most definitely one of ‘em.” “Yeah, right,” Walter said with a chuckle. “That’s what they want you to believe.” Winters raised a speculative eyebrow. He was going to regret this, he just knew it. “They? Who’s they? ” Walter’s eyes widened and he ran his hand over his face in an odd mixture of shock and outrage. “Aw man, I can’t believe this! I thought you were an intelligent guy! There‘s a conspiracy going on here and everyone knows it!” Winters should’ve trusted his initial judgement; he was regretting his question already. “What the fuck are you talking about, Walter?” “The conspiracy, man! What the system want you believe!” Walter exclaimed, rather too loudly for Winters liking. “You’re just a sheep, man…” “So Yoda existed?” Winters asked. Why the hell am I lengthening this damned conversation? “Sure he did!” Walter replied. “A long time ago, in a galaxy far, far away. It tells you right on the screen, man…” Winters shook his head, idly wondering whether it was medically possible for someone to be that stupid. “You’re not a believer and that’s okay,” Walters began, “but I hope you realise that’s precisely why you didn’t win the title.” He had to know the answer to this one. “Because I don’t believe in fictional characters?” Walter shook his head. “No. It’s because you don’t believe in anything, not even yourself.” Did that sound like a vaguely sensible answer? Goddamn, he’s worn me down...Wait. Is that Larry? Fuck… “Thanks sensei,” Winters began, “now liven up. We got work to do.” Walter followed Winters gaze and smiled. “Finally!” You said it brother… They left.
