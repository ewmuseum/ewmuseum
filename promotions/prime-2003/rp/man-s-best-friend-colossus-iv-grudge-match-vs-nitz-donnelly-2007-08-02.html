Man's Best Friend (Colossus IV, Grudge Match vs. Nitz Donnelly)

2 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: August 2nd, 2007 "I'm fuckin' lost." Wade Elliott, PRIME's Blue Collar Brawler, slowly cruised his black Ford F-150 (purchased shortly after Revolution 134) down a poorly-maintained road. He was surrounded by hardwood forest, one hand on the wheel, one holding a cheap TracFone to his ear. "Lost? Lost where?" asked the static-filled voice of PRIME's Universal Champion, Lindsay Troy. "Fucked if I know, somewheres b'tween Portland an' Seattle." "Heh, maybe Tyler has a point about you starting to fly..." she sarcastically replied. "Don'tcha step down that road, Troy. I just ain't never been this far west, is all." "Alright, Drifter, get the bee out of your bonnet. Your rig have a navigation system in it?" "Watch yer mouth." "What? I'm serious. Don't most new trucks and SUVs come with a navigation system?" "Not in no rig of mine. Bad enough Tyler made me get this fuckin' phone. I got a god-damn tongue in my mouth an' a quarter in my pocket. Trouble is I ain't seen no-one fer hours..." answered Wade, leaning forward, tipping up his drifter's hat, attempting to peer through the trees for any sign of...anything. "Okay Wade, I'm saying this as a friend who was dragged to Vegas to watch you assault an old man...we're 7 years into the new Millennium. It's time to catch up with the rest of modern civilization." "Yer lucky I ain't ridin' a god-damn horse to Colossus." "Yes. Let's be thankful for small miracles." Wade continued to search through what he assumed to be the Oregon wilderness. He shook his head, completely lost. "Tyler finds out I got myself all twisted 'round, she'll have my fuckin' throat," said Wade into his phone. "Well, what she doesn't know won't kill her. Do you know what road you're on right now? I can pull out my PDA and try to get you un-lost if you know about where you are. Any road signs out there?" crackled Lindsay through the phone. "Not that I'm seein'. Got myself tangled real good in the sticks. Didn't know the west coast even HAD sticks..." "Well, if you're in Oregon or southern Washington State, sticks is about all there is out there." "Coulda used that information a bit sooner. I'll keep drivin' 'round. I'll call ya if things go from bad to straight fucked." "Try to find I-5 North. It'll bring you up to Seattle. If you're too far east or west, try to get to I-90 then to I-5. I've got a press conference thing and a promo shoot, but I won't be far from my phone." "I won't be hesitatin'." Wade fumbled with his phone, finally discovering the “end” button before pitching it carelessly into the backseat. “This is turnin' out to be more of n' adventure than I generally like...” * * * * * * * Wade found himself on the same dirt-road a few hours later, weaving his way through the trees. Still no sign of civilization. No road signs. No nothing. His steel-blue eyes started straight ahead, his head shaking back and forth. “Deeper into the god-damn rabbit's hole. Better call Lindsay again...” said Wade, reaching an arm to the back seat of his truck, fumbling about for his TracFone. No sooner did he take his eyes off the road than a gray blur ran out in front of his truck. Wade's eyes bulged as he snapped back to the steering wheel and slammed his boot to the break. “JESUS!” The truck skidded along the dirt, kicking up dust and sliding to a stop somewhat cock-eyed. Wade breathed heavily, knuckles white as they gripped the steering wheel. He found his bearings, then turned his gaze to the side of the road, squinting as the dust settled. The salt and pepper fur of a blue-tick hound stood on the right side of the road. It's big brown eyes were locked on Wade's piercing blue orbs, a dead rabbit sat in the dog's floppy jowls. Wade found himself entranced. The hound stood unafraid. It was a thin creature, Wade could see the outline of the dog's ribs. They watched each other for a while longer before the hound turned and trotted away through the woods. Wade tried to keep visual on the dog until it disappeared. He snapped out of his trance and adjusted his drifter's hat. “That's a pet dog if I ever saw one, n' a pet dog means owners...” Wade pulled over as far as he could, killed the engine and popped open the driver's side door, hopping out and onto the ground. He grabbed his TracFone from the backseat, stuffing it into his back pocket before reaching underneath the driver's seat to retrieve Alice; his big, shiny, chrome .357 magnum. He spun open the revolver chamber, noting a full six bullets. He closed it shut and stuffed it into the back of his pants, the black handle sticking out over his brown belt. He slammed the door shut and locked it up, then stepped into the trees, exerting his best efforts to track the blue-tick hound. * * * * * * * The Drifter clambered through the trees, taking steady and careful steps as to not lose the trail left by the dog. The light of the day had started to fade, making way for the glow of evening. The going was steady, however, and it wasn't long before the trees led to a clearing. Pushing a branch of the way, Wade came into sight of a run-down, rotting, piece of shit cabin. Whatever the condition, however, it was human built, which meant some sort of salvation. “Good dog...” muttered Wade. He reached back and checked his gun, making sure his army green t-shirt was covering the weapon. Satisfied, he stepped out of the woods, making his way across a small field toward the ramshackle homestead. “No cars...” he said to himself. Sure enough, not one automobile sat in the driveway. Wade narrowed his eyes, taking slower steps as he approached. He was relieved to see the blue-tick lying on the front porch, gnawing on his rabbit meal. Wade slowed further as he stepped within twenty feet of the house. The dog lifted its head, leaving the rabbit carcass on the porch. It rose to a seated position and let out a low growl through its teeth. Wade stopped. He thought for a moment, then reached into his front pocket, retrieving the remains of an earlier purchased Slim Jim. He put the bite of jerky into the palm of his hand, crouched down, and inched toward the dog, hand outstretched. “Nice pup...” said Wade, in as soft and calm a voice he could muster. “Good boy...” The hound cocked its head and lifted its ears. Wade crept closer. The dog suddenly stood to all fours, causing Wade to stop. Elliott waited, hand outstretched. Eventually the hound took slow and patient steps of his own toward The Blue Collar Brawler. “Good pup,” continued Wade, trying to remain as non-threatening as possible. The dog stepped closer, a foot from Wade's hand. Wade's eyes were locked on the blue-tick, his left hand reaching around back, ready to grab his gun if needed. The dog stretched out its neck, then lapped up the piece of Slim Jim, taking big bites to chew the tough piece of meat. Wade released a sigh of relief, venturing to scratch the dog behind the ears. “Good boy,” Wade repeated. The dog no longer appeared to be of any threat, grateful for the snack and attention. Wade rubbed the hound's neck, noticing an absence of collar. “No owner, pup?” Wade asked the dog as it finished chewing on the jerky. Wade looked up to the house. No one had come out to greet him, and he hadn't heard anything from within. He turned back to the dog. “You squattin' on this property?” No reply from the dog. Wade smiled. He looked around the area, noting the bones of rabbits and other small animals, no doubt the victims of this very blue-tick. “Well...” started Wade, standing up. “...if this weren't yer home to begin with, it sure is now.” The hound licked his jowls. Wade stepped up onto the porch, followed by his new companion. He peered through an open door, seeing no immediate threat. He turned around, looking to the sky to see dark clouds making their way along the sky. He looked down to the dog. “Looks like rain.” The dog cocked its head. Wade gave it another scratch behind the ear before stepping inside the home, to which the hound followed closely. * * * * * * * “'ello?” Only silence filled the cabin. Wade and the hound took steady steps inside. The cabin consisted of one main room with a couple of off shoots, one appearing to be a disgusting bathroom. The main room was a mess; cracked and lifted floorboards, raccoon shit in every corner, dead mice, broken windows, a ripped recliner sat on a moldy rug. The kitchen sink was rusted to hell, and what looked like old coffee grounds deteriorated on the counter. “Well, ain't nobody's called this home fer a while...” said Wade. He looked down to his new friend. “'Cept you, I guess.” The hound only panted. Wade took a few steps further in, the floorboards creaking under his steel-toe boots. He surveyed a moment longer before digging into his back pocket, retrieving his TracFone. He squinted at the tiny screen, which read NO SIGNAL. “Fuckin' figures...” he grumbled. He stuffed the phone back into his pocket and turned toward the door. “Well, this ain't doin' us much good. Best git back to the rig before...” A deafening crack of thunder rushed into Elliott's ears, the hound nearly jumped out of its skin. A loud, heavy rainfall followed seconds later, filling the air with the sound of large droplets pummeling the ground. Wade's face sunk as he stepped to the doorway, leaning against the jamb, staring out into the downpour. “...before it rains...” he finished, shaking his head. The dog only sat. * * * * * * * The rain did not relent as night fell. Wade and his canine companion sat on the porch, Wade in an old rocking chair that, miraculously, did not splinter upon sitting. The dog was on the porch floor, gnawing on the rest of his rabbit dinner. Wade rocked to and fro, watching the hound, the rain pelting the grass and dirt all around him. “Now, here's what I ain't gettin',” inquired Wade to the dog. “I found ya out on that road, a rabbit danglin' from yer teeth, an' followed ya back here to this cabin...” The hound tore off a piece of meat. “...an' that leads me to believe that you've been livin' on yer own fer a while, that an' the fact that you ain't got a collar 'round yer neck.” The dog gnawed. “But there ya were,” continued Wade. “Sittin' on this here porch like its yer own damn home. So that leads me to thinkin' one 'r two things...” The Drifter leaned forward in his chair, as if to make a clear point to the blue-tick. “...either this was yer home all along, an' yer owners didn't feel the need to put a collar on ya before they up an' disappeared to who knows where...OR...” The dog crunched through a bone. “...or yer the driftin' type, just like me.” The hound swallowed a bite. Wade leaned back in his chair. “Whatever it is, the assumed family sure as shit ain't here now, so I guess that means you'll have to come along with me.” The dog stopped eating, pushing itself to its feet before trotting next to Wade, slumping back to the floor at his feet. Wade smiled, reaching down to scratch the dog. “It'll be real nice to have a travellin' buddy. Maybe you can show me how to get the fuck outta here an' put me back on track.” The dog wagged its tail. “Ain't no worries right now, I guess,” he said, lifting his gaze to the heavy rain. “We'll have to wait fer this rain to pass before we...” A low, guttural growl from beyond the porch stopped Wade in mid-sentence. His eyes widened. The dog lifted its head, releasing a growl of its own. Wade stood up slowly, trying his best to peer through the rain. The shape of a large, dark lumbering animal made him jump as it came in and out of view. “Sheeit,” whispered Wade. The dog had risen to its feet. It let out another growl. “Shhhhh,” said Wade, grabbing the dog by the back of its neck. “Quiet boy, let's get ourselves inside.” Wade stepped inside the cabin, leading the blue-tick through the doorway. He closed the door quietly, clicking it shut with an old latch-lock. He moved to one of the intact windows, moving around as if to look around the rain drops. “Jesus fuckin' Christ, that's a god-damn bear out there...” he muttered. The dog growled at the door, teeth barred. Wade knelt next to him, rubbing his belly. “Shhhh, it's alright boy, ain't nothin' to be afrai...” Wade stopped mid-sentence. He lifted his head, a startled expression on his face, to a window on the opposite end. The monstrous, muscular spine of a black bear moved across the window. Wade could hear the animal breathing and grunting. Thump. The house shook, causing a couple pots and pans to fall to the floor as the bear bumped into the house. “Fuckin' Christ that's a big un'...” whispered Wade, still rubbing the hound, who remained quiet. He reached to the back of his pants, retrieving his revolver. Wade and the dog sat crouched in the middle of the cabin for a few quiet moments, Wade turning his head this way and that, trying to listen to the beast. ”ROOOOOOOOOOOAAAAAAAAAAAAAAARRRRRRRRR!!!” The bear released a deafening roar. Wade stumbled backward against a wall, his head next to a window, his eyes wide. The dog rose to its feet, alert, growling once again. Wade remained crouched against the wall. He slowly pushed his thumb forward, clicking the safety of his weapon off. A few more moments of solid rainfall. The hound had stopped growling. Wade lifted his revolver and started to turn his head toward the nearby window. “Maybe it moved on...” Wade turned to the window, and was met with the huge snout of the bear. It breathed through bottom-less pit-like nostrils, fogging the window with each breath. Wade's eyes bulged. “ROOOOOOOOAAAAAAAAARRRRR!!!” The bear's roar shook the window. Wade could say nothing, and the animal's gargantuan paw smashed through the window. Wade threw up his fore-arm to shield himself from the shower of glass. “JESUS!” yelled Wade. The bear reached through the window, trying to find a way to crawl inside. Wade, wild eyed, lifted his revolver, releasing two shots through the window. ”ROOOOOOOAAAAAAAAARRRRRRRR!!!” The bear pulled its arm back outside, disappearing from view. Wade kept aim at the window. The dog was barking wildly at Wade's side. A moment passed. The dog barked. Wade breathed heavily. CRACK. The wall splintered, paneling cracking as the bear smashed its paw into the wall. It smashed the house again, tearing a hole in it. Wade stepped back, terrified, as the bear poked its massive claws inside, ripping away at pieces of wood. It had soon sundered enough siding to stick its head inside, and for the first time Wade could stare the beast in the eye. ROOOOOOOOAAAAAAAARRRRR!!!” Wade, out of instinct, pulled the trigger again, a bullet landing next to the bear's head, sending tiny pieces of wood flying through the air. The bear ceased roaring, only to continue ripping through the wall. “Holy FUCK!” yelled Wade. “C'MON BOY!” Wade ran to the door, kicking it open and bolting into the heavy rain, making a bee-line for what he hoped was the road, and eventually his truck, the dog following close behind. He stopped, breathing heavy, and turned back to the house. Rain dripped off his drifter's hat. “My fuckin' God...” The animal was colossal, a black bear for sure. It stood nearly half the height of the house at the shoulder. Wade could only stare entranced as it pushed its head out of the hole it had just made in the cabin. It turned toward Wade and his companion, and Wade shook in fright as it trotted toward them, sort of stumbling, its tongue hanging out the side of its mouth. “Shit!” Wade turned and ran straight for the woods, pushing branches out of the way as his boots stomped into the mud. The dog ran off in a different direction, disappearing into the night. “Fuckin' dog!” bellowed Wade, bending low as he pushed forward. He ran long and hard until he could run no longer. He stopped among the trees, doubled over in exhaustion. Once he caught his breath, Wade stood straight, turning and searching through the darkness for any sign of the road or his truck. He spat water and saliva. “Son of a bitch...” he breathed heavily. He waited a moment, collecting himself before making his next move. A low growl froze him stiff. He whirled around, pointing his revolver in the direction he had just come from. His breaths came quick. Another growl. He spun around the other direction, Alice aimed at the darkness. “Where the fuck's he comin' from?” Wade asked himself, frightened. A third, louder, closer, angrier growl. Wade turned once again, and let loose a bullet into the darkness. Nothing. Only warm, moist breathing on his neck. Wade closed his eyes and swallowed saliva. Breathing through his nose he slowly turned one-hundred and eighty degrees... ...finding himself nose to nose with the humongous black bear. Wade shook, unable to lift his arm to fire a shot. He stared into the beast's wild eyes, locked, transfixed. “ROOOOOOOOOOOAAAAAAAARRRRRRRRR!!!” The beast's mighty roar blew Wade's drifter's hat off his head, saliva spraying into his face and goatee. Wade fell back onto his ass and into the mud. He scooted back, feet slipping against the slick, saturated earth. The bear stalked forward, its paws laying dinner-plate sized tracks in the earth. Wade scooted himself up against a tree, an angry grimace of hatred and fear on his face. The beast took one last step forward. It lifted a terrifying paw, ready to swat Wade into a bloody carcass. Wade closed his eyes. A dog's barking opened them. From out of the darkness to Wade's left charged the blue-tick hound. It leaped into the air, lips curled, fangs present, and slammed into the bear's shoulder, burying its teeth into the monster's thick hide. “ROOOOOAAAAAAARRRR!!!” The bear turned its attention to the ferocious canine, attempting to throw it off of its shoulder. The blue-tick did not relent, but the bear's power was too much, and with a mighty heave of its shoulder the bear was able to throw the hound off, sending it sprawling through the air and into the mud. Wade watched the dog land, then turned back to the bear. The Drifter curled his lips in, and pointed Alice at the bear's head. ”ROOOAAAAAAAAAARRRRRR!!!!” The bear bellowed, lifting its fatal claws into the air. Wade pulled the trigger. BOOM. The bullet flew true, penetrating the bear's left eye, exiting through the back of its head. The beast released a short grunt, then fell forward, crashing into the mud a mere foot from The Blue Collar Brawler. Wade sat for an eternity, arm outstretched, a white knuckled grip on Alice's black handle. The rain pelted against the bear's thick coat. Wade slowly stood. He took a couple steps toward the fallen beast, the barrel of his gun aimed steadfast at the monster's skull. He knelt down and retrieved his drifter's hat, slapping the soaked head-piece onto his scalp. He stood up straight once again. He pulled the trigger one more time. The bullet cracked against the bear's skull, causing it to shift a little. Wade breathed through his nose, spinning open the chamber of his gun and dropping six empty shells onto the ground. He closed the chamber and stuffed Alice behind his belt. The sound of panting caused him to turn his head. Sure enough, the blue-tick hound trotted toward him, apparently unscathed. He knelt down in front of the dog to scratch it behind the ears, to which the dog happily accepted. “I think I'll call ya 'Angus.'” The hound did not object. Wade turned to the fallen monster. "And I'ma call you 'Nitz.'" Neither did the bear. * * * * * * * A small old man sat at a table, stitching together what appeared to be some sort of animal skin. The walls of the small building he sat inside were covered with various pelts, assorted tools suited for skinning cluttered the building. The roar of a truck's tailpipes caused him to look up from his work. He ruffled his white mustache and pushed himself to his feet, sticking the needle into his brown apron before hobbling to the front of the shop. He swung open the screen door and stepped onto the dirt driveway as Wade Elliott hopped out of his F-150. Wade lumbered around to the back of his truck, Angus sat happily in the front seat. The old man waddled across the driveway toward Wade. “Can I help ya, sir?” asked the old man. Wade tipped his hat before grabbing hold of a blue tarp that covered the bed of his truck. “Well,” replied Wade. “I'll understand if there's time restraints...” He threw back the tarp, revealing the carcass of the colossal black bear. The old man's jaw dropped. “...but I've gotta be in Seattle to whup some yuppie's fuckin' ass in three days, and I need me some new seat covers.”
