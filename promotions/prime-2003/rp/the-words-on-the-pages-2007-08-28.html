The Words On The Pages

28 August 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 8/28/07 “Now, they're gonna come talk to you and they're gonna tell you things. But what they tell you isn't really what happened. So I think you should know what really happened. I was trying to do a good thing.” -Jack Mosley, 16 Blocks The calls had been coming in all week. It wasn’t just the magazines either. I’m not a man who counts many “friends” in this world, but people within my circle had called. All emphatic in their support of this new man. Chatting with opponents before matches? Extending a hand? These were not things that super villain Chandler Tsonda was capable of. I yawned in between sips of lukewarm coffee. My mind was out on a traipse around the universe, whizzing past planetary shades of grey. I didn’t set out to be a champion of justice. But I knew my history. How many times had Hoyt stepped out in front of thousands to call everyone out, on God’s behalf? How many times did it have to happen? It simply happened that, at ReV 136, I could not let it come to pass again. I’m not one of the good guys. Never will be. And I’m not trying to stop the clamor of reverberating jeers for sins past. Wrestling itself is the penance…for a life largely wasted. No reason to measure my salvation by an applause-o-meter. But like I said, there comes a time when one of the bad guys becomes the lesser of two evils. Maybe that’s the only way heroes are made. Ya know, since we’re all evil deep down. My eyes zipped around the New York Times, lingering on certain words, comprehending their aesthetic more than their meaning. I couldn’t focus enough to learn anything from the day’s headlines. More and more, I was deep within the vortex, spinning wildly, flailing out for some sense. Why Rayne? There was nothing particularly likable about him, other than the slice of Chandler Tsonda I could see in him. This was not a young buck to mold…he’s a fellow thirtysomething glory hound. Why had the spirit compelled me towards friendsh…no, amicable acquaintance with him. The ten million dollar question of the moment: what would become of the A-List? Its de facto leader was content to pal around with the flavor of the moment female. I was doing something…new. And the other two lieutenants were getting chased around by two costumed douchebags. When we had promised the new A-List, I’m certain no had expected…this. No longer a super group, the collective had quietly splintered. No, not all the way, but without completely selfish villainy propelling our actions…some of the faux camaraderie had disappeared. Don’t get me wrong, I dig the List. Always will. If nothing else, Danny Ferguson taught me to look out for number one. And even if he’s forgotten what that means…well, I guess we’ll see about that. But I admit, I don’t know the future of the List. When it comes down to it, there will be no smoke in the wind ending. It’s Hiroshima or bust, when it comes to demolishing the greatest spectacle ever known to PRIMEates. And I just remembered that I need to call Dr. DiPendo. The rail-thin Italian sat in Oz, waiting for me to find the answers behind the curtain. I was tentative about going back to his office. Sure, I would keep learning things about my psyche, about myself. But there’s only so much you can know about yourself before it’s too much. And then being alone with yourself is the scariest thing on earth. Coming from a man who is constantly, resoundingly, alone…well, less powerful things have killed more powerful men. Back to my eyes, beams scanning meaningless human existence in print. They find a word as cruel and cold as its content: assassin. The story referred to a bombing in some Godforsaken land. Everything is on fire and the world burns…but in the end, we all run from the fire rather than grab a bucket of water. Heh, such is life. Reaching back into my memory, I try to find something personal about the word. I remember the liberating epiphany of second grade: it’s ass-ass-in. But there is something. “Chandler, you’re a cold-hearted assassin. You’ll never love anyone or anything as much as you love yourself, you cruel, cruel bastard.” Blade-edged words from an old romance. Pain dulls, but truth rings true forever. Was probably right then. Probably right now. But to be…an assassin. An interesting ambition and call to arms. The Assassins of old were a secret society, a mystic cult, toiling across the land, exporting their brand of politically guided killings. Careful with their application of death and keenly focused on its merciless efficiency. To be so free…I can only dream. What a life it would be, a lone Assassin in a world of emotionally plodding men, women, and children. To speak and hear in the gurgles of morbidity, to be versed in murder, and to erase all feeling. Assassins were drugged with hashish and allegedly followed orders unblinkingly. Submission was total and perfect. A strange inkling in a morning of strange thoughts. But what could it mean? Had she meant in my calculated womanizing? Or in my self-punishment through wrestling? I thought, as an Assassin would, to Rayne and Dusk. Granted, it made little sense to try and analyze emotionlessly what had already been defined as ineffable. I didn’t know why I kinda liked Rayne, or at least harbored some growing respect towards him. Medieval Muslim cowboys, man, fucking wild ones. I couldn’t stop thinking about the ideal behind it all. Take all the bad guys out to pasture. No questions asked or issues debated. And as my eyes traced the outline of Pope Someridiculousname XII, my brain made the logical leap to Hoyt again. How to repay a man who has seen every possible attack, every conceivable act of revenge known to man? The greatest Assassin tactic was one that did not kill a single enemy. It was more personal, more powerful in its cerebral nature. For those who they saw as reparable cogs of the machine, there was a special touch. When the target woke up to a bright, shiny medieval day in the area of Persia, he had a nice new touch to his bedchamber: a knife through his pillow, right next to his dome piece. The message was clear: the Assassins were here. They didn’t need you dead. Yet. And so you could live, but you could never be sure that one more fuck-up moved that knife a couple inches left, pushing your mind right. Dead men make far fewer mistakes. But scared men…hell, they no longer control their own destiny. And so it would be. Hoyt Williams, at some point, was going to need a knife through his Tempur-Pedic. Fear of God would pale in comparison to Fear of the real. A glorious new motivation.
