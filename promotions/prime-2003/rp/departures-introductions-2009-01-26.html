Date: Monday, Jan. 26th, 2009 Snow Drop Lanes 7:18 PM Telluride, Colorado November 26th, 2008 “You’ve sure got a lot of nerve.” The sweet sultry drawl comes from a brunette perched on the other side of the Shoe Rental counter at Snow Drop Lanes. The man working looks up to reveal his face and stands up to reveal his company-issued pastel blue and yellow bowling shirt, with the name ‘Veljumin’ embroidered onto the left breast. “Ignoring me like that.” “Oh, I’m sor-“ “What’s that say?” She still can’t read it, even though she is now mounted on the desk. Anyone behind her might have a good day after seeing what Veljumin wishes he was seeing. So he tried a peek down the shirt. “Vel…jew…mine?” “Well-YOU-mean. Veljumin. Nice to meets yous.” She giggles. He smirks. His blue eyes match with hers and she smiles a bit wider. Her head turns, looking around. He looks around as well. “What else do you do, Veljumin?” “I works at the bowlings alleys! It’s not a bads jobs, good pays. Better benefits.” Veljumin holds up his own bowling ball, smiling widely as the ball has ‘Veljumin Rippa’ etched on it in yellow script. He sets it down on the counter, leaving a slid crater. “Oh nos!” He drops the ball down and it rolls to the ground, smashing his big toe and causing the burly, foreign giant to crumble into a pile and crash into the shoe rack. Every stinky, smelly, RENTED shoe falls onto his face, head, and company-issued pastel blue and yellow bowling shirt. Lying in the pile of muck, the big-man writhes in pain but manages to catch the look of the woman, smiling at him a way he’s never seen before. All the pain subsides. “What’s going on here!? I’veneverseenthisbeforeinmyentirelifeyou’resuchadisgracerubbabubba…” The owner of the bowling alley, from about three seconds out, throws his arms up and approaches the counter, cursing under his breath and muttering things only bosses can mutter. The woman, frantically, reaches over the counter to snatch a bag of peanuts and pours them into the fresh ball-size dent. She begins eating them, nonchalantly. “What- what is this, Veljumin? NAPPING on the JOB?!??” The clipboard goes into the air and lands on the floor of this quiet, little bowling alley in Telluride, Colorado. Filled with ski-bunnies, snow-dudes, and old-time miners - who have been ‘cordially banned’ from all the pubs and bars in town for obscene acts of dementia and alcoholism – all look up to investigate the racket. Veljumin stands up. “Weeeellllllll??? We’re waiting.” “Wells, sir-“ “Actually-“ A peanut goes up into the air as the brunette opens her mouth and catches it as it falls. She looks at the counter. “-This is the new system you ordered, sir.” “New system?” “Well, hah, they call it a system but really it’s just a new way to accommodate the customers and patrons with fresh, top-of-the-line peanuts whenever they like. And you ordered it, about a month ago. We just installed it, today! Hooray! There ya go.” Veljumin smiles widely, throwing his arms up and flailing them like a school-girl. “Hoorays! We dids it, boss! We mades the customers happy!” The boss smiles and nods in excitement. “Oh, well- well, I’m sorry, ma’am. I should be thanking you, not yelling at anyone. Hhahah-hahaha! How much do we owe you for this state-of-the-art device?” “Twenty-thousand dollars.” Veljumin screeches, not being able to hold back his grip of American money yet, and looks at the brunette, who wears a stone cold glare on her face. “Or we can just remove it and you’re left with, well, the same thing I see at every other bowling alley. Twenty-thousand now.” The boss grins nervously and looks up to Veljumin. He leans in closer to the woman and narrows his eyes once behind her head for cover of the public eye. “Look, honey. I was just playin’ your stupid little game to please the retard, okay? You think I’m gonna give you twenty grand? Come on, I’m no sucker. I was hopin’ I could repay you, heh…“ He gives her one of those looks. “…some other way, ya know what I mean?” She smiles, running her finger down his bowling shirt, around the buttons as she takes a deep breath. “Twenty-thousand dollars, jerk off.” He scowls at her. “Or I’ll take this place apart. Piece by piece. Comprende, stupid?” The owner’s look gives her the notion he’s not going to comply. She grabs him by the collar and gives him a right hook to the jaw. He looks back at her in a daze. “Twenty-grand! Or it burns…” He looks at her. She glares back. Her green eyes peer deep into his. She punches him twice more. His nose bleeds as does his jaw, which is hanging there, probably broken. “You had your chance, scumbag.” The woman turns, locking eyes with the clerk. He cowers away a bit but eventually stares back at her, unafraid. “Tear it down.” “Mes? I works heres, lady. Tearing its downs would get me fire-“ “You want to work here for the rest of your life? Wherever you’re from – you came here to work at a fucking bowling alley? Tear it down. Set whatever it is that I see in those eyes of yours free. Set yourself free.” The big, blonde behemoth behind the desk looks back at her, pondering the words in his head. (Or translating them.) He looks around and looks down at his boss, who is still in the grips of the woman of the day. She cracks him one last time, dropping him to the floor before he lets out a morbid moan for help. “Where we go from here, leave up to me. Just tear this fucking place down.” The blonde man needs no more convincing, so he steps back and kicks through the counter-top with one of his massive trunks. He shoulder tackles the remains and splinters through to stand face to face with his Queen of Destruction. She looks up to him, smiling, and then points to the oncoming crowd. He glares, no longer so friendly or customer service-oriented. “Let’s go!” “Where?” “Anywhere. I know some people. We can get by on our own.” Veljumin picks up a table and throws it back as the crowd approaches, buying them more time. This was no regular Veljumin, he was beginning to think. She could see that in his eyes. “Just ask yourself – is this what you want? Or do you want something else? Money? Drugs? Land? Women? Glory?” Veljumin’s eyes shoot down to her’s once again - away from his thoughts and values - to answer the one calling he will always follow: “Glory...” The woman smiles and grabs his hand. The two run out the door and into the parking lot, where they jump into a black Mustang that has parked in the Handicap Zone. Veljumin opens the door just as she is getting in but he stops, looking over the hood. Stopping she looks at him in a hurry. "Your names." She cocks her head. "Veljumin, we have to go! NOW!" He stands still. “Your names.” She throws a hand to her hip and one in the air. “You want all of ‘em!?” “I’m not going anywheres until you tell me your names.” She laughs, giving a little giggle in the middle. She pulls out a bag, full of cash. Her eyes widen as she watches Veljumin’s grow in anxiety. “Rich, darlin'! Rich!” -------------------------- January 4th, 2009 Midtown Manhattan VFW The Circuit’s ‘HotWire’ Scrap V The King Oza (w) “Hell of a match, Tommy.” What? I lost. Fuck you, prick. A young, sweaty Tommy O’Connor sits on the bench in a Midtown locker room, taping a possibly broken wrist and dislocated thumb in front of one of the Circuit’s ‘hands-on’ guys for the night. Ross LaRue. “Doc’ll be here any minute. You just-“ Tommy stands up and smashes Ross in the face sending him to the ground. What really sent him to the ground was the wall that his head bounced off of afterward. Tommy winces a bit, since he used the injured hand to do the silencing, and tries to flex and bend the injured limb. The door swings open. “Tommy! What’s up, pal?” The doctor looks down and then at Tommy, who puts on a fake look of concern. He even leans in for a closer look. “Holy shit, what’s his deal?” “Oh, he was tired of all the talking. Said somethin’ about a nap...” The man in the white polo shirt and blue gloves was Dr. Eric Friedman, one of the Circuit’s best at everything – drugs, surgeries, stitches. You name it; he was the man to see. He sat down in a spinny stool and held Tom’s wrist. Tom’s eyes carefully follow the doctor’s. “Broken?” The doctor looked over his glasses and then gave a slight head jerk. A sigh escapes from Tommy’s lungs and he furrows his brow, pulling his arm away from the older man. “Fuck it. I’ve got worse to worry about. The King is still out there, running his mouth about his fluke win.” The doctor smiles up at his fresh, full-bodied bombshell assistant. He then looks Tom right in the eye. “Like what? More gambling debts to pay off, Tom? More hookers, booze, and cars you can’t-“ Tommy stops looking at the doctor and turns to his boots. Looking at the ground, he raises an eyebrow to cue he was disinterested in anything that was being said. “Just fix my hand. Fix it all. Fix it now. Just fucking fix it. “You’re show, boy. I’m just tryin’ to-“ The door swings open again. This time it is a fan. A cute fan. She is brunette, has green eyes, and pretty much all around gorgeous. She approaches the situation with curious vigor. “Oh, poor baby. Get a little dinged up?” “Scrap is the name, lady. You lost or somethin’? The luxury suite doesn’t exist down here.” She laughs a bit. Tommy winces as the Doc tapes his wrist tighter and tighter. “On the contrary, I think you’re lost. You don’t remember me, do you, Tommy?” He pauses. Thinking harder than he can, especially since he gets hit over the head on a regular basis with heavy, metal objects – he finds nobody in his head who he can recall. “It’s only tattooed onto your body, dummy.” He looks down to read the tattoo he had received while in another alcohol and drug inspired black-out: “Zuh?” She points to his battered ribs. “Ru- RUBY! Well, well, well, look whose back from the dead!” “That’s right, soldier.” She does a half-assed pose. He looks down again, rubbing the tatt’, remembering how the next day he had more tattoos and one said ‘Ruby,’ never knowing why or who it was. His eyes lock on hers. “That was almost three years ago… did you get lost?” “Well, ya know what they say, it’s hard to get somebody famous out of your head, I guess.” Tom’s face shows a small grin. His eyes don’t show anything. “Famous? Get real, lady. What are you really doin’ here?” “Serious. I was just wondering if, you know, you’d like to go out some time or whatever. Nothing serious. Whatever it is you do with your spare time. “I think you can imagine what I do with my spare time.” “Yeah, let’s do that.” Yafuckinkiddingme?! “Alright, babe. I guess if somebody’s got to find all the fun-havin’ freaks in the world, why not me? Right, Doc?” Tom smirks and looks to his hand. The doctor pulls his thumb out and pops it back into place. “Goo-aaahhhhhhhhhhhhhh!!! Fuckmesideways!!!!” The doctor steps back and smiles. He pats Tom on the shoulder over one of his many tattoos of Celtic origin. “Good luck, Scrap.” The two medical assistant’s leave. The Circuit’s reigning Hardcore Elite sits in a room, all alone, with a woman who he has no idea what to do with. “Truth is, Tom – I had to get out of this life.” “So you just leave without saying anything? Fuck off.“ She stands up, walks half-way across the room, and slaps him within two seconds. She glares at him with her deadly greens. “Shut the fuck up and listen, would ya?” He stands, pissed as hell, and crosses his arms. Then he rolls his hand as in ‘Get on with it!’ to cue her forward. “This place, this Circuit, is shit. You know it. I know it. I left and went to Colorado. Started a job as a bank teller, small apartment – life is nice there.” “Yeah, that’s the point. This place is shit. It’s the last pure thing left that wrestlers like me have to come to. We come here to bring in the fans by doing the best shows, the hardest hits, and the most real-to-life action there is. We can’t afford nice houses or fancy lives like that. You think places like Sin City can offer that? Get real. Those places produce garbage on National TV on a weekly basis. You go have your cable TV and drive-thru dinners. I’m fine right here.” Ruby sighs, placing her hands on her hips. Tommy cocks his head and waits once more. “There’s more to life than this. More than proving you’re the best by killing yourself.” Tommy’s eyes light up then narrow down. He draws closer to Ruby and whispers: “No. There isn’t.” She shakes her head in dismay. He is pissed, even more now. “The last thing I want is to take what I’ve built for the people who respect what we do here and put it in the hands of some sap, suit-filled company that has one thing in mind – profit. Profit won’t measure legacy and glory. Profits won’t show respect or overall skill. Profits can’t even determine the champion, when you look at it. Profits are simply numbers that destroy the sport I know and love. Fuck commerce.” “Then come with me.” “With you?” “That’s why I’m here, Tom. I need your help.” “My helps?” I can’t help anybody. Tom’s eyes scan the room. “With what?” “I have a friend. His name is Veljumin Rippa. He is from Sweden but is a real gem when it comes to physical power. He destroyed an entire bowling alley, for me.” “For you, huh? Sounds like a real winner.” Ruby rolls her eyes, latching her arms around his neck. “Please, Tom. You’ll like him. I promise.” Scrap’s eyes wander around the room before looking back at her. “For me?” She kisses his cheek as he scowls a bit. His head jerks up and down, slightly. “What do you need me to do?” “Be his partner.” Tom’s eyes narrow once again. He turns to her with a squished expression over his sweaty mug. “Where?” Ruby looks away, smiling, and tries to avoid eye contact. “Umm… PRIME. Heh?” Tom’s eyes close. His head falls backward as if it is unhinged at the neck. He lets out a deep sigh. Yafuckinkiddingme?!!?? -------------------------- Sal’s Gym Sacramento, California 8:57 PM January 26th, 2009 The loud thud of a wrestling ring floor echoes off of the concrete walls at the San Diego ‘Wrestling School.’ Tommy O’Connor is inside the locker room and lifts his head, shaking it in disbelief. This guy better get his shit together. ‘Scrap’ stands up, pulls his green T-shirt over his long, red mane and head, and then throws on his black jacket. On the way out he throws the gym bag strap over his shoulder. He walks through the locker room doors of the poorly-lit facility to catch another lesson from Sal himself. This time, however, it is being given to the other half of Wicked Ways, Veljumin Rippa. “No, no, no! Try rolling it. Like this…” The short, brittle grey-hair takes the Shark and tosses him three feet in the air. Veljumin lands on his back and he bounces in pain. “Craps, old man! I’m new to-” Without remorse, Sal grabs the blond baby and throws him from a flat, prone position to the turnbuckle. He runs up and throws the 300-plus pounder over his head with a lightning quick Dragonrana. Tommy winces even before the Shark can hit the mat. “Scraps! You leaving?” “Yeah, headin’ to the hotel.” “Craps!” Veljumin spots the ringpost from the elevated view as he flies through the air. He finally lands, on his face, and lets out a monstrous groan. Snot is bubbling out of his nose and the drool is mixing in, nicely. Sal approaches the ropes, moving Veljumin’s body with his foot, and leans over to look at Tom. Tommy tightens the grip on the duffle bag strap hanging over his shoulder. “Gonna go catch some rest. Thanks for the old-time training, Sal.” “No problem, Tommy.” Sal grins at his feet, noticing Veljumin starting to move again. “Check ya later. Tell Maria I send my best.” Tommy nods and begins walking toward the big metal doors that would reveal true sunlight. “How about I give her that forty-grand you owe me, instead?” Tom stops. His head rises but doesn’t turn. He stares forward. Letting out a deep breath, Tom turns to look at the old man who seems anxious for a gift. Sal raises his palms. “Don’t give me that look, kid. It’s been twelve years now. We’ve got our kids’ kids to feed, Tommy. A mortgage. Car payments.” The Irish Lion runs his hand over his moustache and then looks around. “Sal, you’ve always been kind to me. But right now, I have no cash. No income. No-“ “You’re a professional wrestler, Tom. You’ve hit the jackpot. Don’t try and fool me. You played the cards, I just said I’d help you out. The cards just fell the wrong way. So, please…” Sal shrugs, implying that there’s no other choice. Tom glances to his feet, pondering his options. Veljumin finally makes it to his feet, shaking his head a bit and rubbing his neck. He looks at the two others in the isolated gymnasium. “I can get the money to you after the show.” Smiling, Sal pats Veljumin’s back. “You’ve got a good partner, there. Go ahead. Hit the showers, we’ll reconvene tomorrow.” Veljumin hops over the ropes after shaking Sal’s hand and heads for the showers. He looks back to Scraps. “I’ll see ya at the hotel, kid. Ruby’s comin’ to get you after she finishes up at the radio station.” Veljumin nods and then heads for the showers once again. Tom looks at Sal. He lets off a small grin. “Glad we got all that sorted out. See you tomorrow, Sal.” Tommy turns and begins for the doors again. Sal watches him eagerly. “Another thing, champ.” Tommy’s nostrils flare as he stops, again. His eyes narrow as he slowly turns over his shoulder toward the last person in San Diego he trusts. Sal was now standing by the office. “Can I ask ya another favor, Tommy?” Growing impatient, Tom crosses his arms over his chest and waits for the request. “This place ain’t gonna make it another year.” Scrap cocks his head. Sal throws his hands up. “I mean, look around. The lights are barely that, the equipment is almost shot, and all the places up and down Redwood Ave are being swept up by real estate guys trying to build parking garages. Let’s face it – I’m dead in the water.” Tommy’s eyes wander to the equipment and lighting fixtures. He looks back at Sal, who is wearing a pleading look of hope. “Can you spare me some cash to get this place back and running? I’ll pay ya back, kid. I swear it. I just need an advance and the banks are all too scared to let up anything risky. But you know that this place will last. It’s got history.” Tommy continues to watch Sal speak but lets his mind stray away. Pathetic. His eyes come back and lock onto Sal’s. His face begins to fall as his temper rises. “And I suppose you’ll need an oil-change and tire-rotation, too? Right?” Sal is taken aback. “You said it yourself, Sal. I hit the jackpot. I am the professional wrestler. That’s more than you can say for your entire life.” “But I was-“ Scraps’ hand shoots up and silences the old man. “Shut the fuck up. You will speak when I am finished. Back to what I was saying; you never did the things I did. You couldn’t even make it out of this putrid cesspool or into a real job, even. The saying goes, ‘where there’s a will, there’s a way.’ The problem is, you don’t have the willpower to support your family or save your business. It’s sad, really. Why would I invest my money in a guaranteed failure?” Sal, standing speechless, looms opposite the redhead with his eyes filling with tears. Tom glares at him, waiting for an answer, and finally snaps the silence. “I’ve waited longer than anyone should have to get my chance at a real life. A new life where debts, gambling, hookers, and drugs don’t dictate what happens. And here we are. I’m finally in the spotlight and all of a sudden I’m expected to come up with things I’ve never had. You think I have forty-thousand just from signing with PRIME? You don’t think that maybe wrestling a match first might have something to do with it? Winning, too? The guys who think that way are in for one hell of an ass-kickin’.” Sal’s face is expressionless as Tom keeps going. “I certainly won’t be the one caught with my pants down on this one, bub.” Tom gets in Sal’s face. He quickly wraps his hand around the old man’s neck in a firm, non-lethal manner. Sal’s face gleams despair as the only thought running through his head is dark, sad, and cold – the thought of death. The struggle that Sal puts up is brief. He finally peers deep into Tommy’s devil-bred greens. “Tell Maria I send my best.” Tommy throws Sal into his office, causing the owner’s head to hit on the wooden chair and the rest of the body on his desk. The office becomes a cluster-fuck. Tommy puts on a big smile. He heads for the doors. As he pops the bar to open the metal door, he lights a smoke and throws a black skull cap on. He is quick to figure out the cold sidewalk and walks toward wherever it is he will be tonight.
