When I Didn't Exist

21 October 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 10/21/05 I walk outside to fiddle with a pack of cigarettes. San Diego isn’t usually this cold, but El Nino or El Gato or some weather system named after the Spics is tearing us a new one. I can still hear the party inside; some rapper with a name that’s a misspelled household object is inaudible over the acid bass. Every girl in there is a 17-year old sperm bank. Then again, think about who I am. I’m on some short balcony that overlooks the city. It’s gorgeous at this time of year, but I’m a sucker for my city. You don’t appreciate it as much when you travel as much as…I used to. I’m new to the whole “cool cigarette smokers” cult and I’ve already committed the cardinal sin of forgetting my lighter at home. There’s nobody out here except for some Abercrombie college slob who will clearly wake up with permanent marker penises on his face. I figure that this house must be pretty nice when it’s not the site of the best rager in the city. There’s a nice little patio set out here and the flowerpots aren’t usually filled with piss, I guess. I need a fucking light. Who am I kidding? I don’t even crave the dirty, acrid taste of these things yet. They taste like used coffee grounds. I don’t ask myself why I started; I just smoke them as a way to get away from the questions. “I’ve gotta go grab a smoke” is one of those magic phrases that people don’t know how to respond to. It’s like I just told them my parents are dead; then again, how the hell is a college kid gonna say anything to me? The door opens behind me. I don’t look back. I try not to let them see my face. It’s easier to avoid the questions that way. Come to think of it, a lot of this shit is just to avoid the questions. For a while, they were asking me every day why I came down to their posh neighborhood. Why I was around so much now, why I wasn’t in New Jersey taping my “boxing shows.” A year ago, I would’ve corrected them and said “wrestling, you semen-eating fuckbox.” You know what it’s like now, though. Footsteps. Jesus Sandal-Wearing Christ. Time to be mean. Time to be the guy I used to be on TV. The guy I’m too fucking pathetic to be anymore. I turn around and let loose everything that’s brewing up inside me. Fuck subtlety, I’m too old to be pretending that I live in their muddled reality. Yeah, it’s psycho-babble bullshit to me too. It’s some girl. Call her a slut or pretend that I care why she’s on my balcony? “What do you want? The bathroom and available penises are inside and I’m trying to have a pleasant smoke to myself.” That’s me. There’s Chandler Tsonda, the Model Citizen. She looks me over with these pretty eyes. Eyes that should be looking at some beautiful art gallery. Looking at something that she won’t find in this house with the carpet that smells like Busch Light. I can tell she knows what she’s doing around me, even when I haven’t talked to her yet. She smells like flowers and peach Schnaps. “You’re not smoking.” It’s a fucking terrible habit, smoking. Leaves you wide open to ridicule, stains your teeth so you look like a homeless Limey, and you smell like a fire pit. For what? For this awkward escape route from society. And I can’t even get that right. I look down at my pants, like I’m gonna find a lit cigarette in them. My shoes have a scuff on them. God, these fucking college kids have no respect for me. But as soon as I think this, I think, why the hell would they respect me. I look up at this girl and she’s staring at me with those fucking innocent-but-I’ll-fuck-you-til-you-fall-over-dead eyes again. I wanna tell her to turn around and leave me alone, but maybe she’s as bored as I am with this existence. Maybe we’ll run away together and get married in Vegas. Maybe we’ll have beautiful little Chinky-honky babies. “You got a light?” My voice isn’t as harsh now because I don’t have the heart to tell this girl the truth. I don’t tell her that I want her to leave. I don’t tell her that I’m a waste of her time, no matter who she is. She looks taken aback. Wait, maybe she just looks pissed. Nope, nope, she’s genuinely surprised. It’s too awkward not to say anything at this point. “Well, you gonna stand there like Doug Flutie’s autistic son or you gonna hand me a lighter, b…?” Trail off. Maybe she’s offended. Maybe she’s leaving. She shifts her weight to her right side. That’s her good side. She reaches in her right pocket and pulls out something; in the dark, I can’t see shit except her face. That’s not some metaphorical bullshit; the moon is shining right down on my patch of land out here that she’s invaded. She flicks once and that slim, inviting flame hovers over the lighter. She looks up at me, thinking. In the split-second, I realize that she’s sizing up whether I’ll catch the lighter; I mean, I’m not wearing an Aztecs shirt that smells like rotten vagina and a backwards hat, so am I man enough to catch it or will she just make things more awkward by tossing the lighter to me? God, I love these moments. Silence. The lighter hits my palm. If I were a writer, I’d tell you that the arc of the lighter through the slivers of moonlight is like angel wings cascading down over…no, I said too much. I don’t say thanks. Who knows what to say to someone who says thanks anyway? She’s obviously not gonna leave me out here with her lighter and she’s probably come out here to have a smoke. I take her for a hash girl. I grasp a cigarette from the box between my fingers. They’re long and bony and they reach into the box easily to clasp onto the cancer stick. It slips out easily, but I try to get fancy. It falls between my second and third fingers and falls to the ground. She stifles a giggle. Newbie smoker crisis: do I pick it up? Can I still smoke it if it’s been on the ground? Are they like paintballs, where if they hit the ground they’re tainted forever? Did they hit a pile of errant piss? I pick up the cigarette and pretend like I’m looking at it very carefully. All the while, this mystery is looking at me, sizing me up. Maybe she’ll take a step back inside and decide I’m some psycho who’s gonna kill her and throw her off the balcony and listen to her bones crack on the pavement, then calmly walk back inside and resume partying. Resume partying? Please. I open up the lighter and let the small fire lap at the tip of the cigarette. I worry it won’t light, that I’ll be forced to reach into my pack for yet another one. No way to save face. It lights. The glow is enough that she can see my face now, if she couldn’t before. It’s still silent and God is it nice not be talking. I don’t know if I should throw the lighter back or what. “You need this back?” I try to sound like an asshole. Like I don’t care and I won’t thank her and I won’t even acknowledge that I needed the lighter. She nods, but doesn’t interrupt my precious silence just yet. Then she steps forward and her hair catches the light. It’s awful. It’s red and it‘s loud and it’s not conditioned and it’s not curly or straight. I’m not shallow, I just think in terms of perfection. Modeling is perfection. But I’m not a model anymore. And I’m not a wrestler. I’m a guy at a party. Do I hand her the lighter or wait for the touch of her hand against mine? Is that weird? She takes it from my hand and totally avoids the weird hand graze. It’s all one swift, brilliant motion as she flips open, lights, puffs, saves the world, and flips closed. She’s got her first ring of smoke before me, dammit. I wait three….four….five seconds and then close my lips around the little cylinder of relief. Smoke builds up inside my mouth and lungs; it should be calming, but I can feel my heart rate rising. My heart knows I’m actively trying to kill; I’m trying to pull his plug and now he’s trying to jump out of my chest and leave with this girl. “You go to school here?” And it begins. The ineffable horror of conversation; it’s like a huge bat that swoops out of this beautiful night and just starts ripping out pieces of my scalp. I’m not good at the whole being a human thing; I’ve come to grips with it. I was much better at putting hairspray in people’s eyes, then moonsaulting them to death. “C’mon, toots, you know damn well I don’t go to school here.” I’m a fucking twenty-seven year old. Too young to die and too old not to know what I’m doing anymore. “I’ve seen you around. I thought maybe you were a red shirt athlete or something.” Maybe it’s true, but the way she says it stings. She knows that I’m the weird guy who won’t ever really leave this life behind. That I’ll be going to these college parties. I don’t have people in my life, so I become a nonexistent person in everyone else’s life. The music inside has changed now. It’s some trance or something; all that house, trance, techno shit is the same to me. It’s made by and for people who aren’t cool enough to do LSD. I'm a self-appointed music sage now too. “Yeah, well…” I begin to speak, but like many other times, I don’t have the heart. To finish, to tell her that this is the most I’ve talked to someone in a week, to ask her to marry me. There’s that dream again. What’s the worst that happens? She spits in my face, calls me a shitface loser, and dumps the flowerpot of piss on me. I won’t stand against the torrent of insults and urine. “I’ve been involved with some sports here.” You’re a terrible fucking person, Chandler Tsonda. Rot in hell. I go home and fuck her brains out. She eats up that athlete shit. The End. Reality. “Yeah, well...don’t worry about me. I’m just somebody who used to be somebody.” Oh my Christ. Did I really just use a battered action hero line on a girl? More importantly, do I think it’s going to somehow attract her? See in movies it works because girls believe in the human spirit. Real life is different. The human spirit is bullshit and girls dig beautiful people who don’t have major psychological issues. She looks thoroughly convinced that I’m who I say am. I realize that I haven’t looked at her chest all night. I’m scummy, but I’m not tasteless. Her tits are hidden better then the Holy Grail under that horrid off-white sweater, but they threaten to emerge as she contorts her body to look up at the stars. I feel a sappy bullshit question coming on so I turn towards the city and take another drag of the cigarette. It doesn’t come. She enjoys the silence, just like I do. For a second, I think about looking up at the stars, but I know that they’re the same stars I’ve seen every night of my life. I even subscribe to the school of obnoxious assholes who dissuade people from their secure notions about stars by telling them that we see stars as they were many many years ago and so by now, they might already be burnt out and we wouldn’t know. If I tell her, I’ll piss her off. She’s got something ahead of her; by the time she catches up to the stars of tonight, she’ll be some place better then this. Me? … One drag for her. One for me. She blows a perfect ring up towards her stars. I stop following it with my eyes and look back at her. She glances down so it doesn’t seem like she was looking at me. I want to turn and go back into the party, but it’s about time to go home. Before people start filing out, I have to, so I’m not left with a room full of people who I should’ve been. Who I’m better then. Who would’ve watched me on TV one day. My cigarette is done. Let the pain fester in my throat, to be healed by the slightly chilled air. If anyone deserves it, it’s the Model Citizen. And let me have my time back. Let me have my life again because you took it from me goddammit and I want my fucking life the way it was and it’s childish and I don’t fucking care…just because. She turns and walks inside. No hesitation or last-minute thoughts about our babies or her stars or my career. She’s left only the echo of a life, her burnt cigarette on the ground, sparks flailing about. I go back to my empty balcony and my cigarette perishes too. I throw it in the piss flowerpot, to put it out of its mercy. Look at the city. Square one.
