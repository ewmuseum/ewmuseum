Because Sometimes Script Just Works Better

7 November 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: November 7th, 2005 Not in this case, unfortunately. Nothing can make crap like this ‘better’. Seriously. The highest point my writing has reached is the level of toilet humor. I’m worse than Seinfeld, for Christ’s sake! Wait, no. Scratch that. I’m still better than Seinfeld, but not yet as good as Dead Like Me. Somewhere above Arrested Development, if only for the fact my camera doesn’t appear to be operated by an epileptic, but below Family Guy. Right beside Schindler’s List. And somewhere in the world, a fuck of a lot of people turn over in their graves. Now, this is where I’d normally delve into a Forsaken adventure, probably giving you a brief synopsis of what led up to Clyde interjecting with some completely retarded comment, and thusly hilarity does ensue. But not this time. No, this time it’s different. This time…it’s personal. Actually, it isn’t. But I’ve decided my writing doesn’t seem more like a blog than a roleplay as it is, so I’d like to touch on a subject that is very near and dear to my heart: Canine Homosexuality, or ‘CH’ for short. DID YOU KNOW? …in the wild, one out of every eight dogs is gay?…there are more gay dogs per square mile than there are people?…being bitten by a gay dog can make you gay too?…he’s not just cleaning himself? To learn more about CH, please visit www.mydogisgay.com. Knowledge is power! And now back to our previously scheduled program. Walkins: DICKWRINKLE! What can I say? I’ve found a Dr. Curiosity-like formula that no one else has seemed to notice yet, and I’m planning to abuse it for all it’s worth. Sure, I may not get anywhere with this particular formula, considering it’d be one of those accidental spells that always go horribly wrong or smell particularly foul if it were in Harry Potter, but at least this way I don’t have to try. Because trying is for losers. Now please step aside as I claim my titles and smile while doing so. Because somehow I can find pride in winning off a no-show. …You know…in case something bad happened to the computers of Sky and Shad…that kept them from writing for a week…not that I’d know if anything did…ahem… Moving on. James popped awake, his eyes barely able to focus as an onslaught of light hit them. He figured they were dead, but Clyde miraculously didn’t screw up, and instead swerved the car out of oncoming traffic and back into the proper lane. Acting like nothing happened, Clyde started humming along to the song on the radio, while James stared at him with a mix of shock and hate on his face. Farwell: What the hell is wrong with you?! Clyde turned his head to look at James, a smile spreading across his lips. Not paying any attention to the road at all, Clyde’s hands slowly started to shift to the left as the car began to glide into the oncoming lane once again. Walkins: Good morning, sleepyhead. Farwell: Clyde! The road! Walkins: Technically, it’s a highway. James had but a moment to narrow his eyes at Clyde in further hatred, before lunging over and grabbing the steering wheel, moving them out of danger in the nick of time as their car narrowly missed being crushed by a semi. And then went off the road into the forest beside the highway, and finally straight into a tree. The car spun to the side and rolled down the hill leading from the highway into the forest, finally coming to a rest on its roof amidst a grouping of trees. In an indistinguishable area, among trees that look the same no matter where you are, Clyde and James hung upside down in their car, both men unconscious. Twelve hours later… James awoke with a great pain in his head, and for a moment he figured he’d died and probably gone to hell. He only hoped that if it were the case, then Clyde would have gone to heaven. He could deal with a thousand years of torment if it meant being rid of Clyde for a single minute. Alas, it was not to be. Farwell: Ohhh…my head… He cringed and put a hand on the back of his neck, before realizing he wasn’t hanging upside down anymore. In fact, he wasn’t even in the car. Somehow, James had been released from his restraints and pulled over to a clearing in the center of a group of trees, just a few meters away from where their car landed. It was dark and Clyde was nowhere to be seen, leaving James disoriented from the crash. Farwell: How did I…oww, my head…did I hit my head when we crashed…? Clyde’s voice made him jump at first, and he was pretty pissed for the whole ‘almost dying’ thing, but in truth was a little happy to know he was alive. Walkins: I thought the car might explode, so I pulled you free after I came to. Clyde stepped out of the woods with a pile of sticks and logs in his arms, his eyes turning to Farwell’s head as James rubbed the back of it in pain. Walkins: And you…uh…fell…when I cut you down. James made a move to sit up, but ended up having to lie down again after the wave of pain that washed over his head, while Clyde went to work on starting a fire. Farwell: Well, we should freeze to death tonight. Walkins: Not to worry--I have matches! Grinning and giving James a thumbs-up, Clyde reached into his front pocket while his tongue slowly peeked out from the corner of his mouth, really concentrating on finding the matches. A slight squishing sound could be heard as Clyde furrowed his brow and fished around a bit more with his hand, before stopping altogether. Walkins: Now I remember; I wet myself when we hit the tree. Turns out, I don’t have matches, after all. James didn’t even sigh. He didn’t care anymore. The end was nigh, and if God had some mercy on his soul, a bear would kill him before the cold did. But there was one thing bothering him. He needed to know what he was about to die for. Farwell: Clyde? Walkins: Yes? Farwell: Why did you swerve into traffic and yell out ‘dickwrinkle’? Clyde stopped trying to light the wet match and turned to look at James. Walkins: My song was only fourth on the charts. Farwell’s eyebrow cocked slightly. Farwell: ‘Your song’? Walkins: Yeah, “Clyde Wash”. It was only fourth on the charts, and I got kind of mad about it… Farwell: So you tried to kill the both of us? And how can that song actually exist? There are laws that protect songwriters from people like you! Clyde shrugged. Walkins: Apparently there’s another law that says you can’t copyright crap. Farwell’s eyes bugged out of his head a little, and he held a finger up to his lips to quiet his partner. Farwell: Shh! The Educator might hear you! Checking to make sure no plagiarists were around to steal new ways to lose, James settled down a little when he didn’t hear some copying and pasting going on, and Clyde went back to trying to start a fire. Needless to say, it was going to be a long night. One hour later… Seated on a log in front of a quasi-fire, Clyde and James shivered as they tried to warm themselves by the dying flame. And to give you an idea of just how shoddy this fire was, it had the workrate of TCM. Hooray for cheap shots on people who don’t even read my work! So there both men were, coming close to freezing to death, but still one thing continued to plague James. Leaning over to Clyde, James nudged him with his elbow, rousing Clyde from his entry into hibernation. Or hypothermia. Farwell: You know…we’re just about set to die…and all I can think about is how much I hate Samoa Joe. This time it was Walkins who cocked an eyebrow. Walkins: Samoa Joe? What’d he ever do to you? Farwell: He just sucks, and it’s been bothering me forever how everyone hangs off his nuts. He’s mediocre, dammit! The most entertaining thing to come out of him is that stupid dance he does before his matches. I swear to God I once fell asleep during one of his matches and woke up to him doing the same shitty chin lock he’d been doing twenty minutes before! He’s the new millennium’s solution to Ric Flair…if Flair multiplied a few times and then sewed his bodies back together. Clyde eyes James, perhaps wondering if the hit to the head caused some sort of slowness. Walkins: C’mon, he can’t be all bad. In fact, I remember people saying how awesome his match with CM Punk was. Match of the decade! Farwell: Punk could pull a six-star match out of a broomstick! I’d be surprised if he couldn’t get match of the something out of a living being. Walkins: I don’t know, James. That broomstick can go. Cut to CM Punk running the ropes as a broomstick lies in the middle of the ring. Back and forth, back and forth, back and forth. The fans are on their feet cheering the display, and sweat pours down Punk’s forehead as he busts his ass to carry the broomstick, finally dropping an elbow down across the handle. A loud crack can be heard throughout the arena, as the fans start to quiet and the camera pans to three other broomsticks and a dustpan in the front row. Upon seeing the broomstick in the ring break in half, the largest of the brooms in the audience falls to the side. And now back to The Forsaken. Farwell: I still say Joe sucks. Same with Monty Brown. Except no one pretends he’s good. Another hour later… Walkins: James! James! I found a way out! Shouting from within the woods, Clyde’s excited voice causes James to wonder if his luck hasn’t begun to change. His head still aching, but feeling far better than earlier, Farwell slowly stood and turned to follow Clyde’s voice, pushing through branches and shrubs as he finally made it to a clearing and saw the highway. And their car, which was lying at the bottom of the hill. And then it dawned on James. Farwell: You mean to tell me our car crashed thirty feet away from the road?! Clyde nodded. Farwell: And we could’ve just walked out of the woods and to the road at anytime, as opposed to nearly dying and talking about poor workers? Clyde nodded again. James simply took in a breath, walked forward, and smacked Clyde upside the head. Walkins: Ow! What was that for? Farwell: For being you! And another smack. Farwell: And that’s just because I can! Rubbing the back of his head, Clyde turned away and started walking up the hill, with James not too far behind. The two men finally reached the top and stuck out their thumbs to try and hitch a ride in time to get to ReVolution and job out to Teh Gayness like we all know they will. Farwell: Remind me to kill you after I take a shower. I think a deer peed on me when I was out. Sniffing his shirt, James turned away in mild disgust, just as a truck pulled up to the side of the road and the driver rolled down his window. Trucker: Y’all boys looking for a ride? Farwell: We are. We had a bit of an accident and need to get to the nearest town. Do you think you could give us a lift? Trucker: Sure can. Just… The trucker paused for a second, eyeing Clyde while licking his lips. Trucker: There ain’t ‘nuff room up here, so the little one’ll have to sit on my lap. Walkins: Yay! Pony ride! Farwell: No, Clyde! James grabbed Clyde by the arm, keeping him from jumping into the truck and being subsequently molested. Farwell: Thanks, but…uh…we’ll catch the next ride. Trucker: Suit ya’selves. Rolling his window back up, the trucker took a second to wink at Clyde, to which Walkins responded with a wink and thumbs-up of his own, unknowingly arousing the trucker. The disturbed look on Farwell’s face said it all, as he pushed Clyde along down the road, and the trucker finally gave up and drove away. Farwell: Why the hell does everyone want to molest you? Walkins: Because I’m Captain Clyderisma, bitch! Farwell’s eyebrow cocked again. Farwell: That doesn’t make any sense. Walkins: It doesn’t have to! Damn rights, it doesn’t. And just for the sake of wrapping everything up in a neat little package, The Forsaken did get to their hotel that night, thanks to a little old lady that went four hours out of her way to get them there, just for Clyde to steal her purse in the end. There. Happy ending, ahoy.
