Destiny & Coincidence: Part I (ReV 88)

23 January 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Jan 23/06 Part I: Destiny Philadelphia - 2006 Iggy and I pulled into ‘The Angry Irishman Pub’ parking lot. I turned off the engine and the car went from a majestic roar to a soft, submissive rumble. The emission cloud that had followed us all the way from the Wachovia Center slowly dissipated into the Philadelphia night sky. He seemed to be a bit pensive. I’m not really sure what was on his mind, but then I haven’t seen him in 20 years, and I haven’t heard a word from him since about 1990. He probably has a ton of questions. Poor kid, he’s had a rough go at it. “So, you wanna head inside?” I asked, almost awkwardly. “Sure.” He replied. We popped open the doors and stepped out, the cherry lights from the cop car across the parking lot were definite attention-grabbers. I couldn’t focus on much else. I supposed to myself that they were taking away a couple of rowdies. Bar fights - so useless - a bunch of inebriated morons causing fights to prove their manhood. A little self-confidence would go a long way. “You coming?” I guess I was lost in thought. Iggy pulled my attention back to the bar. I joined him at the top of the short steel staircase where he stood in the doorway from which he had beckoned to me. I walked behind him into the bar. Hardwood floors and country music. The TV’s were playing, of all things, the (closed-captioned) ReVolution that we had just left. The two of us bellied up to the bar. “Barkeep! Gimmie some Morgan on the rocks.” Iggy called out. The bartender was a cute twenty-something with dark red hair and a mischievous smile. Too young for me, but right up Iggy’s isle. He hardly seemed to pay any attention to her at all. Odd, I thought. Back in my day I doubt I would have taken long to ask if I could meet her family and seek their approval. “For you?” she asked me as she began to prepare his drink. “I’ll just take an Orange Juice,” was my automatic response, almost rehearsed. I guess the question was a long time coming in the conversation of awkward smiles and silences we had been participating in thus far. “So, Ignatius, your dad ever tell you how we met?” “The war.” “Is that all he said?” “Yeah. He never really talked about it, you know?” I leaned in on the bar, thinking about the events of that day, even now it almost seems surreal. Like the hand of God came down and made it happen. For the first time all night Iggy seemed interested and responsive. Vietnam - 1969 “JESUS! RUN! FALL BACK! FALL BACK!” the Canadian Sergeant yelled out as his Corporals and Privates scurried about like Cockroaches in the light trying to avoid a barrage of incoming mortar attacks from the Red Chinese Army. It used to be a rice field harvested every year by farmers just trying to make a living, trying to survive. Now the only harvesting done was by Death, but those who occupied the field still strived for survival - some things never change. It was wet and the heavy rains only made it worse. It was boggy and muddy and disgusting. The water was hip-deep and made it very hard to move, let alone run. BOOM! Another bomb hit the field sending mud and water dozens of feet into the air, in some cases people, or at least parts of people. The water became more and more red, stained with the blood of friend and foe alike. Death had no bias. Shrapnel fragments tore through the air at amazing speeds wounding, maiming and, in some cases, even killing people. One man, Private Philippe Lisieux, a French-Canadian from Quebec, was among the Cockroaches scurrying from the light of those bombs burying themselves in the terrain on the rice fields. BOOM! “HERE!” yelled the Sergeant again. Sergeant Jacques Lavelle, from Montreal, Quebec huddled his men into a three-sided sandbag bunker. The water had soaked into the sacks and they were heavy and solid. The rain poured down. Each man was inside and accounted for. Lisieux stood at the outer most point in the makeshift bunker and watched the field. He saw Death, in his black shroud, with his pale skull-face poking out ever-so-slightly from the front of his cloak. The creature reached out his scythe and scooped in the souls of men cast down in the field. Men who weren’t finished their living. These men were fighting, but he coaxed them to give up. Lisieux swore as he watched he would not so easily give up such a fight. He would not allow his very soul to be so easily taken and tossed into a sack like a rich rice harvest. Death looked at him and Lisieux looked back with a sneer on his face and determination to live in his eyes. Death simply smiled and looked towards the sky. Lisieux did the same. “CHRIST!” Yelled one man in the platoon as he pointed up. A mortar charge was sailing down on the sandbag bunker and they all knew they were doomed. “Damn you…” Philippe uttered as he awaited his fate. Destiny is something one cannot avoid was the conclusion reached by Philippe in those precious last seconds of life. BOOM! Death moved in. “Hey!” yelled out a friendly voice. Philippe awoke to a smack on the cheek. “Wha--?” “Look, I found you here, face down, it looks like your company was hit in that bunker about 15 yards east. Are you alright?” He couldn’t believe it! How had he survived? Philippe looked over and saw Death reaping what war had sown. Reaping his friends, his buddies, his brothers. But not him! He had escaped Death’s cold hand. He had won the fight! He had beaten fate! “I can’t feel my legs.” “Well, they’re still there. Let’s get you outta here!” “Who are you?” “Name’s Noble, John Noble. Private, U.S. Military Corps. We’ll do proper introductions later, for now let’s get off this God-forsaken field!” Noble picked Lisieux up with a fireman’s carry and hauled him out of there, slowly but surely. After 400 hundred yards, the length of four football fields, in knee-high mud and guts, Noble came to a clearing above water and saw a Med-Evac chopper and an ambulance. “Got a wounded friendly here!” Noble yelled out to the driver and Corpsmen. They rushed in and helped him load the man into the back of the ambulance. “We got no room for you, man! You’ll have to wait for the next trip!” one of the Corpsman stated rather frankly. “Tell my unit I’m here, will you? I stayed behind during the shelling to look for survivors!” “He the only one?” The driver asked, tucking his chin to try and keep the sheets of rain out of his eyes. “Yeah. I think so.” “God damned war. What’s good for?” “Absolutely nothing,” Noble started, “I’m with U.S. Military Unit 605. My C.O. is Major Johnson.” “Can do. Good luck!” was the man’s response as he hopped onto the truck and yelled at the driver to go. Lisieux looked back and saw Noble standing there, and beside him… Death. Death looked ominously at Lisieux as the ambulance drove away, brandishing his scythe, his bag of soul slung carelessly over his left shoulder. As the rain poured down still, Lisieux didn’t suppose it was over. Philadelphia - 2006 “I saw your dad again two days later at a medical unit well behind the front lines. He had some burns and nerve damage, but the Doc said he was going to be okay. I suppose it was fate that brought us together.” “I don’t believe in fate. Life’s just a big, funny coincidence. And sometimes it’s neither big nor funny. Heh,” Lisieux retorted. His sarcasm was almost reassuring. I felt uncomfortable. He wasn’t the six-year-old kid I had last seen. Things were strange. Our life experiences had driven us apart, it seemed. A common ground was coming slowly. “So I guess that’s how you became friends, eh?” Iggy re-started the conversation. “Yeah. I think he always felt like he owed me something. But, man, I’ll tell ya, there’s no rush like knowing you saved a guy’s life. Nothing can top it. Nothing, nowhere. All the thanks I needed was his life, and his living it.” Iggy called the bartender over. She smiled at him and leaned in. “What can I get for you now, handsome?” “I guess I’ll have another Rum with a little extra spice.” Iggy said raising an eyebrow. I couldn’t help but smile. The liquor was already loosening his tie. Once he got another drink or two into him he’d be a blast, I was sure of it. “You want me to top up your orange juice?” she said, winking at me. I smiled and nodded, adding, “Save your winking for the young fella, I’m old enough to be your poppa.” She laughed and Iggy nudged me a ‘thanks’ for the recommend. “Is that how you two got into wrestling together?” “Yeah,” I began, “your dad was a fan, but I had the connections. I got his foot in the door and the fans couldn’t keep their eyes off him, he was really an entertainer. He was a good man, your father. A good friend. We used to tour all over the world together. Sometimes, though, he did seem really detached. One show we did for the NWA in Texas, in ‘73, I remember he didn’t want to even go out to the ring.” “What? Really?” Huston - 1973 The thick, musty air in the locker rooms was enough to gag a maggot. Even still, those little garbage diggers didn’t have to deal with trash like Arnie Yoast. Arnie was the local promoter. He was working with NWA to promote this big show in Huston. Noble and Lisieux were being billed as ‘The Veterans’ - odd for a couple of early 20’s punks to get a billing like that, but they did serve in Vietnam. They were put up as the pre-Main Event match and given a lot of hype and promotional time. Noble and Lisieux arrived at the venue. Two hours to showtime. The air was bulky and hot, really hot. The pair got out of the car - a brand new blue Chevy Camaro - bought, and driven off the showroom floor. Lisieux grabbed his bag from the hatch and started walking towards the entrance door of the venue when he froze in his tracks suddenly. “What is it, Phil?” called out John as he was closing the hatch after grabbing his bag out. “I thought I saw… someone, er, thing. In the doorway.” “There’s nothing there, man. You getting pre-match jitters?” “Naw, no way!” exclaimed Lisieux as he and Noble walked into the venue, but the thought was on his face, the fear was in his eyes, the uncertainty in his voice. Noble knew something was up. An hour later, a rowdy liquored-up crowd was chanting for a match. There was a team in the ring, and ‘The Veterans’ music was playing, but Lisieux refused to go out to the ring with Noble. Noble couldn’t figure it out, but Philippe just stood at the edge of the curtain looking out at the ring. An old nemesis stood there amidst the wrestlers, and unknown to everyone in that room. The Grimm Reaper was waiting. Standing in the ring. Beckoning to Lisieux. Harkening to him to enter the fray. “I can’t do it, John. Not tonight. I’m sorry.” Held in the firm grasp of fear, Philippe turned away and went back to the locker room. He sat on a bench there and put his head in his hands. “I thought this was over.” Noble, not sure what else to do, just went out to the ring and started a handicap tag match. The crowd was angry at first, but they warmed up to the match. Noble really held his own. Lisieux looked up and his heart jumped into his throat. “You can’t run forever.” Death said, as it sat beside Lisieux on the locker room bench. “Why don’t you just kill me, if you want me so badly?” Philippe said fearlessly. Death shook it’s head. “I’m no murder, Mr. Lisieux. I have a job like anyone else.” “You don’t seem to mind it.” Death put down it bag in front of the bench. A green glow was emitted by the bag. “Do you enjoy your work?” “Yeah. So?” “Is it so wrong to enjoy what you do?” “You rob people of their souls!” Lisieux said standing to his feet and backing away from Death. “Look,” it said, also rising to it’s feet, standing an impressive 7 feet, “we had an appointment on that rice field four years ago. You managed to get away from me then, but not forever. This has to be made right. It’s nothing personal, but when my next opportunity comes you better believe that I’ll be there. You’re coming with me one way or another, Mr. Lisieux. Everyone dies. Everyone is reaped. What you’re doing now… it’s messing everything up. It cannot be allowed to continue. Haven’t you ever wondered why you are able to see me?” “I have…” “Only the dead and the doomed can see me. Mr. Lavelle saw me, too, you know. In that field.” The memories came rushing back, in vivid Technicolor. Lisieux stood there, stunned. Death’s words made sense. But he didn’t care. He HAD made it out alive and he deserved the opportunity to live that life. He cheated Death and that was Death’s problem alone. Finally Lisieux replies, “I don’t care. Tonight’s not your night. Not me.” “Not you. I guess that’s true. But I won’t leave here empty handed…” Having spoken the words Death vanished, in the blink of an eye he was gone. Philippe was just glad to have him gone. He went about getting back into his street clothes content that he had survived his second encounter with Death… …Then it hit him. ‘He won’t leave empty-handed, what does that mean?’ Lisieux thought to himself. ‘Wait!’ Lisieux burst out of the locker room and tore through the hallway and ran through the curtain to ringside. “John!” he yelled, Noble turned to look at him and at the same time his opponent, on the top turnbuckle slipped and fell backwards to the outside, landing on his head. He started twitching and convulsing. The Medics ran in quickly and the audience was in an uproar massive confusion ensued. But in the middle of all the chaos a chill went down Phillipe Lisieux’s spine as he saw Death walk through the crowd, the echoless click of his scythe on the concrete as he went, the Grimm Reaper stopped at the foot of the fallen wrestler, Jack Knight, and snatched up his soul in a boney right hand. He held it gently as it writhed in fear and uncertainty then he stuffed it in his sack and looked at Philippe until he was lost again in the chaos of the arena. “What the hell just happened?” Noble asked Lisieux as he finally made his way out to him in the crowd. “I -- I don’t know.” Philadelphia - 2006 “He would get like that sometimes, it held him back. He was a great wrestler, it’s true. But he could have been a true legend in the sport. I never did understand how he always seemed to see things like that coming.” Iggy had taken in almost a whole bottle of Captain Morgan’s Original Spiced Rum by this point and had changed his attentions to one Jack Daniels. “You’re gonna get alcohol poisoning, Iggy.” A smile burst across his face, “An’ yer gonna get O.J. poisoning!” “That’s not how O.J. kills.” I replied quickly, thinking I was rather witty. Ignatius rolled his eyes, “Clever, John, clever.” Okay, maybe I wasn’t as witty as I had first thought. “So anyway, for the next seven years your dad and I toured all over together. We went to Australia, the UK, all over Europe, Russia, and even a brief stint in Japan. Then he met your mother.” I was worried as soon as I’d said it. I remember as a kid he used to ask me and Phil why he didn’t have a mom when everyone else had one. That was a heartbreaking question. One I never cared to address. Still, I had opened the proverbial can of worms… and maybe an old wound. I’d have to contend with that pending the next thing out of that young man’s mouth. “Yeah, ma. We don’t talk much. She lives out in Ville Marie. Tell me about her. Dad didn’t really.” I figured I may as well go with it. Whatever happens, happens. I’ll just tell the truth. “He was head over heels about her. So we went home and he got married and I was invited to be the best man. I was glad to.” “I bet ma looked beautiful that day.” Every young man wants to know his mother was beautiful. He was deprived of her growing up and that’s not right. The emotional wounds he carries must be tremendous. What could I say? “Yeah, she did. Then a year or so later the news came down that she was pregnant. You were born Aug--” “--ust 28, 1980. I know. Then she left.” There it was. I knew it was coming. I couldn’t explain it away or excuse it. But it wasn’t like she didn’t care. I tried to explain… “It wasn’t like that, Ig. She-- she got really depressed. She didn’t abandon you. She left you with your dad and-- she needed her space. She couldn’t deal with it.” …but he didn’t really seem responsive. I don’t know if he talks to her or how often, but I doubt he’s ever dealt with these emotions. He’s a good kid, but he needs approval and guidance. “Yeah, yeah, I’ve heard it all before. She’s a good woman. I mean, I love my mother, it’s just… nevermind, John.” I guess it was time to change the topic. It was funny to see his insecurity here at this bar. He had such a strong front. Triple Crown champion and a 26-year-old Hall of Famer. That HAD to count for something. Anyway, I figured it was time for some damage control. “You’re dad took you around everywhere. You’re all he talked about. He hired a nanny to come around to all the shows on the tour and take care of you when he was in the ring… or the infirmary. We stayed tight until I left the tour and active wrestling back in ’86.” “I remember you used to send Christmas cards and phone us up every once in a while to stay in touch… until we moved. I remember missing Uncle Johnny when we moved. Dad just had so much on the go. He always seemed to be looking over his shoulder.” I never did understand that. After that day in Huston he did always seem a little nervous. I guess I always chalked it up to seeing a guy slip and die in the snap of a finger. The frailness of life is astounding when you look at it’s importance to us. “Yeah, losing touch with you guys was rough for me, too. Your dad and I were the very best of friends for over 15 years… I guess we were just meant to lose touch.” “Fate again?” he supposed callously. I just nodded. I believe in fate. Fate laughs at probabilities and scoffs at coincidence. Destiny is the thing on which we can rest our assurances. Without it everything is left up to chance, I don’t know that I want my life left up to some sort of cosmic coin toss - a roll of the celestial dice. When I win, when I lose and when I die I can take solace in knowing that that is how it was supposed to be. It was my time. It is my time. Fate cannot be avoided, and is often encountered on the road people take to avoid it. When a thing is meant to happen, it will.
