Da Funk

18 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/18/06 All the little things keep creeping up on me. The momentum is like some huge fucking tidal wave that has somehow made its way, silently, inland to my doorstep. And I hate to be a poet, but this is some terrifying siege. Every once in a while, when things are totally “ass-fucking-Zhang Ziyi” type excellent, the wave cracks, on a Biblical scale, down upon me. In a familiar scene this week, Karina Wolfenden was an accomplice of this wave. The chance, once again, to climb mount K-Wolf, was presented to me. It was maybe the best fight I’ve ever given her and she still took a win. It’s like I show up for the first four acts and just when she senses the curtain going down, she activates hyper mode. It’s that intrinsic ability to eke out a win on sheer guts alone: I just don’t have it. And that feeling, that ineffable personal recognition of doom is how I know when I’m back riding the wave. When you’re on the wave and in that paramount of funks, time passes slowly. It’s not just perspective, time is a more powerful force when you’re desperate. It tugs harder on knees and shoulders and everything inside and right now it’s a powerful fucking pull. If there was a way to fast forward to Lisieux on Wednesday, I’d take it in a heartbeat. I’d take a match running on empty over a week in the dol-fucking-drums like this one. I know it’s gone from bad to worse when the car doesn’t start. A bad day is not a symptom of a car not starting; a car not starting is the inevitable symptom of a bad day. No matter how many times I rotate the stubborn metallic token, all I get is unfulfilling groans from the car. Swearing and slamming my hands against the dashboard won’t be necessary. I call AAA and an annoyed operator tells me that someone will be there “within the hour.” Sports radio is the only thing on of any interest and it’s just two guys talking about the perpetually shitty Padres. After the punishing hour and a half, the magic-car-starter man arrives. He’s got cables upon cables and after some ritual in which he spends at least twenty minutes looking at the car and issuing puzzled “Hmmm”s, he deduces everything. “Yeah, well, your problem’s gonna be a uhh…new battery and an oil change.” I love this hazy yet definite future tense. Apparently, my problem’s going to be the battery and oil, but right now…who in fuck’s blazes knows? “You’re gonna want to get the car into the shop before you do anything else.” I plunge a katana into my throat, as the funk finally does me in. I wish. As this automotive shaman collects his things, I pick up the phone to dial up my favorite Eater of Faces who is Illustrious. “What’s the secret handshake?” “Huh?” “DON’T DELAY YOU VIET CONG APE, THE CLOCK IS TICKING!” I’m thoroughly confused at all times by the Illustrious Face-Eater, but usually I can decipher Adam Dick’s statements from somewhere within there. Now, I’ve got nada. “Dude, I’m certain that the new team statement of decorum said absolutely one hundred percent no PCP use.” “Goddammit private, show me the secret handshake.” “We’re on the phone, you buffoon!” Silence for a moment. Clearly, this has only vaguely occurred to Face. He’s been in his own funk lately, except it seems like his wave of terror is peeking in through the windows every couple of minutes, making him all tweaked. “What do you want,” he grumbles. “I’m busy making a fucking kickbitchass fort so that they’ll never find me.” Talking to Face when he gets like this is a transcendentalist experience; you hardly believe it’s you talking to this off-again, on-again lunatic. “I need a favor.” “I WON’T BE YOUR DRUG MULE!” “On second thought, nevermind.” Clearly, this not the guy I want to be discussing my emotional peaks and valleys with. “And maybe lay off the coffee...and, ya know, drugs.” “Click.” “That wasn’t you hanging up. That was just you saying the word click.” “Umm…I gotta go.” And like that, I’m disconnected from one of my few allies in the world. I’d try Danny, but I dunno what the guy has for time. Besides, this month is supposed to be my month of independence. Fergs and Face are off competing for tag belts; I need to forge my own path. I heard Billy Collins say once that he hated how people use the word “suddenly” to just randomly create tension, but I can’t say this any other way than that I suddenly get a vision of the one person who can deliver me from a funk. I do plan on heading to the mechanic’s shop, but my own dharma requests that I repair my emotional fragility…or something gay like that. * * * * * I made a joke once about how Shari’s apartment complex could be called Perfidious Acres. It’s still true, albeit recycled wit. The lobby of her building stinks like cannolis and money. It’s that type of polished interior that looks like a cheap, artificial reproduction of a human environment. There’s no coziness, no life force on the walls. Then again, considering the “life force” I’ve seen on the walls at some of the places I’ve been, the body-fluid-lacking partitions are nothing to scoff at. She’s changed rooms since the last time I was here during my semi-triumphant run at the GTT. But of course, it ended like every big-time test of skill since: I was there at the end and hey I could hang with those guys, but no banana. I don’t know this from Shari; she’s the definition of “hard-to-get,” ignoring my calls entirely, but leaving enough information with the front desk to be found, if I want it bad enough. I won’t bother with regaling you of all the bad memories that pop up when I knock on the door, made of wood that’s too hard and too hollow. It’s a surprise that she doesn’t make me wait for five minutes, but opens the door before I can even finish knocking the obligatory five seconds. “Interesting.” Well that’s the weirdest fucking greeting I’ve received. To make things even weirder, she opens the door wider and turns, walking into the main room of her apartment without another word. I guess it’s better than the clichéd door slamming in my face, but it makes me doubt whether my lock-box here has been emptied of its reserve carnality. When I get to the main open space of the apartment, Shari is just sitting, watching TV. I’ve never seen this numb indifference from her and for a second, this human twinge of worry strikes at me, but there’s no time to worry about Shari. I need physical release from this funk…and I’m willing to try it with my own person anti-heroine for shits and giggles. In case you’re just joining our viewing program, Shari and I have never gotten along. We have co-existed together, for years at a time in a common space (not a “home,” more like a demilitarized zone). We have done the relationship thing, followed by the cheating thing, followed by the vendetta thing, followed by the lonely midnight booty call. Repeat at will and you’ve got us. “Don’t throw me a parade or anything, Jesus.” It’s always best to start with baiting her because that leads to arguments and arguments lead to sex. Figuring her out is no more complicated than taking all logic and reversing it. “What would you like, Chandler?” I’m used to the chiding, but this type of reception is still strange. “I’d just like a visit, I guess.” “I really didn’t expect this.” I’m about to interrupt that I didn’t expect to want to see her, but she’s not talking about seeing me. “I always thought I’d be the one who gave in and finally came to see you. Be careful; you keep acting like this and you might start to care about people.” “So…what? You’re gonna sit there on the couch and pretend like you’re completely unaffected by my coming here? Don’t kid yourself.” And we head into the bad place; where vitriol comes out from its, “contents under pressure” container and gets slathered all over the walls. “The peaks of your existence come from having your holes filled and, sadly, it doesn’t matter by whom.” Her voice is soft, but incredibly harsh, in reply. She stares right at me while she’s talking to me, sounding like a eulogist. “Sometimes I feel like you almost get it, like you can actually skim the surface of why you’re such a plague to everyone around you.” She stops, but looks unapologetic. I feign indifference with a slight toss of the shoulders and a haughty glance. “What do you want me to say?” My voice is lowered but now I want to accuse her of taking this whole thing out of context. Shari knows that I came here for my libido’s sake. “I know I’m not the first to come here looking for a little release; surely your little toy shop of horrors can’t be pleasing you all that much.” She hates when I talk about her vast library of vibrators; suffice to say, it consumes more electrical power than the nation of Chad. “They’re far more emotionally appealing than you.” It’s going to go back and forth unless someone is the bigger person and decides that we need to figure out our shit together. “What about if I pay you?” That works, right? We can’t come to an emotional agreement, so I can make it up to her in other areas. “Are you out of your fucking mind?” There’s silence for a long time and Shari may be crying, but she’s looking at the floor, so it’s hard to tell. Her face is still beautiful though; it’s stout and flattened like an arrowhead, but bouncing with energy…so long as she’s smiling. “Please leave, Chandler.” I don’t move. I can’t bring myself to plead, but I want to be fulfilled. I want this funk to be lifted and I selfishly want Shari to do it all for me. I don’t care that these years between us have made her fragile and possibly shattered. I don’t care. I don’t care. “Leave.” “No.” She looks up and yes, she’s been crying. She isn’t wearing makeup; doesn’t need it. Still, there are trails leading from cheek to chin, leaving salt particles in their hateful wake. She looks exasperated, tired of it all. Her voice makes me cringe; she is rearing back for a shout, but what comes out is vulnerable and meek. “What are you going to do, then?! Are you gonna fucking rape me?!” The only noise in the apartment is loud sobbing. She thinks I’m a monster; rightfully, so. I want to comfort her, but I fear that my touch will probably drive her mad. It’s a familiar feeling, leaving her crying in her apartment. But it’s not like she’s going anywhere. The only difference is that this time, my appetite hasn’t been sated. In that way, we’re both disappointed in this rendezvous. We’re thirty years old and we’re still doing this. That’s us. And as I get into the car to drive off, the sinking feeling only grows. The car starts and sputters and I have no doubt I’ll make it to the mechanic’s shop, albeit slowly and painfully. Still, I’ve gotten no respite from my fears. There is no panacea. Welcome to the funk.
