Musings

22 May 2007

Date: 5/22/07 I find it difficult, at times like this, to really get up and get moving towards a different goal. Life is built intrinsically on a goal to goal basis. You move from one to the next in a never ending cycle, your achievements piling up as your hair grows grayer and your joints grow sore. And sometimes you just want to exhale. Sometimes you just want to enjoy the ride, but you can't. Your breath catches. Your heart feels too heavy. Whenever I'm just downright exhausted, it feels like my heart is about to explode. Not in that large explosion sort of way. Kind of like an implosion of sorts. Like a little pin is jabbed through it, causing pain to surge with each murmur. The stench of nicotine fill the atmosphere, even outdoors. Seventy degrees and it's dark outside here as the lights flicker neon in the City Of Angels. Part of the reason I chose to live in the Medici in Downtown was because it acted as a sort of status symbol, coming from where I was. But I also liked the lay out of the place. And I always was partial to a place with a balcony. Many a lonely night has been spent out here, with the steady breeze of exhaust and wind hitting me in the face. A glass ashtray, colored a darker yellow, rest just to my left on a small table try. It's a small thing, plastic and green. I do not have to reach out far, because gravity takes care of my ashes. And there were plenty of those. The butts, looking like miniature wine corks stabbed into the ground like a javelin, stand upward from my jabbing motions. When I finish off a smoke, I literally slam it down to kill it. And if it isn't a damn ashtray, it's a forceful toss to the concrete. Smoking calms my nerves at times. Times like the ones I precariously find myself in. Times where you are questioning yourself, be it what you did or what you are doing. And it is never a quick and easy fix. True self introspection rarely is. We are all flawed human beings, trying our best to put up a facade. To fight to be better for it. To have success and to be accepted. I know my flaws, and I have come to live with them. But my biggest issue is one of waste. My career in wrestling to be more precise. Nine years is it? Eight? It bleeds together in a stupor haze. But what I do remember is the fight to get here. To make it to the top level and to put a stamp on it. To make my bank account as fat as it possibly can for as long as I can stay healthy. I pay four thousand fifty dollars for my one thousand six hundred square feet in this city with a view and a pool, where as a few months ago I was living in a shithole with bars on the windows. But in the end, I feel I've grown stagnant. Getting up, the end of another cancer stick hitting the ashtray, I look out at the world I made for myself. A world built upon broken dreams and broken relationships. Of one night stands. Of sleeping on hotel room floors. To the top of the top. Working for PRIME. Being the man in PRIME. But it felt hallow, empty almost. I'd lost my running mate. I'd lost my girl for a while. I'd become nothing but a piece of dog shit, eating table scraps and hoping for the best. And even here, now that I am back at a place that is supposed to be the top tier, I just do not feel everything is right. What the hell is it? She stands in the door way, a bulky t-shirt cascading down her body. Her wild mane framing her head like a lion, her face shrouded in darkness. She's up, for whatever reason. Me...I got my reasons. I'm not exactly used to someone living with me anymore, and my insomniac bouts were allowed to run without a check. And she knows that. At first she took it as a sign that I didn't care for her. But now she understands. We both understand. It's been weird, having her back like it was before. After all that had happened. After cracking her jaw. After we both abused our resources. And karma...sometimes she can get you. I'm not a good man, but for whatever reason I keep getting ace's on my deal. You get only one life to live, and one second chance at this life to give it what you have. Which makes me wonder. When this run dries up...when it ends...where do I go from here? Where do we go from here? She doesn't know about the brochures and packets from UCLA and USC. From UC Santa Barbra and Fresno. From San Diego State. She had, for a year, tried to goad me into looking into school. To get back and finish up my bachelors for whatever it was. And with the amount of dates I worked, it was impossible. I didn't want that life. I didn't want the mundane, monotone existence of punching that clock. But on some days, like today, I do. I would like to be able to fade away into obscurity and live on a ranch somewhere with Melissa and have five kids. Would love nothing more than to right the wrong that was that first marriage. In the world of fantasy, she is supposed to be ready to go. When your girl stands there, like that, you are supposed to grab her and make her yours. But this is reality. She groans, stepping onto the porch. She waves her hands back and forth, trying to get the stench of tobacco as far away from her nostrils as possible. “Brandon...come on.” Her eyes are half closed, her face in a pained expression of fatigue. “I know.” I say, looking at her. “Can't you stop smokin'?” Asking rather than telling. She always wanted the best for me, but rarely in the middle portion of the marriage had we ever legit dealt with things head on. We always brushed them under the carpet, putting a pretty face on everything. Lying through our teeth. “Soon Missy. It's late...shouldn't yah be in bed? Don't you got to be to work in a few hours?” “Yeah...” she moans, resting against the side of the building, her feet smacking against the cement in soft thuds. “Yeah I got it. We only have a few weeks left...or days. Days maybe. Maybe weeks. Who knows...I sure as fuck don't right now.” I laugh at her, and she flashes me that smile in response, giving me the finger. “Yeah yeah yeah...for us who actually work real jobs we don't get the chance to just sit up all night.” I just shake my head towards her. “Missy...you're going to bed.” “Only if you come too.” It was a soft whimper almost, as if I was going to be her big teddy bear to ward off the demons of the night. Which to many is a sick form of irony. The big bad wolf being a big softy. But it is tough some days, to do this. To get on that plane. To say goodbye to her. And I didn't know it until now. Maybe she is the reason for this feeling. Maybe she is what is making it not worth it. The thought that history could very easily repeat itself. But I do not go with her, not immediately. I just sit back in my chair, smoking a cigarette, putting up this large front of toughness that I can't let be cracked. Not for our sake.
