Becoming I: May 11

10 May 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 5/10/2006 Ask most people what Hokkaido is, and you’ll get confused looks. Americans aren’t especially good with geography, it seems. But ask a Japanese person, or someone who actually bothers to look at a map every once in a while, and they’ll tell you that Hokkaido is the northernmost of the four Japanese home islands. Why does this matter, you might wonder? It matters because, on the island of Hokkaido, Kenjiro Ito ceased to be a young kid with stars in his eyes and became, you know, Kenjiro Ito. The Bringer of the Ice Age. The Cocky Osakan. One of the biggest douchebags in wrestling, bar none. The long and the short of it was that Ito LOATHED Hokkaido. Almost as much as he hates do-gooders like Johnny Noble. But unfortunately, when push came to shove, and Ito needed a place to train, the Hokkaido WrestleFactory was the only dojo for him. It was where he had been trained to begin with for the life he now led. “Fucking freezing,” he groused, even as he stepped out of the taxicab. Hokkaido had a reputation for being rather cold, and it was living up to it on this day. With his duffel bag slung over his shoulder, Kenjiro Ito looked even more surly than usual. His displeasure seemingly grew as he stomped towards the WrestleFactory. It had been some time since he’d returned. After all, there was no need to train for the stupid spot shows he’d been doing here in his homeland while waiting for a real promotion to come calling. “Fucking PRIME and its obsession with fucking spotfests,” he muttered, clearly annoyed. “Didn’t have to put up with this shit in Jolt. I got one on one matches and beat everyone in my path. Only Ambassador Champion worth a damn, fuck, only PERSON in Jolt worth a damn! And it’s the same shit here in PRIME, except I have to face four no-talents in one night instead of one to get a shot at a belt that they should just GIVE me for joining this shithole.” Was he arrogant? Yes. But as per usual, he felt he was justified in being so. Kenjiro Ito was the rare man who WAS everything he said he was. The unique individual who could back up 95% of the shit that streamed from his mouth. Kenjiro was as dangerous in the ring as with a live mic, something that had made him one of Jolt Wrestling’s most successful, if not popular, stars. That was one of the reasons he hated the Hokkaido WrestleFactory. It reminded him of when he was a nobody. Back then, he was just another kid with a dream. A kid with skills, yes, but still a kid. Not important in the slightest. Now, he couldn’t imagine how he’d lived back then, being just another face in the crowd. Fuck, his entire life now was designed to stand out. He was wearing a powder blue custom-tailored suit, designed to his every specification and in his favorite shade. Snazzy black shoes completed his outfit, and those fuckers had been polished to within an inch of their lives. Kenjiro Ito didn’t want to be just another unmemorable schlub walking the streets of New York or Tokyo. Fuck no. He wanted to be THE story, the man that mattered. And that, ultimately, was why he was on the doorstep of a place that he loathed with every fiber of his being. By training here, and getting back into peak condition, he would become the #1 contender to the PRIME Internet Championship. Grudgingly, he raised his right hand, and knocked on the front door three times. The wait was excruciating. The WrestleFactory didn’t let outsiders in, due to its long and storied program of training that had been developed in absolutely secrecy. Only those training and teaching at the dojo generally were allowed inside, though exceptions were made for former students as well. People like Ito. Slowly, the door opened, revealing a young-looking Japanese man. No, Ito thought. Not him. Anyone but him… “Kenjiro!” the other man exclaimed. “You look well! It’s been so long, I doubt you even rememb--” The Bringer of the Ice Age fought valiantly to prevent the forming scowl from overtaking his face. “Of course I remember you, Ryusuke.” Whether I want to or not… “You must be here to train,” Ryusuke said, continuing on with his native Japanese. Ito was having slightly more difficulty than usual with his native language, presumably due to his time in America. “Yes,” Kenjiro replied, a simple, terse response. “Come then,” came the response. “I will show you to your quarters. The master put me in charge of affairs such as these.” Ryusuke led the way into the building, with Kenjiro following at a bit of a distance. He found himself appraising the man from behind. For the most part, he looked the same as he had those few years ago. Still well put together. Still walking with the air of dignity that identified him as Ryusuke Fujishima, Chosen One. “Where are you competing nowadays,” Ryusuke called over his shoulder. “Last I heard, you were in Jolt in America. You ever coming back to Japan? I’d love to come watch you wrestle, you know.” Ito forced the bile down his throat long enough to respond. “Still in America. This time with PRIME. It’s a bit more sports entertainment-ish than you’d like, but it pays the bills, so I’m not complaining. Besides, after that stunt I pulled at my first press conference, I don’t think Inoki-san’s hiring.” Fujishima chuckled, a low, rich laugh, before responding. “No, I suppose not. You know the Japanese press pilloried you for that. You were supposed to be the next big thing.” No, Fujishima-san. You were. “You know me,” Ito replied. “Always the rabble-rouser. You honestly think I could have tolerated having to use a Boston Crab as my finisher? Fuck no. The IIA’s my baby.” “Does it still have an unblemished record,” Ryusuke asked, turning a corner and beginning down the hallway that led to the dojo’s dormitory. “Of course!” Kenjiro replied. “Ain’t NO ONE kicking out of the IIA. I’ve even come up with variants for it, so I’ve got one for all four guys I’m fighting at Ultra Violence.” Fujishima frowned. “That mean you’re fighting in one of those deathmatches? I thought you were smarter than that, Kenjiro.” “Nah, it’s just the name of the PPV. It’s a one fall to a finish match, there’s just five guys in the ring. If it’s possible, I’d like to get some ring time with four of the trainees, get used to having four guys in the ring with me. They won’t all be attacking me at once, hopefully, but with that many people in the ring, they can come at any angle.” “Pay per view? We’ll order it for the dojo then,” Ryusuke said, with a grin. “God knows you’re one of the dojo’s best known students. Since you showed up, we’ve gotten a lot more applicants. You know that sumo wrestler, Izumo Yoritomo? He just graduated. Off to make his way in the wrestling world, just like you. And he showed up because he heard you’d trained here. You’re good advertising….well, except for the fact that people always want us to teach them how to ‘talk shit like Ito-san’.” Fujishima grimaced, while Ito grinned. “Hey, you can be straight laced if you want. Me? I’m loud, and I’m proud. Besides, it’s not bullshit if you can back it up, and I always can!” Kenjiro fell silent for a few minutes, as they paused outside the door to the dorms. “Room 3, the bottom bunk,” Fujishima said. “Same one as always, if that’s okay with you.” Ito nodded, and looked into the eyes of the man whom he had once trained with. The question that had been on his mind since first meeting Ryusuke at the door could no longer go unasked. “How’s your neck,” Kenjiro Ito asked, eyes casting downwards to the floor. With a knowing smile, Fujishima responded. “It’s fine, Kenjiro. One of the interesting things about the line of work we’re in is injuries that end our wrestling careers tend not to stop us from living full, healthy lives. Insurance covered the medical bills, and Sensei Kanda hired me to look after the day-to-day affairs of the WrestleFactory. I even help teach the trainees on occasion…that’s why I keep myself in ring shape.” Another pause, before Ito sheepishly said, “I haven’t done the belly to belly off the top since that day. I’m sorry, Ryusuke.” Ryusuke Fujishima, a man once considered to be the next Jumbo Tsuruta, looked at Kenjiro Ito, the former Jolt Ambassador and Triple Crown Champion, and shook his head. “There’s no need to apologize, Kenjiro. It was the heat of battle. Accidents happen. I know you’re sorry for what happened, but really, everything’s worked out okay. I came to grips with the fact that I would never step into the ring professionally long ago. I’ve injured my fair share of trainees accidentally as well. It happens, especially with the style we teach here. Let the past remain in the past.” Ito fell silent for a few moments, thinking about that, until his friend spoke up again. “Shall I send for you in an hour? The afternoon class will begin then, and judging by the fact that you’ve been living well in America, you need all the exercise you can get…” Finally, Ito chuckled a bit. “Certainly. I’ll be ready. Just need to change into my gear. Wrestling in a suit isn’t especially comfortable.” “Then I’ll leave you to that, and…” Fujishima said, offering a bow which Ito returned. Before leaving, he said two more words, two words that made Kenjiro Ito’s guts roil. “Welcome home.” ------------------------------------------------------------------ Lying here on the same cot that I slept on for three years of my life, I can’t help but be bitter. Ryusuke Fujishima was supposed to be the future of puroresu, not I. No matter how hard I trained, no matter how many times I repeated my moves, he was always better. Always. I’d been taught all things were possible with hard work. Hard work had gotten me to be a star baseball player for my school team. It had gotten me, believe it or not, pretty good grades as well. But I didn’t want to go to university. I didn’t want to play baseball. I wanted to be a professional wrestler. No, not just ANY professional wrestler. The best. EVER. How could I be the best ever when a student in my class was already better than I was?! I’ll never forget the day. May 11th, 2002. The day that Kenjiro Ito as the world knows him was born. I am sorry, Ryusuke Fujishima. I am sorry for breaking your neck. I am sorry that it had to be you, a man who has not a mean bone in his body. But put me back on that top turnbuckle, four years ago? Put me back in that situation, with the world to gain and nothing to lose? I would break your neck again. Nothing personal, Ryusuke. Strictly…business.
