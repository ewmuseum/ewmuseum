Dual Halo: 10 minutes with Your Sovereign...

15 March 2008
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 15/03/2008 The World of Wrestling Obsever’s chief on the road reporter Macy Williams takes a deep breath and desperately tries to compose herself. This is it, she tells herself, this is the big time. How could it not be? Granted she doesn’t know a whole lot about the mysterious man known to the world only as Union Jack, but he is royalty after all and the British monarchy are always plastered all over the news feeds in some form or another. Princess Diana is still big news, hell, she’s been dead ten years and the British press still write a new story about her every damn day. And then there’s all this nonsense about her two delusion boys, Harry and William, who actually want to go to Iraq and serve on the frontlines. Fucking idiots. Yes, she persuades herself, this is the big time. The door of his grandiose dressing room quietly opens all of a suddenly and Jeeves, Union Jack’s elderly butler enters, taking a lengthy bow. “Pardon me, ma’am. Allow me to introduce ‘Your Sovereign’ Union Jack…” With the elegant introduction, he proudly strides into the locker room proudly already kitted out in his wrestling attire; all-in-one union jack tights, including mask and gloves, along with an overly flamboyant sequined cape. The man looks like a peacock looking for a mate, Tyler silently decides, standing and promptly freezing on the spot. Should I go all out and curtsey, or just shake his hand? She opts for the latter. “Hello Mr, um, Jack…I’m Macy Williams.” Union Jack ignores her extended hand and bizarrely pauses, extending his arms out wide for Jeeves to dutifully unclip his master’s cape and fold it over his arm. Then, once clear, Union Jack carefully lowers himself into the comfortable grasp of the expensive-looking leather chair opposite the roving reporter with a thankful sigh, not once setting eyes on the woman. “Tea, Jeeves,” he pompously demands. “One’s mouth is as dry as Ghandi’s flip-flop…” With a sigh, Jeeves bows. “Very good, sir.” Jeeves scurries off to a back room, whilst Tyler scrunches her face up confused. What the hell kind of accent is that? It sounds nothing like the queen…doesn’t even sound like Hugh Grant. And what kind of aristocrat uses phrases as vulgar as that? What the hell is going on here? Union Jack finally regards the roving reporter carefully, developing a facial expression under his mask that Tyler decides to take as disgust. “You’re the interviewer they sent?“ A flash of anger quickly washes over Williams’ face, but she soon removes it. “Yes,” she replies, through gritted teeth, “why do you ask?” “Well, you’re a woman aren‘t you?” He replies. “Surely a single mother to twelve children and living off the state? Why work?” A wave of recognition splashes over his face, prompting him to nod knowingly. “Ah! Benefit fraud, eh?” “Benefit fraud?” Williams repeats, finding it difficult to further bury her anger. “Just what the hell are you implying? That women should just stay at home and cook and clean and looking after their husbands and children?” Union Jack shakes his head. “Of course not, luv. I don’t expect you to have a husband, being a single mother is quite a popular occupation as of late. You get more money if the child’s father isn’t on the scene and if you’re a legal immigrant, your children don’t even have to be in the country…” Williams shakes her head bewildered and decides to change the subject entirely. “Union Jack, if I may say you don’t much sound like an aristocrat…” Union Jack stares at her blankly as Jeeves scuttles into the room, with a tray in hand on which lies a teapot and two cups. Williams does not notice it right away, but when the butler places the tray down upon the dressing room table it becomes quite clear that he is in the throws of laughter. “An aristocrat indeed! Hahahaha, good one ma‘am!” Union Jack turns and shoots Jeeves a stern look, who is visibly struggling to compose himself whilst pouring the tea. “That will be quite enough of that, Jeeves and no Ms Tyler, you may not say that. I’m a very important person, so I suggest you do what you came here to do; my time is something you cannot afford to waste.” Tyler shakes her head. The arrogance of this man is astonishing, she decides, he must be British nobility! Remembering her career and conveniently forgetting her pride, her razor sharp mind and desire to succeed takes control and the interview begins. “So, Mr Jack it is quite obvious that you’re a man of some wealth, so I guess the question is, why?” Williams prompts, the record button of her Dictaphone at the ready. “Why choose to involve yourself in the world of wrestling?” A smile formulates upon Union Jack’s mouth. “Simple. I turned on the TV, watched a wrestling show for the first time and thought ‘I could do that’.” Williams raises both eyebrows speculatively. “That’s how you decided to embark on your career? You didn’t follow it throughout formative years and develop a passion for the business?” Union Jack shakes his head. “No. I was just bored.” “You were bored? Seriously?” Williams rolls her eyes and shakes her head. “How the hell did you get anybody to even train you? Any half decent trainer out there would sooner snap your ass in two rather train somebody up who isn’t even interested in the damn business!” “Money, ma’am,” Jeeves interjects, placing their respective cups upon the elegant oak coffee table (oh, the irony!) before them. “Like Jeeves said, money,” Union Jack confirms, immediately scooping up his hot British beverage from the table. “I have money in abundance and I just threw it at them. Apparently they love money more than they love passion.” Williams digests the notion and is surprised by how readily she embraces it. “So having no real passion for the business, how did you fair learning the trade?” “Much to my chagrin,” Jeeves began, “sir was a natural.” Williams raised an eyebrow. “Your chagrin?” Union Jack nods. “Jeeves helped train me.” “My father worked carnivals in the fifties,” Jeeves explains, offering a tray of biscuits (cookies for you damned American’s!). “I wanted to follow in his footsteps, but he wouldn’t allow me to. He demanded that I get an proper education…” “…and look where it got him!” Union Jack interrupted with a chuckle. “A skivvy…a bad one at that! No Bourbon’s, Jeeves?” “No sir,” Jeeves sighed. “Sorry, sir. The shipment didn‘t arrive yet, sir.” “So when you learnt your trade,” Williams begins, “did you get your bookings easily? When did you decided to come to America? When--” “Excuse me, sir,” Jeeves interrupts. “It’s three pm. You’re overdue for photo shoots in the main lobby.” Union Jack nods and immediately rises. “My public awaits…” Williams also rises, her face filled with anger. “What about our interview?” “Your ten minutes are up,” Union Jack tells her, allowing his faithful butler to refit his majestic cape. “Just be glad that I even granted you a moment of my time!” “But--” Jeeves bows, as Union Jack leaves the room. “Excuse us, ma’am.” Williams hurls her Dictaphone down in anger. “God damn it!”
