In The Hands of God (Prologue)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 1978 Her skin was nearly as pale as her son's. She was beautiful and seemed to shine so bright that were the sun to burn out, she could rise up and take it's place without argument or question. Ask any man that had known her and they'd tell you she seemed to draw men into orbit. If you placed in her the ocean by a rocky shore, she would be a beacon to lost sailors. She was a siren out of the tales of Odysseus. Her beauty was without doubt and eventually would be matched only by her utter lack of sanity. There was a time when she was a beauty queen. A goddess standing among mere mortals, whose worshippers seemed to fill the majority of Truth or Consequences, New Mexico all the way to the lid. The fair-skinned princess, who would've no doubt make some rich man a great trophy someday, seemed to throw away her future the instant she graduated from high school. She had gotten pregnant. The only man she knew who truly loved her, a black man who left town, appeared never to have loved her at all. Before announcing it to her family she told him first. He called her a whore and a slut and kicked her in the stomach before leaving town, like the coward he was. Her name was Rachel, his Chris. She still had notebooks with their names written all over it, a heart drawn around them to capture the love she had thought they shared. Their relationship had fueled the racial fires of the town at the time. Though Sierra County did not have the sort of racial bigotry found in the Southeast that year of 1978, it was enough to start several fights in their highschool. She could not have cared any less about any of that. An accepting girl, who'd give any human the benefit of the doubt, proclaimed her love for Chris was so strong that it impaired her vision with a great and wonderful color-blindness. In the matter of time it took for him to cum inside of her, perception changed. Ashamed and embarassed, she hid her pregnancy from every one. She practiced all sorts of horrible things to prevent showing the baby in her womb. She starved herself and when she could no longer, she'd purge whatever she stuffed inside of her as quick as she'd get it down. It was the worst eight and a half months of her life. Eventually people would begin to take notice of how weak she appeared. The baby in her stomach was fighting for every nutrient he could, arguing with the body of the mother and stealing the little amount of energy she could produce without eating on a regular basis. It took a physical toll on her. A loving mother and father she had of her own walked in on her laying on the floor, passed out cold and unable to wake up. Finally, she would wake up in the hospital. Her hazy eyes would make out images of her weeping mother and father. "What's going on?" She asked them. "You fell, Honey," Her father said with warm eyes. "It'll be ok." She closed her eyes once more. Then something unexpected was said that rocked poor Rachel to her frail and weak core. "Your baby... why didn't you tell us about the baby?" Her heart-broken mother asked. Distraught her father couldn't stop himself from crying out, "Oh, God!" Rachel wept. As surely as any mother would do but with an entirely different reason. She cried until the tears stained her pale complexion. Even in that hospital bed with a cesarian scar she'd have the rest of her life, she was the most beautiful, young lady in the entire town. But inside of her skin, that beautiful soul of hers had been tainted by the man she'd loved and the inner turmoil she'd felt for the entire stint of her pregnancy. What cruel, horrible God would allow a piece of trash like Chris to live on and do the same to countless other women? What kind of horrible, sick bastard was this God anyway? The God she thought she knew from every Sunday she'd ever spent in her life was only vengeful like this in the Old Testament. Nearly two-thousand years later, he was punishing her for sharing her virginity before she was married. She knew this to be true. When the child was brought into the room by a nurse and given to her, she only wept more. She loved him! She named him Rhett, from her favorite character in her favorite book and movie. "He was born with Albinism," the nurse shared. Only then did Rachel, the new mother, realize what her baby was. His skin was paler than hers. His baby hair, though usually babies were born with darker hair, was so blonde it was nearly white. He had Chris' nose. He even had Chris' smile. She was disgusted. Though however much she loved him and indeed it was a lot, everytime she'd look at him over the course of the rest of his life she would only see the one man who she had loved and lost, thanks to him. Rachel Locke would blame everyone the rest of her life for the birth of Rhett. Everyone but herself, anyway. In years to come, she'd do terrible and horrible things as she dwelled on the awful things that had happened to her. Life wasn't fair and it was all her baby's fault. Yet, she loved him. Though she would never figure out the right way to express it, she loved him. In the next 7 to 8 years to come, she would be the bane of her child's existence. A crazy and unfit mother of one who never deserved it and never asked to be born in the first place. Her love would twist to a hateful obsession of all things around her and it would be exercised on his poor heart... ...and, for the most part, he would only lament with quiet weeping. As she did in that moment, holding him tightly.
