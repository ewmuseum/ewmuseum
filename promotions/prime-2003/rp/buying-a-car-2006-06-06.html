Buying a car

6 June 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: June 6 “So, tell me son,” the man says with a slimy grin, “what is it that you're looking for? A big truck, a nice van, what can I do ya for big fella?” The day had finally arrived. It was, at least in Titan's mind, time to purchase a vehicle. He'd kind of reached the point where hitching a ride to the airport from his family was getting old and decided instead to find something that fit his needs. Of course, every dealer he visited was severely lacking in what he needed: namely something he could fit in. Right now, he was looking at a rotund car salesman on a dealership lot, fighting every impulse that picked up on the deceptive signals the man was giving off. “Let's just start with something I can fit in,” Titan said with a sigh. He already felt greasy from dealing with the man. “Well sure thing, big fella!” he said with a huge fake smile. “Why don't you follow me?” In a lot filled with the gleaming shine of new cars that can't ever be reproduce no matter how much you wash, the man led the giant towards the back of the lot where the larger vehicles sat. Titan shook his head at the assortment before him. In his military days, all he had driven was a Humvee, and most of the time, he hadn't even driven it, instead put in the back like some sort of well-guarded VIP. Here, before him, stretched a series of trucks, vans and jeeps. Titan already knew that Jeeps weren't going to work in any fashion. So, of course, they were the first thing that the salesman led him to. “Now, this right here is the Jeep Wrangler Unlimited, being the Wrangler with the most options and...” “No,” Titan said simply. “You sure 'bout that big guy?” the man said, turning and sticking his hands in his pockets. He was already digging in, and it was just the first vehicle. “Do you honestly think this is the first lot I've gone to today? I can't fit in these things,” the giant replied, already feeling slightly annoyed. “Well, that's fair enough. Let's take us a look at these trucks over here.” Titan shook his head, watching the man's walk. He may as well be rubbing his hands together. He was salivating over the chance to sell one of these trucks, probably trying to load Titan up on options. Titan let the man walk on, in his irritating little waddle. The man walked with one foot in front of the other and accentuated almost everything he did with over-exaggerated hand motions. Titan was really wanting to just kill the man from all the annoying signals. “Now, this right here is the two-thousand and six...” He looked around, confused at losing his seven-foot tall prospective customer. * * * “Son, what did you do?” Titan's mother asked him. “Well, mom, I wasn't about to buy a car from someone like that. So I went back inside and asked for someone else. So I got a pleasant little retired fellow,” Titan noted with a sigh of past relief. “So what did you end up getting?” she asked with a smile. “Well,” he said with a smile, “where is dad?” “Oh, he went down to take his car to the mechanic so you could pick him up,” the older woman said with a smile. Titan laughed in response. “Well, why don't you step outside and we'll see?” the big man replied. So they did, and there, in the bright Georgia sun, sat a brand-new black Cadillac Escalade. His mother turned on him, her eyes wide and her mouth already set to lecture. “Where did you get the money for this?” his mother demanded. “Mom, I was in the military so long, I had more cash than I needed in my account. So, I figured since I could fit in this, why not go for it?” he said with a shrug. He knew his mother's shock was just an act. He'd known that expression long before Profile 21 ever came along. She smiled up at her big son. “Well, aren't we going to go get your father?” * * * The big SUV came to a halt just outside of a large, brownstone-style apartment building. With a laugh, a slim, redheaded woman of about five-foot-five ran out of the building in heels and pulled the passenger door open. She jumped inside like a giddy child and turned to the big man in the driver's seat. “Oh my God!” Stephanie shouted with glee. “This is amazing! You bought an Escalade!” Titan could only smile at her. She looked great in her little black cocktail dress. He resisted the urge to tug at his collar again. The big man hated ties and dress shirts; they always felt too tight. But the sheer joy on her face made him forget about that. “Well, you know, I could fit in it,” he said with a smile to her. She laughed and smacked his arm. “This is great!” she exclaimed. “I almost don't want to go to dinner, just ride around in this.” “So you want drive-through?” the big man asked. “No!” she said, hitting his arm again. “I just almost want to skip dinner altogether.” “Nope,” the big man said, “not happening at all.” She laughed, and Titan smiled. A girlfriend, a nice car, happy parents... maybe this is how regular people lived.
