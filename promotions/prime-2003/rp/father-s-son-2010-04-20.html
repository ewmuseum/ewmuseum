Father's Son

20 April 2010
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 4/20/10 ~Recovering~ Mid April in Ohio “So how’s he doin’?” Matt opened the refrigerator in his parents’ kitchen and surveyed each shelf till he locked on to the last remaining bottle of Blue Moon. “You should ask him.” “Tried. He didn’t seem too interested in talking.” “He’s been kinda mood lately.” “Can’t say I blame him.” Matt twisted the cap off his beer and tossed it on the countertop near the sink. “So what’s the deal, Mom? How’d the surgery go?” “…Good.” “What’s ‘good’ mean?” “It went well.” Christine Ward grabbed the bottle cap and shot her eldest a glare as she marched across the kitchen and dumped it in the recycling. “But, the doctor had said he thought he might be able to get almost all of it through the surgery, and based on the pathology, he thinks he only got ninety-percent.” “Only? Isn’t ninety damn-near a hundred?” “Not to your father.” “That’s ridiculous.” “The doctor originally told him he thought he would be able to get ninety-nine percent of it. So I think your dad got his hopes up a little bit.” “Still, that’s ninety-percent less cancer than he had a month ago.” Matt stepped into the archway, leaning against its frame as he stared into the living room. His father sat in a heap on the edge of the couch, sleeping, legs propped up in front of him. His t-shirt looked old and worn, punctuated by a bit of dried blood on the side, staining it brown where the surgery wound had seeped through, and the pair of sweats he wore looked like they were straight from the 1980s. They probably were. As he watched his father sleep, mouth hanging half-open, television blasting some muscle car show for an audience of none, Matt tried to think back, struggling to remember the last time he’d seen his old man in a pair of sweatpants. “Is he sleeping?” “Yeah.” Matt turned back to the kitchen and took a drink from his beer. “Has he been sleeping a lot?” “Not really. Not at night, anways.” “You gettin’ any sleep?” “A little bit.” There was an uncomfortable silence as Matthew tried to think of something to say. Asking ‘why’ was pointless; he already knew the answer. ‘Sorry you can’t sleep’ sounded meaningless. Taking another drink of his beer, he opted for the general offer of help. “Listen, if you or Dad need anything, just let Mary or I know. We’ll do whatever we can.” “Thanks, honey. I appreciate it.” Christine opened up the cabinet next to the dishwasher and pulled out a large pan, figuring it was as good a time as any to start on dinner. “But unless you have a wench, I don’t think there’s much you can do.” “Whaddaya mean?” “Something to drag your father off of that couch. He just sits around all day.” “I thought the doctors told him to get up and walk around as much as possible.” “Has your father ever listened to his doctors? The first day we got back from the hospital, he really tried. He got up off the couch every half hour or so. Weather was nice, so we took a walk down to the street corner. I wanted to go around the block, but he said he didn’t think he was up for that. So we turned around and headed back. The next day, he complained about the pain, said he must have walked around too much, and he’s been glued to the couch pretty much ever since.” “Is he takin’ his pain meds?” “No. He refuses.” “Is he outta his mind? Those things do wonders. There’s a reason doctors prescribe ‘em. Help my ass out a ton. Tell him get off the couch, move around, take his medication and get back on the road to recovery.” “I dunno, Matthew. To be honest, I think part of him has already given up.” “I sure as hell hope not.” “Me too, sweetheart.” Matt craned his neck over his shoulder, looking back into the living room. There on the corner of the couch, he saw the blood-soaked leftovers of a complex surgery. He saw a wardrobe of old ratty clothes, and a mountain of exhaustion. The one thing he hoped to see was the one thing he didn’t. Just a little bit of fight. ~Pondering~ Chainz… I’m not really feelin’ it. At all. This isn’t the kinda dude a guy looks forward to squaring off against. Especially given the context. Sure, there’s the possibility of walking out with a newly won championship belt, and all the accolades that come with, but that’s not much comfort. Win or lose, you’re likely to be less concerned with title belts, and more concerned with getting that gash on your head stapled shut, or whether the doctors will be able to pop that particular body part back in place. It really is a daunting task, given the guy’s physical strength and his mental derangements. The sorta thing that makes ya wonder why you’re even putting yourself through this. At least, it makes me wonder. Why in God’s name am I doing this? It isn’t for the glory or the fame. I’ve achieved everything I ever dreamed of in this business. Multiple world championships, Dual Halo victories, tournament victories. They’ve already got my bust in… well, wherever the hell the PRIME Hall of Fame is. I’ve already had my moment in the spotlight. And it sure isn’t for the financial gain. I’m making good money, but not the sort of money I was making with PRIME back in 06 and 07 when I was main eventing every PPV in sellout stadiums. Those paychecks were damn near impossible to walk away from. These are nice, but nothing I can’t walk away from. Besides, contract specifics aside, cash isn’t a problem . Between 4w and PRIME, I’ve made enough money to provide for my family… I wouldn’t need to ever work another day in my life. It must not be for the pure, undying passion for the business. If I’m questioning what the hell I’m doing, the commitment can’t be that strong. And yet here I am, set to step into the ring on April 24th with one of the most vicious, sadistic men in the professional wrestling industry. I must be stupid. This guy has mauled people, earned a reputation as a monster. It’s not that I haven’t been there before, countless times, it’s that now… I’m not sure what’s fueling it. It’s not the money, it’s not the fame, not the passion. The Intense Title is prestigious, but is it worth the punishment for a past-his-prime wrestler? The smart thing, it would seem, would be to quit and walk away (before April 24th if I’m real smart). Slip quietly out the back door into a peaceful retirement. Give up. Just stop fighting. Maybe I’d get a cushy seat at the desk with Nick and Richard, calling the action instead of being right in the middle of it. Maybe be driven crazy by my wife and kids, or killed by the boredom of every day routine. But I won’t do that. I’ll fly to Texas, drive to the AT&T center, hop into the ring, and I’ll swing for the fences, hoping to take off Chainz’ head before he takes off mine. And maybe when its over ten or twenty minutes later, I’ll take his title too. Not because of the riches. Not because I love this stuff too much. Because I don’t know what’s looming on the other side of quitting. It’s really that simple, I guess. Keep fighting, cuz there’s something terrifying about the repercussions of givin’ up. ~Worrying~ Mid April in Ohio “He needs to do what the doctors told him, and laying around all day ain’t what they told him to do.” Matthew hadn’t been home from his parents for five minutes and his hands were already shaking as he ranted, finding no more an audience than his father’s television had. “Did you say somethin’, baby?” Mary headed down the hall towards the living room, as her husband marched into the kitchen. “Forget it.” “No. What is it? I just had to put the kids down for a nap.” “I was just talking about Dad. He’s not taking care of himself.” Matt rummaged through the one of the cabinets, pushing bottles and cups aside. “Have you seen my painkillers?” “They’re behind the sink.” “Thanks.” “So what’s he doing? Or not doing?” “Listening to his doctors. Or my mother.” “Shocking.” Mary began picking up random articles in the living room… toys on the floor, shirts thrown across the back of the recliner. “A husband not listening to his wife. Who would have ever imagined!” “Cute.” Matt twisted the cap off of his painkillers and popped one of the pills. Looking over the bottle, he held it up, giving it a shake, its contents rattling inside. “He isn’t taking his medication. He isn’t getting up and walking around. The guy’s got cancer, and it’s like he’s not even taking it seriously.” “You should talk to him, sweetie.” “He won’t listen to me. He’s stubborn. Real thick skull, and eventually, it’s going to get the best of ‘im.” “I worry about the same thing.” “Dad losing the fight?” “Not exactly.” “Then what?” “Nothing, it’s just,” looking into kitchen, she stared at her husband, worn and weathered, “ya know what they say... you are your father’s son.” Before Matthew could make any response, the telephone rang, pulling away his wife’s attention. Heading for the phone, Mary took the mess of clothes and toys tucked under her arm and sat them in a heap on the edge of the couch.
