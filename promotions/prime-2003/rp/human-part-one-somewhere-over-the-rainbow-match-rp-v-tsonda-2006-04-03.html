Human : Part One - Somewhere Over The Rainbow (Match RP - v Tsonda & X3)

3 April 2006
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/04/06 Somewhere Over The Rainbow.Written: 3rd April, 2006. 'People are fragile things, you should know by now.Be careful what you put them through.' - Editors. I - The Beginning Is The Middle. Smiles brought warmth to the nippy morning, while joyous laughter replaced the birdsongs that were yet to return from warmer climes. A fault with the streetlamps left overlapping halos of faint amber tint to the sidewalk, while a hurried conga of slapping footsteps followed the short stretch of yellow brick road to where the stopping bus overshot its mark. Despite the half-hourly cycle of arrivals and departures, the playful children dismounted the swings and see-saws to rush to the fence, their energetic waves being reciprocated by a few of the elderly passengers nestled into their warm bus seats. The sweet aroma of the lingering exhaust fumes were soon swept away by a well-timed, chilly gust, tumbling it down the street on a flying carpet of dried sycamore leaves. Already counting down the remaining 29 minutes, the swings were restored to their pendulum motions, and the world sped back up around the accelerating round-about. Proud and mothers kept dutiful watch from the corners of their eyes, investing the rest of their focus in the gossip that bounded about, while the competitive fathers repeated words of encouragement as their offspring unwittingly jousted for athletic supremacy on the climbing frames. On the wrought iron bench backing up against the fence, and pair of doting grandparents sat, guarding a carrier bag full of sweets and treats for their rare weekend visit. Every passing minute in such close vicinity to the youthful, energy-enhancing noise seeped the elixir into their ears and deep into their very souls, smoothing wrinkles and lifting baggy eyes. But behind them, one further watcher protected her ears from the age-defying laughter, the Dark Side of the Moon's track 4 - Time - holding its ground as a prog-rock barrier. The more enthusiastic the play, the more notes joined the siege from the white earphones of her I-Pod. 'It could well be that I'm in the wrong place...' The slightly off-natural wording of Karina Wolfenden was lazy in parting from her lips, each word being pondered rigorously in a screening process before being released into the crisp air. Under the vast, flawless Kansas sky, Kari stood, hands in pockets, staring through the mesh of the fence until eyes lost focus, blurring the scene before her. With both hands, she revealed herself from under a faux-fur lined hood, matted-down strands of blonde, green, and black released into the refreshing wind. Scrutinising emerald eyes again referenced the travel information retrieved by a gloved hand from her pocket, matching town, matching street name, matching neighbouring buildings. 'Yup,' she sighed, sending a frosty plume out before her, 'it could well be.' To her left, enticed passers by were drawn into the Ruby Slipper novelty shop, tempted by the refuge from both the cold and the mundane. Each bell chime released a customer back onto the street, deep red bag tantalising in the mystery of what oddities it could contain. To her right, an Italian deli, releasing wafts of garlic, basil, and thyme out into the air-streams, spreading the entwining scents exponentially out through the pristine air, climbing towards the endless topaz above. It was safe to say, there would be no wrestling booking in the vicinity that day. 'Amazing... isn't it?' 'Huh?' a slightly-startled Wolfenden softly released, as a voice, teasing with the most modest hint of the Emerald Isle, remarked. To her left a man was stood, not so presumptuous as to make eye-contact. A hunched body barley kept its head above the five foot mark, while nervy muscles seemed to make his whole body fidget incessantly. The well-to-do air about him was complimented by his smart, Sunday-best attire, topped off with a snug-fitting, velvety textured green waistcoat. From the right breast hung a gold pocket watch, aged to a bronzy copper, whose ticking seemed to synchronise to the heartbeat of all around. A white beard, more furry than bristly, helped mask the double chin that exemplified the homeliness of the man that almost warmed the air. 'Isn't what?' Wolfenden cautiously engaged his random comment with. As she finally replied, the man's eyebrows perked up, seemingly lifting his whole body a few inches higher. As if her words gifted him the invite, the man's head turned to her, glistening, youthful eyes a stark contrast to the aged, whiskery white eyebrows above. 'Always the watched, never the watcher,' he continued to fleet with the obscure by remarking. 'They've no self-conscious reservations, no concept of embarrassment, and not a blush to be spared. If they tumble and fall, they bounce right back up, not look around in a deathly-pale panic to make sure nobody saw the most inoffensive of indiscretions.' Karina Wolfenden's eyes narrowed a little, out of swelling curiosity. 'Imagine the world if, every lunch hour, the ties came off and the top button was un-done,' he continued in his enthusiastic, mild Gaelic tone. 'Briefcases left in the office, phones turned off, and not a post-it note in sight... and the working world came to a halt just to enjoy, well, this.' She'd always been told never to listen to strange men at bus-stops. His sentences were structured to allow contemplations, appeasing the fast, almost panicked delivery with which they were reeled off. Every passer-by was complimented with a warm smile, while the words refused to be flat, each and every one given just the right amount of punch. 'The whole world is mankind's - no offence intended - playground,' followed, while Kari shrugged off his concerns of sexism. 'We've just grown out of playing in it. We lock ourselves away from prying eyes, and we think just letting the most pure of pursuits bring a smile to our faces is a guilty pleasure. Only when nobody is watching, judging, can we again get close to tasting those sweet-summer flavours of youth.' There wasn't the usual roll of jaded emerald eyes to meet his remarks, no fake yawn to mark her exit, and not a dismissive, smart-ass comment to blow him off with. The man watched as his words seeped into the K-Wolf, rather than bounce off her defences, as if he was reading every reaction to his ponderings. 'Oh, how rude of me,' he then declared, aghast. 'I would be Mr White. Forgive me. I do tend to rabbit on sometimes.' 'Karina,' she returned the favour with. 'Wolfenden.' Again, she checked her travel information, looking from paper to playground, from scribbled note to smiling faces, crumpled paper to enthusiastically natural laughter. The question had to be asked... would she rather the building she was expecting still be there? As another gust of wind whistled a tune through the diamonds of the fence, Mr White couldn't help but pick up on Karina's visual pondering, verging on bewilderment. 'Well, I can certainly tell you weren't expecting this.' Despite all thoughts of what she was there for having been shuffled to the back, his prompt returned them to the forefront. 'And you wouldn't be far wrong...' Kari conceded. 'So,' he appeared to take delight in delivering, stretching the vowel out to the point of snapping. 'That would lead to the question of just what you were expecting.' The ticking of the man's pocket watched seemed to be getting louder to all but him as his eyebrows punctuated the thought-provoker. Even the chime of the Ruby Slipper's door was stamped over by tick-tocking footsteps. 'Something a litt-' her stalling words finally gave clearance to be divulged, before the snapping shut of the man's watch cut her off. 'Sorry, so sorry,' the panicky, sincerely apologetic words repeated as his fidgeting threatened to turn into something better resembling a folk dance. 'I'd lost all track of time, and now I'm late for cards and iced teas. Mustn't be late. No, mustn't be late.' With the eccentricity he'd just about managed to keep bottled-up oozing from every pore, the man paced back and forth for a few moments, deep in thought. 'Mustn’t be late. No, mustn’t be late. Oh, but before I go... it will challenge you a lot less than a question, but will keep you warmer tonight. Hopefully you'll have caught her on a baking day... tarts to die for.' Shaking between pursed fingers, a card was extended to Karina. 'Dinah's Guesthouse, Cheshire Lane,' Wolfenden read from the card, before looking up to see Mr White scurrying across the road, just managing to hop to the side of a pot-hole. 'Mustn't be late. No, mustn't be late...' Plunging hands back into pockets, Karina watched the sight of the departing man be lost in the blur of passing cars. Resting back against the fence, she rode the feeling of its faltering resistance before it snapped tight, supporting her weight. Dropping her head back, Kari felt the wind's tickle across the end of her nose as she looked through the deep blue overhead. 'How do these things always happen to me?' II - The Middle Is The Beginning. 'Kansas... yippee,' was expelled with enough sarcasm to make even the most cynical Gen-X'er feel she was being a little angsty. Dropping down onto the steps, feeling the chill of the pale grey stone through her clothing, Karina let out a misty sigh into the New York morning. With plans for a quiet weekend shattered, as they tended - as a rule - to be, the lack of a rest she'd feel on Monday morning was already starting to preview within her facial features. Still pursing her mouth to try and expel the bitter after-taste of the bank's excuse for coffee, the K-Wolf tried to remember which of the plethora of pockets adorning her she'd dropped her cell-phone into. 'My life would be so much easier without the cast of 6 billion extras...' Kari bemoaned. Continuing to mutter and splutter under her breath, she attracted the attention of a passer-by. Feeling in a benevolent mood, one of the suited-and-booted men broke away from the flock, fingers mounting an expedition to the deepest, darkest confines of his trouser pockets to search for loose change. Approaching the muttering young woman, devoid of make-up, and with a maroon beanie struggling to restrain tufts of golden blonde and green hair, he formed a restrained smile of pity. Eager to do his good deep for the day, he rummaged a little deeper, not quite realising quite how expensive the 'homeless look' wardrobe that Karina Wolfenden was apparently sporting really was. Although she saved a little by shredding the left knee herself. 'Here you g-' he went to warmly offer, just as Karina looked up curiously to see who was casting the shadow upon her. With the handful of loose change held out before her, narrowing emerald eyes looked down from his subtly proud expression to his loaded palm, then back up again... gaining an offended scowl on their travels. 'You're not...' his head then knowingly cocked to the side in realisation. 'No,' was harshly delivered in as shorter time as it could be spoken. 'So I'll just...' fumbled through his lips as embarrassment left him cringing. 'Yes,' she replied, not so much a suggestion as an order. As he nervously laughed, wondering if he could casually turn the comedic misunderstanding into loose conversation, Kari's eyes shepherded him away. Bumbling a farewell in such a way that Hugh Grant would disguise a judgement of 'loser' with a cough, he dropped the change back into his pocket, before offering a finger-wiggle wave. With her soured expression breaking premature wrinkles across her forehead as she watched him leave, the K-Wolf went back to phone hunting. 'I thought wrestlers were meant to be recognised by everyone in the middle of the street,' Kari then grumbled as another few pockets were patted down. 'Must be because I didn't kill my parents. Annnnnnd, bingo.' Finally having hunted down her phone somewhere along her left sleeve, Kari scrolled through the most recent calls received. 'Now,' she decreed, scrolling down to the seventh spot. 'Time to sort the travel arrangements.' Inhaling a deep breath in preparation, Kari made the call, rolling eyes back and forth in time will the ringing. Under her breath she urged them to answer until, finally, they obeyed. His greeting crackled down the poor line, immediately followed by a resonating cough, then the 'ptuh' of spitting. 'Hi, this is Karina Wolfenden,' said... well, Karina Wolfenden, oddly enough. 'You called last week about a booking in Harristown.' Bopping her head from side to side as the man replied, first of all correcting Harristown to Harrisonville, and then reminded her of her exact reply when contacted. As he reeled off the less-than-graceful refusal he was given, Kari motioned with her free hand for him to skip to the end, before even herself wincing with a pronounced 'ouch!' as he brought the recollection exercise to an end. 'You caught me on a bad day,' was the less-than-remarkable explanation, fobbing off any fledgling pangs on guilt. 'Change of heart, call it. If the bank and travel is with me Saturday, I'm there.' And that, ladies and gents, is how to travel for free in the United States. III - The Beginning Is The End. 'Why do I feel like a dead woman should be narrating me right now,' Karina's thoughts enquired as the eerie, pristine scene was lit grandly before her, the last of the passing clouds clearing from the sun's path. The lane itself looked as if the numerous 4x4's and sports cars had never left their driveways, not a single strip of rubber blighting the asphalt. Even the road markings looked as if they'd been measured to the millimetre, the symmetry enough to leave someone a little queasy. She cautiously tread the pale paving slabs, almost afraid that if she made a mark on the flawless stone, she'd be bundled into the back of a van and disappeared. More than just a little creeped out by the idyllic surroundings, usually taking that as a sign that something baaad is about to happen, she again checked the address handed to her by Mr White. 'Cheshire Lane,' she matched to the ornate sign, which wouldn't have surprised her one bit if it was polished daily. Maybe even twice. As she continued to wander, her feet cut through the shadows of the white picket fences, each bordering lawns of a green so lush that chlorophyll alone couldn't have been responsible. A creeping sense of paranoia had her attention darting to windows, where Kari couldn't help but shake the feeling that someone had just been watching her through the merest of gaps in the curtains. 'Why, heading down to Kansas to figure out why some weirdo won't stop using my bank account as an IM would be a fucking marvellous idea,' the K-Wolf sarcastically applauded herself. 'And as for not just turning around and going back to the normal world when the building a wrestling show was meant to be at doesn’t actually exist... well, that's Nobel prize thinking there.' Spinning 360 degrees as she continued her stroll, still not a soul was in sight, until she completed her spin to catch sight of a slick, black feline dashing across the path to take refuge under the Chrysler parked up on the curb. 'Oh, joy,' the overdose of cynicism forced out, 'now just give me a mirror to kick in.' Subconsciously avoiding the cracks as she finally reached the identikit house, distinguishable only by the number on its letter box and a few different shrubs in the flower beds. Adjusting her jacket's collar, and taming a few wisps of fringe, feeling for some reason like she should make the little effort to smarten herself up, Wolfenden began the ascent up the driveway. Immediately, the homely smell of baking hit her nose sweetly as they skipped downwind to her, almost hooking the nostrils and yanking the K-Wolf up towards the front door. The fragrant scents of stewing berries overloaded the senses, leaving time an after thought, so in the blink of an eye the door appeared before her. Slipping her rucksack down onto just the one shoulder, she cleared her throat without needing to, then swung the heavy door knocker down twice. Shuffling back down from the top step, her cynically suspicious side wondered what prissy, overly starched type would be revealed. 'One sec,' a hurried voice, more than a little younger than Kari was expecting, called out. Scurrying footsteps could be heard getting closer, then more distant, before finally sounding just a few feet away. As a trio of locks were turned over, the door creaked back to show the slightly flushed face of a woman barely older than the K-Wolf herself. With a headscarf half slipping sown her face, and a loose apron ghosted in flour, the young woman wiped a trail of deep purple from her freckled nose, all smiles. 'Hello,' went her warm welcome, making a point of opening the door fully, invitingly. 'Please excuse the state of me!' Visibly relaxing, Karina dropped her rucksack beside her. 'You come recommended,' Kari casually revealed, even matching the smile of the woman as she noticed she had the same pair of embroidered jeans. 'So hopefully you've room for me tonight?' 'Of course,' energetically ushered Karina back up the steps. 'Come in, come in.' Finally removing the headscarf before it fell down her face, the woman pulled her sandy blonde hair back, showing strands of purple. Wiping her feet on the bristly welcome mat, Kari took a look around the hallway, finally looking at ease with its normality. 'So, are you Dinah?' 'Oh, no, no,' she denied, looking embarrassed at the comparison. 'I'm Abigail, Dinah's my mother. This is usually her thing, but she's been ill recently... so, I stepped into the breach.' 'Ah, hope she gets well soon,' the uncharacteristically benevolent Karina offered up, instantly having warmed to Abigail. 'I'm Karina... Wolfenden.' As usual, she dangled the surname out there, wondering if there's be a bite of recognition. There wasn't. Without needing to vocalise the introductions, the pair shook hands instead, eyes meeting to add an extra touch of sincerity. As Abigail's look broke off, as if she was worried about being seen as intrusive, Kari's lingered, wondering quite what it was about Abigail that failed to make her seem a total stranger. 'Are you okay?' Abigail enquired, her self-conscious side picking up on Kari's attention. 'Oh, yeah, fine,' the K-Wolf answered, breaking off and rubbing her eyes, then perfectly mimicking a yawn. 'Just a little tired from the journey.' Drawing out the motion, Kari left her arms raised, stretching back, as she continued to observe her surroundings. Abigail's graduation photo sat proudly on the hall table, beside a wedding photo that's age would suggest being of her parents. Seeing that Karina was beginning to take an interest, Abigail stepped over to the table, rearranging the pile of post into no particular order, before resting back upon it, shielding the contents from the curious Wolfenden. 'Do you want me to take that for you?' Abigail asked snappily, gesturing to Wolfenden's grounded rucksack. 'You must be aching from having lugged that here.' 'No, it's fine,' Karina assured, intrigued eyes taking a glancing wander around. 'I'm stronger than I look.' 'Right then,' Abigail ushered, staying rooted to the spot. 'I'll show you to your room.' Rather than lead the K-Wolf away, Abigail gestured for Wolfenden to go first, the politeness enough to mask any conspicuous actions. With Kari heaving her bag down the hallway, Abigail's smile softened out to a more natural of expressions, followed by a short puff of a sigh. After taking the brief moment to compose, she shook her head back into its perky state, then cheerily caught back up to Karina. As footsteps played a creaky tune up the staircase, the proud display of photos was left unguarded. Propped up against the wallpaper at the rear of the hall table, almost entirely obscured by the numerous larger frames, was the photo Abigail was guarding. Two parents, three children. Like a children's puzzle, 4 identities could be pieced together by the veritable photo-album that surrounded it. But one of the children had no graduation photo, no wedding photo of their own, and no smiling image captured at their parent's silver wedding anniversary. A young woman, looking as if she'd only just grown into her once awkward frame, who drew attention straight into the most startling of emerald eyes. Blonde hair, each layer seeming to have its own natural shade, cascaded over fierce cheekbones, bangs linked by a chain of light freckles that mapped the path across the bridge of her name. The smile so subtle that it made the Mona Lisa's look like a forced, ear-to-ear grin. And in the shadowed mystery between behind the frame, a name adorned her back on the photo, flowing letters looping through one another. Imogen. 'It could well be that I'm in the wrong place...' she'd muttered earlier that day. To be continued, and preceded, in Human : Part Two - Dearest, The Shadows.
