Wearing Thin

Date: ReV 163 In his 15 months with the Louisville Courier-Journal, Keith Baxter had been stuck with plenty of annoying assignments. This particular one was starting to take the cake. A tip from an old mentor had gotten the 24-year-old in contact with a former NFL player turned pro wrestler who just happened to be across the river in Cincinnati for the week. Keith really didn't care for wrestling, in fact, he'd been battling his mind for three days trying to remember the name of the company the guy worked for. PRICE, or PRIDE ... something like that. But, his editor loved the stuff, and considering the fact that the sports department was very likely next in line for the paper's series of dramatic cutbacks, getting in his good graces wasn't the worst idea. Of course, Keith was rapidly regretting ever pitching this story. His interview seemed to be harder to track down than bin Laden, and this guy hadn't done Keith the favor of sending in grainy videos from a cave every few months. Three times already, he'd set up a meeting that had fallen through. But, finally, after an epic game of phone tag, he'd finally pegged the guy down to a solid place and time. But, predictably, Keith had been waiting in the simulcast lounge at Churchill Downs for nearly 20 minutes, ingesting dangerous amounts of secondhand smoke as his patience wore ever thinner. Eight hours to deadline on a fucking profile story, and he hadn't gotten quote number one from his feature subject. This wasn't exactly his preferred method of doing business. He scanned the crowd for the umpteenth time, but still couldn't find the guy, who he'd come to recognize through the few DVDs his editor had lent to him. All he could see was a collection of middle-aged men hunched over folded copies of the Daily Racing Form, looking up every few seconds to peruse the bank of television screens showing races from all across the country. Keith pulled out his note pad, ready to rip out the pages of research he'd prepared and just move the fuck on with his life. "I'd go with the seven in the next race at Hollywood Park," said a voice from behind him. "She runs great on soft turf." The young reporter wheeled around, ready to explain that he wasn't here to throw his meager savings away on the hopes that a 1200-pound animal could run faster than eight other 1200-pound animals. "Thanks for the tip, but I'm..." Troy Douglas smiled as he saw Keith's eyes widen ever so slightly. A week past his 32nd birthday, and Troy still enjoyed the simple pleasure of scaring the crap out of somebody. "You Baxter?" The kid nodded, still regaining his bearings. Finally he did, and when he spoke, he didn't seem all that happy to have finally tracked down his elusive interview. Probably because the interview had tracked him down. "You know, I've been trying to find you for five days, and my deadline is coming up in--" "Geez, Ben said you were wound a little tight, but you're taking this a hell of a lot worse than the other three." "Hey, I'm just trying to get my job done, buddy, and when I don't know if I'm going to have a job in two months or ... other three?" Troy grinned. The kid was a little overanxious, but he'd finally caught up to the gag. "It's Castor's little idea of a joke, kid. He picks one of his old interns, fresh out of school, tells 'em that I'm an easy interview, then I string them along for as long as possible. Can't stand doing it, personally, but I'm pretty sure you can attest to the fact that Ben Castor's got more than a few issues." Actually, Troy loved pulling the routine, but he figured he'd led the kid along enough. Besides, any more stalling and this guy might try to stick his ballpoint pen in an extraordinarily uncomfortable place. "You still want the interview?" "Well, I've waited this long." "Then come with me. I can't stand all this smoke." *********************************************************** For someone who had gone to school for journalism and had spent a good portion of his adult life associating with journalists, Troy Douglas had very little tolerance for reporters. Although much of that probably came from eight years of dealing with so-called wrestling "journalists", Troy's threshold for meaningless questions had basically evaporated. But, Troy wasn't quite prepared for the first question this particular reporter would ask him after they'd settled down in a corner of Churchill's VIP area, the two sitting across from each other in stiff leather chairs. "So, exactly how many times have you injected yourself with illegal performance enhancing drugs?" "Excuse me?" Considering he'd been very specifically told that this was a light profile piece, Troy was very much taken off-guard by the audacity of this kid's question. Ben was right. This kid got right to the point, and wasn't giving any ground. "Hey, you think that I'm the only person Ben Castor likes to play jokes on?" This time, it was time for Troy's eyes to widen. "That little son of a bitch ... uhh, that's off the record, of course." "Got it." Troy looked away for a moment, watching a mad dash of horses storm home at Arlington Park. In the distance, he could hear that familiar racetrack bustle; a few people plowing towards the pari mutuel windows, ready to collect on their big score, the rest throwing their wasted tickets on the scuffed linoleum floor. In a strange way, he'd always felt connected to the gambler with a chronic case of losing. Troy realized a long time ago that, both personally and professionally, his career was going to be defined by loss. He lost his football career, he lost his father and his fiance, and at multiple points he was fairly certain that he'd lost his mind. He also lived, every day, with the realization that when he stepped inside a wrestling ring, the vast majority of people still expected him to lose. No matter how many places he'd gone, no matter how many titles he'd win, no matter how many times he'd reach the main event, he was always going to be the guy everyone expected to come up short. He'd fought the stigma, fighting his way from creaking old high school gyms to massive, state-of-the-art arenas and stadiums, but it still persisted. And, for the last few years, every time some "insider" publication, every time some wrestling fan with a Web site and a few connections had been able to score an interview with him, the first question was always the same. "So, Troy, when are you going to finally win the big one?" The same fucking question, every fucking time. It wasn't hard to see why his affinity for reporters had waned over the years. His life was a constant state of flux, but that one question always remained. Even when he'd won the PRIME Intense Title just two months after signing with the company, he still wasn't able to escape being pegged as the guy who couldn't win The Big One. He'd been called a chronic underachiever and the King of the Midcard, which were among the nicer monikers to be attached to his name, if he was being completely honest. Coming up short against Chandler Tsonda in his first PRIME main event wasn't going to help silence the critics. And Kaiser Vashaun parading about, telling anyone and everyone with a set of ears that Troy was a fraud and that the Next in Line was the uncrowned Intense champ didn't go a long way towards allaying that situation, either. So, this wasn't exactly the best time in Troy's life to be sitting across from some ambitious young reporter who Troy's old college roommate had decided should be the latest victim of a long-running practical joke. With everything on his plate, being skewered in the media wasn't something Troy was ready for right now. One only had to look towards the recent troubles of the Risen Star himself to realize how easily you could fall out of the world's good graces. "...your feelings on that?" "Excuse me?" Apparently, Troy's mind had wandered more than a little bit, while Keith Baxter had kept on barreling forward, concerned with deadline and deadline only. If this kid wanted a story, he was going to get a story. But, this time, Troy was going to set the agenda. "I was asking about--" "It's not important." "What are you ... " Keith was clearly taken aback by the fact that this ... wrestler was so casually disregarding questions that he'd been waiting for five days to ask. "I know you're on deadline, so I'll make this quick," Troy said. He relaxed in his chair, took a deep breath, and looked Baxter right in the eyes. "You want my story? I'll give you my story. But, you're getting full disclosure, kid, so don't expect this to be some little puff piece that'll make the M.E. happy. Professional wrestling ain't the cartoon world you see on TV, so throw that fucking preconception out the window, and let's get going." Keith Baxter hadn't pushed a single button, and he got exactly what he wanted. His fingers might be worn to the bone from typing fast enough to make deadline, but at least now he knew it'd be worth it.
