Whatever it takes...

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Various Tom Walczak stands in a dark, damp, apartment in Brighton Beach. The apartment is filled with nothing more than a desk, and a pair of chairs, but Walczak does not take a seat. Standing in the middle of the room he stares at the man sitting in front of him, not saying anything, just letting the sweat build up on the top of his bald head. “Will you help me?” Walczak is shaken out of his trance by the heavily accented voice of the man. He takes his hand, and wipes some sweat away from his forehead before answering the man. “What? YOU contacted me. YOU wanted me to come here. YOU said that you knew my parents. I did not ask you to help me, and now I come here and you need help. Fuck that I am out of here.” Having wasted the majority of his day waiting for the man this is not the outcome that Walczak had envisioned. Not wanting to deal with the man anymore Walczak turns to walk away, not discovering anything new about his parents. “You may want to take a look at this before you leave.” Maybe it was instinct, or curiosity. Or it could have been hope, the hope that this thing would lead to finding something out, but whatever it was Walczak turned around, and made his way back to the man, who still sits there calmly, hands folded across the desk. “What is it?” Not saying anything the man points to a picture lying in the top right corner of his desk. It is an old black and white photograph, with a man with his arm around his wife, who is cradling what must be their newborn baby boy. The man takes up the majority of the picture, and he bears a strong resemblance to Walczak. “Who is that?” “It is you.” Walczak picks the picture up off of the table bringing it closer to his eyes, squinting to get a better glimpse. Looking closely at the child in the picture he sees that is in fact him, and he places it back in its original spot. Walczak stands in front of the man not staring at him, but his eyes still focused on the picture. “So those…they are….my parents.” The shock could be heard in Walczak’s voice as he barely manages to get the sentence out of his mouth. For the first time in a long time Walczak’s heart pounds heavily in his chest. It does not pound because he is scared, but because he is anxious. For the first time in his life he saw his parents, and now he wants to find out more. “Yes, they are, and if you want to find out more you will help me.” The man across from Walczak holds all the power. He knows that Walczak wants to find out more about his parents, and he will do almost anything to get it. However, Walczak does not like to be in the debt of others, and by agreeing to help this man that will be exactly what happens. “What do I have to do?” “Take a seat.” For the first time since he has been in the office Walczak pulls the chair out and takes a seat looking at the man. He relaxes, and his heart slows down to a steady beat. He knows now that he will do whatever it takes to find out more about his past, and now he can just calm down. “I have a ticket for a plane to St. Petersburg that leaves in two weeks. You are going to be on it.” “You know that I wrestle right. I can’t just get up and leave for weeks at a time.” “Not a problem Mr. Walczak. I have arranged everything that you need in Russia.” “Hold on….St. Petersburg, Russia. Not St. Petersburg, Florida?” The man behind the desk gives a chuckle, but to Walczak it is no laughing matter. There are very few places that he despises more than the United States, but Russia is one of them. His childhood was haunted by the Communist oppression of Poland, and there is very few Russians if any that he did not despise directly after meeting them. “Did you think that I would call you in here today if I needed something taken care of in the United States? No, no, no, you see I have some very….let’s just say important dealings going on in the homeland, but I am not exactly welcome there, and I need someone to represent me.” “Why me? I am not Russian, nor do I have any idea what your business dealings entail, and I am not in a hurry to find out.” “Mr. Walczak, would you say that when you walk into a room your presence is immediately felt?” “I would like to think so.” “Do you like the fact that when people see you they are scared? That they tend to tremble in your presence?” “I don’t know if I would go that far.” “I would, and that is exactly why I need your help. I have taken care of all of the business aspects, and all I need you for is to make sure that they go through. That no one thinks that they could bully my associates, if you know what I mean.” “So you need me for muscle?” “Now you are beginning to understand.” Being someone’s muscle would not be that bad. It’s not something that Walczak is unaccustomed to, and if it would help him get what he wants then it works for Walczak. “Tell me what I need to know.” “You are going to be picked up by my associate Dmitry, and he will fill you in on everything else that you need to do. That is all I have to discuss with you right now, so you can leave.” “I will, but I still don’t know who you are.” “You don’t have to.” With that not another word is said. Normally Walczak would not like the fact that someone is being so brief with him, almost discourteous, but he is doing this for himself, and not for the man. Date: August 21, 2007 Location: St. Petersburg, Russia A crowd makes their way out of Pulkovo Airport Terminal II, all of the people on the flight coming from Los Angeles. One man rises above the rest of the people, Tom Walczak is head and shoulders above the rest of the passengers, and he makes his way into the waiting area not sure of who he is looking for. He places his gym bag down on the ground, and looks around hoping to see someone who has a sign with his name on it. “Mr. Walczak.” Walczak turns around looking at a small bearded man standing in front of him. The man wears a rather thick jacket for the warm weather, and looks rather secretive, as if he wants no one to see him. “I assume that you are Dmitry.” “Follow me.” Dmitry walks in front of Walczak, and picking up his gym bag he follows the man outside. He points towards a black Lincoln Town Car, and Walczak hops in the front seat pushing it all the way back, but his knees still smash into the dashboard. He tosses his bag to the back seat trying to make himself more comfortable. Dmitry starts the car, and makes his way away from the airport. “Alright, before we go any further I need to know what the fuck is going on. I came to Russia, and still have no clue what is happening.” The man continues to drive not acknowledging Walczak, and cutting in and out of traffic nearly missing getting into a couple of collisions. “Listen to me. Slow the fuck down, and tell me what the fuck is going on.” “There is a briefcase under your seat look inside that is all that you need to know. We will be at your hotel room soon, and you will have the rest of the day to get acclimated to our country.” Walczak picks up the briefcase, laying it on his knees and opening it up. However, he is not exactly happy with what is inside. He first pulls out a slip showing that he has to pick up a suit from the local tailor. Walczak looks over at Dmitry, but again no response. Next he pulls out a smaller steel case, and opening it up he sees that it contains a black, .45 Caliber Automatic Pistol. “What the fuck do I need this shit for?” “We are here.” Walczak looks over and sees that the car has stopped in front of The Astoria Hotel. He looks over at Dmitry once more, but sees that he has exited the car, and then his door suddenly opens. “You are already booked at the hotel, and everything is paid for.” Shaking his head Walczak gets out of the car knowing that there is no use to argue. Taking his gym bag and the briefcase he makes his way into the hotel lobby, not sure of what will happen next. Date: August 21, 2007 Location: Astoria Hotel, Room 214, St. Petersburg, Russia The bathroom door opens up, steam pouring out of the room. Moments later Tom Walczak enters the bedroom, ducking through the door, so as to avoid hitting his head. He puts on a pair of boxers and falls down on his bead hoping to get a good nights sleep after his nearly twenty four hour journey. He turns around resting on a pillow and grabbing for the remote control. Walczak flips through the channels hoping to find out what is going on in the United States, specifically with PRIME. He flips through each channel listening carefully. As he comes to the news, a picture of a man is in the top corner of the screen, and for some reason Walczak recognizes him. “…..Nickolaj Rulin is one of Russia’s most feared mobsters, and while he has not been seen in the country for over five years, he still controls much of the organized crime in the country. Recently a string of murders occurred in St. Petersburg, and they are believed to be linked to Rulin, and Police are looking for any information they could find about him or the murders…” Walczak still cannot put his finger on whom the man is, but he is certain that he had met this man before. “…Brighton Beach…” The meeting two weeks ago, it was with Rulin. He had been secretive, and had mentioned that he was not welcomed in Russia. Throughout this whole journey he had not thought of what his responsibilities entailed. Walczak just knew what the reward for him would be. “…What the fuck did I get myself into…”
