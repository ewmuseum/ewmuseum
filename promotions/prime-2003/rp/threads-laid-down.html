Date: Friday the 13th Supershow vs. Enemigo III Transcript of Initial Session Date: 05 February 2009 13:30 Patient: Dakota Leah Sinclair Presiding: Angela DeWitt, Psy-D. Patient is seven-year-old Caucasian female, brown hair, blue eyes. Referred by family physician due to symptoms of AD/HD and several violent incidents at school. Patient was accompanied to interview by her mother (Madison, 35) and Summer (7), but first session is scheduled as a solo session. Authorization to record obtained from mother. (Recording begins.) DEWITT: There we go. Good afternoon, Dakota. I’m Dr. DeWitt – you can call me “Angie”. DAKOTA: Dee. Momma only ever calls me my whole name when I’m in trouble. DEWITT: You’re not in any trouble here, Dee. DAKOTA: Cool beans. I’m in some trouble at home, though. Patient’s brow furrows with her frown. Seems concerned. DEWITT: Did something happen, Dee? DAKOTA: (mumbling) Kind of. DEWITT: "Kind of"? DAKOTA: I’m not supposed to say; Dad’s pretty mad. DEWITT: It’s okay. Anything you say in here is our secret. Did something happen with your dad today? Patient screws up her face in confusion – it’s neither a smile nor a frown. DAKOTA: No! I... The nibble of her lower lip indicates nervousness. DAKOTA: I kinda... punched Jesse Romain in the face. A lot. Pretty hard, too. Nerves dissipate for a smile. DAKOTA: It was kind of awesome. DEWITT: And why did you do that? DAKOTA: He said that Momma and Dad were jerks. DEWITT: “Jerks”? DAKOTA: Well. Jesse said it different, but Momma says ladies don’t say bad words. DEWITT: Why would Jesse call them jerks? DAKOTA: He’s a dick. Patient slaps both hands over her mouth, eyes going wide. DAKOTA: Oh gosh - please don’t tell my mom I said that. I try to suppress a smile – I’m not sure how well it works. DEWITT: I won’t. Tell me what happened. DAKOTA: OK. My mom and my dad were on TV yesterday, and they said some mean things about people and Jesse... Jesse said that they were, y’know, that word. And that my dad wasn’t worth anything and I don’t really remember what happened, except I was standing up and Jesse was down on his back and I... I felt pretty proud of myself. DEWITT: Why did you think you had to punch him? DAKOTA: I know Dad’s not a good guy in the ring, and that he and Momma do things that... that they tell me not to. But I was so mad when Jesse called them worthless. ‘Cuz they’re not. They’re my momma and my dad. * * * * * Madison Sinclair sat cross-legged on a padded chair, leaning in front of a vanity mirror, mascara wand in hand. She’d been in front of that mirror for ten minutes now, meticulous in her ministrations. A dab here, a curl there, a brush-stroke there... it was very much like a master chef putting together the perfect recipe. Piano music played from the radio on the nightstand behind her, a soft tinkling that she never really listened to. Ten years ago, this was a much less involved process, but with the passing of time, she found herself here more, allowing cosmetics to fill in holes she hadn’t known were there. “Are you sure about this?” she asked, one eye opened wide to allow her to curl the brush against her eyelashes. She didn’t have to turn to see if he was behind her; if there was one thing she was always sure of, it was that Hunter would be there. He breezed in from the bathroom, tucking his dress shirt into the belt of his slacks, favoring the mirror with that smile that used to make her weak in the knees. She envied how easy it was for him; pull on that smile and he was Peerless in an instant. “Of course I’m sure,” he said quietly, his baritone only getting richer with age. “It’s just one guy in a mask, and I don’t even have to win, I just have to prove to Tyler I’m worth keeping around.” She spun on the chair, adjusting the fall of her skirt across her knees as she regarded him with a cool, blue-gray gaze. “That’s not what I meant, and you know it. You could have the pop again – Lord knows there are enough assholes in that place that you could be popular again.” He shrugged his shoulders slightly, turning away from his reflection. “We talked about this. Popularity brings too much... other stuff with it. I’d rather leave an arena to a chorus of boos than a mob of fans looking for my autograph. Makes things easier.” “I suppose you’re right.” Her tone implied she thought he was very far from right. “I just... you think we can pull this off?” “Babe.” Hunter wasn’t lecturing – not quite, at least. “We have done far worse than this. You know what the terms of my deal are – I can leave the state in pursuit of my employment and that’s it. If we’re going to do this...” She sighed, slipping up to her stocking feet. “Then we’re going to have to get her to sign you.” Madison padded across the hotel room, reaching up to smooth down his collar. “I think you like it.” “Ten thousand people jeering me, calling my favorite girl a slut, wanting people to kick my teeth down my throat?” He lifted a hand to swipe an errant strand of hair from her face. “Why would you think I’d like that?” Her palm settled on his chest, one brow rising doubtfully. She didn’t say anything; she didn’t have to. “Okay,” he admitted. “Maybe a little. When do we meet them?” Her lips quirked. “After the show. Ceci’s got it set up. If everything goes according to plan, you impress the higher-ups and sign your contract, then we run the meeting.” “Just like old times?” She nodded. “Just like old times. Except you have to spike your hair still.” He blew out a sigh. “Why the hell did I decide that was a good idea? I miss being able to run a brush through it and calling it a day.” Madison stepped softly back to the vanity, stopping only to turn up the piano concerto. She levered herself down into the seat, tugging her suitcase towards her by its zipper. Within, she’d find her leather pants and the stiletto-heeled boots she’d packed for the trip to Tacoma. There was something else in that bag, but she didn’t think she’d need it. At least not yet. * * * * * Cecilia Sicarii stood on the corner of Harborview Drive and Rosedale Street Northwest at the entrance to Jersich Park. The Fisherman’s Memorial statue, commemorating Gig Harbor’s ocean-going founders loomed in the dusky distance, forever pulling its net up out of the water. The small woman favored baggy clothes, camouflage pants and a sweatshirt to ward off the chill, mottled gray and green hood drawn up over her signature, multihued dreadlocks. Thin, cruel lips curled in a frown – the man she was supposed to meet was late, and that made her nervous. Those nerves made her pace, crossing to the statue and back. “I’ve had just about enough of this cloak and dagger bullshit,” she muttered. “Would that there was another way.” The voice was light, airy – and it seemed to come from nowhere. She snapped her eyes to the statue itself, hand falling to the small canister holstered at her hip. Squinting already beady eyes, she was able to make out a bird that had found perch on the Fisherman’s shoulder. Not a pigeon or a gull, like the others she’d seen, but a brilliant red macaw, digging its black beak into its feathers. A smirk slipped across the Little Dynamo’s features as her heavy-soled combat boots carried her back to the statue. “You’re late, y’know.” A man in a derby tipped the brim of his hat at her as he pushed out of the shadows. “Couldn’t be helped, miss.” He reached up with one hand, offering a biscuit to the bird that was his only companion. “Not every day an old man gets called to work.” Sicarii folded her arms over her meager chest. “So you have them?” The man bent into the unforgiving light cast by the streetlight that hung above them, his smile a red slash across his face. “Of course I do. Would I have come otherwise?” With a deep exhale, the lady they called the Rat extended one green-nailed hand. “Give it over, then you and your bird can go back to enjoy your retirement.” A wrinkled purple cloth bag whipped at her, golden thread winking in the overhead light. She caught it with a frown. “A Crown Royal bag?” Sicarii turned to lean against the Memorial, fumbling with the knot the pouch’s drawstrings were bound into. The bag’s contents shifted in her hand, the soft tinkling barely heard over the lapping waves. Wrenching the bag open enough to peer into it with one eye, she turned back to the shadows. “It’s all he...” She never finished her question, as the macaw took to wing, following the man into the docks. Sicarii didn’t give chase, closing her tiny fist around the bag. She drew the hood back up over her head, jamming her hands into the pockets at her waist. She did not look up again as she clambered into the backseat of the yellow cab that had been idling nearby. If she had, she might have noticed the light reflecting off the telephoto lens of a camera retreating into shadow. * * * * * Just shy of twenty-seven hundred miles from Tacoma, two seven-year-old girls up way past their bedtime fell backwards into the arms of their Uncle Darien, as the pyrotechnics on the television announced the appearance of their mother and father. “OhmygodohmygodohmyGAWD,” Dakota squealed. “It’s time! It’s finally time!” Her sister didn’t say anything (which wasn’t uncommon), simply thumbing her glasses up her nose and planting her chin onto her hand. As the music kicked into gear, Dakota leaped to her feet, “rapping” along with the entrance music. “I’m the baddest of the bad! I’m the best that you’ve ever had!” Darien Thorsen, retired wrestler and full-time babysitter for the Sabuani twins, couldn’t help but smile a little as he watched his friends on the television screen, doing what they did best. Not for the first time, the phantom ache in his left knee twinged with... can legs be jealous? Sometimes, he thought it could. Thirty minutes later, with Summer curled up against him asleep, Darien’s leg stopped hurting, though. * * * * * Across the street from the home, a red hatchback, paint flaking off the hood, sat unnoticed. Its sole occupant flipped closed a file folder containing the transcript of a little girl’s doctor’s visit, lips pressed together into a thin line. * * * * * “Two minutes, Mr. Sabuani.” The words had come with a soft knock on the door, impelling the man who long ago dubbed himself the "Raja of Roguishness" to hop to his feet, rolling his still sore left shoulder. He picked up a pair of mirrored sunglasses from the bench beside him, slipping them on as he wrenched open his locker room door. They met him in the hallway, Madison in her leather pants and Rat shaking out her mass of braids. The PA system echoed with the arrogant words of Jason Snow and Chandler Tsonda as the trio climbed up the stairs at the gorilla position. A staffer in a blue PRIME polo shirt counted down the seconds, but they didn’t need to – this was the part he knew by heart. Rat dropped to one knee in front of him as the bright spotlight illuminated them, projecting their silhouettes against the backdrop. Madison reached down and clasped his hand briefly in hers, a small smile playing on her face. “Here we go,” she whispered as a siren echoed through the Tacoma Dome. “You ready?” “Stupid question,” he said as Rivers Cuomo began “The Greatest Man That Ever Lived (Variations on a Shaker Hymn)”. She looked up at his face, and saw that Grin – perhaps the only one in professional wrestling that deserved a capital-G. Then PRIME learned one fact that has never – and will never – change. Flashpaper is still the coolest thing ever.
