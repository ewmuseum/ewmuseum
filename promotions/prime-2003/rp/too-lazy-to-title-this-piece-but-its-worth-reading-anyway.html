Date: Today I’m driving drunk. Exhaling stale tequila, I try to keep my eyes on the road. Saturday night in San Diego. Fuck if I’m going to spend a week in Asshole, Ohio. Besides, I try to steer clear of red states, my theory being that getting called “chink,” “boy,” or “slant-eye” isn’t much fun when you can’t legally knock the guy’s fucking teeth into his stomach acid. Driving drunk is one of those very human things that never get glorified in the media. Guns, sure. Objectification of women, why the hell not. But never driving drunk. Since we’ve already gotten over the fact that I’m a wretched person whose only destination beyond this life is hell, I can ponder things like this. It’s not fun, per se, but there’s a thrill to the challenge of it all. Then again, I’m not that shitbox who gets into the car when I’m beer goggled so bad I can’t see my hands. I don’t plan on killing some terrified teenage driver who’s going thirty miles below the speed limit because they don’t want a cop to notice that they’re driving past curfew. Those people should be burned at the stake. But not me. I’m not gonna hurt anybody besides myself tonight. Line hypnosis is no joke. Those fucking things are sending my brain in loops. When I get home, I know I’ll be hit with a bout of “the room is still spinning when I close my eyes-itis.” But we’re making a sport of the night. I swerve for no reason, in complete control of the Volvo sedan. This thing is the safest car on earth. They should market themselves to frequent drunk drivers more. “Hey, if you plow into some unsuspecting kid’s Accord, you’ll be the one who retains use of your legs.” I laugh out loud because hey, I’m drunk and need to hear something to make sure I’m not asleep. “What the fuck are…why are you driving?” I jump through the roof of the car when I hear her voice. November’s, that is. She has a name. A nice pretty one. It doesn’t matter what it is because she’s only November. If I start giving them all names, then I might get stuck with one. She’d discover the fact that I’m a corrupted, socially impotent professional wrestler. “Yeah, babe, I’ve got insecurity issues so I touch other guys in tights.” What to do if I get more then one a month? I don’t. I let other things grab my attention. Like this ...Pulse Title business. I’ve been thinking a lot about it. Not important. Back to her; I turn around with a scowl on my face. “No, I’m composing the score for a Broadway musical. The fuck does it look like I’m doing?” I get like this when I’m drunk. No joke about how that’s the case most of the time. I’m no alcoholic; life would get too depressing if I was. She’s been around for a couple weeks. We just left a cocktail party. I resent the cocktail party more then almost any other plea for social interaction. “You’re drunk. Pull over. This is insane.” Oh well someone’s on the high horse tonight. Despite the fact that she’s been passed out in the back seat for a good hour, it looks like she’s taken this convenient time to get a conscience. She’s right, of course. But I’m not letting November drive my car and I’d like to sleep in my comfy, shitty apartment tonight so there’s only one option. “I’m fi…okay.” Don’t slur your words, you drunken fuckbag. “I have a high tolerance and there’s nobody on the road.” I’m right too. I did this like every night when I was eighteen. And if I made the decisions of my adult life based on what happened when I was eighteen, I would still perform oral on females. Suffice to say, a lot has changed. If you think my mouth is going near that chimera that passes for female genitalia, you are a very stupid person. Just one of the many reasons why I name the girls by the month. “If you don’t care enough about me to listen to me on this, then let me get out of the car right now.” God, I wanna let her out of the car so badly. She’s giving me the perfect way out. No awkward call to say what we both dread. No post break-up speech about how we just were never meant to be. None of the hypocritical twentieth century namby-pamby bullshit for me. Do humans really think like this? Are we given the beautiful gift of complex thought so that we can use it to figure out ways to get rid of people who try to get close to us? Freud and I could have a field day, I tell you. I don’t say anything and I can feel the female anger waves in the air. There’s a good twenty minutes of drunk-driving fun before we get home. I can’t wait it out. “It’s not that simple. It’s not even a big deal.” “Fine,” she spits out, hating me as much as she hates herself for being with me. I try my best not to let the car slip out of my control. Every curve, endorphins strangle me and the exotic joy of truly not knowing what each turn has in store for me. I’m starting to get woozy. I’d rather fall asleep at the wheel and kill us both then not be able to save face. But this episode doesn’t end like that, compadre. Because I’m telling the story and if I die, the story immediately ends. If I don’t live, I don’t get to walk into that arena on Wednesday night and validate this whole comeback. Ugh…don’t call it a comeback, actually. This is a return. A much needed one. For you people and the fans. Like I need this. I need a shrink and some Vicodine, but that’s about it. But like I said, this title issue has weighed on my mind. I treat this industry like I would any hard drug: dabble here and there but don’t ever let it get you. I taste more then tequila shots with that last burp. I think that was my dinner, some seafood dish. And yes, I was doing tequila shots at a cocktail party. There’s a reason I don’t get invited to these things. I look back at her. She’s totally lucid now, arms crossed like the martyr she’d love to be. I can see it now: I’m dying in a fiery wreck and she walks over, arms crossed, to tell me that she told me so. The rancid taste combined with her sour look upgrade me from woozy to queasy. No way I’m puking in my car. Hold it in, trooper. “Keep your eyes on the road,” she utters as I press the cold skin of my palm to my tepid forehead. I don’t turn around because she’s the one that needs this to become a huge fight. It’s downright tropical in this car. I close my eyes for a second while desire to purge myself grows. I turn on the air conditioning and I hear November’s voice right in my ear. Yelling. No, screaming. My ears are popped. I pull over. Open the door. Heave all over the place. Seafood, tequila, crème brulee. It’s all combined in one large molecule of food sitting on the ground next to my door. November’s too mad to keep yelling. Is she lying back down to cry or to accept whatever fate my inebriated adventure brings her? This is my life. She doesn’t care and I don’t care. Close the door. Pull back onto the road. Claw my way through the darkness. At the end of all the darkness is an apartment, where a cold distant feeling has embedded itself in everything. Eventually, we get there. I scratch the car while parking it. I carry snoozing November into the apartment and set her down on the couch. I bring blankets in and tuck her in. I’m far too fucking chilled to sleep. * * * * Forgot to close the shades. I wake up at 6: 07 and the goddamn sun is rising. I wanna throw up, but I decide instead to go back to sleep. I remember that I’ve gotta get up early for church. Psyche. * * * * Now I’m up for real. November is up and puttering around. Yeah, she’s a putterer. She straightens pictures that aren’t seen by anyone but her and me. I don’t smell breakfast; looks like I’m still in deep shit from the whole endangering her life thing. She doesn’t say anything as I walk by on the way to the bathroom. I look in the mirror. I look like raw Sunday morning, no other way to put it. I brush my teeth to get the lingering film of booze off of them, all the while using the mirror to glance over my shoulder in case she tries to corner me for “a talk.” As Dave Chappelle once said, every time we have to talk, it’s about some shit that I gotta do. I’m on a very lonely interstate to Guilt City, USA. And apparently I’m being transported there riding on a shitty metaphor. I hear her humming in Italian. Yeah, November’s Italian. She doesn’t appear to be part of “the family,” but I wouldn’t know until I was lying dead in a dumpster looking up at some Deville-ian schmuck’s face, hearing that “I just can’t remove this giant cock from my mouth” Italian-American accent. I think mobsters would be ten times more intimidating if they spoke like human beings instead of pigs from some shitty country that hasn’t mattered in five hundred years. You assholes, there’s a reason that the Pope doesn’t live in Italy, but the Vatican City. Think about that, next time you need to wear your clever F.B.I. shirt or laugh at that wash-up hack Bob DeNiro. But this is about November. She’s muttering something like “munda giambarone,” under her breath. She knows I hate it and she knows I know what it means. It usually functions as the PMS beacon, but today there’s an added “umph” to the pissed offed-ness. Does she even know what I’m up against? One of the biggest weeks of my life. She has the gumption to raise that cocky little eyebrow of hers to question me? I’m trying to win a Pulse Title, babe. And if you’re not along for the ride, for the long haul…well you’re probably not anyway. Regardless, this is gold week and I don’t have time to play emotional games. I pass her again as I head to the bedroom. I fall back into layers of blankets and lay very still. For the first time in a long time, all I can think about is wrestling. Beat Wolfenden. If I can’t beat a woman, kill me now. In the ring, what a joke. And this is coming from a model. Outside the ring, she’s a walking catastrophe. Wait no: inside the ring, she loses that title to me. November comes in. She’s looking at me with those hard, stone eyes. I think she doesn’t listen to what her eyes tell her. Her eyes tell her the truth. I look up at her and say nothing. Beat Face-Eater. I’ve seen schizophrenics with more stable personalities. I don’t like him and I won’t let myself lose to him. Just so we’re clear, that meaningless winning streak ends now. She lies down on the bed next to me. She pins my arm down with the full brunt of her body weight. She curls up in the space between my arm and my chest. She sighs loudly. I wonder exactly what she knows. Beat Collins. Who? The bed is much warmer with her in it. She’s asleep on my chest and in my arms. Things are perfect there for a second. I sit up and place her gently on the bed alone. I ponder this meager existence of a wrestler against the paltry, petty objective of sports entertainment. Human or wrestler? Be a man. Win the Pulse Title.
