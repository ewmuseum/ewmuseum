Date: Today Testimonial: Adrienne Diamond, Manager I went to bed around 10:27 pm that night. It was around the time that the Windsor news ended. They always cut off early for commercials. After my late dinner (it's not good, I know, but my stomach was grumbling and I'd be waking up in the middle of the night if I didn't get something inside of me... a little caesar salad wouldn't be too bad), I headed upstairs. Ms. Teshigahara would've already been asleep. She turns in around nine. Not much of a night owl. Van said once, 'What if somebody tries to attack the house then?' Emi huffed rather dismissively, saying, 'As big a game you talk you should be able to defend yourself on occasion.' I would be up early, same as Emi, but there was that time difference. For me, it's more just because I'm used to staying up later. Emi sleeps almost when it gets dark. Guess that's the kind of life she's lead. As I passed Van's room, I saw that he was intently watching something. He was really focused on it. The remote was in his hands, and he was rewinding and fast-forwarding. I thought it was odd because he'd usually just used TiVo so he could catch his shows that were on during the day. But he didn't mess with the playback features. Looking in, I saw that it wasn't TiVo, but his DVD player. He was watching wrestling. His matches. At first I made the connection to PRIME, assumed he was just catching his past performances in 'Teh Pwn' or now with 'Teh Greatness'. But it was different. Smaller arena, brighter set-up... it was a Rush Pro Wrestling show. Two bodies were in the ring aside from his own: Amy 'Aimz' Campbell and Gideon Poe. I made the proper connection, then; it was the match for the RPW Eclipse Championship, the first wrestling title he won. Without a word, I came in and sat next to him on his bed. He put his arm around me, but otherwise all his attention was on the screen. He was watching everything; what he did right as well as what he did wrong. The moves executed and escaped, where improvement could be made. He didn't say anything either, but I could tell what he was thinking. There was only one time in the past, since he'd started wrestling, that I'd seen him like this. Even during his excursion into the Eclipse Division, he was rather laid back. No, he'd only been like this before his match against The Marvelous Brothers, in the short-lived team with Skylar Green. I remember what he was like then. I was off of television because of the 'Hessian' indicent that saw me injured, but Van still came to talk to me. Not just a phone-call, he visited me every week. Sweet of him; sure, he's not the brightest bulb ever, but he cares. But as I was saying, he was that focused in his team with Green. I remember Skylar. He was chronically patient. I think he'd lost his temper once in his years of wrestling. That is control. And he was a good partner for Van, who was a bit wild and undisciplined. Van needed that support. He needed a person who could put up with him professionally, but wasn't being paid to do it. He latched onto that, Van did. They didn't win any tag team matches for the whole two months up to their pay-per-view, but Van was still appreciative that Skylar didn't dissolve the team. For Green's part, he did what he could before the shows to give Van some lessons. Being a part of a team is a very different thing that being on one's own. Even though Skylar was never part of a high-profile team himself (and rarely had competed in tag team matches since being on 'the radar' in wrestling) he still knew about it. The man was just an encyclopedia of wrestling knowledge and he imparted whatever he could onto Vangellus. I can't tell you how Van took that. Most people had given up on him. Even those who were nice to him backstage didn't think very much of him. But Skylar gave him a chance to prove himself. Van didn't want to let him down. Seeing the lead-up to The Kannon Effect (Christian Kannon's first PPV at the helm of RPW) as a learning period, he didn't kill himself with pressure to succeed. But on the days leading up to that match, his first high-profile tag team contest, he didn't want to lose. Understand that Van never 'wants' to lose, but he doesn't consider a loss anything bad in the scheme of things. He knows what he's seen in wrestling, and that is this: a person can have 20 losses to their name with no wins, but just a little bit of momentum swing and the right matches won and they can be back on top in no time. Look at Dani Fuhrer/Vangelus Olsig... everything turned around for him after he pinned Hoyt Williams in a tag team match. Van, this Van who isn't concerned with losses in the long rung, didn't want to lose. It wasn't that the match would mean a whole new beginning for him, though. It was just that after he was finally starting to get it... after he'd done well with Green in a match against Invincibleness and then performed admirably in a 10-man tag team match, he didn't want to fail Skylar. I could see him the same way now, lying there with him watching the matches he'd had in the past. To see what he had been a year ago, fast-forwarding to how he is now... he understood, as I had, that his performances as of late were not even what he had done back when he was still the carefree kind of guy. I didn't know what changed, and maybe he'd tell me. But he wasn't the same. He wanted to be that way, though. He wanted to win. Maybe not for himself, but now that he had dedicated himself to this team with Scott Matthews, and seeing how he was quickly taken out of the equation in the Tropical Turmoil rematch... he didn't want to fail 'Rampage'. He didn't want to have to rely on a big guy who was still as inexperienced as he was to carry the team. I stayed up with Van for another hour, just watching. Analyzing. Admiring. He found a stride. A focus for his energies. Scott Matthews was the best thing to come around for Vangellus in a long time. They're champions now. It would be best for Van, I think, if he enjoyed a long reign. Testimonial: Teshigahara Emi Sachiko I woke up early. Six in the morning. I'm not much for the amenities of modern culture, but the alarm clock is something that I can deal with. At least when it's set to 'wake to music'. Some nice new age ambient is a good start. Adrienne and I had another alarm clock that released shrill screeching at the designated time. My first task after fully waking up was to pick up all the pieces of the shattered clock from the floor, followed by caulking the dent in the wall. Morning exercises are the first order of duty. Starting with katas with my boken, it follows with simple resistance exercise (push ups, sit ups, squats, etc.). No weights. And then meditation. All the same. I always get back to the room in time to join Adrienne in the shower. I know that she waits for me, she knows what I know... doesn't change anything. I got up one morning to see that Oxios was already up before me. Not like the time that he had us going around the old RPW building. That was a complete waste of time. This time, he was just exercising. Oxios doesn't get up before me. In fact, he sleeps well into the afternoon. Not surprising, he doesn't have an early bedtime. That childish thought of 'no parents, I can stay up as long as I want.' So to see him up was unusual. Out from the back of the grounds, I could see that he was just ending a run. Right behind his property is a forest, he likes to explore it like a kid, but this time it was an obstacle course. Getting back, he did his own calisthenics. Stretches, flips, shadowboxing. He actually looked focused for once. His skinny body-type... it's entirely a matter of high metabolism. Food shoots through his body so quickly he doesn't gain any weight. And believe me, he should be gaining. The man is a glutton. Doesn't eat anything healthy. Pizza, hamburgers, ice cream... he counts ketchup as a vegetable. If it's not processed, it's not going into his mouth. Seeing him like this was a bit odd, but I didn't let it distract me from what I was doing. Still had my own exercises. But every now and then, out of a curiosity I can't explain, I did look over at him. He wasn't playing around. Did what he was doing and stuck to it. Not that my impression of him is changed, but he does seem to be taking small steps to grow up. It's only professionally at the moment. He's actually studying tapes, and even contacted a trainer to learn how to wrestle. At least as far as that goes, he's doing what he needs to. Still has a long way to go. Completely immature to deal with. Same as he always was off of the camera. Slowly, there's two sides to him developing. The on-camera is a bit more serious and focused... but off-camera he's the little boy in the adult's body who hired me without my consent. Maybe, just maybe, if he were to make a complete change, I could occasionally stand to be around him for more than five minutes. Testimonial: Richard Hargrove, Webmaster and Information Specialist I was a bit surprised actually when Van came to me and asked me to dig up what I could of his old matches. The RPW dvds didn't sell very much. Merchandising didn't do too well since the money was poured into the show itself. The hope was that once they hit the bigtime and started turning a profit, then Kannon could put some resources into republishing the old shows. It took me days to find and hack into the computers necessary to get the files for what Vangellus requested. There were a few matches he wanted. Match One was his biggest match, even bigger than his pay-per-views. Oxios was part of a big team, one that worked well even though it was the first time they'd all been together. He worked to his strengths and played a support role pretty nicely. Match Two was the first time that Oxios won a title. He was in against two more-favored competitors, and that was how he won. They concentrated on each other and he was able to sneak in the win with a good unexpected attack. In Match Three, Oxios showed his first flashes of being something special inside of the ring, not just outside. It was his first singles win, and done in good fashion. Back then, Van was uncanny at escaping people's finishers. I remember once in a match against Violence Jack, he escaped the R'lyeh Anthem eight times. Eight. No one before or since has done that. In Match Four, Vangellus fought tooth and nail with a much stronger Hessian. He came with a fight; for as small as he was compared to the giant, he fought like he was possessed. In fact, Oxios fought like he was Hessian's size. It wasn't about a high-flying offense. The match was little more than a backstage brawl, the two throwing VERY stiff strikes back and forth. Van won that match, even as Hessian was supposed to have destroyed him. Match Five, his first major tag team win. He'd won a match with Invincibleness a couple of weeks before, but that was an interference count-out victory. This was a clean win, and he and his partner worked as a good team. Van couldn't have asked for a better way to start off that new turn in his career, which saw him going on to win the Eclipse Title. Vangellus probably stopped his own momentum by not continuing in wrestling after RPW closed. He was doing great there, but now he's back at square one in PRIME. But I hear that he's trying to get back on track. If he can, then who knows? The sky's the limit. Testimonial: Justin Williamson, former PRIME tag team champion 'Who the hell is Vangellus Oxios?' That's what I was thinking when I he called and identified himself. This guy talked on the phone as if I was supposed to know who he was, so I hung up right then and there. He tried to call again, but I ignored it. And it went like that for three days until he faxed me to apologize for his absurd behavior. The next time that he called, I answered. He explained that he was in PRIME, was in a tag team with Scott Matthews. That name I was familiar with from back in Rush Pro Wrestling. We'd started out in the company, there at the ground floor before it was 'hip' to show up there. We had a mix of names, but it was all about starting something new. I was there as The H.I.T. (changed from Hardcore Isoceles Trapezoid to Horribly Insane Trapezoid) for the first few shows. There until Karina Wolfenden took me out. I won't go back into that now that it's been covered on PRIME television. Water under the bridge now. Oxios explained that they'd actually won the Tag Team titles for all of twenty minutes before losing it in a match in which they weren't the deciding fall. Shame on them. I explained that it was worse in that case, as they both were taken out of the match so that they weren't even a factor in the ending. It was only made worse when Oxios explained that they were both still rookies. Matthews was in a team called Invincibleness with a little guy named Gabe, but they were tag champions. I could chalk it up, though, to lack of competition in that division. In PRIME it must have been the same way if Matthews and Oxios were getting the belts when between them they have knowledge of maybe four wrestling moves. They did at least impress me well enough that they wanted to improve, and were willing to come to one of the best technical wrestlers to learn how to better ply their trade. Of course, I'd been out of the training game for a while... I wasn't a full-time trainer since that class back in Primetime Wrestling Academy, though I still dabbled in it. Now that I'm not a full-time wrestler, I charge snot-nosed brats exorbitant amounts of money to prove to them that they don't have what it takes. Those are all ones trying to get into the sport without much work, though. Matthews and Oxios were in, and champions at a point. On this, I reduced my fee by $100 (to the low, low price of $400 per session) and agreed to train them. They'd better pray that they're ready for my lessons.
