Date: Chapter 2 of "Five Years in the Making" February 5, 2003 A hotel room in Edinburgh, Scotland... For twenty minutes he'd been sitting next to the phone, wanting desperately for it all to be some horrible nightmare that he'd eventually wake from. From the moment he'd hung up the phone, he'd been shaking his head back and forth irregularly like some disjointed metronome, hoping that if he denied it enough, maybe it would cease to be true. If Troy shook hard enough, maybe he'd be able to turn his brain into an Etch-A-Sketch and erase the moment from his memory. It wasn't helping. His whole body was numb, and for a man who spent a good portion of his day in various degrees of unbearable pain, it was a wholly unique sensation. And right now, he wasn't sure he'd ever be able to rid himself of it. All he knew was that at this moment, he'd take a million times the pain he'd ever felt over this ... this ... nothingness he'd been enveloped in since he hung up the phone. The pain was gone. Rational thought was gone. The entire world was gone. All that was left were those two sentences that had broken him into more pieces than decades of football or pro wrestling ever had or ever would. They're gone. They're dead. For twenty minutes, those four little words had echoed through his head, bouncing between his ears so rapidly they'd become permanently burned into his synapses. In four words, the two people that meant everything to him were taken away. In four words, his spirit was completely shattered, and he was all but certain he'd never be able to patch it together. In four little words spoken to him an entire ocean away, Troy Douglas saw his entire universe washed away with the tide. It was a while before he could even manage to stand up. He just sat there on the edge of the bed, nearly catatonic, brushing his feet absentmindedly on the cold shag carpet every few seconds, not knowing what would happen if he moved from that spot. He was pretty sure that if he even dared to stand, he'd step right off the edge of the earth and never return. Which, at the moment, didn't seem to be the worst possible option. Finally, he awkwardly planted his feet on the ground and tried to leverage himself to a standing position, but God or gravity or sheer emotional weakness wouldn't let him. It was a good thing, too, because after all that deadness that had plagued him since he set down the phone, the emotional crush finally hit him at hurricane force. "Oh, God..." And then came the tears. Unabashed. Unashamed. Unrestrained. It flooded over him, and there was no dam, no levee to hold the rushing waters back. Time passed, but he didn't know how much. To be honest, he didn't care either. Not like it mattered all that much anymore. After a while, something finally helped Troy put a stopper in the tears. To this day, he never quite knew why, but as he saw the sun peek over Edinburgh's office buildings, he finally managed to calm himself. It was a tenuous calm, but for now, it was all he had to cling to. What he did next, he did as quickly as he could. Three phone calls in rapid succession, trying not to give himself any time in between to slip into either emotional extreme. He stuffed his clothes into his suitcase haphazardly, not caring if he left anything behind, and he moved like a zombie out of the room and into the hallway. He didn't look back. He didn't want to look back. Besides, the image of that room, on that night, was something he knew he was never going to be able to escape. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ "...free to move about the cabin." DING! Somewhere over the Atlantic Ocean, the seatbelt light on the British Airways 747 faded to nonexistence. With it, an entire planeload of returning tourists, businessmen and assorted travelers pulled themselves from their seats, stretching as the aircraft made its Transatlantic crossing. Except for Troy Douglas, of course. He hadn't noticed the light going off, or the flight attendant's message, or anything since the wheels pulled off the tarmac. He just stared out the window, watching the endless blue mass 30,000 feet beneath him pass by, every moment bringing him closer to a reality he didn't want to deal with. He knew when the wheels went down in Charlotte, he was going to have to bury the man who raised him and the woman he was going to spend the rest of his life with. It was only natural that he was contemplating smashing his head through the window and letting himself fall six miles into that massive, empty expanse of water, right? He could've gone on staring at the ocean for days, but the stewardess' hand reaching across to touch his arm pulled him out of his reverie. "Can I get anything for you, sir? Coffee, tea, juice?" The fact that she wanted to know his drink preferrence in the face of everything he'd had to come to terms with in the last few hours was extremely jarring. Automatically, robotically, he managed a reply. "Uh ... sure. Coffee. Black, three sugars." Dad. It was only appropriate at this moment that Troy parroted the exact same was his father took his coffee every morning. And it was that little reminder, that little pointless nugget of information that had been buried in some barely used corner of his memory, which caused Troy to reach under his seat for his carry-on bag, pull out a pen and a stack of blank paper, and start to write furiously. The eulogy he never wanted to write was exploding out of him like fireworks on the Fourth of July. He wouldn't stop until the plane touched down at Charlotte's Douglas Airport nearly five hours later. Except, when he was done, he didn't have one done. He had two. Which made it that much worse. ~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~ Atlanta, Georgia Five years later... He left the restaurant in high spirits. As a general rule, Troy abhorred promotional appearances and autograph signings. He hated being paraded around by some clueless PR representative like a giant carrot at the county fair, but PRIME had allowed him enough freedom to make his own bookings, so he was able to do some favors for friends and keep his public appearances to an absolute minimum. This one had gone particularly well, and as he walked down Peachtree Street back to his hotel, he smiled. And, after all, why not? As a victim of a career that had seemingly conspired with the fates to keep him from ever truly being content with his lot, Troy's life had finally seemed to turn around. His success in PRIME was more than he'd ever anticipated, and after years where turmoil was his only constant, he was finally set in something that -- by a professional wrestler's standards -- actually resembled normalcy.
