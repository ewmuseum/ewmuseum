Keeping Up (ReVolution 150, singles match vs. Tony 'The Grin' Gamble for the Intense Title)

29 January 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: January 29th, 2007 "Excuse me, sir? I’m sorry, but no pets are allowed in the hotel." "Well whad’ya ‘xpect me to do? Leave ‘im out in the truck all fuckin’ night?" "There’s nothing I can do about it, sir. Hotel rules." * * * * * * * "Sir, I’m afraid I can’t allow you in the restaurant without proper footwear. We don’t need dirt and mud tracking on our carpet." "And what if all I got are these here boots?" "Then there’s nothing I can do for you." * * * * * * * "Hey, buddy, we got a dress code in a club like this. You ain’t gettin’ in wearin’ that." "I’d tell ya to go fuck yerself, but I ain’t all that fuckin’ surprised." "Then get the fuck out." * * * * * * * His dirty boot struck a dented can of Millter Lite, skipping it along the pavement before coming to a rest. He stared down the street, eyeing two hookers, powdering themselves in preparation for potential coitus. He shook his head. "It’s movin’ too fast, Angus." Angus whined. Wade Elliott, PRIME’s Bad Dog, looked up to the lights of Arizona, as if searching for an answer. "The world’s movin’ a million fuckin’ miles an’ hour." He kicked at the ground before finding an empty bench and taking a heavy seat. Angus padded next to his master, taking a seat of his own. "It’s leavin’ fellers like me behind." Another whine from the bluetick. "Fellers like us." Wade dug into his jeans for his pack of Marlboro Reds, retrieving a lone cigarette from the box. He chucked the container and popped the cig into his mouth, bringing his silver Zippo to its end. He flicked it open and turned the grinding stone. Only spark. A few more turns proved nothing. He sighed a heavy, guttural sigh, and threw the Zippo and cigarette in frustration. "Fuckin’ figures." He fell against the backrest of the bench, drawing the palms of his hands down his forehead and cheeks. He leaned forward, turning his gaze back to the big city of Phoenix. "Feelin’ kinda small, cowboy?" Wade turned his head to find the spectre of Wild Bill Hikock had joined him on the bench, not to his surprise. "Ain’t feelin’ like no fuckin’ giraffe," replied the Blue Collar Brawler. Bill chuckled behind his thick gray handlebar mustache. "Gamble got ya down?" Wade’s already somber expression turned very sour. Just hearing the word "Gamble" turned the viscosity of his blood up about ten notches, but he soon recovered. "Naw, ain’t him." "Lindsay?" "Her business ain’t my business." "Then what’s got ya all twisted up?" Wade closed his eyes and breathed heavy through his nostrils. "The world’s a big fuckin’ place, Bill, and it moves faster’n I can handle. Everythin’ was a lot smaller back in Pine Ridge, and everythin’ moved a helluva lot slower." He turned his head to the dead lawman. "I can’t keep up." Hikock nodded. "I can follow yer meanin’," he said, as Wade stared at the ground. "I went off to Deadwood to look fer gold. ‘Course, I ended up playin’ too much poker, but ‘round the time McCall put a bullet to the back of my fuckin’ head the world was starting to pick a whole lot’ve speed." "Yeah?" asked Wade, turning to face Wild Bill. "Sure as fuckin’ shit. Big steam engines to do the minin,’ puttin’ smoke in the air, trains to bring folks wherever they fuckin’ wanted…" He paused, taking a deep breath of his own. "Part’ve me’s glad I weren’t around to see it take off sprintin’." Wade hung on his words, feeling a stronger connection to the ghost, that for reasons unexplained, had been following him as of late. "Yer a dyin’ breed, Wade. World ain’t got room fer the drifters anymore. Everythin’s shiny cars, nice clothes, big fuckin’ house with a fuckin’ pool. Yer one’ve the last sons’ve bitches who lives life day to fuckin’ day, town to fuckin’ town, knowin’ who the fuck he is an’ what he fuckin’ stands fer." Bill stood from his bench, eyes set across the street. "Just like I was." The lawman started off, undeterred as cars drove through his cloud-like body. "So what do I fuckin’ do about it?" called Wade. "Just you focus on gettin’ that Intense Belt back," replied Bill before fading away into a brick building. Wade sat for a while, gazing off into nothing. Eventually he stood himself up, which stirred Angus, who had made himself comfy next to the bench. "C’mon, pup." Angus sprung to his feet, falling into step with the Southern Sparkplug. "Let’s find the truck an’ git some sleep."
