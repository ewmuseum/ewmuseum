If You're Going To San Francisco...(ReV 69, vs. ETHAN Knight)

4 July 2005

Date: 4th of July “The Young Lion,” Marion ‘Hemlocke’ Sterling had once called him, but right now, at this moment, Nova thought that Ethan Knight more closely resembled a panther… …because, make no mistake about it, despite (or perhaps in spite) of the pale skin and longish almost bleached-blonde hair, there was something very, very dark about him, something utterly cool and calm that set Nova more on edge than he wanted to admit. They stood across the ring, sizing each other up. Nova felt as though he was doing a rather less successful job of it than his opponent was, and hoped foolishly that it didn’t show on his face. The lights above the crowd surrounding the ring had all been extinguished, leaving only a sterile white fluorescent glow to blanket everything inside the four turnbuckle posts. That never happened. Nova pushed the thought out of his head; it was pointless to argue semantics with himself…regardless of whether or not it had ever happened before, it had obviously happened now. The light seemed to hover around Knight’s body like a chalk outline, his skin almost white and his hair glistening. As for himself, Nova felt exposed under the light, his dirty-blonde hair, longer than Ethan’s, tucked back behind his shoulders and trademark blue bandanna, his wild beard strangely off-setting his opponent’s clean-shaven face, his trademark half-grin standing in complete opposition to the expressionless mask of ‘The Infinite.’ Knight took a step forward and then glided to one side, eyes locked firmly on Nova the entire time. Nova decided to move in closer, maybe press the height and weight advantage (something he was certainly not used to doing at 6’3” and 256 lbs.). Knight watched him advance, an almost disinterested look on his face. Ignoring it, Nova paused momentarily before lunging for his opponent’s legs, attempting a takedown that never arrived as he grabbed two armfuls of air. With no time to turn, his world exploded in color as two feet drove themselves into the back of his head, sending the 5-Star Champion tumbling forward into the corner. Nova pushed himself up with his arms, his head throbbing. What the hell had just happened? Did Knight flip over him and manage to kick him on the way down? Was that even possible? Again chastising himself for thinking in ‘possibilities,’ Nova decided that was indeed what had happened. Amazing. He glimpsed Horace Tully’s somewhat bulkier frame as he used the ropes to pull himself up, couldn’t help but imagine that there was something…off…about him, the same off-ness that existed about Ethan Knight, and about this whole… Nova cut himself off with a shake of the head…madness. Concentration was the key here, and if he didn’t keep it, he’d be face-up before he knew what had happened. Nova turned to face Knight, who stood now in the center of the ring, the only evidence of his move-for-counter victory present on his face in the form of a satisfied smirk. Nova did his best to return the show of confidence in a toothy grin of his own, but his heart just wasn’t in it. Nova stepped forward, feigning a lock-up before slinging a clothesline as hard as the speed of execution would allow…and got nothing; but it didn’t feel like Knight had evaded him. He thought for sure he had caught him, but Knight had simply disappeared, and a moment later, Nova felt a hand wrap around his neck and yank him off his feet, spiking him onto the mat with a neckbreaker. Nova lay on the mat, his breathing coming in rasps and the cigarettes now laughing at him, calling him a fool, an addict, a junkie. Out of the corner of his eye, he saw Hemlocke standing by ringside, her flowing green locks blending into her gown. He quickly reverted his gaze back to Knight, who now pulled him to his feet before irish-whipping him into the ropes. Nova reversed the whip, and as the Covenant member returned, Nova went low to toss him overhead…but mid-heave, Knight seemed to corkscrew around, landing feet-first on Nova’s right shoulder before turning and flash-kicking him in the temple (think Guile from Street Fighter II). As Nova lay several feet away sprawled out, Knight landed on his feet in the ring. Impossible. Fucking impossible. Half-confused, half-groggy, Nova painfully wrenched his head up from the mat. He didn’t know how Knight was doing any of the things he was doing, but shouldn’t the crowd be going approximately half past bat-shit right now? They weren’t, though. In fact, Nova couldn’t make out the faces of anyone in the crowd, odd given that the fans in the front row were only several feet from him. Everyone appeared as silhouettes, and stranger, everyone in the arena seemed almost…well, no almost about it…they seemed to be swaying slightly, back and forth, in a rhythmic motion. Nova couldn’t entirely tell, for the massive black slow-moving space surrounding the ring, but there also appeared to be several tiny purple flames burning throughout the crowd. Nova looked over, and his eyes widened, surprised to see Horace Tully standing only a few feet away…his grin now unnaturally pronounced, stretched too tight and wide for his face, and in the lens of his sunglasses, Nova saw yellow-orange flames reaching up to lick the top of the frames. Horrified, Nova rolled onto his knees and scooted away from the edge of the ring, standing up and turning to face Knight. Better to continue the ass-kicking than stare for another second longer at that disfigured, garishly cartoony smile, and those… …eyes. Nova’s mouth dropped open. Before him, Ethan Knight stood, his weight rested lazily on one leg. His skin was more pale than it had been minutes prior, now a dried-out, feature-less white, and his eyes…his eyes were as visible to Nova as if he were nose to nose with ‘The Infinite.’ In them, Nova saw his own face, covered in blood, his mouth hung open and his eyes closed, lids caked with gore. Before Nova could cry out, the image disappeared from Knight’s eyes, replaced by black as his pupils dilated fully. A moment later, they constricted once more, revealing viscerally red irises. Suddenly Knight was not in front of Nova but above him, plummeting to the mat as the air whistled around him, and for a moment, a brief flash, Knight was not Knight but Bruce “Violence Jack” Shanahan. The image left Nova as soon as it arrived, and with an effortless gainer-like flip (as if this violence could be compared to competitive diving!), Ethan curved his body around to land chest-to-chest on top of Nova, driving the air out of the self-proclaimed “King of the Friend or Foe Tag Match” with an ass-on-whoopee-cushion effect. Knight raised up onto his knees, and Nova rolled over on the mat, clutching his chest, and his gaze fell again on Hemlocke. His mouth hung open (and not from the gasps of air he’d attempted to draw) as he saw the serpentine pattern of her dress now floating translucently across her cool, collected face, as well as her hands, the only other area of her body not covered by dark green fabric. Nova had seen the effect countless times after ingesting several grams of psychedelic mushrooms, but this wasn’t a tripping party…this was a wrestling match. Almost as though sensing Nova’s confusion and oncoming panic, a small smile crept up at the corners of Marion Sterling’s mouth…a knowing smile that oddly complimented her glinting yellow eyes. Knight wrenched Nova to his feet and slung him against the ropes. It felt, to Nova, as though his back had barely come off the ropes of the far side before Knight was hurtling through the air, twisting his torso around to connect his right heel with Nova’s forehead. Again, in a flash, Knight was Violence Jack, limbs extended, tattoos swirling and twisting across his body wholly unnaturally as he prepared to crack Nova’s skull with his foot. The blow left Nova staring up at the singularly focused lights, his vision a blur. Looking out into the crowd as he tried to focus, Nova could see that the nightmarish silhouettes were no longer simply swaying back and forth, but teeming and dancing maniacally around thousands of roaring purple fires. At ringside (if you could still even refer to this din of cacophonic chaos as an arena), Hemlocke’s hair flowed out away from her head, defying gravity, as the greenish patterns of her gown swirled across her face. “HA, HA, HA, HA, HA, HA!!” Horace Tully’s head was thrown back, his mouth open wider than any human’s mouth was meant to span as he roared with…‘amusement’ isn’t a strong enough word…unadulterated delight. Knight was grinning, too, as he grabbed a handful of Nova’s hair and yanked him up to his feet. The 5-Star Champion saw Knight raise an arm, and something glint brightly in the air… …and then his vision flashed white, followed by a flash of red, before returning slowly, darkly. Trembling, Nova looked down to see Knight’s hand spattered with blood, gripping the handle of a blade now sunk deeply into Nova’s side, underneath his ribs…the blade that had murdered Knight’s old partner Jago Sensaki, according to rumor. A single tear dripped down his cheek as his mouth hung open, desperately sucking in the air that wasn’t going to keep him alive any longer. One hand wrapped around Nova’s shoulder, Knight wrenched the blade out of his side, and Nova felt warmness as blood spurted before pouring down his leg to the mat. A second later, Knight plunged the blade into Nova a second time, pushing it in until his hand threatened to submerge in the wound. A second flash of red. Gently, as Nova dropped to his knees, his body shivering in the throes of death, Knight knelt with him, one hand still on his shoulder and the other still clutching the handle of the blade protruding from the 5-Star Champion’s mid-section. Knight leaned in, whispering something inaudible in Nova’s ear before ripping the blade again from his side and releasing his grip on his shoulder. Nova wobbled on his knees for a moment, his eyes glossy and vacant, before laying back on the mat, feeling a puddle of blood expanding out around him. After a moment, Nova stopped breathing, finding it oddly unnecessary…and, giving up what remained of his muscle control, allowed his head to roll over, landing softly in the growing slick of blood with a… SPLASH! Nova opened his eyes suddenly, aware of the wetness dripping from his face and soaking his clothes…but not the wetness of blood… …rather, the wetness of…*sniff*…Iced Tea? “Oh, my God…” Nova looked up, tea dripping from his nose and beading up in his beard, to see a woman standing in the aisle next to his row, a lid-less cup clutched tightly in her hand. Her face was beet-red, her expression aghast. “Oh, God, I am so sorry! I don’t know what happened! The bus hit a bump, and the lid came off my…oh, God, I don’t believe this…” Bus? Nova looked around at other rows before peering out the window next to him that showcased hilly, rolling terrain. Oh, right…the bus taking him from Anaheim to San Francisco. He remembered now. “Oh, let me help you…God, I still can’t believe that happened.” The woman he had almost forgotten about was now desperately trying to blot his shirt with napkins. Without thinking, she began moving down, blotting his waist, his thighs… “I think I can handle that, thanks,” Nova said plainly, gently grabbing her hand and taking the sodden napkin wad out of it. “Oh,” the woman replied, her face turning even redder (something Nova had viewed moments before as wholly impossible), “of course. My apologies. Jesus, what a piece of work I am, huh? Here…” She offered Nova the rest of her napkins, and he gave her a reassuring smile before taking them and wiping off his face. He motioned to the empty seat next to him. “It’s kind of wet, but you can take a seat if you want.” The look on her face said ‘confused,’ but Nova interpreted it as ‘torn.’ “Oh,” she started, “oh, that’s so nice of you after I spilt my drink all over you and accidentally groped your…well, I mean, I already have a seat a few rows back, and…” Her reply faded out as she smiled amiably at him. Nova shrugged. “Sure. And don’t worry about the drink, it’s no big deal, really. It’ll all be dry here in a bit, and I could do worse than have iced tea for a scent.” She barked out a laugh, a little too loudly, and eyed the vacant seat one more time. “Well, sure, why not? I’ll just get my things.” A minute later, she returned with a suitcase and her purse, sliding the former into an overhead storage compartment and the latter under the seat in front of her. She slid into the seat… “HO!” she cried with a tiny laugh, standing back up quickly, staring down at the seat, “you weren’t kidding about the dampness, were you?” “Afraid not,” Nova confessed before reaching a bag at his feet and removing a t-shirt, laying it across the chair, “try this.” “Oh, really, you don’t have to,” the woman weakly protested, staring down at his effort, “I hate to be all this trouble…” “It’s really no problem,” Nova said firmly, knowing she’d accept, and she did, sitting down on the t-shirt and delicately crossing her left leg over her right. “Thanks again, Mr…?” the woman probed. “No need for ‘Mr.s,’” Nova replied with a dismissive wave, “you can call me Nova.” “Chris” was a name that close friends called him by…there was an intimacy to it that Nova guarded greedily, the right to refer to him by his middle name (he never went by his admittedly strange first name) something to be held with a measure of sanctity…at least in Nova’s eyes. “And why is that?” she asked, cocking an eyebrow, thinking she already knew the answer. “It’s not the beginning of a pick-up line, if that’s what you’re anticipating,” Nova said, taking pleasure in the noticeably surprised look in her face, “it’s a moniker I’ve come to be known by in my business.” “And what business is that?” she continued with the questions, her interest noticeably piqued, despite her now-obvious attempts to appear cool and reserved. As Nova continued talking, one thing became even more clear than it had been from the outset: He could nail this woman if he wanted to. Probably in the bus lavatory, if he played his cards right, but definitely when they got off in San Fran. All it would take would be a, “how ‘bout a drink?” and he would be in like Flint. She was hanging off his every word…it was brilliant. She was soaking up every detail, every anecdote, about something only minutes ago she had probably not given a second thought… …professional wrestling. Nova could never understand it, how some people were ashamed to be involved in the business of professional wrestling. Sure, there were pricks who talked down to you, women whose noses curled up like heinous ass had just been ripped…but nine times out of ten, it was in presentation. Guys bitching in the locker room about chicks peacing out as soon as they heard about the business were, on the whole, full of shit. You talk about it like it’s the fucking papacy, they’ll think of it that way. You half-whisper it under your breath and avoid eye contact, they’ll think you’re a douche-bag. Nova was whole-heartedly convinced that, with the proper enthusiasm, he could tell a woman that he sprayed the shit out of horse stables, and they’d be enthralled. On the flipside, he figured, he could also her he was the CEO of Money & Co., and without that same confidence, she’d be half-asleep before he finished talking about how much dough he raked in. Nova noticed this woman, now, in a way that he hadn’t really been given time to before…her suit-coat masked breasts that were probably a C cup brassiere size. She hadn’t missed any meals, but there was something sensual in the curvature of her hips, thighs that promised rough loving but weren’t overly ample. He could imagine her hair, now pinned-up, falling loose over her shoulders, her eyes, now shielded by black frame glasses, rolling back in ecstacy. Nova noticed all of these things, and re-affirmed, to himself, that he could nail this woman if he wanted to… …and at once decided not to pursue it. His dick would feel emasculated, but the rest of him would feel better for having let the ass go. What had been terminated between Ariel and him was sacred, special…not something to say, “Well, it’s over…who’s ready to fuck?” Their relationship deserved a period of respect in its closure, a period to reflect. He hoped, prayed that she felt the same way. The thought of her with another man made Nova simultaneously nauseous, angry, and terribly sad…but he was going to have to get used to it. With a hissss, the bus came to a stop. “Well,” Nova said cheerily while his balls screamed defiantly for him to plow this bitch, “it was really good talking to you. Good luck.” The woman stared at him openly. She thought for sure they’d be going out for a drink. She made no point of hiding her surprise as Nova reached over her head, grabbed his other bag, gave her another grin and a nod, and turned, walking determinedly up the aisle of the bus and down the steps onto the street. Stepping down onto the sidewalk, Nova breathed deep and took a look around. The old Scott McKenzie tune immediately sprung to mind… “If you’re going to San Francisco, be sure to wear some flowers in your hair…” Nova imagined that forty years ago, he might have done that very thing. Cinching up his blue bandanna and sliding on a beat-up old pair of black Ray-Bans, Nova walked a few blocks before stopping to look up at two street signs posted at an intersection. DIVISADERO ST. HAIGHT ST. A wide grin spread across Nova’s face. He was back in “The Haight,” the name given to the surrounding district, and more importantly, Haight St., the Hippie sanctuary back before Nova’s parents ever decided to bump uglies…the site of the Summer of Love, the street where George Harrison walked with a guitar in his hands followed by a crowd of flower children, the place the Grateful Dead once called home. Divisadero St. divided Haight St. into Upper Haight and Lower Haight. Turning right, Nova walked up past several Victorian homes before stopping in front of a plain wooden door, the green paint chipping somewhat. He peered at a small box mounted on the wall, and pressed the red button located underneath the speaker. “Who is it?” came the fuzzy reply out of the box. “You know who the fuck it is,” Nova attempted to snarl, but he was unable to restrain a grin, “unlock the door.” Nova heard a buzzing sound followed by a clicking noise, and pulled open the door. He walked up the steps, a fuzzy light/dark green hybrid fabric, and glanced at the walls, of the same color in stucco. The smell of ganja was heavy, even in the hallway, and as Nova ascended the top step, the door to the apartment on the right flung open, revealing a man several inches shorter than Nova, with long gray hair and a beard of matching color. A red bandanna tied around his forehead, round-rimmed sunglasses slipping down his nose, a leather vest over a white t-shirt, and a cigarette dangling from one hand, the man looked as though he could be Nova, albeit several years down the road. “CHRIS!!” The man lunged forward, wrapping his arms around Nova, who couldn’t help but grin out of excitement and pride at being so warmly, genuinely received by The Fox, a man he held almost unparallel respect for. Dana Foxer earned his reputation, initially, as a volunteer war correspondent in Vietnam. Since no one wanted to call him Dana (including himself), “The Fox” became his wartime moniker. His photographic depictions of the war became the stuff of legend, most famously his work in the Ia Drang Valley in November of 1965, the bloodiest period of the Vietnam War for America. It was said at times the only way some of those pictures ever got developed was because The Fox grabbed up a fallen soldier’s M-16 and helped carve his way out of an LZ and onto a chopper, though he never endorsed for or against the stories. The hardest marines in I-Corps or anywhere else had all heard of The Fox, and the mention of him brought a smile to the grunts’ faces quicker than the phrase “R & R.” Nova met The Fox in a bar in Nashville, TN, the latter flying out to New York and the former working shows with a piece of shit wrestling company whose owner was too drunk to care if his employees showed up for the “tapings,” which were in serious danger of losing their illustrious timeslot of 7:30 AM Sunday mornings on cable access. They hit it off famously. “Shit, man!” The Fox said, his glazed eyes widening as he yanked his cigarette arm away, “sorry, not trying to burn ya…wouldn’t wanna give ya another scar before you even step inside, eh?” The Fox was referring to the only fight he and Nova had ever gotten into, where he had cheap-shotted the younger, stronger man, leaving a scar above Nova’s right eye and teaching him a lesson about how to handle things outside a wrestling ring. “Welcome back to Frisco,” The Fox said amiably, leading Nova into the apartment. The smell of grass hit Nova like a wall. Just hanging in here for five minutes would get you stoned to the bone. The sounds of Dylan flowed out of The Fox’s old-timer shit-piece record player. “Lay, lady, lay…lay across my big brass bed…” Nova set down his bags and stepped over a two-foot blue glass bong on the floor to take a seat on the couch. Looking around, he felt himself not necessarily go back in time…but more like step out of it. Posters, covers, paintings on every inch of the wall…Frank Zappa and the Mothers of Invention, Janis Joplin, Hendrix shredding his guitar, as well as some of The Fox’s photographs from Vietnam…and candles around too, candles melted over small Buddha statues, and one candle in particular, the base melted to a soldier’s helmet from Vietnam that had “READY TO DIE” scrawled on one part, and “WHERE IS LEE HARVEY OSWALD WHEN WE NEED HIM?” on the other. The Fox almost never lit that candle, and never for very long at all. “Chris, you remember Barry, right?” The Fox asked, bringing Nova back to reality. Nova smiled at a man sitting in a chair opposite him, dressed in a suit and tie, a black briefcase at his feet. Jesus, the company ol’ Foxer kept… “Sure I do,” Nova replied, nodding to Barry, “how’s Wall Street, Barry?” “It’s holding up,” Barry replied, “how’s…uh, fuck…what was…” “Wrestling,” The Fox finished for him, winking at Nova. “Yeah, wrestling,” Barry said, “how is that?” There was no sarcasm or condescension in his question, despite the fact that Barry probably brought in some nasty fuckin’ dough from his stock broker job…but that was the way it was at The Fox’s apartment. Cool people from all walks of life, brought together by their sometimes random associations with Dana Foxer. They spent the next few hours getting high…listening to Nova describe his life on the road, Barry talking about acquisitions, and, their combined, they even got a few war stories out of The Fox, as well as some of his more fucked-up nights with the likes of Keith Moon, Jerry Garcia, Richard Pryor, and the host of other random people who, like Nova and Barry, had each their own connection with The Fox. It was what Nova needed before flying to Denver…a chance to forget that there was a Brandon Youngblood, or a PRIME, or titles, or any of the bullshit. A chance to forget that his personal life had, metaphorically, been in a car accident, reduced to a comatose state. A chance to get away from the sometimes infuriating atmosphere of PRIMEates and staff who labeled him the Company Stoner, and giggled like it was a gimmick, some hot exotic commodity, instead of a predilection for getting high and an appreciation for the culture associated with it, though that gripe, in all honesty, was infinitesimal. He had needed to get away from it all, if only for a while, so as to not kick down any more hotel room doors and wail on neighboring guests’ walls with towel racks… …and his visit to see his old friend at Haight St. provided him with that opportunity. It was therapeutic, and that night, Nova slept better than he had in weeks. ------*~*~*------ He found himself at the mercy of Knight once again. He lay on the mat trying to push himself up, willing his muscles to continue their rigorous program of withstanding punishment from ‘The Infinite.’ Knight spared Nova the trouble, planting a boot in his ribs before reaching down and grabbing a handful of sweaty hair from the back of Nova’s head, wrenching him up to his feet. A swift, well-placed knee to the gut doubled over the 5-Star Champion as he gasped for air. Nova’s eyes drifted up from the mat to the crowd, shapeless black silhouettes jumping and dancing around the purple fires that had so frightened Nova before. It was going to happen again. Looking into the fires, Nova somehow knew it already. As if on cue, Knight’s hand grasped Nova’s throat and pulled him back upright. Unable to breathe, Nova could only watch as Knight’s free hand flourished an object that gleamed in the eerie lighting of the ring… …but as his hand stabbed downward, one of Nova’s own shot out and grabbed it by the wrist. Knight’s eyes widened for a split-second before narrowing in anger as he put all his strength into the plunging motion of the blade…but Nova would not relent to the charade this time. Bruce Shanahan’s Sect of Black Wisdom, his Covenant, were a frightening crew, frightening to Nova because they were real and deadly and did not, as the hokey Dark Age had, succumb to the stereotypes that made so many others laughable instead of intimidating. Yes, they were frightening to Nova, but he believed he could push the blade back, knew it like he hadn’t known it before, so he did. He thrust Knight’s arm back, squeezing the wrist and causing him to drop the blade. Moments later, after a hook to Knight’s ribs that caused him to release the choke, Nova slung the arm that had held the blade over his neck, and cocking his head back only to whisper something inaudible into Knight’s ear, hoisted him up and planted him in ring with the Remix to In-NOVA-tion. At that moment, Ethan Knight was gone, as was Horace Tully, Hemlocke, the black silhouettes of the crowd, and the purple fires. The arena lit up fully, revealing people standing silently in the crowd, staring down at Nova, now by himself in the ring. Nova panted heavily, looking around at the fans packed into the stadium, all eyes locked on him in absolute silence. Ignoring them, Nova sat down in the middle of the ring, exhausted, and lit a cigarette.
