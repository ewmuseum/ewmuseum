The office of the reverential Hin See is something rarely addressed. So rarely addressed that I feel wholly comfortable making up its description according to my whim, which, in case you haven’t noticed, is a little bit on the slackjawed side of stupid right now. So without further ado . . . Hin See’s office has four walls, a ceiling made of cheap fluorescent lights, and – drumroll! – a floor. Thought I was gonna go all out, didn’t you? Sucker. Obviously, the makeup of the room isn’t important. Nor is the layout, so allow me to . . . lay it . . . out . . . er . . . allow me to break it down for you nice and simple like. There’s a large desk with a bunch of nonsense on it (some note about a package being too large to deliver, then a letter from the Transylvania County Sperm Bank, demanding that management tell Vampir Nosferatu they are NOT – repeat: NOT!!!1! - a cosmetics or face-paint depository), and a huge – we’re talking Hoyt’s mouth here . . . Yeah. – high-backed chair, currently facing away from the door, with PRIME embossed across the back in rich gold lettering. MAD lettering, even. The chair is angled slightly so that one of the armrests, the left one, is visible. Perched on this armrest is Mr. Bojangles, curled up contentedly and purring, and also a metal cast containing the left arm of Hin See himself, presumably sheathed in armour because of some fiendish game of Indian Sunburn with The Dark Age, not to mention threats of a continuation if Hin See doesn’t . . . make . . . er . . . that is, if he . . . ah . . . Let’s just say there are threats. And now, just to demonstrate how flawlessly I could segue (pronounced seg-way . . . yes, this is how it’s spelled) if I wasn’t in a mood to highlight and brag about it . . . Speaking of threats (okay, so that’s the worst possible way to segue . . . fist yourself), the man standing just inside the door, glaring at the back of the chair from under the brim of his grey hat, looking almost private eye-ish in his uncharacteristically tacky grey suit, is Angelo Deville, who positively relishes threatening people. Still speaking of threats . . . Angelo Deville: If you don’t stop looking at me like that, I’m gonna punch you so hard you’ll fly back in time to when Los Diablos last won a match. (editor’s note: against a team other than mismatched washed-up has-beens) From the other side of the chair sounds a confused grunt. Hin See: The hell are you talking about? I’m facing away from you, toolbox. If you think you weren’t expecting Hin See to sound like a rasping James Earl Jones, you should see my fucking face, man. The Deville frowns and looks to his left for consoling, where a little blonde girl in a pink skirt and pigtails – known to us as Ellie~! – tries to cheer him up by handing him a penny. Before he can spend it all in one place, Deville turns his gaze on the leashed white dog at his other side, who shrugs and does the cartoon-dog-famous “aruro”. He’s such a brain, this cartoon dog. Hin See: And why do you have a dog here? Mr. Bojangles wakes up to hiss, then settles back down with a smug look when the claw starts stroking between his ears. Hin See: After that shit with the rabies, is this really what we need? Does this somehow strike you as a good idea? Deville exchanges another look with his companions, who both shake their heads in warning. Angelo Deville: Indeed it does. Swelling with arrogance, Deville begins to lecture. Angelo Deville: The dog is here for Los Diablos, to serve as a reminder that even when they think they have me, together they don’t even have a clue. That even when I’m fucking with them, and they’re being fucked with, knowing I’m fucking with them, I’m fucking them over on other levels beyond their wildest fathoming. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: He’s symbolic. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: I fucking rule. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: Way more than they do. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: And even while they think they know it. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: They have no clue. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: They’re clueless. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: In the dark. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: Lost. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: Gone. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: Because I’m me. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: I fucking rule. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: Look at the dog. Hin See: ENOUGH! Mr. Bojangles is sent leaping and yowling, legs scrambling, as the mightily clenched claw crashes into the armrest, punctuating the shout with a bang. Hin See: What the fuck was that? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: Your life story? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: Your autobiography? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: Are you selling a book? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: Is this why I’m paying you? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: So I can look at a dog? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: To see your life? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: And Los Diablos’ future? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: In a fucking dog? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: What? Crowd: WHAT? Mr. Bojangles climbs back up to his perch, glaring death at Deville, then hisses with laughter and rubs up against the claw. Hin See: And what the hell happened at Ultra Violence? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: Was Jimmy having an off-night? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: Was he lost, too? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: Clueless? Crowd: WHAT? Hin See: Didn’t know where he was? Crowd: WHAT? In response, Deville smirks, leaning back and spreading his hands before him as if that’s the most preposterous thing he’s ever heard. That’s how he begins his response, at least. It continues thusly: Angelo Deville: No. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: I paid him a gratuity before the match and let him know there was more where it came from. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: I bribed him. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: Paid him off. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: Scratched his back. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: So he’d scratch mine. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: Scratches are good. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: Except from rabid dogs. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: I fucking rule. Crowd: WHAT? Angelo Deville: Look at the dog. ~><>…<><~ Earlier that day . . . “Oooooooh!” exclaimed Eleanor Kannon-Hall, pleased as piss in snow that we’d switched over to past tense novel. “You have mail, Angie!” Bubbling her way through the front door of the house I just invented for Deville in whatever town the house show was set to take place in, ignoring everything that happened in their past for the sake of the theme of this roleplay, she waved the envelope at Deville with one hand and cradled the dog to her bosom with the other. Deville walked down the hallway I won’t describe (Do you really want to hear about how the wall was cut-in at knee-height, giving way to an aquarium full of cannibalistic fish, or about the patterned friezes and hanging plants decorating the walls? Or about the open closet and the skeletons spilling out of it? That’s just filler, brother.) and accepted the letter with an arched eyebrow. Waggling his fingers over it, Deville nodded with satisfaction as the glue magically un-stuck and the letter folded out, suspended in mid-air, to be read. “Dear Angelo,” he began reading, his lips moving silently for the most part as he scanned, picking up bits here and there. “. . . raiding gate funds . . . trying to bribe us . . . a tenth of what you gave . . . still yours . . . The Official Officials.” Deville tore his gaze away from the letter long enough to smile and “awwww” at Eleanor, pressing a hand to his heart. “And what’s this?” Deville’s eyes narrowed as he picked up reading at the bottom of the page. “PS: This message will self-destruct in fifteen seconds.” Looking way more agitated than anyone who had just received a mailbomb had a right to look – alright, fine, bad choice of phrase – Deville crumpled up the letter and launched it out through the front door, where it swirled up on the breeze at supersonic speed. Presumably – nay, factually, since I’m in charge – the letter floated on that divine breeze all the way down to Mexico, where it detonated with so much force that Kano, for a very brief but memorable second, actually rocked. Seriously. No. Seriously. Right, so the explosion levelled Mexico, rendering it a charred ruin devoid of wealth, culture, and intelligent beasts. Shockingly, no one noticed. “What’s wrong?” Eleanor, her voice tinged with worry as she eyed the vein bulging on Deville’s forehead, brought us back to the here and now with. “Not only did those cocksuckers rip off Inspector Gadget, but they ripped off Inspector Gadget ripping off Mission Impossible!” Trembling, turning red, Deville seemed to pulsate. “What the hell?! That’s like bane-age squared! RRRRRRRRRRRUAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAH!” Eleanor squealed and covered the dog as Deville literally exploded, turning green and doubling in size. Luckily, no one lost an eye, as Deville’s pants magically grew with him, but I digress. “I CAN’T STAND STEALING OR RECYCLING IDEAS! DEVILLE SMASH!” Smashing through the wall, The Incredible Deville disappeared into the neighbourhood, pistol-whipping with prejudice anyone and everyone who dared to quote a movie slogan or catchphrase. Eleanor looked at the dog, who shrugged, then noticed the glowing envelope on the table beside her. “What’s this?” Giggling with excitement at being able to answer her own question, forgetting she’s Eleanor, not Ellie~!, Eleanor snatched it up and began reading. “Mail today or die harder and faster than Boda’s time at the top.” Harrumphing, tearing off the yellow post-it note, Eleanor shared another look with the dog, who slapped a paw against his forehead and groaned. “It’s addressed to The Department of Im . . . Im . . . um . . . Oh! It says: ATTN: Cash bribe enclosed. NOT spam! Bribe! B-R-I-B-E, not S-P-A-M!” Indeed, the envelope did say all that, spelling included, right under the addressee. Then there was a series of dots, the same message in Braille. And after that a phone number, which, when dialled, played a recorded message that stated simply “It’s a bribe, asshat.” With a look of horror, Eleanor dropped the dog and the envelope both, and slapped her hands against her cheeks, then screamed like a young child actor who had just discovered the Neverland ranch was in his future. Abandoning the senseless melodrama just as quickly, she snatched the envelope back up and nodded to the dog. “Something tells me this is important. Think we should save Angelo’s behind and mail it for him?” The dog nodded. “You’re so smart,” Eleanor chirped, picking up the envelope and the brainy dog and skipping out the front door. ~><>…<><~ Hin See glares.
