War (Part II)

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: You'll see Funerals are the rituals we create to help us face the reality of death, to give us a way of expressing our response to that reality with other persons, and to protect us from the full impact of the meaning of death for ourselves. Some funerals fulfill these functions beautifully. Others fail. ALLA BOZARTH CAMPBELL March 6th, 2009 Larry Connors Residence Time: 1:45 PM The gut wrenching screams of Larry Connors still fill the emptiness of Apartment 304. Two weeks have elapsed since the untimely death of his soul mate. Everybody in their lives envisioned them as the couple who would grow old together. They would sway back and forth in rocking chairs sipping lemonade while grandchildren roamed the front yard playing catch. The photos of her brutal murder are etched into his mind, eyes having stared at them hundreds upon hundreds of times. She was slaughtered at the hands of ruthless individuals with no compassion for human life. Her bones were shattered, quarts of blood spilled through the alley, and the last breath from her body whispered faintly into the night. To the general world, Maria Connors will remain a statistic of the system. To Larry Connors, she will still remain his everything. No amount of time will be capable of healing the wounds of his heart and his soul. In his mind, Larry knows the identity of his culprits but his body can't rise from the stupor to extract revenge. The necessary revenge. Instead, his miseries are being alleviated by the foul stench of vodka. Sloppily pouring another shot into his East Carolina University glass, Larry throws the liquid back with reckless abandon. Scattered throughout the master bedroom are a cavalcade of beer and massive alcohol bottles. Aged and new pools of vomit lie inches away from his body which is cradled in the fetal position against his closet door. Even though, as a child, his father stoically reminded him after every bender that "drinking never solved any problems", Larry is left with no other viable alternative. The life he was working so hard to establish has been decimated. His daughter is left motherless and living with friends unable to fully explain her whereabouts. One day, Larry will have to tell Cynthia that her mother wasn't involved in an accident. He already knows that it will be one of the most difficult conversations a father could ever have with his daughter. Another haphazardly poured shot, another empty shot glass. Perhaps, he thinks, if enough alcohol batters his body, Maria will come back and all of the problems will dissipate. Observing the grief is Larry's brother, Steven Connors, face blank as a sheet of white paper. Lanky arms are folded across his emotionless chest. His stance is comparable to a military sergeant about to force his men to fall into line. No tears or alcohol bottles are present at his feet. While they are blood brothers, their internal makeup makes them two different entities. Steven desperately wants to encourage Larry that the time for sulking is over and business needs to be attended to. However, no amount of logic, regardless of accuracy, will soothe the pathetic man trying to purge himself into the loving arms of his dead wife. So the burden of responsibility falls upon his shoulders. He knows Larry will come around in due time. Christian and company are responsible for bringing his brother into a state of debauchery and he must rectify the wrong. The soldier in his body has been dormant for far too long and one of his men is badly wounded. Brandishing the .50 caliber handgun in his pocket, Steven turns and leaves Larry to mope. He's going to make this right. Downtown Raleigh, North Carolina March 6th, 2009 Time: 6:54 PM Under the jurisdiction of Christian Daniels, Matthew Smith is a businessman and legal extraordinaire while Brandon Smith is technologically gifted. Through their ingenuity, every scheme of the Smith family and Devin Shakur has gone off without fallacy. They are integral portions to the decade long success of Christian Daniels and his ability to escape from legal trouble. "I won't deny it, I'm a straight ridah You don't wanna fuck with me Got the police bustin at me But they can't do nuttin to a G."   But in the privacy of their own company, without any watchful eyes lingering over them, they like to indulge in a lifestyle that mainstream society would consider acceptable. Both men are rich beyond their wildest dreams, capable of acquiring any material object on a whim, and want to reap the benefits of their materialistic wealth. After Maria's death went off without a hitch, these two deserve a weekend to do whatever their hearts desire. Slowly tuning his head to the beat of Tupac Shakur, Matthew shouts, "Turn that shit up, nigga!" Brandon obliges his brother's request and slides down the windows of their 2008 Rolls Royce Phanton Coupe. Making a left onto James road, the devilishly handsome duo are now fully jamming to the beat of a great lyricist taken before he reached his prime. "Awww yeah, now we're groovin!" Brandon shouts, but instinctively drops the volume in a flash when the vehicle comes to a stoplight. Fifteen cars behind the lead, they couldn't have picked a better spot to be. Brandon spots a gorgeous woman in a Toyota Prius next to him and can't resist working the charm. "What's your name, baby?" Brandon asks, leaning his shoulders out of the car onto the door handle and flashing his million dollar smile. It has a way of melting hearts that rivals a certain Underground Pimp. With a face redder than a rose, the young woman who can't be older than twenty one responds, "Lisa". After a small pause, she quickly regains her sense of confidence, mimicking his posture, moving her body toward his and resting her elbows in front of the rolled down window. "What about yours?" None of the parties see a man clad from head to toe in black, crouching against nearby trees, watching Matthew and Brandon like a hawk. "Brandon..." The natural charisma continues to pour from his words. His standing isn't devalued by the fact that he's hanging from a car worth over half a million dollars. "...Lisa, always loved that name, got a panache on it that I can dig. What you doin' tonight?" "You know, typical Friday, about to have dinner and hang out with my girls." Brandon gives a quick cursory glance to his left and plots the amount of time he's got left before the lights change, fifty eight point seven seconds. "Hey Lisa, I got a better idea for you..." "Oh yeah, what's that?" Turning his full attention back to the potential conquest, Brandon pulls a lamented card from his pocket and hands it to Lisa, taking an extra second to gently caress her silky smooth skin and stare into her ocean blue eyes. "I own this club up by Hillsborough, The Republic, why don't you bring the girls up there and we can have some...real fun." Taking a hold of the VIP pass, Lisa returns the gesture, running her finger around his wrist and licking her lips. With traffic void on the left side of the road, black military issue boots rush across the pavement at the driver's side of the Rolls Royce. "I'll take you up on that..." Lisa invokes some of her own charm, producing a seductive smile at Brandon. "...And see you later tonight, bab-OH MY GOD!" Matthew and Brandon quickly turn their heads and come face to face with a .50 caliber handgun. "SHIT!" Instinctively, they try to scatter away from the scene as the first shot blasts through the back window. No luck. They are boxed in by other cars while their stubborn drivers scatter for cover and refuse to budge. The attacker calculated the same amount of math when making his move, twenty point seven seconds until the light changes. You've got them now soldier. Matthew isn't about to give up. He frantically pushes forward, trying to nudge a Cadillac out of his way, avoiding shots two and three, but the brothers are stuck. Everybody within earshot drops down, covering their heads in fright. The shots pumping from the .50 caliber resonate loud and with purpose. Horrified screams cloud the intersection of James Road and Hilton Drive. Those frozen in fright are yanked to safety by brave eyewitnesses. The lack of silencer is indicative that the attacker wants to make a statement. Shots four and five rip through the drivers side window with ease, but Matthew and Brandon manage to duck. Unfortunately, they no longer have protection and the Cadillac has put on its parking brake. Now with a good look at Matthew, the attacker fires a sixth shot. The businessman emits a guttural scream when the bullet strikes his forearm. "GODDAMMIT!" The attacker reloads with frightening speed. No hesitation. No nervousness. Brandon sees the holes in the window and tries to hide in the footrest. The attacker puts his gloved fist through the window and makes Brandon pay the ultimate price. One shot slams into his ribs. The blood curdling scream is almost indescribable to human language. "GET US THE FUCK OUT OF HERE, MATT!" Matthew's right hand finds the wheel and his foot pounds the accelerator, indifferent of being surrounded by other vehicles. The Rolls Royce crashes into Lisa's Toyota Prius, but the blonde haired blue eyed beauty has already fled the scene into a nearby ditch. You can't avoid this. This has to be done. Finish the job, soldier. With great tenacity, the attacker slings open Matthew's door and unloads the rest of his weapon, alternating shots into the bodies of Matthew and Brandon. Blood spatter stains the windshield, steering wheel, leather interior, and brings the sounds of Tupac Shakur to a stop. Once the gun has been emptied, the attacker takes a long look at his victims. Brandon is barely clinging to life. If luck assists him, he will make it into the ambulance alive, while the best case scenario for Matthew is permanent paralysis from the neck down. Odds are he won't last another hour. The mission has been completed, but the attacker still stands there. Underneath the glare of street lights, a light sneer escapes, almost as if he wants to do more. GO! GO! GO! MOVE SOLDIER! Hearing the voice in his head, the attacker flees into the night. He doesn't make it 20 feet before a multitude of 911 calls have been placed. The soldier in his head will point out the fatal errors and cause many sleepless nights. He stood there too long. His height and weight were established by several people. The police will know the brand of weapon and search for all owners in the area with a .50 caliber. There aren't many. The attacker hopes all of his inside knowledge can keep the focus off long enough for the case to go cold. Furiously pumping his legs, the attacker clears the remaining distance and disappears into the trees, rushing through the branches that slice at his sweater and mask. Move soldier, move soldier, move soldier. Finally out of public eye, the attacker leaps a chain length fence in a single bound and rushes around an unlit home. Most residents of Raleigh neighborhoods turn their lights off when they hear gunshots and this home is no exception. Almost. Almost. Almost. The attacker leaps over the front yard fence and into his waiting vehicle. Rapidly, he reaches into his pocket, grabs the keys, and floors the accelerator, leaving the carnage behind. Once he's a reputable distance away, the mask covering his face is removed. Steven Connors will ditch this particular vehicle and three other vehicles on seldom used back roads before he makes it ten miles from the scene of the crime. For the next 48 hours, he will lay low as he knows those are the most critical hours to a homicide investigation. By the time the first plethora of 911 calls have concluded, Christian Daniels, Devin Shakur, and Jason Hardy are already out of their home, flying toward the scene. They won't beat the ambulances and will be forced to go through the most strenuous wait inside a hospital room imaginable. March 6th, 2009 Wake Medical Community Hospital Time: 8:30 PM Nine hospital staff members had to pin Christian Daniels down and prevent him from breaking into the surgery room to see the damage done to his brothers.  Reports coming over the scanner's police channel were dire and Christian needed to know, even if he was breaking every hospital regulation imaginable. The pleading of Devin and Jason to security was the only thing that saved Christian from spending a night in jail. Instead, two police officers stand feet away, ready to haul him out of the building if he looks at them the wrong way. The trio anxiously waits in silence, huddled together in a triangle to avoid the glances that others in the waiting room are shooting them. They've all had their share of injuries and waited for each other hours upon hours in the ICU waiting room of Wake Medical Hospital, but conditions were never this grave. None of their lives ever hung in the balance. In the twenty plus years of being the patriarch of the household, Christian never experienced someone taking a direct shot at his family. Mike Connors, Lance Robinson, and Marcus Galloway, the previous generation of Connors, were like silly putty in his hands, easy to manipulate and turn against one another. They never came close to killing any of the 'Nefarious Nine' from the Smith family. If the doctor's words, while forcing Christian away from the operating room, were indicative of anything that number would decrease by two once the surgeon emerged. Amidst the gloom, the triumvirate, and most importantly Christian, have to accept that the game has changed since their last collective stint in North Carolina. No longer do they have unlimited control that will always go uncontested. No longer is Christian the alpha dog who orders all the hits. The other side now has a monster of their own, one willing to defend the honor of his family to the very end. If this man is brazen enough to shoot two of his brothers in a crowded street, how devious and conniving can he become under greater pressure? Devin and Jason hold hands and offer the same olive branch to Christian, but the mammoth man refuses. Even though they are his own, this isn't something to pray and be hopeful about. The best possible scenario he overheard is permanent paralysis for both from the neck down and the worst is death. They will never be able to fully recover. Christian takes complete responsibility because this is his fault. Now he must deal with the monster that is putting his foot down for Larry Connors. From around the corner, a man covered in scrubs and a surgeons mask utters, "Mr...Daniels..." Jason and Devin's heads rise to meet the surgeon while Christian barely shoots a glance over his shoulder. Undoing the facial protection, his full and grizzled face is shown to the trio of Smith brothers. Devin's face is full of worry. Christian defines apathy. Jason's former CIA and FBI background training put him into a trance with the surgeon. Before any words come from his mouth, Jason notices the facial features that he never wanted to see. The surgeon's eyes are shifting slightly back and forth, nostrils flaring, fingers slightly twitching, posture slouched, and his lips are curled back in a hint of disgust. He turns around and wraps his arms around Devin, who draws the same conclusion when the surgeon heavily sighs and wipes his brow. The unflinching face of Christian Daniels holds position. He doesn't pay attention to the surgeon telling Devin and Jason that they did everything possible to save Brandon and Matthew's lives. He doesn't listen to anything. He's picturing Steven Connors, a man he drastically underestimated, being beaten to an ungodly pulp by his own two hands. February 25th, 2009 Brown-Wynne Funeral Home "Now if everybody would allow for the family to leave first, you may express your condolences in the next room around the corner." Preacher Rick Wilkerson states, closing the Bible and folding his hands across the Good Book. One pew of grieving family members somberly rise to their feet and slowly exit. Larry and Steven Connors step out first, with Larry having to be held up by Steven just to make it through the door. Behind the brothers are Maria's parents. Charles Stephenson has to assist his elderly wife much in the same fashion that Steven has to hold Larry. Rounding out the pew are aunts, uncles, and cousins from both sides of the family. With a small hint of reluctance, Pastor Rick commands the rest of the mourners to rise pew by pew and depart from the church while a video of memories plays on a monitor against the church wall. The casket is respectfully shut, but everybody feels compelled, obligated, to give one final look. A few of Maria's international business associates deliver a deep bow of respect, hands touching their feet. Once the entire procession has departed, two pairs of Oakley sunglasses capture the attention of Rick Wilkerson. He's more than aware of who is sitting in his church, but the oaths he took over thirty years ago have forced him never to discriminate upon anybody who decides to enter the  house of God. Christian Daniels and Devin Shakur stand to attention and walk out of the pew with military precision, waiting at the back of the line. Their grief is feigned but Rick doesn't know that, and the reassuring hand that Devin places on his shoulder doesn't do much to manifest their deception. "That was a beautiful service, Pastor. I felt it gave her the proper respect she deserved." "Thank you, Devin. How close were you to Maria?" Devin will thank himself later for refusing to remove his sunglasses because his eyes tell a much different story than the rest of his face. "I worked with her multiple times over the last decade. She was a good hearted person and didn't deserve this. Nobody does." With a nod, the Pastor excuses himself and cuts through portions of the line in order to direct traffic. A few elderly women throw themselves into his arms when seeing the 'Memories' video playing on another screen. Christian turns around, takes off his glasses, and rolls his eyes at his brother. The hour long wait will be more than worth it when Larry and Steven get a good look at their faces. In what is known as the Condolences Line, Larry and Steven are standing near the entrance door. Chairs rest immediately behind every family member in case the stress becomes too unbearable and white roses, Maria's favorite, decorate the room. Also showing their support are a good amount of the Raleigh Police department. Even though the Connors have been demoted and thrown off the Smith/Shakur case, officers are always supportive of one another when the death of a dearly beloved is involved. For an hour, Larry and the rest of the family weep uncontrollably. Even a few of the police officers shed a tear for Maria. She was a nice woman, always brought the best blueberry pie to meetings, and had a deep respect for the police force. Steven calculates in his head how many people attended the funeral and knows that not many can be left. He gives a nod of his head, realizing that little more pressure can be applied to this family tonight. They need to go back home and sob in the privacy of their own homes. An immediate wave of disgust fills the room when a man has to duck underneath the top of the door frame and enters the start of the line. Directly behind him is the actual murderer of Maria, Devin Shakur. The Raleigh police on hand can't hide their repulsion, a few sliding their hands around to ensure that their service revolvers are ready to fire. Christian Daniels barely acknowledges their existence, instead looking over at the family. Steven snarls like a pitbull, unintentionally forcing Larry's eyes up to see the cause of his brother's anger. Larry's body collapses into the chair at the sight of Christian. The biker even came dressed for the occasion in an Armani suit. Closing the gap, Christian crouches down to Larry's seated height. Steven and Devin look at one another with blank expressions, Devin still being aided by his Oakleys. For his first time in the role of villain at a funeral home, he feels that his performance is stellar. Larry can't bother to look up, but he knows that a pair of eyes colder than an Antarctic winter are staring at him. With an even more bitter expression, Christian puts his giant hand on Larry's quivering shoulder, "I'm sorry for your loss. Your family will be in my thoughts and prayers." If Steven was allowed, he would reach into Uncle Jim Connors pocket, pull out his service revolver, and put a bullet between Christian's eyes. However, Steven knows that he cannot. In the human game of chess, Christian picked the perfect move and the perfect place to reveal one of his essential plays and express false sentiment. After delivering the same emotionless expression and words to Steven, Christian and Devin exit the room, refusing to acknowledge the rest of the family, business associates, or police department. Christian vowed never to shake the hand of an officer in his life and even in a church, he's going to hold true to his maxim. With the line finished, Larry drops to his knees and covers his face, sobbing uncontrollably. Seeing Christian sent him over the edge and not even the hug of longtime friend Susan Rouse can ease his pains. He wants his vodka. He wants his release. He wants to try and forget. But he'll never forget those eyes. He'll never forget those words for as long as he lives. March 9th Poole Funeral Home Four luxurious cars file into the immense parking lot of Poole Funeral Home. They have the privilege to park wherever their weighed hearts desire since only four other cars fill the lot.  It doesn't take them long to synchronize their parking and pull into the four closest spots to the front door. Stepping from the first car are Christian Daniels and Devin Shakur. Once again, both are dressed to the nines, but the sunglasses from Maria's funeral are gone. Out of the second car appear Jason Hardy and the Smith's professional assassin sister, Rachel. She seldom shows her face in public, but the death of two brothers will bring even the hardest of individuals out from hiding. She can't hide her agony, thick shades covering her otherwise piercing emerald green eyes. From the Hummer drops Michael Blaze, electing to drive on his own because he was the closest to Brandon and Matthew. Three times on the fifteen minute drive over, he pulled over and sobbed. Emerging from the fourth and final car are Chris Smith and Mallory Daniels, husband and wife, immediately clutching each other hand in hand. They have known each other since elementary school and share the deep pain that the other is feeling. Together they make up the remaining seven members of the Smith clan and are here to face the music. They have requested the brothers be given one service because separation would be disrespectful to family unity. Tonight, they have to watch as a preacher makes up some kind words to say about their brothers, encounter old friends who will come to their mansion for an after gathering, leech off their food, and perhaps ask for a little money. The Smith crew don't run with the friendliest of packs. Jason mutters under his breath, choking on his words, "Just wanna get this shit over with. Fuckin' hate funerals." And once they see who is standing by the automatic opening doors, that sentiment is echoed by everybody in the group. Even though the Smith clan have magnificent poker faces, they cannot hide the shock at seeing who has decided to impede their entrance into the church. Larry and Steven Connors, dressed in suits, arms folded downward across their abdomens, imitating soldiers paying tribute to casualties of war. The normal confidence of Larry, invisible over the past few weeks, has resurfaced and he appears cool, calm, and collected. Steven keeps the same stance and face that he used when bringing everybody to this spot. Rachel reaches into her long trench coat pocket, fiddling around and trying to assemble a pistol from scrap parts. Christian looks over and puts a hand over his sister, shaking his head and thwarting her attempts. They all know she could pull the deed off without the Connors boys seeing any of the assembly. Jason presents a ball point pen. In the annals of Washington DC literature, Jason once picked up a book '1000 Ways to Kill Someone with a Ball Point Pen'. Christian turns around and puts a halt to him as well. Meanwhile, the external expression of the brothers hasn't changed. On the inside, their hearts are skipping beats. To see the Smith family stressed and on the defensive is exactly what they wanted. Christian takes the step forward, hands at his side, and walks up to the two brothers who each place a hand on his broad shoulders. Everybody behind Christian once again positions themselves to attack. "We're sorry for your loss. Your family will be in our thoughts and prayers." Christian spots a sliver of grin on Larry's face and growls, showing teeth, trying to avoid starting a fight that would put two more bodies into the grave. He's never felt this kind of rage in his life, but he cannot kill two police officers in a funeral home. Instead, he takes another step forward, forcefully removing Larry and Steven's arms off his shoulders, "This isn't over...You know that and I know that..." Steven looks around Christian's broad shoulders at six malicious lions ready to pounce, and calmly returns his glance up at the gigantic leader. There isn't an ounce of fear in his voice. "We're ready for you Christian...we're ready." Instantly, Christian responds, "No...No you aren't. Nobody in your family has ever been ready. Ask Paps. Ask Marcus and Lance. Ask Maria. Ask Dad." With more velocity, the same two hands place themselves once again on Christian's shoulders. His nostrils flare and fists clinch. Larry and Steven show equal expressions of rage. "Ask your brothers." With those words, Christian is left to stand on the welcome mat. Larry and Steven walk through the Smith clan, muttering the words "Sorry for your loss" until they reach the automatic doors and exit. Jason and Devin swear they see hops in their steps. Fourteen eyes shoot death glares at the Connors Boys but neither Larry or Steven turn back to see any of them. March 9th, 2009 Location: Smith Mansion Entertainment Room Time: 11:45 PM This is the room where everybody from the Smith clan used to live life to the fullest. Their favorite place, the card table where everybody in the room at one point or another attempted to rig the poker game, feels empty. Now two of their own are never going to experience those fun times again. They will never get the chance to tip odds in their favor. They will never again get to watch a basketball game on the massive television screen or listen to the family's eclectic music taste over the speaker system. Christian's expression remains the same from the funeral, a blank sense of rancor. Faint whispers resonated throughout the condolences line about Christian's mental state, but everybody assured the concerned that he would be alright. He doesn't like to show outward emotion. After returning home, he declined tissues and shots of alcohol on multiple occasions from everybody else in the room. They have all openly cried, consoled one another, and shared their favorite memories of Brandon and Matthew. Christian doesn't move a muscle, stuck in a thinking man's pose. If he hadn't gotten himself involved with the motorcycle gang 'Death' to begin with back in 1991, this chain of events 18 years later would not be taking place. He would not have gotten two of his own brothers killed. Yet, even on the defensive, Christian never takes anything lying down. That won't change even with two men he raised resting six feet underneath the Earth. Inhale. Exhale. Hands clamped underneath face and eyes staring straight ahead. While his speech might indicate a man who is not well educated, his intelligence in the art of warfare is second to none. In eighteen years, nobody has ever gotten one up on Christian Daniels. The stakes have always been high, regardless of scenario, and he knew at some point death would come calling for someone close to him. Showing one side of his humanity, he just never thought it would actually happen. His blinking rapidly increases. Christian moves his hands and draws the eyes of everybody in the room. They are analyzing him much in the same capacity they critiqued Devin when Larry brought the pictures of Kimberly to their front door. They know, even in the privacy of his own home, and even amongst his own brothers, he will never cry. When a pair of eyes have laid witness to the amount of bodies and decomposing corpses as Christian's, a tolerance is established and crying evaporates from the lexicon of response. But he intends to make the opposition wail like little children. Mainly, because of who his next target is going to be. Christian looks over at his brothers in arms and finally speaks, "Get Big Slick outta my drawer, figure up a plan...Larry thought seein' his wife was a disgustin' sight...Wait until I cut his daughter from ear to ear. "
