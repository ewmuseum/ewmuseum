The Nobody Puts Up His Dukes, Part II (PTC ETD RP)

24 March 2005
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 03/24/05 Somewhere along the coast of the Gulf of Mexico, a lanky kid with long blonde hair sits down in front of a lap-top computer. Character name: NovaFederation: PRIME “I don’t believe in the sun,How could it shine down on everyone?” - Magnetic Fields, “I Don’t Believe In The Sun,” 69 Love Songs There are no walls, and yet Nova feels claustrophobic, constrained. He can’t see anything…or maybe he can, and everything is just black. He hears a noise and turns around. “Kirk Cameron, star of the family-friendly Disney series, Growing Pains? Dressed in a zombie costume? What are you doing here?” “I’m not really Kirk Cameron, star of the family-friendly Disney series, Growing Pains, dressed in a zombie costume,” Kirk Cameron, star of the family-friendly Disney series, Growing Pains, dressed in a zombie costume, replies, “I’m actually Denis Leary.” Denis Leary rips away the Kirk Cameron, star of the family-friendly Disney series, Growing Pains, dressed in a zombie costume, costume. “Whoa,” Nova replies, “so if you’re really Denis Leary, and not Kirk Cameron, star of the family-friendly Disney series, Growing Pains, dressed in a zombie costume, can I ask you a question?” “Umm…sure,” Denis concedes, “but I was just about to go on one of my long-winded, fast-paced rants, the style of which helped bring my edgy brand of stand-up to the fore-front of 90s comedy.” “Hmm…let’s not do that,” Nova says after a moment of consideration, “okay, here’s my question…is the ‘bad-ass’ persona thing just an overcompensation for only having one ‘s’ in your first name?” “I might be able to answer that question,” Denis begins, “if I were really Denis Leary…” “You’re not?” Nova inquires, lighting up a cigarette. “No,” Denis replies as he begins to rip away his Denis Leary costume, “I’m actually…YOUR CHILDHOOD SOCK-PUPPET, GUYBRUSH THREEPWOOD, JR.! AND I’M HERE TO DEVOUR YOUR ETERNAL SOUL LIKE SO MANY BOXES OF STOUFFER’S LASAGNA!” “NOOOOOOOOOO!!!!” Nova shoots upright in bed, panting heavily as a cold sweat glistens on his upper body. The dream had haunted him for the better part of a year, and he vowed a long time ago to interpret it, no matter the cost. He’ll have the answer…someday…but today is not that day. No, today Nova pursues a wholly unrelated conflict… …today, Nova must find a way to overcome thirteen of PTC’s most dangerous mid-carders. I’ve got three cigarettes, one of which is broken in half, but I’ll smoke it anyway, a Vespa scooter with one tire that’s annoyingly low on air which means the seat will ride into my crotch and make me nauseous, it’s twelve-hundred miles to the HSW show, it’s dark, I’ve got UV-resistant, protective eyewear on, I was lying about the Vespa scooter, and I don’t know how far away the HSW show is… …let’s roll. The Nobody Puts Up His Dukes, Part II “The fungus fell through, man.” Nova watches as a disappointed frown overtakes the painted visage of PRIME’s own psychotic clown, Ozric Mortimer. “How? I thought they were a sure thing.” “The guy thought he was gonna yield twenty ounces, and almost all of ‘em crapped out,” Nova explains, “the batch yielded eight.” “Eight?!” Ozric repeats, dumbfounded, “Jesus H…and we couldn’t grab two eighths out of eight ounces?” “Bought right out,” Nova says with a sigh. “Damn, man…,” Ozric groans, “I really wanted to trip, too. There goes our Tuesday night, eh?” Nova lights a cigarette. “Maybe not…you ever Robo-trip?” “Robo-trip?” “Yeah, man,” Nova says with a grin, “think of it like dirty tripping…you chug four ounces of extra-strength Robitussin and it can rock you pretty hard.” “No kidding?” Ozric replies, skepticism still present in his voice, “I dunno, man…sounds kinda like high school shit to me.” “Say what you want, man,” Nova says with a shrug, “but it does the trick…and how.” “So where do you find this stuff?” Ozric asks. Nova pulls the car into the local Kroger. As the pair walk in, all eyes converge on them…a 6’5”, trench-coat-clad clown and a 6’3” bandanna-sporting, long-haired, fully-bearded hippie. They turn onto the pharmaceuticals aisle, and Nova bends down, snatching up two white boxes. “Here we go.” Oz looks down at the boxes in his friend’s hands. “Maximum-strength cough formula?” “Dextromethorphan is the chemical in Robitussin that fucks you up,” Nova explains, “you don’t want the added sinus relief crap…that stuff’s awful for you. Maximum-strength cough is the way to roll. A four-ounce bottle for each of us sounds about right.” “If you say so,” Ozric murmurs indifferently. “I do, indeed,” Nova replies with a nod. Back in their hotel room in Houston (the site of PRIME’s ReVolution 59), Ozric cracks open his bottle. “Ugh…I haven’t drank this stuff since I was a kid…and I thought a spoonful was bad.” “Yeah, it’s pretty wretched stuff,” Nova agrees, “I’d recommend a Coke or something to help wash it down.” “Do we have any?” Ozric asks. Nova reaches into their mini fridge and pulls out two cans. He tosses one to Ozric and raises his bottle in the air. “A toast! To getting curb-stomped by thirteen men and thus tainting not only my own fragile inter-federated reputation, but the reputation also of my representative home fed! Huzzah!” “To getting bent over a turnbuckle and violated by Angelo Deville, after which he’ll probably break my neck in two places and burn my closest relative’s house down,” Ozric replies, “Cheers!” They turn up their bottles. Ozric takes a long pull and yanks the bottle away from his mouth. “Oh, God…” Nova coughs and almost gags as he sets his bottle down on the counter. “Ugh…” “This shit is awful!” Ozric gasps. “Suffer through, boss,” Nova groans, wincing as he grabs his Coke and chugs part of it, “whew…” Ozric takes another pull from his bottle. “I dunno, Nova…” “Dude, if you puke it up, that’s five bucks down the drain…literally,” Nova says to him before taking another pull from his own bottle. “Fair enough,” Oz replies with a gulp, “so how long does it take to kick in?” “Well, after you finish the bottle, you’ll probably feel like crap for about an hour,” Nova explains, “but then you should be money.” “I feel like crap now,” Ozric replies, coughing after another pull from the Tussin bottle. Thirty minutes later… “Nova, you alright?” Ozric asks. “Gimme a sec,” Nova mumbles, bracing himself against the wall of the bathroom with one hand and running the other through his mane, “Ugh…this stuff can fuck with your stomach worse than shrooms sometimes…” “Tell me about it,” Ozric says with a chuckle, “I felt like I was gonna shit myself for about twenty minutes there…” Nova laughs. “If I didn’t think I would vomit, I’d smoke a cigarette.” Another forty-five minutes pass… “Doesn’t the ground feel bouncy?” Nova says, turning to Oz, “it feels like I’m walking on bubbles.” “Yeah,” Oz agrees, “the ground is…um…bubbles…bubbly.” “Exactly!” Nova yells before breaking out in a fit of laughter, “exactly, man! Shit! It’s like…God, what song…” “‘I’m Walking On Sunshine’!” Oz finishes his friend’s thought. “That’s it!” Nova replies quickly, “only…instead of sunshine…” “It’s bubbles!” Oz says, a note of intellectual triumph in his voice. “Holy crap,” Nova says suddenly, his eyes growing wide, “dude…are you reading my mind?” “Um…I dunno,” Oz replies, confused. “Let’s test it,” Nova decides, “I’m gonna think of something, and you tell me what it is.” Nova closes his eyes and squints. Oz stares at him intently for a moment. “What your thinking about is…homicide and discreet body disposal.” “Jesus, no!” Nova recoils at his friend’s morbid response, “I was thinking about, what if guys could, ya know, stick it in the pooper and get each other pregnant, and if they could, what the lovechild of Columbo and Matlock would look like.” “Oh,” Ozric replies, disappointed. “Let’s go to the pool,” Nova says, brushing off their failed telepathy experiment. “Ahh,” Ozric sighs happily as he slides into the heated pool, being careful not to splash water on his face-paint, “I feel…great.” “CAN-OPENER!” Nova shrieks, landing in the water and causing an enormous sonic-boom splash effect that cascades over Ozric. “Dude, chill on the splashes!” Oz yells. “Sorry,” Nova offers weakly, “I forgot about your face-paint…” “You forgot about my face-paint?” Oz repeats incredulously, “how? How is that possible? That’s like me forgetting you have long hair!” “Dude, sorry,” Nova replies softly, “Jesus, call the Angry Police, Ozric Mortimer is radiating negative vibes…” “Sorry, I just have a thing with getting my face wet,” Ozric says apologetically. Nova paddles around the pool for a moment. “Oz, do you think I can win the Royale?” “Oh, my God,” Ozric sighs, “we’re in a heated swimming pool, trying to relax, hyped up on dextro-whatever-the-hell-you-said-it-was, and you wanna talk about wrestling.” “What can I say, man?” Nova says with a shrug, “it’s been on my mind.” Oz tucks his thick black hair back and removes a joint from behind his ear. He puts it in his mouth and lights it, blowing smoke out of his nose. “Man, I know what your problem is…” “What?” Nova asks, “because I feel really unprepared for some reason, and I don’t know if…” “If you’d hold up a sec,” Oz interrupts, “I might tell you. I think your problem is…you’re not thinking about this like a regular wrestling match.” He passes Nova the joint and stands up to his full height in the pool, accentuating his words of wisdom with exaggerated hand motions. “You’re thinking about this like…like…” Oz holds one hand high into the air and the other just above the water. “You’re thinking about this like PTC is way up here, and PRIME is way down here. It’s not, man. It’s just another match. You got your head all fucked up putting this Royale up on a pedestal like it’s the Clash of the Titans. I mean, if these guys were so good, wouldn’t they have been picked by their respective federations for that Seven of the Best thing?” “Hey!” Nova snaps. “Okay, let’s rewind a little bit,” Oz back-pedals, “but seriously, man, you have to go into this match like it ain’t no thing…like it’s just another week in PRIME.” “How do I do that?” Nova asks, exhaling deeply into the air before handing the hooter back to Oz. “Man, you’re six-and-two in PRIME,” Oz says before taking a puff, “that’s nothing to sniff your nose at. Just wrestle these guys like you wrestled Tony Rolo. Wrestle them like you wrestled Razor Blade, and JTF, and me, for Christ’s sake.” “And what if I wrestle them like I wrestled Karina Wolfenden, or Hoyt Williams?” Nova interjects, “which, in case you missed it, was ‘very shittily.’” “You can’t,” Oz replies non-chalantly. “Oh?” Nova cocks an eye-brow, “and why is that, sir?” “Because ‘shittily,’ isn’t a word,” Oz explains, “I typed it into Word once, and a little red squiggle came up underneath it.” “Oh, well, if Microsoft Word says it, it must be true,” Nova says with a roll of his eyes, “I take it you also trust the tabloids and the inter-web, right? Well, fine, I’ll rephrase…what if I fuck up? That blunt enough for ya?” “So what if you do, man?” Oz says, “it won’t be the end of the world. What you have to get into that drugged-up skull of yours is that respect doesn’t come only with victory. It comes with giving your opponents a hell of a fight, and anyone who thinks you’re not gonna give those cats a hell of a fight is an idiot.” Oz hands the joint back to Nova. “I’m going up to the room, man.” “I’ll see you up there,” Nova says with a nod. Ozric returns the nod and climbs out of the pool. Nova lies back against the concrete steps of the shallow end and takes a deep drag from the spliff. He watches the wind whip the smoke around in the air, and thinks about what Oz said, on top of what Rage told him and the words of encouragement from his wife. It looks like the only one who doesn’t believe I can do this…is me. They’re all right, of course. I can do this. I’m as capable as any of the men I’m competing against. It’s just that I’ve never had this many eyes on me at once…all waiting for me to make a wrong step. I know I have a tendency to over-analyze things. Truth be told, the times in my career where I’ve truly excelled, truly raised the bar for myself in terms of competitive performance, I’ve just gone out there and, to quote a ridiculous, embarrassing pop-culture slang phenomenon, “gotten ‘er done.” I guess I just have to do it. … So let’s do it.
