The Game, And How To Play It

30 June 2007
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: 6/30/07 (Saturday) Recognition slowly set in for the man on the other side of the train, and he shifted uncomfortably. With an eyebrow half-cocked, he droned out the conversation of the rest of his party - an exhilarating discourse on fantasy football - and slowly scanned the crowd. Yes, Larry Johnson vs. Frank Gore was a pivotal topic and his voice needed to be heard...but he was being watched. He knew it. The car was packed with people heading, like he and his friends, to the Dodgers game. There was no way the average person could find their stalker in the mass of people. Then again, the average person didn’t watch as much Law & Order as he did. It wasn’t any of the chicks - none of them had that kind of allure. After all, a superspy needed to be ready to get busy with someone to prevent her cover being blown, right? Everyone knew that. Besides, in keeping with tradition, none of the ladies on the train had given him a second glance. They were sober, after all. It wasn’t the homeless or near-homeless guys, either. They called too much attention to themselves by asking for cash and muttering incoherencies. It was possible that one of them had stared him down after being denied a handout. He always thought privately that he looked more affluent than he actually was...considerably more affluent, on second thought. But this was a dangerous kind of feeling he had, the kind that results in getting kidnapped and shipped to China for two years of torture, not the kind that results in getting stabbed in the side by a rusty makeshift shiv. Quietly and methodically, he narrowed the list of suspects to three: 1. The father holding the son. Kid couldn’t have been more than five. Probably a doll. If they can do all that shit with the robot cars for that new movie, then they sure as heck can make a lifelike boy robot. 2. The man in the suit. What the hell is wrong with him, conducting business on a Saturday? A Saturday when BRAD F’N PENNY is pitching against the Pads, for Chrissakes! Is he not American? By default, that makes him suspect #1, ignoring the previous order of this list. 3. The douche sitting a few rows away. Actually, he didn’t look like a spy at all, but the just-bought-it-yesterday freshness of his jersey and cap really turned the man off. Plus he was wearing those Oakley-looking sunglasses with the orange lenses that aren’t particularly wrong or offensive, but which always make you think mean things about the person wearing them. This guy was a cock, but not a spy. Still, he seemed familiar somehow. The man purses his lips and shifted his body again, trying to disguise the fact that he was giving everyone a second pass. A few rows away, behind a set of orange lenses, Danny Ferguson made a mental note of the man’s body language and then looked out the window at the approaching stadium. You learn a little about self-disguise from Michael Mann. The director's penchant for having actors BECOME their roles led to a lot of in-depth character studies and a virtual boot camp of how to adopt a personality. One learns to notice a hitch in someone's step, or a way they drag out their vowels, or a particular way of wincing - little things that help define the performance. Naturally, these tricks stay with the actors long after shooting, and they prove particularly helpful as Hollywood party tricks, or, say, disappearing into a train full of people on the way to the Dodgers game. The train pulled to a stop and everyone in blue and white started to shift toward the doors. Danny followed suit, shuffling as best he could to get close to his subject. The ebb and flow of the crowd pushes him down the aisle, right in front of the man he’d been studying, and it was time for the ultimate test of his inconspicuousness. "This the stop for the game?" he asks the man. Snapped out of his crowd-surveying, the man looked at Danny and turned a darker shade of pink. A real fan would know where the damn stadium is, he thought. Who the fuck does this jackass think he is? Someone next to the man nodded yes. "Thanks, bro," Danny replied, heading towards the door as his new friend continued to give him ‘the look.’ As he shifted out with the rest of the fans, picking up the stiff-legged limp that everyone walks with in sports-event crowds, he couldn’t help but smile at how well that went. If Danny had smarter friends, they might muse about the juxtaposition: a man who Hollywood had all but swallowed whole was now enjoying double the freedom that he once had. They tell you that you can get away with anything when you're famous. The problem is that you rarely get a chance to DO anything worth getting away with. Angry publicists, studio reps and the advent of blogs and camera phones made Danny and his peers virtual prisoners of their own $20 million paydays. Of course, complaining about that problem would be like throwing vodka on a fire just because it's wet. So you don't say anything, you 'suck it up' as the Internet paparazzi so eloquently demand, and you let the consumers do as their name implies and eat you up slowly, instead of in one giant bite. In the shadow of Dodger Stadium, Danny paused at a corner, craning his neck to check down both streets, but still maintaining his adopted mannerisms and body language. Maybe he's not that good at this, he thinks. Maybe people don't recognize celebrities so much as detect them, as if star power sets off an internal radar system in Joe Average. Or maybe people are so used to seeing the same photos and promotional b-roll footage being used to talk about the latest "Fall of Danny Ferguson" story - his agent quitting or his publicist quitting or his studio dropping his contract or whatever was next - that they couldn't process him in jeans and a Dodger jersey. Without the suit, without the bodyguards, and most noticeably, without the crowd of casual gawkers, he is goddamn stealth, and not that stupid Jamie Foxx movie. Like, real stealth. Top Gun stealth. "Fuck my momma, supastar, you really just bullshittin' out here by the bleachers?" Well, mostly stealth. With a smile and a shake of the head, Danny turns to greet Matthew McConaughey, the proclaimer of the above vulgarities. He's got the same 'yeehaw' grin that he has every time you ever see him, and that's probably partially due to the model-or-at-least-softcore-porn-actress-hot brunette on his arm. McConaughey reaches out for a hug, then gives Danny some awkward southern boy dap. "What up, DF, sellin' dick for two cheap seats?" "Just waitin' on you, cowboy," Danny says. "I'm impressed you made it within 15 minutes of the opening pitch." "Shiiiit," Double M drawls back. "I'm Johnny-On-The-Fucking-Spot lately, ever since the gaddamn LA County court took the license away. Speed Racer here operates within a set of traffic laws that ain't exist anywhere in the 48 contiguous." With that, the brunette gives Matthew and elbow en route to offering the hand to Danny. "I'm Julie," she insists. Danny removes his hat to greet her and reveals a distinct absence of his trademark red locks - replaced instead with the kind of blonde-orange stubble that comes in before it gains full color. Matt grimaces a little at the look, pretty shocked. "Damn, brotha, you lose a bet?" Ferguson runs a hand over the fresh stubble, still getting used to the feeling himself. He lets the hand drift all the way back, then slides it to his neck and back around to his chin, where an equally-long five o'clock shadow has grown in. "Just trying something new while I have the opportunity...or lack thereof." "You look like a fucking bum." "That means a lot coming from you." Julie purses her lips thoughtfully before chiming in and changing the subject. "So you're the wrestler-guy?" Danny hangs his head and Matthew shoots his gal a look. He prepares to offer an apology on her behalf, but the PRIMEate looks back up. "Yeah. That's me." "Sorry," she says, "I-" "It's fine. I've just taken a lot of shit for it lately. For being the 'wrestler guy.'" "These motherfuckers gotta run their mouths 'bout someone, D," McConaughey offers. "It's just your time around the track." "I just don't get it, Matty. I mean, I get the shit with Allan. I know where those articles come from. I know why people think I can't survive in town without him. But why does a side gig mean anything? All the screenwriters smoke pot, all the actors snort coke and all the execs put needles in their arms. I like to beat people up. Why is my quote-unquote addiction so much worse than everyone else’s?" "Because that shit’s out front. You stumble in this fuckin' race and the rest of the herd will eat you alive. Ya ain't gotta outrun the bear to survive in Hollywood, ya just gotta outrun the S.O.B to yer right." Matthew regrets the comparison nearly instantly, recognizing that he was practically lapping Danny in a footrace with his multiple projects in the pipeline. Before he could offer some positive reinforcement, Julie yearned to be part of the conversation once more. "Did you, um, know that guy?" Danny sighed. "Janie-" "Julie." "-I'll tell you the same thing I told Larry King, the same thing I told Nancy Grace and the same thing I told Fox: I didn't know him, I never met him and I don't see any similarities between the two of us. There's nothing I can add to the conversation." Julie and Matthew both give the "oh" nods and a few seconds of awkward silence passes before McConaughey resorts to his usual icebreaker - ball busting. "Not like you to turn down face time, kid. You sure you ain't fucked up?" "Heh," Danny offers, looking away at the crowds filing into the stadium. "I just...I think I just like being off the grid for a while." To punctuate his statement, a fan approaches Matthew with a pen for an autograph. He sighs and tries to hide the frustration. "Hey, I'll catch you in there, partner. Where you sitting?" "204, row b-" "I'm in 203, dude. I'll find ya." "Awesome!" The fan skips away and Double M spitefully turns back to his company. "Two-oh-fucking-four," he curses quietly. "Maybe I'll see you on the kiss-cam sucking your friend's cock, assface." "See what I mean?" Danny smirks. The pariah role took some getting used to, but once he'd learned to love it, he'd wondered how he ever lived before. "I walk among them, but I am not one of them." "That Yoda?" "Something like that." "Maybe I should tank my fucking career so I can ride a limo to a baseball game and still pretend I'm 'the common man.'" "Um, a, you can eat shit. B, I rode the train." At the second part, both McConaughey and his female companion practically jump back, completely startled at the possibility. It was as if they had no idea who this person was in front of them. "Dry-humpin' Christ, Danny, if shit is that hard for you I'da spotted you cash for a rental. Or I could've asked Dale Jr. to swing by and pick you up. I swear, she drives at the speed of fuckin' light, we still would've made it here before the anthem." Julie shoots him a look, which he blows off. Danny chuckles. "It ain't about cash, Mick. I mean, after all," he laughs, puffing out his chest a little, "you're talking to a guy raking in $50k PER SCREEN this weekend." Matthew playfully dismisses him with a wave. "Limited release." "High demand." "There was high demand for Brokeback, too." "Weren't you supposed to be in it?" Danny needles back, smiling ear-to-ear. "Oh, fuck you." McConaughey sputters. "That was just a video of you blowing me." "Well I guess now we know who everyone was paying to see." Matthew tries to come up with something, but he's speechless at Danny's last jab. Julie breaks out laughing and her man turns a little red, not quite mad but admittedly defeated. "Fine," he manages, "go earn your indy paychecks and be king of the no-names, see if I care." "I'm telling you, man, it's exhilirating. You should try being a Hollywood castoff for a while. You really get to have some fun. Everyone tells me I need to close this wound and get myself back on track...but I kinda want to let it bleed out for a while." "It's like the Prince and the Pauper," Julie interjects. Almost immediately, the two men fall silent and stare at her. "The what?" Matthew asks. "Is that Paramount?" Danny adds. She giggles. "Sorry. Aiming a little high with that reference, I guess." Danny and Matt exchange a look. Ferg gives him a shrug and McConaughey adopts a wise-ass smirk. "You'll have to excuse her. Julie goes to night school. She likes to mouth off when she forgets who paid for her tits." "I heard that," she grumbles. "I SAID it so you would hear it," he snaps, half-turning his head to look her in the eye. "Now which one of us is the fucking idiot?" She falls silent and Matthew nods, content with his work. He turns his full attention to his fellow actor and fans out a set of tickets. "'Bout time to hit the Home Plate Club, son." "You sure you want to be seen in there next to the industry joker?" "Not like they're gonna recognize you in that 'shamefully single' look you're sporting. Besides, I should ask you the same fuckin' question. You sittin' next to my People's Most Beautiful ass will screw up all this goddamn art house cred you somehow picked up." "Whatever. When summer '09 comes around you'll be begging to be double-billed next to me." "LA's got a long fuckin' memory, Danny. I can't make it three months without some asshole on TV making a naked bongos joke. You know this." Behind them, the PA announcer in Dodger Stadium crackled to life, starting to rattle off the starting lineups. Danny pauses for a second before giving his friend a nod. "The critics will remember, but they only see the movies once and they never pay. The box office will forget all about this in a year or so." "Some year." McConaughey gestures open-handedly toward the crowd, directing them towards the stadium. "I've had worse." The three begin assimilating into the masses who are making their way into the stadium. "Nice jersey, by the way. Glad you had time to stop at Target on your way." "I bought this online!" "When'd you take it out of the plastic, an hour ago?" "Fuck off, ok?" Slowly and surely, they disappear into the crowd, and for the first time in the last month, Danny feels comfortable.
