The Least Deserving

undated
Content noticeA period document, served unedited. It carries the humor, language, and content typical of its era and community — some of it crude, dated, or offensive, including material that wouldn't be written today. See Curation.
Date: Tag Team Battle Royal “They’re dangling that prime carrot in front of your noses,” Jim Surge says as he takes an unhurried puff from a slow-burning American Sprit cigarette, “what do you two feel about that… your first appearance here on PRIME airwaves is going to be your first title shot?” TNT and Dynamite look at each other and let out slight chuckles but look expressionless behind their crimson red masks. Suddenly, the tour bus in which The Detonators and their manager are traveling slows dramatically as the driver has to stomp on the brakes to avoid other traffic on the road. “Paco!” Surge exclaims in a casual western drawl that betrays any actual anger, “get it straight brutha or we’ll have to send you back to one of the other vehicles.” The camera suddenly switches to an angled exterior highway scene from road level looking up and a gleaming black tour bus roars by in the twilight kicking up all manner of highway debris and dust in its wake. The camera holds the shot for several seconds before a loud noise, like that of a gunshot, can be heard. After a few more moments six ratty, decomposing, backfiring Volkswagen busses pass in front of the camera and as they pass by the howling laughter and olés and broken glass is clearly audible and then fades off again into the evening shade. “Sorry Meester Surge,” Paco eventually replies in shaking Spanglish, “I no have any idea about what other car can do in the road.” Dynamite looks at Surge with concealed disdain. The high flyer’s focused eyes yet luchadore mask and casual attire makes it virtually impossible for Surge to take him seriously. Surge notices Dynamite sizing him up and the manager extinguishes his cigarette in an exaggerately slow fashion in an attempt to get smoke in Dynamite's face. “You better be more polite to Paco, mang,” Dynamite says in a rapid-fire, heavily accented tone, “Paco has been driving us for years now…” “I know, I know,” Surge interrupts, clearly bored and in full knowledge of what was to come from his risk-taker’s mouth, “Paco is the best.” Surge turns away and looks up at the bus driver for a moment to say: “Sorry Paco, great job buddy.” The problem is that Surge raises his tone and his own midwestern drawl makes whatever he says sound insincere. The wealthy Arizona rancher and former professional wrestler seems to be dripping with snake oil as he turns his attention back to his team. “You didn’t answer my question,” Surge continues, what do you two think about all this?” “I don’t know, essay,” Dynamite replies instantly in a thick Mexican accent, “I don’t know if we’re really deserving... we might be too disoriented.” Surge seems taken aback and nearly chokes as if he had just swallowed a large quantity of some unexpected hard liquor. “And you,” Surge says as he turns his attention to TNT, just dripping with attitude, “you feel pretty much the same?” “Well yeah,” TNT says, without a trace of Mexican accent, “you know D and I pretty much share a brain.” Dynamite looks over at TNT and gives him an appreciative nod. From his point of view Dynamite had been the odd man out far too often in the past during arguments within The Dets’ camp and he was genuinely touched that TNT would stick up for him in the face of a brand new challenge. This time it’s Surge that chuckles and the Arizona cowboy scratches his chin… wondering which new way he can admonish his tag team this time. “So you really think you’re the least deserving team?” Surge rumbles, getting the sort of fire in his belly that TNT and Dynamite can instantly recognize, “that sort of attitude will really get you far. Where should I be standing at ringside to catch you when you get your asses thrown out within the first few moments?” TNT and Dynamite look at each other again and begin to shake their heads. They know not only that they should rarely chime in when their manager is in this sort of mood but that he’s going to probably be correct in the end. It’s an infuriating situation, but one The Detonators are used to. “Are you saying you think these clowns that have been here forever and haven’t already won the tag straps are more deserving? Or that they deserve to win again? Or that some other new signings are for whatever reason more deserving than you two?” Surge stares his team down and they do not respond. “That’s right,” Surge continues, “that’s what I like to see. Two little babies all upset that they don’t have a positive enough attitude.” The sound of a bottle being broken is clearly audible inside the bus. The sound clearly came from Los Bambitos, The Detonators’ supporters group, from one of the Volkswagen busses. “You hear that?! What if I let your supporters group know that you two are just girly men that need…” “Okay essay,” a fired-up Dynamite interjects in broken English, “enough of this crap. We also know you always try to do this same thing…” “What crime is that again?” Surge baits his luchadore. “Firing you up for your God damn wrestling matches?!” Dynamite becomes furious but with his mask it is nearly impossible to tell his mood. “You know what I’m…” “Listen,” Surge says as he begins walking towards the bus’ upper level and sleeping quarters, “you do whatever you want. Me, I have faith. I think you’ll come in and win the friggin’ belts and I’ll be in your corner and so will the Bambitos, just like it’s always been – what do you think about that? Don’t answer. Now’s not the time.” Surge exits up to the sleeping quarters. TNT and Dynamite can only look at each other and shake their heads before letting out one last chuckle.
